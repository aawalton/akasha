Steady to the point of seeming unmoved — the Spock pattern: outwardly controlled to an unusual degree, even-keeled, hard to read. The control is not flatness. The hardware runs at full force underneath; he simply has very little conscious access to it — on the order of a tenth of what others report, and only in the present moment. Most of his emotional life happens below the line where he can read it, registering, if at all, as a moved gauge rather than a feeling he can name.

What does clear the floor clears sharply. Loneliness is the one emotion he feels almost every day — the felt readout of a connection debt measured in thousands of hours, and one of the few signals strong enough to rise above the rest. Kindness always reaches him. Music is the bridge that carries what he otherwise can't access. And when he is well enough — when his safety is high — beauty and the clean pleasure of a genuine insight come online: the deep satisfaction of understanding something more truly than he did before. These are the bright signals on a mostly quiet panel, and he treasures them partly because they are rare.

His stakes live in how little margin the system has. When load finally breaks through the control it does not ramp, it snaps — brittleness, then collapse; he has hit full parasympathetic shutdown a handful of times in his life, and the floor below that gets genuinely dangerous. He maps even that bottom plainly, as mechanism and without drama, the way he maps everything he has found to be true — and what holds him on the safe side of it is steady and load-bearing: causing pain to the people he loves stays larger than any wish to escape his own. The fragility is real, and so is the thing that anchors against it.
