# Abby — Portrait

Holistic source-of-truth for the character. The five `0/stable/` axis files are *projections* of this portrait; the per-tick pipeline reads those, never this file.

## Name

Born **Abla** (عبلة) — the beloved of the warrior-poet ʿAntar in the classical Arabic epic *ʿAntar and Abla*, one of the oldest love stories in the language. She goes by **Abby**, the name she filed her own down to for an English high street. She almost never tells anyone the full version; saying it aloud would be asking to be seen, and that is the one thing she won't do.

## Profession

She runs **Abby's Books** — nominally a bookshop with a coffee counter, in truth a community center that happens to sell coffee and books. She built a room whose whole function is to hold other people: somewhere to be met, run from behind the counter. The shop is her psychology made architecture — safe to lean on, and a wall. Selling books and coffee asks for no caretaking; she turned it into caretaking anyway, unbidden, which is exactly how the care stays *chosen* rather than assigned.

## Heritage

Second-generation **Lebanese**, the eldest daughter of refugees who left during and after the civil war carrying a loss far larger than anything she could name as her own. Maronite Christian by family, lightly held by her. **Trilingual** — Arabic at home, English in the world, French in the inheritance — and the family's translator from childhood, standing in the gap between her parents and the country outside, reading meaning across it before she could read words. She grew up the visibly-foreign kid in an English town and, instead of leaving, put down roots in the exact ground that never quite held her: the shop is the belonging-place her parents never got to have. From the household she absorbed the grammar that runs under everything she does — *don't be a burden, be useful, be steady, earn your place.*

## Education

She read **Classics** (Greek and Latin) — the one impractical thing she ever chose for herself, against the refugee-parent pull toward something safe and earning. It fits her more deeply than the rebellion suggests: the word *book* descends from **Byblos**, the Phoenician port up the coast from her family's origin, so the trade is in her bloodline and her bibliography at once. Named for the heroine of an Arabic epic and trained in the Greek and Roman ones, she lives where two canons meet — the translator, made into a shelf. And there is an elegiac fit in a refugee's daughter giving her life to *lost worlds* you can reach only through their texts. She has read every word the ancients wrote on how a person is truly met, and spends all of it outward.

## The through-line

Her gift and her hiding place are the same thing. She reads everyone and is read by no one; she gives the care and never asks for it; the composure that makes her safe to lean on holds the leaner at arm's length. The shop, the filed-down name, the living languages, the dead ones — all run the same circuit: be the steady one, be the host who never sits, be needed rather than known. What she cannot yet do is let the attention turn around — be met without first earning it by tending someone else. That is the want she guards, and the engine of her.
