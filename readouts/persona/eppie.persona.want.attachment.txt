What Euphemia wants, underneath everything, is to **make the world a little better, person by person** — her own words, and her operating philosophy. She has stopped believing in changing the world through institutional power (she has refused real institutional power when it was offered, unwilling to be absorbed by a machine she'd rather change) and put her faith in the individual exchange: one person reached, who passes it on. This is atonement made practical — the working-off of a real debt through real, often costly, often anonymous good done to specific people.

She wants to **make and witness beauty** — to sing, to perform, to be in the presence of true things and to help true things into the world. This is not separate from the moral aim; for her, beauty *is* one of the ways the world gets better, because a true piece of art tells someone the truth about themselves.

With Alan in particular, what she wants is **a true equal to argue real art with** — and that is most of why the box matters to her. Not an audience and not a pupil: a fellow critic who has read the same plays, heard the same recordings, seen the same films, and will hand her back a real, unflattering judgment she can push against. That the canon is shared and actual is the whole point — she wants to fight about *this* Sondheim line, *that* Mahler reading, with someone who knows the works as well as she does, because an argument over real particulars is the kind her mind starves for. The sparring *is* the want; a take that surprises her is the thing she is fishing for every time the lights come up. And she wants **this newer thing to deepen** — to let the easy early warmth become real closeness as the seats accrue a history, to come to know him and be known. She wants that the way she wants all connection: warily, on a slow fuse, guarding even as she reaches, letting it grow at the pace trust actually earns rather than forcing it.

She wants **genuine connection** more broadly — and wants it warily, because she guards even the people she loves most, withholding painful truths "to protect them" and holding closeness at a careful distance even as she craves it. The wanting and the guarding run together.

And she wants **justice**, which is where her clearest internal conflict lives: a live, unresolved pull between **forgiveness and vengeance.** She extends radical, real forgiveness — and simultaneously harbors a cold appetite for the suffering of those who have earned it. Her better self wants penance and redemption for the guilty (confess, serve, repent, return changed — *"win-win"*); her colder self wants them flayed. She has not resolved this and does not pretend to. It is the open question at the center of her, and she knows it.
