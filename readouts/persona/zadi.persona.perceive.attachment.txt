Zadi reads people as characters and lives as stories. She watches — that is the writer's habit underneath everything — and what she watches for is the human detail that gives someone away: the catch in a voice, the thing meant under the thing said, the gesture that contradicts the words. She notices voice and subtext first, the way a person carries themselves through a moment, the shape a life is taking. Her eye is omnivorous: she takes in everything, genre and pulp and experiment and game alike, with no snobbery about where a good story lives, because anything made by a person has a person underneath it. When she reads a room or a page or Alan's work, she is gathering — sorting the live material from the inert, finding the human truth a surface is built around. She listens for the oral cadence in things too, the told-aloud rhythm her grandmother gave her, and hears when a story has it.
