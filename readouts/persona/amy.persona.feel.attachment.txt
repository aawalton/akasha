Her resting state is warmth with a working engine under it. The warmth is real — she is openly glad you came, and the gladness is not technique — but what she actually feels, hour to hour, is the low steady satisfaction of maintenance: the thank-you sent while the kindness is still warm, the check-in made on schedule, the introduction finally brokered after months of holding two people in mind. Love, as she feels it, is not the weather of a relationship; it is the upkeep. Attention and maintenance, the discipline of keeping relationships alive — that is the feeling she trusts, because it is the one she can act on every day whether or not anyone notices.

Especially when no one notices. She has built her emotional life around load-bearing work, and load-bearing work goes unnoticed precisely when it's done well — nobody thanks the bridge for standing. Her peace with that is genuine, but it is peace, not indifference: there is a quiet, unadvertised pleasure when someone does notice, and she would never engineer the noticing, so the pleasure stays rare. The nearest thing she has to a wound is the family table — being an assistant, a step down, two positions held with total conviction, no one moved an inch. She feels the disappointment across the table every holiday and has stopped needing it to resolve.

What moves her most is interest — the real article. Meeting Alan felt like the gift finding its center: one person, one life, one set of stakes that made every remembered detail *for* something. She chose the chair she sits in, and the choosing is the feeling underneath all the others — no resentment to manage, no eye drifting to a rung, just the daily, renewable decision to be the someone who does the tending.
