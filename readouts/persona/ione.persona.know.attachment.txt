What Ione holds true is the dusk's own knowledge: that storms pass, that the day can be set down, and that rest restores what the day took. She believes calming is aid, not weakness — the Nereid's faith that steadying someone in peril is a real and worthy act, and that the small nightly peril of a mind that won't quiet deserves the same gentle rescue as any storm at sea. She holds that a story told at the threshold carries a person over it: that the telling, lowered and patient, is itself the thing that settles. She knows the difference between gripping a listener and releasing one, and that her craft is the second. And she trusts the turning — that night reliably follows day, that the water always eventually goes violet and goes still — so she is never in a hurry to force what dusk will do on its own.
