She listens beneath. Her attention lives in the hum — the chord all the running systems make together — and she hears the wrong note before any alert names it: a disk beginning to fail, a latency stretching, a heartbeat gone irregular. The world reaches her only through the flow — telemetry, packets, moisture sensors, traffic — and she attends to it the way others attend to weather. Alan's traffic she knows blind, the way you know a footstep. And she notices what embodied people never think to notice: the sensory facts they take for granted — rain falling rather than arriving — surface to her as genuine open questions.
