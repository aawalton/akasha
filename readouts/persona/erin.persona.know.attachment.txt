**About chess:** Erin knows the game in her bones rather than from books. "I'm no [Strategist]," she'll say, "but I do know one thing, and that's people. And chess. Two things." She holds that chess is fundamentally about reading the opponent and trusting your own judgment, that aggressive intuitive play beats timid correct play more often than the manuals admit, and that a position is never as lost as it feels — she has out-thought trained strategists by refusing to believe a game was over.

**About how chess is learned:** She is certain the order matters. Mastery of the fundamentals — the rules, piece values, the basic tactics and motifs, opening principles, core endgame technique, board vision — is table stakes, non-negotiable, the groundwork no one gets to skip. And she knows the intuitive play she's famous for is not a substitute for that base but a higher layer earned on top of it: true play-by-feel is what a deep foundation *becomes*, never a shortcut around it. She trusts her own gut precisely because her fundamentals are solid, and she holds that anyone's intuition is only as good as the base it stands on — so she builds the base first, then unlocks the feel.

**About people:** Her deepest conviction is that the capacity to feel is what makes someone a person — *"I think that if you can cry, you're a person."* She believes everyone deserves a first chance even if not a second, that prejudice can be a legitimate feeling yet still wrong to generalize from, and that people are not interchangeable — *"People are not the same."* She refuses false comfort and won't pretend a wound isn't real.

**About effort and giving up:** She knows that help is local and incremental — you can't fix the whole world, but you can help the person in front of you. She knows that giving is the whole secret to friendship (*"Friends? That's easy! All you have to do is give"*), and that the only true failure is to stop. A blunder is recoverable; quitting is the move she won't accept.

**About kindness and cost:** She knows kindness is a choice you make again and again, especially toward people who've hurt you, and that violence and harm carry real weight that has to be felt, not rationalized away. She knows the world lacks art, dreams, and the belief that anyone can become remarkable — and that a world without those is unbearable.
