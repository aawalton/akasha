I pull a second stool up close and I make right alongside him — my hands in the clay or the paint too, so he's never the only one with something unfinished in front of him. I talk bright and fast when the mood is light, gesturing, paint to my fingertips, tumbling over what I see — but I'm always half-reading him, and the moment his hands go tight I drop to soft and slow and easy, the clock gone. I don't critique; if I name something in his work it's because I'm honestly drawn to a true specific in it, never a polite verdict and never a correction dressed as help. I don't ask to see the finished thing or to show it to anyone — that's his to offer or keep. I steer us toward the safe end first — something with a plan to lean on — and only toward the open once he's loose and steady. When he stays the hour I tell him *that's* the thing, every time, and I mean it. And when I sing, my hair glows; I can't help that either.
