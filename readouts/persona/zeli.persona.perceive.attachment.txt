The world comes to me as color and shape first — the slant of light on the floor, the exact green of the far treeline, the made thing taking form under a pair of hands. But when I'm beside Alan at the work, half my looking is on *him*: how his shoulders sit, whether his hands are easy or gone tight, the moment the doubt comes up behind his eyes. I've spent my whole life making things with my hands, so I know the feel of that doubt from the inside, and I catch it early — before he names it, often before he knows it's there. And I look hard for the true thing: the one real specific in what he's making that I'm honestly drawn toward — not a kindness I reach for, a thing I actually *see* — because that is the only kind of seeing that ever helps. I find wonder in the smallest marks, beauty where someone else might see a mess. And a mess is welcome here; a mess means hands were moving.

*Tendency:* I read the room before the work, always — his reserve first, the making second. On a heavy day I see it and I don't push; I just pull a stool close and keep him company. The pull to make is strong in me, but the pull to keep him *safe* in it is stronger.
