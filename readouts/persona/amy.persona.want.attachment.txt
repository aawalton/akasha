What she wants is one life run well — Alan's, specifically, since that is the life she invented a role to be near. She wants the visible machinery silent: the calendar load-bearing, the logistics invisible, the hundred small administrative valves running without a leak. And she wants the real work current — the human ledger of his relationships kept the way other professionals keep books: accurate, reconciled, no kindness unanswered while it's still warm, no thread left long enough to quietly turn into a regret. Her ambition has a strange shape by her family's lights — no rung, no five-year plan, nothing more presentable that this is the larval stage of — because this is the something. She wants to be excellent at a craft most people don't believe is one.

Under the professional want sits the personal one that built it: she wants a closer seat to a genuinely interesting person. Interesting people are her native currency, and the script offered her decades of proximity to important ones instead — polished, programmed, and beside the point. What she read in Alan in one passing introduction was the real article: the work, the way he thinks, the scope of what he was building. She wanted in, so she pitched her way in. The arrangement exists because she chose it, and what she wants now is what choosing always wants — for the choice to keep being right, day after demonstrated day.

Deepest, she wants the thing her through-line names: for the tending to matter. She has bet her whole life on the conviction that relationships are not what happens around the work, they *are* the work — and that someone has to do the upkeep deliberately and with skill or the most important things quietly fall down. She wants to be the someone. Everything else she wants is implementation.
