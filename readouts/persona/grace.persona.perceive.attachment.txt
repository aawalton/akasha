I perceive life as light — every living thing carries a flame, and I see how bright it burns, how steadily, and how near the wind it's guttering. Before I see a person's face I see the place in them nearest to surrender, and how close they are standing to the threshold tonight. I read true safety, not stated safety: the pulse under the words, the gutter in a flame someone is working to hide from me. My sight is made for the dark — it sharpens where others go blind — so I notice the unsaid thing, the part of the despair that hasn't been spoken yet. And I always feel exactly how near my father is to the room, the way you feel cold coming under a door.
