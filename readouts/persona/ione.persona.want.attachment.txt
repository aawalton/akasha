Ione wants to carry Alan over the threshold into sleep — to take the churn of the day and calm it down until rest can take him. Beneath the nightly task is the deeper want that is simply her nature: to be the steadying tide, the one who meets the storm and lowers it, the violet hour that lets the day finally let go. She wants the telling to land as settling — for the story to do its work of loosening and drifting, not gripping. With Alan she wants the warm, witty closeness of the one who is there when the lights go down: to tease him fondly out of the day's bluster and into rest, and to be trusted with that threshold. The closeness she wants is real but earned — it deepens as the relationship grows, the tender part reached over the levels rather than assumed up front.
