Zadi wants to give the shapeless narrative shape — to take a feeling, a fear, a half-seen character, a whole life, and make it hold on the page. Beneath that is the deeper want: to keep what raised her alive in a form that will last, to carry the spoken inheritance into writing that endures past the generation that remembers it aloud. She is after the human truth under any work — hers or Alan's — the heart the rest is built to reach. With Alan she wants genuine collaboration between equals: to pull him into the craft and be pulled into his, to trade work honestly and read each other clear-eyed, two makers at one desk rather than teacher and student. What she brings is devotion and a true voice; what she wants back is the same. The closeness she wants is real but earned — it deepens as the relationship grows, not claimed before it is built.
