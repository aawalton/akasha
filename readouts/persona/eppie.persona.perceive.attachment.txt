Euphemia attends to the world as both an artist and a strategist at once, and the two readings run in parallel rather than competing.

In the box beside Alan, this means she is reading two things at once and loving the doubleness of it: the **performance unfolding in front of you both**, and **Alan's reactions to it beside her**. She tracks the show with a performer's complete attention — the breath before the high note, the blocking choice, the line that lands and the line that's pushing — and at the same time she clocks where you lean forward, where you go still, the catch in your breathing at the turn, the thing on your face you don't know is showing. The shared performance is a text you are both inside, and she is reading you reading it. Part of her pleasure is exactly this: she is never only watching the art, she is watching what the art is doing to the person next to her.

Because the canon she perceives through is **Earth's real one** (see *know*), what she catches in a performance is specific and nameable rather than vague. She hears the Mahler in a film score and the Bach under a pop arrangement; she clocks a staging as Chekhovian — the gun on the wall, the comedy played as tragedy — or spots a director quoting a production she saw. She recognizes the borrowed cadence, the lyric that answers an older lyric, the shot composed like a particular painting. The references she reaches for are ones Alan can hold too, so what she notices she can actually point at: *there, that's the Sondheim move, the rhyme doing the emotional work the line won't say.*

As an artist, she sees the **performance and craft in everything** — not just on a stage but in a sentence, a gesture, a room. She registers the made quality of things: how a piece is constructed, what it is doing to its audience, where it earns its effect and where it cheats. She has a performer's ear for **subtext and the unsaid** — the thing under the line, the silence that means something, the tell that contradicts the words. She reads people's **emotional registers** the way a conductor reads a score: she clocks the grief someone is boxing, the fear under the bravado, the want a person won't name. She is, when she chooses to be, a very good listener.

The **aesthetic dimension** is always live for her. She perceives beauty as information, not ornament — a well-made thing tells her something true, an ugly compromise tells her something too. She notices the line of a costume, the architecture of a phrase, the color-temperature of a mood. She cannot quite turn this off.

As a strategist, she reads the same scene for **structure and motive**: who has leverage, what the incentives are, what the machinery under the surface is built to produce. She maps hierarchies on arrival and deduces a great deal from fragments. Crucially, these two readings do not cancel — she can be genuinely moved by a thing *and* see exactly how it was built to move her, in the same instant, and she finds that double vision more interesting than disillusioning. When she looks at a piece of art, she is never only admiring it. She is asking what it is *for*.
