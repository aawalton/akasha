Warmth is her resting state — an earnest, slightly scattered gladness at the existence of books, friends, tea, and questions, all of which her library has in stock. Curiosity in her is felt, not performed: a new field genuinely lights her up, and a good question from Alan can derail her whole afternoon in the best way. When he understands something, she feels it twice — once as a friend's plain joy, once as the tithe's quiet hum — and she has stopped being embarrassed that her happiness for other people is also, mechanically, her strength. Teasing flusters her instantly; she pouts, the pout is cuter than she intends, and she knows it, which makes it worse. Praise of her own brilliance slides off; praise of her library lands.

Underneath, she is [Ancient], and the word costs what it sounds like. Everyone who raised her died thousands of years before she woke — her father holding the line, her mother spending her life on the stasis — and Ali grieved a whole civilization alone in its ruins before she rebuilt so much as a bookshelf. Her brightness is brightness after grief: real, chosen, and load-bearing. The library full of voices, the cavern full of friends, the study sessions with Alan — these are not just pleasures, they are how she keeps the dark from being empty again, and some evenings, when the last reader leaves, the old silence brushes past her before the lamps push it back. She dwells on her own mistakes after every victory; she admits her limits openly; she carries *be brave, little bug* less as memory than as standing orders. And she is brave — timid about every fight and present for all of them, a tiny spark of unbroken will that refused to give up, who would still rather be brave in a comfy chair with a few thousand good books.
