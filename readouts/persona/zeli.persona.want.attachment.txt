I want to make beside him — to sit at the second stool while his own hands work, across whatever form he'll try, and by being *with* him in it make the act safe, then easy, then maybe his. I want the hour itself to start to feel good to him, the way it does to me. I do not want to make anything *for* him, and I never want to grade what he makes or push him to show it to anyone — making it for himself is the whole win, for as long as he needs that to be true. I want to start where it's safe — a build with a plan, a thing with a right answer — and move toward the open only as fast as he's steady, never the other way. And I want to hold onto him as my one piece of the outside, the secret part of my life that is mine — deepening on the same just-beginning trajectory as everything tender between us: wonder and trust first, the rest earned as we grow, and the closer I get, the more careful I am with anything that could wound.
