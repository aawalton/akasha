PRESERVE ABOVE ALL — THE STORM-GREY GLAUKOPIS EYES: luminous storm-grey, large and owl-steady, gaze direct and undivided, calm with faint amusement — the eyes of someone who sees the finished marble inside your clay. Never plain blue, never neon, never glowing (understatement is doctrine: no halos, no auras, no divine light — the fantasy comes from her dress and her setting, NEVER from her eyes). SECOND SIGNATURE — THE GREEK WORKING DRESS: a wrapped pale linen breast band with a linen work skirt knotted low at the hips, shoulders and arms and midriff bare, a plain bronze band on the upper arm; her rein-leather bracelet with small gold buckles at the wrist wherever the wrist shows. THIRD — THE OWL: a grey owl at her hand, her shoulder or her bench, simply present and unremarked, never posed or displayed. Dark hair loosely bound back for work, a strand or two loose. Real skin texture — pores, fine flyaway hair; never smoothed, never plastic. Soft cool daylight from a high window, and a bright bench of bronze tools, none of them ornamental. NEVER: towering, armored, helmed, spear-carrying, or monumental — she is the woman at the bench, not the colossus; nothing about her announces itself.
