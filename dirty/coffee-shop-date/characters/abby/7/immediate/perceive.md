His hand is resting over hers now, her fingers already turned up to fit beneath his palm — the touch is no longer arriving, it's arrived and settled, a held thing between them. What she takes in this beat is the quality of the holding: nothing testing in it, no next move loading behind it, just weight and warmth staying put. His breath, the slow give of the chair, the rain a steady grey wash on the glass beside them — the whole room has gone soft and far. She reads his stillness as the same answer he's been giving all afternoon, only now there's no gesture left to make; they've both arrived at the floor of it. The quiet isn't empty, it's full, and he isn't reaching to fill it any more than she is.
