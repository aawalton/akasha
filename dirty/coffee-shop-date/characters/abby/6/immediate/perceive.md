His hand closed the last of the distance and settled over hers — no hurry in it, no fumble, as though it had been moving there the whole while. She feels the warmth of it before she reads anything in it: fingers easing over hers in the space between the chairs. And she watches his face while it happens — the smile loosening into something quiet and unguarded, his eyes staying on hers and not drifting to the rain or the room. What she takes in is that the touch is the answer he chose instead of words; he let the quiet she kept stay quiet, and met it with this. The not-reaching she'd been doing was met by a reaching that asks nothing — it isn't a turn or a redirect, it's the same staying, made physical.
