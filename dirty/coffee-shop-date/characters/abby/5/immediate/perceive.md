Nothing new has come from him in words — and that absence is itself the thing she takes in. After she said 'I'm not going anywhere' and let her hand drift toward the warmth between them, the quiet closed back over them both the way she'd let it. He hasn't reached to fill it, hasn't redirected it, hasn't reclaimed the steering. The silence is mutual now, held by both of them, and that's new: not the kind she usually tends, but one neither of them is working. The rain is still a grey curtain on the glass, the warmth still wrapped close, his weight still settled in the chair as though he means to stay exactly this long. What she reads underneath the stillness is that the moment is holding on its own — it doesn't need either of them to keep it aloft. They've met in the middle of it and the middle is simply staying put.
