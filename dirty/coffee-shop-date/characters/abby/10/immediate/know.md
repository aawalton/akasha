She understands the room is doing what a café does as the afternoon goes long — emptying on its own unhurried rhythm, table by table, the world quietly making room. And she knows what that means for the two of them: each thing the room sheds only leaves them more plainly alone together, more held. First the light dimmed, then the song turned, now a table emptied — and none of it asked anything, none of it disturbed the joined hands. The pattern is clear and gentle: the world is thinning down around the thing they're in, and the thing only grows quieter and surer for it. There's still no next thing required. The guarded want stays answered. The only fidelity remains the same — to keep letting it be exactly what it is, and now to let the growing solitude of the room be part of that, not a thing to fill.
