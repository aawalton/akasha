To stay, and to let the room go on emptying around them without marking it — to let the bell and the gap and the lower murmur pass over the way the light and the song did. She wants the warmth to keep being allowed, his hand in hers, the rain steady, and to receive the growing quiet of the room as openly as she's received everything else this afternoon: as more of the same shelter, not less. She wants only to have this and let having it stay enough — to be the one who stays in the middle of it with him while the world clears its edges.
