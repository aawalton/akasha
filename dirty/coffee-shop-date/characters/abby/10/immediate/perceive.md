The room thinned by one. She clocks the small bright note of the bell at the front, the brief pull of cold wet air at the threshold, the door easing shut — and then the warmth folding back over the gap the leaving pair left. The afternoon murmur dropped a shade lower with them gone; the slack hour widened quietly through the emptier space. But the thing she reads most, the way she always does, is what the departure didn't touch: his hand still over hers, her fingers still fitted beneath his palm, the rain steady on the fogged glass, the amber leaning another faint degree toward evening. The shelter in the back corner didn't loosen for the room emptying — if anything it drew closer for it.
