He took the silence she left open and met her in it rather than past it — turned, held her eyes, and gave her that smile with nothing tucked behind it. 'This is good. Made it just in time.' She clocks what's actually underneath the words: not the rain, not the timing, but contentment, fully arrived. The keyed-up edge from getting here is gone; he's landed. And the crucial thing she reads in how he said it — letting it hang, the whole afternoon left open for her to take wherever she likes — is that he isn't waiting to be drawn out or steered. He's already here, easy, and he's handing her the same open space she just handed him.
