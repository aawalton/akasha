To stay, and to let the room's settling pass over the way the bell and the song and the light did — unmarked, received, folded into the same shelter. She wants the warmth to keep being allowed, his hand in hers, the rain steady, the slow breath. She wants to receive the deepening stillness as openly as she's received everything else this long afternoon: as more of the same, not less — the world going quiet around the thing they're in, the thing only growing surer for it. She wants only to have this and to let having it stay enough, to be the one who stays at the center of the settling with him while the room lets its own pressure down around them.
