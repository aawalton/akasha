The machine sighs behind the counter — a low, soft release of held pressure, then a faint tick of cooling metal settling. She clocks it for what it is: the small involuntary sound a room makes when nothing's called on it for a while, carrying only as far as the front, reaching the back corner not at all. What she reads, the way she always does, is everything it doesn't touch. His hand still over hers, her fingers still fitted beneath his palm. The rain steady on the fogged glass. The amber leaned another faint degree toward evening. The shop settling deeper into its own slack-hour stillness — the room exhaling now, the way the leaving pair, the turned song, the dimming light all came and went without disturbing the join.
