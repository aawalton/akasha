Nothing has moved, and that is what she takes in. The room has leaned a faint degree on without them — the gold along the floor thinning toward amber, the grey beyond the glass dimming a shade, the hour turning toward late afternoon all on its own. The rain holds steady and softer against the windows; the machine stays idle. His hand is still over hers, her fingers still fitted beneath his palm, the weight unchanged. What she reads in this beat isn't a new gesture from him but the absence of one — the holding simply continuing, unbroken, while the light slides quietly past. The afternoon is moving without asking anything of either of them, and she feels herself inside that motion rather than apart from it.
