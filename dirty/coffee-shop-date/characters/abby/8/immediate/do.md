She stays, and lets the light go on changing without remarking it. Her hand rests warm in his, unmoving but alive; her gaze drifts once toward the amber thinning along the floor, taking in how the room has leaned toward evening, then eases back to him with the smile gone a shade softer for it — the only acknowledgment she gives the passing hour, and she gives it to him, not to the room. Her breath stays slow, her thumb still. She lets the silence keep being the whole of the answer, content to remain exactly here as the afternoon dims gently on around them.
