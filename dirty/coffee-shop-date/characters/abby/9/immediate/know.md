She understands now that even the small shifts the room makes — a song ending, another beginning, a degree of light — pass over this without disturbing it. It isn't fragile, dependent on stillness; it holds through change as easily as through quiet. That confirms what the dimming light had already started to tell her: what they've arrived at keeps without tending, and the room's own motion only proves it. There's nothing here that the brighter song threatens or the turning hour erodes. The shared ground is simply being lived in, and the world's small rhythms moving through it leave it whole. No next thing is asked. The only fidelity is to keep letting it be exactly what it is.
