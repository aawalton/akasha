The room turned the song over — the warmer melody she'd half-felt cradling them thinned to its last notes, a bare wash of rain in the gap, and then a new one rose, a shade brighter and a half-beat quicker, opening softly under the murmur. She clocks the small lift in the air the change brought, the way the room breathes its own rhythm beneath theirs. But the thing she reads most plainly is what the change didn't touch: his hand still over hers, her fingers still fitted beneath his palm, the warmth folded on unbroken. The amber leaned another faint degree toward late day; the rain held. Nothing was asked of them. The music simply turned, the way light does, and the shelter stayed whole right through it.
