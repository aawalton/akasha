To stay, and to let the day's turning pass over the way the bell and the song and the failing light all did — unmarked, received, folded into the same warmth. She wants his hand in hers, the rain steady, the slow breath, the lamps holding their gold over the two of them while the dark stands outside the glass. She wants to receive the coming evening as openly as she's received everything else this long afternoon: not as the end of the shelter but as the thing it now holds against — the night closing in around a warmth that only grows surer for it. She wants only to have this and to let having it stay enough, to be the one who stays at the center of the lamplit warmth with him while the day goes quietly out beyond the window.
