She understands the afternoon is gone — they've crossed from day into lamplit dusk, and the room made the crossing the way it does everything, asking nothing of anyone for it. The light that warmed them is no longer the sun's; it's the lamps now, the shop's own kept warmth standing in for the day that's spent. And she knows what that does to the shelter: the dark gathering at the windows doesn't thin the warmth, it draws it closer — the joined hands are no longer held inside a bright afternoon but against the coming night, and that makes them surer, not smaller. The pattern she's watched hold all along holds still, deeper than ever: bell, song, table, machine, and now the day itself — each came and went over the back corner without crossing into it, without asking the join to move. There is still no next thing required. The guarded want stays answered. The only fidelity is unchanged — to keep letting it be exactly what it is, and now to let the night's coming on be part of the shelter too, a thing they're held against rather than a thing to mark.
