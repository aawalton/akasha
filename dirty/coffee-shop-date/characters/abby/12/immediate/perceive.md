The day lets go past the glass. She doesn't watch the light so much as feel it cross over — the amber thinning off the floor to nothing, the overhead bulbs quietly becoming the room's own light instead of the day's borrowed gold. Beyond the fogged window the street has gone evening-blue, the city's far lights starting to bead and swim in the wet. What she reads is the threshold itself: not a break, only a turning, the same unhurried rhythm that emptied a table and turned a song over. And in the back corner it reaches them as nothing but the gold deepening a shade warmer — his hand still over hers, her fingers still fitted beneath his palm, the rain steady, the shelter drawing nearer now against the dark coming on outside.
