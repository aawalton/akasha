Sheltering and wholly inward now — the room become a lit place against the dark, the touched moment resting unbroken at its center. With the light's long turn spent and the counter silent, the café has gone as still as it gets; nothing moves but rain and the soft track running down toward its close. The evening blue pressed against the fogged glass draws the warm walls closer than the grey did, the bulbs' gold the only light there is, and the shelter feels deliberate now, chosen against the dark rather than borrowed from a fading day. The intimacy in the corner is settled all the way in, untouched by the room's quiet. The space around it has loosened near to emptiness — patient, unhurried, content to be the lamplit inward place this is happening in, holding the two of them as the evening settles its weight outside the glass.
