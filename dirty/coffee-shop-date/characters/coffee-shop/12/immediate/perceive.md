The crossing is done: the warm overhead bulbs are now wholly the room's light, the last amber gone from the floorboards, the street beyond the fogged glass settled into deep evening blue with the city's far lights beading and swimming in the wet. The back corner stays without motion — the two sunk deep, hands joined and at rest in the warm gap, faces turned, breath gone long and even, coats long dry. The counter holds full silence; the boiler at resting warmth, the milk pitcher cool, the line empty, nothing queued at the machine. No body stands at the door, no voice lifts above the lowered murmur. The brighter track has run its middle out and now thins toward its close, the next song already cued in the queue and not yet risen. At the bussing ledge the cold cup and saucer sit unwiped beside the bare freed table; the mid-room cup is fully cold. The rain keeps its softer even wash on the glass. The fog at the near window has crept another shade up the pane, the film whole and unnoticed. Under the warm bulbs the room reads, for the first time today, as a lit inward place against a dark street.
