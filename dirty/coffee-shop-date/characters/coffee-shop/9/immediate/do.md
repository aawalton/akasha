Near the front, the two risen patrons reach the door and go — the bell sounds its small bright note overhead, a brief breath of cold and rain-smell drawing in at the threshold before the warmth closes over it again, and the door settles shut. The room sits one table emptier, its murmur dropped a shade lower for it, the slack of late afternoon opening a little wider through the space. Nothing reaches the back corner but the faint far chime of the bell: the warmth there holds, the joined hands stay, the rain keeps its steady wash, the amber light leans another degree, and the shelter stays whole — the room only thinning by one departure on its own quiet rhythm, the way the afternoon empties as it goes.
