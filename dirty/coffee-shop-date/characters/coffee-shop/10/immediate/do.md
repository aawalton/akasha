The espresso machine, idle this long, lets its held pressure go — a low soft sigh as the boiler bleeds down, a faint settling tick of cooling metal behind the counter, the small sound the room makes when nothing has called on it in a while. It carries no further than the front of the floor; it reaches the back corner not at all. The warmth there holds, the joined hands stay at rest in the gap, the rain keeps its steady wash, the deep amber light leans another degree toward evening, and the shelter stays whole — the room only settling deeper into its own stillness, the way a café sighs into the slack hour as the afternoon goes long.
