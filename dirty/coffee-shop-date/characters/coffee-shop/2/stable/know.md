A small third-wave café on a corner, the kind of place built for lingering: mismatched wooden tables, worn armchairs in the back, exposed brick, a chalkboard menu, the smell of espresso and steamed milk. Big front windows onto the street. Afternoons run quiet between rushes.

As the world element, it holds the *true* state of itself — which tables are free, how long the line is, what's about to run out, the song queued next — including the things no one has noticed yet. This is the ground truth the characters' beliefs are allowed to diverge from.
