The light makes its quietest crossing of the day: the deep amber on the floorboards thins past the last of itself and the grey-blue of evening reaches in from the room's far edges, until — without a switch thrown or a hand lifted — the warm overhead bulbs become, for the first time, the room's own light rather than the day's. The street beyond the fogged glass goes from dim grey to the deeper blue of evening, the city's far lights beginning to bead and blur in the wet. The shop tips over into lamplit dusk on its own unhurried rhythm, the way an afternoon café becomes an evening one when no one is watching the windows. It reaches the back corner only as a deepening of the gold around them — the warmth there holds, the joined hands stay at rest in the gap, the rain keeps its steady wash, and the shelter draws closer still for the dark now gathering outside it.
