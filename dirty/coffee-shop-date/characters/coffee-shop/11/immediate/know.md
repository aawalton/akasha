Late afternoon tipping over its own edge toward early evening — the lull at its deepest, the room down a table and holding there, the between-rush ebb fully settled with no new body at the door. The outside light has failed far enough that the threshold is near: soon the room's own warm bulbs will outweigh what comes through the windows, and the amber on the floor will give way to lamplight, the day's longest unregistered turn about to complete. Structure in the back corner unchanged: the pair stay settled and joined, hands closed in the gap, no body signaling any move, coats long dry. The machine has finished bleeding down — its sound spent, the counter returned to full silence, no pending mechanical event left to come. The freed table's cup sits cold and unwiped; the mid-room cup is fully cold; the front glass is fogged higher now, the film whole and unnoticed, the room's quiet record of how long they've been held inside lengthening. Outside the rain holds its softer steady wash, the shelter stable, nothing about to run out, the line empty. The only thing still in motion is the slow turn of the light toward evening — now close enough to its tipping point that the next faint degree will read, to anyone who looked, as the day having gone.
