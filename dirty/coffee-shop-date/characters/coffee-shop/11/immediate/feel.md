Sheltering and wholly close, the touched moment resting unbroken where it landed, deeper still now that even the machine's small sound has died and the room holds nothing but rain and the soft track and the two of them. The café has gone fully quiet under the slack hour, the silence behind the counter complete. The softened rain draws the walls close; the deep amber, thinning now toward its first hint of evening blue at the edges, lends the shelter the long-held warmth of a day going gently out — the room about to become, without anyone marking it, a lit and inward place against a darkening street. The intimacy in the corner is settled all the way in, untouched by anything the room does or stops doing. The space around it has loosened nearly to emptiness — patient, unhurried, content to be the place this is happening in, holding the two of them as the afternoon spends its last gold and tips quietly over into evening.
