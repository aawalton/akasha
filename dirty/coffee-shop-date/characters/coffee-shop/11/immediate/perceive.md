The machine's sigh has finished; the faint settling tick of cooling metal behind the counter is spent and gone to silence, the boiler down to its resting warmth, the milk pitcher cool on its tray. No body stands at the door, no voice lifts above the murmur, the line empty and nothing queued. The brighter track rides on through its middle, even and soft beneath the lowered hum, asking nothing. At the bussing ledge the cold cup and saucer sit unwiped, the freed table bare beside them. The rain holds its softer even wash on the glass, the grey beyond gone another hair dimmer. The fog at the near window has crept a shade higher up the pane, the film whole and unnoticed. The gold along the floorboards has passed into deep amber and begun to lose even that — the first true grey-blue of evening seeping at the room's far edges where the light no longer reaches, the overhead bulbs not yet warmer than the day but on the verge of becoming the only light there is.
