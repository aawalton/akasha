Nothing has come at him to be read — and that absence is itself the thing he takes in. The room made its small lean toward late afternoon: the gold along the floor thinning to amber, the grey beyond the glass dimming a shade, the rain steady and softened against the windows. None of it asks anything of him. Her hand is still in his, fitted beneath his palm; her smile soft and resting; her breath gone out slow. The foreground hasn't changed because nothing in it needed to change. What's new is only the hour moving — the warmth folding on unbroken while time goes by without a single demand in it.
