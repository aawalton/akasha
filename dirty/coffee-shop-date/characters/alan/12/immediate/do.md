He stays. His hand keeps hers, the hold easy and certain, no fresh pressure and none needed — the settled weight of his palm over her resting fingers carried straight across the turn of the day. His gaze stays soft on her, the smile still and unhurried, his weight eased back into the chair as though he means to stay exactly this long, which he does. The light crossing to lamplight, the evening pressing blue at the glass, the steady rain — all of it passes without a word, the touch going on being the whole answer, the long hour carried forward into dusk without his reaching for any of it.
