The light has tipped over a threshold he half-registers — the amber along the floor thinned out, and somewhere in there the overhead bulbs quietly became the room's own light instead of the day's. Beyond the fogged glass the street has gone evening-blue, the city's far lights beading and swimming in the wet. It reaches him only as the gold deepening a shade warmer around them, gathering nearer. Her hand is still in his in the space between the chairs, turned to fit. The rain keeps its steady wash. He notices the crossing the way you notice a system pass a state boundary cleanly, with no seam — afternoon to dusk, nothing snagging, nothing announced.
