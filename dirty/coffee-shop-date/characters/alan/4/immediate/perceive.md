He'd already named it — that she wasn't reaching, that he liked her letting it show — and then asked her, without making it a question, to stay right here with him. That was his move last beat; he's the one who just spoke. So what he's taking in now isn't a new line from her but the shape of his own offering still hanging in the air, and her steadiness holding under it: she hasn't flinched, hasn't deflected the warmth, hasn't reached to lighten it. He clocks that he's said the thing — that he saw what she let through and welcomed it — and that it landed clean, openly, with nothing clever wrapped around it. The room's still quiet, the rain still on the glass, her gaze still on his. The afternoon is exactly the thing he hoped for, and he's just put words to it.
