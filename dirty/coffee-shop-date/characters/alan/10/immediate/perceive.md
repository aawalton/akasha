The bell — that small bright note near the front, the brief pull of cold wet air at the threshold, and then the door easing shut and the warmth folding back over the gap. The two who'd been gathering their coats are gone. The room sits one table emptier; its murmur has dropped a shade lower, the slack hour widening through the space. Nothing in the foreground moves: her hand still rests in his in the space between the chairs, the touch at rest. The amber leans another faint degree toward evening; the rain keeps its steady wash on the fogged glass; the machine stays idle. He registers the departure and the small emptying for exactly what they are — the café thinning on its own rhythm as the afternoon goes long.
