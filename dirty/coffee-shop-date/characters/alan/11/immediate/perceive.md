The machine's small sigh behind the counter — a held pressure bleeding down, then a faint tick of cooling metal, the involuntary sound a room makes when nothing has called on it for a while. It carries only to the front; it doesn't reach the back corner at all. Here nothing moves: her hand still rests in his in the space between the chairs. The amber has leaned another faint degree toward evening, deeper now; the rain keeps its steady wash on the fogged glass. He half-notices the machine's settling the way you notice a system idling down — a thing relaxing because nothing is demanding of it — and the noticing arrives gentle, without pulling him anywhere.
