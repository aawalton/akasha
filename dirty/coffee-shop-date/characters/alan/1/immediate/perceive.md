They've crossed the floor and dropped into the two worn armchairs, angled toward each other — the room had practically saved them for this. Rain runs grey down the window beside them; the city's gone soft and distant. Abby's hood is back, her hair half-damp, and she's settled in close. And then: the silence. Neither of them filling it. The afternoon murmur, the machine's idle hiss, and this small held quiet between them.
