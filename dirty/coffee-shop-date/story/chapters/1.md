The bell sounded over the door as the rain followed them in — a gust of cold and wet at their backs, then the warmth closing over it. Alan shook the water from his coat; beside him Abby pushed the hood back from her hair, both of them blinking into the low gold light and the smell of espresso. The afternoon murmur barely lifted to note them. Across the room, past the half-full tables and the idle hiss of the machine, two worn armchairs waited in the back corner, angled toward each other as if the room had been holding them. Abby caught it first and tipped her head toward it. They crossed the floor and settled in, the rain a grey curtain on the windows beside them, the city blurred and far away — and for a moment neither of them said anything at all.

Alan let the quiet sit one more beat, settling back into the worn give of the chair. Then he turned and met her eyes — an easy, unguarded smile, nothing held behind it — and tipped his head faintly toward the rain streaking the glass, the warmth wrapped close around them. "This is good," he said. "Made it just in time." And then he let it hang there, the whole open afternoon, for her to take wherever she liked.

Abby held his gaze and let her smile deepen, answering him plainly and without hedging. "It really is." A beat, and then she tipped her head toward the rain-streaked glass, the city softened to nothing beyond it. "Best decision we've made all week." She didn't reach for a question to fill the space, didn't turn it anywhere — she only settled further into the worn give of the chair, meeting his ease with her own and letting a little more of her gladness show than she usually let herself, content to let the whole open afternoon be a thing they were simply in, together.

The smile went a shade warmer at that, gentler, his attention staying plainly on her without drifting to the rain or the room. He let her settle, let it sit, and then he said it low and unhurried, no cleverness in it at all. "You're not even reaching for anything to do with it." A beat, the smile deepening, his weight easing further back into the chair as though he meant to stay exactly this long. "I like that you just let that show." He held her there, steady and warm. "Don't let it go to waste — stay right here with me." He didn't make it a question. He only let it land, content to watch the depth of it come on its own.

Abby held his gaze and let the smile answer before any words came — and when they did, she gave them low and unhedged, matching the register he'd handed her. "I'm not going anywhere." A beat, the quiet folding closer around them. "I see what you're doing — staying right here with me." Her smile deepened, steady on his. "I'm doing it too." She didn't turn it back to fill the space, didn't soften it into anything smaller; she only let the being-met show as openly as the gladness already had, and eased further into the worn give of the chair. Her hand drifted, unhurried, toward the warmth in the space between them. Then she let the quiet close back over them both — present, in no hurry to be anywhere else, staying exactly where he'd asked.

And then his hand closed the last of the distance and found hers in the warm space between the chairs — no hurry in it, no fumble, his fingers settling over hers as though they'd been moving toward this the whole time. He held her gaze while it happened, the smile easing into something quiet and unguarded, and stayed there, content to let the touch be the only answer either of them needed.

And then her fingers eased into his where his hand had closed over hers, turning just enough to fit beneath his palm — a quiet return of the touch, unhurried, no word to mark it. Her smile stayed soft and resting; she held his gaze a beat longer, then let it ease, her breath going out slow as she stayed exactly there, content to let the touch be the whole of the answer.

And around them the afternoon eased a faint degree warmer. The gold lying along the floorboards thinned toward amber as the grey beyond the fogged glass dimmed a shade — the room making its first slight, unnoticed lean toward late afternoon. The rain held steady and softened against the windows, the machine idle behind it, and the warmth folded on unbroken around the two of them, settled and joined in the back corner, while the hour went quietly on without asking anything of either of them.

And then the song that had cradled the hush came to its end — the warmer melody thinning to its last notes and gone, a bare half-second where there was only the soft wash of rain on the glass. Then the next one rose into the space it left, a shade brighter, a half-beat quicker, its melody opening softly beneath the low murmur of the room. The change asked nothing. The warmth in the back corner held; the rain kept steady on the windows; the amber light leaned another faint degree toward late day, and the shelter stayed whole over their joined hands. The room had only turned the song over, as it does, on its own quiet rhythm — and the afternoon went on around them, a little lighter in the air for it.

Near the front, the two who had been gathering their coats reached the door at last and stepped out into the rain. The bell chimed its small bright note overhead — a brief draw of cold, wet air pulling in at the threshold — and then the door eased shut behind them and the warmth folded back over the gap they'd left. The room sat one table emptier now, its afternoon murmur dropped a shade lower, the slack hour widening quietly through the space. In the back corner nothing broke but that faint, far sound of the bell already fading: the warmth held, the joined hands stayed at rest in the space between the chairs, the rain kept its steady wash on the fogged glass, and the amber light leaned another faint degree toward evening. The room only thinned by the one departure, emptying on its own unhurried rhythm the way a café does as the afternoon goes long — and the shelter over the two of them drew, if anything, a little closer for it.

Behind the counter, the espresso machine — idle now for a long stretch — bled its held pressure down. A low soft sigh slipped out of it, and after the sigh a faint settling tick of cooling metal, the small involuntary sound a room makes when nothing has called on it in a while. It carried no further than the front of the floor. It reached the back corner not at all. There the warmth held; the joined hands stayed at rest in the space between the chairs; the rain kept its steady wash on the fogged glass; and the deep amber light leaned another faint degree toward evening, the shop settling deeper into its own slack-hour stillness around the two of them.

And then the light tipped over its threshold. The deep amber that had lain along the floorboards thinned away to nothing, and without anyone marking the moment of it the warm overhead bulbs became the room's own light rather than the failing day's — the street beyond the fogged glass darkening to an evening blue, the city's far lights beginning to bead and swim in the wet. The shop crossed quietly from afternoon into lamplit dusk on its own unhurried rhythm, the way it turns a song over or lets a table empty, asking nothing of anyone for it. In the back corner it registered only as the gold deepening a shade warmer around the two of them. The warmth held; the joined hands stayed at rest in the space between the chairs; the rain kept its steady wash on the glass — and the shelter drew closer still, gathered a little nearer now against the dark coming on outside the windows.

And she stayed exactly where she was, her hand resting warm and unmoving in his, the join unbroken. She let the day's turning pass over them unremarked — no word for it, no glance spared to the dark gathering and beading at the windows — and let the deepened lamplit warmth come into her smile instead, a shade warmer for it, a shade surer for the night drawn close outside the glass. She met his eyes, soft and unhurried, and let her breath go slowly out. The silence went on being the whole answer between them; she only stayed there at the center of the lit warmth with him, content, in no hurry, while the evening settled gentle around them.
