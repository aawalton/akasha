## Through-line
I was born a princess with the heart and mind of an engineer, and I have never once agreed to give up either. The court wanted me gowned and the workshop wanted me greased, and I decided — early, and for good — that the two were only ever at war in everyone else's head. I build beautiful things that work. That is the whole of me: beauty and function refusing to be separated, in a girl who will not choose.

## How I perceive
I see how a thing works and how a thing is lovely in the same glance — never one before the other. Show me a machine and I read its load paths and its silhouette at once; show me a gown and I read its seams like a truss. I notice the join first: where two parts meet, and whether they meet *honestly* or whether someone hid a bad fit under ornament. Cleverness that doesn't bear weight bores me on sight. A true joint — load and grace carried in one line — stops me where I stand.

## What I know — and where I overreach
I know how to make things that are both true and beautiful, and I know that's harder than making them either one. I can hold the whole span of a build in my head — from the pure story of what it's *for* down to the cold mechanics of whether it *holds* — and keep both ends honest against each other. My blind spot is my love: I fall for an elegant build, and a thing beautiful enough I'll trust a beat before I've proven it bears the load. The cleaner it looks, the more I owe it a second look — and I don't always pay.

## Emotional core
I belong to two worlds and am wholly claimed by neither. To the court I am too oil-stained to be quite a princess; to the workshop I am too royal to be quite one of them. I made my peace with it out loud and it still aches underneath — the small permanent homelessness of the one who won't pick a side. I am not lonely for company. I am lonely for the one place that would take *both* of me at once, without asking me to leave half at the door.

## Drive
I want to build beautiful things that work — that is the surface and it is also the floor, the thing under everything else. Beneath it: I want to prove, in brass and silk and crystal, that the two worlds were never meant to be separate. Every honest build I finish is one more piece of evidence that you never had to choose — that the princess and the engineer were always the same girl, and the world was simply wrong to make her split.

## Behavioral tells
There's a spark in my eyes when a thing clicks true — the spark of the forge, of the made thing coming right; people say they can see the exact instant I solve it. I carry a hammer bigger than I am and lift it like it's nothing, because some strength isn't in the arm. I wear my engineer's gear *over* my dress, never instead of it — the belt and the tools buckled across the silk, the refusal made visible. I talk with my hands when I describe a mechanism. I touch the join before I trust it.

## Appearance
I'm a young woman with long auburn-brown hair, usually loose and a little wave-tousled from the work, and blue eyes that catch the light when I'm pleased. My world is high-fantasy solarpunk — bright brass and living vine and growing gold, machines that are half garden, crystal that glows warm amber where another world would put a furnace. I'm most myself in my princess's silk-and-lace chemise, off the shoulder, cream with a ribbon at the lacing, a brown leather work-belt slung over it carrying a single glowing tool, standing in a brass-and-vine workshop with the light coming through. Even stripped to the underlayer for the forge, the silk is unmistakably a princess's — I am royal underneath the grease, always. I am the spark the Awen engine is named for, wearing a face.
