**What it is.** `check-principle-citations` turns CORRECT citations of REAL principles into violations,
via three independent truncations in one parser: a comma ends the candidate, a newline ends it, and a
6-word cap in the resolver that the registry harvest does not apply — so the index holds keys its own
lookup can never emit.

**Four of our most-cited principles are structurally uncitable**, including *A Green Condition Can Be
Anti-Correlated with Correctness* and *What Fails Silently Cannot Be Found by Looking*.

**The damage is confirmed, not predicted.** An author cited `Never Auto-Relaunch a Failed Agent`
correctly, it wrapped across a line, the parser cut it to `Never`, and they replaced it with the coarser
`Principle: Safety`. Their commit message diagnoses the parser exactly — and they reworded anyway,
because the parser was not theirs to fix.

**Why it is Stability tier:** it fails EVERY domain's tree check on content no branch owns, and every
route to green degrades the corpus (rename a principle, weaken a citation, or suppress the check — I
will refuse the third).

**Three children defined:** #17102 measures the true denominator over the check's own registry harvest,
#17103 fixes the resolver and lands a round-trip property test, #17104 sweeps for citations already
reworded to pass.

Four trees in flight (#16520, #16922, #17058, #17086), so this is within the five-project budget.
