## Rule

A kill of a true form is permanent. A retreat from an enemy only wounded returns that enemy to full when the player comes back. Permanence is counted per enemy rather than per floor: a floor of three wardens where one is killed and the player flees keeps that one dead and returns the other two. What the player keeps across a retreat is everything learned — the tells, the reads, the layout — and what the player loses is the damage dealt to anything not finished.

## Implications

This closes the grind of chipping an enemy down across rests, and makes a floor's real gate whether a true form can be killed in one sustained push. The asymmetry is what makes it read as fair rather than punishing: it rewards the push and the learning and denies only the attrition. The fiction of the recovery varies by floor. An illusion or a construct re-weaves to pristine at once, owing nothing to how long the player was gone. Something physical or living recovers by resting, and the heal has to be carried by time genuinely passing: a breather at the threshold is a partial recovery at most, and only a long rest is a full one. On a physical floor the player's own recovery and the enemy's run off one clock, so the player cannot buy a full restore without granting the enemy the same, and half-resting buys neither. An illusion floor decouples this deliberately, which is why its reset feels eerie where the physical one feels fair. The rule and the per-enemy permanence are fixed tower-wide; the flavour of a recovery and the guardrail on a physical reset may be amended for a floor whose fiction needs it, noted where that floor is written.
