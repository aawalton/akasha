## Rule

Three skills have emerged in play and climb the same seven rungs. EMBER CHANNEL conducts his own ember into a held weapon: self-sourced, transient, fixed to the element. EMBER BURST releases it all at once as an outward pulse into a zone, the area counterpart to the channel, and its axis up the ladder is aim, metering and where the pulse is placed. ESSENCE INFUSION is a different kind: it extracts an essence from a raw source and binds it into a separate object, so it is external-sourced, persistent and element-neutral. Being a motor technique rather than an element, infusion has no matched affinity and takes no affinity bias, which is why the cold-ichor imbue took none, the element being wrong for his ember. It still adds by its rung like any skill. Its axis is binding quality: whether the bind holds under pressure, how long it lasts before it bleeds off, and what the focus costs. Binding an essence of the pole opposite the binder's own costs more and binds shakier, and that tax shrinks each rung: heavy at novice, noticeable at journeyman, merely costlier at master, and immaterial at sage.

## Implications

A skill's rung is judged against the general ladder, and what a skill's own entry adds is only its axis, meaning what improving at THIS skill improves. Where the ladder says a journeyman does it when they set themselves to it, for the burst that is a metered pulse onto a lured or massed target from stable ground, and for infusion it is a stable bind made with the source in hand and time to work. Applying a skill to something it was not drilled for, or fusing it with footwork or another essence, reads as a strong expert and is the natural sign of reaching toward grandmaster. Integration is a grading signal rather than a rung of its own: the seven rung names are locked and there is no eighth. The live placement of every skill is the character sheet and nothing else.
