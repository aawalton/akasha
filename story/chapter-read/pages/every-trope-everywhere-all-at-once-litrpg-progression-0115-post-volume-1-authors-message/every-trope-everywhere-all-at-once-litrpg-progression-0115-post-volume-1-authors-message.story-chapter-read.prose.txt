113 Chapters and 200k words for this series' first volume. Gosh, I did not plan for it to take this long. This is another case of an author writing way too much, sometimes unnecessarily, who could easily trim it down to around 160-180k words. The problem with serialized fiction is that I have to keep up with a certain upload pace, which naturally led to some chapters or content that could easily be cut out. But oh well. If I ever do decide to go back and re-edit everything in the future, you can treat some of the things you've read as bonus content.

Alright, now it's time for me to get to the real deal: something I've been wanting to say for a long, long time. I just had to wait until the first volume was finished.

I had many ideas when I first started writing this story. I knew right away that I wanted it to start as something lighthearted, comedic, and filled with stupid moments. I always intended for this story to head in a much darker direction, or an "Age of Tragedy," per se. I just had to figure out when.

I initially planned for it to be much later in the series, maybe by volume 4 or 5. As some of you may already know, several future arcs have already been teased throughout this novel so far, such as Arcstar Academy and finding Kthal, the man who killed Kaelor's lover. But I realize that would take too long. It didn't make sense for the "true" story to begin so late. I knew I had to change things; otherwise, I would feel as though I would betray my readers by making the story go in a completely different direction.

So I made the difficult choice to reveal the truth now. I didn't intend for the god of Tragedy to reveal himself this early in the series, or much about the Genre Gods at all, but I felt it was the right decision. Even things like Narrative Weight weren't meant to be revealed until later on.

I have mixed feelings about the way I handled things while writing this series so far. The biggest issue I have is undoubtedly having to keep to a schedule. With so many hours spent per day at my desk to write, I realized that I've come to dread publishing serially, not the act of writing itself. I love writing fiction and have been doing it for almost four years now, under completely different pen names, writing styles, and even genres. Hell, this was only my second time writing in first person, and having to write in Allene's voice all the way through was a great learning experience.

But because of needing to keep to a schedule, I had to compromise on plotting and detailing. It was this very reason that made the first volume over 200k words long in a span of four months. Some of you may love having so much to read, while others may hate having to read so-called "filler" content. I understand both sides.

At its core, this is a character-driven book. The plot is there to serve these characters, for them to spend time together, to bond, to laugh, to cry, and to suffer together. I've always written my novels with a focus on the slower moments and character interactions above all else. Some of my favorite scenes involve exactly that, such as during the Skycurrent's Night festival when Meriel, Reina, and Allene fall asleep under the stars while being surrounded by griffins. Or the Kaelor and Allene interaction in chapter 79, where Kaelor revealed the truth about his dark past.

This book, I feel, is a very specific niche. Being meta-aware is the first. Then there's making Tropes and Genres actual forces in the story. Then there's starting the book off thinking it was going to be an enjoyable adventure with a found-family party, just for it to reveal that the world is far more dangerous and keeps more secrets than what's on the surface.

Not to mention that this is a LitRPG, a very popular genre in the serialized market. But the story doesn't obey many of its conventions. The characters are put first, not numbers going up. Allene isn't the strongest in the party. Stats are gained through tropes as opposed to quests or killing monsters.

But here's the problem: stats completely oppose this story. In a story taking place within "the Narrative", stats become meaningless when certain things happen because the Narrative decides it to. It just doesn't fit.

So the solution was to get rid of stats completely, as seen in volume 1's final chapter. This wasn't planned until recently. Allene's System being altered, however, was planned, though not necessarily this early.

Tropes being a means of progression is... a tricky thing. There are only so many of them a single person can remember while writing. This can become especially frustrating when hundreds of readers read the same chapter. Some don't notice the missing tropes. Others do notice, but understand that writing a [TROPE INITIATED] would likely ruin the flow of the chapter. But there are also many tropes that I just miss completely, which can understandably be frustrating for people who think it's obvious enough to at least be [COMPLETED]. For these reasons, I knew from the start that I would've had to either significantly reduce the number of trope initiations as the story went by, or change the means of progression. That's how I came up with the idea of the God of Tragedy taking over a part of the System to inject some of his own rules. I'm happy with how that turned out.

Anyway, those are my thoughts about the current state of the story. This message has turned out to be a lot more rambling than I thought. Whoops...

But there's still one more thing I need to talk about, and it's probably the most important part of this entire message. Everything I've talked about above—what I've learned from writing this story, what I think worked or didn't, and what I want to do differently—has led me to make a pretty massive decision about the future of this series. I'd like to reveal what I have planned next.

This isn't Volume "2," and it isn't quite a sequel, either.

It's a reboot.

Title: The Age of Tragedy

Teaser: The story follows Kaelor, a Firstborn with the highest Narrative Weight in the world, known simply as “The Protagonist.” Because of this, many are willing to do whatever it takes to strip him of that title, whether through torment or death. Even a god has set his eyes on him. But Kaelor is not alone. Among his allies, he is accompanied by a demon born countless eras ago, possessing the knowledge and power that no other has. Kaelor must do everything he can to keep himself, his allies, and his family alive, even if it means losing everything else.

Genres: Dark Fantasy, Adventure, Tragedy, Action

That's right. Kaelor will be the protagonist. Everything that happened in this novel, Every Trope Everywhere All At Once, will be considered legacy and contained within its original continuity. It will not be a required read and will simply contain earlier versions of characters, events, and worldbuilding. Some broad ideas may carry over or even stay the same, but details shouldn't be assumed canon. It will not be a LitRPG and will read like a traditionally published novel with a streamlined plot. The book will not require knowledge from Every Trope.

The Age of Tragedy will be the first book of the rebooted series and the main canon. Everything forward is its own continuity. Now look, maybe I could pull some moves inspired by Marvel and make multi-timeline hopping between Every Trope and The Age of Tragedy canon, buttttt I don't think that would fit the story at all.

The book will begin exactly where Every Trope's final chapter left off, when the crew is attacked by a necromancer and a half-skeleton dragon, with maybe some minor detail changes. Kaelor was always planned to be a second protagonist in this story (hence why he's the only character besides Allene to receive a POV), but now he will become the main protagonist, full stop. The book will revolve around the Tragedy God and Gal'Meth's plan to bring out Kaelor's strength and power, turning him into a warrior as powerful as those from the ancient eras.

As a reminder, Firstborns were the first race to walk this world. Now, few remain. There have been sneak peeks at what Firstborns are and how powerful they are sprinkled throughout Every Trope, but The Age of Tragedy will highlight their true strength.

This will be a major shift in tone compared to Every Trope. Expect explicit language, graphic violence, death, gore, disturbing themes, and sexual content. It will be in third person, with a bigger emphasis on prose and description. Basically, expect all the typical things you would find in a dark fantasy novel if you went to a library and pulled out a book in its section.

Oh, and the found-family trope will be very prominent. You'll still love Allene, Meriel, Reina, Talon, Aeris, and Kaelor. They’ll still make jokes, despite the darker setting.

Allene will still be there as one of the most important and pivotal characters in the entire series, arguably even more important than in Every Trope, where she’s the actual protagonist. How, you might ask? Well… you’ll see. (This has already been planned out, I promise!)

I know, I know, reboots are typically for shows and movies rather than for novels, but I feel that this is the right decision, and something that you and I will genuinely enjoy. If this doesn't sound like it's the right fit for you, I understand. It's a big switch-up from what you signed up for by reading this story. But I truly adore the characters and lore I've created, and I cannot bring myself to abandon something I spent so much time doing, and I know how much readers loathe it when authors abandon their books. As opposed to an indefinite hiatus, I think this is the better choice.
