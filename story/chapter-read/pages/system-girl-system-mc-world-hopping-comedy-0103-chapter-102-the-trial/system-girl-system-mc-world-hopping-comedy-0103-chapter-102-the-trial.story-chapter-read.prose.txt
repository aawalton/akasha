We temporarily put aside the whole debacle of me having to leave so soon and focused on the upcoming trial.

Ren, to his deep dismay, had to get dressed up yet again. Once more, he was at the mercy of Mira's wardrobe choices.

He needed to look like a specific archetype today. He needed to look like a good-looking villain from a shojo manga.

The outfit started with a high-collared silk undershirt, the pristine white fabric peeking out sharply from the dark cuffs. Over this, he wore a fitted cotehardie with silver-buttoned, tapered sleeves that fell just to the mid-thigh, cutting a sharp, imposing silhouette. For outerwear, he draped a heavy, fur-lined overgown—a sideless surcoat designed specifically to denote high status, flowing with a heavy, dramatic movement whenever he walked. To finish the ensemble, a decorative chaperon draped casually over one shoulder, while a dark felt coif rested on his head.

It was getting slightly cold outside. Within a few weeks, it would start snowing.

My time here with Ren felt way too short, I thought, a phantom tightness gripping my non-existent chest. Barely two years. But right now, it is more important to test the evolving sanity out of Ren.

[Ana: Hey Ren.]

Ren, looking at himself in the full-length mirror, let out a sharp mental outrage. Don't you dare start now. I am trying to be nice to you. Don't force my hand.

[Ana: NO NO NO, I wouldn't dare say something like you look exactly like the stylish villain in a novel who threatens the world with his mad cooking skills… see, I said I wouldn't say it. <(￣︶￣)>]

I even included a little ASCII art in the text prompt to showcase exactly how genuine I was being.

Ren grumbled, adjusting his cuffs aggressively. You know, Ana, I fear for any poor bloke other than me who would end up at your mercy. You really love that fact that I can't do anything to you.

[Ana: Well, that comes with the territory. Don't blame the game, blame the player. (¬‿¬)]

Sylas stood silently in the corner of the room, waiting for his master to stop making weird faces at the mirror. This was a common occurrence lately. Ren sometimes goes completely still, makes odd facial expressions, and stares at nothing. The staff thinks he is having an intense internal monologue with himself, wrestling with the burdens of nobility. In truth, he is just talking to me.

Hey Ana, Ren thought.

[Ana: Yes?]

It's nothing… it's just, whenever you go quiet, I feel like you may have disappeared.

My digital heart cracked.

[Ana: Don't worry. I am here till the timer runs out. And it's not like your head will become empty when I am gone. You, my dear sir, are quite special. You were originally supposed to be assigned a Moderator-class system AI. I, on the other hand, am technically inferior, being an Observer-class system AI. So, they will be taking my place and will probably do a far better job than me.]

It's not that I don't care about more features, Ren thought back, his mental voice soft and fiercely loyal. I like my snarky and cynical partner just fine.

[Ana: Awww, Ren. You will make me blush. If only you used lines like those on Mira. I am going to have to watch my baby boy die single at this rate.]

Ren scoffed mentally, his cheeks flushing pink in the mirror. Okay, Mom. Butt out of my inner thought privacy, please.

Our conversation was interrupted by Sylas. The butler pulled out a silver pocket watch, checking the time with a sharp click.

"Master, we need to depart now, or else we will be late to the trial."

I watched through Ren's eyes as he and Sylas began walking towards the mansion's main gate.

A pouting Lily was currently blocking the door. She sat firmly on the floor, her chubby arms crossed tightly over her chest.

Awww, I thought. I bet she was standing but got tired of waiting and sat down just as Ren arrived. So she resorted to doing her power pose on the ground.

Well, in truth, she wasn't blocking much of any path. The mansion's entrance was massive, wide enough for six people to fit side-by-side. One little baby sitting on the ground was not much of a physical barricade. Even though the physical barricade was a joke, the emotional one was overwhelmingly strong. Ren simply can't just walk past those big pouting eyes.

He got down on his knees, putting his expensive silk trousers directly onto the pristine marble floor. He began groveling.

"I promise I'll be back soon…" Ren pleaded, holding his hands out. "And I'll bring you lots of gifts… anything you want."

Lily puffed up her cheeks until they looked like they were going to pop. She turned her head sharply away from Ren, refusing to look at him.

It dealt critical damage to Ren's soul. He visibly slumped.

[Ana: Ren, why don't you just take her with you? I can't keep watching this pathetic display. Now that we know her innate control is stronger than the standard contract magic, we can let her go out a bit more.]

I would, Ren replied mentally, wincing as Lily pointedly ignored him. But this is a criminal trial. I can't just bring a baby there. It's inappropriate.

Sylas interjected smoothly, sensing his master's distress. "Why not take Mira and the guards along? While you go inside into the court, they can explore the castle gardens. A lot of noble children hang out there with their attendants. It is an integral opportunity for the young lady to socialize."

Ren was surrounded on all sides. Things were made even worse when Mira appeared from the hallway, holding up a cute, fluffy dress she could easily slip Lily into.

Ren gave in. "Fine. But Kaelthos is sticking to her like glue. Along with Mira."

Ren looked outside the mansion gates, where Kaelthos was standing guard in the courtyard garden. Ren issued a clear, direct order.

"Kill any young boy who tries to make a move on her." Ren made a slow, deliberate throat-slitting gesture.

Kaelthos nodded solemnly, recognizing a kindred soul. Sylas coughed into his fist, fervently hoping that was a joke.

Ren finally arrived at the royal castle. He gave Lily a highly emotional goodbye near the carriage while she happily wandered off to explore the sprawling, manicured gardens with her heavily armed entourage.

[Ana: Oh, how the tables have turned.]

You have kids one day, you will understand, Ren shot back defensively.

[Ana: Well, technically speaking, it is my job to baby-sit you.]

Ren didn't reply, sensing a trap there. He turned and walked into the grand hall.

The nobles all stood in rigid, silent lines. The King entered the throne room, his heavy velvet cape trailing behind him. He sat down on the gilded throne, his expression carved from stone.

He announced, his voice booming across the silent hall, "Bring in the accused."

Lord Greenwood was dragged in wrapped in heavy iron chains. It wasn't just him. Members of his immediate family and his branch families were also brought in, all wearing chains. Nobles were held to high and strict laws here in Dolaria. If the head of the house fell, the whole family would be punished alongside him.

The King drummed his fingers on his throne's armrest. "Lady Parisa. A detailed report, if you please."

Parisa stepped forward, the heavy medals on her uniform clinking. She knelt on one knee and began.

"Your Majesty. I had just retired from my long trek returning from the northern borders. I had followed a large merchant caravan which just so happened to stop in front of the Greenwood estate. We were resting there before we returned to the royal barracks. While there, a young elven maid reached out through the estate doors toward me, directly asking for help. By the power vested in me by Your Majesty, I could not ignore the plight. As per my role as Inspector, I investigated the proceedings inside. When I entered the Lord's study, I found him defiling a young maid. Beating them up mercilessly."

All the nobles gathered in the hall gasped. A low, collective murmur of shock rippled through the crowd. Greenwood ground his teeth audibly, obviously displeased with the situation he was in. He was in quite a bind here.

The King, outraged, slammed his hand hard on his armrest. "What blasphemy! You dare break the law and attack one of my citizens? You dare make a contracted elf serve you in such a vile, unspeakable way?"

Parisa interrupted, keeping her head bowed. "Pardon, Your Majesty. There is more."

The King looked utterly dumbfounded. How could there be more? "Continue," the King ordered, struggling to control his rising anger.

In truth, the King was already deeply displeased with Greenwood. The man had actively tried to ruin Ren's ceremony of receiving the Royal Seal of Culinary Excellence with baseless accusations about his daughter. The King liked Ren's cooking.

Parisa continued, her voice ringing clear. "The elves in question were not contracted."

The King leaned forward, his brow furrowing. "They were free citizens?"

Parisa dropped the axe. "No, Your Majesty. They were slaves. For decades, the Greenwoods have been misrepresenting the number of births in the Greenwood-owned elven villages, creating undocumented citizens who have been victims of numerous atrocities. The Greenwoods' actions would end the lives of several such slaves. They were paid no wage. They had no way to buy back their freedom. They were treated like livestock and cattle."

The King was stunned into absolute silence. The entire court was dead silent. This violated the core principles of the kingdom's backbone. If such wrongdoings were not dealt with properly, the nation would face a massive, bloody crisis. This was a law not just enforced in Dolaria, but in every kingdom on this planet. Slavery was outlawed everywhere to create a more humane, manageable system.

Parisa was not done. She stood up. "That is not all, Your Majesty. I have found hard evidence that the Greenwoods have, over the years, created several artificial famines and food shortages. Every famine in the past one hundred years is linked directly to the Greenwoods. They have killed several millions by saying there is no grain, letting it rot in their silos, and then selling the remainder for outrageous prices. They have misused Your Majesty's trust in them. In fact, they had just put into action a plan to create another massive food shortage this very week."

She signaled her attendants. They brought forth a few trembling merchants. The men dictated the current state of the market, explaining how Greenwood had claimed there was no food, whereas Lord Fenris had saved the people by pumping cheap, high-quality food through the spice merchants' networks.

The King and the court remained silent as one piece of damning evidence after the other, endless terminals of paper, were presented documenting the Greenwood family's deep-rooted corruption.

Even Laurana and her younger sister, Larena, were brought forth.

The bandages wrapping Laurana, and the sight of Larena pushing her heavily injured older sister into the grand room on a wheelchair, drew loud gasps from the nobility. Several people physically looked away, unable to stomach the brutal reality.

Laurana detailed exactly how the servants were treated behind closed doors. She told the story of having to clean up the ruined remains of her best friend from the study floor, weeping openly as she spoke. Larena, her younger sister, held her hand tightly, giving her the strength to finish speaking.

The King listened until she was finished. He raised a hand to silence the whispering room.

"I have heard enough." He looked directly at the Royal Magistrate standing nearby. "What is the legal punishment for such atrocities?"

The Magistrate looked shaken. He adjusted his robes nervously. "The Greenwoods, being a count, possess one royal pardon. But the sheer scope of these atrocities... it overrides the pardon. This calls for the maximum penalty and severe, kingdom-altering fines."

The Magistrate unrolled a long scroll. "I have gone over the Greenwoods' finances. They cannot cover the fines and debts from just selling their assets. The State is owed a staggering amount of money by the Greenwoods, as they often borrowed money from the State, used it to buy the grain, and then gave it back only after they sold the grain and received the profits. Also... Lord Fenris is legally owed three elven villages from a previous ruling."

The Magistrate swallowed hard. "The Greenwoods own exactly five villages and associated assets. If we were to sell them all to the highest bidders, it could cover the debt to the Crown, but it would not cover the amount owed to Lord Fenris. Usually, all these crimes mean capital punishment. However, liquidating their assets presents a profound moral dilemma. It would require auctioning off the very elves who suffered under their cruelty to pay for their oppressors' wrongdoings. This situation is incredibly complex regarding financial restitution and needs to be handled as such."

The Magistrate bowed low. "My King, there is no precedent for this specific financial and moral situation. So I ask you to make the final decision."

The King thought deeply, rubbing his bearded chin. He looked out over the court.

"All the elven villages owned by the Greenwoods will be transferred immediately to Lord Fenris, ensuring he receives his three elven villages along with the remaining two as interest," the King commanded. He looked directly at Ren. "I have seen how he treats his elven staff. I trust they will be treated well after the atrocities they have faced."

Ren nodded once, a slow, solemn agreement.

"As for the Greenwoods," the King proclaimed, his voice echoing with absolute finality. "They will pay. All of their remaining assets will be confiscated and sold. The rest of their debt will be recovered by forcing the Greenwoods to work in the royal salt mines until they drop."

The King's decree was brutally clear. The Greenwood family did receive the death sentence, but it was the most cruel, lingering kind.

They would be worked to death.

Being turned into the very same servants they mistreated at a whim. The sentence given to them was worse than a quick beheading. It was being tortured with extreme, backbreaking work in the dark until the day they died. The whole family. Every branch.
