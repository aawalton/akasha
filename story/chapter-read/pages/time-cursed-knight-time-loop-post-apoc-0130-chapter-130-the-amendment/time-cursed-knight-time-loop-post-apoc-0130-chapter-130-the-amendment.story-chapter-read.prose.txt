Silberwald. Secretariat of the Royal Finances.

The window behind Sera's desk overlooked the rooftops of the administrative quarter, where slate tiles and copper guttering descended toward the harbor. On clear mornings, she could see the masts of the coastal haulers.

This morning wasn't clear.

Fog had settled over the capital, blurring the distinction between roofline and sky. The mana-glow lamps along the Secretariat's facade still burned at half strength, their light smeared across the wet stone.

Sera reviewed a shipping manifest. The numbers were routine. Grain allocations for the eastern provinces. Timber surcharges from the northern trade roads. A discrepancy in the Mana Ore tonnage declared at port versus the tonnage received at the Ministry's processing yards.

She made the note. Set the manifest aside.

The next document was a request from House Meltzer for an exemption on the harbor storage tariff applied to their consortium's private warehousing facilities. The request cited precedent from a fifty-year-old ruling.

Sera wrote DENIED in red ink at the top of the page without reading the rest.

The window latch clicked.

She didn't look up.

Lutz pulled himself over the sill, set both feet on the floor, and eased the window shut behind him. He stood for a moment, catching his breath. His boots left no prints on the carpet.

"You could use the door," Sera said.

"The guards log every visitor," Lutz replied. "Two of them have been replaced since last month. I don't know their handlers yet."

Sera set her pen down and looked at him.

"Kaltbach."

Lutz reached inside his coat and produced a folded sheet of paper. He placed it on the corner of her desk.

"Caravan rider from the northern trade road. Arrived at the eastern gate this morning. The official dispatch hasn't reached the palace yet."

Sera unfolded the paper. Her eyes moved across the cramped handwriting.

"Was this her work?" she asked.

Lutz's expression didn't change.

"We haven't been able to place anyone inside the action wing," he said. "Too compartmentalized. Still, whatever happened in Kaltbach seemed to be their work."

Sera looked up from the paper.

"Who did it?"

"We don't know. He was wearing a Regular Army uniform. The garrison doesn't have him on any transfer roster, any deployment order, any pay record."

Sera's jaw tightened.

"The wand," she said. "What about the wand used?"

"Still in the town." Lutz shook his head, a movement so small it barely disturbed the air. "Kaltbach is effectively sealed. The mutant population has barricaded the square. The surviving garrison soldiers pulled back to the town hall and aren't coming out. Everything on the ground is contested."

"Can we get someone in?"

"Not before the Hexenzeit arrive." He hesitated. "There's a complication."

Sera waited.

"Some of the Kaltbach residents have begun petitioning the Republic of Wolkenmark for annexation."

The pen in Sera's hand stopped moving.

"Annexation," she said.

"A delegation of town elders crossed the border two days ago. They're requesting that the Republic extend formal protection over Kaltbach and the surrounding settlements." Lutz's voice remained flat. "No answer yet."

Sera stood.

She walked to the window. The fog had thickened. Even the mana-glow lamps had become uncertain smears of blue.

"House Hexenzeit will have to respond," she said. "Kaltbach sits inside their sphere of influence. If they don't send forces to the border, the Republic reads it as abandonment. If they do send forces, the Republic may mirror the deployment on their side of the line."

"And then a frontier dispute becomes a border standoff."

"And a border standoff becomes a diplomatic incident between two powers that cannot afford to fight each other." Sera pressed her fingertips against the cold glass. "One spark."

Lutz said nothing.

Sera turned from the window.

She crossed back to her desk and sat down. The shipping manifests lay where she had left them.

"I don't understand her," Sera said.

Lutz's brow shifted.

"Elara," Sera said. "I know she controls the coexistence cells. I know the network answers to her through intermediaries. The Academy infiltrations and the pressure on the neutral houses were all her work." She paused. "But there's no coherence. No strategy. Months burning noble estates that had no political alignment. Months disrupting Academy research that had nothing to do with her succession bid. And now this. A revolt in a mutant settlement on the Wolkenmark border."

She looked at the report on her desk.

"Each action damages someone. The damage is scattered." Her fingers drummed once against the desk. "It looks like someone throwing stones into a pond and watching where the ripples go."

Lutz considered this.

"Unless the chaos is the objective," he said.

Sera turned to him.

"Destabilization as strategy," Lutz continued. "If every institution is responding to a different crisis, no institution can coordinate a response to the real threat. The Ministry chases arsonists while the Iron Cross chases narcotics. The Academy hunts infiltrators, the northern garrisons a border revolt. And while everyone is running, nobody is watching the person who lit the fires."

Sera leaned back in her chair.

"There are rumors," she said carefully. "About arrangements. Beyond the court. Beyond human factions entirely."

"I've heard them," he said.

"Do you believe them?"

Lutz didn't answer at once.

"The pattern fits someone who wants the Empire looking everywhere except at her. Allies or not. Same result."

Sera exhaled through her nose.

She could not believe that a princess of Silberwald had made common cause with the things that had driven humanity to the edge of extinction.

And yet every move still lined up with the enemy's interest, or she could not prove it didn't.

Sera did not know whether Elara was a traitor or simply the most reckless political operator in the history of the Crown.

She opened her mouth to speak when the door burst open.

Lord Gerold von Esslinger came in without knocking.

Sera's hand moved toward the drawer where she kept a sealed-crystal panic ward. Then she recognized him.

Esslinger was fifty-eight. A provincial lord from the Kasimir bloc. His face was the color of a radish. The wind had wrecked the oiled arrangement that usually hid his baldness, and his coat was buttoned incorrectly.

"Lady Sera." He braced one hand against the doorframe. His chest heaved. "The court. Now. They are— Varen's people are moving."

Lutz had already stepped sideways, his body angled behind the open door, invisible to anyone in the corridor.

"Moving how?" she asked.

Esslinger gulped air.

"A petition. Filed this morning through the Parlament. They're pushing the creation of a new title. A barony." He wiped his forehead with the back of his hand. "Varen's bloc has already gathered enough supporting votes to bring it to the floor before the morning session ends. If we don't counter now—"

Sera was already standing.

She pulled her coat from the hook beside the window, shrugged it over her shoulders, and fastened the silver Dravenmoor clasp at her collar in a single practiced motion.

"A new title," she said, moving toward the door. "Who?"

Esslinger fell into step beside her. His breathing was beginning to steady.

"The broken Hexenzeit," he said.

Sera's stride didn't break. Behind her face, a gear turned.

"And the territory?" she asked.

"Kaltbach."

They descended the Secretariat's main staircase at a pace that made Esslinger wheeze. Two clerks pressed against the wall to let them pass. A junior accountant with an armful of ledgers froze mid-step and stared.

"They want to create a barony," Sera said, half to herself, "for a disowned Hexenzeit firstborn, over a mutant settlement currently in open revolt on the Wolkenmark border."

Esslinger nodded miserably.

"A collateral branch," he said. "Separate from the ducal house. New arms, new charter, new territory. Legally independent of Roderich's authority."

Kaltbach was remote. It was poor. No mineral deposits, no tax base that would justify a barony. Its economy was subsistence agriculture supplemented by seasonal trade with frontier post riders and the occasional Wolkenmark caravan.

On paper, granting Kaspar von Hexenzeit a barony over Kaltbach was a polite exile. A way of removing an uncomfortable figure from the capital's political theater and placing him somewhere he could do no damage.

"What does he gain?" she murmured.

Esslinger glanced at her.

"My lady?"

"Varen. What does he gain from placing Kaspar in the north? Moving him to a border settlement strips him of proximity to everything that makes him useful."

She pushed through the Secretariat's ground-floor doors into the fog. Esslinger's carriage stood at the curb, emblazoned with a vine-leaf crest. Sera climbed in without invitation. Esslinger followed, wheezing.

The driver didn't need instruction.

Sera stared at the fog beyond the window.

"Unless," she said, "the value is the precedent."

Esslinger waited.

"A titled lord with Crown authority can operate independently within his holdings." She turned the implications over. "If Kaspar builds his weapons factory outside the capital—"

Esslinger shook his head before she finished.

"With respect, my lady, I don't believe that's the play." He had recovered enough breath to speak in full sentences. "An industrial facility in Kaltbach makes no sense. The logistics alone would be crippling. Every raw material would need to be shipped north along trade roads that barely support grain carts. The cost per unit would double. Triple, perhaps."

He tugged at his incorrectly buttoned coat without noticing.

"And that's before you consider the labor problem. Voralberg Magitek's advantage is its proximity to the Academy. Researchers, glyph specialists, crystal cutters. Those people live in the capital because the Academy is in the capital. You cannot relocate an entire research program to a frozen border settlement and expect anyone worth hiring to follow. The best minds in the Empire did not spend a decade earning their credentials so they could practice their craft in a village that doesn't have a functioning bathhouse."

Sera said nothing.

"Then why?" she said quietly. "Creating a title is expensive. Varen is spending favors, burning goodwill, accelerating a petition through the Parlament before anyone can organize opposition. He is paying a real price for this. Which means he expects a real return. And I can't see what it is."

Esslinger said nothing more. The carriage climbed toward the palace district.

The court was already in session.

The Hall of Deliberation occupied the western wing of the Parlament, a long, high-ceilinged chamber with tiered oak galleries rising on three sides. The imperial seal hung above the speaker's podium, flanked by banners in the Crown's colors.

The galleries were full.

Sera had not expected that. A petition for a minor barony in a frontier settlement should have drawn half-empty benches and the atmosphere of boredom that characterized the court's administrative sessions. Instead, every tier was occupied. Noble houses in formal colors sat with military officers in dress uniform and Academy faculty in their robes.

Varen's people had been busy.

The prince himself wasn't present. That was expected. Varen attended the sessions where his agenda was threatened.

Falke was present. The captain sat in the second tier of the western gallery, his uniform immaculate, his expression carefully neutral. Two other officers from Varen's inner circle flanked him.

On the floor, a clerk from the Chancellor's office read the petition aloud.

"...pursuant to the Crown's authority under Article Seven of the Charter of Noble Creation, and in recognition of distinguished military service to the Empire, the petitioner requests the elevation of Kaspar von Hexenzeit to the rank of Baron, with territorial jurisdiction over the settlement of Kaltbach and its surrounding demesne, as defined by the attached survey..."

Sera took her seat in the Kasimir bloc's section of the eastern gallery. Esslinger settled beside her.

The debate had already begun.

A lord from the Varen bloc stood at the speaker's rail. Sera recognized him as Baron Holst, a minor house with shipping interests.

"The precedent is clear," Holst said. "Military distinction has been the basis for noble elevation since the founding of the Empire. Kaspar von Hexenzeit defended Grenzheim against an elven assault. He was commended by the Iron Cross. He was acquitted of all charges in a public tribunal attended by a foreign head of state." He paused for effect. "The Crown has the authority. The candidate has the merit. The territory is available."

Benches shifted.

A woman from the Kasimir bloc rose.

"Available because it is in revolt," she said. Her voice was dry. "The petitioner asks us to grant a title over territory the Crown does not currently control. Has anyone informed the residents of Kaltbach that they are to receive a lord they did not request?"

Holst ignored it.

"The pacification of Kaltbach is part of the mandate," he said. "The candidate has been dispatched under Iron Cross orders to resolve the situation. The title is contingent upon successful resolution."

"Contingent." The woman's eyebrows rose. "So we are granting a title before the condition is met."

"The formalization follows the achievement."

The woman sat down.

Sera watched.

She counted votes. The Varen bloc had the numbers to pass the petition if it came to a straight division. Kasimir's allies could delay but not defeat.

The debate continued. Three more speakers from each faction. Arguments about precedent and the legal status of a disowned firstborn.

None of it mattered. The votes were already aligned.

Sera was calculating which amendments would give the Kasimir bloc a window into Kaspar's operations when the voice came from the far end of the gallery.

"If I may."

The Hall fell quiet.

Princess Elara stood in the royal observation box.

She had been sitting there since before Sera arrived. She had set aside her Academy robes for a deep blue gown in the old imperial style, high-collared, heavy enough to hold its shape without ornament. Her dark hair was drawn back from her face.

"Your Highness," the clerk said, bowing.

Elara descended the three steps from the observation box to the speaker's rail.

"I will be brief," she said. "I support this petition."

No one spoke.

The Varen bloc's surprise was visible. Falke's composure cracked for half a second before reassembling. Two officers beside him exchanged a glance.

The Kasimir bloc looked at Sera. She kept her face empty.

"Kaspar von Hexenzeit," Elara continued, "is a hero of the Empire. His service at Grenzheim, his conduct during the tribunal, his contributions to the advancement of Imperial weapons technology all demonstrate a character worthy of elevation." She paused. "I see no reason to oppose the recognition of merit, regardless of which faction benefits from the optics."

A murmur rippled through the galleries.

Sera's fingers pressed together beneath the rail where no one could see them.

"However," Elara said.

The murmur died.

"A hero of Kaspar von Hexenzeit's stature should not be diminished by a grant that fails to match his accomplishments."

She let the words settle.

"Kaltbach is a frontier settlement." She turned her gaze toward the Varen bloc's gallery. "To grant him jurisdiction over a village and a population of discontented farmers..." She tilted her head. "That is not elevation. That is exile dressed in ceremony."

Sera's stomach dropped.

"If we are to honor Kaspar von Hexenzeit," Elara said, "then let us honor him properly."

She straightened.

"I propose that the territorial grant be expanded to include the southern town of Grenzheim and its garrison district."
