After crashing into the ground from my thunder strike, Gal’Meth roared so loudly that the Elven Tree itself shook. Nearly half of its leaves fell to the ground, but no matter how many did, the tree regenerated them all in an instant.

The demon tried standing, but I didn’t let him. I pointed my dagger at him. Outside of that gesture, there was no other warning—no rumbling clouds, no sound. Not until thunder struck him again.

But the lightning attack was more than just one. I continued chaining them one after the other in the same spot to the point where I couldn’t even see Gal’Meth anymore.

Meriel, who was standing on the opposite side of the thunder, sent dozens of fiery spells his way. She kept an eye on Allene’s resting body, which was fortunately far away from our spells. Our attacks were constantly backed by the Elven Tree’s roots as they sliced away at the demon.

The visceral, deafening roars continued. It sounded like a primal cry—not a cry from someone who was able to reason. It sounded like the pain and suffering of a hundred wretched beasts condensed into one.

When Meriel and I finally stopped our attacks, something deep in my soul convinced me that the fight wasn’t over. Yet, when all the smoke settled, it revealed a far too optimistic sight.

Gal’Meth was on his knees, his flesh charred and his wings completely torn. Blood covered every part of his body, dripping into the mist below and disappearing. Every bit of hair he once had was gone. Burned to nothingness.

“Get Allene,” I told Meriel. “I’ll handle this.”

“Will you get her soul back?” she asked.

I began walking toward the demon. “I will.”

She nodded and went over to Allene’s body.

I clutched my chest. The pain in my heart was unbearable, and my breaths grew shallow, resembling gasps rather than breathing. I had cast too much lightning magic to make sure the demon was truly defeated. Too much for my own body to handle.

But I had to make sure he was defeated. I had to.

Because he was the most horrifying thing I had ever faced.

I gathered myself, slowing my breathing and feeling the pain slowly releasing. Once I was ready, I stood in front of the demon. His face was covered in burns, and he didn’t move despite my presence. It was hard to tell if he was even alive, but I knew he was.

He couldn’t have possibly fallen from our attacks.

“Give Allene’s soul back,” I said. “And I will spare you.”

No response.

“Ah. So now you’re pretending, are you? You, who once acted so high and mighty, only to be defeated by bolts of lightning and fire in your own realm.”

Silence yet again.

I gritted my teeth, kicking his near-motionless body.

“You’re not dead,” I said. “Don’t disappoint me. Say something, or else I’ll reach into that cosmos within your body and pull her soul back out myself.”

Gal’Meth slowly looked at me, but only with his eyes. The rest of his body stayed perfectly still.

“If you… do that… you’ll lose the arm,” he said. “I… can only imagine what it’s like… to live without any.”

I frowned. “Are you mocking me?”

“I’m protecting you.”

I laughed in disbelief. “You? A demon protecting me? From trying to save my friend?”

The demon looked to the side, where Meriel and Allene were. Meriel sat on the ground, letting Allene lie on her lap.

“You should… be careful what you wish for,” Gal’Meth said. “Being a savior comes with the risk… of saving someone you shouldn’t save.”

The demon chuckled to himself—not the manic chuckling we’d heard from him before but a softer one. “You should heed my advice. I see what others don’t.”

“Like what? Souls?”

“Yes.”

“Then what do you see in ours?”

Gal’Meth struggled to raise his arm, pointing it at his chest cavity, where Allene’s soul resided alongside countless others.

“Her soul is… strange. That much is true. But through it, I see a life of laughter, joy, and spontaneity. I see a life nearly everyone yearns for. A life that stays true to itself.”

“And you’ve taken that life away from her,” I said.

Gal’Meth shook his head. “No. I’ll give her soul back.”

“What…?”

“I’m not your enemy. Nor am I a demon who wishes to exploit his own brethren. We Firstborns are… limited in this world. We’re not who we once were. And it pains me. It was peaceful then. We were the first to walk this world before the rest came. After they did, peace was no more.”

“Before the rest came?” I said. “Who? Do you mean the other races?”

“The other races that stole our faces,” Gal’Meth said. “The modern age speaks of us as if we resemble them. But how could that be if we came first? They resemble us. They stole our identities. Not us.”

I frowned. “You’re the one who said I was wearing the face of a human.”

“I was only repeating what everyone else thinks.”

He mustered every ounce of strength he could to squeeze his fist. “They must be punished for what they caused.”

“You’re letting anger speak for you,” I said. “Just like any other demon.”

He looked at his own hand. “Perhaps spending so much time as a demon made me this way.”

He tried to sit up, but he couldn’t.

“Go on about what you were talking about earlier,” I said. “Our souls. You told me about Allene’s, but what do you see in mine?”

Gal’Meth stared at the Elven Tree above. One of the leaves fell on his face.

“Whereas Allene’s soul depicts serenity, yours is… the opposite. I see sacrifice. Dread. Horror. And above all—tragedy.”

“That doesn’t sound like me.”

“No?” Gal’Meth said. “Not even after your closest ally killed your lover? Not even after losing a limb in battle?”

I shook my head. “Those mean nothing. There are others out there who have experienced far more tragedies than me.”

He laughed. “How humble. I suppose you’re right. But for as long as you live, tragedy has yet to fully set its sights on you. Perhaps you’re safe now… but I predict your tale has only just begun. So be it. I’ll give your party the Soul Transference spell.”

I clutched my dagger. “I find it hard to believe you’d just give it up like that.”

“Look at the state I’m in now,” he said. “Do you think I can fight like this?”

I looked at his body. Every part of his body was burned and bloody. He stood no chance against us anymore. Even a child could defeat him in his current state.

…

No. No, that wasn’t it. I knew the truth. I just didn’t want to admit it.

“You did this on purpose, didn’t you?” I said. “You let us defeat you.”

“Why do you think that?”

“Because something tells me a demon with the rank of Sovereign has more to show than everything I’ve seen so far.”

Gal’Meth smiled at me. “Your eyes see well.”

“I’m blessed by the winds,” I said. “They show me what others fail to see.”

“Then I suppose we both see what others don’t.”

Gal’Meth snapped his fingers, and an invisible wave washed over the realm. The Elven Tree began to fade away, and the glow with it.

“What’s going on?” Meriel said from afar. “Kaelor—”

Once the invisible wave reached her, she froze. Her mouth stayed open mid-sentence. Allene’s body was still resting on her lap, just as still as she was.

“Meriel?” I called.

She didn’t respond or move. Whatever just happened, it had rendered her frozen in time.

“What have you done…?” I said as I turned to Gal’Meth.

My eyes widened. He was… standing. And his wounds—his burns, his blood, his injuries—they were all gone.

How was that even possible?

“Allene and Meriel are hiding something from you,” Gal’Meth said, stretching his arms. “Something they can’t tell you or the others. Would you like to hear it?”

“Hiding something…?” I muttered. “How would you know about it?”

“As I said—I hear everything, even while my soul resides within the Soul Anchor Ring. I heard every conversation Allene has had, so long as the ring stays with her and her [INVENTORY].”

I raised a brow. “[INVENTORY]? What do you mean?”

“A System skill, as Allene calls it. She has several. Haven’t you noticed something strange about her? She has magic that is rather… unorthodox. Magic that doesn’t seem to belong in this world.”

“I’d be lying if I said otherwise. How did she even get them?”

Gal’Meth shook his head. “I don’t know. She mentions words I do not understand. The System. Tropes. The Narrative. Do you know what any of these mean?”

“I don’t. But that’s strange… Why would she hide something like that from me? From the rest of the party?”

“She didn’t hide it from everyone. The elven one knows about these things.”

I looked at Meriel, who was still frozen in time.

“Why her? Is something about her different?”

The more I thought about it, the more the pieces started to fall into place.

“Actually, I have noticed something strange,” I went on. “Meriel suddenly became stronger during our battle with Aurelius—right after she had suffered a grave injury. It was like a second wind. Every spell of hers became far more potent, and she seemed to have more control of her mana than usual. What was even stranger was that she was able to cast these spells without a staff or grimoire, even though she was never able to do so before. I had thought that this was just temporary, but it seems I was wrong.”

“But that’s not the strangest part,” Gal’Meth said. “You’re starting to realize it now, aren’t you? About the rest of the party members?”

I nodded. I didn’t want to admit it, but he was right.

“None of them noticed it,” I said. “Reina, Aeris, Talon—they never said anything about Meriel’s sudden increase in power. Aeris might’ve questioned it initially, but she never asked anything about it again after that. But why? Why am I the only one who seems concerned about it? I just never said anything about it because the others weren’t either, but now I realize something must be wrong. It’s almost as if…”

“An external force is steering them away from questioning it?” Gal’Meth said.

I didn’t say anything.

The demon crossed his arms. “Whatever the case is, that’s something you’ll have to find out on your own. What do you think about all of this? Do you still trust Allene?”

I slowly turned to her.

“She has a reason for all of this. She must.”

Gal’Meth nodded. “Then hold onto that trust for as long as you can.”

He walked over to the other two. I pointed my dagger at him.

“Don’t you dare hurt them.”

He glanced at me. “Would you be able to stop me even if I do?”

“…”

I followed right behind him, putting my dagger away.

“Why are you helping us?” I asked.

“Not them. Just you.”

“Because I’m a Firstborn?”

“Because we’re similar in every aspect,” he said. “I see a part of myself in you.”

I narrowed my eyes. “How am I supposed to trust a demon?”

He gave it a second’s thought before turning to me.

“I’ll earn it.”

With that, he reached into his cosmic chest cavity and pulled out a soul—Allene’s soul. He crouched down and released it, letting it slip through her slightly opened mouth as he returned her soul.

Then, he snapped his fingers. The entire realm began to collapse.
