corePremise: In the world of Aethel, where the System has shaped civilization for four hundred years, sixteen-year-old Wren Galley walks into the unmapped frontier called the Thornwild — not to fight, not to conquer, but to pay attention. Wren is a Wayfinder, a class the System has never issued before. She has no combat skills, no attack abilities, no damage-dealing spells. What she has is an unprecedented capacity for Sympathetic Bonding — forming deep, two-way relationships with the places she explores.
povApproach: Third person limited, Wren primary. Her internal monologue is warm, observant, and gently self-deprecating. Sensory descriptions lean toward touch, sound, and smell over sight.
emotionalCore: A story about a girl who carries places with her the way other people carry memories of friends. It is about the quiet power of paying attention, the ache of letting go, and the stubborn joy of building something good in a world that keeps trying to swallow it.
antagonistForce: The Thornwild isn't unmapped because no one tried. It's unmapped because something already claimed it. A massive, hostile imprint — possessive where Wren's is respectful, overwriting where hers is communing — has bonded with the land so aggressively that it has erased the region's original identity.
heroinesPurpose: Wren's gentle cartography is the counter-force. Her Sympathetic Bonds don't just map the Thornwild — they heal it, peeling back the corruption to reveal what the land was before it was claimed. In the heart of the Thornwild, buried under centuries of hostile overgrowth, she finds the ruins of a settlement that the corruption tried to erase entirely. Wren decides to restore it.
