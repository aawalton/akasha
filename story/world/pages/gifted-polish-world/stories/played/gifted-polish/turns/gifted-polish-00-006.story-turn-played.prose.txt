You lie still and listen. You want a word out of it, one whole word, any word at all.

First you sort the voices. Two deep ones, maybe three, a little way off. And one close above you, a woman's, that hums between the things she says, as if she can't quite stop.

They talk low and quick. You can follow the shape of it, a question going up at the end, an answer coming down flat, someone cutting in. The sounds inside the shapes smear together, like people talking at the bottom of a swimming pool.

You strain after any piece you might know. A the, an and, a yes. The beat of it is wrong for English, though. The stresses land in odd places, and the sounds run on without the little stops you'd expect between words.

You keep at it a good while, and words open in your mind.

name: Path of Eavesdropping
:::

So the listening counts for something, even if you can't make out a single word yet.

You push harder. You spread yourself out through the body, into the fingertips and the soles of the feet and the curve of the back, as close to the wall as you can get, as if getting nearer would let the words through.

The body doesn't take it the way you meant. All of it jerks at once, the way a leg kicks out when you're falling asleep, and one heel drives hard into the wall.
