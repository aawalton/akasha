Cold comes first: a damp chill up your spine, and the smell of crushed clover under your cheek.

You're lying on your side on the ground. Above you, where a ceiling should be, the sky is the washed-out blue of a mid-autumn afternoon with thin cloud dragged across it. Wind moves over you, open and steady, and it carries sheep, wet grass and a faint trace of woodsmoke from somewhere far below.

When you push yourself up, the hand you push with isn't yours. It's smaller and fine-boned, with clean short nails, and the wrist is narrow enough to circle with a thumb and finger. Hair falls across your face, long, warm and heavy, and you have to brush it away to see. The thin sleep shirt and soft shorts you went to bed in are still on you. They hang differently now and sit on a body that curves where yours never did, and the chill finds every place the cloth doesn't cover. Your feet are bare and cold.

You're in a ring of pale stones. It may once have been a building, but now it's a broken circle of tumbled blocks, none higher than your waist, with clover grown thick between them. At its center, so close your shoulder brushed it when you sat up, stands a stone plinth. Something is carved into its face: a long, shallow arc in bands, worn so smooth by weather that you can feel it better than you can see it.

Past the stones the hill drops away into gorse. A narrow track threads down through it, and far below, where the land flattens, there's a town with walls, a river shining like a dropped ribbon, and smoke rising from a hundred chimneys. The sun is past its peak and already leaning west.

Then the air in front of you lights up: a rectangle of soft, glassy blue at reading distance that stays put when you turn your head. It's as solid as the stones, with clean white letters you can read without trying, in an alphabet you've never seen.

:::status-assessment
name: Nala
level: 1
note: Class: none; Location: Wayshrine (fallen), Tallowmere Hills
:::

It's flat and exact, like a form at the DMV.

Then, under the last line, smaller words write themselves in. They come in fits and starts, the way someone types when they've stopped to stare.

*...oh.*

*Hi. Hello. You're new: Level 1, and no shoes.*

*Your feet look cold.*

The wind keeps going. Down in the valley, a bell rings once, very small, and goes quiet.

*Are you okay?*
