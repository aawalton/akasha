The last thing you remember is your back window. A book open on your knee, the evening going blue outside the glass, your eyes getting heavy.

Then cold, wet grass against your cheek, and a smell of earth and something like old smoke.

You open your eyes.

Grey stone. A tall slab of it, a few feet from your face, rough and spotted with pale green lichen. Beyond it, another. And another. They stand in a ring around you, each one taller than a man, leaning a little, with nothing on top of them but sky. The sky is pale, washed-out blue, the sun not up yet.

You push yourself up, and your arms are wrong.

They are thin. The wrists are narrow, the hands small and pale, a few freckles across the backs. You turn them over. They do what you tell them, a half second late, as if they are still learning how.

Hair falls across your face when you look down. Long, heavy, dark red. You pull it back, and it keeps going, past your shoulders, past your shoulder blades.

Your shirt is your shirt. The old dark grey one, but it hangs off you now, the collar sliding off one shoulder, the hem down near the middle of your thighs. Your black tights are still on, soaked through at the knees. Your feet are bare, and small.

Your chest is not your chest. You look, once, and then you look at the stones instead.

You get up. The ground is lower than it should be.

In the middle of the ring sits a flat grey stone, waist high, with a picture cut into its top: a door, and beside it a key. Someone has left things on it. A green copper coin. A dried flower, brown at the edges. A crust of bread with something's tooth marks in it.

Outside the ring, the hill falls away. A road, two wheel ruts and a strip of grass, curls down the slope and into a valley full of white mist. Where the mist thins, you can see roofs. A few of them, dark and sharp-edged, and a thread of smoke going straight up.

Birds are starting somewhere below. Your breath shows in the air. Your feet are going numb in the wet grass.

The coin on the stone catches the first of the light.
