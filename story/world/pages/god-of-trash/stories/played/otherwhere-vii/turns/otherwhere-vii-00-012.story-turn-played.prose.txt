"From far enough away you haven't heard the name," you say. "And no one's who still walks this earth."

Hild holds that a moment, watching your face. Then she reaches out and takes your hand and turns it over.

"Soft," she says, and draws her thumb across the palm. "Blistered in a morning. You've never turned a field."

She looks down at the clogs, and at the scabbed cuts above them.

"No name I'd know. No kin, no shoes." She lets the hand go. "Orphan, exile or runaway. It's one of the three."

She sits back.

"I'll not press you past that. You'd not answer it straight, and I've no need. I've known a few of each, and they come in with that same look about the mouth."

She reaches into her basket and sets a small pot of grease on the board, by your cup.

"Comfrey and lard. Work it into the palms tonight and the blisters will hold." Her finger rests on the lid. "A penny, or a morning's picking. Which I'd sooner. Yarrow and comfrey by the bunch, a quarter-penny a bunch, fresh cut. The riverbank's picked bare; the good wet ground's north."

She turns to Joan, and the fever has a week in it, and Bet watched again tonight.

Joan nods. She takes the little pot and sets it up on the shelf, and doesn't mention paying.

"She slept badly," Hild says. "And the fever takes a child here in some years."

At the door she stops with her basket on her arm, and looks back at you a long moment.

"A woman alone needs a place, and Ashford can be one. It holds, and the folk in it hold."

"So. Are you for staying here, or for the road?"

"I'll put in a word for you in this village, or I'll hold my peace about you. Which is it?"
