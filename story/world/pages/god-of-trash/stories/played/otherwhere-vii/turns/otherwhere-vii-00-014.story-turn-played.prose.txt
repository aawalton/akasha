You go straight to the shelf and take the little pot down, and pry the lid off with your thumbnail.

It smells of lard and green leaves. You scoop some out on two fingers and work it into your palms.

It stings on the open blisters, sharp enough that you suck in a breath through your teeth. Then it cools.

Hild is still at the door. She watches you do it, and her mouth goes flat.

"Tonight, I said." She sets her basket down on the step. "By day it softens the skin, and the first thing you grip after tears it open. Slept on, it heals." She nods at the pot in your hand. "That's one of four you've had out of the pot. Mind the other three."

She picks the basket up again.

"First light at the mill, if you're coming. I'll not wait long."

She goes. Her clogs knock away across the yard toward the green.

Joan watches her go. Then she looks at your palms, shining with grease.

The corner of her mouth twitches.

She nods at the two wooden buckets by the door, and at the water butt beside them, half empty.

"Butt wants filling before the men come in thirsty. Two buckets, to the well and back."
