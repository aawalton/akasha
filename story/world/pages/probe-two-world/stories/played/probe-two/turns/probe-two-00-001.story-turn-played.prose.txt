You stand in the cobbled square. A well sits at its middle, a round shaft of fitted stone with a rim about waist-high, and a wooden bench stands beside it. The morning light lies flat across the cobbles, and the stones around the well are worn smooth.

You walk over to the well, your footsteps loud on the cobbles, and stop at its rim. You rest your hands on the stone. It is cool under your palms, and the joins between the blocks are tight enough that your fingers find only thin seams. A wooden bucket sits on the rim a little to your left, its rope coiled in loops beside it.

You lean out over the rim, your weight on your hands, and look down the shaft. The walls go down a long way. Near the top the stone is pale grey in the daylight, and each course below is a shade darker than the one above it. Far below, at the bottom, lies water. It is still and clear, and a small patch of morning light sits on its surface.

At the edge of your sight, just past the well's base, a stone stair leads down out of the square. You straighten up, your hands still on the rim. The bucket sits by your left hand with its coiled rope, and the top step of the stair is a pace to your side.
