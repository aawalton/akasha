"Thank you, that's very kind," you say, and take the shoes and the coat from him. "May I know the name of my garden knight? I'm Nala."

Maddox snorts. His ears go red.

"Knight," he mutters, and looks off at the weir. The corner of his mouth twitches, and he flattens it straight again.

"Orrin Maddox," he says. "Low Bank just calls me Maddox." He looks back at you. "Nala."

You step into the canvas shoes. They're far too long, and when you take a step your heels slide loose inside them, lifting and slapping down again.

The coat is heavier than it looks. It hangs past your knees, and the cuffs swallow your hands whole. But the wool is warm at once, and the cold you'd stopped noticing goes out of your arms.

"Turn those cuffs back," Maddox says. "And mind the shoes on the Stair. The steps are worn smooth." He tips his head at the coat. "Bring them back any morning. I'm here at first light, every day. Kitchen on Tanners Row's right on your way. Porridge'll be on now."

Then he nods once, takes up his stick, and goes on along the path toward the far benches, boots crunching on the gravel.

You're left on the path by your bench, in his coat and his shoes. The gate is a stone's throw off. Past it, Weir Street climbs away from the river, toward the police post at the top, and Tanners Row with its kitchen, and the Stair somewhere beyond. The lamps along it are still lit, all the way up.
