"Sorry, I meant the Institute, of course," you say. "Still waking up, it seems. Point me in the right direction? I'm not afraid of the hills. Give me a chance to clear my head."

His eyes stay on yours a moment. You can see him take note that the question about your head went by without an answer.

Then he lets it go too. He turns and points his stick up the street past the railings.

"Up Weir Street," he says. "Past the police post at the top, then straight over Tanners Row. Past Tanners Row you'll find the Long Stair. Near three hundred steps, old stone. It comes out at the corner of Guildhall Square, and the Institute's on Mercer Street, just off it."

He lowers the stick. "Or there's Hill Road. Winds round the long way. The buses climb it from half past six."

His eyes drop to your feet again, and he shakes his head.

"Not up the Stair like that, you're not," he says. "Hang on."

He walks to a small tool shed by the gate, unlocks it, and rummages inside.

He comes back with a pair of men's canvas shoes, grey and scuffed, plainly too big for you. Over his arm hangs a brown wool coat, long and heavy and worn, with a button missing.

"Lost property," he says. "Folk leave all sorts in the park. These have never been claimed."

He holds them out to you.
