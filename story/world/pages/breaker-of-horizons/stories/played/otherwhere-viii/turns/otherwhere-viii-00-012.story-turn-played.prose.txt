You take off Maddox's coat and hang it on a peg.

The crate under the bed slides out with a scrape. You take out a shirt. It's enormous on you, the shoulders down at your elbows and the hem near your knees, and you put it on anyway.

Then you lie down on the narrow bed. The plan is simple: sleep until the work below stops, and the stopping will tell you it's time to learn.

The straw mattress is lumpy, and smells faintly of pipe smoke. The room is warm, and quiet for now.

Your body won't settle. It isn't tired. As far as it's concerned, it has only just woken up.

For a long while you lie looking at the slope of the ceiling.

At last you slide into a thin, shallow doze.

Then, below you, the grinding wheels start up, and the whine comes straight up through the boards. Files rasp. A hammer rings. Voices rise under it all.

You're awake with a jolt.

A few minutes, no more. Your head is thick with it. Your mouth is dry, and you realise you haven't had anything to drink since you woke in the park.

Feet come up the back stair, quick and light. Someone raps twice on the door.

It opens on a girl of about seventeen in a grey canvas apron, with ink on her fingertips. She looks at you on the bed in the huge shirt, and her eyebrows go up.

"You're the new bench hand?" she says. "Benches started. You're meant to be down."

She keeps one hand on the door frame.

"The master only pays a hand for the hours they're actually down at a bench," she says.
