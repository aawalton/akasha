You run after him across Mercer Street. The canvas shoes slap at the cobbles, and one nearly flies off your foot; you curl your toes and keep it.

You reach him at the side door just as his key goes into the lock.

"Master, may I have a word?" you say, a little out of breath. "I have knowledge to share with a man of learning. I believe it would help you advance your position at the Institute."

He turns, the rolls in one hand, the key still in the other. His face is tired, and you can see it already closing.

"Advance my position," he says flatly. "I run the place."

His eyes go over you: the long lost-property coat, the too-big shoes, your red hair uncombed and loose down your back.

"If it's a secret sequence you're selling," he says, "or a charm that never fails, I've bought my last."

He starts to turn back to the door, and stops.

You're standing within arm's length of him. He stays there, half turned, and doesn't move.

His eyes narrow. His head tilts a fraction, the way a person's does when they're listening for a sound in the next room.

He studies your face. It goes on far longer than a stranger at a door is usually looked at.

You don't feel anything. You can't tell what he's looking at.

He draws the key back out of the lock.

"One question, then," he says. "What do you know that I don't? Show me. Don't tell me."
