"Oh, yes sir," you say, and get up off the bench.

Standing is worse than sitting. Your balance sits somewhere new, lower and further forward, and the gravel is nearer than it should be, as if the world has been taken down a foot.

The man on the path is in his fifties, broad and weathered, in heavy boots and a long dark green coat. A small brass badge sits on his breast. He carries a long stick with an iron tip, the kind for spearing litter off a lawn.

He stops a few paces off. His eyes go to your bare feet, then the loose shirt, then your face.

Something in his face shifts, and the morning bark goes out of him.

Close to, you come up to about his collarbone. You have to tip your head back to meet his eyes.

"Could you help me get oriented?" you ask. "I'm not sure how I ended up here. Where am I, exactly?"

The words come out easily, in the same tongue he spoke. You didn't reach for them. They were just there when you opened your mouth, the way your own language always is, and only now that they're out do you notice they weren't English.

"This is Weir Gardens," he says. "Low Bank."

You look at him blankly.

He tips his head at the river, white and loud over the weir. "Carrowgate. On the Carrow." He says it slowly, the way you'd give directions to a tourist. "Maddox. I'm the warden. Twenty years I've walked these paths at first light."

Behind him, the park gate stands under an iron arch, and raised iron letters run along the top of it. Angular shapes, curling at the ends. You have never seen them before in your life.

They read WEIR GARDENS to you all the same.

The air is cool on your arms and on your bare feet. Above you the plane trees are in new leaf, small and pale. Over the rooftops the sky is turning from grey to a thin light.

"Someone take your things, miss?" he asks. There's no edge in it. "Shoes, bag, the lot?" He lets that sit a moment. "Or you've just had a bad night of it. I've seen both. Neither's any shame."

"There's a police post at the north end of Weir Street. Five minutes' walk. And if you're hungry, the kitchen on Tanners Row feeds whoever queues at dawn. About now, that'd be."

He hooks a thumb in his coat pocket. The iron tip of the stick rests in the gravel by his boot.
