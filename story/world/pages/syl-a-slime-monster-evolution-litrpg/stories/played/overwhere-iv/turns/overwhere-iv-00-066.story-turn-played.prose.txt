At the Brook and Barrel, you ask for a bath. Two copper buys a wooden tub behind the kitchen.

The serving girl heats the water in the copper, carries it out pail by pail, and bars the washhouse door.

Soap, a towel, hot water to your chin. Seven miles of moor soak out of you.

Supper in the taproom: mutton stew, bread, small ale. Marta waves off your coin again.

The room talks only of the head on Ilsa's counter.

A wolf the size of a horse, says one table. A demon out of the old quarry, says another.

You walk back to the gatehouse and sleep in your curtained corner of the bunk room.

At dawn you drill with the watch, a spear from the rack in your hands. Your body knows the moves now.

Porridge after drill. Then across the square to Tobin's.

The spear is on the bench, fitted and oiled.

A leaf blade a hand and a half long, dark grey, with a bright edge and a riveted socket.

The shaft is new seasoned ash, cut to your height, with an iron cap on the butt.

Tobin wipes his hands. "Seventy-five more," he says. "Counted, this time."

You count it out on the bench. He counts it again.

"Heard about the wolf's head." He hands you the spear. "That old practice spear. Did it hold?"
