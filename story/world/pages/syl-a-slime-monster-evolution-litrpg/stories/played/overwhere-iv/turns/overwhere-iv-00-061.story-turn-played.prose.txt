You keep your eyes shut. The rend and the sense come from the same place. They must fit together.

You hold your sense wide on the hollow under the rock. The shape there is as clear as anything seen.

You fix on the throat, felt rather than seen, and reach for a line.

For an instant something catches: a flicker of the fold, laid right where you feel it.

Then it's gone. The line doesn't open, and it doesn't take any warmth.

You try again, and again. Each time the flicker comes, nearly whole, and slips away.

It isn't a wall. It's like a word on the tip of your tongue. Something you can learn.

By the sixth try it's the same lesson over. Trying again here won't teach you more today.

All the while, holding your sense out to thirty paces drains you steadily.

Ten minutes gone. The beast hasn't stirred. The wind still blows from it to you.

Behind your ribs, the warmth has thinned to a little over half.
