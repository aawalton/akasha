That's progress for today. You open your eyes and back away from the gully's edge, slow and quiet.

You circle north, up onto the gully's rim. Soft heather underfoot, the wind still in your face.

You creep along the rim toward the gully's head, feeling ahead for each step.

Fifty paces. The heather muffles you. Below and ahead, the gorse hides the hollow under the rock.

You edge closer, looking for a gap to see through.

Under the heather, a stone you took for solid turns beneath your foot.

It rolls, and goes clattering down the gully side, knocking others loose.

Below, the great shape under the rock is on its feet before the last stone stops.

It bursts out from under the shelf: grey-black, tall as a pony, the ridge on its back raised.

Silent, it makes straight for the scramble below you, at a dead run.
