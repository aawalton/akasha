You hand Dace the horn. "Great! I'm hunting the Tangle tonight."

"If there are enough goblins to be bothering them tonight," you say, "I'm going to make sure there aren't tomorrow."

"I should be okay on my own, but I'll be glad to know you're nearby, so I can fall back in your direction if I get overwhelmed. Is that okay?"

Dace looks from you to the split boulders and back. "It's mad," he says. "But aye."

"Come out at Tull's ford calling my name, so Merrit won't fire on you."

"We'll keep a lantern lit in Tull's fold all night. Steer by it."

He tucks the horn in his belt and jogs off downhill toward town.

You walk south along the Tangle's edge as the light goes. A half moon hangs over the trees.

At full dark you cross Tull's ford and find the deer trail where it leaves the wood.

Under the oaks it is near black. Your sense holds the trail for you: roots, ruts, a pace at a time.

You go slow and quiet, deeper in. The warmth behind your ribs fills as you walk.

Two hours in the dark. An owl. Something small bolting through the leaf litter.

Then, far up the trail and coming toward you: many feet, the knock of wood, a clink of iron.

A big band, walking down the deer trail in the dark, toward Tull's ford.
