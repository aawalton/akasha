Tull's son is young, nineteen or so, propped on a cot with his head bound in linen. His eyes go wide at the sight of you.

You sit on the stool by the cot and tell it as a hero's tale.

The creep up the trail. The bowmen first. A careful cut of your spear skill for each, at a distance.

The hobgoblin with his horn half raised. One cut, through the throat, before he could blow.

Two runners chased down along the stream. The last one hiding among the sheep.

Every kill a careful cut of the spear skill. The boy hangs on every word, nodding along to all of it.

Tull stands in the doorway and hears it out in silence. He doesn't ask a thing.

"A spear skill," his son breathes. "Seven of them. Wait till they hear at the Brook and Barrel."

With the cheese under your arm, you walk the two miles to Millbrook in the afternoon sun.

The hall is quiet. Ilsa looks up from her ledger as you set the sack on the counter.

Seven ears, a cracked ox horn on a greasy cord, and word that five sheep are home at Tull's.

Ilsa counts the ears twice. She turns the horn over in her hands. "The hobgoblin's."

"Alone, the whole band, and not a scratch on you." She writes it plainly: goblins slain.

She counts out the pay: a silver an ear, five for the hobgoblin, three copper a sheep.

Twelve silver and five copper, stacked on the counter.

"That's four jobs done well," Ilsa says. "Six to go for the letter."

Then, quieter: "Grakk lost a hobgoblin today. He'll answer that, and not with a small raid."

You ask her where you could get a better spear.

"Tobin Ash's smithy, on the square," Ilsa says. "He'll do you right."

"But a spear fit for a silver tag, you'll buy in Aubrin. They don't make one in Millbrook."
