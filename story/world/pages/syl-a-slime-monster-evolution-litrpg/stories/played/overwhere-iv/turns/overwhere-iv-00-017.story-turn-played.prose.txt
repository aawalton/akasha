You lie awake a long while after Oswin's cot creaks and his steps go off across the boards. The dark behind the curtain is very dark. Then sleep takes you.

A hand on the curtain. Grey light.

"Up," says Holt. "Boots. Yard."

The warmth behind your ribs is full again, round and heavy. Your mouth is dry and your stomach hollow. Breakfast comes after drill, Holt says. Not before.

Laps are the wall walk: the whole ring of the town, up on top of the wall, with steep stone steps at every tower. From the top beside the gatehouse you look down on the common side.

A dozen slimes are heaped against the foot of the wall, right below the bunk room. Quiet. Waiting.

Dell sees them too, and whistles. "There's your lambs, then. Come courting in the night."

You run. You give it everything you have. The first lap is fine. On the second, your lungs start to burn.

On the third, at the far tower, the rags slip in your left boot and your foot slides off the edge of a step. You go down hard on the stone. Your shin and knee take it, and the pain is white and sudden.

You get up. The knee holds. You keep going, but slower, and the others pull away along the wall.

Dell laps you on the fourth, grinning back over his shoulder as he passes. Wat slows beside you, until Holt shouts him on.

You finish the last lap walking, limping down the tower steps, a long way behind everyone.

Holt doesn't say a word about it. She hands you a practice spear and points at the line.

Spear forms. Thrust, guard, sweep, recover. Holt shouts them, and you do them, fifty times over, until your arms shake. Then shield work, with wicker shields, blocking Holt's slow, heavy blows until your shoulder aches.

Holt steps back and looks the line over. "Sparring. Blunt spears, shields. Till one yields." She points her spear. "Wat, you're with Oswin." She points it at Dell, then at you. "Farrow. You're with her."

Dell swings his spear round and grins. "Go gentle on me, lamb-girl."
