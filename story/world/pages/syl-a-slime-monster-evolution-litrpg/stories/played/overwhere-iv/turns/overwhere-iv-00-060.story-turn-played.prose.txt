"I'll hunt the wolf today," you tell Ilsa, and ask her the way to the old quarry.

"Out the north gate, left at the shrine's stone cross, up through the north-west pastures."

"The quarry's two days on, up the old Crowstone track. You can't walk there and back in a day."

Two miles out, the town gives way to open sheep downs. A stone hut squats beside a fold.

A young shepherd leans on the fold wall. Fen Carrow, he tells you. He's lost two ewes in three nights.

"Dragged off whole," he says. "Up the track." He points at the mud by the gate.

Paw prints, as wide as a man's spread hand. Drag marks run from them up the grassed-over track.

At the pastures' far edge the cart track runs on north-west, over moor. You close your eyes.

Your always-on sense gives you the ruts underfoot: firm, grassed over. You walk, slowly.

Beside the track the moor is tussock and bog holes. Your sense feels them, but not what's soft.

The prints you saw in the mud are gone now, faint dents among a thousand others.

Every so often you stop and reach wide, out to forty paces. Sheep. Hares. Small things only.

The wind is in your face all the way, blowing down the track from the north-west.

Hours of it. Five miles up, the ground ahead drops away: a steep gully crossing the track.

Shapes thick and spiny choke its sides. Gorse. A beck runs in the bottom.

You reach wide. Up the gully, at its head, a hollow under a shelf of rock. Bones scattered.

And lying among them, thirty paces off, something huge. Four legs. Tall as a pony, curled asleep.

A ridge runs down its spine, hard and thin as a row of blades.

Its ribs rise and fall slowly. It hasn't stirred.

Eyes still shut, you reach for a line across its throat. There's no spot for your eye to fix on.

The line won't form. It doesn't open, and the warmth behind your ribs stays where it is.

From the track the gorse stands between you and the overhang. With your eyes open, you wouldn't see it.

Your sense holds the shape of the gully: above the overhang, a lip of loose stone, steep and high.

The beast sleeps on, thirty paces away.
