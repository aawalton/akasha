"Great! I'll read the handbook first."

You carry it to the hearth table and open it.

Three hundred pages of small print, the corners thumbed soft. You read the way you always have: fast.

Ranks: bronze, silver, gold, diamond. Everyone starts at bronze.

A strong newcomer who shows what she can do at a city hall may be made provisional silver. Provisional lifts after one major assignment. Until then, a provisional member can't take red-zone work.

A party that clears a dangerous dungeon may earn gold. Three labyrinths, diamond.

City halls issue crystal tags that hold coin and record kills, maps and quests.

Bounties are paid on proof: an ear, a pelt, a core. Loot recovered is the finder's.

Identify comes to most within days, it says, by closely studying living things to know them.

Classes run basic, intermediate, advanced. Spellblade is listed as intermediate, and little more.

Goblins are tier I. They grow into hobgoblins, then into tier III Greater Hobgoblins. A camp is ruled by its strongest, most often a hobgoblin, and it raids by plan.

Green slimes are to be killed on sight, by guild order.

Dungeons lie on islands, underground or under the sea. Their brick walls hinder teleporting.

You turn the last page. In three hundred pages, it doesn't say a word about space or dimension.

You look up. Ilsa is watching you from the counter, her pencil still, her eyebrows high.

"You can't have read all that," Ilsa says. "Not in half an hour. Folk don't read that fast."
