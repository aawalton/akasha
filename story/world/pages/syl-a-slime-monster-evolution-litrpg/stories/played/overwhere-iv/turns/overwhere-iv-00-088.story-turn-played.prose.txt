You kneel by each of the six in turn and cut the core from under the breastbone, the way Wren does.

Six small cores, sticky and dark. You wipe them on the grass and bag them with the ears.

Then you go up the trail, slow, toward the smell of smoke, your sense spreading wide every while.

The cleft ends. The trail climbs on through oak and yew, deeper than you have ever been.

A mile and more on, between one reach and the next, a dry stick cracks loud under your boot.

Twenty paces ahead, two goblins rise from behind a fallen trunk beside the trail. Watchers.

For a heartbeat they stare at you. Then the smaller one turns and bolts up the trail toward camp.

The other snatches a horn from its belt and lifts it to its lips, drawing breath.
