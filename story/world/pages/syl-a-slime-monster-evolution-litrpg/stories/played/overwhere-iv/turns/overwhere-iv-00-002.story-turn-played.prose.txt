From where you sit in the grass, you call across to him. "Hello! I seem to have gotten lost. Could you tell me where I am?"

The voice that comes out is higher and lighter than you expect. It lifts at the ends of the words with a faint lilt you did not put there.

The driver is a big man, forty or so, with the reins gone slack in his hands. Two brown horses stand at the shafts. The cart behind him is stacked with grain sacks, and while he stares, the horses drop their heads and crop at the verge.

His eyes go to your bare feet, then to the grey shirt slipping off your shoulder, then to the black tights. He goes red to the tips of his ears. He looks hard at the sky instead.

"Lost?" he says to the sky. "Lady, you've been robbed, is what. East road, was it? Brigands on that road all month, stripping folk to their smallclothes." He jerks his chin toward you without lowering his eyes. "Look at your feet."

"You're in Millbrook, love." He says it slowly, setting each word down on its own, the way you would give directions to someone a long way from home. "Southern, by your talk. Wendmere Vale, this is, barony of Tarrow. That's the town, up past the bridge."

"Garrett Pell. I carter for the vale." He is already twisting round on the seat to dig underneath it. He hauls out a patched brown wool coat, and you can smell the horse on it from where you sit. He holds it out at arm's length, still not quite looking at you.

"Take it, go on. I'm hauling this grain to my sister's mill by the gate. Ride in with me? Marta at the Brook & Barrel's kind to strays. She'll see you fed, and keep the gawkers off you."

Something cool bumps your ankle. The melon-sized slime is back, bobbing against your foot, the red stone turning slowly in its middle.

Up on the road, Garrett keeps the coat held out over the wheel, one eye on the town gate. The sun stands almost straight overhead.
