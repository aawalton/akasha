You watch the slinger, twenty paces off atop the oak. Not its face. Its arm.

The sling whirls, a blur over its head. Its eyes are on your chest.

Below it, Dace reaches the foot of the oak and swings up at its legs.

The goblin's arm snaps. You drop.

A hair late. The stone cracks across the top of your shoulder instead of your chest. It burns through the jerkin, and then you're down in wet leaf mould, the roots hard under your hip.

It isn't broken. It hurts, and your arm still answers.

Dace's swing bites bark where the slinger's feet were a heartbeat ago. It has skipped back along the trunk, out of his reach, and it's digging in its pouch.

Flat on the rim, you watch its fingers find a fresh stone and fit it to the sling.

The sling starts to turn again. Slow, then faster.
