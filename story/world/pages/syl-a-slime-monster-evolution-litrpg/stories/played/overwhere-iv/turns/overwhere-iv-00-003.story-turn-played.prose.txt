"Gladly, thank you."

You cross the footbridge to the road. The planks are warm from the sun under your bare soles. The slime with the red stone rolls after you through the grass as far as the water's edge, then stops there and bobs.

Garrett reaches down a big hand. You take it and climb, and you go up onto the seat far too easily, as if half of you stayed on the road. You have never been this light.

He drops the coat across your lap and busies himself with the reins, eyes front.

"I find I need to take my mind off of recent events," you say. "Would you talk to me about yourself and the area?"

He brightens at once. "Well, that I can do. Get up, Barley. Moss, you too."

The horses lean into their collars, and the cart rolls up the road toward the grey wall and the turning wheel.

"Twenty years hauling these roads. Grain to the mill, wool and jelly barrels east to Aubrin." He settles back. "Two girls at home, Pip and Wenna. My sister minds them while I'm out. Their mam passed. Fever, three winters back." He says it plainly, and clicks his tongue at the horses.

"The vale's quiet, mostly. Sheep, apples, market every seventh day. You'll like it." He tips his head. "Mostly. Goblins out of the Tangle been taking sheep off the edge farms this month. And the Red Hand on the east road. That'll be who had your boots, I'd wager."

His voice drops. "The baroness would've seen to them, once. Old Maud. Firm, but fair. She's been poorly since summer. Her nephew Cedric runs things now. Smooth as butter, cold as the brook. I keep clear."

Then he is off again, cheerful. "Need a salve, go to Old Selby. Witch, they say. Best back-rub in a jar I ever had. Brookside Four at the hall are good lads, but green. And stay clear of Crowstone Quarry."

The mill comes up beside the gate, a tall stone building with its great wheel creaking round in the race. You listen for the grinding and hear only the wheel and the water. Under a lean-to by the mill door, grain sacks are stacked high, and the ones at the bottom sag, dark with damp.

Garrett stops talking. He looks at the sacks a moment too long, then away.

"Hesper's shaft's cracked," he says. "Weeks now. Can't grind, can't pay for the mending till she grinds."

The cart rolls to a halt at the open gate, in the shadow of the wall. A guard in a leather jerkin steps out, spear grounded, and looks straight past Garrett at you.

"Day, Garrett. And who's this?" He turns to you. "Name and business in Millbrook, miss?"
