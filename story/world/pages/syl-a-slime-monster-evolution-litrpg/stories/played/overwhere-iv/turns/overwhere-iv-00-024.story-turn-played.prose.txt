"Okay. Maybe wolves, maybe goblins or something like it. Point me where to go, and I'll take a look."

Hobb points past the orchard, to the far field and its dry-stone wall where it runs along by the trees.

Pim slides down off the orchard wall to come with you. Hobb catches him by the collar. Bran stays too, growling, pressed hard against Pim's legs.

You walk down the far field alone. The grass is wet with evening. Past the stones, the Tangle is a black wall.

You stop by a fence post near the wall. You have an idea: fold the spear mid-thrust, and push it on past your reach.

First try. You thrust, and reach for the warmth. The click comes early. The point jerks wide, and the shaft wrenches in your grip. Held any looser, it would have torn clean out of your hands.

You grip harder. Thrust, and let the click drop just as your arms reach full length.

The spearpoint lands a foot past where your arms can reach. Splinters fly off the post.

It works.

The jar runs up both wrists, as if you had hit the post with a hammer instead. You shake your hands out.

Again. And again. By the fifth thrust, the click meets the thrust every time. Clean. A foot farther than any spear in your hands should go.

The warmth behind your ribs sits at about half now. Your wrists ache. You stop there.

At the wall, by a gap where the stones have fallen in, the mud is churned. You crouch to look. Tracks, small and bare-toed, with long narrow toes. They aren't paws. A clump of torn fleece is trodden into the mud beside them.

Out past the gap, in the black of the Tangle, something moves. Two small shapes, bent low.

They come out of the trees toward the gap, quick and quiet. Thirty paces off, and closing. They are child-sized, with grey-green skin, dressed in scraps of hide. One carries a flint-tipped spear. The other has a rusty knife, and is grinning at the sheep in the next field.

They haven't seen you.
