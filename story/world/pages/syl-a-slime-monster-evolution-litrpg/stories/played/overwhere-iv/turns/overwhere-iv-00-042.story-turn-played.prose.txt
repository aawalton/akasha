"Axe is no good," you say. "Skill's specialized for spears."

The herdsman shrugs and stumps off to bring his oxen round from the gate.

You step up to the trunk, twenty feet from the butt, and make a show of it.

You swing the spear down along the line you want. A black line follows it, and the trunk parts.

Then limb by limb, down the shaft. A sweep of the spear, a line, and each foot-thick limb drops away.

Five cuts. The warmth behind your ribs is nearly gone now, a last thin thread of it left.

The trunk lies bare and straight, the crown and upper limbs in a heap beyond it.

The herdsman comes back leading his oxen, looks the shaft over, and whistles low.

"Spear skill," he says again, shaking his head, and starts chaining it to the sledge.

You walk back to town, down the brook and through the square to the hall.

Inside, Ilsa is at the board. Slimes, wolves, Selby's herbs, guards for the east road. There isn't a raid notice among them.

"The oak's down," you say. "Trunk's cut and limbed. The herdsman's dragging it to the mill."

It's hardly mid-morning. Ilsa looks at you a long moment, and you can see her working it out.

She smiles, slow, and counts two silver onto the counter. "I'll take your word for it."

She pushes the coins across. "So. What would you like next?"
