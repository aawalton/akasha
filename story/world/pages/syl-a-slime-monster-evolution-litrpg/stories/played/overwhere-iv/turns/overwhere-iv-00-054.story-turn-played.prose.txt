You fix your eye on the goblin's neck and reach for a line. The ember gutters. The line doesn't open.

There isn't enough warmth left for a rend. The goblin shakes on, its knife slack in its fist.

Your own spear is blunt. The club goblin lies behind its alder, a rusty iron knife at its belt.

You take that knife, walk back, and finish the sheep-watcher. It doesn't fight.

Goblin LV 2 defeated. Experience gained.

The clearing is still. Seven raiders, and not one of them standing.

You go body to body with the notched knife and take an ear from each, the two downstream too.

On the hobgoblin: a cracked ox horn on a greasy cord, and a heavy, notched iron cleaver. His belt pouch holds six copper and a woman's plain brass ring.

The bows are crude, warped things with bone-tipped arrows. The rest carry clubs and rags.

You take the ears, the horn, the cleaver and the pouch, and untie the sheep's rope from the alder.

The five sheep are tied neck to neck on the one rope. Led by it, they follow, slowly.

Back down the deer trail, out of the Tangle. Two hours at a sheep's pace. The sun climbs past noon.

The trail comes out by the ford, a short walk above Tull's fold.

A lean, grim man in his fifties is mending the broken fence, a cudgel near his hand. Farmer Tull.

He sees the sheep and sets down his mallet. He counts them twice.

Then he grips your hand hard. "Five," he says. "I'd not hoped for one."

He has no coin to spare, he says, but he fetches a half wheel of hard cheese from the cottage and presses it on you.

From inside, a young man's voice calls out, hoarse. Through the open door you see him abed, his head bound. Tull's son, the one they clubbed in the raid.

Tull jerks his chin at the door. "He'll want to hear it. How they died."
