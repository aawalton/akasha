"Thanks!"

You tuck the bronze tag under your jerkin and clump back down the lane to the gatehouse. In the bunk room the practice spear lies on the cot where you left it. The shaft is smooth with other people's hands, and the blunted iron head is heavier than it looks.

Wat watches you go out through the gate with it. "Slimes? Mind your eyes, miss."

Out on the common, the slimes are everywhere. Thirty or so small ones bob in the grass, and here and there a bigger one sits, big as a melon. At the far end, down by the water, three children with jars are working. An old shepherd sits on a stile by the sheep.

You pick a fist-sized slime on dry ground, well away from the brook, and set your feet.

Something odd happens as you come near. It stops wobbling. Then it drifts toward you, slow and calm, like a cat coming to be stroked.

Your first thrust is clumsy. The spear goes in at a bad angle, but the blunt head punches through the jelly anyway and meets the core with a small, hard crack. The slime slumps into a puddle on the grass. The red core in the middle of it dulls to grey.

A window opens in front of your eyes.

Blue Slime defeated. Experience gained.

You fish the core out with two fingers. The jelly nettles your skin at once, a hot prickle, and you wipe your hand hard on the grass.

Then you go slowly. You work on your stance, then your grip, then how far to step in before you thrust. Every slime you come near does the same thing as the first: goes still, then drifts toward you and waits.

The second kill is cleaner. By the fourth you can judge how deep the core sits. Each one opens its little window as it falls. Your body learns the thrust faster than you expect it to, the step and the drive and the pull back all running together into one motion.

You take seven in all, every one of them small. You keep well clear of the children's end.

After the seventh, the window stays open, and a new line comes up under it.

Racial Experience threshold reached. Human is now LV 2.

Warmth runs down through your arms and legs. The spear feels lighter in your hands. Your breathing, a little ragged a moment ago, settles and goes deep and even.

Seven cores clink together in the jerkin's inside pocket. On the stile, the old shepherd is still watching you.

As you wipe your hands, a melon-sized slime rolls out of the long grass toward you. A red stone floats in its middle. It bumps against your boot.

"Oi!" A boy of about ten comes running up from the brook, his jar swinging. "Not that one! That's mine!"

He plants himself between you and the slime, fists on his hips, jelly to the elbows. "That's the fattest on the whole common. I saw it first. You spear it and I'll tell."
