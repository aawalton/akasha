"I'll be careful," you tell Ilsa, and go.

Two miles south-west to Tull's ford. From there the trail is plain. Churned mud, wool caught on thorns.

You follow it into the Tangle slowly, stepping on moss where you can, eyes ahead.

Three miles in, the trees thin toward a clearing of alders. You stop, and reach for your sense.

It spreads out to forty paces. Seven bodies: six small and squat, one far bigger. Five more, woolly. Sheep.

You creep forward to where gaps in the alders show the clearing.

A shallow stream. Two goblins with short bows sit on the far bank, watching the back trail.

Three squat by a fire on the near bank, gnawing a sheep. A big one dozes against a log in the middle of the clearing, a horn on a cord at his chest, an iron cleaver by his hand. The hobgoblin.

The seventh you can't see. Your sense finds it at the edge of the clearing, by the tied sheep.

You fix your eye on the nearer bowman's neck. A black line. He topples into the stream.

The others freeze. You're already looking at the second bowman. A line. He drops where he sat.

The hobgoblin is on his feet. His hand goes, not to the cleaver, but to the horn at his chest.
