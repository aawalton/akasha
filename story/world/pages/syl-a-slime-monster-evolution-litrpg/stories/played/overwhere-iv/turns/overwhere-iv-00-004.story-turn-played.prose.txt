"Nala," you say. "Suppose my business is not getting robbed again, for now." You spread your empty hands on the coat. "Not that I have anything left to be robbed of."

The guard is young and lanky, all wrists and neck under the leather. His mouth twitches. He fights it back into a straight line, and holds it there for about a breath.

He loses. The grin breaks through, and he ducks his head and reaches up to a shelf inside the gate arch for a fat ledger. He opens it on his knee and writes slowly, the tip of his tongue at the corner of his mouth, saying it aloud as the pen goes. "Nala. Robbed."

"Wat Barrow, miss. Gate watch." His eyes drop to your feet, and he winces. "Won't be asking you for toll, then."

Garrett leans over. "Found her on the common, Wat. Red Hand, sure as rain."

Wat's grin goes. "Then the captain'll want you. Anyone the Red Hand's had goes to him first. Orders. He's after where they hit you, how many, and which way they ran off." He taps the ledger with the pen. "After, you see Sister Anwen at the shrine. She gives out bread at dusk."

A door opens in the gatehouse wall. A broad older man in a worn leather coat steps out into the arch.

Wat straightens so fast his spear knocks against the stone. "Captain Hale, sir. Robbed traveller."

Hale looks at your bare feet. He looks at the coat in your lap. He looks at your face. He takes his time over each.

"Robbed," he says. "Where on the road, miss? How many of them? Which way did they go?"
