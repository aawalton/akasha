You feel the club coming in your close sense and throw yourself aside.

It whistles past your ear and thuds into the trail where your head was.

You turn and run, down the trail toward the ford, spear in hand.

A sling whirrs behind you. You weave; the stone hums past and cracks off an oak.

Your sense lays the trail out a stride ahead: every root, every rut. Your feet land true.

Your back burns and your side bleeds, and you run. The feet behind you fall back, and back.

The shrieks thin out behind you, until there is only your own breath and your own feet.

On and on through the dark, two miles, until the trees thin and the air opens.

The Tangle's edge. Across two hundred paces of meadow, a lantern glows in Tull's fold.

In the fold, a little flame blooms in a raised hand, and turns toward the trees where you stand.
