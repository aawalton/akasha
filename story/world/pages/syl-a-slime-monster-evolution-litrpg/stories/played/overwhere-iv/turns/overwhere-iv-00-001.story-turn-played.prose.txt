The last thing you remember is your back window. A book open on your knee, the evening going blue outside the glass, your eyes getting heavy.

Then sun on your face, long grass tickling your ear, and something cool and wet bumping gently against your ankle.

You open your eyes. Blue sky. A few white clouds. You turn your head, and a round blue blob the size of a melon is sitting against your foot, see-through, with a small red stone floating in its middle. It wobbles. It bumps your ankle again, curious, and rolls away through the grass.

You sit up, and your body is wrong.

Your arms are thin, the wrists narrow, the hands small and pale with freckles across the backs. Long dark red hair falls over your shoulder. Your old grey shirt hangs off you, the collar slipping to one side. Your black tights are still on. Your feet are bare, and small.

You look down at the rest of yourself once, and then you look at the blob instead.

You are on a wide green common beside a brook. Sheep graze a little way off. A wooden footbridge crosses the water, and beyond it a road runs up to a small town with a grey stone wall, a mill wheel turning slowly beside its gate.

The moment you wonder what is happening to you, a window opens in front of your eyes. Flat, clear, steady, like a pane of glass with writing on it.

Name: Nala

Race: Human LV 1

Class: -

Status: Healthy

You did not ask it for your name. It knew.

Then the window flickers, once, the way a screen does when something too big is trying to load, and for a moment there is a line under the others that is too bright to read. It settles. The line is gone. The feeling it left is not: a warmth behind your ribs, patient and huge, that feels like yours.

On the road, a cart has stopped. The driver is staring at you.
