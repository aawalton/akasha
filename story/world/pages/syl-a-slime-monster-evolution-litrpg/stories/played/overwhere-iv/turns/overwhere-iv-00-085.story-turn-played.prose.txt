"Sure," you tell Dace. "Don't mind some company after a night like that."

Wren kneels by the two hobgoblins with her skinning knife, for ears and cores. Dace takes the heads.

You sleep against the fold wall in the grey hours, deep, until the sky pales.

You wake with the warmth behind your ribs full again, and the ache gone from behind your eyes.

At dawn Tull comes out with a folded grey wool cloak. "My wife's," he says. "It's yours."

"There's a place at my table whenever you come." He grips your hand hard and goes back in.

Aldo is telling the sheep, the cob and anyone near that he saw you kill two hobgoblins.

The five of you walk the two miles into Millbrook at first light, the hobgoblin heads in a sack.

At the hall, Ilsa takes one look into the sack and sits down slowly.

You tell it together. Ilsa listens to the end, then counts out coin onto the counter.

"Five silver each for the hobgoblins. Three for your watch on Tull's, owed."

"And a gold from the hall's purse, for a strike broken and a farm saved." The coin gleams.

She pays the Four their three silver each, then pulls a fresh sheet toward her and inks her pen.

"A strike broken is a major job, same as a camp cleared. That's your silver letter, Nala."

"It goes east with the morning carter. Your tag comes back in about ten days."

She looks up. "Six more dead in the cleft? Their ears are a silver each, if anyone fetches them."
