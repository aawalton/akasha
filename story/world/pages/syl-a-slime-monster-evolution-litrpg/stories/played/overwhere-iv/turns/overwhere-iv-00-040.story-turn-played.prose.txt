You walk a slow circle round the oak, looking up through its crown.

It leans north, over the byre. To the east, open grass runs a hundred feet to the brook.

The herdsman and his oxen wait by the west gate, well clear. The ground to the east lies empty. East, then.

You take your place on the west side, fix your eye low on the bark, and reach for the warmth.

A thin black line, level, sinks a third of the way into the trunk. It doesn't make a sound.

Then round to the east face. A second line, angled up to meet the first, stopping short of it.

A strip of heartwood still holds between them. A hinge.

The oak holds for a breath. Then it creaks, deep, and begins to turn off its lean.

It swings east, slowly, then all at once, and comes down on the open grass with a roar.

The ground jumps. Leaves shower down. The crown lies on the grass, short of the brook. The byre stands.

The warmth behind your ribs is lighter, a quarter spent.

At the west gate the old herdsman is on his feet, gaping. From there he can only have seen it fall.

He hobbles over, shouting. "Never seen one come down so quick! What'd you cut it with, girl?"
