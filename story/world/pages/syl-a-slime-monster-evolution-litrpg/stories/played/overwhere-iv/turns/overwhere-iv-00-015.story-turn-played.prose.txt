"That's fine," you tell Ilsa. "What are they going to do? Rob me again?"

You turn to Holt. "For now, we might as well get to sleep. Training in the morning, right?"

"Dawn," says Holt. "Wall laps first. You'll hate them."

Ilsa's pen is already scratching as you all go out. She does not look up.

The square is dark now. Across it, in the porch of the little shrine, a figure stands back out of the lamplight. It stays perfectly still as you pass, with Holt and Wat and Dell talking round you. When you look again, the porch is empty.

Back at the gatehouse, Holt sends you off to bed and goes out to see the night guard on the gate.

Behind the curtain, you unlace the big boots, pull the rags out of the toes, and lie down on the cot. The blanket is rough and smells of cedar. The warmth behind your ribs has crept back up while you ate and talked. It isn't full, but there is more of it.

The bunk room goes quiet. A cough. The creak of a cot. Far off, the slow turn of the mill wheel.

Then a voice, low, just the other side of the curtain. The grey watchman.

"Oswin Pike," he says, barely above a breath. "Don't answer loud. Just listen."

"That light. Keep it to yourself from here on. Don't show it off, not for anyone. And stop Ilsa's letter if you can. Before it goes east."

A pause.

"I've seen slimes follow someone before. Once. Years back."

Through the gap at the curtain's edge, you can see his grey shape, still standing there.
