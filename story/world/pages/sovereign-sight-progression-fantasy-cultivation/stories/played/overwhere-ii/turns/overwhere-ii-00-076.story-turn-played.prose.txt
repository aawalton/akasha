You take the four bars. They're cold and heavy in your palm.

"Thank you," you say.

"Don't thank me. You earned every one of them, and Crake's five besides."

He sweeps the rest back into the box, locks it, and hangs the keys at his own belt.

"The watch pays the five bars once they've seen him. That'll be some days yet. We march at first light. The Pells carry Crake; the rest of us walk the prisoners home."

He digs in his pack and sets a waterskin and a heel of bread on the straw beside you.

"There's the well in the yard too, if you'd rather. Then sleep. You've run two dozen miles."

He settles against the barn door, spear across his knees, to take the next watch himself.

The lamp gutters low. Around you the Loddons breathe slow and even in the straw.

Under your skin, your penned Water presses at the shut Locks, a dull ache at your throat and palms.
