"Thank you for the offer," you say, "but I expect I'll need larger challenges than I can find here to grow. If you have any larger issues I can take care of before I go, I'm willing. After that I'll be on my way."

You heft the purse.

"I'd also like to take the chambers from the last five wolves, once they're carted back."

Dray nods slowly, as if he'd expected just that answer.

"Aye. A Talent your size was never going to sit in a watch cottage. When you go, I'll write you a Reeve's letter naming you. It'll serve as papers."

He waves a hand at the carcasses in the yard.

"Keep whatever you cut from them. I only want the carcasses burned. My two men and the pony go up for the den four at first light. They'll be back by mid-afternoon tomorrow. They'll not go past the cairn, mind. The one by the tarn, fetch yourself or leave."

He turns his mug in his hands and looks south, across the valley, toward the white peaks of the Whitecombs.

"Larger issues," he says. "There's two."

He takes a slow swallow from the mug.

"Greymaws don't come off the Whitecombs for nothing. Something drove them down. I fear it'll follow. I've no one fit to climb up there and look. I'd pay well."

He sets the mug down on the step.

"The other's a man. Oswy Crake, a Water thief on the Carrowmouth road. Ten silver bars on his head."
