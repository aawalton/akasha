"Who? I'm Nala, just a Talented passing through. Garth here asked if I could help his Wren—"

The old woman's eyes never leave your face. Her hand dives into her apron and comes out with an iron nail.

She is quick. Before you can step back, she presses the nail flat against the inside of your wrist.

It is cold and dead and numbing on your skin, like the hooks and the nails over Garth's door. That's all.

Goody stares at your wrist for a long moment. The skin is unmarked. She lets out a shaking breath.

"Cold iron," she mutters. "Doesn't burn you. Then you're no drowned thing come back."

"Goody," Garth says, low. "What's got into you?"

"Liss Aske." Goody is still staring at your face. "My dearest friend, when I was a girl. Red-haired, just like this. Drowned in Hollow Tarn, fifty-one winters gone. Twenty-five, she was."

"And here's her face at my door, not a day older." Her voice cracks on it.

Garth looks at you sidelong. "My mam told me of her. The Aske girl the tarn took."

Goody's nose wrinkles again. She leans in close and sniffs at you like a hound.

"And you smell of the Sea, girl. Clean and deep. I've never smelled that on a living soul."

Garth clears his throat. "Goody. She drew the rot out of Wren. This morning. Out of her leg. Pulled it out like a splinter, into salt on her hand. We burned it after. Wren's leg is clean."

Goody's sharp black eyes snap from Garth to your hands, and she goes stiller than before.

"Clean," she repeats. "Rot. Drawn out." Her mouth works. "I've buried four this winter."

Without taking her eyes off you, she grabs a shawl from the peg behind the door.

"Horne's girl, Tansy. It's past her elbow. She's three days left in her, if that. Whoever you are, Liss's face or no, will you come to the mill now?"
