"This will be much harder than Wren," you say. "The rot is intertwined with her life force. I'm amazed she has lasted this long. You have a strong daughter."

Tansy's mother presses her lips together hard and nods. Tears run down her face.

"I need a container to hold the rot, and a fire ready to burn it. I'm starting now."

Goody is already moving. She comes back with a wide earthenware basin and sets it by the bed.

"Kitchen range is lit below," Horne says roughly. "Hot as a forge. I'll stoke it."

You sit on the edge of the bed, lay your palm on Tansy's forearm above the bandage, and shut your eyes.

You find the tide and let the pull reach in, narrowed as fine as you can make it.

The tangle is everywhere. Sour threads, wound through and through the girl's own warm life.

You take the nearest thread and ease it free. Slowly. It is like the knots of computer cords you untied so many times back home, one loop at a time.

It comes. Then the next one comes. A few grains of grey salt prickle up under your palm.

Behind you, Goody draws a sharp breath. The air in the room turns cold, cold enough to raise gooseflesh, and it smells of the sea.

You work on, thread by thread, the pull straining in your grip like a dog on a lead.

Further in, the threads get finer. They cross and double back on themselves, and you can't tell where one ends and the next begins.

You tug at one that feels sour. It isn't only sour. Warm life comes with it, all in a rush.

The pull seizes on that warmth, hungry, and drinks before you can clamp it back.

Something warm and foreign pours up your arm and into your own well. The girl's own life.

You wrench the pull shut. Your palm comes away with only a pinch of grey salt on it.

Tansy gives a thin gasp. You open your eyes. Her face has gone from grey to ashen, and her lips are a pale blue. She is cold under your hand and shivering hard, and her eyes roll up and flutter shut.

Her mother cries out and clutches at her. Under your palm, through your tide, the black tangle is almost all still there.

A big hand closes on your collar. It hauls you up off the bed and back against the wall.

"What did you do to her?" Horne roars, his face an inch from yours. "What did you do?"
