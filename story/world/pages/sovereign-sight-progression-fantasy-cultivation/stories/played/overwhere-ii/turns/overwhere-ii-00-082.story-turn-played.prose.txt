"Gladly, Sir."

Sir Edric inclines his head. "First light, then, at the ford. I'll lodge at the Lantern. We'll ride to Callow Beck and leave my horse where the tracks begin."

You go back to your routine for the rest of the day: Lantern broth, the cottage, the tide.

You turn it on your thighs, the last and largest bones in your body.

They go smoothly, steadily, loop after loop, a deep warm ache and no worse.

By midnight both are dense and cool. Every bone in your body is refined.

You lie in the dark and feel it: a frame like oak, like iron, from skull to heel.

In the last hour of sleep, the black stair comes. The call from below is louder still.

At first light Sir Edric waits at the ford on a tall grey horse, his cloak beaded with mist.

You walk at his stirrup two hours south across the valley to Callow Beck. He tethers the grey.

Together you climb the goat tracks into the Whitecombs. He climbs steadily for his years, but slower than you.

He won't be hurried on rock; he tests each hold. You wait for him at each bend.

Near the top, the cold salt air comes down to meet you, out of nowhere, from the stone.

The split crag stands as you left it: rock torn open, a dark crack breathing sea-cold air.

And under your feet, under the whole mountain, the vast slow pull, deep as the sea itself.

Sir Edric pulls off his glove and lays his bare hand flat on the split stone, eyes half shut.

He stands a long moment, very still. His jaw tightens.

"Tell me of the pull," he says, without opening his eyes. "What you feel, here, now."
