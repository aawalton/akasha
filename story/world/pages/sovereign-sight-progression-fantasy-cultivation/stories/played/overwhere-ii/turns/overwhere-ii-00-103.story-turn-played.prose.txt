"Food and water, then up to the pool."

You drink from the rain barrel, dipper after dipper. The pounding in your head eases.

You eat Ebba's oatcakes and cheese by the fire. Hawise eats standing, watching the gully.

You climb the goat track beside the gully, the black trickle running down beside you.

Three hours up, just under the snow line, you top the rim of the Callow cwm.

Dead ravens lie about the rim, grey-scaled at the beak, as if they drank and fell.

The pool has doubled. It is sixty paces across now, black and steaming in the cold.

At its middle the water swells, slowly, then eases. Swells again. Like breathing.

Your well leans toward it hard, as hard as it once did at Hollow Tarn.

On the cwm's lower lip a fresh crack, a yard wide, splits the rock.

The black trickle slides out through it and down into the gully.

Loose scree lies heaped beside the crack.

Hawise stops well back from the water. "That's not a pool. That's a mouth."

Through your heels you feel the crag's deep tug swell with the water, and ease with it.
