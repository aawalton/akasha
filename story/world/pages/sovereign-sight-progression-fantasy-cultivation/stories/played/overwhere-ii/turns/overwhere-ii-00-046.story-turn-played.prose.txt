"It just fit," you say, and shrug.

You turn inward to your well. It's near brim-full, barely lowered by the first arm. Your mind still feels clear enough for another.

You slow your loops and send your Water down the thread to your right palm.

You push it into the skin and pull it in to soak. Push and pull, like surf.

The searing wave rises from your fingertips, and you ride it up your wrist.

Halfway up your forearm your attention slips. The push surges too hard.

Tiny beads of blood well at your pores all along the forearm, stinging.

You rein the tide back to steady, and the beading stops.

The wave climbs on past your elbow to your shoulder, and ebbs.

Your right arm matches your left now, pearly and tight and cool, speckled with dried blood.

You're thirstier than ever, and a hollow hunger has opened under your ribs. Your head feels worn thin, the way it did after Tansy.

Anselm has watched it all in silence. Now he holds out a wet cloth, and a clay cup of water from the shrine's jug.

"You bled," he says. "Were you pushing too hard?"
