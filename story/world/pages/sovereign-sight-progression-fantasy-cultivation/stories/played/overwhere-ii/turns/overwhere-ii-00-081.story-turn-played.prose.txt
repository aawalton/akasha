You come down the lane to the Reeve's door and give the knight a crisp guard's salute.

"Sir."

Sir Edric's grey brows lift a fraction, as if he has never seen a salute quite like it.

He answers with a short bow from the neck, the way a knight greets a sworn equal.

His eyes rest on your face a long moment, then drop, briefly, to the slow rise of your breath.

"Nala," he says, low and unhurried, the vowels clipped. "Sir Edric Hale, of House Varrow."

"I heard on the road in that Oswy Crake is dead, and by your hand. In Lady Varrow's name, I thank you. He's been a blight on that road a long year."

He glances up the valley, toward the white heads of the Whitecombs.

"The stone here hums. Faintly. Even the Reeve's doorpost leans toward those peaks."

"Dray writes that you found a crag split open up there, and something pulling under it. I'd have you lead me up to it tomorrow, and tell me of the pull, there, on the spot."
