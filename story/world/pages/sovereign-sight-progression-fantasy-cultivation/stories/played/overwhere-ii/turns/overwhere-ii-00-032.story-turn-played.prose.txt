"Sounds good," you say.

Anselm falls in beside you, and you walk on down the river lane toward the Ford together. Every few steps his eyes drop to the chambers in your arms. He keeps his questions to himself.

In Wendle Ford, folk at their doors stop what they're doing and stare at the grey knots of bone as you pass. A woman shaking out a rug holds it still in the air. Two boys by the footbridge nudge each other.

The Reeve's house stands beside the green, under the only slate roof in the village.

Anselm knocks. The door opens on the Reeve himself, stout and grey in a leather jerkin, chewing his breakfast.

"Reeve Oakes," Anselm says. "This is Nala, who drew the rot from the Horne girl."

The Reeve looks you up and down: a small red-haired woman in a borrowed cloak, with no pack. Then his eyes drop to the chambers in your arms, and he stops chewing.

"Is that the white-eye's?" he says. "Nothing else on the fells grows one that size."

He lifts the cracked chamber off the top of your bundle and turns it over in his thick hands.

"My watch lost a man to that beast. And you, a slip of a lass, put her down?"

He looks up from the chamber to you.

"Where are the carcasses, and who saw it done?"
