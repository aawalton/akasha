You shut your eyes and slow your loops, as Anselm showed you.

You press your Water out along the thread to your left palm, and on into the skin of your arm.

Then you turn Undertow on it. You push your Water out to the skin, and pull it in to soak.

Push and pull, in turn, like surf on a shore.

The nettles, the scald and the numbness come all at once, in one hard, searing wave.

You hold the tide steady through it. You don't let it surge.

Beside you, Anselm's breath comes short and ragged, like a man's at the edge of the sea.

The wave rolls slowly up from your fingertips: your wrist, your forearm, past your elbow. It climbs to your shoulder and stops there, with nowhere left to go.

The searing ebbs. You open your eyes. The light through the door has moved; a quarter hour has passed.

Your whole left arm, fingertip to shoulder, has a faint pearly sheen. It feels tight and cool.

The pink scar of the greymaw bite is still there, sealed under the new skin.

Beside it, the skin of your right arm feels thin and raw, as if asking to be matched.

Anselm stares at your arm, then at your face. His own bare forearm hangs forgotten at his side.

"I felt it," he says. "A tide turning over and over, right beside me. That was the whole arm, in a quarter hour. My skin took me three years."

He shakes his head slowly.

"I know no Keeper teaching that uses a Talent to drive its own refining. How did you think of that?"
