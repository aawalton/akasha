"Do you have an iron pot or bowl of some sort?" you ask Garth. "I think I can help after all. If not, any container will do."

Garth stares at you for a long moment. Then he gets up, takes the pot off the fire, and scrapes the last of the porridge out of it.

He sets it on the floor by the box bed. Plain black iron, still warm.

You reach for it with your sense. The tide slides over it easily. It is nothing like the numb nails over the door.

"Wren, I'm going to put my hand on your leg. It shouldn't hurt."

Wren nods hard, her eyes huge. Garth turns back the blanket.

Her calf is swollen around a black bite. Dark veins creep out of it, up toward her knee.

You lay your palm over the bite. The skin is hot. The sour knot of salt is right there under your hand.

You shut your eyes, find the tide, and let the pull reach in. Slowly.

It wants everything it touches. You narrow it, hard, down to the sour and the wrong.

Something warm and clean is tangled all through the rot. Her own life, flowing. You ease the pull around it, strand by strand, and take only the salt.

Deeper down, under the rot, you brush against a small, still pool. Asleep. Like your own well, in little.

The pull leans toward it. You hold it back, and the pool stays where it is, untouched.

Garth sucks in a breath.

"It's cold," Wren whispers, and shivers. "It's all prickly."

Under your palm, the black veins ebb. Thread by thread, they draw back toward the bite.

It takes a few long minutes. You keep the pull narrow the whole way, and you never let it slip.

Then the last of the knot comes free, and your palm is suddenly gritty and heavy.

You open your eyes. Across the bed, the hair on Garth's forearms stands up in gooseflesh.

You lift your hand. A mound of damp grey salt sits in your palm. It reeks of rotten fish and brine.

The bite underneath is an ordinary red wound now, clean and puckered. The dark veins are gone.

You tip the salt into the iron pot. It lies there, grey and faintly glistening.

Down in your own well you feel a small, cool ebb where the work drew from you.

Wren wriggles her toes and gasps. "Da. Da, it doesn't hurt. It really doesn't."

Garth kneels by the bed and presses his big hand to her forehead. She is still warm. He looks at the clean wound, then at the grey salt in his pot, and then at you.

His voice comes out rough. "What in the Ancestors' name are you?"
