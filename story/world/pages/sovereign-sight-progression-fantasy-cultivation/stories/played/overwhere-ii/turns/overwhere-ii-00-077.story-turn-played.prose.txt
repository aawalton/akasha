You lie down in the straw, the bars in your coat, and let sleep take you.

You sleep black and deep. Your sleep stays empty of dreams; even the stair stays away.

Near dawn, something stirs you: a loosening in the soles of your feet.

Penned Water rushes through them like a held breath let go, and the ache there eases.

Then your palms open, one and then the other. The same rush. The same relief.

Last of all, your throat. The dull ache you have carried since dusk drains away.

You sink back under. In that last hour of sleep the dream returns.

The black stair, going down. The call from below, louder than it has ever been.

You wake to grey light in the barn doorway. The Loddons are stirring, and the Pells are lifting the hurdle.

Your Water is yours again. You can feel it, deep and full. But it moves stiff and slow.

When you reach for the damp in the straw, Undertow answers weakly, like a limb gone to sleep.

The needle dart still hangs in the side of your neck, a small cold sting when you turn your head.

Your mouth is parched, your tongue thick, your stomach a hollow knot.

Outside, Dray is already on his feet, with Harl roped between Col and Rob.

"Morning," he says. "We march in a quarter hour. Half a day to the Ford, at the prisoners' pace."
