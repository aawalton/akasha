You drive your loops fast. The flow runs hot in your arms and legs, and you edge up the scree, setting each foot down on solid stone.

A pebble shifts under your boot. You freeze. In the cleft, the five sour knots lie still.

You reach the mouth of the cleft unheard, spear low. The reek of rotten salt hangs as thick as fog.

In the gloom inside, five grey shapes lie heaped among the sheep bones.

You step into the mouth and lunge at the nearest.

Pull and push go out together in one surge. The greymaw slides toward you and the spear drives ahead of your arm, and the point punches through scale.

It strikes the bone chamber. A cold gush of brine bursts out over your hands, and the greymaw sags dead.

The pack wakes snarling. For the first few heartbeats they are slow and clumsy, legs tangling, heads swinging.

The cleft is narrow. Two can come at you at once, and no more.

The first lunges. You push, and it slams back into the rock wall.

The second you pull straight onto your thrusting spear. It dies with a gurgle of brine.

The one you threw comes again. You push it off its feet, then pull it back onto the point.

A third gush of brine. Three lie dead in the mouth of the cleft.

The fourth scrambles over its dead packmates. You step aside and spear it through the chest.

It thrashes, jaws snapping a hand's breadth from your face. Then it goes still.

At the back of the cleft the limping greymaw hangs back and does not come.

As the fourth dies, it scrabbles up the back of the cleft and out over the rim.

It lurches away up the scree on three legs, toward the corrie lip and the tarn beyond.
