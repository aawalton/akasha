"I'm sorry about your sheep, Ebba. We'll take care of them, then get you down to the town until this is sorted."

You charge up the pasture, the cold-iron spear low.

The ewes come down off the slick together, heads low, like a flock.

You push the tide down your arms. The ash shaft is too dry to throw; it barely stirs.

But your arms move fast. The thrust snaps out and back faster than a strike should.

The first ewe takes the point in the neck and drops. The rest scatter.

Hawise blurs past you, steel flashing, and a second ewe folds at the knees.

The four left circle back onto the slick, then charge again from it.

You meet them. Thrust, back. Thrust, back. Two more fall at your feet.

Hawise takes the fifth. You catch the last mid-leap, and it crashes onto the crust.

Six grey-scaled carcasses lie on the pasture. You and Hawise both stand untouched.

Hawise wipes her blades on a tuft of grass. "Quick. Good."

The longhouse door unbars. Ebba steps out, two boys behind her, one with his arm bound.

"Burn them on the midden with peat, the way I did the goats. I'll pack."

At the slick's lower edge, black water trickles slowly through the crust.

A thin black thread is creeping down the grass toward the longhouse.

Hawise follows your eyes. "The Lady said hold it. That's still moving."
