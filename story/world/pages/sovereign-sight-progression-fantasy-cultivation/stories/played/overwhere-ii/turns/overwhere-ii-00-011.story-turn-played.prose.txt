"Thank you. I'll gladly accept." You take the boots and the cloak from him carefully, with both hands.

Garth nods once and looks away, at the fire.

You sit on the stool and pull the boots on. They are a little large, but with the laces pulled tight they fit. The cloak is heavy, soft brown wool, and it smells faintly of cedar from the chest.

"How far to the Ford?" you ask. "Ten days is a long time. I'd hate to be too late for her."

Garth frowns. "Ten days since she was bit, lass. Not ten days she's got. There's no knowing how long she's got."

"The Ford's three mile down the lane and along the river. Hour and a half, at my pace."

"Could you take me there now?" you ask. "I suspect I'll need a guide before they let me see her. I hate to ask you to leave your girl. Or you could just introduce me to Goody Brannoc."

Garth looks at Wren, and his jaw sets. She is still flushed, but her eyes are bright and clear.

"Go, Da," Wren says at once. "I'm better. Burr can mind me. Go on."

Garth breathes out slowly. "Goody brought Wren into the world. She'll hear me out, at least. And Horne will let in anyone Goody says." He nods. "Aye. I'll take you."

He reaches for his crook and his sheepskin coat by the door.

You stand. As you do, your tide brushes the iron pot by the bed, and you stop.

The grey salt in it is not dead. It feels sour and slow, faintly crawling, like a thing that is seeking.

Its faint reach leans toward the nearest warm life. Toward Wren, lying an arm's length away.
