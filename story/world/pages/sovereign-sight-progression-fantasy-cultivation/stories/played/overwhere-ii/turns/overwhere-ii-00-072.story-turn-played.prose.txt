"Can we catch him?" you ask. "I'm sure I can make it at a run, but I'd need a guide who could keep up with me."

"You don't need a guide," Dray says. "The road runs right past the tollhouse door. Twelve miles, downhill. But you'll have company. At First Depth I can run all night like a horse at a trot."

He turns to his men. "Col, you and the Pells hold the prisoners. Bind Rob's arm properly. We'll be back by dawn."

"He's got half an hour on you," Wil says from the straw. "He jogs and walks. He won't think anyone's coming."

You and Dray leave the barn and the lamplit yard, and run.

Down the black lane. Out onto the valley road. Away downhill.

Your Locks are shut. Behind them your penned Water presses, cold and restless, with nowhere to go. You run on refined muscle alone.

You run faster than any man could, long and easy, and Dray's stone-heavy stride keeps pace beside you.

An hour after dark the half moon rises, and the road shows pale between the hedges.

You run on. Your breath comes steady, and the miles fall away behind you.

An hour out, your refined ears catch it ahead: quick, light feet on the road. Then a stumble.

Then a scramble of thorn, and a sharp intake of breath, off to the right.

The footsteps have stopped. Someone small has gone to ground in the hedge bottom.

You slow, and Dray slows with you, both of you breathing hard in the moonlight.

In the black of the hedge: the pale glint of a too-big coat, and a boy holding his breath.
