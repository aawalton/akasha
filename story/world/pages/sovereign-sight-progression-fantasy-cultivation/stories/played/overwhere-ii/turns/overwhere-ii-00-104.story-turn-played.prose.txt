"One step at a time. Help me build a dam across the channel. Keep the run-off back."

"Hold it, then," Hawise says. "I'll pack."

You set your tide against the black trickle at the crack and lean. It stills.

Hawise hauls scree from the heap, stone after stone, and rams it into the crack.

The black water presses at your tide, cold and heavy, but it's steady work.

An hour on, the crack is packed tight from its foot to the lip.

You let go. The plug holds. Below, the trickle in the gully thins and stops.

Out on the pool, the swells quicken. Three, four, fast as panting.

Your well lurches toward the water with each one.

Then the swells slow again, back to their long, deep breathing.

Hawise wipes her hands and looks out at the steaming black. "Holds. For now. That's the run-off stopped. The Ford's two hours below the farm. The Keep's five. Where to, free blade?"
