"I'm not certain," you say. "I would gladly do so if I can. But I'd need to visit one of the stones first. Do you know how far it is to the nearest?"

Garth's brow creases. "A Charterstone? There's none in Wendlemere. Nearest's at Carrowmouth. Down on the coast, in the harbour square. Three mile to Wendle Ford, then the valley road. Two days' walk from the Ford. One, if you can get a seat on a hound cart."

He shakes his head. "But it's dark now, lass. It gave its marks on Threllsnacht eve, a fortnight gone. Won't give another till next Threllsnacht. Two Carrowmouth lads took theirs this year."

He turns his cup in his big hands.

"I don't see what a stone's to do with rot, any road. That's where Aspirants go for their marks. Not for mending." A pause. "I sent to Varrow Keep for help a week back. No answer yet."

His jaw works. He looks at the blanket over Wren's bad leg, and doesn't say any more.

In the box bed, Wren pushes herself up on one elbow. Her eyes are bright with fever, and they are fixed on you.

"Are you an Aspirant? Truly? Did you come from the Spires? Is that why you've got no boots?"

"Wren," Garth says. "Lie down."

Wren does not lie down. "Can you do magic? Can you show me? Just a little one?"
