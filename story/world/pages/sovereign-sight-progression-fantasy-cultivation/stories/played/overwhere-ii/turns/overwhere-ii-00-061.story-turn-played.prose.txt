You sleep again till dawn. At the Lantern you eat breakfast for a copper.

At the smithy you step to Hob's right side, so he can hear you over the forge.

He takes the spear down from its pegs: a seven-foot ash shaft, and a dark, leaf-shaped head. A crossbar sits just below the head. When he lays it across your hands, the weight sits forward, heavier at the business end than the watch spear ever was.

"Balanced for bracing against a charge," Hob says. "Cold iron bites the Sea-twisted where plain iron cuts. Keep it oiled. That's the other half bar, and not a copper more."

You pay him.

In your inward sense the spear's head sits at the end of the shaft as a dead, numb patch. Undertow won't touch the iron. But the tide is still there in your arms, ready to drive them behind it.

You cross the green to the shrine. Inside, cool and dim, Anselm is sweeping by the altar vessel.

You tell him your skin is refined whole, and every muscle too, in four days.

Anselm sets the broom down very slowly.

"Four days," he says. "My skin alone took me three years. Refining goes outside in: skin, then muscle, then bone, then organs, and last blood. Most Talented Descend once skin and muscle are whole, and refine bone after. But each part refined before a Descent makes its Tribulation easier to bear. I don't know any Keeper practice for bone. I've only heard it aches deep, and goes slow. The canticles say Descent first calls in a dream of water going down, once you are ready."

He looks at you closely, his tired eyes sharp.

"Has it called you yet?"
