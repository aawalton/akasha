"Yes, if you'll take me."

Goody is already out the door, her shawl round her shoulders, moving fast for someone so bent. You and Garth follow.

The mill is a stone's throw along the race. It is a big stone building with a house joined on, hung with slate, the finest thing you've seen in this valley. The great wheel turns in the race and groans as it goes.

Goody hammers on the house door. It opens on a heavy, grey, hard-faced man who fills the doorway.

"Sedge," Goody says. "This is Nala. She's to see Tansy."

His eyes go over you. The loose shirt under a dead woman's cloak. The boots too big for your feet.

"Talented," he says flatly. "A stranger. I've had my fill of promises."

"She drew the rot out of Garth's Wren this morning," Goody snaps. "I'll vouch for her. Move."

Horne looks past you at Garth. Garth nods once. Horne's jaw works, and then he steps aside.

Up a stair is a warm room. A girl of fourteen or so lies propped on pillows, grey-faced and sweating. A worn woman sits by the bed holding her good hand, and she doesn't look up when you come in.

Tansy's right arm lies on the blanket, bandaged. Black veins crawl out above the wrappings, past her elbow, up toward her shoulder. The room reeks of a bitter poultice.

You reach out with your tide, and your breath catches.

This is nothing like Wren's small knot. It is a sour tangle, soaked all through the arm, threaded deep into the girl's own life, strand around strand, far deeper than Wren's ever went.

And there is so much of it. It would heap up far past what one palm could hold.

Behind you, Horne's voice is low and hard. "If you hurt her, Talented, I'll see you hang for it."

The woman by the bed looks up at last. Her eyes are red and raw.

"Please," she whispers. "Please."
