You follow Garth's lantern into the cottage, and he bars the door behind you.

Burr sniffs your bloody sleeve and whines. On the settle by the fire, Wren sits up, wide-eyed.

You sink onto the hearth stool and half shut your eyes. You drive the loops fast through all five threads.

Then you gather the flow in your left palm and turn it inward, and push it into the torn flesh.

It comes unsteadily. The restless brine in your well rides in with your own clear Water.

The wound stings as if it's packed with sea salt. You hiss through your teeth.

Under the sting, your own Water goes to work. The torn edges draw together, knitting from the ends inward.

Then the sting of the brine swamps it, and the closing stops halfway.

The ends of the crescent are sealed in shiny pink skin. The middle is still open, raw and weeping.

The deep throb is gone. What's left aches like a scraped knee.

Garth sets the lantern down and stares at the half-healed bite.

"It closed!" Wren says. "Half of it did. Can you do the rest?"

Garth fills a basin from the kettle and sets a clean rag beside it.

"Let me wash that before you try anything more," he says. "There's wolf spit and salt in it yet."
