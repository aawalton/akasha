"Great," you say. "I'll scout the Whitecombs tomorrow. Then we can talk about Oswy Crake after, if I'm successful solving the first. Could I borrow a bunk until I leave, though? I'd like to stay close while I'm working on these."

"Sleep in the watch cottage while you work for me," Dray says. "It's empty. I'll ask nothing for it."

He picks up his mug again.

"For the Whitecombs, go first to Ebba Callow at Callow Beck. She knows the tracks. Bring me sure word of what drove the greymaws down, and it's a silver bar."

He walks you across the green to the ford, to a squat stone house beside it.

Inside is one room with a sleeping loft, a rope bed, a table and a cold hearth. The dead watchman's few pots hang by the chimney, grey with dust since midwinter.

Dray nods to you from the doorway and leaves you to it.

Your throat is dry as dust. Out past the door the river runs loud, a stone's throw away.

A knock sounds on the open door. Anselm stands there, the hem of his orange robes still muddy.

"They told me at the green you'd be here," he says. "You said the shrine, once the Reeve was done?"
