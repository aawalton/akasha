"Give me the spear," you say. "You take Burr and Wren inside. I'll take care of the greymaws."

Garth stares at you. Small, red-haired, in his dead wife's cloak. Then he puts the ash spear in your hands.

He scoops Wren up, drags Burr in by the scruff, and bars the door behind them.

The spear is long, and heavy for your size. You drive the flow hot, and it feels like a broom handle.

You stand in the lane before the fold. Behind you the ewes crowd together, bleating.

The sun slides down behind the western fells. The light goes grey-gold, then grey.

They come down the fell fast and silent. Four shapes against the heather, big as ponies.

Three run ahead. The fourth hangs back on the slope above. It is twice their size, and it waits.

The smell reaches you first. Rotten brine, like a beach full of dead fish.

Then you see them clearly. Grey scale in shaggy fur. Webbed claws. A second row of teeth.

Your tide reaches out on its own, and you feel them. Sour with rot. And deep inside each one, something else: behind the breastbone, a hard chamber of bone, brimming with Water. Dense, cold, and strong.

The pull leans toward those chambers the way it leaned toward Wren's rot. Hungry.

The first greymaw leaps for your throat.

You shove with the tide. It flies back, tumbling.

It rolls, shakes itself, and gets up unhurt, snarling.

The second comes in low. You drive the spear into its shoulder, and it shrugs the point off.

So you stop holding the pull back.

You reach into the nearest greymaw's chest, grip the bone chamber, and pull hard.

Water floods out of it and up your arm into your well, brackish and cold and strange.

The greymaw staggers mid-stride. Its legs fold. Its eyes glaze. In ten heartbeats it is down, and still.

You turn the pull on the second. It yelps once, stumbles, and drops into the mud.

The third wheels to run. Your reach catches it at the fold wall, and it slides down dead.

Thick grey salt crusts your palm, reeking of rot. You close your fist on it.

Your well is brimming, sloshing with foreign Water, restless under your breastbone.

Three pony-sized greymaws lie in the mud. In the fold, the ewes have gone silent.

Up on the slope, the big one rises to its feet.

A she-wolf, twice the size of the others, grey-scaled. One eye is milk-white. The other is fixed on you.

She lifts her muzzle and scents the air, as if she smells the sea. Then she starts down toward you.
