"What can you tell me about Crake?" you ask. "What are his crimes? How does he pick his victims? What's known about his abilities? Does he work alone?"

As you ask, you reach for Undertow. The tide rises from your refined legs, over your hips, into the thin skin of your belly.

"I know him from the watch notices in Carrowmouth," Dray says. "Oswy Crake is a Mendicant. He steals Talents' Water. He drinks it at a touch, and sells it bottled in Carrowmouth."

The tide reaches the raw scrape over your ribs. It can't refine the skin there until it has healed it, and the healing stings, and then the refining stings again on top of it. The work slows to a crawl. You keep your face still and listen.

"Three Talented have gone missing on the Carrowmouth road this past year," Dray goes on. "He picks the ones who travel alone and wouldn't be missed. And he doesn't work alone. He rides with four men. They carry saltsteel manacles, and darts that choke a Talent's Locks."

The tide climbs over your chest to your collarbones. You open your mouth to ask where he camps.

"Where does he—"

At your throat, where a thread ends, a cold jolt shoots through you.

The tide lurches out of your grip. Water spills, and your well drops near dry.

Across your trunk the refining slips away undone, like a wave running back down the sand.

Your skin prickles hot and raw from hip to jaw, and your ribs throb.

You sit very still, breathing, while the Sea begins to fill your well again. Your mind feels scraped thin, the way it did after two limbs. You can't refine any more tonight.

"You've gone white," Dray says. "Are you all right?"

He eyes you a long moment. Then he answers the question you didn't finish.

"Where he camps, I don't know. Only that the drover was robbed near Grey Shaw. A Talent alone on that road is what he hunts. Just so you know what you'd be walking into."
