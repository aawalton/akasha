"Hmm. I don't think the iron is stopping it. Let's try burning. If that doesn't do it, I'll pull it back into the pot, and we'll bring it with us."

Garth grunts. He drags a basket of dry gorse and split ash out from beside the hearth, heaps it on the peat, and works the bellows until the fire roars up yellow and hot.

You lift the lid and tip the grey salt into the heart of the flames.

It spits and crackles like wet fat. A stink of rotten brine rolls through the room. Wren pulls the blanket up over her nose, and Burr sneezes and backs away under the table.

You keep your tide on it. The sour, crawling reach shrinks. It flickers. Then it goes out.

When the flames die back, all that's left on the peat is a smear of clean white ash.

You feel the pot, the hearth, the whole room. There's nothing sour anywhere now but the smell.

Garth props the door open to let the stink out, kisses the top of Wren's head, and takes up his crook.

You set out down the lane together. Behind you, Burr whines at the door by Wren's bed.

The valley opens up as you walk. Bare brown fields, sheep on the slopes, smoke rising from farms. Garth limps along steadily and says little. You slow your stride to match his, warm inside the cloak.

Across the river, the white mountains stand up into the cloud.

"Whitecombs," Garth says, with a nod at them.

After an hour and more, the lane meets the river and runs along beside it to a village. Stone houses. A ford, with a stone footbridge beside it. At the east end, a mill wheel turning.

Garth leads you past the mill to a small, crooked cottage by the mill race, with bunches of herbs hung drying under the eaves.

He knocks. "Goody. It's Garth Marsh. I've brought someone."

The door opens on a tiny, bent old woman with sharp black eyes, wiping her hands on a rag.

Her nose wrinkles, as if at a smell. Then her eyes go to your face, and she goes very still.

The rag falls from her hands. The colour drains out of her lined face.

"Liss?" she whispers. "Liss Aske?"
