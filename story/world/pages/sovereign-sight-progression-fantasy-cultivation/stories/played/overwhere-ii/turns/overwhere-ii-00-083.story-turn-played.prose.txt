You stand still beside him and let yourself feel it, and tell him plainly.

"A slow, vast pull, far below. Like a tide under the mountain, drawing on my well. It's stronger than when I first stood here, on day four. And it comes in slow swells, rising and easing."

You feel the rest as you say it. "And now, with my bones refined, I feel it in my frame too. A deep tug, down through my heels."

Sir Edric listens without a word, his hand still flat on the stone, his eyes half shut.

When you're done he takes his hand away, slowly, and flexes the fingers as if they're cold.

"Then we feel the same thing, from two sides," he says. "The mountain's root is bowed upward under us. Like a dam with a flood behind it. This crack runs down past where I can follow. Deeper than any fault I've touched. And there are new cracks, hair-fine, running east and west along the range from here."

He pulls his glove back on. His face is calm, but he takes a long moment before speaking again.

"I ride for Varrow Keep at dawn, to tell Lady Varrow this myself. In her name, I'd ask you to come to the Keep, and tell her what you told me."
