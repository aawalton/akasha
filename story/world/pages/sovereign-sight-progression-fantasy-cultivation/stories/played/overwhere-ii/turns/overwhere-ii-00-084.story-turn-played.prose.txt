Something in Sir Edric's face eases. "Good. We leave the Ford at dawn."

You climb down off the Whitecombs together, and the pull fades behind your heels with every step.

That night at the Lantern you eat broth and bread, and buy a road bundle for the morning.

In the last hour of sleep, the black stair comes. The call is louder again.

At dawn Sir Edric waits at the ford on his grey. You take the road east, up the valley.

He rides at a steady walk. You keep pace beside his stirrup easily, past scattered farms.

Somewhere past noon he speaks. "A word about Lady Varrow, before you meet her. She's cold of manner. Don't take it to heart. She never forgets a debt."

The valley narrows. Toward evening an old grey tower-house rises on a crag at its east end.

Varrow Keep. Its roofs are patched, its walls stained dark with old rain.

You pass under the gate as the light goes, and behind you the bar thuds down for the night.

A steward takes you in hand: a hot supper in the hall's corner, a small cold room, a straw bed.

"The Lady receives guests in the morning," he says. "Not after dark."

In the last hour of sleep, the black stair comes again. Louder.

In the morning, after bread and small beer, the steward comes for you.

He leads you up a winding stair to a long, draughty hall, a fire smoking at one end.

Sir Edric stands beside a high-backed chair. In it sits a woman in grey, very upright, watching you.
