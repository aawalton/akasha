"Come with me," you say.

You take the woman's arm and walk her out of the Lantern and across the green to the Reeve's house.

She goes white. Halfway across she tries to twist free, but your grip holds like an iron band.

Tam Oakes lets you in. Dray looks up from his table.

"I'm more certain than not that this woman is trying to lead me to the bandits," you tell him. "Whether by intent or coercion, I don't know. Could we turn this to our advantage?"

You watch her as you say it. Under your fingers her pulse leaps, and her breath stops.

Dray looks her up and down, slow. Whatever he sees settles it. He believes you.

"Crake sent you," he says.

At the name she breaks. She sags against your grip and weeps.

"Don't kill my brothers. Please. They're fools, but they're all I have."

Dray presses her, quiet and steady, and it all comes out. Her name is Bet Loddon. She has no son. Crake paid her two silver, and she is afraid of him.

"They're at Ashlin Farm," she says. "It's been empty since autumn. All of them: Crake and four men. My brothers are in the barn's hayloft, with crossbows on the yard. Big Harl's inside the farmhouse with Crake, by the hearth. And Pip watches the road. I'm to bring you up the front lane alone, before dusk. A lamp in the window means they're ready. If I'm not back by nightfall, he'll know it's gone wrong, and move on."

Dray sits back. For the first time since you met him, he looks almost eager.

"Ten bars on his head, and three Talented gone missing on that road. I want him. I know Ashlin. There's a beck gully behind the barn; you can reach its back wall unseen. So we turn his trap. You walk up the lane as bait. My watch and I come up the beck behind. Stone has no skin, so his touch can't drink through my hands. But we have to move by noon, to be there before dusk. Will you walk in as the bait?"
