You turn your back on the tarn and climb back over the corrie lip.

With each step down the scree, the pull on your well fades. Once the tarn drops out of sight behind the lip, it's gone, and your well settles quiet under your breastbone.

In the cleft the four dead greymaws lie where they fell, stiffening in the cold.

You saw off each right ear with the spear's edge. Five ears weigh down your knapsack on top of the chambers.

Then the long walk down: past the half-eaten ewe, past Marsh Croft, down the river lane beside the loud water.

By mid-afternoon your mouth is dry and your throat rough with thirst.

In the yard of the Reeve's house by the green, four greymaw carcasses lie in a row, flies already at them.

Dray is sitting on his step with a mug in his hand. He stands when he sees you.

You swing the knapsack down and tip the five ears out onto the step beside him.

Dray stares at them, then at you. He counts them twice with one thick finger.

"Five," he says. "The whole pack, in two days, and not a scratch on my watch."

He goes inside. He comes back with a heavy leather purse and a small cloth bag.

"Two silver a head for nine heads. Eighteen pieces, counted." He sets the purse in your hand. "The watch's silver bar for the white-eye, as sworn. And a bar for the hunt, which you'll put to Hob's spear."

He presses the cloth bag on top of the purse. The silver is heavy in your hands.

"I'll send two men and a pony up for the den carcasses, and burn them."

He sits back down on the step and looks at you for a long moment.

"The valley needs someone like you, Nala. The watch cottage by the ford stands empty. It's yours, if you'll stay."
