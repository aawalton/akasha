You walk to the slick's lower edge and plant the spear butt in the grass.

You reach into the black water with your tide and push.

It's heavier than plain water. Cold, and leaning downhill like a tide that wants to come in.

Your well leans toward it too, the way it did at the Callow pool.

You lean back. Slowly the black thread draws back up the grass, into the slick.

The slick's lower edge lifts and begins to roll uphill, its crust cracking as it goes.

It fights you every pace. Twice it surges back and you have to take the ground again.

It's plain work, like towing, but slow. The sun slides west across the Whitecombs.

Close to two hours on, the whole slick lies heaped black in the narrow gully mouth.

The pasture below lies bare, grey with salt, steaming faintly.

You ease off, breathing deep through your refined lungs.

At once the black water at the gully mouth leans down again. A finger's width. Then two.

Ebba stands at the longhouse door with a bundle on her back, watching.

"That gully's narrow. A bank of peat and stones would hold it, I'd think. But the sun's low, and I'll not have the boys on the mountain after dark."

"Bank it now, or get them down first?" Hawise says. "Your call."
