You eat porridge with Garth and Wren at the table, hot and thick. Your mouth is still dry. You drink two cups of water straight off, one after the other.

Wren keeps looking at the grey chambers piled at the end of the table. She looks away, and then her eyes go straight back to them.

"I'll take these to the Reeve," you say, "and tell him what happened."

"Do," Garth says. "The bounty's for beasts brought in dead, mind. He'll want to send a cart for them."

He stays to see to the ewes. Wren waves from the door, Burr at her knee, his tail going.

You set off down the river lane with the four chambers bundled in your arms. The she-wolf's is heavy and cold on top, the crack running across it like a seam.

The morning is bright and cold. Beside the lane the river runs loud over its stones.

A quarter hour down, a young man in orange robes comes hurrying up the lane toward you. It's Keeper Anselm. He is muddy to the knees, and grey under the eyes like a man who hasn't slept.

He stops short. He stares at the chambers in your arms, then at the linen on your forearm.

"I felt it last night, at dusk, from the shrine," he says. "A great drawing, up at Marsh Croft."

He swallows.

"I came running, and lost my nerve at the foot of the fell in the dark. I'm sorry."

His eyes go back to the linen.

"Was that the greymaws? Are you hurt?"
