You tell Dray how it went. The creak in the loft, the keys in the barn, the bolts at your legs. The charge, the ring coat, the dart. Then the pull on Crake's head, and the spear through his neck. And the venom, and your Locks shut tight, and Wil's word that it wears off by dawn.

Dray listens. As he does, the grey fades out of his fists, back to flesh. When you finish he looks at Crake a long moment.

"I've seen First Depth fighters do less," he says. "Far less."

Then you speak for the three Loddons: clemency, under guard, and a chance to work it off.

Dray rubs his jaw. He looks at the kneeling brothers, then at Bet.

"Robbers hang under the Reeve's law. But a man who yields and names his master may go to the mines instead." He turns to the elder brother. "If your cellar proves true, Wil, I'll write Lady Varrow for you both. The choice is hers. Three years in Varrow Keep's quarry, under guard. Not the rope." His eyes move to Bet. "Bet's no robber. She speaks before Lady Varrow, then she can go home to Carrowmouth."

Bet bends over her brothers' shoulders, weeping again, but differently now.

Wil bows his head to the straw. Then he leans close to Dray and murmurs the cellar's place.

Dray nods, grim. "A rider goes to the Carrowmouth watch at first light."

By the door, the bound man spits.

"Not you, Harl," Dray says. "You hang, unless you've something worth your neck."

He turns back to you. "And Crake's head was worth five bars dead. Every coin of that is yours. We bed down here tonight. The Pell twins carry him home on a hurdle at dawn."

The wounded watchman sits on a bucket, grey-faced, while another binds his slashed forearm with a strip of shirt.

"The boy Pip," Wil says, low. "He'll run for Grey Shaw, to the old tollhouse. Crake's camp. There's a stash there. Bottled Water, coin, and Crake's ledger. Pip'll grab what he can and vanish. He'll be there near midnight."
