"What do you expect the consequences to be," you ask, "either way?"

Anselm sets down his cup. He thinks it through, frowning.

"If I write it all, the Keepers in Carrowmouth will send a senior Keeper up the valley," he says. "As soon as the road dries. They'll want to see you for themselves. They'll urge you to take the Chartermark and go south to the Spires."

He turns the cup a quarter turn on the table.

"And a full letter reaches the Spires within a month. A Talent like yours will draw them. Letters pass through many hands in Carrowmouth, too. A great Talent's name draws ill notice as well as good."

He looks up.

"If I write it bare, only that a great Talent is in my charge, it slows them. But they'd still come to see. That much I must write, whatever you ask. If I hid you and they found out, they'd take the shrine from me."

He spreads his thin hands, apologetic.

"So: all of it, or the bare bones. Which would you have?"
