"I'll gladly accept the porridge and then be on my way," you call, and follow him out.

Burr trots at your heel down the muddy lane. The stones sting your bare soles, but they don't cut. The flow keeps you warm. Your breath barely smokes, while Garth's puffs out white beside you with every step.

Marsh Croft is a low cottage with a turf roof, a stone fold full of ewes, and a byre. Iron nails are driven all along the fold's top rail, and a row of them over the cottage door.

You feel them as you pass under. A flat, numb band overhead, the same dead patch as the hooks in the barn.

Inside is one smoky room: a peat fire, a table, a ladder up to a loft, and a box bed by the hearth. It smells of peat smoke and wet wool, and under that something bitter, like a herb poultice.

A girl lies in the box bed. She is thin and freckled, nine or so, her face flushed with fever, and she watches you.

Garth ladles porridge into a wooden bowl, adds a lick of honey, and hands it to you with a cup of water.

It is hot and plain, and it is the best thing you have ever eaten. The cold goes out of your bones.

"Are there any threats in the area I should be aware of?" you ask between spoonfuls.

Garth snorts. "Aye. Greymaws. Wolves big as ponies, scale in their fur, stinking of rotten salt. Came down off the Whitecombs at midwinter. Forty sheep this winter, a watchman and a carter."

"Wolves aren't all that's come down off those mountains, neither."

He doesn't say what else.

"They hunt at dusk and dawn. Shun fire and cold iron; that's why the nails. Found their prints below Aske's barn two nights back. Salt-rank. You slept lucky, lass."

"And keep off deep still water after dark. Folk say the Deep Children rise and call you in."

He looks at the girl in the bed, and his voice drops.

"A greymaw had my Wren at the fold. A week back. Bit her calf. Goody's poultices slow it, but the bite's gone black with rot."

The girl pulls the blanket up to her chin and says, bravely, "It doesn't hurt much, Da."

Burr pads over and flops across your bare feet with a sigh, as if he means to stay.

Garth watches the dog a long moment. Then he looks at you, his rough hands knotted together on the table.

"You're Talented," he says quietly. "I don't hold with it, as a rule. But I'll ask."

"Is there aught you can do for her?"
