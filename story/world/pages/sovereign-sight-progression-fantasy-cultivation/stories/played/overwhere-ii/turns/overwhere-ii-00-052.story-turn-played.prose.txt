The peat catches. Bramble and Sorrel burn on the midden in a stink of salt and scorched scale.

You stand with Ebba by the smoke and tell her what you saw up there: the black pool, the salt crust, the goats' charge.

"Black water up there," she says, "and my goats turned to fish. At my age."

She watches the fire a while.

"Greymaws denned under the crag above that cwm since my grandmother's day. At midwinter I heard that crag split in the night. Like the sky breaking."

The last of the goats falls to ash. You take your leave and walk north toward the Ford.

Your throat is raw and your head pounds. Every hour without water leaves you a little weaker.

Night has fallen by the time you cross the green to the Reeve's house. Four greymaw carcasses lie in its yard under sacking. The watch is back from the den.

Tam Oakes lets you in. Dray sits by the fire with his boots off, and looks up.

You tell him all of it: the pool, the goats, the ripple in the black water, and how your well leaned toward it, the way it leaned toward the tarn.

"A black pool smelling of the sea," Dray says slowly. "I've seen one before, on the coast, in the militia. A tidepool. They draw Aberrants in, and turn beasts that linger by them. Like your goats."

He rubs his jaw.

"But the greymaws ran downhill. A pool draws things in. Something drove them out."

You tell him what Ebba heard: the crag where the greymaws denned split at midwinter.

"Then that's where the answer is," he says. "An hour above the pool, by the sound of it."

Then his face goes still.

"Your well leaned at the tarn too, you said. Hollow Tarn's no puddle. If that's a tidepool..."

He doesn't finish. He counts out half a silver bar in coin and sets it on the table.

"For the pool. The rest of the bar when you bring me what drove them off that crag."

He takes a long look at your cracked lips, then pushes a jug of water across the table to you.
