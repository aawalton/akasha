"Yeah, I'll need to prepare a bit before I'm ready for that," you say. "Sounds like you'll get to keep me a while longer after all."

Dray's tired face eases into something close to a smile.

"Glad to hear it. The cottage is yours for as long as you like. Better you take him ready than take him soon."

You bid him good night and cross the dark green to the Drowned Lantern.

The woman behind the counter sets down mutton stew, bread and ale for a copper. You eat every scrap.

Then you walk back to the watch cottage and sleep the night through.

You wake at dawn on day five, rested and clear-headed, your mouth a little dry.

The raw prickle has gone from your skin. The bruise over your ribs has faded to a dull yellow ache.

Your mind feels whole again. Sleep has washed away the wear of last night's lost refining.

Down the ford road, the ring of Hob's hammer carries through the cold dawn air.
