You scramble out of the cleft and up the scree after the limping greymaw.

On three legs it is slow. You gain on it with every stride, the flow hot in your legs, the loose stones skittering away under your boots.

It lurches over the corrie lip. You crest it a few heartbeats behind.

Below you lies the tarn, a black lake cupped in grey crags, still as poured ink.

The moment you see it, your well stirs under your breastbone and leans toward the water. It pulls at you like a tide drawn by the moon.

The greymaw limps along the east shore, where worn stone steps run down into the dark water.

You run it down beside the steps. It turns at bay, snarling, its bad leg trembling under it.

You pull it onto your thrusting spear. The point sinks through scale into its chamber.

A gush of cold brine spills over the steps, and the last greymaw dies.

As you pull, the black water of the tarn ripples toward you, against the wind. Your well leans harder toward it as the ripples come. They lap at the lowest step, and still.

You kneel and saw the dead greymaw's right ear off with the spear's edge.

The crags stand silent, empty of birdcall. The water lies flat as glass. The air smells faintly of the sea.

The worn steps go down into the tarn and on, out of sight, into the black.
