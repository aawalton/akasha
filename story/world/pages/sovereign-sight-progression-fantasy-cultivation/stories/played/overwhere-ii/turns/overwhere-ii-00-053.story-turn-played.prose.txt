You drink the whole jug, slowly. The ache in your head begins to loosen.

You thank Dray and cross the green to the Drowned Lantern for a hot meal and more to drink. The woman behind the counter takes your copper and sets down a bowl of mutton stew, a heel of bread and a mug of ale.

Warm and full, you walk back to the watch cottage and bar the door.

You sit on the bunk, bare your left leg, and begin as Anselm taught you, Undertow giving and drawing.

The tide climbs from ankle to hip, and the skin drinks it in, as your arms did.

Last you come to the sole, where your threads end. A cold jolt shoots up through your loops into your well.

The tide lurches. Water spills from your grip, your well drops low, and your breath catches.

You hold on and finish the leg. But your well is near dry, and you must wait an hour for it.

The Sea fills it back to the brim, slow and steady, while you sit in the dark.

You begin the right leg, ready this time for the jolt at the sole.

When it comes, you ride it like a wave. The refining runs smooth and swift, root to toe.

Your right leg shines faintly pearly in the candlelight. Both legs feel tight and cool, like your arms. Now only the skin of your trunk, neck and head feels thin and raw, asking to be matched.

You lie down, and sleep takes you at once.

You wake at dawn on day four, rested and whole, your mouth a little dry.

Out on the green, stalls are going up for market day. A carrier's cart stands by the Lantern.
