You keep to your routine. Broth and bread at the Lantern, then hours on the bunk in the watch cottage, refining bone.

Your hands and feet finish first. Then your ribs, one at a time, each one aching like a fever before it goes heavy and still.

Your spine is the worst. Each vertebra sends a cold shock down your arms and legs, and you sit through every one.

Every night the dream of the black stair comes back, stronger. By the second week you wake from it each night cold and salt-damp, the pull deep in your chest.

By the morning of day sixteen your hands, feet, ribs and spine are refined whole. Your skull, hips and long bones are still to do.

You are finishing your broth at the Lantern when the door bangs open.

A woman stumbles in, red-eyed and wild-haired, mud to the knees.

"Is there a healer here?" she asks the room. "They say a woman here drew rot out of a child."

The woman behind the counter glances at you before she can stop herself. The stranger sees it and comes straight over.

"Please. My boy, at Ashlin Farm, half a day down the road. He's burning with fever. He can't keep water down. Please, I've no one else."

You watch her as she talks. The tears are real enough. But her eyes keep going to the door.

"How old's the boy?" the woman behind the counter asks.

The stranger hesitates, a breath too long.

"Eight. Nine come summer."

The woman behind the counter leans close to you. "Never seen her before," she murmurs. "I know most faces in this valley."

The stranger grips your sleeve.

"Please. Will you come?"
