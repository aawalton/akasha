"Sleep now. Best to deal with the rest rested, and with light."

Hawise nods. "I'll take first watch."

In the longhouse Ebba has left oatcakes and goat's cheese on the board, and peat by the hearth.

The rain barrel by the door holds the only clean water on the farm.

Hawise builds up the fire and sits by it, blades across her knees.

You lie down on a pallet by the wall. Sleep takes you fast.

Through the night the black stair calls, louder than it ever has.

You wake in grey light, salt-damp, your shirt clinging cold to your back.

Your throat is cracked dry. Your head pounds, and your tongue feels thick.

Your last drink was at the Keep, a day ago.

Outside, the bank across the gully mouth still holds. The black water leans against it.

Six carcasses lie stiff on the grey pasture, frost on their scales.

Above the gully, the thin cold trickle from the Callow pool still runs.

Hawise stands in the doorway, stretching. "Quiet night. The mountain kept to itself. Up to that pool, or back to the Lady?"
