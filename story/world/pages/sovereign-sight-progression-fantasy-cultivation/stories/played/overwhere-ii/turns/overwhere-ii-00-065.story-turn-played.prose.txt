"Gladly," you say. "I'll bring my new spear; can't be too safe with the wolves around."

You grin, eager. The corner of Dray's mouth twitches in answer.

"Good. A spear in a lone traveller's hand won't alarm anyone on a road with wolves. My watch and I leave an hour ahead, by the fields east of the road, out of the boy's sight. When you reach the lane's end, stop and talk. Hold the crossbows' eyes on you while I close. When I'm at the barn's back wall, you'll hear a single crow's caw from the gully."

An hour later Dray and his four watchmen slip out of the Ford eastward, across the fields, and are gone behind the hedgerows.

At noon you set off down the valley road with Bet beside you, the cold-iron spear on your shoulder.

The road runs down the valley between budding hedges, past fields and empty folds.

Bet walks close, eyes down, too frightened to look left or right.

You watch the hedges and the road ahead. Both stay empty.

By mid-afternoon the Ford is long behind you, and Bet hasn't said a word in an hour.

Then, low, without looking up, she speaks.

"Will my brothers hang?"
