"Come on down," you call up. "If there are any others and you help us subdue them, that will count in your favour."

The ladder creaks. A broad, bearded man comes down first, empty hands held open. Wil.

A thin, wild-eyed younger one follows, shaking so hard the rungs rattle.

Unbidden, both of them kneel in the straw, hands open on their knees.

"There's no one else," Wil says. "Just Big Harl down the gully, and the boy, Pip. He ran."

His eyes go to the dart in your neck, then away.

"That venom shuts a Talent's Locks till about dawn. No longer. It wears off."

Bet stumbles in from the yard and drops beside her brothers. She pulls the younger one's head against her.

Boots and voices come round the barn's corner. Dray, mud to the knees, his fists still grey stone.

Two watchmen march a big man between them, bound at the wrists, limping, one arm hanging. Behind them a third watchman clutches his forearm, blood running between his fingers.

Dray takes in the barn: Crake in the brine, the kneeling Loddons, the dart in your neck.

"Gods. You did it." Then, sharper: "Are you hurt? Is that one of his darts?"

Before you can answer, Wil lifts his head. His voice is low and steady.

"Crake has three Talented chained in a cellar in Carrowmouth. In the Salt Lanes. I know which cellar. I'll tell it, Reeve, if it buys my brother's life and mine."
