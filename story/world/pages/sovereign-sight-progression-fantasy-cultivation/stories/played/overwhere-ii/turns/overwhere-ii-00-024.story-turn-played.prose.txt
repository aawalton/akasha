You run at her.

The spear is in both hands and the flow roars hot through your legs. The heather flies past. Above you the she-wolf lengthens into a charge, huge and silent, jaws open.

At ten paces you reach with the tide for the bone chamber in her chest, and pull.

It is deep. Far deeper than the others' were. It fights you like a rope in a current.

In the same moment you shove with the push against her body, straight into her charge.

Push and pull at once. The tide strains two ways inside you, and you feel it stretch toward breaking.

You hold it.

Her charge slows, as if she is running through deep water. Her legs go heavy.

You drop to one knee and plant the butt of the spear in the turf, point up, square in her path.

She comes on anyway, too heavy to stop, and meets the braced point full in the chest.

The iron head punches through scale and sinks deep. The crossbar slams up against her breast. Something bursts inside her, and a cold gush of brine sprays from the wound, reeking of the sea.

Even so, her jaws snap sideways as she falls, and catch your left forearm.

The teeth grind through the tough skin of your arm. Hot pain. Blood runs down to your fingers.

Then the jaws go slack. She slumps over the spear, and her milk-white eye goes dull.

You wrench your arm free, gasping.

Inside you, something in the tide gives way once more. Push and pull have stopped fighting each other. You know, the way you knew the name, that you can do both at once, whenever you will it.

High on the fell, the rest of the pack begin to howl. Thin, broken, fleeing howls. They fade away up the screes, farther and farther, until the fell is quiet.

Your well sloshes, brimming with cold foreign brine. It churns under your breastbone, but it doesn't hurt you.

Your forearm throbs, torn in a ragged crescent. Under your sense, a thin sourness is seeping into the wound.

Behind you, the cottage door scrapes open. Garth stands in it with a lantern, staring.
