"Don't know for sure. Here. But that's a mystery for another day."

Maud's eyes narrow. She lets it lie, with a look that says she'll ask again.

You tell her about the Callow pool: the spill, the Warped ewes, the bank and the plug.

You tell of the black you parted from the water, the bead, and the chamber that holds it.

You tell of the pool's middle, out of your reach, and the swells that cloud the shallows again.

Maud listens without a word. At the chamber, her mouth tightens.

"A tidepool. Newly opened, where the Horizon's gone thin. No tidepool was ever bailed dry, girl. The Sea feeds it from beneath. And that thing in your bag? Keep it sealed. Far from wells, and stock, and children."

"The Keepers' records tell of new tidepools ringed with cold iron. Stakes sunk in the bed round the throat, where the black rises. It slows it to a trickle."

Anselm speaks up. "Hob Tarrant could forge them."

"Forearm-long stakes. Thirty, I'd guess, for a pool that size. Ten a day, if he sweats. I'll write to Deepwatch tonight. Mending the Horizon is the Spires' work. And I'll see this pool with my own eyes before I ride on to the Keep."

Her face darkens. "But the throat's out in the middle, in the black. Deeper than a man. And every soul who's drunk or bathed in a tidepool came out Warped."
