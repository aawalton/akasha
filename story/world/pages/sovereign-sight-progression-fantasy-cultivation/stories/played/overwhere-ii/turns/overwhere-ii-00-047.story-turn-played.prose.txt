"A bit," you say. "Nothing some food, drink and sleep won't fix."

You drain the cup of water in one go. Then you take the cloth and wipe the dried blood from your forearm.

Anselm refills the cup. He goes into the back and comes out with bread, a wedge of hard cheese and a jug of small beer.

"Then eat," he says. "I'll not have the valley's hero faint in my shrine."

You eat. The bread is coarse and the cheese is sharp, and bite by bite the hollow under your ribs fills.

Anselm sits across from you, watching you eat, turning his cup in his thin hands.

"There's a thing I must tell you," he says, "before I do it."

He sets the cup down.

"I write to the Keepers in Carrowmouth each week. The carrier comes on market day. A Keeper must report any great new Talent in his charge. Yours is the greatest I've seen."

He meets your eyes.

"I mean to write of you. Your name, Undertow, what I've felt, what I saw today. I'd rather you heard it from me first."

His hands tighten on the cup.

"Is there anything you'd have me leave out?"
