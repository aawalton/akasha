"Just a Talented," you say. "I hadn't tried to use my Talent quite like that before. I'm glad it worked."

Garth stares at you. One eyebrow climbs, and his mouth twists, as if to say: just, is it.

But he only nods, slowly, one hand still on Wren's hair.

"Anyone else get bitten you know of?" you ask. "I wouldn't mind some more practice with my new technique."

Garth gives a short, rough laugh. "Practice." He shakes his head. "Aye. There's others. Two of my own ewes, out in the fold. Greymaw raked them through the rails. Gone lame and sour."

"And at the Ford, the miller's girl, Tansy Horne. Bitten ten days back. She's bad, they say. Worse than Wren was. Horne's rich as a lord, and he's spent silver on her for naught."

"Goody Brannoc at the Ford tends all the rot-sick. She'd know who else."

Wren tugs at your sleeve. "Will you come back? Will you tell me what Talent you've got?"

Garth gets up, stiff on his bad leg, and crosses to a wooden chest under the loft ladder. He kneels and lifts the lid slowly, as if it is heavier than it is.

He takes out a pair of worn leather boots and a folded brown wool cloak, and holds them for a moment.

"These were my Anni's. Wren's mam. She's three winters gone." His voice is steady and quiet. "She'd not have you walk the thaw barefoot, after this. Nor would I."

He holds them out to you, the boots on top of the cloak.
