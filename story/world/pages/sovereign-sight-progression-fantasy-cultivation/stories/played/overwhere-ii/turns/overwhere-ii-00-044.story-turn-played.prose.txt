"Sorry, I've been a bit busy," you say. "We can talk now. I'm free for a few hours at least."

Anselm's tired face lights up. He leads you across the green to the shrine of Threll.

Inside it is cool and dim. Before the altar stands a stone vessel of Water that glows faintly, blue-green in the gloom.

He sits you on a bench beside it and sits down facing you, his hands folded in his sleeves.

"The Keepers teach that every Talent draws from the Sea, and grows by Depths," he says. "Surface first, then First Depth, and on down. Each Depth is reached by a Descent. And each Descent brings a Tribulation, a trial in the Sea you must face alone."

He leans forward.

"But you don't Descend until your body can bear it. That's what the daily practice is for. Skin first, whole, before any muscle. An hour at dawn and an hour at dusk. You slow your Current, and press your Water out into the skin, one limb at a time."

He gives a small, rueful smile.

"It prickles like nettles, then burns like a scald, then goes numb. Then you move on. I've spent three years on my skin, and I've never gone deeper."

He rolls back one orange sleeve and holds out a thin forearm, pale and smooth.

"Will you try it with me now? Your left arm first."
