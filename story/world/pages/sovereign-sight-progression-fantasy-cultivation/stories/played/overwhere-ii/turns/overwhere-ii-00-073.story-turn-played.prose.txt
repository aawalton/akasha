You step back and let Dray take the lead.

"Out you come, lad," he says, gruff but not unkind. "Nobody hangs a boy of thirteen."

The thorns rustle. A thin, freckled boy crawls out in a too-big coat. The whistle-seller from market day.

He holds a little clasp knife out in front of him, shaking so hard the blade jitters.

Dray doesn't reach for it. "Put that away. Crake's dead. She killed him at Ashlin."

"Liar. Nobody kills him. He's—" The boy's voice cracks.

Dray lifts one fist into the moonlight. Before the boy's eyes it greys and hardens into stone.

"I was there, lad. It's over."

Pip stares at the stone fist, then at you. The knife drops into the road.

He cries, hard, the way a child does. Then, all at once, he goes quiet.

"There's a stash," he says, hoarse. "Under the tollhouse hearth. Bottles and coin and his book. I'll show you. Just don't hang me."

"Nobody's hanging you," Dray says. "You'll work for Tam Oakes at the Ford, under watch, not in a cell." He turns to you. "We go on to Grey Shaw tonight, and take that stash before anyone else does."

You walk the last miles at the boy's pace, Dray's hand on his collar.

Your mouth is dry after the long run. You haven't drunk since the afternoon.

Near ten, birches loom white in the moonlight, and a tollhouse squats beside an old toll bar.

One end of its roof is gone. Inside are cold ashes, and a broad, flat hearthstone.

Dray levers the stone up with his stone hands. Beneath it is a dug pit, lined with sacking.

Rows of small stoppered bottles, cold to the touch, each one sloshing faintly. Stolen Water.

A heavy iron coin box, locked. And a fat leather ledger, its pages close-written.

"The keys are on his belt," Pip says, very small. "Were."

Dray holds a bottle up to the moon, his face grim.

"By law this goes to the watch. But this came out of Talents like you and me. You've earned a say in it. What would you have done with it?"
