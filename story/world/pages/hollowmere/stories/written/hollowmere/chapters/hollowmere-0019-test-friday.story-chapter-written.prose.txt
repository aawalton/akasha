On Friday you wake at five with your heart going.

It's dark. The radiator knocks. You lie still and listen to your heart thudding against the mattress, quick and hard, as if you've been running, and you know at once what day it is.

Test day.

You can't lie there. You get up and pull on your swimsuit and your robe and the fisherman's jumper over the top, and go down through the sleeping house to the water.

---

The swimmers are out in a thin rain.

A fine grey drizzle, hardly more than mist, drifting across the shingle and pocking the flat water. The register is under a plastic sheet. The second-year has her hood up. A handful of girls are standing about in their robes looking damp and resigned.

Shiv is at the folding table, signing. She looks up as you come down the shingle.

She takes one look at your face.

"Buoy," she says. "Now."

You go in. The cold hits, and for once it's a relief: something simple, something that only wants you to breathe. You swim out beside her through the rain, the drops pattering on the water all round your head, and grab the buoy, and hang on.

Your hands are shaking on it. Not from the cold.

Shiv sees.

"Breathe with me," she says. "Come on. In." She breathes in, slow, and you do. "And out. All the way. Out, out, out." Her breath goes out in a long white plume over the water. Yours goes with it. "Again."

You breathe with her. In, and out, and out. Again. And again. The rain falls on you both. The buoy rocks. And slowly, slowly, your hands stop shaking.

"There," says Shiv.

She looks at you, water dripping off her eyebrows.

"It's a glim and a warm stone," she says. "That's all it is. You've done both a hundred times. A *hundred*. I've watched you." She splashes you, gently. "So stop being a gom."

---

At breakfast Bea doesn't eat a thing.

She sits at the long table with a plate of toast in front of her and doesn't touch it, and drinks three cups of tea, one after another, very fast, and talks the whole time about anything at all, the weather, the rain, rowing tomorrow, Morwenna's headband, until her voice runs out and she just sits.

Kit sits with you.

She sits down beside you with a tray with one cup of black coffee and one slice of dry toast on it, cut into triangles, and doesn't say a word. Her face is white. Not pale; white, like paper.

Under the table, you take her hand.

Kit grips it. Hard. So hard her ring digs into your knuckle and the bones of your fingers grind together. She holds on for a long moment, staring straight ahead at the wall.

Then she lets go.

And picks up one dry triangle of toast. And eats it, slowly, all the way to the crust.

---

At nine it's Penhallow's study.

You've barely sat down when she looks at you.

"Ashby," she says at once. "Whose wanting?" She folds her hands on the desk. "I said Friday. It's Friday."

The room goes quiet.

They're all looking at you. Kit, beside you. Amara. Priya, Lin, Shiv. The round window behind Penhallow full of grey rain on the grey mere.

You think.

You think of Shiv at the buoy on Wednesday, water running off her nose: *Yours, eejit. Whose else would it be?* You think of the long pose. The lamplight on your skin, and a dozen pairs of eyes, and Lin's, and how it went through you like the cold, and then the ease came. You think of the low wall by the lodge in the mist, and crying for a girl you never met, and telling her you'd look after her life.

You think of Alan, in his chair, by the back window.

"Mine," you say.

Penhallow waits.

"Whoever I was before," you say, slowly. "Whatever I was. It's mine now." You look at her. "I'm the one who wants."

Penhallow looks at you over her glasses for a long moment.

Her kind blue eyes are very steady. Very sharp.

"Hm," she says.

And then: "Yes." A small nod. "Good."

She reaches for the shortbread tin, and pushes it across the desk. Not an inch. All the way. Right across the blotter and the papers, until it's sitting in front of you.

Across the table, Shiv is grinning at you sideways.

---

The rest of the hour Penhallow keeps it light.

She puts the kettle on, the electric one in the corner, and makes tea for all of you in an enormous brown pot, and hands round mugs. And she tells stories. About the old days. About her own first test, nearly forty years ago, in this same college, in the same Practice Hall.

"I set my eyebrows on fire," she says.

You stare at her.

"Second attempt," Penhallow says, quite calmly, sipping her tea. "The glim went up rather more than I meant. Whoosh." She makes a small gesture at her own face. "I passed. They grew back."

Priya shrieks.

And Amara laughs, and Lin laughs, and Shiv laughs so hard she has to put her mug down, and even Kit's mouth goes in at the corner, white as she is.

---

At eleven it's Sigils, and every line wobbles.

Every single one. You draw the warming spiral and it comes out like a snail that's been trodden on. You draw the mending line and the two halves wander off from each other. Down the room, all round you, the whole group is the same: pens jerking, ink blotting, girls sighing and screwing up paper.

The tutor stands at her easel and looks at you all for a long moment.

Then she sighs.

"Glims," she says. "Just copy glims. A page of them. For calm." She sits down at her desk. "Breathe."

You copy glims. The stroke, the loop, the hook. Over and over. Down the page. It helps, a little.

---

Lunch is the six of you at one end of a long table, pushing food about.

You and Bea and Kit, and Lin, and Shiv, and Priya, all squeezed up together on the benches with plates of shepherd's pie, all of you pushing it about. Even Shiv is only picking. Priya is making a small neat wall of peas along the rim of her plate.

Amara isn't with you.

She's at the far end of another table, apart, with a cup of coffee and a book she isn't reading.

---

At two it's the Practice Hall.

The benches have been pushed back against the walls. The middle of the floor is bare: the old scorched boards, wide and empty, with the grey light from the high windows falling on them. At the front, a table, and behind it, the demonstrator, with a clipboard, a pen, and a basket of smooth grey stones.

Forty first-years stand along the walls.

The hall is very quiet. The talking has stopped. You can hear the rain on the windows, and someone's shoes squeak, and somebody coughs and stops.

The demonstrator looks at her list.

"Ashby," she reads. "N."

First. Again.

You walk out.

Out from the wall into the middle of the scorched floor, and every eye in the hall turns with you. Forty pairs of eyes. You feel them land on you, the way the lamplight landed on you last night. All at once, all over.

You stop in the middle of the floor.

You breathe out.

The sigil. The stroke, the loop, the hook turning in. The word.

The glim comes.

Steady. Pale. A clear soft light above your palm, quite still, not a flicker in it. It hangs there, and it holds, and holds, and the demonstrator counts under her breath, her pen tapping, and it holds a long count before you let it go.

She holds out a stone from the basket.

You take it. It's cold. Cold right through, a stone from the shore. You hold it in your palm and draw the spiral over it, loose, all the way in, on the out-breath, and call.

It warms. Steadily, from the middle out. Bathwater-warm. And holds.

The demonstrator reaches across the table and touches it with two fingers. She writes something on her clipboard.

"Pass," she says.

You walk back to the wall.

Your knees have gone. Completely gone, like water, and you don't know how you're walking on them, but you are, and you get to the wall and lean on it, and Bea's hand grabs yours.

"Ashworth," reads the demonstrator. "K."

---

Kit walks out into the middle of the floor.

She walks very straight. Her chin up, her back like a rod, her black skirt swinging. She reaches the middle of the scorched boards and stops, and turns to face the table, and forty girls are watching her.

You can feel it in the hall. Some of them remember. The first glim, the first week. Her hand stopping in the air.

Kit's hand rises.

The stroke. The loop.

And at the hook it freezes.

Her hand stops in the air, rigid. Her whole body stops with it. The hall goes very, very still. Not a cough. Not a shoe. Just the rain, ticking on the high glass, and forty girls holding their breath, and Kit standing alone in the middle of the floor with her hand in the air and her face like chalk.

Her eyes move.

Along the wall. Looking. Searching the faces.

For you.

You're there. You're right there, against the wall, where she can see you. And Bea is beside you, gripping your hand. And Lin, on your other side. And Shiv, a step along, her arms folded, leaning on the wall, nodding at Kit. Slowly. Once.

Kit finds you.

You breathe out.

Slowly. Visibly. So she can see it. Your shoulders dropping, your chest going down, your breath going out of you long and slow, all the way.

Beside you, Bea does it too. A huge exaggerated breath out, her whole body sagging with it.

Then Lin. Softly. Her shoulders lowering.

Then, rolling her eyes so hard you can practically hear it, Shiv. Her arms still folded. A long, patient, theatrical sigh.

Kit breathes out.

You see it. Her shoulders drop. Her chest goes down. And her hand moves, at last; the hook turns in.

The glim comes.

Small, at first. Then steady. Then clear. A pale bright bead of light above her hand, in the middle of the scorched floor, in front of forty girls.

It holds.

The demonstrator counts it. She counts a long time. Then she nods, and holds out a stone, and Kit takes it in her palm, and draws the spiral, and breathes out, and the stone goes warm. On her first breath. You can see the air shimmer over it.

"Pass," says the demonstrator.

Kit walks back to the wall. Very straight. Very slowly. Her face perfectly composed and completely white, and her ears scarlet.

She reaches the wall beside you, and turns, and leans on it.

And her hand finds yours.

---

The names go on.

"Lindqvist, B."

Bea goes out like a soldier. Her glim wobbles. It wobbles badly, swaying, dimming, and your heart stops, and then it steadies, and holds. Her stone warms. Properly, steadily, the right spell. "Pass," says the demonstrator, and Bea comes back to the wall nearly crying, her lip going, and you put your arm round her.

"Osei, A."

Amara's glim is bright and perfect. Of course it is. Clear as a lamp. And her stone, in her palm, steams, actually steams, in the cold air of the hall. "Pass." She smiles round the hall, dazzling, as she walks back.

"Raman, P." Pass.

"Zhao, L." Lin's glim comes up soft and round and lustrous, like a pearl. Pass.

"Doyle, S." Shiv casts hers with a yawn. A real one, enormous, behind her other hand. Pass.

Two girls from another group, near the end, get the other answer. "Again next week," the demonstrator says. Kindly. Quietly. Just to them. They walk back to the wall with red faces, and the girls round them close in.

---

After, while the hall is emptying in a rush of noise and relief and shrieking, you see Amara slip out of a side door.

You go after her.

You find her on the steps outside. In the drizzle, on the wet stone, sitting with her knees drawn up and her coat round her, looking out at the grey quad.

Her eyes are dry.

You sit down beside her. The step is cold and wet through your skirt.

Amara doesn't look at you.

"I was going to," she says. "Cry. I came out to." She looks at the rain. "I sat down here to. I've been sitting here waiting for it." She shrugs, slowly. "And I can't be bothered, Freckles."

You look at her.

"Is that progress?" Amara says.

"I think it might be," you say.

She leans on your shoulder. Just leans, for a minute, her head against yours, her braids cold and damp against your cheek, in the drizzle.

And then she laughs. Softly. To herself. To the rain.

---

In the evening it's the Drowned Bell.

Friday night, and the folk session, and half of Hollowmere is crammed in under the low black beams. It's hot and loud and smells of beer and woodsmoke and wet wool. By the fire, three fiddles and a squeezebox and a girl with a drum are going at a reel so fast you can't see their bows. People are standing on the benches. People are singing.

Your crowd comes in through the door together, out of the rain, Shiv first, then Bea and Lin and Priya and Kit and you.

And the landlady, behind the bar, a big red-faced woman in a striped apron, sees you, and reaches up, and grabs the rope of the old brass ship's bell that hangs over the bar.

She rings it. *Clang. Clang. Clang.*

"*First-years!*" she bellows. "*Test Friday!*"

And the whole pub cheers.

The whole pub. A great roar, going up off the beams, and people turning round and banging the tables and whistling, and somebody you've never seen before thrusts a glass of cider into your hand.

Half a cider, you remind yourself. You remember what half a cider does to this body.

It goes to your head anyway. Warm and fizzing, straight up, in about four minutes.

---

Shiv drags you into a reel.

You don't know the steps. You don't know any of the steps. You've never danced a reel in your life, in either life. But Shiv has your hands and is spinning you round by the fire, and there are girls all round you in two long lines, clapping and stamping and whooping, and someone grabs your elbow and swings you and passes you on, and you go the wrong way, and the wrong way again, and crash into Priya, and Shiv hauls you back.

You laugh until you ache. Until your sides hurt and your eyes stream and you can't breathe.

When you finally stagger off the floor, Kit is in the corner.

She's sitting on the end of a settle in the corner by the window, with a glass of lemonade, watching. She's been watching the whole time. When she sees you coming she moves along, and makes room.

You fall into it beside her, breathless.

Kit looks at you.

"I passed," she says. Low. Under the noise of the fiddles. As if she's still checking. As if she has to keep saying it to make sure. "In front of all of them." Her eyes are very bright. "I passed."

And she kisses you.

There. In the corner. In the middle of the Drowned Bell, with half of Hollowmere crammed in round you, the fiddles going, the fire roaring. Her mouth on yours, cool from the lemonade.

When she pulls back she's gone scarlet. Scarlet everywhere.

And she stays. Right where she is, beside you, her shoulder against yours. She doesn't move away.

Bea comes back from the bar with her arms full of crisps. She sees the two of you. She squeezes in on your other side, packets cascading into your lap, and leans round you and grins at Kit. Both dimples.

Lin comes and sits on the floor at your feet, with her back against the settle and her sketchbook on her knee. She's drawing the fiddlers. Quick lines, the bows, the elbows, the stamping feet. After a while, she leans her head on your knee, and keeps drawing.

---

Across the room, by the fire, Yusra is off duty.

You didn't see her come in. She's in a dark jumper, with her hair down, among a knot of third-years by the hearth, and she has a pint in her bandaged hand.

She catches your eye across the crowded room.

And lifts her pint to you. The way she did on your very first Friday here, across this same room.

You lift your cider back.

---

Later, on your way to the loo, you have to squeeze past the fire.

Past Yusra.

She turns her head as you pass.

"Passed?" she says.

"Passed," you say.

"Told you," says Yusra.

And she holds your eyes. Just a moment. A moment longer than she needs to, in the firelight, with the fiddles going and the heat on your face. Her dark eyes on yours, steady, warm.

Then she turns back to her friends.

---

At eleven you all walk home along the black shore path together.

Arm in arm, the whole lot of you, in a long ragged line across the path, in the dark, with the rain stopped and the mere lapping invisible on the shingle beside you. Singing. Badly. Shiv leading, something Irish and endless with a chorus that the rest of you only half know, so you just sing *la la la* very loudly in the right places.

At the gates, Kit peels off towards Ashcombe.

She stops. She kisses you, quickly, on the mouth.

And then she turns, and kisses Bea on the cheek.

Bea stands absolutely still in the gateway. Her mouth falls open. Kit walks off across the quad into the dark, very fast, and doesn't look back, and Bea puts her hand up to her cheek, and looks at you, astonished.

---

At your door, Bea stops.

The corridor is dark. Everyone else has gone to bed. She's standing in front of 14 with her hands in the sleeves of her jumper.

"Can I stay?" she says. "Just sleep." Her voice is small. "I want to be close."

You open the door.

The bed in 14 is narrow. Barely wide enough for one. You get in, and turn to the wall, and Bea gets in behind you, and curls against your back. Her knees behind yours. Her arm over you, round your waist. Her breath in your hair, warm, smelling faintly of cider and crisps.

You lie in the dark, held.

After a while, Bea moves. She kisses the back of your neck. Once, softly. Just under your hair.

"I'm proud of you," she whispers.

And you sleep.
