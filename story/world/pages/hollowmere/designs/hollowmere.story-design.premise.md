A slow slice-of-life story of found family and a yuri harem, told chapter by chapter, with Nala at its heart.

Nala is Alan. One evening he dozes over a book at his back window and wakes in a narrow dorm bed in a woman's body: about twenty, petite, pale and freckled, with long dark red hair. It is the morning her first term at Hollowmere Academy begins. Everyone here knows her as Nala, a new first-year: there is a trunk with her name on it, an acceptance letter, a timetable and a key. She has none of this Nala's memories. She remembers being Alan, and she does not know how she came here or whether she can go back.

The world is an alternate Earth where magic is real, known and ordinary, taught and licensed and part of daily life. Hollowmere is a residential college of magic, and every student is an adult in her twenties. Nala has to learn magic from nothing alongside everyone else, and learn this body and this life while she does.

The story is slow and lives in the days: classes, meals, the dorm, lessons gone wrong, late nights, small kindnesses, the girls she meets. Out of those days she gathers a family of girls who choose each other, and a harem around her, one girl at a time, each closeness earned on the page.

Nala is attracted only to girls, and she is not naturally monogamous: she can love several girls at once, and wants to. The story treats that as who she is rather than a problem to fix; what the girls make of it, and of each other, is part of the story.

When the story reaches sex, because a girl and Nala have reached it, it is written fully explicit, with the feeling between them in it. It comes when it is right, and never before.

Every character is an adult, and no man is ever a love interest.
