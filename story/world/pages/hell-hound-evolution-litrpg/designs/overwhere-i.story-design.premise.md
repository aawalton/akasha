Alan, a man from Provo, Utah, falls asleep over a book at his back window one evening and wakes somewhere else in a woman's body. He goes by Nala from the moment he wakes, and everyone in the fiction knows her as Nala. He does not know where he is, and he finds out the way anyone would: by looking, asking and living there. He is the player; he can recognise the place as the story goes, but nothing outside the fiction ever tells him.

The world is the one this story sits in, in the season its read story's latest chapters reach. Nala arrives whole and alone at the wild edge of a region where people live, far from anywhere the canon's people are, in a place of the world builder's making drawn from how this world's settlements, roads, peoples and wilds are. She is one of the travellers from another world that this world receives, come into a grown body rather than a newborn one. How and why she came, and why the body came out as it did, is a secret the world builder holds.

What woke is a human woman of about twenty-five, and she is who Alan is now. She understands and speaks the common tongue of the land and reads its script as if born to it, and cannot say how. The world's System reaches her as it reaches everyone here, and it greets her as it greets one of its own newcomers.

Nala is this story's overpowered isekai protagonist. She arrives with one decisive advantage that fits this world: a cheat skill, a blessing, a bloodline or a System perk, of a kind this world's own rules could grant. The world builder defines it as a real mechanic page in this world's terms, with what it plainly is in its description and its costs and limits held as data, and settles it before her second turn. She discovers what it does by using it. It is strong enough that the ordinary dangers where she starts are no match for her, and it has room to grow.

The game invents nothing the world does not have. Status, skills, levels, classes and what practice does are used only the way this world uses them, and her advantage is one this world's rules could hold.

The game master and the writer never name the source work, its author, or the fact that it is a book. No narration or panel says where Nala is. Names, places and customs appear only where a native or the scene would naturally show them. Alan's knowledge of fiction is his own and is never answered from outside the fiction.

Play is tuned for fun, competence and delight, with low stress. This is a power fantasy: Nala's survival is not in question in ordinary play, and threats are there to be overcome, often spectacularly. She can be hurt, outwitted, embarrassed or set back, but never killed by ordinary bad luck or an unlucky roll. Only a choice she makes with open eyes against a foe far beyond her puts her life at stake, and the game master shows her that plainly before she makes it. Stakes come from her choices, the people she comes to care about, mysteries, and the scale of what she takes on. Progress is real: she still grows, earns and discovers, power is not handed out every turn, and what she gains she gains in play. Warmth comes from the people who earn it.
