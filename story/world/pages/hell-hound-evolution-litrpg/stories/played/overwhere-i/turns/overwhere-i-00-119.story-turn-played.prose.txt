"I was born in the wilds, no home, no lord, no papers," you say. "Would Wendlow be fine to claim? I'm tired of the questions."

At *born in the wilds*, Varne's pen stops a breath above the page. Her eyes stay on your face.

Then the pen moves on, and she doesn't ask about it again.

"Wendlow will do." She writes in a small, quick hand, saying it as she goes. "Home, Wendlow. Lord, the margrave, served through this bench."

She looks up.

"That makes you mine to call. A sworn hunter answers this bench against outlaws and beasts, and is paid at the Board's rates for it, the same as any slip on Grete's wall." The pen scratches on. "There's no fee for the papers. And a sworn hunter of Wendlow passes its gate without toll."

She sands the sheet, tips the grit off, and sets it aside, unsealed. A stick of red wax and a brass seal lie beside it.

Below the bench, the clerk rises. He lifts a heavy book with iron-shod corners from his desk, carries it up, and lays it open on the bench between you.

"The oath's spoken here," Varne says, "one hand on the town book, the clerk to witness. Then I seal them."
