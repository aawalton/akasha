You go down to hear what he has to say. It's three minutes along the towpath to Hobb's landing, the roar of the weir growing as you come.

The bargeman meets you at the foot of his plank: a lean man, sun-browned to leather, his left hand short two fingers. He looks you up and down, lingering on the blade at your hip, and something in his shoulders lets go.

"Jory Pell, master of the Wend Maid." He jerks a thumb at the barge behind him. "You'll be the Board's hunter, at last, with that sword."

On the other barge, the Pike, a crew of three sit in a row on the deck and watch you in silence.

"It rose under my bow at dusk two days back," Jory says, "and took my bowman Dickon clean off the deck. Just gone. His bolts skipped off its back like hail off slate."

He jerks his chin across the water at the stove-in hulk against the far bank.

"The Heron. Stove in at dawn a week ago. Her crew swam clear. Lucky." He spits over the side. "Near noon on sunny days it hauls out on the weir stone to lie in the sun. I've watched it. Keep off the pool's edge, mind. It took the miller's dog off the bank a week back."

"Where it lives, I'd guess the pool. Past that, it's anyone's guess." He rubs the stumps of his fingers with his thumb. "Both crews will add a gold between us if it dies. We need to get downriver."

Jory squints up at the sun climbing over the weir, already warm on the stone.

"Today's sunny enough, I'd say."
