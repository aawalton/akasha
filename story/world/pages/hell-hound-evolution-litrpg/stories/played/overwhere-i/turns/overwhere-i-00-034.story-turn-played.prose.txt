You reach for water and will it wide, spreading your hold through the channel on both sides, feeling for anything like the first one so you can pull it out.

The water answers as pushes against your hold. Small darting ones. Slow ones. A drift of weed.

They come without shapes. It's all press and flick and weight.

Downstream, near the bank, something heavy pushes back in a thick, writhing knot.

It feels big. You close the hold on it and heave.

The water bulges and lifts, and one of Jory's wicker traps comes up with it, stake and all. It's packed with eels. The wicker cracks in the air, and the trap bursts across the bank.

Eels spill writhing into the mud. Stakes and split wicker scatter after them.

You let the water fall. There was no lurker in it.

Jory stares at the wreck of his trap, then at you. His face goes dark red.

"That was a good one," he says. "One of the few I had left."
