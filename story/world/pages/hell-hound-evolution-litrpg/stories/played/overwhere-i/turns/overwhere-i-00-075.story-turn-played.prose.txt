Behind your boulders you hold two water lenses in the air, one in line behind the other, and sweep the treeline through your spyglass, slow and careful.

The sweep takes about a minute. The pines stand still and silent the whole way along.

You find the shooter's stand: a patch of trampled needles, a dropped bolt, and a line of scuffed needles leading off north.

The shooter is gone.

You turn the lens on the pit. The gallery mouths are dark and still.

The crossbowman you dropped lies on his face in his gallery mouth, his crossbow under him, dead.

On the pit's north wall the lens finds a goat path, climbing up and out to the forest.

Its dust is freshly scuffed.
