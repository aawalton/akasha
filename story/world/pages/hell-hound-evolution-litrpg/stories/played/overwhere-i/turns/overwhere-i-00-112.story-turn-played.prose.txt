You climb over the tail-race wall and cross onto the apron, the stone warm and wet underfoot. You circle the pinned body wide, well clear of the head, keeping a good long stride between you and those fangs in case anything in it has a last twitch left.

It reeks: river mud and scorched flesh and something rank beneath, like a fishmonger's drain in high summer. The flies have already found it, settling in a buzzing crust along the torn crown.

You spin up a water disc above your palm, thin and fast, a blur of silver hissing in the sun, and set it to the thick neck just behind the skull. It bites. Scale and gristle and bone spray away in a fine pink mist, and you hold it there, steady, for the better part of a minute, until it shears clean through.

The head rolls free on the stone, heavy as a sack of meal, and comes to rest on its side. The jaw sags open. The fangs are as long as your hand.

Up the bank, Jory and both barge crews come picking their way down to the apron in a straggling line, and at the mill a bar scrapes back and the door creaks open.

You kneel beside the spikes and cut into the pale belly, working toward the liver. It's slow, slick, stinking work, twenty minutes of it, the flies crawling over your knuckles, until you lift the liver aside and there it is beneath: a green bag the size of your fist, whole and glistening.

You draw out Mother Sallow's waxed twine and tie off the duct, two tight turns and a knot.

Then your blade slips on the slick flesh. The point nicks the sac. It bursts in your hands.

Bile sprays your wrists and forearms, green and hot, and the skin there lights up like you've plunged your arms into nettles. You hiss through your teeth and shake them.

The jar stays empty.

In the gut beside where the sac lay, something catches the light. You pull it out: a brass-buckled belt, slimed and stained, with a bowman's knife still in its sheath.

Jory has come up beside you. He goes quiet, staring at the belt in your hand.

"That's Dickon's."

For a moment he only looks at it. Then he digs in his coat and presses a gold coin into your stinging palm and folds your fingers over it.

"From both crews, like I said. You earned it."

A stout, grey man in a flour-dusted apron has come down from the mill. He names himself Tam Hobb and eyes the head on the stone, then you.

"Eighty pound if it's an ounce. A long haul to town." He jerks a thumb back at the mill. "Take my mill barrow. Send it back on any barge coming down; I mean to grind by afternoon."
