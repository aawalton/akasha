You kneel by the first slide and bind earth and water again, trying to find more lurkers the way you found the first. The heavy, wet pull settles behind your breastbone.

This time you send the ripple out gently, a shiver rather than a push.

It comes back, and this time you can read it.

The empty den under you feels hollow. The channel is water, weed and small quick lives.

Forty yards up, in the bank under the second slide, there's a warm, heavy weight.

It has the same feel as the lurker you killed: long, dense, alive. It's lying still.

As the ripple touches it, the weight goes rigid and stays that way. It's awake, and it has frozen.

Past fifty yards the ripple blurs out into wet ground, all of it one smear you can't tell apart.
