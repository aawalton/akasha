You set out to find the third hole, spiralling your search outward. Earth and water bind into the heavy, wet pull, and you send a gentle ripple out from where you stand.

Past fifty yards it blurs, as before. Inside that, it finds no warm, heavy weight.

You walk on up the channel, fifteen yards, and send another, sweeping wider.

This time, near the edge of its reach, it finds something.

Under a big alder on the bank, a hundred yards up from the first slide, lies a warm, heavy weight.

It's longer and denser than the other two. It lies loose and slow. It's asleep.

Around the den the wet earth is laced with something hard and knotted, like a net. Roots.

The alder's roots wrap the den on every side. The only open way is the tunnel down to the water.
