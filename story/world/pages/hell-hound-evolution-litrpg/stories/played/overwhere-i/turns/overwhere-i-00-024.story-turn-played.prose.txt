You pick up the tusks, one in each hand, and set off north through the alder carr.

The carr thins to the oat strips, rooted and torn where the sounder fed at dawn. The fields are empty. Far off, smoke rises from the charcoal mounds, and the reed beds are busy.

Some twenty minutes on you reach the south palisade, muddy and bloodied to the elbows.

The green is quiet this morning, with most of the village out at work. In the Stag, Garrick is sweeping, and Agathe Morrow sits at the long table with her tallies.

Garrick stops sweeping. He stares at the tusks, and at the state of you.

You lay the tusks on the table in front of the reeve.

Agathe turns one over in her hands, then looks up at you. "The old boar," she says. "The one rooting our oats."

She unlocks the chest and counts five silver coins out onto the table.

"The hide and meat are yours by rights," she says. "I'll send a cart for the carcass. If you'll sell the meat, I'll buy it for the village at two silver."
