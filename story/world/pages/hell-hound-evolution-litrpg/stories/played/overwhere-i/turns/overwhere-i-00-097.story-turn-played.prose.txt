You thank the sergeant and walk back to Osric's cart, where the tarred cask holding Ghost-Eye's head sits wedged among his bales. It's a solid thing, salt packed round the head to the lid, and it would fold you in half if you tried it with your arms alone.

So you don't. You reach for the pressure behind your breastbone and bind two threads of it together, fire and earth, fire for heat and earth for weight to stand under, and let the working sink into you.

Heat runs down your arms like fever sweat, prickling in your palms, never burning. You hook your fingers under the cask's rim, lift, and swing it up onto your shoulder in one motion. It settles there like a sack of washing.

You take the dead man's sack in your other hand, the salt inside shifting, and set off up the high street under the weight of both.

Wendlow crowds in on either side, timber and plaster leaning over the street, shop boards swinging, a smell of bread and dung and river. Heads turn as you pass. A woman stops mid-haggle with a basket of eggs. A carter reins in to stare. A petite redhead with a cask on her shoulder and a bulging sack swinging at her knee is plainly the best thing anyone has seen today, and by the second corner a tail of boys has fallen in behind you, jostling and whispering, keeping a careful three paces back.

Ten minutes up the street, on the left, a sign hangs out over the door of a long timbered hall: a great spread of stag's antlers, bleached white.

You shoulder the door open.

Inside it's dim and smells of woodsmoke and old leather. Antlers crowd every wall, dozens of racks, and between them bounty slips are pinned up three and four deep, yellowed and curling, some so old the ink has gone brown. A long counter runs across the back of the room.

By the hearth two hunters are dicing on an upturned barrel; the rattle stops as you come in. Behind the counter stands a lean grey woman, sleeves pushed up over forearms ridged with old scars, her hands seamed and nicked like a butcher's block.

Her eyes go to the cask on your shoulder and stay there.

"That's a Drakewolf cask or I'm a goose." She raps the counter once with a scarred knuckle. "Set it down, and tell me whose head."
