"I'll take all three," you say, and count thirteen copper into her broad palm, one at a time, while she watches each coin land.

Supper is mutton stew at the long table in the common room, thick with barley and pepper, a heel of brown bread to wipe the bowl. Osric eats like a man who expected to be dead yesterday. Tobin falls asleep over his. The drover has the hearth end of the bench and the floor with it; the others call him Hob, and Hob Tanner holds forth to anyone who'll listen and a few who won't.

"Factor Ivo Tessaly sold me duds!" He thumps the table, and the bowls jump. "Dark crystals, empty as a drum, sold to me as full!"

The tub takes a quarter hour of kettles, the innkeeper's girl trotting back and forth with them, never quite looking at you. Then the door shuts, and you sink into hot water up to your chin with a cake of lye soap, and the road and the quarry and the fen come off you in grey layers. You stay in until the water goes lukewarm.

Upstairs, your room is narrow, a straw tick on a frame and a door with a bar. You drop the bar into its brackets, set the sack where your hand can find it, and sleep. The night passes quiet.

You wake whole. You roll out your stiff hip, flex the thigh, prod your ribs: the last bolt wounds have closed in the night to tender pink seams, sore when you press them, and nothing worse.

At six the cart rolls out of the inn yard. You settle on the tailboard beside Tobin and take up the push again, a steady breath of air at the cart's back, easy on the mule. It steps out like it knows the road ends today.

Farmland slides past, hedge and stubble and the odd farmhouse with its smoke going straight up, all of it quiet. A little after half past eleven the road rounds a last hedge, and there is Wendlow: a long grey stone wall standing by a broad brown river, roofs crowding up behind it, and a gate standing open in it.

The gate watchman works down the line, taking a copper a head and asking each stranger's business.

Then he stops in front of you. His eyes go from your face to the bulging sack on your shoulder, to the fine crossbow slung beside it, and back.

"And yours, woman?"
