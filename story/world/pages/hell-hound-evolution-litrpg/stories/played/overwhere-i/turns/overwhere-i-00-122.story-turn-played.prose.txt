"Fine. A bit boring, but should be fast."

You tuck the papers into your pack and walk out of the Moot Hall, down the broad steps into the noon light.

The square is busy. You stop first at a carter's wagon, where a heavy man is roping down sacks, and then at a wine factor's stall under a striped awning, and ask each of them after what's left of the quarry crew.

The carter shrugs without stopping his knots. "Came up the south road. Haven't heard a thing from the east."

The wine factor leans on his barrel. "Only what the taproom's saying. Some woman hunter took the quarry captain's head off and carried it in." His eyes drop to the sword at your hip and stay there a moment.

On the way down to the west gate you ask two more traders, a pedlar folding up his tray and a woman selling eggs from a cart. Both shake their heads.

At the gate the watchman sees your antler badge and waves you out with a nod.

The road runs west along the Wend's bank under a high afternoon sun, farmland on either side, the river slow and brown on your right.

Three miles out, a wool cart comes the other way behind a grey ox, piled high with bales, its carter walking at the ox's head. He's a lean man, burned red across the nose and neck, and when you hail him he stops gladly enough.

"Jory Webb," he says. "Bound for Wendlow's market."

You ask him about trouble on the road. He spits into the dust.

"Paid a silver toll on day seven, near Fenwatch. One man alone, with a crossbow. Stepped out of the trees and held it on me till I paid." He rubs his jaw. "Talked like a northerner, that burr they have. Hard-faced. Didn't give a name."

He looks back west, the way he came.

"Thought the quarry lot were finished. Seems one of them's set up on his own."
