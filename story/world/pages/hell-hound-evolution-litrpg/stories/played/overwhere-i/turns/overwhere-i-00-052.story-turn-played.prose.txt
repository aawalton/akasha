You rise from behind the last hummock and wade out into the open marsh toward the channel, meaning to start firing the moment a wolf comes within a hundred yards.

You've gone twenty yards when the reeds burst apart, north of you and south-east behind you.

The two biggest wolves come at you from both sides, scales flashing, a hundred yards out.

You turn, weave a slug and loose at the north wolf. It takes the slug in the chest and reels.

You swing to the one behind you. A slug smashes into its shoulder, and it runs on, limping.

Back to the north wolf. The next slug takes it through the neck, and it drops in the sedge.

The south wolf is sixty yards off. Your slug takes it square in the chest, and it goes down.

You stand in the open marsh, the two biggest dead on either side of you.

On the shore Ghost-Eye barks once. The last wolf with it turns and lopes west into the pines.

You wade on toward the island, pack open, a slug ready in your hand.

A hundred yards off the island, Ghost-Eye plunges into the channel and swims straight at you.

Your first slug takes it high in the back as it swims. It snarls and keeps coming.

The second only grazes its crest. The third tears into its shoulder, and blood darkens the water.

The fourth strikes it full in the chest. It lurches, and its swimming falters.

It's still coming. You loose a fifth, and the slug takes it between the eyes.

Ghost-Eye goes limp in the middle of the channel, and the water around it stills.
