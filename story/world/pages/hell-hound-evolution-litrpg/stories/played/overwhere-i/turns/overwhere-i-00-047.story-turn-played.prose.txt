You slip back down behind the dead alders, out of the watcher's line.

You scoop black fen muck and smear it over your arms, neck, face and clothes, to mask your scent. It stinks of rot and peat, and dries tight on your skin.

You circle south to the head of the reed-hummock line and start along it, bent low, to get as close as you can unseen. Hummock to hummock you creep, knee-deep, easing each foot out of the mud.

Twenty minutes on, some 350 yards off the island, you edge round a thick reed hummock.

A grey heron bursts up out of the reeds at your feet with a clatter of wings and a harsh croak.

On the island's north-east point the watcher's head snaps round toward the sound. It gives one sharp bark, and the whole pack is on its feet, facing you.

Ghost-Eye stays on the shore with the two biggest wolves, between you and the pups.

Six wolves plunge into the channel and swim it, then come up onto the marsh. They fan out into a crescent as they run, splashing through the knee-deep water toward you.

The crescent's ends swing wide, as if to herd you toward deep water.

At this pace they'll be on you in about a minute and a half.
