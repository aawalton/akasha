You stay flat on the road, low as you can make yourself, and spin up your slugs: pointed, grooved, turning to a drill's whine.

The crew are running for the quarry pit, sixty yards off the road. The nearest runners are fifteen yards out, the farthest thirty.

You start with the crossbows. Your first slug takes the first crossbowman between the shoulders. He staggers, and runs on.

A bolt hisses out of the trees from the shooter you still can't see, slips past your air ward and bites into your shoulder.

Your next slug clips the second crossbowman. He lurches, but keeps going.

Voss and both crossbowmen drop over the lip into the pit.

The three burned blademen stagger after them at half pace.

You drill one between the shoulder blades. He pitches forward onto his face, then crawls on.

Another bolt hisses out of the trees. Your ward flicks it away.

Your next slug drops a second blademan to his knees. He drags himself up and on.

Your last slug cracks off the stone of the lip as the blademen tumble over it.

The crew are in the pit now, behind stone and out of your line.

Down the road behind you, the mule, the cart and the cask have gone out of sight.
