You bind air and earth into the stride and pour off the knoll toward the cart track.

The ground kicks back under every step. You hit the track and run it north-east at a gallop's pace.

Your hurt thigh rides easy. The stride carries your weight, and the miles fall away behind you.

As the low hills draw near and the pines close in on either side, you raise your air ward around you.

The track bends left through thick pines. As you round it, a crossbow stock shows among the trunks.

The first bolt comes, and your ward slaps it aside into the trees.

A heartbeat later a second comes from further along. Your ward reaches for it too late.

It tears a hot line across your ribs and skips away down the track.

Two men crouch in the pines twenty-five yards off the track: the crossbowman who looked back at your knoll, and another in a helm.

Still striding, ward still up, you loose a fire beam at the first.

It takes him in the shoulder, through his leather. He reels back against a trunk, smoking.

Both men work their crossbow cranks, winding for the next volley.
