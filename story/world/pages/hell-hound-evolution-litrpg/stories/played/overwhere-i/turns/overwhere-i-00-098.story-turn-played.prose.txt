You swing the cask down onto the boards with a thump that rattles the dice on the barrel, and let the working go. The heat drains out of your arms and leaves them trembling a little. You hitch a stool up to the counter and sit.

"Grete Holm," the grey woman says. "I run the Board. Whose head?"

"Heads," you say, and tell her.

The Greyfen first: the Drakewolf pack on the pine island, the long wait in the marsh, the two biggest coming at you out of the reeds, and the alpha with the milky eye swimming the channel to finish you, and not finishing you. Then the east road. The toll at Cutter's Quarry, the fight in the pit and among the pines, and how it ended: Harl Voss and every man who held the quarry with him dead.

Partway through, her eyes go still on you. It's a small thing, a settling, the way a hand goes still on a bowstring, and it lasts a moment. When it lifts, her brows have drawn down.

You lay out the proof. The cask on the floor beside you. From the sack, packed in grey salt, the head of Harl Voss, which you set on the counter facing her. Then the ears, eight of them, every one a right ear, in a row along the wood. Then the tags: nine little tin discs on their cords, laid out beneath the ears like coins for a toll.

At the hearth the dice stop. The two hunters have turned on their stools and are staring at the face on the counter, and the hall goes so quiet you can hear the fire.

Grete draws a fat ledger out from under the counter and opens it to a page of names in a cramped hand. She picks up the tags one by one, turns each to the light, reads the name scratched into the tin, and runs a scarred finger down the page until she finds it. She nods. The next. She nods again. Nine times.

Then she comes round the end of the counter, works the lid off the cask with her thumbs, and looks in at Ghost-Eye. She grunts at the size of the skull, at the pale-scarred crest, at the empty left socket where the milky eye used to be.

She puts the lid back. She goes behind the counter again and looks at you, slow and careful, her scarred hands flat on the wood on either side of the dead man's head.

"Your level says ten. Harl Voss was twenty-four. Who else was in it with you?"
