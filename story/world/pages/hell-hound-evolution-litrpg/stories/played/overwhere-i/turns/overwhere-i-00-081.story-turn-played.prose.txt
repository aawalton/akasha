You let the stride go and plant your feet on the track, the air ward still humming around you.

You loose a beam at the wounded man's head as he cranks. It sears along his neck. He snarls, but stays up.

He swings his crossbow up and looses. Your ward knocks the bolt spinning into the needles.

Your second beam takes him full in the head. He drops among the roots, dead.

The helmed crossbowman throws down his crossbow and bolts up the track toward the camp.

You beam after him as he runs. It catches him low across the back of the neck.

He pitches forward onto the track ten yards on and lies still, smoke rising off his collar.

The bend falls quiet. Only the jays are screaming now, up the track toward the hills.

You turn north-east. The pull behind your breastbone has gone thin, near the bottom of your well.

Up the track, toward the smoke in the hill fold, the other two are still ahead.
