You nod to yourself. "Okay, ten percent every ten minutes, or just under two hours to full. Not too bad. I have water; let's see what we can do about shelter."

You pick a flat patch of bank above the waterline and sit down in the middle of it. You reach for earth, and you will a small, solid dome of stone around you: no way in or out, only small gaps for air.

The pull answers slow and heavy. Stones rise from the streambed, about a barrow-load at a time, and come up the bank to you.

The first ring settles round you, flat stones laid on flat stones. You let that pull go, and the ring stays, resting on its own weight.

A second draw. A third. The wall climbs in rings as you sit, leaning inward, up to your shoulders. You leave gaps between some of the stones, and pale red light and air come through them in thin slits.

Mana is running down fast. Once it jumps up by thirteen on its own, then goes on falling.

With the fourth draw you try to close the top: a last ring leaning in, the stones meeting overhead.

They won't sit. The inward stones slide, and you have to hold them up by the pull alone. Above your head the whole top grinds against itself.

The pull slips.

The roof and half the upper wall come down all at once. You throw yourself flat and sideways, and stones crash in around you and out across the bank. The noise is huge, a rolling, clattering roar thrown back off the water and the far rock. Stones bounce down into the shallows. Dust and grit drift over you.

You're unhurt. You lie in a broken ring of stone with grit on your knees and palms and your ears ringing. The lowest ring still stands, knee high and gapped, around a heap of fallen stone.

Forty spent, on a shelter that didn't stand.

A while later, from the far side of the ford, comes the scrape of boots on the ridge track.

Two people come down out of the pines above the ford and stop at the edge of the water. A lean, weathered woman in patched green leathers leads, a longbow in her hand and a spear across her back. Her black braid is streaked with grey, and the tip of her left ear is missing. A younger companion comes down behind her, and halts when she raises a hand.

The woman looks at the dead beast in the shallows for a long moment.

Then she looks up and across the water, straight at the ring of fallen stone, and at you in it.
