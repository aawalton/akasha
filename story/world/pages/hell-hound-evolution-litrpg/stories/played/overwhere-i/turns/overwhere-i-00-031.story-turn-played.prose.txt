You fix on the bubbles and reach for water, willing it to close round whatever lies below.

The channel answers. The water there thickens and tightens, and you feel a weight inside it.

The weight jerks, hard. Something long and strong twists against your hold.

You hold. The pull behind your breastbone steadies, cool and wet and sure.

You heave. The channel bulges, and a mass of brown water lifts clear of the surface. Inside it a reedlurker thrashes, long as a man, sleek and brown, its crocodile jaws snapping.

You swing it over the bank and let the water go.

It drops onto the mud three yards from the channel, and the water splashes away from it.

On land it's clumsy. It heaves itself round, webbed claws skidding, its small yellow eyes on you.

Jory yells behind you. The reedlurker gapes, hissing, and starts dragging itself toward the water.
