"He'll be safe enough as long as he's with me," you tell Bet. "If he runs off on his own, though, that's on him."

Bet nods, tight-lipped. "He never ran from anything but chores."

Tobin goes red to the ears.

You pay Garrick two silver for bed and supper, and two copper more for a hot bath. In the back room you sink into the wooden tub and soak until the fen is out of your skin, then sleep. The night at Fenwatch passes quiet.

At five, Osric's mule cart stands before the Stag in the grey light, the salted head stowed aboard in a tarred cask.

At the gate Rowan waits with Sedge to see you off. Sedge pads up, stretches its lizard muzzle out, and for once sniffs your hand.

Hessa hands Tobin a full quiver and gives him one curt nod.

The cart rolls out on time. Beyond the village the track becomes the east road, and the road climbs out of the fen country into pine forest, quiet mile after mile.

About three in the afternoon, some seventeen miles out, the road bends past an old stone pit. Cutter's Quarry.

A big man bars the road with five others at his back: grey-bearded, scar-lipped, a long-hafted axe in one hand and a round shield on his arm. A Drakewolf in a spiked collar sits leashed at his side.

"Voss," Osric calls, easy as a neighbour, and reins in. He pays the silver toll without fuss, as if he has done it a hundred times.

Voss's eyes slide past him to you. He looks you over and laughs. "This the best guard you could hire, Osric?"

Then his eye falls on the tarred cask. "And what's in that?"
