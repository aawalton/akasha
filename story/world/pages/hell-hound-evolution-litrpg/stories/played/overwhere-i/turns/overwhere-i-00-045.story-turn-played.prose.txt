You drop the working. The two lenses fall back into water and spill away between the alder roots.

You settle low against a grey trunk and keep still, watching for any wolf that comes your way, the west wind cool on your face.

Bare-eyed, the island's east edge is a dark line of pines, the pack grey specks in its shade. You can tell when a speck moves, but you can't tell which wolf it is.

Midday heat hums over the fen. Frogs creak in the reeds below the rise.

Your mana fills back in slow tides, ten minutes at a time.

The specks stay in the shade through the hot hour.

By half past twelve you're full, and your legs are rested from the morning's wading.

The watching speck stands, and another rises out of the pack to take its place.
