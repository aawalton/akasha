You walk fifteen yards up the bank, past a white stake, until the second slide is within reach.

Earth and water bind into the heavy, wet pull, and you send the grip down into the bank, toward the warm, frozen weight.

The mud closes round it. The weight jerks and thrashes, but the grip holds, heavy and slow.

You haul. The bank heaves and splits, and the reedlurker comes up out of the mud, twisting. The grip carries it up and drops it on the bank, well back from the water.

It rolls, hissing, jaws wide, and scrabbles for the channel.

You let the grip go, bind fire and wind, and throw the blast.

It takes the reedlurker full on. It shrieks once in a burst of steam and scorched fur.

When the steam clears, it lies still on the bank.
