"Great!" You scoop the pearl back into your pack and fish out the little wrapped bundle beside it: Ghost-Eye's ears, both of them, stiff and grey-green. You set them on the counter. "These go with the head."

Grete sweeps them up and sets them beside the skull behind her. "They ride with the head. Its bounty's paid already."

You reach for the sack again, for the ears and the tags, and Grete tips her chin at the iron-bound chest at the end of the counter.

"Those went in with the payout. They're the Board's now."

Next, from the bottom of your pack, Ewan Dell's little tin token, from Sallow Hythe. You set it on the counter.

Grete turns it over with a fingertip, and her face softens a shade. "Marta Dell's. I know her. It goes west to her on the next cart."

Last, you lay the sealed letter beside it, out of the dead man's sack, its wax still whole.

Grete picks it up, looks at the seal, and breaks it with her thumb. She unfolds the paper and reads. Partway down the page she goes still, the same stillness as before, but colder.

"That's the guild counting-house seal," she says, low. "Only the factor uses it."

She folds the letter once along its crease and slides it into the pocket of her vest.

"Don't speak of this to anyone. It goes to the magistrate." Her eyes meet yours. "I'll leave it unpaid. It may be worth more to you that way."

You leave Antler Hall with the badge in your pocket and the pearl in your pack and walk a quarter hour through Wendlow, down out of the high street and along lanes that narrow and twist, until you find Glass Street and, halfway along it, a door painted bright blue.

Inside is a narrow room lined with glass cases. Coils of fine wire hang from pegs, trays of rings glint under the cases' lids, and somewhere among the shelves a few stones hum, a sound you feel in your back teeth more than hear.

Behind the counter is Ilse Varrow: small and sharp, about fifty, ink on her fingers, a pair of spectacles hanging on a cord at her chest.

You set the drake-pearl on the counter. Ilse lifts her spectacles to her nose and bends over it, close enough that her breath clouds the crystal.

"A ring," she says, straightening. "Three gold, three days, reach to fifty yards. A rod: four gold, four days, sixty yards. A rod fills one hand while you hold it. The pearl treats every element alike, lengthens all."

She taps the counter beside it.

"Half before I start. Once it's set, it belongs to the focus for good, past drawing on or selling."

Ilse holds out her palm for the pearl.

"Ring or rod?"
