"Sorry about that," you say. "You can call it even against the reedlurker."

Jory looks at the dead lurker on the bank, and the red goes out of his face.

"Even? One of those dead is worth a sight more to me than a basket of wicker." He waves a hand. "Keep the skin, and the reeve's coin. It's them I want gone, not paying."

"Any other traps I should avoid pulling before I try again?"

He points along the bank. "Eight traps on sixty yards of this channel. Three of them still whole. Each one's tied to a peeled willow stake on the bank. White as bone, you'll not miss them."

You look along the bank. Pale stakes stand here and there in the mud, and the second slide lies forty yards up.

"You pull anything by a white stake," Jory says, "it's eels, not lurker."
