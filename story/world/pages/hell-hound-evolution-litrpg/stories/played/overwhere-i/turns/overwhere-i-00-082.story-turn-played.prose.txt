The pressure behind your breastbone deepens, as it has at every level before.

You take the short sword from the helmed crossbowman's belt.

You drive it through his neck, then through the other man's among the roots. Both are dead already.

Sword in hand, you slip back off the track into the pines and turn north.

You circle wide through the trees, slow and soft-footed. The woods stay quiet behind you.

Forty minutes on, the ground rises, and you come to the rim of a hill fold from the north-west.

Thirty yards below lie a turf hut, a smoky fire and four charcoal mounds facing the track.

Three men crouch behind the mounds with crossbows, watching the cart track.

By the hut door Voss packs a sack, glancing again and again at the track.

You lie flat in the pines on the rim as Voss knots the sack shut and looks up the track again.
