From the rim you draw a thread of fire tight, thin as a needle, and loose it as a beam at the sitting blademan's head.

It burns straight through. He slumps back against the wall, dead, and his sword slides off his knees and rings on the stone.

The man beside him, half-senseless, only stirs. Your second beam takes him in the head, and he goes still.

The gallery falls silent but for the tick of cooling stone.

You turn back to the top of the goat path and set off north-east after the four men's boot prints.

The trail runs hurried through the pines, plain enough, and you follow it slow and quiet, favouring your hurt leg.

A quarter mile on, the ground turns to bare rock and deep beds of needles between the trunks.

You follow scuffs across the rock for long minutes, until they meet a game trail.

There you crouch and look closer. The marks you've been following are slots, small and split. Deer. The boot prints are gone.

It's near twenty to four. Somewhere ahead are the four men, the shooter from the trees among them, and you've lost their trail.
