"Nala."

You stand up out of the broken stone ring and wander down to the ford. The crossing stones are cold and slick under your bare feet, and the water barely wets your ankles.

At the carcass you ask, "Mind if I watch? I've never seen a beast skinned and quartered before."

Hessa glances up at your bare feet, then back to the work. "Watch, then. Stand off the blood side."

She has the beast rolled on its back. She slits the hide from belly to throat in one long pull.

"Down the belly first. Then each leg, inside, down to the paw." She cuts as she says it.

She works the hide back from the flesh with short strokes, peeling it as she goes. The fur is matted and stiff. The skin under it is thick and pale, threaded with fat.

At the chest she stops and holds the pelt open round the burned hole. Its edges are crisp and black.

"That spoils it. Pelt's three silver now, not four." She says it flat, the way she'd say the time.

She guts it neatly and lets the guts slide off into the current. "For the eels."

The heart comes out small and hard as a fist. She sets it on a flat stone.

She starts on the first foreleg, jointing it at the shoulder, naming the cut as the knife goes in.

Without looking up from the joint, Hessa asks, "Where are you from, Nala? Not the march."
