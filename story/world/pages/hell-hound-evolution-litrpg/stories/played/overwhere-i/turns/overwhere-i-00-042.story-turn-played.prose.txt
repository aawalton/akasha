Your mana is full. You walk out of Fenwatch past the last fields, to where firm heath meets the edge of the marsh, and start with movement: pairs of elements, to see which carries you fastest.

You will air and earth together. The ground kicks back under each stride, and you cover the heath at a horse's gallop.

On the marsh the same weave sinks into the soft ground and slows you to a fast run.

You will air and water. You skim over the wet marsh like a skater, gallop-fast again.

On the dry heath the skim finds nothing to ride and doesn't speed you at all.

You try fire and air at your back. It pushes you no faster than plain wind, and scorches the heather behind you.

Your plain Surge wind still wins a short dash. Each weave, though, lasts its full minute.

Next, range. You point at a lone stone, then at marks further out, and loose a fire beam at each in turn.

The beam holds whole to about thirty yards, then spreads and cools, gone by fifty.

You weave air and earth into a rock bullet. It cracks away faster than any sling stone. It flies about a hundred and fifty yards, and lands true on your marks out to about sixty.

Last, you weave fire and air and push it out wide. Flame fans in a half-circle ten yards deep. The heather in the fan blackens and smokes, each tuft scorched about half as badly as a full blast would scorch it.
