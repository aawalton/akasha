You draw a slug from your pack and rise to one knee behind the last hummock.

You weave air and earth around it, then pour a second weave into the same slug, giving it half again its force, all the power you can put behind it. The weight of it drags hard on your mana.

You sight on the big wolf on the shore, to see whether it will come or run. About two hundred and ten yards. You lift your aim for the drop and loose.

The slug cracks away spinning, and you know at once the arc is off.

It falls spent and short, and slaps into the channel a few yards from the shore.

Ghost-Eye's head snaps round to the splash, then to you, its crest rising. It plants its forefeet at the water's edge and snarls across the marsh at you.

The three wolves with it close up at its sides, snarling too, and hold the shore.

Behind them, the last of the pups vanishes west among the pines.
