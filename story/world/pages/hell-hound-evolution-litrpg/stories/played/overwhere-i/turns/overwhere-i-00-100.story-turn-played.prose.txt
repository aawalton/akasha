"I'm Nala," you say. "Nala Arthur."

Grete writes it on the receipt in a square, careful hand, Nala Arthur, the letters upright as fence posts, and signs her own name beneath with a flourish that looks well worn.

Then she reaches down into the strongbox and the gold comes up.

She counts it onto the counter in stacks of ten, each coin set down with a small flat click, the stacks standing in a row beside the ears like a little yellow wall. One stack. Two. Three. She keeps going until there are six, and then she tips the box toward you so you can see the bare iron bottom.

"That empties the box. Voss, eight of his men and Ghost-Eye make seventy-one. Board owes you eleven."

She pulls a slip of paper from under the ledger, writes on it, eleven gold to Nala Arthur, signs it, and presses a blotter over the ink.

"Good in two days. The tax rider brings the magistrate's purse on the ninth; bring this back then."

She slides the chit across beside the gold. Then she reaches over and lifts both heads, the one from the salt by its hair and the cask by its rim, grunting under the cask.

"These hang in the hall." She tips her chin at the walls, the antlers crowding them. "Ears and tags go in the Board's chest."

By the hearth the two hunters sit with their dice forgotten in their fists, staring at the gold on the counter.

Grete sets the heads down behind her and comes back to lean on the counter, forearms crossed, scars and all.

"There's a hunters' roll, free to join. Rolled hunters get first call. First call on Board contracts. You'd answer to the Board for your kills, and bring word of any monster you see."

She taps the ledger with one scarred finger.

"Want your name on it, Nala Arthur?"
