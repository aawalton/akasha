"Sure, that sounds fine," you say. "What's on the East road that needs killing again? So I'm not going in blind."

"Swear first. Then you'll have all I know of it." Varne nods to the open book.

You lay your hand flat on the page. The vellum is cool and dry under your palm, older names faded brown down its margin.

The clerk comes round to stand at your shoulder and speaks the oath a line at a time, in a dry, careful voice. You say each line after him.

"To keep the margrave's peace."

"To keep the margrave's peace."

"To answer the bench's call against outlaws and beasts."

"To answer the bench's call against outlaws and beasts."

"To speak true."

"To speak true."

He takes the book back to his desk, dips his pen, and writes your name at the foot of a long column of others. Then he signs beside it as witness and sands the line.

Varne holds the stick of red wax to a candle until it drips, lets a coin of it pool at the foot of your sheet, and presses her brass seal down into it. She lifts the seal away clean.

She folds the sheet once and holds it out across the bench.

"Nala Arthur, sworn hunter of Wendlow."

You take it. One folded sheet, light in your hand, the red wax still warm under your thumb.

"Now, the east road." Varne laces her inked fingers. "My first call: rid it of what's left of Harl Voss's crew. The Board proved Voss and eight of his men, your work. The crew was reckoned at a dozen-odd. Who is left of them, and where, is the first thing I want you to find out."

She taps the bench once.

"Two gold a man at the Board's rate, the same as before: his right ear with its tag, or him alive. Take what time you need, but I want word before the next tax rider, ten days from now."
