You pick your spot from the landing: the mill's tail-race wall, a chest-high run of mossy stone at the weir's east end, twenty yards from the eastern third of the apron where the slick of crushed weed is broadest. Close enough to hit anything. Low enough to hide behind.

You leave the towpath and creep toward it along the bank, slow, heel and toe, keeping the mill between you and the pool. Ten yards out, a loose stone turns under your boot and drops against another with a dull thud.

You freeze. Out on the pool a single ring spreads across the dark water, wide and slow, and smooths away.

You cover the last stretch and lie down flat behind the wall, cheek to the cool moss, and wait.

The sun climbs. Eleven comes and goes. Heat beats down on the weir stone and shimmers off the bare apron, and the water sheets white over its face, and the pool turns its slow eddies.

Near half past eleven the water by the weir humps up and parts, slow and careful.

A head breaks the surface first, long and flat and grey-green, and then the rest of it comes, hauling up out of the pool onto the apron in long muscular heaves. It's huge: twenty-five feet if it's an inch, thick as a man's waist, its scales dull and wet and plated like old armour. It drags itself onto the sun-warmed stone and lies belly-down along the slick, its head resting flat on the rock, the scaled crown turned up to the sky.

One eye stays open, fixed on the bank.

You let it settle. You let it lie there a long minute, and another, until the great body slackens on the warm stone.

Then you reach behind your breastbone, past the familiar warmth of fire and the cool of wind and the weight of earth, for the element you've barely touched. Lightning answers like a held breath, sharp and bright and crackling in your fingertips, every hair on your arms standing up. You call it twice, two points in the clear air above that flat skull, and bring them down together.

Both bolts crack down onto the Wyrm's head at once. The thunder slams off the mill wall like a hammer on a bell, so loud you feel it in your teeth, and the whole length of the beast jerks and goes rigid, twitching on the stone, its tail drumming once against the apron.

Over the lip of the wall, you fix your eyes on its crown and let the fire come up through them.

Twin beams lance from your eyes across the twenty yards and burn into the scaled crown, and the scales give. They blacken, crack, and split, and the beams bite through into what's beneath. Smoke boils up off the apron, thick and greasy, and the stink of scorched flesh rolls over you. The great head sags against the stone, burned and torn open.

The beams die. White afterglow swims across your sight in two blinding smears, and the weir dissolves into glare.

Through it, a long blur heaves on the stone and drags itself toward the water.
