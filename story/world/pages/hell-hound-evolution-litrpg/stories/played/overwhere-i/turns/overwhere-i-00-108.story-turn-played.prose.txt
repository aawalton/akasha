Breakfast is a bowl of porridge with a spoon of honey melting into it and a mug of small beer, in the taproom by the window. You pay Nell two copper on your way out.

Then the river. The towpath runs flat down the Wend's east bank, packed hard by hooves and boots, under a long line of willows trailing their fingers in the current. You walk it for two hours. The morning comes up clear and warm, a light west wind blowing in your face off the water, smelling of mud and green things.

At a bend the willows thin, and the whole of it opens out below you, two hundred yards off: the weir, the pool, the far bank.

Hobb's weir is a slanting stone apron across the river, sixty yards from bank to bank, the water sheeting white down its face. The mill squats at its east end, stone below and timber above, its great wheel standing still. Above the weir, at Hobb's landing, two barges lie moored and idle, their crews sitting on the decks and keeping well away from the rails.

You draw two lenses of water out of the air and set them in line on a thread of wind, one before the other, and put your eye to the near one. The far bank leaps toward you.

Below the weir lies the plunge pool, deep and slow and dark, some fifty yards across, its surface turning in lazy eddies. Against its far west bank a barge lies half-sunk, canted over, its bow stove in like a kicked crate.

A broad slick runs up the weir apron from the pool, a smear of crushed weed and mud, wide as a cart, as if something heavy hauls itself out there and slides back. On the apron's dry top lie fish bones, bleached white, a snapped oar, and a single torn boot.

You swing the lenses west. In the alders on the bank, hung over the branches like washing, is a long shed skin, grey-green and ragged, lifting a little in the wind. You follow it from end to end, and keep going, and it keeps going.

You sweep the pool for the Wyrm itself. Around the sunken barge, along its stern, up the west bank and down the east, back over the open water, again and again. The dark water gives you back eddies and sun-glare and your own patience, and nothing more. Whatever is down there is staying down.

You let the lenses go. They fall apart into a little patter of drops on the towpath.

Down at Hobb's landing, one of the bargemen is on his feet, a hand shading his eyes. He's spotted you. He waves his arm over his head, broad and urgent, beckoning you down.
