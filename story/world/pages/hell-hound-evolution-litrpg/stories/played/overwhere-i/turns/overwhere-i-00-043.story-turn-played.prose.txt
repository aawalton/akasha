You stay on the scorched heath to work on the rock bullets, for speed and for aim. You set stones on tussocks at thirty, sixty and a hundred yards.

You will a stone from the earth sleek and pointed. It firms in your palm in a few seconds.

You will grooves down its length, spiralling, for rifling. The stone keeps that shape when you let go.

A minute of earth working leaves six grooved slugs in the heather at your feet.

You weave air and earth to fling the first, willing the air to spin round it as it leaves.

The first slugs wobble and fall short. The spin and the grooves fight each other.

You shape six more, resting between shots for your mana to come back. Shot by shot you bring the spin round to match the grooves.

By about the twelfth, the slug cracks away clean. It flies a third faster than a plain bullet, and strikes as hard.

The spun slugs land true on the hundred-yard stone, and carry on to about two hundred. The plain bullets you throw beside them wander off the far marks past sixty.
