"I'm a bounty hunter," you say, "here to turn in some bounties." You press a copper into his hand.

His brows climb. He looks at the sack again, longer this time, then turns his head and whistles. A boy perched on a barrel by the gatehouse door hops down.

"Fetch Sergeant Coyle. Tell him bounty business."

The boy is off up the gatehouse stair at a run.

Behind you Osric counts out three copper, one for himself and two for the cart's wheels, with the air of a man who has paid it a hundred times. Tobin digs his own copper out of his purse and hands it over himself, chin up, chest out, as if he'd just bought the town.

The watchman pockets the coins and nods at the fine crossbow on your shoulder. "That goes in unstrung, mind."

Boots on the stair. Sergeant Coyle comes stumping down from the gatehouse, a thickset man with a grey-shot beard and a belly that leads the way, wiping his fingers on his tunic as if you'd called him from his dinner.

"Bounties, is it?" He plants himself in front of you. "Let's see."

You swing the sack down, loosen the knot, and open its mouth. He bends and peers in at the salt, grey-white and caked, and at what's packed in it. Then he gives a long, low whistle and straightens up slowly.

"Harl Voss, by the Almighty." He looks at you differently now, up and down, the way a man reweighs a coin. "That's no small fish."

He jerks his thumb over his shoulder, past the gate, toward the town.

"Antler Hall. Up the high street on the left, sign of the stag's antlers. Ask for Grete Holm."
