Supper at the Bell and Barrel is eel stew, rich and peppery, with black bread to mop it and a mug of small beer. Bram takes eight copper for it and the bed together.

Nell Cooley sets down your bowl and eyes the ragged hem of your cloak where it trails off the bench.

"I stitch tears, two copper apiece," she says. "Say the word."

The taproom is loud tonight. The bargemen have the long table and their mugs up, toasting the river serpent's death over and over, each toast louder than the last. The river is open again.

Word has caught up with you. You can see it travel down the room: a nudge, a nod in your direction, a murmur. *That's her. The one who did for the quarry bandits, and the wyrm too.* Heads turn. A few stay turned, staring outright, and one old bargeman lifts his mug to you across the room.

You sleep behind a bolted door and wake rested at first light on day nine.

Porridge with honey and small beer, two copper, and then up the high street to Antler Hall.

Grete looks up from her ledger as you come in and waves you to a bench by the hearth.

"Rider's due mid-morning. Sit."

The fire ticks. Near ten, hooves clatter up the high street, a lot of them, and through the open door you catch the tax rider going by, four guards around him and a strongbox strapped behind his saddle.

By half past ten the Board's share sits in a canvas bag on Grete's counter. She counts out twenty-six gold in neat stacks and pushes them across to you.

"Both chits, paid." She tears the two slips across and drops the halves into a box under the counter.

Then she pins two fresh slips to the board beside the reeve's old one, smoothing each flat with her palm.

"Eighteen gold: a mantis-beast, Level 20, taking sheep and a shepherd on Coldbrook Downs. A day's walk north. Pays on its head or its forelimbs." She taps the second. "Twelve gold: Cal Ferrin, the Ferryman, Level 16, robbing folk at Lowcross ferry. Alive or dead. Half a day east. And the reeve's eight for the Greyfen crystals still stands."

From the canvas bag she draws a folded note, sealed with a blot of red wax. She breaks it, reads it, and her mouth tightens a fraction. She holds it out to you.

"Magistrate Varne asks Nala Arthur to the Moot Hall at noon." Her eyes meet yours. "Mind your tongue up there."
