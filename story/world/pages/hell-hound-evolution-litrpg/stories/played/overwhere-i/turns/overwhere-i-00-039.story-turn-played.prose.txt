You walk up the bank until the big alder is well within reach. Jory follows ten yards behind.

Water and fire bind into one working, and you will the heat down into the flooded tunnel beneath the roots, to boil the water there as hot as it will go.

The water answers. You feel it heat, then churn, then boil.

Within a minute a white plume of steam hisses up out of the air hole among the roots.

Under the bank something heavy thrashes, and the roots shudder.

The thrashing moves downward, toward the water, then jerks and stops halfway.

The plume thins. Beneath the alder, the bank is still.
