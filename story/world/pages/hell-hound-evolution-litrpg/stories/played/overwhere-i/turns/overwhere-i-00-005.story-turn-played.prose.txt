You push your hair out of your face. "Nice! Okay, time to try the ward."

You step to the water's edge and reach for the pressure again, and this time you focus on earth. You will the stones of the riverbed to rise and close round you as flowing stone armour.

The pressure answers slow and heavy, like a weight settling low in your belly.

The streambed stirs. Flat grey stones lift out of the water, shedding drops, and fly to you.

They meet your body with soft clicks and lock together, overlapping like the scales of a fish. Plates cover your shoulders, your chest, your back. Smaller ones run down your arms and legs. Over your bare feet the stones close into smooth, jointed boots.

You lift an arm. The plates slide over each other with it, flowing, and they don't pinch.

It weighs almost nothing. The weight seems to rest on the pressure behind your breastbone, not on you.

You rap a knuckle on your chest plate. It rings dull and solid, like a stone step.

Wet grey stone gleams all over you in the pale red light, beaded with river water. You walk a few steps, turn, crouch. The armour moves as if it were your skin.

After about a minute the pull behind your breastbone loosens.

The plates let go all at once. They slide off you and clatter down into a ring of wet stones round your feet.

Behind your breastbone there is a small heavy hollow where the earth came from, already filling in.
