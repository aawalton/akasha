"Let's do the ring," you say. "I like to keep my hands free."

You lean on the counter, eyes on the trays of rings under the glass.

"Anything we can do to make it stronger? I've gold to spare, say up to fifty gold. What options do you have for me?"

Ilse settles her spectacles on her nose and gives you a long look over the top of them, the look of a woman who has heard customers say *fifty gold* before and has learned to wait for the coins.

"Plain ring, I'll have it set by the afternoon of day ten." She raises one inky finger. "The honest part first. A focus buys reach, or holds mana. The force is all yours to bring. And reach counts for held workings. Loosed beams, slugs and bullets fly as far as they ever did."

She turns to a case behind her, unlocks it with a key from her apron, and lifts out a small velvet tray. On it sits a second pearl, clouded and milky, a little smaller than yours.

"Mine. Beside yours, two pearls reach seventy yards. Seven gold, setting included, and a day more on the ring."

She sets the pearl tray down and slides another along the counter beside it. In its hollow lies a dull grey stone, smooth as a river pebble.

"A well-stone. Twenty gold, three days more. Holds forty mana. Fill it from your own, ten a minute, easy. Draw on it, and the forty is back in half a minute."

She taps the counter between the two trays.

"Half up front covers the extras too; the rest when I hand the ring over. Band metal's all one to a focus. And I set at my own pace, whatever you pay."

Ilse folds her inky hands on the counter.

"So. Plain, second pearl, well-stone, or both?"
