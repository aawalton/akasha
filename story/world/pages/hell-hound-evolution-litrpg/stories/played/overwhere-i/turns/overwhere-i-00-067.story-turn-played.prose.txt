"Sure, I don't care who comes," you say. "Let's pack it now and we can go with Osric in the morning."

Hessa grunts. "Plain enough." She jerks her chin at Tobin. "You can go."

Tobin whoops loud enough to turn heads across the green and is off at a run, shouting for his mother.

Osric settles his hat back on. "My cart leaves the Stag at five tomorrow. A guard walks beside it or sits the tailboard, as suits her." His smile thins a little. "I'd not ask, but Harl Voss's men have robbed carts on the east road this summer."

You count four copper into Garrick's broad palm for a sack of salt.

In the Stag's back room the two of you pack Ghost-Eye's head in it, working the salt into every fold and hollow and round the empty socket. One sack does it.

It takes about an hour. A little after six the head lies salted and wrapped.

A woman comes in from the hall with a packed bag over her arm, reed-dust on her sleeves. "Bet Ashdown," she says. "I weave reed. Tobin's my boy." She sets the bag down by the door, and her hand stays on it a moment. "Will you bring him home whole?"

Garrick wipes his hands on his apron. "Bed and supper tonight is two silver," he says. "The free night was last night."
