Four hundred yards west, a granite knoll rises bare above the pines.

You limp to it and climb, your thigh aching with every step up the stone. You top it in a quarter of an hour.

The afternoon sun beats full on the granite. You draw water out of the air and set two lenses in line before your eye.

To the north-east an old cart track cuts through the pines toward a line of low hills.

Far along it, thin smoke rises from a fold in the hills where the track ends.

You sweep the track for long minutes, the lenses wavering in the heat. The track lies empty.

Then jays start scolding along it, two miles off, and you swing the lenses there.

Four men cross the track's one clearing in single file. The third limps badly.

The last man carries a crossbow. At the clearing's edge he stops.

He turns and looks back down the track, straight toward the knoll where you stand.
