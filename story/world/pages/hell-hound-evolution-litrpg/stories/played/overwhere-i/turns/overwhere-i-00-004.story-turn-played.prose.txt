You grin. "Oh, this is going to be fun! Let's try that burst of speed."

You turn to face down the open, stony bank. You reach for the pressure behind your breastbone and focus on wind. You will the air ahead to part and the air behind to pull you forward, and you start to run.

The pressure answers again, cooler this time. It rushes out through your whole body.

Wind opens in front of you like a door, and a push at your back throws you forward.

The bank streams past. Your bare feet touch the stones only every few long strides. The stream is a blur at your left, and fifty yards of shore go by in a few heartbeats.

You let go of the wind, and it lets go of you. You skid to a stop on the stones, balanced, your feet unhurt.

Your hair has blown across your face. Your heart is pounding, more from delight than effort.

Behind your breastbone there is a small cool hollow where the wind came from, already filling in.

You're breathing hard, but you aren't tired. Your legs feel light.

Back upstream, across the ford, the dead beast still smokes in the shallows. Over the far ridge the chimney smoke goes up thin into the pale red sky. The cart track runs west into the dark pines behind you, and east across the ford and up the ridge.
