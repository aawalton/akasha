"Only you and Grete as far as I'm aware," you say. "I figured with the seal it must be something someone cared about, but it's not something I can hunt, so I never bothered to look inside."

Varne's eyes rest on your face for a long moment. She's quiet and very still, as if she's listening for something under the words.

Then her shoulders ease a fraction, and she nods once, as if a sum has come out even.

She folds the sheet along its creases, turns in her chair, and lays it in a press behind the bench. The key grates in the lock. She turns back to you.

"Then it stays that way. You'll speak of this letter to no one. Grete knows to hold her tongue."

She lays her inked hands flat on the bench.

"A hunter who can keep quiet is worth something to me. And a stranger with no lord and no papers gets asked about in my town, sooner or later." She lets that sit a moment. "For your silence, I'll write you papers as a sworn hunter of Wendlow, under my seal. Papers like that answer any gate, any reeve, any watch that asks who you are."

She draws a clean sheet toward her and wets her pen. Below the bench, the clerk's scratching stops. He sits with his own pen lifted, listening.

"I'll need to write your home and your lord on them," Varne says. "Well, Nala Arthur?"
