You loose a beam down at the kneeling blademan. It sears his chest, and he falls back screaming.

Your second beam silences him. He lies still beside the mound.

You skirt the rim to the fold's east lip and pick up the trail Voss left into the pines.

Laden with the sack, he has left deep prints, plain in the needles, running east beside a stream.

You follow at a careful walk, stopping often to listen. Behind your breastbone your well slowly fills again.

For nearly two hours the trail runs on beside the stream, and the light goes long and gold between the trunks.

At dusk you stop in thick pines at a bend, where the prints climb the bank ahead.

Up the bank, a hundred yards on, a rock overhang sits above the stream, its mouth turned toward you.

In its shadow Voss sits with the sack at his side and his sword drawn, watching the trail.

He unslings his shield, props it upright before him, and settles the sword across his knees.
