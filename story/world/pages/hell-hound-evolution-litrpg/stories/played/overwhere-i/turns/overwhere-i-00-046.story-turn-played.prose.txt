You weave air and water and hold the two lenses in line again, low in the alder shade, to count the camp.

The new watcher sits on a fallen trunk at the island's north-east point, facing the fen. Its gaze sweeps across the dead alders and on. It keeps watching the fen.

You count along the shade: nine grown wolves, spread over about forty yards, a few yards in from the edge.

Ghost-Eye lies apart from the rest on a raised mound of roots, half again their size.

You rank the other eight by size, largest to smallest. Their levels stay hidden to a lens.

Some sixty yards back into the pines, three pups tumble over one another. All twelve are there.

You measure by eye. The marsh runs about 570 yards west from you, knee-deep water and sedge, open. A channel about thirty yards wide rings the island, dark and deep. The island itself is about two hundred yards across.

After a few minutes, with every wolf counted, you let the lenses go.

A line of reed hummocks runs south-west from the dead alders, and gives cover to within two hundred yards of the island.
