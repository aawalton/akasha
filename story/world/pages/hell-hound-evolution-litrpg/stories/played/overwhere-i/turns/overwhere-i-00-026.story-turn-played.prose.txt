You scrub your clothes in the bathwater and wring them out until the worst of the mud is gone.

You hold them up, dripping, and reach for water, wind and fire, willing all three into one working.

The three pulls tangle behind your breastbone. The working slips and falls apart.

You reach again, for only two this time, fire and wind, and will them into one.

They bind. A hot, dry wind pours from your hands, heavy with the smell of wet wool. You turn the clothes in it, and in about three minutes they're dry and warm.

You dress and go out into the hall, where Garrick is back at his sweeping. You ask him about the other silver bounty on the slate.

"Reedlurkers? That's Jory Weir's trouble." He points you toward the east gate.

Jory's smoking shed stands by the gate, thick with fish-smoke. Jory Weir is a gap-toothed old eel-man, and he's hanging the night's catch. It's only a few eels.

He talks freely once you name the bounty. "My traps are wicker, sunk in the channels at the fen's edge. Half an hour north of the ford. They come by night and tear them up. Half my traps this month. And last week one bit my grandson's leg."

He spits into the smoke. "Three of them, there are. You going after them?"
