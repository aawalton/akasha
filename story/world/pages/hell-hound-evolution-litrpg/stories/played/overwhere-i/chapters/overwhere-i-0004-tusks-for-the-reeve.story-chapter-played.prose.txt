You reach for fire, and this time you will it down into your arms, your back, your legs, instead of out of your hand.

Heat floods your muscles like fever sweat. It runs hot all through you, but it doesn't burn. Your body feels dense and strong, as if you could haul as much as two men.

You wade into the wallow, take the old boar by both tusks, and heave.

He comes, a little. His head lifts, and the great grey body slides a foot through the mud.

Then the wallow holds him. The mud sucks at his flank and belly, and at your calves.

You set your feet and heave again. Your feet skid on the slick bottom.

Each minute you renew the fire and feel its hot pull behind your breastbone.

On the third heave both tusks turn slick in your grip. Your hands slip free and you sit down hard.

The boar slumps back, rolls, and settles deeper, sunk in the wallow to his shoulders. His head lies half under the brown water now, the tusks you held sunk out of reach.

You let the fire go. The heat drains away and leaves your arms trembling and your clothes soaked through.

You sit waist-deep in the mud beside a carcass that weighs as much as four grown men. You check your mana. At the rate the fire-strength drains it, you have about eight minutes of it left.

You lift a thin disc of water out of the wallow and set it spinning fast, edge-on, above your palm.

You bring it down against the boar's neck behind the jaw, where the head lies half under the water.

The disc bites. Brown water and blood spray up as it grinds through bristle and hide. Twice it skips off a sunken tusk and throws muck in your face, and you have to set it again.

Each minute you renew it, and feel the cool, wet pull of it behind your breastbone.

In the third minute it grinds through the neck bone, and the head comes free.

You let the water fall. The disc splashes back into the wallow.

You hook your arms under the head and lift. It's as heavy as a grown man, and slick with mud. You stagger it up the bank and into the alders, hugging it to your chest.

Within fifty yards your arms burn and your legs shake. You drop it and drag it by a tusk instead.

Roots catch it. You heave it free, again and again, your feet sliding in black leaf mould.

Your arms cramp. Your grip gives, and you go down on your knees beside it, breathing hard.

You've come a few hundred yards at most. The oat strips are still out of sight beyond the carr.

You check your mana. It's lower again.

You sit back on your heels and call the water disc up again above your palm.

This time you will it thinner and faster, and faster still, until it's a hissing blur you can barely see.

You set it where the first tusk springs from the jaw. It sinks in almost without a sound. In moments the tusk drops into the leaf mould. You turn the head over and take the second.

Well inside a minute you let the water fall. The disc spatters across the leaves.

Both tusks lie in the leaf mould beside you, yellow and curved, each as long as your forearm.

You pick up the tusks, one in each hand, and set off north through the alder carr.

The carr thins to the oat strips, rooted and torn where the sounder fed at dawn. The fields are empty. Far off, smoke rises from the charcoal mounds, and the reed beds are busy.

Some twenty minutes on you reach the south palisade, muddy and bloodied to the elbows.

The green is quiet this morning, with most of the village out at work. In the Stag, Garrick is sweeping, and Agathe Morrow sits at the long table with her tallies.

Garrick stops sweeping. He stares at the tusks, and at the state of you.

You lay the tusks on the table in front of the reeve.

Agathe turns one over in her hands, then looks up at you. "The old boar," she says. "The one rooting our oats."

She unlocks the chest and counts five silver coins out onto the table.

"The hide and meat are yours by rights," she says. "I'll send a cart for the carcass. If you'll sell the meat, I'll buy it for the village at two silver."
