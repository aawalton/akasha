You scoop the three coppers off the trestle and follow him out, not at all sure what is happening.

Outside, the green is dark and cold. A half moon hangs over the downs, and the sky is clear and hard.

Behind you the drinkers spill out of the Sheaf and stand about the grass in knots, watching the reeve's door.

Aldous doesn't take you to his door. He goes round the side of his house instead, to the back.

Behind it is a small stone byre. Inside, a cow shifts her weight, and the warm smell of her comes out into the cold.

He stops by the door and speaks quietly, the way he spoke on his step.

"You're held, mistress. No token, no home I can find, and a room that's turned. That's why. The Ford sends a patrol through tomorrow. You'll bide here till they've seen you. Not under my roof. Not in the lock-up either. That's for drunks and thieves, and you're neither."

"There's a loft over the cow. Hay, and warm with her. I'll bar the door on the outside. I'll be sat up at my back door all night. I'll hear the ladder. Don't make me ring that bell."

Something in the straw yips. A half-grown pup scrambles up out of it and barks at your bare feet.

"Hob's. He'll bark at a mouse all night. That's by the by."

The back door of the house opens. The stout woman comes out with a blanket over her arm and a heel of bread. She pushes both into your hands and looks at Aldous as if she's daring him to say a word.

"Anyone behind my house sleeps warm. Every soul of them. I don't care what Hattie Rook says."

She looks at your bare feet a moment longer than she needs to. Then she goes back in.

Aldous opens the byre door. Inside is dark, and the cow's breath, and a ladder going up into a square of hay.

"Before the soldiers come, and ask it their way. Is there aught you'd tell me? Just me?"
