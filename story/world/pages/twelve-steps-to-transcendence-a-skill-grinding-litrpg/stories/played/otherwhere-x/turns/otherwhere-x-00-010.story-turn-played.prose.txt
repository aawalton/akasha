"I told you I come from far away," you say. "Far enough that the tongue of Sulon is not the only tongue. I'm fluent in a few tongues, and can understand several more."

For a breath the whole room holds still. Then it starts talking all at once, low and fast.

"A few tongues," says someone at the back. "There's one tongue. Folk that have more aren't folk."

"Past the walls, then," Hattie says, and she says it like a door shutting. "That's what she means. There's no living soul past the walls. None that's a person."

The smith looks at his hands. At the far trestle, the lad has gone white.

Martha keeps her eyes on you. There's something in her face that is close to respect. It isn't enough to hold the room.

"That's a straight answer," she says. "I'll give you that. It's a strange one."

She looks round her room. Cups set down. Benches pushed back. Faces she's known all their lives.

"And my room's turned on it. Aldous. She's yours tonight, not mine."

Aldous is already moving. He doesn't raise his voice. He doesn't have to.

"Enough. I won't have anyone tried in an alehouse. Drink up, or go home."

He stops beside you, close, with his back to the room, and speaks low, for you alone.

"You'll come out with me now. Not a word more in here. Bring your coppers."
