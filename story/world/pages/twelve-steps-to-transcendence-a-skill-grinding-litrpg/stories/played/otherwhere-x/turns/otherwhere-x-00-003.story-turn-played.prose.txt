"Hi there," you say. "Would you mind pointing me in the direction of your parents?"

His face splits in a grin. "Pointing? I'll take you. My da's the reeve of Harrow, he is."

He swings the switch, and the whole flock wheels back east, squawking at the change.

"I'm Hob," he says over the noise. "Da's Aldous Crane. He keeps the roll. Everyone has to answer to him."

He doesn't wait for your name. He's onto the dogs already, every dog around howling all at once, round about three o'clock.

"And the air hummed, like bees in a wall. And all the crows went up off the Mile at once. Mam says it's nothing. Da went quiet, which means it's something."

The road runs down into the village. Behind you the sun slips under the rise, and the light goes grey-gold.

Hob steers the geese round the back of a long thatched building with a sheaf of grain painted on the sign above its door. The smell of mutton stew comes through its back shutters. He pens the geese in a fold of woven wattle, talking all the while, to you and to the geese both.

Then he leads you out onto the green. A well. A tall post with a bell hung at the top. Cottages all round it, walls of pale daub under heavy thatch.

An old woman sits by her door, shelling beans into her lap. She squints your way, the way someone does who can't make much out.

A man at a gate stops with a bucket in his hand. In a doorway, two women lean their heads together.

The grass is cool under your sore feet, and they go quiet on it. Every eye on the green follows you across.

Hob marches you to a house on the east side, by the bell post, its walls set on a footing of stone. He bangs on the door with the switch.

"Da! I found a lady on the road! She's got no shoes!"

The door opens. A lean man of about fifty, grey at the temples, a napkin in his hand. Behind him, a table with supper laid on it.

He looks at your face and goes still, the napkin halfway to the table.

Something crosses his face, fast. He looks harder. Then past you, at the dusk, and back to you.

A stout woman with a ladle peers past his shoulder. She frowns at your bare feet.

The man speaks slow and careful. "Evening, mistress."

"Your name, if you'd give it. Where's your home, and what's your business in Harrow?"
