"I take it the bell is to invite more to come and listen?" you ask.

Aldous shakes his head, once. "It calls folk in. Everyone's indoors by dark, with wolves about."

Across the green, the watchers are already turning away. Doors shut, one after another, as the bell goes on ringing.

"While we wait, could you tell me more about your country?" you say. "I love collecting stories, so I would learn yours as well if I may."

He studies you a moment. When he answers, it is plainly, the way you'd answer a child who has asked the way.

"This is Sulon. The king's land. Farms and sheep, a village every few miles, all the way round. Lord Kell holds the vale. His steward comes at quarter days. The king's soldiers keep the roads. Far off there's the walls, and past the walls, monsters. That's all most of us know, or want to."

The bell stops. In the quiet, a dog barks somewhere, and someone hushes it.

"I've been to Wexley more times than I can count. Aldermere once. Never the capital. That's Sulon."

He shifts his hand on the door. "Now one of mine, since you like a trade. How does a woman come to be on Harrow Mile at dusk, barefoot, no token, no road behind her?"
