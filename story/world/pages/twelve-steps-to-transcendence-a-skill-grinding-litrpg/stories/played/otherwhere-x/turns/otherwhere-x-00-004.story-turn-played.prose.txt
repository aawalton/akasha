"I'm Nala," you say.

His hand closes on the door frame. The knuckles go pale. His face doesn't change at all.

"Nala," he says, quietly, as if he's testing the weight of it.

"Never heard that one," Hob says.

"I think my home is far away from here," you say, "but I'm not sure where here is precisely, so I couldn't tell you how far."

The man's eyes narrow, a fraction. "Here's Harrow. Lord Kell's vale, on the king's road."

"As for business, I suppose I'm looking for a warm meal and roof to sleep under for the night. I could sing for my support, or tell stories if you'd like. I have a feeling I have many you'll have never heard the likes of before."

The stout woman pushes in beside him, ladle and all. "Stories! Look at her feet, Aldous. Anyone on my doorstep gets fed. Songs, now. The Sheaf's wanted a singer since the fiddler died."

Aldous lifts a hand without looking round. She stops, lips pressed tight.

Behind you, across the green, doors are opening. Faces show in them, turned toward the reeve's step, grey in the failing light.

Aldous keeps his eyes on your face, steady and careful.

"First things. Have you a road token, mistress? A reeve's letter, or a stamped tally?"
