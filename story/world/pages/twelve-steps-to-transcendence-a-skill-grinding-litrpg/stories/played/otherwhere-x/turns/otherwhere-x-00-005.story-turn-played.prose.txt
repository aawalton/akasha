"None of these," you say. "I am from a place so far away that there are no reeves, no tallies, and the roads are made from liquid stone."

Hob's mouth drops open. "Liquid stone! Like porridge? Do the carts sink? Is it hot?"

He keeps going, one question on the heels of the last. Aldous lets him run on. The whole time, his eyes stay on your face.

Across the green, people are drifting out of their doorways, to stand and watch the reeve's step.

The stout woman comes up behind Aldous with the loaf from the table. She tears off the heel and pushes it into your hands.

Aldous looks past you, at the people watching. Then back at you.

"Hob, in."

The boy goes, protesting all the way. Aldous swings the door wide and stands aside.

"Come in off the step, mistress. What's to say next isn't for the green."

Past him is one warm room. A hearth. A board laid for supper. In the corner, a chest bound in iron.

Beside the door, on its post, the bell starts to ring. Slow and heavy, one stroke and then the next, rolling out over the green.

Aldous stands with one hand on the door, the bell ringing over your heads and the whole green watching at your back.
