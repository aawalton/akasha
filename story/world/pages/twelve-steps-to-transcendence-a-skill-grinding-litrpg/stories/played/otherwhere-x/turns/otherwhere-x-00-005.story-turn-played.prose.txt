"None of these," you say. "I am from a place so far away that there are no reeves, no tallies, and the roads are made from liquid stone."

Hob's mouth drops open. "Liquid stone! Like porridge? Do the carts sink?"

Aldous doesn't smile. He looks past you, at the green. More people have come out onto it now, standing in twos and threes, watching.

"Hob, in."

He steps down onto the doorstone beside you, so his back is to the green.

Past him, through the open door, you can see one warm room. A hearth. A board laid for supper. In the corner, a chest bound in iron.

The stout woman comes out with the loaf from the table. She tears off the heel and pushes it into your hands.

Aldous speaks low and even, for your ears only. He picks each word before he says it.

"No token, no home you can name, and a tale of stone roads. I'll not call you a liar for it. But I can't account for you, and I answer for everyone in Harrow. A patrol from Tarrant Ford rides through tomorrow. You'll bide in Harrow till they've seen you."

Beside you, on its post, the bell starts to ring. Slow and heavy, one stroke and then the next, rolling out over the green.

"You'll not sleep under my roof, not with my boy in it. You'll have the loft over the Sheaf. Martha takes my word. If you can sing as you say, she'll feed you for it."

Hob's head pokes out past his mother. "Can I come hear? Da, can I?"

Aldous doesn't look round. He holds your eye.

"Will you go quiet to the Sheaf and wait for the patrol?"
