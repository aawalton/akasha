"I truly don't know," you say. "Some magic brought me here, beyond my understanding. I'm hoping learning more about your world will help me understand. What is the name of the kingdom? Who is the king?"

Aldous takes the questions straight, one at a time, the way a man does who keeps a roll.

"Sulon. I told you that one. Osric is the king. Osric, second of the name, of House Sable."

"He's a name on the coin and on the tallies to me. I've never seen his face, and I don't know anyone in Harrow who has."

"Lord Kell holds the vale. The king's steward for the north sits at Aldermere. I answer up through the lord and the steward. Never to the king himself. That's not my place."

"What's said of magic and monsters?" you ask. "Out in the world at large."

He leans on the doorpost. It's an odd thing to be asked, and he weighs it before he answers.

"Every child here knows about mana. Cores. Skills. It's common talk, like the weather. You wake at fourteen and find out what you can do. Some heft a cart. Some heal a cut. I've little to speak of, and I'm glad of it. Tobin the hunter's the only one here with more."

"Monsters are past the walls. The walls ring the plains, and folk keep this side of them. Rifts open now and then. When one opens, soldiers come and the village empties out. I've never seen either one. Most of us die that way, and count it a good life."

He looks at you a while. "Being brought here by magic. I've never heard a tale like that in Sulon. That's a thing for past the walls, or for a fever. There's no third answer I can give you."

Bess fills the doorway with a bowl in her hands. "Aldous Crane, she's barefoot in the cold."

She holds the bowl out to you. "Mutton. Eat it off my step if you won't come in the house."

Aldous doesn't say a word about the bowl either way. He watches your face over it.

"There's no bed for you here. I've a boy in this house and I don't know you. You'll sleep in the Sheaf's loft. Martha takes my word, and my word is you're here till morning."

He walks you across the green. The Sheaf's windows are yellow, and the room inside is loud.

Inside is one long smoky room: trestles, a hearth at the far end, a dozen faces turning.

Martha Deane comes up from the stewpot with a ladle and looks you over, head to foot.

"Aldous Crane, what have you brought me?"

"A stranger with no token. She answers for it tomorrow."

Martha sets a bowl of mutton and a hunk of dark bread on the nearest trestle in front of you.

"The fiddler's dead, and the Sheaf hasn't heard a song since. You say you can sing. Sing, then."

The room goes quiet enough to hear the fire, and every face at the trestles turns.
