A slow slice-of-life story of found family and a yuri harem, told chapter by chapter, with Nala at its heart.

Nala is Alan. One evening he dozes over a book at his back window and wakes in a narrow bed in a rented room in a woman's body: about twenty, petite, pale and freckled, with long dark red hair. It is the morning she is to register as a delver at the guild hall in Emberdeep. Everyone here takes her for Nala, a newcomer come to try the Deep: there is a pack with her name stitched inside it, a letter of introduction to the Delvers' Guild, a thin purse and a room key. She has none of this Nala's memories. She remembers being Alan, and she does not know how she came here or whether she can go back.

The world is one of lamplight, old stone and quiet magic. Emberdeep is a mountain town built around the mouth of the Deep, a vast old dungeon whose bottom no one has found, and delving is a trade there like any other: the Delvers' Guild registers and ranks parties, finds are sold in the market, healers mend whoever comes up hurt, and the taverns and rooming houses live off all of it. The ember-stones carried up from below light and warm the town. There is no school here and no term to keep: Nala has to learn delving from nothing, the ropes and lamps and maps, the guild's rules, the Deep's habits, and whatever small magic it stirs in her, and learn this body and this life while she does.

The story is slow and lives in the days between delves: the guild hall, the market, the rooming house, meals, mending gear, counting coin, nights at the tavern, small kindnesses, the girls she meets. The delves are real, with real danger and real reward, but Emberdeep is warm rather than grim: people come up hurt and are mended, and nobody dies to make a chapter hit harder. Out of those days she gathers a party and a household of girls who choose each other, and a harem around her, one girl at a time, each closeness earned on the page.

Nala is attracted only to girls, and she is not naturally monogamous: she can love several girls at once, and wants to. The story treats that as who she is rather than a problem to fix; what the girls make of it, and of each other, is part of the story.

When the story reaches sex, because a girl and Nala have reached it, it is written fully explicit, with the feeling between them in it. It comes when it is right, and never before.

The first girls, for the world builder to make her own and add to:

- Wren Tolley, 21, a scout and lockpick lodging across the landing from Nala: small and quick, sandy hair she cuts short herself, sun-browned, hazel eyes, a gap-toothed grin. She talks fast, is brave to the edge of stupid, and sends half her coin home to a fishing family of eight.
- Elowen Fairweather, 20, who registers the same morning as Nala: a hedge-witch's daughter with a little light-magic, pale, ash-brown hair to her shoulders, wide green eyes. Nervous, earnest, and braver than she believes.
- Sigrun Halvard, 24, a ranked delver with a long blade, between parties since her last one broke up over an injury: tall and slim, a white-blonde braid, pale grey eyes, a thin scar through one brow. Quiet, exact, protective, and slow to trust.
- Adaeze Obi, called Dae, 23, a healer at the Lantern House by the Deep's mouth: slim, dark brown skin, close-cropped black curls, gold-rimmed spectacles. Steady hands, a dry wit, and a temper about delvers who take stupid risks.
- Hana Seo, 22, a guild cartographer who maps the upper levels: slender, a long black plait, ink on her fingers, sleepy dark eyes. Soft-spoken, exacting about every line she draws, and secretly afraid of the dark.
- Cosima Vell, 22, who keeps her late grandmother's appraisal shop in the finds market: olive skin, dark waves to her waist, gold hoops. A shameless flirt and a hard haggler, quietly sinking under the shop's debts.

Every character is an adult, and no man is ever a love interest.
