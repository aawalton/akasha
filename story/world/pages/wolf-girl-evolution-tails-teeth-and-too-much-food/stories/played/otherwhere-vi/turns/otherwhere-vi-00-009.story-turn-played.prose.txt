You strip a cluster from the nearest shrub and eat the whole handful, seeds and all.

They are just as sour as the first one. The sourness goes into the hinges of your jaw and makes them ache.

They sit small and cold in your stomach. You are barely less empty than you were.

"Status," you say, and the faint letters come up again.

Every line reads exactly as it did the last time you looked.

You let it go, and the letters fade.

The shaking has changed while you crouched here. It comes in hard, jerking spasms now, through your shoulders and your jaw, and you cannot stop them.

Your fingertips have gone numb and white. You can barely feel the staff in your hand.

Your thoughts come slow and thick, as if they have to push through cold water to reach you.

You reach for another cluster. Your fingers will not close on it, and the berries spill loose into the moss.
