You stand in the frost and shout at the sky. "Great. The System isn't just useless, it also has an attitude. I would have used a roof if there was one!"

Your shout goes out over the misted water and comes back off the far slope.

A grouse bursts up whirring from the bank grass a few paces off, and is gone into the pines.

Letters hang in the air in front of you, small and steady.

【Noted. The forest is unmoved.】

You don't answer it. You get the staff under you and set off down the stream.

The first quarter of an hour is one long argument with your hip. It grates at every step.

Your feet come back to you as burning rather than feeling, and your hands come back as aching.

Then the walking begins to work on you. The hard spasms ease off, and your chest loosens.

The sun tops the east rim and lays full light along the gravel and the water. You walk with your face turned into the warmth of it, and you don't stop.

The valley narrows and opens by turns. Birch stands in among the alder, and the stream talks over its stones.

Deer have crossed in the night. Their tracks are cut deep in the mud of a bend.

A heron stands in a shallow pool and watches you pass. It goes on watching after you are by.

Hunger sits under your ribs like a closed fist. Your mouth is dry as cloth.

Blood has started to show in your footprints. The stones have worn through one sole.

Your hip catches, the scrape on your shin pulls, and your sore soles find every stone in the valley.

By mid-morning the sun is high, and the pines are thinning out ahead.

The trees open. Ahead is the sound of water much bigger than the stream's.

You come out onto a wide, slow river running west, all glitter on a gravel shore.

Dry sedge and birch bark stand along the gravel there, and both strip easily in the hand.

At the meeting of the waters stands a low smoking mound, and beside it a turf hut.

A shaggy brown dog lifts its head at the hut, barks, and looks straight at you.

A man straightens up from the smoking mound and stands, looking at you.
