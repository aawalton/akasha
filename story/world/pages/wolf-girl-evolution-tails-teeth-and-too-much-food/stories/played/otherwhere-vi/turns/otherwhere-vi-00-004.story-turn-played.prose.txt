You kneel at the stream above the fall, cup your hands and drink, long and deep.

The water is so cold it aches in your teeth. It tastes of nothing but stone.

You wipe your hands on the shirt and take the trail along the west rim of the ravine.

It bends away west among the pines. Within a few hundred paces the water is out of sight behind you, but the roar of the fall still carries through the trees off to your left. You keep it there, on your left, as you go.

Under the pines the trail is a paler strip in the dark, soft with fallen needles. It is kinder to your feet than the stream bank was.

Ahead on the trail, something big lurches up out of the dark with a snort.

A doe and a smaller yearling crash away through the trees, white tails flashing, and are gone.

Your heart hammers. It keeps hammering for a long moment after the crashing has faded into the woods.

Farther on, the trail splits at a boulder cracked clean in two. The right fork climbs on west, away from the sound of the water. The left fork drops into a steep, stony gully, toward the roar.

You take the left fork, down the gully toward the water.

It is steep and loose underfoot, but dry. You go down half-crouched, one hand on the rock beside you. The stones bite your sore soles at every step, and by the bottom your feet feel raw, though the skin still holds.

After what feels like a quarter of an hour, the gully opens out onto the stream below the fall, and you are in moonlight again.

Here the stream runs wider and slower. Alders lean out over it. Behind you and above, the fall roars down the ravine.

A smell hangs over the water somewhere downstream, of churned mud and something musky and animal.

Your wet tights are ice against your skin. You are shaking now, all through, and your teeth knock together.

You look back up the gully.

On its lip, black against the moonlit sky, two lean wolf shapes stand perfectly still, looking down at you. Their eyes catch the moonlight. Both of them stay silent.

One steps back into the shadow of a boulder, and then it is simply not there any more.

The other stays where it is, head low, its eyes on you.
