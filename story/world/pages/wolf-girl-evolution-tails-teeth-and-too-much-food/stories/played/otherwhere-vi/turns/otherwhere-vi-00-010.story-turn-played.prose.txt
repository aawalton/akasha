You get up off your knees, leaning your weight on the staff, and look for anywhere out of the wind.

Your thoughts come slowly. You climb the dry bank above the berries without any clear idea of where you are going.

Under the pines it is near black. You feel your way between the trunks with the end of the staff, tapping, a hand out in front of you.

You go too far up. You come down again. For a while you lose the sound of the stream altogether, and then find it again, off to one side.

The wind comes down the valley from the north. Wherever you stand, it finds you.

At last you come back to the cowberry shrubs above the water. They are the only low cover you have found.

You lie down among them on the needles and curl up tight, knees to your chest, the staff beside you.

The shrubs don't stop the wind. It comes through them, and through the wet shirt, and into you.

You shake, and shake, and at some point, without knowing when, you sleep.

You wake in the dark, and you don't know why. For a moment you don't know where you are.

Far off to the north-west, the wolves are howling. Many voices, long and rising. It goes on, and then it stops.

You sleep again, badly, in pieces. Every time you half wake, the cold is in your bones.

When you open your eyes again, the sky is grey and pale between the pines.

Mist lies along the stream below. Frost silvers the bank, the shrubs, and your own sleeve.

The shirt and tights have dried on you in the night, and the outside of the cloth is stiff with frost.

Your mouth is dry. Your stomach is hollow, and griping.

You can't feel your feet. Your hands are clubs. Your whole body aches as if someone beat you while you slept.

Faint letters are already waiting before your eyes.
