You hand the shovel to Jory, lift the door-hide, and go in out of the grey morning.

The hut is warm and smoky. The fire has been built up, and a pot of porridge bubbles on the hearth.

Wat fills a wooden bowl to the brim, slaps a lump of butter on top, and pushes it at you.

"Sit," he says. "That's what watchers are for. To be fed first."

The porridge is thick, hot and salted, and the butter runs gold across the top of it.

You eat all of it. He fills the bowl again without asking.

Burr comes in behind you and sits by your knee, watching every spoonful go by.

Jory comes in from the clamp, takes his own bowl, and sits on the bed of fern opposite you.

"You'll sleep the day out by the fire, once you've eaten," Wat says. "As a watcher does."

Jory eats a while in silence. Then he looks up at you over his bowl.

"A guest owes news of the road," he says. "And yours is still to come."

He asks you what it is like, the far country you come from.
