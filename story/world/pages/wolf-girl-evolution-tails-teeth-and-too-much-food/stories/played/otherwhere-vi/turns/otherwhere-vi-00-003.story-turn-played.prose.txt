"Okay, definitely isekai'd," you say aloud. "Not much to work with, but if I can survive the start, I might be able to get stronger. I have water, so next I'll try to follow it to civilization."

You get up out of the moss. It lets go of you with a wet sound, and the soaked tights cling cold to the backs of your legs.

At the east edge of the hollow you find the stream again, and you turn to follow it the way it runs, downhill and out among the pines.

There is no path. The banks are pine needles, roots, moss and loose stone, and you pick your way along them slowly. Every few dozen steps you stop, and look, and listen, before you go on.

Just below the hollow, a narrow trodden line comes out of the trees and crosses the water. The mud at the edge is pocked with small split hoof prints, sharp-edged and fresh.

Where the stream runs open to the sky, the big moon lights it bright as a lamp, every stone and ripple clear. Where the pines close in overhead, it is near black. There you walk with one hand out in front of you and keep to the sound of the water.

Somewhere behind you an owl calls. The wind moves in the tops of the trees, and the pines creak high overhead.

Ahead of you, something small scurries off through the needles. You hear it go, a quick dry patter, and then it is gone, and you never see what it was.

The ground is hard on your feet. Needles prick your soles. Roots bruise them. Loose stones turn under your small bare feet, and each time your weight goes a little further than you expect before it settles.

The walking warms you a little, in your chest and down your back. Your hands stay cold. Your feet stay cold.

After what feels like an hour, your soles ache at every step. It is a dull, bruised ache, though, without the sharp sting of a cut.

The ground ahead drops away.

The stream goes over a lip of rock in a small, loud fall, white in the moonlight, and pours down into a ravine. The sides of it are steep and dark, rock under moss, and the moss is slick and shining with spray.

Along the west rim of the ravine, a narrow trail runs off through the pines. It is dry underfoot, and pocked with the same small split prints.

The trail leads away from the water. A little way on, the dark under the trees takes it from sight.
