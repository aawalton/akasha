The brackets beside HP and SP are new. The rest of the lines read as they always have.

You let it go, and the letters fade.

A hide comes out through the doorway and lands on your shoulders. Wat grumbles something, and is gone again.

You wrap it round you, and the night air stops mattering.

On the hour you walk the clamp's round, all the way, looking and smelling, and then you sit again.

Burr comes out and lies across the doorway beside your stump, and sighs.

Far in the north the wolves howl, stop, and howl again. The trees at the edge of the gravel stay dark and still.

On the third round, on the river side of the mound, you catch a smell of sharper smoke.

A red glow shows through the turf, low down, the size of a hand. It brightens as you look at it.

You fetch the shovel, dig damp earth off the heap, and throw it square on the glow.

The glow goes dark at the first shovelful. You stamp it down, and throw on another to be sure.

Walking on round with the shovel, you find a second thin place starting to steam, and you pack that too.

The rest of the night stays quiet. The moon crosses the river and goes down behind the pines.

Grey light comes up in the east. Mist lies on the Carrow, and the heron is back in the shallows.

The door-hide lifts. Jory comes out yawning, and walks the round himself before he says a word.

He stops at the two dark patches of fresh earth on the river side and looks at them for a long moment.

"I've known men sit that clamp a month," he says, "who'd have slept through the first one."

"There's porridge," Wat shouts from inside. "Watcher eats first."
