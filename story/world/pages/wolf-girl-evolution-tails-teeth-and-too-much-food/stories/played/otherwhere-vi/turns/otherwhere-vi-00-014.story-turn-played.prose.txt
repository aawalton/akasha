"No one is hunting me," you tell him. "But I'm owned by no village either, which means the village might not be safe for me. Are there other options for healing?" You hesitate. "If I increase my level, or my stats, would that be enough?"

The old man grunts at "hunting", the way a man does when he sets down a stone he has been carrying.

He answers the level question first, and flatly. "A level makes you harder. Not whole."

A hurt still wants rest and care, he says, whatever the letters tell you. A leveled man bleeds the same as any other.

"Rest heals. Sleep under a roof, eat, keep off that foot, and a body mends itself."

The temple at Wenmarch heals with light, he says, for silver, and that is two days on past the ford. Neither of them has silver to spare for a stranger's foot. He says it without any shame at all.

The younger man comes back in for the kettle.

"Jory," the old man says, and tells him what you said about the village.

Jory stands there thinking about it, the kettle in his hand. Then he says he is a Brackenford man. His mother is headwoman there, and one he speaks for goes through the gate unasked.

"Guest-right's a night and a breakfast," the old man says. "After that it's work, pay or the road."

He slaps his knee. His knees are bad for sitting the clamp at night, he says. Someone has to sit up with it and call out if it flares, and he would sooner sleep.

"A guest who sits the night watch eats at this fire till the raft goes down."

"That's so, Wat," Jory says. In five days, you would ride the raft down to Brackenford with them, spoken for.

Wat looks at you across the fire. "Can you sit awake in the dark," he asks, "and not panic?"
