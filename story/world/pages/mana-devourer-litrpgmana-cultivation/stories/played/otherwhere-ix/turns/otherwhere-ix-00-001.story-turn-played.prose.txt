The last thing you remember is your back window. A book open on your knee, the evening going blue outside the glass, your eyes getting heavy.

Then a sound like a thousand tiny bells, all around you, very close.

You open your eyes.

Sky. Deep, clear blue, bluer than any sky you have seen, going faintly violet down at its edges. No clouds. The sun is well past noon.

You are lying on your side in grass. Only it is not grass, not quite. The blades are stiff and pale and almost clear, like thin strips of frosted glass, and when the breeze moves them they knock together and ring. That is the bells. Thousands of them, stretching away in every direction.

You sit up, and your weight goes wrong, and you put out a hand to steady yourself. It lands short. Something stings. You lift the hand and look at it. Small, pale, freckled across the back, with a narrow wrist and thin fingers. A fine red line across the palm, beading, where one of the blades has cut it.

A long fall of dark red hair slides over your shoulder.

Your shirt is still on you, the dark grey one, but it hangs loose now, down around your thighs, the collar slipping off one shoulder. Your black tights are there too, one knee already nicked through. No shoes. Your bare feet are small and pale, and you draw them in carefully, away from the edges of the grass.

You say something. It comes out in a woman's voice.

You get to your feet, slowly, and look around.

Flat. Flat and pale and ringing, in every direction, all the way to the horizon. The glass grass comes up past your knees. Here and there a stunted tree, black-barked and twisted, stands alone with its few leaves glinting like metal.

Far to the east, right on the edge of the world, there is a thin dark line. Too straight to be a hill. Too regular to be trees.

Nothing else. No road that you can see. No smoke. No sound but the bells.

Then, off to your left, not far, the ringing changes. A patch of the grass is moving against the wind. Parting, and closing behind something low that you cannot see, and parting again.

It is moving toward you.

It stops.

The grass around it goes still, and in the stillness you hear, very clearly, something breathing through its nose, and sniffing.
