You take a spine quill in each hand, each one by its blunt root, and crouch low.

Your eyes keep moving. The grass to the south. The bird overhead. The grass again.

The bird is broad-winged, and the pale edges of its wings glitter as it turns. It only circles, high and patient.

The beast comes out of the grass on the far side of the kill and stops, snout lifted.

It is thigh-high and heavy through the shoulders, its back a thick mat of pale quills. It looks at the carcass. Then it looks at you, crouched low and bleeding beside it.

It doesn't rattle. It doesn't circle. It puts its head down and comes straight at you.

As it reaches you, you drive the right-hand quill at its face, hunting for an eye. Its eyes are small and sunk deep under a ridge of short brow quills, and you can't find them. The quill glances off the hard brow and snaps in your fist.

Its jaws close on your other ankle, the good one, and bite down hard.

It hangs on and twists. It is heavier than the first one, and it drags you half off balance. One leg torn at the calf, the other held in its jaws. There is nothing left to stand on.

You still have one whole quill, in your left hand.

Its throat is right there below you, working as it grinds. Bare, and pale, and unquilled.
