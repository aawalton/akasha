You keep your shoulders still and turn after it, slowly, a little at a time, so it stays in front of you.

It stops the moment you come square to it. Quills up. Watching.

Then it slides on again, and you turn again. Shift, stop. Shift, stop. The grass crunches and rings under your bare feet every time you move them, loud and bright, right up against your ankles.

Your legs are wrong for this. They are shorter than they should be, so each step lands short of where you meant it to, and your weight sits lower and wider in your hips than you are used to carrying it. You keep having to find it again.

Halfway round, your heel comes down on a stiff clump of blades, and the clump rolls under it.

Your balance goes. You lurch sideways, arms flung out, and your eyes drop to your feet as you catch yourself.

The rattle stops.

You look up. It is already coming.

Low and fast, the grass splitting ahead of it, and silent now. Its blunt head is down and its mouth is open, and it is going for your ankles.
