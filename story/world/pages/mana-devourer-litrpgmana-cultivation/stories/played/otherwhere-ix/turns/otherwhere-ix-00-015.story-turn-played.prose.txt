You let yourself go over, and as you fall you throw your weight down onto it.

You grab for its flanks. If you can heave it over, the belly is right there for the blade.

The short quills along its sides snap off into your palms as your fingers close. Your one whole quill stays clenched in your left fist by its blunt root. The other three lie where you laid them out, in the grass beside the kill.

It doesn't shift. Its legs are braced wide, and it is far stronger than you are. It is like trying to roll a stone. You heave, and it stays exactly where it is.

It wrenches its head sideways, and your own weight drags against its jaws on your ankle. The teeth grind deeper. You hear yourself cry out.

You end up on your side in the glassgrass, your palms full of glass and bleeding. Your ankle is still locked in its jaws, and its body lies square across your legs.

The beast plants its feet and starts to drag you backward through the grass, toward the kill.
