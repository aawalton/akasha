"Hello?"

You say it loud and steady, out over the grass toward the place where the sniffing is. It comes out in that voice again, the woman's voice, higher and lighter than the one you are braced for.

You straighten up as tall as you can go, which is not very tall, and set your hands on your hips. The collar of the shirt slides a little further off your shoulder.

"Can you understand me?"

It doesn't answer, not in words. The sniffing stops. The grass around it goes quiet, while the rest of the plain keeps ringing in the wind.

Then a dry rattle, from right where the sniffing was, like a cup of glass beads given one hard shake.

The blades part, and you see it.

It is low and broad, about as high as your knee, with a grey-green hide. Its snout is blunt and lifted, working at the air. All along its back stand pale quills, glassy and packed close, so much like the grass around it that for a moment you can't tell where the grass stops and the animal begins.

It doesn't come on. It holds a few body lengths off, quills raised and trembling.

Then it starts to move sideways. Slowly, one foot and then the next, it circles out from your left and around toward your back. Its snout stays on you the whole way.

Your palm is still bleeding. A drop runs down the heel of your hand and onto your wrist. When your hand shifts, the snout dips after it.

Your head turns to follow it, and it stops and rattles again. When you are square to it, it goes still and only watches.

Then it moves on again, unhurried, and slides out past the edge of your sight toward your back.
