You turn to the dead beast's back. The quills lie flat now, laid down along its hide.

You take hold of one near the spine and pull. It won't come.

You try a slow twist at the root instead, and it slides free, whole.

The root end is blunt and dull, not glassy, about as long as your thumb. Held by that end, it doesn't cut your fingers at all.

The quills along the spine are the longest, a hand and a half long and as thick as a finger. The short ones on the flanks snap at a touch. The spine quills are the ones worth taking.

You prick one into the soft skin of its belly. It goes in easily.

You press it against the bone of its jaw, and it snaps with a small, clean crack.

A knife for soft things, then. Not for bone or hide, and not for twisting.

You work your way down the spine until you have five of the long ones laid out on the grass.

Overhead, high up, a pale shape with glittering wings has begun to circle.

To the south, the ringing is closer now. The grass is parting in a steady line.

Something low and broad is coming through it, straight toward the kill, at a lumbering walk. Its back rises above the grass, bristling pale, and it is bigger than the one at your knees.
