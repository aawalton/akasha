You crouch and draw the edge of your forearm, slowly, along a standing blade of glassgrass.

The skin parts in a thin red line, the same as it did when you first woke.

But the cut is shallower than the one on your palm, and the bleeding stops almost at once.

Tougher, then. Not that much tougher. The glass still cuts you.

You kneel over the dead beast and put your mouth to the torn throat.

The blood has already gone thick. It barely comes, a slow dark ooze. You work at it and get a mouthful, then another. Dark, gritty and bitter. It tastes of iron, and faintly of ground glass.

Your stomach heaves at the third. You stop before it comes back up.

There is a little warmth in your belly from it, like a bite of something. But your mouth is drier than before, sticky with salt, and you are thirstier than you were.

Across the Flats, off to the south, the glassgrass is ringing against the wind.

Something is moving through it, low down and a long way off, toward the smell of the kill.
