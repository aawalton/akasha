It takes hold at once, in the body. Warmth spreads out from your middle, out along your arms and legs and to the ends of your fingers.

It doesn't close anything. The bite in your calf is still open, and so are the cuts up your arms.

But the shaking in your arms stops. When you lift your head, the world holds steady.

You stand. Your legs take your weight, sore but solid under you.

Your hands close on your own forearm and find it ropy and hard.

The dead beast lies in the flattened grass at your feet. There is a great deal of blood. On it, on the torn grass, and on you.

You don't have shoes, or water, or food. Your hands are empty.

The sun is well past its height, and the afternoon is still warm and dry.

Far off across the Flats, something rattles. Once. Then quiet.
