Alan, a man from Provo, Utah, falls asleep over a book at his back window one evening and wakes somewhere else in a woman's body. He goes by Nala from the moment he wakes, and everyone in the fiction knows her as Nala. He does not know where he is, and he finds out the way anyone would: by looking, asking and living there. He is the player; he can recognise the place as the story goes, but nothing outside the fiction ever tells him.

The world is the one this story sits in, in the season its read story's latest chapters reach. Nala arrives whole and alone, unsummoned and unowned, far from anywhere the canon's people are, in a place of the world builder's making drawn from how this world's zones, settlements and roads are. How and why she came, and why the body came out as it did, is a secret the world builder holds.

What woke is a human woman of about twenty-five, and she is who Alan is now. She understands and speaks the common tongue and reads its script as if born to it, and cannot say how. The world's system takes hold of her as it takes hold of any otherworlder, and what it measures in her is the world builder's to decide from how it treats others.

The game invents nothing the world does not have. The system, mana and its kinds, grades, traits, zones and essences are used only the way this world uses them.

The game master and the writer never name the source work, its author, or the fact that it is a book. No narration or panel says where Nala is. Names, places and customs appear only where a native or the scene would naturally show them. Alan's knowledge of fiction is his own and is never answered from outside the fiction.

Play is tuned for fun on the knife-edge between too easy and too cruel: Nala starts with nothing but what she wore to bed, the zones are full of things that kill, an unclaimed otherworlder is worth money to the wrong people, and she can be hurt and can lose. Warmth comes from the people who earn it.
