*No book that will let me cast a spell to read a book?* you think at Links. *I'm a speed reader, four thousand words a minute, so I can read fast. But I'm sure magic could make that faster.*

"Not on any shelf in my lit rooms," he says in your head.

"A plain book like Courtesies goes at the reader's own pace. Your speed will serve you there."

"A working text is different. Its power unfolds only as you understand it. The fastest reader alive can't hurry it."

You go to the low shelf a few steps from the Counter and take down Counter Keeping.

Then you roll one of the hall's tall ladders along its brass rail to the west gallery, and climb.

High on the gallery you find Courtesies of the Many Peoples, a thick, plain volume, and carry it down under one arm.

You sit on the step of the Counter's platform with both books beside you, and open Courtesies first.

At your pace, its first part, on the commonest peoples, takes a quarter hour.

Elves give a stranger their house's name before their own. To an elf, a bare name sounds cold.

Satyrs take a refused offer of food as a slight, and a shared meal as a friendship begun.

The Bectiwode are beetle-like, the size of a Great Dane, and they chitter as they speak.

To the Bectiwode, a low chitter back is warmth. A silent answer is coldness.

The Aracnio greet with a dip of the forelegs. A hand thrust out to shake startles them.

You read on through the rest of Courtesies. The whole book takes you some two hours.

Then you open Counter Keeping. It's a working text of the Library's own magic, dense and exact.

For some three hours you work through it page by page, as you did Shelf Sight.

You follow every word. But the power in it stays folded shut, and the space behind your eyes stays still.

You reach the last page. Your sight stays empty of windows.

"It hasn't taken," Links says in your head, flatter than usual. "It won't, until you've learned something new."

"Another power, from another book. Then Counter Keeping may give way to you."

"Until then, the Counter won't lend a single book."
