You drop down and grab it, both hands, just behind its head, where the salt has already dried its skin rough.

It's easy to hold. The skin there is like coarse sandpaper under your palms, nothing slick about it at all, and you shove it over sideways, hard, onto the white heap of spilled salt.

It lands in the salt, and you lean your weight on it and pin it there.

It shrieks. It thrashes under your hands, and the salt hisses against its skin like water on a hot pan. The puckering spreads fast now, racing back along it.

Its whole body is shrinking, the wrinkled grey drawing in and in along its length.

It twists its mouth end round against your grip, and its teeth catch your forearm.

The nip is shallow, a stinging scrape along the skin, but it makes you hiss through your teeth.

You keep your weight on it. You keep it pressed down in the salt.

Its thrashing is weaker now, slower, each heave smaller than the last. But it isn't still yet.

Under your palms its skin crackles faintly as the salt goes on drying it.
