"Time for bed, Links," you tell him, and you walk back through the amber hall and down the passage to the quarters.

You hang the blue robe in the wardrobe and set the slippers at its foot. Then you slip naked under the covers.

The bed is soft and wide. The day's ache seeps out of your arms and your back, and sleep takes you fast.

Morning finds you rested and whole, the old fullness humming back in you, and the Library's lights brightening beyond the door.

The smell of fresh bread drifts in from the kitchen.

From the hall beyond the passage comes a sound you haven't heard here before: a slow creak of wood and brass.
