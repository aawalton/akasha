"Okay," you say. "Is this 'minutes matter' urgency, or can it wait a few hours if needed? And is there a book on bookworms I could read to prepare? I'd like to know what I'm getting into. Are they dangerous like this?"

Links considers you, his big head tilted to one side, as though a Librarian asking questions first is something new to him.

"Not minutes," he says. "Every day they're left costs me power. A few hours won't sink us."

"Dangerous? They bite. Engorged like that, their teeth are nothing to laugh at." A pause. "And one of them has grown bigger than the rest."

His eyes flicker blue, text scrolling through them. "There's a book. Should be right about..." He pads down off the platform, and into the mess of books scattered round the foot of the desk. Under a broken chair he stops, and noses out a thin volume toward you.

The cover is plain, the lettering worn but readable: Bookworm Care for Library Assistants.

You sit down on the edge of the platform with the guide in your lap, and you read.

Links settles nearby, curled on the floor with his chin on his paws. At the back of the hall the chewing goes on, slow and patient and wet.

It takes you about an hour. Somewhere in there, sitting still, the ache goes out of your legs.

A normal bookworm, you learn, is a cute little thing: a grey worm a few inches long, ringed with color at each end. They eat magic residue and book dust. Each one has an affinity of its own. They're needed to make magical quills, and their droppings feed the soil that magical herbs grow in.

They have no eyes. Their mouths are round, rimmed with rows of small, jagged teeth, and they bite.

And salt dries a bookworm out. It leaves one helpless, curled up and still, but it doesn't kill it.

You close the book. You know their habits now, and their bite, and what salt does to them.

"Salt's in the break room," Links says, before you've said a word. "Off the hall. Big box. It keeps."

The broom leans against the platform beside you. At the dark back of the hall, the chewing goes on.
