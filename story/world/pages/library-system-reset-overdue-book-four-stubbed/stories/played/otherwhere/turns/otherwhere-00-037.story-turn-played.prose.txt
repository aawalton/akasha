Behind the Check-in Counter you find a short passage, and at the end of it the quarters: a snug room, panelled in wood.

You peel off your bloodied shirt, then the rest of your clothes, and leave them in a heap on the floor.

In the bathroom you scrub off the blood and the grime with water from the cold tap, shivering the whole time.

The bite on your arm stings in the cold water. But it's clean at last.

You drag the dust sheet off the bed. Underneath, the bed is clean, and soft, and wide.

You collapse into it naked and exhausted, and you're asleep almost at once.

As your eyes close, the Library's lights sink to a low amber for the night. You sleep the whole night through, undisturbed.

In the morning the lights brighten again, and you wake rested and strong. The bite on your arm has knitted to a tender pink scar.

From somewhere beyond the hall drifts the smell of fresh bread.
