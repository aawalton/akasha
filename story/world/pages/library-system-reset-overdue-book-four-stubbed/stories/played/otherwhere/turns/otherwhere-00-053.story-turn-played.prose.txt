You lie a few more minutes in the cooling water, loose and warm right through, before you climb out.

You pull the plug and dry off on a thick linen towel, then take a second blue robe from the wardrobe.

It smells of cedar, and it hangs long on you. Your slippers are still out by the gloom, so you go barefoot.

You cross the hall's dim gold to the arched door and follow the short corridor to the kitchen.

The kitchen is warm from the great oven, and a honey-glazed loaf still sits on the oak table.

Your stomach growls. You open the pantry on jars of honey and bins of roots and vegetables.
