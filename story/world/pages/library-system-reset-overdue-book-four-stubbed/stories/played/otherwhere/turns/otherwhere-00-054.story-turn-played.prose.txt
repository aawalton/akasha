You take the loaf with you. It's dense and nutty under its honey glaze, and you tear into it as you walk back into the hall.

At the gloom's edge, your first robe, belt, pouch and slippers lie by the honey jar.

You buckle on the belt and pouch and step into the felt slippers, a crust still between your teeth.

You fold the spare robe over your arm, walk it back to the quarters, and hang it in the wardrobe.

Then you aim a thought at Links. *Okay, Links. You need more power. How do we get it for you?*

"Put my books back," he says in your head. "Every book returned to its right shelf is a point."

A pause, the kind he takes when his eyes are flickering blue. "Some three thousand lie about the hall. The spine marks match the shelf marks."

"Eight books and your taps run hot. Patrons pay a point each too, once the counter works."
