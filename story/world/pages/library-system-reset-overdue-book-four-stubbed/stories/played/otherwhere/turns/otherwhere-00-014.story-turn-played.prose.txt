You go quietly. You circle out wide around the bookworm, toward the gloom where the scoop lies, and your socks make no sound at all on the floor.

It turns anyway. Its blind head swings round as you go, tracking you, steady and exact, as if it can feel precisely where you are without needing to hear a thing.

As you pass it, it lunges sideways, fast. But you're far enough out. Its teeth close on nothing.

You reach the scoop in the gloom and snatch it up off the floor.

On the next heap back, a second small shape lifts its head. It holds very still, listening, its round mouth toward you. It stays on its heap.

You circle wide again, back toward the box and the light, and the salted bookworm drags itself round after you, turning to keep its face to you the whole way.

At the box you fill the scoop. You set your feet. This time you take your time over it, and you aim.

At the last moment the bookworm jerks its head aside. The salt pours down onto the floor beside it in a white heap.

You're still following through when it lunges.

Its teeth rake your calf. It's a glancing bite, a quick tearing drag, and the black tights rip open under it.

It stings, sharp and hot. A line of blood beads up through the torn fabric.

You stumble back a step and stay on your feet. The bite isn't deep.

The bookworm is right at your feet now, beside the spilled salt, its mouth open and working.
