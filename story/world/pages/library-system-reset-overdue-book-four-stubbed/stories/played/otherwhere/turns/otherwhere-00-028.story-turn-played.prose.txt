"Fine," you say. "We do this the hard way then."

You press your bloody sleeve against your shirt, then down along your tights, soaking the blood into the cloth.

Then you get down and roll in the broken oval's salt. It crusts to the damp cloth, gritty and white, and where it finds the bite in your arm it burns like fire.

You fetch the chewed broom. Its bristles are bitten ragged down one side, but it still sweeps. You sweep the oval's salt together into one heap, a foot and a half across and ankle-deep.

Then you walk toward the chewing.

At twenty feet, a small bookworm rears up off its heap of books and roars its wet, squelching challenge at you.

It comes off the heap after you at a slow walk. You back toward the salt, and it follows.

At the heap it stops short. Its blind head sways at the salt's edge, and it will not come on.

You lunge to drag it in. It jerks back, and you sprawl into the heap alone. Salt sprays out across the floor, and the crust flakes off your shirt in patches.

It lunges at your face. You roll aside, and its teeth snap shut on air.

It coils just past the scattered salt, head low, between you and the dark.
