You open the wardrobe. The smell that comes out is cedar, not the dust you'd braced for.

Inside hang three robes of deep blue wool. They're whole, without a single moth hole, after all these centuries.

They're plainly cut for someone taller than you. On you they'd be long, but wearable.

At the foot of the wardrobe sits a pair of soft felt slippers.

Beside them lies a leather belt, a drawstring pouch hung from it.

Behind you, your own clothes still lie in their bloodied heap on the floor. The smell of fresh bread drifts in.
