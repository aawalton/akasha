You drag yourself up off the floor, aching all over, and run back to the edge of the gloom for another sack.

You hug it to your chest. The honey-salt crust has held, though flakes of it fall away as you move.

Behind you comes a ripping sound. The worm's teeth have split the lodged sack; it gapes wide, gagging up gouts of salt.

You run straight at the open mouth and ram the second sack in deep.

This time you let go at once and spring back. The thrash only clips you on your way out.

Even a clip from that bulk jars your whole body. You stumble, and now you're hurting badly.

The worm convulses, the salt burning deep in its gullet. Its grey hide puckers, and cracks.

Its thrashing slows, and its huge length sags. But it is still alive.
