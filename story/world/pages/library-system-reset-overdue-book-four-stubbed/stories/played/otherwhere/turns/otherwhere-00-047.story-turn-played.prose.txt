You walk to the kitchen and carry three more sacks out of the pantry, one trip at a time. On the last trip, six sacks are left on the pantry floor behind you.

You set two down by the honey jar at the gloom's edge and hug the third to your chest.

Your salt crust has worn patchy in the night. Honey shows through it in places.

About forty feet out, the worm rears from its chewing and roars, then surges at you.

It comes fast, head low, straight for the bare honey patches.

You thrust the sack at its mouth, but it jerks its head aside at the last moment.

The sack glances off its teeth and thumps down whole on the floor.

The worm snaps back at you, and you twist away. Its teeth close on air an inch from your hip.

It rears over you, mouth wide, the sack lying between you.
