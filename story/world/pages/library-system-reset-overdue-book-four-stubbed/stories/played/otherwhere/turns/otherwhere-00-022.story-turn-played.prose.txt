You crouch by the scuffed gap, weight forward on the balls of your feet, and wait for it.

The second bookworm comes. It noses into the gap and squeezes itself through, and the salt on either side scrapes its flanks raw as it goes.

It shrieks the whole way, a thin, high sound, its skin puckering along both sides.

As it clears the line, you throw yourself on it.

Its skin is wet and slick. It slides straight out from under your arms like soap in a bath, and you hit the floor on your side, hard.

It rears over you and lunges at your face. You jerk your head aside, and its teeth clack shut on air an inch from your cheek.

It coils between you and the gap, its blind head swaying toward you.
