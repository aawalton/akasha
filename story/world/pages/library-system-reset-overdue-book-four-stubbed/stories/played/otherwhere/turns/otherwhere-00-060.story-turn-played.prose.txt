You wind down the spiral staircase into the round chamber, lit its dim blue-green.

The walk across the springy matting to the trunk is an ordinary walk now.

The two knots of light pulse side by side. You lay a hand on each, shoulder-width apart.

Heat burns up your arms and through your veins, and the chamber falls away.

You see the Check-in Counter long ago, bright and busy, with patrons of every kind lined up before it.

Scaled and feathered and furred, tall and tiny, all of them waiting their turn at the carved desk.

The vision thins, and you're back at the trunk, arms tingling, breathing hard, unhurt.
