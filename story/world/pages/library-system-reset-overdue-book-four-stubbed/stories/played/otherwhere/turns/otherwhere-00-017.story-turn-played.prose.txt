Still sitting back on your heels, you say, "Okay. That was harder than expected. Is there an easy way here that I'm missing? Or were you really expecting me to take on a room of these with a broom?"

Links's ears go back flat.

"The broom was never for hitting them," he says, stung. "You sweep with it. You push the salt onto them with the bristles, from past where they can lunge. And they won't cross salt. Lay an unbroken line of it, and a bookworm stays on its own side. Ring one in, and it's penned. Then you can take your time."

His eyes go to the scrape on your forearm, then down to the torn tights at your calf and the line of dried blood there. The runes on his flanks slow.

"I could claw them myself," he says, more quietly. "Every swipe costs power I haven't got."
