You take a loaf and tear off a chunk. The bread is dense and nutty, its crust glazed in honey.

Eating as you go, you wander the length of the kitchen under the hanging copper pots.

A wide door opens onto a long staff dining hall. It's dim in there, the tables all under dust sheets.

At the kitchen's far end, beside the great oven, there's a low door. You duck through it.

It's a pantry. Jars of honey line the shelves, and bins of roots and vegetables stand along the wall.

Stacked in the corner are a dozen sacks of coarse salt, each of them about twenty pounds.
