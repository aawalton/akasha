You dig the scoop into the box and bring it up heaping full again.

This time you hold still. You stand your ground at the box, in the gold light, the scoop ready, and let it come to you.

It drags itself closer through the scattered books, one side still puckered and grey. Twelve feet. Ten. Eight. Its round mouth works as it comes, the rows of teeth opening and closing on nothing, wet.

At about five feet, you fling the salt straight at it.

The throw is hurried. Half the salt sprays past it in a white fan and pours away across the books behind. But the rest hits it full in the mouth.

It shrieks. Its whole front end puckers and shrinks, drawing in on itself, and the wrinkling creeps back along its body, further than before.

And in the same instant it lunges.

It's fast, far faster than that slow drag, a few feet in a single heave, teeth first. You jerk back. Its teeth snap shut on empty air a hand's width from your shin, with a wet clack you feel as much as hear.

As you jerk back, the tin scoop flies out of your hand.

It hits the floor ringing, skitters away past the bookworm, and spins to a stop out in the gloom behind it.

The bookworm drops back onto the floor. It's shrunken now along half its length, grey skin creased and drawn tight, but it's still moving. It lies between you and the scoop, four or five feet from your feet, its blind head weaving from side to side.

Further back in the dark, the other shapes stay on their heaps, heads raised, listening.
