Morning light lies flat across the cobbles of the square. The well stands at the middle, a ring of fitted stone with a wooden bucket on its rim and the rope coiled beside it. The bench sits beside the well, and over it leans the old tree, its branches reaching out past the bench's end. A few dry leaves still hang from it, brown and curled at the edges.

You draw your sword. The iron comes out of the scabbard with a short scrape, and the leather grip sits warm in your hand. You step in under the tree, past the end of the bench, and set your feet on the cobbles, one a little ahead of the other, until your weight rests even between them.

Above you, one of the brown leaves comes loose from its branch with a faint tick. It drifts down slowly, turning over as it falls, showing its pale underside and then its dark face, sliding a little to one side and then back.

You follow it down with your eyes and keep the blade low. It swings past your shoulder, then your hip. A foot above the cobbles it tips onto its edge, and you cut, one short stroke across. The edge goes through without a catch. Two halves of the leaf come apart and keep falling, a hand's width between them.

They land on the cobbles a moment apart and lie still. The square is quiet. Across it, the steps on the north side and the green door on the east stand empty.
