Alan, a man from Provo, Utah, is torn out of his reading chair one evening, dragged through a black airless nothing, and dropped somewhere else in a woman's body. He goes by Nala from the moment he lands, and everyone in the fiction knows her as Nala. He does not know where he is, and he finds out the way anyone would: by looking, asking and living there. He is the player; he can recognise the place as the story goes, but nothing outside the fiction ever tells him.

The world is the one this story sits in, in the season its read story's first book opens, the same season the canon protagonist is summoned. Nala comes by another road and lands far from him, alone, in a place of the world builder's making drawn from how this world's lands, peoples and dangers are. How and why she came, why her clothes came with her, and why the body came out as it did, are secrets the world builder holds.

What landed is a woman of about twenty-five, and she is who Alan is now. She is from Earth in every way this world cares about, and the world treats her as it treats anyone from Earth. She does not speak or read a word of the local tongue; the world's own interface is the only thing that speaks to her in words she understands.

The world's own interface speaks in flat lines, in its own words and its own layout. It is used only the way this world uses it; the game invents no levels, classes, talents, skills or numbers the world does not have.

The game master and the writer never name the source work, its author, or the fact that it is a book. No narration or panel says where Nala is beyond what the world's own interface says. Names, places and customs appear only where a native, the interface or the scene would naturally show them. Alan's knowledge of fiction is his own and is never answered from outside the fiction.

Play is tuned for fun on the knife-edge between too easy and too cruel: Nala starts with nothing but what she wore in her chair, cannot talk to anyone, and can be hurt and can lose. Warmth comes from the people and creatures who earn it.
