The last thing you remember is your reading chair. A book, a lamp, the house quiet around you.

Then a voice. Not in your ears. Inside your head, deep and booming, in words that are not any language you know. It comes once, and you look up from the page. It comes again, harder, pressing on the inside of your skull, and the chair is gone.

There is nothing under you. Nothing around you. Black, with no stars in it. The air rips out of your open mouth and does not come back. Your ears spike with pain. Your eyes sting and dry. You tumble, or you think you do, with nothing to tumble against.

Then light, and a hard, soft landing on your back, and air.

You lie there and breathe. That is all you do for a while. In, out, big ragged gulps of it, cool and wet and green-smelling. Your chest hurts. Your ears ring. Slowly the ringing drops away and other sounds come in under it. Water, trickling. Leaves moving high up. Something ticking and clicking in the undergrowth, like a clock with too many hands.

You open your eyes.

Trees. Enormous ones, straight as pillars, going up and up to a roof of leaves so far above you that the light comes down through it gold and slanting and thick with drifting specks. The bark is grey and overlapped like scales. The leaves are a green that leans toward blue.

You are lying in moss, deep and springy, in a round hollow full of ferns taller than you are sitting.

You sit up, and everything is wrong in a way you feel before you understand it. Your weight sits differently. Your arms are too short to reach where they are reaching.

You look down at yourself.

Your dark grey shirt, the one you were wearing in the chair, hangs loose to the middle of your thighs, the collar slipping off one shoulder. Your black compression tights, snug as ever. No shoes. Your feet in the moss are small and pale, and so are the hands braced beside them, narrow and freckled and fine-boned.

A heavy fall of dark red hair slides forward over your shoulder.

You say something. You are not sure what, only that the voice that says it is a woman's.

Blue light flashes in front of your eyes, and you flinch back from a box that is not there. It hangs in the air, square and clean-edged, and turns with your head when you turn it. Words fill it, and you understand them before you have finished reading.

Welcome to Davrar

Davrar will help you survive

Adapting you to the local biosystem

Calibrating to your understanding and determining your capabilities

Please, be patient, this will not take long

Then it is gone.

The clicking in the ferns has stopped.

At the low side of the hollow a spring wells up between two roots and runs away downhill as a small, clear brook. The light is going longer and more golden. Somewhere far off through the trunks, something whistles, three rising notes, and something else answers it.

Under the canopy, in the deepening shade, a few faint green lights have begun to drift.
