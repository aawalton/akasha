You twist. You try to come round in the mouth of the log, to get your hands on the jaws and catch them, and then your legs up and around its neck, and squeeze until it can't breathe.

The roots hem your shoulders in on both sides. The weight on your back pins your hips flat to the ground. You wrench anyway, and get half round, one shoulder scraping on the torn wood.

One hand finds it. Fur, coarse and thick. A cheek, hard as bone under the fur. The hot, wet edge of a lip, and teeth behind it.

You grip, and your fingers have no strength left in them. They slide off.

Your legs are still out in the ferns, under its weight. You can't get them free. You can't get them up.

The jaws open.

They close a third time, deep in the side of your neck.

The pain goes very far away. It is still there, but it is somewhere a long way off, happening to someone else.

In front of your face the dry dark of the log, with its smell of rot and earth, goes darker. Then there is nothing.

You don't feel it drag you out of the log's mouth by the neck, or up the slope into the thick ferns.

You don't wake.

You die there, in the ferns above the hollow, on your first night in Davrar.
