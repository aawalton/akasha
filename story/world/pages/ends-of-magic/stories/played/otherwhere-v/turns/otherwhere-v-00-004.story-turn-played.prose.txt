You turn from the clawed trunk and start back toward the fallen log, quietly. *Get inside. Narrow passage, all that rot. Whatever that was, maybe it won't get at me in there, maybe it won't even smell me.*

The log's mouth is eighty yards off, down the slope and across the hollow, and you can't see it. You can't see anything. You go by feel. Ferns to your waist, cool and brushing. Moss under them, then a root, then moss again. The hard edge of a bark scale under your heel, and you shift your weight off it before it cuts.

You set each bare foot down slowly, and keep your breath slow and shallow through your mouth.

The green specks drift around you in the black. They don't light anything. They are only points to steer between, a few here, one there, a fern edge glowing and fading as one brushes past.

Your mouth is dry. You haven't drunk anything since you landed.

Around you the ferns tick and click as you pass, falling quiet right at your feet and starting up again behind you.

About halfway to the log, the ticking behind you stops.

It stops in a patch. You hear the edges of it as you go. The quiet is not staying where it started. It is moving. It slides along behind you, low in the ferns, and the ticking dies ahead of it and picks back up where it has been.

It is closing in on your back. It is coming on faster than you walk.

The pieces fit together all at once: something large is moving under the ferns, toward you, from behind.

Ahead, somewhere in the dark, the log's mouth is still forty yards off.
