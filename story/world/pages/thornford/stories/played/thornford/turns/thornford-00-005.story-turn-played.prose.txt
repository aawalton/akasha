"I'm definitely thinking, but it won't be your name. If you want me to know it, you'll have to tell me. I have a feeling it will be something you will quite enjoy."

She laughs down at you, bright and sudden.

"Shaestrel," she says. "You may have that one for nothing."

She crouches among the leaves until her face is level with yours, close enough that you smell cut grass again. Her eyes go over your face slowly, the way you'd run a seam for a dropped stitch.

"Enjoy it, will I," she says. "Hm. You'll ask me for something with a door in it."

She stands, takes the grey cloak from the stool and swings it round her shoulders, and the leaves stay where they fell.

"I'll come back tomorrow when the lamps are lit, for the first fitting. Have something for me to stand in."

At the door she slides the bolt back with one finger.
