[A fix to the last turn: the evening's work ends at a quarter to eleven.]

*Okay, fae rules.*

*What better needle for fae silk than the stem of a leaf?*

You crouch by the birch leaves on the floor. They're still fresh, green and supple, as if they came off the tree an hour ago. You choose the one with the longest, stiffest stem, nip the leaf away with your snips and pare the cut end to a point with your penknife. There's no eye, so you split the blunt end with the tip of the knife and pinch a fine silk thread into the split.

Then you take up the cut end of the silk again.

The point touches the weave, and the weave doesn't flinch. It lets the tip in a hair's breadth, as easily as any cloth.

Then the stem bends. You ease it, and it folds over in the middle, soft as a blade of grass. A bead of green sap runs out of the crease onto the silk.

When you dab it with your thumb, it spreads. A green smear the size of your thumbnail sits in the grey-green now, darker than the cloth around it, and it won't lift.
