Her smile opens a little, enough to show the edge of her teeth.

She hitches herself up onto the edge of the cutting table beside the silk, and her boots swing clear of the floor. The silk slides with her. Where it folds it turns green, and goes grey again as it settles.

"I'm of the Spring Court," she says. "You'll have guessed that from the door."

She tips her head at the bolt, still shot home in its bracket.

"So here's my bargain. You make me a gown from this silk, with your own two hands, and have it done by moonrise on Beltane night. Do that, and you may ask me for one thing."

She holds up a finger.

"And if it's not done by moonrise, you owe the Spring Court a year of sewing on our side of the ford."

She wets the pad of her thumb on her tongue and holds it out to you, the way a bargain's struck at the cattle market.
