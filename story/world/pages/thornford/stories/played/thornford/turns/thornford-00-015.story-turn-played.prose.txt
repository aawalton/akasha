"Just in time for the final fitting. Let me know if you need any final alterations."

Shaestrel tugs the cord at each shoulder, and her dress falls about her feet in a drift of birch leaves. She steps out of them.

You lift the gown from its hook and hold it open, and she steps into it. The silk slides up over her hips with a sound like rain starting. You do up the hooks down her back one at a time, from the waist to the nape. It sits close at every seam: the shoulder where you moved it, the sides under the arm, the waist you pinned twice.

You kneel with the pin cushion and set the hem on her, turning it and stitching it to just above the floor while she stands still for once.

When you stand, she turns before the small glass on the wall. In the shadow the silk is grey. Where the lamplight catches it as she moves, it goes the green of new leaves.

"No alterations," she says. "Not one."

She turns from the glass to you.

"I'm in the silk now, seamstress."

She steps close enough that the hem brushes your feet.
