"I guess its time for me to start on the silk then."

You lift the calico off over her head, careful of the pins. She shivers once, takes her grey cloak from the stool and wraps it round herself. Then, instead of going to the door, she hitches herself up onto the end of the cutting table and tucks her bare feet under the hem.

"Go on, then," she says. "I'd like to see the first cut."

You unpick the toile seam by seam and carry every pinned change onto the paper: the shoulder set back, the sides taken in under the arm, the hem marked to the floor.

Then you unroll the silk. It runs out across the table grey and green, and you smooth it flat with both hands and pin the paper to it piece by piece. The shears are cold and heavy in your hand. The first cut goes in along the bodice with a long soft sound, and the silk falls away from the blades clean.

She watches the whole while with her chin on her knees, and her eyes follow the shears.

The lamp burns lower. When the last piece of the skirt comes free, a length of silk is left on the table, about as long as your arm.

Shaestrel slides off the table, gathers it up and drapes it over your shoulders. It's cool, and lighter than it looks.

"That suits your hair," she says.
