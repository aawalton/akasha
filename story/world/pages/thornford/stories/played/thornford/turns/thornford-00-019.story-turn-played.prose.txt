You kiss her hungrily, testing and exploring her mouth, and she meets you with both hands in your hair. She tastes cool and green, like rain on new leaves. When you break apart you're both breathing hard.

"I feel a bit overdressed for this dance. Would you help me with that?"

She laughs, low in her throat.

"Turn about's fair, seamstress."

She finds your apron strings behind you and pulls them loose, one and then the other, and lets the apron drop. Then she starts on the buttons of your bodice at the collar. She takes them one at a time, unhurried, the way you took her hooks. Each time one opens she leans in and kisses the skin it leaves bare: the hollow of your throat, your breastbone, lower. Her mouth is cool and her breath is warm, and by the last button you are holding your breath.

She eases the dress off your shoulders. It slides down your arms and falls on top of the silk on the floor.

Your shift is the last of it. She gathers the hem in both fists and draws it up, slowly, over your hips and your ribs, and you lift your arms so she can pull it free over your head. She drops it behind her without looking.

You stand bare in the lamplight from the shop door, your hair loose down your back.

She looks at you for a long moment. Her eyes go over you slowly, the way they went over your face the first night when she was reading it, and her lips part.

Then she sits down on the edge of your narrow bed and holds out her hand.

"Tell me what you want, Nala."
