You kiss her hungrily, testing and exploring her mouth, and she meets you with both hands in your hair. When you break apart you're both breathing hard.

"I feel a bit overdressed for this dance. Would you help me with that?"

She laughs, low in her throat.

Her cool fingers find your apron strings first and pull them loose. Then the buttons of your bodice, one at a time from the collar down, slower than she needs to, the way you did her hooks. She draws your dress off over your head and drops it on top of the silk on the floor.

Out in the shop, the lamp burns down.

Later, you lie tangled together under the blanket in your narrow bed. The Row is quiet outside. Her head is on your shoulder, and her hair is cool against your neck and smells of cut grass.

She traces one of the freckles on your collarbone with a fingertip.

"This one's shaped like the ford," she says. "Tomorrow I'll show you the real one."

She kisses it.
