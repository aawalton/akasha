Nala is a seamstress in Thornford, a market town in a Highland valley where a stone ford crosses the river Elt. She rents a narrow shop on Weavers' Row with a cutting table, a treadle machine and a back room she sleeps in. She is good at her work and behind on her rent.

Four times a year, on the quarter days, the border between the mortal valley and the fae country thins at the ford, and the fae courts come across to trade. They buy what they cannot make: iron tools, salt, bread, and cloth sewn by mortal hands. Fae glamour can make anything look like anything, but it fades at dawn. A mortal-made thing stays what it is.

Shaestrel is a fae of the Spring Court. She is quick, teasing, and fond of wagers, she speaks with a Highland lilt, and she can make a face, a coat or a room look however she likes for a night. She can read what is likely to happen to a person the way a weaver reads thread, though the reading is often wrong in the details. Fae cannot lie outright, so she says a great deal that is true and arranged to mislead.

Three days before Beltane, Shaestrel comes into Nala's shop after closing with a bolt of grey-green silk and a commission. She needs a gown for the Spring Court's Beltane dance, made by mortal hands so that it will still be a gown at dawn. She does not offer money. She offers a bargain: finish the gown by moonrise on Beltane night and Nala may ask Shaestrel for one thing. Fail, and Nala owes the Spring Court a year of sewing on the far side of the ford. Fae bargains bind both sides exactly as they are worded.

Nala does not yet know why Shaestrel cannot buy a gown from the town's established dressmakers, why the silk will not take a needle on some days, or who else in Thornford has an interest in the Spring Court looking foolish at the dance. Someone does.

The story is a three-day race to finish the gown, and a slow-burn romance between two women who are each trying to get the better of the other. Whether they come to trust each other, and whether it becomes more than that, is decided in play. The fae are dangerous, the town's dressmakers' guild is jealous of its trade, and a bargain read carelessly can cost Nala a year. The mechanics decide whether an action works.
