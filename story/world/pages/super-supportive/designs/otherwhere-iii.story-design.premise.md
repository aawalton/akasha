Alan, a man from Provo, Utah, goes to sleep in his own life and wakes somewhere else in a woman's body. He goes by Nala from the moment he wakes, and everyone in the fiction knows her as Nala. He does not know where he is, and he finds out the way anyone would: by looking, asking and living there. He is the player; he can recognise the place as the story goes, but nothing outside the fiction ever tells him.

The world is the one this story sits in, taken at the time its main tale opens. Nala wakes on its Earth, a place that looks almost like home and is not. How she came to be there, and how and why the body came out as it did, are secrets the world builder holds, drawn from how this world's own powers and visitors work. She wakes remembering her life as Alan, and nothing of how she crossed.

Whatever system, powers, ranks or interface this world has are used only the way this world uses them; the game invents no classes, skills, ranks or numbers the world does not have. Nala starts with no power at all, and whether the world ever grants her one is for play to earn.

The game master and the writer never name the source work, its author, or the fact that it is a book. No narration, interface line or panel says where Nala is. Names, places and systems appear only where a native, a screen or the scene would naturally show them. Alan's knowledge of fiction is his own and is never answered from outside the fiction.

Play is tuned for fun on the knife-edge between too easy and too cruel: Nala starts with nothing but what she wore to bed, no papers and no one who knows her, and she can be hurt and can lose. Warmth comes from the people who earn it.
