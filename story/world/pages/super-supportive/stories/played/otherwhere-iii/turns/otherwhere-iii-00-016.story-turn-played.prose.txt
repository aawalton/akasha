You step aside, over by the big plants along the wall, and wait. The crickets go on somewhere in the leaves near your head. The tourists take their pictures: one of the desk, one of each other with the desk behind them, grinning, one more of the desk.

Behind the desk, one grey hand moves. It passes over something below the counter, out of your sight, and comes back to rest.

A minute later the tourists both frown at their phones. One shakes his. The other holds hers up high, turning it this way and that.

"WiFi's dead," he says.

"Figures," she says, and they grumble their way across the floor and out through the glass doors, and the doors sigh shut behind them.

The lobby is empty. There are the crickets, and under them a faint, even hum that comes from the golden ropes.

You cross the terrazzo to the desk. Your boots squeak on it, every step, loud in the quiet. Up close the ropes are brighter, and they move on their own, slow, like weed in a current. The skin where they wrap his wrists is raw, and it doesn't look new. It looks old, and sore, and long past healing.

You stop at the counter and keep your voice low.

"You don't know me, but I have a feeling I can trust you."

"And I have a tale to tell that I don't think you've ever heard before. Could we speak privately?"

His black eyes stay on the monitors. The four slits of his nose flare, all together, and you hear the soft pull of air as he breathes in. Breathes you in.

The pause goes on. It goes on longer than it needs to.

His head tilts a fraction toward you.

"Privately." His voice is high, higher than you expected, and under it there is something like glass breaking, a long way off.

He lifts one wrist an inch off the desk. The glowing ropes sway with it, drifting, like jellyfish.

"I am bound to this desk. This lobby is as private as I am allowed." The wrist settles again. "And now it is empty. I made it so."

His eyes stay on the screens.

"I am bored, little stranger, and you are not boring. Tell me your tale."
