"Thank you," you say, "but I'm on my own for now."

"Yesterday I would have had you call my boyfriend, but that ship has sunk."

You look up at her.

"Thank you for your kindness. You've really been a lifesaver today."

Denise nods, once. She lets the phone go. She doesn't ask again.

Instead she crosses to the water fountain by the wall, where a paper towel dispenser hangs, and tears off a sheet. She takes a pen from the pocket of her scrubs, flattens the towel against the wall and writes on it, pressing hard so the pen won't tear through. Then she comes back and folds it into your hand and closes your fingers over it.

You can see the writing through the thin paper. DENISE, in capitals. Under it a phone number.

"You need anything, you call that," she says. "Doesn't matter the hour."

"Our social worker comes on at eight. I'll have Marcus send her straight to you." She tips her head at the triage window. "She's good. Beds, clothes, the works. And my break's around nine, so I'll come see you."

She goes. On her way back up the rows she stops at the triage window and leans in, and says something to Marcus too low to carry this far. He nods.

At the STAFF ONLY door Denise turns and lifts a hand to you. Then she pushes through, and the door swings shut behind her.

The clock over the doors reads 5:29.

On the chair beside you sit the clogs and the folded fleece. The paper towel is in your fist, soft already at the creases.
