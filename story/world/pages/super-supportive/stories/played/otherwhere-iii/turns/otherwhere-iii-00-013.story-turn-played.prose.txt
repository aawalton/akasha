You turn back to the screen and keep watching. The show goes round again.

Skiff on the pier, dripping, the microphones pushed at him. Hannah Elber's photo, smiling, and under it the date of the memorial: FEBRUARY 13. The weather map, the lake blue and white, snow tapering by noon. The turquoise water and the white towers. The lawyer pointing out of the screen. The classroom with its notebooks.

Along the bottom the crawl comes round with them: REMINDER — ALL AVOWED MUST REGISTER.

Outside the glass doors the grey goes to white. The snow is falling thinner now, fewer flakes, drifting more than driving.

The waiting room fills a little. A young mother comes in with a toddler on her hip, the child red-cheeked and limp and too quiet, his head on her shoulder. She sits two rows up and rocks him. After her an old man in a flat cap, holding one hand up against his chest in a thick wad of bandage gone brown at the edge.

At 8:15 by the clock over the doors, a woman comes out of the inner door with a hospital badge on a clip and a folder under her arm. She looks once round the room and walks straight down the rows to your corner.

She is somewhere in her mid-thirties, her dark hair tied back tight, and her eyes move quickly, taking you in: the blanket, the bracelet, the socks, the clogs and the fleece on the chair beside you. She hooks a chair out of the row in front with her foot, swings it round to face you and sits.

"Nala? I'm Priya, the social worker. Marcus and Denise both flagged you for me."

She opens the folder on her knee but doesn't look down at it.

"I'm not going to grill you about last night," she says. "I care about tonight."

"So, first thing. Do you have anywhere safe to sleep tonight?"
