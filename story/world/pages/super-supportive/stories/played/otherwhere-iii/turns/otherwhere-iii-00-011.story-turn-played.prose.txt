You settle down into the chair under the hot blanket and turn your eyes up to the TV.

It is a morning show out of Chicago, the sound off, captions running white on black along the bottom. Two anchors at a desk. A skyline behind them, lit up in the dark.

Across the top of the screen, small, a caption gives the date: SATURDAY, JANUARY 31, 2037.

The lead story comes round. SKIFF HAULS EARTH-SHAPER FROM BENEATH LAKE — SUSPECT IN INTENSIVE CARE. The picture cuts to a pier in grey half-light. A man stands in the middle of a knot of reporters, soaked through, his hair plastered flat, water still running off him. He looks strained. Microphones crowd in at his face and he talks into them, and the captions lag behind his mouth.

Along the very bottom a crawl slides by under everything. Hero sightings, one after another, by neighborhood. Then: REMINDER — ALL AVOWED MUST REGISTER.

A new segment. A photo of a woman, smiling, in a uniform with a patch on the shoulder. SIX MONTHS SINCE HERO HANNAH ELBER ANSWERED A SYSTEM SUMMONS. SHE HAS NOT RETURNED. The captions go on: an A-rank Adjuster; once battlefield support to Chicago hero Arjun Thomas. Footage of the two of them together, somewhere with smoke. Then: A MEMORIAL IS SET FOR FEBRUARY 13 ON ANESIDORA ISLAND IF SHE HAS NOT RETURNED BY THEN.

Weather. A map of the lake, blue and white. Snow tapering by noon. High of 19. Colder tonight. More snow Monday.

Ads. Turquoise water and white towers: VISIT ANESIDORA. A lawyer in a grey suit pointing out of the screen: HURT IN A SUPERHUMAN INCIDENT? YOU MAY BE OWED. A classroom of adults with notebooks: LANGUAGE AND CULTURE CLASSES AT THE ARTONAN CONSULATE — ENROLLING NOW.

Sports. The Bulls lost at home last night. The Blackhawks play tonight.

Then the anchors again, and the skyline, and the lead story comes round a second time. You watch the whole cycle through again, all of it, the pier and the crawl and the smiling woman and the towers over the water.

Through the glass doors at the front, the dark outside goes slowly grey.

At 6:40 by the wall clock, a nurse leans out of the inner door with a clipboard. "Nala Arthur?"

She takes you back to a bay with a curtain on a rail and a paper-covered bed. A doctor comes in, young and tired, a woman with her hair in a knot and shadows under her eyes. She sits on a rolling stool, peels off your hospital socks and looks at your toes under the light. She presses each one with her thumb and watches the color come back.

"Wiggle them for me."

They wiggle. They sting, but they move.

"Frostnip," she says. "Nothing worse. Keep them warm and dry and you'll be fine." She types a line into a laptop on a cart and stands. She doesn't ask about papers.

By 6:55 you are back in your corner chair, the blanket round you again, cooler now.

At 7:05 a woman sits down in the chair beside you. She is in her fifties, with reading glasses on a chain and a tablet in her lap. The lanyard round her neck reads REGISTRATION.

"Ms. Arthur?" She turns the tablet a little toward herself and frowns at it, not unkindly. "I ran your name and birth date for insurance, and I can't find a match anywhere."

She looks up at you over the glasses.

"Do you have a Social Security number I can try? Or any ID at all?"
