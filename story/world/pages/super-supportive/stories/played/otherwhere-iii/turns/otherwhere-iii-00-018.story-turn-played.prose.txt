"Gorgon," you say. "That's right. It was driving me mad, not remembering."

You put both hands on the counter.

"And the year is 2037 by Earth's reckoning? Then I've come both over ten years into the future, and likely across a parallel timeline as well."

You look at the black eyes, which are on the monitors and not on you.

"Okay. I can't tell you how much it means to me just to be believed." You let out a breath. "I'd forgotten you could sense emotions as well, or I'd have had even more reason to come to you."

He keeps watching the screens.

"My biggest problem is that I don't know what I should ask for. But I know that you are wise beyond human understanding, and kinder to us than we have any right to expect." You stop, and start again. "I'm not anxious to go back where I came from. This world is fascinating to me. But my aspirations go beyond normal human desires. I know the true battle being fought, and the price paid by those who wage it. I know you are no friend to chaos. I want to become a knight. Not just an Avowed, and certainly not a mortal. And I don't know how to even start."

He has gone flat. It happens at the word *knight*.

A dry click sounds in his throat. Then a second one. After that he says nothing at all.

"Not that," he says at last. "Not here. Not to me."

The golden ropes flicker along the length of the desk, quick, like water over a stone.

"Ask me for what I am permitted to give."

You press him. You ask the question another way, and another. The click comes, dry and small, and the click is all that gets past his throat.

He glances once at the bank of monitors. At the empty lobby in them, all forty feet of terrazzo with nobody on it.

"Nothing said here is kept," he says.

Then, level, the way a readout is level:

"The Contract will not choose you. That door shut while you grew. Selection comes once, between fifteen and seventeen. Everyone in this city knows it."

He keeps his black eyes on the monitors. He does not look at you.

With two grey fingers he draws a leaflet out of a stack at the corner of the desk and sets it on the counter. CONSULATE 4 — SPRING CLASSES, it says. Artonan Life. Logograms. Culture. Beginners Wordchaining. Under that, in small print: free to residents, weekday evenings downtown, and some classes carry credit.

From the same stack he takes an enrollment form and fills in the top line in neat block letters.

"Nala Arthur," he says. "That is all they ask of anyone here."

He rolls a pen across the counter to you with one finger.

"The office upstairs takes those Monday. The desk here takes them today."

The crickets chirr in the plants along the wall. The ropes hum. Across the street, the clock on the bank sign reads 9:40.

Weekday evenings, downtown. At Harbor House your bed is held only till seven.
