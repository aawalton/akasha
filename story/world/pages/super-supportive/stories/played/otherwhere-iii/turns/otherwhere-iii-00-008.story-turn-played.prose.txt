His face softens at once, all of it together, the way a big man's face does.

"Aw, I'm sorry, hon."

He backspaces, three quick taps, and types the new year. Then he glances up at your face once more, and nods, a small nod, to himself.

He rolls his chair to the side of the window and reaches through a gap for your arm. He wraps a cuff round it. It looks too big going on, your arm is so thin. He clips a plastic sensor on your fingertip, then runs a thermometer across your forehead, brow to temple, cool and smooth.

The cuff squeezes, hard, and lets go with a sigh.

He reads the thermometer and hums.

"Ninety-six point one. You're chilled through, girl." He points down at your feet. "Wet socks off. Let's not keep that ice on you."

The socks come off grey and sodden, heavy with slush, and your toes underneath are red and puffy and stinging. Marcus holds out a plastic bag, and Denise's socks go into it, and he knots it shut and hands you a new pair: thin, blue, with rubber treads in rows on the soles. They are dry. It is the best thing you have felt all night.

He gets up. Behind him is a steel cabinet with a glass door, stacked with folded white blankets. He opens it and heat breathes out, and he pulls one down and comes round and drapes it over your shoulders. It is hot, almost too hot, like laundry straight from the dryer.

He takes your wrist and snaps a paper bracelet round it. The print on it reads ARTHUR, NALA. 01/22/2016. A barcode.

"Frostnip's gonna wait behind the worse stuff, so it could be a while," he says. "Grab any chair."

The chairs sit in rows under the muted TV. A clock on the wall above the doors reads 5:14.

On the TV, a shot from high over the lake: grey ice to the horizon, broken in a long jagged line. The captions scroll underneath. Skiff's chase is still the lead.

The man is still asleep across his three chairs with his coat over his face. Somewhere down the row, someone coughs.
