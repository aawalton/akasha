"No," you say. "Not yet."

Priya nods. She doesn't look surprised.

"Then let's fix that first." She takes her phone out of her pocket, thumbs a number from memory and holds it to her ear.

"Hi, it's Priya at Uptown Memorial. I need a bed tonight for a woman, name of Nala Arthur."

She listens. Her eyes stay on you while she does, steady. "Great, thanks," she says, and hangs up.

"Harbor House," she says. "Women's shelter, in a church hall. It's on Sunnyside, ten minutes' walk from here. Doors open at five; dinner's at six. They hold your bed till seven. Miss it and it's gone." She taps the phone against her knee. "They'll only ask your name."

She pulls a card out of the folder, a hospital card with the logo in the corner, and writes on the back of it, fast. The shelter's address. Under it, a phone number and the words MY DESK. She hands it to you.

"The catch is the day," she says. "They won't let anybody in till five. The Uptown library's open nine to five. It's free, it's warm, and they leave you alone."

She gets up and goes off through the inner door.

When she comes back she has a white plastic bag, knotted at the handles and bulging, and a plastic card the size of a credit card. She sets the bag down at your feet and opens it for you to see. A puffy coat, dark green, rolled tight. Grey sweatpants. A pack of underwear, still in the plastic. Thick socks. At the bottom, a pair of snow boots, scuffed at the toes, laces tucked in.

She holds out the card. "The card's got twenty bucks on it. That'll get you around for a while."

She tips her head toward a door in the side wall with a sign on it. "There's a bathroom right there if you want to change."

Then she clicks her pen and tucks it away in the folder, and looks at you straight.

"What else do you need from me before you go?"
