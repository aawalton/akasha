You remember dying. A car, a wet road, your parents in the front seats. You were young, a few weeks short of college, and the three of you went together.

You remember the Ocean of Souls after: warm currents, other souls from other worlds, time enough to say goodbye before your parents rose to be born again.

Then a shadow that should not have been there. Claws in your soul, teeth, a voice that laughed. A demon swallowed you whole and rode you up toward the light, and the light burned it far worse than it burned you.

In the dark of the womb it was weak and mewling, and you tore it apart until nothing was left of it but pieces sinking into you. Words lit up behind your closed eyes. Welcome to the Myriad Paths. You kicked twice to prove you could choose, and for months you counted and recited in the dark, and watched your numbers climb.

You were born into a stone room among people in skull masks, who drew blood on your forehead. Days later steel rang in the halls. The woman who bore you held a knife to your throat, and pushed it in, and the world went black.

You woke against a stranger's chest, your neck bound tight, the world a bouncing blur of green and white. Snow on your face. Two days of cold and swaying, milk dripped onto your lips, and a voice you could not understand murmuring over you.

Now the swaying stops. Boots on wooden steps, a door, and warmth rolls over you: two fires roaring, pipe smoke, pine. A deep voice calls out in the language you do not have yet, and an old man's voice answers it, sharp and then glad.

They talk over you. The old man's voice goes hard at one word you keep hearing, and you feel his eyes on you, cold and careful.

Then hands take you from the woman who carried you. Softer hands. A blur of dark hair and a face close enough to see: bright blue eyes, and a smile that changes the whole room.

She touches your hair and says a word, softly, then again, looking right at you. "Nala." And once more, like a promise. "Nala."

Behind her the old man's shoulders sag, and he says something that sounds like giving in. The riders speak a little longer, then their boots go back out into the snow.

A notification blinks at the corner of your blurry sight. Where your Status said Unnamed, it now says Nala.
