"Okay..." you say, and swing your feet down off the velvet. The rug takes your shoes, thick and soft, and you stand up off the chaise. For a second the chandelier tilts, then settles.

You look across at the desk. "I'm on my feet," you say to the concierge. "Check me in?"

She inclines her head, a small exact movement. "Very good, sir."

A leather guest book lies on the marble in front of her, bound in oxblood to match the chairs. She opens it, smooths the page flat with two fingers, and turns the book around to face you. The page is cream and nearly bare. One line is written on it in a neat black hand, every letter upright and evenly spaced, and from where you stand you can read it plainly.

It is your name.

"You are expected, sir," she says. "You are our only guest."

She folds her hands on the open book, one over the other, and straightens a fraction. When she speaks again it is in the voice of someone reading terms from a card.

"To check in, sir, you will make me come." Her grey eyes stay level on yours. She tips her chin toward the luggage cart. "And you will make her come."

The red mouth does not so much as twitch.

"Then you will come inside one of us. Which of us is your choice." She lets a beat pass. "When that is done, the gate to the stairs opens. Not before."

She says it without a blush, without a flicker, exactly as she would give you the checkout time.

The bellhop laughs out loud. It bursts out of her, bright and helpless, and she has to catch the cart's brass rail with one hand. "Oh my God. You said *come* like it was a line in a ledger." She turns her grin on you. "She rehearsed that. I swear she's been practising it on the service bell. *Ding.* You will make me come. *Ding.*"

"You are on the staff," the concierge says, dry as dust, "and the staff do not heckle the guests."

"Staff heckle the staff, though." The bellhop pushes off the cart. She crosses the few steps to you with her hips rolling under that tight red skirt, the gap in her teeth showing in her grin, and stops close. Close enough that you can see the freckles go on down her throat and under her collar. Close enough to smell her, something warm and sweet like sugared almonds.

She lifts one hand to the top brass button of her red jacket and thumbs it open. The jacket gives a little. The next button down takes the strain instead, pulled tight across her heavy tits, the brass straining in its buttonhole.

"Don't let her make it sound like paperwork," she tells you. "So." She tilts her head, green eyes bright. "Which of us are you starting on?"
