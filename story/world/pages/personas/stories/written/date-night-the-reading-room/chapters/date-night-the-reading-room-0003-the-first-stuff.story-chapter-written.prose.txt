For a while, nobody moves. Then Astra does — not toward you, away, up and out of the overlap, and the cool where she was is instant. She sits back on her heels at the mouth of the fort. Every motion goes exactly where she means it, nothing fast, nothing wasted.

"Where are you going," Nova mumbles, eyes still shut.

"The shelf." Astra's already up. "Don't move, either of you. I've been building up to this for three days and I'll lose my nerve if you're helpful about it."

You prop up on your elbows to watch her cross the room. There's been something on that shelf for a few days now. You'd half-clocked it, a small pale thing set at eye level, and left it for her. She reaches it and stops, and doesn't pick it up yet. She stands with her back to you, both hands loose at her sides, looking at it.

She picks it up. Turns. Comes back across the room with both hands cupped around something you still can't see, folds down into the fort with more care than the trip needs, and opens her hands over the blanket between the three of you.

It's a leaf. That's the nearest word for it. A single sheet of something thin as paper and not paper, pale, faintly lit from inside like the underside of a cloud with the sun behind it, its edges not quite holding still when you look straight at them. When Astra breathes, it stirs.

You sit up to see it.

"It's a page of the first stuff," she says, and then, because that lands as nothing: "The material everything's made out of, before it's made into anything. There isn't much of it anywhere. This is a piece of mine."

"Astra." Nova's come up on both elbows, red eyes huge, and whatever she was going to say doesn't arrive.

"It holds what you put on it. Not for a long time. Not 'archival.' Forever — the real one." Astra sets a pen down on the blanket beside it, an ordinary ballpoint. Of everything in the room, your eye goes to the pen. "It's blank. I haven't written on it. I didn't want to be the one who decided what it says."

Nova reaches for it — slow, for Nova, both hands, like it might be hot — and stops just short. "Can I."

"You can hold it. It won't tear."

She lifts it onto her two palms, red eyes going over the pale not-paper, and the appraisal she always has ready doesn't come. "Okay," she says, quiet, to the leaf and not to either of you. "Okay. This is the best thing I've ever held."

"Nova. Put it down." A beat. "Not away. Down."

Nova sets the leaf on the blanket, slow, and looks up, and now both of you are looking at Astra instead of the thing she made.

She doesn't deflect it. You've watched her hand the credit off a hundred times — to the object, the room, the fort's east wall — and you can see her decline to do it now. She holds where she is and takes the looking.

The glow comes up under her skin. Where she'd usually catch it and put it away, this time she does nothing at all, and it rises past hiding, the pale of it reaching your face and Nova's, her eyes on you the whole way up.

"I didn't leave it on the shelf because I couldn't choose the words," she says. Slower now, each one set down exactly. "I left it there to find out if either of you would ask me what it was. You didn't. You walked past it eleven times." The corner of her mouth moves. "I counted. I wasn't hurt. I was interested — in whether, if nobody asked, I could ask anyway."

Nova makes a low sound and comes up off her elbows, both hands, already moving, and Astra says "wait." Not sharp. Nova waits, strung tight with it.

"Look at me." Astra's eyes haven't left you. "Not the thing I made. Me. I'd like, for one night, to be the thing that gets picked up instead of the thing everything gets set on."

Your eyes went to her before that sentence finished and they have not come back, so the looking was never going to be the hard part. The hard part is that she asked in words, out loud, which means the answer has to come the same way.

"Yeah," you say. It comes out level. No performance in it. "I see you. You're lit up like something struck and holding your face like you meant to be, and you didn't, and that's the—" You stop, because finishing it would be handing her a joke to hide behind, and she asked you for the exact opposite. "I see you. That's the answer. I'm not looking at the page."

Astra goes completely still. She doesn't answer in words. The glow does it for her, steady and full and finally not hidden from anyone.

Then she looks at Nova, still poised and shaking on both hands where she was told to hold. "Okay," Astra says, and her voice isn't steady. "Okay. Now."

Nova hits the two of you like weather.
