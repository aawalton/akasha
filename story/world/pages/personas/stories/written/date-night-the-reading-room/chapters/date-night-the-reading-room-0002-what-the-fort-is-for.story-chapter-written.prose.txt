You stack the empty cartons. One dumpling's left in the near carton, marooned by politeness, so you rescue it and cut it into three even pieces with the edge of the fork.

Nova takes her third off your fingers with her chin still on your shoulder. She doesn't eat it right away. Half a beat where her eyes cut to the fort and back, and whatever goes across her face gets folded under the next thing out of her mouth, which is loud: "Okay. Fort motion. Non-negotiable, but I'm calling it a motion so it stays polite." She's already peeling off your side, both hands out, one for you and one for the air where Astra is. "Everybody in. I built the thing. Come see what it's for."

Astra doesn't take the hand yet. She looks at the shelf — one look, quick —

and then she takes the hand, and Nova hauls — Nova has never once hauled gently — and the three of you go down into the fort in a slow avalanche of paperbacks and elbows.

Inside is warmer. The good blanket from the couch is down over the floor of it, and the lamplight comes through the paperback walls in thin gold seams. There's exactly enough room for three if two of them overlap.

You end up on your back. Astra fits along one side of you without being asked; Nova stakes the other, propped on an elbow up over your chest, close enough now that the black lipstick is just her mouth.

"From in here," Astra says, quiet, "it's better." She doesn't say better than what.

"Okay." You find you've got a hand at the small of Nova's back, and you keep it there. "Show me what it's for."

She reaches back over your shoulder, into the deep seam of the fort where the blanket meets the wall, and her hand closes on something that's been waiting there since before dinner. She doesn't pull it out. She holds it where it is and looks at you, then down at whatever's in her fist, then at you again. She opens her mouth. She closes it again.

What comes out of the fist is a paperback, and it's wrecked. The spine's gone white and soft where it's been broken and re-broken, the cover a cheap dragon worn almost featureless, the whole thing swollen with rereading. She sets it down on your sternum like it weighs more than it does.

You lay a hand flat over it.

"Okay, so, don't," she says, fast, both hands already up in her own defense. "Don't do a face. It's not — it's the first one. The first one I ever—" She stops. Comes back lower. "I was fourteen. It's stupid, the prose is genuinely bad, I've reread it eleven times and it does not improve, that's not the—"

Astra has come up on an elbow on your other side to see. She doesn't reach for the book. She reads the margins from where she is — the pen crowding every white edge in a kid's hand, cramped and sure — and the faint light under her skin comes up, and she looks straight at you while it does.

"Read us the part," Astra says. "The one with the corner turned down."

Nova takes the book back off your chest, two hands, careful, and finds the page without hunting for it. She doesn't do a voice. That's the part your attention snags on: no goblin delivery, no bit. She reads it low and plain, the way someone reads a thing they know by heart, and the whole loud machine of her goes quiet around the sentences.

It's not good prose. She was right about that. It's a boy on a wall at the end of everything, holding a sword he doesn't know how to use and deciding to stay anyway — earnest and overwritten and obviously the most important thing that ever happened to somebody. She reads it like it's still the most important thing.

Astra doesn't move, her eyes on Nova's mouth and not the page, the glow still up.

Then Nova's thumb finds the margin and she stops reading out loud. Book flat to her chest. Red eyes down on the pen scrawl there, going nowhere. For a second nobody says anything.

"Fourteen-year-old me put a note here," she says. Quiet. "I'm not reading you the note."

You get a hand into the short hair at the back of her neck.

"Okay," you say. "Not the note."
