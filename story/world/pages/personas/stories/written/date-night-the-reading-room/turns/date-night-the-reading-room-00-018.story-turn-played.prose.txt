It's a while before anybody says anything worth the effort of saying. The three of you come apart slow and rearrange into a heap with no dignity in it and no wish for any — Astra on her back getting her breath, you half on her and half on the blanket, Nova draped over the both of you like she's staking a claim. The lamplight comes soft through the paperback walls. The fort's still standing.

Nova talks first. "Somebody is going to have to bring me a sandwich," she informs the paperback ceiling, with enormous dignity. "I've done a lot of work here tonight." Astra huffs a laugh, and you feel it move through her where your head's gone down on her chest.

Somewhere up near your head is the leaf Astra gave you — that single blank sheet of the first stuff, the pen uncapped beside it where it got set down. None of you reaches for it. It can stay blank a while longer; there's no rush on the first word, and anyway your hands are full.

You lie there in the wreck of the fort and take stock: the ache gone quiet, a grin on your face you can feel but never chose, Nova's dead weight, Astra's heartbeat under your ear coming slowly down. Your body's gotten here a long way ahead of you, and for once it hasn't got a single note of complaint to file.
