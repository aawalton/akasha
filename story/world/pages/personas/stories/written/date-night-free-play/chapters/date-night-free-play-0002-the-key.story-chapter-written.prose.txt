The high-score table scrolls up while the song loads, and there it is, third from the top: AWN, 942,110, a date four months gone. Awen's tag. The night she took this exact cabinet off Aura and has not once let it drop since.

She barely notices her own name. What holds her is the company it's keeping — the two lines above and the eleven below, all Aura's, AUR and ARA and one Awen won't dignify by reading twice, the whole board three tags deep, same as the building itself. Except that one row. Four months. Awen has watched Aura wipe a stranger's record off the fighting cab the way you'd straighten a picture in your own hallway. And she left this. A worse score than a dozen of her own, sitting third from the top in her own house, and four months on she still hasn't cleared it.

Awen doesn't say anything about that. She files it — she's filing a lot tonight — with the carried thing and the covered sentence, the parts of Aura she hasn't finished reading yet.

"You're stalling." Aura drops onto the pad beside her, shoulder to shoulder, already thumbing the difficulty up past where it's polite. "I can hear you getting sentimental at my leaderboard. Stop dating the machine. Date me."

"I'm studying the competition."

"You're third."

"I'm studying how you'll feel in a minute about being second."

The song hits and the talking stops, because this one you can't do slow. Arrows come up faster than banter, and for a while there's only the double thud of four feet on two pads and the light off the screen going frantic on both their faces. Aura is better — Aura is always better, she's three thousand years of hand-eye and no patience for a thing she can't win — but Awen is beside her and keeping up, and every so often their elbows knock on a crossover and neither of them fixes their spacing.

The song lands its last note. Aura won it; the screen says so; neither of them looks at the screen.

They're both breathing hard. Aura's ponytail has fully lost its argument with the evening and she doesn't push it back, and she's looking at Awen instead of the board she just topped, still up on the balls of her feet like the song might come back around.

"Okay," Awen says. It comes out smaller than she meant it to. She reaches into her bag.

The wrapping's been off and on so many times the tape has stopped believing in itself; it gives without a sound. Then it's just the thing in her palm, in the blue light off the cabinet, and Awen makes herself hold it out flat instead of explaining it.

It's a key. Brass, because brass forgives being handled and only gets better for it. The blade she cut herself, by hand, on the mill in the back of the shop, to the pattern of one particular lock. The bow — the part you hold — she spent longer on than she'll say: an openwork wind rose, the eight points of the compass fanning from the center, and between them enamel the exact deep blue the cabinets throw in a dark room. It took her three tries to get the blue to lie down flat. She got it on the third.

She doesn't say what it opens. She wants to see if Aura gets there on her own.

Aura takes it, and doesn't say anything, which is the first strange thing — Aura fills a silence the way nature fills a vacuum, on principle, and she isn't filling this one. She turns the key over once. Her thumb finds the wind rose and stays on it a second, and Awen thinks, she likes the pretty part, everyone likes the pretty part —

and then Aura turns it back and looks at the blade. The working end. Runs the pad of her thumb along the cuts, the specific ones, the ones that fit exactly one lock in the world, and the fast goes out of her face.

"This is your shop." Not a question. Slow — one whole unhurried sentence, and Awen has never once heard her talk that slow. "You made me a key to your shop."

"Front and back — same key. The back door sticks; you have to lift while you turn. I keep meaning to plane it down and I never—"

She stops. She was about to say she never gets around to it, and it isn't even true — she likes the door exactly that stubborn. But Aura has closed her hand around the key, all the way, the wind rose vanishing into her fist, and she's looking at Awen, gone still on both feet for once, and a sentence about a sticking door is the least interesting thing in the building.

Aura looks down at her own closed hand, then back up. "You know I've got every record in this place." Her thumb moves once across the top of her fist. "Every machine, every tag. This is number one now. I'm not standing it on a shelf."

She's got a carabiner of keys at her hip — arcade, back room, the roll-gate out front — and she threads Awen's onto it right there, brass ringing once against steel.

"Okay." The ring drops back against her hip. "Put the bag down."

Awen puts the bag down.

And then Aura kisses her — or she kisses Aura; it has never once been possible to say who starts it, they have argued about this, there's no replay to check — and it isn't a first kiss, they're miles past first kisses, but there's a cold little edge of brass caught between them at the hip and Aura, the fastest thing in the world, is kissing her like there's no queue behind it and nowhere after this she has to be, and that

— and that does it. Awen's hand finds the back of Aura's neck sure as a light switch in the dark, no looking needed, and pulls her in past whatever careful thing she was still doing. The key's edge digs in between them and neither of them moves it. Aura makes a sound into her mouth that isn't a word, and Awen swallows it and gives one back.

Aura kisses all-in, no pacing left in her now — the slow gone, the fast back but aimed — both hands going up into Awen's hair and working the pin loose until the whole dark weight of it comes down, and she laughs against Awen's mouth, the pin still shut in her fist like she won it.

"That was a good pin," Awen says.

"I'll build you a better one." A lie; she couldn't build a sandwich; they both know it and neither cares. She's already walking Awen backward — past the dark cabinets, toward the stairs at the back — the bag left on the floor, the key cold at her hip. "I'll build you a whole—"

Awen kisses her quiet before she has to finish lying about it.

They go.
