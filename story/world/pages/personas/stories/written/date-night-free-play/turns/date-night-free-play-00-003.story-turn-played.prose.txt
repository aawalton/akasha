"That didn't happen."

Aura's out of the seat before the replay finishes drawing it, standing over the screen like standing closer will renegotiate the result. The photo-finish freezes both cars at the line and then, unkindly, gives it to the inside one by a bumper.

"It's on the board." Awen stays put a second longer, hands still on the wheel, the four degrees of slack gone quiet now she's stopped fighting it. "You can glare at it as long as you want. It'll keep saying the same thing."

"You drove the broken one."

"I drove the one nobody else wanted. That's just manners."

Aura makes a sound that isn't a word. Then, with enormous dignity, she reaches over and taps the cabinet twice — some private thing, machine to owner — and steps back. "Fine. Invoking the sulk. House rules: one song's length, and you don't get to talk to me during it."

"You wrote that rule so you'd have a legal way to lose."

"I wrote that rule because I'm a genius." She's already drifting toward the rhythm cab in the corner, the one whose marquee keeps ticking its broken little pulse, weight up on the balls of her feet again — the sulk apparently involves picking a song loud enough to be a statement. "Dumpling's still yours, by the way. Whenever. I pay my debts. I just do it resentfully."

"I know. It's one of your best qualities."

"It's my worst and my best at once. I'm complicated." She reaches the rhythm cab. Doesn't start it. Her hand goes flat on the panel and stays there, and for a second — one whole second, which on Aura is a geological event — she just stands with her back to Awen, like there's something to read in the flicker.

Awen watches the back of her not start the song.

Then Aura says, "Hey, so—" at completely normal speed. And stops. And starts a different sentence, faster, the real thing — whatever it was — folded back up and put away: "—you are going to want to sit down for how badly I'm about to lose to you at this next, too, out of spite."

"You said no talking during the sulk."

"The sulk hasn't started. I haven't picked the song." She finally jabs the panel, and the cabinet wakes up loud, some four-on-the-floor thing with too many arrows, and she rolls her shoulders like a woman cracking her knuckles before a heist. "Now it's started. Get over here and lose politely."
