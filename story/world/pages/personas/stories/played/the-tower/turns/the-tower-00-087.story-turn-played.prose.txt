You get the maul off your back before anything else — the whole weight of it, head and haft and the drum-core still lashed to its chain — and lay it down in the burning midden, head-down into the hottest of the coals you raised. Let it drink while you are gone, and maybe you come back to a weapon instead of a club. Your pack goes down beside it, the day's salvage and everything else with heft, left on this side so you can be light on the other. What crosses with you is the lantern, the dead man's snapped blade at your hip, and you.

Then you give yourself room. You walk it back from the edge — six steps, eight — until the drop is a black line at the far end of a short wet run and your boots have something to build speed on.

You do not let yourself weigh the width — that is how men die a stride short. You fix your eyes on the far lip, on the dry stone past the seam and not the seam itself, and you go.

The run is four strides and not one of them is clean; the wet stone drags at your boots and half your care goes just to keeping your feet under you. On the last stride your leg finds the lip and you throw yourself off it, up and out, everything in your legs spent in one shove —

— and then there is nothing under you at all.

Black air. The raw breath of the shaft rising past your face. For one stretched instant you are a weight in the dark —

— and the far edge comes up and hits you.

You take it badly and you take it alive. Your chest slams down on stone, your legs swing out into the black below, and for a heartbeat you hang half-over with your fingers clawing for purchase — then a palm catches a rib of rock and you haul, scraping and cursing, over onto solid ground, and lie there with your cheek on cold stone and your pulse going wild in your ears.

A stride to spare. No more than that. Behind you, across the seam, your fire burns small and orange with the maul standing up in it like a brand. Ahead — close now, once your legs will hold you — the wreck of the seat, the grey dead shape, the two things spilled in the muck beside it.

And in the dark just past them, the warmth that had lain so still comes unstuck. Something low shifts its weight across the stone — a heavy, dragging, wrong-sounding scrape, the sound of a thing that does not move well. Then it stops. Still, and listening, a few short steps away. Your maul is a bright point of heat on the wrong side of the drop, and you could not leap back to it now from a standing start if you tried.
