The trail climbs gently between the walls, and she's close enough that her sleeve brushes yours every few steps. You like it. On the next step you lean over and bump your shoulder gently into hers.

She glances at you, surprised, and then she laughs without any sound at all, just a breath let out and a bright look.

A few steps on, she bumps you back, a little harder, her eyes fixed on the trail ahead as if she hasn't done a thing.

So it goes, on up the canyon. Now and then you knock your shoulder into hers, and every time she answers in kind, a bump for a bump, the canvas of her jacket rasping against your shirt. The creek keeps pace beside you under the willows, and the maples burn red up the side draws.

"I'm Alan," you say. "What's your name?"

"Alan," she says. She tries it slowly, as if she's tasting it.

She touches two fingers to her own chest, just below the headphones. Her lips part. Then she tilts her head toward the walls, the way she did up on the boulder, listening.

From far up the canyon, late and faint, the walls give a word back. It's yours, the last one you spoke: "...name."

She lifts her fingers from her chest and points up the canyon, to where the sound came from. Then she touches her chest again and smiles.

She bumps your shoulder once more and walks on beside you, up into the cold shade between the walls.
