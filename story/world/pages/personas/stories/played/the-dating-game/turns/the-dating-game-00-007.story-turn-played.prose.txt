You laugh and cup a hand under the spout and splash her back.

Echo ducks, too late. The water catches her square on the jaw, and she laughs without a sound, just a bright breath, dripping, wiping her chin with the back of her wrist.

The cold feels great after the climb, cool on your face and the back of your neck where the sweat had been. You both shake it off and walk on, the gravel crunching, the walls high and banded on either side.

After a few steps you turn to her.

"So, I know this might be sensitive, but I noticed you mostly repeat things I've said. Why is that? No judgment, I'm autistic myself and that's not uncommon for autistic kids, so it's not unfamiliar for me."

Her steps slow. She looks at you for a long moment, and her face softens all the way through, plainly touched.

"No judgment," she gives back softly, and rests her hand on your forearm. Her fingers are still cool from the fountain. They stay there a moment, then lift away.

Then she shakes her head, gently. It isn't that.

She stops on the trail and turns to face the canyon wall. When she speaks again, the voice is different: older, colder, level as a verdict. It isn't hers.

"You shall have the last word, and never the first."

It sounds like someone else's sentence, spoken over her a long time ago. The walls take it and throw it back down the canyon, fainter each time: ...never the first ...the first.

When she turns back, her own warm voice has returned. "Never the first," she says, and touches her fingertips to her lips.

She points to her mouth. Then to the walls. Then to you.

Her grey-green eyes stay on your face, searching it, anxious and hopeful at once, for any sign that you understand.
