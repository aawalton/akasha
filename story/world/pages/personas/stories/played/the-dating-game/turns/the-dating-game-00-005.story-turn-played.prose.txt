"You're Echo?" you say. "That's a really pretty name. I'm a big fan of unusual names."

She stops dead on the trail and turns to face you, and her whole face lights, freckles and grey-green eyes and all, as if somebody has thrown open a window behind it.

"Echo," she says, nodding hard, two fingers pressed flat to her chest just below the headphones.

She looks at you then the way a person looks at someone who has finally, after a long time of calling, heard them.

"A really pretty name," she gives back, softly, turning your words over like something she's been handed to keep. Color rises under her freckles, pink spreading out across her cheeks and down toward the collar of the jacket.

Then she bumps your shoulder, harder than any bump yet, and says your own name, warm: "Alan."

You walk on together. The creek talks beside you, and the gravel crunches, and a little further up the trail splits.

The main trail keeps climbing, on up the canyon between the tall banded walls, into shade that goes deeper and colder the higher it goes.

The other way, a narrow side path drops off through the willows toward the creek. Through the leaves you can see where it ends: the water spreads out there and pools, dark and still, under an overhanging ledge of pink stone.
