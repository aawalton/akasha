"Usually just around the neighborhood," you say. "Sometimes up the canyon, into the forest. I've watched the sun rise from the top of the mountain a few times."

At "the top of the mountain" Grace's smile deepens, slowly.

"Then you've kept the whole night through, start to finish," she says. "Not many do."

She looks up the street toward the dark mouth of the canyon, as if she can see you up there on the peak, waiting on the light.

While you've talked, the gold in the sky has gone to rose. Down the block, the first streetlight flickers, then catches.

Grace glances at it. Then she rises from the step in one easy motion.

She lifts the brass storm lantern by its handle, strikes a match, and touches it to the wick. The wick catches, and the lantern's small light comes up steady and warm between you.

"That's my hour starting," she says, and her gold eyes rest on you a moment, unhurried.
