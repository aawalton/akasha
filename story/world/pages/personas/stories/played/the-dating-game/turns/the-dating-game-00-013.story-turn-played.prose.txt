"Really? I've read the first six books there, but I can't understand why they're so popular. What do you like about it? I've gotta go with The Wandering Inn. I mean, 16 million words and it still is constantly surprising me, nothing else I've read even comes close."

At your puzzlement over Carl, Echo laughs, soundless, just that bright breath let out, and there isn't a flicker of offense anywhere on her face.

She touches her fingertips to her throat and gives you her answer in two words. "The voices."

Then she shows you. She runs through a handful of them, one breath each, every one different. A gravel-throated dwarf, low and rumbling, grinding out a growl. A haughty purr of a cat, all silk and disdain. A bright, chirpy game announcer, far too cheerful. Each one is so distinct that for a breath it's as if someone else has stepped onto the trail beside you, and then gone again.

She drops back into her own warm voice, grinning, a narrator's pleasure plain all over her.

At "16 million words" her eyes had gone wide, and now she gives the number back to you, awed. "16 million words."

She blows out a long breath and laughs, her eyes going up and away for a moment, as if she's weighing how long it would take to read that aloud.

Then she looks at you sidelong, from under her lashes. "Constantly surprising," she says, and her eyes stay on you, not on the book.
