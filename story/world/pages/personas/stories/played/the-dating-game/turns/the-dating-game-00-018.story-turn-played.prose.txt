You sit in the chair at the control desk.

"Okay, you tell me what to do, I'm yours for as long as you want me. Otherwise, I'll gladly just listen."

Through the glass Echo flushes at the first part, pink rising up under the freckles, and laughs without a sound.

"Tell me what to do," she gives back, amused, and takes you at your word.

She points to the big headphones lying on the desk in front of you, then mimes putting them on, both hands cupped over her own ears.

She points to a red button on the desk and holds up one finger. Press it once.

In the booth she turns to the book, and for the first time she sees it properly. It's your edition, the one of its kind, bound in leather, the gold leaf on it catching the booth light.

She runs a fingertip slowly along the gold. Then she looks up at you through the glass, and the wonder is plain on her face.

She turns to the first page and leans in to the microphone, and her narrator's voice fills your ears, warm and close.

"The inn was dark and empty when the traveller arrived."

She reads on, through the hills and the mountains, unhurried, every word set in its place.

When the young woman in the story calls out, Echo's voice turns young, scared and breathless.

"Hello? Is…is anyone…?"

A little further on, the same voice comes again, small and plaintive. "I just wanted to go to the bathroom."

She holds her place with one finger and looks up at you through the glass.
