You're still feeling social, so instead of going in you walk on past your own front door.

You stroll on down Apple Avenue in the late gold light, nodding to the quiet yards as you pass: the trimmed hedges, the bikes left on their sides in the grass, a hose coiled by a porch.

A few doors along, a small rented house sits back from the street, very still.

A young woman sits on its front step, resting, her forearms on her knees.

She looks twenty-two. Her hair is long and straight and near-black, parted in the middle and falling past her shoulders.

Beside her on the step sits a brass storm lantern, unlit.

As you come level with the house, she lifts her head and looks at you.

Her eyes are gold, and they seem lit from somewhere inside. Her mouth is red.

She smiles, slow and warm and unhurried, as if she has all the evening in the world.

"You look like a man who's had a good day," she says, her voice low and warm.
