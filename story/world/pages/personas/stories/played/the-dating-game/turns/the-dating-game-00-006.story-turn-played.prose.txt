"This side path will take us up to Kyhv Peak," you tell her. "It's a bit of a longer hike, about two and a half hours from canyon entrance to the top, but the views are great. It's a little harder on conversations though, since the trail is narrower, so I'm thinking let's stay on the main trail for now. The main trail also has the best drinking fountains, fresh cold mountain spring water."

Echo looks down the side path again, past the dark pool under the ledge, to where the path picks its way across the creek and climbs away up the far slope.

"Kyhv Peak," she says, and tips her head back, looking up toward it, somewhere high above the walls.

Then her eyes come back to you, and there's a glint in them. "For now."

She turns up the main trail beside you, and in two steps she's close at your shoulder again, her sleeve brushing yours.

The trail climbs. The walls rise higher on both sides, and the shade between them goes deeper and colder, cold enough that you feel it on your bare forearms.

At the trailside a drinking fountain stands, running cold mountain spring water in a steady arc.

Echo gets there first. She bends and drinks, long and grateful, her hair falling forward around her face, and when she comes up the water is beading on her freckled chin.

She straightens, holds her fingertips under the spout until they drip, and flicks the cold drops at you. They land on your cheek and your grey shirt, icy.

She grins.
