You squeeze back, and her hand stays in yours, and the two of you walk on up the trail like that, hand in hand.

"Wow, three thousand years," you say. "So, what do you do to pass the time? I've thought a lot about what I'd do with endless time, since that's how my life feels anyways. I have a goal to learn everything, I read a lot of books, sometimes watch shows and movies, especially anime, listen to music. What are you into?"

She listens with her head tilted, swinging your joined hands a little between you as you walk. At "read a lot of books" her whole face lights.

"Learn everything," she gives back, admiring, and squeezes your hand.

Then she clears her throat, and her voice changes. It turns warm and shaped and unhurried, a narrator's voice, the kind that settles in at the start of a long story.

"In a hole in the ground there lived a hobbit."

She places every word just so, each one set down carefully in its spot, and for a moment the whole canyon seems to hush to listen: the creek, the wind in the willows, the gravel under your feet.

Then, in the bright, quick cadence of a radio announcer: "You're listening to BYUradio."

She lifts the black-and-gold headphones up off her collarbones and settles them over her ears, and points to herself with her free hand.

She holds the pose for a beat, grinning at you from inside the headphones. Then she slides them back down to rest at her collarbones.

Her hand stays in yours as you climb on together into the cold shade.
