"Hi there!" you call up to her. "Would you be interested in some company? I'd love someone to chat with on the hike."

She listens with her head tilted, the same tilt she was giving the walls, and as you talk her smile widens.

"Company," she says. She turns the word over slowly, as if she likes the weight of it. Then, warmer: "I'd love."

She swings her legs over the edge of the boulder and slides down the face of the rock, the canvas jacket scuffing on the stone, and lands light in the pale dust beside the creek, a few feet from you. Up close, the freckles don't stop at her face. They run on down her neck and under the collar of the jacket. The headphones sit silent at her collarbones.

She nods up the trail, to where the canyon bends deeper between the walls.

"On the hike," she says, half a question and half an invitation.

You start up together. Gravel crunches underfoot, and the creek talks low beside you. Behind the bend the climbers' voices thin and fall away, until all that's left is the water and two sets of steps.

She walks close at your shoulder, closer than a stranger usually walks, and keeps glancing over at you, bright-eyed.
