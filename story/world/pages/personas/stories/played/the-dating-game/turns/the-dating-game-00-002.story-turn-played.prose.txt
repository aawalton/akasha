You eat at the table by the back window with nothing to hurry for: eggs, toast, coffee cooling in the one mug. Out that side of the house the valley lies open all the way to the water, roofs and treetops and the long grid of the city running down to Utah Lake, a flat pewter sheet with the west mountains pale beyond it. A train crawls along the far side of town, too far off to hear.

When the plate is empty you rinse it and the mug and stand them in the rack. You dress in your favorite things: black shorts over black compression tights, the loose grey athletic shirt, and the light blue Ecco slip-ons, still dusty from the last time. You pull the door shut behind you and head uphill.

The streets steepen toward the bench. Lawns give way to scrub oak, and the sidewalk runs out at Rock Canyon Park, where the grass bowl sits under the mountain and a few early dogs are towing their people toward the trail. Past the bowl the gravel starts.

The canyon closes fast. Within a few minutes the walls stand up on both sides, grey quartzite tipped on end, and the city behind you narrows to a wedge of blue. The creek runs low this late in September, thin over the stones under the willows. Up the side draws the maples have gone red, bright slashes of it among oak just starting to bronze. The shade is cold enough to feel through your shirt. Your shoes take on a fresh coat of pale dust.

At the first climbing walls, ropes already hang in colored lines down the rock. A man in a helmet forty feet up calls down, "Take!" and sits back onto the rope as his belayer hauls in the slack. The canyon hands the word back off the far wall, and then again, fainter, from upstream.

Then it comes once more, a beat too late and from somewhere closer than it should be: a woman's voice, softer and lower, giving the word a shape his never had. Nobody at the base of the rope looks up.

Around the next bend the trail runs beside a boulder the size of a car, and a woman sits on top of it with her knees drawn up. Mid-twenties, maybe. Long dark brown hair, knotted by the wind, and freckles thick across her nose and cheeks. She wears a rock-grey canvas jacket over something pale, and a pair of black-and-gold headphones rests at her collarbones, the cable running down her back into nothing you can see. Her head is tilted toward the walls behind you, listening.

Your shoes crunch closer on the gravel and she turns. Her eyes are grey-green, like the creek stones, and they settle on you and stay there.

"Take," she says quietly, and smiles. She pushes a tangle of hair off her face and doesn't get down from the rock.
