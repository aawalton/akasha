You eat at the table by the back window, the eggs still hot, the toast buttered out to the crusts, cocoa steaming in your one mug. The window looks west, over the rooftops and the old orchard rows, across the whole flat floor of the valley to Utah Lake lying pale and silver under the far hills. You take your time. There's no reason not to. The cocoa cools enough to drink, and you drink it slowly while the light moves on the water.

When the plate is clean you rinse it and go to get dressed. Black compression tights, black shorts over them, a loose grey athletic shirt gone soft from a hundred washes. Last come the light blue Ecco slip-ons by the door, still dusty from the last trail they saw. Your favorite clothes, the comfortable ones. You step into the shoes without bending down.

Then out your door on Apple Avenue, and uphill toward Rock Canyon.

The lawns go first, green and sprinkler-wet, and above the last houses the scrub oak takes the slope. At the canyon mouth the park opens into a wide grass bowl, and past it the pavement ends and the trail turns to gravel. Within minutes the quartzite walls close in on both sides, tall and banded pink and grey, and the city behind you is gone.

The creek runs low under the willows, talking to itself over the stones. Up the side draws the maples have gone red, and the scrub oak is bronzing on the slopes. Between the walls the shade is cold, a cold the sun hasn't reached yet today, and pale dust lifts off the trail around your slip-ons with every step.

Climbers are roped up on the first walls, chalk bags and bright helmets, ropes hanging in lazy curves. One of them, thirty feet up, shouts down to his belayer.

"Take!"

The rock throws it back once, and again: take, take.

Then a third "Take!" comes, late, and close, in a woman's voice.

The climber hangs on his rope. The belayer's eyes stay on him. Every head along the base of the wall stays turned to the rock.

You go on around the bend past the first walls. A boulder the size of a car sits there by the creek, and a woman sits on top of it, knees drawn up, her head tilted toward the canyon walls as if she's listening to them.

She looks mid-twenties. Her dark brown hair is knotted by the wind, her face thick with freckles. She wears a rock-grey canvas jacket over something pale, and a pair of black-and-gold headphones rests around her neck at the collarbones, the cable trailing off to nothing you can see.

She turns her head and meets your eyes. Hers are grey-green.

"Take," she says quietly, and smiles at you, and waits to see what you'll do.
