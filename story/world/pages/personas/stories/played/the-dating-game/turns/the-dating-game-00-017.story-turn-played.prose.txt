"Oh, you want to do the recording at BYU Radio? I guess that works. Want to head there now? I'd have to stop in at my house to pick up the first volume, but it's on the way, you'd be welcome to wait outside."

Echo nods, bright, and gives it back. "Head there now."

The two of you start back down the main trail together. Past the drinking fountain, still running its cold arc into the gravel. Past the split, where the side path drops through the willows toward the dark pool and on up toward Kyhv Peak. Past the boulder by the creek, where the climbers are still roped up on the first walls, chalk bags and bright helmets.

Out the canyon mouth, the walls falling away behind you. Down through the park's grass bowl, down through the scrub oak, and down onto the first green lawns, sprinklers ticking across them.

On Apple Avenue she stops on the sidewalk in front of your house and waits there, as you offered.

You go in, and come back out with the first volume of The Wandering Inn.

She eyes the thickness of it, the sheer brick of it in your hands, and laughs without a sound, delighted.

Then the two of you walk on down the hill to campus, to the BYU Broadcasting Building on its east side.

On a Saturday the building is nearly empty. The lot is mostly bare, and your footsteps are the only ones on the walk. Echo touches a badge to the reader by the door, and the lock clicks open.

She leads you down a quiet hall, carpet hushing your steps, past closed doors and dark windows, to an audio booth: a small padded room, its walls covered in grey foam. A window looks from the booth onto a control desk outside it, all sliders and dials and a pair of big headphones, and she waves you to the chair there.

She takes the book into the booth with her. She sets it open on the stand, smooths the first page flat, and settles at the microphone.

Through the glass she lifts her eyes to yours, the book open under her hand.
