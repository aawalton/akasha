"Good night, Grace."

She smiles back at you over her shoulder and turns toward the far gate.

You watch her lantern move off between the dark rows of stones, small and gold under the pines.

It passes through the gate and out of sight, and the cemetery is left to the evening.

You walk home up the hill. The air is cooling, and the streetlights are on all along Apple Avenue.

Your house is quiet: one mug in the drying rack, one coat on the hook.

You kick off the dusty Ecco slip-ons by the door, as you did last night.

You go to bed, and the long day lets go of you. Sleep comes easily.

Somewhere deep in the night, you dream.

In the dream it is morning, high up Rock Canyon, where the walls give way to pine forest.

Sun slants through the tall pines into a small clearing beside the trail.

A young woman is in the clearing, mid-workout, moving as if the day simply has moving in it.

Her hair is long and loose, auburn-chestnut, red only where the light gets at it. Freckles cross her nose. Her eyes are a vivid green, and long pointed ears show through her hair.

A phone on a small tripod stands on a stump, and she talks to it brightly, her hands going.

Between one word and the next she glances past the phone, straight at you, and her face lights.
