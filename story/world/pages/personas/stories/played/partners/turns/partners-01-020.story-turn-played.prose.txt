"Oh — I'm sorry if I presumed. I really should have asked instead." The apology comes out ahead of your thinking, and the truth right behind it: "I guess I just couldn't imagine going to that meeting without you. I'd be lost on my own. Could we make it official? I clearly need help here — and while I don't have money to offer for a salary, I could offer room and board. I trust you. I'd much rather have you with me — or even represent me — as we work through this. You could choose whatever name for our relationship here you'd like."

"Stop — don't apologize. Don't you dare apologize for that." The composure goes honestly soft. "Alan. Do you understand what you just did? All my life I've wanted exactly one thing — a seat like this: close in, hands on the real work of one person's life, trusted with the whole of it. And every single time, I've had to build the chair myself and carry it into the room and sit down in it before anyone could tell me not to. No one has ever once simply turned and offered it to me." A breath; she meets your eyes. "You just did. Day two, over the washing-up, apologizing for it. The presuming wasn't a trespass — it was the loveliest thing anyone's handed me in years. So no. You'll not take it back with an 'I'm sorry.'"

And then she takes the naming, and you watch her do it the way she does everything — deliberately.

"So yes. Let's make it official — all of it. And you've handed me the naming of it, which is no small thing to give a woman like me; names are my whole trade, and I don't choose them carelessly." A moment, choosing. "Steward. Your steward — steward of Hearthholt. Not 'help,' not 'assistant,' not one word you'd ever say with an apology in your voice. A steward is the one trusted to hold what isn't hers and keep it well, in another's name — which is precisely what I am, and precisely what I have always been, for rather a lot of things that were never mine." The warmth sharpens. "And mark the word, because it earns its keep twice: by noon the Compact will offer to hold this house 'in stewardship' FOR you — meaning away from you. So let me be the true article. I'll steward it for you, in your name, into your hands. Same word. Opposite direction entirely."

Softer, then — and precise about exactly one more thing:

"That names the work, and the work's the part that needs a word today — for factors and brokers and the vale's wagging tongues. The other thing — the thing on that couch in the small hours, the 'not never' — that one I'll not name yet. And not because I don't feel the shape of it; because I do, and it's still growing, and you don't pin a name to a thing that's still deciding what it'll be. That word we choose together, when it's ready — out loud, in daylight. You've my promise on it."

"As for room and board — I'll take it, though know the money was never the point; I've means enough of my own, and I'd do this for nothing, which is rather the whole secret of why I'm any good at it. But the room—" and here something she filed away the evening she arrived surfaces, wondering "—do you know, this house settled that already. The evening we met, mid-sentence, while I was still deciding whether to cross your threshold at all, a door down the corridor opened itself and made up a bed. The house voted before either of us did. We're only now catching it up."

Then grave — the gravity of a keeper receiving a trust:

"And 'represent you' — hear me be serious a moment. To act in a man's name, to speak for him at his own table, is not a convenience. It's a trust, and I'll carry it like one. I will never once spend your name on a thing you'd not spend it on yourself. You have that from me." Settled, sure: "So — noon. I'll be in the room, at your side, your steward of record. You say little and warm; I'll handle the paper and the factor's kindness. Between us they'll find nothing to reach for."

A small smile, and the whole arrangement laid in your two hands.

"Now — is that the arrangement you meant? Say the word, and it's real."
