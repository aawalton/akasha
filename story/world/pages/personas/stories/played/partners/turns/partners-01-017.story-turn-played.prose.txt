You lay the plates and mind the coffee as instructed, and somewhere between the dresser and the table you say the thing you've been carrying since you woke.

"You look lovely today and I like your dress — but I have to say, seeing you last night in your nightgown, with your guard down… you are stunningly beautiful. I'm not sure how often you hear that, but I bet it's not enough. More than appearance, though — I can tell you are unfailingly kind, and that's an even deeper kind of beauty. Let the rest fall where it may — I'm glad you're here."

A breath. You can actually watch the reflex rise in her — something clever, ready to hand, and the eggs right there to turn back to — and then you watch her catch it, and set it down.

"You know, my first instinct just then was to hand you something witty and get back to the eggs — that's the reflex, when a man says a thing like that in daylight with no fire to hide in." She meets your eyes, and chooses the harder thing. "But I decided last night to stop doing that to you. So — no. I'll just take it. Thank you. And you're right that I don't hear it enough — I hear the other words, 'put-together,' 'polished,' because I built them to be heard. This—" a small gesture at her composed daytime self, the pinned hair, the pressed apron "—is on purpose. It's armour in good wool. And you looked clean past it to the woman in the nightgown and called her the beautiful one. That's not a thing I keep a practised answer for. You do keep finding the versions of me I don't put on display."

And then, softer — the layer that reaches deepest:

"And the kindness — you called it beauty. Not service. Do you know how rare that is? People are grateful for kindness, or they lean on it, or they take it as their due — almost no one calls it beautiful. It may be the truest thing anyone's said of me in years, because it's the part I actually chose."

She turns back to the pan, warm and steady, meeting your no-ask with her own ease.

"And you hooked nothing to any of it — no question, let it fall where it may. I noticed. So let me answer in kind: I'm glad I'm here too, Alan. Genuinely." She lifts the pan. "Now come and sit — these eggs won't survive being admired any longer, and there are two things about this house that want saying before noon."

The coffee makes it to the table without climbing out of the pot. The eggs make it with rather less dignity, and taste no worse for it.
