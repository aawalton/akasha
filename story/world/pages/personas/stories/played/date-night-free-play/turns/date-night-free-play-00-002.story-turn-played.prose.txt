The light-tree drops green and Aura is gone. Not fast — gone, a whole car-length up the track before Awen's even cleared the start gate, her ghost-line the perfect one, the one the machine wants you to drive.

Awen doesn't chase the ghost. She drives the wheel she's got. Four degrees of slack before the front end answers, so she turns four degrees into nothing, early, feeds the correction in before each corner asks for it. The car goes where she meant instead of where the column's backlash would send it. It's slower on paper. It's honest. And that's worth about a length a lap, corner after corner.

"You're doing something." Aura doesn't look over. She never looks over; she watches the road like it owes her money. "I can hear you doing something over there. Stop it."

"Driving."

"Sure. That's your fixing-my-machine-with-your-face sound — I'd know it in my sleep."

"Can't fix it from here." Awen takes the hairpin. The wheel fights her the way it always does and she lets it, hands already four degrees ahead of the fight. "I'm just being polite to it."

Aura's shoulder drops a half-inch on the next straight. Awen has seen that shoulder a hundred times; it used to mean the race was over. Tonight she just files it, keeps her line, and comes out of the chicane closer than she went in.

"Artemis drove like you," Aura says, apropos of nothing — or of the gap Awen's been eating into all lap. "Never met a straight shot she wouldn't complicate. Drove me up a wall."

"You didn't have cars."

"Chariots. Same principle, worse suspension." The gap holds; the gap shrinks. "She'd have loved you. It would've been unbearable. Two of you 'respecting the mechanism' at me while I—" the last corner comes up and Aura takes it flat-out fast, the ghost-line, the greedy one, "—won."

She doesn't win. Not clean, anyway.
