Aura sprawls half on top of her like she's claiming ground, and Awen works the knots out of her hair, the ones they just put there. The cabinet-blue comes up through the window and the empty arcade hums its after-hours hum below them, and for a while neither of them keeps score of anything.

Then Aura reaches down over the side of the bed, digs her phone out of the jacket on the floor, and hands it over without a word.

It's open to a note. The title is just a date, eight months back. Under it, a list.

first night — drank the bad well whiskey neat, didn't blink. asked which cab I loved best and called my bluff when I lied

took my SFII ghost round two. informed me my hinge was loud

rhythm cab, 942k. have not cleared it. not going to

owe her one (1) dumpling, indefinite, on purpose

It goes on. Months of it. Every record Awen's ever taken off her in that room, every number — and threaded between the numbers the other things, a whiskey, a hinge, a dumpling held hostage, logged in the same flat hand as the scores.

Awen scrolls. She doesn't trust her hands not to do something stupid, so she keeps them on the phone.

"You keep a scoreboard of me," she says. Quiet. Exact.

"I keep a scoreboard of everything." Aura's chin is on her chest, watching her read it. "You're just winning."
