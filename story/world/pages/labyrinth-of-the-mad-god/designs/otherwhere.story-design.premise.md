Alan, a man from Provo, Utah, is pulled out of his own life on the night the world ends and wakes somewhere else in a woman's body. He goes by Nala from the moment he wakes, and everyone in the fiction knows her as Nala. He does not know where he is, and he finds out the way anyone would: by looking, asking and living there. He is the player; he can recognise the place as the story goes, but nothing outside the fiction ever tells him.

The world is the one this story sits in, taken at the moment its first book opens. The night Earth's core reaches its first level, the System culls the planet, and those who reach a portal are carried into the space between worlds, rewritten, and sent into a tutorial that a hostile god has turned cruel. Alan is one of the survivors. His tutorial is his own, in a place of the world builder's making drawn from how this world's tutorials work, and not the one the canon protagonist is sent to.

The rewrite did not carry Alan's body across whole. What woke is a woman of about twenty-five, and she is who Alan is now. How and why the body came out as it did is a secret the world builder holds. As with every survivor, the System walled off the worst of what she lived through on the way, so she wakes remembering only the evening before, and the rest returns when she is ready for it.

The world's own interface speaks in flat lines, in its own words and its own layout. It is the world's layer and is used only the way this world uses it; the game invents no levels, classes, skills or numbers the world does not have.

The game master and the writer never name the source work, its author, or the fact that it is a book. No narration, interface line or panel says where Nala is. Names, places and systems appear only where a native, the interface or the scene would naturally show them. Alan's knowledge of fiction is his own and is never answered from outside the fiction.

Play is tuned for fun on the knife-edge between too easy and too cruel: the tutorial is set to kill, Nala starts with nothing but what she wore to bed, and she can be hurt and can lose. Warmth comes from the creatures and people who earn it.
