You turn and run.

Back the way you came, as fast as you can go. The barking swells behind you and above you, and the canopy comes with you. Branches crash overhead, first behind, then level, then ahead. They are keeping pace without trying. Fruit smacks into the litter at your feet. A stick whips past your ear, another cracks off a trunk, red pulp bursts on the leaves beside you.

Low to your left, something in the undergrowth breaks away from you, fast, a rush of leaves going the other way. You don't see what it is.

The creeper curtain is where you left it. You hit it at a run. It takes your shirt and your hair at once and holds, and for a second you are stopped dead with your head dragged back and the thorns in your collar. You wrench forward. Something tears. Hair rips out at the roots, a whole hank of red left hanging on the hooks behind you, and you are through and running.

Your left foot comes down on a root. The cut sole flares white-hot, the foot rolls under you, and you go down hard.

You land on your hands and one hip in the leaf litter. Your hair is across your face. Above you, directly above, the barking goes up into a shriek.

Something hits the side of your head, above your right ear.

White light. A ringing, high and thin. For a moment the ground is gone and so is the sky, and you cannot tell which way either of them is.

Then the litter is under your palms again. You find an egg-sized stone lying beside your hand. The side of your head is hot and wet. You touch it and your fingers come away bright red, and more is running, warm, down your neck and into your collar. Your scalp is split. Your head throbs with every heartbeat. The heels of your hands are scraped raw and your hip aches where you hit.

You get up. You run again, one hand clamped to the side of your head, blood slick between your fingers.

You stop picking gaps. You just go downhill, toward the surf. You can hear it now, under the barking, and you go at it straight, through whatever is in the way.

Light opens ahead. You burst out between the skirted palms onto the sand.

The sand is hot now. Much hotter than when you left, hot enough that it stings, and the cut sole burns with every step. You stumble a few more paces down the beach, out into the open, before you look back.

The barking stops at the treeline as if it has hit a wall. The branches along the edge shake, then settle. Every ape stays in the trees. Their noise pulls back into the forest, grumbling, and fades.

You are breathing hard. Your heart is slamming. The side of your head is wet and your hand is red.

One ape has stayed. It is a half-grown one, smaller than the rest, with a white blaze on its brow. It sits at the very edge of the fronds, in their shade, with a red fruit in one hand.

It isn't barking. It takes a bite of the fruit and watches you, head tilted, chewing.
