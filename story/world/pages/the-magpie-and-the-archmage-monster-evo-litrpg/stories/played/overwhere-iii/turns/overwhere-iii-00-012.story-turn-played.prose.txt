You take the knife and belt the sheath at your hip, over Tobin's coat.

Out the south gate, the road runs down between the winter fields. The ruts are frozen hard. Two miles is slow going without shoes. Your soles sting, and your stomach grumbles the whole way.

The sun is well up by the time the shrine and the gold-leaved beeches come into view. Frost still lies white in the shade under the trees.

You find the edge path running west from the shrine, and you look for the currents.

They are there, running along the wood's edge the way they did yesterday, cool and faint. Where a current brushes the roots of an old beech, it pools and slows.

On the north side of the first such root, in the frost, sits a cluster of pale mushrooms. Each one holds a faint, cold glow, like frost catching the light.

Frostcaps.

You kneel and cut them at the root, one by one, carefully. Every one comes away clean.

You follow the current to the next root, and the next. Each one leads you to more.

At one root the mushrooms look the same at a glance, only grayer, and they have no glow at all. You lean closer. They smell of wet ash. Gravecap. You leave them where they grow.

In well under an hour you have fourteen good frostcaps wrapped in the front of your shirt.

A little farther on, the current bends toward a huge old beech, its roots heaped with frost. The biggest cluster yet glows under those roots.

Something is already there, feeding. It is a big gray-brown rabbit with a small glow about it, and a pair of little forked antlers on its head.

It freezes mid-chew, nose working, and fixes one dark eye on you from between the roots.
