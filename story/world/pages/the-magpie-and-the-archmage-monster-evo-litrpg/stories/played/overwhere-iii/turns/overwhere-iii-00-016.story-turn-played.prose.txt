You walk back along the edge path to the old beech and the dead rabbit.

You kneel at the root and cut the four whole frostcaps free, one by one, clean at the root. Eighteen now, wrapped in the front of your shirt.

A blue current runs on west from the old beech, and you follow it.

The frost is thinning in the sun, melting to dark wet patches between the roots.

A quarter hour along, the current brushes the roots of a beech split down the middle by lightning. On its north side, in the last of the frost, four pale mushrooms stand in a huddle.

Three of them hold a faint cold glow. The fourth holds none, and it smells of wet ash.

You cut two of the glowing ones at the root and leave the rest.

Twenty frostcaps, every one whole, wrapped in your shirt against the cold.
