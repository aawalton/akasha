"Thanks, Marda."

Marda grunts and goes back to her ledger.

You walk out the south gate and the two miles down to the crossroads.

You sit against the shrine's warm stone. The white-gold threads seep in for three slow hours.

The shake goes out of your hands. By early afternoon your well is brimming again.

You walk the two miles back and turn down Brannagh's lane.

One carter sits on the bench, his palm wrapped in a bloody rag.

"Axle bolt," he says. "This morning." The gash is deep, but clean, no gray in it.

You lay two fingers along it. The first Mending Weave knits the deep part closed.

The second smooths the skin over. He flexes his hand and laughs out loud.

He pays Brannagh ten copper, and she passes it straight to you.

"That's all for today," Brannagh says. "Your washerwoman's due at the dusk bell."
