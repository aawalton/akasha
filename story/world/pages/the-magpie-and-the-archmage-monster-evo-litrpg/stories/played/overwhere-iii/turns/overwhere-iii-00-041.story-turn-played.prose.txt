"Thanks for letting me know. I'll be there," you say. "As for the magic, I'm not entirely sure how it works. If you figure it out, please tell me."

You say it with a smile. The girl's eyes go huge.

"I'll find out first, and tell only you." She sticks out a hand. "Pip Carrow. Reeve's daughter. And you're...?"

From the desk, without looking up, Marda says, "Nala."

To your sight, the post's thin currents lean toward the girl, as if she draws them without knowing.

Pip stays at your elbow, reading along, until Marda sends her home at half past four.

You read on: hill boar, tree devourer beetle, thornwing owl, wolf.

Giantmaw hyena, Level 12 to 18, deep wood only. The whole entry is two words: "Do not."

Then Marda's blight page, the ink still fresh: corrupted beasts drop blightstones. Burn the dead.

At the dusk bell you walk up to Brannagh's lane.

A stout, red-cheeked woman of forty sits on the bench, talking fast, a burly carter beside her. Her left shin is bound. Under the cloth, the dog bite is torn and ringed in purple.

"They say you drew it out of Ivy Marsh. Draw it out of me, love, please."

To your sight the blight here is fresh: a dark smear in the torn flesh, not yet sunk deep.

You push out your white-gold thread and pull. The cold bites up your arm, harder than you expect. The black comes anyway and clots into a seed stone. The wound is clean.

You shift to stitching. The mending weave drags, and the torn edges fight you the whole way. It closes, but puckered and uneven, rougher than the clean seams you're used to.

"It'll scar," Brannagh says, peering at it. "But it's shut, and it's clean."

The carter pays Brannagh ten copper, and she hands it straight on to you. She spoons the seed stone into your clay cup.

Outside the window, the lane has gone dark.
