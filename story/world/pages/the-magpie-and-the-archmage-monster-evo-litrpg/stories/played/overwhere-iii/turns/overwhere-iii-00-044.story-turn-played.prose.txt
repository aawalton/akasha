You sit at Brannagh's counter with the receipt book. The one-eared cat settles by your elbow.

The hand is cramped and faded, and you read slowly.

The first page, in the mother's hand: a potion only "takes" if its brewer has mana to pour in.

At the noon bell, the carter's wife bustles in, talking already, and plants herself on the bench. She unwinds the linen from her shin. The seam is puckered and uneven, and clean all the same.

You lay a holy thread along it and stitch. This time the thread runs easy, and holds.

The pucker eases out under it. The seam lies flat, a thin pale line and no more.

She stares at her shin, then runs a thumb down it. "Well," she says. "Well, now."

She squeezes your hand hard, and goes out still talking, to anyone in the lane who'll listen.

You go back to the book. Salves: goose-grease and yarrow for wounds, marigold for burns. Teas and poultices for coughs, fevers, sprains. Page after page, slow going.

By mid-afternoon you're near full again inside. You mark your page and walk down to the post.

"Will you let me work the Guild's stones?" you ask Marda.

She thinks it over. "Unmaking them is the point," she says. "Whatever's left of one is the healer's."

She sets the paled stag's blightstone on the desk with her tongs. To your sight, it's a dark smear.

You thread holy current into it. Cold bites up the thread into your hand. The thread holds.

A second weave, then a third. Each time the black gives a little, and the cold eases.

The stone sits on the desk well paler than it was, near half gone to gray.

You're near empty inside. Marda looks from the stone to you, and doesn't say a word.

You walk back up to Brannagh's and read on as the light goes.

Near dark you reach the healing potion: snowroot, comfrey, spring water, simmered an hour, with mana poured in as it simmers.

Your stomach growls. You haven't eaten since last night.

You're two-thirds through the book, and inside you've filled back to a little over half.

Brannagh comes round with the lamp. "Shop's shutting. Book's here at first light."
