You settle on the bench outside Brannagh's door and let the afternoon go by.

You watch the currents drift through the lane, white-gold, slow, and try to read their shapes.

They curl and fold and slide past each other. Whatever they're telling you, you can't make it out.

Your well fills back to the brim as the light goes.

At the dusk bell the washerwoman comes up the lane, a cloth bundle under her arm.

She rolls up her sleeve. The old scald covers half her forearm, puckered and shiny.

You lay your palm on it. The first Mending Weave sinks in deep and sure.

The second loosens the tight shiny skin. The third smooths it flat and pale.

The washerwoman turns her arm in the lamplight and stares. Her eyes are wet.

"Years," she says. "Years I've hidden that." She unwraps the cloth: three hot oatcakes.

"For the healer." She holds them out, the smell of butter and oats rising in the cold.
