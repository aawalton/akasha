Past the edge of the box, the threads still hang in the air along the road. They are still leaning toward your hand.

Then the cold reaches you. The dirt under your bare feet is hard and cold, and the air goes straight through the thin shirt. Up close, the beeches' gold is dead leaves, dry and curled, rattling on the boughs. The sun is low and doesn't warm you at all.

The singing gets louder. A cart comes up the east road behind two mules, loaded with casks and sacks. The driver is broad and bald, his scalp sunburnt, his beard gray, and he is singing at the top of his voice and well off key.

He sees you in the middle of a verse, and the song stops.

He reins in at the shrine. First he reaches up and touches its slate roof, quick and without looking, as if he always does. Then his eyes go to your bare feet, and to the man's shirt hanging off you, and his whole face creases with worry.

"Gods, lass. Who robbed you? Were you set on out here, this close to the wood?"

He is already shrugging out of his coat. "I'm for Merrowgate. Climb up, you'll freeze."

He leans down and holds the coat out to you. Behind him the mules stamp and blow, their breath white in the cold.
