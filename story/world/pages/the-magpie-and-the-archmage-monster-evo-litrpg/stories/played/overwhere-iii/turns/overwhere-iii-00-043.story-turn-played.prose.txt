You walk back across the Wool Square to the Crook and Candle as the dark comes down.

The common room is warm and loud. As you come in, a few carters raise their cups to you.

Supper is barley soup with pork sausages and black bread. Bet won't take your coin for it.

"Ivy's my cousin. Eat."

You eat. You pay eight copper for the bed.

It's the same small room under the eaves. The night passes quiet, and by morning the frost lies hard on every roof.

You wake rested, full to the brim inside, and go straight to the south green at the dawn bell.

Maud nods once. This time she shows you how to hold the staff: hands apart, weight low. Then she pairs you with the same lanky young watchman. "Tam. You're with her." He grins. He goes no easier.

He comes in fast. You step aside, the way Maud showed you, and crack your staff across his ribs.

Tam sits down hard on the frosty grass. The watch whoops. Maud almost smiles.

Laps, fieldstones, more bouts until the watch changes. You're sore to the bone, and glad of it.

At Brannagh's, a tiny, soot-grimed old woman is already on the bench, ten copper in her fist. She's a charcoal-burner from the wood's edge.

"Let's see you work, then," she says. It isn't a question.

Her right palm is gray and stiff to the wrist. A splinter off a blighted stump went into it.

You push out your thread and pull. The currents come heavier than you've ever felt them. Too heavy. The pull skids off the blight, and cold jolts up your arm. The black doesn't come.

The old woman snorts.

A blue box opens.

[Your trait <Mana Weaver> has reached [Adept] level.]

[Mana Weaver – At [Adept] level, you see currents and auras far off. They lend your workings more.]

You breathe, ease the thread down, and pull again, slower.

The black comes out in one long strand and clots into a seed stone. The palm pinks. The splinter hole is small, and it closes on its own as you watch.

The old woman flexes her fingers. Grunts. Drops the ten copper in your hand.

"Stumps like that crowd my kilns now," she says. "And gray-furred things move there at dusk."

Brannagh spoons the seed into your cup.

"Do you have any books on healing I could read?" you ask her.

"One," Brannagh says. "My mother's receipts. Salves, potions, draughts. Read it at my counter. It never leaves the shop. For wounds and bones, you want Sister Wenna's herbal at the chapel."
