"I'm not sure," you say, and nothing more. Your eyes stay on the currents running along the road.

Tobin looks at you a long moment. Then his eyes go over your hair, slowly, as if he is hunting for a bruise.

"Took a knock to the head, I'd wager," he says, gentler. "Well. It'll come back, or it won't."

You reach out with your intent toward the nearest current, the one running beside the cart.

One thread of it bends toward you. It follows your will the way water follows a stirring finger.

You let go. Within a breath or two it slides back into the current's course, as if it had never moved.

You bend it again, and this time you hold it close. It sits cool and patient at your fingertips, and it feels ready to pour into you, if you gave it somewhere to go.

You don't know where somewhere is yet.

Tobin's eyes stay on the road and the fields, and on the dark line of the wood behind them.

"I'll see you to the Crook and Candle," he says. "Town's one inn. Warm, and a bed."

The cart rolls on for half an hour. The sun goes down red behind the fields, and the cold sharpens under the coat.

Merrowgate's wall rises ahead, gray fieldstone, with a gate in it and a lantern already lit beside the gate.

A bell rings out over the town, slow and deep, as the mules pull up at the south gate.

A guard steps out with a lantern in one hand and a big ledger under his arm, and raises a hand to stop the cart.

"Evening, Tobin." His eyes go to you, to the coat, to your bare feet. "And who's this? No papers, I'd guess. Name, lass, and your business in Merrowgate. It goes in the book."
