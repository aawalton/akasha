At the Crook and Candle it's beef and onion stew with black bread. You eat it all.

You pay Bet three copper for supper and eight for the bed under the eaves.

You sleep deep and wake at first light with your well full.

You walk to Brannagh's lane as the shutters come up. Two people wait on the bench.

One is a washerwoman, her forearm puckered with an old scald scar. Her name is off the slate.

The other is the bitten lad's father, cap in his hands, boots muddy from the road.

"The fox came back in the night," he says. "Killed three of my hens."

"The post's bounty is past what I can pay. Can the healer do aught about it?"
