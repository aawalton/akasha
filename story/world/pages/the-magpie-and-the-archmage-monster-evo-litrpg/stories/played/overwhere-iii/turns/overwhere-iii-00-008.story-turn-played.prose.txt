"Yes, thank you again," you say.

Tobin nods, satisfied. "Keep the coat for now. I'll be on the square." He goes up the stair.

Bet takes a candle and shows you up to a small cold room under the eaves. There is a straw tick, a wool blanket, a shuttered window and a peg on the wall, and nothing else.

You lie down under the blanket and Tobin's coat, and sleep takes you fast.

You wake to gray light at the edge of the shutter and a bell ringing slow and deep over the town. Your stomach is empty again. Last night's stew is a long time gone.

Downstairs the common room is quiet, the hearth banked low. Bet is busy and doesn't look up.

Outside, the air is sharp. Frost whitens the cobbles, and the cold bites your bare feet on every step. Across the square, carts are coming in to set up. Tobin's isn't among them yet.

You walk back down the street to the south gate, to the squat old tollhouse just inside it. The board by its door is papered with notices, and you read them as easily as English.

RATS IN THE CARROW WOOL STORE. 30 COPPER.

TWENTY FROSTCAP MUSHROOMS FROM THE WRENWOOD'S EDGE. 20 COPPER.

GUARD THE SALT WAGON TO APPLEGARTH AND BACK. 40 COPPER.

A BLIGHTED BOAR SEEN BY THE SOUTH ROAD. KILL IT: 1 SILVER 50 COPPER.

BLIGHT BOUNTY: A SILVER A STONE.

At the bottom, in a rough hand: Any word of Cal Fenn, to Jory Fenn. He pays what he has.

Inside is one room. A desk, a bench, a stove ticking with heat, a stair going up. A long sword hangs on the wall above the desk.

Behind the desk sits a broad-shouldered woman of sixty or so, her gray hair cropped short, a cane leaning beside her. She looks up at you, at the coat, the bare feet, the red hair. Her face doesn't move.

"Door's open, so come in or don't," she says. "What are you after?"
