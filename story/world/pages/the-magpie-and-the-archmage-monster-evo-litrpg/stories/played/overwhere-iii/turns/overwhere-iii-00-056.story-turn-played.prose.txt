You sleep deep under the eaves and wake at first light, your well full and your hand easy.

You walk to Brannagh's lane as the shop opens. One pair waits on the bench.

A girl of about nine sits with her mother. Her upper lip has knit crooked.

"She fell on a stone step," her mother says. "Two years back."

Brannagh nods at you. "First on the slate. Go on."

You set two fingers by the girl's lip. The first Mending Weave sinks in deep and sure.

The second runs clean. The puckered seam softens and starts to draw straight.

The third catches on old knotted tissue and bites back. A sharp ache stabs behind your eyes.

You hold it through. The seam smooths flat. The girl's lip sits even now.

The girl touches it, then grins wide at her mother. Her mother covers her mouth.

You walk down to the post. Marda tongs out the half-pale stag stone without a word.

This time you bend the end of the weave round and close it on itself, a loop.

It's fiddly. The loop wobbles, then catches and holds.

The snap-back hits the stone, not your arm. A dark vein tears loose, and the stone pales deep.

The second loop closes easily, as if your hands have always known how. Another vein goes.

Only a thin dark thread is left in the stone. One more weave should crack it.

Your well is dry, and your stomach growls loud in the quiet post.
