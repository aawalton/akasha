You back away fast, up the road, step after quick step.

The jackalopes outrun you in a few strides. Closing, they bunch together, shoulder to shoulder.

You turn and braid cleanse and lash together, then sweep the braid across all three.

The lash cuts each one. The pull strand bites into all three at once.

Blight jerks up out of each of them, a single pull's worth, and the current carries the cold away.

The smallest tumbles, kicking. The other two stagger, shrieking, fur smoking where the lash cut.

They come at you anyway. The first lunges with its black-glowing antlers. You sidestep it.

The second snaps at your calf. You skip back, and its teeth close on air.

The biggest butts at your hip. You twist aside, and it skids past through the frost.

All three wheel around, panting, blight still dark in them. The smallest can barely stand.
