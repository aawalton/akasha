You pocket the seed stone and go after the wolf, your braid ready.

The prints end at the water's edge. You work the banks, downstream first. The banks there hold no sign.

Upstream the brook runs over stony shallows where no print could hold.

You keep on, eyes on both banks, the charcoal smoke growing ahead.

Half a mile up the trees open on a clearing. A low hut smokes beside a charcoal mound.

On the south bank's shingle you find wet claw scrapes, leading up out of the water.

Black-matted fur is caught on a bramble. It stinks of rot.

The scrapes lead past the mound, toward a brush lean-to behind the hut.

The hut door creaks open. A stooped old woman in a gray shawl steps out, soot on her hands.

"Lost, love?" Her voice is soft and kind. "There's nettle tea on, if you're cold."

Your sight shows you more. Around the old woman hangs an aura of sick violet-black.
