Without a word, you draw hard on the brook's current, braid lash and pull together, and strike.

The old woman jerks aside, quicker than any sixty-year-old should move. Your braid scorches across the charcoal mound.

Her kind face goes flat. A gray shield shimmers up around her, and she whistles, sharp.

A blight bolt spits at you. You twist, and it only grazes your shoulder, cold and burning at once.

You throw a second braid. It cracks against the shield and frays apart.

Gray-green rot mist rolls out across the clearing. Your arms go heavy, and your breath tastes of rot.

Two wolves burst from the lean-to, huge and black-matted. One has a torn ear.

The first sinks its teeth into your calf. The second tears your forearm. Blighted cold spreads from both.

You braid a third time and drive it through the shield.

The lash cuts. The pull strand bites into the woman's own well and tears at the blight inside her.

The woman shrieks. The bone ring on her finger cracks.

Her old face slides away. Beneath it she is forty, hard-jawed, with black-ringed eyes.

She flings another bolt. You throw yourself flat, and it hisses past.

The torn-eared wolf's teeth close on your shoulder. The other snaps at air as you roll free.

You reach for your well. One spark is left, not enough for another braid.

The woman presses a hand to her chest and steadies. The wolves circle low between you.
