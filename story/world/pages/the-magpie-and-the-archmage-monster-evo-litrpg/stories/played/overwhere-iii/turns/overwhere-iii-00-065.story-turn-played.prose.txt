"I'll take it back to the post," you say.

Fairley digs out an old leather pouch and flicks the stone into it with the end of his stick.

It's the size of a hazelnut. He cinches the pouch shut and hands it to you.

He cuts the fox's tail, then tucks five copper and a dozen eggs in a cloth into your pack.

"The farmers' due for a tail. And you'll never pay for an egg or a bed at Fairley Farm."

You walk the hour back west along the Thornmere road, the pouch held away from your side.

At the post, Marda tongs the stone out of the pouch and turns it in the light.

"Fox?"

You tell her how it went: the braid down into the sett, the lash, the leap.

Marda counts out a silver onto the desk. "The stone's its own proof. Full bounty."

"One weave'll crack that. Only a scrap of blight left in it." She sets it apart in the lead box.

"It's yours to crack when you've mana again. Three specks, I'd guess."

She leans back. "You took a blighted beast yourself. You're past needing my leave."

"Hunt small blight where you find it. Bring me the stones."
