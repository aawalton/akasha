"Yeah, definitely the small one first, that's the deer?"

"The deer," Marda says. "North bank of the Wren Brook, below the ford, the woodcutter says."

You walk out the south gate and down to the crossroads, then an hour into the Wrenwood.

The Wren Brook runs loud and cold. Beside its water, the blue current runs thick.

Upstream, smoke rises from the charcoal-burner's hut.

Three hundred paces below the ford, the frost on the north bank goes gray.

The deer lies there, two days dead, its throat torn out. Its hide has rotted to black sludge.

The blight still seeps in it. You feel it before you reach the bank.

As soon as it's in reach, you braid a pull strand into a Current Lash and strike.

The lash only cuts dead flesh. But the pull strand bites deep and draws the blight up out of it.

The braid pulls twice at once, two draws like a bite's. The current carries the cold off your arm.

The blight clots on the bank into a dark seed stone. Around it, the gray frost turns white again.

You pick up the seed stone.

Then you see the prints. Wolf prints circle the deer, bigger than any Wrenwood wolf's.

They lead down the bank and into the brook. On neither bank, up or down, do they come out.
