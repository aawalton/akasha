You walk out the south gate and down the road, two miles between the winter fields.

The beeches at the crossroads rattle their dry gold leaves. Magpies chatter at you from the boughs.

The little slate-roofed shrine sits at the corner where the roads meet. Two currents meet there too. Among their threads of every color, a few white-gold ones run where they cross at the shrine.

You sit down with your back to the shrine's stone. It's warm, even in the cold air.

You close your eyes and rest.

After a while you feel it. The white-gold threads drift toward you and seep in, warm as sun.

Inside, you fill as you sit, steady. Faster than you ever fill in town. Twice as fast, near enough. Brannagh's mother was right about the shrine.

By mid-afternoon you're full to the brim.
