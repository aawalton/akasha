"Does it give back more mana than it costs?" you ask. "If not, I don't know that it would help me much quite yet."

Brannagh scratches the cat behind its one ear, thinking.

"One jackalope's antlers, ground, make two draughts. Each fills a small store of mana. My mother said a pot gives back about three times what you pour into it. And she poured at day's end, from what a night's sleep would fill anyway. I never had enough in me to try it. Whether it's a bargain for you, only you'll know."

She pushes the loaf and cheese across the counter toward you.

"If it's getting it back faster you want," she says, "my mother had a saying. The old shrine at the crossroads gives back what a day took, to them as rest there. Maybe it's old wives' talk. I never had the mana to test it."
