You find the lane off the Wool Square, and a crooked green sign over a low door.

Inside it's dim and low-ceilinged, hung with bunches of drying herbs, and it smells of mint and woodsmoke. An old woman, bent and sharp-eyed, looks up from the counter. A one-eared gray cat watches you from beside her elbow.

"Marda sent me," you say, and set out your twenty-three frostcaps.

The old woman turns each one over and checks every root cut, muttering to the cat as she goes.

"Clean. All of them." She counts twenty-three copper out onto the counter.

As you reach for the coins, your sleeve rides up your forearm, over the pink seam of the bite.

The old woman's hand shoots out. She catches your wrist and turns it to the lamp.

She studies the seam, close. Then she looks up at you, sharp as a thorn.

"Who closed this?"
