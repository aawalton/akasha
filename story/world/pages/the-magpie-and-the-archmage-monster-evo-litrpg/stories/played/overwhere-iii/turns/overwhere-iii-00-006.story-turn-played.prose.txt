You follow him in out of the cold.

The common room is warm. A big hearth roars at one end, six trestle tables fill the floor, and a stair goes up at the back. Carters and drovers sit over their cups. A few of them glance at the coat and your bare feet, look away, and then look again.

A big, red-armed woman of fifty or so turns from the hearth, and her voice fills the room.

"Tobin Wick! I heard you from the square. Those mules deserve better, and so do my ears."

Tobin grins into his beard. He takes you by the elbow and steers you forward. "Bet, this is Nala. Robbed on the road, knocked on the head. I'm standing her supper and a bed."

Bet looks you over sharply, top to toe. Her eyes stay longest on your bare feet. Her face doesn't tell you anything yet.

"Eleven copper for hers, then," she says.

Tobin counts coins into her hand without a word.

Bet sits you both at the table nearest the fire. The heat soaks into your cold feet. She brings two bowls of mutton stew, black bread and a wedge of hill cheese, and goes back to work.

The stew is thick and hot and salty. You haven't eaten since before you woke up here.

Tobin eats like a man who has driven all day, and says little.

At the next table, two drovers talk low over their cider.

"...Cal Fenn. Seven weeks now. Last seen crossing the Wren Brook ford, after that blighted stag."

"Town looked ten days. Only his brother Jory still goes out after him."

"Blight's thick in there this winter. Guild's paying a silver a stone for it."

"Silver a stone, and nobody fool enough to go fetch it," says the other, and drinks.

Tobin's spoon has stopped. He is staring into his bowl, his jaw tight.
