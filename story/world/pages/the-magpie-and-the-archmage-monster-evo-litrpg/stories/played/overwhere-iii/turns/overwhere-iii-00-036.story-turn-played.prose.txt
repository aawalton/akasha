"Gladly," you tell Maud. "But I need to finish Garrick first. He's got it bad. Cob after."

Maud grunts at being made to wait. "Brannagh's, then. I'll send him there for noon."

You walk to Brannagh's lane, your ribs still aching where the staff caught them.

Garrick is awake this time, propped up with a bowl of broth. The purple has drawn back to his knee. He sets down the bowl and puts out his leg without being asked.

You push out your white-gold thread and pull. Cold bites up your arm. You hold it.

Another third of the black draws out and clots at the lip of the bite into a seed-sized stone. The purple shrinks back to a hand's width around the wound.

You pull again. The cold is worse, but the last of the black comes, and another stone with it.

To your sight, the wound is clean.

You shift the thread to stitching and draw the mending weave across it. Under the light, the torn calf knits slowly and closes to a long pink seam.

Garrick looks at it a long while. "What's your name, lass?"

"Nala," Brannagh says from the door.

"Nala." He says it slowly, like he's putting it somewhere safe. "I owe you. My boy Aled comes down on market day. He'll bring you a sheepskin coat, fleece-lined."

Brannagh sits down hard on her stool and laughs out loud. With her spoon she tips the two new seed stones into your clay cup.

"Every blight case I hear of, I'll send to you. Ten copper apiece. What do you say?"
