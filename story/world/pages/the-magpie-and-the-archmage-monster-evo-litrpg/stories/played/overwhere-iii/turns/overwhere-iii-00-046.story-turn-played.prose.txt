You walk up to Brannagh's with Pip at your heels. The bench is full, and folk stand in the lane.

A drover with a burn scar up his forearm. A girl with a split lip healed crooked. Six or seven more.

You tell them straight.

"I'm happy to fix your scars for free, as goodwill. Gladly. But scars come last, after fresh hurts and blight, and I don't have much mana to work with. I didn't know you'd be coming today, so I've already run dry cleansing blightstones at the post."

You look round at them. "If any of you knows a way to raise my mana, or bring it back faster, I'm all ears. Otherwise you might have to wait a week or two, or set a time with me in advance."

The drover shrugs. "Waited ten years on this arm. I'll wait a fortnight."

The others take it well, without a grumble. Brannagh takes a stub of chalk and starts a list of names on the slate by her door.

A carter's wife says Thornmere sells mana draughts, a silver a flask, "for them as has a silver."

Most of them leave something on the counter for the healer: eggs, a brown loaf, a wedge of cheese.

The lane empties. Brannagh sets the chalk down and taps her mother's receipt book.

"You read the mana draught. Antler, frostcap, honey, a night's steeping. I never brewed it. Too little mana to pour. You've mana to pour, when you're full. I've the honey and the pot. Bring me the antler and the frostcaps, and we'll see if it takes."
