You walk out the south gate and an hour into the Wrenwood, to the Wren Brook ford.

Above the ford, the path climbs beside the brook into the deeper wood. You follow it alone.

A furlong up, the brambles are trampled into a wallow, and the frost around it is gray.

Your sight finds the blight: a dark smear, thick and heavy, lying low in the thorns.

You draw white-gold close over your skin. The Holy Ward settles on the first try, a faint shimmer.

You braid lash and pull onto the lent current and send it into the brambles, at the head.

It strikes hard between the eyes. The boar heaves up out of the wallow, squealing, bristles black.

It's huge, a hill boar with tusks like hooks, and it comes straight at you.

The charge slams you off your feet. The ward takes the edge off it, and the tusks never break skin.

You roll up, ribs aching, and send the second braid. It lands on the skull again.

The boar staggers, wheels, and slashes at you. You twist clear by a hand's width.

The third braid strikes. Black drains out of the boar's bristles along the pull strand.

The last of its blight comes away. Its eyes clear to a plain, wild brown.

Your well is down to the dregs, too low to pay for another braid.

Ten paces off, the boar stands blowing hard, blood running down its snout, a plain hill boar now.

It lowers its tusks at you and paws the frost.
