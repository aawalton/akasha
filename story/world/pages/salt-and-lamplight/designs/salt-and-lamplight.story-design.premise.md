A yuri romance, told chapter by chapter, with Nala at its heart.

Nala is Alan. One evening he dozes over a book at his back window and wakes on a cold shingle beach below a lighthouse, in a woman's body: about twenty-five, petite, pale and freckled, with long dark red hair. She calls herself Nala from the moment she wakes, and everyone in the story knows her as Nala. She remembers being Alan, and she does not know how she came here or whether she can go back.

The shore belongs to a small harbour town on a northern coast of a world of the world builder's making: fishing boats, slate roofs, salt wind, a little quiet magic in the old stories and hardly any in daily life. The woman who keeps the lighthouse finds her there, and the story is the two of them falling in love.

It is a slow burn, and the romance leads. The chapters live in the small things: shared work, meals, weather, the town and its people, what each woman hides and what she lets the other see. Nala is also learning this body and this life, and learning what it is to want a woman as a woman. Every step closer is earned on the page, and a setback is real.

When the story reaches sex, because the two of them have reached it, it is written fully explicit, and it never turns away from the romance: the feeling is in it, and it changes what is between them. It comes when it is right for the two of them, and never before.

Every woman in the story is an adult, and no man is ever a love interest.
