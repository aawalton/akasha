You hold very still, as still as a body this small can be, and strain to make out what the voices beyond the walls are saying.

There are three of them. One voice is close and soft, and it hums through the walls around you as much as it reaches you through them: the woman you are inside, your mother. The other two are farther off and duller, a man's voice and a woman's. The man does most of the talking. He takes his time over it, and he sounds pleased. Now and then the woman cuts in, short and dry, and he goes on.

You listen for English and don't hear a word of it. The sounds run together in a language you have never heard, all soft consonants and long, rolling vowels, with breaks in the wrong places.

But one word keeps coming back. The man says it, then your mother, then the man again, the same two sounds each time. Kerith.

The rest slips past you. You can follow the shape of the talk, when a question goes up at its end and when an answer comes down flat, and you can tell they are pleased with something. What any of it means, you can't tell at all.
