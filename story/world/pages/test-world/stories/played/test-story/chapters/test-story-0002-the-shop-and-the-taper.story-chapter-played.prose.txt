You cross the square and step through the green door into the Test Shop. Shelves behind the counter hold blades, coats, lamps, ore and small red bottles.

The shopkeeper looks up and smiles. "Back again? Most folk come in twice."

You nod at the shelves, and she waits while you look.

You set the brass token on the counter. The star stamped on it catches the light.

The shopkeeper turns it over once. "That's a tester's token. Keep it. It means you've been here before."

She slides it back to you, and you tuck it away again.

You uncork a healing draught and drink it down at the counter. The red liquid tastes of iron and honey, and warmth spreads through your chest.

Your scrapes and bruises close and fade.

You set the empty bottle on the counter. The shopkeeper glances at it. "Anything on the shelves catch your eye?"

You point to the lanterns on the shelf behind her. "I'd like to buy one of those."

She reaches up, takes a tin lantern down and sets it between you. "Eight copper, and that's with the oil in it."

You count eight copper onto the counter, and she sweeps them into her palm.

She pushes the lantern across to your side of the counter, then slides a lit taper after it.

You swing open the lantern's little glass window and touch the taper to the wick. The wick catches, and a steady yellow light fills the glass.

The shopkeeper nods at the flame. "Where are you taking it?"

"Thank you." You lift the lantern by its wire handle and hold it up, and the light swings across the shelves.

You nod toward the back of the shop. "What's beyond your back door?"

She glances at the door behind the counter. "Wooden steps, down to my cellar."

She tips her head at the lantern in your hand. "It's cool down there, and dry. Barrels along one wall, and a single hook for a lamp." She points past the shelves, toward the square. "There's a stone stair at the far end. It comes up by the well." Her eyes go back to the lantern in your hand.

You set the lantern down, step back into the middle of the shop and draw your iron sword. On the counter, the taper you lit the lantern from still stands burning, and you fix your eyes on it.

You step in and cut once, level, through the middle of the taper. The blade passes clean, and the burning top half drops onto the counter. The flame ducks and gutters low on the way down, then rights itself and burns on.

The shopkeeper has stepped back from the counter. She stares at the two halves of the taper. Her eyes go from the burning half to your bare blade. "What was that for?"
