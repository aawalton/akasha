"I'm afraid I am new to the lands," you say. "You would know better than I. Based on the targeting nature of the damage, only affecting Zhao Jun, I would have expected humans as the source, were it not for the tracks. A mortal beast I would not expect to forage the same place every time. However, spirit beasts can have unusual behavior, though they are far more rare. So you have two options: the likely behavior from an unlikely source, or the unlikely behavior from a likely source. I cannot tell you which it is in this case, but it would be wise to be ready for both."

Tie Bo hears you out. His face stays still the whole time. Then he shakes his head, slowly.

"A boar comes back to a place it likes. Again and again, till it's cleaned out or killed. That part's natural. Zhao Jun's rice is just the first it meets, coming down the river." He pauses. "It's the size that isn't natural."

He shifts the bow on his shoulder.

"I tracked it twice this spring. Up to a wallow in a boggy hollow, below the gorge. By the prints and the wallow, it's as heavy as two buffalo. No boar is that big." He lifts a hand, well above his own head. "Tusk marks on the pines. Up here."

The hand comes down. For a moment he stands quiet, looking at the lamp burning in the shrine.

"Last autumn a boar gored my brother. Tie Shan. A torn-eared boar, big even then. I put my spear in its flank before it broke away. Shan died of the wound before winter."

He says it flatly. His jaw is tight.

"My grandfather told of a white stag in the hills, long ago. A spirit beast. I never saw one." He breathes out through his nose. "I think it's the same boar. I think it's grown into something no spear was made for."

Then his voice changes, and it is a hunter's voice, plain and practical.

"Here is how I'd do it. From dusk tomorrow, we watch the broken wall. Downwind. A line of sharpened stakes in front of it. Me behind them with the barred spear, and two steady men beside me. The Fang boys uphill with torches and gongs, to drive it down on us."

He looks at you again, straight on.

"I'd want you there, honored spirit. Out of the line. Behind us, safe. If you can tell what it is by the look of it, I need to know before I set my spear." A beat. "Will you come?"
