You hold the three sticks to the little flame until their tips catch and glow red. Thin grey smoke curls up from them, sweet and resinous.

You speak calmly and clearly, so the crowd can hear.

"Old Grandfather of the Three Stones. Your people respect the old ways and honor their ancestors as they have for many years. May they be safe and prosper, that they may continue to do so for many more."

The smoke from the three sticks goes straight up in the still air, in three thin grey threads.

A murmur runs through the crowd. Somebody near the front says, "Straight. Look. Straight up."

An old man at the side presses his hands together and bows his head. Then a woman beside him. Then more. Heads bow all through the crowd, a ripple of them running from the front to the back. Somebody's grandmother is wiping her eyes on her sleeve.

The sharp-faced old woman has stopped watching your feet. She is frowning at the smoke instead. Then she looks down at your shadow once more, where it lies long and ordinary on the ground in the lamplight. Her mouth purses. She keeps it shut, and she stays upright while the heads around her bow.

Beside you, Gu lets out his breath, very quietly, like a man setting down a heavy load.

The incense is still burning in your hand. The smoke still rises straight.

The two big broad-faced young men at the front can't hold still any longer.

"Honored spirit!" the bigger one calls out. "I'm Fang Da! This is my brother, Fang Er! We heard about the beast in Zhao Jun's rice! When there's a hunt, take us! We're not afraid!"

"We'll stick it like a pig!" Fang Er shouts. A few people laugh. A few people hush him.

At the back, the weathered man with the bow shifts his weight. His eyes are on the two brothers, and he keeps his silence.
