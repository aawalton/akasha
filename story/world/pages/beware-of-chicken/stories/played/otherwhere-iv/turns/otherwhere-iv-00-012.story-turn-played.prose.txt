You turn to the two brothers.

"You two look like just the kind of strong lads who would do well on a hunt," you say. "But I'm afraid that's not up to me. You'll need to talk with Headman Gu and Zhao Jun about that. I have great knowledge, but when it comes to the hunt itself, Headman Gu is more mighty than I."

The Fang brothers grin at each other as if you had handed them a prize.

"Strong lads!" Fang Da says, loud enough for the back rows. "You heard her. Strong lads!"

A laugh runs through the crowd. The Fangs puff up like a pair of roosters.

Gu takes the three smoking sticks out of your hand, gently, with a small bow. He turns to the shrine and bows to it three times, and plants the sticks in the sand of the burner. Then he turns to face the crowd.

"You heard the honored spirit. The hunt is the headman's business. So hear the headman." He leans on his cane. "Tie Bo will lead it, if he will. Nobody else. Tie Bo knows boar. The Fang boys can beat, with torches and gongs, if they can keep their mouths shut for once."

The laugh this time is louder. Fang Er goes red to the ears.

"Zhao Jun. You come to my house tonight and tell me all of it about your walls. All of it."

Somewhere in the crowd, Zhao Jun ducks his head.

"The almanac says tomorrow is good for hunting. We will see. Now go home. Eat."

The crowd loosens and breaks apart, talking, and people keep looking back over their shoulders at you as they go.

The tea-stall woman moves among them with her basket, handing out steamed buns. She stops in front of you and bows, and holds the basket out with both hands. Steam rises from under the cloth.

"Lin Qiao, from the stall under the camphor. Please, honored spirit. They're still warm."

Her small boy stares up at your hair with his mouth hanging open, until she tugs him away by the hand.

The square empties. The last light goes out of the sky, and the lamp at the shrine burns on by itself.

One man stays. The lean, weathered one with the bow, who has stood in the same place at the back all this time. He comes up now, slow and quiet, and bows to you, not deeply.

"Tie Bo," he says. His voice is low and rough. "The hunter. Zhao Jun says you told him it might be a boar. Or a spirit beast."

He looks at you steadily.

"Which is it?"
