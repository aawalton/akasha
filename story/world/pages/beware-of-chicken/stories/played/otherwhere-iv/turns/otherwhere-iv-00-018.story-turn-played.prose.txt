"Beyond the infestation of insects?" you say. "Hmm. Have you tried diatomaceous earth? That is a decently good repellent for ordinary ants, but I'm not certain it would be easily available here. I'm afraid my answer for you is similar to what I told Tie Bo. I have a great deal of general knowledge, but when it comes to the specific flora and fauna of this region, you likely know much more than I."

Granny Hua's eyebrows go up.

"Dia-what earth?" She tries the words again under her breath, and shakes her head. "Never heard of it. I'd wager every soul in these hills would say the same, and there's none dug anywhere near."

She takes the stalk out of your hand and turns it in the sun.

"Ants, yes. They climb up to tend these." She taps one of the black clusters with a green-rimmed nail. "Plant lice. The ants guard them for the sweet dew they give off, like a herd of goats. Kill the ants and you've done half the job. That's half an answer."

She points with her chin at the base of the stems by the gate. Around each one lies a grey ring of ash.

"I ring my stems with wood ash already. Ash and shell lime turn ants back. Everybody knows that."

She drops the stalk and looks at you for a long, level moment.

"Now, that's an honest answer. I think better of one who owns what she doesn't know. Better than the Lanqiao fortune-tellers, who know everything and charge for it." Her eyes narrow. "But I never heard of a spirit of knowledge that didn't know plant lice."

Tie Bo shifts his feet. He looks from one of you to the other.

"Warm hands," Granny Hua goes on, "and a heartbeat, and there's not a mark on you. And a head full of odd words, and you haven't a notion what's crawling on a weed in my garden."

She folds her arms.

"You're no spirit, girl. I don't know what you are. But you're no spirit. And I'll not hand poison to someone who's lying to the whole village about what she is. I'd not do it for a boar. I'd not do it for anything."

Tie Bo is staring at you now. His face has gone very still.

Granny Hua jerks her chin at you. "So. What are you, then?"
