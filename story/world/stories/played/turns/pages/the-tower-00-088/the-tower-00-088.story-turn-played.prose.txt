Coming up off the stone is as ugly as going down on it was — one hand flat to the rock, the bad grind of the leap still in both knees — and you cross to the wreck low and ready, the lantern out ahead and your own heat banked up behind it, drawn from your well and held at the wick: a flame you can throw the moment anything here gives you the reason.

The seat is kindling. The high chair the Host wore his welcome in is so much splinter now, and what spilled from it lies in the muck where the gold let go. You crouch and lift the first thing free: a lens the size of your palm, clear glass gone the grey-white of a blind eye — clouded, the way the lenses you cut from the stalker's eyes were clouded — and colder than the wet rock under it, still working somehow to stay that way. You reach at it with your ember-sense to take its measure; your sense slides off and comes back told nothing. You wrap it and stow it anyway.

The second is the folded light: the mantle the haven's welcomes were cut from, slack and grey now and shed in one piece. It weighs nothing. It spills through your fingers as you gather it, and where the lantern grazes it a little of the old warmth wakes and guts out, like breath blown on a coal with no coal left under it. You fold it down small and pocket it. A smith keeps the stock even when the blade it makes is years off.

The grey thing you leave where it fell. It has gone to husk — cooling weight the forge has no use for. Nothing else in the wreck holds your eye.

But the other heat does. It has been there the whole while, off past the drop's far shoulder where your light gives out — the live warmth you crossed the dark to stand near, banked low, close. You turn the lantern full on it, and the fire you are holding with it.

You do not have to wonder what wears it. With the haven dead there is nothing left to weave a kind face from, so the dark hands you the true one instead: low to the ground, wrong-jointed, a lean cold shape that only ever wore a man's face in light it was paid to. It has been hurt. It carries one side of itself badly, and the stone grinds under it as it drags itself around to face you.

Then it gathers the ruined weight of itself to move, and your fire is already drawn back to throw.
