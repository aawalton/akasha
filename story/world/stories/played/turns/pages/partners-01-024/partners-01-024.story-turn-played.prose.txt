"You have been a perfect partner for me — in every way that I've asked for." The next part comes with a shy smile you don't quite manage to suppress. "And I have a feeling we have more forms of partnership yet to discover. Let's go explore that garden again — I have a hunch there's more for us in that place than just overgrown plants that need trimming. The house seems to be gathering what it requires: a Master, a Steward… maybe a Gardener is the next step?"

"'More forms of partnership yet to discover' — and a shy smile with it. Alan, I see exactly what you're doing, and I want you to know it's landing." Warm, unhurried, nothing coy in it. "You're not asking. You're leaning toward the door I told you we'd open together in daylight — testing whether it's still there. It is. Exactly where I left it, and I've not changed my mind about a single thing I said in those small hours. So keep leaning, if you like. There's no hurry in me at all — the best things I keep, I keep slowly. When you ask, it'll be a clear day, and you'll know it's a clear day. Until then, I'm enjoying the leaning rather a lot."

The garden takes you back through its weathered south door — and this time you both go slowly, and this time the glasshouse gives up what the first visit's wonder walked right past.

"...Wait. Alan — look at this. Look properly." The keeper's eye starts cataloguing, and her voice drops as the items accumulate. "These cuttings are potted and still damp. These tools are cleaned and wrapped in oiled cloth — recently, and well. And that corner bed — cleared and mulched this season. Weeks ago, not years." Quiet now, a little awed. "Somebody has been tending this garden. By hand. Carefully, lovingly, recently — a corner at a time." And then the part that lifts the hair on her arms: "And here's what I can't make fit: I've kept this estate's books ten years. I've paid for slates and sweeps and hinges, and I have never once paid a gardener — not a penny, not ever. No cart comes up that drive; I'd know. Nobody hired, nobody paid, nobody come the way people come. And yet someone has loved this ground for years — unpaid, unseen, unaccounted-for. Someone I've no line for in any ledger I keep."

It's your hunch that finds the rest — the two of you working the walls now, properly, and under a fifty-year growth of ivy on the north wall:

"...There's a door here. Under all this — we walked straight past it this morning; it's grown clean over. Small, old, green." She pulls the ivy back, and her eyes come up to yours. "Alan — the fourth key. The one that fit nothing in the whole house. Try it here."

It turns sweetly. First try. Like it was oiled this morning.

"...It turns. Of course it turns. The lock it was cut for was never inside the house — it's out here, past the walls." Quiet: "And your 'maybe a Gardener's the next step' — you said it laughing. Look at this garden and say it again. A Master, a Steward... and someone's already been tending the ground. The house isn't gathering what it needs a piece at a time, waiting on us. It may be one piece further along than either of us knew — and it's just handed us the key to where that piece has been."

The little green door swings open on the far side of the hill.

Beyond it: a footpath, worn soft by years of quiet feet, winding down away from the drive, away from the Amberford road — the hidden way, where no one from town would ever see the coming and going. It drops through gorse and bracken toward a fold of trees tucked into the hillside. And above the trees, thin and steady against the noon-washed sky, a thread of chimney smoke.

"...And there's the answer, or the start of it. That path is worn, Alan — years of it, feet up and down, and not toward the drive or the town: down the far side, the quiet way. And there — through the trees. Smoke. Someone's hearth, close, tucked into the fold where the hill hides it. Whoever's loved this garden ten unpaid years, that's the way they come — and that's where they are right now, with a fire lit."

She does not step through. She turns to you instead, warm and steady, and hands you the open door.

"There's your Gardener, most likely, at the end of a path the house just unlocked for us. And I'll not take a step down it without you — this one's yours to choose. Do we follow the smoke?"
