And then the light tipped over its threshold. The deep amber that had lain along the floorboards thinned away to nothing, and without anyone marking the moment of it the warm overhead bulbs became the room's own light rather than the failing day's — the street beyond the fogged glass darkening to an evening blue, the city's far lights beginning to bead and swim in the wet. The shop crossed quietly from afternoon into lamplit dusk on its own unhurried rhythm, the way it turns a song over or lets a table empty, asking nothing of anyone for it. In the back corner it registered only as the gold deepening a shade warmer around the two of them. The warmth held; the joined hands stayed at rest in the space between the chairs; the rain kept its steady wash on the glass — and the shelter drew closer still, gathered a little nearer now against the dark coming on outside the windows.
