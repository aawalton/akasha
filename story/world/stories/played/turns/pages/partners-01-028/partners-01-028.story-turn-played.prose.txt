Her acceptance goes through you like warmth through cold hands — and then her question stops you dead.

*Tell me your happiest dream.* You're not used to having your desires met so directly. Somewhere far back, in the life you can't quite reach, you learned that your needs got met after everyone else's — never before. Asking out loud still feels like reaching for something that isn't yours to take. So you sit with it, longer than you mean to, while she waits without hurrying you. And then you give her the truest thing you own.

"I dream of having a found family. A house filled with women who are loving, intelligent, beautiful — and most of all, kind. Who love me, and who love each other. I think that's why this house has called me here. And why you were the first to meet me."

"...Stop a moment, before the dream." She's watching you with those listening eyes, and what she catches first isn't the dream at all. "I watched that question land on you just now, and I watched how long it took you to answer — and I've read enough people to know that particular difficulty. Nobody ever asked you your dream before, did they. Not really. You learned young that your needs came last — after everyone's, never before — and asking them out loud still feels like reaching for a thing that isn't yours to take." The deepest tenderness she's shown you yet: "So hear this from me first, before I say one word about what you actually want: your needs do not come last in this house. Not last. I've spent my whole life putting others first, Alan — but I chose it, freely, from a full cup, and that is a wholly different thing from being taught you don't get to be hungry. I know that difference better than almost anyone alive. And I'll not have you living the second one under a roof I keep. Whatever you dream — you're allowed to want it. Start there."

Then — because she's decided never to pretend to you — the honest thing:

"Now — the dream itself. And I'll not pretend, because I've just spent an hour deciding not to pretend to you ever again. It cost me something to hear. Not much, not for long — but something true, and you should know it landed. Ten minutes ago I laid myself completely bare on this couch and chose you like you were mine, singular — the one life I'd run well. And you've answered by telling me I'm the first of many." Steady — no drama in it, just the honesty of a strong woman doing honest arithmetic. "That's a real adjustment, and I'd be lying to wave it off. Not a wound — I want that clear, it isn't a wound. It's only that I chose 'partner, just you' with my whole chest, and now I must choose it again knowing it means something larger and stranger than I did when I said it. So give me the moment to do that honestly. You've earned honest — and honest costs a beat here."

And you can watch the moment turn in her — the keeper's eye and the connector's warmth waking together, a breath behind the pang.

"—And here's the other half of my honest, a breath behind the first: I've been reading this exact shape all day, and you've just handed me the word for it. A house that made me a room before I'd chosen to stay. An office within the hour I was named. A garden tended by a hand my books never paid. The wood leaning toward you. A warden at your door by dusk. I said it myself at that garden gate — the house may be further along than either of us knows. And it is, isn't it. It's gathering. Master, Steward, Gardener — and it isn't done." The connector lights, entirely genuine now: "Alan — you dream of a house full of women who love you and love each other, and you've said it to the one woman in this vale whose entire gift is exactly that: knowing people, weaving them, keeping a web whole so no one falls through. To most women you'd have described something strange. To me you've described my native country. I don't know if the house designed this — I can't know it, and I'll not pretend to. But if it did, it chose its first tender well, because tending a family into being is the one thing I was built for."

She takes the word you gave her, then, and hands it back with its true weight.

"And you said a thing I want to pick up and hand back, because you may not know its weight. You said I was the first to meet you. First. Let me tell you what first is going to mean — because I get to define it, and I have. First is not a rank; I've no wish to be one of a numbered row. First is foundation. A found family does not hold itself together, Alan — it's the hardest thing on earth to keep whole: all those hearts, all that history, every way love among many can fray and curdle and go unsaid. Someone has to tend the web. Someone has to be the keeper who makes the plural actually work — who sees who's owed a kindness, who's quietly hurting, which two haven't found each other yet — and does the unglamorous upkeep so the whole thing stands. That is not a lesser seat than 'only love.' It's the load-bearing one. The cornerstone." Level, her eyes on yours: "So here's how I hold 'partner, level, no rung' and a house of many in one breath, and mean both: I'm not level with you by being your only. I'm level with you by being your co-founder. You dreamed the family; I'm the one who'll build it and keep it whole. That's a thing only I can be — and it's exactly as load-bearing as the man at its heart. That's my first. I'll take it."

And then, the reckoning done, she chooses — the way she does everything.

"So. I've felt the whole of it now — pang, recognition, and the shape of my own place in it — and I choose it. Not swept, not talked round: chosen, both eyes open, which is the only way I do anything. Partner still — only now I know the size of the word, and I choose the size." Warm, and the terms come with it, light but real: "Two things I'll ask back, and they're small. One: honesty, always, the way you just gave me it — no woman in this house ever learns her place from anyone but you, plainly. Secrets are how found families rot, and I'll not keep this one on those rails. Two: you let me actually do it — tend the web, build it well, and trust that when I tell you someone needs you, or a knot's forming between two hearts, I'm reading it true. That's the job. Give me the job."

The keeper's eye goes wry, then — careful, and honest about its own limits.

"...And I'd be a poor keeper not to say the quiet obvious thing: we've a warden coming to dinner at dusk. A woman the wood likes, who tended your garden unpaid for years, at the door of a house that's gathering. I don't know what she is to this yet — I can't, and I'll not presume it onto her before she's had the chance to just be a person to us. But I'll watch the evening with new eyes, set a beautiful table, and let the house show us what it's doing at its own pace. Together."

A steady, radiant, level smile.

"Now — I've made your dream mine, out loud, one hour into us. Tell me I've read it right. And then let me go and start on that dinner — the first night of a found family ought to be fed properly."

Somewhere down the corridor, unbidden, a lamp warms.
