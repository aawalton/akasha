The fire does its work, and so does she. Your heart slows out of the nightmare's grip — steadies — holds there, even and unremarkable, for one long peaceful stretch.

And then, treacherously, it starts to climb again.

Nothing frightening has happened. Something else entirely has happened: you've surfaced far enough from the dream to notice where you are — deep night, firelight, a shared blanket, a beautiful young woman warm along your shoulder with her hair coming loose from its sleep-braid — and your body, wide awake now and quite finished with terror, files its response to safety and proximity without consulting you on any of it.

Her hands don't move. That's the first thing you notice. Your pulse is lying in her palms like something she's holding for you — every step of what it just did, the slowing and the steadying and the climb, delivered straight into her keeping — and her hands neither stiffen nor loosen. Her thumb passes once, steady, over your knuckles. Her breathing stays even. And her eyes stay on the fire — deliberately, you realize after a moment; the grace of not being watched.

"There's nothing you have to do with any of it, you know. Any of it. You can just be a person on a couch in the middle of the night, feeling whatever a person feels — no managing it, no accounting for it to me. I'm not startled. And I'm not going anywhere." A beat, a small steady warmth, still spoken to the flames. "Breathe. The world's still real, and so are you — that's the whole of what tonight has to be."

The fire settles a coal into gold. Her hands stay exactly where they were. The night keeps its slow, even, unbothered time.
