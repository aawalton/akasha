Charlotte’s abilities made setting up temporary camps easy. She could dig through the stone with ease, and there was nothing roaming the narrow caverns that could make it through her acidic webs. Not without making a lot of noise and giving them plenty of time to wake up and get ready. The makeshift shelter was ready before the soul storm even arrived, and the two settled in for bed right after.

Before bed, Elise decided to take a quick dreamwalk over to Sophie. It had been a few days, and while she told Sophie she would be traveling, if she had the time, she didn’t see much reason not to visit. Besides, she could use someone to talk to that could hold a real conversation. Charlotte was still too inexperienced, and Erun was Erun. The two of them made the talkative, scatterbrained Sophie sound like a wizened sage.

She arrived just in time to see Sophie melting some kind of metallic crawler into a pile of putrid biological slag. It was an extremely overkill way of dealing with the crawlers, but it was also extremely impressive. The amount of raw energy Sophie was outputting was so far beyond what Elise was capable of that it was almost comical.

Sophie sensed Elise immediately, and her eyes flickered yellow as she glanced over, but she quickly looked back at the dead crawler, pretending she hadn’t seen anything.

“Gimme a second,” she said telepathically.

“Alright,” Elise replied.

The reason for the delay was obvious, and also, unfortunately, a common occurrence. Jelor considered Sophie to be a national asset, which meant keeping her safe was a national interest. They were pouring a lot of resources into developing her talents and skills, but they were also pouring a lot into “protection,” which doubled as surveillance. She had her privacy when she was at home, but whenever she went anywhere else, she was constantly shadowed by high level guards. Some of them could even sense Elise’s visits, which was annoying.

Luckily, as much as Sophie was watched, she also had a lot of leeway. It had been nerve-wracking at first, but the more Sophie grew, the more valuable she was, and the less the king could control her. Not that he was really trying, but she was able to push back on things and tell the guards to mind their own business. She had to assure them she wasn’t talking to the government of another nation, which was technically untrue if the fey were counted as a nation, but as long as she said that, she could talk.

Sophie killed another three crawlers, going just as overboard every time. A tenth as much fire would have been enough to kill them. Melting them was completely unnecessary. It looked to Elise like she was working on power output rather than efficiency right now, though, so that was probably intentional. It also had the side effect of burning through her mana pool very quickly, so when she finished the other crawlers, she was done.

As Elise watched her work, she felt a pang of some kind of emotion she couldn’t explain. Sophie had grown up a lot, in more ways than one. She wasn’t getting taller anymore, having stopped right around six feet, but she was definitely getting older. She was pushing 15 years old, and while still visibly a teenager, it was hard to equate the current Sophie to the one from before Elise left.

She had gone up in Level as well, having surpassed Elise. Her Class was not going up quite as quickly in level as Elise would have expected for someone in her position, but it also wasn’t that surprising. Each Tier was slower than the previous one, and Sophie’s Class was a rare one that took more to level than other Classes. Still, she would reach 6th Tier in just a few more months, making her one of the youngest to ever do so. The youngest humanoids, at least. Elise technically had her beat, based purely on time with the System, but monsters gained more from killing than humanoids did, and Elise had gone through quite a few lucky encounters that pushed her much further than she would ordinarily have gotten.

Elise had been regularly visiting Sophie the entire time she was in the cave, but it was not quite the same as being there in person. There was a firm layer of separation that she couldn’t breach no matter how hard she tried, and seeing Sophie grow so much without being present had an odd effect on her mood. It wasn’t quite sadness, or anything like that, but it wasn’t a pleasant feeling either.

Sophie announced that she was out of mana to no one in particular, then reached into her pocket and pulled out a small glass marble, rolling it to the corner of the room away from the crawler remains. It stopped about ten feet from any walls and flashed with light and mana. When it faded, there was a stone cube twelve or so feet across with a thick metal vault door in the front. A portable shelter, made by the same person who had made the portable library Elise saw in the auction in Everspring. More importantly, it was warded with powerful privacy enchantments, preventing Sophie’s bodyguards from listening in.

The exterior of the shelter was very brutalist, and designed to be defensible, but the interior was the exact opposite. The walls were so thick that the interior was quite small, but it was very pleasant and cozy, with a bed, a desk that could be converted into a cooktop, and a shelf that was intended to hold weapons, but that Sophie had converted to a bookshelf. As soon as the door shut behind her, Sophie let out a long sigh, and turned around, flopping backward onto the bed.

“That looked fun,” Elise commented.

“I’m trying to max out my {Incinerate} Skill,” replied Sophie. “Apparently this is the best way to do it, but it’s so boring. Melt 4 crawlers, rest for half an hour to recover my mana, melt 4 more crawlers, rest for another half hour, melt, rest, melt, rest. I don’t even get Class experience for this anymore. The System decided it’s too unfair, I guess, and that the crawlers no longer stand a chance.”

“Why do it here instead of just incinerating some wood or something on the surface.”

Sophie shrugged. “Killing monsters gives more skill experience than not? I dunno. They said this was the best method, so I do it.”

“Huh. I wonder why killing monsters would make Skills level faster…”

Sophie shrugged again, though Elise knew she didn’t understand the full meaning of what Elise had said. Now that she knew Skills were just spell constructs, it made absolutely no sense that killing monsters would help them level faster. That wasn’t how spell constructs worked, which meant that the System was deliberately encouraging that for some reason.

“So, how’s the travel?” asked Sophie. “Done already?”

“No, still moving,” said Elise. “We just got to the destination, though. Kind of. We're in the right area, at least.”

“The other city with the other crazy lich, right?”

“Presumably. Except I don’t think there’s another lich here.”

“Why not?”

“There’s a lot of undead.”

Sophie sat up. “Okay, but doesn’t that mean it’s more likely to have a lich?”

“I don’t think so. They’re so disorganized. If there was a lich controlling them, you think they’d have more purpose.”

“Maybe he’s asleep,” suggested Sophie. “I read that liches can sleep for decades.”

“Sophie, this city is pre-System. It would have to be sleeping for millennia.”

“Well… Okay, maybe not. But still, if there are so many of them, there probably was an evil undead necromancer lich, right? A long time ago?”

“…Maybe. But even then, they probably aren’t alive anymore. Otherwise, they’d be doing something. Probably.”

“I guess. What kind of undead are they, though? Humphrey was talking about maybe taking an expedition to the Badlands soon to work on purification Skills, so I’ve been learning about different types.”

“Abyssal Draugr.”

“Draugr? Oh. Yeah, then there’s probably no evi lich.”

Sophie seemed almost disappointed, for some reason.

“Why do you say that?”

“Draugr are naturally occurring undead. They can’t be created. So yeah, those weren’t made by an evil lich.”

“I see…”

That confirmed that Erun had somehow done something. Elise couldn’t imagine that his entire cavern could be completely devoid of these naturally-occurring undead otherwise. Not if it went through the same thing the others did. And she also couldn’t imagine that another lich in a similar position to Erun would allow all these draugr to exist.

She didn’t voice these thoughts, though. She knew that sharing too much information with Sophie was dangerous. Even letting Sophie know that the cavern was pre-System was pushing it. The last thing she wanted was to get her adopted sister in trouble with the System for knowing too much.

It was for that same reason that she had not been teaching Sophie about all the System-less soul development she was doing. Everything with the celestials was dangerous, but if Elise went as far as to teach Sophie how to get around the System, things would get truly dangerous. If she tried teaching Sophie, and Sophie got a System bounty placed on her head, she would never forgive herself.

Besides, Sophie actually seemed to have things handled. Irylax had done something to her before ascending. Elise wasn’t entirely sure what it was, because Sophie was uncharacteristically tight-lipped about it, but Elise could sense that her soul was developing beyond what it should have been for its level. Whatever she was doing, it was helping, and in the future, when it was safer, Elise would be able to instruct her properly. Or see if she could convince Aya’Rhun to take her in.

Sophie chattered on for the next half hour, telling stories about random things that had happened in her classes. Elise shared a few as well, but her days in the caverns were fairly monotonous, so there wasn’t much to say. Plus, she enjoyed listening to Sophie talk. It was a nice break from reality, and a reminder that in spite of everything else, life was going on just fine back home.

A knock on the door interrupted a story about a squirrel that broke into the Academy pantry and got drunk on cooking wine. Sophie stopped mid-sentence with a sigh and got up to check the door. Elise watched as she said a few words about being out soon to her guard, then closed the door and turned back around.

“I gotta go,” said Sophie. “They want me to do two more rounds before we head back.”

“It’s fine,” said Elise. “It was good talking.”

“Yeah… See you tomorrow?”

“Maybe. We’ll see.”

“Alright, then. Seeya!”

Sophie turned to open the door again, but Elise called out to stop her at the last moment.

“Wait!”

“Yeah?” asked Sophie turning around, her hand on the door handle.

Elise mentally wrestled with whether or not she could continue for a few seconds.

“I–” she started. “Hypothetically, if you found out you weren’t who you thought you were… what would you do?”

“If I wasn’t who I thought I was? What do you mean?”

“Like, everything you thought you were was a lie. Your parents weren’t your real parents. Your home wasn’t your real home. But you can’t remember what actually happened, so all you know is that the truth is missing. You know that you don’t know, but you don’t know what you don’t know. Y’know?”

“Well,” Sophie said slowly. “I mean, if you don’t remember, does it really matter?”

“I think it’s important.”

“Right,” said Sophie. "But, like, does it matter for everything else? Even if I had other parents I forgot about, my parents are still my parents, y’know? Just because stuff happened before doesn’t mean that the current stuff isn’t real. And I know you had a life before you met us… but you’re still my sister, right?”

“…Yeah, you’re right,” said Elise with a smile. “Thanks.”

“No problem. So… Now goodbye?”

“Yeah. See you later.”

“Seeya!”

Elise returned to the cave, where Charlotte was lightly snoring in a web hammock. She felt like a small weight had been lifted off her chest. Not a huge one, but the conversation with Sophie definitely helped, especially there at the end. She was still anxious about what she would learn if and when she learned the truth about her past, but Sophie was right. Whatever had happened in the past, it wouldn’t change what was happening now. It wouldn’t change who she was today.

Her time on Earth had been real. Her time in this world was real. Sophie was still her sister. Charlotte was her… other sister? Daughter? Niece? Oberon was her mentor. The System was her enemy. Whatever she learned about her past, that wouldn’t change. She would let it.

She drifted off soon after, and once again dreamt of the past.

“Isel, that’s– that’s incredible!”

The voice was Ur’s, but he sounded younger.

“Pretty cool, right?” said Isel smugly.

“How did you do it?”

“I dunno. I just… did.”

“You should show your father!”

“I’m not sure…”

“Do it,” said a third voice. The same other female voice Elise had heard in the previous dream. “It’s really cool.”

“Seriously. I bet he’ll be impressed! I’ve never seen anything like that! It’s… It’s almost revolutionary!”

“‘Almost’?”

“Well, it’s still got a few—ahem—kinks to work out. But once we can figure those out…”

The dream ended there, and Elise woke up once again confused. The dreams never made complete sense. They weren’t in chronological order. They never had enough context for her to figure out what anyone was talking about. But they gave her hints.

Ur and Ber’Isel had been friends once. Close friends, by the sound of it. And Ber’Isel figured out something revolutionary that Ur got excited about… But what was it? Was it related to the System?

Charlotte woke up not long after, and Elise had to stop wondering to instead focus on the day ahead. The soul storm came and went, and Elise and Charlotte spent two or three hours eating and practicing aether pulses. They chatted a bit, with Charlotte asking more about respect for the dead, and Elise continuing to poorly explain it, and close to what Elise thought of as noon for the underground caverns, they finally went back to the main cave and fought through more draugr.

Overnight, the draugr had moved about, and the path they opened up was gone. Elise didn’t actually mind, though. It gave her plenty of time to practice, and she was fairly certain that there was no second lich in the castle. They had to check, and she wanted to get back to Erun relatively soon, but there was no deadline, and at the moment, she was more interested in laying the draugr to rest than she was in assuaging Erun’s loneliness. She would definitely be coming back here later to cull the rest. There was no way she was letting them all remain like this.

She made good progress in replicating {Suggest} as she and Charlotte slowly worked their way toward the castle. They likely wouldn’t make it there that day either, but they would thin the numbers of the draugr, as well as get a good idea of the city’s current state.

While exploring, they found another manor with a room where the draugr were staring at a portrait of Ber’Isel. This time, the painting was slightly more intact, so Elise decided to take it into Astrid’s Star. She didn’t sense any obvious enchantment, aether or mana-based, but she wanted to take a closer look at it later, if possible.

They reached a little past halfway toward the castle when Elise decided it was time to head back. Further than the last time, but not far enough. Elise expected they would get much faster, and they probably would have already made it if they were seriously pushing for it, but at this rate, it would be another two or three days.

She fired an aether spike at the draugr she had been experimenting on, killing it instantly. It was only the third time it had happened that way. It was still not comparable to {Power Word Kill}, and it would probably never be, unless she managed to figure out the Rune of Death on her own and incorporate it into the spike like it was in the Skill, but it was serviceable. She doubted there would be any ordinary draugr able to withstand it by the end of the next day.

They turned to leave, making it only about halfway back out when they encountered another group of draugr that had moved into the empty space. Elise prepared for battle, looking for a suitable target in the back of the group while Charlotte took on the bulk of them. However when she scanned the draugr in the back, she noticed that one was different.

It was a woman, and about half of her was more human than almost any other draugr. Her face was almost completely intact, with just one of her eyes—the left one—a corrupted black. Both eyes had the same hollow emptiness that the rest of the draugr had, but her expression was twisted into one of sorrow, and there were dried lines of blood running from her eyes down her cheeks. Elise couldn’t tell if it was actually dried blood, or if it had been merged into her skin when she was resurrected, like her clothing had. Her right arm was largely human too, and the normalcy spread across half her chest to her neck. The clothing pattern on her skin looked like long, flowing robes.

Her left side was another story. Her left arm had been completely replaced by tentacles, and her left breast was a giant eyeball. The mass of corrupted flesh continued down to her legs, and from the waist down, it was impossible to tell she had ever been human to begin with.

More importantly, though, at least for Elise and Charlotte, this one’s soul was stronger than the others. Much, much, much stronger. This wasn’t a monster they could afford to experiment with. She opened her mouth to tell Charlotte that, but at that exact moment, the draugr opened her own mouth and let out an ear-splitting shriek laced with aether. Elise stumbled, momentarily dizzy, and when she recovered, she heard the sound of rumbling coming from all around.

The draugr had called in a horde.
