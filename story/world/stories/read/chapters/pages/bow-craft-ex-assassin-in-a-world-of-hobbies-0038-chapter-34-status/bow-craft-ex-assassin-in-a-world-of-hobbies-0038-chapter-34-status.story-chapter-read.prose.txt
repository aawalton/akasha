Chapter 34Status!

Saying ‘status’ did not, in fact, open a floating panel.

“You’re going to have to meditate,” Lei-rei explained. “First-time setup is triggered by having a clear mind for at least five minutes.”

“Damn. I only have a clear mind when I’m in a life-or-death situation.” Craft chuckled. That's just sad.

Lei-rei tilted her head. “I was under the impression you had undergone similar training to me.”

“Sounds like they made you sit under a waterfall.”

“Beside one, yes.” She shifted her legs to be more comfortable. “Normally, a manager would supervise opening your status for the first time, but if you have bows hidden in that storage skill you mentioned, it’s a justified enough reason to open your status prematurely.”

They wouldn’t get out of there, otherwise.

“Right.” Craft shifted his legs, sitting cross-legged now. “First time for everything, I guess." He closed his eyes. Ah. He looked at her again. "How is it supposed to happen anyway?”

A shadow fell over Lei-rei's eyes as she thought about how to best explain it. “Meditation is focus training. If you focus your senses on a single sight, sound, smell—it doesn’t matter—it will just be like falling asleep. Most make do with the sensation of their own breathing, but this doesn’t work for everyone. You’ll have to discover what works best for you.”

He closed his eyes again. The forest wasn't as quiet as he thought: the cicadas' chirping and the hollow howling of wind past the petrifying trees were a constant and loud hum, enough to fill his mind.

The sound of a leaf falling was enough to knock him off-focus. He opened his eyes, surprised any of that had even happened. “It’s surprisingly difficult.”

“Hm? Why did you stop?”

“A leaf fell.”

She blinked and perked up. "I'm surprised you entered meditation so easily."

“Is it not supposed to be that way?”

“It's fine. Next time, corral your focus to return. The goal is not to have infinite focus, but to be so fast at bringing it back that there is almost no distinction.”

Is that how it works? "Sure, let me try again." He closed his eyes again, and he focused on the cicadas and howling wind. So loud, he thought of them, and next thing he knew, he was thinking of the ecological distribution of cicadas in this world. Cicadas living in a petrifying forest didn’t make sense. Maybe they weren’t even cicadas at all.

It took him a solid few seconds to realize his focus had veered off. Following Lei-rei’s instruction, he let the sound of cicadas fill his ears again—

“Ah, they stopped.” He sighed and opened his eyes, looking to Lei-rei. She was still watching him. “It felt like I did better there, at least.”

She nodded. “You’re improving. That must have been a minute. If you keep trying, we might be able to look at your status before the day ends.”

“The cicadas stopped shouting, though, and I can’t really focus on the wind. It’s too creepy on its own.” He thought for a moment and met her gaze. “How about you keeping talking to me for five minutes?”

She raised her eyebrows. “Infiltrating a castle is easier.”

“Damn, you’re right.” He hummed. Some people were naturally gifted at having infinite topics, but Lei-rei wasn't one of them.

Luckily, he had experience in interrogating her kind of person. He just had to find an ‘in’: some topic or interest that would flick a switch and blow the hinges off the floodgates for a two-hour impromptu lecture series.

He faced her again. “How about how you and Nightshade first met?”

Although she turned her head slowly, her gaze had snapped towards him. “I might be able.”

Craft smiled and closed his eyes.

“We originally met in a certain town in the down-realm, but we were mere acquaintances then,” she continued. “I thought she was just a normal girl. I didn’t think I would have anything more to do with her than a brief interaction, but I suppose that’s just how it always begins.”

Craft, having closed his eyes, could better hear the qualities of her voice: low, steady, and subtly intonated, not totally cold.

“I learned she was a traveler. At that time, I was…on a mission. Having someone already familiar with the land to guide me would have been beneficial, so I took her with me.” There was a light chuckle. “I underestimated her ability to turn anything into a sightseeing trip.”

It was her intonation Craft found himself listening to, not the words on their own.

“It’s a fearsome ability,” she continued. “Peace had to be fought for in that era, and yet around her, it was as if I didn’t need to fight for it anymore.” She paused. “A mission or a journey, I couldn’t tell the difference between the two anymore.”

Craft, already beginning to float in his mind, found himself agreeing.

“No one had told me about such emotions.”

The sound of her words became puddles in his mind.

“After five years of loyalty—”

At this moment, he simply forgot about himself,

“—and five more years of gratitude—”

and drowned in the intent of her story.

“—it felt so quiet.”

I realized I’d been happy all along. Craft never did hear those last words.

There was a technological beep, and his eyes snapped open. In front of him was a translucent panel similar to what he’d seen in that tunnel that transported him to this world.

[Initializing first-time setup…]

[CAUTION: Your Personal AmaSys Terminal (“terminal”) will open in Assisted Setup mode. Others will be able to see your terminal and your personal data, except any data you requested to be hidden prior to re/incarnation. Say “Enthusia is the bestest” to continue.]

“You know, I’ve always thought Enty’s pretty weird,” Craft remarked.

“Everyone thinks so to some degree.” Lei-rei scooted over beside him. “I took an assistant manager position some time ago. I’ll help you through this.”

Craft raised an eyebrow. “Is it that complicated?”

“Not at all. There just happen to be a hundred features, when in reality, you’ll only need a few of the most useful ones to start with, but which ones will depend on the person. That’s why we have managers to help you narrow down what you should use based on your needs.”

Rather than limiting software features, Enty took the approach of letting people customize their experience, huh? He shrugged. “Alright, then. ‘Enthusia is the best.’ ”

The text faded out, and the panel expanded. A moment passed, however, and it remained completely blank. Is there some kind of manual for this?

As soon as he had that thought, an icon with a question mark appeared on the upper right, and as soon as he focused on it, a search bar appeared.

“Whoa,” he blurted. Mind-machine interfaces weren’t an alien thing to him. Rather, it was how buttery-smooth it was that got him.

“The speed makes some people dizzy,” Lei-rei remarked. “It seems I don’t need to teach you how to use it, at least.”

The first thing Craft thought was “features,” and it appeared in the search bar, bringing up instant results. If he was going to use this thing, he’d rather be aware of the full breadth of its power—

“Hold it,” Lei-rei said. The panel rippled like someone had flicked an LCD screen. “Don’t get adventurous.”

“Right.” Technology was a trap in its own way, tempting people to ever grander illusions of greater powers, causing them to spend more time unlocking features rather than actually using them. “Just use what I need, right?”

“Close the guide for now”—he did—“You only need the basics for now.”

Anima and Hobbies. Life in Amatoria revolved around them, and the terminal provided a niche interface to track them.

“Don’t Overdose,” Lei-rei topped off her explanation. “The symptoms are…much worse.”

“This is oddly complicated…”

“If you have a concept of self-moderation, it isn’t.”

“Hitting milestones with your Hobby grants you a choice among three rewards. The rewards are never directly related to your Hobby, though. It would defeat the point of doing it as a Hobby.”

“That said, one of your rewards might help us get out of this situation. Just think about seeing them to open them up.”

He wasn't sure how to take this. Amatoria had three realms, and none of them were named ‘expectation.’ Indeed, if such a realm had existed, it had been obliterated a long time ago, and all he could do now was nod in grief, lamenting the loss of that hypothetical paradise.

This ‘Some-knowing Eye’ will likely use visual overlays, but skills aren’t limited to fetching information. There are physical skills, mental skills, magic skills—the scope of what could be handled by the system is vast.” Lei-rei shrugged. “But because you can’t have it be related to your Hobby, we end up with musclehead martial artists capable of fitting a mountain in a pocket, and they wouldn’t entertain using it in combat just because it would ‘taint’ their martial art.”
