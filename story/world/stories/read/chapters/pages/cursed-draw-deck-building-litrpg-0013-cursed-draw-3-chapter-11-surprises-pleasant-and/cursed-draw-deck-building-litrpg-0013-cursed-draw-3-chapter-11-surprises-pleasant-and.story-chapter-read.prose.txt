Trine Blackwinter was feeling both sad and determined. She had never been overly concerned about winning the Frostwing Trials. Sure, additional funds to fuel her campaign against the rest of Frostlands would be nice, as would the glory of victory going to Tribe Blackwinter. She hadn’t cared all that much, so while having to withdraw was a minor hit to her pride, it hadn’t been all that bad.

Even having her attempt to assassinate the heir to the Zolotoy family foiled had only been a minor frustration. Sure, it had cost her the company of Goret mercenaries she had been working with for the past few years, but that was what mercenaries were for; They killed and died so you didn’t have to, and not every plan worked out.

It had been a little horrifying to see the dark-haired boy with the hook reveal his true nature and slaughter Brother Tarven like he was nothing. Trine wasn’t squeamish, though; she had seen plenty of horrifying people, and equally horrifying events. Her uncle, the lord of the tribe Blackwinter, had performed plenty of feats that left Trine’s younger self shaking with fear.

All she’d had to do was collect her things, flee Skalean, and move onto another scheme. There were rumors of a coalition of cities coming together further east, and Trine saw it as an opportunity to start some serious chaos that would leave at least three city-states at each other's throats and ripe for invasion.

That hadn’t been what happened. Much to her chagrin, Trine had been interrupted in the middle of riffling through her hired room so that she left nothing behind that could be traced to her when a Manifested Shadow-Flicker Gargoyle had come through her window. The monster was about the size of a child, and clutching a letter bearing the black snowflake icon of her family. Trine hadn’t needed to see the image to know the little creature belonged to one of her cousins or her brother.

Gargoyles were a common sight within every section of the Frostlands, but Shadow-Flickers were only found populating the icebergs off the northern coast of the Frostlands. If anyone who wasn’t a member of her family had them in their Decks, Trine had never seen it.

The monster’s skin was grey with black dappled marks covering it, and while not overly powerful, it could move at an appallingly high speed once it got flying. They weren’t much good in a battle, but as messengers, they were second to none.

With a sigh, Trine had snatched the letter from the little creature's clawed hand, mere moments before the gargoyle had Demanifested and vanished in a burst of blue light. A manifested monster could only last for an hour, so she knew that this one must have come a long distance, maybe even all the way from her family territory.

The instructions were in a coded language that Trine had been forced to memorize when she was only five, but she still balled up the letter and stuffed it into the shuttered lantern she was using to see her room in the early morning darkness once she was done reading it.

Trine’s uncle had always had a soft spot for her, and had treated her well, made her powerful and respected, had given her a Deck of her own, had backed and funded her plan for the tribe’s growth, and had showered her with praise more often than not. None of that meant his word wasn’t law; in fact, it very much meant the opposite. If Uncle Manan gave her an order, Trine and everyone else with the surname Blackwinter had little choice but to obey with gusto.

Sitting down at the end of the bed in her rented room, Trine had clenched and unclenched her fists for a minute or two before letting out a feral screech. She wasn’t sure how her family had known within the hour that she had failed to kill the witnesses in the Storslået Palads hotel, and thus compromised her anonymity within Skalean, but they had.

The orders within the letter had been extremely clear, while her uncle appreciated that Trine had no choice but to pull out of the tournament lest she be recognized and arrested. He was still more than a little invested in the prize that would be given to the top four entrants: a map leading to a new world. Since Trine could no longer take home the prize, she was under strict instructions to follow the people who did to the location of the landmass that would soon rise out of the ocean. It hadn’t been much of a guess to know that once there, she was supposed to secure the Chaos Lock and bring it back to Blackwinter territory.

Almost everyone with a few ranks to their name wanted the opportunity to explore a newly risen world, but that idea wasn't why she was upset. No, the reason Trine felt like tearing out her hair was that such a trip would take months and require an enormous expenditure of resources. That alone wouldn't be a problem, but it meant that all of her plans for the next couple of years would collapse entirely. On top of that, she would need to stay in Skalean for the duration of The Frostwing Trials, while taking pains to remain undiscovered. Trine had been wearing one of the fireproofing masks of the Goret Brotherhood when her assassination attempt had gone awry, so Trine's identity hadn't been fully compromised, but there were only so many women walking around the city with fiery red hair that fell to the small of their backs. Now that number was about to be one fewer, and Trine was devastated by the thought. She'd spent years cultivating her long and lustrous hair; it was the one concession to vanity that she had allowed herself, and now, since she could no longer pack up and leave Skalean, she was going to have to cut and dye it.

So that is what the Blackwinter noble had done. The whole process had almost cost a full black Chip. She had plenty of funds and access to her family's accounts should she need more, but it still felt like an extortionate amount.

This was especially the case, as while the woman who performed the styling did a good job, leaving her with a black bob that sat straight and only ran down to her chin, Trine couldn’t help but feel like she was paying to look worse than she had. Still, if there was one feature that could easily make her recognizable, it had been her hair; with that transformed, she was free to remain in the city, she’d still moved accommodations before changing her hair, just in case.

Now, the woman who had believed herself to have a better than fifty percent chance of winning the entire True Deck section of the Frostwing Trials had no choice but to play the role of a mere fan watching the event, and making efforts to find out everything she could about the remaining contenders. She was good at intelligence gathering and had made significant use of the city’s poorer children to act as an impromptu spy network for her, but it was still frustrating. Most of these players were frankly awful, and made obvious mistakes or tripped over their own feet trying to avoid attacks that weren’t even that fast. Yet they and not she were the ones bringing home the accolades and enjoying the cheers of the crowd.

It wasn’t true of all of them, of course; some of these people were real monsters who would have given her all sorts of problems to face. Those were the ones she focused most of her intelligence gathering efforts on.

Four days after her botched attempt to kill the pair of Zolotoy princes, she was sitting in the stands of the Arena closest to the northern wall of Skalean, the ever-so-creatively named ‘Northern Arena.’ There were two reasons the now black-haired woman was here. The first was obviously to watch the matches, since a few weeks from now, she was going to be locked in a race across a new and mysterious continent with at least eight of these people and likely their allies too; every piece of information she could put together about their Cards and abilities could prove potentially invaluable going forward. She especially didn’t want to get caught off guard again, as she had been in the lobby of the Storslået Palads. If someone had asked her before that attack if the tallish dark-haired boy could Manifest Cards into reality, she would have sworn up and down that his Deck was Cursed and he couldn’t. That had proven frustratingly, and lethally incorrect. If anything, it was a firm reminder not to take things for granted and to make sure she did her research before making such an overt move in the future.

In particular, Trine was there putting up with the press of people and the terrible arena food in order to watch Jackson Matsui. She had seen him compete once already, and the sheer overwhelming force of his Cards had left her almost speechless. On top of that, he was easily the strangest-dressed person she had ever laid eyes on. He looked like he hailed from one of the Jade Sea nations, but in her experience, they didn’t wear the sorts of clothes he wore; in fact, in her experience, no one did.

More importantly than that, today Jackson would be facing off against the brown-haired girl with the cutesy beast Cards that had been part of the Zolotoy entourage. Not only would that mean Trine should get a decent look at Jackson’s Harbinger of The Infinite Void Deck against a decent opponent, but she would get to enjoy watching one of the people who had helped ruin her plan the other night get thoroughly trounced, which should be at least mildly entertaining.

It wasn’t that Trine thought the brown-haired woman who had helped protect the younger prince twice was a pushover; she simply didn’t believe anyone in the tournament had the kind of power required to stand up to the Cards in Jackson’s collection. Trine had seen him play Xu-De, Queen of Nothing. And the Card had practically given her nightmares.

​

From the corner of her eye, Trine spotted the second reason she was in the Northern Arena today. A thin and short teenager, with hair and skin so pale it was almost white, was making his way over to her through the press of fans sitting in their thousands across the raised stands. The boy was dressed in the heavily furred style of the Blackwinters, which Trine tended to avoid whilst away from home, and his hair was cropped close to his head.

“Trinny!” He called with a wave and strode over to her with a friendly smile on his face. “ I knew you’d be hard to recognize, but wow, you look like a different person.”

Trine snorted as she fully turned to face her cousin.

“That's the idea, Simo.” She replied before inclining her head for the briefest of seconds. With five brothers ahead of him, the seventeen-year-old wasn’t really what could be considered an heir, but he was still of the main Blackwinter family line and therefore a royal. Trine couldn’t offer him a full bow out in public like this, but she would still treat her cousin with the respect he was due.

“You arrived quickly.” She added, doing her best to avoid letting the irritation that her family had sent back up already, like they assumed she would fail.

“Duh.” Said the prince of the far north. “Dad sent me weeks ago because he figured you’d win this whole thing and need to get to the new world as soon as possible. He even made me stop off and negotiate a little fleet for us to use.”

He was a lunatic whose Deck made no sense, he enjoyed torturing small animals, and had managed to hide the fact that he’d murdered at least two servants within Tribe Blackwinter’s home castle, but Trine had to admit. She’d missed her youngest cousin more than a little.

“So…you aren’t here because your father thinks I’m a failure?” The former redhead asked slowly.

“What?!” Simo said with an exaggerated eye roll. “ You have been stacking so many wins, the rest of us are starting to look bad. I’m here because he thinks I could learn a thing or two from your presence.”
