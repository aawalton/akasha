ARC I: THE ACADEMIC EXILE

SYNOPSIS: Livia (Mental Supporter Lv. 4) receives a critical System Update: the powerful but draining Therapist’s Insight skill. Testing it on Miles Vexley, she uncovers a deep-seated trauma rooted in betrayal—and pushes far enough to glimpse the real wound beneath the smirk. Livia realizes her role is no longer about managing surface-level neuroses. It is about deciding how deep she is willing to go.

Livia Marcelline Quinn, Mental Supporter Lv. 4 and unwilling server icon, had been sitting on a cracked stone ledge in the Twilight Arena for twenty minutes trying to think about nothing.

This was not going well.

Her Therapist’s Log was open in her lap, and despite her best efforts it kept pulling her eyes back:

Bron: Fear of erasure. Basket-weaving. Monitor.

Nyx: Phobia improving. Ten seconds. (Do not think about the smile. Ongoing.)

Phina: Slime romance stable. Jiggle chart active. Glimmer glows.

Alaric: Confessed to High Paladin. Progress. Still betting. Complicated.

Miles: TBD. Probably smug. Something locked and heavy. File for later.

She had written file for later three times across three different chapters now. Later was accumulating.

The Twilight Arena sprawled below her ledge—a coliseum that made the guild’s base camp look like a meditation retreat. Players clashed on the floor below, spells cracking against shields, faction recruiters bellowing about “Exclusive Guild Trials!” from every entrance. The noise was a physical thing, layered and relentless.

I am their shrink, their meme queen, and their burnout survivor.

I flunked psych, got memed across dimensions, chose Empath Surge at a critical juncture, and I am sitting in a coliseum trying to have five minutes of peace.

This is fine.

[Achievement Unlocked: Arena Wallflower. +10 Observation Points (Passive)]

“Observation points for surviving this circus,” Livia muttered. Her progress bar read Mental Supporter Lv. 4: 900/4000 EXP. Her Empath Surge was running its constant background hum. Her Empathic Insight was cataloguing the emotional weather of every player within thirty feet, which she was learning to tune down to a manageable murmur.

She had been sitting here long enough to notice a pattern.

The pattern was gaps.

Not in the data—in the space between what people said and what was actually there.

She had been reviewing her session notes, and the more she read them, the more she noticed: every patient had presented with one thing and been carrying another. Bron had complained about boredom. The real issue was erasure. Alaric had complained about losing bets. The real issue was his faith flickering out. Phina had described a romance problem. The real issue was a woman who had been carrying eighteen months of devotion alone because nobody had ever told her it was worth taking seriously.

She had helped all of them.

But she had helped them with the surface layer first, every time, and the real thing had taken longer to reach.

I’m missing something, she thought. I’m getting better, but I’m still reading the sheet music and hearing a different song.

She wrote in her notepad: Pattern: presenting issue vs root issue. Always different. Empathic Insight shows the surface. Something deeper needed. What’s under the surface? How far down does it go?

Her HUD flickered.

› Skill evolution detected.

› Your Empathic Insight is developing a deeper layer.

› New skill available: Therapist’s Insight Lv. 1

A holographic panel opened:

Therapist’s Insight Lv. 1: Reveals the root emotional wound beneath presenting issues. Cost: 20% mental stamina per use. Warning: What you find cannot be unfound.

Livia stared at that last line.

What you find cannot be unfound.

That’s not a skill description. That’s a warning label.

[Achievement Unlocked: System’s Favorite Lab Rat. +5 Curiosity Points (Risky)]

“Fantastic,” Livia muttered. “Now I’m a psychic sponge with a warning label. Just what I needed.”

She was still staring at the panel when the guild teleported in.

They arrived with varying degrees of grace.

Bron landed and immediately checked that his plushie had survived the teleport. It had. He looked relieved. Phina arrived mid-ode, Glimmer on her shoulder, the slime’s golden glow faintly visible even in the arena’s harsh light. Nyx materialized near a shadow and then immediately stepped out of it, flashlight adjusting. Alaric landed, stumbled, checked his flask.

Miles arrived last.

He always arrived last.

He leaned against a pillar at the arena’s edge, arms crossed, smirk in place, scanning the coliseum floor below with the ease of someone who had been in a hundred arenas and found all of them mildly beneath him.

Alaric shouted, “I bet 300 gold we wipe unless Buffer therapizes us!”

Nobody took that bet.

Livia’s Empathic Insight was doing its usual cataloguing. Bron: anxious energy, directed outward. Nyx: low-grade vigilance, stable. Phina: serene. Alaric: pre-bet excitement simmering under surface calm.

And Miles…

Miles read as blank.

Not calm. Not content. Blank. The way a room reads blank when everything in it has been very deliberately put away.

I have a new skill, Livia thought, and immediately: Do not use it on Miles. You are not ready for what it finds.

She used it on Miles.

[Therapist’s Insight: Active]

[Patient: Miles Vexley]

[Presenting: Functional. Controlled. Guild leader. Asset to team.]

[Root pattern detected: Betrayal. Secondary pattern: Trust avoidance. Severity: Critical.]

[Origin: unavailable. Historical depth: restricted.]

[Note: Patient has constructed extensive deflection architecture. Smirk, sarcasm, and leadership identity are load-bearing walls. Do not remove without a plan.]

Livia’s notepad slipped from her fingers.

She caught it.

Betrayal, she thought. Someone actually did that to him. And the system won’t even say when.

Miles caught her staring.

The smirk faltered. Not much—maybe two seconds, a microexpression she would have missed before Empathic Insight—but it faltered. Replaced by something flat and sharp.

“What’s that look, Buffer?” His voice had lost its usual edge. Not soft—emptied. “Trying to crack my code?”

Livia retrieved her notepad from her lap. Load-bearing walls, the skill had said. Do not remove without a plan.

She did not have a plan.

She had a notepad and a skill she’d been warned about.

“You seem off,” she said, carefully. “Not performing-off. Actually off.”

Miles pushed off the pillar. “Off? Me?” The smirk returned, but it was doing the thing she had started to recognize—running half a second behind, like a curtain being drawn after the fact. “You’re the one digging for ghosts in a duel pit, rookie.”

[Therapist’s Insight: Patient Deflection: High. Increasing.]

“I’m not digging,” Livia said. “It’s right there. Whatever hurt you. It’s still… present. Not healed. Not even scabbed over. Just— there.”

Miles went very still.

Not the controlled stillness he usually projected. A different kind—the stillness of something that had been hit precisely.

“Be careful,” he said. His voice was quiet. “Rookie.”

“Or what?” Livia said, and surprised herself with how steady it came out. “You’ll deflect harder? Make another joke? Turn away and walk off like you always do when something gets close?”

The arena noise seemed to pull back.

Miles looked at her for a long moment. Something moved behind his eyes that she recognized, now, from three weeks of watching people decide whether or not to open a door.

He opened it. Just slightly. Just enough.

“There was a guild,” he said. His voice was even. Deliberately even, the way voices get when the person speaking them has rehearsed this in their head many times and decided that evenness was the only way through. “Before this one. Three years ago. Best team I’d ever led.”

Livia did not move. Did not write. Just listened.

“We had a legendary drop on the line. One of the big ones—the kind that changes your server ranking permanently. The other top guild offered them a trade.” He paused. “Me. For the drop.”

Silence.

“They took it,” Miles said. “Every one of them. People I’d run a hundred raids with. People I’d covered for, vouched for, built something with. They voted. Unanimous. Traded me to the enemy guild for a legendary item drop.”

He said it the way you say something you’ve said to yourself many times—flat, factual, all the volume turned down to a frequency that sounds like acceptance and is not.

“I was their leader,” he said. “I trusted them.” A pause. “They traded me like loot.”

Livia wrote nothing.

There was nothing to write yet.

“Miles…” she started.

“Don’t,” he said. Not aggressive—just done. The door was closing. She could feel it in the way his posture shifted, the smirk beginning its return. “I don’t need therapy, Livia. I need you to stop looking at me like I’m another broken project.”

He walked away.

She let him.

[Therapist’s Insight — Patient: Miles Vexley]

[Patient Deflection: Critical. He has closed the door.]

[Underlying emotion: Terror of vulnerability. Secondary: Grief. For the team. For who he was before it.]

[Progress: 0%. Direction: He opened the door. That was not nothing.]

[Note: Some wounds need time before they will accept treatment. Do not force. Do not forget.]

Livia watched his back.

“You’re not a project,” she said, quietly.

She didn’t know if he heard.

She wrote it in her notepad anyway: Miles. Traded by his former guild for a legendary drop. Three years. Unprocessed. He told me. Then closed the door. He opened it first. File for: when he’s ready. Not before.

Then, beneath it:

He called me Livia.

Not Buffer.

Once.

The Therapist’s Insight panel was still open, faintly translucent, hovering at the edge of her vision the way unused interface elements did until dismissed.

For a second, she caught her own reflection in it—faint, ghosted across the readout, her face superimposed over the words Do not remove without a plan.

She wondered, briefly, what the skill would find if she turned it on herself.

The thought made her close the panel.

Not yet.

The arena chose this moment to escalate.

A player’s meteor spell misfired on the floor below, sending a cascade of sparks up toward the spectator ledges. Bron roared “Protect Buffer!” and tackled a crate that had not been threatening anyone. Phina’s composure broke long enough for Glimmer to roll into the walkway, where it sat glowing in mild confusion. Alaric, who had been mid-bet, lost his footing and grabbed Nyx’s arm, which caused Nyx to flicker into his neon purple silhouette briefly, which caused three nearby players to scatter.

Livia stood up.

“Everyone.” She activated Group Therapy Lv. 2, her voice carrying through the Empath Surge. “Save it for the raid. Not for the crate.”

The guild settled, in the way the guild settled: imperfectly, loudly, with Bron still holding the crate at arm’s length as though unsure whether it had been sufficiently defeated.

[You stabilized a pre-raid meltdown. +1200 EXP. Mental Supporter Lv. 4: 2100/4000 EXP.]

Miles clapped, reappearing at her shoulder. His smirk was back in place. Not the brittle version from ten minutes ago—something more deliberate, rebuilt.

“Not bad, Buffer,” he said. “Keeping us sane. Barely.”

[Therapist’s Insight: Patient Trust: Marginally increased. Note: He came back. He didn’t have to.]

Livia looked at him.

He looked back—not the closed-off version from earlier, not quite open either. Somewhere in between, and holding steady there.

She nodded once.

He nodded back.

Okay, she thought. Okay. Not now. When he’s ready.

The arena’s chaos resumed around her. Bron was now attempting to explain to Alaric why the crate had posed a structural threat. Phina had retrieved Glimmer and was back to composing. Nyx had retreated to a shadowed corner and then immediately re-emerged, flashlight on, and chosen a different spot with better ambient lighting.

[Achievement Unlocked: Trauma Detector]

[System Note: You have now glimpsed what each member of this guild is carrying. Bron: erasure. Nyx: darkness. Phina: loneliness. Alaric: lost faith. Miles: betrayal. You chose Empath Surge. You chose to feel all of it. The system asks: are you still sure? The system already knows your answer.]

Livia sat with that notification for a moment.

Then she wrote in her notepad:

Guild: Emotional landmines, every one. Miles: Trust Deficit. Betrayal. Three years. He called me Livia.

Approach: Survive their baggage. Show up anyway. Wait for the doors to open.

She closed the notepad.

“I’m either a therapist or a masochist,” she muttered.

Possibly both, she thought. Probably both.

She was too tired to be offended by either option.

I think I stopped caring.

Across the arena, Miles stood at the railing, looking down at the dueling floor. His back was to her. His posture was the careful kind—the kind that takes effort to maintain.

He opened the door, she thought. First time in three years, maybe.

He’ll open it again.

I’ll be here when he does.

[System Note: Emotional Supporter Livia Marcelline Quinn.]

[Observed behavioral pattern: chooses presence over withdrawal.]

[Pattern stability: increasing.]

[Classification: Pending Review.]

[The system notes: this is taking longer than expected.]

[END OF CHAPTER 10]
