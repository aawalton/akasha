"The lookout shifts have already been changed," Feroy continued. "The guards who fought tonight have returned to eat and rest. The men and women who remained here at the manor have been sent to the gates. They are fresh, and they can hold the walls until morning."

"And if the survivors of the pack come back?"

"The horns will sound, and the resting guards will return. But based on my experience, I do not think that will happen."

Kivamus finally allowed himself to accept that the fighting might truly be over.

"We need to tell the villagers in that case," he said. "They have been shut inside the longhouse blocks for hours."

"I will send runners to every block," Feroy said. "They can tell the people that the immediate danger has passed. The villagers may come outside again if they wish.”

"They will all be too tense to sleep anyway."

"The adults certainly won’t," Duvas agreed. "The children may still manage it."

Kivamus glanced toward the manor hall. “Yeah, Lucem and Clarisa already went to sleep."

"That is because Madam Helga and Gorsazo kept them away from the windows and did not let the fear outside reach them," Duvas said. "The rest of the village children may be sleeping just as peacefully."

But their parents would not be.

Those adults had spent the night wondering whether the palisade would hold and whether their family members stationed at the walls would return alive. Even after hearing that the danger had passed, many of them would probably remain awake until sunrise.

Feroy called a nearby servant over and gave him instructions to find several others. Within moments, runners were leaving the manor grounds for the longhouse blocks.

Hudan returned from the servants' hall while Feroy was speaking.

His shoulders looked less tense now.

"How is he?" Kivamus asked.

"Nurobo said the bone was not broken," Hudan replied. "The wound was deep, and the man lost a great deal of blood, but the bleeding has stopped now. His fever was already beginning to come down. Nurobo has given him some herbs to help him sleep."

Hudan smiled. "Maran will not be walking for some time, but he will survive.”

"Thank the Goddess," Duvas murmured.

That news spread almost as quickly as the declaration of victory had.

One of the maids carried it toward the kitchen. Another told the guards still arriving from the manor gate. Within minutes, men who had been asking worriedly about their wounded companion were smiling and speaking more freely.

Madam Nerida walked out of the servants' hall and clapped her hands twice.

"Food is ready!" she called. "There is bread and chicken stew. Sit down before it gets cold."

"Hell yes!" one of the guards shouted.

Several of the maids laughed.

The guards began moving toward the benches and wooden tables that had been placed outside the servants' hall. Lanterns hung from posts and nearby branches, lighting the area well enough for them to eat. Bowls were carried out, loaves were cut apart, and the smell of fresh bread and stew quickly filled the yard.

Hudan looked toward Kivamus.

"Why don't you join us, my lord?"

"I already ate a few hours ago," Kivamus began. Then he stopped.

He had spent most of those hours pacing between the gate and the servants' hall. His legs ached, but now that he could smell the food, he realized how hungry he was.

"Actually, you know what? I’ll join you."

Hudan grinned.

Kivamus sat beside him at one of the tables. Feroy joined them and accepted a bowl that looked nearly twice as full as everyone else’s. Kivamus had a sneaking suspicion that he had managed to find some dried Rizako mushrooms from somewhere to put in the stew. The man would kill for them, if need be.

Duvas came as well, though he declined the food and sat yawning beside Kivamus.

Around them, the returning guards ate while servants and maids moved between the tables and benches. Some families remained close together, unwilling to separate again so soon after spending the night afraid. Leah and Syrene had returned to their own bench, but the sewing cloth had been set aside while they watched the gathering with smiles on their faces.

The manor ground no longer felt like a place waiting for disaster.

The past few weeks had been filled with adzee sightings, distant howls, and the constant fear that the pack would finally attack. Tonight, it had.

And Tiranat had won.

The danger had not ended. Torhan was still alive. Zoricus was still waiting for an opportunity. Mercenaries would arrive within a month, and another pack of adzees might claim the southern forest before winter.

There would always be another threat.

Kivamus looked across the tables at the guards eating beneath the lanterns, then toward the smoke still rising from the ovens.

That was all right.

Tiranat would keep preparing for the next one.

- Widel -

Widel was walking north to the fourth longhouse block, she being the person allotted to inform the residents in this block that the danger was over, since she also lived here. Four other new guards who lived here were walking nearby.

It was already after midnight, but judging from the sounds coming from ahead, almost no one in the fourth longhouse block seemed to be sleeping.

Widel could understand why.

She had eaten at the manor after being relieved from duty, but even the hot food had not settled her nerves. Every time she closed her eyes, she saw the adzee moving near the firelight below the southeast watchtower.

She had known they were large. Everyone in Tiranat had heard the warnings in the past weeks, and she had heard about the younger one killed during the winter attack.

The one that had approached her tower had been much larger.

Even from above the wall, its shoulders had looked heavy enough to knock a man aside without slowing. When it charged, Widel had gripped the watchtower railing so tightly that her fingers hurt.

But she had believed the wall would hold.

Tiranat had spent months strengthening it. The guards below had not run, and Feroy had been among them. The scorpion crew had kept working even while the beast came closer.

And the wall had held.

The guards had killed the damned beast.

Widel had not been the one aiming the scorpion. She was still too inexperienced with the weapon, so she had helped draw the string back and load the heavy bolts while a more practiced female guard handled the shooting. It had still taken both of them, along with another woman on the platform, to keep the weapon ready.

The scorpion had not been mounted properly either. Darora had brought it to the southeast tower after dark, but there had not been enough time or light to install the full turning mount. He had fixed it onto a temporary support instead. Its movement had been limited, but it had moved far enough.

Far enough to put three bolts into the adzee before Feroy and the ground guards finished it.

Widel reached the gate of the fourth block with four other guards who lived there. Two were men and two were women, all equally tired and stained with dust or sweat.

The gate was shut.

Widel knocked against the wood.

Movement came from the other side. Then a man's voice called, "Who is it?"

Widel stared at a small gap in the gate, recognizing a young man who worked at the coal mines.

“It’s Widel, you idiot. Open it."

A wooden bar scraped aside.

The gate opened just wide enough for them to enter, and Widel immediately saw that nearly everyone in the block was awake.

The central courtyard was packed. People sat beneath the open sky or stood near the inner longhouse doors, talking in low voices. Many had brought their sleeping mats outside because the summer heat made the longhouses unbearable at night. For the past few weeks, people from all four blocks had been sleeping in the open outside the blocks, just to get some breeze in this sweltering weather, since it was way too hot to sleep on the bunks inside. But for the last few nights, they all had been huddling inside the blocks, and the courtyard has been as packed as it could be.

The youngest children were already asleep beside their parents, but most of the older children were awake and watching the returning guards with nervous faces.

Several burly men still held machetes or wood axes. Some of the young women were even armed with kitchen knives, not that it would have done anything other than tickle an adzee. They all had gathered near the gate in case an adzee somehow broke through the village wall and reached the longhouse block.

The moment Widel entered, questions came from every direction.

"What happened?"

"Did they break the wall?"

"How many were there?"

"Did anyone die?"

"Let us speak first," one of the other guards said, laughing tiredly.

That only made more people crowd closer.

Widel glanced at the guard, and asked if he wanted to reply but he shook his head. Widel shrugged and raised both hands.

"The danger has passed," she said loudly. "The pack attacked, but the walls held. The guards killed five adzees."

For a heartbeat, the courtyard was silent.

Then everyone began cheering.

People clapped, shouted, and hugged those standing beside them. A woman near the nearest longhouse began crying and laughing at the same time. One of the returning male guards was seized by his mother before he could take another step. A toddler started crying after being woken up by the sudden noise.

"We killed five?" a boy asked excitedly.

"The guards did," Widel said with a smile.

"Did you kill one too?” another girl asked Widel with wide eyes.

“I helped the guards bring one down.”

That answer was apparently close enough, because the kids grinned at her as though she had personally wrestled the beast to the ground.

"The baron has lifted the order to remain inside the block," Widel continued once the noise eased. "You can leave the courtyard now if you want. But nobody is allowed outside the village until the baron allows it."

Two teenage boys near the front, probably brothers based on their looks, or even twins, exchanged excited looks. They appeared to be around fifteen.

"We want to see the bodies," one announced.

"Then wait until morning."

"I am going to sit on the biggest one," the other boy said.

The first kid shoved his shoulder. "I am taking one of the fangs. Or maybe even the skull.”

"You are not touching anything until the manor says it is safe," Widel snorted. "The butchers will start while it is still dark but the bodies will take most of tomorrow to clear. Maybe longer. There will be another announcement before working hours about whether anyone is allowed outside the gates."

The first boy’s disappointment lasted for less than a moment. "I will still sit on one tomorrow."

"And I am still taking a fang."

Widel shook her head.

Neither boy had gone anywhere near the wall during the attack, but they were already dividing the trophies between themselves.

At least that courage might be useful if they joined the village guards in a few years.

The supervisor of the fourth longhouse block pushed through the gathering. He was an older resident with thinning gray hair and a patched-up tunic hanging loosely from his shoulders. He had been placed in charge of food allotments, maintenance, and settling disputes between residents for this block.

"Widel," he said. "Has the manor truly declared it safe?"

"The immediate danger has passed. Guards are still watching every wall until morning, but the rest of the pack has withdrawn. People can leave the block, but not the village."

The supervisor nodded slowly, relief showing plainly on his face.

Someone behind him called, "Then why are we still standing here?"

Several people turned.

A young woman raised her voice. "We should celebrate! We have been trapped inside this block since yesterday, and Tiranat just defeated a whole pack of adzees. Let us go to the new market block, make some fires, and cook something!”

People immediately began agreeing.

The supervisor looked at Widel.

That made her hesitate.

Until recently, no one had asked her for permission to do anything. She had spent most of her life obeying whoever held power over her. Now dozens of people were looking at her as though she had the power to decide whether they were allowed to celebrate.

She glanced at the other guards.

None of them objected.

"I don't see why you can't," Widel said. "Just don't go too close to the gates and do not interfere with the guards."

Another cheer filled the courtyard.

People began moving before the noise had even faded. Doors opened. Baskets, pots, blankets, benches, and whatever food could be spared were carried out, as if they had already been preparing to celebrate, as if Tiranat would win before the guards had even killed the beasts. Children who had still been half asleep became fully awake as soon as they heard the word celebration.

Widel moved aside before the crowd could carry her through the gate with them.

She was exhausted. Her arms ached from reloading the scorpion again and again, and she wanted nothing more than to lie down.

But there was no chance she could sleep after seeing an adzee come that close to the wall.

She glanced at the other guards and they all decided to follow the villagers toward the market block.
