The Frigidi and troglodytes had together brought an army hundreds strong. Normal raids were small, dozens of men at most and something the villagers could handle. This was on a whole other scale. As far as they knew, the Frigidi were all initialized. This village had no chance

Or at least, they would have had no chance if not for a few important points.

Firstly, the legion had been in the village for some yet unknown reason to Caleb, and he watched their testudo—ranks of men holding shields tightly together—holding against the army of elementals and initialized. They held the tide back a dozen meters outside the walls of the keep, but as they killed attackers they were forced to retreat back. Behind the wall of men, the uninitialized auxiliaries of the legion watched on, attacking with spears and pila as opening presented themselves. Whenever a member of the testudo fell, an auxiliary would take up their enchanted blade and fill the gap. If the auxiliary should be so lucky and the man they replaced died, they’d find themselves a newly initialized legionnaire. Behind them on the walls, the Frost Dragon Hunters of the village fired specially made bows designed for their strength.

Even with the legion, the village would have eventually fallen had Caleb not dispelled the storm. The legion had lost a great many if the pile of neatly stacked dead behind their line hadn’t been there before the battle—something Caleb severely doubted. The storm’s departure had leveled the playing field, and along with it, the lightning had evened the numbers. All around the village lay the burnt husks of dead Frigidi and troglodytes who’d died by the storms pent up wrath.

With the outcome of the battle shifted from near certain if slow victory for the people of the glacier, they were forced to play their full hand.

In war on Alterum was a delicate balance. A nation or people couldn’t exist without the powerful initialized to protect them from the destruction of wild beasts or neighbors. Each nation was loathe to risk their powerhouses, or even commit them to battle lest they be killed or their enemies capitalize on their absence on other battlefields. So battles were fought with lesser men. The empire had their legions of common initialized soldiers, while other nations had their armies of mundane men backed by powerful heroes.

Caleb’s village had no powerful initialized. The empire was their guarantor. The Frigidi could always have wiped them out, but feared the wrath of the empire if they did so. The raids were likely meant training for their initialized to level them, not signs of true animosity.

But something had changed and the Frigidi had come to wipe them out, whatever the cost. It was only by luck that the legion was there to defend them.

Caleb thought he knew what had changed, but he tried to distract himself from that for now as the Frigidi’s hero entered the fray.

A man walked into the battle from the rear, and men both Frigidi and troglodytes moving out of his way in both fear and reverence. He wore no armor at first, but as he moved the snow around him began to swirl until he was invisible in a storm of white. The storm settled and in its place stood a snow white man 6 meters tall wielding a blade of black ice.

He advanced on the legion, and brough is blade in a low horizontal slash, swiping them away like a child knocking his toys off the table.

A horn blew from the front line as the remaining legionaries formed together, but the horn was not for them.

A figure appeared on the wall above clad in a simple tunic of the empire and a collar around his neck. He wore no clothing to combat the weather nor armor to defend himself. He held a simple spear, and he jumped off the wall, landing in a roll and then ran out to meet the giant.

The giant of snow and ice didn’t notice the new challenger at first, and he continued his rampage against the helpless legionnaires. The survivors had managed to reform a wall after the initial attack and to both Caleb and the giant’s surprise, when the next swing of his blade met the shields head on, it was halted. The combined strength and skills of testudo was more than a match for the hero when met head on.

The collared man reached the giant just after the attack, moving with a speed Caleb couldn’t match even with his magic. The man drove his spear deep into the white giant’s leg, and it came out the other side red.

The giant whirled on the new attacker, bringing his sword around win a white arc that took the top of the keeps stone wall off with it. The empire’s hero ripped his spear from the giant and leaped over the blade, making another stab while mid air, but the stab did no damage, travelling only centimeters into the armor that must have been half a meter thick.

“We need to help him,” Caleb told Grisim as he watched the battle.

He’d found more of the potions the ritualists had used and downed one he tried to recover. As his mana sense had told him, they’d been mana potions and they quickly restored all his mana.

Grisim for his part ran into the nearest house and started raiding their larder, shoving food into his mouth. Part of his Accelerated Recovery skill allowed him to replenish stamina when at rest, but it gave him a ravenous appetite.

They moved cautiously towards the battle as they waited for Grisim to recover the majority of his stamina. The skill required him to be “at rest” but there was a lot of leeway in what that really meant and walking towards a battle at a sedate paces counted.

Bog didn’t seem concerned about the battle, and kept tripping as he walked, constantly trying to see his three new talons on his right claw. Caleb looked at them now and saw they were the same green mana steel he’d used to craft Kelia’s ring.

The thought of Kelia made him suddenly worried. He knew the storm hadn’t gone toward her but the rational part of his mind wasn’t in charge when it came to worrying about her. He tried to push the fear aside as he took in the battle.

The legion had reformed into a wall and was holding back to the keep in the section damaged by the blade while the invaders stood back themselves. Neither side wanted to get close to the warring heroes but none looked anywhere but that.

The keep was on a hill in the center of the village, the sides of which were barren and devoid of any constructions. Caleb and Grisim broke from cover of the buildings as they reached this, and ran through the enemy ranks unnoticed until it was too late and they’d run past.

As we ran, Caleb sensed the ground littered with pila from the army around him. The small throwing spears were miniscule compared to those he was used to, but they would do.

The first one he pulled to his hand sent a shock through his whole body.

Meta Steel Pila

Weapon type: Spear

Material: Steel

Aspect: Steel (moderate)

This thrown disposable spear he’d just picked up from a battlefield littered with them had a moderate meta aspect to it. Suddenly Caleb felt a child playing at his masters forge. He’d been so proud of his greater aspect achievement, and now found the empire treated that only as a step above expendable.

He didn’t let his disappointment blind him of the opportunity though and he pulled more up to his hands.

While they were still a hundred meters away, Caleb threw the first pilum. Aided with just his affinity to guide it, it sailed the distance, landing a few meters away from the giant in the snow. He grabbed another and threw again, this time striking the target in the leg.

The pilum was designed to stick to enemies' shields, weighing them down until they became a hindrance. This pilum stuck, but the giant paid no heed to the weight. It did however turn to see where the attack came from, and the collared warrior took that opportunity to land another blow.

The attacks of the empire’s hero were fast, but lacked the penetration needed. With the giant focused on him, the armor held up against his attacks, but as Caleb had just proven, the armor was weak if the blow unseen.

“Go help him, stay on opposite sides,” Caleb said to his brother and Bog. “His armor is weak if he doesn’t see the attack coming. I think he has to reinforce it.”
