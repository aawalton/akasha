Jade didn't back down from the demon lord's intense stare and refused to give ground. His offer had come as a complete surprise, one that she wasn't sure what to think of. Was he sincere? Even if he was, could Vastrozun control his violent kin well enough to prevent new conflicts from arising in the future? And was Jade comfortable with essentially releasing the demons into unknown lands where they could wreak havoc among those who already lived there?

As she considered, Jade came to an unsettling realization. She had no qualms thrusting this problem upon someone else's shoulders. Her goal, before anything else, was to protect Earth. If she could do that while helping others that was all the better, but the world where her friends and family lived came first. Did that make her a bad person? By some metrics, probably. That was just something she'd have to live with, because she'd already known her answer before Vastrozun had even finished speaking. The details didn't matter, she got what she came for.

"I agree." Jade said, taking care not to look at the others. She didn't want to see their faces, they couldn't afford a slipup now. "Make your oath, and the relic is yours."

Silence greeted her proclamation. Neither her companions, the demon lord, nor the angel spoke. They all just stared at her, as if wondering if this was a trick. Jade gestured with the relic again, holding it out. She didn't want to give them time to rethink this. Her heart thumped wildly in her chest, and she found herself falling back on old breathing rhythms she'd used in her competition days to maintain an appearance of calm.

"Well? If you don't want it, then I'm going to use it."

"You-"

Again, Vastrozun halted Ira's angry outburst with a small gesture. The angel fell back, glowering at Jade with an intensity that made her skin prickle.

"Very well. Bring it here, and we shall begin."

Slowly, Jade closed the distance to the raised dais upon which Vastrozun's throne sat. She climbed the steps, more concious than ever of the extreme power disparity before them. He bid the angel join them and, beneath her piercing gaze, repeated the oath that would protect Earth and the nearby lands of the Labyrinth. The dark crystal clutched in Jade's hand pulsed, like something deep within its swirling matrix acknowledged the words. That done, Jade silently presented Vastrozun with the relic. He took it from her, holding it with reverence. She half expected one of them to immediately attack the moment she relinquished it, but they didn't. The demon lord had eyes only for the relic.

"The lost gods consumed much of their power creating our prison. Most have been slumbering for over a thousand years. As you might guess, that has made relics of this type exceedingly difficult to come by."

"Is that so?" Jade kept her tone as casual as possible. "The last time I tried channeling power like that it nearly killed me."

"Unsurprising." Vastrozun chuckled. The sound was completely incongruous with his menacing visage. "Mortals can learn to wield divine essence, but it is a long and slow process. The time is well worth the effort, however, as true mastery allows you to fuse such essence with your very spirit."

Jade thought of how she'd drained Seraphine, leaving a hint of radiance in her own demonic aura. It hadn't been a pleasant process.

"And what does that do to you?" she asked, mostly just to keep the conversation going. The more he focused on talking, the less he-

"This."

Without warning, Vastrozun raised the foot-long crystal shard and thrust it into his chest. The relic disappeared into a roiling tide of shifting shadows, but Jade heard the creaking screech of punctured armor followed by a sickening squelch as the demon lord drove the artifact deep into his flesh. Violet energy arced from his body like bolts of lightning, vaporizing small sections of the flagstone floor around him. Jade and her companions scrambled away, and even Ira took a step back. Behind the throne, the vortex of dark power flared. A shadow spread across the room, dimming the faint sunlight streaming in through the high windows.

"My lord!"

The angel tried to approach Vastrozun, but he stopped her with a raised hand. When he spoke, his words were strained.

"Stay back, Ira." The demon lord grunted. He sounded strained, but no less strong. "This will take but a few moments."

The world shook. Dust and small chunks of stone fell from the ceiling, and the cathedral groaned like a wounded animal. Luis shot Jade a concerned look.

"Uh, should we be getting out of here?"

"Not yet."

They couldn't leave. Not until…

"The nearest portal just closed." Tara said. Her visor was flipped up, and she was rapidly keying in commands to a small terminal on her arm. "I think he's drawing in the dimensional energy of the closest gates."

"Is that safe?" Fenrin asked.

"How should I know?!"Jade was rapidly having a change of heart.

"Maybe we should--"

"Aaaaarrrgh!"

A titanic roar drowned out the rest of what she said. They all spun to see Vastrozun doubled over, clinging to one arm of his throne for support. His mantle of darkness writhed like a raging bonfire, and for the first time, small gaps appeared between its tendrils. A hint of the demon lord's ashen skin shone through, revealing a corpse-like complexion that didn't seem to fit the creature's powerful and commanding voice.

"What?!" He snarled, eyes blazing with rage and wheeling on them as motes of brilliant light began flaring to life in the depths of the darkness that surrounded him "What did you do?!"

Several days earlier

The goddess Elaria stood before them in the tranquil clearing of her forest glade, gesturing with the divine relic in her hand. Her somber expression contrasted sharply with the deity's radiant, flawless beauty, imparting Jade with a sense of profound regret.

"There is still a chance to stop a new cosmic war before it begins. If you can make your way through the host of demons standing between us and him, this artifact would give you the power to defeat Vastrozun…" Elaria paused, letting her words hang in the air between them for several long moments. Finally, she released a heartfelt sigh. "Or so I wish I could say. Alas, this task won't be so simple."

"Simple?" Jade asked, unable to keep a note of incredulity from the word. "None of this sounds simple to me."

She felt Naomi's gentle, calming touch on her arm. Jade exhaled slowly. Her friend was right, now wasn't the time to get worked up. They should at least hear the goddess out.

"Sorry." Jade shook her head. "This is just a lot to take in at once."

"No apologies necessary." Elaria waved away her words. "You're right to be skeptical. I said it wouldn't be that simple, not that even that task would have been easy."

"So, what do you actually want us to do?" Fenrin asked. The half-elf had crossed his arms and was studying the goddess with an appraising look that didn't strike Jade as very respectful either. Then again, the goddess had called them here to ask them for a rather monumental favor. Deity or not, they were due their unreserved reactions.

"Vastrozun has already imbued his spirit with a small but significant amount of divine power. Mostly Dark essence, but with others as well. It is by using this divine energy that he has granted many of his kin forms of power."

"Right." Tara said. "You mentioned before that that's why a lot of them are so aggressive. Not everybody can handle that kind of strain on their mind and body."

"Especially not when they didn't claim in through their own strength but instead were gifted it." The goddess agreed. "Vastrozun is different, though. He has properly integrated this essence into his mind, body, and spirit, greatly fortifying them as a result. Even with a tool as potent as a relic of Destruction, you would be hard pressed to destroy him before he defeated you. Doubly so, as the Destruction and Dark essences have a relatively high affinity with each other."

"If that's true, then why did you make this?" Jade asked, looking back at the crystal shard still held in Elaria's hand. "Wouldn't it have been better to forge a relic out of an essence more--" she paused, trying to think of a word that wasn't "destructive", as that would be confusing in this context, "harmful to him? I have a demon form, and when I tried to absorb Seraphine's essence, even that tiny amount almost killed me. According to the system, the only reason I pulled through is because my spirit is still mostly human."

Jade paced, working through the problem in her head as she spoke.

"Celestials are made of Light essence, right?"

"The majority of them are, yes." Elaria replied, eyeing Jade closely. Under normal circumstances, that kind of attention would have unsettled her. But something was tugging at the back of Jade's mind, and she wasn't about to get distracted.

"But you think his defenses are strong enough that even a relic comprised of Light essence wouldn't be enough to defeat him. If you did, you would have created one of those."

"That's right."

Jade remembered her own experience with celestial Light essence, how her body and spirit had burned from the inside out, like a star had formed in the center of her soul. Seraphine's attacks had hurt her, but it wasn't until she'd absorbed a piece of her power that she'd been nearly annihilated. And that had only been a tiny fragment of the power the angel had held. If she had let Jade drain her at the beginning of the battle while she was still at full strength, the angel wouldn't have even needed to fight. Jade imagined she would have exploded long before doing the angel any real harm, even considering her own regenerative abilities. So, wouldn't it be better to…

Jade stopped pacing and snapped her fingers. That was it.

"You understand, then."

"I do."

"We aren't supposed to waste time attacking the demon lord head on. It would take too long to defeat him in battle. He's probably more powerful than we can even conceive of. Even if we hit is weaknesses, he'd wipe the floor with us before we could finish him."

"What else can we do?" Tara asked. "It's not like we can beat him another way."

That gave her a number of blank stares. Only Fenrin nodded, understanding lighting up his eyes.

"He is. Without more power at his disposal, his ability to maintain these gateways to the rest of the cosmos for a significant length of time is questionable. The Labyrinth system is doing everything it can to force them shut. They're in stalemate, and he will be searching for a way to gain an advantage."

"And you said Destruction essence has a high affinity with his own Dark essence…" Jade continued, pointing at the relic. Now came the important question. "Lady Elaria, what about the affinity between Light and Destruction? Can they be combined?"

The goddess graced them with a soft smile. It lit up the glade with all the warmth of the afternoon sun emerging from a cloudy sky.

"It is not the greatest, but it's not impossible. Especially if the mixture needn't last for a great deal of time."

Jade focused her senses on the artifact again, but even suspecting what it really was, all she could feel was the volatile, destructive force it contained. There was no hint of anything else.

"Will anyone be able to tell?"

"No, not so long as enough of its destructive force remains. After no more than five uses the veneer will rub thin."

"So, it can still be used like you originally said?"

The goddess nodded again. Jade supposed that made sense. For this to work, the deception had to be maintained until the last moment. If they couldn't wield the relic how they supposedly intended to, the entire plan wouldn't hold up to scrutiny. Maybe it would be best to find an excuse to use it once or twice before confronting the demon lord, even. If word of their actions made it to him, it would make their apparent intent all the more convincing.

"Wait, so that's really…" Tara said, taking a step closer to the relic. She brushed her fingers against it, and Jade sensed her probing it as well. "But how can we be sure he'll go for it?"

"If he's in as desperate need as the goddess thinks he is," Fenrin continued, "then we just need to put ourselves in a position that allows him to take it from us."

The conversation continued for another half hour as they went back and forth on the details. According to the goddess, essences of different types had never been fused in this manner before. It had taken a large portion of what meager power she had available to craft it, but the result was more than worth the trouble. Nobody would suspect its true nature.

Some of them were more enthusiastic about this plan than others, but eventually, they all agreed to it. Jade felt more than a little guilty about that, it was clear that some of her friends were only coming along because she refused to force the task on someone else. But she couldn't do that. She knew what the power of Light felt like to a demon, what it did to them. This could work. And if there was a real chance, then they had to take it.

It was a good plan, better than any of them had expected to find when they'd first entered the goddess’s realm.
