Jane stared at Allen. “You think the king wants to marry his son off to me?”

The idea was almost absurd. Then again, Jane realized it was only almost that way because she herself found it hard to imagine.

In that moment, hundreds of things people had said about her in her hearing started to come together. Things she had initially brushed off. She was famous, they said. Important. Pretty, even. A world-shakingly powerful force of magic, if she included her aunt’s overblown language in the list.

No, no. It can’t be.

"That's ridiculous.” She shook her head. “I'm a baker. I make bread for a living. Nobody thinks of me as an archmage anymore."

"Everyone thinks of you as an archmage. You're the one who forgets." Allen's voice was gentle, but firm. "I'm not saying the rumors are true. I'm just saying, if they were, you'd be exactly the kind of person a king might look for as a wife for his son. You saved this town, Jane. Twice. That's not nothing."

Jane opened her mouth to argue, then closed it again. She wanted to dismiss the idea entirely, to laugh it off as impossible, but Allen wasn't the type to say things he didn't mean. And he wasn't the type to worry about nothing.

"Even if that were true," she said slowly, "it wouldn't matter. I'm not interested in princes. I'm interested in you."

Allen smiled, in his own incredibly earnest way. There was no way not to trust him when he did that.

"I know. I’m not worried about that, Jane. I'm just saying you should be prepared. For whatever happens. A prince would be lucky to get someone like you, and even if you don’t want to see that, you should. Just so you can be prepared."

Allen reached out and took her hand, his calloused fingers intertwining with hers. That was the end of the conversation, Jane realized.

A person who was really worried for themselves would have looked to her to reassure them, to tell them again that she had no interest in princes when tinkers were available. Allen didn’t seem to need that. Even if it all happened the way he seemed to expect it to, the only thing he appeared to be worrying about was how she would feel. Just like always.

"Come on," he said. "Let's keep walking. We've got a long afternoon, and I don't want to waste it standing around being serious."

Jane let him pull her back into motion, but her mind was already churning with new worries to add to the old ones. Showcases and recipes swirled with princes and rumors that may or may not have been true.

She pushed it all aside, focusing instead on the warmth of Allen's hand in hers and the simple pleasure of walking beside someone she cared about. Whatever was coming, it could wait until tomorrow. Today was a Lee-day, and she intended to enjoy what was left of it.

—

The next day, Jane was well into her morning baking when a very sleepy-looking Bella stumbled through her door, carrying a message Jane had hoped would take longer to arrive.

“They are here.” Bella closed the door behind her and leaned against it, panting. “The king and his family. Messengers are going around to let people know the town has declared a trade holiday. Whatever goods you were planning on making, just make them and leave them in your delivery box for anyone to take. The town will compensate you later.”

Jane stood frozen with a bowl of dough in her hands. "Already? I thought we had at least a couple more days."

"So did everyone else." Bella was already moving toward the kitchen, pulling her hair back and looking for an apron. "They arrived late last night, apparently. The word is they didn't want to make a fuss, so they just showed up at the inn and went to sleep. The council found out this morning when the innkeeper sent a runner."

"The inn? The royal family is staying at an inn?"

"Where else would they stay? We don't exactly have a palace. Tell me what needs doing. We need to be near the town meeting hall by midmorning, and you've got a lot of bread to finish.”

Jane's hands started moving before her brain caught up. She had prepped enough ingredients the night before for a full day's worth of baking, but she didn’t need to use all of them right now. And with Bella helping, even the reduced work went faster than it otherwise could have.

The loaves came out of the ovens one after another. Each was exactly right, despite the rushed circumstances. Jane arranged them in her delivery box outside the shop door, leaving a small sign explaining that they were free for the taking. It felt strange to give away her work like that, but the town would compensate her later, and there were more important things to worry about right now.

"Go change," Bella said, shooing her toward the stairs. "I'll finish cleaning up down here. You've got maybe ten minutes before we need to leave."

Jane ran upstairs and threw open her wardrobe, staring at the contents with uncertainty. She didn't have anything truly formal except dress robes suitable for an archmage, and she wasn’t about to go out in those. Luckily, this wasn't a royal court. This was Glenfall. Even the appearance of the royal family only called for a bit more formality than usual, not a complete transformation.

She settled on a dress she had bought a few months back, something nicer than her everyday clothes but still normal enough not to risk over-dressing. She brushed her hair quickly, decided against any elaborate styling, and hurried back downstairs.

Bella looked her over and nodded approvingly. "Good choice.“

They stepped out into a street that was already filling with townsfolk. Everyone was moving in the same direction. The tide of people swept the girls along, carrying them toward the opposite side of the lake where the town's main meeting hall stood.

It would have been overwhelming if not for the fact that Glenfall was a collection of twisty, almost unplanned roads that naturally separated traffic into smaller chunks. For the first time, Jane found herself grateful for the city's chaotic layout. The narrow streets and unexpected turns broke up the crowd into manageable pieces, giving her room to breathe even as they moved steadily forward.

It was only at the bridge that she got a good sense of the enormity of it all.

The bridges spanning Glenfall's lake were huge, wide enough for multiple carts to pass each other with room to spare. Jane had crossed them hundreds of times since moving here. She had always thought of them as generously sized. Now, looking at the mass of humanity packed onto the stone span, she realized she had never imagined they could be crowded.

But they were crowded. People pressed in from all sides, moving forward in a slow shuffle that was more like the flow of honey than the movement of individuals. Jane kept her hand on Bella's arm to avoid being separated, and even so, she felt the pressure of bodies on all sides.

Bella looked around and whistled. "It's easy to forget just how many people live in this city. I've never seen this many of them out and walking at once."

"Neither have I." Jane craned her neck, trying to see over the heads in front of her, but it was useless. She was too short, and there were too many people. All she could see was a forest of shoulders and backs, with occasional glimpses of the lake glittering on either side. "How are we supposed to see anything?"

"We'll figure it out. Just keep moving."

They made it across the bridge eventually, though Jane couldn't have said how long it took. When they finally reached the other side and spilled out into the streets beyond, she felt a rush of relief that was almost physical.

The meeting hall stood ahead of them, its familiar facade looking somehow grander than usual in the morning light. The street in front of it was larger than most in Glenfall, a deliberate design choice that allowed for gatherings and public announcements. Today, even that large space was loaded to the gills with people, packed so tightly that Jane couldn't see where one body ended and another began.

"This is impossible," she said. "I can't see anything from here."

Bella was already scanning the area. Her eyes lit on something, and she grabbed Jane's hand.

"This way. I have an idea."

She pulled Jane sideways through the crowd, weaving between bodies with a determination that brooked no argument. People grumbled as they pushed past, but Bella ignored them, focused entirely on whatever goal she had spotted. Jane followed as best she could, trusting her friend's instincts.

They emerged from the crowd near the shore of the lake, where a stone fence ran along the top of a steep bank. The fence was meant to keep people from falling down to the water below, a practical safety measure that Jane had seen but never really noticed. Now, looking at it with new eyes, she saw what Bella had seen.

"Up," Bella said.

Jane pulled herself up onto the wide stone top and found her balance. The fence was broad enough to sit on comfortably. From this height, she could finally see over the heads of the crowd.

The view was worth the climb. The meeting hall rose before them, the raised entrance providing a natural stage for whoever stood there. And on that platform, surrounded by a small cluster of official-looking people, stood the royal family.

Jane recognized the king immediately. She could hardly avoid it, even if she hadn’t seen him give speeches before. His face was on coins and in paintings throughout the kingdom. He was wearing traveling clothes today, which surprised her a bit. They were of the finest make, but much less formal than what she would have imagined a king would even possess.

The queen stood beside him. She watched the crowd with an expression of polite interest, occasionally responding to a question from one of the few town officials on stage with her. And beside them both, looking distinctly uncomfortable with all the attention, stood a young man who could only be the prince.

Jane studied him with more curiosity than she had expected to feel. He was handsome, she supposed, in a sort of delicate, fragile-looking way. He had dark hair, elegant features, and a trained sort of upright posture that looked almost painful to maintain. He seemed about her age, and he was scanning the crowd with a faintly nervous expression.

He doesn't want to be here, she realized. Poor man. This is ruining his vacation.

She felt a surprising pang of sympathy. She knew what unwanted fame was like, at least. If the prince was here against his will, dragged around the kingdom by his father in search of a suitable bride, then he had her compassion.

But no more than that. To her relief, she found she wasn’t particularly attracted to him. He was, as Bella would have put it, not her type.

Bella pointed. "The king's fiddling with something.”

Jane looked more closely and saw that she was right. The king had stepped away from his family and was examining some kind of object, a small device that glinted gold as he turned it over in his hands. He held it up to his mouth, frowned, then rapped it against one of the columns.

"It's an amplification device," she said, recognizing the shape. "Something magical to make his voice carry over the crowd. And it's broken."

"You can tell from here?"

"I can guess. I don’t know what he’s going to do about it, either. That kind of enchantment is fiddly. You can't just bang it against something and expect it to start working."

The king tried anyway, rapping the device against the column one more time before holding it up to his mouth again. Whatever result he got, it clearly wasn't what he wanted. He turned to one of his attendants and said something that Jane couldn't hear, his expression shifting from irritation to resignation.

"This is going to be a problem," Bella said. "How's he supposed to address the whole town if nobody can hear him?"

Jane was already thinking the same thing. The crowd was enormous, stretching back from the meeting hall in all directions. Even with the raised platform, even with the natural acoustics of the open space, there was no way the king's voice would carry to everyone. Without the amplification device, most of the people here would just see his lips moving and wouldn’t hear a word.

The king turned to Xand. After a brief exchange, Xand looked out over the crowd, as if searching for one thing in particular. He found it in Jane.

The town official made eye contact across the distance, sending Jane a plaintive look that dripped with the need for help.

Oh, shoot. I guess there’s no way around it.

With a sigh, she started climbing down from the fence.

"I need to go fix something," she said to Bella. "Stay here.”

“No way.” Bella immediately joined her. “You’d never get through, and I’m not missing any of this.”
