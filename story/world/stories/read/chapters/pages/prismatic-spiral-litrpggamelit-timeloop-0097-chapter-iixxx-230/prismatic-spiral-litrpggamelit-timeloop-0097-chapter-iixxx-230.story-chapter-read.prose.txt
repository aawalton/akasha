Chapter II.XXX (2.30)

With a dozen Mend’s, Mitsuko barely managed to keep The Terror in one piece as the many eyed leviathan burst from beneath the sea. Several of its eyes morphed together and glowed an ominous black.

She threw herself to the deck as the beam of darkness blasted into the deck. Tumbling to a stop, she instinctively cast Mend on the ship. While she’d successfully dodged the attack, not everyone else was quite as lucky. Mitsuko quickly scanned the bodies. Rather than bruised, burned, or broken flesh, those who’d come into contact with this particular beam were a stony gray and trapped in the moment.

Not temporal magic. Something more insidious. Petrification. Mitsuko had only come across the ailment twice in her travels and both times had been a bitch to work around it.

She was about to move on from the victims and refocus on the fight but then her eyes caught on a small figure. Its size made it easily overlooked, but there was no mistaking the stony form. Holly.

Mitsuko no longer cared about wasting this loop. She was going to burrow back into the fucker and pluck out its eyes from inside.

But, when she dashed across the deck and reached the gunwales, sword in hand and ready to leap at the leviathan, she found… nothing.

“Damn you!” she shouted at the sea. “Coward! Get back here! Fight me!” She hurled her ice blade at the sea. It splashed in the water and floated there.

Nothing.

“Aghh!” Mitsuko hurled another ice blade at the placid water. Then another. She continued to swear and scream at the sea. The leviathan didn’t return.

“Doubt it has much of an ego to damage,” a voice said behind her.

Mitsuko whirled around, sword raised and eyes narrowed.

Sett raised his sole arm in surrender, a smile touching the corners of his lips. “Relax, girl. Shishkababing me won’t getcha anywhere. Just saying that most sea critters don’t care about taunts and threats.”

“I’m not in the mood for your banter, Sett,” Misuko warned darkly.

He blinked, then a full smile marred his face. “You know my name? Do you’re research on me? That why you stood between me and the captain earlier?”

Mitsuko didn’t bother to answer. Instead she retreated back to where Holly had fallen. Sterling sat beside her, though, for once, the cat had nothing to say on the subject.

Running her hand over the cold stone, Mitsuko examined the statue which had been her friend just minutes earlier.

“You know, if I’d pulled it off, bet that crust would have granted us free passage,” Sett said behind her. “It just wanted a single life. Instead, the captain cost us half a dozen. All you had to do was stand aside. It all could have been prevented.”

“Sett. Shut up or I will kill you.”

“Fine, fine.” Despite not looking up at him, Mitsuko could hear the grin. As if he’d won a battle rather than lost friends.

Then Mitsuko blinked. She realized that while there were shouts all around her, getting the ship back moving again, one voice was distinctly missing from the throng.

It didn’t take her long to spot Captain Alina’s form among the stone statues. So Sett had gotten what he wanted regardless of her interference.

“Don’t,” warned Sterling.

“Don’t what?” Mitsuko asked the cat.

“You’re going to take out your anger on Sett. It will only serve as another hurdle. Unless you plan to kill every person on the ship after him and pilot the vessel yourself, I highly recommend you don’t lash out at a crew member that objectively did nothing to earn your ire.”

Mitsuko growled. Sterling was right. And, at the moment, she hated him for it.

“I’m going to murder the Azure guardian a hundred times over for this,” she promised.

“An excellent proposition,” Sterling agreed. “However, how about we set our sights on a more immediate objective? Despite everything that has occurred, we did secure early passage to Porcelain Island.”

Mitsuko grunted. She attempted to cast Mend on Holly’s stone figure. Nothing. There was a wall in place blocking out her magic.

“That won’t work. Not at your current level,” Sterling said.

“At level ten I’ll be able to reverse this?”

“I cannot speak on the future potential of your abilities. Unless you wish to expend a question?”

No point in wasting one since he’d practically already confirmed it. But as Mitsuko sat on the deck, cradling her friend’s petrified body, Mitsuko’s mind went to other potential questions. There were still so many mysteries about the Prismatic Spiral.

Mitsuko wasn’t the only person on the ship grieving for a lost friend. They might be smugglers and degenerates, but the crew still cared for their companions. Those hit by the petrification beam were trapped in the moment. As if… frozen in time. It reminded her of a city full of frozen people.

“Who was the past Champion?” she asked. “You said he failed. How?”

Sterling stiffened. “Those are… two very different questions.”

“Fine. I use two questions. Tell me what happened.”

Sterling took a step back and then deflated. He folded his legs under himself and curled his tail around his body. After a minute of silence from him, Mitsuko was starting to think he might refuse to answer.

“His name was Atticus. He was from a highly regarded family in Tempus City. Since I was likewise a notable figure in the city, the sages convened and designated me as his primary guide.”

There was a pause in his explanation. “Is that it?” Mitsuko asked. “You’d better not rob me of this question with that answer.”

“I request your patience. I am simply collecting my thoughts to find the optimal means in which to explain the events that transpired.” He paused again. “Atticus was a genius in regards to temporal spellcraft. With the potential to surpass all of us sages. He was the obvious choice to tackle the growing threat. And so, we implemented the Prismatic Spiral.”

“Didn’t that take a ton of time?” Mitsuko asked. “I can’t see this timeloop system being designed on a whim. Wouldn’t it be simpler to use that time to teach the student rather than craft a convoluted timeloop?”

“Is that a true question or simply an exclamation of fruition due to your inaccurate knowledge?”

“Fine. Don’t explain. Continue on about your pal, Atticus.”

Sterling’s glowing green eyes focused at the petrified sailors behind her.

“He learned all of our spells, just as you did now. As I said, he was a genius by all metrics of the word. You are the only individual in over a thousand years to step onto this land with an equal amount of temporal potential. The Prismatic Spiral allows us to directly imbue you with our talents. He was a terror. Truly powerful.”

“Then how did he fail?” Mitsuko asked. She immediately recognized this as essential information for herself. If someone like that could fail, then she needed to be aware of his pitfalls.

“Temptation. He hungered for more. He was not content with our spells.”

“And someone offered him more power?”

“Some… thing.” Sterling finally broke his gaze from the statues to glare up at the sun overhead.

Mitsuko followed his gaze and frowned. Then the insight crashed into her. Pieces of a puzzle clicked into place.

“A Titan?”

His attention snapped back down to her.

“It was a Titan,” Mitsuko guessed. “Like when I challenged the sun. The sun continues on despite the Prismatic Spiral. And the guardians act the same. They are the only ones other than you and I who have their memories carry over from loop to loop. And the Prismatic Archipelago is one of the only major locations in the world not connected to the World Dungeon.”

“Yes,” Sterling admitted. “Our foe is indeed a Titan. There are a few of them and most slumber. Sekai, the World Dungeon, stirred a few years back. Even with my soul trapped in the mechanisms for the Prismatic Spiral, I could feel him reaching out. I think his awakening is what triggered our Titan. Our timeframe for prevention rapidly decreased.”

Mitsuko nodded slowly. “What is his name?” Then, when Sterling opened his mouth, looking cagy, she narrowed her eyes. “Don’t you dare say it will cost me a question.”

“This is dangerous information,” Sterling said. “I… do not want to say more than necessary on the topic. A different sage might be a better source of information. They will know what questions you’ve used and can answer for me.”

“If you misspeak then they might banish you to Ashen Island?” Mitsuko guessed.

Sterling bowed his head in defeat.

“Fine. I’ll ask a third question and take the bait from earlier. How did you create the Prismatic Spiral?”

“That, I can speak on,” Sterling’s voice perked up. “We’d crafted it years previous to its activation, long before we located a suitable Champion. This wasn’t a threat that came from nowhere. We knew its power was looming over us. However, activating the Prismatic Spiral required our very souls to be consumed so we naturally put that off for as long as possible.”

“So when Atticus failed to put down the threat, you locked down the area affected,” Mitsuko surmised.

“We installed a safety net in case of failure. I admit, to my chagrin, I was against those measures. They were tedious to install into the Prismatic Spiral’s mechanics and I saw no world in which a Champion I chose would ever fail. Thankfully, my peers insisted it was necessary, so I reluctantly complied with their demands.”

“And this is your last chance? If I fail it’s all over?”

“We will contain the threat within the archipelago. However, our influence is limited. The remnants of our power will be expended in a few hundred years. Hopefully, the world will move to develop a new solution before the threat shatters our bindings and expands beyond our borders.”

“Your very souls were incorporated into the Prismatic Spiral?”

“Indeed.”

Mitsuko gripped Holly’s stone hand. “Then… what happens to you when I win?”

“Mitsuko.” Sterling’s voice was softer than she’d ever heard from him. “You need not ask. You know the answer.”
