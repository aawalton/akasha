The dirt path stretched ahead, winding deeper into the unknown. The little sprite nestled against Elynara’s cheek buzzed softly, its warmth a quiet comfort against the cool air.

She had been following the road for a good while now, just taking in the sights around her. Breathing in the air. Listening to the sounds.

There was much she didn’t remember seeing before. And even more that felt strangely familiar, like echoes from a dream she couldn’t quite place. Still, she observed.

At first, the forest felt full of life. Leaves rustled in the breeze, their gentle swaying whispering unseen secrets. Birds called from hidden perches, a living chorus woven through the creaking branches. The scent of damp earth clung to the air, rich and grounding. Sunlight filtered through the canopy in shifting golden beams, casting fleeting patterns on the path like dancing specters.

But still… something was off.

It wasn’t anything specific, and certainly didn’t feel like a threat, but… there was a faint misalignment – like a painting hung ever so slightly askew. A quiet wrongness that didn’t belong to the trees or the wind, but affected them all the same.

She just... couldn't figure out what it was, exactly.

But as her steps took her further down the old path, the feeling lessened. As if whatever wrongness she sensed could no longer stand the sunshine warming everything up.

Given how intense it felt, It must have been nearing noon already.

And as she walked ahead, her steps lightened, and she once again simply soaked it all in.

It all felt so real. Wild, vibrant, and untamed.

And she thoroughly enjoyed every bit of it.

Every so often, she would close her eyes and reach outward, trying to sense the mana around her. It was still difficult to grasp, slipping away the moment she focused too hard, or not showing up at all when she got too distracted by the passing butterfly.

Yet, something felt… different. Thinner. The strands of mana in the air were weaker than they had been near the altar.

The further she walked, the more noticeable it became.

The trees grew a little sparser, their once-rich leaves paling into duller shades. The fresh, crisp air turned more stale, and the lingering scent of life slowly grew weaker.

Even the soil beneath her boots, once damp and soft, became just a shade lesser under her foot. The birdsong too grew less common, and straining her ears didn’t seem to help much either.

Everything was just a little… duller, somehow.

She frowned. The shift wasn’t natural. The world itself felt more drained, as if something had been slowly leeching the life from it.

Still, she pressed on, shaking off the unease with a bright smile.

"So what if it's a little gloomy?"

She twirled a strand of her green-tinted hair, forcing cheer into her voice.

"That just means I'll be the one to bring the color back!"

The sprite chirped in approval, but despite her bravado, the feeling of wrongness remained.

With the initial excitement and emotions slowly burning out, the dullness of the long walk made her thoughts wander about.

What she felt, what she saw, the mana she could now wield, the many questions she had.

Her thoughts swirled about as she took in the gradual changes to her surroundings, how lifeless the world felt compared to the little glade she woke up in, and how the plants seemed less vibrant and green here, weaker but still surviving.

Until finally, the blinking dot in her vision became impossible to ignore.

Elynara blinked and focused on it, wondering what the deal with it was.

But as if it was just waiting for her undivided attention, a simple interface flickered to life.

Name: ElynaraLevel: 1Mana: 20 / 20 (+1.5/h)

Stats:Fitness: 8Agility: 9Willpower: 12Intuition: 11

Skills:Mana Manipulation – Lvl 1Mana Sense – Lvl 1

Traits:Mana Attuned – A body attuned to mana generates and absorbs it more efficiently. Gains an intuitive sense for mana flow and structure.Spirit Sight – Ability to perceive spirits and lingering magical traces. Its strength grows with use and focus.

Curious, she poked at it mentally, but nothing changed. It wasn’t interactive beyond observation. Only one thing reacted: her mana regeneration. When she focused on it, more details appeared.

Base: +1.0/hOther: +0.5/h

Other? Her brow furrowed. The environment? The sprite?

If the looks of the trees around her were any indication, she could kind of guess.

Her gaze landed on another part of the status, but with no explanation on what the stats meant she could only shrug. She didn’t even know what the norm was, after all. Her skills came as no surprise, she worked for those!

As for the traits… She was fairly certain they were the boon Celestia spoke about.

Probably helped her get her skills so quickly too. Or so her scattered memories seemed to show, convincing her that they ought to have been harder to learn.

A big smile formed on her face, the little fuzzball deciding her momentary distraction was a perfect time to boop her on the nose, again!

“Hey, I’m thinking here you little troublemaker!”

She glared at the green fuzzball, but it was hard to remain angry at the joyful little thing for long. It was simply a little playful and… it seemed to like her smile?

Huffing, she returned to thinking about her status.

Minimalist. Functional. No tutorial, no explanations, no hidden functions as far as she could tell.

Everything hinted at it being just a passive system quietly tracking her state.

It didn’t seem like much, yet it was still useful to know her exact condition anytime she wished.

“Not bad.”

Dismissing it came just as easily, the blinking dot disappearing entirely. But now that she knew what it was, merely thinking about her status pulled it up again.

Bringing it up and away a few more times in quick succession, she smirked at how convenient it was. And it somehow knew how to adjust so her eyes wouldn’t hurt!

"Not bad at all."

Actually, was she even ‘seeing’ it? Probably? Maybe? It certainly didn’t seem too eager to tell her.

Mysteries for another time.

The sun dipped low, stretching shadows across the road. No towns, nor any signs of civilization – only the growing cold, the thinning forest, and the hush of twilight settling in.

She wasn’t foolish enough to keep walking in the dark. And she certainly didn’t have the leg strength for it either, after walking for hours already!

Straying slightly from the path, she found a small copse of trees that offered some shelter.

It wasn’t much, but it would do.

Unclipping her satchel, she took stock of her supplies. Everything from the altar remained intact: water flask, rations, cloak.

Looking around she quickly located some dry branches and a few smaller stones.

Hauling them together took some effort, and her newly created fireplace looked rather shoddy… But it was something!

Except, her so far helpful memories seemed to end here.

How did she start a fire now?

What little she could remember seemed to say she needed to rub or strike some things together? But what?

Looking confused for a moment, she glanced at the little sprite, but the little bugger didn’t seem interested in helping whatsoever.

“Alright, let’s see if that works.”

Hint, it didn’t work.

Rubbing two pieces of wood together only got her hands tired. Striking seemed even less successful, at least rubbing produced some heat!

Finally fed up she grabbed one of the nearby stones and started striking. At first a little unsure, but as the failures continued to mount… soon enough she was just randomly whacking against all the stones she gathered.

“Why. Won’t. You. Work?!”

And then, just as she was about to give up on the notion, one of the stones produced a spark.

“Yessss!”

The rest was thankfully a breeze. After hauling the stone over to the kindling, and a few strikes later, the fire was underway.

Exhausted, she plopped down next to it, extending her tired hands towards the growing warmth.

A better way of starting the fire must exist… Striking and rubbing, maybe she can do that with mana? A thought for another time.

Soon after a few sips of water and some nibbling of her rations, she hauled a couple bigger branches into the fire, pulled the fabric around her shoulders and settled into the coziest spot she could find.

The air turned crisp and the sprite, sensing her stillness, flitted about before curling against her. Its glow was faint but steady.

Lying on the grass, she stared up at the sky and simply breathed.

The night stretched vast above her, an endless sea of stars. Not too bright, yet still so vivid, almost shimmering with impossible clarity. The sky itself felt alive.

Nothing she could put her finger on, just a little more… there.

Then, she noticed the moons.

The first was full and luminous, casting a very faint bluish glow.

The second shimmered with a soft purplish-white light, subtle, almost a mirage. She had to squint at it just right for the shimmer to become more visible.

And the third… wasn’t whole.

At first, it seemed like a normal crescent, hazy but recognizable. Yet as she stared, realization settled in.

It wasn’t a natural moon phase, but it looked like something had taken a bite out of it. Jagged fragments, large and small – mere hazy specks of dust at those distances – drifted in slow orbit around what remained from before whatever cataclysm tore it apart.

A strange weight pressed against her chest.

If a thing as vast as a moon could break… how much had this world already lost?

She stared at the three celestial bodies for a long while, just trying to take it all in. To comprehend their beauty, and the ridiculous scale of the devastation.

Her fingers curled against the grass, a whisper escaping her lips.

"...Just what sort of cataclysm has led to this? Do you know, little one?"

The sprite merely hummed softly against her cheek, as uncertain as she was.

Morning arrived with golden light filtering through the trees. Elynara stretched, shaking off the lingering chill. The fire burned out at some point during the night.

The sprite buzzed to life beside her, its glow a little brighter than before.

She watched it curiously, then tested something. Reaching into her core, she drew out a wisp of mana and extended it toward the creature.

It absorbed it instantly.

Its glow pulsed, and it spun excitedly before nuzzling against her cheek.

She laughed. "So you do eat mana, huh?"

It trilled in response, clearly pleased.

Encouraged, she resumed practicing. Drawing in mana, shaping it, releasing it.

Each attempt came smoother, more natural.

Towards the end of her practice, two tiny spheres flickered between her fingers, their density shifting under her will.

Her control remained clumsy, but the progress was undeniable.

It all felt so amazing, yet focusing for so long still felt rather draining.

As good a time as any to get up, stretch, and get ready for the day!

A quick meal and a drink later, she patted her slightly wrinkled coat straight, nudging the little fuzzball into its preferred spot.

With her energy restored and confidence growing, she set out once more.

The road stretched ahead, but the landscape had changed. Sparse trees gave way to open fields. The weakening scent of the fading forest was replaced by fresher air.

Signs of human presence appeared, an old signpost here, remnants of a wooden fence there, faint cart tracks embedded in the dirt.

Then, in the distance, she saw it.

A town.

Stone walls, wooden rooftops, banners fluttering weakly in the breeze. A medieval-looking settlement, nestled between rolling fields and a winding river.

Her memories stirred – it was all oh-so-familiar, yet just out of reach.

A shiver ran through her body, anticipation rising within her.

Whatever waited beyond those gates, it would be her first true step into the world.
