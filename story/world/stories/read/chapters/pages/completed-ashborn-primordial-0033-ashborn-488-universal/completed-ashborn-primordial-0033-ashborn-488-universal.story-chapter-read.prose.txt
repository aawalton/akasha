“Are we sure it’s safe?” Maiya asked.

Vir could only nod, though he was anything but sure. “I do not believe Janak would send us to our deaths. Not after all he’s invested in getting us here. As for what lies on the other end, however, I can’t even begin to guess.”He expected Ashani to chime in as she always did with her Imperium knowledge, but when she didn’t, he turned to her and asked if she had any guidance to spare.

She shook her head, her lips taut. The silence that followed was awkward, to say the least. Vir had at least expected some kind of theory as to what this gateway might lead to, even if she didn’t know. The fact that she didn’t offer one was unsettling.

Regardless, he steeled himself. “Stay close. Follow behind me,” he said, stepping through first.

The Gate was utterly opaque, unlike any he’d ever seen. The sensation that followed was different as well.

Traveling through a regular Gate felt like nothing at all—like stepping foot through a picture frame with nothing in between.

This, however, felt very much like drifting through the same endless sea he had once experienced when his soul was reforged within his Chakra mindscape.

Adrift on an endless current, traveling to some faraway, unknowable place.

That journey had been far worse. Even the smallest lapse in focus then would have cost him everything, leaving his soul reincarnated and memoryless. This time, their bodies and souls thankfully remained intact. Merely transported to somewhere else.

Somewhere not on any of the realms.

After what felt like both an eternity and no time at all, Vir emerged, prana cycling and weapons at the ready, prepared for whatever awaited them on the other side.

But what greeted them were blue skies and towering white spires that gleamed like glass. And all around them were people, laughing and smiling, and all dressed in vibrant silks and jewels, floating on carpets that shimmered with magic. Children fired magic orbs at each other, playing some sort of game, and the air itself seemed to brim with energy.

Ashani and Maiya stepped through right behind him, both as stunned as he was.

“What is this…?” Maiya breathed, looking up at the impossible city.

“Welcome,” said a kind-faced woman approaching them. Her voice was serene and melodic in a way that no normal voice could sound. “I am known as Yuma, and you stand within the Prime Imperium capital of Mahādi.”

“Mahādi?” Vir repeated in disbelief.

“Indeed. We have been expecting you, travelers from our sister universe.”

“Universe!?” Vir’s mind reeled. “What exactly is that supposed to mean? And where are we?”

Yuma smiled serenely. It suddenly clicked in Vir’s mind who she must be. If she spoke the truth, and Vir had no reason to doubt she did, then this woman was the Yuma. The one whose name graced the Panav’s Bloodline Arts and whom demons revered. The goddess of legend.

Vir gulped. As much as he thought he’d grown accustomed to Imperium gods and goddesses, he couldn’t help but be awed by her presence.

“But of course,” she said. “Please, this way.”

Vir, Maiya, Ashani, and the wolves followed her through the radiant streets of Mahādi, and Vir could only gape at the sight. The city was both alive and glorious. Exactly as it must have been at its height. Towering spires gleamed with inlays of gold and prana alike. Sky bridges linked floating terraces. Prana fountains cascaded upward in glittering arcs.

He’d lived in Mahādi for years, but that Mahādi had been dark, dead, and silent. A mausoleum to a lost age. This one was vibrant and full of all the grandeur he expected of the Imperium.

The resemblance was uncanny, almost identical to the vision Ashani had shown him years ago.

The wolves, for their part, didn’t seem to care overly much, sniffing random objects and people as usual as they passed, and often receiving pets in the process.

“Any ideas?” Vir asked the goddess. Well, his goddess. He supposed she was but one among many in this place.

Ashani didn’t respond at first. She stood frozen, eyes wide, and to Vir’s surprise, tears welled in them. “I had no idea,” she whispered. “No idea such a place could exist. Oh, Janak…” she trailed off, her voice trembling.

While Vir found her reaction somewhat strange, he felt it was understandable. For her, this must have been like being reunited with a version of her own people. A reflection of what they once were.

A return to a home she thought dead. Nostalgia for a time four millennia past.

Vir turned his eyes upward, to the dozens of flying carpets weaving gracefully through the air, people laughing and conversing as they passed overhead.

“Can you explain what this is?” Vir asked finally.

Yuma inclined her head. “You must be very confused, and that is natural. You might think you’ve traveled back in time, but I assure you, this is not the case. This universe, though similar to your own, is entirely separate. It is its own creation, with no direct link to yours… except for the bridge created by the one who calls himself Janak.”

Vir frowned. “You mean to say there’s no Janak in this universe?”

“That is correct,” Yuma said gently. “In this world, no such being exists.”

Vir’s brow furrowed. “But how? How can another reality like this even exist?”

“As far as we understand,” Yuma said, “this is but one of countless realities. Every choice, every decision made in one universe spawns another in which that choice was not made. Or made differently. Every moment gives birth to an infinity of others. Every possibility, every outcome, is realized somewhere. The result is an infinity of infinities—an endless web of universes.”

Vir struggled to process it. “An infinite number of universes…” he murmured. “Is this making any more sense to you?” he asked, turning to Maiya.

The scowl on her face, however, said it all. “If it wasn’t her who said it, I’d have called it grakshit.”

“Indeed,” Yuma replied. “Even we struggle to fully understand the concept, strange as it is. In some, the Prime Imperium has fallen, as it has in yours—long ago. In others, it fell only recently. And in ours…” Her eyes gleamed softly. “In ours, it has yet to fall, and perhaps, may never fall at all.”

“Every possible choice, every possible option,” Vir murmured, “exists somewhere? So ones who died… might be alive in one of these universes?”

Yuma’s expression softened. “In an infinite number of them, most likely,” she said quietly. “Those you’ve lost, your precious ones, undoubtedly live on elsewhere.”

Vir’s heart caught. “Can I travel there? Could I… bring them back? The same way I came here?”

Yuma’s gaze grew sorrowful. “In theory, perhaps. But it is highly inadvisable. Such travel comes at great cost—one that even we dare not pay. Allow me instead to show you something. For there is a place better suited for explanations.”

“Lead the way,” Vir said, glancing back at his companions as their small entourage followed.

They walked for a while in silence, each consumed in their own thoughts. Eventually, one question rose to the top of Vir’s mind. One he couldn’t help but ask.

“How did you even know to expect us?”

Yuma smiled again in that serene, radiant way only a true goddess could. She seemed every bit the benevolent divinity the legends spoke of, and Vir still struggled to believe he was speaking to yet another mythical figure of the Imperium. Of an alive and thriving Imperium, no less.

“It is quite the story,” she said. “But suffice it to say, the Janak of your world contacted us some time ago. Though his communication was… limited, he conveyed enough for us to piece together what was happening. Through various divinations, we predicted your arrival on this day, at this very hour.”

“That’s… convenient,” Maiya said skeptically.

“Call it auspicious,” Yuma replied, her smile widening faintly. “For you’ve arrived during one of our greatest festivals.”

As they walked, it was impossible not to notice the celebratory energy that filled the streets. The air was thick with laughter and song as people reveled in the celebrations to the beat of ceremonial drums. Drums, Vir noted that bore great resemblance to those found in both the human and demon realms.

In one open plaza, men and women hurled shimmering orbs at one another. Vir tensed, thinking they were spells, until the orbs burst in puffs of colored smoke, splattering bright pigments across the white garments of those hit.

Instead of anger, laughter erupted all around as they returned fire, staining one another in every hue imaginable.

“So this is what things were like,” Maiya said softly. Like Vir, she had been stunned into silence until now. “Honestly, I was expecting hordes of monsters or something to fight.”

Yuma’s smile faded into something sadder. “I’m afraid you may get your wish soon enough. I cannot speak for the realities beyond this one, but your journey ahead will be long and likely fraught with peril.”

Vir desperately wanted to ask what she meant or how she knew but held his tongue. She’d promised answers. He could wait a few minutes.

They turned another corner and found a training arena where warriors sparred with the sort of elegant and precise movement that only masters of the art could exhibit, channeling streams of prana in patterns that were oddly familiar.

“Kalari!” Vir and Maiya said in unison.

Yuma nodded. “You know of it? Our festival includes contests of strength and demonstrations of skill in the ancient art of Kalaripayattu. A celebration of combat mastery in all its forms.”

“What a glorious place,” Vir said in a voice full of reverence. He was reminded again of the heights the Imperium had once soared to… and of how far the modern world had fallen. Even with all their recent progress, both realms felt hollow compared to this. A shadow of their former brilliance.

And, as Vir knew well, shadows had no chance of ever becoming that which they once were.

Eventually, they stepped onto a large magic carpet wide enough to carry a dozen people. Or, in this case, four people and six wolves. It lifted gracefully into the air, carrying them high above the mythical city.

Vir stared in awe as they ascended. Below, the streets curved in intricate spirals, glowing softly with channels of prana, as though designed to appear beautiful from the air. The sky was alive with movement as hundreds of carpets flew at different heights, weaving between the spires in perfect order.

Maiya reached for his hand, lacing her fingers through his. Together, they gazed down in wonder, watching the endless motion below as they sped toward the central spire, which, like his own Māhadi, was the tallest structure in the city.

“Is he here?” Ashani asked quietly. They were the first words she’d spoken since she arrived.

Yuma shook her head. “I’m afraid not, madam Automaton.”

“Then why bring us here?” Vir asked. “Did the gate send us to the wrong place?”

“Not here,” Yuma said simply. “The answers await elsewhere, where it is safe to speak them.”

Vir frowned, a chill creeping through him. He thought he understood, though he didn’t want to believe it.

Safe to speak? Did that mean that, even here, in this entirely separate universe, this other reality, the Fateweavers were listening?

If their reach extended across worlds—across universes—then they were far more powerful than they could ever have imagined.

Vir fought back the despair that threatened to eat him alive, though he didn’t entirely succeed.

For how was one supposed to fight such an enemy?

He prayed that Yuma held the answers. Because if she didn’t, all truly was lost.
