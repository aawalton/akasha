The regular fire in the fireplace continued to crackle as the lich that had originally summoned Asher to this world ignored the fallen queen for a moment, its own fiery-eyes still staring intensely at him. Zygrul, as Asher finally knew it was named, almost seemed to have forgotten the other lich was even in the same room as it. This didn’t appear to be new behavior, as the fallen queen somehow snapped her bony fingers, trying to get Zygrul’s attention.

“Crush the realms, Zygrul, pay attention!” she shouted, her voice practically dripping with venom. “I won’t entertain your schemes or studies any longer! Hand over my phylactery this instant or I will enthrall your very own summon and command it to gnaw on your bones for the next hundred years!”

Asher found himself now staring at this new lich in horror, as the thought of someone powerful enough to simply enthrall a greater demon that someone else had summoned was not a calming one. He desperately wanted to know what the liches were capable of, but both Identify and Judgement failed to get any sort of reading off of them whatsoever. It was similar to when he looked at Draken. The skills weren’t being blocked, it was as if the two liches weren’t even registering.

Zygrul appeared to consider this threat, before nodding, leaning back in its chair and turning to look at its fellow lich. “Calm yourself, Hylm, you will receive your phylactery soon enough. Though if you are able to put your irrelevant desires to the side for just a moment, you may find that you do not need your phylactery at all.”

The pink fires in Hylm’s eyes flared ever brighter, and she stared at Zygrul. Her tiara seemed to shine brightly for a moment, before she spoke. “Explain.”

“I have a hypothesis I have not run past the rest of the Council,” Zygrul said. “Our objective, which I succeeded in doing, need I remind you, was the creation of the element of Eternity. It took six hundred and thirty two years for the element to form, which would mean that, assuming that is a consistent timeframe without fluctuation, it would take another six thousand nine hundred and fifty two years for enough elements to manifest for the others. Assuming everyone stood by their word and did not attempt to undo their undeath, as any decrease in the number of phylacteries would assuredly increase the length of time for a new Eternity element to manifest.”

“You’ve already explained all this before, get to the point,” Hylm snapped. “And need I remind you that you were also the one who allowed this infant to make off with our treasured element!”

“Irrelevant,” Zygrul said, earning a scoff from his companion. At this point, Asher attempted to speak up, only to find that he couldn’t open his mouth. Some invisible, outside force held it shut. Rather than try to fight it, he decided to wait a little longer. Even if he was in most likely the most dangerous situation he’d ever been in, he was still getting a shocking amount of information out of the liches simply by being in the same room as them and listening to their conversation. He could only hope that in order to conduct their ritual, the greater demon would have to release him at some point or another, during which time he’d make his move.

“Do tell me how allowing an infant to run off with the fruits of our labor is irrelevant,” Hylm demanded, her voice positively dripping with venom.

“Following the Council’s agreement, none of you would have attempted to undo your undeaths until twelve elements of Eternity were manifested, and thus the fact that this one left our care for a few months is irrelevant,” Zygrul explained. “It has now been recovered. If anything, we obtained excellent new data for the previously unknown element. Logically, this unexpected addition to the experiment was an overall boon.”

“I forgot what it was like trying to argue with you,” Hylm said, actually sighing of all things. Asher stared at the fallen queen in confusion, wondering why the two liches seemed so incredibly different. When he looked at Zygrul, he didn’t even see a person. From the very first moment he’d laid eyes on the lich that brought him to this world, Asher hadn’t considered it a human being. Zygrul was like some sort of unfeeling construct, designed to analyze the world around it and only look at numbers. Hylm, on the other hand, while clearly being a lich, still felt at least somewhat human. She was definitely angry, for one thing, which finally made it all click into place.

Zygrul doesn’t seem to have emotions at all, Asher realized as Hylm took a moment to choose her next words carefully.

“If this is going to go on much longer, can you at least dismiss your unsightly demon?” Hylm demanded, her flaming eyes flickering as they shifted toward the giant demon that had been standing silently all this time. “You know how I feel about your bad habit of dragging up denizens from the lower realms.”

“Demons are the most cost-effective and efficient uses of tritessence,” Zygrul explained. “Creatures from the abyss are unable to follow simple instructions, and the cost of actually finding sentient beings in the wild realm is—”

“Zygrul, I asked you to dismiss your summon, not for a lecture on the application of tritessence on the realms,” Hylm snapped, fidgeting with her tiara again. Plucking a gem from the crown, she waved her hand and the gem shot across the room. Asher flinched as it smacked him in the forehead, burning for a second before latching into place. “There. The child is not going anywhere.”

“If that is your desire, so it shall be,” Zygrul said, turning toward the greater demon. “Leave us. Ensure that my plan for the others is progressing according to schedule.”

“Understood,” the greater demon said, releasing Asher’s head and wrists before turning and walking out of the room. The moment the greater demon closed the door behind it, Asher tried to slip into the astral and escape.

Only to find that his skill didn’t activate.

Frowning, Asher turned to run, only to blink as his feet remained right where they were, as though glued to the floor. Finally realizing the gem Hylm stuck on his head had to be doing something, he tried to use Complete Resilience’s purge effect, only to find that his skill wouldn’t go off. No, that wasn’t quite right…

He didn’t want his skill to go off. It was almost as if right before he actually tried to do anything, there was a split second where his mind was no longer his own, and he decided against doing that action. He couldn’t move. Couldn’t speak. Couldn’t even activate his skills. Not because he was being physically prevented, but because he didn’t want to do any of that. Desperately, he tried to activate Complete Resilience again and again, experiencing that strange flicker of dissociation enough times in quick succession to realize what was happening.

He couldn’t do any of that… because he didn’t want to disappoint the lich sitting before him. The flashes of powerful emotion that swept over him for a fraction of a second at a time to make him change his mind were one that he’d come to experience quite frequently during his last two months on this world.

He didn’t want to upset the one he loved.

“Are you aware that the Love element is actually forbidden in the Noala Kingdom?” Zygrul asked, watching Asher try and fail to best the spell the lich had put him under. “Along with many of the neighboring countries as well. I hypothesized it was largely your own doing that the element became forbidden. I recall a time where it was a treasured element. Its users highly sought after for the benefits they could bring to society.”

“I swear, you enslave one kingdom to follow your beck and call and nobody ever lets you forget it,” Hylm muttered, shaking her head. “Regardless of my past, the child won’t move from that spot or do anything for fear of disappointing me. Now, explain what you teased me about earlier. Why wouldn’t I need my phylactery?”

“After witnessing what the element of Eternity has been capable of, I hypothesize that I will be able to split the element,” Zygrul said, causing the pink fires burning in Hylm’s sockets to go out for a moment, as if she’d blinked in shock.

“That is impossible,” she said, giving Zygrul her full attention. “Elements can’t be split. You know as well as I do that Ployrn spent an entire millennium trying to do just that with every element he could get his hands on.”

“Ployrn’s studies into elements themselves have borne much information, such as the ritual of extraction,” Zygrul agreed. “However, I believe this to be a unique property of the element of Eternity. Consider it for a moment. What happens when you attempt to split an element?”

“The two halves fade away,” Hylm said. “Always. Without exception.”

“Yes. However, the element of Eternity is eternal,” Zygrul countered, thrusting a finger toward Asher’s chest. “I hypothesize that, rather than fading away, the two halves of the Eternity element would retain enough of this property that they would form two elements of Eternity rather than simply dissipating like any other element. Do you understand what that means?”

“It means rather than waiting seven millennia for this plan to complete, we could theoretically all reverse our undeath within the year,” Hylm said, her voice shaking slightly. After a moment, the lich seemed to collect herself, the fires in her eyes burning brighter than ever as she stared at Zygrul. “Why tell me this? Why not go straight to Lethinal?”

“I have my reasons for summoning you here first,” Zygrul said, getting up from his seat and picking up his staff. Wandering over to the fireplace, he stared into the flames, the purple fires in his eye sockets acting as an unnerving echo for the real flames burning before him. This put its back to Asher and its fellow lich, but Zygrul didn’t seem to mind. “For starters, allow me to admit something. I am not actually keeping the phylacteries within this base. I have them in another secure location, where the incredibly powerful curses and protective magics placed upon them won’t be able to hurt anyone who would see the Council harm.”

“What?!” Hylm shouted, all but jumping to her feet as she turned toward Zygrul. She sounded panicked, almost fearful in a way as she seemed to completely forget Asher was even in the room with them. First hearing that she might not have to remain an undead abomination for the next seven thousand years, followed immediately by the knowledge that her own soul had been hidden from her... It was no wonder she appeared so shaken. “You know the rules that were put into place! We were to be kept informed of the precise location of our phylacteries at all times! Lethinal will have your soul for this!”

“Hardly. I intend to vanish from this realm for a while. And so long as nothing happens to the many layers of magic surrounding your phylacteries, none of you will ever find them without me. Not that it would really matter even if you did.”

“We can just disrupt our physical forms, you idiot, we’ll reform right beside them once more!” Hylm all but shouted, still staring at the unnervingly calm lich before her. “You know you can’t hide them from us! What is your end goal here?!”

“Perhaps,” Zygrul said, finally turning around and staring at Hylm. “However, you are the only one who is aware of what I’ve done. And I doubt you’ll get the chance to tell the others.”

Even as it spoke, the purple fires burning in the lich’s eyes darkened, the color draining from them so rapidly that they almost appeared black. Asher started at the sudden realization that Zygrul wasn’t staring at Hylm, but over her shoulder instead. As the very lich that summoned him to this world stared at him, Asher experienced something utterly horrifying he’d never felt before.

His very tritessence being erased from his body.

This wasn’t like the sensation of moving about in the astral realm, where even the slightest of motions cost a person their tritessence. There, it felt as though that energy were being syphoned. As if the astral realm itself had a use for it and was charging for the privilege of moving around in its realm. Whatever Zygrul was doing was completely different. It felt as though his tritessence was being deleted, removed from existence for no greater purpose than Zygrul didn’t want it to exist anymore. It was a truly horrifying sensation, and far scarier than the thought of dying.

Asher wanted to leap back, but the Love artifact pressed into his forehead prevented him from doing so. He was forced to stand there and experience a sizeable chunk of his energy, his tritessence, being wiped clean from his own soul under the lich’s gaze. It all happened so fast that Asher barely even had a chance to gasp, and before he could start to panic, the horrifying sensation stopped as suddenly as it had started.

Staggering back a step, Asher blinked at the sight of the gem that had been on his forehead lying atop the carpet. The gem had clearly fallen off while he was distracted experiencing Zygrul scraping the energy out of his soul. He stared at the gem for a split second in confusion, his Identify making quite clear that whatever skill had once been imbued into the gem by Hylm, it was now completely inert.

Asher snapped out of his shocked trance at the same instant Zygrul finished speaking to Hylm. The fallen queen’s hand shot up to her tiara, no doubt going for another gemstone to throw at the lich who had all but just declared war on the rest of the Council. But her back was still toward Asher, and she hadn’t seemed to realize her skill had been eaten away by Zygrul’s strange power.

Asher wasn’t about to let his chance slip away.

A single use of Distortion was all it took. Compressing the space between him and the fallen queen before she could utilize her Love element again, Asher lashed out with Crescent, his blade traversing the space between them in an instant.

Lopping off the lich’s head and extinguishing the pink flames burning within her skull once and for all.
