Ashton sat down cross-legged, preparing to form his core. Right now, he was essentially trying to make as much skin-contact with himself as he possibly could. It was easier to establish a flow of mana like this, since bouncing mana through the air was deeply inefficient.

He hooked his hands together, and unsummoned the elementals. It was necessary to use as much of his magic as possible for this. Plus, being entirely focused on just his own body instead of having the sensory input from the wisp would also be helpful. There was a reason why people usually closed their eyes for meditation.

And once he did, Ashton took control of his mana. He began to push it around his body, which in itself wasn't all that hard, but he had to be able to build up a fair amount of momentum. Starting at his heart, going down into his stomach, and then through his right leg into his left, back through his stomach and heart and then into his left shoulder, through his fingertips back out of his right shoulder and once again into his heart.

This was a fairly standard flow. There didn't need to be any special techniques to this. Of course, there were plenty of people that believed that with special breathing- or mana-manipulation-techniques they were able to form a superior core, but that just simply wasn't it.

So many people put importance into matters like the position of your core or the way that it was attained, but all of that was largely irrelevant. The position only made a minor difference in a particular few sorts of techniques, or if you were planning on also forming a magic circle at some point like Ashton. Not that this was a common thing to do.

The most important part for core formation was to keep the flow stable and not introduce any impurities in the process. Not that even that really mattered in the end, anyway. Once you had any semblance of a core, it could be further refined and improved. Sure, you started at a slightly lower point, but many folk actually came out more proficient after going through such situations.

Introspection and study of your core and your own mana was probably one of the most important parts of learning how to use techniques properly, and when one had a less-refined or impure core, that introspection was something they could not circumvent anymore.

Not that Ashton needed any of that. All that he needed was the minimal amount of mana in his body and time. He didn't have to do it all at once, either, though it would be much better for him if he did.

Before long, once the basic flow of mana was stable, Ashton could work on speeding the mana up. It had to go as fast as possible while he was still able to keep control of it. There were loads of reasons for this, but there were two that were the most important.

The first was that, as already mentioned, there were plenty of impurities stuck in one's mana that disrupted the stability and pattern. But since Ashton was only controlling the mana itself, this speed was basically a good way to force these impurities to the surface, like they were being dragged out by centrifugal force.

Ashton could already feel himself starting to sweat, and his skin was getting stickier by the second. Sure, most of this wasn't actually a real, physical sensation, but mostly a disruption of the senses due to the concentrated impurities, but that didn't mean it felt any less uncomfortable.

And the second reason why Ashton was making his mana move so fast was that his particular method of forming a core was built around making your mana collide with itself.

In this case, he would, at some point, disrupt the flow of his mana just enough that the flow coming up from his stomach and flowing down from his shoulder collided with each other right in Ashton's heart. Usually, cores were formed in the abdomen, but Ashton had his reasons for choosing to do it within his heart.

While the position of the internal core didn't matter, a magic circle could only be constructed around the heart, since that was the location where all mana eventually coalesced. That was why most monsters had their magic stone somewhere in their heart, too.

Sure, there might be ways to construct magic circles in other places in the body. Ashton knew that there were some mages that tried to place them into their heads, in the hopes of boosting their intellect or something along those lines. But the only one that Ashton actually ever met that even tried something like that had turned into an absolute crazed madman.

Yeah, maybe things were different for humans than hapis, but that wasn't a risk that Ashton was willing to take. Not that he needed to anyway. He might not be the smartest person in the world, but his memory was doing a pretty good job filling the gaps here and there.

Either way, by constructing both his internal core and his magic circle in the same location, Ashton would be able to make them build on each other. They could strengthen each other. Most importantly, it would be much easier to construct his magic circle in the first place.

Ashton would need help wit that anyway, as he only knew the most basic of theory around it. It was another case of him being kept away from the art of magic.

But well, it shouldn't be too hard to figure out. While Ashton was forbidden from learning about it, there would be so many books available that described the process and he could figure it out from there. For someone with magical talents like Ashton's, it should be easy enough.

And for now, the focus was on forming his core.

Ashton was using every ounce of his mana for this. He had recovered plenty while he was talking to Alice and while he was sewing earlier.

After a while, Ashton's mana finally reached the speed he needed it to be at. And so, he slightly changed the trajectories.

As mana hit against mana, it was like sparks flew through Ashton's heart. He could feel a small mote of something that had formed and solidified through this, but that wasn't enough. This would just be dissolved if Ashton let it be.

So, while taking control and stabilising this mote, Ashton once more sped up his mana. Another little while later, it was fast enough to once again collide, this time aiming directly for that tiny something.

Ashton repeated this process again and again, each time depositing more of this strengthened mana onto this proto-core.

But no matter what, Ashton could not lose control of this, or he might actually die.

Currently, this process was technically no different than forcing the formation of a magic stone, just that Ashton was keeping this highly compressed, unstable mana under control so that it wouldn't actually start the crystallization process.

And if he lost that control and the mana suddenly and randomly began to form into a magic stone in the middle of Ashton's already weakened heart, he might not make it out of this.

Not that he had any reason to believe that he wouldn't make it. With every particle of mana that was added to the proto-core, Ashton's confidence increased. The speed at which he was able to form this core was ridiculous.

It was like his soul remembered what it was like to have one, and it wanted him to regain it.

Sure, maybe last time he had a core it was in his abdomen, but what did that matter?

Ashton kept pushing and pushing, forcing himself past the slight muscle-spasms created by the sparks of mana wildly shooting through his body.

It felt like true electricity was coursing through his veins, crackling as it mixed with his blood. This was a truly invigorating feeling. It was a shame that Ashton wouldn't be able to feel it to this degree anytime soon again. After this, he would only be able to slowly grow his core and would need to refine it after continuing, so there would be nothing like this for a while. Maybe never again.

So, Ashton decided to revel in it, and truly enjoy it.

The feeling of mana flowing through him was something that Ashton would have missed for the rest of his life. Though, he could have lived with that if it meant that the world didn't start to end.

"There it is!" Ashton finally whispered with a triumphant grin on his face. His senses were going absolutely wild.

The soft brushing from the wind flowing into the tent felt like someone was trying to scrape his skin off with sandpaper. The distant voices of people standing guard during the night outside sounded like an orchestra of screams. Every moment of cold or warmth felt like being stuck in a frozen tundra or a burning desert.

This was exactly what Ashton was looking for. That moment of hyper-sensitivity that came with the formation of your core. This feeling would fade soon, so he had to act fast.

After all, he was currently able to sense his own mana extremely well, too. And so, he grabbed the proto-core, that mass of concentrated mana, and began to tighten it.

He squeezed down on it as hard as he could while stabilising the pattern. He couldn't allow even the slightest misalignment during this process.

The tightness in Ashton's chest didn't matter. The pain in his shoulders and arms was irrelevant.

Ashton couldn't let this fail, or else his mana would be so disrupted he would be useless for days, even if he did somehow survive the magic stone that would form in his heart.

As Ashton felt the heat rise up his throat, he took the proto-core, and shifted its frequency and its pattern just ever so slightly.

And then, like it was all an illusion to begin with, the painful sensitivity was gone.

That didn't mean that Ashton's senses weren't still raw and hyperactive right now, but it simply wasn't as painful as before.

Slowly, Ashton stood up. His legs were weak and unsteady. He walked out of the tent, a broad grin on his face as his cloak dropped to the ground behind him.

He felt himself step on a stone, and slowly picked it up. The weight and shape and texture was being ingrained in his palm, as an energy welled up in Ashton's chest.

He pulled his arm back, and threw the stone as he pushed out that energy. But this time, compared to what Ashton did out of anger in the gym earlier, it was easy. It felt right.

The mana that was shifted by his core allowed Ashton to use a technique. And the moment that Ashton's fingers split from the stone that had practically molten into them, a blast of air flowed up his arm and down his body, rustling the blades of grass on the ground he stood on.

It didn't create a violent loud sound as it did before. This was soft. It was controlled—intentional.

Ashton didn't know where the stone landed exactly, but he knew that–

[You have killed a [Hobgoblin]. For your valiant effort, you are awarded 18 Coins]

The message appeared and confused Ashton for a moment, but he couldn't help but laugh.

"I'll just take that as the universe telling me I'm doing a great job."
