The message was lost in an endless stream. Even he didn’t have time to read it, let alone anyone else. Pursing his lips, Gary tried to follow the chat.

Five minutes later, he gave up. There was no useful information whatsoever, only people grieving their planet or warning about monsters.

“So everyone reasonable just ignores the chat… Figures,” he muttered, closing it off. At least it had slowed down somewhat—Gary hoped it was due to people being busy rather than dead.

“Whatever. I’m a proud gamer. I’ll figure it out myself—then maybe write a guide.”

Gary paced through the forest, his pencil at the ready and casting wary glances all around. The trees were sparse, but their foliages were dense. Anything could be hiding in there.

A bush rustled. Gary jumped in place and aimed a shaking pencil at it, only for the rustling to die down and nothing to happen. He walked backward until he’d put some distance between them, then resumed walking.

“Can you even be a weapon?” he asked his pencil. “You’re sharp, sure, but it’s just graphite. How deep can you penetrate? Can you do more damage than my fist?”

He paused.

“How did I become like this?”

Pat Rick #09874: WE’RE ALL GOING TO DIE!

That Pat person had discovered the chat later than Gary, but he was spamming non-stop. He wasn’t the only one, either. At this point, the floor chat could well be called the destructionist lobby.

“It can’t all be trash,” he muttered. With a few mental maneuvers, he set the chat to the side of his vision and made it half-transparent. Focusing on the rolling messages made him dizzy, but he pressed on, splitting his focus between watching his surroundings and the chat.

There were two things he was looking for. Other people close to his location, or information on how to use the stagnant energy in his body. He didn’t have high hopes—with the chat scrolling as it was, no sane person would bother typing.

Mary Pheebs #98734: I THINK MY MOTHER IS DEAD. SHE’S SPEAKING TO ME!

Pat Rick #09874: WE’RE ALL GOING TO DIE!

Torga Ba #19023: There are stronger monsters. Careful.

Angus Trail #26441: YOU’RE TAKING ME OUT, OF THE ORDINARY!

Gary appreciated people like that Torga Ba, who found it in them to share useful information. He redoubled his attention on his surroundings. Finding allies was a priority, but not dying was even more so.

When the next rustling came, he was ready. He turned around with pencil in hand.

Disabled Orc Warrior, Level 2

This guy would have flourished in a more understanding society. He’s green, he’s mean, he’s nasty, but he makes a great lemon pie. Too bad orc culture frowns on men who adopt house hobbies. It’s a shame, really.

Anyway, uh, have fun killing him.

A lore-accurate orc walked out of the bushes. It stood six feet tall, about the same as Gary, and sported a mean set of tusks. Its skin was green and filled with tribal tattoos—including one of a cauldron. Seeing the tattoos was easy because it was shirtless, displaying sculpted muscles. More importantly, the orc wielded a bulky serrated sword as long as its torso.

Gary looked down at his pencil, suddenly feeling stupid.

The orc growled. Then, it hopped menacingly towards Gary on its single leg.

“You have got to be kidding me,” Gary said, edging backward. The orc wasn’t fast. It was strong and held a big sword, sure, but he could outpace it at a comfortable jogging speed.

“Hey, I don’t want to fight you!” he said.

“Coward!” the orc growled. “You are a man! Men don’t run!”

It swung its sword, which cut harmlessly through the air in front of Gary. “No, seriously, you can just go! Resist your society’s toxic masculinity!” Gary insisted, but the orc was having none of it. It accelerated slightly. Its single leather boot stomped against the grass. Its heavy breathing was the only thing reaching Gary.

“You shame us both!” the orc growled.

“What exactly am I supposed to do?” Gary asked aloud. The orc swung a bit too wide—it almost lost its balance and had to make small hops to stay upright. In the meantime, Gary had turned around and was running the other way. The orc growled in outrage.

“Come here and face me, coward! For honor!”

“Give it a rest, man! Catch you another day!” Gary shouted. He kept going until the orc was nowhere to be seen, then stopped to lean against a tree. “I really should exercise more…” he told himself, panting.

He cupped his chin.

“I couldn’t have done it…could I? I mean, killing a cripple, it really wouldn’t sit right, especially with that sob-story description. And what was I supposed to do, stab it through the throat with the pencil? Punch it to death? Assuming it didn’t get a hold of me, in which case I’d be a goner. I couldn’t even take its weapon, because it disappears as soon as I…”

He trailed off.

“Oh, damn.”

Gary ran back where he came from. “Hey, orc! Orc! Come here, I’ll fight you!” he shouted through the trees. A roar answered him from the near distance. Turning that way, Gary soon found the one-legged orc hopping menacingly his way. Its eyes held unbridled fury.

“Excellent! You chose honor! Come, and let one of us die as a warrior!”

“We’ll see. You actually scare the shit out of me, you know.”

“I am very intimidating!”

“Not you specifically, just the fact that you exist. If you’re here, then so are your able-bodied friends, and I doubt I can outrun them,” Gary said, settling against the orc. He held the pencil tightly. His breath quickened. He was suddenly terrified and on high adrenaline. He could do anything.

The orc’s eyes widened. “For this insult, I will have your life!”

It lunged, and Gary calmly stepped back, dodging a slash. The sword whooshed before his face. He then darted sideways, trying to circle the orc, which was delayed by the need to recover from its swing. Gary arrived behind its back and pushed.

As the orc collapsed, Gary was left stunned. That had been real skin. Real flesh. Strong, corded muscles. If that thing got him, he would die, and if he hurt it, it would die.

The orc rolled onto its back and roared, spitting farther than its sword could reach. Gary stayed out of the range of both, snapping into focus and realizing his chance was gone. He waited. The orc grew tired of swinging at the air and returned to its foot in a display of surprising agility. It lunged again.

Gary stepped back. The orc followed, hopping aggressively with its sword swinging. Gary tried keeping it at a distance, but the orc suddenly lunged deeper, barely missing him. In its haste, it lost its balance but still slashed at Gary as he darted to the side. He ducked under the swing.

He realized he had no idea how to fight. He’d almost died twice just now. He was one mistake away from the end.

What am I doing? I can’t beat it like this!

Panic took over. He followed the momentum of his previous dodge to escape, but his haste led to a mistake, and he tripped. He expected the sword to cut into his back. It never did. Turning around, he found the sword lodged in a tree, with the orc off-balance and hanging from its handle to keep itself from falling. It would recover any time now.

Gary acted before thinking. He shot up and stabbed his pencil into the back of the orc’s hand.

Blood shot out. The sensation traveled along the body of the pencil and made Gary nauseous. The orc screamed and let go of the weapon, tumbling to the ground. Gary found himself still holding onto the pencil—his grip had been so tight it slid out of the wound as the orc fell.

The sword remained stuck to the tree, right next to Gary. He eyed it for what must have been a split-second but felt like an hour. He grabbed the handle with both hands and pulled. It resisted. He sawed it from side to side, trying to get the blade out of the wood. It wasn’t working.

The orc roared and crawled towards Gary. Its hand left drops of crimson blood on the ground. Gary roared and finally pried off the sword, taken off balance by its weight but managing to stay on his feet. He hugged the weapon and ran away.

“No!” the orc roared behind him, but Gary was completely out of reach. He turned around and held the sword aloft—it was heavy, but he could handle it.

“Sorry!” he said. “I’ll pay you back! This is a good chance to follow your dreams, right?”

“I’ll skin you alive and wear your family’s ears as my necklace!”

He’d never seen someone so angry as the orc at that moment. Its lips shivered as it rose onto its single foot and stomped forward so rapidly it fell again. When it roared, the trees shook.

“Bring it back!” it roared.

Gary turned and ran. He grabbed the sword from the back to make it easier, put the pencil in his pocket, and kept going until the orc’s cries were just another sound of the forest. He plopped down on an old log.

Gary Steel #00301: Guys, you can steal weapons from the monsters! If you don’t kill them, they don’t disappear!

He watched the message get instantly buried. Maybe it would help someone.

The sword was heavy. Gary raised it high—its handle was made of wood and tanned with unknown leather, while its blade was long, serrated, and bulky. It was a weapon less meant to slash and more to shatter bones. Not the best for Gary, whose last gym session had been months ago, but one hell of a lot better than the pencil.

The thought of holding a sword—a sword he’d earned—somehow made him smile. “Now we’re getting somewhere!”

A hunter prowled through the woods. A man in dirty clothes, wrapped in vines which supported a heavy weapon behind his shoulder. A barbaric sword—blade jagged and serrated, sharp ends sticking out like the waves of a lethal sea. The back of the blade was thick, adding weight to every strike, while the leather on the handle was coarse, worn out over the course of many battles.

The hunter stopped and listened. He climbed on a branch. He waited.

Soon, a new form crested the bushes. Four hooves pressed on the dirt, intense by the weight they carried. The sunlight cascaded through the leaves to land on a boar wearing its skeleton outside its body as armor. It walked slowly, aimlessly even, without paying the slightest bit of attention to its surroundings.

Boared, Level 2

These guys think they’re all cool and sophisticated. Oh, look at me, I’m smart enough to get bored of eating grass but not smart enough to find something else to do, boohoo.

Anyway, boareds are easy pickings. They won’t even try to see you coming so hit them hard and fast. Just, uh, try to finish it in one strike, yeah?

The hunter fell from the branch. The sword came down hard, cracking onto the boared’s skull—more by luck than skill.

The sound was horrible.

As Gary tumbled onto the grass and prepared to run, the boared collapsed into a heap. A huge wave of relief washed over him, but he knew it wasn’t over. The corpse flickered like a mirage—or rather, the air over it did. Only then did he relax.

Slowly, the air’s flickering went away. A bit of it turned into invisible energy that seeped into Gary’s body. The rest just…disappeared. But he knew there was more, because the amount he received was so abysmally tiny it couldn’t possibly be everything.

That was par for the course though. What he really wanted to see was…

As the energy infusion finished, Gary held his breath and opened his status screen.

Gary Steel #00301, Level 0

Floor: 1

Strength: 3

Speed: 3

Vitality: 3

Fortitude: 4

Wisdom: 4

Advance your cultivation to access more System functions.

“Yes!” he exclaimed. “I got one Strength and Vitality!”

He cheered and did a little hop, then looked around for something to test his strength on. A big rock lay at a tree’s roots, reaching up to his calves. He grabbed it with both hands, squared his waist, and lifted. It came up easier than he thought.

“Alright!” he yelled, letting the rock thud to the floor. He pumped a fist. “I’m strong now! Let’s fucking go!”

A few moments of celebrating later, he reopened the screen.

“Still Level 0 though. Ugh. Just level up already!”

Unfortunately, the universe wasn’t in the habit of listening to mortals. Gary kicked at the dirt, then realized the boared corpse hadn’t dissipated yet. His work on it was evident. The creature’s skull had caved in at the top, and odd purple liquid mixed with blood flowed from its nose.

But he’d gotten the energy.

“Oh,” he said, covering his mouth. “Oh shit. Shit. Is it not dead?”

The body dissolved moments later, leaving Gary a relieved, terrified heap on the forest floor.

“I’m so not built for this…”

Killing this monster and that squirrel hadn’t leveled him up yet, but it gave him something. That had to be the clue. A small part of their energy spread across his body. The problem was, he had no idea what to do with it.

“I’m going to need some water, too…” he thought, flinching at his dry throat.

He sat on the grass, allowing himself a moment of reprieve after a battle well fought. His gaze flickered to the ever-scrolling floor chat. A hundred messages rushed past every second.

“How do they find the energy for this?” he wondered.

Suddenly, between all the destructionist spiel, his eye caught something.

Torga Ba #19023: There are other people here. Powerful ones. I ran into one of them, who shattered the monsters like he was some sort of superhero. He warned me to stay away from people like him. Just passing the message.

Torga Ba… Whoever you are, you’re doing God’s work.

Garry Steel #00301: Thanks for the tip, Torga!

It was the same guy who’d messaged about the existence of stronger monsters earlier. There could be more messages by him, or more people saying the same things, but Gary couldn’t possibly read the whole chat at the rate it was refreshing. Torga would probably miss Gary’s message, too, but that was okay.

“Other people, huh?” he wondered. “Maybe they’ll know what to do with this energy. Getting stronger is good, but most of it just sits there and doesn’t level me up. There has to be something I can do with it.”

Garry Steel #00301: Has anyone figured out what to do with the monster energy yet?

He watched as intensely as he could for a few minutes, but no response came his way. Either nobody knew, they weren’t talking, or he’d just missed the message. He shrugged and got to his feet.

“Water and food it is. If I can hunt anything else on the way, I do, and if anything strong shows up, I run. Or I try to.”

The highest-level monster he’d met so far had been level 2, but he’d caught mentions of level 5s and above in the chat. Given the difference between level 1 and level 2 monsters, a level 5 would insta-kill him. Monsters aside, even something as mundane as a bear could spell his unavoidable doom.

Gary felt very conscious of his mortality.

“Then again, maybe bears count as monsters too?” he wondered, pacing deeper into the forest. He didn’t actually have any idea how to find water. He just hoped to get lucky.

Not too long afterward, a new sound reached his ears. It wasn’t that of a stream. Instead, it sounded like rhythmic, slow creaking, as if someone was rolling an old wooden wheel across the forest floor.

“People!” Gary exclaimed, and rushed head-first towards the sound.

Gary rushed through the woods. The creaking persisted, haunting and ominous, replacing all the other sounds that had somehow disappeared.

It took Gary a moment to realize something was off. By the time he did, the creaking had grown louder, and he dove into a bush head-first—only to slam into a thin wooden branch inside it.

“Ow,” he whispered, crawling deeper into the bush. The thorns and scrapes were nothing before the intense fear he now experienced.

Something was wrong with this creaking. Very wrong. Hiding in the bush, Gary struggled to calm his drumming heart, which beat of its own accord. The air vibrated to an unheard tune—a tune of horror.
