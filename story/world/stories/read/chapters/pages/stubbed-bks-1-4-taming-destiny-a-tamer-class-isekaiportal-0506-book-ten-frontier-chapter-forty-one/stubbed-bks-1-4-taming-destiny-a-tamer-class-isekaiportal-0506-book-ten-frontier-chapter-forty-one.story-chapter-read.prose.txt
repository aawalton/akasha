“Sor’az!” I greet as the stocky damayar captain steps through the door. “Well met,” I say, standing up and approaching him, my fist outstretched. He presses his own – larger – fist to mine in a greeting that isn’t quite a fist bump, but is more similar to that than a handshake. “How have the winds and tides treated you?” I eye the obvious signs of wear on his clothes. “With some roughness, I see.”

Sor’az chuckles good-naturedly and exchanges stiffer nods with the other lords before returning his attention to me.

“Ah, t’ sea’s a harsh mistress as always. No need te fret. Say, d’ye have a drink? Me throat is dry as a bone.”

I smile at him.

“As long as you don’t mind fermented milk.”

His expression twists into a bit of a grimace.

“Tha’s more a drink for t’ Netheyar. No tulis?” I’ve learned that tulis is a strong alcohol equivalent to vodka, created from underground tubers very similar to potatoes.

“Not yet,” I tell him apologetically. “But hopefully we’ll start production of that soon. We have tila instead, if you’d prefer. Or water.” That’s not infested with parasites now. I don’t mention that bit.

“Bah, been drinkin’ water fer tendays,” Sor’az grumbles. “Give me t’ milk then.”

I gesture towards an empty chair in the room filled with comfortable chairs and chaise longues. He eyes the cushioning suspiciously, then settles into it with a groan. “Ah, tha’s good fer me old bones, tha’ is.”

I hand him a mug of the milk, then wait impatiently for him to quaff a good part of it. I send a quick look at Valence when I hear him take in a breath as if to speak. He closes his mouth and we wait in silence for Sor’az to finish.

“So, how was the journey?” I ask as soon as he’s lowered the drink.

“We all made it back in one piece,” he says first. I feel a small release of the tension in my shoulders – I’ve seen enough of this continent to know that that wasn't at all guaranteed. “Though t’ ship needs a good bit o’ repair.”

“You didn’t get caught in the manastorm a couple of tendays ago, did you?” I ask anxiously.

“No, thank t’ gods,” Sor’az replies. “Saw it from a distance. But t’ sea beasts were more’n a little agitated afterwards. More attacks in t’ last few tenday than t’ last two afore tha’.”

My eyes flick over his form, noticing every line of fatigue that creases his usually smooth skin, the tiredness that seems to weigh down his limbs. I didn’t really need his words about it having been a tough few days – I can see it.

“Well, you’re here now – you can take a break in the city.”

Sor’az hums noncommittedly.

“What?” I ask him.

“We’ll be fine on t’ ship, ah’d say,” he replies with an unusual neutrality to his voice.

I frown at him, exchanging a glance with the other lords as he doesn’t elaborate.

“I believe Sor’az may be concerned about the reception of the damayar,” Josiah suggests tentatively, his expression troubled. “They are not traditionally…well-received.”

I want to exclaim at how ridiculous that is – particularly here when we’re all working towards the same ends. But I hold myself back – I know enough to be confident that railing at my allies is not going to help the situation in any way. And Sor’az’s own story is a testament to just how prejudiced people can be against them.

So, instead, I clear my voice of as much emotion as I can as I speak.

“Well, that won’t happen here,” I say firmly. “If any of your crew have any problems, you come directly to Loran, Sarran, or me about them.”

“An’ you’ll believe them when it’s their word agains’ one o’ the locals’?”

“Absolutely,” I agree immediately. “Their word isn’t worth any less because they’re a different species.” Sor’az gives me a long, searching look, clearly wanting to believe me, but not certain he should. So I grin at him. “Anyway, I have two telepathic leopards who can quickly determine who’s lying if there's a contradiction. Well, three, technically, though I doubt he’d cooperate.”

“T’ree?” Sor’az asks, his sharp eyes taking in the subtle grimaces that pass over the other lords’ expressions. “Sounds like a story.”

“It is,” I chuckle ruefully. “But let’s get to that later. I’m far more interested in your story of the last few tendays.”

Sor’az sighs gustily.

“Aye, ah thought ye might be.” He pulls the side table that’s next to his chair around so it’s now in front of him, then, with a sigh, pulls a rolled-up scroll out of his pouch. Unrolling it, there’s a beautifully-drawn map.

I lean forward to see the details, aware of the other lords shifting around me to see it more clearly as well.

The map is so much better drawn than the one the merchants gave us – this almost looks like an illustrated picture.

I see the coast that stretches up in a north-easterly direction – the compass drawn in the corner of the map makes that clear. On a side note, I find the very similar development of a compass between Earth and Moriax to be fascinating, even if the Moriaxan one apparently works on Energy – of course – rather than magnetism.

Returning my attention to the map, I see how the cliffs that we have near us reduce as the land goes further north, implying that the land itself is lower – the mountains that form our horizon aren’t even visible on the map. The forest doesn’t seem to be quite so dense – close to shore there are several sections which look more like plains, though the treeline to their west is still visible. The land looks fertile and well-irrigated – there are several rivers. I also notice something else that piques my interest.

“Are those settlements?” I ask, pointing at the two beautifully-drawn ports – I can even see that they’re undergoing construction making me almost certain that I know who they belong to. Roughly speaking, anyway.

“Aye,” he agrees, then pulls out several other – smaller – pieces of parchment. He unrolls them and lays them near the settlements they obviously belong to.

There are two flags near the closest settlement – which is still as far away as the Goldmine settlement is from us. One of the flags, a brown flag with a mountain in the background and a faceted gemstone in the foreground, is one I recognise.

“House Deepdelve,” I murmur, a little surprised to see them here. I would have thought they’d need the funds to rebuild the damaged sections of Zlona after the beast wave engineered by Torrent Senior attacked it. Then again, maybe they hope to gain wealth through the competition that they can then use to propel themselves to new heights. “I don’t recognise the ones they’re allied with.”

“House Waterwhisperer,” Elarion informs me. “They rule Southpoint in Crownseat.”

“Waterwhisperer…that’s a bit of a mouthful,” I comment, raising an eyebrow.

He shrugs elegantly.

“They are known for being capable of communing with the water around them, so it is appropriate.”

“Sounds like Water-Shaping to me.”

“It’s not,” Josiah contributes. “I’ve heard that they speak with the spirits in the water rather than controlling the water directly. Certainly, the one time I visited it with my father, their Skills felt…odd.”

I hum in response, my eyes drifting up to the next settlement. The fiery orange of the flag would be clue enough even without the device embossed on it in charcoal black: a shield with a flame in one upper quadrant, a flaming sword in the other, and a phoenix triumphantly spreading its wings in the lower section.

“Flameform,” Elarion murmurs, and Layton and Josiah immediately follow his gaze with sounds of interest. “Jareon made it to the continent. I have to admit, I’m impressed.”

I understand his implications – as dedicated to fire as Jareon is, he can’t have found crossing the ocean to be an easy prospect. Just like Kalanthia disliked coming out of my tattoo too often due to how far away the earth was from her feet, Jareon would have found himself significantly suppressed in such a humid environment.

“No doubt House Tidehunter helped,” Layton points out, tapping the pale blue flag next to Jareon’s, one that shows a stylised river crossed with a spear.

Elarion makes an impatient gesture.

“Their speciality is hardly ocean voyages,” he counters. “The only boats they are known to produce are ones only suitable for a river mouth.”

“Where are House Tidehunter based?” I ask, as this is another House I can’t quite place.

“In Griffgain,” Elarion answers. “Icemeet territory – where the marshes meet the ocean.”

I eye the land they’ve settled dubiously.

“They’re a bit out of their element there, then. I don’t see any marshes in that area.”

“That’s probably why they’ve settled the river mouth,” Josiah points out.

I hum in agreement, then lean back with a frown. Jareon, Elarion, Elyra, and myself…there’s still one Great House heir unaccounted for.

“Did Kyrian turn back to Moriax?” I wonder, then flick a glance at Sor’az. “You didn’t see any trace of them – or of House Bladestrike or Siren?”

He shakes his head.

The lack of Kyrian’s presence makes me a little uneasy. I don’t know where I stand with him – whether he’s seeking revenge or has learned an important lesson. For all I know, he could be dead.

If they all got killed by a bunch of sea monsters because I took so much from their ships, does it make it my fault? I can’t help wondering. Then I shake the idea out of my head. I didn’t ask Kyrian to attack us. I even gave him the option to walk – sail – away. If he pressed on, as weak as his party was after our confrontation, then the consequences are on his head. Not mine.

Intentionally shifting my focus onto something more worth it than that particular Great House heir, I look around at my allies.

“So, should we invite these others to join us for the tournament?”

“Is there any reason not to?” Valence asks. He’s been rather quiet so far – watching and listening more than contributing. “We are surely going to make contact with them later. If they find out that we excluded them from the initial diplomatic outreach, they would surely feel offended.”

“That’s a good point,” Josiah agrees, “though there’s a question of logistics. If Captain Sor’az is delivering the invitations to those in the north, he can’t deliver them to those in the south – or investigate that unknown party.”

Sor’az shifts restlessly, the lines of tiredness in his face deepening.

“After you’ve had a break and been able to repair your ship, of course,” I hurriedly interject. “And only if you are willing to do so.”

“If we’ve had time to recover then me crew an’ ah would be willin’,” he agrees with a hint of something that isn’t quite relief in his voice.

“That still leaves the question of how we deliver all the invitations in time,” Josiah interjects. “Elarion, you were so determined that the tournament should happen within the next two months, but it will take Captain Sor’az at least a tenday to go north–”

“If t’ weather’s fair. If not, add another few days te reach t’ furthest settlement,” Sor’az interjects.

Josiah nods at him.

“There you go. And then he has to come back down here. And then he has to go all the way to the south – it’ll take two months to do the roundtrip!”

“Then we send one of our ships south,” Elarion replies with a shrug. “Or are none of the captains capable of sailing alone?”

“That’s not the problem – we need all our ships for food production right now, unless your farms are ready to take more of the burden of feeding our population,” Josiah replies a touch hotly.

“There’s another option,” I break in just as Elarion opens his mouth. “Sor’az can go north; my companions can go south.”

That brings a thoughtful silence to the room.

“You’re thinking about your flying companions, I take it?” Layton asks a moment later. I nod in response.

“It might work,” Elarion comments thoughtfully. “If you send the ones capable of conversing, at least.”

“There will have to be something about them that indicates immediately that they are tamed beasts – or they will likely be shot down before they can get close,” Valence muses, making a very good point. My fingers clench at the thought of that happening – and so far from home. Maybe this isn’t a good idea.

“Perhaps a chestpiece with the Titanbend crest,” Josiah suggests. “Everyone knows about Titanbends and beasts.”

“It’s a fair point and a good solution,” I agree, pushing my doubts away. I have Bond Teleport – if something happens, they can signal needing help through the Bond and I’ll pull them to me. “I’ll need to make sure they approach in the daytime so the crest is easily seen. And perhaps the enchanters can imbue them with protective enchantments to ward off any accidental attack.” If Noir is willing to go, then I might be able to equip him with a couple of Skills that would help him.

“And what of the unknown flag far to the south?” Josiah presses.

“Captain Sor’az could perhaps sail that direction after returning from the north,” Layton suggests. “It’s not like that is as urgent.”

“That we know of,” Josiah returns darkly. “And if he has to go south again, why not send him south to begin with?”

“Not all of the settlements the merchants highlighted are on the coast,” Valence points out. “How is he supposed to get the message to Stagrun? Or Woodfell?”

I send Sor’az an apologetic grimace, feeling that the discussion is taking place without any space for his input – and he’s the one it involves.

He sends me an eye roll, then lifts his voice.

“Ah’ll go north,” he declares. “An’ when ah’m done, ah’ll return south te scout out tha’ settlement. After any repairs o’ course.”

“Of course,” I agree in the silence, making it clear that I support his decision. “Alright, it looks like we have a plan. Sor’az, rest up – take the time you need, but the sooner you can leave, the better.” I give him an apologetic smile that he returns with a huffing sigh that is only half serious.

I turn to look at the other lords. Josiah looks a little put-out. “Let’s write the invitations together – I’m sure you know better than I how to speak to each of these Houses,” I offer as an olive branch. “Then I’ll speak to my companions and get the letters sent off to those in the south.”

For a moment I wonder if we’re maybe biting off a bit more than we can chew – it’s only been a tenday since we were starving and fighting off waves of beasts, and yet now we’re planning to host a diplomatic event with four Great Houses, and seven Lesser Houses?

Then I push the thought away. Fortune favours the bold, and the sooner we connect with the other Moriaxar settlers, the better.

Whether they leave the tournament as allies or enemies is another question. But if I can get them all in the same place, perhaps I can convince them that surviving the threats of the Lost Continent is more important than fighting each other for the Moriaxar crown.
