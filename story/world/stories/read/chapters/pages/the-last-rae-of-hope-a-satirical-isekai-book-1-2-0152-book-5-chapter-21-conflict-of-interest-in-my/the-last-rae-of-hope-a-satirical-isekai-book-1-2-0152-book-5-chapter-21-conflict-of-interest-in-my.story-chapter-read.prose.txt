Father Baram, his eyes haunted, muttered to himself, “I see nothing, nothing! I know nothing! I was never even here!”

After a stiff heel turn, he fled, his dirty robes fluttering in the chilly evening breeze.

Olethros watched him for a moment, then gingerly picked up the blessed stick he left behind. The instant his fingers touched it, his eyes widened.

“This..!”

Nora grinned broadly as she locked eyes with him. “Right?!”

“What? What’s going on?” I asked, my nerves tightening.

He stared at the stick. “It’s a blessing that’s compatible with animus… From a priest?”

Nora’s head bobbed up and down. “But wait, there’s more!”

She raised her staff, and I felt the gentle rush of healing.

It’s not like that was necessary…

At best, I only had a few lingering bug bites from the jungle.

“See? It works to bolster amity, too! It’s like… the complete magic package!”

For some reason, Olethros was looking at me instead of her. “Fascinating.”

It’s cheating!

“Wait until I tell the others!” she crowed. “Blessings for everyone!”

***

Chester greeted us once again upon our return to Aziza’s Bastion, and a silent yet profound exchange took place between him and Tetora.

“Kids will be kids,” Tetora said with a shrug, folding his arms.

Chester stared at him for a few moments, eyes unblinking.

“Well… What do you want me to do about it?”

With an angry swish of his now-poofy tail, Chester led him off through a portal. Tetora glanced back at us once before disappearing, his ears flat in defeat.

“I’ll start with your shield and sword,” Nora suddenly exclaimed, skipping the part about consent. “And then Tetora’s claws… Oh, but I’m not sure I’d trust Beigaldi with it. Galenus might need a buff, though. He gets tired with transporting all the time. And of course, your staff, Olethros—”

“I would prefer to study the stick first, thank you,” Olethros demurred.

“Okay, sure.” She turned to me. “Let’s go to your room!”

“Actually, can this wait until tomorrow? I’m a little tired right now.”

For just a moment, Nora looked like I slapped her across the face. She recovered quickly, however, and started bouncing on the balls of her feet. “Tomorrow then!”

Then, to ice the cake, she grabbed Olethros by the wrist and pulled him through a different portal, leaving me to stand there all alone.

Well, I never!

I threw myself through the opposite portal mirror, ending up in my old illusory bedroom. The moment my eyes landed on my overstuffed pillow, however, I knew what I had to do.

Unfortunately, its seams were practically indestructible, and I learned “beating the stuffing” out of something is sometimes harder than it seems.

If you’re asking, “Why is Rae so mad?” then know you’re not alone.

“I don’t understand!” I shouted into the pillow.

I should be grateful for this discovery! There’s a higher hierarchy above Euphridia that even affects the world here!

The hierarchy belonging to that crazy old man.

“May I come in?” Olethros asked from nowhere.

“Aren’t you already here?” I countered.

“Yes and no. I wouldn’t dare fully intrude on a private reckoning.”

“I’m not having a—” I stopped, then exhaled hard through my nostrils. “Fine, I am. I don’t know how it’s going to turn out, though.”

“That means you’re doing it correctly,” he replied.

I rolled onto my back and looked up at the ceiling. “You can come in if you want.”

He appeared with a flicker, taking a seat on my beanbag chair.

“I think I’m jealous,” I admitted, sitting up on the bed and crossing my legs. “Not a good look for a hero…”

Maybe that alone means I’m not a hero at all.

“Go on,” he urged gently. “I think you’re touching the edges of it.”

With a wobbly inhale, I admitted, “She can heal and bless… She can use animus and amity, and now apparently something in between. And she gets a magic phone? But all I can do is—” I stopped. “Keep working hard just to get back to where I used to be. How fair is that? Except I’m being petty.”

“Petty?”

“I’m mad… That I’m stuck playing by the rules. I want to cheat, too. But I can’t!”

He clasped his hands together. “Why not?”

“Because… The rules… If I don’t follow the rules…”

“Which rules?”

“What do you mean, which rules? The rules here!”

“You mean Euphridia’s rules?”

The name landed dull and heavy.

Olethros trained one eye on me. “I have a suspicion that you fear letting her go.”

I flinched, but he continued, saying, “I would be afraid, too, if she had ever included me in a way that made me feel welcome. Even if what she asked of me was unfair. Wanting to be loved is a powerful desire.”

Tears trickled down my cheeks. “I’m so sorry…”

“You don’t need to apologize. You’re going out of your way to rectify it. Back to your fear—it’s worth exploring.”

Explore my fear?

Of letting Euphridia go?

“I should be all gung-ho about it, but… Well…” I glanced at Will and Faith. “I’m literally staring at the consequences, aren’t I? Those were forged by Euphridia herself. If I let her go… Will they stop working? Will what little power I have going for me just… stop? I’ll be abandoning everyone and everything…”

And she’ll learn nothing.

Olethros remained silent, which I took as confirmation that I was on the right path.

“I’ve met a few gods… And frankly—frankly...” I swallowed hard. “I remain unimpressed with any of them. I mean, my goodness, I think Mother would make a better god than some of them, if for nothing more than her personality. How foolish does that sound?”

“You’re not foolish, you’re discerning.”

“Discerning?”

He nodded, settling into the beanbag chair as if he’d done it a thousand times before. “You demand certain qualities in your new God.”

“New God? I don’t think I can shop around, you know.”

“Are you certain of that? Nora did not seem particularly pious in her blessing request, so I’d suspect any of your requests to the Grandfather of Gods would be just as readily approved.”

President Abrams…

It was probably the safe, contractual bet. But still, I couldn’t see him as the God I wanted for me.

For a moment, I considered asking Clare, but the amount of paperwork that would likely be involved nearly floored me.

“I’m not going to solve this tonight…” I rolled over with a loud yawn. “I think I’d like to sleep now.”

“Pleasant dreams, Rachel,” he replied as he faded away.

***

The sharp smell of antiseptic hit my nose. Everything was a bit blurry, but the steady beep of a nearby monitor told me I was still in the world of hospital white. Mother was asleep in a chair nearby, and at the foot of the bed stood a woman I had never seen before.

She wore black, comfortable pants and a fuzzy purple jacket. She was older, with prominent laugh lines even when her lips were still. Her hair was a dusty rose, naturally streaked with silver platinum, its abundance pulled back into a low, loose ponytail.

“Oh, good,” she said as she turned. “You’re awake.”

“Who—” I croaked, my throat uncomfortably dry.

“A friend of the family,” she said, pointing to Mother. “You can call me Rhea.”

She stepped closer, her aura warm and unmistakably pink as she smiled.

“I heard my granddaughter has been giving you quite a bit of trouble, dear,” she said, clucking her tongue. “I’ll keep her and that husband of mine out of your hair for as long as I can. Speaking of which…” she patted my head gently. “I love that color. It complements your eyes.”

I tried to sit up, but I was too weak to push up off the soft bedding.

“If they ever give you any trouble, you just call for Ol’ Rhea Titanis. Understand?”

“Rhea…. Titanis…?”

“Yes, dear. Abe’s got his heart in the right place, but he doesn’t know how to follow through properly. And she’s got him wrapped around his finger thrice! But if you ask me for help, well, dear, I’ll make sure you get it. No strings attached.”

“Strings…”

I looked at my limbs covered in wires.

“You’ve got a sense of humor! You’ll do just fine, dear, just fine.”

Mother started to stir, and Rhea let out a chuckle. “There’s my bitter dark troll, looking as striking as ever! But honey, sleeping slumped like that isn’t good for your spine. I’ll make sure they get you a proper bed.”

Mother stood straight up, arms rigid at her sides. “Chairwoman Rhea?!”

Rhea just laughed and patted her on the shoulder. “Relax, dear, relax. Personal visit, not professional. But could you tell me when the next acquisition meeting is? I forgot to mark it on my calendar again.”

***

“Walk me through it!” I shouted the moment I saw Nora.

She dropped Tetora’s steel claws and threw her hands behind her back. “Rae! Uh… I was just—”

“Go on, go on,” I said, handing her back the claws. “Tell me what you think about when you bless something!”

She looked down at the ground. “It’s a little… I mean…” She took a deep breath. “You know I can be a little crass, right? That’s because I’m sort of proud of the fact that’s the way I am. So, I lean into that. Sure, I make demands, but I’m… I’m making those demands with my ideal self. Rae, I like that I’m a loudmouth. And I want my patron to accept that I’m going to be that way, even if others think it's a flaw.”

So, I should ask for help with what I truly want to do, while being my ideal self, and offering up something in exchange.

Even though it might be good for me, I didn’t want to let Euphridia go completely.

Because she might go off and cause the same trouble for someone else.

I gathered Will and Faith in my hands.

You two okay with this?

They both glowed their assent.

Okay, Rhea Titanis.You probably know what I’m planning to do here in Speranza. And about Relias. And I don’t really care about forgiveness for it—he had his chance. But I think there’s just a bit more work for me even after that. So, will you help me teach her a lesson? I’ll even throw in a free one for your husband. I won’t be satisfied unless they both grow from this.

My sword, shield, and I all glowed a very bright—

“Pink!” Nora gasped in awe. “Rae, you’re… um…!”

“Pink,” I agreed with a tight smile. “Even matches my hair, wouldn’t you say?”
