I wasn’t nearly confident enough in my spearmanship to engage a woman with a weapon made of her own magic. Rather than continue my descent, I pulled back the spear, and held a hand out. A burst of flame ignited the area in front of me, generating enough lift to slow my fall.

When aimed straight down, my fire magic was powerful enough to create a sort of pseudo-flight.

A horn echoed in the distance. I didn’t have enough time to give it more than a thought before Freyna burst through my flames. I yelped, but wasn’t fast enough to fully dodge the wind weapon. It had transformed from a spear into a sword, and the wind bit deep into my cheek, sliding across my face with a spray of blood.

Gravity, more than any effort on my part, pulled me out of range of the deadly woman.

I… couldn’t hurt her. Even with all this, I still couldn’t hurt her.

The ground was approaching fast, and my mana was running low. I could feel the effects of mana exhaustion coming on even though I was gaining nearly two hundred points a minute. I’d entered this fight well short of maximum. Adding points to Mana Pool hadn’t magically created more mana.

Freyna wasn’t content to let me fall. Instead, she blitzed downward until she was falling right beside me. She reached out and I screamed as tiny wind shards began to slice all over my body.

“Back off!” I screamed, my voice empowered by 314 points in Public Speaking, and I thought I surprised her. For just a moment, she wasn’t flying but free falling with me. She veered off, back into the sky.

I reached the ground and shot out gout of flame, which slowed my descent just enough to keep from breaking something. My new Jumping skill also helped, turning a fatal landing into a painful roll.

Elsee. She’d… they’d killed Elsee, and I was realizing that even with all this power, I wasn’t going to be able to avenge her.

“Why!” I shouted up at her, my voice dwarfing the sounds of battle, flame, and wind. “We’re fucking farmers! What did we ever do!?”

The woman sneered, and I screamed as more wind magic cut into me.

“Every last Tacurian will die for what your people took from me,” she hissed as she redoubled the attack. “I’ll see your nation cut to the bedrock, and erase all evidence of your existence from history.”

She could kill me. I knew she could. I’d seen what her wind magic could do. Why wasn’t she? She’d used the tiny cuts attack instead of just slicing my throat open while we’d been falling.

The attack relented. Bleeding from a hundred small cuts, I looked up to see crossbow bolts beginning to pummel her. Turning back, I saw that Pemolar’s new gates were open and half the townsfolk had poured out, many of them aiming up into the air.

A streak of water magic shined in the moonlight as I watched Freyna block the murderous icicle. Edra?

Freyna grinned.

“It seems your people don’t appreciate the beautiful walls you built them. Fools.”

Crossbow bolts weren’t enough to get through her wind magic, but I watched as she began to dart around in the air like a manic hummingbird.

Bolts shattered into splinters that rained down from the sky, but she made a strong effort to avoid the water magic.

“Fall!” the queen shouted, and pain shot through my ears, even as half of our fighters sunk to their knees.

I glared, but didn’t attack again. These precious seconds were restoring my mana. I might be able to do something if I could get back up to a few hundred.

Suddenly, horsemen rode up around me, and a hand was proffered down. I blinked bleary eyes as I looked up.

“T-tom?” I gasped. I’d never been so happy to see Reid’s Dad in my life.

“Don’t get me wrong!” he shouted. “I’d love to murder that fucking bitch as much as you, but we’re spent. We’ve got to get back to the castle!”

As if on cue, I heard the distant whirl of trebuchets launching their deadly payloads once more. It had grown too dark to see, and I didn’t think they would be effective against the walls, but they might easily crush us while we were out here in the open.

I nodded and took his hand, only to yelp as a spear of wind hurtled down and stabbed into Tom’s horse. The creature let loose a howl and bucked horribly, throwing Tom off as it died.

I leaped, catching the man and tumbling to the ground in a heap before we slammed against another tree stump.

I saw that his ears were covered with bandages wrapped around his head. He’d made some sort of makeshift earmuffs. I didn’t know how effective they might be but anything had to be better than bleeding out my ears every time I engaged Freyna.

The queen’s counterattack had stopped the constant barrage, freeing her to go on the offence. I yelped as massive blades of wind, glowing an ethereal green, poured out of her. They were easily visible but hundreds of times more dangerous than the small nettles of wind she’d been using to attack me.

I watched the dark silhouettes of men and women that I probably knew be sliced in half, but I’d become inured to that. I’d burned soldiers alive myself on my way out here.

No. Not men. Eschal. The enemy.

That attack confirmed it, though. She wanted me alive. That mercy didn’t extend to my fellow villagers.

Again, I heard a distant horn. The north? Who would be blowing horns up there?

Freyna looked satisfied, and I saw her wind weapon reform into another spear. Muscles aching in pain, bleeding from shallow cuts all across my body, I untangled myself from Tom and leapt into the air before placing myself right in front of her next attack.

She paused.

I grinned. I didn’t move, and didn’t dare waste mana attacking. Instead, I threw jets of flame toward the water, hovering unsteadily with pulses of inferno as my mana drained. Steam suddenly burst all around the both of us and she screamed as the boiling water scalded her in a way she couldn’t defend against.

Above the stream, I dodged the worst of it, but I didn’t have the energy to hover anymore. I screamed as I fell back through my own attack.

Freyna was still up there, but she didn’t have time to do more as the water attacks resumed. I grinned savagely as I saw her scalded face, but it was too dark to make out more than that. Glowing blue water magic attacks continued to pepper her from the ranks of Pemolar’s soldiers. They were still far away though. How far out had Tom ridden to reach me!?

Even so, Freya seemed only slightly inconvenienced. Despite the barrage of water magic, she still managed to send blades of wind down into the mass of soldiers. This time, our fighters were ready for them. Earth magic called up boulders that blocked the wind. Some soldiers even parried the attacks with weapon skills, and a fire mage sent an inferno that seemed to burn the wind blade.

Esoteric talents brought up floating bubbles — wait. That was Eyka! She had a common talent that she used for laundry! That didn’t seem to matter as an army of those bubbles captured one of the wind blades. It cut right through, but each bubble bleed off its deadly momentum.

I swore I watched one of the blades of green light transform into a bird before flying off. Despite all this, Freyna was still on the offensive.

Without warning, the air around her body condensed, and I watched it burst. A familiar parody of earth magic cast on the air finally made the woman scream in fright. I couldn’t see where that attack came from, and neither could Freyna.

“Elmerina!” came a sudden scream from a voice I’d know anywhere.

The battlefield suddenly lit up as I watched a fireball fly from the north, hurtling straight towards the Wind Witch.

It slammed into the wall of wind that had blocked nearly everything I’d been able to throw at it, and then it exploded. Disoriented from the Earthen attack and the constant barrage of icicles, Freyna wasn’t prepared for the fireball to explode and I watched her burnt body careen toward the southeast.

“Yes!” I screamed. I let myself fall back to the ground and reached out.

Freyna landed, enraged, burned, but not dead. I lined her body with mana as best I could from so far away, but wind magic flung it away. A shockwave ripped the ground, heading straight for the wind witch, but she flung herself back into the sky, before the exploding earth and dead tree stumps could harm her.

A third horn called out in the distance, even as I turned north and saw auburn hair running toward me. Another massive fireball burst into existence before blasting towards Freyna. Unfortunately, this time, she was ready. She dodged it and put up a barrier before it exploded just behind her. Even with the barrier, the exploding fireball sent her hurtling back toward us.

This appeared to be intentional as it brought her closer to another attack of exploding air that could be no one but Berkenem. He must have recovered and found his sword. Icicles continued peppering the witch, coming from different directions, distracting her.

At that moment, a wave of arrows erupted from the treeline. The enemy army still hadn’t been entirely routed. Horror filled me as I realized just how many people those arrows could hurt. Another volley of rocks hurtled towards us as well, but in the dark, and well above the light of the fires below, I couldn’t see where they landed.

From the north, just beyond the river, gouts of flame burst into the air, filling the night sky like dragon’s breath. The torrent of flame torched most of the enemy army's arrows before they could land. Hundreds of flames, all produced from experienced mages.

For a brief moment, I saw the golden armor of Denarla’s city guard rushing out of the northern tree line. Hundreds and hundreds of them, all following my Mom as she rushed far ahead.

I almost wept. Now? They show up fucking now!?

“You! Again!? Who are you!?” Freyna screamed. Furious, she held out a hand in a now familiar gesture. Mom was screaming my name as she ran straight toward me, unprepared.

“Mom, get down!” I shouted.

Too late. The arc of wind sliced forward as fast as any arrow. My heart stopped, terrified that I was about to lose my mother, too. I saw the terror in her eyes as the blade of wind approached.

At the last moment, a wall of earth exploded out of the ground, and the wind smashed into the solid earth, destroying it but leaving Mom covered in rubble, but ultimately safe.

“Omoali…” I breathed in relief. The leather armor I’d made gave her the ability to raise walls from the earth. She must have followed Tom and I but I didn't see her.

The distant soldiers crossed the river, eager to engage the Eschal threat. The remnants of Neogon's army, who hadn’t left the walls before, were now pouring out of Pemolar as the tide began to turn.

They must’ve been ordered to stay behind the walls. Sensibly. I’d been suicidally furious, and I suspected all of the town's residents felt the same. I was just faster. As I glared at the suddenly worried queen, that fury redoubled, but I knew I had no realistic way to enforce it.

I’d have to become stronger. I'd have to craft more.

As the golden armor of yet more Tacurian soldiers descended from the north, while the troops from the walls spilled out in an effort to take down the limping Eschal army, the queen gave one last scream of fury and fled, flying out over the treeline in a burst of green light.

It was over.

Pemolar’s Hill had won.

A body slammed into me. I had a brief moment of panic and almost attacked the person before I realized it was my mother, squeezing me in a death grip, crying.

I broke down sobbing, too. In relief. In despair. I couldn’t tell.
