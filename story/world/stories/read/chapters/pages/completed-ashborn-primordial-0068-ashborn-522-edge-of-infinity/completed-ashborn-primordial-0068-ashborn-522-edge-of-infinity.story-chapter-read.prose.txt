Janak seemed to have granted them the most lavish room in the whole spire.

It might well be Vir’s last opportunity to enjoy such comforts, and he appreciated the gesture.

Their quarters were on the upper floors of the spire, requiring a magically powered lift to get there, and despite the panoramic views of the spire they’d moved into in Mahādi, this one put theirs to shame, and it was only by being at its very center that Vir realized the whole city extended outward from this one building.

It was the nexus. The stilled heart of the once-alive city, looming tall over it all. Even if the view was polluted by the black haze of an impossibly vast Prana Swarm.

A Swarm they now knew to contain the tattered remnants of Siya’s soul.

There was no balcony, of course. Going outside would have spelled certain death.

Vir stood at the Imperium glass, lost in his own thoughts, oblivious to the passage of time.

Maiya sat on the bed behind him, and he didn’t need his Third Eye Chakra to feel her eyes on his back.

“There has to be something else,” she said finally. “Some other way.”

He didn’t turn around.

“Janak has had millennia to work on this,” she continued. “The full might of Imperium knowledge at his fingertips, and his answer is to stick you inside that machine?”

Her words dripped with accusation, but Vir had known her too long not to understand the truth behind them.

“You’ve always been good at uncovering things others couldn’t,” she said, jumping to her feet and pacing, working herself up. “Things about prana that no one else has even thought to try! What if Ashani closes Tears full time? She can at least take care of the largest ones while the Asuras destabilize the rest. We’ve already multiplied their ranks, but we can press civilians into the program as well. Then you could find another way.”

Vir turned from the window. Maiya stood with her hands on her hips, looking defiant, but it was just a front to hide her shaking hands. He could feel her Ice and Wind prana roiling within her.

For the prana of a Prime mejai like her to be so thrown into chaos, she had to be close to breaking.

Vir knew, because his prana felt the same.

He sat on the impossibly comfortable bed and motioned for her to sit. She did so instantly, looking up at him with hope.

“It could take years, if I ever found anything at all,” he said gently, holding her hand in his. Even after all these years, the warmth and softness of her hands never failed to calm his heart. “By that time, what would be left? Who would be left?”

He watched the flicker of hope dim in her eyes and felt guilty for extinguishing it, even if he so desperately wanted to believe her.

“And what are the chances I’d uncover something Janak couldn’t, with all his time and everything the Imperium left behind?”

Maiya shook her head so hard her flame-red hair whipped around her face. “You don’t have to do this!” she cried, panic breaking through. “You’ve sacrificed more than anyone for the realms already. You don’t owe its people anything!”

“That’s where you’re wrong, Mai,” Vir said softly, unsure how he was managing to stay so calm. Perhaps it was because she was falling apart that he couldn’t. “When I became Raja, I became something both more and less than a demon. I gained the power to lead my people, but I also took on their hopes. Their dreams. Their lives.”

His eyes met hers, and he offered a small, sad smile. “A ruler’s life does not belong to him. My fate was sealed when I accepted my crown. You, of all people, know this.”

Maiya stared at him for a long moment.

Then something in her seemed to break.

Her shoulders slumped, as though weighed down by something immense.

She sat in silence for a long while, watching the Swarm circle the tower as it had for thousands of years.

“So that’s it, then,” she said at last, her voice hollow. “If you enter that machine, the realms might be saved. Or maybe nothing happens. Or maybe it makes things worse. You’ll die. And yet…”

Tears welled in her eyes, and her words caught.

“And yet,” she forced out, “if you don’t, the realms are doomed.”

She looked down at her hands.

“It’s hopeless.”

“There is hope,” Vir said gently, stroking her hand. “There is always hope.”

The tears began to fall. Slowly at first, then faster.

“It’s cruel!” she cried, her face twisting.

“It is,” Vir said, pulling her close.

She tried to pull away, but he stood with her, refusing to let go, wrapping his arms around her.

Her resistance broke quickly, and she wept into his shirt as her body trembled in his embrace.

They stood there for a long time, holding onto each other as if they could carve the moment into memory.

“I keep thinking about the machine,” he said quietly, his voice buried in her hair. “About how long Janak has been waiting, with his daughter right there, circling his walls.”

Though Siya was Janak’s predecessor’s daughter, it was obvious he loved her as his own.

“I keep wondering if there’s enough of her left to know where she is.”

Maiya’s arms tightened around him.

“And I think about our world,” he continued. “The Garga. Cirayus and Ira. Everyone who has no chance at all if the Tears keep spreading.”

He paused.

“And you. What kind of life would you have if they do?”

Maiya pulled back just enough to look at him, fresh tears streaking her cheeks.

“You have no idea how much I want to tell you not to do it,” she said, her voice shaking. “To choose yourself. Because I love you. Because losing you scares me more than anything.”

She fell silent, but he didn’t interrupt.

“But I know you,” she continued. “I’ve known you since we were children. There isn’t anyone in any universe who knows you as well as I do.”

“And I know that’s not who you are. That you would never choose yourself when so many lives are on the line. That if I stopped you, I wouldn’t just be dooming everyone… I’d be giving you a regret you’d never escape.”

Her voice broke.

“So as much as it tears me apart to say this, as much as it will leave me broken for the rest of my life… save them, Vir. Save everyone. Janak said our enemies know. But if anyone can defy the impossible, it’s you.”

Vir squeezed his eyes shut and pressed his lips to the top of her head, holding back his own tears.

“I thought you’d outlive me,” she said, trying to laugh, but it came out weak and shaking. “By centuries. I thought we’d have time. I thought I could find a way to extend a human lifespan. Stay with you long enough that you’d never have to face any of this alone.”

She let out a bitter breath.

“What a cruel joke that you’ll be the one to go first.”

Vir said nothing.

Because what could he say?

He thought about what the Panav’s Ultimate Sacrifice truly meant. Not just his life, but everything he was. His soul. Every future he might have had.

No reincarnation. No afterlife. Just… nothing.

He couldn’t tell her that.

“You mean more to me than life itself, Mai,” he said instead, pulling back to look at her. “I love you. I worship you. I cherish you with everything that I am.”

Maiya met his gaze, and he knew she was holding back just as much as he was.

That she wanted to beg him to live.

That she didn’t spoke volumes about who she was—and why he loved her.

“Then go,” she said finally. “Practice. Don’t waste what time we have left.”

He nodded.

He lifted her hand and kissed it, closing his eyes as he took in the familiarity of her touch.

Then he let go and sat cross-legged on the floor at the foot of the bed.

The Crown Chakra mindscape blossomed into existence around him—a perfect reflection of the three realms. What had once felt like his personal domain now felt like the rough outline of something far greater.

He sat at its center and imagined what it would mean to fill it. To stretch his awareness across the realms and to give life to every soul within them.

He thought about Maiya, sitting behind him, her back to his, whom he'd have to ferry along.

And then, with a deep breath, he cleared away those thoughts and began.
