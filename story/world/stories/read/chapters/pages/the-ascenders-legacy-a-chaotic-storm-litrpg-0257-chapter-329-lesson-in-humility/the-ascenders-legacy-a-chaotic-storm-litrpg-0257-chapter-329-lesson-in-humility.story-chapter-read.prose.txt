Aodhán wasn't a fool, not by any standard. And he'd most certainly grown from the impulsive, reckless youth he'd been eleven months ago when he'd first arrived in AEFLYM.

So why hadn't he chosen to leave when confronted by a Super Void-Hyena-Bat nearly twice his tier?

The answer was simple.

At the 38th tier, there was no way he could defeat a creature 37 tiers above him, regardless of any power-up or path enhancement. It was foolishness to think he could. But Aodhán hadn't stayed to defeat the Super Void-Gnasher.

He stayed because he couldn't bring himself to leave—not now when he was finally being challenged. The other battles had been interesting, enlightening even, but they hadn't been much of a challenge, and Aodhán wasn't quite done experimenting with his new path yet.

He still had a few skills to test out, and what better way to do so than on a target he couldn't defeat?

Nevertheless, Aodhán had no desire to lose either, and with a burst of willpower, he teleported, evading a blistering beam of void essence by a hairsbreadth.

He reappeared high in the sky, the cloud he'd summoned so close he could touch it. With Stealth activated, he should have remained relatively unseen, but the skill seemed completely futile against the Super-Gnashers because they all turned in his direction immediately, tendrils and beams of nothingness lashing out toward him.

"Okay, new plan," Aodhán cried out as he teleported again, summoning Varéc out of his spirit space at the same time. "We have to take them on one by one. Isolate and—" A blistering beam of void essence smashed toward him, and he and Varéc had to teleport again.

"Isolate and conquer!" he shouted over the furious shrieks of the Gnashers. "We'll handle the weaker ones first and move up from there. But the most important thing is to stay away from—" Aodhán's words were cut short as pain slammed into him, a void beam ripping nearly clean through his shoulder.

Muscles and tissues vaporized instantly as nothingness ate through them—before a massive surge of energy and willpower from his core put an immediate halt to it—gold lightning arcing out of the wound to heal him.

But that single attack—that slight brush with death—forced Aodhán to confront a bitter truth: he really could die in here.

The thought was like a bucket of freezing water, and every trace of excitement on Aodhán's face vanished immediately. Still grimacing in pain, he glared at the source of the attack—the tier 75 Super-Gnasher—and muttered, "Stay away from that one, Varéc, and anyone above the sixtieth tier."

Varéc growled in annoyance, a furious anger simmering within him, but he obeyed, and a second later, they teleported again, appearing right above one of the weakest Super-Gnashers in the group. The tier 75 Super-Gnasher might be out of their range, but the same didn't go for many of its comrades who were between the 40th and 50th tiers.

Those were perfectly within range, and the instant they arrived above it, Aodhán attacked—a jagged beam of violet lightning spearing down to skewer the Gnasher. At the same time, Varéc attacked, fangs and claws ripping through hide and bone with very little resistance.

The Gnasher shrieked, shock and pain lancing through its system as Varéc furiously ripped its body apart—enough to expose its core.

"Fall back!" Aodhán shouted to Varéc, and they quickly teleported, barely evading the cascade of void essence that ripped through the air in that moment, eroding the injured Gnasher to oblivion and further destabilizing the fabric of space.

But Aodhán and Varéc had only just begun, and with cold calculation, they picked out their next prey, ripping it apart with cold precision and animalistic rage.

For the next twenty minutes, they ripped through the weaker members of the horde—attacking with various skills and pulling out just in time to avoid the retaliation of the rest of the horde.

Varéc maintained the use of his claws and fangs, both of which had become so densely coated in chaos with the activation of Synchronized Carnage fifteen minutes ago. The activation wasn't a fallacy either. Aodhán and Varéc were fighting in such synchrony—attacking and teleporting with such fluidity and cohesion—that it was nearly impossible to understand where one began and the other ended.

But they only managed to kill a few dozen members of the horde before the Super-Gnashers lost it—their rage at his indiscriminate killing sending them over the edge. They screeched and cried, furiously attacking by the hundreds.

Void beams, flares, and surges shook the rift to its core, and despite activating {Embody Lightning—Silver} and {Embody Lightning—Violet} to both teleport and phase through the attacks, neither Aodhán nor Varéc could escape all of it.

A void blade sliced sharply across Aodhán's back—cutting to the bone. At the same time, a beam gouged a pound of flesh off his leg, and another pierced straight through his right shoulder—the same shoulder he'd taken the first injury on.

Varéc wasn't left out either. Not only had he lost mass, but he'd also lost tangibility as beams accurately found their target due to his larger size.

It wasn't a fun battle. It wasn't a game.

There was no time to strategize or bask in the glory of victory—only the cold certainty that should he make an error of judgment, he could die here.

He could lose his life.

But Aodhán wasn't ready to give up yet, and with a growl of pain, he harnessed the quintessence of a full seal, raised his hands to the roiling clouds above, and activated {Spear Rain—Lightning}.

Thunder boomed as the energy rushed into the clouds and came down as a rain of spears—massive, rippling spears of pure electricity that struck down like an army unleashed.

Authority gleamed on their tips, and in the spirit of his new path, no spear was wasted. The storm heeded his intent like a command, and each spear found a target, stabbing straight into the skull of every Super-Gnasher around.

The spears had barely struck before Varéc unleashed another volley of spears, these ones piercing into necks before exploding brutally.

Many survived, too strong to be felled by a couple of spears, but many also died, their skulls splitting open from the force of the lightning's explosion to spray blood and brain matter into the air.

Aodhán teleported toward the nearest Gnasher—injured but still alive—and grabbed its neck. Leech activated instantly, but he was barely able to absorb more than a few strands of its energy before he had to teleport again, cursing as a maw appeared from chaotic space to snap down on the position he'd just been in.

That was the problem with the Leech skill. Despite the reduced restrictions of not requiring contact, the skill still required him to remain in a location. Teleporting around wasn't helping his case, and since {Embody Lightning—Violet} only worked on him, he couldn't pull the creatures along with each teleportation.

A series of attacking and dodging ensued after that. Aodhán and Varéc took multiple injuries. The Gnashers, however, didn't get such privilege. Each one they descended on died, even if they had to take injuries for it.

But with each death they caused, the Super-Gnashers only grew more furious—so furious that they began to do something strange. They flew toward the tier 75 Super-Gnasher and plunged themselves into the depths of its open maw.

Aodhán didn't even need to sense the Super-Gnasher's rising tier to figure out what was happening.

The other Super-Gnashers were sacrificing themselves to boost the strength of their leader—a leader whose tier had climbed to the 80th tier in only a few seconds.

Aodhán stared at the sight in horror, wondering what sort of monsters would sacrifice themselves for another to gain strength. He couldn't understand it at all.

One thing he did understand, though, was that he didn't want to remain here by the time that Super-Gnasher reached the 90th or, worse, 100th tier. He wanted a challenge, not a death sentence, and he wasn't stupid enough to think he would stand a chance against the Super-Gnasher at such a high tier.

All he had succeeded in doing so far was eluding it. His skills—even when powered by quintessence and chaos—had had absolutely no effect on it, but he would be a fool to think he could elude it any further.

And without hesitation, Aodhán urged Varéc into his spirit and teleported, heading straight for the rippling exit far in the distance. Fate, however, wasn't so benevolent, and the moment he reappeared right at the mouth of the exit, another mouth appeared—a maw of razor-sharp fangs tearing its way out of chaotic space to swallow him whole.

Aodhán barely teleported in time, appearing far away from the exit only for a jagged claw to slice deep into his face—just barely missing his eye. Aodhán jumped again, eyes wide as he realized just how fast the Super-Gnasher had become. So fast now that it reached his location even before he could.

The realization struck like a bolt of lightning.

He was slow.

He was teleporting, yet he was slow.

And he was at the mercy of a tier 83 Super-Gnasher.

His attacks had no effect. And he couldn't run any longer.

He was trapped.

He reappeared again, and this time the claw took his eye—slicing so deep it clanked against bone. Pain stabbed into his brain so hot he almost forgot to phase through the next attack as the claw sliced deeper.

But even in his intangible state, void essence burned, blistering through his essence like acid. Screaming, he teleported again, knowing that the moment he reappeared, he would find the Super-Gnasher waiting.

His mind searched his status screen for an idea, for a skill, for anything he could use, but nothing came—not until he reappeared, and rather than a clawed limb, he found an open maw, fangs gleaming as a beam of void so condensed gurgled within the Gnasher's throat.

He didn't think. He simply acted.

Chaos surged densely around him, pressing against the fabric of reality like chicks to the call of the mother hen—and then it tore through, the force of his spirit screaming as chaos manifested in reality as a blade and surged down the Gnasher's open throat.

It was only a blade, barely the size of his arm, but chaos was, well, chaotic, and even that little completely destabilized the beam growing within the Gnasher's throat.

The Gnasher screeched from the backlash as the skill dissolved in its throat, exploding in a wave of nothingness. It wasn't enough to kill it. It barely even injured it. But it bought Aodhán enough time to teleport again and tear his way out of the rift, panting and bleeding.

He stumbled the moment his legs touched ground, realizing the gravity of what he had just done. He'd basically created an origin plane breach, pulling chaos directly out of the origin plane to manifest it in reality.

But then again, this wasn't the first time he had done it. A memory of the bloody phoenix flashed in his mind, and Aodhán remembered how he'd tried to break free from the phoenix's hold by attacking it with a chaos sword.

The manifestation and manipulation of chaos was something only Mythics should have been able to do. But he had done it twice now. The thought troubled him, and he groaned. His relationship with chaos was weird, to say the least. He really needed to start reading up on it to gain a better understanding.

But before that, he touched a hand to his wounded eye, feeling the zap of electricity as Lightning Regeneration worked to bring him back to full health, draining massive amounts of willpower to do so.

The sight of so much blood humbled him greatly, and Aodhán tried to remember if he had ever been this injured in a battle before. The answer was no. Back in Lutia, things never got this bad before; someone, or an ejector, was there to pull him out.

But it seemed that despite his importance to the sect, he had to be his own ejector, his own cautioning voice.

Breathing heavily, he lay on the ground for a moment, notification texts scrolling across his vision as he gave himself a chance to regenerate.

Dread Tempering counter: Unique+ → Unique++ (1103 >> 1561/7200)

The notifications caused a small smile to bloom on his face. He hadn't had the chance to really leech any of the Gnashers as he would have liked, but it seemed the little he had gathered from them all had been enough to push him up a tier.

The dread tempering counter had also risen, though not quite as much as he would have liked either. It did, however, give him some satisfaction to see that he had killed over four hundred and fifty creatures in less than three hours.

It wasn't quite a record, not after Abyssos, but it was a good haul. And had he had the chance to gather the cores of the Gnashers he'd killed, he would have been swimming in a pool of potential sect points by now.

Still, it had been a fulfilling delve.

Not only had he been able to test out all his new skills, but he'd also gained some much-needed experience against void-affinity creatures.

Smiling, he looked up with his one good eye, pushed himself to his feet, and after a shuddering breath, began walking toward the exit where the elder waited, a crooked smile already on the man's face.

"Three hours in a void rift and you're this mangled?" the elder chuckled. "You'll have to tell me all about it."

"Not today," Aodhán muttered, exhaustion seeping into every word. "I almost died in there."

"Several times, by the look of you." The elder pressed a flask into his hand with a smug smile. "I would pity you, but I don't. Arrogance is the curse of your kind."

Aodhán frowned as he accepted the flask. "What's that supposed to mean?"

"Exactly what I said," the elder continued, his smile still in place. "The void teaches no mercy. No affinity does. And until you start treating every creature—no matter how weak—as a real threat, arrogance will remain your bane." His smile widened, and he gestured toward the exit of the Verge. "You still have two unused hours. Come back anytime to use them."

Aodhán glanced uneasily at the elder before walking out of the Verge, not quite sure what to make of the man's statement. Was he really so arrogant? Was it arrogance to want stronger opponents? To believe he could defeat any creature as long as he gave it his all?

Those thoughts stuck with him as he made his way back home and found the house still locked. Crixis hadn't come back yet. He walked into the house and promptly made his way to his shower for a long bath.

He collapsed onto his bed after that and slipped almost instantly into sleep.

But even as exhaustion dragged him under, the question followed him into the dark.

Was he really so arrogant?

Aodhán woke up the next morning to the sound of falling rain and rumbling thunder. His muscles ached, and his spirit felt sore from the amount of energy and chaos he had channeled the day before, but he was fully healed, and when he opened his eyes, his vision was crystal clear.

Groaning, Aodhán raised a palm to his eye, searching for a scar, and when he found none, he smiled in relief. Varéc growled softly within his spirit, still tired from the previous night's battle, and after sharing a few thoughts, went back to sleep, leaving Aodhán completely alone in his own mind.

Sighing tiredly, Aodhán sat up, core sense spreading to encompass the house as he slowly pushed himself to his feet. Crixis had returned at some point and was now meditating in his room.

They needed to talk about what had happened with his aura earlier, but that was a matter for another time. His training with Elder Gumi began today, and by the looks of the clock at the edge of his vision, it was barely an hour away.

"Big day, Aodhán," he whispered to himself as he made his way to the bathroom, his muscles spasming in protest.

He had truly gone above and beyond yesterday, and though he hadn't used all his seals and willpower, he had definitely used all his offensive skills, and his pathways weren't too happy about that.

Water essence hummed the moment he entered the bathroom, and Aodhán let himself sink into the feeling, the patter and rumbling of the storm outside helping him relax further. Multiple tendrils of water wrapped themselves around him, and with a grimace, he pushed several strands of energized willpower through his strained pathways.

Painful discomfort lanced through his spirit as he activated Ruinous Baptism, but that discomfort only lasted a moment as the tendrils wrapping around him suddenly glowed gold. They lingered on his skin for a moment, and then they vanished—diffusing straight through his skin and into his spirit.

A wave of refreshing currents surged within his spirit a moment later, and Aodhán groaned audibly as the soreness within his spirit lessened considerably. It was like taking a bath after a very long and stressful day, and Aodhán couldn't have loved the skill more.

The thought that his spirit had just gotten its first shower made him chuckle, and with a smile, he activated the skill again and again until he felt as right as rain, his spirit and mind brimming with a newness and freshness that felt completely illegal.

By the third baptism, his knees actually weakened, and he had to steady himself against the sink, laughing softly at how something so gentle could leave him feeling so light.

He stepped out of the bathroom a few minutes later, put on his sect robes, grabbed the pass Elder Luseph had given him, and made his way out of the house and into the pouring rain.

The cold water soaked into his hair and robes instantly, but for once he didn't mind. After the void rift, real rain felt almost holy.

A series of spatial jumps took him to the administration office, where he met Elder Vasmos, who, after he stated his purpose, hurriedly took him up to the elder sect and to Elder Gumi's runic workshop—a large, imposing building completely buried in powerful runes.

The workshop stood out even amongst the grandeur of the elder sect, radiating an aura of power and mystery so dense it threatened to drown him.

But it wasn't the building's magnificence that captured Aodhán's attention. It was the sheer number of Mythics he could sense within it—their cores blazing with an intensity so bright it was like a gathering of suns.

These weren't just any Mythics.

These Mythics were powerful—geniuses in their own right—and there were hundreds of them within the workshop.

Feeling slightly wary, he glanced at Elder Vasmos. "Is there an event going on in there?"

"No, why?" Elder Vasmos frowned.

Aodhán glanced back at the building, his frown deepening. "There… there are hundreds of Mythics in there. Surely, there must be some sort of event occurring to garner such a gathering."

Elder Vasmos glanced at him in confusion, and then she burst into laughter, her eyes crinkling at the corners. "Oh, Aodhán, those are Elder Gumi's apprentices. She's the most prestigious runic architect in the entire sect. Surely, you didn't expect her to be some recluse, did you?"

"What?" Aodhán stuttered. "I… no."

He glanced back at the building and shook his head in shock. He wasn't quite sure what he had been expecting Elder Gumi to be, but having hundreds of powerful Mythics serving as her apprentices certainly wasn't it.

And Aodhán couldn't help but feel a tinge of unworthiness.

He had requested this opportunity to monitor the interface creation process and assist with it. He'd intended to show off his knowledge of runes to Elder Gumi and impress her so she could take him on as a disciple and teach him more.

But if his competition were Mythics, just what could he bring to the table here?

If Mythics were apprentices… what would that make him?
