Moonlight - Alder

"You are owed this," Those four words had caused more dissonance to Alder than any other set. He wasn't sure if there were any four words more damning than those. He had heard them the first time he killed someone.

The sun had just begun to crest the horizon into evening when the rolling of a metal carriage called those close to the center square. The Enforcers came with two well-bred war horses and a gold-inlaid wagon so white and smooth that Alder had mistaken it for the elusive porcelain his brother had told him about.

From the wagon, an ugly, scraggy, unkempt creature with skin that draped around the bone was dragged. It fidgeted and flinched, drawing back from the noise as it tried and failed to claw its way back into the cart. He remembered the eyes most of all, how they peered out from the hair and bore into him. He didn't know what to think or the scared thing. Luckily, he didn't have to think for long. The Enforcers told him of those with tar'd souls and how they could manifest in a beast like what they had brought.

The armed men commanded everyone to appear, and it took two bells to gather everyone from the surrounding farmland, especially on such short notice. No one complained, as Enforcers deserved more respect than that. Many were excited, muttering something about Enforcers and the mark of Gladus. Lots of pieces of paper were drawn from a box once everyone appeared.

Alder drew the lot with the mark of Gladus on it and got the title of Enforcer to go along with it, whether he wanted it or not, but why would he not want it? "You're one of Gods chosen now," his father had said, "they've picked our family due to the pure souls we've cultivated for generations. We are owed this. You are owed this." His new position was sanctified by his own hand, in the blood of a heretic and a rock chosen by the village elders. He was seven at the time.

He trained, he fought, he learned, and what he learned was that he knew nothing at all. The days never had consistent hours, no one could explain how electricity or magic worked, and what lay beyond the edge of the world remained unknown. Some things just didn't make sense and were not the domain of a mortal such as him. The questions he didn't know were the realm of the divine. He didn't understand it, but all of it was evidently true from what he saw with his own two eyes. So, he believed in obedience. In learning from the humans who reached for immortality and from the God beyond them.

In time, he saw strange occurrences, talked to the ancient people who lived for hundreds of years, and those who shaped the culture and world he lived in. They didn't know anything either, well, that wasn't true. They knew one thing.

Things happened because God decreed it.

Alder never found any evidence to the contrary, so he believed them and left the decision-making in the higher-ups' capable hands.

Being an Enforcer was grueling work. Long hours, high stress, little sleep, and many became cursed by heretics to have nightmares for years. Alder was perhaps the most successful Enforcer of his time, and as such had been cursed many times. A younger alder could differentiate the screams of every heretic he put to the blade. He couldn't tell anymore. They all melted together. No, being an Enforcer wasn't an easy god. So he believed the higher-ups when they told him, "you are owed this."

The higher-ups saw a lot Alder was owed for, and the benefits set him up for life. The first is that he was allowed to pick a wife. Typically, marriages would be arranged by the higher-ups. No one had any say, but he did. He picked his friend Serin, a girl a few years younger than him, but who he had known for years. They were given a house and more money than either of them knew what to do with. Everywhere he went, Alder was praised, and gifts were thrown at him. He did not stay loyal to Serin very long. No one seemed to mind, and when he confessed to a higher-up, they laughed, patted him on the back, and said, "Good. You are owed that much. Go have fun."

In time, he came to believe those words more and more. He had gone through the difficulties of life, had killed at a young age, and proved himself greater. He was making the world a purer place with every tar soul'd person he killed. He did his duties. Work was grueling, he was protecting everyone, and so he deserved the respect and gifts. They were owed to him, and any time he passed them up, that was a gift on his part.

Then Serin fell ill. She got sick and grew frail, and no one would help. She didn't need much. Just a healer to stay by her for a month. He was owed that much for his service. No one gave it to him. Even when he begged the higher-ups. At first, they promised, then when he pushed, they waffled on it, then one of his mentors came right out and said it.

"Alder. Healers are expensive and their time is valuable. We need them for the benefits and increased lifespan. You of all people should understand that. I know you love your wife, but you will live forever. Wives die. I suppose that's our fault though. We didn't get you another one. We'll start that up right away. Two, just so this doesn't happen again. You are owed that for your service," and Alder went along with it.

Even as the wife he loved withered and rotted away, he planned his next wedding. Because he had been told to. Because he was an Enforcer who did good, and so deserved good things. Other people didn't matter that much in the face of a cosmic net positive. But, despite all of that, Alder still loved his wife— as twisted as that love was. He didn't want another woman. He wanted her. So, he went to the higher-ups again.

He argued eloquently, more eloquently than he'd ever spoken before. He mirrored and mimicked and parroted their arguments, about how well he was doing, about how he was owed, and how he wanted Serin healed, not another wife. The higher-ups did not take kindly to that.

They pushed the wedding forward. Alder was present, so was his bedridden wife. She died in the week after Alder's second wedding and the week before the third. He locked himself in his study. He only left when called upon for official Enforcer business. For all intents and purposes, though, Alder was dead to himself and, more importantly, to the higher-ups. All that was left was a man full of regrets and wishes that would never be answered, only called upon to find and kill heretics. His position as a higher-up would never come to pass anymore, and he would never become immortal. He had proved himself incapable of the role.

The final straw for the higher-ups was when he refused to find and kill Maerin. He had found her; it wasn't hard to find the girl. She was still holed up in the high priest's castle. Even getting to her wasn't that hard. She had been better versed in magic than he would have assumed, but he had been fighting since he was seven. There wasn't a contest. He didn't kill her. Instead, he turned her over to the fighting pits and the executioner there.

There were only two people who could legally kill. Anyone with the mark of Gladus and executioners. He didn't want to kill Maerin, one of Serin's only friends and someone who thought of her as a sister. He just couldn't bring himself to strike that blow, and so he hoisted it off on someone else.

Things might have ended there if Krevith had actually struck her down, but he had not. So Alder was punished for his actions and sent to the trials to die and be reborn. He had every intention of dying. To go out in a blaze of glory protecting someone else, but the opportunity never presented itself. Death reared its head many times, especially in the cells where the Fiend had come close to killing him, but that's not how he wanted to go out. He was afraid, and so acted in fear.

He never found out why Nico was also in the trials, nor Orlan, just that the two ancient people had once been important in the past, but no longer were. He took that to mean that the higher-ups were cleaning house. Nico had told Alder that they were here to pave the way and that he would be saved, as he was owed to, if he helped kill the Survivors.

Alder knew better now, though. He wasn't owed anything. The things he enjoyed were there because the higher-ups allowed him to have them, and the moment what he wanted didn't align with their vision, they cut him loose. So, he didn't believe Nico in the slightest. He believed the other man believed it. The mad look in the scholar's eye proved that much, but Alder himself did not.

It's why when he saw the two women hiding behind the barrels near the stairs on the ship, he turned them the other way. It's why, when the sounds of fighting happened, he refused to give the gun to Nico. It had partially been a mistake, as a hefty board to the back knocked him unconscious for the few moments it took Nico to take the gun that ended up shooting the gladiator Krevith. Despite his efforts, one of the women ended up dying because of Nico. The other one died too, in a way. The Parasitic Fiend had their body now.

He thought he might die underground. He had jumped at the opportunity to take one of the paths and hold back the encroaching beast of darkness. It wasn't the heroic way he wanted to go out, but he didn't think he'd get a better opportunity. It was that or get murdered by Maerin.

But then a cave-in had happened, and he was buried, cut off from the shadow monster and everyone else. He waited for the end, but it did not come in the way he wanted it. Instead, the Instance finished, and he found himself in a field, surrounded by those he had either hurt, hunted, or failed. Not a single person there liked him, and he was pretty sure most wanted him dead.

He took it as a sign. He was not going to get a heroic death that would wipe away some of the meager tar on his soul. He would either life, knowing he could never scrub it away, or go with Maerin and hope that by giving her some solace through his death, he might hope to purify it just a little.

It was just that he may not get the chance.

When he woke up in the new world, he did not know what to expect, but he certainly didn't expect the bars of a jail cell. He wasn't unfamiliar with them, just unfamiliar with the side that he found himself in with the uncomfortable beds and low privacy. It had taken some asking of the guards, most of which just laughed or ignored him.

He was on death row. The execution date hadn't been set yet, but if the rumors were true, a date would be selected soon. If one were selected, he'd at most only live for at most three months. He wondered how they'd do it. Magic? Firing squad? Single combat? He tried not to mind. He told himself, "you are owed this."

He had come to the world expecting death. It would just happen a little different than he assumed it would. He wanted it to be Maerin, wanted her to kill him. He still wanted that. There was still time for it, too. He was a Visitor now. If he could find her in the Dream, maybe they could make something happen. If he died outside an Instance, he would wake up back in jail, but if he drained his Presence and had her kill him in an Instance, then perhaps that would work.

There was more that he needed to do, though. He needed to survive until then, and he only knew one way to do that. People in his world knew different levels of the Dream; some knew about it vaguely, others knew details about Boons and Restrictions, and some—like him—were gifted with more specialized knowledge to make his job easier. Visitors always went to the Dream when they slept. It was just a rule of the Dream. He didn't need to know why. It just was. One of the Enforcers' best ways to hunt involved conscription, where an Enforcer would force another to become a Visitor. The problem either sorted itself out with the person dying in an Instance and becoming a husk in the world, or it allowed them to sneak up on a sleeping target with little chance of them waking up. It was hard to do as a Resident, which is why he had never done it personally. He knew there were ways with special pieces of paper or the gaze of God directly, but all it took from another Visitor was giving some Presence.

Maybe he could die in an Instance, maybe not, but the Dream gave power to those who could take it. If he couldn't find Maerin in the Dream or in an Instance, it would be best to have a backup plan. A hundred or so death row inmates with the powers conveyed by the Dream would certainly be enough to break out, and then he'd have all the time in the world.
