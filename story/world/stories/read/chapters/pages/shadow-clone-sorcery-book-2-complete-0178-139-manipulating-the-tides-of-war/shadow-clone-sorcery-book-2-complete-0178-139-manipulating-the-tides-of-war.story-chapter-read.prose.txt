Once deep enough in the caves, Morph Prime sought an appropriate alcove before stopping. He raised a shadow veil and blended it into the surroundings through illusion. He also raised sound and magic-concealing barriers. It was unlikely the ravens or any other pursuers had followed them into the caves, but Morph Prime refused to take any risks. He existed for such work, and it was the closest to what El-Prime and his clones used to do in the Realm of Greater Beings. It was where they excelled and what Morph Prime did better than any Stalker or anyone else.

“It’s time we talk,” he said, facing Astrid. She had already dismounted her giant mountain goat, and her tattoos glowed white. The Jotun woman maintained a neutral stance, but it was clear she was ready for a fight. Morph Prime doubted he had the strength to stand up to a prepared offensive. The position of warchief seemed more than just a title.

It appeared that each of them represented a certain archetype. Morph Prime imagined that the bulk of their subordinates adhered to similar professions and roles.

Bjorn was a berserker, using rage to increase his strength and stamina. Furs had carried most of Ornn’s body, concealing his tattoos. However, the armor of ice and stone covering his shoulders and the ram-horn helm suggested he was some sort of warrior. It wouldn't surprise Morph Prime if the Jotun led a cavalry. Frigg was a healer and a witch, perhaps an animancer who took part in the creation of chimera warbeasts. He couldn't be sure about Saga. It wouldn't surprise him if she, too, was an arcane practitioner, diviner, or communications specialist. Gorm’s memories suggested he blended magic and close-range combat but preferred a more commanding and support position. He used songs and chants to galvanize his forces and empower them with magic.

Morph Prime also saw flashes of the late warchiefs in action. The Jotun used duels to settle disputes between clans. The current chieftain had used a similar forum to claim his position. Occasionally, the warchiefs entered their arenas. Gorm preferred sending champions to fight in his stead, but he had witnessed several. Ornn’s son combined heavy armor of stone and ice with clubs to act as a juggernaut of sorts. Another warchief, much like Xander, focused purely on defense, tiring out his opponent and then overwhelming them with blunt force trauma.

Finally, there was Astrid, the newest and most inexperienced warchief. Her contemporaries appeared to suspect that growing up with the chieftain and a probable secret romance was behind her promotion. Her presence and tattoos gave away little about her. However, after leaving the chieftain's tent, she had armed herself with a pair of daggers, which would be longswords in the hands of a human, and an axe with a chain attached to the handle. A bow wrapped in white cloth and a quiver stuffed with arrows hung from her saddle. Her snow-white cloak had turned dark grey after entering the caves, suggesting a camouflage enchantment. The equipment and the way Astrid carried herself suggested she specialized in sneak attacks and ambushes, perhaps assassination.

The subordinates who had helped her prepare for the departure were all similarly dressed, young-looking, and carried similar equipment. Morph Prime wondered whether the bulk of them were from her clan, explaining why she wished to avoid the war. It was likely her people weren't particularly well-liked, and there already appeared to be friction between the warchiefs and the clans under them.
