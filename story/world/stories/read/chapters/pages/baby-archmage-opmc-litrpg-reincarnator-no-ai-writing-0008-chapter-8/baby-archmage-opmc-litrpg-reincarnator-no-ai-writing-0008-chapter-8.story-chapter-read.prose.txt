I spent a few hours every day reading with Danek. He was a shy boy at first, but I could tell he was really happy to have a friend. I liked him too. He was really smart and always considered what he said. He already had a fairly high Skill level in Reading and Writing, so I couldn't keep up with the speed at which he flipped those pages.

I had explicitly resolved to not want either Skill, because they weren't related to magic. The System had listened to me so far, which rhymed with what my parents had taught me about intent when working with it.

With Danek I learned about the plants and animals of the jungle. Most interesting was the Silverback. It was a massive gorilla monster that prowled in the mana-rich environment of the inner jungle. Its territory was the old ruins where the village Shamans had apparently learned their magic from.

Sometimes the Silverback would chase Junglers and even kill them. It also kept the other beasts from growing, so it was a tyrant, but fortunately it never left the jungle. It made gathering resources from the jungle hard, but nobody had enough power to defeat it. While it wasn’t an awakened beast, it was close to that.

The ruins were also extremely interesting. There was a sketch of a stepped black pyramid in a swamp. It was believed that they were made by the ancestors who were powerful sorcerers who lived on the island in the old days.

This naturally piqued my interest, but unfortunately there was little information. Only that there was a spellbook that the shamans had copied, and that inside the pyramid was a trial to an inner sanctum no Shaman had managed to ever pass.

I knew immediately that I would need to visit the ruins. Maybe with my Mythic Skill I could unlock secrets the elders couldn’t? If so, that could advance my magic substantially.

My mother was a Jungler too… Hmm… I just needed to convince my mother to take a two year old into a jungle where a massive demonic gorilla rampaged.

I sighed. She probably wouldn’t go for it. She was overprotective and unreasonable like that.

After my reading lessons, I would spend some time with my mother before getting down to practicing my magic. This was wonderful. It was the first time I didn’t need to hide my abilities. It was so freeing, both of my parents were very supportive. I'd told them about Piraka's difficulty infusing potions. Father took this as a clear path to my goals, but mother was worried about me revealing too much to Piraka.

Either way both of them encouraged me to train hard and that was what I did.

Mother set up a comfy little corner with beast skin rugs and candles for me to meditate and train my Skills. Whenever I wanted to take a break, mother was there with a snack and tea and she asked me about my progress.

And progress was pouring in. Although the reading practice was taking a lot of time in the morning, my magic training days were longer than ever. Before I had had all kinds of interruptions in addition to the secrecy. I didn’t need to focus on learning to walk or talk. While my body still needed a lot of sleep, I wasn’t constantly interrupted by intrusive naps. And the tea mother brewed me kept my mind alert.

As a result of switching up a gear, the System rewarded my diligence.

[Mana Manipulation level 8]

[+1 Mana Flow]

[+1 Magic Power]

Meditation was becoming second nature to me. I sometimes caught myself slipping into a meditative state even if I was simply walking in our backyard to stretch my legs or when I was eating. It was this calm, but alert state where I felt confident, happy and ready for anything. The higher level I got, the easier it was to slip into this state, and the deeper it was. Since the Skill’s level was getting higher, the gains were slowing down, but since I engaged with Meditation constantly, it inevitably leveled up again.

[Meditation level 15]

[+1 Perception]

[+1 Focus]

While my Mana Sight didn’t reveal anything new to me, the existing effects became clearer and stronger. I could sense more and more mana in the surrounding world. It was everywhere. It was in the plants, it was in people, it was floating in the air. However, the amount of mana I perceived with my Mythic Skill was becoming distracting. There were so many particles that isolating them became difficult, which slowed my training. However, because of my diligence, I made progress in this Skill as well.

[Mana Sight level 11!]

[+4 Perception]

[+2 Mana Flow]

Mana Manipulation was still my lowest Skill, but it was actually catching up. I suspected this was because it was an active Skill I had to use intently. Not that Mana Sight didn’t require intent, but I wasn’t actually doing anything with it. After all, I was just looking at stuff. Whereas Mana Manipulation I worked with constantly. It was the reason I was going to bed tired as a rag every night. No amount of mother’s tea could save me from myself. But I never slowed down. I was a baby on a mission. Even when my parents insisted that I take more time to play and rest, I refused.

Besides, this was play. This was infinitely more interesting than making sand structures or wrestling with the other kids. So I kept my mind focused.

However, ultimately I got stuck. Overperceiving the mana particles was currently my key bottleneck. It was just overwhelming seeing all the dancing lights whenever I activated Mana Sight. This was frustrating me to no end, and I shared my troubles with my parents, because they had surely overcome Skill bottlenecks themselves.

Then the next day on a cool night before bed, my parents took me outside to the beach. It was nice and peaceful there. The ocean breeze was cool and invigorating. The sea churned in a soothing rhythm and the cries of beasts in the jungle were distant. Mother set a blanket on the sand and we laid down.

“I think this will help you. Sometimes when you get into a rut with your Skills, you need a new way to practice. You should use your Meditation Skill with the stars.”

This was the first time I had a proper look of the night sky.

It was magnificent.

I remembered my old life and how I had always enjoyed looking up at the stars. I had been a bit of a night owl and there was something mystical in the quiet of a starry night.

However, I now realized that I had never seen the real night sky. On Earth, all I had seen was a light polluted shadow of the real thing.

Countless stars formed nebulas, clusters and constellations on a tapestry of blue, black and purple of myriad hues. Across the sky ran a stream of gold, purple and white, like the brushstroke of a god.

Is that… the Milky Way?

“Beautiful,” I muttered.

“That big streak is called The Royal Road,” mother said. “It is said that Immortals ready to ascend walk across it to reach true Godhood.”

“See that constellation over there?” father said and pointed. “Look how it forms a triangle shape. That is the Magician’s Hat.”

“I think that one is my favorite,” I said.

My parents laughed, and father mussed my hair. “Thought you might like that one.”

My parents taught me a few constellations, but I was mostly fixated on the Magician’s Hat. I didn’t see the Big Dipper, so who knew where in the universe I truly was.

Then two shooting stars streaked across the sky and suddenly there was an explosion. A massive bright light in the sky produced a visible shockwave of rippling air, as plumes of flame illuminated the sky in a mushroom cloud.

“What is that?! Monsters?” I asked and got up.

My parents shared a look.

Father laughed. “Of a kind.”

Then mother spoke.

“Those are Immortals.”

“Oh…” I said as I watched the lightshow in the sky. Like streaking fireworks exploding in a cascade as the two shooting stars chased each other. They were gone as soon as they had come.

“What are Immortals?”

“We only have stories…” mother said wistfully. “This corner of the world hasn’t had Immortals since the ancestors who built the ruins. Nobody from these small fishing villages has even become a Cultivator, let alone an Immortal.”

“Wouldn’t that be something,” father said and laughed. “A Cultivator from our village!”

“Is there a difference between a Cultivator and an Immortal?” I asked.

“The System grants great power, you know this,” father said. “But there are different stages of power.”

“Everyone gets Skills and Attributes,” mother said.

“And Perks and Achievements!” I added.

“Yes,” mother said and laughed. “And if you work hard enough as you age, the System will recognize your hard work and reward you with a Class.”

“My class is the Fisher,” father said. “I leveled up my Spear Mastery, Swimming, Wrestling, Athleticism and Heightened Senses high enough and was awarded the Class.”

“Hard working people get a class,” mother said. “About half of the adults in the village have it. Most of them are Fishers and Junglers.”

“The most talented get it around twelve years old,” father said. “If you keep up this hard work, you could participate in the Silver Sea Tournament in ten years!”

“What’s that?” I asked.

“It always follows the Moon Festival,” mother said. “The most talented older boys with early Classes get to go to Aligapa to compete in a fighting tournament to bring honor and resources to their village.”

Father slapped his chest. “I placed eighth when I competed.”

“He still sometimes sleeps with the trophy,” mother whispered.

I giggled.

“Hey!” father said. “A man must have his pride.”

We laughed and mother teased father, which was funny. After the moment passed, I eagerly continued the conversation.

“What do Classes do?”

“They unlock Class Abilities and create powerful interactions with your Class-related Skills and Attributes,” mother said.

“For example, I have Waterbreathing,” father said.

“No way,” I said. “Papa is a liar.”

“It’s true! I have the highest Swimming in the whole island.”

“As for me, I have Tracking,” mother said. “It is a Jungler Class Ability. I can touch a beast’s tracks or droppings and get an impression of the direction where the beast might be right now.”

“That sounds powerful, but I don’t want any poop touching abilities!”

Mother laughed and pinched my cheek.

“What comes after Classes?” I asked.

“First are the Skills, which most people have. Then Classes, which talented people have. After that comes Cultivation,” mother said. “I think only the governing family of Aligapa, House Salakin, has Cultivators.”

“And their rival families,” father said.

“You know best, you’re the one who has visited Aligapa.”

“If I had known I’d have such an inquisitive son, I’d have made notes,” father said and laughed.

“What is special about Cultivators?” I asked.

“We don’t know much,” father said and scuffed the sand with his feet. “Most we have are fables.”

“It is said they can break the level cap,” mother said. “Skills naturally cap at 100. Class Skills can go higher.”

“Class skills?”

“Those are the Skills related to your Class,” mother explained. “They cap at 120, 150 or even 200 for some powerful Classes.”

“Like the legendary Fisher Tomako!” father said. “It is said he had level 160 Spear Mastery.”

“But a Cultivator breaks the natural limits of the System,” mother said. “There are tales of Sword Saints with Sword Mastery at level 500.”

“How do I become a Cultivator?” I asked eagerly.

Both of my parents shrugged. Father laid a hand on my shoulder. “Nobody knows in this poor and remote corner of the world. All we have are fables, traditions and scant news from trading ships. We know even less about Immortals. All we know is they are all Cultivators who have become something… even more. What? Who knows?”

“Which is why…” mother said quietly. “If you wish to pursue magic. You will— You will have to leave some day.”

The silence after that was weighty. We lowered our gazes from the starry sky to the sea and listened to the waves in the plaintive night. Finally mother sighed and kissed me on the cheek. “We’ll give you some space and keep watch. Practice as long as you want.”

“I will,” I said and paused. “Mama. Papa. Wait.”

They turned and looked at me.

“Thank you,” I said. “For being such good parents.”
