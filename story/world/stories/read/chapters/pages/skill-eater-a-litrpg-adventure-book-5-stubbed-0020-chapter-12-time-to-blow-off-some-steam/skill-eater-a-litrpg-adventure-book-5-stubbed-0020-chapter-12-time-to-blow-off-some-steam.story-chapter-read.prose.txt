When the duo left the Coalition’s base, Shield led them back to the sub-biome where they’d met, raised a hand in farewell, and waited for them to leave.

After following the trail out of the Toxic Marsh, Edge and Sakura started walking toward the free city and away from where they would rendezvous with the rest of the crew. She blasted them with a Disruption surge to dispel any spells King might have cast, then scanned them with her mana-sensitive eyes to make doubly sure they hadn’t been marked with a tracking skill.

When she was done, the scion switched on her magitech privacy device so they could discuss what had happened during the meeting. “Do you think we’re being followed?” he asked.

“Absolutely,” she replied while pretending to stifle a yawn. “We’re being tailed by a flying beast rider and someone with a scrying skill comparable to Remote Viewing. I assume they can read lips, so watch what you say, even while the device is running.”

Edge made a show of stretching out. He lay down with his head propped against his backpack, pulling his hat over his face like he was planning to take a nap before moving on. Meanwhile, he summoned his Guide, which formed a screen that only he could see. Sakura could send messages to it with her magic, so he ignored what she was saying and read her replies instead.

While she acted like they had decided to accept the Coalition’s offer but were hoping to obtain a few concessions first, her true thoughts were revealed on the hovering display.

“What is your take on King?” he asked.

“His mind is sharp as a blade,” she replied. “He is incredibly observant and he has a profound insight into human psychology. King was so convincing that I’m sure I was being manipulated in multiple ways, on top of the inherent persuasiveness of his argument. He could tell we were deceiving him in some manner, but we picked the right strategy to stay his hand until our next meeting.

“I don’t know what kind of ally he would make, but I have no doubts that he would be a deadly foe. He was using a skill that blocked my informational powers, including my ultimate abilities and my mana sensitivity. That wouldn’t be possible if he wasn’t using high-rank magic. Were you able to pick up anything with your skill senses?”

“No,” Edge replied. “Skill-Eater warned me that Shield was a scion, but she couldn’t sense anything about King, and neither could I. Given his position, I suspect he’s at least stage three, possibly even stage four, but if he was using any magic during our meeting, it was obfuscated by a skill strong enough that I couldn’t detect it…”

He trailed off when he felt information flowing through his bond.

“Mental Fortress says he was using a skill that influences emotions and another that can discern truth and intentions from body language and vocal tone. It was able to block the first but not the second, since it was a passive power that never crossed its protective barrier. I would have agreed to an alliance without Fortress’s protection. Manipulative bastard. Do you think King is planning to kill us if we don’t accept his offer?”

“Yes, but we’re more useful to him alive. I’m dead certain that man is utterly ruthless. You don’t rise through the ranks of one of the most powerful organizations in colonized space unless you’re willing to do whatever it takes to get the job done. You didn’t tell any lies, so I doubt he will make a move unless we make a break for it, tip our hand, or miss our deadline.”

When they were done talking, Edge shifted into shadow form, taking advantage of Play’s concealment aspect. Meanwhile, Sakura switched on the mana-muting device she had borrowed from Trapper and used Mirage to erase their presence. They hiked across the Lakelands, laying false trails and doubling back until they were as certain as they could be that no was watching, then made their way to the cave where Trapper’s crew was waiting.

The scions shared everything that had happened, and the team put their heads together to discuss their next steps.

“Since Edge ran into Corpse and Shield on the silver ring,” Trapper said, “there’s a good chance that the Coalition has been monitoring us from the moment we arrived in World’s End. Our precautions should convince them that we believe we’ve shaken their surveillance, but I doubt that’s possible given the resources at our disposal. Going forward, we should assume we are being watched wherever we go, but don’t let them know that you know.”

“They have probably been observing us since we set out from Puppet Town,” Sakura agreed. “But their operations were disrupted when the Wardens drove them out of the free city, so they might not be aware of our recent activities. While they aren’t going to leave us alone, we’re not the biggest issue they are dealing with right now.”

“It’s too bad the Wardens are even worse than the Coalition,” Sasha said. “Or I would leak the location of their base and let them fight it out. But drawing attention from either faction will endanger our people, and I’m honestly not sure that rejecting King’s offer is our best move.”

“There’s no way the planet’s most powerful faction doesn’t have scions of its own.” Jumo ran his fingertips across his scalp. “Both sides must be searching for the rest along with any unbound Unique cores. If the Wardens catch wind of Edge and Sakura’s existence, they will pursue them to the far corners of the planet.”

“We should assume the Ancients have infiltrated both factions,” Mel added. “They’ve been lurking in the shadows since before the System arrived. The monsters must be manipulating events from behind the scenes. We need to avoid them too.”

“I didn’t sense any corruption inside the Coalition’s base,” Sakura said. “King was using a skill to mask his mana signature, but I don’t think there was an Ancient in that facility. It would be extremely difficult to erase all signs of the taint, and no one knows I’m mana sensitive now, or that our Monster Slayer traits help us detect corrupted magic. While the Ancients might have infiltrated the Coalition, I don’t think King or Shield are monsters in human form.”

“Thank the gods for small favors.” Trapper sighed. “We have enough on our plate already. That brings me to one final matter. Manipulation, deception, and missing information aside, King made some compelling arguments. While the idea leaves a bad taste in my mouth, do you think we should join the Coalition after all?”

The crew discussed the matter at length without coming to a conclusion. There were pros and cons to both courses of action, and they needed to learn more before deciding which path was best.

As matters stood, they couldn’t afford to turn King down, but they had two weeks to investigate their options and obtain leverage, so they tabled the matter for now. Sakura hadn’t spotted any signs that they were being spied on via physical or magical means, so the hunters decided to get some rest and head out bright and early.

Edge spent some time in his inner world hanging out with Rue and bonding with his skills, then slipped into his bedroll and closed his eyes.

***

The hunters awoke before dawn—breath misting in the late-autumn air as the horizon shifted from black to navy blue.

While breakfast was cooking, Sakura surveyed the region with Remote Viewing and Edge patrolled the perimeter of their campgrounds. She didn’t spot any traces of observational magic, so after unleashing a few Disruption surges just to be safe, the crew stowed their gear in the floating wagon and set out for the free city.

Edge needed to steal some high-rank skills to make the most out of Extraction and didn’t want to be seen walking with his friends, so they decided to split up and meet at the Lost Traveler later in the day. Sakura would journey with the rest of the team. She was using Mirage to change her appearance, and her disguise was so effective that he couldn’t see through it, even with All-Seeing Gaze.

He set out first, traveling in shadow form. There was a chance the Coalition would find him, but he doubted they would make a move while he was considering their offer. If King wanted to capture or kill him, he would have done so while Edge was in his power.

He chose a route that was parallel to one his friends would take while seeking a powerful beast. The experience would be converted into a bonus after he evolved, but he would have been willing to pick a fight anyway. He needed to blow off some steam after being forced to bite his tongue in the presence of the people who had killed Momo and threatened his home, whether or not King was directly responsible for those tragic events.

By this point in his adventures, Edge had enough Generation to leave Shadow Play running indefinitely without draining his reserves. He glided along the ground until he came across a wooded sub-biome whose name he couldn’t recall.

The canopy was dense, the shadows were deep, and the magicytes were thick enough that they swirled with his passing. However, with the plant-penetration, darkvision, and thermal-vision components of All-Seeing Gaze, he could navigate the murky woodlands as easily as crossing an open prairie on a sunny afternoon, spotting the resident predators well before they knew he was there.

He explored the region for hours as he searched for suitable prey. While he would settle for less if he had to, he was looking for an early stage-three beast that was packing decent skills. If he chose his target carefully, he was confident that he could solo an opponent of that caliber with Opulence boosting the damage bonus from Dragon’s Greed to catastrophic new heights.

Today, Edge wasn’t in the mood to stalk, trap, or ambush. While Trapper would shake her head in disdain, what he really wanted was to take on an aggressive predator and relieve his frustration in a head-to-head brawl. With Harden, Regeneration, and his Organ-Guard, he had plenty of insurance, so he was being a bit reckless but not incredibly so.

Eventually, he came across the tracks of a sizable creature. It was about as massive as an Earth rhino, judging by the depth of the prints. He followed them to a shallow cave set into the side of a modest hillock, and with a little help from Gaze, he could make out the beast sleeping within.

It was even bigger than he’d thought, a towering bipedal predator covered in spiky blue fur that looked like a yeti on steroids. The beast featured bulging muscles, hulking jaws, and hands armed with retractable claws the size of short swords.

That’s perfect. He grinned when he realized what he’d discovered. It was a nanex—brutal creatures that were infamous for playing with their prey for hours before devouring them alive. The species posed a constant threat to the hunters working out of World’s End. Human flesh was one of the nanexs’ favorite foods, and their attacks resulted in several fatalities every year.

Edge was a professional hunter. He had no moral qualms killing for profit, personal growth, or filling his belly, but he was glad to have come across an opponent he could cut down to size without feeling any guilt whatsoever. Before he began, he swept the area for anything dangerous lurking nearby, then pulled out the notes he’d copied from the society’s library to refresh his memory.

Nanexes were direct, physical hunters. They were known to employ a range of skills that enhanced their prodigious attributes and potent natural weaponry, along with an occasional sensory booster, mobility enhancer, or defensive passive.

He wasn’t going to ambush the apex predator, but he wasn’t foolish enough to reveal himself, issue a challenge, and let the immense beast make the first move either. He decided to split the difference and started switching on skills, figuring the nanex would sense the mana emanating from his reactor part of the way through, and attack around the time he was fully powered.

Edge already had All-Seeing Gaze running. He switched on the combined mode just to be safe. It would let him know if anything was approaching from behind, even if it was under the cover of concealment magic. Tiny motes of mana and Disruption emerged from his core, bouncing around the environment before returning to create a magical form of echolocation.

A three-dimensional model of everything within the spell’s radius sprang into existence in the theater of his mind’s eye. The sensory overload had been distracting for quite a while, but by now, he had adapted to this new way of perceiving the world, using Gaze and his eyes at the same time without being overwhelmed or disoriented.

He activated the rest of his skills in the order that would draw the least attention. Edge started switching on his other passives first, since it didn’t count as a combo to use more than one.

He began with Fear the Seasons. It had a passive aspect that boosted his Speed during autumn months. He enjoyed the sensation of the world slowing down as the magic hastened every move he made. Next came Regulate Temperature and Mental Fortress. He doubted the nanex would employ elemental attacks, but a psychic assault wasn’t out of the question. Either way, the skills didn’t drain much mana, so there was no reason to leave them out.

His final passive was Dragon’s Greed.

Since the bonus scaled with the value of his weapon, Greed had become one of his most powerful skills after obtaining Opulence from Helena. When he turned it on, a field of mana flowed out from his core and coated the surface of the glaive, reinforcing its structure and sharpening the blade on a molecular level. It would boost his armor piercing if he struck something hard and oscillate while carving through flesh.

While passive skills were generally hard to sense, he had forgotten just how much mana Greed consumed when enhancing the mastercrafted polearm. He sensed the nanex stir, which meant he needed to act fast.

Certain that it would awaken within a matter of seconds, he switched on Warlord’s Mantle while priming Tidal Stream. Fear could produce a domain too, but he wanted to test out Stream in live combat, and even with his crazy-high Generation, comboing two domains was prohibitively expensive.

A ghostly ruby cloak materialized around his shoulders, billowing though the air was still. Mantle would sap the force from physical attacks and weaken hostile mana, and that was only part of what the aura could do. Energy surged through Edge’s body as Mantle provided a massive bonus to his Power and Speed and a lesser boost to his Reflex and Control. It also enhanced his combat awareness, making it easier to track the movements of his foes.

Meanwhile, gravity mana filled the air as Tidal Stream began to manifest. By now, the nanex knew a powerful opponent was in the vicinity. The predator opened its eyes, following the heady flows of magic in the air. It sprang to its feet in a flash, smiling when it realized a lone hunter had entered its territory.

Edge met its gaze boldly and returned the beast’s savage grin. They were both looking forward to the battle to come, although only one would enjoy it for long.
