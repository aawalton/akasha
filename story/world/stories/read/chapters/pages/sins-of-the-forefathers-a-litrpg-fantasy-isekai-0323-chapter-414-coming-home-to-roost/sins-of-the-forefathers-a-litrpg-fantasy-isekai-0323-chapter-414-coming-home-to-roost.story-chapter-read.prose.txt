“This is a private matter for the Order of the Polaris Reach, thank you!” Maria said loudly over the flood of curious questions. She stood at the door to the meeting room aboard the Ashen Bride, still clad in her armor. My head of the Stewards wasn’t the only one, either. Everyone else currently in the room with me was just as fresh from the field. Some of them had been a part of the tracking team for the Elves I had already…dealt with, while some had not been. Those people looked particularly confused about what was currently going on.

As did the crowd just outside the room's doors. Those people were regular expedition members, from all factions of it. I think we had confused people when everyone had returned to our camp in the tannery district of Rhoscara. Normally, you would have expected a good deal of triumph and relief that our targets had been found and eliminated.

And there was some of that, to be sure. My people, for all that they were dreamers, didn’t tend to reject results when they were presented. Only, it was mixed with an air of frustration and disappointment.

That had drawn some curious onlookers, who followed us when I was herded right here. Among those people were the other Captains. They stood to the rear of the crowd, their backs to a wall. Some of them didn’t look like they understood what was going on, while others who knew me personally…

Well, Bella looked like she could guess what might have happened. For all that we were personally estranged these days, once upon a time, the Pirate Captain had known me fairly well, once upon a time.

“We ask for a measure of privacy at this time, while we sort through an internal matter. Thank you, we will not be taking questions at this time!” With finality, Maria slammed the doors of the meeting room, the strength of which betrayed her own frustration.

But not before some parting words floated through. “But why’s she in there the-”

And then it was cut off. For emphasis, Maria lowered the bar meant to barricade the meeting room as well, there in case of a siege.

Silence fell on the room once more, and I was perfectly aware of the dozen or so pairs of eyes resting on me. I sat just as quietly as I was considered by the members of my own Order, content to let them do so. The people in attendance were, by and large, not any of our regular soldiers and Classers. These were our officers and leaders, those whom we could spare on the journey north. Renauld was here, as were most of the original surviving members of the Nocturne Division and SED. As was Walter, permitted to join this meeting as my personal Squire. But beyond that, Maria had been adamant that this matter about the Order be settled by members of the Order.

Well.

Mostly members of my Order.

Sylvia was the only person not of the Polaris Reach in attendance at this meeting, sitting quietly in a chair off to the right. While I could almost feel the weight of her sapphire eyes in particular, I was still encouraged that she had chosen to sit so close to me.

Maybe I hadn’t fucked everything up. Just…damaged some trust.

I guess I’d find out.

Maria sat down on the other side of the circular table from me, and that seemed to be the signal that things were starting. She met my eyes evenly across the surface from me, and spoke. “You found the saboteurs by yourself.”

“Yes.” I nodded, folding my hands in front of me.

“How?”

“A Thaumaturgic similarity linking ritual,” I said simply. “It led me straight to them.”

I could tell that many of the people in the room didn’t quite understand what that meant, but I did hear Sylvia shift next to me. I wasn’t the only one to notice, as Maria’s eyes shifted over to her. “Sylvia?” She asked promptingly.

“…Thaumaturgy is the Mystical school of links,” Sylvia started slowly. “It’s possible to…identify two similar objects across significant distances, and manipulate the inherent similarity between them. From that point, you simply follow one object to the other. Nathan…what did you use as the focus?”

I turned to her and dipped my head. “A splinter from the mask of the other saboteur your team eliminated,” I said, an apologetic note in my voice. “I dug it out when you showed him to me.”

“Clever,” Sylvia acknowledged quietly. “I…should have thought of that.”

Especially considering we were in the same Thaumaturgy class, back at the Academy. She was more advanced in the School than I was, as well. I had been a late in the semester addition to the course, after all, while she had been in it for the full run.

I didn’t say that out loud, however.

“Nathan,” I heard Maria say, drawing my attention back to her. She met my eyes calmly. “Why did you go off on your own? Why didn’t you offer this…ritual to us?”

“We would have been happy to have you with us, Marshal,” One of the former SED members said quietly. “We remember your capability in the field. It would have been welcomed.”

“Instead, you went out half-nocked on your own,” Maria said flatly, finally letting a measure of her frustration out into the open.

And there it was.

“Nate…” Renauld said carefully. “You’re not just the leader of a Martial Order now. You’re also the lynchpin of this entire expedition.”

“You’re the only one who can actually kill the Mad God,” Sylvia said pointedly, her own displeasure obvious. “It’s not only a foolish risk to test yourself against a squad of trained Elven saboteurs.”

“It’s recklessness on a level that is hard to understand,” Maria said, standing from her chair. She leaned over the table, palms flat upon it, to fix me with a stare. “It’s essentially gambling your luck and ability against the lives of everyone on Vereden.”

The words rung in the room, and nobody…nobody spoke against it.

Because she was right.

As soon as I’d come down off of my vengeance high, I had begun to grow…ashamed. I was likely the last Precursor that the System could…pluck from Earth, or something. The very last person that it could facilitate the transfer of the same flame that had been passed down from predecessor to predecessor. And that fire, capable of bypassing all mystical defenses, was the only weapon possible that could realistically kill Fynneas for good.

Transferring the stolen mantle of Divinity back to it.

It had been beyond reckless for me to venture out on my own in that manner.

And for all of that?

I knew I still would have done it again.

I nodded silently at Maria’s accusation, but didn’t speak.

At my silence, her frown grew deeper. “Why?”

I took a deep breath before I spoke, letting my eyes drift across those inside the room. They lingered briefly on those closest to me, first Renauld, and then Sylvia. I stopped longer when I met the eyes of Walter, standing quiet and still against the far wall.

Just meeting his eyes, I could tell.

Walter understood.

My Squire had recognized that Elf as well, after all. While he’d been too shell-shocked at the sight of one of the murderers of his family to even move, I had…

Well, I’ll admit it. I’d temporarily lost my mind.

But Walter knew why I had gone out and done as I had, and…I could tell his feelings about it were complicated. From speaking to him before, I knew that Walter had difficulties speaking about those days, and I didn’t blame him. It was…hard to come to terms with the fact that our parents had been slaughtered in a senseless, needless raid meant to enslave you.

He’d likely had more cause to hate them than I did, and I had essentially stolen from him the chance to seek vengeance of his own.

And so, he looked…conflicted.

On one hand, the murderers of his family were now dead.

On the other, he’d had no part in it. I had essentially robbed him of even the possibility of personal vengeance.

I didn’t blame him for the storm of emotion in the prospective Palatine’s eyes.

We’d…talk later.

I tore my gaze away from my Squire and finally spoke. “Some of you might be aware of this, and some might not. But at one point, I was…enslaved by House Savoy.”

Some of the people in the room inhaled sharply at that. I…didn’t really speak much about my days in Addersfield. At least, not publicly. I’d been open about the fact that I was Precursor, and had been essentially dropped out of nowhere onto Vereden, into what was essentially nowhere. But I rarely spoke about what had happened to me after that.

The only people who had gotten the full story in this room…were Renauld and Maria.

Sylvia had been there, if only briefly. I didn’t need to tell her anything.

At my words, I could see a flash of realization cross her face, and a sigh of resignation escape her.

Maria’s eyes glinted, but she didn’t speak up as I continued.

“The reason I went out on my own…” I said slowly. “Is because I recognized the Elf you took down. He…was part of the group that captured and sold me the Savoy. And if he was here…”

“Then the rest of the group that captured you must have been here as well,” Maria finished for me grimly. She sighed and shook her head, as I nodded silently.

I wouldn’t say a word about what they’d done to Walter’s family caravan. That was…his business to tell, and he wasn’t speaking up.

Maria sat back down, and I noticed that there was more…sympathy on the faces around me. Not from everyone, and to be perfectly frank, I thought that was fair.

“You allowed your emotions to cloud your judgment,” One of my fellow former Nocturnes accused flatly.

I winced…but nodded. “Yes,” I admitted quietly, gazing down at my hands for a moment. “I did. I couldn’t control myself at the sight of him. I know, I know that I should have said something. I even know that if I had just asked, Captain Maria would have been happy to allow me to assist in the hunt. But…I just…couldn’t. I don’t think there’s anything that could have kept me from going out there and making sure I ended them. On my own, and permanently. And I realize that’s…a problem.”

My current second among the Order visibly relaxed at my pronouncement. “Good,” She breathed in relief. “It’s good that you recognize that, Nathan.”

This time, it was me who stood up from their chair. I think many people were a bit surprised when I bowed my head to the room in contrition. “I apologize for my actions and the instability it might have caused. And yet…” I raised my head, cutting off the next words I could see Maria was about to say. My tone was still apologetic when next I spoke, but I was firm. “I can’t guarantee it won’t happen again. I’ve…long since realized I have a problem when it comes to slavers. I have difficulty in holding myself back from immediately elminating them. I believe that if I had stayed in the Principality, all those months ago…I might have been the one to found and lead the Unchained.”

Silence fell on the room once more, and I saw the people inside of it exchange glances. To my surprise, my words caused most of the tension within…

To vanish, as if it had never been there in the first place. Even Maria finally cracked a resigned smile, while I felt Sylvia’s hand reach over and slide into my own. When I looked down in surprise at her, she greeted me with a soft smile. I just blinked at her in confusion, but my attention was stolen when Maria spoke once more, always the spokeswoman for my forces.

“That’s fine,” She said, sounding mildly amused for the first time since I’d been dragged in here. “You might not realize this, Nathan, but Herztalians are used to eccentric leaders. We’re not put off all that much when the powerful who lead us into battle have their…little idiosyncrasies. There are worse fates for the Order of the Polaris Reach than for us to be led by a man who has a, shall we say…”

“Pathological, frothing at the mouth hatred of slavery?” Renauld butted in with a grin.

Maria just rolled her eyes at him in a tolerant manner. “Let’s go with that, yes.”

“We could have it worse, I suppose,” Another member mused, stroking his chin. “Our Marshal could have been Shacklock.”

I think all of us took a moment to imagine that fate, and a collective shudder ran through the room.

“But you’re going to be attending meetings with the Mind Healers when we get home, to try and address this,” Maria said firmly. When I blinked at her and raised a finger, she just narrowed her eyes at me again. “No arguments.”

I shook my head in fond amusement, shrugged my shoulders, and nodded. “I suppose so. I’ll see them if I get the chance.”

“When,” Sylvia said firmly, standing to join me. “When you get the chance. We will return to Blutstein…one day.”

I smiled at her, reaching out to nudge a strand of her golden hair behind a Mithril ear.

“When,” I promised.

One day.
