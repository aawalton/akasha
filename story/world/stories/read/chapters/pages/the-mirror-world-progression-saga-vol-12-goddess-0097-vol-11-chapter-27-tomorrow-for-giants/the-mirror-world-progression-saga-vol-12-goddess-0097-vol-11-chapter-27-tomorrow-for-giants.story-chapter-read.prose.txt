The Servitors, cleromages, minotaurs, demonkin, frogmen, and those of other stripes distributed themselves amongst the guardians of Traesh, as Samantha and Azure alone among them floated down toward the central ritual area. Glae was wearing fancy ritual robes of silver, gold, and black, and wore an unusual, spiky crown, one side rough stone, the other fine, gleaming silver, ornate in its designs though free of jewels.

As usual, his face was cast in a perpetual contemptuous sneer. Nonetheless, he smiled and gestured with his hands out. “Welcome, Sam. Azure. I see you’re hiding the artifact, still. No doubt you have questions before you offer it up. Speak.”

Samantha nodded coolly to him as she came to a stop, still hovering significantly from above, so she was looking down on him.

On cue, the voice from afar yet within spoke again. ‘There’s never any end to our petty feuds, is there? Would you listen, would you believe, if I told you I’m utterly sick and tired of them? I wish you could be in the same way, but I fear it truly has become all that you are. An eternal enmity, damning us all to eternal misery. If only you could make an exception. One exception… to see so much change.’

Distracted by the voice, Samantha paused and glanced at the ritual platform, which soon was its own distraction. The entire top surface housed a massive, intricate rune of swirling symbology oozing powerful meaning. The aesthetic form was like a god of calligraphy had labored over his magnum opus for years to produce it. For the ‘rune nerd’ that she’d become, this design was enough to make her weep from its sheer beauty and complexity.

One could also weep for the aura within it. Deep grief and agony were poured into it, the kind fit to consume empires or worlds. That it could fuel a World Spell was trivial. She could spend hours unraveling just its aural meanings, but principally, she sensed three things: a sense of gleeful, deserved, honorable breaking of old, decrepit conventions; a sense of balancing scales and enforcing long overdue fairness on a world scale; and a vicious, angry grudge, pulsating and chomping at the bit, like a chained wolf wearing down a long-forgotten anchor.

Or a titan.

Glae laughed in authentic joy. “How lovely! You can appreciate what is before you. Nothing has spoken better of you than that, to date.”

Samantha smoothed out her stricken expression and fully opened her physical eyes to meet Glae’s own soberly. With effort, she resisted the natural desire to squint at the material world.

Even her natural mind was not to be trusted in this situation, with so many intense things triggering reactions. Though she almost always could ‘process away’ the insanities threatening her fractured ‘present’ psyche, in this moment, in her rival’s presence, it was particularly disturbed. The feedback included such wonderful things as screams of rage and rants of global revenge. Each was a window, a shard to view the world, then prevent. Collapse back into herself, absorb, and collate.

More than ever, she understood why her eyes bled.

A deadly calm Samantha declared, “This is a life’s work in the completion.”

Glae held her eyes, something in his countenance indicating he was disturbed by what he saw within. He said nothing for a long moment, and it almost seemed like he had trouble maintaining the glare. With a deep breath, he did so, however. “In a manner of speaking, yes.” His voice was incredibly flat, his face the stone-cold, poker kind. “Elements of the ritual have been in process for a long time, but only the fuel of my lover’s death at the hand of the Sage, in absolute Sympathy, allowed today, the full culmination. Our revenge. Even if I fail to destroy him in the end, this will wound him, and the weakening will improve our chances.”

Samantha nodded slowly and chose to be the one to look away. Meaningless childishness. Why bother? “I need to know about World Spells, first and foremost.”

“Do you? It seems to me you only need to understand this one.”

Samantha didn’t reply. She idly inspected her nails.

Snorting, Glae said, “Fine. I’ll humor you as a bonus to your cooperation, since you’ve put me in such a good mood.” It was thick with sarcasm. “As rumors surmise, World Spells require a class at Level 20, usually a spellcasting one. The actual details make them a hideously bad investment for anyone concerned with personal power. Only immortals have the time, because they require a heinous thing: levels. Virtual levels, so to speak. You have to give up an entire collective of [Experience] equal to what would be your next level. That’s in addition to other ridiculous requirements, such as impossible ingredients like the First Page. The Sage simply had the numbers and ‘free time’ to do multiples. I don’t know if there are additional restrictions to multiples yet.”

Glae shrugged helplessly. “Most use those virtual levels to obtain more personal abilities, especially with an advanced class. The levels do nothing else, mind you — they cease to give ELs — but the abilities can be powerful. Multiple classes don’t matter, either. There is only one ‘tracked’ state beyond 20, the actual leveled classes simply giving different access.”

“Fascinating. And World Spells aren’t always an entire world, are they?”

“The requirements are less or the potency greater for mere regions.” He paused and looked down at his rune. “That suffices for the basics. Can you guess what I aim to do?”

Samantha considered it. “It’s not only an aim, but a purpose, isn’t it? It’s influenced by class, and your class was designed already, honed to a specific general goal. And I sensed a state of ‘balance.’ It must be Antimagic. Exciting, if so, but it also makes me a bit nervous.”

Glae smirked smugly. “A dispelling, yes — the ultimate dispelling.” His face got more intense as he stared at his rune wide-eyed, half-mad, and formed a clenched fist out in front of him. “Finally clear away his despicable violation of the soul of our precious world.” His fist was shaking, his teeth bared as he practically spat the words out, seething with hatred. “Make way for healing, make way for our children to remember what we fashioned; what and how we fought for them.” He lowered his hand and took a breath, calming down rapidly. “This will forge a final legacy of freedom and herald a new age for the people of Calrenazzod. Today, we start over… in memoriam.”

Samantha felt both wonder and trepidation at this statement — both excitement and nervousness. She quickly realized what he meant. “You’re dispelling the World Spell of Forgetfulness! Lifting it. Lifting the curse.”

Glae’s eyes shot up to hers. “That is exactly what I’m doing, yes. I wish Klymene were here to see this brand of vengeance executed, to see the way she preached proven true, the way of her people, to preserve, to heal, to quench the chaos. So it is a fitting gift at her gravestone, finer than flowers. Today, the elves will have their way; tomorrow will be for giants.”

“You’re bloody crazy!” Azure suddenly called. “I don’t care if the Sage decided to sit on his hands for Matrazere, there’s no way he’ll let this happen without interfering! Did you set this up to draw him to your lands, have him fight Samantha while you finish your ritual, and then, you clean both of them up afterward?!”

Samantha’s eyes got wider and wider. “Whoooa… I didn’t even fucking see that!” She put her fists on her hips and glared at Glae. “Yeah, you got some ‘splainin’ to do, Traeshan!”

Glae burst into laughter, cringed, and facepalmed, finally shaking his head in disappointment. “Fools, as ever. I’ve tried drawing that bastard awake and here, or even his moronic protege, for decades, with no luck. That’s why I pivoted to other strategies with the Naugite invasion, which you then screwed up! Granted, you’ve accomplished even more than I expected of Zadkiel, but this remains a trap for the Sleeper, and he knows it. These towers aren’t just for show, and if he thinks he can ignore them like the mobile zones, well, let him think it.

“The closest connection his system has here are those ruins far south, and that is a tenuous one that he can mostly make do one thing — unleash undead. He might do that, but it won’t stop this, not by a long shot.”

He met their eyes again with his unique brand of contemptuousness. “He will not come. He knows he will win simply by waiting and waiting and waiting endlessly, forcing us into his traps. Still, you’re not entirely wrong. I have these sprinkles of guards for a reason, and now you’ve added your overage of reinforcements, including the mightiest of minotaurkind.”

Samantha and Azure continued glaring at him. Finally, Samantha asked, “What, then?! Don’t keep us in suspense, here! Why are the guards here?”

Smirking in clear pleasure to hold all the cards on them, Glae replied, “The Sage has certain preparations against the interference of his spellcraft. I have made various countermeasures, but I have strong doubts that I can prevent all the triggers from happening. There are certain types of unstoppable summons the ancients utilized. Old… connections that cannot be broken or interfered with.”
