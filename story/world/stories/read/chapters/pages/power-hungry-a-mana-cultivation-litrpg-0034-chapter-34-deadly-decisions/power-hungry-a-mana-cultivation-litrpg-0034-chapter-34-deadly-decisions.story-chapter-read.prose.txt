Cale pushed his hoverboard to go faster, zipping through the maze, guided by Aura. There were some skulking and screeching manafiends, but despite their speed, they could not catch Cale when he was moving solo. It felt good to ride again.

But there was an undertone of dread. He was worried he might find trouble topside. The timing of the communications jam was too uncanny. Darius was strong, but if there was ever a moment for the enemy factions to strike, it was while he was still in Core Formation.

What will I do if he’s dead?

Cale zipped forward, making his way towards the labyrinth’s exit. If Darius was dead or captured, him and the recruits would be done too. There was no need to worry. Maybe if he acted fast, he wouldn’t be too late.

When Cale emerged topside where the biting red wind blew at him, he couldn’t understand what was happening. His mind wouldn’t make sense of it.

Because nothing was wrong.

The Sullied cast him a weary glance and went back to cleaning out the corruption at the base of the spire. The supplies were neatly piled next to the doorway Cale had emerged from. He took a bottle of water and drank.

“Huh,” Cale muttered to himself.

Cale approached DR-12, who was meticulously scrubbing at a patch of corruption on the spire’s base. The Sullied didn’t react at first, only acknowledging him with a slow turn of the head.

“This one greets you,” DR-12 said.

Cale crossed his arms, keeping his stance casual. “Hey. Quick question—Fiara can’t get a message through to Darius. You guys seeing any interference on your end?”

DR-12 blinked once. “This one has no communication devices.”

Cale frowned. “Huh. What about the rest of you?”

DR-12 tilted his head, then swept a slow glance at the other Sullied, who were still working in silence. He turned back. “None have reported issues.”

That was an odd way to phrase it.

Cale studied DR-12 carefully. The ridge-faced Sullied’s expression was blank as always, but there was a measured feel to it. Like he was waiting.

Cale pressed. “You sure? This doesn’t seem normal.”

DR-12 nodded once. “This one is certain.” A pause. “Is there reason for concern?”

The way he asked the question—flat, neutral—sent a prickling sensation up Cale’s spine.

“I don’t know,” Cale said, watching for a reaction. “Is there?”

DR-12 didn’t answer.

Not immediately.

Then the Sullied shrugged. “This one thinks the spire is mysterious. Perhaps it is causing interference.”

“That so..?” Cale asked. “Did you notice anyone or anything around this area? Enemy operatives? Murderbots, any movement?”

“This one has been focused on work,” DR-12 said. “None of my brethren have said anything. This one sees nothing, knows nothing.”

“Uhhuh,” Cale said. “Weird timing for the interference, huh?”

“Random things happen. Sometimes good, sometimes bad.”

Something in the way he said it—it wasn’t dismissive, but it wasn’t neutral either. It felt like an attempt at neutral.

Cale’s jaw tensed. “Right. Well. If you hear anything, let me know.”

DR-12 nodded once, the sunken eyes never leaving Cale’s. “Of course.”

As Cale walked back toward the spire entrance, he felt the weight of DR-12’s gaze lingering on his back.

His sense of dread not exactly mollified, Cale ascended the steps towards the door to the upper sanctum of the spire. He pushed the door aside and was met with a tightly spiraling staircase. The floor and the stairs alike were lined with tiny sigils connected by lines. Same as the walls. The sigils seemed to become denser and more intricate the higher Cale ascended the stairs.

Darius turned to him, a questioning and impatient look in his eyes. He was sitting cross-legged on a pedestal, shirtless and his umbrella resting in his lap. He broke the sit, letting his other leg hung on the floor, while still perched on the pedestal.

“What is it?” he asked.

“Communications got jammed,” Cale said. “Fiara tried calling your device, but couldn’t get a connection. We don’t know why. I came here to tell you that, and the fact that we’re ready to proceed.”

“Really?” Darius said, his eyes narrowing. “That is odd. Maybe it is a feature of the spires. But Ravia never said anything about it…”

Cale said nothing, he just let Darius work it out. His mentor would arrive at the best judgement.

“You asked the Sullied anything?”

“Yeah, I talked to DR-12,” Cale said. “To see if they knew what was causing the jamming and if they had seen anything.”

“Nothing?”

“Nothing,” Cale said. “But they were shifty.”

“They’re Sullied. It’s in their nature to be shifty.” Darius said then he sat there for a moment, rubbing his chin. “It’s probably nothing. I’ll enter the trance. Go back and start the ritual.”

Cale nodded. “Alright…”

Darius probably knew better. But he was clearly impatient to start, and maybe that clouded his judgement. Cale had an ill feeling about this, but maybe he was just paranoid.

Darius took out a thin glass vial with three pills stacked on top of each other. He popped the cork with his thumb, rolled the three sky-blue pills, like small round sapphires, in his mouth and swallowed. “Starshard Ascension pills. These will put me in a deep trance. A bomb next to my ear won’t break it. You’re on your own, kid.”

“Good luck,” Cale said.

“Thanks,” Darius said. “Make sure the door locks behind you.”

Cale made his way down the thin staircase spun around the axis of the spire. His sense of dread grew. But if there were no enemy operatives around, that left only the recruit. The recruits couldn’t have caused the jamming. And all communication of the Sullied was monitored, so Darius would know if they struck a deal. They had no incentive to sabotage.

Cale’s mind returned to DR-12 and the conversation they had. His mind was alert and rapidly fishing for each and every detail of face, tone of voice, choice of words.

Random things happen. Sometimes good, sometimes bad.

The question was… was this random?

No. It wasn’t random. That was the thing. That line… That line was not a random thought. That line had been rehearsed.

Cale turned and banged on the inner sanctum’s door. “Darius! Darius. I think the Sullied are planning something!”

He banged and tried to yank open the door but it gave no yield. Darius did not answer, he must have been in a trance already.

Crap. Why do I keep finding myself in the hot seat?

Cale gathered himself, forced himself into an even breathing rhythm and started cycling his mana. He was hoping he did not need it.

Cale rushed down the stairs, jumping three or four at the time, while purchasing balance from the railing. When he got on the floor level, he tackled the doors open.

The Sullied were gone, their equipment left exactly where Cale had last seen them. They had gone down the minute he had gone up.

Cale didn’t know why or at whose behest or how. None of it mattered. He rushed towards the doors leading to the labyrinthine sanctums below. He needed to stop the Sullied.

How?

They were all Mana Circuitry, and there were three of them. Cale looked at his hoverboard. That was the only advantage he had. For all he knew, the Sullied had no equipment on them. But if they had already tricked them all once, they could have done it again.

Cale opened the doors leading down, but he did not move. He crouched and waited, trying to listen to the faintest sounds below the surging wind outside. A creak of a knee, a steady rhythm of breathing.

They had to be waiting in ambush. Anything else was foolish. Killing the messenger between the two forces was the obvious play. But Cale had a trick up his sleeve. Ghost Step. Their only chance of stopping him would be to hit the hoverboard to cut off his movement.

So how do I make sure they don’t hit me or the hoverboard.

Cale grinned. He hopped on the hoverboard at the top of a ramp leading to a T-section.

He accelerated, pushing the speed pedal down on his backfoot. Then he gathered mana in his muscles. He would need to do two things here. Get some extra speed and perform the Ghost Step technique.

First the speed.

Cale concentrated, exhaled and kicked the ground sending the hoverboard sliding off the wall and into the ceiling. He tensed every muscle in his body and contracted the mana into a curled up ball. Then he released, relaxing everything. A green outline enveloped him as he slid along the roof. A metal bar appeared in his vision, striking hard and fast in a gray blur. It passed past Cale. He rematerialized. He dropped down from the ceiling back on the hoverboard.

Cale glanced back and saw DR-12 holding a thick metal bar like a baseball bat. His ridged face was contorted with fury, his deep-socketed eyes bulging with bloodlust.

Gone was the pretense.

Cale smirked and sped off.

“Stupid runt!” DR-12 growled. “After him!”

Cale darted into the depths of the tunnels, the Sullied pounding after him. Their footsteps hammered against the stone, a relentless rhythm that was gaining on him. They were fast. Much faster than Cale could ever hope to run. Their Mana Circuitry cultivation gave them bursts of unnatural acceleration—predator’s speed. But they didn’t have equipment.

Cale kicked off the ground, pushing the hoverboard to its limit, the air slicing past his face as he surged forward. The tunnels blurred, dark and twisting, barely illuminated by the thin lines of Nevani sigils on the walls. His breath came sharp, his muscles burned, but he had no choice.

If I didn’t have my hoverboard, I’d be dead meat.

The chase stretched longer than he would have liked. The Sullied weren’t losing ground. Foot by foot, Cale fought to widen the gap, bobbing and weaving through the maze, guided only by Aura’s sharp, urgent directions. Every twist of the corridors felt tighter, the walls closer, his speed pushing the limits of his reflexes. And he had to keep his muscles and mana tensed up, which made everything extra hard. He couldn’t slow down. Not even for a second.

Because he had a singular objective.

Get to the power room first. If he could just make it there, if Fiara took his place— they could start the ritual. Then none of this would matter.

He took a hard left— and the second he did, he released the constriction on his mana and muscles.

Two manafiends launched from the darkness.

Shit—

It was extremely hard to relax while two murderous demons lunged at you in a blur of fangs and teeth. But practice makes perfect.

A clawed hand ripped through his torso like smoke— not hitting flesh, just phasing through the afterimage of his body. He sped past in a blur, barely solidifying again before he hit the next turn.

Behind him, the manafiends shrieked in outrage— but they had new targets now.

A chorus of angry Sullied voices rang out as the two hostile forces crashed into each other.

Cale smirked and didn’t look back.

More distance. More time. Keep moving.

His muscles ached, his lungs burned, but finally— he saw it.

The double doors of the power room.

Cale barreled through without hesitation. The second the doors swung open, the recruits snapped to attention.

Weapons half-drawn. Wide, questioning stares.

Cale didn’t stop moving. He skidded to a halt, chest heaving.

“Start the ritual. Now!”

The words came out sharp. Almost a command.

Fiara was already stepping forward. “What is happening?”

Cale turned, already pushing off the hoverboard, already moving back toward the door. “The Sullied revolted.”

A sharp inhale. Shock rippled through the room.

“What?” Madia said. “I don’t understand.”

Cale didn’t care, he addressed Fiara. “Start the ritual. Seal the room. I’ll buy us time.”

Ragnak sat down in his sigil and gave a thumbs up. “You got it!”

Artan exhaled, his blade catching the dim glow of the sigils. He met Cale’s gaze, measured. Then, he nodded. “I hope you are as lucky as you are brave.”

Cale gave him a wry smile. “Me too.”

Then he hopped back onto the hoverboard.

And charged back into the storm.

Cale rounded a corner and waited, watching for the Sullied to come.

“What are you planning to do?” Aura asked him as he caught his breath.

“That is an excellent question,” Cale muttered. “Any ideas?”

“Ideas are not my primary function,” Aura said. “But as a databank, I should inform you of something.”

“Yeah?”

“As you know I have the blueprints of this facility.”

“Saved our ass just now.”

“Indeed,” Aura said, perking up. “I am as mighty as I am benevolent. But what I am trying to say is that there is a secret room below the power room.”

“That so?” Cale said, weighing the possibilities. “I’m not sure it's going to help us prevent the Sullied from breaking into the room.”

“Perhaps not,” Aura said. “But it is tactical information. Use it as you will.”

Cale considered it. Maybe he could break the floor and make the enemies fall in. But with what? He had no offensive techniques and his strength alone as a Body Composition cultivator was not enough to break Nevani stone.

Angry voices filled the corridor.

“Who cares where the runt went?” One of the Sullied snapped. A thin and tall man with monkey arms and sharp chin.

“Because,” DR-12 said carefully, as if explaining something to a child. “If he gets out and finds help, we’re dead meat.”

“Help?” the third one, a burly woman with fat lips and lazy eyes said and chuckled. Cale had never seen a woman get so well away with a shaved head. “Where? Hoverboard miles back into the fortress? We’ll be done here by then.”

“You know as well as I do that there were a lot of operatives from other factions here,” DR-12 said testily. “They’re watching that runt. He’s got some special Integra, I hear.”

“We should take it,” the thin man said as they approached the sealed door.

“You think we’ll get away?” the woman said.

“No,” Dr-12 said. “But if I can ruin Darius Roas, that is enough for me.”

“I’ll take my chances,” the woman said. “Let’s get the kid, take his tech and then survive long enough in the desert for someone to trade with. Gilded Gear might offer us a deal. They sure as shit have an operative watching this.”

“They’ll be traitors if they do,” DR-12 said. “They won’t go to war for your Sullied ass.”

“No,” the woman said. “But they might for the kid’s Integra.”

That one hit Cale. It brought him back to the brutal reality that this was real danger. These people would kill or capture and sell him to be dissected by one of the factions. The sullied had been unpersoned enough that they would have absolutely no mercy.

The Sullied tried to smash and push their way into the door, but the mana seal kept them out. The whole corridor trembled as the full strength of a Mana Circuitry cultivator kicked the door, putting their back into it. But the door did not budge for now.

Cale weighed his options. He really weighed them. He could escape. He could just leave and try his luck with another faction. He could go to Arcane Veil, telling them he changed his mind. And Darius would fail his advancement.

I don’t want to do that.

As much of a bastard Darius could be, Cale owed him. Darius had taken a chance with him. The man could have just killed Cale in the ruins. Sure, Aura would have been lost with him, but Darius couldn’t be sure of that.

And while half of the recruits inside were complete idiots, they were still Cale’s comrades. He tried to think like a cold-blooded cultivator would in this situation. But he found he didn’t want to.

Loyalty meant something to him. He asked himself why, but couldn’t find an answer. It meant something because it did. The same way it wasn’t okay punching a child, because it wasn’t. And Cale would be damned if he didn’t follow that instinct.

He came to a very simple realization.

If I turncloak now—run to Arcane Veil with my tail between my legs—will I ever be able to look myself in the eye?

No.

Not in this life. Not in any.

That was not a version of Cale he could bear.

And that realization made things very simple.

If he would rather die than sell his soul, then he would.

But first he would fight.
