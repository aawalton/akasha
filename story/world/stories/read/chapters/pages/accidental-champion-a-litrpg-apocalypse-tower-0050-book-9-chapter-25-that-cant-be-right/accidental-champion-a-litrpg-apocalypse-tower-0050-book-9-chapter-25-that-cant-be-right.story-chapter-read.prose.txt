Xavier, Queen Aecia, Brarri, Yalia, and Evali flew back down to the planet, passing through the planetary shield with ease, once more making it back into the grand throne room.

He’d tried to think of another plan, but the beams had started firing randomly out into space. He knew that soon enough, it would be impossible to avoid them, and he also knew that none of them would be able to survive a hit from one of those damned beams.

Returning to the planet was the best and only plan he had.

While up there, he had been able to locate the enemy time mage—or at least the ship the field came from—but there wasn’t anything he could do about it. Yes, he’d been able to break through their force fields, but he’d only been able to do that with time.

There was no way he’d manage that now.

Trying would only get him killed.

My life is worth more than such recklessness.

When they’d passed through the planetary shield, the enemy’s voice in his mind finally ceased its incessant chatter.

He’d thought he’d figured out an elegant, masterful way of defeating the ships’ crews, by taking the fight to them, but in the end it had only backfired, almost getting him killed—not to mention the queen and her people.

He stood on the throne room’s wraparound balcony, gazing up at the stars above, pushing his natural sight past it and to the ships in space.

“You’re going to have to let time pass,” Queen Aecia said. “We’ll fight them on the ground—you’ve already proven that you can. Why do you hesitate?”

“I’m not hesitating,” Xavier replied. “I’m deliberating.”

“Procrastinating, more like.” The queen came to stand beside him. “I’ve never met anyone as powerful as you. Not even Mother was strong enough to do what you’ve done. Why do you wish to circumvent the System’s plan for this floor? You can handle the waves, can’t you?”

“I can handle the waves. Even the other Champions, given the right conditions. I’m confident about that.”

“And when that time mage arrives?”

“Things might get complicated, and something tells me the fleet doesn’t only possess the one time mage. That many Denizens, there are bound to be more—and they know some of what I can do now.”

“So why not let the waves come? One every ten minutes. After what you did up there in… not even a second of universe time…”

Xavier pursed his lips. This queen was perceptive. It wasn’t the waves he was worried about. It was time he was worried about.

The time that would pass as he waited for each wave to arrive.

And the time that would pass back home, for all the planets under his rule, every one of them inside a time dilation field of their own.

Not only that—for the threats that would come once he allowed time to pass.

Jhanku’s agents, and likely still others would come.

He’d ensured his friends and allies would be strong enough to defend his sectors, but what if he’d been wrong?

He briefly did the math. Ten minutes. Three million times, maybe a little less given the ships he’d already taken out.

That was three million minutes.

Over 57 years…

And after only three hours of Standard Universe Time back in Arrack’s base, decades had passed for his people in the Silver River and Ventorin sectors because of the time dilation fields.

So how much time would go by for his people in 57 years?

There were 499,320 hours in 57 years. If an hour was roughly equivalent to a decade, then all he needed to do was times that number by 10—

No…

That can’t be right…

Almost 5 million years!

Every muscle in his body went slack as that number hit him. He’d known time dilation was powerful, but a number like that?

He managed to keep his grip on the railing and straightened back up.

They would assume he was dead.

He hadn’t even alerted Adranial, nor anyone else, of which floor he’d entered. No one knew where he was, and the System prevented him from using his universe-spanning Communication Stones.

That was why he hesitated.

Yes, he had no doubt that he could kill every single one of those waves if he let them through in the way the floor was supposed to function.

But the cost was too damned great.

Leaning heavily against the balcony’s railing, he took a moment to explain this to Queen Aecia. The woman deserved to know what was going through his mind. It was her world he was here to protect, after all.

Queen Aecia stared at him a long moment after he’d finished. “Two sectors, and every world within them under time dilation fields, no one affected by a lack of cooldowns… such a thing sounds like madness. The power you wield, that you’ve given others to wield…”

“It’s real. I’m telling the truth.” Xavier held out his hand. A memory orb appeared hovering over it.

Queen Aecia eyed the orb, reached for it, then stopped herself. “I believe you without needing to see. You are worried for them. Worried what will happen in your absence. Such a long absence… Are you to quit this floor, Xavier Collins? Is that why you have told me all this? I can see why you would, in your mind you are trading the protection of my world for the protection of two galaxies… and my world isn’t under your domain, so it lacks importance.”

“Your people are important,” Xavier said, but even he could tell his voice lacked a certain conviction.

“But yours are more important.”

“There is no exiting this floor.” Xavier glanced up at the sky once more, though this time he wasn’t looking to the ships. He was imagining the void—for that was the only exit he could think of. It certainly wasn’t one the System would have conceived.

Even if it had, that was the one place the System couldn’t reach him.

He’d abandoned floors in the past. Queen Aecia’s people deserved to be protected, but she was right—his people were more important.

Five million years. Even if no harm came to my sectors, even if they managed to fight off everyone in the 57 years that passed in Standard Universe Time, I’d be nothing but a memory.

Still, he wasn’t ready to abandon this floor yet. He had options. A lot of them. That was what he was going through as he stood there. And he could do it for a damned long time inside that field of his.

“I need time to think,” he said.

Queen Aecia inclined her head. “Then take it.” She retreated back into the throne room, returning to the others, chatting softly with her entourage.

Xavier closed his eyes and withdrew into the rooms of his mind. This would be the perfect time for him to ask a question of Roln. A lot of questions. He missed the mental construct, and not just for his knowledge. The dead Wanderer was still huddled in the same spot in the corner of one of his mind’s rooms.

He went there now, with his full consciousness. Even the other version of the dead Wanderer, inside Xavier Prime’s mind, hadn’t known what to do. As knowledgeable as he was, he hadn’t been the one to insert himself in Xavier’s head—that honour went to the leader of his Wanderer faction.

Roln didn’t know how to copy himself. Even if he did, Xavier didn’t want to settle for a copy. He wanted the original Roln.

I’d be dead if he hadn’t protected my mind.

Perhaps it was foolish. The mental construct didn’t possess a soul. He wasn’t a real person anymore. He wasn’t even a ghost. He was more like a program running inside Xavier’s head.

What did it matter if he had the original or not?

It matters to me.

Xavier spent some time with the dead Wanderer, trying to talk to him, but also trying to examine the connection in his own brain. This was a skill he didn’t possess, one he’d never bothered to learn. The dead Wanderer could improve the neural wiring in Xavier’s brain.

Why did he have to learn how to do it himself?

Now, without the wanderer, learning the ability was probably the only way he would ever be able to actually fix Roln.

He spent a good few hours on that, making a very small amount of progress, before he withdrew his main consciousness from the dead Wanderer’s room and instead started running different simulations for how to deal with the fleet.

He could get back out there, into space, and confront them head on a second time. But nothing had changed.

The problem was the shielding.

It was too damned powerful. Nothing he’d thrown at those shields could get through them but the slow manipulation of their base energy using his Immortal Soul lens, and time. With the enemy time mage, he simply couldn’t figure out how to recreate those same conditions to break through them again.

In a way, it highlighted just how weak he was. Though perhaps weak wasn’t the right word.

Vulnerable.

When he’d first received Time Alteration, he’d never imagined the spell would be something he’d grow to rely on to this degree. He’d never imagined he would use it in such a way, or be able to spread it to so many people. All the limitations it had possessed had been things he’d managed to circumvent, ultimately making it the most powerful tool in his arsenal.

And yet again, taking that tool away—just like it had been taken away when he’d faced Arrack—had left him vulnerable to the true powers out there.

Xavier hadn’t cowered behind a time dilation field back on the fifth floor when he’d faced down wave after wave of the Endless Horde.

But god, so much had changed since he’d been on that floor. And that tower floor had its own time manipulation going on—only an hour would pass on that floor whenever any Denizen entered it, meaning it didn’t much matter how long he took to defeat the various waves, he could stay there as long as he liked without much changing back home.

This was an entirely different scenario.

He couldn’t let 57 years pass in his own universe, and he certainly couldn’t let millions of years pass for his people.

Xavier gripped the railing so tight the stone crumbled beneath his fingers.

An idea formed in his head. He didn’t know if it would work. The System and its timers were an interesting thing. He used to assume whatever timer the System gave him worked on the universes time, but that wasn’t always the case.

Back on Earth and on other planets in the Silver River sector, Xavier had studied the art of creating dungeons and taught that art to others. It was the best way he could find for his people to gain levels and advance. He’d even considered completing dungeons himself to gain Mastery Points in order to reach B Grade, but farming dungeon after dungeon hadn’t appealed to him as much as moving through the tower floors had. Each tower floor held something new, something potentially interesting. And the breakthroughs that he’d made on tower floors in the past made the draw a strong one for him…

But he’d learnt a great deal by creating dungeons. One of the things he’d learnt was that he could force a System timer to change depending on how the time dilation field he used influenced it.

He’d discovered this early on in his dungeon experiments, which was fortunate, otherwise his Denizens would have had to wait a very long time while in a time dilation field for many of the dungeons to readmit them, as most of them had timers.

He’d also needed to ensure that the time dilation fields around Earth didn’t interact with the restriction timer that was still around the planet, protecting it from any Denizen of a higher level than the highest levelled inhabitant from coming to the planet.

All of this meant that Xavier might very well not have to let time pass to kickstart the System’s timer on this floor. If he manipulated it in the right way, he could get it moving at the rate of speed he was currently experiencing within his own shield.

This, however, had consequences.

Xavier ran countless simulations, trying to figure out if there was an alternate plan that he could use. Something a little less risky.

There wasn’t.

It was either this or try to escape the floor through the void—which had its own risks.

And might very well not even work.

When Xavier finally opened his eyes, he knew exactly what he needed to do. He walked over to Queen Aecia and her entourage and explained his plan.

They exchanged a glances, but none argued.

“You can really do that?” Brarri asked. “And sustain the shield for long enough?”

Xavier nodded. “I believe it will work.”

Queen Aecia. “Then let’s get started.” She looked at her throne room’s ceiling. “Bring forth the enemy. Show them that coming here was a mistake.”

“We’ll have to kill every single one of them,” Xavier said. “I won’t give them a chance to surrender.”

Queen Aecia locked eyes with him. “I am no longer concerned with that.”

Xavier glanced about the vast hall. He wasn’t sure where the enemy would arrive, but something told him it would be here, in the throne room. He looked at the fleet of enemy ships through The All-Seeing Eye. The ships were no longer stationary. Their beams were still shooting out almost randomly, trying to find where Xavier and the others were hiding, not aware they’d fled. They remained inside the other time dilation field.

Xavier cracked his neck, released a breath, then pushed his time dilation field outward, expanding it first around the planet itself, then farther so it contained the fleet. Then, finally, he pushed it toward the barren moon.

The Champions would arrive. Receive their notifications. Make their choices. He wasn’t going to bother trying to convince them to fight for him.

It didn’t take long for a notification to appear in his vision.

You are the only party to choose to fight for Galadsea.

As the Empire of Servitude’s ships cannot break through Galadsea’s shields, their crews will be teleported down to the planet in waves every ten minutes.

The countdown timer for the first wave starts now.

Xavier placed the timer in the top right corner of his vision. It started ticking down, synced to the time within his own field, just as he’d planned.

As Xavier’s time dilation field had encompassed the entire area that the floor was supposed to happen on, and thus the entire area that the timer influenced, he was able to force the timer to adhere to the same time stream.

There was a catch to what he’d done, however.

From all his testing, it could only be done once. If he altered the time stream even by a fraction, the timer would revert back to normal, and he wouldn’t be able to influence it a second time.

That meant he couldn’t slow down or speed up his time dilation field. Not if he wanted to clear this floor in any reasonable amount of time.

At least the enemies won’t be able to cooldown their spells…

Xavier stared at the timer ticking down.

He couldn’t help himself—he smiled. This hadn’t been his original plan. Facing a hundred thousand peak B Grades and a strong A Grade while they were frozen out of his field had been incredibly easy.

Facing them where they could move and fight and cast spells?

That would be anything but.

He relished the challenge.
