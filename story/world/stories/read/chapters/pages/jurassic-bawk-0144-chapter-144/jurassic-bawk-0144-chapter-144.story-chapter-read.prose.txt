“Right!  Right!  Move to the right!” Phasima yelled as the ship took another broadside.

“Bawk!” Charlemagne yelled back, not appreciating the advice.

“Perhaps you should increase speed,” Phatagin suggested.

“No, slow down!” Josephine hollered from below deck.

“Bawk!” the rooster repeated.

Charlemagne ignored the blathering of his shipmates as he focused his full attention on controlling his vessel.  It was incredibly frustrating that he could not simply take off and destroy the enemy ships by himself, but some irresistible force had frozen his body and forced his consciousness high into the air.  The entire world felt flat and lacking in detail.  Even the rooster’s mana senses had been affected by the change.  If only his hearing had been similarly impacted.

“Ahhhhh!!!” a shrill voice screeched as the ship took more hits.

The rooster responded with a flex of will, causing the cannons on the starboard side of the ship to fire.  Charlemagne watched as fifteen tiny black balls shot out and arced toward one of the dozen smaller vessels swarming everywhere.  Most splashed into the water, but a few hit their target.

“Very nice!” Phatagin cried.

“Yeah, right,” Phasima rebutted with a snort.

Charlemagne was too busy steering the ship to snap at them again, once again using his mind to tell the vessel to turn around so he could fire the cannons on the other side of the ship.  The vessel took a few glancing blows as it swung away from the battle and made as tight a loop as the galleon could handle.

Another volley blasted out from the port side of the ship as soon as it completed its turn.  The closest of the black-sailed ships erupted into flames and then almost immediately sank below the waves as it split into pieces.  The rooster took grim pleasure in the destruction, forgetting for a moment that he was still in combat until a volley rattled the ship.

“We seem to be taking a large amount of damage,” Phatagin noted quite unhelpfully.

Charlemagne ignored the pangolin’s comment and turned his ship again, this time directly into the fray.  By sheer luck, the tactic worked.  The identical pirate ships had only middling aim, and, by sailing directly between them, the rooster succeeded in getting them to damage each other.  But the real damage came from being able to fire off both broadsides, one after the other.

Boom!  Boom!

Thirty cannonballs slammed into the pirate ships, every single one scoring a hit due to the rich target environment.  Charlemagne crowed in triumph as another ship sank.

“Why are we stopping?” Phasima yelled.  “We’re sitting ducks here!”

The ship shuddered as another round of impacts sent wood splinters flying everywhere.  Josephine screamed again.

“This is untenable,” Phatagin complained.  “We shall be ripped to shreds!”

More cannonballs struck the ship as Charlemagne waited for his guns to reload.

Boom!  Boom!

Another enemy ship burst into flames and sank.

“Woooooooo!” Phasima cried.

The rooster crowed in triumph before turning his attention to the small green rectangles that appeared next to each of the ship’s cannons.  He had learned from experience that the guns would not fire again until the rectangles had grown back to their full length.  While he waited, he surveyed the battlefield.  There were still ten ships swarming around him, using their smaller size and maneuverability pass rapidly in and out of his line of fire.  Luckily for Charlemagne, the black-sailed enemy ships didn’t seem to have a battle plan.  Even better, they lacked coordination, more often than not interfering with each other as they sought to line up broadsides.  Several actually fired when their own allies were in the way, causing substantial damage.

All of this meant that the ship took less damage than it should have despite violating one of the core tenents of naval combat, which is “you go slow, you die”.  That being said, there were still ten enemies firing as quickly as they could, so Charlemagne’s vessel took continued damage as he waited for his guns to reload.  As soon as they were ready, he fired.

Had the rooster waited a few seconds, he would have caught the ship coming up at nearly point-blank range on his port side with a full broadside.  As it was, the enemy ship was heavily damaged.  But its cannons were still functional.

Boom!

The ship rocked again as a devastating attack slammed into it.  Screams rang out as splinters impacted the crew like shrapnel, and one unlikely sailor was sent flying overboard.  Well, the pieces that used to be a crewmember, that is.

“What’s happening!” Josephine screamed.

“We’re getting creamed!” Phasima responded.

“Bawk!” Charlemagne shouted back.

The trio’s yelling was interrupted by the hum that presaged another incoming salvo.  Charlemagne could do nothing but watch from his strange vantage point above the ship as the shots approached almost lazily.  That is, until Phatagin let out an enormous yell.  The cannonballs suddenly lost velocity, splashing harmlessly into the water a few moments later.

“I…I was successful,” he panted.  “But I do not believe that I have the strength to intervene again.”

“Bawk?” the rooster asked.

“There does not appear to be any ambient mana at all,” the pangolin answered.  “The only mana that I am able to sense is within your Core or Phasima’s.”

“Bawk!” Charlemagne ordered the Former Empress of the Forest.

“All right, I’ll give it a shot,” she answered with a shrug.  “It can’t be worse than sitting here and getting shot to pieces.”

The Vessarian reached into her Core and sent out a wave of fire-aspected mana toward the nearest enemy vessel, the one that had just fired into them at close range.  Moments later, the ship began to smoke.  After a few seconds of direct contact with Phasima’s impromptu flamethrower, the enemy caught fire and sank.

“Whoohoo!” Phasima yelled as she swapped targets.

Another of the black-sailed ships fired a salvo, but they only succeeded in damaging one of their own vessels that was sailing toward Charlemagne’s starboard side.  The noise pulled his attention away from what the Vessarian was doing and back to managing the ship.  Seeing that the cannons were ready, he fired again.  Two more enemy ships sank.

“Got another,” reported the former Empress of the Forest as her latest target caught fire and sank. “There’s only six left!”

“That is quite enough for me,” grumped Phatagin as he strained to deflect a stray cannonball that was on course to hit the ship’s stern.  He managed to push it off course enough to miss their ship entirely.  Unfortunately, the missed shot was just a prelude.  Moments later, a tight cluster of shots struck near the aft of the ship.

“Bawk!” Charlemagne demanded.

Phasima gamely targeted another ship, this time one that was quite a distance away.  She grunted as she struggled to control the tight beam of superheated mana across such a long gap.  This time it took almost ten seconds for the ship to catch fire, and the Vessarian looked absolutely spent once it sank.

“I’m tapped out!” she declared.

“Perhaps now that there are only five enemy vessels left, we could pursue them one by one?” the pangolin suggested, hoping that Charlemagne would take the hint and get the boat moving again.  The rooster bawked his agreement and ordered the vessel to accelerate to full speed ahead.

The battered ship began to lurch forward, but something was wrong.  They were barely moving compared to their previous speed.  The only silver lining was that the pirates were still completely unable to take advantage of the situation.  They continued to sail in and out of Charlemagne’s line of fire.  Seeing that both sets of cannons were ready, the rooster fired again.

“Charlemagne, you need to wait until you have a good shot to take it,” Phasima told him in the aftermath of the double volley.

The rooster considered the suggestion.  It was true that he hadn’t hit anything, but the cannons were already regrowing their green rectangles.  If he waited to shoot, the cannons would be wasting valuable green rectangle growing time.  But, on the other hand, was the point to regrow the rectangles, or to sink the other ships?

The rooster thought hard for a moment.  He eventually decided that, yes, regrowing the rectangles was not as important as sinking the black-sailed ships.  It was more important to hit something than to just shoot as often as he could.

“Bawk!” he shouted back in agreement.

Things went a good bit smoother after that.  Charlemagne waited until he had a good shot lined up before firing his port cannons, heavily damaging another ship.  He then focused on the starboard side, scoring a similarly effective blow moments later.  That led to a whole new problem that occupied all of his attention.

The cannons were no longer synchronized.

While the rooster wallowed in confusion, the ship took another volley.

“We need to move faster!” Phasima yelled.  “Phatagin, can you make us go any faster?”

“We are far too massive for me to affect in that manner,” the pangolin complained.  “Can you not replenish your Core and continue burning the enemy?”

“If I could, I would have already done that!” Phasima shot back.

Ignoring the bickering, Charlemagne came to another decision.  He watched as both sides of the ship regrew all their rectangles, and then waited until an enemy came into range.

Boom! Boom!

The rooster fired both sets of cannons simultaneously.

“It really is unnecessary to fire all cannons at once.  Perhaps you could consider allowing me to tell you when the time is right,” Phatagin called out.

“Bawk!” the rooster shouted back in anger.  He was not about to allow someone else to call the shots.

Charlemagne ignored any further suggestions as he slowly hunted down and sank the remaining enemy ships.  It took him longer than he expected, because the final ship seemed more interested in constantly running into the invisible barrier that limited the battle to a fixed area.   The ship continued to take damage throughout this process, and two additional crew members were lost overboard.  The rooster considered that a small price to pay for victory.  After what seemed like an eternity, the last enemy ship was nothing more than a crow’s nest and bubbles.

Charlemagne crowed in triumph as the world returned to its normal orientation and his feet were no longer stuck to the deck.  He reveled for a few moments in just feeling his mana circulate through his body.  As he breathed, he noticed that the ambient mana had returned and that it was quite a bit thicker than it had been previously.  Perhaps it was being in the presence of the Ark, or perhaps it was the legendary weapon that the Squiggles told him he would find inside.  Charlemagne’s mouth began to water with anticipation.

He couldn’t wait to eat the weapon.

“Uh, Charlemagne, maybe you should give the captain orders to start fixing up our ship while we go into the Ark?” Phasima suggested.

“The ship does appear to be heavily damaged.  I suppose it is fortuitous that Charlemagne was able to capture so many other vessels,” Phatagin added.

Josephine emerged from below decks, looking worse for wear.  She glanced around, taking in the ship’s sorry state in an instant.  The sails were shredded, the hull was cracked in multiple places, and one of the masts was listing at an alarming angle.

“I can handle the orders here, so long as you let me sit on that island while you’re in the Ark,” she offered, pointing to the tiny patch of sand that barely qualified as an island.  “By the way, we may need to stay here for a while until the ship is repaired, since it’s heavily damaged.  And, is there any chance you noticed some sort of indicator that gave you an idea of how close the ship was to sinking?  That would be good to know in case we encounter future combat situations.”

Charlemagne focused his attention on the ship.  As he did so, a giant rectangle appeared in his vision above the deck, floating in midair.  It was almost entirely blank, with a tiny red line on the left-hand side.  If he was reading it right, it meant that the ship had barely taken any damage at all.

“Bawk!” he answered happily.
