The third member of the Council of Death died by his hands, and Asher took a shaky breath as he watched The Lost Lady’s body crumple to the ground beside her own head. Her hair went just as limp as the rest of her body, and Moxy crashed to the ground, coughing out another mouthful of blood as the thick spear currently embedded in her chest fell out, allowing the blood and viscera to flow freely. Even after everything Asher had seen in his time killing on this world, the giant, gaping hole in Moxy’s chest was enough to make him feel queasy.

“Moxy!” Samantha shouted, rushing over to her and dropping to her knees in the large puddle of blood. The hunter’s face blanched as she took in the state of the warrior, and tears welled up in her eyes as water began pouring out of her hand. “I… I can use Soothing Spring! Maybe it will help!”

“That wound is far too large for your skill to work on,” Rulfar said, his voice somber as he walked over to join them. A quick glance back confirmed that the minion he’d been fighting against had finally fallen, and Rulfar sighed as he stood over Moxy’s body. “I doubt even Moxy’s Life element would be enough to patch something like that up before it killed her.”

“I can’t use my skills…” Moxy got out weakly, her voice little more than a whisper as she gave them all a shaky smile. “My blended skill… Overheal… I won’t even be able to move for a couple of hours… probably my least favorite skill…. why I never showed it to you all before now.”

“You did it though,” Samantha said, sniffling as she held the proud warrior’s dying body in her arms. “Even if Asher finished her off, you’re the one who kept her distracted and made it possible. If she hadn’t turned her full focus on you, who knows what would have happened.”

“Yeah… I’m awesome,” Moxy agreed, coughing up more blood as she turned to look at him. “Well… guess that means you’ll get out of any more sparring sessions, eh, Runt? Thanks for giving me the chance to go out in the mother of all battles… It’s everything I ever wanted.”

“Anytime,” Asher said, grinning sheepishly at her as he cleared his throat. “I’ll admit, I was going to let this go on until you figured it out yourself, but the longer this drags on for, the more embarrassing it’s starting to feel.”

“Drags on for…?” Moxy repeated, blinking in confusion until she looked down at her ruined body. “Wait… I’m not bleeding?”

“Nah, I used Permanence’s second-tier effect on you the moment you had a wad of hair the size of a telephone pole shoved through your chest,” Asher admitted, grinning at the three shocked looks he got. “I can stop someone from bleeding out, evidently even if they’re missing a good quarter of their chest. Doesn’t even use tritessence after the initial application!”

“Runt!” Moxy said, laughing and coughing at the same time as her weak grin turned genuine and her eyes lit up at the realization she wasn’t going to die just yet. “You bastard!”

“Just be lucky she didn’t go for your heart,” Asher said, leaning down and inspecting her horrific wound more closely. “I can stop you from bleeding out, but I can’t force a body to stay alive if it’s missing all the important stuff. Your heart, brain, and one lung are intact by the looks of it. Though seeing as I’m quite literally looking at your ‘intact lung’ with my own two eyes, you’re in need of some serious healing.”

“Thank the realms for my resistances,” Moxy chuckled, spitting out another mouthful of blood. “This would probably hurt pretty bad otherwise.”

“I’ll get started on healing her the best I can,” Samantha said, so happy to hear that Moxy was going to be okay that she didn’t even look annoyed with him for his bad prank. “Asher, can you pull out Kroult? Even if Moxy’s pain is dulled and she’s too proud to admit it, I bet she’s still hurting. Kroult’s Pain element can take that away.”

“Oh. Huh, I completely forgot Kroult had a Pain element,” Asher admitted, gritting his teeth as he pulled the lich from his rift. Moving things into and out of his rift on the material plane cost almost no tritessence, but at the moment, Asher had almost no tritessence. He needed a good couple of minutes to just sit down and rest, but there was always more work to be done.

The moment Kroult appeared, he spotted The Lost Lady’s fallen body and the green fires in his eye sockets flared in shock.

“You killed her. You actually did it.”

“I’m taking credit for this one!” Moxy called up weakly. “Asher can have the next!”

“Moxy is willingly offering up the next lich?” Rulfar asked, frowning as he looked down at her. “…Are we sure she’s not dying?”

“It probably feels to her like she’s dying,” Asher admitted. “I’d recommend avoiding getting to the point where I have to do this to you. Not just because of that, but because I can only do it to one person at a time. If two people ever end up in this bad a shape… let’s just say please don’t force me to make that kind of decision.”

“Noted,” Rulfar nodded, hefting his axe as he looked around the cavern. “Well, I suppose we did what we came here to do. All that’s left now is doubling back for those minions and seeing if Richard’s dagger works as promised.”

“Grant is still missing,” Asher reminded him. “I’m hopeful that we’ll find him standing there aimlessly now that The Lost Lady is no more, but with our luck, her death might force him into some sort of berserker rage. We shouldn’t let down our guards just yet.”

“True. Though we can take it a little easy at least,” Rulfar said, shrinking back down to his positively tiny seven-foot-tall frame.

Asher wasn’t certain what benefits drinking a potion of Lesser Restoration would even have, seeing as Moxy’s stomach was sort of… gone. But he still left Samantha and Kroult with a couple of them anyway as he and Rulfar headed off to pick up the minions they’d left alive. Despite having had his face angled up the entire time he’d been controlled, Kroult’s memory once again proved to be fantastic, as the lich was able to walk them through exactly what turns they had to make to reach the farm from their current position.

As the two of them walked, Asher realized he and Rulfar didn’t really ever spend any time with one another without at least another member of the team around. Trying to find something to talk about, he grinned up at the large man.

“So… Unyielding Blood. Pretty cool blended skill.”

“It has its uses,” Rulfar said, staring straight ahead with a hard look on his face.

Asher could only guess that Rulfar wasn’t in the mood to talk, and he took the hint, letting the topic drop. Yet after another minute of walking through blank, boring tunnels, Rulfar surprised him with a sigh.

“I’m sorry… I know you’re just trying to make conversation. And from a tactical standpoint, knowing all of our teammates skills is obviously for the best. It’s just… I have strong feelings about my Blood element. Strong enough that half the reason I agreed to join up with the three of you when you sought my aid against the Council of Death was because I thought I might be able to figure out a way to use their extraction ritual to rip it out of me.”

“What?!” Asher asked, staring at the bloodhound in shock even as the northman shook his head.

“Don’t worry, that ship has officially sailed. After seeing just what The Lost Lady’s experiments ended up doing to these men… I realized I’d never stopped to think about what the people who underwent the extraction ritual ended up looking like afterward. Of course the Council of Death wouldn’t care if the people in the ritual turned into nothing more than empty husks. It was just that the thought of having this curse removed from my soul… I leapt at the opportunity without thinking it through.”

“You’re seriously that upset about your Blood element?” Asher asked, trying to take all these revelations in stride as they walked. He’d picked up on the fact that Rulfar had been interested in the extraction ritual, but he’d never imagined it was because he wanted to use it on himself. “I mean, I know it’s a forbidden element in the Noala Kingdom. But it’s not forbidden everywhere.”

“It is not because it is forbidden. It is because of what it represents,” Rulfar said, his voice turning hard once more as his eyes looked at something only he could see. “My family. My tribe. It is all gone. Wiped out by a terrible roaming monster unlike anything our country had ever seen. I am the only survivor of my entire tribe. I’d left to go on a hunting trip, and when I returned, everyone was dead. Rivers of frozen blood filled the camp, and corpses in various states of being eaten were all that was left of those that I once called my kin. And in the midst of it all, sitting there like a present, was a Blood element.”

Rulfar sighed, running a hand through his hair as his face took on a tired expression. “Understandably, I was a little shaken up at the time. I was a much younger man, and far quicker to act without thinking. I took the Blood element in as my third element, determined to use the blood of my people to enact vengeance. Only, as I worked to track the monster down and do so, I discovered an elite slayer team had already been dispatched from the capital and had taken it down. You see, our tribe was only the last in a long line of devastating attacks, unbeknownst to me.

“Suddenly, I was without purpose. The Blood of my people went from feeling like I was carrying around their dying wishes to feeling as if I was dragging along their unwilling souls. Like they couldn’t be allowed to rest so long as I lived. I am not ashamed to admit I considered taking my own life, but the thought of meeting my family again in the afterlife and having to see their faces… it stayed my hand.”

“You believe in an afterlife?” Asher asked without thinking, wincing as he realized that was probably the wrong thing to say after Rulfar had bared himself like this to him. Thankfully, the bloodhound merely smiled.

“Indeed. I believe I will see my family again one day. It is a good feeling.”

“I remember you mentioning a while back that your people also believed in a tenth hell, despite everyone else saying there are only nine,” Asher said, deciding to roll with the topic rather than force Rulfar to continue dwelling on such painful memories of his dead tribe. “You never explained any of that.”

“There is a legend passed down among my people of a horrific monster unlike any other,” Rulfar said, his face turning dark and stormy. “A horrifying foe that is responsible for more death than any plague or famine. Its appearance, abilities, and even name has been lost to time, but its existence is remembered. We believe it crawled out of a tenth hell, and it was our people’s sworn mission to help force it back there one day.”

“That’s some goal. Doubly so if you don’t even know what it looks like.”

“Indeed it is,” Rulfar said, giving him a small smile. “But that is something for me to think about. Anyway, you don’t need to worry. I no longer have any interest in the Council’s extraction ritual. The thought of turning myself into anything like these minions of The Lost Lady… it is almost as bad as the thought of claiming my own life. My family wouldn’t want something like that for me, so I will instead continue to carry the burden of my people as I have all this time. One day I will perish, be it in battle or some other cause, and then I can finally let my people rest.”

“I hope this doesn’t come off as rude, but I hope that day is far from now,” Asher admitted. “You’re a really good guy. Not to mention really easy to spot in a crowd.”

“I’ll admit, not a strong suit I’ve had people point out before,” Rulfar snorted. “But don’t worry, unlike a certain battle maniac who currently has a hole in her chest, I’m in no rush to die. My people have waited this long, I doubt another few years will make any more of a difference. I’d rather spend my life doing good so I can meet up with them again with a smile on my face.”

“That’s the spirit!” Asher grinned, facing forward again as they neared the cave Kroult had directed them toward. “Let’s start with seeing if we can’t reunite these people with their families!”
