Waking up to the real world, Ferris decided to tackle the unpleasant task of dealing with his ruined bed.

He found Mother Lily and brought her to his room to see the damage. After entering the room and seeing the bed, she grabbed him and started a close examination.

“Ferris, dear, are you hurt anywhere?” she asked, looking him over from top to bottom. “And Jade, as well?” She shifted her gaze to the slime on the ground.

“We are fine, Mother Lily,” Ferris said, more assuredly than he felt. “We had an… accident in our cultivation, but it should be resolved now.”

“Oh, you poor dears,” Mother Lily lifted him up into a large hug. Ferris still wasn’t used to the woman’s affection, but after today, it was sort of… nice?

“These things are never simple or easy,” Mother Lily said, setting him down. “Know that you aren’t the first child in my care to have such problems. I am here for you if you need me.”

Ferris still resented being called a child, but at least she was taking it better than he had feared.

“Now,” she said, moving to stand over the ruined mattress, “I am afraid we won’t be able to get you a new mattress until tomorrow. I have a futon that I can bring up for tonight after we clear away this mess.”

After taking a closer look at the damage, she turned back to him.

“Please don’t take this too hard,” she said, “but the clan has strict rules about the use of hazardous abilities outside of the training halls. There is a penalty of three merit points for any first offense, no matter the intention or reasons.”

Ferris nodded, a bit numb to the losses of the day. Those three merit points were three days of backbreaking work, and it meant he wouldn’t be able to afford Acid Resistance in time for his meeting with Serena. At the same time, he couldn’t really fault the clan for having a strict policy. It was nice that Mother Lily believed his intentions, but that didn’t change the reality. How else would they keep people from practicing dangerous abilities in their rooms? If things had gone differently, this accident could have hurt or killed the other residents of the house. Ferris certainly didn’t want Brix to practice explosive alchemy one room away.

At the same time, Ferris didn’t regret his actions. Jade’s life was more important, and the three merit points were a small price to pay in comparison.

“I understand,” Ferris said, “and I will take action to prevent such accidents in the future.”

Mother Lily ruffled his hair.

“Don’t be so serious,” she said, “now, help me clean this up. Then we will get you a new set of sheets.”

They gathered everything up and moved it out of his room. Ferris carried what he could with his crutch, and Jade followed them around, but Mother Lily did most of the real work. Soon, they had a sleeping pad rolled out on the ground with a new set of bedding laid out on top. It wasn’t quite as nice as the mattress had been, but it was comfy enough.

Afterwards, Ferris felt his stomach complain. He had skipped both breakfast and lunch, feeding his portions to Jade. Dinner was still a way off, so he decided to take Jade out to get some food. Mother Lily would likely have given them something to eat, but Ferris also wanted to get out of the house.

There were a couple of cafés near the library, and Ferris picked one at random. It wasn’t quite as colorfully decorated as the one he had visited in the sixth branch, but it was nice; more of a scholarly vibe. They had an assortment of pastries, and he splurged on ordering a full set.

He ate two of the puffy sugar-coated treats in a rush before slowing down enough to feed Jade alongside him. They worked their way through half a dozen sweet rolls together. The slime rippled happily with each piece that Ferris broke off and gave them, but something was missing.

After a second, Ferris realized that Jade wasn’t rating the food. Jade had rated every single bite of food since Ferris had taught the slime the eating game the day they had bonded. Jade loved that game. Ferris had even started to record Jade’s ratings for new food in his journal.

When Ferris had learned that Jade could retain some memories long term, the slime had said the food rating game hadn’t been ‘preserved.’ Jade simply played the game every time they ate, at least three times a day, so they never forgot it. He had even tested this recall method for a few days with constantly asking the same question to ‘keep a memory alive.’ It worked, but Jade forgot any context beyond the words of the answer.

Jade hasn’t rated any food at all today, Ferris thought, but then corrected himself. No, Jade did rate breakfast… before they were hurt. But nothing since.

“Jade,” Ferris said over the bond, “can you tell me again what you know about your memory?”

The slime swirled a bit in confusion, but answered.

“I can protect important memories, but not many. The rest get used up.”

The slime was still getting smarter day by day, yet at a gradual pace. It was hard to notice as it happened, but sometimes Ferris would be impressed by Jade’s ability to communicate better. It didn’t help if Jade had no new information, though.

Ferris considered Jade’s word choice. The slime had said the same thing last time, that memories are ‘used up.’ That was a strange way to talk about memories, but he hadn’t noticed it before. Jade often had strange or different perspectives on the world.

If there was anything that Ferris had seen ‘used up’ today, it was the ants in the slimespace. In fact, they regularly died every time Jade ate food, throwing themselves into the Consume Pool.

Ferris had seen several examples of the creatures in the slimespace, substituting the equivalent of a mundane body. The slime had the Digest River instead of an intestinal track. What about thoughts? The slime had no physical brain, after all, but was clearly intelligent.

Does the slimespace somehow magically recreate the ability to think and remember with… a hive of ants?

The idea was strange, but it felt like a better possibility than anything else he had. Ferris was searching for a way to help Jade remember for longer than a day, and this was his first potential lead.

Speaking of the duration of Jade’s memory, Foreman Carter had claimed that slimes forget things after a day. Ferris’ experiments showed variance between how long it took for Jade to forget, anywhere from eight hours to over twenty-four. What if Ferris tried the same tests, but based on how many ants had died in the slimespace? He could easily just feed Jade to cause more ants to jump into the Consume Pool and be dissolved in acid, or cause injuries to make the ants repair the dome’s wall. Even better if he tested both separately.

It was a bit morbid to think of. Ferris had never quite accepted the casual disregard the grey ants showed for their own lives, throwing themselves into acid. If eating and healing both required the ants’ death, and the ants were somehow the slime’s… brain, how could Ferris protect Jade from forgetting?

The obvious answer would be the Acid Resistance ability, but somehow extended to also protect the ants in the slimespace. Ferris had no idea if it was possible to change the ability in that way, but he was excited to have a way forward.

Feeling better from both the food and the theories, Ferris taught Jade the food rating game again. The slime had completely forgotten the high watermark from the magical berry. Everything was currently ten out of ten. Ferris would have to see if he could get more of the berries, or something similarly magical.

Pulling out his journal, Ferris looked over the notes he had taken on Jade’s food ratings and considered what to do next.

His meeting with Serena was in four days. After the three-merit penalty, he wouldn’t have enough points to purchase the technical manual for learning Acid Resistance. He would be able to afford it for the meeting after, though he didn’t have any way to contact her and request a delay.

Instead of working on Acid Resistance, he decided he would ask Serena to perform another test of Jade’s healing.

Gripping his hand into a fist, Ferris looked down at Jade. This time, he would need to ask her to seriously hurt the slime.

He didn’t like it. In fact, he hated it. But beyond the emotion, he’d lost all logical reasons against the test. He now had evidence that the slimespace could survive the flood. Neither his familiar’s Heartfire nor Core Tree had been affected by it. The dome hadn’t completely gone away or shrunk.

He needed to know better how the entire process worked. This was information that he could have gotten last time, but he’d been too cautious. That hurt him today, as he was forced to make decisions risking Jade’s life without knowing the full picture.

At the same time, he could test his theory on Jade’s memory. It would be inconvenient, but Ferris would avoid anything that harmed the ants in the slimespace for three days. If his theory were correct, Jade would fully recall all memories for all three days.

Unfortunately, this meant Jade couldn’t eat anything. The slime would not be happy, and Ferris would lose the generous qi production from the Digest River. Still… it would be worth it.

Then, at the meeting with Serena, Ferris and Jade could test the opposite. Let as many ants die as possible and recheck Jade’s memory.

With a plan, Ferris fed the rest of the pastries to Jade while enjoying the afternoon. He watched the slime closely for the rest of the day. He even checked on the slimespace three more times before sleeping. Mother Lily gave Jade an extra generous portion for dinner—above and beyond the usual—and Ferris let the slime eat two more bundles of straw afterwards.

The next morning, Ferris started a new page full of numbers in his journal. He read them all out to Jade over the bond, and then decided to make another page full of names, one full of types of food, and lastly one with species of fish for good measure. For the next three days, Ferris wouldn’t do anything that caused the slimespace ants to die.

After that, the great fasting began. Jade was not pleased with being denied breakfast, lunch, dinner, and all the extra made-up meals in between. Ferris had to deal with a couple of small tantrums on the first day, especially when he ate in front of the slime.

Ferris also took his meals hidden away in his room, with just Jade as company. He wanted to be discreet about anything related to Jade’s abilities and nature, and he didn’t want the other residents of Lily House to know he intentionally wasn’t feeding Jade. The slime’s appetite had become such a large part of mealtimes that they would notice it as odd. It was common for cultivators to sequester themselves away while working on something in their soul, so Ferris leaned into that as an explanation, rather than give any hints to Jade’s weaknesses.

Foreman Carter had mentioned that slimes could go for weeks without any food, but to hear Jade tell it, Ferris was torturing them to death.

But it worked.

Three days later, Jade hadn’t forgotten a single thing.
