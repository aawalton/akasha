I stretched slowly, feeling my spine unwind as the morning chill clung to my skin, and let my gaze drift across the duchy's main training grounds.

They sprawled wider than I had expected, far wider than Sealrite's crude yard had allowed, and wider still than the common grounds I'd used in preparation for the tournament. The duchy had clearly spared no expense in their creation. Packed earth formed the central drill field, beaten pale and hard by years of marching feet and fighting hands. Even now it bore the scars of discipline, its air still thick with remnant magic and spent energy. Ruts carved by shield walls marked the dirt in long, parallel furrows, while scuffed lines dug deep where soldiers had no doubt drilled in tight formation, again and again, until their bodies moved without thought.

Beyond the central field lay the weapon yards, divided into multiple segments. Each was occupied, ringing with dull, rhythmic violence. Steel met wood in steady cadence as Paragons tested themselves against pells sunk deep into the ground, the posts chewed to splinters and wrapped in leather and rope where the blows had grown too punishing to leave bare wood. Some of the posts hummed faintly, touched by a defensive enchantment I had come to recognize with ease, the magic making the faux opponents harder to damage and slower to break.

My eyes moved to the archery range. Long lanes stretched away beneath fluttering pennants, targets painted in fading reds and blacks. Arrows bristled from the straw butts, clustered tight at the center by veterans and scattered wide by the newly freed and still learning. All of them trained with the same expression, though. Determination and focus were their vanguard. A quiet, desperate need to move forward and leave behind what had been done to them in the Cael colosseums.

Some I had freed personally. Others had come later, after I'd seized control of the duchy and closed down the slave trade in Sealrite. The gladiator pits had been part of my original plans, but the practice sat poorly with what I was becoming, with the reputation that was quietly, stubbornly shaping itself around me.

That of a hero.

Along the edges of the grounds stood the barracks and supply sheds, practical and unadorned, though somewhat dated compared to what I remembered from Ordite. No ornament, no comfort beyond what was needed to keep bodies fed, clothed, and functional. The true military barracks lay outside the city center, in the more fortified quarter of Elyndor where the duchy's standing forces were housed. These were for my followers only, the ex-slaves and Paragons both, though not for much longer.

They would march again soon. There was more of Lysoria that needed to be purged.

I rolled my shoulders, felt the left one pop, and exhaled slowly as I watched them. The Paragons moved with coiled purpose, power riding just beneath the surface of their skin, their eyes sharp with a hunger that never quite left them. They were my elites, cultivators growing at speeds that should have been impossible, carried along by the current of the Desire System in ways ordinary practitioners could not fathom. Many had already gone, departing to walk their own roads. But some had chosen to stay, at least for now.

The ex-slaves were different. They hadn't passed through the Desire System's metamorphosis, had chosen instead to build themselves back up through sheer, grinding will. Blood and sweat and tears and stubborn refusal. After months of training and recovery, they were only now beginning to show true progress. They were still weak, incredibly so compared to the fast growing Paragons, and lacked that particular intensity the System seemed to press into everyone it touched.

Many still carried the evidence of what they had survived. Brands. Chain marks. Behaviors that spoke quietly of long months spent learning not to resist or speak. Not to want. Some bore scars the morning light did nothing to soften, flesh that would carry its history until their heart cores grew strong enough to begin rewriting it.

Yet they bled in the same dirt as the Paragons. Gripped the same weapons. Followed the same drills.

Elyndor may have given me an army, but Sealrite had blessed me with survivors and believers.

And soon, the country would give me a kingdom.

It just didn't know it yet.

In the meantime, it was the survivors and the believers who had the potential to provide me with the most power.

I clapped my hands, the sound echoing across the training yards. "Paragons, continue your training. Those without my Paragon mark, join me at the center field. It is time to discuss the next stages of your cultivation paths."

It would have been nice had there been a stage of some kind in the center field so I could keep my eyes on all the seedling-cultivators, but there wasn't. Other than a sizable patch of sand for combat demonstrations and an expanse of grass surrounding it, there were only a few logs and chairs for seating. To their credit, none of the followers sat in my presence, instead crowding where I stood on the sand.

"Sit," I commanded, gesturing to the ground with a flick of my wrist. They obeyed without hesitation, their eyes brimming with curiosity and desire. That was what I provided them, after all, access to desire, and answers to curiosity. For a brief moment, I mulled over the idea of imparting everything Orpheus had taught me in the dreamscape. If they all learned the truths about dantians and the method for creating Shen energy, there was a strong possibility of this group becoming some of the most powerful cultivators on Graedon, or at the very least on the Pularean continent.

Caution, however, warned me against it. Doing so risked exposing myself to the scrutiny of the continent's most powerful, whether that be independent cultivators or royalty. While my heart energy was quickly climbing the realms and my discovery of both mana and Shen energy was expediting my path back to power, I remained, for the moment, still at the gold realm. High, yes, but not high enough to fend off entire armies. If the intelligence I'd gathered held true, the Pandorian Empire fielded entire squadrons composed solely of bronze and silver realm cultivators. Enough of those, and even I could fall.

News limited to that of my deep understanding of heart energy might raise curiosity among scholars, but scholars of heart energy were likely common enough. A lecture on a new type of power, however, would be groundbreaking. World breaking, even.

Not wanting to deal with that, I chose to teach them the way I had learned on Ordite. Even without knowledge of Shen energy, I was certain the seedling-cultivators would come to dwarf those of Graedon, at least when it came to heart energy, given how fundamentally weak many heart cores here were.

"Many of you have managed to form a bronze core," I said, looking out at the sea of faces before me, pride mixed with the occasional flash of shame. "Some of you have not. Each individual moves down the path of heart cultivation at their own pace. It is not a race. Fast does not mean better. Slow does not mean stronger. Compare yourself to the you from yesterday, not to the person sitting beside you." I cleared my throat, stalking the edges of the sand pit so I could look at them all. "For those who have reached the bronze realm, listen closely. For those who haven't, pay just as much attention, for I will not be teaching this twice." Silence followed, the attention of every follower rapt and focused. "Once your heart reaches the bronze realm, you will discover something called heart attribute energy. Some here have referred to it as an affinity, but that is not quite correct. A heart attribute is the fundamental nature of your heart energy, the inherent makeup of your core, whether that be fire, water, metal, or even death. Heart attributes are near limitless in possibility. Before you progress beyond the bronze realm, strive to reach a pinnacle understanding of your attribute." I paused to let that settle. "An energy affinity, at most, is a type of energy your heart energy resonates with. This can include your attribute, but it is not limited to it. Someone with the heart attribute of fire has an affinity toward fire, but also toward heat, toward lightning, even toward wood energy."

As I spoke, I found myself wishing, not for the first time, that Nasq was there with me. The lesson felt half-baked without someone to speak to the magic side of things. And though I had awakened a mana core and learned to wield it, that had come more through instinct than genuine understanding. I could no more explain how I used magic than I could explain how I forced the two cores to interact with each other.

"For most of you, your heart rings will be bronze, in the likeness of your core realm. For some, if your attribute is powerful enough, your heart rings will take on a shade matching that attribute." I shrugged. "That is difficult to determine and not entirely relevant, but it does indicate the strength of your innate talent for your main attribute." I drew in a breath of cool air and let it settle before continuing. "The most important part of your cultivation now is to lean into your attributed energy. Your heart will continue to generate energy, energy you have gathered for cultivation. But that energy is raw. It is unattributed, a mixture of all attributes at once. In order to progress, you must refine it, keeping only the energy that matches your attribute. You may choose to retain some energy you have an affinity with, but take great care in doing so. It will broaden how you can apply your energy going forward, but at the cost of your mastery over the main attribute. I cannot tell you which path to take. Only you can know what is right."

At a few worried looks, I offered a dismissive smile.

"If you have already begun cultivating in the bronze realm without refining, you will need to empty your core of all its energy." They nodded. It wasn't the easiest process, but it wasn't particularly difficult either. A heart core was like a battery. The more powerful it became, the more it could store, but emptying it was simply a matter of pushing oneself until it was completely drained. Had any of the followers already formed a ring around their bronze core using unrefined energy, I would have needed to break it. That would have been more painful and time-consuming, but none had reached that point, so it was a moot concern.

"Those of you who are simultaneously working on a mana core, I am seeking to find a suitable magic mentor." Some nods, but no one spoke. Nasq's current state was no secret, nor was my concern over it. They all knew it was best to keep quiet, and they were right. "When you begin approaching the silver realm, I strongly advise waiting for me before you make that step. The progression can be treacherous if not handled properly, as it requires bringing your true self to the forefront of your existence. Without fully accepting yourself first, it can be quite jarring, even destructive, to your Reformation."

When someone spoke out with questions about the Reformation, I silenced them with a wave.

"A Reformation is difficult to explain. As is what I mean by your true self. It is something you must experience to understand, and understand to experience. If I try to explain it any further, it will only confuse you and put you at greater risk when facing that obstacle."
