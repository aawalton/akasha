“Leave him!” Rayle shouted. “He’s already dead.”

Rayle, you bastard! I gritted my teeth.

I gripped my left arm, using every combination of healing skills at my disposal to stop the bleeding. None of them worked. If I knew some fire spells, I would burn the stump itself and cauterize the wound. Sadly, I had never been able to invest in that field. All my money had gone for better weapons, and now I couldn’t even use those.

“Hold still.” Avon tried to restrict my movements while casting one of his healing spells.

I knew that he was trying to help me, and yet my body couldn’t stop reacting on its own. A foot away, my severed limb lay on the ground, still holding a bronze short sword.

“Forget the weapon!” Avon shook me, catching my glance. “If you stay, you’ll die.”

The air shook as two monstrous entities clashed in the distance. A Blade Serpent versus a Sword Dragon. Supposedly it was a fight for the ages, and naturally I, and the other idiots, had given in to temptation.

Forces beyond imagination slammed against each other, shredding everything in sight. Mountains were torn and thrust into the air like confetti, forests were torn out of the ground… even the two cities that had summoned the creatures had effectively been vaporized out of existence.

“It hurts!” I shouted, hoping the action would reduce the pain. It didn’t.

The plan had seemed so simple. Wait until one of the creatures was wounded enough so we could swoop in for the kill. The merchant had assured us that a single hit was enough for the Grand Arcana to consider us active participants. The whole strategy had been focused on that.

“Brace!” Rayle shouted as he cast his Light Shield.

He was the worst kind of asshole, who would constantly discuss morals between trials, then use the nearest person as a shield. Quite the irony that he got the Light Guardian card. We used to be reluctant friends at some point, though that was before he had joined the ranks of the Grand Arcana holders.

The ground erupted in all directions. The only spot that remained unharmed was the small circle of calm that was covered by the Light Shield’s dome.

Why didn’t you use that the first time?! I shouted in my mind.

The piece of shit had been so sure that we were out of reach that he hadn’t even entertained the idea.

A single stray strike had flown our way. The chances of it hitting anyone from such a distance were astronomically small. Of course, I had ended being the lucky winner. It had severed my arm before I could even feel the pain.

One hit, and our grand foolproof plan had crumbled to dust.

“If you don’t leave him, I’ll leave you!” Rayle shouted.

“Fucking Rayle!” I screamed through my pain. “I’ll see you in hell!”

* * *

I thrust my saber in the direction of the aelf noble. The hilt of the weapon bit into my hand, draining just enough blood for it to triple in size. Treating itself to a bit more, the blade then transformed into a giant platinum serpent that extended forward, biting the aelf’s entire head off. The rest of the creature disappeared in a puff of red smoke, leaving a single purple cube on the arena floor.

“You didn’t need to go all out,” I said as I unsummoned the weapon.

Why waste time? The Serpent Blade countered. If you’re not getting me treats, better be fast about it.

That was so typical of him. A day had passed since the end of the last trial, and Harlequin had yet to announce the next. In theory, that was good; it gave me a chance to rest and boost up a bit. With nothing else to do, I had spent most of the time fighting monsters at the arena. Every hundred killed earned me a reward. If I did it in less than an hour, the reward was far better. Of course, there was a small caveat.

While Arcana cores boosted all of my stats by a small amount, I was the only one who could consume them. Booster spheres increased only a single stat, based on their nature, yet they could also be used to boost any of my cards, which was what Serp wanted.

“I’ll do another round after this.” I took the core from the ground and swallowed it.

The cube clawed its way down my esophagus, though by now the pain had become rather manageable. A few hundred times more, and I would hardly feel it.

You promise? Serp asked with the intonation of a child who had been lied to too many times.

I waited until the cube was fully down. “I promise.” I took a gold coin out of my inventory.

Why was I thinking about the time I lost my arm? It was a traumatic event, to be certain, though there were dozens just as bad, if not worse. Probably it had to do with the new batch of participants.

Harlequin had been very specific that the new groups would contain precisely ten participants. Up till now the groups had consisted of at least twenty, though that was before we had been designated as special. No longer were we viewed as common trash, which could only mean the trials were going to get harder.

During my previous run, I hadn’t met Rayle until five years into the Grand Arcana. He had been an asshole from the very start. The only reason people tolerated him was because he pulled his weight and proved to be surprisingly good in combat. Could he have been the one who betrayed me? I definitely wouldn’t put it past him. After I had lost my arm, things between us had gone downhill fast. I blamed him for my arm, and he blamed me for ruining his grand plan. We rarely interacted, barely tolerating each other enough to kill monsters instead of going for our throats. He would have been the last person I would have shared my plans with. Then again, I had been drunk.

Old man? Serp urged me. You okay?

“Yeah.” I snapped back to reality. “I was just thinking about something.”

I glanced at my arm. I had spent a small fortune to buy it back during the previous run—roughly half as much as it would have cost me to buy a Grand Arcana. The time reversion had erased all that from happening.

“Let’s go.” I tossed the coin.

The moment it touched the arena floor, the first monster appeared.

According to the arena rules, I had to wait for an hour before killing the final opponent. Only then would I be granted the lesser reward. With my current skills, that proved more difficult than killing them outright.

Goblins, I thought. All the battles started with goblins.

One strike was enough for the saber to hit the middle monster before it could make a move. Devouring its blood and flesh, Serp’s blade split in two, each thrusting in the direction of the remaining creatures. A split second later, they were dead as well.

“Don’t kill them this fast,” I said.

Why bother with the small fry? The big ones can survive way longer if wounded.

There was no point in arguing. I let him direct the fight, doing the bare minimum. If he cheated himself out of his reward, it would be on him.

Meanwhile, I kept on thinking who could have betrayed me. There were probably two dozen people I had considered close. Out of them, over half had died before my final trial. Sparta remained one option. We had been lovers, and we were on track to end up like that this run as well. She had been the only person I remembered sharing my plans with, though I still refused to believe she could stab me in the back. Avon was also unlikely. The man had saved me more times than I would care to admit. Even so, it was slightly suspicious that he wasn’t there when the hydra had attacked.

Rayle was a viable suspect. I remembered catching a glimpse of him shortly after I had entered the trial. He hadn’t said anything, pretending he didn’t notice me. The only reason I hadn’t considered him a serious possibility until now was that he already was someone I was cautious about. Learning that there was an organization linked to my betrayal changed the math. Given that, everyone was a suspect.

Waves of monsters died one after the other. Every ten or so, I’d cut off the creature’s legs and leave it to slowly die, wasting as much time as possible. It was tedious, and it wasn’t pretty, but in the end an hour passed, allowing me to kill off the remaining waves and earn a red booster sphere.

“It’s the last one,” I said as I picked it up.

Yeah, yeah. Gimme!

“I’m serious. There are other things I need to boost.”

He consumed the sphere without giving it much thought. Not that I expected anything different.

“Your attention if you please, participants!” Harlequin’s voice boomed from the main hall.

Hearing him always sent shivers down my spine. I had to admit that after the last trial; he was treating us considerably better than before.

“Please gather at once.”

Good thing I finished fighting, I thought.

When a rule-master demanded something, he had to be obeyed, regardless of the tone. I had seen enough people be fooled by his cheerful mood to think that they wouldn’t be killed if they disobeyed. That had ended up being their final mistake.

Brushing the sweat from my forehead, I left the arena. All five of the remaining participants were there by the time I arrived at the mail room of the preparationhall. All of us had been pretty much keeping to ourselves since the last trial. Four of them had deities to obey, which they slavishly did. The only other pactless was Marcus, and he had made it clear he had his own agenda going forward.

“I am sad to announce that the time of rest has come to an end,” Harlequin said from his red and yellow throne. The monster still wore a crown made of a jester’s cap, bells and all. A smiling porcelain mask stared forward. It didn’t look at anyone in particular, but we all knew it could see everything.

Liar, I thought. He wasn’t sad at all, itching to see us slaughter or get slaughtered in the next trial.

“Tomorrow, you will continue on with your trials,” he went on. “However, there are a few things you need to keep in mind.”

The jester stood up from his throne. The moment he did, the large piece of furniture dissolved into a puff of red and yellow smoke, each color twisting around the other as both faded away.

“You are not to mention your latest trial.” Harlequin extended his index finger. “It never happened. In effect, your next trial will be your sixth. You have only completed five trials. Is that clear?” The porcelain mask turned to each of us in turn.

I nodded, as did everyone else.

“Splendid. The second change is that you have been given the possibility to choose which group you’ll join next.” The monster extended a second finger. “Normally, your exploits would have been enough to guarantee you a spot in my group—that group being the best—but requests have been made that I shuffle things a bit. Thus, you will be able to choose from three.”

The moment he said that, three plaques floated in the air above him. All of them were blank, made of different metals.

“By the rules of the Grand Arcana, these groups are similar, but have minor differences.” Harlequin rubbed his hands. “The golden group is my group. The silver is another non-trash group, though overseen by the loser Scaramouche. And then, there’s the bronze group, belonging to Scarpino.” A note of hatred and disgust was audible as he said the name. “You get to choose which group you belong to.”

No one said a word. I suspected they had already made up their minds on the matter.

“Oh, one final thing. The best isn’t allowed a choice.” My name appeared on the golden plaque. “The best goes to the best. Everyone from second place onward gets to decide.”

It was impossible to tell whether that was a good thing. Harlequin definitely made it out to be, but I knew better than most how much he lied.

“Silver,” Cathy said. She had been the first person I had approached upon entering the Grand Arcana for the second time. It was a pity to see her go, but that was part of the game.

“Same.” Marcus nodded.

“Two for Scaramouche.” There wasn’t a hint of disappointment in Harlequin’s voice. “Anyone else?”

“I’d like to stay,” Eleonor said firmly.

Given the deal I had made with her patron deity, it was a given that she would.

“Silver,” Reginald said as well.

That surprised me. The old man didn’t like me much, but he hated Marcus even more. There had to be something else that had made him go there.

That left only one person undecided. Every instinct told me to remain calm. I ignored it, looking over my shoulder at Sparta. Finding her this run had brought some joy to this shitty game. Was I going to lose her again?

“I’d like to stay,” she said. Her smile wasn’t lost on me, and neither was the glance she darted my way. “As you said, it’s the best group.”

“Indeed, it is.” The bronze plaque in the air crumbled to dust.

Before anyone could add another word, Harlequin clapped his hands together. Yellow mist filled the room, making it impossible to see. When it had cleared, the monster was gone… as were all members of the other group.

“That was fast,” Sparta said with a chuckle as she went up to me. “I guess it’s better than a long goodbye.”

“Yeah…” I didn’t know what to think. The departure was way too fast. “You decided to stay.” I looked at her. “Why?”

“This is the best group.” She smirked. “Also, someone needs to keep an eye on you. There’s no telling what trouble you’ll get yourself into.”

“Yeah…” The question was whether she was part of that trouble.
