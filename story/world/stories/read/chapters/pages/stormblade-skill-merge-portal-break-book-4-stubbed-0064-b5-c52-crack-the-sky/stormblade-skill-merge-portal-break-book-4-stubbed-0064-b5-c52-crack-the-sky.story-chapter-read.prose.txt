When the portal slammed shut behind her, Ellen didn’t waste any time.

Sure, part of her—a very insistent, panicky part—demanded that she find a way back to her and Kade’s portal world. Kade needed her help. He needed all the help he could get. The God of Thunder was unleashing his full strength against him.

But no. They’d talked about this possibility. She’d known she might be cut off from him, unable to join their power against the storm dragon. If that happened, she had three jobs, depending on where she ended up.

If she found herself on Earth, her plan involved summoning the Stormshade Hammer and using it to clear out huge swaths of Phoenix. The Tides of Darkness would roll through the city, purging monsters and wounding Paragons badly enough to turn the tides, no matter how badly things were going. Even better, that’d free up the other A and S-Rankers to join in any fight against the God of Thunder himself, giving Kade the help he’d need.

On the other hand, she might find herself cut off in their portal world. In that case, she’d check the exit portal to Earth and execute Plan One if she could. If not, the Black Tower and Void Garden would likely have been ravaged by the God of Thunder’s presence. She’d turn her efforts toward rebuilding the defenses as best she could in the time she had. Hopefully, Kade would return quickly and with allies. If not, whatever repairs she could make would have to be enough.

And then there was the nightmare scenario.

The one where she was stuck in the God of Thunder’s empty, red-and-blue-skied portal world, by herself. If that happened, neither Kade nor Ellen could think of a good way to return to Earth or rejoin any fighting at the Lightning Tower. She’d be on her own, deep behind enemy lines, unable to escape.

If that happened, she had a very specific, very actionable task. Destroy as much as possible. Leave as much of a trap behind as she could. Prepare for the God of Thunder to return to his world. Then, prepare to fight for her life.

So, as the portal slammed shut and Ellen found herself trapped on the flat platform at the center of the God of Thunder’s portal world, she took only a second to pull herself together.

“Right. Resources.”

Stamina: 453/600, Mana 821/1620 (+20)

She had plenty of Mana and Stamina to work with, plenty of powerful spells to use. Her first thought was to rip into the portal world itself, destroying as much as possible as quickly as possible. She could do that easily enough. But the trap had to be set. Instead, she could build something devastating. Something destructive. Something that’d put even an SS-Plus-Ranked Paragon on the back foot.

Her eyes swept across the wreckage of the pillars she and the Portal Tyrant had destroyed.

That.

That was it.

She got to work, pushing out the Shadow Cell in every direction so it washed across the massive spires, digging weak spots into each of them in that familiar checkerboard pattern. Her every scrap of focus bored in on the monolithic stone pillars. She had to come close to breaking them. Close, but not all the way.

And she had to do it quickly.

But if she did it right…If she did it right, it’d be ready for Kade when he got there.

I’d been right.

As I stepped through the portal into the God of Thunder’s world, the Shadow Cell assaulted me. Squares of armored maelstrom vanished. Lightning crackled to fill the gaps. I shouted wordlessly into the storm of darkness, and the assault let up slightly.

In the minute that she’d had, Ellen had been busy.

The world of the God of Thunder had been one of blue and red light, a gray-clouded sky for the colors to reflect off of, and massive, dagger-like towers. Some had been small; others were miles high. There’d always been an electric tension in the air, like the whole world was held together by the God of Thunder’s will and nothing more.

But this…this was insane.

Ellen’s shadestorm raged. The Shadow Cell ripped at the pillars and towers, and at the very floor below her feet. The color in the sky faded, reds and blues washing over with dark shadows as Ellen tore and shredded the world around her. With forty-five seconds of uninterrupted time, she’d made the best use of her S-Rank power against an undefended world.

I recognized her objective instantly.

The God of Thunder didn’t. “The Shadow Witch,” he thundered from far overhead.

She ignored him, too busy working with her Unique skill. I threw myself back into battle, ignoring my screaming wounds even as they protested.

Here, in the dragon’s world, the God of Thunder was truly ascendant. He hovered overhead, ethereal lightning-and-storm cloud wings encircling the platform and weaving between his towers. Pulsing electrical surges traveled up his long body from tail to maw in waves of blue, white, and yellow. His aura surged out like a wall of lightning, ripping across me and opening new blisters in the old, half-healed ones.

Against that, I pulled my own aura in. The Stormshade Anvil forged me into a perfect, honed weapon. My own lightning paled in comparison to his, but I didn’t care. Cloudburst shut down every spell I could cast, and I hammered in behind the effect, sword thrusting out toward the God of Thunder’s face. He whirled, searching for Ellen, and I stabbed a second time. A third. Slipstream ate into my old mentor’s skin, dozens of sword strokes landing across his body.

Everything short of Stormbreak, I used—but not to hurt the God of Thunder. No. My only goal was to keep his attention off Ellen.

Wherever she was hiding, her magic was hard at work. Most of it seemed focused on the God of Thunder. Black Hole kept him pinned in the Cloudburst, while an Abyssal Storm tore at his flank. But I knew Ellen, and most of her firepower was being poured into multiple Shadow Cells, each being echoed by Arcane Resonator. She wasn’t trying to kill the God of Thunder.

At least, not directly.

It took me a moment to realize exactly what she’d done. The trap she’d laid. And when I did, I threw myself away from the God of Thunder, Stormrunner and Lightning Dance pushing as hard as they could.

One more Shadow Cell. It ripped into the gigantic, towering stone pillars across the north and east sides of the portal world, opening cracks wide enough for Mana to surge out.

The Mana itself did the rest.

It was like the storm to end all storms had decided the God of Thunder was a fly in need of swatting. Lightning bolts the size of skyscrapers broke free, dozens per second, rupturing my eardrums before the thunder even had a chance to boom. They gouged into the God of Thunder’s body, one after another, like the world’s largest machine gun. Everything I’d ever done—every Stormbreak, every Hand of the Thunder God, even the empowered attack that had finally ended the Placid King—added up to maybe three of the beams hitting the God of Thunder.

The shockwave cracked the sky. The gray layer of clouds, the blue and red lights, all of it gave way to a jagged slice of black—a slice of black that sucked inward, ripping reality itself into it.

“No!” The God of Thunder bellowed against the storm. Even as his body was thrown across the portal world by the sheer fury of an explosion that dwarfed even Angelo Lawrence’s best, even as the rift in the sky grew from a crack so narrow the Storm’s Blade wouldn’t have fit into it all the way to a gulf wide enough for me to walk through, he fought against the rippling Mana explosion.

He fought. And I ran.

The explosion didn’t stop. It rippled on and on, an endless bass drum barrage. The rift grew. The wreckage of one pillar, then another, then more and more, vanished into its maw.

“No, this is impossible! This can’t happen yet! My preparations weren’t complete!” The God of Thunder’s tail lashed out, inches from my face, as the rift picked us both up—along with a figure in robes on the far side of the shattering portal world. “I can’t fail here!”

“You just did,” I shouted back.

Then I cast a single spell. Lightning Chain wrapped around Ellen’s waist. It pulled us together, and as it did, I activated the portal key to the Lightning Tower. We slammed into it from opposite sides, vanishing through it and landing on the hard, metal floor of the throne room.

“Are you okay?” I asked Ellen. “He didn’t hurt you, did he?”

She rolled her eyes, then turned them to the portal. “Kade Allen Noelstra, you maniac. You’re covered in wounds, your stomach’s bleeding all over the floor, and your eardrums look like they ruptured and healed three times in the last thirty seconds, and you’re asking me if I’m okay?”

It took me a moment to realize she was screaming at me to be heard. Her own ears trickled blood, too, and she had a few deep cuts on her arms and chest, but nothing lethal. I relaxed and reached out to shut the portal between my world and the God of Thunder’s—to let a sudden, uncontrolled attempt at ascension tear him apart and be done with it.

The God of Thunder raged.

A minute and a half. That’s all it had taken. He hadn’t even seen the Shadow Witch slip into his world, but the only time she could have was just before he’d slammed the portal shut and surged into Earth. A minute and a half later, his hopes and dreams and twenty millennia of cores and Mana collected from a long, long list of pupils and disciples-turned-prey succumbed, one by one, to the rift in the sky, prematurely and without even a chance of ascension or the destructive ripples that’d accompany it. Only his world would die to this abortive attempt.

The sheer fury of his portal world’s detonation astounded even him, and this was the slow, messy version. If he’d had his way, every tower would have ruptured together, forcing the crack in the sky all the way open in one fell swoop and letting him ascend, never mind the consequences.

But as devastating as the blow to his chance at ascension was, the God of Thunder’s head was still clear.

He took stock, even as he spun in the storm’s grasp, slamming against chunks of Mana-rich stone the size of icebergs. Broken limbs all across his left side. A partially severed right wing, and a pulverized left one. Ribs cracked all across his body. His tail was missing halfway down. One eye had popped like a fruit from the sheer waves of pressure.

Worse, the pressure inside him had redoubled as preparations for ascension vanished into the void.

The God of Thunder crashed into the platform. It had buckled like a piece of metal in a vice, but it was the most intact thing in his portal world. He slid behind it as it rocketed upward, toward the rift. A plan. He needed a plan—a way to avoid the call of the rift between universes. It wouldn’t be enough to allow him to ascend, and any attempt to pass through it, accidental or not, would only destroy him.

There. A blue-white-ringed portal, hovering mid-air. A way out—if he was fast enough.

He gathered all his remaining power, absorbing as much Mana from the dying world he’d once called home as he could. He’d need it all to break Kade’s determination—and defeating him physically wouldn’t be enough at this point. Kade needed to break. Then he pushed off, electricity and rain forming around him as a shield.

And as he hit the portal and surged through it, two thoughts passed through his mind.

First, that it made no sense that the Shadow Witch could have caused this disaster. There had to be an explanation that didn’t involve Kade’s allies. His former pupil was a reckless maniac who’d throw himself repeatedly against an unbeatable foe with no regard for his future, but even he wasn’t stupid enough to empower an ally not only to his level, but possibly beyond it.

And second—but far more horrifying—what if he hadn’t?

“Kade!” The God of Thunder screamed as he smashed through the portal, an instant before I could close it. His massive, draconic body hit the Lightning Tower’s wall like an out-of-control missile. Metal screeched and gave. Lightning crackled overhead. The tower swayed and groaned. But even as his body blew a gap through the side of the tower, it stayed upright.

“Kade! Answer me! What did you do to the Shadow Witch?”

I held the Storm’s Blade out in a high guard, looking down the blade. Behind me, shadows surged as Ellen pushed out the Stormshade Hammer in a wall of shadow, ripping it from every flickering surface to form a weapon that dwarfed even the Paragon in front of us. “I did nothing to her.”

“Lies! You’re lying to me. I remember everything. The broken cores. The moments you spent back-to-back, meditating on your Paths. But I don’t remember what you did to make her this strong. IT doesn’t matter, though. You’re in danger, kid. She’ll betray you for power. They all do.”

“You think that because you did.”

“Yes. Yes, I did. That’s the way of the universe, Kade Noelstra. She knows it. Look at her.”

I didn’t. Not because I believed the God of Thunder and didn’t want to see that truth, but because if there was one person in the universe I could trust, it was Jessie—but if there were two…if there were two, the second would have to be the woman standing right behind me, wielding a hammer of shadow and lightning that loomed high overhead and threatened to crash down on me.

“Look at her, Kade!” the God of Thunder screamed. “If you don’t believe me, I’ll prove it to you!”

Still, I didn’t look, and I didn’t second-guess myself. Ellen had my back, and I had hers.

She touched my shoulder. “Remember, Kade,” she whispered, so quiet against the raging storm overhead and the God of Thunder’s crackling, desperate-sounding shouts that I almost couldn’t hear her, “that I’m with you for the long haul.”

Promises. The God of Thunder and I had both made them. But I’d done everything I could to keep mine—to be the kind of man my father wanted me to be, the kind of man who kept to the spirit of his word, not just the letter of it. And the Paragon before me, his body savaged and broken by the sheer force Ellen had unleashed, hadn’t. He’d trained countless generations, only to betray them.

The Stormshade Hammer hung overhead. It needed something to strike against, though.

That was one thing the God of Thunder hadn’t realized. That by throwing away his apprentices, he was reducing the risk of betrayal. And maybe he was even correct to do so. Maybe the promise of another universe would tear into Ellen’s mind and turn her into someone who’d stab me in the back for power.

But I didn’t believe that.

So, as the Stormshade Hammer slammed down, lightning and wind and shadow all competing to hit the God of Thunder first, I slipped through the air with Stormrunner and used the Stormshade Anvil. If Ellen needed a surface to hammer against, I’d gladly become that forge.

Instead, the world stopped. It went black. And when it started again, Ellen and I stood in an endless void, lit only by a single fire.

“What?” I asked.
