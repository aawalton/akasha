~~It’s almost time, I thought, nerves bubbling up.

The girl in the mirror stared back with wide eyes, wearing a lilac dress that appeared as if the piece of inanimate clothing knew it didn't belong. Not to mention, who would've guessed silk could be so uncomfortable. I tugged at the frilled sleeves that covered way too much of my tanned arms, a far cry from the boyish clothes I usually wore. But the frantic beating of my heart made me forget about the strange sensation almost immediately.

My hair was bunched up into an awkward bun that almost managed to hide the fact it wouldn't have reached my shoulders if gravity had its say. I found its shade of dark brown just as dull as the teenage girl it was attached to. A lie pretending to blend in. And yet I wondered if, fitted with this mascara, they would see me as a person for once.

"Come on, Jacqueline! We don't want to be late," Rita called from downstairs.

Adjusting my sleeves for the last time, I shot the stranger in the mirror a final glance and hurried down the steps. Rita was waiting for me in the doorway, looking right at home in her light-green dress. Her eyes crinkled mischievously as she caught sight of me.

"How many pockets does that thing have, and which one is the Jacqueline I know hiding inside?"

I batted her arm on reflex, then quickly smoothed out the wrinkles the movement caused. How did people function wearing this shit on a day-to-day basis? Maybe it was a payoff sort of thing. Hello 'wanting to crawl out of my own skin', meet 'being noticed'. Three, two, one, fight. I wonder which one of you is going to come out on top.

I gave Rita a halfhearted shrug, the dismissive gesture the exact opposite of the brawl inside my squirming nerves.

As I tried to squeeze past her, she took a gentle, though firm hold of my arms and spun me around. "Jackie, it's going to be alright. They’re gonna love you."

My smile came a little easier this time. I put a fist over my heart.

She smirked. "Yeah, save the gratitude for when you dazzle the hell out of our new mates. Which, by the way, is going to happen, a hundred percent. I have no doubts."

Part of me wished she'd tone back her enthusiasm a little. After all, this get-together was going to be a simple meeting between a first-year class to spend an afternoon in the Glasshorn Bar. Normally, I would have skipped the event—too many bad experiences. But somehow, against my better judgment, Rita had convinced me otherwise.

We arrived just on time, but the ruckus was already in full swing as a crowd of young students filled the tavern with their chatter. The smell of spiced apple filled the air, which, I supposed, was a slight upgrade from the sour stench of beer I was accustomed to. Like my clothes, my usual choice of taverns ranged from shithole to even worse shithole. But hey, at least people didn't expect you to talk when fists flew instead of words. No strange looks, no folks treating you like you were wrong in the head. But this place? This was a respected establishment, at least as respected as you could get here in Gulling.

Conversation gradually died down as we approached the long table with curious faces looking our way. Apparently, we were the last to arrive.

They swarmed us with introductions for the next minute, offering a hand—or in Rita's case, sometimes a familiar hug—one by one. I tried to remember all the names as I smiled, shaking hands.

A few awkward moments passed as some of them held on, waiting expectantly for me to introduce myself, but I broke the grip quickly and moved on to the next person, trying to ignore my discomfort. Though I was used to it, it never got any easier.

I lightly touched Rita's elbow as soon as the cursed greeting ritual came to a close. She glanced from me to the confused looking students, practically reading my mind in the fraction of a second.

"This is Jacqueline, and no, she's not a shy one," Rita hooked an arm through mine with a fond smile. "She can't talk, but trust me, even on her worst days she could surge-wrestle any of you to the ground."

The first reaction was always shock. A brief moment as people processed the news, quickly turning to one of three things; pity, mirth, or badly veiled nonchalance. I saw the effects of Rita's words ripple through the crowd like a spell, some averting their eyes while others locked on me like a target dummy. I had to fight the sudden urge telling me to run, but anxiety kept my legs rooted in place.

A polite cough interrupted the silence, and I looked up to see a boy—Dallern, was his name?—offering me a steaming mug. He said something about the drinks being amazing here, and that it was on the house, but I was simply glad the awkward moment had been broken. He took the seat on my right. I looked around for Rita to see her squished between a group of her friends I didn't know.

"...and they only said Thursdays, but, well, you know how it is. Then we got Plinon's dad to rent out this place, so here we are. What do you think?" Dallern asked, the boy he mentioned, Plinon, listening to the conversation as well.

Flustered, I took a sip from the mug, just barely avoiding scalding my mouth as the sweet taste of cider warmed my insides. I grinned at them, holding up a thumb.

We talked for a time, them going on about random topics and me trying to respond in a way that they could understand. For the most part, they could not. When I mimed holding a pen and writing over my palm, raising my brows in question, Dallern just awkwardly shrugged and started talking about something else, unable to figure out that I was asking about his studies. When I snagged a cooked shrimp from his plate and pushed my serving of fries toward him with a smirk, he just looked at me confused instead of taking a bite. They couldn't even figure out the fist over my heart was gratitude, and not some crude gesture meaning to offend.

The giddy feeling I started out with slowly died out, giving way to disappointment. Here I was, finally getting a chance to meet these people. People who’d play a large part in the next phase of my life, those I’d see and interact with every day at school.

And while they had tried at first, their interest was quickly snuffing out.

"...the prow shaped like a spear," Dallern was saying, then paused, looking at me. "You know, the prow? The front-most part of a ship?" He slowed his speech, like he was talking to a four year old. "P-r-o-w?"

I tried, I really did. I tried my best to let him know I was listening, but the longer he went on, the more he seemed to be convinced something was wrong with me. He talked in fewer words and with an increasingly bored tone. Awkward silences stretched longer until the boys weren’t even looking in my direction to see my replies. They soon forgot about me altogether, engaged in conversation with someone on their other side of the table.

I opened my mouth—but of course, no sound came. I tried catching the attention of the girl on my left. When our eyes met, she gave a polite smile before turning away.

For long minutes, I just sat there, listening as people around me yelled within familiar groups, laughed, having the time of their life. Rita was leaning across the table, playing coy as she pretended to peck someone. Lighthearted banter was thrown at a boy struggling with his order from the attractive waitress. Forks clattered, voices rising and merging with the commotion in the warm tavern.

Nobody saw the mute girl stand, holding back tears as she walked outside. Nobody showed an ounce of concern for someone as worthless and incomplete as her. Retreating to her empty home, sitting on the bed of her empty room, no one saw as loneliness broke her. Nobody would care if she was gone the next day.

She tried to take her own life that night. It was only blind luck that her father checked on her before it was too late.

By morning, Jack was Scarrd.~~
