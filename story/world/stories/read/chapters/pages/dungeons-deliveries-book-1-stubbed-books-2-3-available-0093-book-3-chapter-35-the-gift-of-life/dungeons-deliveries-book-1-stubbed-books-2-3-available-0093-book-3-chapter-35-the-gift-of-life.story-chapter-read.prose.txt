Gnomes swayed to the steady beat of drums as one. Half watched their hopeful queen’s reaction and the others eagerly awaited the arrival of the gift. With some gnomes connected to the orange of the Soil Mind, the Ritual’s procession was shared with across the network of tunnels.

Petal planted his feet with his back towards the Blightwood edge. He would act alone tonight. The beasts beyond would learn the might of his bellows and the devastation of his trowel. It would be the first true response to the Beast of Blightwood. Fear turned his mind as the tramping feet of the gift bearers sounded.

Thankfully, Gaia reached out and reminded him of her presence, and of his active [Rite of Rootbound Chorus].

“Boanne,” Petal called as he practically shook from the strength of the Ritual building. “Of mud and sky, of muck and pond and lake and gravel and grit! Of life and bones! Humbly and graciously, we welcome you. Our greatest gift is ourselves, but this you have not yet learned, so for now, accept the meager offering ordered by Sun and Shade, Ruler of All Under Cloudless Skies, He Who Comes with Girth and Whisker, hefty in heart, soft in step, sharp with claw, and patient in attack, Emilio!”

From around dense brush, a hundred gnomes carried both the gift and their ruler, who sat politely atop it.

Are you impressed? Emilio said in his mind. How wonderful is she? Do you think she likes it? Do you think she likes me?

Petal saw the spark between his king and the dragon. Tapped into the Ritual, he witnessed it thread over from Boanne to Emilio in a shade of yellow-green. The gift was spectacular, and frankly necessary for the might of his people, but the end result of this Ritual depended on their connection.

He heard the smallest rustling from the Blightwood and forced himself to show no reaction. Now was not the time to act.

The constructed gift made him proud. It had been quite difficult to source everything, but the gnomes could dig anywhere, including the concrete under a few Familiars R’ Us.

Twenty cat condos they’d taken, and a hundred different plants they’d gathered with aide from Runs with Wind. The most interesting rocks had been brought from the deepest of the gnomish stores. Coloured pebbles polished from the creeks waters were piled high. Bones too, found in their expansion, aged from a decade old to as far back as many millennia, were brought forth.

It had taken all of Petal’s patience, any group of gnome that could help, of which there were several hundred that had come to offer aide, and the better part of six hours. There were no instructions for what was to be made, and the papers were summarily burned, ashes infused into corkboard.

When he saw her reaction, and how noble Emilio presented on top of it, it appeared to be worth every bit of stress.

The bases of all twenty condos had been lashed together with living vines and secured only with nails found across their territory. Flowers bloomed up the scratching posts and various platforms. Enriching it to truly bear life had been quite a tricky bit of magic, and the gnome who’d figured to be one who typically tended the Soil Mind. Petal decided then, that upon their victory over the Beast of Blightwood, the gnomes would institute schools and halls of their own. They must get organized as a people beyond warfare. If they were to be a great race, then they would require social tools like the humans had.

He grinned to himself as the thought finished. Only a few moment ago, he’d feared the slow death of gnomes, and now he was planning their future. How decisions made now would set the bedrock of their culture forever. The Ritual itself would be shared through the ages. Only, Petal wouldn’t be there to see it.

As tonight, for the final part of the Ritual, the Eternal Gnome needed to die.

The woods rustled behind him again, and Petal Gravewhistle let his hand fall to the pockets of his mud-colored bathing suit, pulling out a little speck of the Soil Mind.

All told the gift, the Throne of Life, was ten feet tall, and best of all, the beds on top provided the perfect snoozing spot for a dragon.

There was wood and sawdust. Metal and plastic. Fruit and roots rooted in packed soil woven with stone, and a small group of elderly gnomes had even mounted a thousand of the best pebbles of their people. Once, every gnome had been only a thing relegated to sit forever without action, chipping and baking under the toil of weather. They were now living things, and it was fitting their highest seat would follow suit.

The bearers of the Throne of Life let go, rushing to join the watchers. Rather than fall to the ground, it floated a foot above the earth. Boanne’s eyes widened, and Emilio puffed his chest out more, blinking his green eyes at her.

The greeting was going well so far.

Petal beckoned the hiding beasts of the Blightwood by scratching his butt, the little light of the Soil Mind in his hand twinkling. This was a hint to them of what was to come. No other gnome would see the small slight, and he found it delightful that he would carry a secret into death. If all went well, he wouldn’t be able to tell the tale through their fungus. Only those watching would have a say.

Gaia gently pricked him with an errant thorn under his foot. Petal breathed deep, [Rite of Rootbound Chorus] building along the air. Now was the time to act.

“Boanne, life takes all forms,” Petal announced, looking from Emilio to Boanne, who couldn’t keep their eyes off each other. “A cat ascending, a dragon who can soar all, a people who should not exist yet do. We wish you to be our Queen alongside our King. We have brought four gifts as our first act together. The Throne of Life is the first. Treasures will be added and tended to henceforth. Now, Boanne, I ask you for all gnomes. Do you wish to bear the second gift?”

Boanne turned to Petal, finally able to take her eyes off Emilio.

“I do.” She said aloud, causing a stir amongst the attending gnomes. Their queen could speak!

Emilio, to his credit, managed a casual lick of a paw.

“Your second gift is a name.” Petal continued, bracing himself. “We name you Soil Solaris.”

The name struck against the Ritual, sending wind swirling around the crowd in a sudden gust.

“Do you wish to hear the third gift?” He called over the wind.

“Yes, I do.”

Petal smiled as the very mud beneath his feet began to turn. It had already begun. The gnomes were breaching the wall and their enemy hadn’t a clue.

“Your third gift is a promise. A promise of ourselves.” He paused and before shouting. “Gnomes! Where are we?”

“Under the Sun and Shade!” Every gnome chanted back together.

“And who are we?!” He bellowed back.

“All Under the Sun and Shade!”

Their chant echoed everywhere, cracking the air with their belief. It bounced around and then infused the Ritual as if swallowed whole.

“Soil Solaris, do you wish to hear your final gift? It is a thing that no dragon possesses. That no dragon could possess. The first of its kind.”

Boanne tilted your head at him, and Petal almost belly laughed at her reaction. Emilio often made the same quirk when spoken to.

“Yes, I would know it.”

“The final gift is our first seed of eternity. My core. To be cultivated by only you.”

The dragon reared as the drums picked up, and a moment later, every gnome in attendance stood together.

Weapons were drawn and hefted. Five whole companies, or five hundred trained gnomes, had been strewn about and told to blend in. From a bush along the edge, Petal knew that his wife Marzipan waited amongst the fighters. Many of the gnomes stepped forward and haphazardly organized in front of the dragon, causing a great ruckus at the front of the Blightwood. Despite the clamor, Boanne hadn’t taken her eyes off Petal.

“Your core?” Boanne asked sadly. “But that would mean you would perish.”

Petal stepped out of the Blightwood back into the gnomish territory, the Ritual skill reaching it’s crescendo.

“Aye, my hopeful queen.” Petal answered. “But only if you’ll have us.”

The Ritual wove her in, and Petal saw a thread extend from the dragon towards him. One from him shot out too, reaching to meet hers. He began one final breath of life and was pleased that there was hardly any room left in him to fit more of Gaia. Boanne turned from Petal to Emilio.

“Have you?” Boanne said. “No. I won’t have you. You’re not mine to have. But join? Yes. I will join you.”

Her decision struck against the Ritual. Petal breathed a long sigh of relief. It had gone far better than he expected.

“Your Girthness, your Queen calls. Now give her what she deserves.”

Emilio blinked at Petal, pushing against his mental block.

Show the people who you are, my friend. He said to the Eternal Gnome.

Petal had a moment to feel the pride in his chest swell, and then a great cast of lightning cracked from the cloudless night sky.

It struck him, igniting his skin and beginning the smoldering of his body. The pain lasted the briefest moment, and then Petal got a System message.

[You have been martyred by a Demigood!]

[Rite of Runebound Chorus activated.]

[Eternal Gnome germination has begun. You are again capable of death.]

[You are destined to die this night under a blaze of glory. Upon your death, your core will be become an Eternity Seed.]

Petal looked over the gnomes. Every bit of him screamed to fight. Not a single rousing verse made itself available to him, as there was little poetry in death. He turned to face the Blightwood while reaching into his pocket and took out his trusty garden trowel.

Then Petal Gravewhistle leapt to the first monster and beheaded it in a flash without a word. He heard the tramping of feet upon the ground behind him, and sprinted into enemy territory as the front, grinning like a mad man when he heard who sounded the charge.

“Charge gnomes!” Delilah, who reminded him of his wife, shouted over the noise. “Charge deep into unknown soil!”

Petal Gravewhistle, smoldering away from the outside in, would die this night, but first he would fight as far into the Blightwood as he could to plant the Soil Mind.

And from a high tree, above the chaos of the charge, three drones tore through the sky for a mission of their own.
