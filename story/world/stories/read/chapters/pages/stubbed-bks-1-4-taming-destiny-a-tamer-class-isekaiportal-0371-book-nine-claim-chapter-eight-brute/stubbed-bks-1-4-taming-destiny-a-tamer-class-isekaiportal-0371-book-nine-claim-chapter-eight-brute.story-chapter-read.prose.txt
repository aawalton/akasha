Kalanthia, we need your domain, I tell her grimly. She doesn’t argue, but leaps out of my tattoo to land on the deck more lightly than a leopard the size of an elephant has any right to. Those nearest her scramble away, either her size or the threatening snarl already rumbling out of her mouth making the ballista bolts and sea serpent more appealing.

“Brace yourselves!” I shout, using Air-Shaping to press my voice across the ship – and to try to limit how far it goes beyond the ship’s bounds.

That’s as much warning as I can give them before Kalanthia’s domain rolls over them. I support those Bound to me by sending a steady sense of reassurance down our connections. I’m glad to see them turn and grab those among their fellows who make a break for the side of the ship, clearly intending on throwing themselves over it instead of facing the angry Tier three whose domain promises nothing but death.

Many freeze in place, their faces going pale and their eyes widening. I wonder how many will need to change their trousers after this. Five people lose themselves to blind fear – two sailors, two fighters and a mage.

The mage jumps into a hatch in the ship itself, hiding below decks. One of the others is an archer up in the rigging and they fall to the deck below with a sickening thud. I wince as I see one of Maxen’s group run over to the fallen fighter, a potion already in hand – hopefully it’s not too late. The other three all go overboard, preferring the vagaries of the sea and our enemies to Kalanthia.

Noir, Ivor, I need you, I call and the two alcaorises emerge eagerly. I hate to put them at risk, but I’d never forgive myself if I didn’t ask them to try. See if you can save those three, I ask and they send me quick agreement.

At least they shouldn’t be at immediate risk of attack – if our ship has been badly affected by the edges of the attack, Kyrian’s, on which Kalanthia is focussing most of her attention, is doing far worse. A good third of the crew has turned and run towards the far railing and I suspect that at least some of them have fallen overboard in the crush.

Approximately another third has been just as badly affected, but they’ve chosen to freeze instead of fleeing – many of them are crouched on the deck with their hands over their heads in meagre protection.

As for the last group, they are very clearly the Classers who have put at least some points into Willpower. They resist with differing degrees of success and are our biggest threats.

Already the mages have started attacking us with jets of water and blades of force. The Air Mage seeks to deflect and defend rather than attack, but that proves to be frustrating nonetheless as my mages have to struggle to even get through to the ship. Still, it quickly becomes clear that the level of the mages Kyrian and I each have are roughly equal in strength and training. For now, it’s a stale-mate.

But that won’t last long; we can’t just sit here. As the Classers without ranged Skills stride over to the unmanned ballistas and cannons to continue the bombardment, I give my own orders.

Maxen, resume firing. “Captain Tolly, activate our emergency shields and get us out of here.” We have to hope he only had that one shield-breaker.

Yes, sir!

“Aye, my lord,” Tolly agrees, grimly, his eyes glancing at the sea serpent that had paused briefly in its attack – perhaps as Kyrian was forced to concentrate on resisting Kalanthia’s domain – but is now beginning to shift again.

My own eyes flick to where Maxen is reorganising both his squadron and the sailors.

“I’ll do my best to distract the serpent. Loran, Sarran, Captain, I leave the battle in your hands.” I tell the three present, already striding over to that side of the ship in order to get closer. How long can you keep the suppression up? I ask Kalanthia as I move. I know that her domain is a passive effect, but this is more than just her domain – this is her imposing her will on others. And, if it’s anything like a Battle of Wills, it takes effort.

Not long if I continue as I am now. Longer if I stop trying to suppress those who have mostly fought off my influence.

Concentrate on those who you can still keep pinned down, I decide after a brief moment of thought. Right now our priority has to be escaping the trap – our emergency shield won’t last long and after that we’ll be defenceless. We need the time to escape.

If it was just emergency shield against emergency shield, I might consider staying close to Kyrian’s ship – theirs has been active for longer. But I have a nasty feeling when I look at how bright the shield is in my magic view that theirs is actually a secondary shield, rather than an emergency one. And that changes the situation entirely – in a battle of attrition, they will win. And I’m not willing for us to be sitting ducks for Kyrian’s ballistas, cannons, and arrows as well as his mages.

I wait until Kalanthia has sent me acknowledgement, and then turn my attention to my own task.

Ivor, I need you to watch my back, I ask the alcaoris grimly. Noir, don’t take risks, but see what you can do to cause problems for our enemy. Be careful! They’ve both finished saving our people from the sea and I feel the strong urge to call them back to safety in my tattoo. But I’ll be all too vulnerable while I’m dealing with the sea serpent and I can’t risk someone attacking me while my back is literally turned – Ivor’s shield will help and, worse comes to worst, he can air-lift me out of the situation. And Noir is more capable of escaping a situation by using his distraction Skill than Ivor is.

Vaguely hopeful, I try to use Dominate when the water serpent turns to glare at me, opening its mouth to reveal its sharp teeth formed of pressurised water like the one that tried to bite me out of the sky earlier.

My Skill fails entirely. Actually, it doesn’t even activate. I’m not entirely surprised – this is no natural creature – but I would have kicked myself if I hadn’t tried.

As soon as I see a white dragon come in to land behind me, I leap on his back and then close my eyes. Leaving behind awareness of my body and the greater battle, I focus on sending water-magic tendrils down into the serpent that is very obviously gearing up for an attack.

Once down in the mess of magic that makes up the sea serpent and the water around it, I can feel the battle that’s already raging. As Alyna said, the Water Mages are doing their level best to either dispel or take control of the serpent. But I feel how ill-suited most of them are to the task.

The mages’ great reserves of mana serve them ill when facing a construct driven by a will greater than their own. At first confused – surely Kyrian hasn’t put that many points in Willpower – a little more investigation yields the truth of it. Kyrian may not have all that much Willpower of his own, but whatever Skill they use to create these constructs of water – or, in his father’s case, steam – embeds something of a consciousness within their creation. The more material that forms the creature, the more will and determination it has to carry out its master’s orders.

Curiously, it’s this will that caused the serpent to pause when Kalanthia released her domain – it might have enough will of its own to resist my mages who are working outside their areas of expertise and at a disadvantage, but Kalanthia is another story.

My discoveries give me an idea. Currently, the mages are working ineffectively. Some are trying to disperse the serpent wholesale. Others are trying to wrench control over the currents. Still more are trying to destroy the water serpent’s form by striking it with spikes of water. This last is actually worse than useless – even as I watch, the water just sinks into the body of the serpent and makes it stronger.

Alyna, tell the mages who are trying to disperse the serpent and those who are trying to attack it to instead pull bits away. A bucketful, a cupful, a teaspoonful, it doesn’t matter. Only that the serpent steadily loses mass.

I feel her acknowledgement and, a few moments later, I hear her response.

I’ve passed on your instructions, she tells me, a lot more calmly than the last time we spoke.

Honestly, I didn’t need her response – I can see for myself that the mages have changed focus. And for now, it seems to be a losing battle – the serpent’s will to stay intact is stronger than the mages’ wills to fragment it.

But that’s where I come in.

Diving in, mentally speaking, I press my own will against the serpent’s. I might not be able to use Dominate but this isn’t all that much different. Except, instead of attempting to chain the serpent, I seek to loosen the bonds that hold the beast together.

If anything, it reminds me of when I faced the Beast Tamer samuran and unwound the chains around the body of the raptorcat she had captured and was trying to use against me.

This is more difficult than that – my Water-Shaping Skill is still only at Novice level and doesn’t allow me to have as much influence as my Dominate Skill would – but the principles are the same. And in a battle where Willpower is the biggest factor, I have an advantage.

I sense the mages working frantically, their magic acting almost as if they are bailing out a boat with cups and buckets while I force the construct to focus its attention on me. Slowly, but with increasing speed, we start making progress. As I’d concluded earlier, the more material the serpent loses, the easier it is to pull more away.

But the tide turns twice a day and it’s not only the construct that we have to deal with – this one isn’t as detached from its creator as the steam dragon was from Torrent. A flash of rage heralds Kyrian reconnecting to his creation. The beast that had previously been acting according to orders now becomes little more than a puppet.

At the same time, concern spikes through Ivor.

I’m vaguely aware of my body moving rapidly, but, as deep in the serpent’s magic as I am, I could not say how we are moving or why. I can guess, though – I feel Kyrian’s fury and refusal to acknowledge what’s happening, though it’s detached like he’s speaking through a thick pane of glass. No doubt he’s trying to attack me somehow. I have a brief moment of gratitude for my own forethought and hope that Ivor is able to keep us away.

Frustration spikes on Kyrian’s side – Ivor must be doing a good job. Evidently deciding to take another tack, Kyrian adds the weight of his own will into the serpent’s. With his Water-Shaping Skill, he attempts to recreate the serpent’s body. And his Skill is far stronger than my own.

It’s a real fight. But in comparison to my struggle against the Pure Energy when it attempted to destroy my body and soul, it pales into insignificance.

I bolster my own Water-Shaping with my Willpower, and use Willpower Leverage to increase my Wisdom, the stat that is so instrumental when it comes to connections. I’m not attempting to do anything delicate; I’m not attempting to create. Destruction is so much easier – all I need is will and brute force. And thus, my Novice level set against Kyrian’s Journeyman level – or perhaps even Master level – in Water-Shaping doesn’t fail me, though it requires all of my concentration.

Kyrian and I battle above while my mages nibble bites of the serpent like mice devouring a whole cheese wheel little by little.

Finally, we reach the tipping point. The serpent has lost so much mass that it is unable to hold itself together. I feel more than hear Kyrian’s scream of frustration as his construct vanishes back into the water.
