‘Nolan?’

“She was Esme’s twin sister.” Nolan stared distantly at the outlook with the cigarette between his fingers. “Identical. Hybrid feline and fae, with clear feline appearances from their mother’s side. Used to mess with people by switching places at the family shop. Smart as hell too, the both of them.” His voice carried a heaviness as he spoke, my heart breaking for him. “When I first met Esme years back, I hadn’t the slightest clue where my life was headed. I was 15 and had just escaped the wrath of my mother. That selfish bitch didn’t have to blink twice before she decided to use her own children as guinea pigs for her own experiments, or trade us off to the devil for her own twisted games. I landed in an fae village called Grusna, where everything was so fucking bright and colorful. The weather was always nice, and the people were friendly. As a Seiddayan, I was skeptical. There was this tug of war between land at the time, between the humans and the fae, but Grusna was in a world of its own, and prioritized peace and prosperity over politics. The Reves ran a small apothecary with their daughters outside of the farm I was living in at the time, Zev’s father’s farm, and I came by every few days to trade herbs I’d foraged from the surrounding forests.”

He took another slow drag, the smoke curling between us.

“Esomi was the gentler of the two. She had a radiance to her that could melt your heart. Sugary, sweet. Always had this way of making you feel like you mattered, you know? Esme was brilliant with potions, but Esomi... she could heal more than just bodies. She took after her father, and it was Esomi’s idea to stretch their apothecary business to alchemic venues. I’ve never seen anyone so passionate about mixing herbs and chemicals, testing potions and elixirs. That’s how we ended up getting close. I brought her rare ingredients, she’d craft her things, and we’d celebrate whenever she forged something outside of her father’s textbooks. She’d spend hours in that little workshop behind their house; of course, her father learned that I was into her, and didn’t allow me in her workshop after business hours. He was very protective of his daughters, especially Esomi, but unfortunately, so was Zev.

He had his eyes on Esomi also. When we first met, I lied and told him and his father that I was an orphan. They took me in, and we became good friends. We worked together on the farm from sun up til sundown. I liked to unwind everyday by visiting Esomi with the herbs I’d collect after work, which at first, didn’t bother him, until he noticed I was hanging out with her on the weekends as well, on a more personal level. At the time, the two weren’t together. In fact, Zev never made it clear to me that he liked Esomi until after we started taking each other seriously. It made our friendship rocky, and after a solid four years of being good friends, things got real sour. It was getting harder and harder to live in the farmhouse with his bitter jealousy over my shoulders each and every day. He’d make mistakes on the farm and turned around and blamed me for it, trying to get his father against me. It worked, and I left. By that time, I was already nineteen. I worked with a local blacksmith and saved enough money to rent a small cabin on the outskirts of Grusna. That was when Esomi and I really got close. Without Zev breathing down my neck or her father watching us like a hawk, we could finally just... be ourselves together.”

He flicked ash from his cigarette, his eye distant with memory. “Zev was delusional. He swore Esomi was using me to make him jealous, when Esomi had never looked his way. She said she was into dark-haired men, with broad shoulders and a rough attitude.” He chuckled. “She called me her opposite, and she loved me for it. We courted in secret for a year before Esomi brought our relationship to her folks. Her older sister didn’t like the idea of us being together, mainly because I wasn’t a mage, but her twin sister Esme and mother were open-minded. Eventually, Dr. Reves caved in with Esomi’s persistence, and I’d like to think I won him over when I started working for the family business part-time as a delivery clerk. Everything was going great, until the night Zev went missing. We weren’t close, and our friendship had fallen apart, but I still cared for him like a brother. After days of searching and no signs of him, his father grew desperate. He came to me, hoping I could help. He didn’t know I was already helping, doing my own searching along the outskirts of our town. I didn’t deny him the assistance, and took it a step further by wrangling weapons, potions, and dungeon items to help me comb through the forests around Grusna. Esomi found out what I was doing and insisted on joining me. I was against it, but she was so goddamn stubborn. I had to get her father involved—I’d never forgive myself if I allowed her to go with me and she’d gotten hurt.”

Nolan’s voice caught slightly. He took a moment to compose himself, staring at the cigarette burning between his fingers.

“Within 48 hours into my search, I found a cave system, and in that cave system, I found Zev half-dead, naked, and broken. The cave was a trap, and Zev was bait. I was attacked by a masked man, and as soon as I showed him Scarlet, he regretted targeting me. He was lucky to get away when he did, and I could have chased him, but Zev mattered more. After I recovered him, I realized that the man who’d kidnapped Zev was an organ harvester.”

My eyes grew.

“This one wasn’t working for the Leech King, he had his own disgusting business, operating solo. The last place Zev had been spotted was at the edge of town near the old mill. The harvester had been watching our village for weeks, picking out targets. Young, healthy people who wouldn’t be missed immediately.” Nolan’s jaw tightened. “Zev wasn’t the first. The bastard had a pit of bodies ready for his incinerator in that cave, and Zev was lucky to have dodged it that night.”

‘I’m sorry, Nolan….’

“I remember the bastard saying that I wasn’t his type. I was human, and he was furious about it. That was the first time I felt a wrath so deep that it cut me down to the core. Seeing Zev like that, and then later finding those bodies in that cave…. I’d never thought….” Nolan paused as he picked his head up to flick his cigarette on the ground and crush it with his heel. “Zev was missing 60% of his skin, and he was unable to fully heal, even with Doctor Reves’ interventions. He was in such bad shape that he doubted he’d make it. Zev was bedridden, his mind gone, and his vitality spent. Because of the magical affinity of fae, they tend to live long lives, staying youthful far longer than any other race. But after being tortured, and having his organs recycled and regenerated for so long, the harvester had taken decades off Zev’s life. The process of repeatedly harvesting his organs had tainted his mana flow. Doctor Reves said his body would never recover on its own, and that one day, his potions would stop working. Every time I thought we’d get a breakthrough, he’d just revert back to his former self. I couldn’t bear the news, and neither could Zev. His image, more than his life, was everything.”

‘That’s horrible….’ I said, listening in on the villain’s story. I was still so very shocked that Zeverus and Nolan were even friends at one point, the concept boggling my mind.

“Esomi paid him periodic visits, but Zev saw those visits as the most debilitating. Esomi told me once that he became so annoyed at her coming over. She remembered him saying, ‘I hate the way you look at me, with those eyes filled with so much pity.’ Esomi stopped visiting him after that, and that was when he demanded no more visits. Segregation became his best friend, and no one else mattered. Until… he went missing again one morning. And this time, I was unable to find him. Hell, I tried for months. But knowing Zev, he didn’t want to be found. I couldn’t find my closure, and neither could his father. He went into a bad bout of depression, and left his home and his farm to his neighbors. The man who'd given me another chance at life left without saying goodbye. It crushed me, but after a few years of mourning and heartbreak, I was over it. And then, an unexpected visit. Zev came back, looking good as new. It was like nothing had happened. He was youthful, happy, and confident in a way I’d never seen before. But there was something off about him—something cold behind his eyes that hadn’t been there before. He said he’d found a way to heal himself, that he’d discovered power beyond anything we could imagine. I should have been happy for him, but I couldn’t be, especially since he wouldn’t tell me this all powerful secret behind his complete 180.

Esomi was overjoyed when she heard he’d returned. She wanted to celebrate, to welcome him back properly. Zev took this kind gesture as her being into him. Again, more delusions. Because by that time, Esomi and I were already married, and she was… she was carrying our baby daughter. It was hard to tell Esomi was pregnant—she always had a very slim figure and she wore these flowy dresses. So when she told Zev she was pregnant, he couldn’t handle it. That night, Esomi went missing….”

My heart dropped.

“I, ugh… lost them that same night, my wife and my daughter… to the hands of my once best friend, a man I’d called my brother….”

Nolan’s hands trembled slightly as he reached for another cigarette. The silence stretched between us, heavy with grief and unspoken horrors. His story took a dark turn, one I’d never expect….

‘Nolan, I’m so sorry. I had no idea—’

“He made me watch,” he interjected. “The same torment he went through years before, he gave my wife the same treatment. And he bragged about it the entire time. I was strapped down, helpless… I couldn’t do shit for her. Yeah….” Nolan breathed, trying to hold back his tears. “I was his captive too, but he let me go once he was done with her. He said he let me go, so I could live with the guilt of not being able to save them.” Nolan let out a dark gut twisting chuckle. “That sick son of a bitch said he wanted me to feel the same hopelessness he’d felt when I found him in that cave. That watching someone you love suffer and being powerless to stop it was the greatest torture of all.”

He lit his cigarette with shaking hands, the flame briefly illuminating the raw pain etched across his face.

“My daughter... she was still in Esomi’s womb when he killed them both. I never got to hold her, never got to see her face. We’d already picked out a name—Melody. Esomi wanted to call her that because she said our love was like music.” He brought his head down—I was speechless. “So, now you understand why I didn’t want to go in there. I didn’t want to relive all of that. Seeing Elara, and what Zev did to her, made me think of my Esomi. Zev wants me alive, but his boss doesn’t. Zev could have killed us, but instead, he tossed us before a dungeon. That’s what he likes to do, work his power over you, to make him feel strong, to make him feel powerful. It’s a sick thrill he gets, because he knew for the longest time that he was helpless and weak. He hated the idea that I’d saved him and continued to insist that he didn’t owe me shit.” Nolan scoffed. “Saving him… is a regret I have to live with every fucking day.”

‘You didn’t know, Nolan….’

“Yeah, I didn’t. I didn’t know he’d turn into the very thing that robbed him of everything. And now, he’s the one behind the knife, doing the same thing that was done to him to innocent people.”

‘Why didn’t you tell me all of this sooner?’

“It wouldn’t have changed things, would it? And here and now, it still changes nothing. Nothing, really….”

I felt my heart shatter for him. The weight of his confession pressed down on my chest until I could barely breathe. Everything made sense now—his protective nature, his reluctance to get close, the way he'd fought so hard to keep me safe.

‘It changes everything,’ I said softly. ‘It changes how I see your strength, Nolan. You’ve been carrying this alone for so long.’

He let out a bitter laugh. “Strength? I failed them both. I couldn’t protect the people who mattered most.”

‘You didn’t fail them, Nolan. You were made helpless by a monster.’

“A monster I saved,” he countered, his eyes distant. “That’s the joke of it all. I saved him, and he destroyed everything I loved in return.”

‘You couldn't have known what he would become. The man you saved wasn’t the same creature that took them from you. That thing wearing Zev’s face—that's not your friend anymore.’

“Sometimes I wonder if he was always capable of it. Maybe the torture just stripped away whatever humanity he had left, and revealed what was underneath all along.”

‘Or maybe it broke something in him that can’t be fixed. Either way, you aren’t responsible for his choices.’ I moved closer to Nolan, my scales glinting in the dim light. ‘You showed mercy to someone who needed it. That wasn't wrong.’

Nolan stared at the horizon, smoke curling from his lips. “Mercy,” he repeated, the word hollow. “Such a tricky word. Some people deserve it, most don’t. When the gods were here, they talked about balance—light and dark, mercy and justice. They preached it, and at one time, I listened. Until I made that mistake that day. If I’d left him there to die, or if I’d never found him, do you know how many lives would have been saved? Not just my family, Freya. Where is the balance in that? I used to think I was doing the right thing. Being the good guy, helping people, saving lives. But every time I see what Zev has become, what he’s done to others... it just brings back so much guilt. And now, he knows all about you,” Nolan said as he got up. The gunslinger looked down at me with heavy concern and said, “I’d be damned if I let him do it all over again.”

‘What are you saying, Nolan?’

“You’re going to be the one who kills that son of a bitch.”

My eyes grew.

I felt a chill run through my scales as he continued, “I’ve seen what you’re capable of, Freya. That darkness you’re so afraid of? It’s exactly what we need to end him.” His voice hardened. “I can’t do it myself. I wish I could, but I’m nowhere near Zev’s level. While I care for you a great deal, I wouldn’t suggest you facing him without knowing for sure you’d win. Freya, with Theron’s powers, you’d be fucking unstoppable. All you have to do is boost your necro proficiency and learn how to use the darkness as a tool, just like Theron did.”

I looked at him a bit confused. He crouched down and took me by the shoulders. “Don’t worry. I won’t let you get sucked in. Your willpower is high, and you have a good head on your shoulders. This isn’t about revenge, this is about undoing a mistake. Zev and his king, they terrorize people, rob them of their lives, their families, their dreams and their fucking sanity. Normally, balance would be redirected by the gods, but they aren’t here. You confirmed it. They are trapped. Now, I don’t know what that’s all about, and I don’t know how to reverse it, but one thing I do know is that you weren’t sucked into that realm. You’re here, Freya, in the present. You can stop Zev, the Leech King, and that mother fucker Nyx and his stupid tournament! You can do it all. Build your thralls, become more proficient, level up, and take it all down!”

I paused, looking at him wearily.

“I’m not going to force you to do something you’re not ready for. I understand that you’re scared of the darkness inside you. But fear isn’t always a bad thing. It keeps us cautious, keeps us from diving headfirst into bullshit. And it keeps us self-aware. What matters is how you use it.”

I looked down at my claws, flexing them thoughtfully. The darkness that had saved us all now seemed both a blessing and a curse. ‘You really think I could take down Zev? And the Leech King?’

“I’ve seen what you can do with just a fraction of your power. You bound a dungeon boss, Freya. With not even a fourth of your proficiency. You manipulated darkness like it was an extension of yourself. And that’s just the beginning. Sure, you needed a conduit, but you’re still growing, still learning. Imagine what you can accomplish with patience and proper training.” He looked at me more intently. “I thought what you’d said was a joke, about saving the world. Because how could a small dragon hold the fate of Etheryn in her hands? But I supported you anyway, because we are desperate. I was desperate. I wanted you to grow strong, to believe. But I haven’t been more sure about us winning that tournament until now. And it goes beyond that. You’re not only going to save it, you’re going to be the one to repair it. And you won’t be alone.”

‘No pressure or anything!’

Nolan chuckled and straightened himself out. “I’ve never left your side, have I?”

This was immense.

“We find Esme her ingredients, we dungeon raid, you get stronger and more proficient, and when the time comes, we face Zev again. He’ll lead us to The Leech King, and we’ll finally shut down their operation.”

‘All in time before the tournament?’

“That’s the plan.” He smiled. “I know I don’t say this enough but, I’m proud of you, Freya. Or should I be calling you goddess?”

‘Ew,’ I cheesed. ‘I think I liked it better when you called me short fry.’

He smirked. “So, what do you say?”

I smiled. ‘Hell yeah! I’m in!’
