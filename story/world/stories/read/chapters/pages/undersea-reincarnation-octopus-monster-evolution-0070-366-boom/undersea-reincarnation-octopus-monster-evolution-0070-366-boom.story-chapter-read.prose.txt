Henry didn’t really sweat when he cranked the skill nexus pressure up the first time. Especially because he gave his spirit time to get used to the pressure. He didn’t flag on the second round either. But on the third, a low grunt escaped his beak. By now, the nine orbs hovering above him looked a little foggy. The pressure applied to the water was immense, and it wanted to decompress, which was putting more pressure on his twenty-seven skill instances.

It was a balancing act. Between maintaining the squeezing force on the nexuses even throughout his arms and maintaining the orbs intact, Henry felt like he was walking on a tight-rope with weights around his ankle, around his wrists, and with a ball balancing above his head. All the meanwhile, he had to repeat a bunch of nonsensical hymns without stopping for a second.

It would have been impossible for him to maintain this level of focus in his old life but right now? Well, he could hold on to this state for a while still. It was certainly taxing. But while keeping things constant was within his reach, he knew that his soul was slowly approaching its limit.

The strain had ramped up quite a lot. It wasn’t easy to compare to levels he had reached against the Stinglance, but it wasn’t far off. Though this time, it was spread out much more evenly.

Taking a deep breath, Henry went arm by arm, compressing Control Water instances a little bit more and that was done, he upped the pressure on all the orbs hovering above him until he thought he tasted blood.

From the corner of his eye, he noticed the center of the nine orbs slowly crystallize as the water began turning solid under the tremendous pressure and somehow, the orbs seemed to shine a little more, as if they were capturing and reflecting the sparse moonlight a little better.

He was progressing well. It felt like he was basically cooking himself in the oven, like soft clay turning solid and thanks to all the work his Octominds and Control Water were pulling, a few notifications came through. One of them immediately caught his eyes, and it made complete sense that the Aspect containing Trickster Domain and Control Water gained some levels.

Thaumaturgy of the Many-Faced Trickster (B): Level 6 → Level 8

It must have already been close to a level-up, after the fight with Attal, Henry realized. The strain from handling the soul-exercises and managing the Octominds was bearing fruit. Ever since his encounter with the Stinglance, this Aspect has progressed the most, but he couldn’t dwell on that right this moment. He wasn’t done yet. Not even close.

Henry wasn’t sure how long it took to stabilize the upped pressure and by now, he knew that he couldn’t increase it any further. Anything more, and he was almost sure the “soul muscle” would severely tear, and even if his estimate was wrong, he wasn’t willing to put himself out of commission for days.

Anytime, now, he thought, breathing hard as his body and spirit pulsed with pain. But Henry didn’t relax his grip. He held on, breathing slowly and deeply, pulling in the mana from his environment and channeling it to Life Surge, which kept the healing, soothing energies flowing throughout his soul. Henry didn’t let any thoughts wander in. He relished in the pressure. Pictured it reinforcing his soul. Reinforcing those fibers. He allowed his spirit to get used to the pressure. He ignored his clones. He ignored his environment. He just focused on the cold water entering his gills.

In. Then out.

It might have taken minutes. It’s also possible it had taken an hour or two. But eventually, the strain of the pressure eased up, even though he didn’t relax the force he was applying to highly reflective ice while all throughout his spirit, he could feel a wave of energy rippling out of his core, spreading all the way down his arm, echoing back to the core where it would start over. Every spot it traversed, the strain would lighten. It was barely noticeable from a single pass, but after dozens—if not hundreds of back and forth—it was obvious. And his spirit wasn’t the only part that benefited.

The skill nexuses, each maintaining an instance of Control Water, grew a little energized by every wave. And with every wave, Henry felt like he could pressurize them even more. It was as if all of a sudden, they weren’t made of the same soul-stuff. They felt much more resilient.

Henry didn’t fight the change. He just let it do its thing. And by the time the waves of energies stopped and Henry’s awareness of his surroundings returned, he couldn’t feel the pressure anymore. When he looked up, the orbs were still there though by now, a whole damn parade of all sorts of fish was surrounding him, some of which were luminescent, which caused all the orbs to glitter like disco-balls.

Wait…

Henry looked up toward the surface, and he realized that either his eyes got a little too used to the darkness, or that he’d just spent all night below water. But while that sent a little bolt of panic through his chest, the anxiety was immediately washed away when he noted the two new notifications waiting for him.

Thaumaturgy of the Many-Faced Trickster (B): Level 8 → Level 9

Predation of the Trickster (B): Level 4 → Level 5

Lifeweaving of the Trickster (B): Level 6 → 7

New Trait Unlocked: Depth-Tempered Soul (B)

Depth-Tempered Soul (B): Hardens the soul against external attacks and internal strain, allowing for a much more intensive mana channeling with less risk of self-injury.

New Trait Unlocked: Rupture Casting (B)

Rupture Casting (B): By dangerously compressing the constructs that bring your abilities to bear, you greatly empower the potency of their effect to the point where temporarily, your skills can match and surpass those of a greater rank.

Henry grinned as he quickly parsed through the notifications, but unlike back when he’d first unlocked his first passive ability, he didn’t have a class anymore. It had fused into his species so right now, he could feel two potent, incredibly heavy constructs floating in the middle of his chest, trying to find a spot in his soul where to latch on.

He had to guide the two new abilities to their rightful spot. Immediately. But his bandwidth was a little strained right now, to say the least. He couldn’t disarm all of the instances of Control Water all at once, not with the pressure each of them was under. So, slowly, Henry activated Trickster’s Fortitude, traced back its location in his soul, then planted Depth-Tempered Soul right next to it, where the trait quickly and seamlessly snapped into place. As for the Rupture Casting, he didn’t really have anything similar to graft it next to, but it did fit pretty well under the Aspect of Thaumaturgy.

Once the second trait took root in its rightful place, Henry finally began working on winding down.

First, he began by decompressing the skill-nexuses of the first arm and instantly, the strain on those same nexuses nearly tripled. Henry tried to keep the pressurized ice under control, but he knew what was coming and so, he spared a quick illusion to scare away the curious fish around him.

They were quick to scatter. Which was for the best. Less than a second after he began reducing the force he was applying to the ice, the pressure slipped his containment, and the ice detonated like a grenade, sending shards of pressurized ice that would have minced the mundane—and even lower rank—creatures. The shards ended up prickling his skin, but that was mostly it.

Over the next few minutes, he quickly went through the rest. Detonation after detonation, the surrounding fish were scared away, and once he was done, Henry let out a tired sigh as he finally allowed himself to look through his clones and see what was going on.

That was when he sensed it again.

Freezing in place, Henry looked up while his Octominds readied themselves, but the only thing above him was clear waters and some lazily swimming blue fish. He stared for a few more moments, switching to Mana Sight, but nothing felt out of the ordinary.

What the hell is going on?

Henry felt a shiver run down his arms. He looked around and really tried to see anything out of the ordinary, but there was nothing. Still, Henry could swear he’d sensed something watching, but when nearly a five minutes went by without him sensing or seeing anything, he decided to leave.

Whatever was happening, it didn’t look like he was going to figure out just now. So he took a look through his island-clones, and pretty quickly, he had sight of Velistraine, Attal, and likely, everyone else of note, which was when his wariness got replaced with annoyance.

God…dammit. I’m late, he tiredly cursed, then Henry began changing back into his human form. Already, Ash and her opponent were standing face to face in the western side of the island, while Maurice—suddenly noting Henry’s change of focus—began peppering him with questions.

“[Henry! Where were you? Did something happen? You weren’t answering! Did you find something fun?]”

“[Sorry. Was training. I’ll be there in a second,]” Henry answered through his clone, which was pretty effective at calming Maurice down and while he worked on his disguised form, he quickly reviewed what his clones had seen. Okay, nothing too bad, Henry thought as he glanced over his back once more.Velistraine had tried speaking to his Maurice-assigned clone but got no response, and Attal had just shown up a few minutes ago, having likely been sequestered within his lab. The clones surrounding him he’d set to watch for threats had noted nothing, so while Attal might have still been the one whose presence Henry had noted, it was unlikely.

No… that makes no sense. I already got a good sense of him when we fought. This was something else. Or I’m just imagining it, he thought as he swam up to the surface. He didn’t believe that last part. But what could this be? Divination, maybe? If so, who was it?

I guess I’ll keep an eye out, he thought, still feeling uneasy. But again, he wasn’t about to resolve this right this second, so he might as well go and watch the fight. Breaking through the surface, Henry lifted himself up with Telekinesis. He considered switching places with his clones, but… he had a few seconds to try something else. So why not?

Turning his attention to Rupture Casting, Henry tightened his grip around the Telekinesis’s instance, and the purple light surrounding him flared in intensity. Pressuring the nexus came almost as easily as breathing. And when he took stock of the strain it was causing his soul, Henry was happy to find none. So Henry threw himself in the vague direction of the others.

He broke the sound barrier instantly.

Laughing as the waves flashed below him, Henry launched himself higher and higher, taking in the sights from above. At least he’d done what he came for, and the new ability was working great. Now the question was, on what was he going to test these new abilities? And how far could he push them?
