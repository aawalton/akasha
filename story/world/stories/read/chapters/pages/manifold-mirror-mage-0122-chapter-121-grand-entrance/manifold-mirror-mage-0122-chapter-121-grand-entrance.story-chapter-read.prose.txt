The stately crystalline doors swung open, revealing a glittering pathway of stars connecting the grand entrance-way of the Crystal Ring Academy with the dungeon-seal. A small crowd of onlookers watched as a grand procession marched forward, led by Headmaster Vespertine and a small formation of Inquisitors.

Gio stood next to his mentor, both of them politely bowing in the presence of the approaching royalty. Though Saint Baphelus was the undisputed master of his domain, he had explained that showing deference to the visiting queen would show his friendliness, as well as give her a measure of face for this affair.

These weird rules of etiquette and politics… why is it important that she be ‘given face’, anyway? Aren’t we supposed to be allies?

As the Queen’s posse inched closer and closer across the pathway, Gio gradually began to feel her radiant presence.

Chandrika’s mother, Witch-Queen of Ujjbala Sūrya, and a powerful [Sage] in her own right, appeared in the doorway. She wore a silken veil with intricate jewelry seeming to drape elegantly across her body, from the crown of her head, tied to her wrists, and linked to armor-like rings on each finger. She was accompanied by a small party of similarly dressed, albeit less regal, men and women, as well as a few fascinating humanoid machines.

The machines that accompanied the queen looked to be part mechanical warrior, and part weaving machine, or perhaps some type of loom. Each automaton consisted of a system of filigree framing that was laced with hundreds of silken strands of golden thread, similar to the appearance of Chandrika’s magic. They held long and thin brass spears that were threaded like oversized needles, and seemed to constantly rattle with a pleasant rhythm, as their inner mechanisms constantly wove their fibers into some unseen work.

One of these constructs stood out slightly to Gio’s justification sense, causing him to startle just slightly. He looked at the feminine construct, as its spool-like eyes trained on him for just a moment too long.

I surely must be mistaken… because that one feels… off. I’m probably just still a little frazzled from earlier.

Headmaster Vespertine, who was impeccably dressed in her ceremonial robes and a stylized updo, walked just to the side of the Queen as she guided her into the center of the school. Just behind them was Chandrika, and Gio nearly shivered as he caught her icy eyes.

Oh no… Chandrika looks absolutely furious.

His best friend was good at hiding her emotions, but to those who knew her, she became easier to read. She currently had her face schooled into a neutral, diplomatic expression of polite disinterest… but there was a certain something to the purse of her painted lips and the way that she twirled the edge of her Saree in her fingers that spoke of a flaming river of wrath raging just beneath the surface of her expression. One of her mother’s minders seemed to notice this, sneaking a careful glance in Chandrika’s direction.

“I greet thee, O wise Sage.” Saint Baphelus intoned as the procession finally approached the dungeon seal.

Queen Aayana bowed to Saint Baphelus, which was then in turn copied by her entire procession as the foreign queen returned his gesture.

“Venerable Saint Baphelus, you and your wife honor me with such hospitality. It is a boon to see the tenets of learning and the love of magecraft upheld so devoutly in this school.” The Queen enunciated.

She turned to Gio.

“And this must be the young man that I have heard so much about.”

Gio’s heart nearly stopped. It felt like a crossbow bolt had hit him square in the forehead as the Queen addressed him. He felt an urgent, desperate need to do something. But what? His mind raced for the answer as his whole body alerted with the haptic buzz of sudden panic. He quickly followed the feeling to its source, the justification which gushed forth like an unbidden faucet. A single quickening urge- not a command, per se, but something that must be done, lest the proper order of things be broken.

KNEEL.

There was a moment where time stood still. A small voice in his mind lashed out in anger, as pure impudence thrashed against a cage within his mind where it had been so carefully contained for so long. He may be a commoner, but he was not insignificant. Who was this woman to him, that her very presence should be enough to force him to fall to his knees?

A glinting spark of defiance danced behind Gio’s eyes. Chaos.

But Gio turned to Chandrika. His best friend. What purpose would his arrogance and pride serve in this moment? Why shouldn’t he kneel to a woman who had clearly garnered the respect of all of the people present- several of whom had the power to make his life vastly more difficult? Why not kneel to the mother of his friend?

So, Gio prepared to kneel, in respect for this woman’s station- and out of respect for Chandrika.

Only, the justification was wrong. He didn’t need to be kneeling to her at this moment. There was someone else present who was vastly more important and deserved his supplication more than she did.

What?

Gio continued panicking, as more than a second had passed, and he was frozen solid in his indecision.

Why can’t I kneel? Why does it feel like I shouldn’t be kneeling to the Queen? If not her, then whom?

He mentally went through an impromptu checklist of powerful people who might be overriding the Queen in this moment.

Saint Baphelus? No, his master did not demand such formalities, and his station as founder of the school transcended human rituals, only requiring such strange contracts with the spirits that went so far above Gio’s means that a simple gesture such as kneeling could not possibly begin to prove any sentiment at all. His mentor was important, certainly… but not the being that should be addressed before the Queen.

Similarly, Headmaster Vespertine was the recognized Headmaster of the school. Her claim to the academic process was more hands-on than her husband's, but, interestingly enough, she ranked noticeably lower than him in relation to the spirits, despite her rank as master of the Crystal Ring Academy.

Fascinating.

The fact that Gio could feel this minutiae with magic was interesting to him, but in this moment of searching, he felt it prudent to move on rather than dive deeper into that particular intricacy.

Chandrika was a non-starter, and so were the other attendants and low-ranking individuals present. None of the Inquisitors registered as much of anything to his justification sense, even though Arnault was likely among them.

I’m running out of options.

The spirits themselves? Not a chance. The dungeon seal? Inanimate.

Then what? Wait…

The construct that had stood out earlier.

Gio stepped past the Queen, earning a gasp from several attendants and a proud smirk from Saint Baphelus. He knelt in front of the construct, knowing deep down that he was correct, but despite himself, hoping that he had not just made a dire error.

“Very well done. Raise your head, Savior of my granddaughter,” echoed a silk-smooth voice.

Gio raised his head to watch a brilliant cascade of golden thread leave the construct. The strands wove together in mid-air to form a spritely being made from light and threads. The ethereal child-like entity before him had flawless, poreless skin, and silken strands of hair flowed from her head to billow mid-air like they were caught in a phantom stream. She twirled before him, as a glowing dress knitted itself onto her body, and she turned to address the lich.

“It appears that I have lost our little wager!” She cooed.

“A feat for which I cannot claim credit. In a manner of speaking, he taught himself that particular trick.” Baphelus pridefully stated.

“You must be Chandrika’s Grandmother, the Grand Weaver of Bangla. I am honored to greet you and the Queen.” Gio said, having practiced for this moment.

I don’t know what I expected… but this wasn’t it.

“Indeed. Are you now satisfied, Grand Weaver?” Chandrika icily asked. “As I have stated, I do not believe that Giorgio is our enemy.”

His friend’s voice seemed to grasp and tear away a portion of the mirth from the room. Several of the attendants cringed, and her grandmother's smile faded.

“Chandrika…” the sprite fretted. “As I previously explained, I needed to test the boy. If he had reacted poorly, he would have become a suspect in my eyes. As it is, I am officially declaring him innocent.”

Innocent? What… am I ‘innocent’ of?

“I defer to your greater judgement.” Chandrika mechanically replied.

The Queen stepped closer to her daughter.

“Dear… you may speak your mind. We are among friends here.” The Queen stated.

Chandrika simply retreated into herself. Like her grandmother, she appeared to be as emotionless and taciturn as a doll.

“That won’t be necessary, my Queen.” Chandrika bowed.

Her mother pursed her lips in dissatisfaction.

“Come, little spider.” Pleaded the Grand Weaver of Bangla, floating closer. “You’ve been icy cold to us this whole morning. Is this any way to greet your grandmother?”

Chandrika’s glacial facade chipped. Beneath the frozen surface, lava flowed… and started to flow outward.

“Is that what you think you are? My grandmother?” Chandrika lashed. Several attending members of the Queen’s group clammed up, eyes trying to look anywhere else. A gasp rang out from someone involuntarily as time seemed to stop.

“All I see is a weapon. A political show of force meant to intimidate. The thing that I see before me is not my grandmother.” Chandrika wrathfully spat.

The doll-like being pouted. “I know that you don’t like this form, but it is not a lie, dearest. I am no more a frail old woman than the lich is. After a certain point of power, the form one wears changes as easily as one might change one's clothing. This is just one face that I wear-”

“No, this face is the one you wear when you want to send a message. You are not the woman who taught me how to sew. You are not the source of my wit, nor my sense of humor. You are a weapon that is brought out when the country needs to scare its enemies. Similarly, that-” Chandrika pointed at her mother. “Is just a Queen.”

Chandrika stepped toward her.

“A politician. Not my mother.” Chandrika scowled. “Not the woman who taught me how to be proud. Not the kind-hearted woman that I have tried to model myself after, nor the woman who always inspired me to reach for a better version of myself.”

Chandrika’s mother wordlessly watched as her daughter raged. The room was deadly silent as Chandrika continued her rant.

“And YOU-” She drew a finger like a knife in front of one of the Queen’s attendants, drawing her out of the crowd of attendants. “Are not my Aunt. You’re a tool of the Queen. A spymaster. A servant. I don’t care who told you to do it. How DARE YOU use a social skill on my friend in my presence?”

The woman glowered at Chandrika. It seemed that she would be the only one to interrupt the ranting.

“This is unbecoming of a Sage, Rika. You’re embarrassing us in front of foreign political elements.” Said Chandrika’s apparent Aunt.

“Embarrassing?” Chandrika whispered. “I’m embarrassed. I’m embarrassed by all of you. I have never been more ashamed of my family. Do you know how excited I was to introduce you to my friends? How I wanted to show my grandmother the things that I had been working on in the Sewing club? How eagerly I awaited the moment when I would show my mother how I could now cast her signature magic?  No… none of you get to be embarrassed. You want me to act like a sage? Fine.”

Chandrika started to glow. Her eyes became incandescent beacons, and a fiery crown appeared atop her head, charging the threads of her Saree with liquid sunlight.

“I will speak now as a representative of my ancestors.” Chandrika declared, her voice echoing with a chorus of disembodied spirits, as figures started to fill the air around her.

The Queen, Chandrika’s Grandmother, and the rest of the foreign delegation gasped as a court of illusory figures stood beside the young Sage, halfway between the two parties - on one side, her family, and on the other, the school she had grown fond of.

“You have misstepped, degrading and risking the alliance between our country and the City of Rings,” Chandrika announced, imperiously deriding her mother. “You once told me that the Ringed City and the Crystal Ring Academy within its walls were among our most trusted allies. Was this a lie?”

“No.” Her mother replied. For her part, the Queen did not sputter or surrender any measure of her dignity, as she accepted her daughter’s outburst.

“And yet… You arrive here with your full cohort of war-makers, spies, and politicians.” Chandrika said. “You treat this city as if it were enemy soil, when Headmaster Vespertine has taken me under her wing so graciously. Do you doubt your own judgment so much? Or is this incredible woman not the ally you made her out to be, before you sent me here to her place of power?”

The Headmaster wore a schooled neutral expression as she stood beside her husband and listened. This was a family matter, and though this moment’s repercussions would likely echo in the days to come, she would not step in and speak over Chandrika.

“You are correct.” The Queen replied. “Eudoria and Saint Baphelus, as well as the royal family and extended nobility of the City of Rings, have been stout allies of Ujjbala Sūrya for many years, in both trade and national defense. I sent you here to learn True magic under Eudoria… and it appears that you have learned much, while we weren’t paying attention.”

Chandrika held her mother’s stare for a long moment. Gradually, the ghostly figures behind her faded.

“Then why?” She asked. She turned to her grandmother. “Why do this? Why fruitlessly attempt to intimidate my mentor and put my best friend through an insulting test?”

The strange spiritual being that was the Grand Weaver of Bangla shifted in mid-air, compressing in on herself until she wove her fibers down into… an old woman. An aged, tiny woman who looked like a more grey and wrinkled version of Chandrika’s mother shuffled across the dungeon seal to embrace Chandrika.

“Little spider…” Chandrika’s grandmother said. “Let me be clear. We didn’t want to suspect Eudoria… but there were too many questions. Too many coincidences. And your friend was highly suspicious… almost perfectly so. I believe that he has secrets of his own, doesn’t he?”

Gio’s heart beat faster. He still hadn’t told his friends about Rio. But… what did Gio or his reflection have to do with anything? What were they even talking about?

“I am afraid that I must finally step into this conversation, concerning that matter.” Headmaster Vespertine announced. “Giorgio is a valued student of our institution, and any actions of his are our responsibility to bear. Likewise, any secrets of his are rightfully his own to keep.”

That surprised Gio. He turned to the Headmaster, who nodded slightly in his direction.

I… she’s going to stand up for me?

“What actions?” Chandrika hotly replied. She had not run out of steam, removing her Grandmother’s arms from her. “As I said, what sense does it make for Gio to save my life, just to frame me for cursing another student whom I’ve never spoken to?!”

Ah… so this is about Sterling. They thought that I framed Chandrika for it?

The pieces started to click together. The odd boy who had been puppeted by the blue strings during the examination. Gio remembered Sybil declaring that he had been under some kind of ‘Fatebond’ curse, and Decadence afterward informing him that it was apparently Chakraverti family magic.

“Dear, it doesn’t make sense… but you must understand my position.” Chandrika’s mother said. “Eudoria is one of the most frightening masters of intelligence-gathering and statecraft in the world. We could not be too careful and had to respond as if this were a carefully crafted plot. If we had come here without being suspicious, and fallen into some kind of trap…”

“While I appreciate the compliment,” Eudoria grinned. “I cannot help but feel that perhaps this misunderstanding may have been the intended result of this… incident?”

Queen Aayana’s expression shifted into a contemplative one.

“A rift between Ujjbala Sūrya and the Ringed City ...Indeed.” She tutted. “But this draws forth only more questions. Who would benefit from such an arrangement?”

Chandrika’s aunt turned to Gio. Her eyes seemed to dart to… his hair.

“Ataraxia,” she said.

“Giorgio has never been to Ataraxia,” Baphelus said. “And he is my mentee notwithstanding.”

“And he has passed our little test,” Arseya noted. “But… still, he is an element that must be accounted for. Perhaps, though he is not my enemy, my enemy uses him without his knowledge?”

“I’m sorry… What, exactly, am I being accused of?” Gio asked.

“You, my dear, are a strange boy who has befriended my granddaughter, who mysteriously seems to be undetectable to my divinations, and has reported some amount of ability to detect the Weave. You were a complete unknown to me, and I am not used to complete unknowns. I had my daughter perform a… test of loyalty upon you, which you passed with flying colors… but without knowing anything about you, I do not feel confident that you are not somehow being puppetted by some force that would seek to undermine the relationship between our countries.”

So… the weird kneeling justification was some kind of loyalty test?

“It was a test that you had no right to administer.” Chandrika sniped.

“No, I allowed them to do it,” Baphelus interjected. “Your grandmother asked me about it via messenger earlier today. In fact, we made a bet on it.”

Both Chandrika and Gio turned to Saint Baphelus with incredulous expressions.

“What? Don’t look at me like that.” Baphelus chortled. “I knew you would pass. It was simultaneously a chance to clear your name, as well as make you look good. Do you know what the test was?”

“Some kind of… justification-based test? As soon as the queen addressed me, I felt the urge to kneel to Chandrika’s grandmother, even though I didn’t know that she was here.” Gio answered.

“In a way, yes. It wouldn’t have worked if you couldn’t detect justifications. Madame Mata, would you care to explain?” Baphelus asked.

Chandrika’s Aunt addressed Gio. “I used a skill on you. [Discern Loyalty]. Normally, the skill will only let me know if you’ve made any oaths of fealty to national leaders… for example, it informed me through system dialogue that you are a natural-born citizen of the Ringed City. But, because I am a [Queen’s Attendant], and because I am in the presence of my mother and sister, it allowed me to create an argument with my magic that ‘a true ally of my country would kneel to my mother, the Grand Weaver of Bangla before my sister, the Queen’ - You would not have been able to detect the skill if you were unable to sense justifications in magic… nor if you were not ‘a true ally’.”

Gio tried to follow the confusing trail of logic.

“That’s…”

“Convoluted!” Chandrika’s grandmother laughed. “And I bet your mentor that it wouldn’t work.”

“It’s an old trick.” Baphelus shrugged. “But a good one. We used to use it to ferret out spies back in-”

“Dear,”  Eudoria warned.

“Well… let me cut myself off before I get lost in tangents,” Baphelus said. “The point is, though you would not automatically have been a suspect if you had failed the test, it goes a long way to establishing your credibility. The fact that you detected it means that you consider yourself an ally to Chandrika and her family.”

“That’s… good.” Gio hummed.

“And now that we have established that my friend is my friend, we can get back to the part where it was incredibly rude and presumptuous that you’ve all decided to test him in this manner, when all he has done is save my life,” Chandrika noted.

“That still does not explain why he is undetectable to the Weave, dear,” Arseya said.

Chandrika grimaced. “I… I guess that’s true, but I still don’t believe that Gio is being used against us. Whatever it is that’s causing him to mess with our magic is…”

She seemed to be searching for the right words, without finding any.

Baphelus looked toward Gio.

Gio knew what he had to do. He smoothed out his uniform, feeling his pulse in his ears. Why was he so nervous all of a sudden? He had been planning on doing this eventually, anyway.

Gio coughed. “So… I can explain why your magic doesn’t work well around me.”
