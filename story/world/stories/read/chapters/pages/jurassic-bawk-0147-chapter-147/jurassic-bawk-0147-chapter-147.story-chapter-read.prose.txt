Charlemagne watched with satisfaction as his ship absorbed another Knight’s boat.  This one had already been upgraded once, which apparently meant that it counted twice for the purposes of triggering an upgrade.  Once the absorption was complete, the rooster’s vessel shone with a blinding silver light for a while.  When the light faded, the ship had gotten even stronger.

“Bawk!” the rooster ordered, satisfied with his preparations.  He had been loath to wait until the captured vessel had arrived in order to challenge the defenses outside Bluebeard’s Chamber, but his newly upgraded ship looked so much more capable that he felt the time had been well spent.  It was time to overcome the final obstacle and finish this Floor.

The vessel surged forward as the rooster took flight, joining Phatagin and Phasima in the air.  The plan was simple: the boat would move into range on its own, allowing the trio to fight from the air.  Charlemagne was particularly interested in seeing how much the Mask of the Predator enhanced the pangolin’s combat ability.  While he did not find himself in need of Items that boosted his martial prowess, he figured that equipping the hens and chicks on his farm might be useful.

A hint of a novel feeling bubbled up from the rooster’s subconscious, bringing with it an ill-defined but all-too-real pain, as he thought back to the combat squad that he had put together at his first farm.  While he couldn’t remember the names of the hens, or their faces, he missed them all the same.  He vowed to himself that he would stop at nothing to protect his new farm.

Charlemagne’s daydreaming was interrupted as his ship passed through the invisible barrier that triggered the enemy ships to attack.  The rooster’s body was suddenly yanked out of the sky, his feet once again stuck to the deck as if glued, while his consciousness looked down from above.

“Bawk!” he yelled down at himself in frustration.

“Well, I guess that answers that question,” Phasima laughed.  “The Knight must have to stay on the ship, but the Squire doesn’t have to.  That’s a pretty nifty piece of information…I could probably sell that.”

Josephine, having heard the Vessarian from her hideout below deck, popped her head out for a moment.

“I doubt that.  You’ll probably find that it’s covered by your NDA.”

“Could we perhaps focus on the battle ahead?”  Phatagin asked.  “There seems to be a significantly greater number of enemy vessels than last time.”

“Well, we’ve got a better plan and a bigger ship,” Phasima shot back.  “Let’s see who can sink the most, eh?”

“Bawk!” Charlemagne agreed as he tried to remember how to operate the different ‘controls’ that would allow him to join in the fight.

Phasima shot off with a burst of fire mana.  She laughed as she weaved around cannonballs, causing the pirate ships to waste their fire or, even better, to hit their own allies.  Making the most of her mana, she briefly tagged each of the enemy ships within range with a blast of fire, ensuring that they remained focused on her as she pulled a large clump of them away from Charlemagne.

Phatagin, for his part, couldn’t move as quickly as Phasima, but he was even harder to hit.  He used his Momentum Core to great effect by robbing incoming cannonballs of their speed and then using the stored energy to accelerate those most likely to hit their own allies.  Although he lacked the sheer offensive capacity of the Vessarian, his boosted cannonballs did incredible damage.

While his two allies were causing havoc within the enemy’s ranks, Charlemagne accelerated straight ahead and began unloading barrages from both the ship’s cannons, which now numbered twenty on each side.  He went with the tried-and-true method of firing both sides simultaneously, which lowered his overall effectiveness but prevented him from losing track of which side was ready at any given time.  His cannons were also significantly more powerful than they had been in the last battle, requiring fewer shots to put an enemy ship down.

But that didn’t mean that Charlemagne’s ship remained unscathed.  The battle was intense.  Shots flew everywhere, while several enemies attempted to ram the rooster’s vessel once their cannons were crippled by a broadside.  One actually succeeded, mostly because Charlemagne started turning the ship the wrong way and had to correct himself.  His vessel’s reinforced hull shuddered under the impact but held firm, and a final broadside both dislodged and sank the enemy ship moments later.

Over on her end of the battle, Phasima was switching from evasion to offense, targeting the most damaged ships and putting them down with bursts of fire from close range.  While only a few ships had been outright destroyed, she had successfully crippled an entire flank while moving them to the other side of the tower.

Phatagin was even more successful.  His cannonball acceleration trick had proved quite deadly, allowing him to sink almost half of the ships that were in his vicinity.  Unfortunately, he was now experiencing the downside of too much early success as most of the remaining ships were undamaged and the less crowded conditions meant that he had to work harder to get enemy ships into the right positions.  Still, it wasn’t the worst problem to have.

Charlemagne was pleased at how well that the battle was going.  His sturdy vessel was shaking off enemy fire and returning it with greater power and volume.  His allies were doing their jobs, taking the pressure off of him as he cut down his foes one by one.  And Josephine hadn’t even screamed once.  Of course, thinking all of this meant that something bad was bound to happen.

And it did.

One moment, Charlemagne was launching a double broadside that eliminated another of the black-sailed enemies, and the next he was staring at a message from the Squiggles, wondering how that had even happened.

Charlemagne’s bawk of rage was cut short as the enemies Phatagin had been entertaining found a new target for their cannonballs.

Brett flopped down on his sofa and let out a long exhale through his nose.  The past few weeks had been trying, to say the least.

“I never knew that ants and termites could be so problematic,” he moaned to Felissa.  “They never really bothered humans all that much.”

Felissa, who had been napping on the sofa’s armrest, opened one of her eyes and yawned, showing off her sharp fangs.

“There were quadrillions of them when the System Integration happened, and they didn’t depend on technology to tame the natural world.  What did you expect would happen?”

“I dunno, I guess I just figured that humans would just keep stomping on them as usual.  I mean, do you know how many bacteria live inside a human body?  But most humans with more than a few levels under their belt are safe from being taken over.”

“Yes, but that’s different.  A level 20 bacteria or virus can’t kill a level 2 human without the right skillset.  And many felines have gained Skills that help defend them against microbes.  I assume it’s the same for humans?”

“Yeah,” Brett agreed.

“Well, there’s no Skill that protects you from having your brain consumed by an ant, so I think your comparison is faulty.”

The human deity sighed again.

“Perhaps you’re right,” he admitted.

“Of course, I’m right!”  Felissa exclaimed.  “The issues are completely different.  The microbes and parasites aren’t really a problem anymore: the ones that could take over humans and felines have already done so, and all they got for their trouble was getting their hosts killed.  That’s a genetic dead end, so to speak.  As long as we ensure that the System doesn’t start offering microscopic creatures more movement options, it’s not an issue.  Formua Integralis on the other hand…”

“Is an issue,” Brett finished.

“For now, yes,” Felissa agreed.  “But think about it for a while, does she have to be?”

Brett thought about it.

“I mean, she could have chosen another course, sure.  But that ship has sailed…she’s turning the Amazon into a giant methane farm.  And then there’s whatever’s going on in the Great Vasyugan Mire, which is probably something similar.  All that extra methane and carbon dioxide aren’t going to kill anyone, at least not for a few years yet, but she’s basically trying to engineer Earth’s climate to better suit her followers.”

Felissa gave Brett a pointed look.

“Oh, come off it.  Your felines benefitted just as much as humans did from technology.  Remember laser pointers, salmon-flavored kitty treats, and heated floors?” the human deity complained.

“Yeah, but felines don’t get cold the same way that humans do,” Felissa shot back.  “Don’t get me wrong, I’ve had salmon.  It’s to kill for.  But you’re acting all high and mighty over a change that will amount to what?  A few degrees Celsius?”

“That’s just for starters, but yeah.  But think about it…termites eat wood and emit both methane and carbon dioxide as waste.  Imagine if they are allowed to breed unchecked for a decade.  The last I checked, plants aren’t a part of the System and there aren’t any plans to integrate them.  What happens when a group of high-level termites goes to town on the world’s surviving forests?”

“We’ll just have to vote in a patch over her objections to add some sort of cooling effect, then.  I can’t see the majority of GOD wanting the polar ice caps to melt.  Well, some of the ones that rule the oceans might, but we can just outvote them, too.”

Brett sat up and ran his fingers through his hair and he considered the idea.

“Yeah…yeah!  That’s true.  Magic is the answer.  And, if that doesn’t work, there’s always the option to choose violence.”

“Now you’re talking,” Felissa responded as she pulled her upper body back into a stretch that left her front limbs extended.  “But how are you going to stop that many ants and termites?  An adult human can kill a lot of them, but only above ground.  Once they go down into their tunnels, it’s almost impossible to get to them.”

Brett smiled.

“We’ll form a coalition of myrmecophages, of course.”

Felissa nodded slowly as she considered the ramifications of such an alliance.

“Then we’ll need anteaters, aardvarks, aardwolves, birds, lizards, snakes, frogs, toads, and pangolins.  That’s a diverse group…how are we going to wrangle them all?  I can’t imagine you’d want your role in this to be public.”

“Hmmmm, we need someone with prestige.  Someone that is either pretty high up in the overall rankings, possesses the right connections, or has a well-known Champion,” Brett mused.  “You know, it just so happens that Charlemagne’s Party still contains a pangolin, maybe we use that?”

“But he’s not on Earth.” Felissa objected.  “You know the expression better than anyone: ‘out of sight, out of mind’.  We need someone whose Champion is around to serve as a threat.”

“Well,” Brett replied after a moment’s thought.  “Let’s start pulling things together.  If Phatagin the pangolin returns, we can use his arrival to kick things off.  But maybe we should get an alternative, someone who’s well-connected.  Ah, I have just the deity in mind.  Can you reach out to Hyaenus for me?  And keep it quiet?”

“I hate that clown!” Felissa hissed.  “Fine, I’ll do it…but you owe me!”

The human deity chuckled.

“That’s fair.”

“Big time,” the feline deity insisted.

“Maybe Phatagin will return to Earth soon, which means you’re off the hook,” Brett answered, turning serious for a moment.  “Although if he and Charlemagne return at the same time, that might cause more issues than it would solve…”
