Once again, I had barely felt her approach. If it hadn’t been for the lingering effects of the Crystal Fields, she could have come up to me, and I’d have never known.

Many things went through my mind. I wanted to say linked to our erased past, maybe even compliment her skill. Instead, something completely different came out of my mouth.

“How long were you watching?” I lowered Serp.

“Almost all of it,” she casually replied, turning in the direction of the shattered patch. “The moon ray caught my attention, so I came to check it out.”

Of course. Despite her friendly exterior, we remained in the Grand Arcana. Back in our past run, I knew I couldn’t compete against her, so I didn’t even try. Here, things were different, and she was fully aware.

“Clearly not something to be considered a victory condition,” she added. “Even if it were rather large.”

“A deity request.” There was no point in hiding it. She must have seen the goddess appear, either way. “No cards, no booster spheres.”

“Pity. It was an interesting fight.” She turned towards me. “So, what’s next?”

“Next?”

“Come on,” She let out a chuckle. “I know you better than that. I can tell that you’ve already decided. Tell me and I’ll help you out.”

This crap again. It was normal hearing it from Eleonor or others, but seeing Sparta say it infuriated me. This was a side of her I didn’t know she had. Or maybe I just hadn’t noticed?

“I don’t want to be indebted to your deity.” I looked her in the eyes. They were normal, which meant that I wasn’t talking to Mercury right now.

“Is that what you’re worried about?” She placed her hand on my shoulder.

The normal reaction was to move back before she got a chance to do it. Old habits died hard, however, so I let her.

“You won’t. You’ve helped him enough times, so think of this as compensation for saving me during our fight with Hyena.”

That was too good to be true, and she knew it.

“In fact, I’m here to ask whether you’d be interested in being recruited.”

The moment she said that, I went on guard. Neither my Listener nor Observer cards let me notice anything out of the ordinary, but they would only work if my opponents didn’t have Thief cards. At this point, it was safe to assume that most of them had a way to hide from me.

There were far better cards to remain undetected: the Chameleon, the Noonlight Shadow, even the Passenger. All of them belonged to Houses that we didn’t have access to, which made the Thief the staple of the day.

I took a step back, raising the tip of my saber.

“You know I’m not interested in pacts.” Subtly, I concentrated on my senses, ready to act at the slightest unusual sound.

“I’m aware.” The smile remained on her face, though the rest of her expression had turned deathly serious. “That’s not what I’m here to offer. What do you know about the factions?”

There it was, that word again. Technically, I was in an informal alliance with Hypnos and whatever faction he was part of. And still, my knowledge on the matter remained rather scant.

“Tell me.”

“Here?” She glanced around. “Don’t get me wrong. I like moonlight and crystals as much as any girl, but I could think of a few better places.”

I unsummoned my blade. If she wanted me dead, she had several opportunities to attempt it. On my end, I still hadn’t used up my Eight of Chalice—ensuring that the first strike that pierced me wouldn’t count.

“Let’s go.” I turned in the direction of the forest.

The first minute we walked in silence. I kept an eye open for ambushes along the way, and she seemed somewhat reluctant to be the one to start the conversation. This definitely wasn’t the Sparta I knew, not fully, at least. The past her would have gone straight to the point without hesitation or fear of consequences. Of course, in her case there almost always were none.

“So you know someone called Rayle?” I broke the silence.

“Rayle? Don’t think so. Who is that?”

“Someone I was warned to watch out for,” I lied.

She whistled.

“If it’s someone that has you on edge, he’s definitely worth getting to know.” She attempted a joke. It fell flat.

“I didn’t say he has me on edge.” My ego didn’t allow me to let this stay. “What about the rest of the newbies?” I shifted the topic slightly.

A crow darted out of the forest, flying dangerously low. It didn’t appear to be any threat, so I chose not to react. Sparta, on the other hand, drew her thread-dagger. Aether lights danced in the night, forming a series of scribbles in the air. Moments later, bloody bird parts fell on the forest road.

“Yes.” She didn’t deny it. “Some are part of my faction.” She shifted the topic as well, going back to the one she had originally started. “They’re from different groups, have different deities, and have even joined the Grand Arcana at different times. The thing that unites us is a set of common principles.”

“Like an organization,” I said, keeping an eye out for other birds.

The death of the crow hadn’t gone too well in the forest. A series of other noises had surrounded us, though none posed a danger.

“Or a guild.” I turned my head towards her slightly.

“I guess it’s close enough. There’re no headquarters, no official charter or rulebook. What it boils down to is a set of people who have grouped together through the various trials and stages of the Arcana. We follow a set of deities, of course, but even there things are flexible.”

“Like the Chosen of Anubis,” I said deliberately to gauge her reaction.

“No!” she snapped. “Not like them! They’re breaking the pact to get a little power without bothering with the consequences. We…” She paused for a split second. “Other factions stay within the confines of the rules. There’s nothing wrong in working to get ahead, just not by pissing off the rule-masters.”

Sparta stopped.

“I’ll say it to you straight. We want you on our side. You’ve proven you’re strong, and you’re an anomaly. You’ll only grow faster from here. With a bit of direction, you can get a lot more done in the next year than you could possibly imagine.”

You’re aware I have three Grand Arcana cards? I thought. At the very least, she suspected about two. And still she seemed to be confident that her faction had a lot more to offer. Sadly, it was all too common that participants within the Grand Arcana would lie.

“I’m off to capture an albino creature,” I said.

“What?” Sparta blinked.

“You asked me what my plan was. That’s my plan.”

“So, you’re joining us?”

I caught a glimmer of hope in her eyes. I really hated doing this to her, but there was no way I’d agree to something so reckless, especially if Mercury was involved.

“Let’s get closer first.”

“One doesn’t exclude the other.” The usual smile appeared on her face. “Tell you what. After I help you out, I’m yours for the rest of the night.”

“That might be tricky. A local noble placed me in a barracks with a hundred other people.”

“Who says we have to go there?” She slid her index finger along my cheek. Once again, I let her.

I really don’t like her, Serp grumbled.

Shut up, I thought.

“So, that creature.” The woman switched to business mode. She was smart enough to know that pushing from this point on would be counterproductive. “What’s so special about it?”

“No idea.” I decided to play her game. “It’s a lynx, which means it’s fast and can spot us from a mile away.”

“That’s… not entirely true.” The corners of her mouth curved further upward. “You haven’t boosted your Thief, have you?”

“I had other uses for the arena boosters,” I said plainly.

“Good thing that I stumbled upon you, then.”

I had no doubt in my mind that I would have captured the creature on my own, but I couldn’t deny that Sparta considerably sped up the process. Through her combination of cards and specials, we were able to pass inches from a creature without disturbing it.

Definitely more than a card, I thought. Mercury had to be involved, but for the moment that was to my benefit.

Unlike the previous albino creature, the lynx was part of a pack. Lying on a thick tree branch, it was snoozing along with several dozen more creatures of its kind. They didn’t share its color, of course, and their status boxes were slightly different. None had cards, neither did they seem remotely strong enough to drop a sphere.

Want me to take them out? Serp offered as we approached them.

I thought you didn’t like biting animals, I countered.

I prefer not to, but beats having to watch you waste time.

Don’t worry. I won’t waste time. “Freeze!” I ordered using the Emperor card.

Dozens of creatures froze up under the weight of the order. Unfortunately for Sparta, she was one of them. My action had caught her completely by surprise, causing her to stop in her tracks. Any hiding skills had ceased to have an effect, revealing her presence. Not that it mattered, for all the beasts that could potentially take advantage were in a lot worse state than she was.

“Sorry,” I whispered as I dashed in the direction of the albino lynx. Several of the others on the branch dropped off as petrified birds. I didn’t go out of my way to kill them, although I didn’t save any of them, either. There was only one goal.

Ten feet from the albino’s tree, I cast a spell circle beneath my right foot. A pillar of stone shot up from the ground, letting me jump right onto the branch.

“Sleep, sleep is a verb,” I started singing as my right hand grabbed the creature by the neck.

It tried to struggle, but the effects of the Emperor’s card still held it firmly, allowing nothing more than a tremble.

“Sleep is a doing word,” I continued.

The trembling lessened. A few seconds more, and the creature in my hand was sleeping as if it had been domesticated its entire life.

So far, things had gone a lot smoother than expected. I had feared that I’d spend the rest of the night destroying parts of the forest as I chased after the albino creature until it was out of stamina. Now came the tricky part—haggling.

I looked around. There was no sign of a merchant. During the fifth trial, he had appeared of his own volition. Clearly, that was no longer allowed. From here on, the only way to engage in trade was to go to the merchant, not the other way around. Although there always were exceptions.

“Merchant,” I said, using up the merchant token I had won during a previous trial.

Instantly, a humanoid figure appeared on the ground by the tree, looking up at me.

“You called, Gregory?” he asked with a smile.

The being was dressed in a set of clothes that seemed to fit the trial-world: comfortable knee-high boots, traveler clothes, and a multi-colored tunic that insulted the senses even in the darkness. Several gold chains hung around the neck of the being. On the outside, he had taken on the appearance of a normal, slightly balding, middle-aged man, yet there was no doubt that a deity stood behind the mask.

“Another interesting specimen you have collected,” the merchant said. “Although not as impressive as your last.”

There’s just no pleasing you, is there?

The merchant alluded to having met us before, but I got the distinct impression this wasn’t the one I had dealt with in the past. He was a lot less enthusiastic and a lot more reserved.

“Bring it here, so we can conclude our trade,” the merchant ordered with the unmistakable authority of a greater deity. It couldn’t be one of the major ones, surely. They would never sully themselves to visit a trial this low.

Quietly, I obeyed. Using my Acrobat card, I leaped off the branch as gently as possible, holding the lynx with both hands. Thankfully, the beast didn’t wake up.

“Here.” I handed it.

The moment the merchant’s hand came into contact with the lynx, the creature disappeared.

“Is there anything else you wish to barter?” Golden eyes stared right at me.

“I’d like Sparta to wake up before—”

“This is your trade.” The merchant cut me off. “You used the token, so only you get to participate.”

That was unexpected. I didn’t remember anything of the sort happening during the previous run.

“And she’s not asleep,” he scoffed. “Her deity is forcing her to pretend, but he’d been quietly watching ever since I arrived. Are you done pretending, Mercury?” the merchant shouted. “Hiding in a puppet doesn’t suit you.”

I heard a noise coming from the direction of Sparta. The woman had just stood up and as the merchant had said, her eyes were glowing with golden light.

“I wasn’t hiding,” she said in a voice no different from her own. “I just didn’t want to disturb you. Since you invited me, though.” Sparta walked up to the merchant, maintaining a steady distance between her and me. “I might as well join. Unless that causes any issues, merchant.”

The two had to know each other, and based on the way they spoke, they weren’t particularly friendly.

“As long as you just watch. So, Gregory, what will it be?”

“How much platinum can I get for that?” I asked.

“A coin,” the merchant said unapologetically. “The lynx is nice, but nothing special. We get one of those every hundred trials.”

A hundred trials? How many groups took part in the Grand Arcana. Going by the numbers alone, that means there had to be several thousand. Even if the same groups went through the trial-world multiple times, the dead had to be constantly replaced.

“I could give you five bricks of gold,” he offered. “Halfway to awakening your blades again. I’ll even throw in a pair of stamina-drain gems. Very useful in the long run.”

He was not wrong, but the way he presented things was very unlike any merchant I had seen before. Rather, he gave the impression of someone reluctantly playing a role he strongly disliked.

“Can you trade cards?” I took a different approach.

The merchant frowned as if I had said something wrong. He clearly wasn’t fond of the topic.

“He got you there.” Mercury laughed.

“I’m only allowed to offer Minor Arcana, and you already have a lot of those. How about—”

“I want a full set!” I quickly said. “But I’ll pay for that in gold.”

Finally! Now that it was confirmed that merchants could sell cards, everything changed. It was an oversight, no doubt. From what it seemed, this trial wasn’t supposed to have free merchants or albino creatures, and yet here we were. Now, I planned to take full advantage.
