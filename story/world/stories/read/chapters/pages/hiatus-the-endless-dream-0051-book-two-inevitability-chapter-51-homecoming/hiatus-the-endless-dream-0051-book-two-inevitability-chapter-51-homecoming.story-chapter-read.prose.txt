I woke to the sound of beeping.

Rhythmic. Pulsing.

The ceiling tiles took the shape of the grid-like pattern popular in schools and hospitals-

Oh hell no.

I sat up. The Dream sent me back wide awake, and I quickly recognized the machinery and uncomfortable plastic bed covering. The IV tubing pressed uncomfortably into my arm. I could feel the needle underneath my skin as I sat up.

There wasn't a way in hell I would stay in a hospital for a moment longer. I grabbed the IV needle and gave it a gentle pull. Blood started to slowly pool and dribble down the sides of my arm. A box of tissues across the room caught my eye. I stood and used a few wads to clot the blood.

A cauldron of emotions roiled. It was an uncomfortable combination of nostalgia---of falling asleep in the car and waking up in your bed---and also the dread I felt at being in this gods damned building. Relief came quickly as I realized I was still wearing the same clothes. I couldn't have been here for too long if they hadn't changed me into a patient gown. Time worked differently in the Dream. The System had told me that the locations I was moved faster than the outside world, but I never knew when it was telling the truth or not. In this case, it appeared honest.

I stepped out of the room, looking right, then left. Everything seemed normal at first. There were people in the halls. They stood placidly, wordlessly gesturing to each other in pairs or trios. No sounds came from their mouths, and their faces were shrouded in smudged, hazy pixelations.

My heart thudded. If I were still hooked to the machine, it would be beeping like crazy. I practically threw myself back into my room. I had higher Lucidity and Assimilation now. Did the hospital have an Instance layered on top of it? Of course it did. I couldn't stay here. If I fell asleep here, I'd be dragged into another Instance.

I spotted my phone on the side table by the bed. I snatched it and called the only person I could think to call. It only rang once before I heard my dad's voice on the other side, overflowing with confusion. "Dorian?"

"Dad," my voice came out hoarser than I would've liked. "What am I doing at the hospital?"

The sound cut off. My heart sank as I thought the call had dropped, but it seemed like he had only muted himself. A second later, the sound came back, and his voice was shaky when he responded---swirling with emotions I couldn't quite grasp. I only recognized the underlying panic. "Hey, Doe," it was the nickname my family had for me. Rowan had said it softened my edges. "Where are you now? Are you in your... the hospital room?"

"Yeah. Dad, you didn't answer my question." I started to grow worried. Did something happen while I was out? Was I out for longer than I thought?

"I'll explain in a sec. I'm coming up, just stay there. Okay? I'll stay on the phone."

Waves of exhaustion crashed against me. There wasn't the brain fog of sleep. It was a deeper exhaustion. I knew it would never stop. I knew that. I had just thought it would slow down, if only for a moment. All I wanted to do was curl up in my bed for a month, and even then, I wasn't sure it would be enough.

I thought of the pills my brother was taking, the unprescribed ones I had given to the doctors. I suddenly regretted not keeping a few of the bottles.

I was tired.

I leaned my back against the wall, and that lean turned into me sitting. Before I knew it, my knees were drawn up to my chest as everything sank in. Entire other worlds, the fabric of the cosmos stitched together, monsters being real. I had killed someone. Multiple someones. Directly and indirectly.

Nate and Maggie had been Visitors. I had condemned Nate to the fate of a puppet for the Dream. Half the Survivors were dead and would never be coming back, and one of them was at the mercy of a parasite. I had done what I needed to survive. To win.

They were never coming back.

My hands shook, and the shaking reminded me of the vibrating plants. I tore at my arms, crushing myself as tight as I could manage. Who were they all? Why were they in the situations they were in? Did they have loved ones?

I didn't know the answers. I didn't want to know the answers. I intentionally avoided learning anything about them as best I could. I thought it wouldn't hurt if I didn't know, but now I only had questions.

The worst of it all. I wouldn't change a damn thing. It worked didn't it? I had come out the victor both times. So, what use was the loathing? What was the point in regret if I would do it all again?

I felt a hand on my shoulder. I acted on instinct.

Every nerve in my body fired.

Both hands gripped the thing that had touched my shoulder as I slammed it with all my body weight into the wall. A kneeling figure crumpled. I jumped to my feet, preparing to stomp when someone else grabbed me underneath my armpits. I sturggled squirmed.

I launched my head back, and I felt the impact of a nose. The grip loosened, and someone reeled back. I raised my foot again, ready to curb stomp whoever had touched me, but the figure had recovered.

They were on me. They wrapped me tightly. I struggled and bit down hard, trying to tear flesh.

The figure let out a hiss of pain. "It's okay. It's me. It's me. Doe it's me."

I kicked, adrenaline rushing. I couldn't get a good grip on my assailant's neck. I let go and reeled my head back for a head-butt. I stopped just in time.

It was my father. Long hair, light skin. Built.

What?

What was happening?

Why was he here?

I didn't know. I didn't know anything.

"S-sorry." My voice sounded distant.

My mother was by the doorway. Her expression horrified.

Behind me, a nurse held her ... face. Her nose? Why couldn't I see? Her face was smudged. The other nurse was by my mom, the worker was making an expression. I couldn't tell what it was. It was wrong. All of this was wrong.

My dad still had me wrapped in a hug, but he had loosened it greatly. I still couldn't breathe. He held the back of my head with his other hand he rubbed my back.

"You're okay. You're okay." His voice was light, but it cracked. His breath was hitching. It felt like vibrating.

I struggled and squirmed, wanting to be anywhere but here.

He let go, and I stumbled back against the wall, sliding down to the floor.

My dad slowly reached out again, looking at me, but whatever he was looking for, he didn't find. He let his arm drop and gave me a strained smile, "What are you doing up out of bed?"

I glanced over the nurses and then to my mom. She held her arm close to her chest, palm slightly covering her mouth as she bit into the side of her thumb. I had the same habit.

"Why are you all so worried? I don't even know why I'm here, Dad. I just woke up in the hospital". It took me a moment to understand I was speaking. The words just came out. None of them made sense. Of course, they were worried. I had just attacked my dad and the nurse.

The nurse cleared her throat. Her words sounded choppy, "Your parents brought you in for a check up. They couldn't wake you and with your family history we wanted to keep you just in case."

I could feel my face slacken, my head dropped into my hands, and I wanted to scream.

Stupid. Stupid. Of course, this would happen. How could I be so stupid! I was in that Dream for twenty-seven hours non-stop, and five of those hours may have had an entirely different time dilation. I knew that my body would be unresponsive when I was in the Dream. So why. Why hadn't I considered that?

How selfish of a son was I that I would scare them like this after Rowan? I looked up, words catching in my throat. It was all too much. There was too much to do and say, but I couldn't tell them about any of it.

A Dream world? Other worlds entirely? A System that forced me into Instances? I couldn't tell them that. They'd think I was mad. Even if they didn't, they'd be so burdened with the information, and utterly helpless to do anything about it.

My breath was shaky, and I shivered in place. I didn't dare look up. Moisture was pooling at the corners of my vision, but I kept it at bay long enough to get some words out. "Can we go home? Please."

I didn't see the reactions. My face was buried in my knees and elbows. I heard talking, but I refused to process the words, instead focusing on the nothingness of the static. I let it consume my vision. The grayscale color and randomness were just interesting enough that I could focus on them and ignore everything else. I didn't pull up any specific words or stats. I just looked at the static.

I felt arms wrap around me. I stiffened. I could hear sobbing. Could feel warm tears stream onto my scalp. Soft hands brushing my hair. My skin crawled. My breath quickened. It was my mother. I wanted to push her away. To scream at her to give me some damned room to breathe. The desire only grew. My body started shaking uncontrollably. My mind was firing off too fast to be of any use. There were so many things I wanted to do, but I knew they were fucking awful actions. So I retreated even deeper.

I vaguely remember arguing voices in the hallway, mostly between mom and dad. One of the nurses left, and not long after, a doctor joined. Words were directed at me, but I didn't really hear them. Some part of me understood: I lifted my arms when they told me to, I breathed in deeply when a stethoscope was placed against my back, and I heard my voice answer questions in an overly chipper voice.

It wasn't mine. It couldn't have been my voice. It didn't sound right.

I got patches of sentience, brief shocks that woke me from my stupor, if only for a moment.

"–hallucinations–"

"No, ever seems normal," I replied

"–ever– ideation–"

"Never," I replied.

"–danger to–"

"No, I was just startled. Sorry." I replied.

Then the doctor and nurses left, and I was alone again.

By the time my mother entered the room, my fugue state had somewhat subsided, enough for me to start processing things again. I think I gave her a smile when she came in.

Mother walked forward with tentative steps, as if on a tightrope. She clutched her hands close to her sternum. Her posture was slightly hunched, and she seemed so much smaller than she ever was before. I got the wavy portions of my hair and my darker skin from her. I had also inherited her mind, which is how I knew she would never believe me if I said I was fine.

"Dorian," her voice cracked. Neither of us knew what to say, but she found the words first. "The doctors are going to keep you for another day. Just to make sure everything's okay."

No. No, no, no. I couldn't let that happen.

I didn't know if the hospital was safe. I didn't know if the smudged faces were part of an Instance or not, but I knew my parents' house was safe. I knew that for a fact. I couldn't stay here. I wouldn't. I thought back to standing next to a hospital bed, staring at the unresponsive face of Rowan. We had killed him. We had buried my brother alive, because we didn't know about the Dream.

Anger and something else bloomed in my chest, and my hands shot up to my face. "No. I don't want to."

I hid behind my hands as my mother spoke, "Why?" A question, but it didn't sound like one. It was halfway between a beg and a command.

"I just want to go home." I was practically begging.

"It will just be for one night. Just let them check on you." The same tone.

I felt myself curl into myself more, "Please. I don't want to be here."

I could hear her approach, felt her arms wrap around me tightly, as if as soon as if I would turn to sand in her arms. "I'm– I'm so sorry. I'm so so sorry," she shook, vibrated. I stiffened, unmoving. "I can't lose you, too. I can't."

What was I doing? This was pathetic.

I gently hugged her back, gave it around thirty seconds, and untangled myself from her limbs.

I plastered a smile on my face, trying to make it as gentle and lively as possible, "I'm doing fine. Must've been exhausted was all. I'm. I'm sorry I made you worry like that. Nothing's wrong."

She locked eyes with me, wiping tears from her own. A bitter smile was on her face. "Why are you lying to me?"

I froze, as if caught with my hand in the cookie jar, but before I could come up with a more convincing lie, my mom continued.

"I know something is wrong, Doe. I knew something was wrong with Rowan too, but I– I'm not making the same mistake again."

My lips were dry, my throat felt like sandpaper, "Just. I'll do the scans. I just don't want to spend the night."

It was the wrong words, her eyes narrowed, "I don't get it. You both tried to avoid going specific places. He'd always sleep at our house or his and nowhere else no matter what. You both look at things that aren't there and pretend like that's not happening!" Her voice was almost a yell by the end, raw and scratchy.

There was no way out I could see. There had to be a path I could take, where was it? What lie would get me out of this?

I didn't have time to think, "Why?" Mom asked, "Why can't either of you tell me anything?"

Too much. It was all too much. There was no way out. The pressure built and built, and I- I broke.

"Because it doesn't make any sense. Because you'll think I'm crazy and lock me up. Because nothing about it makes any sense and I can't explain any of it!"

It was loud. I don't know when I started breathing so rapidly or when the tears came, just that my body was reacting and my mind couldn't keep up.

"Because it sounds like I made it up and that it's all in my head, but I know its not and the things I learned are eating me from the inside and it's hopeless and there's nothing I can do about it and there's nothing you or dad or doctors or even Rowan could do and I don't know what to do!"

I felt arms around me again. I pushed weakly against her, but I had no strength. I sobbed, burying my face into my mom's shoulder as all the hopelessness of the past few days refused to go unaddressed any longer. I tried to push it back down, but the dam had broken. I gripped onto her shirt and was afraid.

Afraid of what would come next.

Afraid of what she'd say.

I was just scared.

She was crying too.

At some point, we stopped.

She took a deep breath.

"Just the scans. Then we go home." She held the back of my head, kissed its crown, and said it again. "Just the scans, then Dad and I will bring you home."

I didn't say anything, just nodded, and gripped her shoulder tighter.
