I sat bolt upright the moment the dream about Oliver ended. “Like hell you didn’t have any regrets, Teenage Rachel! You didn’t even say goodbye to him—only good night! You got the bad ending, and you didn’t realize it until—”

Wait. Stop.

Where the heck am I?

The wooden ceiling rafters were low and unfamiliar. I was in a cot. Through a small window, I saw gray winter light stream in sideways from… the ocean?

There’s no ocean near Chairo.

Oh no.

Chairo!

I reached up and touched my head. Everything was intact, but my helmet was gone.

At the other end of the room, in a chair never meant to accommodate his size, sat Aleph. His head and horns were low, with his hands hanging between his knees.

“You’re fully awake,” he said quietly.

“So it seems,” I replied, rubbing my eyes.

I could smell the salt in the air, and once I was quiet, I could even hear the distant sound of waves crashing. “Where are we?”

“Nearhaven,” he confirmed, straightening slightly. “You’ve been asleep for a little over a day now.” He paused. “The others are considering their next move. If you will excuse me, I would let them know you’re—”

“Aleph.”

He stopped a few feet from the door.

“I think I have a lot of things to do today,” I murmured, rubbing at my face. “But my first priority involves you."

He sat back down primly. “I will not resist.”

I opened my mouth, then thought better of it. “Okay,” I said instead. “So, I get to apologize without interruption then, huh? For things like… oh, not explaining what I was doing back then, for not saying goodbye before I disappeared. And for not thinking about what my absence would do to the people I left behind.”

I pulled my knees up to my chest, still sitting in the cot. “I told myself, I think, that I was protecting everyone. But I didn’t consider that disappearing had its own consequences. That it would make you worry—” I stopped and looked down, knowing I was about to loop. “I’m so, so sorry.”

He was silent and still for several moments. When he started to speak again, his voice was a rumbling whisper. “I also seek forgiveness.” He looked at his hands. “Not for the same reasons you listed. I am sorry that you felt that leaving was the solution. That you were somehow a problem, and that your absence would better serve the people around you.” He shook his head slowly, his horns catching the sunlight. “That was not something I ever wanted for you.”

I cleared my throat and slid my legs over the side of the cot. “Well, if anyone were to ask, I would say that we were both, most likely, coming from a place of stubborn conviction,” I noted with a wry sigh.

He flinched somewhat, but I was relieved to note the smallest smile briefly pass across his face. “That is overly gracious of you.”

“I’m allowed to be once in a while.” I paused. “Aleph?”

“Yes?”

“Can I… have a hug? Please?”

He stared as if hesitating, but then rose to full height, crossed the room in just under two full steps, and put his giant, comforting arms around me in the greatest bear hug an ox could give. It wasn’t the end or the fix for anything, but it was absolutely the number one thing I needed right then and there.

With a quiver, I eventually asked, “Do we know how many… how many were…”

“No,” he replied, hugging tighter. “We’ve heard nothing from Chairo nor the Bastion.”

“Oh…”

And then, patient as always, he let me cry it all out without any interruption.

***

Nearhaven was not what anyone would call inviting, at least in the wintertime. The coastline was mostly just straight cliffs, its gray rocks worn smooth by the weather. There were a few small coves, though I didn’t feel the need to venture into any of them. Sand was also present, and the dark and coarse granules found their way into everything with the help of frequent, stiff breezes.

The settlement that had grown up in the sheltered center of the island was a mix of stone huts, many with different styles of wooden and thatched roofs, as if repairs were continual and shaped by whatever architect took charge of the project. The result was a bit chaotic, especially when only patches had been applied, but all in all, it worked well enough to keep the inhabitants dry, which was priority one.

Aleph was quiet as we walked, just as much a stranger to the area as I was. No one was outside yet, though I believed that was purely intentional. Paradise hung visible in the northwest sky, and I found myself watching it instead of my steps. I caught its occasional flicker, trying to parse what exactly was causing it.

Sparkles? Or just sunlight?

Nora and Aziza had set up in a side hut-turned-tearoom along opposite edges of a large, rectangular table. Both raised their voices in tandem with their teacups. There were hand gestures, interruptions upon interruptions, and a tendency to slam a free hand down on the tabletop to emphasize a point.

“What are you two fighting about—”

“Rae!” Nora crowed upon setting eyes on me, then cleared her throat. “Oh, but if you’re going to start shouting, you’ll need to wait your turn.”

“There are turns?”

Aziza glared at me, but without the customary evil aura backing it. “We reserved this time to argue, and I won’t accommodate a plus one. However…” She took a loud slurp of tea. “If you wish to linger on the side for a status update, well, I suppose that’s different.”

“You’re invited too, Aleph,” Nora added, gesturing with her teacup.

“I wouldn’t dare,” he murmured, backing away slowly. “I should find a task suitable for me—somewhere else.”

At least one of us got away.

“Tell that one she shouldn’t summon her aura recklessly,” Aziza said to Nora. “We don’t want to call any unnecessary attention to us right now.”

Nora set down her teacup and folded her arms. “Why don’t you tell her yourself?”

“I don’t want to, frankly.”

“Why? Because you don’t want to admit she rescued you?”

“She did so only out of necessity.”

I scowled. “Actually, Olethros convinced me it was for the best.”

“He is maximizing resources for his own agenda.” Aziza sniffed.

“Whatever you say, Mistress Aziza,” I muttered, glancing at Nora. “And what’s this about unnecessary attention?”

Nora nodded. “Paradise seems a bit distracted right now, we think, but we don’t know how long that will last. It’s not unreasonable to think we survived, since it seems he has at least some control over Naught, but let’s keep everything as manual as we can until we figure out our next move.”

“So… no dark mage spells, either?” I asked.

Nora nodded. “The whole village insists on it. They weren’t exactly happy to see us, but after telling them the circumstances under which we arrived, well, you get the picture.”

Aziza gripped her teacup in both hands. “Galenus is no fool. I’m certain it was him who sent us here, and I am even more confident that he is on his way as well—somehow.”

“Somehow?” I asked dubiously.

She gritted her teeth. “I do not have specifics, only hypotheses.”

I noticed a biscuit on the table and reached for it. “I hope Amos and Tarek are alright as well—”

“At least wash your hands!” Aziza screeched, pointing to a nearby dry sink. “You filthy mortal. And don’t mention that traitor’s name in front of me again.”

While angrily washing my admittedly dirt-covered hands—Do germs bother demons or something?—I inhaled through my nose. “Who? Amos?”

“Yes, that one! Abandoning me for my brother, of all things.”

Well, at least she was admitting their default relationship.

“I’m sure he has both his reasons and regrets,” I replied, snatching the biscuit from the table before either could protest. “Olethros can be quite a handful.”

Aziza pursed her lips. “So, you’re saying there may be an opportunity to win him back.”

“Not really. But back to Olethros—”

“On the beach, up to something for sure,” Nora answered. “But I think you should talk with Inutilis and company first. Biggest building, center of the village. Can’t miss it.” She then practically leered at Aziza. “Now where were we? Oh yes, I think I was telling you about how I optimized the Bastion’s animus efficiency by over seven percent.”

“Seven point zero-zero-one percent doesn’t deserve such rounding. And even if your account is half-accurate, it is still an estimation only!”

“You guys are arguing over—oh never mind, carry on, you two.” I sighed before stuffing the biscuit in my mouth, afraid I’d make their ridiculous bonding session somehow more absurd.

***

The main building was exactly where Nora said it would be. It was a bit small to be called a meeting hall, but more than a simple hut. Tetora, his sister Taika, and General Ragnerus all turned around as Councilman Inutilis, plain as ever, looked up from his desk.

“Captain Lightbringer,” Inutilis said with a wary smile. “I’m glad to see you upright.”

I nodded. “I find it’s important to be so for at least a few minutes each day.” With an awkward exhale, I folded my arms behind my back. “Know that it wasn’t the plan to show up without quorum, but there were extenuating circumstances—”

He waved dismissively. “We’re aware. If anything, Captain, I feel like you’re a little late. Did you happen to catch the stars last night?”

“No…” I replied, shaking my head.

He took a deep breath. “They moved.”

I flinched. “Moved? How?!”

He spread his hands. “They moved. Not all at once, mind you. Some went left, others went right.”

That's not how they work!

“Was there some sort of message in them?” I demanded.

Ragnerus grunted. “No. It was unsettling.”

“—For the weak,” Taika added hastily. “I know they’re just stars. But the other villagers were just as the General said. Unsettled.”

“He’s baiting us,” Tetora growled. “Trying to figure out where we are.”

“Maybe… Except Taika just proved why it wouldn’t help.”

Tetora’s right ear flopped. “She did?”

I nodded. “If it was unsettling here, it was unsettling everywhere. The idea that he moved them without any specific message, well, that’s the message, isn’t it?”

Ragnerus folded his arms. “You are saying he is doing this to elicit animus.”

“Exactly. He’s making a show of power—that’s all this is. Either he’s got full control, and he’s just flexing on the idea that gods don’t need to explain themselves, or he doesn’t, and he’s trying to scare us into giving ourselves away. Either way, we’re not taking the bait.”

“Perhaps. Now, Captain,” Ragnerus reached into his trench cloak. “I have fixed your helmet.”

I reached for it. “Oh, thank you—”

He did not let go at first.

“Did I do a good job on the repairs?” he asked, his brow furrowing.

I glanced at it a little more carefully. “I can’t see anything out of place. Oh, but there are a trio of gemstones now, I see.”

“I did not use animus, you understand.”

“Now I do, yes.”

A self-satisfied grin stretched his jaw. “Perhaps I will learn to repair other equipment through heated forge work and encrusting. I am pleased to be such a skilled learner of both practices.”

“Why didn’t you just—oh, no magic, right?”

He nodded. “I must use some to keep my form. But I am optimizing my disguise through manual labor! It is a fascinating thing to experience.” Then he cleared his throat. “As a novelty only.”

I nodded as he handed over the helmet, certain the feeling would eventually wear off once he realized how tedious it could be.

“Wear it proudly!” he commanded. “A vessel containing all the marks of the demon lords and their sovereign! May its collectively created countenance blind thy foes.”

“Does that mean Aziza and Olethros helped?”

The demon general stiffened. “Minimally.”

“And Aziza… she’s, I mean, with you, and your brother—”

“She has joined our compact, yes.” Then he fixed his collar, muttering, “She will remain loyal to our cause throughout, even if she acts as if she is much beset by it.”

“So, he’s finally a true demon king…” I whispered to myself.

That’s what he’s wanted, right?

Acceptance.

He got it. He actually got it. And I—

I need to—

“Good for him!” I shouted suddenly, the words tumbling out as I tried to accept them at face value. “Good for him indeed! Long live Demon King Olethros!”

The door opened behind me with a squeak. I turned to see Vernie giving me a strange look. “I think affirmations like that are usually announced in the target’s presence, yeah?”

“I just...”

“Don’t worry about it,” she said with a summoning wave. “Come on, why don’t you go tell him that yourself? He’s up on the bluff, and he wants to talk to you.”
