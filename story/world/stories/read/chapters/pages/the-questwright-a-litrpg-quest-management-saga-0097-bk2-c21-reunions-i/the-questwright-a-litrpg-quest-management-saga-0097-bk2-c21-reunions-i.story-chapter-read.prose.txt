Cass did not black out.

It was less that Adya couldn’t hit hard, and more that she had held back on her punch. Even as he fell upon the dirty flagstones, a quote from his mother’s lessons came back to him.

“When you punch something, punch through it; otherwise, you won’t have any power.”

Painfully, his body hit the ground, Cass’s reflexes saving him at the last moment from going in headfirst. Why they hadn’t saved him from the punch in the first place, he had no idea. Genuine surprise, maybe. Even as that thought entered his mind, he began to consider what might occur next as-

Why am I thinking about that while I’m lying here and dust is filling my nose?

Somebody strong picked him up off the road and dragged him over to the side and away from the dangerous thoroughfare. It goes without saying that it was Gary, as Cass thanked the man while rubbing his chin.

“That hurt.”

Adya moved in front of him right away, “How could you let yourself get kidnapped!”

A part of Cass was still completely overjoyed to see her and drank in every detail of her face, including the angry ones.

“It’s not like I wanted it to happen!”

“How would I know that! You made the decision completely on your own to have every monster in Liora chase you across the city! How would I know that you didn’t let yourself get kidnapped, too!”

For the first time since Cass and Adya had met, they settled into a heated argument. With the care they had for one another, neither of them allowed themselves to push things too harshly. Cass would point out logical fallacies in her argument, then Adya would return with a new angle of attack, even as the other four members of the group looked on with amusement.

Just as a red-faced Adya looked like she was going to explode, Orla loudly proclaimed, “Just kiss again and get it over with!”

Whuff.

Cass scrubbed his hair in frustration, “Stay out of it, Bella.”

Gary slapped his back with a meaty hand as he laughed, “You love each other, just accept it. There’s nothing to be embarrassed about.”

“It’s true, like my love of Dallan Vale. He’s just so-”

“Shut up, Orla!” Cass yelled at her as his cheeks flushed for multiple reasons. His eyes trailed to Adya, who wouldn’t meet his eyes as her cheeks also held a cheery tinge to them.

And then, a special moment came.

Looking Cass in the eyes, the large draft-horse neighed, stomped, flicked her tail, and performed all other manners of communication possible for an animal without the control of its vocal cords. Bella told a long story of the kinds of things she and Adya had gone through, including the emotions she’d sensed most commonly from the Lioran Scout. All told, it took several minutes, and even passerbys were commenting on how expressive the horse had acted.

Cass took a beat to calm down, “Really, girl?’

“She’s right, you know,” Gary added without having any idea of what Bella had said.

Orla looked from Bella to Cass, then Gary, “What the fuck is happening!”

“Cass is getting put in his place,” Gary said with a smirk. He looked at Pellin, “Right?”

The System Engineer shrugged.

“Either way, this isn’t the place to have any kind of conversation,” Adya said while studiously not looking around. “We’re being watched. Cass, do you have somewhere we can go?”

He scratched his chin, “It’s no surprise that people are listening in. I can tell you for sure that the Guildtower is out.”

“Guild…tower?” Pellin asked.

“But I met a strange old man just before you guys got here. He said to come over to Caligula’s End. It’s a bar only about ten minutes from here.”

“Good a place as any,” Gary said before taking the reins of a large gray horse. “Let’s head that way while you tell us a little about what’s been going on since you disappeared.”

“Kidnapped,” Cass corrected, “And just a general overview, since there are ears everywhere in Liberty. I guarantee it’s not as exciting as how you all got here.”

Bella pestered him to ride her, but she wasn’t as sneaky as she thought herself to be. Her emotional broadcasting told him she was up to something. Cass intimated that the moment he was in the saddle, she’d begin asking him to activate the Stalwart Quest that would make him sore for days. Instead, he chose to walk, lightly holding her reins as they moved away from the gate. She wasn’t happy, but he knew she was content, and that would have to be enough for now.

While they moved, Cass gave them a brief rundown. He didn’t talk about his new Quest-types or anything to do with the Quill, but he did speak about Ana, Maggie, and her two guards, as well as some general thoughts he had on the city. While everyone else was, as expected, disgusted by how he framed Liberty, one of them was not.

“Finally! A city after my own heart!” Orla beamed as she looked around. Just as she took a deep breath before speaking again, they unfortunately passed a refuse dump. The retching crawling up her throat oddly reminded Cass of what Scroungers sounded like as they died.

They got to the bar faster than expected. A stray thought entered Cass’s mind that the reason they were moving so quickly was a combination of the crowd moving that way. From what he could recall, the lottery was meant to start just when the sun began to fall into the evening.

Crossing from one side to another, the party looked the place over with a distinct lack of enthusiasm.

“It’s, uhh, impressive.” Pellin put in, his eye tracking the lack of people within.

Gary put a hand on his shoulder, the two seeming to have grown quite close over their travels together, “I’m sure it’s better looking on the inside, right, Cass?”

“Nope, not really.”

Caligula’s End was just as Cass had seen on his map. It was a bar with an overhang shaped like an X, held up by thick metal poles around the area. Across the covered spots were tall, sturdy tables with matching stools set against them, bits of dust and dirt spread around the area. In the center, where the two overhangs met, a man stood behind a thick and long wooden bar, studiously cleaning a glass with a spotless rag. He looked up as they approached.

“No horses in the End.”

Bella took that as an affront, her long neigh saying all kinds of filthy atrocities.

Cass put a hand on her side, “Jeez, Bella, calm down. I wouldn’t even talk that way about the Dark Guilds.”

“She’s been like that since you were kidnapped,” Gary commented.

“And lost an eye,” Orla added in.

Pellin, Cass, Bella, and Adya all paused to glare at her, seven eyes matched in intensity. She looked at the group before shrugging, “Sorry.”

“Can we get our drinks to go?” Adya asked, if only to move past the awkwardness.

The man tilted his head, then shrugged, “Long as you’re payin and return the glassware, that’s fine. What do you want?”

All of them ordered the same thing based on a short description, along with a few large bowls of water for their animals. The man served it up before telling them they could hitch the horses to the thick wall beside the place. Gary, the bravest of them all, took the sip first.

“That’s…That’s not bad?”

Orla held it up to the sun, “Why’s it red?”

“The menu called it a smoothie,” Pellin stated. He took a sniff of it, “Smells fruity.”

“I doubt the place would stay open if the drinks they served could hurt you.” Cass took his own sip, “Oh, I quite like that. Refreshing. Though I don’t know how I feel about the chunky bits.”

That got everyone else to take a drink, and before long, they were talking and laughing as they used to during breakfast. Pellin gave his oddball comments. Orla yelled. Cass steered the direction of the conversation, and Adya silently watched. The only real change was Gary being added in, and that was all to the better in the QuestWright’s mind. He was the missing piece of their puzzle, as the happy man turned every comment into something silly or heartfelt.

But there was an edge to their conversation that had been missing before. A slightly more manic note to Orla’s exclamations, and a little more of a subdued response from Pellin. As ever, the observant Adya noted it all, Profile ticking in Cass’s vision.

And through everything, Cass felt the warmth of Adya’s gaze burning in his chest. It was something he had dearly missed, doubled whenever Bella looked his way, if for different reasons. What had once felt like a burden had now become something he would never take for granted again.

They were still talking about nothing when the last of the glasses was drained empty. Gary helpfully stacked them together, then Cass volunteered to buy the next round as Orla had covered the first.

He made his way over to the barman, “Five more to go, please.”

“You gotcha, buddy.”

While he worked, a series of odd sounds coming out of eyesight behind the bar, Cass leaned against a chair. That’s when he noticed that the one next to it was occupied.

It hadn’t been only a moment ago.

“So, QuestWright, you found your friends.” The old man from before said, a single wrinkled finger stirring an amber liquid within a thick glass before him.

“How do you keep doing that?”

“Getting a drink isn’t hard.”

“No. How do you keep appearing like that?’

“Why ask how? I could explain it, but you’re not ready for that. No Tier 2 is.” The man took a sip, then smacked his lips, “Good batch there.”

“So,” Cass said, sitting down across from him. “You said I should drop by-”

“One day,” The old man finished. He put his drink down, then gave Cass the same intense look he’d had on the road to the gate. “I didn’t expect that one to be today.”

“My friends and I-”

“Gary the Baker, Orla the Merchant, Pellin the Innovating System Engineer, and Adya, the Close-Quarters Scout. Eclectic group you have there. I’m particularly interested in your Bonded, Bella. Out of your whole group, she’s probably the most unique member. Not like the other, stupid horses around here. Bet there’s a story there.”

Cass threw his hands up. “Your hatred for normal horses aside, how do you know all of this?”

The man tapped near his eyes, “I see things. And what I see is a group of newly arrived, directionless people, a smart horse, and a one-eyed kid trapped in a situation with no real way out. Am I wrong in that?”

“Why point out that I’m missing an eye?”

“Beauty is a burden, and one I wouldn’t say you’re afflicted by. At least, not anymore. But you haven’t answered my question.’

“I…” Cass considered the old man and whether to trust him with anything.

This old guy keeps popping up out of nowhere, and knows things he shouldn’t. What that might be, I don’t know, which means I need to push.

“Earlier, you said we’re both survivors. What do you mean by that?”

The old man took a full draw on his drink, emptying it quickly. Then, he snapped his fingers in the air, and Cass got a show as the glass in front of him disappeared, only to be replaced a moment later with a full one.

Without taking his eyes from the man across him, he said, “Will, I’m gonna need some privacy.”

“You got it, Boss.” The barman said, then reached out a hand toward them. Cass’s ears popped as he felt something lightly settle across his body. The warmth in his chest began to ice over as Adya seemed to notice something was different.

“There we go. Quick sidebar question for you, kid. I know I confronted you in the street first, but I have to know. Do you just gravitate to the oldest, wisest-looking man you can find? Or do you have some kind of sense to you?”

Cass thought it over as the man’s clear, deep-blue eyes looked at him, then shrugged, “You said I should come, so why not now when I’m out and about.”

He blinked, once, then nodded, “So be it. Before you get worried, right now, nobody can hear us. You can never pay a good barman enough who has that kind of skill.” He took a long drink, half-draining the cup before setting it down. “As for your earlier question, young man, I’m Alan Callahan…”

There was a long pause as he waited for Cass to react in some way. When he instead smiled and nodded, saying, “Nice to meet you again, Alan.” The old man grew incensed.

“See! Nobody knows my name! It’s a damn shame, I tell you. Save the world, and people forget you, just like that.” He snapped his fingers, and his glass disappeared, replaced by a full one. “I wasn’t ready yet, Will. That was a snap for emphasis. Put it back.”

“You saved the world, Alan?”

“Yep. The world’s a bigger place than little Liora, now isn’t it, young QuestWright.” He glared at his glass until it was replaced with the old one, then shook his head after taking a drink. “Cooldowns.”

“Not that I doubt you, Alan, but how did you save the world?”

The man smiled at him, yellow teeth set in a grin that was one part happy, one part sad, then shrugged. He drained his cup and didn’t have to snap his fingers for it to be replaced this time. “You don’t need to hear it.”

“I don’t think it would hurt to have you say it,” Cass said, unintentionally inching closer as he watched Adya approach from the corner of his eye. Her face had a dark cast to it that made him nervous.

“Let’s wait on your little friend to join us first.”

Looking at Cass, the man’s eyes stayed perfectly still. The moment Adya got within five feet, his hand snaked out so fast it became invisible, then she was in the chair next to him, arms flung out.

“What was that!”

“Scouts these days are worthless!” Alan yelled. “You’re a shame on our Calling.”

Checking to make sure Adya was physically okay, Cass looked over at him, “Alan, I need to know. What level are you?”

The man smiled at them both, his lips cracking as they stretched, “I’m stuck on my 8th gate, little survivor.”
