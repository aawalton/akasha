Ellen stared at the Shadow Cell as its bars faded.

She’d tested out her spells against Kade, of course, but something had told her it’d be a bad idea to unleash her new Unique skill on him. Every one of her spells had been…manageable…for him. Bruises, cuts, the works, sure—but he could heal through that.

What the Shadow Cell had done to Magda, though…she was glad she hadn’t tried it. Kade had been…well, he’d been stronger as an S-Ranker than the last time she’d seen him fight at A-Rank, but he always felt strong. And, intellectually, she’d known that the gulf between A and S-Ranks was massive, but until she felt it herself, she hadn’t known.

Stamina: 581/600, Mana: 1491/1530

Even as she watched, her Mana ticked up rapidly, Infinite Well filling her core almost as quickly as she could spend her energy on spells. It was almost intoxicating. In a way, she could see why the S-Rankers in Phoenix acted the way they did. Why they seemed almost universally drawn to power and authority. She wanted nothing more than to join Kade in his fight against Kalorwan and continue stretching her new skills and spells to their real limits—whatever those were. Nothing more than to use her skills to achieve things, to grow in power in whatever way she could.

But Kade was right.

They’d planned for this.

So, as the Shadow Cell disippated, she sprinted to the control panel outside the sparring room and opened the intercom. “Jessie, this is Ellen. Kade tried to convince you to name his snake Gouda.”

That was the code. Something that only the three of them would have known. Something incredibly stupid. But it worked. The door unsealed, and Jeff opened it. He had his sword, shield, and armor ready, and behind him, a handful of low-rankers bristled, their own spears, swords, and spells prepped for battle.

Ellen rolled her eyes. “Let’s get moving. We’re heading to the garage. Stay behind me; the portal broke over there, but we need the trucks.”

Jeff nodded and fell in behind Ellen. He looked a little sad about it, but she didn’t have time to console him. The sheer tyranny of rank was at play.

She did look over her shoulder, though, and saw Yasmin carrying Jessie. She swallowed painfully; hopefully, Kade wouldn’t see. If he did, he’d go berserk trying to protect his sister.

Lightning arced across my armor.

It reached out as I pushed my aura out—not the Stormkin Avatar, but the one that powered up my attacks. I rarely used it anymore. Dad was much stronger. But sometimes…sometimes, I just needed to finish a fight myself.

This thing had entered my world. It had threatened my people. My family. I needed to kill Kalorwan myself. And then, when I’d finished, I needed to invade that portal in the garage, kill every monster inside, and not stop until I…

Why?

My lungs seemed to run empty for a moment, and with the sensation of emptiness came a secondary one of…peace? Not calmness, but peace. Why did I need to kill and fight? Wouldn’t it be easier to just—

Stormfall.

The Storm’s Blade whipped forward, impossibly fast. The air collapsed behind it, cracking like a jet fighter passing overhead. It stabbed into Kalorwan’s armor. The shockwave exploded outward. The portal metal held. The scallops and barnacles didn’t; they flew everywhere in a cloud of chum that hung, stinking, in the air. So did some of the monster’s chest. Gore poured from the wound.

I kept pressing. As long as Stormfall was running, I was the aggressor.

Kalorwan didn’t give up, though. His sword flailed out, cutting a groove into my own armor and opening up my off-hand’s bicep all the way to the bone. I grimaced, gritted my teeth, and pushed through it as Stamina flooded the injury. Lightning Chains. I tried to cast it, but my arm reacted slowly, and the spell finished too late.

Torrentborne stance. I shifted my weight, putting my body behind the Storm’s Blade and focusing on parrying—and on my arm.

He’d done something. The monster had…

It was numb. Unresponsive. No pain—not even when I stopped pushing Stamina toward it. A poison?

I hadn’t fought against poisoners much. There weren’t many opportunities; most portal monsters wanted to kill and fight, driven by the voice screaming at them to kill and keep killing until there was nothing left. Some of the higher-ranking ones had more free will, but even ones like Yalerox’s servant, Tathrix, were more brutal. More aggressive.

Quellat hadn’t been a poisoner.

But this monster was. The massive sword was a front; he wasn’t trying to beat me in straight combat. But at the same time, I couldn’t let myself get hit too much.

I pushed Stamina back toward the wound in an attempt to slow the poison’s spread. Then I focused on Kalorwan. The world shrank. It was only the monster and his sword against me and mine.

The atrium exploded a moment later.

Thunderlord stance. Laminar Blade and Stormfall. My sword blurred, punching toward Kalorwan’s chest over and over. His spun in an arc. It deflected thrusts almost like a fan blocking a stick. Some got through, and where they did, fresh wounds opened across the monster’s body. Watery gore poured from them, then cauterized shut and started healing. The S-Rank Paragon’s Health was all but overwhelming; I could cause as much damage as I wanted, and it hardly seemed to matter.

Tables and chairs disintegrated all around us as our auras pushed out against each other and our weapons flashed. The sheer speed would have been impossible to follow for anyone below B-Rank, and impossible to react to below A. I shifted to Torrentborne just long enough to activate Stormrunner and threw myself into the air, then went back to Thunderlord. A massive tentacle appeared from nowhere, slamming into the floor. It gave way, and I soared through the gap, turning and cutting Kalorwan’s leg.

He howled, as much in rage as in pain, and leaped after me.

My lightning caught me and jerked me in place. I reversed. The Storm’s Blade punched through Kalorwan’s chest plate, just below his neck. Watery gore sprayed out of the exit wound.

His eyes narrowed. A tentacle wrapped around the electric blade, pushed against it, and bled more water as he forced himself off my sword. I pulled it free. Small tendrils splattered against my face like severed fingers.

Kalorwan’s sword came up. Mine did, too. Lightning and water clashed. A peal of thunder blew out every window in the building. Our battle was destroying the Desert Wind Guild’s headquarters. But I didn’t care. The only thing that mattered was that this monster, this Paragon, had attacked Jessie.

And I had to make him pay. To exact retribution.

My lightning wings arced me away from the monster. I shifted stances and forced my numb, unresponsive arm into Cyclone Forms. Rolling Thunder. The whole Storm Sigil combo—Ariette’s Thunderhead, Brendan’s Storm Sigil, and Hand of the Thunder God.

I aimed. Then I fired.

The beam of lightning hit Kalorwan. It washed over him and sliced into the Desert Wind Guild’s facade. Stucco shattered. So did the concrete and rebar behind it. Flames erupted and were just as quickly put out by the sheer force of the fully powered Hand of the Thunder God. The building creaked ominously, and I cut the spell off before it could obliterate the whole structure.

And as it faded, Kalorwan’s Health finally started to fall behind. I’d done too much damage, too quickly. Tentacles writhed and burned, their green-brown surfaces nothing but scorched, red flesh. Steam rose from his entire body, and his armor glowed orange where the spell had hit him the hardest.

But he wasn’t dead. I hadn’t killed him, and I hadn’t collected his core.

One breath. Just long enough to glance at the walkway between our home and the garage. I caught Yasmin’s dark hair and Jessie’s body in her arms.

Two emotions. The first, fear. Why was she being carried? Was she hurt? Had I failed to protect her? And with that fear came…not fury. No. This was colder. Wrath.

Kalorwan and Magda had tried to hurt Jessie.

I used Stormbreak.

There was no need for exclusion. Everyone I cared about was far enough away to avoid hitting them, and it was just Kalorwan and me. Flowers blossomed around us both, their petals alternating between bright yellow, pulsing blue-white, and an emerald black that seared my eyes. My Mana plunged downward. A negative zone formed around me, and a positive zone around Kalorwan.

Then they equalized, and the flowers of lightning pulsed with sheer power.

I held nothing back; even as the lightning storm roared around me and thunder blew out not only the guild building’s last shards of glass but every window in northwest Surprise, I shifted stances again. The Stormbreak raged on and on, electricity rippling through my body—and through Kalorwan’s.

By the time it was over, he wasn’t in any shape to stop me as I put the Storm’s Blade through his eye. Lightning crackled, and after a moment, the S-Ranked Paragon of the Stilled Breath was dead. I collected his core—an S-Ranked water core that pulsed with a sickly yellow-green color—and broke into a sprint.

Jessie was hurting, and I needed to check in on her.

“Kade!” Jessie yelled. She swung her fist and hit Yasmin across the face. It wasn’t intentional; she didn’t hate the woman or anything. She just refused to be carried around like a sack of potatoes when her brother was destroying everything they’d worked so hard for. She had to stop that maniac before—

Another burst of lightning bathed the parking garage in brilliant white light. She closed her eyes against it, screaming for a split second before biting it off. “Kade, you idiot! Quit breaking our base!”

“He can’t hear you!” Yasmin yelled back, just inches from her face. The support’s eyes were wide, and she looked over her shoulder at the raging clouds and crashing lightning all around Kade. Jessie looked, too. It was mesmerizing, in a way, watching two S-Rankers go at it all-out, without any thought for what that meant for their surroundings. And she knew Kade couldn’t hear her. He got this way sometimes, after all. In his head, and only concerned with the fight.

“Bad news,” Jeff said. “Ellen says the cars are all fried. That portal opened up and dumped what looks like the Central Arizona Project’s entire daily flow of water into the garage.”

“Explains the smell,” Yasmin said. She put Jessie down—finally—and jogged over to look herself. “Yeah, that’s gonna cost a bit to fix.”

Jessie hobbled up behind her and stared at the destruction.

It wasn’t anywhere near as bad as what Kade was currently doing to their headquarters. But it was bad. Cars and trucks had been shoved up against the parking garage’s walls, and standing water had flooded it to a full foot deep. It stank of salt and dead fish, and of something Jessie couldn’t place. None of the vehicles would be running any time soon, and some would need time at a shop before they started at all.

But her eyes were locked on the gold portal. It shimmered, a dark, midnight-blue tone at its center. A Paragon portal of at least S-Rank. Her Governing Council Representative training took over, and she cleared her throat. “Everyone, get back. We’re going to wait for a team that can clear this to show up. I’ll contact the GC and let them know we need—“

“No. You’re going to stay right there.”

A pair of arms wrapped around Jessie. She stiffened and half-turned as Kade picked her up in a massive hug. “I’m glad you’re safe, Jessie. I’m so glad.”

Jeff glanced at the portal and raised an eyebrow. “Safe, huh?”

“Well, relatively speaking, I mean,” Kade said.

Ellen cleared her throat. “Of course she’s safe. She was with me. If she’d been anywhere near you, though, she’d—“

“Yeah. That’s why you were escorting the evacuation, though, right?”

“Right,” Ellen said. “Anyway, we’ve got an S-Rank portal right in the middle of Phoenix. What are we supposed to do about this?”

Kade stared at it for a long time as Jessie watched him. Then, finally, he cleared his throat. “Ellen and I will deal with it. You all need to get away from here. Go to the GC tower or to the Portal Tyrants’ keep. Anywhere but here. It’s too dangerous here.”

“But Kade, I want to—“ Jessie started.

Kade cut her off, voice sharp and hard. “Go. Now.”

Yasmin dragged her away by one hand, and Jessie—wincing as the motion jerked on her elbow and shoulder—followed, leaving Kade behind to face whatever the S-Rank portal spat out without any back-up except for Ellen.

Magda was dying.

She’d already be dead, were it not for the God of Thunder’s cruel touch. His lightning had coursed across her body—her beautiful, sinuous body—and charred the cubes of missing flesh, stopping the bleeding. Her single remaining eye stared at her ruined length. Purple and green skin was marred not only by her master’s and enemies’ hands, but by her lover’s as well.

“Now, now, Magda,” the God of Thunder’s voice broke through her horror. She squeezed her eyes closed, trying to block out what had happened to her. If she couldn’t see, it wasn’t real. But no—the pain, the overwhelming agony of the last day, was still there, no matter how much she tried to ignore it, like a monstrous hammer that wouldn’t stop slamming into her tail where it had broken and re-healed incorrectly as the Placid King ripped her from her portal world or the burn marks spiderwebbing across her body or the raised lumps of raw red flesh that wouldn’t stop—

“Magda, focus. Breathe.”

The Paragon of the Sliding Surge’s eye opened. She forced air through her gills. It burned. But it helped.

The God of Thunder loomed over her. Lightning coursed around him, and rain poured down in sheets, splashing against her myriad wounds. It didn’t heal her, but even the barest hint of moisture was a relief, soothing her too-dry, cracking skin. “My master…he…he will be furious with you. I was supposed…to…”

“Die? Quite.” The God of Thunder sounded almost amused. His eyebrow raised, clouds shifting as the motion all but reshaped his very world. “You were. However, the Placid King has miscalculated. Your assault was meant to be the bait for Kade to enter his world. My former pupil is far too smart for that. He’ll see through it. Your master is about to have a very…interesting…time.”

“But…the Placid King is too strong for the Stormblade to kill.”

Silence. The God of Thunder paused overhead. Magda wilted. The motion sent a wave of agony through her body, even as the scraps of her Health tried desperately to put together the detached ribs and second jawbone that the Shadowstorm Witch had destroyed. “It won’t be enough. I can’t…”

“Nonsense, Magda. Pull yourself together. Your body has never interested me. I only care about your core, and the core-bonding will remain the same no matter how badly my disciple and his friend maul you. Focus on healing and let Kade take care of the Placid King. I’ll let you know if anything interesting happens so we can watch together.”

“Thank you, my lord,” Magda said automatically. Her eye closed, and she pretended to sleep. But inside, she shivered. The God of Thunder had slipped; her core was all that mattered to him. If she didn’t recover, would he harvest it for himself? He’d saved her life, though—pulled her from the trap the Shadowstorm Witch had sprung around her on Earth and spirited her away from even the reach of the Placid King. She was…safe here. Wasn’t she?

She didn’t know, and she was powerless to find out—or to stop him, if that turned out to be the case.
