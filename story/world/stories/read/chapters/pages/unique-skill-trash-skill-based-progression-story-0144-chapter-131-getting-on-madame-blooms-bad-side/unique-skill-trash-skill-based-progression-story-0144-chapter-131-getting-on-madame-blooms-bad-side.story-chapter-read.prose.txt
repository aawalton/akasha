Chapter 131

Getting on Madame Bloom’s Bad Side

“None of that now. I was originally sent here to evaluate you, as a possible substitute for your mentor Madame Bloom. But after seeing you just now, I have changed my mind.” The Matriarch spoke, her eyes seeming to judge every part of me, as I could see the intense blue glow within her eyes that showed she was reading my status screen.

“Changed your mind?” I ask wondering just how much of the past few moments she had just seen, and if there was anything I should be wary of. Judging by the unusual stiffness in Anastasia’s posture, I realize it is likely a lot, and that I should be wary.

“Yes,” The Matriarch began as she strode closer, only to stop a moment later as she spotted my sword at my side. The sword from that had been confiscated from me, until time for the duel and then never requested back. Honestly, I didn’t even question its weight or addition to my body, until I felt the Matriarch’s piercing gaze on me and my sword. Then to make sure there was no confusion about what drew her attention, she asked out loud, “that sword?”

Turning to Madame Bloom, she had a note of question and more to her words. An almost accusation, being noted in her question that Madame Bloom identified immediately.

“Yes, it is what you are thinking,” Madame Bloom replied. Hearing that, the Matriarch’s eyes grew wide for a moment, then turning back to me held out her hand and all but demanded the blunted blade.

“Let me see it,” the Matriarch asked. I paused for only a moment, as I reached down to unbuckle the strap from my waist. An almost imperceptible nod from Madame Bloom my only encouragement that this was what was expected. Taking off the sword, I handed it to the Matriarch still sheathed in its scabbard.

Holding out my hands, I placed it in her outstretched arms. Her hands dropping ever so slightly at the unexpected weight of the stone sword.

“Huh, it is heavier than I expected,” the Matriarch commented, noting her own miscalculation of weight. Then I watched as she pulled out the blade, seeming to inspect it up close. Her eyes close enough to make out the individual flecks of stone patterns that formed during its creation. She even ran her finger over the blade, gently at first, but then harder once she realized it was not sharp at all. “So this is it, the legendary DiVoratore Clan sword?”

“Yes,” Madame Bloom replied crisply, not giving any extra words. Her answer crisp and efficient, as if she expected anything extra that was said to be used against her. At least that is what Madame Bloom’s suddenly tight body posture expressed to me.

“You gave it to one of your own progeny?” The Matriarch asked, a note of challenge in her words. As they spoke, it was clear that this Matriarch also had some understanding of not just the sword, but the sword’s former wielder. The DiVoratore ancestor who Madame Bloom had more than a simple respect for. It was likely this respect that caused Madame Bloom’s entire demeanor to change as she suddenly stiffened her posture as she glared at the Matriarch.

“Use your eyes and truly look at the girl before you, before you speak to me about nepotism,” Madame Bloom’s words came as a slap to the face of the Matriarch. In that one comment, it was clear that whatever propriety, or civil conventions were being adhered to before were suddenly dismissed as the Matriarch had clearly crossed a personal line with Madame Bloom. Alarmed by the sudden shift in demeanor, the Matriarch paused, her body tensing from the sudden jolt of anger. Then looked to the sword, and finally to me. That’s when I could see her taking me in. Taking in my silver hair, pink eyes, and overall pale complexion. Realizing I looked nothing like the prototypical blond hair, emerald green eyes, and overall healthy complexions one would associate with a member of the Bloom estate.

“Wait, you’re a—” the Matriarch began, the words catching in her throat as she realized I was not a Bloom family member. For a moment I wonder what her issue is, but it only took a second and quick rebuke from Madame Bloom to confirm where the confusion came from.

“Yes, she is a fully awakened DiVoratore, whom we have adopted as part of a fostering program. That is why her status states she is a Bloom, as she is now and will be until she comes of age. Then she can either keep the Bloom designation, or revert to the naming of her bloodline lineage,” Madame Bloom replied. Her words were cold, as if she almost called out the Matriarch for believing in her own interpretation of a status screen and the data it represented, versus using her eyes and seeing the details that were right before her. This was a good lesson and one that I would have to be careful of if I earned an Analyze type Skill.

There was a moment of pause, as it was clear that the words had an effect on the Matriarch who seemed to bristle under the comment. But finally she regained her composure after a second, sheathing the blade and holding the blade in her hands. For a moment, I thought she would try to hand it back, but I stopped when I saw her hold back and just grip the blade for a moment too long. I could feel her watching me out of the corner of her eye, even as she turned to face Madame Bloom directly.

“So you think she is worthy of bearing such a blade? Have you even told her of its legacy? Of who and how you came into possession of this DiVoratore family sword?” The Matriarch pressed. Hearing the scrutiny, I could see the moment Madame Bloom about had it, her posture that had been rigid and fragile a moment ago, suddenly firmed. Her feet planted squarely, as she took a wide stance, her toes pointed directly at the Matriarch. Seeing the subtle shift in weight and posture, my mind instantly interpreted the signs as a form of aggression. As it was clear that Madame Bloom was no longer feeling gracious or diminutive in her own house. While she had been more than accommodating a moment ago, it seemed that the Matriarch had stepped too far.

“My Apprentice is worth more than any ten of your fledglings you have brought from the University to showcase your talent, and prestige,” Madame Bloom commented, waving a hand behind her at the other healers who were all wearing the same black healer garments that I had to wear while at the arena. Seeing that this was their formal attire, I began to think that this was more than just a local trend, or way to hide people from being bombarded with requests for help.

“How dare you!” The Matriarch hissed, lips curling in a snarl as she leaned forward and tried to use her half a head of height advantage to intimidate Madame Bloom. However, that did nothing more than anger Madame Bloom who slid her own foot forward, further closing the gap between these two and making her have better balance should she need to move. If this was my past life, I would swear that the Matriarch had the advantage should these two go to blows, yet, after being in this world for long enough. I knew that Madame Bloom wasn’t called general for a reason, that there was a clear purpose behind why everyone dropped everything in their lives to help out their former commander. Now, seeing that she was in trouble, and that the arranged gaggle of healers seemed to be stepping closer to no doubt help the Matriarch, I stepped forward as well.

Madame Bloom saw my movement, and it was perhaps that recognition that caused her to change, to lower her posture. In the end, Madame Bloom was the first to break the stalemate of power, by simply smiling. She smiled and then took a half step back, retreating the step she had taken a moment ago, while her face was more of a smirk.

“I tell you what,” Madame Bloom began, only to shift her cadence as she could clearly be seen plotting something. “You are here for the trial tomorrow, to stand as a witness in front of the condemned?”

That was new, and as far as I knew, the only trial tomorrow was for Sal and the other arena conspirators. That was also the reason why I was to rest today as Madame Bloom already expected me to be there tomorrow.

“Yes, we are using it as a real-world example of what to expect when things go wrong. It will also be a chance to show that sometimes there is nothing you can do but kill corruption at its source, so it doesn’t spread,” the Matriarch stated. Her own posture relaxing as the immediate threat was over, her face turned back to face her students, in what was clearly seen as a teachable moment.

“So you intend to do the pre-ritual prayers at the altar of the seven major healing lineages, and the twenty-one lesser lineages?” Madame Bloom pressed, her tone almost excited as she seemed to be preparing for a trap that the Matriarch either didn’t know existed or was unprepared to counter. Regardless, the Matriarch nodded as if it was a given fact.

“Of course, that is all part of the training. Asking the ancestors for guidance, to help grant us the strength to endure what we must,” the Matriarch continued.

With that, Madame Bloom nodded.

“Fine then, keep the sword. Then tomorrow after witnessing my Apprentice, bestow her the blade, if she proves herself worthy,” Madame Bloom commented.

At that, murmuring broke out, as all of the apprentice healers began muttering to themselves as if they could not understand what they were hearing. However, Madame Bloom was apparently done with the conversation, as she turned away from the Matriarch, all pretenses of manners and decorum dropped as Madame Bloom turned towards Lucia, Luna, and me.

“Come on you three, we have a busy day tomorrow,” Madame Bloom demanded, waving us forward with her hands as she never broke stride past us. From the gait of her stride, it was clear that she was well and truly pissed. Whatever level of decorum Madame Bloom had held for this visitor was gone, replaced with a deep seeded anger that was both undeniable and remarkably refreshing. Especially as this anger seemed to come from a perceived sleight against me, which is something that I didn’t think could, or would, happen in this lifetime.

I gave one glance back to a suddenly confused Matriarch, whose lips quivered with an unspoken question. The question likely pertained to the large and cumbersome sword that was in her hands, a sword that she clearly didn’t think she herself was worthy of holding. But could not give it back to me. That was the last I saw of the Matriarch, as I turned to follow Madame Bloom and the others. All of us turning and moving as one to avoid the gaggle of people behind us.

We turned, right, then the second left, then right again, out to a courtyard that I was not aware of. A small courtyard that held a fountain and some bushes still waiting for winter to be over so they could come back into bloom. Then out that courtyard, and into a different building, which is I assume where Lucia and Luna were residing.

“You two stay here, and try not to go out, until I get back,” Madame Bloom told the two who nodded and then entered the room. No questions asked, but it was clear that we all had questions. But Madame Bloom was far too angered to talk to them cordially, as such she just turned to leave the room and beckoned me to follow her with the crooking of her finger. That was it, no words, just an implied command to follow, which I did.

With each step, I could see how more and more of the anger that was inside her began to wear its way out of her system, until finally we were back at the main house. As I should have expected, we somehow ended up right by my room.

“Rest up, tomorrow you will have a big day,” Madame Bloom commented.

“You okay?” I ask, my words coming despite the clear look of determination radiating off of Madame Bloom. Yet, at the appearance of my word, I could see the last vestige of anger leave her.

“Yes, well I will be, after you show that blow hard what I and the others already know,” Madame Bloom begins.

“What’s that?”

“That you are meant for more than here. That your talents and ingenuity are enough to change the world, so long as the powers that be stop glaring down their noses at the rest of the world, thinking that they are better than everyone,” Madame Bloom continued. As she spoke, I could hear the pent-up anger and animosity attaching themselves like my magical notes of energy added to songs I sing. Except in this case, I only hear emotions being attached to each word spoken. Seeing that she was truly focused on this one goal, I decided to ask.

“What can I do to help?” I ask.

For a moment Madame Bloom pauses, and I can see her debating with herself, but then finally nods as if coming to some accord.

“I want you to put on a show tomorrow. When everything happens, I want you to hold back. To let everyone else wallow in denial and apprehension. Then you go in, save lives, and make it a spectacle that no one will forget.”

There was a pause, as she let that sink in, her eyes staring at me with a cold intense challenge. Then she hits me with the one question that ignites a fire of determination within me.

“Can you handle that?”

I nodded, knowing I would do everything in my power to make her proud.
