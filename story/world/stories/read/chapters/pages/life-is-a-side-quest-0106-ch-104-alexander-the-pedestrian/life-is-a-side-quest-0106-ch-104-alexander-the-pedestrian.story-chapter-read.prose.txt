When he looked about, he saw tight rows of stacked stone housing and the streets busy with mouse people. There seemed to be a bazaar at the edge of his vision. One of the guards poked his leg.

“Look for Min Min in the shop market. She helps travel adventurers.”

“Thank you…”

“Kip Kip is this one’s name. Find me if you have bad trouble and I will help you for more buttons.”

“Thank you, Kip Kip. I am Alexander,” he said, assuming that as they freely gave their names he could do the same.

Alexander noted that, like the structures, the people wore a similar style of old European garb. The effect was far too adorable. Little caps and cloaks were donned by the mouse men. He saw one driving a large snail to pull a cart full of wares toward the market. Mixed among them were other types of creatures—crow people and a fox that each stood a head taller than the rest—but still he towered over the crowd.

He received a few surprised glances, but they turned their stares away quickly enough to give him the impression that while he was uncommon, he wasn’t such a rarity as to cause alarm.

He moved slowly through the street as the residents scurried about around him. Nobody walked here; it was all dashing and abrupt stops to talk to people. He saw holes in the ground and the sides of the buildings with the mice scurrying in and out.

When he finally made it to the market, he finally felt less cramped by the buildings. Short canopies were set up over tables with high-pitched voices shouting out wares.

“Fresh straw! One tan or brown for a bale bundle!”

“Blue for a pint handle of milk!”

Alexander bent down to look at the milk salesman. “Pardon me, could you point me to Min Min?”

“Squeak! Ho, giant. You startle-shocked me!”

“I apologize.”

“Min Min’s shop is set up under the yellow canopy tent that way direction.”

“Much appreciated.” Alexander nodded.

“You buy milk! One pint for blue!”

“Blue… button?”

“Yes, yes!”

“I apologize. I don’t have any blue buttons. If I get one, I will be sure to come by again.”

The mouse sighed; he seemed to really want a blue button.

Alexander nodded and walked toward the end of the row in the direction the mouse had pointed.

He mentally calculated just how many buttons he had on him. If he added up the ones on his shirt fronts, then the ones to button up sleeves and wrists, he figured he had around forty-eight clear buttons. Then pants and his sewing kit added another eight, so fifty-six in total. He guessed the value of a glass of milk in a market at four dollars.

It sounded like he had maybe the equivalent of just over two hundred dollars if the buttons were considered common, but the guards seemed to value his clear buttons higher than normal. No guard would grift him for four dollars. He’d have to feel it out some more. He also had a few individually wrapped bricks of aged cheddar, as it traveled well for trail snacks.

When he arrived at the yellow tent, he was excited to see a feminine mouse decked out in all manner of odd accessories. She had goggles on her head with several lenses on a hinge so she could rotate them in and out of place. Her gloves had what looked like a pair of metal chopsticks attached on the back of her hand. When she flexed her hand, the chopsticks closed around a tiny gear, plucking it out of a bowl. She placed the gear in what looked like a winding pocket watch and used a screwdriver to place a crumb-sized screw to secure it.

Alexander waited patiently for the mouse to finish before he interrupted her. These mouse-men seemed skittish, and he didn’t want to cause her to fumble the precise task. When she closed what he assumed was a pocket watch he spoke.

“Excuse me, are you Min Min?”

The mouse looked up at Alexander’s belt, then slowly trailed up to his face. The goggles caused her eyes to look enormous through the magnification.

“Hail and well met, introduced giant!” she said with a light voice before removing her goggles carefully. “How can this one be of helping assistance?”

Alexander looked at the gadgets around the shop, and he couldn’t help thinking about how new gadgets could be modified by his pedestal.

“Can I first hear about what types of wares you make?”

“Yes, yes! These are fine devices for the travelers!” Min Min said as she ran over to grasp a device. “This makes your poor giant eyes see the herbs with much brightness. Easier to pick the findings.”

Alexander reached out his hand to hold the tiny goggles.

Lenses of Herb Finding

Uncommon

These lenses highlight herbs that can be used in cooking, alchemy, and medicine. The effective distance is determined by your wisdom.

“A fine device.” Alexander nodded. “A bit small for me.”

“Adjustable they are! I have longer straps of leather for you to make adjustment.”

“Excellent. What else?”

“Traps making I have! And escape bombs I make! These twine balls have sharp pointies that cover the ground so you can scurry-run while enemies have hurts on their feet! Many enemies have had their dinner snacks robbed by these.”

“So like a caltrop bomb. Good. Anything else more magical?” Alexander probed.

The mouse looked about for anyone listening in, then skeptically looked at the buttons on Alexander’s shirt.

“Min Min does not show such things to the common customer shoppers, but you are not common, yes yes?” She scurried back behind her shop before returning with a box.

She placed it on the tiny table in front of Alexander, who was now squatting down with his full attention.

“This is most potent device. For bad giants and conjurers.” She wound the side of the box and moved a few tiny sliding panels. When it opened up, Alexander saw a dull magical resource and sighed. He figured he had been correct in guessing there was a premium on magical resources.

“Yes! A null magical resource! Very expensive, but this box will wind countdown. When you set arm the box, you run… then it blows up the null resource, causing a big space area to have all mana workings not work! No castings! No magics!”

Alexander’s head shifted back, fully impressed. This is what Earth needed: new ways to use the magic resources that were for some reason more common.

“Well? You want need Min Min’s wares, yes yes?”

“Yes… I certainly do. I am going to have to travel to get where I am going. I might need your advice before making a purchase,” Alexander said, certain he could afford these items if he was willing to pull out some of his resources. “The first thing I heard is that you could get me a map?”

“Yes! I have many lots of maps! This one is brave and has gone journeyed much in her days. Where do you seek out? This one is interested much.”

“I need a map to an arcanum owned by a warlock.”

The bustle around him froze as if time had stopped. Min Min’s mouth hung open in shock.

“GUARDS! GUARDS!” Min Min shouted and backed away from her table. She pressed a button and a wall formed at the front of her shop.

All the mice around him sprinted away, and three guards came racing toward him with spears in attacking position.

Alexander sighed and raised his hands in surrender. Flick, however, decided to jump out of the shadows and growl at the guards. They squeaked in alarm and jumped back.

“DRAGON!” the guards shouted.

More guards scurried out of the holes around him in a flood, but all kept their distance.

Alexander was about to just cast a floating disc to run away when a blur of white flew from a tall building and blew up a cloud of dust when it landed before him. Tiny sword drawn and a white cape floating behind him, a mouse with a cocky grin stood from his powerful entrance.

“The prince hero!” the mice around him muttered, looking more confident.

The little prince had a white vest and red cravat. Shiny gold buttons were pronounced on his chest.

“Vile giant person! You have met your better match!” he said, taking a pose.

The temptation to use Suggestion and avoid the whole troublesome situation was strong. He used his recent reminder that other wizards were known for abusing their power and reinforced his commitment not to use the spell for simple convenience.

“Good prince.” Alexander bowed. “I feel there is a misunderstanding and would prefer we talk it out rather than come to blows.”

“You speak say such humble words. I have shock-surprised you with my battle aura! Ha!”

“You do strike a strong figure, sir. I am simply looking for directions home and seem to have asked a question in ignorance.”

“He wants to look-find the Arcanum place! He wants to join the Warlock tyrant!” Min Min shouted from behind her walls.

“Looking to seek-join the army of the warlock are you!” the prince shouted.

“No…” Alexander’s mind shuffled to channel some of the fairy-tale nonsense that Ridley would use in their conversations. “I have stolen a mighty weapon from him. While escaping, I managed to leave this plane and return to my world. I found myself back in this world of darkness again and hope to find that portal again.”

“REALLY! You speak facts true?” the prince said in surprise.

The mice around him muttered.

“What of your dragon beast?” the prince pointed.

“He is my companion and feels very protective of me, and I him.”

“Truly?” the prince muttered. “You will come with me and we shall speak-talk of this in the Warren Hall. Min Min.”

“Yes yes, Prince Hero?” she said, lowering her wall slightly to peek over.

“You shall join attend.”

“Do I have to?”

“You must,” the prince said with a charming smile and a wink.

Min Min gave a hesitant sigh.

Alexander gave a little bow and made a mental curse to his seemingly unending parade of side quests.

“Be warned, giant,” the prince said, putting away his sword. “I can feel when you say deceiving lies, but you shall be paid for your story information. Does the giant like cheese?”
