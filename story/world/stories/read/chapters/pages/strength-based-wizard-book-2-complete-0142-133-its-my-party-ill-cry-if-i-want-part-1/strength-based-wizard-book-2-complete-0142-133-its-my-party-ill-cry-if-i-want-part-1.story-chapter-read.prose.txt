Chapter 133

It's My Party, I'll Cry If I Want (Part 1)

Entering Dead World #53.

We appear in the Realm one at a time. The meat grinder of static leaves my body as my boots touch down onto solid ground. My vision is still washed in white light. There’s a knot in my stomach. It feels different from the typical sensation of stepping into a Gate—the cold finger hooking itself through your belly button and yanking you through a drain to another plane of existence. This time, it’s just my nerves rising to the surface.

I think I’m gonna puke, I think, trying to push down and smother the sensation. This is the Party’s first time in the field together. We’ve spent the past few days training and getting to know each other’s capabilities, but this is where rubber meets the road. It’s sink or swim, and I’m the only lifeguard on duty. If this doesn’t go well, we’ll all be culled by the System. And it’ll be my fault.

I shake my head, telling that voice of doubt in my head to fuck off. There’s no time for this. As the white light bleeds away from my vision, it’s replaced by a new light. The faint glow of white, pink, and turquoise coming off of various gems. The air is cool and dry, and the smells wet and musty. I hear the trickle of running water somewhere in the distance. The others materialize beside me in the dim crystal light of the stones embedded into the natural stone floor, walls and ceiling.

I blink a few more time, and realize we’re standing in a cavern. The ceiling arches far overhead, disappearing into a forest of jagged crystals, each one glowing softly from within. The light refracts everywhere, bouncing off facets and fractures until the cavern looks like it was carved inside a chandelier the size of a high school gymnasium. The floor beneath us is uneven stone dusted with glittering mineral powder. Crystal veins snake through it like frozen lightning. Pillars rise in the distance, natural and sharp, some wider than trees, some thin as spears. Shadows gather where the light overlaps wrong, making pockets of darkness between all the beauty.

And great places for monsters waiting to ambush us, I think, taking note of each location.

I recall the intel Jerome collected on this Gate. Dead World #53. Also known as the Crystal Palance. It’s one of the more common high-leveled Realms Gates produce. Apparently, being summoned into an actual massive crystalline palace is the most common entry point. We got the caverns. The cavern and catacomb sections of Crystal Palace have more variation, and are generally less common. So, there’s less information we can reliably depend on.

“Of course,” I mutter.

Nothing immediately triggers my [Perception] or [Aura Sense], but I look at Raul and Clyde, who are both scanning the place using their Skills.

“Anything?”

“No, my brother!” says Raul.

Clyde simply shakes his head.

Behind me, the last shimmer of the Gate collapses into nothing. The connection to Earth vanishes, leaving us inside the glowing cavern. There will be an exit Gate somewhere within this first section of the Realm. There always is. That’s how Gates are established for Exploration Teams. That Gate will be connected to the Gate we used to enter the Realm, and will remain open until we clear the initial Quest generated by the Gate.

I turn to take in the Party. Harvest Guild outfitted everyone. And I mean outfitted. Labonte called it “a wise capital expenditure.” Jerome called it “necessary equipment provisioning.” Raul called it “Christmas for warriors.”

Charlotte stands a few steps to my left, already checking the cavern with sharp green eyes. She wears an enchanted crimson tricorne tilted at just enough angle to look stylish instead of pirate cosplay, though it is absolutely also pirate cosplay. It’s called [Enchanted Tricorne of Spell Sniping]. It increases the accuracy of long-range spell attacks by twenty percent. Over her torso she wears a fitted gilet that provides resistance to most physical damage types. It’s dark, practical, and reinforced in subtle panels under the fabric. Over all that, she wears a blue robe called a [Robe of Storing]. The inside is lined with pockets. A ridiculous amount of them. And each is enchanted with a holding charm to provide a preserving storage space. Charlotte could store enough food from Earth to fuel her different spell builds for weeks, plus potion ingredients, spare gear, emergency supplies, and probably Jelly Boy too.

Ivy Madison stands beside Charlotte in full plate armor. The armor is steel-gray with faint gold lines etched along the joints, reinforcing movement points and probably doing something else (there’s too much new gear to keep track of). Her visor is currently raised, revealing her green eyes and the bitter expression of someone who has run several internal simulations and disliked most of them. I know she was the most verbal about not proceeding if we didn’t get a standard palace spawning location, but was overruled by not only the rest of the Party, but the Guild who paid the fee to acquire the Gate.

I had been surprised by her choice to don full plate. Ivy is a caster. An Architect. Her role is structures, constructs, battlefield control. Most casters go robes, leather, light armor, something that says, Please do not hit me because I am clearly a backline fighter. Ivy chose plate. When I asked why, she had looked at me like I had asked why bridges need supports.

“My priorities include not dying,” she had said.

Fair, I thought.

In case she’s forced into direct combat, she carries a small maul at her hip. It’s compact and brutal, and can definitely deal some damage even in the hands of someone with limited training. On the other side is a [Rod of Flamethrower], which has six charges of [Flamethrower] per day.

Sang-hyeok stands slightly apart from the rest of us, quiet as ever. He wears all-black leather armor beneath a dark trench coat that hangs around him like a shadow. The coat helps his mana regeneration and casting power, specifically when summoning mobs. His [Boots of Air Stepping] look simple, matte black with silver thread around the soles. Jerome explained they allow him to move through the air as though stepping on invisible platforms. Sang-hyeok tested them once during practice by walking calmly upward ten feet, then standing there in the air with his hands in his pockets while Raul applauded like he’d witnessed a miracle. The mana drain is also negligible, which makes them extremely valuable. He also wears several rings, each one adding different effects to his spells. One increases the Strength and Dexterity of his summons for a short period of time. One adds a chill effect. One lets certain summons briefly interfere with physical movement, becoming difficult terrain for potential opponents.

Now, in the crystal light, the Korean man’s pale face looks even paler. His dark eyes move across the cavern. The space around him feels crowded even before he summons anything. But given we’re in Crystal Palace, we knew we were potentially going to encounter tight spaces and corridors. Sang-hyeok will be clutch as hell if we need his summoned spirits to hold a bottleneck position for us.

Raul stands to my right, looking like a barbarian-themed romance novel cover was asked to attend a tactical briefing. He is scantily clad in stereotypical Barbarian attire, and he is absolutely thriving. Fur-lined boots, two heavy leather belts, minimal upper body coverage, and large bracers that cover his bulging forearms. Enough exposed muscle to make any gym mirror blush. It doesn’t look flashy at first glance, except Raul himself makes everything flashy because he is Raul and he has bleached electric-blonde hair and a full black mustache that makes anything look like he’s a lavish professional wrestler persona.

Most of his gear further buffs his Resistances. The bracers on his forearms are called [Exploding Bracers].

“Subtlety is dead,” Ivy sighed when we had first received our new gear.

The bracers were extremely cheap. They can produce a strong explosion centered on his arms, and a pretty powerful explosion. The only downside is that the explosion includes the wearer. Luckily, he largely resists the blast, making them generally safe for him to use. If we’re even luckier, enemies we encounter will have half as much Resistance against the force of the explosion.

Raul also wears brass knuckles enchanted to add a variety of elemental types to his unarmed strikes. Across his back is a great axe with a staggering effect that can proc after a successful strike. The axe head is dark metal, heavy and wide, with runes crawling along the edge like little teeth. Raul keeps glancing back at it affectionately.

Oddly enough, strapped over one eye and wrapping behind his head, he also wears a strange futuristic-looking scanner device. It’s all smooth black metal, glass, and faint blue targeting lines. It supports his analytical Skills. But I can’t help but think he looks like a monkey-tailed alien from outer space ready to tell me my power level is over nine thousand.

Clyde stands near the edge of the group, already half in shadow despite the fact that the whole cavern glows. He looks like a classical assassin or ranger, if that assassin or ranger had worked for Versace. His gear is dark green and black, fitted close, layered for movement, reinforced at the shoulders and forearms. It increases stealth, which is hilarious because Clyde already moves like a whisper with knives and firearms. His bracers are matte black, inscribed with thin silver marks. They increase damage as he chains similar attacks—strike after strike after strike, each one building on the last, up to a five times damage multiplier. He demonstrated them against training targets by cutting the same point six times in under two seconds. The final strike hit so hard the target cracked down the middle.

He also has two [Rings of Summoning]. One-time uses only. Jerome told him to use them only in case of emergency. The Guild Vice Captain had originally thought I would use the rings, but not to overload me, decided Clyde could be trusted with the rings. The two had a quiet sidebar when Jerome had gifted them to the newest member of our Party. I couldn’t hear what they discussed.

When they rejoined the group, I had asked what qualified as an emergency.

Jerome said, “If you need to ask, it is not one.”

Clyde said, “You’ll know.”

Jelly Boy bounces excitedly near my ankles. The slime unfortunately can only equip a new hat. He was given two hats, which he can swap out with his white scaled wizard cap if the situation calls for it. The first is a blue sailor’s cap that reminds me of something I’d see on a classic Donald Duck cartoon. The cap is a [Leviathan Hat], which provides certain water-affinity Skills and abilities, and once per day can allow Jelly Boy to expand to a gigantuan size. The second hat appears to be an ordinary yellow hard hat. The hat is actually a [Hat of Defense] which provides barrier Skills and can increase the strength of structures. Jerome originally wanted to give the hat to Ivy, but Jelly Boy strongly insisted that the hat be his. There was no rationalizing with the slime guy, so the matter was quickly settled.

Then there’s me. I’m largely in my standard set. Hat, cape, boots, fanny pack and jorts. I did, however, get a couple small additions. The first is a magical earring. It’s a simple silver loop, tiny and unassuming. The earring makes me immune to a laundry list of random conditions. From petrification to lycanthropy. I’m also resistant to most curses.  The second addition is a single leather, fingerless glove on my left hand. It looks plain, being made of a brown leather. It’s a tight fit, with the faintest silver stitching around the wrist.

This was something Jerome specifically sought out for me. The glove modifies [Wizard’s Fist] once per day to create a gigantic, non-combative hand that can fly and be used for transport. It can carry the entire Party. A party bus made of fingers. A magical ride-share shaped like the world’s most unsettling high-five. I could go on and on. But it’s useful. Very useful. Transport, emergency extraction, crossing gaps, avoiding ground hazards, moving the whole group quickly if staff flight isn’t practical.

I didn’t get anything else. Not because Harvest was stingy, or ran out of funds or access to kickass equipment. Harvest tried. Labonte wanted me covered in magic items. The problem is me. Specifically, my Spirit stat.

I never knew Spirit directly impacts the number of magical items and equipment a System User can have actively equipped at once. Apparently, if you go beyond your limit, consequences can be deadly. Magical overload. Soul strain. Equipment rejection. Mana feedback. A bunch of words Jerome said calmly while Labonte looked at me like he was watching a beloved investment refuse diversification.

My Spirit is low. Because every time I get free points, Strength sits there flexing in my brain and saying, Come on, buddy. You know what we’re about. And, to be fair, it has worked out. But standing here now, looking at everyone outfitted in specialized gear while I’m limited by my sad little soul capacity, I make a note. I need to start putting points into Spirit. Just a little investment every so often. I do not want to be locked out of more powerful equipment down the road because my inner magic battery is a potato attached to some wires.

My musings are interrupted by the familiar pulsing sensation of an incoming System message.

Ping!

A translucent blue-tinted window springs into view, inches from my face. I read the notification.

NEW QUEST!

New Quest: All Quiet in the Crystal Palace

Description: Welcome to Dead World #53. Something has happened in the Crystal Palace… Something wrong… Access to the Palace has been restricted.

OBJECTIVE: Reach the Palace Throne Room.

Reward: Gate Ticket (Gold) x7
