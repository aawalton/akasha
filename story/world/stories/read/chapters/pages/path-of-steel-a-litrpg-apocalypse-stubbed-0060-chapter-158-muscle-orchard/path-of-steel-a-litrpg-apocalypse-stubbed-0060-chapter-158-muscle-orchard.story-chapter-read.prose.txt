Moe entered a vast room. Its only point of interest was a circular alcove in the center, where tentacles were arranging strange materials. One tentacle was sprinkling white dust into the shape of a pentagram, with different glass cases in each corner and a massive crystal lying dead in the center. Moe quickly averted his gaze—both because the materials tugged at his soul, and to maintain plausible deniability.

“You asked to see me,” he said.

At the back of the room, two yellow eyes opened. “I respect your intelligence,” the puppeteer said in a low, bestial voice. “The first round of missions went as you told me. Do we stay our course?”

“Our common target performed great in the first mission. He should be a field boss by now, meaning he can choose his mission. We know where he’ll be.”

The puppeteer’s eyes flared, revealing the lying, spherical form. “What should we do with him?”

“We’ll be reaching the finale after this round. There will be chaos. This is our last chance to isolate him, so I suggest we spare no resources. I know how important he is to you, puppeteer.”

The black form watched Moe for a long time. “Get it done,” it finally said, before its eyes closed back into slumber. The tentacle sprinkling white dust accelerated. Moe bowed, and holding that position, left the room.

Jester of Hearts, Level 25 (Field Boss)

Let me tell you a little story.

There once was a man named Sam. Sam loved a particular girl. The problem was, she didn’t love him back, but Sam wouldn’t quit that easily. He put on tons of make-up to hide his ugliness, wore gloves to hide the spots on his hands, and bought a hat to hide his growing baldness. He didn’t eat for two weeks to lose some weight.

When he was ready, he went to the girl’s home and rang the bell, only for another man to answer.

Sam was inconsolable. He’d given her his heart, and she’d thrown it away. She’d betrayed him.

To keep on living, Sam needed his heart back. He returned to the girl’s home, struck the guy with a hammer, then demanded she return his broken heart. When she didn’t, he took hers instead. A heart for a heart. If he couldn’t go on living, neither should she. He also took the other man’s, since he was already there.

Sam still lacked his heart. He hadn’t found it in the girl’s house or the nearby trashcans. He did, however, have two new hearts, which he stuffed in a grocery bag and carried back with him. They were pretty. He decided to gather more. Since he couldn’t conquer them, he just had to take them by force. He did, after all, have a hammer.

On the eve of his third murder came the Drafting, and Sam received an offer different than most. He’d be given power and all the hearts he could take. He struck a deal.

As for why he’s dressed as a jester… Well, that’s because Sam was a total clown.

Gary hadn’t expected the violent, visceral reaction he had to the clown. He jumped back and drew his spear. His stomach rose to his throat. He tasted bile. His world span, and suddenly he was back in the forest, watching the jester rip the heart out of Goran right in front of him.

The jester tilted his head at Gary, slapped his blood-soaked hammer against its palm, and whistled a jolly tune.

“Are you okay?” Alicia asked, unaware of the horror she witnessed.

Gary took his time reorienting himself. Finally, he sat on the grass. “Sorry. Give me a sec. This is harder than I thought.”

A golden snake materialized in front of the jester’s face. “Listen here, you!” Goldie said. “I don’t know who you are, but you’re scaring my Gary. If you even think about touching him, I will make things on your body fall that you wish were hair! Are we clear?”

The jester nodded slowly while also licking his lips.

“I like it when they listen,” Goldie said proudly. “Who’s this guy, Gary?”

“Just a clown,” Gary replied.

Evil Shrek squared up to the Jester of Hearts. “Do I detect a hint of minty freshness?”

“We are the same…” the jester replied in a manically cackling whisper.

“Boss, are these guys with us?” Osteo said, finally arriving with the other beef boys.

“Yes, Osteo. They’re with us.”

“I don’t like them.”

“Me neither, but be nice.”

“Okay.”

Delilah, Alicia, and Cog exchanged shrugs. “You don’t want to know,” Asim told them, also making the introductions.

More people had gathered by now. Many had their own beef boy mercenaries, while a few stared at Gary’s summoned bosses with confusion. Finally, the witch leading them spoke.

“We’ll be heading to the orchard, everyone. Follow me.”

“Why say many word when few do trick?” Asim whispered, making Gary chuckle.

The witch started running, followed by everyone. The speed she led them at was slower than Boulderus Johnus during the first mission. As a result, everyone could keep up—even the Jester of Hearts, who still pushed his cart full of human hearts. He dropped one occasionally as if leaving crumbs to find his way back.

The birds chirped overhead, and a refreshing breeze blew through the group as they ran across the grass. The orchard was in a different direction than the three bridges. They reached the river eventually, then sped alongside it, going significantly upstream.

The leading witch didn’t speak much. Gary learned her name was Avernus, and her previous failure to capture the orchard had hit her hard. She hoped to restore her honor.

Unlike Gary’s previous outing, the boxing champion was not with them, and the Core Formation allies were few and far between. Even the Birth of Aura climbers were fewer. Clearly, this mission was low-priority, which was both a blessing and a curse. Blessing, because it was safer. Curse, because gathering tons of merit points would be harder.

Should I have picked another mission? he wondered, but put the thought away. It was too late, anyway.

A grass wall finally came into view. It stood just to their side of the river, bordering the white water, and extended for more than a mile horizontally. Gary couldn’t tell how deep it went.

“That’s not an orchard,” Asim said.

“The Muscle Orchard is a grass maze,” Alicia explained. “It’s meant to stop people from messing with it, and in normal times, climbers can attempt to infiltrate it and steal some sap. Well, if the major clans didn’t have a topper standing guard. He’s gone now.”

“Do we have to fight in a maze?” Gary wondered.

“Or over it,” Cog said.

Avernus, the leading witch, stopped them at the gate of the maze, in the middle of one of its long walls, and surveyed the opposite side of the river. Gary did the same.

“They didn’t send reinforcements,” Avernus said. “Okay, everyone. The troops which defeated us before should be the only ones in the orchard. Our task is to split up, locate them, and eliminate them. Once all enemies are dead, or so few remain that the System deems them useless, the orchard will be ours.”

If no enemy reinforcements arrived, and Chloe was at the bridge battle before, that means she’s not here, Gary thought with relief.

“Should we do anything to the trees?” another witch asked. They made up the majority of this mission group.

“Not yet. Once we win the battle, I’ll distribute equipment to harvest the sap and send it to Satan Gym. For now, just try not to damage them.”

“How do we find enemies?” Gary asked.

“There is no easy way. We’ll have flying summons overlooking the maze, but so will the enemy. Finding targets will depend on your luck.”

“Then, if we’re unlucky, we’ll run into Core Formation enemies and die?”

“Yes.”

Gary wasn’t surprised. All he felt was numb acceptance.

“If you encounter enemies you cannot defeat, use these to call for help,” Avernus said, handing everyone a set of flares. “Blue means Birth of Aura, green means Core Formation, and red means Core Formation above level 35. If you see a flare, and you can handle the enemy it indicates, you should rush to help your fellow climber.”

“Will the orchard fight us as well?” someone asked. Muffled chuckles came from across the group.

“Don’t laugh, that’s a valid question,” Avernus said. “The Muscle Trees are alive, along with many other plants, and they will be fighting against us since the horrors have control of the orchard. There are traps, too, which can harm both us and the horrors.”

“So, we’re going into a grass maze where we’ll split up, the enemies we’ll find are random, and the maze itself is trying to eat us?” Gary asked.

Avernus gave him a stern glare. “Yes.”

“Okay. Just checking. We’ll wreck them.”

“That’s the spirit.” She smiled. “Climbers, beef boys, and pros. And, uh, creepy summons. This is a scary place, but we are the stronger force. We will emerge victorious. See it as an opportunity instead of a threat, and fight to win. For the gymnasium!”

“For the gymnasium!” everyone roared, then poured into the labyrinth’s entrance.

Through the green arch, the path split into five, with more splits down the road. The hundred or so climbers split randomly, each followed by their summons or mercenaries. Gary’s group stuck together. They ran down corridors, turning randomly until the footsteps of everyone else disappeared.

“Is this far enough, boss?” Asim asked.

“Yeah. Let’s do this,” Gary replied.

“See you in victory, brothers and sisters!” Osteo yelled, leading three beef boys and Evil Shrek down a path. The Jester of Hearts followed another Canc and another three beef boys. Each of the two summons was a Birth of Aura field boss—enough to kill anything else in Birth of Aura or delay a weaker Core Formation opponent long enough for Gary to arrive. Hopefully.

Asim with Cog made up the third party, and Delilah with Alicia the fourth. Each was reinforced by two beef boys. They could have split thinner, but Gary chose safety over merit points. The final group was Gary himself, alone. He’d travel fast, over the grass walls, confident in killing or escaping from weaker Core Formation enemies. If he ran into something he couldn’t handle, he’d use the flares.

“Let’s go,” he said, squatting down, then launched himself into the air. The labyrinth came into sight. It was a sprawling expanse of green lines, twisting and curving around each other. Some walls were straight, with square corners. Others were curvy, further complicating the geometry of the labyrinth. The entrance wasn’t too far behind them, but all other sides of the labyrinth were incredibly distant.

This will take a while, he realized.

He knew that, during the first orchard mission, the armies had duked it out before the entrance. Having to discover enemies in the whole wide orchard increased the mission’s duration to hours or even days, but that was true for every mission in the second round. It was part of the design.

Gary floated in the air over the labyrinth, looking rapidly around. On the ground, nestled in the labyrinth and far from each other, were circular spaces each holding a tree. He couldn’t see more from here. Imps and what seemed like ethereal birds duked it out high in the sky, while a few of their number flew over the labyrinth to feed their summoners information. Aerial supremacy would be crucial in navigating the labyrinth.

The ethereal birds brought down Gary’s mood. He knew those were summons of the Deep Blue Academy—Chloe’s clan, which was an enemy in this floor. He hoped he didn’t meet her. If he did, he wouldn’t touch her, and he trusted she wouldn’t either.

As Gary returned to the ground, he caught a green shape shooting out of a nearby path. He instinctively covered himself, but it was an ally—Avernus, the witch leading this mission, stepped into a flying cauldron and took to the sky. Green lights trailed her. She rose high, breaking a pack of ethereal birds before a fireball exploded in her face, sending the cauldron spinning. She remained in it, somehow. More spells flew her way, and Gary spied them coming from a clean-shaven, young wizard on a green flying carpet. Nothing came of their battle, and within seconds, both had glided to the ground, letting the birds and imps duke it out.

Gary shrugged. He landed on top of a grass wall, only to find it covered in extremely sharp spikes. The grass itself was weak, bending under his weight, which made walking on the walls impossible. He could go there momentarily to look around, or jump over the walls to get somewhere faster.

Which is exactly what he did. Gary jumped into the air again, focusing on the area near him, and discovered two young wizards staring at him from only five paths away. One chucked him a fireball, which Gary shattered with a thrust. The flames that washed over him did little damage. As he landed, he barely caught them preparing some magic, then jumped over the walls in their direction.
