Ethan came back to himself with a gasp, struggling to center his mind back in his own body.

“It’s over,” he whispered, not sure he believed it himself.

But the corruption raging inside him promised that it was true. The feeling of competition for The Throne had disappeared the instant the scorpiursi’s stinger impaled the Emperor. There was only Ethan now.

Ethan, and the power of a god.

He looked down, surprised to find that only Valanor remained on the cliffside with him. The hulking warrior was down on his knees, watching the Emperor’s former Champion scuttle away, injured and wobbling, but desperate to get away.

Ethan watched it go, grateful that it wouldn't be a problem for him to deal with. The Leviathan was badly injured, Ethan had been forced to let it take a number of painful injuries in order to focus on capturing the Emperor. Thankfully, he’d managed to trick the beast into stinging itself, and it didn’t appear to be immune to its own poison.

He commanded the serpent to follow it some distance, feeling better about having the Champion rank demon no longer looming over him. He still needed the corruption the creature possessed, but there was delicate work to do now, and he couldn’t risk something going wrong with his control over the Leviathan.

“Will he come back?”

The question took Ethan by surprise, and he looked down at Valanor in confusion. The knight regained his feet, eyes full of pain turning on Ethan.

“Savilar. Will he come back? I know he’d likely lose himself, but I could help him. I could help him find his way back to himself.”

Ethan found that he needed a moment before he could answer. He’d been the one to kill Savilar in the first place, however justified. Now he had to deliver news that amounted to the same.

“Potentia brings creatures back…but that’s part of the corruption. It’s the ultimate manifestation of the power going mad. Pure potential, pure possibility, funneled into the most remarkable, and terrible thing imaginable.”

Valanor waited for Ethan to say it, though he was far too intelligent not to see it for himself.

Ethan sighed. “I have to complete the Union, Valanor. I have to fix what’s broken, and that includes the corruption. Potentia…it won’t exist anymore. And the power that brings things back, that’s Dusk. The god has to move on as well.”

Valanor slumped, but Ethan could tell the man expected the answer. He didn’t speak for a long moment, and Ethan gave him the time. He needed to recover anyway, for what came next.

“At least the Emperor is gone as well,” Valanor said, finally.

Ethan nodded. “Maybe his war started for justifiable reasons, but any sense of decency the Followers once might have had is long gone. They’re just parasites now. Something to be cured.”

Ethan paused, looking down at the remnants of battle between the demons, Bishops, and Nator. “Cal and Dean…?”

“Went to help your…family end the conflict. I doubt it will take long at this point.”

Ethan nodded. “Then I may as well get started.”

He moved away from the cliff, walking back in front of the immense rift, which still bathed the area in white-blue light. He stopped in a place that felt “right” somehow, and began the process of channeling the corruption he’d accumulated.

Valanor had followed, but wisely gave Ethan space as the dark power began to emanate from him. It was strangely easy, after so much struggle to gather it all. But the serpent had the corruption nearly bursting from it, and Ethan had long-ago worked out how to channel it.

He focused his mind and his will on a single point before him, on the place that held The Throne. The reaction was immediate, and Ethan could sense that removing the competition had begun this process already. The power wanted to be mastered.

Ethan was immediately confronted with a new challenge, however. “The Throne”, wasn’t truly a throne at all. It was a metaphorical seat of power, yes, and the term may have come from the Nator, once upon a time. But it wasn’t some mystical chair.

It was a god.

Dusk, himself, was The Throne, having fallen into some kind of divine coma. Yet, Ethan was no god, and he needed something he could interact with. Something he could see and feel, and so he drew on the easiest source of inspiration.

The channeled corruption wanted an outlet, and so Ethan gave it one. He gave it shape. In response, stone erupted from the ground, like a tiny mountain top, no more than twelve feet tall. But in its center, the rock formed into a chair, just big enough for one.

“That it?” a voice asked, and Ethan turned to find that Dean, Michael and Richard had arrived. It was Rich who’d spoken.

“Sort of,” Ethan answered. “How goes the battle?”

“Just some clean-up left,” Rich said. His enormous rifle was strapped to his back, and his eyes kept moving, searching for threats. “Calevaro sent us back. He and his dad are bonding over some demon-slaying, but he said he’d join us soon.”

Ethan nodded, and Dean moved up next to him. The two stared at the stone seat for a time, as if neither could believe this moment had really come.

“So what happens now?” Dean asked. “You’re the Chosen, do you just…sit in it?”

“No,” a stern voice called out, and all turned to see Ethan’s most disagreeable dimensional twin striding forward from out of nowhere. He looked as bad as the first time Ethan had seen him, wearing civilian clothing, all ragged, and filthy. His eyes were manic.

“No,” he repeated. “This isn’t for him, and he knows that.”

“Why not?” Dean demanded, seemingly unbothered by arguing with a copy of his little brother.

“Because he will fail,” Strategus said, voice making it clear there was no possible room for debate. “I’ve been here before,” the man continued, looking at the assembled group with something between fury and longing. “I’ve watched each one of you die so that I could get this chance.”

“Um, Ethan,” Rich began, “who or what is this guy?”

“He’s Ethan from another dimension,” Dean said. “Michael warned me he might interfere.”

Strategus turned a glare on Michael, but there was little heat in it. These were the brothers he’d lost, after all. “I know what I’m talking about,” he insisted. “I held Dusk’s power. I had completed the Union. It failed. Utterly.”

“And what did that failure look like?” Michael asked, appearing genuinely curious. Always ready to gather intel.

“Death,” Strategus answered, and let the word set in for a long moment before continuing. “It ended in death. A mortal mind isn’t meant to handle the power of a god.”

“The corruption?” Michael prompted.

Strategus barked a spiteful laugh. “No, it wasn’t the corruption. I had a plan for that. My Familiars sacrificed themselves to keep it from ruining me.” His voice grew quiet. “It ruined them instead. But it wasn’t enough.”

“But you said you completed the Union,” Dean prompted, “and you survived.”

“No one else did,” Strategus said, his voice flat and his eyes wide. “Three worlds, and all life wiped away. I couldn’t control that much power. It’s not meant for mortal minds. Dusk should guide the Union himself.”

Ethan glared. “You know that won’t work. Dusk is the corruption! It’s why he locked himself away. He couldn’t trust himself to be a part of the Union, because he knew his corruption would spread.”

“Then choose someone else!” Strategus bellowed. “Give someone else a chance to prove me wrong! But it cannot be you! I had your word, Ethan, and you will fulfill your promise. I failed once already, I won’t sit back and watch myself fail again!”

It was at that point that Calevaro appeared, looking at the group with a raised silver eyebrow. “I’m missing something?”

“Evil Ethan is making good Ethan choose someone to destroy the world,” Richard said.

“He’s not evil,” Ethan said. “Just broken.” Strategus didn’t object, he just glared.

Ethan looked out at the group of warriors assembled before him, trying to imagine which one of them might possibly do what he couldn’t.

His eyes went to Michael, the most calculating Bishop. He was sometimes frighteningly intelligent, and singular in his goals. Perhaps he could stay focused on what needed to be done, despite the overwhelming power of Dusk.

But Ethan had seen that calm broken by the blood of Twilight. He’d seen Michael fail before. For all that poise and calm, when the man lost his grip, it was a thing to behold.

Ethan looked to Rich next, who was shaking his head violently. Ethan couldn’t help but smile. Rich was Dusk rank anyway, and Ethan wouldn’t need to consider the laundry list of reasons why he was a bad candidate.

His eyes landed on Valanor next. The man’s eyes were red-rimmed, and the pain was still very much alive. But the man was stalwart in a way no one else could compare to. He’d held to his faith even after the world had torn it to pieces. Perhaps…

Ethan looked at Dean next. His older brother smiled at him encouragingly. Yes, he’d gladly take on this burden. He’d take on any burden. For his little brother, for the world, this was what he was born to do. But had anything prepared him for this challenge? He may have organized a world-wide rebellion, but how did that compare to the power of a god?

Finally, Ethan’s eyes landed on Calevaro, and the man was last for a reason. Ethan couldn’t blame the man for being captured by the Emperor–he doubted that resisting the Follower’s will had been possible. Yet ultimately, he had broken free.

Prince Calevaro, the man who’d chosen to trust Ethan, and to carve out his own destiny. The man who’d been marooned on Earth, and gained the attention of the Olde Masters, and the Yakuza both. A man of precision and control. Of purpose, and resilience.

Ethan’s eyes drifted back over them all, wondering at the absurdity of what he was considering. He was meant to trust them with this impossible task, forbidden from taking it on himself, lest he turn into…

His gaze fell on Strategus, and his other self glared back at him. He’d been waiting for this promise to be fulfilled for some time. The man truly was broken, and what a living representation of what Ethan’s failure would look like.

Everything about the man’s ragged appearance seemed to beg the same question: do you really want to end up like me?

The answer, obviously was no. And yet…

Ethan’s eyes narrowed, and he looked back at the assembled group. He saw trust in their eyes. Hope. And something occurred to Ethan then, something that he hadn’t considered before. He turned to Strategus.

“You lost them all, one by one,” he said.

Strategus’ glare didn’t falter. “And you’ll lose them all at once, if you don’t keep your promise.”

Ethan smiled sadly. “I’m sorry for what happened to you.”

Strategus’ face turned red. “I don’t want your sympathy! I want you to make a different choice! I want you not to become me!”

Ethan shook his head, moving over to stand directly before Strategus–no directly before Ethan Bishop. The man that might have been. But wouldn’t be.

“I can’t become you,” he said softly. “Our paths diverged long, long ago. You did everything you could. You never stopped fighting, and you had more than enough of your own challenges to deal with. But you also lost, and I don’t mean at the end.”

Ethan gestured to the men gathered around them. Brothers, each one. Some by blood, some by bond, but brothers all the same. “You lost this, first. You lost every person you could truly trust, and had to face The Throne alone.”

“There’s only room for one,” the other man insisted.

Ethan shook his head. “The Throne isn’t just a chair. You know that better than anyone. I have a path that was denied to you. I have people I can rely on.”

The other man moved away, eyes widening. “No. NO! You promised! Promised you wouldn’t become me!”

Ethan smiled sadly once more. “And I won’t. I can’t become you, Ethan. Our stories aren’t the same, and they won’t end the same. But this isn’t what you needed. This isn’t what will help you.”

“Nothing can help me!” the other man insisted.

Ethan gently rested his hand on his twin’s shoulder, and his healing rune began to glow softly. “Rest. Rest and grieve, and know that what you’ve feared won’t come to pass.”

“I…I…” the other Ethan stumbled backward, tears running down his face, but finally he looked at the group he’d been trying to ignore. The brothers he’d lost. The friends he’d never made. When he looked back at Ethan, there was the tiniest, fleeting glimpse of something that had never been there before.

Hope.

The Ethan that might have been, but never would be, disappeared.

The Ethan that owned his own fate remained, and he turned back to The Throne, determination rising within him like it never had before.

“It’s time," he said, stepping up to his destiny, then looking back at his brothers. “I’ll need your help.”
