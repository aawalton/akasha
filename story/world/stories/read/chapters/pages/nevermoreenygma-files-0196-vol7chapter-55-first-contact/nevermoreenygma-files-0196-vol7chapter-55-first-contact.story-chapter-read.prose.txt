Chapter 55

First Contact

After putting her affairs in order, Mai finally made her decision.

It wasn’t immediate, but after several nights of reflection and some conversations with old friends, she concluded that she could no longer simply watch from a distance. Even if she was only testing the waters, the Nevermore Initiative offered her more than just work.

When she sent her response through the channel Rein Aogami had provided, she was surprised to discover that Nevermore was linked to ZAIEN, the powerful multinational corporation. While many other corporations and organizations had severed ties or been dismantled under international pressure in the last years, ZAIEN not only survived but strengthened its connections, thriving where others had perished. That said a lot about its leadership and its ability to maneuver in the murky waters of both worlds.

A private ship was sent to pick her up days later in the military base of Yokosuka.

It was an aerodynamic model, designed for long-distance travel with orbital flight capability—a technology still restricted to the world’s most influential sectors, only used to reduce costs between trips to establish colonies on the Moon and the construction of space stations. The ground crew was surprised, but they had already been informed that a lone passenger would be picked up. Besides, it wasn't as if that ship could land and take off from commercial airports without attracting attention.

At first, she felt that it had been a bad idea to eat such a big breakfast, because the multi-G takeoff made her feel as if her stomach and some of her internal organs had shifted out of place, until the spacecraft’s internal stabilization system kicked in. The passenger cabin was silent since she was the only one there, with panoramic windows showing the vast ocean beneath her. As she watched the horizon, Mai wondered how many more secrets awaited her on the new path.

The trip was short, and by the time it began to slow down, she was already over European territory, and the ship was heading toward southern Spain. During the trip, she had been reviewing the instructions she had been given at the Inoue section, which were intended solely for her. She had been reading them over the past few days. It was almost a terse report, but it provided her with enough details.

Her destination was an evanescent land called Siren Island.

It didn’t appear on any maps.

In fact, it had only been definitively discovered and claimed by the hidden world a few years ago. Officially, at least in the hidden world, Siren Island was a disputed piece of land between Spain and Portugal—two countries that, due to the instability of the territory, had been unable to establish real control over it. Documents mentioned its existence centuries ago, but the island seemed to vanish from time to time, as if it had never been there. Technically, Portugal shouldn’t have had a claim, since it was within in the area of the Canary Islands archipelago.

It had had many names in ancient times, and on maps it would appear and disappear as if the cartographers had never been able to agree on it. It had only appeared a few years ago, but it was classified as a type of Dark Event—as were all the evanescent lands—similar to that expedition to the city of Marmuth she had undertaken when she was part of Whisper.

What the world didn’t know… was that Siren Island had been stabilized.

“Miss Izumi, please ensure your seatbelt remains secured,” the pilot said through the communicator.

Mai gave the camera an okay sign. Truthfully, she hadn’t removed it out of fear. At some point, she had expected to be floating, but despite the incredible speed, the cabin had maintained normal Earth gravity the entire time. She checked the time. She had departed from Yokosuka that morning, and in this part of the world, it should have been around seven in the morning.

The ship descended through a blanket of gray clouds, the roar of its engines gradually fading into a controlled mechanical hum as the vertical landing systems activated.

Mai watched silently through the window.

The island appeared before her eyes. Dark cliffs rose above the crashing ocean, surrounded by dense forests, green mountains, and a wilderness that seemed utterly untamed.

Were those pterosaurs flying near the peaks between communication towers?

The briefing had mentioned that the island contained wildlife from multiple eras, but she certainly hadn’t expected to see a flying dinosaur.

Yet amidst that savage land stood something entirely different.

Bases. Steel structures. Landing platforms. Watchtowers. Installations built with technological precision that contrasted sharply with the landscape, as though two incompatible worlds had been forced to coexist.

Nevermore hadn’t merely discovered the island.

It was trying to domesticate it.

“Wow...” Mai murmured, unable to hide her surprise.

For a territory whose existence was barely acknowledged even within the hidden world, it looked more like an international military fortress than a simple operations base.

When the ship landed, a powerful metallic sound echoed through the structure as it locked into the platform. The hatch opened with a hiss, and a gust of salty air struck Mai’s face.

The scent of the ocean was intense, and the early morning heat was already noticeable.

She stepped down calmly, observing her surroundings.

Military personnel, researchers, technicians, and agents moved with flawless efficiency. Humans and various types of fey worked together beneath Nevermore’s symbol.

She passed through multiple security checkpoints: biometric scanners, magical verification, psychic detection. Everything was fast, precise, and overwhelmingly advanced.

“Do you have your assigned sector?” a young fey woman asked. “If you’d like, a vehicle can escort you there.”

“Yes, but it’s close, right?”

“Yes, only about two hundred meters. The red building.”

“I’ll walk. I’ll be fine.”

“Are you sure?”

“Yes. Thank you.” Mai smiled.

Once she crossed the final checkpoint, she arrived at a vast transit zone connected to the heart of the facilities. It resembled a small city, with mostly gray-toned buildings, though the abundance of plants, palm trees, and surrounding forests added vibrant color. It almost reminded her of her time in the mountains of China.

She slung her bag over her shoulder and began walking.

Checking her map, she confirmed where she needed to go. It was detailed enough that getting lost seemed impossible, especially with signs posted in multiple languages everywhere. As she headed toward registration and assignment, she deliberately slowed her pace. She wanted to observe the place and adapt. After all, gathering information about the nature of this organization was also part of what she had set out to do.

Some people glanced at her as they passed. Most wore military uniforms, but there were also civilians, workers in orange uniforms, and helmeted construction crews. To the west, she spotted enormous fences lined with warning signs.

She thought she heard a distant roar.

So there really were creatures on this island.

Then she felt a small impact against her leg.

Mai blinked.

She looked down.

A small barefoot girl was staring up at her. She had bumped into her leg and now looked at her with wide-eyed amazement. She wore a dark dress. Mai immediately wondered whether children should really be in a place like this at all.

She appeared around five or six years old, with dark hair tousled by the sea breeze, gray eyes, and a delicate, almost fragile appearance. However, what immediately drew Mai’s attention was her right arm.

Or rather, the lack of one.

In its place was a black crow’s wing, seamlessly integrated into her body.

Mai froze for a moment.

The girl’s large gray eyes observed her curiously, but there was something unsettling within them. Something far too deep for an ordinary child.

“You’re new. You smell different,” the little girl said naturally, in Spanish.

Mai took a second to respond. It had been quite a while since she had last used Spanish.

“Yes... that seems about right.” She crouched slightly to meet her gaze. “Are you okay? Where are your parents?”

The girl tilted her head, studying Mai as though trying to understand something beyond her physical appearance.

“I’m looking for Papa.”

Before Mai could say anything else, a woman’s voice called out from nearby.

“Raven!”

A woman approached quickly, clearly distressed.

“I’m so sorry,” she said, offering Mai a small bow. “She constantly slips away whenever no one is watching.”

“I didn’t run away!” Raven replied calmly. “I was exploring.”

The woman looked at Mai. “You’re new?”

“Yes. I’m Mai Izumi, nice to meet you,” Mai said, raising her hand politely.

The woman shook it.

“Adaline Garcia. I thought so. Your face... I didn’t recognize it. You just arrived?”

“Yes. I’m heading to registration now.”

“Were you recruited as a field agent? Or technical support?”

“Field agent. Or at least, that’s the idea.”

Adaline raised an eyebrow, her smile becoming slightly strained.

“Well... I hope you like it here.”

“I hope so too.”

Adaline sighed with resignation before glancing at the child.

“This is Raven,” she explained. “The reason we can live here.”

“How?”

Adaline smiled and lifted Raven into her arms. Raven wrapped her normal arm around Adaline’s neck while lightly fluttering her wing. Adaline kissed her cheek.

“Please remember I’m human. My neck breaks if you squeeze too hard.”

“It’s affection,” Raven replied.

“She’s the core.”

“Core?” Mai asked.

“The island’s core.”

“That’s me,” Raven explained proudly. “Otherwise, the animals go ‘Grrr,’ and if I left, everything would shake and sink back into the sea.”

Mai stared at the little girl once more.

For a moment, she struggled to process it.

This child... stabilized an evanescent island?

Before she could ask anything further, another voice interrupted.

“Raven. Where have you wandered off to, you little brat?!”

The tone was calm, but firm.

Raven’s reaction was immediate. Her eyes lit up as she slipped from Adaline’s arms and ran forward.

“Papa!”

Mai raised her eyebrows slightly.

The man approaching looked, at first glance, far too young to be called that.

Silver hair. Small frame. Youthful appearance.

But something about his presence erased any superficial impression.

His gaze was razor sharp.

Mai knew exactly who he was, despite never having spoken to him before.

Gehirn Schmidt. The man behind ZAIEN.

Gehirn effortlessly caught Raven as she launched herself toward him, holding her with complete familiarity.

“I told you not to disappear without warning,” he said seriously.

“But I found someone new,” Raven replied playfully.

Gehirn let out a soft sigh before turning his attention toward Mai. He studied her for several seconds before nodding with a small smile.

“Mai Izumi, I presume.”

Mai immediately straightened herself.

So this was Gehirn.

The true owner of ZAIEN. One of the most influential men on Earth. The same name that had survived the collapse of entire corporations, supported Whisper... and was now, in all likelihood, deeply involved in Nevermore. She had already wondered who was financing all of this.

And he looked like a teenager.

“Yes. A pleasure,” Mai replied cautiously, greeting him politely.

Her thoughts raced.

This boy... controls one of the largest corporations in the world?

And he’s personally here?

Is he really her father?

That implied far more than it appeared.

Gehirn nodded courteously while Raven waved her wing at two dragonflies perched on her head.

“Gehirn Schmidt. Welcome to Siren Island,” he said, narrowing his eyes slightly as he examined her. “I hope your trip was pleasant. We’ll have time to talk later, but first, you should settle in. To be honest, I’ve heard your name a few times thanks to Whisper.”

“I see,” Mai replied, trying not to appear overly curious about Raven and Gehirn. With his silver-white hair and Raven’s dark features, they seemed like complete opposites.

Were they really father and daughter?

And Gehirn... he didn’t seem like a fey, but given the legends surrounding him for decades, perhaps he was something else entirely. A mage? An alchemist?

“Well then, we should go have breakfast. I won’t be here many more days, so you’d better make the most of me,” Gehirn said teasingly to Raven.

Raven puffed out her cheeks and pinched his face while fluttering her wing.

“You always leave too fast.”

“That’s what happens when you’re a busy adult.”

He then turned back toward Mai.

“Miss Izumi, I hope to see you around. I’m glad you joined us.”

Mai smiled and nodded.

What else could she even say?

Truthfully, he unsettled her. She hadn’t expected to encounter someone so important immediately after stepping off the ship.

Adaline smiled at Mai, and the trio began walking away.

“I’ll come play later!” Raven shouted back.

Mai doubted she would have time for games, but she had to admit... Raven was adorable.

Mai waved goodbye, then watched them silently before continuing on her path.

There were many questions Mai wanted to ask in that moment, but she knew it wasn’t the right time. Later, she would discover the connections between them all. Later, she would understand what it truly meant to be part of Nevermore.

For now, the most important thing was to acclimate to the island.

After checking in and being assigned a room, she decided to explore a bit. She planned to talk to the people who had recruited her later, rather than right away. And since domestic staff had been sent to prepare what would become her home, she had a few hours to explore the place a bit, and a fey served as her guide around the island.

Most of the people spoke English, but also Spanish, French, Portuguese, and other languages. Mai had to get used to switching languages constantly.

Siren Island was colossal. From north to south, it stretched over a hundred kilometers—a vast expanse of land surrounded by smaller islands and largely unexplored territory. The nature of an evanescent island made its geography unstable: the land seemed to expand as it was explored, as if each expedition revealed a new fragment of its existence, emerging from the void.

The fauna of Siren was as diverse as it was impossible. In its plains, wild horses ran in herds, while in the more rugged areas, cougars lurked in the shadows of cliffs. Yet this was only the surface. In certain sectors of the island, creatures extinct for hundreds of millions of years roamed as if they had never vanished. There were birds with opalescent feathers reflecting light like liquid mirrors, winged snakes gliding through the trees, and even a few dinosaurs in the deepest parts of the jungle, along with some beasts said to exist only in myths.

It was a place where reality and fantasy blended seamlessly, with no clear distinction between the two.

The Nevermore Initiative had established itself in the northern part of the island. There were no luxuries, but the infrastructure was functional. They had only been building for a short time, yet what they had accomplished was impressive. Across several kilometers, they had erected bases, checkpoints, airports, barracks, recreational areas, and dining halls. This wasn’t just a research station: it was a settlement, a growing community.

The first days were strange for Mai. Although her experience in similar organizations gave her some advantage, she felt out of place. Many of the people there already knew each other, coming from other projects, clandestine groups, or high-secret expeditions. Others, like her, were new to all of this.

The staff was a mix of all kinds of individuals: feys, espers, humans, scientists, and specialists, each with an assigned role. The organization was divided into multiple areas: logistics, security, exploration, research, technological development, and more. Some worked in mobile laboratories, others on patrols exploring the still-unknown terrain of the island.

What was clear was that this had cost a fortune. It wasn’t just a secret base on a lost island, but a project with a long-term vision. Siren wasn’t an improvised refuge: it was a stronghold preparing for something in the future.

And of course, those who would live there had to be as unique as the island itself.

After meeting with Rein later, who told her some details about the place and what was expected of her, she was led to the residential area. There were already houses there—small, but houses nonetheless.

Four days had already passed since her arrival.

That afternoon Mai woke up with a refreshing feeling. The air in the room was cool, sunlight poured through the window, and her body felt relaxed after several days of adaptation. She stretched with a long sigh, arms raised above her head, savoring the moment. The place was truly immense, she had barely explored a small portion of the terrain, yet already felt as if she had seen enough for a year.

Raven, on the other hand, had been there a few times over the next few days. She really was the island’s darling. There were only four other children, so at least she wasn’t alone in that regard. Even so, the games had left her exhausted. Apparently, the kids picked someone each day to follow around and make their work harder. Since she was still getting used to things, she was the new kid and a target of interest for them.

Even so, Mai hoped she might be able to relax for at least a few hours.

Perhaps she could take the day off to go to the beach. How long had it been since she wore a bikini? They said there was a community of mermaids. Maybe she could meet them.

Then, she felt something strange.

There was a shadow in the room.

She frowned and squinted, still a little drowsy. Against the sunlight streaming through the window, a figure was outlined with a strangely familiar silhouette.

A monkey? A chimpanzee on her window?

She jumped out of bed, fully alert. Her instincts drove her to reach for her weapon in case of an attack, but as her vision focused, her brain processed the image correctly.

It wasn’t a monkey.

It was a completely naked fey woman—except for her panties—poised as if to jump into the room.

Mai blinked several times, unable to believe what she was seeing. The newcomer blinked back. There was a moment of awkward silence before Mai reacted again, reaching for her weapon on the bedside table.

Not a monkey. A pervert, maybe?

“Wait, wait, wait!” The girl raised her hands in surrender, stepping back. “Don’t shoot!Don’t shoot!”

Mai kept her gaze fixed on the intruder, still with her heart racing.

“Why are you naked in my room?”

The woman made a guilty expression and scratched her head. Short messy hair, brown eyes. “Mmm… I already suspected this wasn’t my room.”

Mai narrowed her eyes, her brain trying to process what was happening.

A pervert… drunk? At this hour?

“Seriously?”

“Yes.”

“You enter your room through the window?”

“Yes.”

Silence.

Mai exhaled, trying not to lose patience. “Tell me this is at least the first time this has happened to you.”

The girl smiled with unsettling nonchalance. “Eh… let’s say it’s the first time I’ve picked the wrong room.”

Mai massaged her temple with two fingers. “I don’t know what bothers me more: that you’re naked, that you come through windows, or that I woke up thinking there was a monkey in my room.”

The girl laughed, as if it were the most normal thing in the world. “Well, technically, I have primate-type fey ancestry, so… you’re not entirely wrong.”

Mai blinked, incredulous. “Is that supposed to make me feel better?”

The intruder smiled, bowing theatrically. “Sorry for the intrusion. I’m Ishijima Kanade, but everyone calls me Oxy.”

Mai sighed. “Oxy, like oxygen?”

“Yes, because I’m everywhere, bitch!”

Mai rubbed her eyes with both hands. Why did she always end up meeting people like this? On top of that, Oxy’s breasts were enormous and distracting.

“No! Pervert!” Oxy said in a suggestive voice, covering them with one hand while making a fake scared face.

“Who’s the pervert here?! I don’t want to hear that from someone who climbs naked into someone else’s room.”

“…and through the window,” Oxy finished the sentence, abandoning modesty, hands on her hips and chest proudly out.

“That wasn’t a compliment!”

And that's how Mai met Oxy.

Over the following days, Mai discovered that Oxy had absolutely no shame. She had arrived on the island a few days earlier and seemed to have a very flexible relationship with clothing. It wasn’t that she wandered completely naked all the time, but she didn’t seem to care much if someone saw her in her underwear. Mai was used to mixed baths and being naked in the showers back at Wishper bases, but this woman’s naturism was something else entirely.

Despite the chaotic first impression, Mai and Oxy quickly became friends.

Oxy came and went from the island frequently, as her work didn’t tie her to a single place. But whenever she was on Siren, she would appear at Mai’s window, even though Mai had told her over and over to use the door like a normal person.

What surprised Mai most was discovering that Oxy, that Oxy, was the author of the “Lost Children” theory.

She remembered that report from the first time. She had learned about it through Miho, and although she had never delved deeply into it, she knew that the experiment with the control group had taken more than three decades.

Mai wanted to tell her about her own memories related to that theory, but… she knew she couldn’t. There were things she could never share, no matter how much she wished to.

The days on Siren Island passed quickly. Between explorations, training, and occasional encounters with Raven running away of Adaline, or Oxy climbing through the window, Mai slowly acclimated to her surroundings.

But it wasn’t until Rein returned days later that she began to feel she truly understood the structure of the Nevermore Initiative.

This time, Rein didn’t come alone.

***

Mai was in the dining hall when she saw her enter, accompanied by a girl with light purple hair, slightly taller than Rein.

“Mai, I want you to meet Noki, my younger sister.”

The girl gave her a confident smile. “Hey, I guess if you’re here, you passed the patience test with Oxy. You are her neighbor.”

Mai raised an eyebrow. “That was a test?”

Rein sighed. “More or less. Not many survive the psychological impact of a first impression with her.”

Mai smiled nervously before looking more closely at Noki. She had a strangely electric presence, as if the air around her was charged with static. It wasn’t visible to the naked eye, but Mai could feel it in her own antennas and hair. Something similar with Rein.

“You look like you want to ask something.”

Mai nodded. “You don’t have the same face, the same age, or the same hair color, and you don’t look alike at all. How are you two are sisters?”

Rein gave her a look that said, “Well… we’re not blood-related sisters, but we were adopted by the same fey. He rescued us and raised us together. We also have two mothers.”

Mai blinked. “So you have a father, two mothers, and grew up together as sisters.”

“Exactly.”

“…sounds chaotic.”

Noki crossed her arms proudly. “Welcome to our family.”

Mai knew that this kind of bond was common among feys. When they couldn’t form biological families, they created their own along the way. Just like Miho had been a sister to her. And in truth, Rein and Noki didn’t seem any different from real sisters.

What did surprise her was Noki’s ability.

She was fast. Ridiculously fast.

The first time Mai saw her in action during training in the running tracks, she could barely keep up with her movements while Rein looked.

“So, you two…” Mai looked at them. “Team Tempest? Now I get the name.”

Rein smiled. “For my ability to manipulate the weather.”

“And for my speed and kinetics.” Noki snapped her fingers, and a small spark jumped between them.

Mai nodded, processing the information. “Interesting.”

“Want to see how fast you are against me?” Noki asked with a challenging smile.

Mai stared at her. “I like my organs in place, thanks.”

Noki let out a laugh and spoke in Japanese. “Big sister, I like this girl.”

“I told you, little sister.”

Why Japanese all of a sudden?

Before the conversation could continue, a new figure walked in.

Tall, with blond hair that reached all the way down to his ankles, dressed in sport clothes, and wearing the expression of someone who had seen far too much in life and was already tired of all of it. Looked really different from when she first meet him.

Leon Mustang.

“Leo-pon,” Rein said, pointing at him.

“Osu!” The man looked up and grunted in recognition. Then he saw Mai. “Hey! I’m glad you decided to join.”

“At least for a while…”

“Sounds good enough.”

Mai greeted him and looked him up and down. “So are you the babysitter, the supervisor, or the grumpy grandfather of the group?”

Leon stared at her in silence for one long second. Then he smiled faintly. “I like you, little moth.”

Mai noticed he had a kind of charismatic air about him, but also something cranky—like a grandfather trapped in the body of a twenty-something.

“Same, Mr Mustang.”

“Leon is ok.”

“Leon changes his last name every few years,” Noki explained. “Before ‘Mustang,’ he used ‘Jarhead.’”

Mai stared at him. “…Jarhead?”

Leon sighed. “It was a lapse in judgment.”

“A fey with blond hair down to his ankles trying to go unnoticed with the last name ‘Jarhead’?”

“Yes.”

Mai blinked. “And why did you use it?”

“I like blue.”

Silence.

Mai closed her eyes for a second, processing the level of absurdity behind that reasoning.“…That makes no sense.”

“Neither does the world,” Leon muttered.

“Welcome to Nevermore,” Noki said with an amused smile.

I think I want to leave, Mai thought.

Later, Mai would learn that Leon was one of the oldest fey in Nevermore, and despite his somewhat grumpy or sometimes carefree attitude, he had been everything in his long life.

From gangster, crocodile hunter, chef, private investigator, soldier, bodyguard, troublemaker, doctor, and one of the greatest experts in fey medicine, he was now also an agent and one of the leaders of Nevermore.

It wasn't as if Mai could be too surprised. She already had a long resume of professions thanks to Miho and Whisper and as a private investigator.

***

Mai soon discovered that the Nevermore Initiative wasn’t just a paramilitary organization or a high-level research group. It was a diverse network of specialists from all over the world, each with their own field of expertise, unique abilities, and, in a lot cases, a past full of stories one knew better than to ask about directly.

Beyond the oddities, she found herself getting along with many of them—in the dining hall, in training sessions, or during the expeditions conducted on the island. Between laughter, heated debates about scientific theories, and ridiculous bets about who could run faster between Noki and Oxy (spoiler: no one could beat either of them), Mai gradually blended into the atmosphere.

The Initiative had a scientific and medical wing, staffed by feys and humans alike—espers and magicians working side by side. Some were there out of vocation, others because it allowed them to research without restrictions, and others simply because they no longer fit into the outside world.

But what caught Mai’s attention the most was the department dedicated to Dark Events and Antiquity.

The team was a mixture of archaeologists, physicists, linguists, magicians, and specialists in cults and forbidden sects, all united by the desire to uncover the truth behind the anomalous events scattered throughout the planet history.

Among them was Naomi, an archaeologist specializing in North African cultures, particularly Egypt and its yet-unresolved mysteries.

There was also Jonathan Hughes, an archaeologist from Miskatonic University—a man who, according to rumors, had seen enough to consider early retirement more times than he could count.Always with a coffee in hand and dark circles under his eyes that suggested sleep was optional in his line of work.

But they were only two among many. Physicists, linguists, chaos theorists, magicians specialized in forbidden cults… all formed part of the Dark Events and Antiquity research team.

Mai quickly learned that several groups were already deployed around the world, investigating ancient anomalies and searching for clues in civilizations that had vanished long before modern history acknowledged them.

Lemuria, the ruins of Thuria in Europe, and Hyboria—discovered in several locations—were of great interest. And as far as she knew, places like these had been found since the past century, but global conflicts had halted all research. Now that things were relatively calmer in certain regions, many wanted those investigations to resume. Who knew if the key to solving current problems didn’t lie in the past? If so many civilizations had disappeared, what had happened to them? Dark Events? Cataclysms before the Ice Age?

Ruins that resonated with an ancient weight, places wrapped in myth yet apparently containing traces of Dark Events.

It was clear that Nevermore wasn’t only interested in the present and the future, but also in the remnants of the past that might explain what was yet to come.

The research teams in Europe were struggling to operate due to recent conflicts, but they were doing everything they could to carry out their tasks. They weren’t just documenting anomalies and unexplainable events—they were also recovering ancient artifacts and records before they could be lost forever. Some of these discoveries were sent to Siren Island, where they could be analyzed in depth, far from prying eyes and governments that preferred certain truths remain hidden.

Mai wasn’t an archaeologist, but she understood the gravity of what was being investigated.

The fact that entire continents had vanished millions of years ago, leaving virtually almost no trace, was a silent warning that Dark Events might have been behind those cataclysms.

And the most unsettling part was that Siren Island itself was part of that mystery.

Its legend was said to date back to the time of Ptolemy, though it had been known by different names throughout history. Its location was an enigma, shifting across eras as if defying the laws of the physical world.

Some theories suggested that the evanescent island had, at some point, been part of Atlantis—not just thousands of years ago, but hundreds of thousands or even millions years ago due the extinct especies found alive there. If that were true, what kind of events could have caused the destruction of entire civilizations and the disappearance of lands that once existed?

And then there was another legend, more recent but equally unsettling.

It was said that the old generation of feys had fled in the 19th century, warning before their disappearance that a terrible era was approaching.

What had they seen?

Were they referring to the Dark Events?

Could the same thing happen again, wiping out civilization as it had in the past?

Mai had no answers, and for now, she couldn’t afford to get lost in theories and unresolved questions.

First of all, she had to find her place in that organization.
