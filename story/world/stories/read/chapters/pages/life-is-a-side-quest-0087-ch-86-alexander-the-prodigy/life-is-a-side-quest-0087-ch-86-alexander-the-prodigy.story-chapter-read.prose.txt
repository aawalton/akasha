“Fire, flame, dancing light. You bring our glade into a fright. Fleet of foot does find a foe, to catch and carry, bury below.” The voices sang together before splitting into cackles that danced through the trees.

“Everyone remember the plan for encounters?” Brian asked.

Everyone nodded as Johnny moved out in front of the group toward the sounds of the voices. Brian moved up behind him and pulled out what seemed to be a small mechanical crossbow.

“Shit. I already used two fireball spells. I only have one left,” Cassidy said, a nervous note in her voice.

“Don’t you have any smaller spells?” Judah asked.

She turned and glared at Alexander. “Firebolt. A weakling’s spell.”

Alexander was unprovoked by her taunts. He had learned long ago that some people saw everything as a competition, and it helped them achieve more in life. So it was likely good for her to feel she had competition, no matter how unfounded it was. From his first impressions of her, their goals and motivations were worlds apart.

Alexander calmly walked over to Judah and took out a little pin from his tackle box.

“Place this on your shirt,” he told Judah.

“Is this the shield from Zelda?”

“All I know is that it’s a shield pin. I’m not sure from what genre. It worked well for enchanting.”

Judah looked like he had a thousand questions but decided to pin the shield on his shirt quickly, a small smile forming on his face.

“I have a magic Zelda shield,” he chuckled. “My followers are going to have a fit.”

Johnny and his beagle stopped and cupped their ears. Alexander listened closely to the wind. It started to hum. The hum shifted into a buzz. He saw dark shapes flit through the air from the opposite side of the glade, but they were difficult to make out.

“How are we going to be able to see them? It’s too dark to make them out,” Judah asked, pulling out a martial arts staff.

“Hey, support, why not use your puny fire spells to help support us? You have enough meager skill to light some little fires for me,” Cassidy asked with an amused tone.

It was a reasonable suggestion despite the spirit in which it was asked. Rather than using illusion, he decided self-fueled fires would be more efficient. Alexander decided to try modifying Firebolt with his will, as it was by far one of his simpler spells. As he thought of the effect he wanted, his mind entered an abstract state. It visualized the flows of energy that made up the Firebolt spell like colorful strings floating in the air.

He thought of the magnesium tips on the ends of tracer rounds used in aerial combat. When embedded into something, they could burn hot and bright in a concentrated area. If he placed them intermittently, it would provide the light they needed in the glade—something the standard Firebolt spell wasn’t normally capable of. He was forced to expend additional magic points for the added effect and formed five of them in his mind. He targeted five trees equidistant from the center. As everything overlapped and clicked in his head, he unleashed the spell.

“Piercing Firebolts!”

A glow emanated from each of his fingers and shot out in all directions. Where the earlier bolts had been about the size of a fist, each of these was about the size of a watermelon. They hit the trees one after another, burying themselves into the trunks and emitting a fair glow that lit up the glade. Alexander felt a slight exhaustion from the effort, but he was exceedingly satisfied with the results.

You have gained a level in the skill Spell-casting! Spell-casting level 8. You have 1 point to distribute to your Spell-casting skill tree.

Alexander checked his magic points and noted that it took about one-tenth of his mana to pull that off. The first casting of a new spell, he had long noted, often took about 20% more to cast. He hypothesized that there was some waste as he found the balance of the spell.

Cassidy turned around to look back at him, her eyes wide. “What the hell WAS THAT!?”

A pained shriek came from the direction of the twisted voices, redirecting everyone’s attention. Angry buzzing began to fill the other side of the glade and small shadows floated on the edge of the woods. Then, as one, they rushed the party.

“Incoming!” Brian shouted as several dozen creatures flew in, darting erratically through the air.

The first to be attacked were the beagle and Johnny. Johnny had a compound hunting bow but had quickly put it away for a machete. The speed of the creatures was going to be too fast for careful aiming.

Several of them grabbed hold of the beagle and started lifting him into the air. Now that they were latched onto the dog, Alexander could make out little gray-skinned humanoid shapes with sharp teeth and large white eyes. Johnny managed to hit one off the beagle, causing a deep wound that smoked across its chest.

Brian didn’t even bother with his crossbow. He took out a knife and started swinging at the closest creature. The beagle yelped as the creatures began biting him.

“Those are likely pixies,” Judah observed, his voice shaking with a hint of nervousness.

“And!?” Cassidy exclaimed.

“They are fiercely protective of their trees. Setting them on fire will likely draw more of them,” Judah squeaked out quickly.

“Thank you for that timely warning!” Cassidy retorted.

Johnny jumped backward as more pixies began to overwhelm him. He looked at his dog and yelled, “Bubba! Jumbo size!”

The pixies had the beagle about six feet in the air when they suddenly found themselves pulled back down to the earth as the beagle began to grow. In a short time, the beagle touched the ground, and the pixies were moving upwards again—not because of their flight. The beagle had grown to the size of a black bear. He shook his body, throwing the pixies off, and barked. The sound cracked the air, startling them and sending the swarm of pixies scattering in all directions.

“Let ’em have it!” Judah called, finding some confidence as they came swarming at the group.

Cassidy waited until the last moment as the pixies bunched a bit closer together before targeting them.

“Fireball!” she shouted. This time, Alexander felt his eyebrows get singed a bit and was certain his skin would have a flash burn.

She managed to take out about six of the little guys. Alexander used Shield at an upward angle on himself and Linda. As pixies darted down to attack them, they’d bounce off the shield, leaving an easier target for Alexander’s webs or be forced to go in front of the clear firing lanes, allowing them to more easily pick them off.

“Webs,” Alexander called as he saw two pixies bunched near each other. The web proved immensely effective against them; the spread of the attack was difficult for them to avoid. Cassidy started firing firebolts but slowly, taking aim before each shot. She must not have had many mana points left.

The swarm of pixies seemed to have gotten over its shock and started attacking the giant beagle again. It was bigger and likely taking less damage because of its size, but it was still too slow to hit the agile pixies. Johnny started running over to assist his dog. As he sprinted, he fell forward and caught himself with his paws… he shape-shifted into a cougar and leapt onto the beagle’s tall back with ease, swatting a pixie out of the air. The rest of the pixies darted away quickly and did not seem interested in resuming the attack.

“Curses upon you, burn in your soul. For making our forest become less than whole!” the pixies shrieked as they chanted together. It was an odd experience that seemed like something out of a horror movie. “The Queen shall hear and her shadows too; they shall bring their justice to crash upon you.” The last of the pixies limped away in the air, following the others into the forest from the direction they had entered. Everyone quickly moved together into a group.

The cougar jumped off the back of the beagle, which was shrinking down to its original size. Johnny’s form warped out of the cougar, and he stood, dusting himself off.

“Well now, that was somethin’ all kinds of different,” Johnny laughed, ignoring the cuts on his face and arms.

Everyone seemed to have differing reactions to the end of the skirmish.

Judah was videoing Johnny and Bubba before getting on his knees to take pictures of a webbed pixie with his cell phone.

Brian started trying to snap Linda out of her terrified stupor, coaxing her to heal Johnny and Bubba’s injuries.

Cassidy stared angrily at the battlefield like it had personally insulted her. The dead pixies torn apart by Johnny, the half dozen webbed up by Alexander, then at the five trees burning brightly by what was supposed to be a level 1 beginner’s spell. All done by that dorky man with the horrible hat and strap-on fashion tragedy.

Judah poked the pixie with the bottom of his staff. It began to struggle feebly. “What do we do with these guys? Hostages?”

“I don’t see how we transport them safely,” Brian shared.

“Let’s leave ‘em be,” Johnny suggested. “They just wanted to protect their habitat, so nothin’ personal.” He looked down at the pixie and made a thoughtful face.

“You’re not a ranger. You’re a druid, aren’t you!” Judah shouted excitedly, snapping out of his semi-petrified state.

“Ah, well, I guess I lost my mysterious vibe that I was going for,” Johnny stroked his beard with a small grin.

“Well, you are not exactly the mysterious archetype,” Judah shrugged.

Cassidy looked disgusted as she muttered, “Unbelievable.”

Brian was rolling up his sleeves to reveal his cuts as he said, “All things considered, that went pretty well. Good job everyone! Linda, if you could patch us up? I would appreciate it. Then let’s figure out how to get out of here. Quietly.”
