Samar Patag looked so very different under a midday sun. All the demon realm did, what little of it Vir had seen since his return. He thought he’d been prepared for that, but the reality still surprised him.

Vir knew how Samar Patag looked in every conceivable weather condition, yet somehow, seeing the city under a bright blue sky with the sun shining high overhead made it feel suddenly alien. Or, more accurately, like the human realm.

He pushed aside those feelings, however, as he walked with Siya through the main boulevard. He let her set the pace, which was a fair bit slower than his, especially since she seemed to want to stop every third step to look at seemingly everyone and everything.

Vir glanced at their Asuran guard detail while he waited for her to closely inspect the stonework of some merchant’s shop.

They followed at a respectable distance, pretending to look like ordinary demons going about their business, and they did a fine job of it. Vir wouldn’t have known they were there at all were it not for their prana signatures.

They were Cirayus’ consolation prize. The giant had wanted to accompany his adopted goddaughter on this little excursion, but his duties kept him away. Something he had fought tooth and nail to wiggle out of, requiring convincing from everyone from Greesha to Vir.

“This was an impoverished land not long ago,” Vir said, catching the girl staring at a perfectly green, manicured shrub that lined the street. “All of the demon realm was, with only the land near the Boundary being any better. Prana was scarcer here than anywhere else, and with so little to nourish them, many demons stalled in their training, unable to cultivate prana at all.”

“Your Thaumaturge,” Siya said. “He fixed that with his invention, yes?”

Vir nodded, chuckling once again at her astuteness. She seemed to pick things up almost by osmosis, and he sometimes wondered if she could read minds. “It did indeed.”

He gestured at the buildings around them and at the drainage grates that led to channels under the street, as well as the myriad of storefronts with their arrays of magical goods displayed in the windows.

“His inventions have brought prosperity this realm would have thought impossible just years prior. Problems continued to exist, yes, but for a time, things were genuinely promising.”

Siya looked at a group of demon children chasing each other around a fountain. But just as Vir was about to ask her if she wanted to join them, she broke off her gaze and looked up into his eyes. “Then the Ash Tears began, yes?”

“Indeed. What should have been the start of an era of prosperity quickly turned into a living nightmare. I dare not imagine how much worse we would have been without Saunak’s inventions. The granaries were full and everyone was accustomed to the elevated prana, which undoubtedly saved many lives.”

She was quiet for a long moment. “I see hope in their eyes,” she said. “Even now, after all the trauma they’ve been through. Buildings can always be reconstructed. People are not so easy…”

“It’s absolutely true,” he said, staring at the remnant of a Tear that had ravaged this very street just last week. The area had been cordoned off, and it brought him great joy to see demons already working to remove the barrier. He prayed there never would be a need for those again.

Siya continued to pepper him with questions as they walked. About the clans and how they governed themselves. And about Saunak, whom she seemed particularly fascinated by, likely because he understood Imperium magic better than anyone alive. Perhaps she felt a kindred spirit in him.

She especially wished to visit his tower one day.

As for the immediate revulsion that filled his mind at the thought of Saunak getting anywhere close to Siya… Vir had to admit the ancient demon had grown quite a bit softer around the edges these days. To the point where his title, the ‘Mad Thaumaturge,’ had turned almost benign. At least, among the younger demons who hadn’t lived through the atrocities he had once committed. The older ones certainly hadn’t forgotten, let alone forgiven him.

Even still, given the old demon’s adoration for all things Imperium, Vir suspected a conversation with Siya would be the most productive he’d ever had. Assuming, of course, Saunak didn’t simply throw himself at her feet and worship her outright—something Vir could certainly see him doing.

Siya’s questioning barrage was unrelenting, from how the food distribution network functioned across the demon lands, to whether the new prana levels would affect crop yields and what the relative standing was between demon healers and human ones.

Each was as eclectic as they were insightful, and Vir answered what he could, advising her to speak to others when he didn’t know the answer, which happened more than he’d expected. She was, if nothing else, thorough.

Yet where others might’ve lost their patience, Vir could only smile warmly, happy that she’d taken such a keen interest in things.

It was in the middle of one of her more difficult questions that his communications orb pulsed, Maiya’s face sparing him for the time being.

“Hey, you,” she said. “How’s she settling in?”

“Considering she just asked me three questions about agricultural prana distribution that I couldn’t answer?” Vir said, winking at the girl who stared up at him with wide eyes. “Quite well.”

“Honestly,” Maiya chuckled. “I love her more every time I hear about her.”

That got Siya blushing, though she did manage to lean toward the orb and say, “I like you too, Rajni.”

“Maiya,” came her firm reply, though Vir didn’t miss the amused smile on her face. “Just Maiya. How are you finding Samar Patag?”

“Strange,” Siya said. “I like it. How far away are you?”

“A day’s travel by fast airship, I think,” Maiya said. “But no one really knows until we launch more charting expeditions. The Ashen lands are vast, but nothing like what they were, what with the Ash folding endlessly upon itself.”

To think weeks of travel had reduced to a single day simply because the Ash Tears were no longer there to force detours—no longer making entire corridors impassable for months at a time. It truly was miraculous.

“How are things on your end?” Vir asked.

“Settling down, for now,” Maiya said. “I’d always yearned for normalcy, but now that it’s here, it’s kinda hard to believe. I think people are still busy digesting everything. The Tears being gone is the biggest thing on everyone’s mind, of course. Guessing it’s the same over there. The new prana levels and the economic and political implications aren’t even on anyone’s radar yet. I think most of them are just happy to get their first proper night’s rest in years. There’s… a lot of mourning, as you might expect. People finally have the time to grieve.”

“As it should be,” he said. “Any longer and our people would have buckled under the strain. It’s a wonder and a testament to their character that they lasted this long against such a mortal threat.”

“Really does feel like there’s hope in the air again,” Maiya replied.

Her situation was mirrored almost exactly in the demon realm, which just went to show just how similar the two races truly were.

“Say, Siya?” Maiya asked through the orb. “Do you like sweets?”

Despite Siya being the most mature girl Vir had ever seen by far, it seemed that every girl was weak to the prospect of sweet things, because her eyes bulged and her expression turned into one of pure excitement before she wrangled herself under control, coughing awkwardly.

“I am not interested in such things,” she declared, frowning as though the idea was entirely beneath her.

Maiya outright grinned at her reaction. “Well, guess we know where we’re headed next!”

“You should be more honest with your desires, Siya,” Vir said with a chuckle. “Now, I hear the vendors are giving out free treats in celebration of our victory. Wouldn’t it be a shame not to take them up on that?” he asked, winking at the girl.

He omitted that he’d have to practically fight people to get them to receive payment from the Akh Nara, and who wouldn’t give out treats to a cute little girl? Never mind her status as a living goddess to the realm.

Siya’s grip on his finger tightened, and he could practically hear the “Can we!?” written all over her face.

Vir proceeded to take Siya to several food vendors, and in the face of Vir absolutely spoiling her, whatever resistance Siya had melted almost immediately.

Not just that, but every sweet she added to the collection had her coming out of her aloof shell and acting more like a girl her age ought to.

Said collection of baked pastries, fried sweets, and street food quickly grew too much for her hands to hold, but just when Vir was about to offer her help, having judged her too proud to ask for it, she solved that particular problem by simply levitating them around her, reaching for the next one when she was done licking her fingers clean of her current treat.

Ordinarily, Vir would have absolutely forbidden a child from gorging themselves so, but she had the same pranites that coursed through his body, in addition to a slew of other aids that made it quite literally impossible for her to fall ill.

Something Maiya envied very much. One look at her face through the crystal told him just how much she envied the girl. Though she had the same pranites in her body now, they did not stop her from gaining weight, as Vir knew from his own experience.

They went around visiting what felt to Vir like every stall in the city, though the tour did serve as an excellent excuse for Vir to show his face and greet people. It was not lost on him that were he alone, he would have received a far more sterile, distant treatment despite the love everyone had for him.

As much as Siya was a goddess, she was still a child, and children were absolute masters at disarming anyone she met. Rather than show their adoration with prostration, people tried to shower her with candies and gifts.

Which, Vir noted, she seemed to thoroughly enjoy.

At long last, once Siya had her fill, Vir brought her to the castle in the late afternoon, walking her through the main halls and up through the staff residence levels until they reached the royal wing. He opened the doors to the bedchambers and stood back to let her take it in.

“I must apologize,” he said. “It’s probably nothing compared to what you’ve enjoyed. As far as we have come recently, this civilization must seem tragically primitive to your people.”

She gave him a thoughtful look before nodding.

“It is primitive,” she said. “There are many things I had in the Imperium that I lack now. The Vidya network alone contained more knowledge than every mind your entire civilization has produced in its history combined.”

She looked around the room again. “But that has no bearing on my happiness. You have seen my father’s home, yes? You know how we lived. Daddy never liked the luxuries so many others drowned in. This is far more lavish than anything I have ever known.” She looked up at him. “Thank you. Both you and Maiya. I know the cost of what you did to give me this life. I know how hard you are trying to make me feel welcome. I… I appreciate it.”

Vir crouched down so their eyes were level.

“Janak would be so proud of you, Siya,” he said with the most heartfelt smile he had ever given.

“Heck, we’re proud of you and we’ve only just met!” Maiya said over the orb.

Siya nodded bashfully before turning back toward the room to hide her quickly reddening cheeks.

“You were going to show me my bedchambers?” she said in a voice that was just a bit too high.

“Of course,” Vir replied, chuckling. “Your room is right next to ours.” He straightened up and was about to lead the way when Maiya spoke through the orb.

By the look on her face, he knew something was wrong even before he’d heard her words.

“Vir, I’ve just received a report.”

He stopped walking, bringing the orb up to his face.

“Vimana Raaka,” Maiya said. “In Matali. It’s moved. It’s fully active and it’s headed for the demon lands.”

“What’s going on?” Vir asked, feeling a knot tighten in his stomach. “How can it be moving?”

When he’d transplanted the Vimana to the new realm, Vir had been sure they would remain under his control. Bequeathed to him by Janak.

“I don’t know. I’m getting reports that a Matali military skyship was seen entering the Vimana, which makes no sense to me. It began moving shortly after that.”

Vir cursed under his breath. Odd coincidences. One-in-a-million chances that just happened to align… the telltale signs were all there. But how? And this quickly? Their enemy gained power through time. For them to be acting this soon…

They’re desperate, he thought. They must be, in the face of what I’ve done. What no other universe ever has.

“I think we both need to get there immediately,” Maiya said.

“I think you’re right,” Vir replied.
