After ensuring everyone was safely hidden around a corner, I [Remote Launched] a siege bolt core at the door before ducking behind the wall myself and covering my ears.

The projectile flew in an arc toward the massive vault door and detonated with its signature deafening screech that rattled my skull, its sonic waves ripping apart the metal door.

Or at least, that’s what should’ve happened.

The only difference we spotted, once we’d recovered from the sound, was a lone hairline crack that ran a few inches down the door.

“Well shit,” I muttered, already loading another core.

“How are we to get through, then?” Arianna asked in anguish. “Perhaps they did not station guards here when a wall was sufficient to stop us.”

“Ever heard the phrase quantity was a quality all on its own?” I asked, my lips curling up as I ran my hand over the tiny crack. “I’ve got over nine hundred more of where those came from.”

I’ll never forget the look Arianna gave me then. It was a look that was equal parts terror and awe, and even Yashas drew in a surprised breath.

“With that many, we could’ve…” Arianna trailed off, but Yashas shook his head.

“No, bombs such as these are unwieldy weapons. Even with that many, we could not have defeated the enemy’s defenses.”

“Not to mention that’s my lifetime supply,” I said. “Can’t ever go back to the Trial where I got ‘em, now that I’ve ranked up. Besides, they do a great job of turning squishy targets into clouds of gore, but they’re not nearly as effective against machinery. In this case, though, I do think we can overwhelm it with numbers.”

I fired again and again, my rhythm limited by how fast I could activate [Remote Launch] and duck back behind cover, protecting my ears. Each detonation sent shockwaves through the corridor, dust raining from the ceiling, my ears ringing despite the Vigor suit's protection.

Whoever was on the other side of that wall had to be pissing their pants at the show. Or laughing their asses off…

Probably the latter.

Too bad I couldn’t just [Spacefold] through the wall, either. I needed line of sight to use that ability.

The sonic vibrations did work, shaking the molecular structure apart, but it took time. And a lot of bolts.

Slowly but surely, the cracks spread like spiderwebs across the door's surface. Just not fast enough.

Behind us, I could hear distant explosions where Eskil and Ohana were still keeping the compound busy, but that wouldn't last forever.

I gritted my teeth and fired another bolt. Because what the fuck else was I going to do?

My effort paid off, though, because crack number seven finally connected with crack number three, blowing open a cavity just large enough to let me manually wedge a bolt core in, praying all the while it didn’t decide to detonate while I installed them.

It turned out positioning made all the difference in the world, because that single siege bolt blew the whole damned door off its hinges.

The shattered remains of the massive slab crashed inward, almost as loud as the deafening core itself, revealing the chamber beyond.

I was the first into the room, followed by Arianna since we were the sturdiest in the group.

A decision that proved profoundly wise only moments later.

The enclosure was massive, four stories tall at a minimum, which reminded me just how far underground we were.

The shield generator dominated the center—a massive cylindrical structure crackling with blue-white lightning, energy arcing between nodes in patterns that hurt to look at directly. Catwalks crisscrossed the space at multiple levels, but to my dismay, the whole machine was clad in the same unpolished metal the door had been.

No wonder they hadn’t stationed an army here. Why bother, when you could just give it nigh-impenetrable armor? The concept made so much sense, I had to wonder why people didn’t just do that as the default for stuff like nuclear reactors and other targets of opportunity.

Probably because it was too expensive.

Well, the other reason they hadn’t stationed an army was because they didn’t need to. A half-dozen machine gun emplacements barked to life, forcing me to bring out my tower shield and take cover.

But not before I fired my flying blades and the chain whip, sending them rocketing at the gun emplacements.

Bullets would’ve shredded through steel, but thankfully, my armor was made of sturdier stuff.

The Mythril alloy held long enough for my weapons to do their thing.

The flying blades cut the guns in half, while the chain just wrapped itself around the emplacement and minced it into a hundred tiny pieces with its [Buzzsaw] ability.

The barrage finally stopped, and only then did I have the presence of mind to check on Arianna.

Her armor might not have been Mythril, but whatever it was, it was just as sturdy because she shrugged it off.

“I think you can come out now,” I said, motioning for our squishier friends to follow.

No surprises ensued, which was nice. Yashas hadn’t been wrong. There really was nothing living in the large chamber.

No armies or tanks, though I don’t know how they’d have gotten tanks through the hallways down here.

No, there was just one other object, so nondescript I’d missed it earlier for the much larger shield generator.

A black ovoid sat in the middle of the room, almost two stories tall. Tentacles writhed around it, and they reminded me of the same grotesque appendages I'd seen on the brain commanders topside. The surface was glossy, almost like sweaty skin, which for some reason made my skin crawl.

“What in the name of God is that?” Richard breathed.

The pod was already starting to open, segments of its surface peeling back like a blooming flower. Or an egg.

This felt way too much like those sequences before the final boss unveils itself to the hero, and I always got irritated in games where you had to sit there and watch the cutscene instead of pummeling the guy before he was ready to fight.

This, however, wasn’t a game, and so I didn’t.

I started firing.

[Remote Launch] sent siege bolt cores rocketing across the chamber, half toward the shield generator’s armored casing, half toward the pod. There was a limit to how fast I could fire them—not because of my Launch speed—but because any cores caught in the radius of another core would just be destroyed, wasting the core.

Even one at a time, though, the detonations were deafening in the enclosed space, the sonic shockwaves bouncing off the walls and catwalks to create interference patterns that multiplied their volume and made my head swim.

The generator’s armor cracked but it wasn’t down.

So I kept firing.

As for the pod, well whatever inside might not have been alive when Yashas scanned it, but it sure as hell was now, and it screeched an inhuman sound that was audible even through siege bolts' assault on my ears.

The tentacles thrashed frantically, trying to close the opening segments back up, but it was too little too late. My barrage slammed into the thing one after another, punching through the shell and liquifying whatever the hell was inside.

And there was definitely something inside.

A bone-white object fell out, rolling across the floor toward us and for a second I thought it was a weapon.

Then I recognized it for what it was.

A head.

A head I instantly recognized.

I looked at Yashas, only to find his face had gone pale as he stared at a mirror image looking right back, though maybe mirror wasn’t the right word.

The features were identical but the material was wrong, looking like it had been carved from the same stuff as the skeleton soldiers. Its eyes were similarly spheres of bone, without any iris or pupil to speak of.

The pod exploded outward as my continued barrage finally tore it apart, and four figures emerged from the wreckage.

Four bone-white skeletal figures wearing armor identical to ours.

One wore Aerion's distinctive elven plate, lighter than my own because she favored mobility to defensiveness. Another had Arianna's heavy armor and her tower shield, and the third wore elven light armor identical to Richard's.

As for the fourth… Yeah, it was wearing my Vigor suit.

“You don't suppose they've got the same powers as us, do you?” Richard asked weakly.

The clones… or whatever the hell they were… put that question to rest.

The Aerion-clone surged forward with the same dead eyes and predatory grace I'd watched a thousand times, except now it was aimed at us, and holy shit I had no idea it could be so terrifying.

It was a bit like looking at a tsunami or a tornado headed your way. It wasn’t something that could be overcome or thwarted. Just accepted. You just knew you were gonna die, and the thing that was going to kill you just didn’t care one way or another.

Bone-Aerion’s blade immediately went for Yashas, and of course she’d target him, being the most vulnerable target and our lifeline if we wanted any hope of getting out of here alive. It was as calculating as I’d expect under the effects of [Reave].

I was already moving, because while Aerion was giving me a death glare, claiming her double as her own, my Vigor suit's enhanced constitution made me the only one who could afford to take the hit. I jumped between them, using one of my Mythril blades to parry the oncoming attack.

Philip would’ve been proud.

Or at least, he would have until the impact nearly drove me to my knees.

It was in that moment that I knew we were well and truly fucked.

Because as I felt the shock of the blow travel up my arms and into my shoulders, I knew. I knew that despite Aerion’s recent gains, despite knowing these were minute-old clones without an ounce of combat experience, I knew in that instant that these were no mere copies.

It wasn’t just her equal. Its strength, and probably its speed, matched and exceeded Aerion's own.

My Vigor suit's stat boosts were the only reason I wasn’t cut in half. The armor’s condition, however, wasn’t so lucky.

That single strike alone had taken out over 800 points of the suit’s 12,000. Not a large amount, by any means, but I hadn’t encountered anything else capable of even a quarter of that kind of damage.

“Thanks,” Yashas said sheepishly, backing away and bringing his rifle up, though I was pretty sure the shaking muzzle wasn’t on account of his fatigue.

“Don't mention it,” I grunted, bracing for Aerion’s follow-up. “Just doing my—oh fuck!”

Halfway across the room, my own clone's hand came up, and I recognized the motion instantly. How could I not, having done the same thing a thousand times or more?

He was firing something from his inventory. Which meant he not only had an inventory, but that it had something in it he could use at that range to do damage.

That would be one of three things. The bullets, the soulwoven shards, or…

An Aural Siege bolt core suddenly appeared halfway between us, barreling through the air towards us.

My first instinct was a good one, and had my brain had a chance to catch up, I might even have been impressed at my own quick thinking.

I instinctively reached out with my mind to grab it and shove it into my own inventory, my subconscious acting before any conscious thought.

It didn’t work.

Normally, I’d have no trouble snatching an incoming projectile out of the air, but apparently being launched by someone else with my ability—something I never thought I’d ever encounter—seemed to negate mine.

That was interesting because it meant I could use that same strategy against him, but right now, the only thing going through my head was Oh shit.

I needed mobility. Now.

Bone-Aerion was still attacking me, its blade locked against mine, and normally I'd just muscle through with my Vigor suit's raw stats, but I couldn't afford to be slow right now. Not with that siege bolt about to paint the walls with red mist.

That might have been a mortal crisis for anyone else, but this was exactly why I'd built my abilities the way I had. Different suits for different situations. A tank when I needed to absorb damage for Yashas and agility when I needed to save everyone’s collective asses.

I’d never felt this good about my decision on that fateful day when Cosmo summoned me and gave me a list of a million Blessings and no information to act on them.

Because despite the horrible situation, I wasn’t worried.

I didn’t bother shouting. Not like they could react in time, anyway. Instead, I activated [Hot Swap].

The Vigor suit vanished, replaced by my Grace armor in an instant, and whatever loss I felt in vitality was immediately replaced with a sensation of being a god of movement, and holy hell did that feel good. It was a pity I could only use the ability once an hour, or I might’ve switched just to bask in that high.

Unfortunately, I didn’t exactly have the time to bask, because the Aerion-clone that had, until now, just been merely annoying, was suddenly lethal. A single blow would cut in me in half.

But I now had access to an ability I didn’t just moments prior.

Space folded, and Aerion’s clone was a distant memory as I grabbed Yashas and yanked him into my inventory. Arianna was next, who'd been about to face off with her counterpart. I wasn’t exactly sure how two tanks were supposed to hurt each other, and despite wanting nothing more than to watch that probably comedic battle, I pulled her in regardless. Richard disappeared next, and I wished it was his clone I'd managed to kill off with the Siege Bolt.

Because now we all had to worry about dying to sudden and total heart failure, though I hoped our stats at least gave us some resistance against his heart bursting ability.

Aerion was already charging by the time I got to her. Her combat instincts were just better honed than the others’, but her clone moved just as fast.

Then it summoned a spectral Aralez and lunged. The goddamned thing had copied even that.

I [Spacefolded] between them, yanked Aerion into my inventory, before warping out right as I felt a crushing pressure on my heart from Richard's clone.

I warped out.

The siege bolt detonated behind me, and I felt the shockwave despite the distance. My clone had aimed it at where we'd been standing, and if I'd been half a second slower...

No, thinking about stuff like that would just get me killed. And if I died, so would everyone else. Maybe. Probably.

The Aerion-clone's blade came for my throat, and whether it was her boosted stats or bony nature, she did what should have been impossible, and kept up with my speed. Exceeded it, actually. I [Spacefolded] sideways, putting some distance between us, and saw the other clones spreading out with an uncanny level of tactical awareness. They moved like us, fought like us. Hell, they probably thought like us.

Richard's arm extended again, tracking me, and normally I would've counted on my Grace armor to keep him from targeting me, but he was sure to be supercharged just like everyone else.

Arianna’s clone charged forward, shield raised, trying to cut off my escape routes.

And my clone was already loading another siege bolt.

I couldn't fight them. Even if they hadn’t been stronger than the originals, my clone alone carried enough firepower to level this room.

And if I’d learned anything from tv shows and movies where the hero was supposed to fight their evil twin, conventional tactics were guaranteed to fail.

If we wanted to win this, we needed an outside force.

A juggernaut upon whom silly concerns like physics didn’t apply.

We needed Eskil.

Still, our foes had made it abundantly clear they weren’t about to let us escape.

Which was why I had one more thing to do, as much as it pained me.

I pulled a hundred Siege Bolt cores and activated [Remote Launch], firing not at the clones, but the shield generator.

A hundred was overkill, and largely useless as they’d just blow each other up without any effect.

But the enemy didn’t care about that.

The clones reacted instantly. My clone [Spacefolded] toward the reactor, trying to intercept. The Aerion-clone's blade slashed, cutting three cores out of the air a hundred feet away in a move that would’ve been impossible for Aerion. The Arianna-clone threw her shield like a discus, smashing two more.

Seriously? That was just cheating at this point. Why even bother to fashion them in our likeness if they were going to outstrip us in every fucking way?

But I’d anticipated cheat bullshit like that, and there were just too many to stop.

The first core hit the reactor's cracked armor and detonated. Then a second. And by the time the rest got through, the core was already destroyed, the repetitive sonic barrage rendering the marvel of technology into nothing more than a mass of shrieking metal and shattered concrete.

I lingered just long enough to see the reactor's casing fracture and the arcs of blue-white lightning spiral out of control.

Then I [Spacefolded] to the door we’d busted, praying I had enough of a head start to make it.

I was running the moment I reappeared on the other side, my legs pumping as fast as my Grace suit's enhanced agility could manage. Which was to say, really fucking fast.

Behind me, the whole chamber's structural integrity was failing, and the sound of rending metal made me wonder if I’d doomed us all by bringing down the hallway around us too.

I risked a glance back and caught a glimpse through the destroyed doorway—the generator was tilting at an angle it definitely wasn't designed for and blue fluid was gushing out of it like a fountain, our clones desperately trying not to let it touch them.

So there was something even they feared, huh? Good to know, because we definitely hadn’t seen the last of them.

I triggered [Spacefold] again, and when I rematerialized, I was in the middle of blaring alarms and red strobing lighting.

I didn’t care. I just spammed my ability as fast as it would let me, desperate to get away from that deathtrap. Desperate to get topside, out of these caves of steel.

I’d never been more nervous in my life.

We'd accomplished our objective, yes, but whether I'd live long enough to celebrate it was anyone's guess.

More than all that though, the thought of Eskil saving my hide almost made me want to stop and let myself be killed by the enemy.

I folded space again, and prayed our fellow Champions would be there to help us when we arrived.

Entirely oblivious to the fact that the black pod had crafted two more Champions before we’d even arrived.

Two Champion clones that brought me screeching to a halt and sweating in terror.

Because standing in front of me, blocking the surface exit of the Senso-ji temple, were none other than the bone clones of Eskil and Ohana, and they’d already launched their attacks.
