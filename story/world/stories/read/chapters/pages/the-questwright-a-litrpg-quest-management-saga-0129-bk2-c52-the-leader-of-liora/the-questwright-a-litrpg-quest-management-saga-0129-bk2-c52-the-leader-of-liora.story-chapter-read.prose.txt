Hollis, on the day Cass received his Calling…

Gerald Hollis stretched as he rolled out of his cot.

Today was the day.

***

Cassio looked like a rabbit staring at a coyote as he walked up to the Book of Callings. Gerald had hated to fail the boy again and again, but he needed this to be the only option. He, Cassandra, and Dallan had all agreed that Cassio was the right pick to eventually replace him.

Gerald had spent years looking for a successor. Stronger candidates had come and gone, smarter ones too. A few were more charismatic and ambitious. But one by one, they’d rejected QuestWright, looking for a Calling that ‘made more sense’ or ‘had more power’.

None of them had been right.

And then his friends had told him more about their son, and he’d found time in his incredibly busy schedule to watch him. He accepted responsibility for mistakes that weren’t his in his classes, earning others' loyalty. When problems appeared, he stepped toward them, rather than away. And, perhaps most importantly, people trusted him without also feeling threatened by him.

That last quality was rarer than any Calling. So they made a decision early on after his tenth birthday. Cassio would stay close to home, and Dallan and Cassandra would spend every spare moment preparing him for the future they hoped he would one day inherit.

Dallan focused on teaching him philosophy, the same college classes where he and Gerald had first met. Meanwhile, Cassandra helped teach him a little about fighting, but also about making quick decisions when there were no right choices.

The years since had only confirmed his original judgment. That made what was to come very difficult.

Gerald watched Cass put his hand on the Book of Callings, and with a mental push, it accepted his interruption, changing what would’ve been Vanguard into QuestWright with barely a blink. The boy's expression moved from surprise to joy, and then to confusion.

This was the worst part, though Cassandra and Dallan had agreed it was necessary. The Dark Guilds were moving around quickly in the world, and the boy needed to be protected.

He tried to make his voice sound pleasant as Cassio’s parents stared down with anxiety from high in the Atrium, “Mr. Vale, congratulations on receiving your Calling. If you’ll follow me, we can get you sorted out right away. Come along.”

Then, barely minutes later, he left Cassio in that room alone after completely uprooting his life. It struck Gerald to the core of his soul as the boy’s eyes followed him in his mind.

Nothing about this was pleasant, but as the Sovereign had told him during their infrequent conversations, sometimes you just had to bite down and get through it.

That evening, he turned to his history books, losing himself in his old studies until falling asleep. He hoped Cassio had discovered the note he’d left behind.

***

Three days later…

Hollis spotted Thomas behind a too-small desk. He slapped a grin on his face as he approached his old friend.

“Thomas!”

“No, Gerald,” The high-tier Clerk said, wiping down an imaginary spot and cutting him off before he could speak further. “I don’t know what it is, but I want no part in it.”

“Oh, Thomas, come on. We started Liora together! I’ll bet anything that you miss life in a big city. Besides, you can’t get rid of our bond so quickly!”

“I did when I left,” He looked up with a raised eyebrow, “You’re a history nerd, how can you not remember that?’

“Thomas…”

“No.”

“Thomas?”

“No.”

Convincing the cantankerous Thomas Moore to leave his small city and come back to Liora wasn’t easy. It required bribery, cajoling, and not a small amount of begging. But eventually, the terrifying man agreed.

Finally, Cassio would have a proper guide who could protect him. Gerald simply didn’t have the time.

***

One day after Cass was attacked by the Skreels…

“Dallan, calm down.”

“I will not!” Cassio’s father yelled out, pacing back and forth in one of the Infirmary’s waiting rooms. “He shouldn’t have been out there!”

“Where’s the man who left him there, huh?” Cassandra asked, a chain leaking from the inside of her top as it wrapped around one of her hands. She took an aggressive step toward Gerald, “I’m going to beat him till he tells me who the other spies are, then, I’ll let him live so Dallan can cut him to pieces.”

Cassio’s father pulled a thick, weighted knife from behind his back. “I will, too.”

Gerald sighed, “He’s in hiding. I already have Master Chask searching for them.”

“He’s a drunk half the time!” Dallan yelled out.

“And!” Gerald interrupted, “Thomas is with him.”

“Thomas who?”

“Moore.”

“Oh…” Cassandra looked at Dallan and nodded as they both put away their weapons. “That’s fine then.”

***

Gerald left Cassio in bed, finding both of his parents still in the hallway. He pulled them aside to quietly say, “He definitely got something special out there. Felt like the System was watching him, too, though I’m not as sensitive as the higher tiers to it. I warned him not to spread the word.”

Cassandra patted her leg, “He’ll probably tell Gary.”

Dallan nodded, “Yeah, but that boy can keep a secret. He’s from good stock.”

Seeing three of Cassio’s closest friends approaching, Gerald pulled them all into an empty room before they were noticed. “Thomas killed three Changelings the other day.”

“Three?’

“Who kills someone with paper?’

Speaking quieter as he heard their footsteps approaching, he whispered, “He and Master Chask are hunting them while we speak. We’re worried things are getting worse, so I’m going to insert Thomas into Cassio’s life earlier than we’d planned. Do you have any problems with that?”

They looked at each other, but neither had anything bad to say about the high-tier Clerk.

***

Thomas arrived at Gerald’s door on the first day he was back in the city. He’d spent close to a week in the forest with Master Chask, clearing it of any Dark Guild members,.

Somehow, his clothing still looked pristine.

The Maestro nodded, “The boy is worth my time.” Then, he turned around and left without another word.

***

The day Cass became the Guildmaster Pro Tempore…

Gerald couldn’t move as the paralytic had a powerful hold on his body. He heard everything, felt nothing, and worst of all, was completely aware of the world.

A tormented Dev hadn’t left his side the whole time, her crying and fear stabbing into his soul as there was nothing he could do to alleviate her pain. When the door opened past the guards, Thomas entered with several others, but he couldn’t tell who.

I really wish they’d aimed my head in any direction but the ceiling. This is a truly awful experience.

Dev and Thomas shared a back-and-forth before Cassio’s voice rang out with previously unheard authority, “What happened?”

Thomas didn’t paint Gerald in the best positive light, but then again, he was right. He didn’t listen. He never thought the Dark Guilds would go for him this way. A knife in the back, sure. But not a paralyzing agent.

Kara asked a good question, “But why use a temporary poison?”

Because they’re not allowed to kill a sitting Guildmaster, duh.

His mind caught up with what Moore was saying, “...we are leaderless, so I am following the protocol as was instructed to me soon after I first arrived.”

I’m sorry, Cassio.

They talked some more, Cassio opened the letter, then all was quiet for a time. Eventually, a notification struck him.

[SYSTEM NOTICE]

A portion of your power has been automatically siphoned to QuestWright Cassio Vale of Liora.

I’m sorry, Cassio. May your reign be better than mine.

***

Five days before Cass received Hollis’s second letter…

Gerald sat at the head of the table in Meeting Room One. Across from him and at the other end was Julio Cervantes, the QuestWright from the capital. The rest of the table was filled with all the important figures remaining in Liora, from the leader of the Valiants to the remaining Councilmembers. It was a collective who’s who of Liora, while his newest QuestWright, Amelia, was in a chair against the wall.

All the most influential people in the city were here, except for the most important one, in his mind.

“Master Brannic, what is the timetable for more vellums?” Julio said with a scowl. Gerald noted that the capital QuestWright commonly held that expression.

“The processes are in the works,” Brannic replied with aplomb. “We should have a full harvest in the next day or so.”

Gerald looked over at Cassandra Vale, who had taken on Liora’s defenses following the war, “Have we caught sight of any more Dark Guild members?”

“No,” She fingered a trailing chain in her hands as she looked at the young man seated next to Julio. “The last time we caught them, somebody was a little jumpy and shot a fireball in their midst. He didn’t hit anything, but it sure was pretty.”

Dawdy, the capital mage, threw both of his hands in the air, “I’m telling you! One of them had an artifact that turned it at the last moment.”

“Sure, they did,” Cassandra replied, another chain falling out of her other sleeve in what seemed a subconscious manner. “And now, we have no new information on their movements.”

Thomas put a hand on her shoulder, then looked down at her chains suggestively. Cassandra scowled, but they did retract as she got a hold of herself. She hated both men from the capital with a heated fury.

The rest of the room made their reports, and it appeared that Liora was slowly on its way back to being a city. At the moment, most of its citizens were either within the Guildhall, which remained partly untouched, or camped just outside of it. They’d been receiving deliveries of food from the countryside, with additional allotments arriving from the capital whenever possible.

One day, the city would regain its former glory.

As everyone filed out, Julio intentionally bumped Amelia with his shoulder. Gerald had to hold Cassandra back from attacking the man.

“Now’s not the time.”

“He’s a right bastard,” Cassandra said, shifting her glare over to Gerald. “You know how he treated us after they stole my boy.”

“About that,” He nodded at Thomas, who had stayed behind. The Maestro gently took Amelia by the shoulder, and they shuffled out as Gerald continued. “I’ve received word from the Capital. Cassio is safe and sound in Liberty, a city to the north. He’s being treated well and is protected within their famous Guildtower.”

“Have you heard anything about Adya and the others?” When he shook his head, she snorted, “I know Liberty.” Something indescribable entered her eyes, “It’s a cancer to the Guild. They’ll try to undo everything we’ve worked so hard to cultivate in Cass.”

“I highly doubt they’ll be successful,” Gerald said with a smile. “He’s a rare young man. But I thought it would be nice if we all wrote him a letter. I’ll pay the freight, of course.”

“Are you going to write one?”

“I don’t know if he’ll want to hear from me. My last letter was the one that forced him to lead Liora during the war. There is no way that it endeared him to me.”

Cassandra put both of her heavy hands on his shoulders, “He saved the city, Gerald. Countless lives continue on because of what my boy did. There’s a reason children are pretending to be him in the streets right now. Not to take away Cass’s victory, but you had a big hand in that. We all did.”

“Of course, you’re right about Cassio.”

She nodded, “What are you going to do about Julio and his machinations?”

“Julio is a worm. Once Liora starts to get back on its feet, he’ll be recalled to the Capital. Unlike you, I am not worried about him taking my position.” Gerald calmly replied.

The Capital QuestWright had been speaking in the ears of anyone who would listen, attempting to erode Gerald’s base of power in what remained of Liora. Luckily, nobody seemed to care about his superior attitude.

“Well, I’m sure Cass would love to hear from you. Well, maybe not love,” She smirked. “But it couldn’t hurt to fix a few links in the chain.”

“You would know,” Gerald nodded. “I’ll think about it.”

Later that night, Gerald sat at his desk. Several balled-up pieces of paper rested beside his desk. Each covered the page with explanations, justifications, and his reasoning for everything that had befallen the youngest Vale. But none of it worked.

Gerald took a coin out of his robe pocket. It had been with him since before the Reshaping, a keepsake his father had given him, passed down through the family. He tossed it in the air, caught it, and looked at the result.

“You win again, Marcus.”

Gerald wrote the letter.
