“What the fuck do you mean black?” Ark snapped, panicking. “You don’t have black.”

He pulled to another halt, turned left then right. He frowned, unsure of what path to follow as he sniffed the air once more. They were in a new hallway, but Melmarc had seen enough hallways to know that they were not real. And, if he was to judge by Ark’s movements, whatever illusion they were moving through, the fake walls and hallways were mixed with the real walls and hallways.

“Shit,” Ark swore under his breath. “It’s getting harder to sniff out what’s real and what’s not.”

Melmarc had no idea it was even possible to guess with smell. Banishing that thought and leaving it to Ark, he looked back. In the distance the black indicator continued to hover, belonging to a lady called Telwort. An S-rank [Teacher] with a black indicator. He could not even begin to pretend to make sense of what was happening.

“Any hope?” he asked Ark.

Ark frowned, looked back in  the direction Melmarc had been looking, then shook his head. “I smell three walls but I don’t know which one is real.”

An idea came to Melmarc’s head, but first, he was curious about something else. “Why are you worried?” he asked Ark.

Ark shot him an incredulous look. “Whatever this place is, the school wants to keep it as much of a secret as possible. I won’t be surprised if we got expelled for being here.”

“And do you care about being expelled?” Melmarc asked, knowing that Ark did not but not completely sure of why it bothered him.

“No,” Ark snorted in distaste. “But you do. We can’t go getting you expelled.”

That made enough sense to Melmarc. With his curiosity settled, he moved his hand. He felt the familiar weight as the mana pooled to his wrist, white and heavy. It felt like forever since he had used any of the skill.

Ark looked from his hand to the direction where Telwort was coming from, even though he couldn’t see her. “You’ve got to be kidding me?” he sputtered as the ring of mana grew brighter around Melmarc’s wrist. “You remember that skill can kill someone, right?”

Melmarc rolled his eyes. “I know,” he said as his interface popped up in front of him.

[You have used skill Secrecy]

[Remaining Uses: 3/4]

With the ring in place, he raised his hand and slammed it into the ground. It clicked into the tile with a sound like a locked door, which surprised Melmarc, then exploded into a dome of mana as wide as three of the hallways in radius.

Ark looked around. “Oh,” he muttered. “I forgot you had that.”

There were a lot of skills Melmarc could easily forget he had. Apart from [Knowledge Is Power], he hadn’t really used his skills for much. In fact, for a while now, since waking up, he had simply been living a very mundane life.

That’s why you’re bored, he realized as the wall on his left disappeared.

“Bingo,” Ark exclaimed then pointed to the new path in front of them. “I had a feeling he was the dud.”

“Cap,” Melmarc disagreed as they ran down the new path, even though he felt no dissonance. “The wall on the other side disappeared too.”

Ark took a left and Melmarc followed him only to come to a stop in front of a very familiar face. A face they had not expected to run into.

“Mr. Lockwood,” Mr. Okoro said in a confused voice. “What are you doing here?”

Melmarc opened his mouth. “Um…”

He had no reply.

“Oh God yes!” Ark exclaimed in relief. “Finally. I swear I thought we’d never make it out of here alive. There are so many walls.”

Mr. Okoro’s eyes squinted in suspicion while Ark walked quickly to him. Melmarc backed his play, following in hurried steps, hoping he looked as relieved if not confused as Ark did.

Mr. Okoro took an almost unnoticeable step back as Melmarc and Ark came to stand on both sides of him. Melmarc couldn’t blame the man. While it didn’t happen to him very often, he never liked the idea of people larger than him standing around him.

“How did you get into this building?” Mr. Okoro asked, his suspicion refusing to leave.

Melmarc’s eyes moved into the distance. Mrs. Telwort was still approaching, but she wasn’t in a hurry. Had she forgotten about them or had they just been running away from someone who hadn’t even seen them.

“It doesn’t matter,” Mr. Okoro muttered, shaking his head. “You have to leave this place.”

“But my brother,” Ark said quickly. “We need to get his familiar back. We saw someone taking it into the building and just followed.”

Dissonant.

Mr. Okoro paled. “You saw what?”

Ark was nodding quickly, eyes skittish and horrified. “I don’t know why someone would kidnap my brother’s familiar and bring it here. We ran after the person, but they were too fast.”

Dissonance.

Mr. Okoro muttered something under his breath before asking, “Can you describe this person? Do you know what they were wearing?”

“Black?” Ark shrugged, uncertain. “They had a gun too.”

Mr. Okoro frowned. The panic on his face increased and Melmarc felt his fear.

Why does the idea of an armed man kidnapping Veebee scare him?

In the end, the man shook his head and turned to the side. “This way,” he said and reached out to the wall close to him. “You kids can’t be here, and you can’t be caught being here.”

His hand wrapped around a doorknob that appeared on the wall, and a black door appeared. He opened it inward and into a room.

“Thank you,” Melmarc said, meaning it as he walked in, pausing with only one foot inside and Ark behind him. “Is it safe for us in here?”

“Yes,” Mr. Okoro answered. “Now, go in. Quickly.”

Ark looked at Melmarc, and Melmarc nodded. There had been no dissonance in Mr. Okoro’s words, so he entered the room and Ark followed behind him.

The moment they were inside, Mr. Okoro closed the door.

Ark moved immediately, standing with his back to the door. His eyes had lost that skittish expression that he had put on for Mr. Okoro. When he noticed Melmarc looking at him, he grinned. “Must be annoying not being able to lie.”

“It’s not that I can’t,” Melmarc corrected. “It’s that it is annoying to lie.”

“Uhuh.” Ark moved away from the door, then tried the knob. He made a sound that said he was not surprised. “Locked. He must’ve locked us inside.”

Melmarc nodded absently, looking at the wall, taking note of Telwort’s indicator. “He wasn’t lying when he said it was safe for us inside here.” He moved his gaze from the wall to the entire room. “Why did you use Veebee in the lie?”

Ark shrugged, now joining him deeper into the room. “It’s harder to spot a lie when it has truths in it. On another note, this place looks cool.”

“Yeah,” Melmarc agreed, “in an odd kind of way.”

The room was wide, massive enough that if he used [Knowledge Is Power], he wasn’t sure he would be able to fill the entire space, and the skill now had quite the range on it.

“What do you think this place is?” Ark asked.

Melmarc shrugged. “An office?” he guessed.

“Because of the table?”

In the vast room, there were only three things that stood out. First was the color of the walls. It was a deep brown, like oak, very different from the black and white design that was most of the building. It was almost as if someone had cast wood on the walls to stave off the initial design. There were no shelves or paintings, nothing that gave away any form of interest in interior décor.

The second thing that stood out was the ground. It was covered in bright blue carpet, so bright that it was almost neon.

Ark followed Melmarc’s gaze and pointed at the carpet. “Vacuum tracks. Interesting.”

“Recent too,” Melmarc noted, squatting over the tracks.

“You think he was vacuuming?”

Melmarc doubted it. Mr. Okoro did not strike him as someone who would do the vacuuming. Then again, Mr. Okoro was a [Sage], and Naymond had already given Melmarc a specific impression on the [Sage] class as a class of eccentric people. With the thought came the thought of Naymond. He hadn’t heard from him since they’d gotten him out of jail.

It felt like it was forever ago. Then again, the man rarely if not never came to his mind.

Melmarc took his attention from the ground, ignored the chandelier above—three descending circles, each one smaller in size than the one before it—and faced the last interesting thing in the room. A transparent glass table sitting on top of four glass legs.

Ark walked over to it and placed a hand on the back rest of one of the only two seats at the table. Both seats were placed one on either side. One backed the door while the other faced it.

“You think he holds meetings with someone here?” Ark asked.

Melmarc wasn’t sure, so he shook his head. “I can’t even begin to figure out what the room is for.”

Ark looked around again. “I was going to say that’s a first, but you’re kind of right. I can’t even imagine what the room is for.”

Dentist offices had things that gave you a clue about who the occupant of the office was. Teachers had books and maybe scripts. Those in the finance industry had things about finance in their own ways. An empty office was nothing.

Like an empty room, Melmarc thought, remembering the void. That was a conversation he and Uncle Dorthna would have to have once he’d found Veebee. If Veebee is really even here.

Still, he could not shake off the certainty that Veebee was in the building with them, doing something that it wasn’t supposed to do. Satisfying some kind of curiosity that it was not supposed to satisfy.

“Hey, check this out.”

Melmarc blinked, drawing himself away from his thoughts and found Ark standing in front of a screen. It hovered like a hologram above the table.

He walked up to him quickly, eyes darting to the wall. The woman named Telwort was no longer moving. She was just standing in place in the distance.

“What’s that?” Melmarc asked.

Ark shrugged. “A screen.”

“And how did you activate said screen?”

Ark tapped a part of the table and Melmarc found himself looking at a simple glass box. “Sniffed it out. I think it had some kind of illusory effect on it.”

“Let me guess. You sniffed it out.”

Ark grinned. “You know me so well.”

“Then how did you activate it?”

Ark held up his hand, and it ignited in black flames. “I burnt it off.”

Then the flames disappeared.

“Really?” Melmarc ran his hand down his face. “A guy helps us out and you start burning his things?”

“I was curious, and we are here to find Veebee, aren’t we?”

“Fair point.” Melmarc nodded, then poked at the screen. Surprisingly, his hand hit something solid where he had expected his hand to pass through.

[Mana signature required]

Melmarc blinked at the interface, then looked at the screen. The screen showed him nothing, only his interface remained.

Ark poked the screen as well. “Oh,” he blurted. “That’s trippy.”

“Must be the work of a [Crafter],” Melmarc said. It was the only thing that explained why he was getting a message through his interface and not the screen in front of him. “A high rank [Crafter], I think.”

Ark’s hand ignited in flames again. “Think I can burn it off?”

“Nope.” Melmarc raised his hand, putting it between Ark and the device. “Let’s try something else.”

He moved his hand and pure mana grew around it, forming a ring.

[You have used skill Secrecy]

[Remaining Uses: 2/4]

He aimed it at the ground beneath them and let the ring fall. When it touched the ground, it clicked into place again like a lock on a door. This time, that was not all it did. Melmarc watched a small hairline fracture spread through the ground as long as his arm. It moved in a single direction before [Secrecy] took effect, encompassing a good portion of the room.

Ark looked down at the ground while the flame around his hand burnt brighter. The black flame took his attention from the ground and he just stared at it with a smile. “Doesn’t this skill reduce the effectiveness of other skills, and mana?”

“Yes,” Melmarc answered watching he flame continue to burn brighter. “Think we should ask Uncle Dorthna about it?”

“Wanna bet he’ll just ask us to figure it out?”

Melmarc thought about it, then nodded. “Fifty bucks says he’ll tell us.”

“You’re on.”

With that done, Melmarc looked at the screen once more. “Oh, look. I think it’s coming on.”

“I guess it’s a good thing I didn’t burn it,” Ark chuckled. “How would we have…” His voice trailed off at the sight of what was on the screen. “Mel,” he said in a tone of worry, “please tell me you’re seeing the same thing.”

Melmarc’s nod was very slow. “Are you seeing a man standing and undoing his chains wrapped around his wrists and ankles?”

“Uhuh.” Ark’s nod was also slow. “Does your man have one wing on his back?”

“Uhuh. Black and white?”

“Uhuh.”

Melmarc frowned a little. “Do you feel like punching him in the face for being here?”

Ark paused, thought about it, then shrugged. “Nope. But judging by the black and white of his wing and the black and white everywhere, I think that’s just an [August Intruder] thing. What do you think? Should we go find out what kind of intruder this guy is?”

Melmarc leaned forward, looking closer into the screen. “We might not have to go anywhere?”

“Why is—Oh.” Ark looked at the screen too. “Maybe he’s being dramatic.”

Melmarc shook his head. He could feel it. The winged man was not being dramatic. He was not simply looking at the camera.

He was looking at them.

“Maybe he—”

The last chain around the man’s wrist hit the ground, and he disappeared, cutting Ark off.

Ark let off a tired sigh. “Alright then,” he said in a challenging tone. “We’re long overdue for some fun anyway.”

Melmarc turned and faced the other side of the room, and something stirred to life inside of him in anticipation. “Yes, we are.”

The air burst into an explosion of feathers. At its heart, the man stood, one winged and seven feet tall. He looked like a Greek statue.

Melmarc’s interface popped up in front of him.

[You have encountered Bayad the Spotted (S)]

No class. Just a name and a rank. Melmarc was still rummaging through what was happening when his mind triggered.

Spit on it!

Melmarc grinned.
