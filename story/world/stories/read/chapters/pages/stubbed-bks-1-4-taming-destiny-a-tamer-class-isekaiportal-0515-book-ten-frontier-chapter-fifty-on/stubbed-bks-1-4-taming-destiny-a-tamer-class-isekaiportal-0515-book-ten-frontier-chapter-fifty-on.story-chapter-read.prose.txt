I watch thoughtfully as Alyna returns to the settlement. Though she reached out to engage with the Bond quickly, she was more hesitant over what she actually put into it. By the time she was accepting her side and passing it back to me, I was almost expecting her to have made a true Tame Bond – putting in requirements and limits.

Yet, when it actually returned, there was enough trust there to allow it to become a Companion Bond. Her hesitation must have been just over whether she really trusted me enough to give me everything again. Without diving into her emotions now, I can only guess that she, like others before her, assessed that I hadn’t done anything too nefarious when she hadn’t had the option to end the Bond if she wanted; having the option to end it just gives her a little more of a safety net.

We didn’t speak for much longer after that – she seemed a little embarrassed about how open she’d been with her emotions, and quickly beat a retreat.

Turning away from watching Alyna, I focus back on the field ahead of me. By this point, the Plant Mages have finished preparing the land and the farmers have finished planting it. Still, when I close my eyes and activate my magic sight, I can see subtle ripples of mana within the array. Falling into Medium Meditation, I focus on them, trying to work out what they’re doing.

As I’m watching, my mind returns to what I had been thinking before I received a rude interruption from a tree.

Artio had been both giving and taking in his interactions with the trees. He’d been giving them mana, certainly, but he’d been receiving something in return.

And, as I look at the field, I see the same pattern is repeating itself. The plants absorb the ripples of mana left behind by the Plant Mages and the potions, but they also return something to the soil.

I feel a dawning sense of realisation.

Is this why I’ve failed to gain Plant-Shaping every time I’ve tried?

None of my other Shaping Skills work this way. For everything else, I need to press mana that the element recognises into it, and then I request that it does my will with greater or lesser firmness. Each element has its own ‘mood’, of course – earth is slow and requires time to recognise me while fire is content to listen as long as it is able to consume something. Wind wishes to always be moving, while water subtly resists unless I treat it all equally. Lava is a faster version of earth and a hungrier, more stable version of fire while metal is ordered, resisting change, yet it embraces its new form when forced into it. Flesh, on the other hand, is amorphous, requiring precision and imagination in equal measures.

However, every time I’ve tried to reshape my mana into ‘plant’, I’ve failed. And I’ve never been able to feed mana into plants either. But perhaps I was going about it in the wrong way.

Opening my eyes so as not to repeat the earlier incident, I move slowly, almost dreamily to the edge of the forest, my recent Mastery of the Skill allowing me to remain in Medium Meditation as I do so.

Settling at the base of one of the large trees that form almost a wall around the cleared space for the farms, I press my hand against the bole of the tree. Instead of trying to reach inside with my mana, I just…watch.

And the longer I spend watching, the more I notice. Though I could always see the general flow of muted green energy within the plant, now I see just how many shades there are – and that not everything is going upwards through the trunk to the leaves, but that almost as much is returning from the leaves down to the roots.

The tree’s roots are vast, stretching down as far as I can see – which, without using Earth-Shaping, is only a metre or two – and sideways too, as far as the tree’s canopy. It’s actually almost like a mirror below the ground to what it is above it, and the tree roots tangle with the roots of the trees neighbouring it in the same ways as the branches of its canopy interweave with theirs.

Yet there is even more interaction below the ground. I feel a frown form on my face as I notice that something is happening in the areas where the tree roots come into close proximity.

More time spent observing just that bit makes me marvel even more – there is an exchange between the roots of the tree I’m watching and its neighbours, yet the exchange seems to be facilitated through a third party, something that exists purely underground, and takes its own portion of whatever resources are exchanged.

I extend my field of vision to the plants nestled around the base of the trees – to the grass-like equivalent, and the creeping vines, and the pink puff balls that seem to be similar to dandelions back home in their ever present nature.

Though they’re all different shapes, and the cycles are less complex and harder to see in the smaller plants, their similarities to the trees are unmistakable.

I open my eyes and stare unseeingly at the brown bark of the tree. Everything I see seems to be about reciprocity and cycles. Given how stationary trees and plants are, I feel I can be forgiven for having thought their mana systems just as unmoving, like the earth. Yet nothing could be further from the truth. The tree mana, plant mana, has far more in common with blood vessels than the solid stability of the earth.

And, as I think back to my mostly-forgotten biology lessons, I realise that I should have considered that earlier. After all, we did enough at school about them, looking  at onion cells under a microscope and learning about RNA, that it shouldn’t have surprised me that they’re not just multicoloured rocks.

Yet I mustn’t now make the mistake of treating them like living creatures – DNA and RNA are different; Flesh-Shaping and Plant-Shaping cannot act in the same way. However, I just know I’m on the edge of something here.

Considering the matter carefully, I decide that making contact with the roots is a better first step than touching the trunk – the trunk is the plant’s defence; the roots are its source of nutrients and water. I reach into the earth, being particularly careful not to accidentally make contact with the mana there – adding Earth-Shaping into the mix will only muddy the waters.

Surrounding the nearest tree root with my neutral mana, I resist the urge to try to press inside. Instead, I try to infuse it with a sense of reciprocity, of being willing to give something in return for something else. It reminds me uncannily like reaching out with a Tame Bond, only this time it’s just with my pure mana.

Nothing happens immediately, but I’m patient – that this might take time even if it’s working is not inconceivable.

My patience is rewarded. My eyebrows shoot up as I feel a rudimentary intelligence touch my mana curiously. I wasn’t expecting that.

Perhaps calling it an ‘intelligence’ is misleading. It’s more like…an awareness. I have no idea if the tree is capable of understanding that it’s a tree, but it’s certainly capable of realising that I’m offering something and it has an instinctive urge to respond in kind.

My mana is sucked inside the roots and I feel a faint connection form between us. Not between our souls as my Bonds do – it attaches to the very outer parts of my internal matrix, to one of the channels leading out of my Core space entirely. Even when my mana is consumed, the connection endures.

And then I feel something come back. A small pulse of…not Energy, but vitality. It feels like a shot of adrenaline, all sleepiness wiped away from me in an instant. At the same time, I gain a sense of the tree’s greater body – from the inside. Its leaves reach up towards the heavens; its roots sinking deeply into the ground, anchoring it and connecting it with water. For a brief moment, I feel the movement of water from the deepest roots all the way up to where it evaporates from the topmost leaves, and the return movement of sugars down from the leaves to the deepest roots, pressing them downward a bare millimetre more.

And then the connection withers, its task accomplished.

Feeling wonder, I continue experimenting.

Hours pass before I feel comfortable with even my basic findings – plants truly are more complex than anything else I’ve experimented with – Flesh-Shaping perhaps being an exception. I discover that for the connection to be maintained, the flow must continue. It isn’t a question of quantity, but consistency. The tree will not take in mana faster if I offer it more – only if I cover more of its roots. Equally, the vitality and awareness it returns is not directly reciprocal to the amount of mana I offer, but proportionate to what it has to offer.

I can also request other things from it – as long as I offer it the mana in return. Sap beads out of invisible holes in the bark, gleaming very slightly in the light of the sun. leaves drop from the tree, released by it at my urging, only to be caught by the breeze moments after they’ve been set free.

Yet I still haven’t actually gained Plant-Shaping, and I think I know why. Because what I’m doing here is more like creating a temporary Tame Bond with the plant, not actually Shaping it. But all my experimentation has given me a good idea of where to start.

Patting the tree’s bark, I send it feelings of appreciation. I don’t know if it feels them – the last connection we had has withered away – or if it recognises what appreciation is even if it feels it at all, but I feel a welling of affection for this particular specimen after all the time we’ve spent communicating in such a basic way.

My next target is one of the pink puffball plants – a croesus according to Inspect. It’s a common plant with few uses. It’s edible, but not particularly palatable, despite the kitchen’s best efforts, so we haven’t tried cultivating it. However, due to its ability to grow quickly and even in very stony, infertile ground, its nodding pink puffball heads have become a common sight, sometimes even giving the meadows a faint pink shade.

I suppose we should be grateful that the animals like it, at least – the milk and meat the domesticated animals give have been, alongside the meat from the hunters, a good part of why we didn’t starve until Elarion made his breakthrough. But, unlike us, the beasts have taken to their new diet here with gusto, and the recent report Loran gave me indicated that numbers are increasing faster than expected, a relieving message about our sources of wool, milk, and eggs.

However, right now, a croesus has more use to me than anything else. Carefully pulling a seed out of the white puffball that the flower becomes after a tenday or so, I close my eyes and focus on surrounding it with my mana.

I think of reciprocity, yes, but differently this time. Instead of asking for products or awareness, I ask for control.

The awareness in the seed is a tiny fraction of what I communicated with in the tree. This is a dim spark that would barely be seen in a pitch-black room. However, it exists, and it somehow analyses my offering.

I feel the moment it accepts and a connection forms between us, whisker-thin and fragile.

I don’t inundate it with mana – I’ve learned that plants don’t want that. But I do supply it with as much as it can take.

As it absorbs my mana, I feel my own awareness saturate the tiny seed that would fit through the eye of a needle. And as it begins to grow, I feel just how much is in my control.

The plant has…I hesitate to call them instincts – that seems too active for what they are. Yet the plant knows how to grow. It yearns to put down hair-like roots, and then press a minuscule green sprout upwards.

I sense that I could change that order, that I could change the size of each, but to what benefit? For now, I allow it to do what it knows it needs to.

However, as the roots multiply and the stem thickens and sprouts, I take more control. The plant grows according to my requests as long as I keep supplying mana for the changes. I experiment, trying everything I can think of and seeing what happens as a result.

Finally, I put the croesus down, its stems spelling out ‘magic’ in English. Gazing at it thoughtfully, I consider everything I’ve learned.

First, plants prefer to grow than to wither, but, unlike fire, they are not entirely opposed to it, especially if in withering they grow in strength somewhere else. The croesus’s stalks and leaves happily withered away as its roots thickened, and multiplied. And when I urged it to regrow the stems and leaves, they came back larger and sturdier than before.

Second, it’s far easier to control a plant that I’ve grown from a seed than one I’ve merely built a temporary relationship with. I suspect that size also makes a difference. My control seems to increase the more mana I’ve given it proportionately. Which makes sense – the plant reciprocates the aid it’s been given with cooperation.

Third, plants definitely have some sort of awareness, even if it’s not what we would normally call consciousness. Though I wouldn’t say that I’ve been taming the plants, in some ways it is similar. Then again, the earth also takes a good deal of urging and building of a relationship before it will be amenable, so it’s not like there isn’t precedent for that being required for Shaping.

Fourth, plants, more than any other element I’ve learned, are inextricably connected to their environment. Earth supports the roots and offers nourishing minerals. Trace metals are required in countless processes. Air movement allows the exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide, part of the plant’s nutrition process. Water hydrates the plant. And fire, represented by the warm caress of the sun, is essential to photosynthesis. Even Flesh is involved – creatures are nourished by the plants and nourish in their turn with their rotting bodies, while they also act as transportation for the plant’s seeds.

Yet it is not a one-sided dependence – plants are an intrinsic part of that cycle too. Without plants, the land would be nothing but barren, sun-baked desert, rocky or sandy in turns. Dead. No animals would survive, and any water that fell would quickly flow away. No fire would burn – there would be nothing to fuel it.

Everything is in a cycle, I think to myself, and plants are at the heart of it.

A moment after the thought passes through my mind, I feel a cascade of Energy go through me, my Core space abruptly expanding within me as my matrix hums and extra channels grow as my vision goes white.

I couldn’t have levelled up unintentionally, could I? I wonder as soon as I’m able to think.

There’s only one way to find out – I pull up my notifications, sure that there must be information there.
