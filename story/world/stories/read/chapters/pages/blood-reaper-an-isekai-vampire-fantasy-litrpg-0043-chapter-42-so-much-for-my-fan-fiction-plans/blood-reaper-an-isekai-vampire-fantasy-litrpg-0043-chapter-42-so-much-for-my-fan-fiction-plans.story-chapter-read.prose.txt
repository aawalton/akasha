The Thornhide Behemoth was about to crush Tara into a pancake.

As Ace bolted toward her, she looked up at the thick wooden limb raised like a club above her head. She reached for the bow slung over her shoulder, but she wouldn’t be able to notch an arrow in time.

Her eyes went wide with fear.

While running, Ace grabbed a rock the size of a football off the ground and hurled it at the looming monster with all the strength he had left in his body. It ricocheted off its head and landed in the dirt not far off as the monster’s skull rattled. It stumbled, thrown off course, and glared once more at Ace.

He had lost track of Victor and Rachel in the fray, but he had to focus on the immediate threat. Nearby, Olivia flipped and landed on a nearby boulder with one leg extended for balance, her staff held securely in her palm. Her eyes narrowed as she stared at the beast, and she smirked.

Her expression gave Ace pause, but he didn’t have time to figure out why.

“Olivia!” he shouted as he closed the gap between him and Tara. “Distract it!”

“Can do,” she answered.

Olivia darted between the Behemoth's legs, a blood-tinged blur of motion that caught the creature's attention. The creature pivoted, its bark plates grinding against each other. Its antlers swung around, carving a deadly arc through the air as it abandoned Tara to pursue Olivia.

Ace didn't waste the opportunity. He skidded to a halt by Tara and Marcus, his heel kicking up clusters of grass. He grabbed Marcus from behind, his arms clutched around the man’s torso as Ace dragged him behind a boulder. Seconds later, the Behemoth's antlers grazed the stone, sending sparks cascading across the hillside.

"Marcus can't move," Tara said, her palm already wrapped around one of the wooden spikes protruding from his thigh. Dark blood oozed from the wounds as she yanked it free, but thankfully it seemed like the projectiles hadn't severed any major arteries.

Marcus winced. “Fucking hell, that hurts.”

“You’re lucky they’re not poisoned,” Tara said. “You’d be dead otherwise.”

Ace peered around their cover. The Behemoth had cornered Olivia against a towering boulder, but she danced between its attacks with unnatural grace. Each near-miss seemed calculated, her body flowing like liquid shadow between deadly strikes.

Victor circled behind the beast, his face a mask of predatory focus as he targeted the base of the monster's skull. That asshole wasn't fighting to protect anyone—he was waiting for the perfect moment to deliver a killing blow and claim whatever loot this thing would drop.

The System floated beside the man, taunting him in a voice too low for even Ace’s enhanced hearing to pick up, but he was glad for the break from the floating sadist.

"Where are your Duskblades?" Tara asked, her hands slick with Marcus's blood as she worked.

"Soul Meter's too low," Ace said, scanning the battlefield with clinical detachment. "Can't summon shit."

"What? How—"

"This isn’t the time," he snapped, cutting her off. His attention was already elsewhere, his mind constructing and discarding options one after another. "The System said we can spear it in the eye and kill it.”

“And you believed her?!” Tara scoffed.

“Any better ideas?”

“Rachel said there’s something special about the nodes,” she said flatly. “Maybe if we hit them, it’ll go down.”

“Maybe,” he muttered. “Or maybe that will just make it angrier.”

Behind them, Marcus stifled an agonized scream as Tara removed the final spike from his thigh. Her hands hovered over the wound as soft crimson light pulsed between her fingers. The bleeding slowed, then stopped completely. “Rest for a little bit longer, Marcus, but you should be set.”

A thunderous crash drew their attention. Olivia had misjudged a dodge, and the Behemoth's arm had caught her in a glancing blow, sending her tumbling across the clearing. She rolled to her feet, but her movements were slower now, less fluid.

"We need your melee strength," Tara said, her voice dropping to an urgent whisper. "And you need your Soul Meter back up."

"No shit," Ace muttered, watching as Victor slashed at the back of the monster's leg, deliberately positioning himself to spray acid blood toward Olivia. The bastard was using her as bait.

"You need vampire blood," Tara continued. "The System told me once that it’s the only way to restore your Soul Meter."

“And where am I going to get that?” he asked, propping one elbow on his knee as he turned his attention toward her.

“Me,” Tara said, already pulling her collar aside to expose her neck. "The others are fighting. I'm the logical choice."

"No," Ace said, his voice hardening. "We need our healer at full strength."

"And we need our melee fighter to, you know, fight," she countered. "This isn't a debate, Ace. It's tactics. Basic team dynamics—the healer shares resources with the team when the fight goes sideways."

Ugh.

She had a point.

Before he could answer, the boulder protecting them exploded.

Granite fragments peppered Ace's back as the Behemoth's fist crashed through their cover, missing his head by inches. Marcus launched sideways to avoid the debris, his body returned to partial health thanks to their healer. The man limped away, but at least he could move.

Ace, meanwhile, grabbed Tara and rolled, feeling stone shrapnel cut into his skin as they tumbled away from the collapsing rock.

The Thornhide Behemoth skidded to a halt, its massive claws gouging deep furrows in the earth. Dirt and rock dust kicked into the air and swirled around the monster like a fog as its glowing eyes locked onto Ace.

With a guttural roar that shook leaves from nearby trees, it lowered its head and charged.

As much as he needed to refill his Soul Meter, he first needed to get this damned thing off his back and buy himself some time. He briefly glanced toward the forest, but the woodland was a death trap—he'd seen how the beast flattened thirty-foot pines like toothpicks. Leading it in there would be a mistake, since even one falling tree could crush him.

Tara, however, would be safe there as long as he led the beast into the Highlands. It seemed more focused on him, anyway.

The Behemoth slammed its hand into the ground. In the second before it could flatten them both, Ace shoved Tara out of the way, and he darted in the opposite direction. The monster’s fist lifted into the air, and clumps of dirt fell from its fingers as it took aim yet again.

Ace shot Tara a sidelong glance. “Stay in the woods! I'll draw it away!"

He launched off the ground, his boots leaving imprints in the mud as he pushed himself to his feet, and the Behemoth adjusted its trajectory. He circled left and drew the Behemoth's attention with a sharp whistle. Its massive head swung toward him, those emerald eyes narrowing with predatory focus.

Good. Let it chase him instead of the others.

The Behemoth charged. Ace waited until the last possible moment before diving aside, letting the monster's momentum carry it past him. In a fluid movement, he grabbed another rock off the hillside and launched it squarely at one of the glowing nodes on its skin. It slammed into the exposed gap between its bark plates, and those glowing veins of light burned brighter. It screamed in pain, its back arching, and the massive beast stumbled forward as it tried to regain its balance.

Huh.

Rachel had been right—the nodes were important after all.

He grabbed another rock and threw it, this time hitting the node as it burned brightest, and the beast let out another deafening scream of pain.

"Hit the nodes when they pulse!" he shouted to the others. "That's when they're vulnerable!"

Olivia was the first to adapt. She jumped onto a nearby boulder and leapt toward the Behemoth. Her staff slammed hard against a glowing node as it brightened. Viscous green fluid sprayed from the wound, and for the first time, the damage didn't immediately heal. The monster roared, actual pain replacing the mindless rage in its cry. As it clawed at the injury, Olivia landed with an effortless roll and instantly got back to her feet, ready to go again.

"It's working!" she said as she danced away from a retaliatory swing.

Ace felt a flicker of hope. Maybe they weren't totally screwed after all.

“You need vampire blood.”

The echo of Tara's voice echoed in his head, and he couldn’t deny her logic. It seemed like the obvious choice, yes, but it came with significant risk.

Her blood would refill his Soul Meter, sure. But every time he'd consumed blood, he had killed the things he’d fed on. The Nightmare Bear. The Fleshstitchers. He'd never stopped—mainly because he’d never had to stop.

There was the very real risk that, once he sank his fangs into Tara, he wouldn’t be able to control himself. The System had confessed as much earlier today—a low Soul Meter meant less self control.

The Behemoth's roar cut through his thoughts, and it shifted its full attention toward him. It sprang to its feet, faster than before, and its claws sank into the dirt with each heavy footfall.

It was closing in fast, and it looked pissed.

Not far off, a cluster of massive boulders jutted from the grassy hillside. Ace darted toward the outcropping, leading the creature away from the others as he raced toward the gaps between the rocks. The narrow openings would be too small for its massive body, and maybe boulders that big would hold up against its strength.

Worth a shot.

He reached the outcropping in seconds and slid through the nearest opening he could find. Just seconds later, sure enough, the Behemoth’s bulk slammed into the first rock with a thunderous force, and fragments of stone shot in every direction. Without pausing to look behind him, Ace slipped through the gaps in the boulders as the creature thrashed. The beast slashed at the opening, its claws reaching for him even as it slammed its body against the rocks again and again.

Ace ran out the other side and glanced over his shoulder as another plume of rock dust shot into the air. The creature’s full focus remained on the outcropping, and the thick cloud of rock dust obscured the air like a heavy fog.

Good—it didn’t know he’d escaped. That bought Ace precious seconds, but his luck wouldn’t last long.

He had to get back to Tara.

Given how exhausted he was, his limbs felt heavier with each step. Soon, he would be running on empty against a monster designed by the System to rip him in half.

Unless he changed tactics, the odds of survival weren’t great.

He cut sharply back toward where he'd left Tara, circling around the steep hill in an effort to stay out of the Behemoth’s sight.

Ace knew what he had to do. His stubborn pride wasn't noble—it was selfish. To refuse her blood would risk everyone’s lives, hers included, all so he wouldn’t have to face the possibility of his own weakness.

Choice made.

Another deafening roar pierced the air from behind him. The crack of shattering boulders followed.

He didn’t have much time left.

As he rounded the hill, Olivia and Rachel came into view. Victor stood nearby, his calculating glare narrowing as he met Ace’s gaze. Ace also spotted Tara and Marcus at the treeline, but Marcus was already limping back into the fray. Ace bolted toward them, a new idea forming. With a practiced gesture—something he and Olivia had practiced yesterday—he silently signaled to her to distract the beast for him.

She nodded.

Without a word, Olivia shifted direction to head off the monster when it rounded the hill. Rachel, finally breaking from her paralysis, began muttering frantically to herself while backing toward the cave entrance. Victor, predictably, ignored everyone.

The ground rumbled.

No, it shook, as though the very hillside itself was about to split in half.

Above the hilltop, shattered stone fragments shot skyward, arching slightly toward Ace before raining down across the grassy landscape. Ace pushed himself harder as the debris clattered like hail against the boulders littering the Highlands. Clouds of chalky dust curled in the air, stinging his eyes and coating his tongue with tiny, bitter debris.

The destruction was followed by something worse—the rhythmic boom-boom-BOOM of the Thornhide Behemoth's thundering footfalls. Each impact sent visible shock waves rippling through the loose shale, dislodging smaller rocks that skittered and bounced down the hillside.

Not a good sign.

As Ace reached the treeline, the top of the hill exploded in a shower of pulverized stone and dust. A towering shadow appeared within the haze, and the ground only shook more violently as it charged.

Its massive head swiveled on a neck ridged with protective spines, its multifaceted eyes capturing every detail of the terrain below. Those glowing eyes caught the violet twilight. Its mouth gaped open, revealing three separate rows of jagged teeth, each the length of Ace's forearm.

On cue, two blurred figures streaked past—Olivia and Victor attacking in tandem. Olivia somersaulted beneath a blow from the monster’s fist, and crimson energy trailed from her fingertips like bloody ribbons. Victor circled wide toward the creature's backside, the leather of his boots scraping against loose stone as he maneuvered. His Nightmare's Spine whip uncoiled and sailed toward the Behemoth's tree-trunk legs.

Victor's cold eyes briefly met Ace's across the battlefield. The corner of his mouth twitched upward in a smile that didn’t reach his eyes—a predator's acknowledgment before the kill. With a deliberate flick of his wrist, his whip cracked against the air hard enough to break bones.

Olivia’s bones, to be precise.

As their Blood Dancer leapt into the air, her staff raised for a brutal blow against one of the glowing nodes in the Behemoth’s shoulder, Victor’s whip hit Olivia’s side. Her back arched, and she screamed in pain.

The monster’s fist slammed against Olivia’s head, and she hit the ground hard.

"You son of a bitch!" Ace shouted at Victor.

The merc ignored him.

Distracted by Olivia’s unconscious form, the Behemoth raised both hands over its head, ready to deliver the final blow. Instead of intervening, Victor jumped onto a nearby boulder and took the time to carefully aim at the beast’s head.

That asshole was using Olivia as bait.

"Olivia!" Ace shouted, skidding to a stop even though he knew he would never make it to her in time. “Look out!”

She didn’t move.

She must’ve been out cold.

As the Behemoth’s clasped hands fell toward Olivia’s crumpled body, Victor attacked. The man drove the metal-capped tip of his weapon into the Behemoth’s eye. The creature screamed, its hands covering its face as it spun and stumbled.

Its foot missed Olivia by an inch. Its massive footprint left a hole in the ground beside her head, crushing her staff to splinters.

Luck.

That was all that had saved Olivia from being flattened—sheer, dumb luck. Victor’s attack couldn’t have accounted for the direction the beast would stumble, and if he’d been in control, he wouldn’t have let it smash her only weapon.

The Behemoth staggered, its enormous foot coming down on a patch of loose shale. The unstable ground shifted beneath its weight. The creature lurched sideways, its bulk tilting at a sickening angle—directly toward Rachel's hiding place.

"Rachel! MOVE!" Marcus's scream tore through the air, raw with desperation.

She looked up as it fell, and her eyes widened with terror. Her pupils shrank to terrified pinpoints as her brain processed what her body couldn't escape.

Everything happened so fast that Ace could barely process it.

The dust motes, suspended in the burnt orange twilight.

The armored bark along the Behemoth’s back as it fell.

The single bead of sweat on Rachel’s brow.

The way her palms flattened against the rock behind her as she tried to run.

Her scream as it lingered on the air.

The monster’s shadow, engulfing her completely as it fell.

No one could stop it—except for Victor. Victor could’ve intervened. Victor could’ve darted under the beast and pulled her out of harm’s way. He could’ve paused in his onslaught to save her life.

Instead, his whip cracked against the monster’s head again, and he took out its other eye.

In that stretched moment between breaths, Rachel looked at Ace. To his horror, he didn’t see betrayal in her eyes, or even fear—he saw recognition.

It was the look of someone who had always known this world would kill her.

And she was right.
