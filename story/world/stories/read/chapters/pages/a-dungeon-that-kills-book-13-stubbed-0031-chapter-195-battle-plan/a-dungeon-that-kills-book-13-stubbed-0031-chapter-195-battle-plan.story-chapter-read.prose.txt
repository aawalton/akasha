“I believe Celeste has already briefed everyone on what is going to happen, right?” Viktor asked.

“Aye,” said Sebekton, who was sitting on the massive stone chair made to fit his bulk, beside a stone table where a large book lay open. “A powerful enemy is going to invade our dungeon in six days. The very same man whom you encountered during your raid on that outpost. And he’s also... a personal enemy of yours, someone who has caused great harm to your sister, and someone you want to make pay for the crime he has committed.”

“That’s correct,” Viktor said. “But we can talk about vengeance or retribution or whatever after we win. For now, though, let’s focus on how to defeat our foes and defend our dungeon.”

“You are right, Sovereign of the Dungeon,” Khenemhotep said as he stirred in his ornate golden chair, one that was no less marvellous than the throne he had in his own Chamber of the Dead. “Let us speak now concerning the enemies that are about to come. What do we know of their strength and their weakness, that we might turn them to our advantage in the day when the confrontation comes upon us?”

“Let’s talk about that man’s—Xander’s—abilities first,” Viktor said as he raised one finger to emphasize the point. “The last time we met, he was carrying at least four Reliquaries, the first of which is something that grants him the ability to see invisible beings. It was problematic back then, but only because I was attacking his outpost using stealth. This time, though, he’s the one walking into our house, and we’re going to attack him head-on. So the advantage of such an artifact has become far less significant.”

“Next is his flaming sword,” he continued, raising a second finger. “It can be used for close combat, obviously, but it can also launch a blazing crescent of fire with a single sweeping swing to attack from a distance. In a way, it is similar to Sebekton’s axe, which can shoot out invisible projectiles that cut through the air. All in all, a fine weapon. Certainly nothing to scoff at, but not anything extraordinary either. Since we already know how it works, I believe we can deal with it without much difficulty.”

“The third one, however, is a proper headache. Or at least, it used to be. That’s a Reliquary that allows him to switch between the physical state and the spectral state at will. In case you’re not familiar with those terms, when that man assumes his spectral state, he becomes ethereal, invulnerable to most attacks. He does need to switch back to the physical state to launch an attack of his own, though, which creates a brief window of vulnerability we can exploit.” Viktor smiled. “Well, as I said, it was a headache. A solution for this problem has been found. Both Galatea’s warhammer, when imbued by the power of the Light, and my blade of radiance could deal damage to him regardless of which state he happens to be in at the moment of impact.”

“Not just us, Master,” his luminous Guardian said. Unlike the other two, she didn’t sit on a chair but drifted around the Core Room—in that beautifully shiny projected form of hers, of course. “Even the Templars could also coat their own weapons with the Light for a short time, though I must admit that their effect is much weaker than mine.”

“That makes it even more reassuring,” Viktor said with a chuckle.

The minions could make up for their lack of power with their numbers. Yes, he could have them engage the bastard first, gradually wearing him down before he or Galatea stepped in to deliver the finishing blow.

“Now, the last one...” he held up a fourth finger. “I believe it is the winged boots he has on his feet. It’s an artifact that allows him to move around with lightning speed, almost like teleportation.”

“Then we need to lure him somewhere with a lot of obstacles to limit his mobility,” Sebekton said.

“Don’t forget that when he’s in the spectral state, he could pass through everything. Physical obstacles mean nothing to him in that form.” Viktor glanced around. “Does anyone have a solution for this?”

“We can use the Wisps,” Galatea said. “Each of them can only cast a very weak light spell on their own, but if they combine their power, then further amplify their collective threads of radiance with mine, we can weave them into a grid of interlocking holy filaments that can trap any spirit within. This net of light functions on the same principle as my chains, but it would be much bigger, capable of covering a wide area to contain that man’s movements.”

Viktor chuckled. “Good thing I’ve picked Domain of Light, huh?”

And Sebekton joined the laughter with his own booming rumble. “It seems things are going very well for us. Perhaps a bit too well. The ancient priest and I may not even need to do anything at all, as Galatea and her troops could handle the invasion by themselves without breaking a sweat.”

“This is just that man alone,” Viktor said. “He will be leading a team, remember, a group of seasoned adventurers who are undoubtedly handpicked for this mission. Also, Celeste and I suspect he might get some sort of power-up before he kicks our door in. He could possess new tricks that we do not yet know about.”

This was the greatest problem one might face when gearing up for an approaching battle: the lack of information. Viktor neither knew who Xander would bring with him, nor what kind of new abilities the bastard would have acquired. And without such knowledge, formulating a plan was very difficult.

“If there’s a way to gather intelligence...” he murmured.

[Actually, Master, there is one thing I haven’t reported to you. I didn’t deem it important enough to warrant your attention at the time, but now, it could prove to be something useful.]

“What is it?”

[It is about Yvonne. She once mentioned to Orloth that there is one adventurer from Iskora, one of those who currently occupy the west side of Daelin, who has been frequently visiting the caravan station lately.]

Viktor arched a brow. “Is that so? Is he trying to spy or something?”

[No, Master. The first time he came, it was simply to conduct his personal business. And the subsequent visits are... well, Yvonne believes that he has developed an infatuation with her, so he keeps finding excuses to return to the station again and again.]

Viktor suppressed a bark of a laugh. Yvonne was a professional spy who could effortlessly win people over. To anyone who didn’t know better, she was a young woman of great personality and, well, even greater other assets. So he wouldn’t blame the poor bastard for being helplessly smitten.

Now, the question was: how was he supposed to weaponize a lovestruck idiot? Should he have Yvonne seduce the guy, and then extract whatever information she could from him? Though, if he was just a normal, low-ranking adventurer, Viktor doubted that he knew anything actually important. Still, at the very least, he might know the ones Xander was going to take into the dungeon.

A thread worth pulling, Viktor thought. He would revisit it later.

“Now,” he said. “Let’s talk about where and how we would engage the invaders. I know, we’re doing this blind. We know nothing about the other members of the party, and what we do know about Xander himself might already be out of date. But since there’s nothing more productive to address at this moment, we plan with what we have and adjust as new information comes in.”

“Where do you intend to draw them into battle, Master?” Sebekton asked.

“Ideally, the fourth floor. Inside the Domain of Light, where Galatea and her soldiers are most at home and at their strongest. The problem is that those adventurers might not want to push that far. If their goal is simply to win the competition, they need only reach the third floor before coming back. We can’t count on them obliging us by going all the way to our preferred killing ground.”

“So we fight them on my floor, then?” Sebekton said, a predatory excitement glinting in his slit-pupiled eyes.

That was not a bad idea. The Iskora team would almost certainly move faster than the Daelin team, which meant they would reach the great tomb first. He could let them pass through, shepherding them down to the third floor where the real battle would take place. When the Daelin team eventually arrived at the tomb, he would just deny their entry to keep them from interfering in the fight. Khenemhotep could, for example, conjure a massive block of stone at the entrance as he had done before. The problem was—

“I want to fight them somewhere that is neither too confined nor too open,” Viktor said. “Open enough for us to use our advantage in numbers, and confined enough to restrict their movement.”

“The third floor presents a problem in that regard,” Galatea said. “It’s made up of small islands separated by a large body of water. An excellent environment for the aquatic creatures that dwell there, yes, but not a place where either Khenemhotep’s tomb guards or my own soldiers could perform at their best.”

“Not to mention,” Khenemhotep added. “If that foe you have spoken of, Sovereign of the Dungeon, be gifted with a power akin to teleportation, then he will have no trouble traversing the water realm.”

“You’re right,” Viktor said. “We fight them on the second floor, then.”

“The courtyard again?” Sebekton asked.

Viktor rubbed his chin. “No, we will do it inside this time. Let’s attack them in the Great Hallway before the Chamber of the Dead.”

“That long ramp?” Sebekton said with a grin. “Yes, that’s a fine place for an ambush.”

“We will hit them halfway up. Templars and tomb guards will form a pincer. Galatea, you take the top. Sebekton, you hold the bottom. High Priest, you and the undead mages provide magical support. The Wisps, meanwhile, weave their net of light across the walls and ceiling of the hallway, so that Xander can’t escape even if he tries to phase through the stone. I would also consider pulling in some of the other minions. The goblin hex specialists, for example...”

Viktor leaned back, letting the shape of it settle in his mind. The framework was solid, with room to build upon once he had more intelligence. Yes, the bones were already in place. He would add flesh to it later.
