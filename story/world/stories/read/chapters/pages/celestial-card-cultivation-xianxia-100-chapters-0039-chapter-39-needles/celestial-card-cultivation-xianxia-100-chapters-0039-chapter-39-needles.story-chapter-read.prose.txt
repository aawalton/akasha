“Teacher?”

“Yes, Jianyi?”

They were in Elder Sun’s workshop, processing a batch of uncommon essences. It was an easy chore to do, and he continued to be fascinated by the elder’s impeccable qi control.

“If you excuse me saying so, you seem… distracted?” Jianyi wasn’t sure whether it was really appropriate to say this, but in all the time he had now spent in the sect, and under the Elder’s roof, he had never stood on ceremony or given him the impression that he couldn’t speak his mind.

The elder chuckled, no laughed, and stopped starting on the next card. “Oh, Jianyi, do you know how long it has been since somebody said something like that to me?” The elder snorted in a decidedly undignified manner.

“But, yes, you are right. I am distracted.” The smile vanished from his lips, and he looked quite serious. “I have spent some more time in the city trying to root out those demonic cultists, but it turns out that whatever thread I pull, it unravels, and there seems to be a never-ending supply of them. We—I—have not been paying enough attention, maybe for decades, and something unpleasant has formed right under our noses.”

He scratched his head, grimacing.

“They are searching for something, planning something. Something large. And I don’t think I have yet reached the core group, the masterminds.”

The elder stared at the worktable without seeing. “You know, I could flatten that whole city without too much effort, maybe… but no!” he noticed the horrified look on Jianyi’s face. “Don’t worry, my young friend, don’t worry. It won’t come to this. I will use the scalpel, not the hammer, but mark my words, every single one of these demon worshippers will die.”

For the briefest of moments, Jianyi felt futility, futility so deep that he lost the will to…

It ended as quickly as it had begun. The elder restrained his aura, dropped the card he had held in his hand on the table, and left the room without any further words.

Could the elder truly eradicate Shiqiu City? Flatten it with all its citizens? Just like that? Had he done things like that? How powerful was his teacher? This did not sound like something a cultivator in the third realm could do. In the fourth? He didn’t know.

In the three months that Jianyi had known the elder, he had never seen him this emotional. How bad were things really if someone with his experience got frustrated like that?

So many questions, no answers. Lots of material for nightmares. Hopefully, without piercing purple eyes.

***

The first steps were easy. He thought they might be even easier than in the previous sessions. As hoped, running was good for him. That didn’t excuse Ruolan’s spiteful approach, but he did feel like he was making meaningful steps on the path to recovery.

He had used the few minutes and hours every day that he did have to himself to continue his meditation and qi control training. As part of the latter, he had also continued to prepare for the last session of opening his meridians.

Running lap after lap, he decided to make a deal with himself. The day that he would manage to complete 200 laps in four hours would be the day that he made his hopefully final use of the Martial Meridian Art—his stomach and heart meridians were waiting.

Unfortunately, a handful of disciples had approached him after the run, asking him to drop out of the group. He had politely declined. They had not moved beyond words. Yet.

***

Elder Qiao, wrinkled and rail-thin, stood in front of the disciples who had chosen to attend the class on beasts of the Empire. To Jianyi’s surprise, there were significantly fewer than in the initial lecture. He had found it quite interesting, but even if not, it did not seem wise to voluntarily give up on knowledge about the creatures that inhabited their world, and that would be their primary opponents outside of the Celestial Confrontation.

“What is the main reason to hunt spirit beasts, outside of protecting a village or town under attack?”

A few hands were raised, and the elder selected a young woman with long hair held in a bun with the help of a couple of jade sticks.

“Collecting cards?”

“Correct. You are absolutely correct. Of course, cards are not all; spirit beasts also hold many other wonderous things that can be used by skilled crafters, whether that is their hide, teeth, or organs. We will discuss those in this class, and I recommend that you acquire one of the compendiums I mentioned in our previous session so that you can always look things up.

“But yes, cards, to be precise, techniques! Why are cards so critical, beyond the most obvious use in our dantians?”

This time, Jianyi’s hand was the first to be raised, and the elder encouraged him to speak with a nod of his head.

“The creation of essences, which in turn power alchemical products of all kinds?”

“Ah, I haven’t given these lectures in dozens of years; I missed out. This is so much fun!” the elder cackled in excitement. “Exactly, essences. There will be lectures on alchemy later in your studies, but it is important to remember that there is nothing, absolutely nothing in the world of cultivation that works without what spirit beasts have to give us. None of the pills that you use to clean your meridians, none of the potions you drink when you are sick, none of the artifacts you might wield when on the battlefield.

“That is, however, not a call for wanton slaughter of those proud creatures, not at all. It is a reminder that all of our existences are based on living in balance with each other. How could there ever be a rare or even legendary card if you go out and slaughter every Storm Spirit Hawk in their nest?”

The old man looked at them seriously, his usual mirth gone.

Jianyi nodded to himself. That was one of the differences between a plane in balance and one where Wuxing was out of kilter. Just like a tiger that hunted every prey in their domain would starve later, cultivators would stop ascending without spirit beasts—and at the same time, mortals would live in constant fear if cultivators did not protect them from individual beasts or beast tides.

“Before we end the session today, how do we extract techniques from spirit beasts?”

Jianyi had seen Elder Sun do it, even from humans, but he didn’t know how specifically it had worked.

Deng Shuhua raised her hand and was selected. “A spirit needle!”

The elder raised his hand, and a small silver artifact appeared. It was about as long as a finger but thinner, with a pointed tip and engravings that were difficult to see from a distance.

“Exactly. A spirit needle. That is the most common and convenient way to do so. It is, without a doubt, one of the most important investments any cultivator who plans to travel the Empire or fight in the Celestial Confrontation can make. One thing to note, though, while the spirit needle is what we all use, it is not the only way. If your control over qi is high enough, you can do it without one. It is exhausting and, after a pitched battle, not necessarily what people want to spend time on, but it is possible. There are techniques in the library if you are interested. I can only recommend it.”

Jianyi took a note to read up on those if he ever found the time. Maybe it was something that could also help him on his path towards controlling five qi threads at the same time.

***

At the last spear training—or rather running—session, most of the disciples in his group had stayed to the very end, some even until he had finished the 200 laps, well after the four hours had elapsed.

There wasn’t much conversation going on, but Jianyi had noticed that there were now two clear groups. One that had focused their frustration mostly on Ruolan, and he had gained quite a bit of respect for never giving up and always making it to 200 laps with those. Then there was a second group that showed their anger with him more and more.

His progress had also been noted by both groups, though.

176 laps around the training field had he managed as Ruolan called the time. Some interpreted this as good progress, others as a sign that he would never make it.

The Jade group stood close by, and he could see Lu Shan shaking his head and grinning at him. He raised his hand with two fingers. Probably trying to remind him of the two more duels they would have to fight. Deng Shuhua and Tang Miyao stood side by side, looking at him encouragingly.

To his surprise, Elder Fang raised a finger, indicating for him to stop. Despite sweating heavily and feeling sharp pain in his lungs, he, of course, followed the demand and nodded politely.

“Honorable Elder.” It was more of a gasp.

The elder spoke quietly, so his words would not carry. “You bring shame on Elder Sun. I can transfer you to Amber, where the demands are less strict; it would stop the embarrassment for your teacher and yourself.”

Jianyi had to swallow hard. Had he embarrassed Elder Sun? Had he behaved in an undignified way?

That was the opposite of what he wanted to do.

But had he? Had he not given it his all? Had he not gone beyond what his ravaged body could deliver? Had he not improved every day?

Most importantly, did he believe that Elder Sun would care even for a moment? No. He did not.

There was no doubt in his mind that Elder Sun wanted him to follow his path and become as strong as possible.

Despite the stitches in his side and the pain in his lungs and neck, Jianyi straightened up.

“I am sorry if you feel my behavior was anything but dignified. Would you be willing to enlighten me as to what I have done wrong?”

“Careful disciple, times have changed, but disrespect towards an elder will be met with swift punishment,” the elder hissed, eyes squinted.

Jianyi always respected his seniors but did not yield to bullies.

He bowed stiffly. “Honorable elder, I did not mean to show disrespect. It was nothing but a genuine question. You said I bring shame to my teacher, and I am devastated to learn that to be the case. But I cannot improve without you letting me know what it is that I have done.”

“You are a disgrace. Elder Sun has declined to teach some of the greatest talents of this sect and now… you?! A sniveling lay about who cannot even complete 200 laps in four hours. Something I could have done when I was half your age!” Red spots appeared on the otherwise unmarred, beautiful face of the elder.

The badly hidden fury of Elder Fang was unexpected and made Jianyi wary, but things had become a bit clearer. He decided that to extend the conversation would bring only pain to his head. He bowed again. “I apologize, and I am grateful for your offer to move me into Amber.”

The elder continued to stare at him furiously. He needed something to appease him. “If you would grace me with a little bit of patience, I will accept your proposal by the end of the week if I haven’t made it to 200 laps by then.” It rushed out of him.

His gut clenched; that had not been thought through. He didn’t know if he could make it. He had only three more days to close the gap, and his progress had slowed down slightly. No matter. He had said it, and there was no taking it back now.

The elder snorted in disgust and left with a whirl.

Jianyi took a deep breath and returned to complete the outstanding laps.

He had only made it a few paces when he heard steps behind him, and Shuhua and Miyao catching up to run by his side.

He didn’t have the strength to greet them properly, so he just nodded.

“What did the elder want from you?” Shuhua asked, not struggling at all with the pace of the run.

Talking while running was not an option for Jianyi at that moment. It took a few steps for him to find sufficient breath to give a one-word reply. “Amber.”

Miyao, on his other side, scrunched up her nose in confusion. “What about Amber? Oh, does he want to move you to group Amber?”

177. He had completed the lap and focused his energy on getting a semi-coherent sentence out. “Wants me volunteer. Disgrace teacher.” The pain in his lungs threatened to overwhelm him.

“Oh, is this the Elder Sun that he mentioned on the day of the assessment? Is he your teacher?” Miyao asked.

He nodded.

“Why don’t you tell him that you are hurt and that you can easily do this once you are back in shape?” Miyao asked incredulously.

“Knows,” Jianyi gasped.

“He knows?” Miyao sounded offended. “Have you talked to your teacher about this?”

It took half a lap for Jianyi to find the breath to respond. “Will solve myself.” A few paces later. “200 in three days or Amber.”

“That’s what he forced you to do? You are clearly in pain!” Miyao seemed scandalized.

“My… idea.”

“Well, that seems like a bad idea. We originally came to ask you to spar with us. Probably better after you have made it in three days. Anything we can do to help you?”

Jianyi thought about it as he completed the 180th lap. He was somewhat irritated by the offer to spar. He didn’t want any entanglements with the other disciples, but the offer to help him was kind. It must be because Miyao felt some sort of obligation. Maybe she was as keen as Lu Shan to balance her karmic debt.

Shuhua’s driving force was harder to understand, but it seemed more and more likely that she genuinely was looking for friends. An intention that Jianyi struggled to relate to.

“Thanks. No. Will manage.” Then, with the last of his breath: “Need focus.”

Thankfully, the young women understood and left him in peace, but did not stop running and completed the final twenty laps with him.
