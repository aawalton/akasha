Chapter V.XLIV (5.44) - A Decision

The sight of the crone in Zayne’s memories, unchanged after all this time, haunted Kizu for the next few days. During class, his mind wandered back to the image, distracting him. He wasn’t certain if it was something she planted in his mind, or if she truly existed all the way back then. Either prospect was daunting. The slightest brush against his arm caused him to jolt, as if awakening from a nightmare.

“What’s wrong with you?” Aoi asked.

“What do you mean?” Kizu mumbled back.

“Kizu, I’ve just asked you the exact same question eight times. I was starting to wonder if you’d been cursed or replaced by a flesh golem.”

“A… flesh golem?”

“I’ve been reading about them recently. It took me a while to decipher a portion of the gnome necromancer’s grimoire but I’ve finally cracked the code. It’s all about helping a friend of his with flesh abnormalities and creating progeny without standard procreative methods.”

“Like the clone?”

“Different, these are all designed to carry the genes while existing as separate, non-identical, entities. Flesh golems were heavily discussed for several pages. It has so many interesting theories! There's also this strange method of shaving an existing soul and melding it with neutralized soul energy to create a being like a ghost, though not technically undead because the primary soul shaving comes from a living being and can continue to grow and mature, separate from the main body.”

“Do you have something you wish to share with the class?” Professor Grove asked, interrupting Aoi’s undead rambling. The wisp floated over the placid pond at the center of the courtyard used for their Divination B class. “Whispering is not normally a practice employed by those in meditation. Is this a new technique I am unfamiliar with?”

The rest of the class nervously glanced in Aoi and Kizu’s direction. They were supposed to all be entering a deep trance in an attempt to perform an astral connection, the ability to project one’s consciousness out into the world. Kizu, having been familiarized with the concept due to Anata’s ability to force people into an astral form, had accomplished the feat soon after receiving instructions by Professor Grove. The rest of the class, however, still struggled with the spell and the time remained dedicated to the spell. Plus, as Professor Grove had accurately pointed out after his first success, just because he’d managed to cast the spell didn’t mean it was flawless and without need of vigorous practice. He could eject his consciousness, but it required a minute or two of meditation and, even then, he could only travel about twenty meters before his mind snapped back into his body.

“Perhaps they were engaged in a ritualistic chant,” Stina offered. “My coven occasionally uses chants as a means to unite our power to potent levels, channeled in powerful spells that even the mountains would tremble under!”

“While I appreciate the insight shared to class, I am familiar with chants,” Grove replied dryly. “My question was rhetorical. You two both stop your discussion posthaste or you will be ejected from the class. Meditation requires concentration and that is made difficult when your peers next to you are discussing the nuances of flesh golems.”

Both Kizu and Aoi bowed their heads and muttered an apology.

While they spent the next twenty minutes of class in silent meditation, Kizu found his mind wandering to what Aoi had said about augmented soul creations. It didn’t sound like anything he knew about. The only creatures he’d encountered created by the necromancer of Owl’s Respite were the ship’s skeleton guards, the phantasm ghost that watched over the laboratory in the gnomish mines, and the jellyfish that drifted in the lake Owl’s Respite rested on. None of those sounded like progeny. Two of them were clearly undead and the other one was a jellyfish.

Of course, nobody ever said it had to be human. Kizu supposed that maybe the massive magical jellyfish could be a result of strange experimentation. It was completely outside his field of study.

“The stars have stated you have interest in flesh golems,” Stina said, after the bell dismissed their class. “I have experience piecing together corpses of my foes for one of my former master’s more elaborate curses. Do you require assistance, Kaga Kizu? You have supplied me with an excellent protégé. My strength and knowledge remain at your disposal.”

“No,” Kizu said. “It was a random tangent Aoi was going off on.”

“Piecing together corpses?” Aoi perked up beside him. “You’re experienced with—”

“Begone!” Stina flicked her hands in Aoi’s direction in a shooing gesture. “I have no intention to assist your dark pleasures!”

Aoi blinked. “Is this because of how I—”

“Oh, how your demeanor shifts upon seeing an egg to swallow, you viper! I forget no slight! Begone, else I banish you through force forevermore!”

“Fine, whatever,” Aoi muttered. The princess slunk away, her shoulders slumped.

“She didn’t mean any harm,” Kizu said to Stina once Aoi was around a corner. “She sometimes comes off a bit brash, is all.”

“Let us speak no more of those spiders who spin insults in place of web. I wish to instead discuss the progress of my disciple.”

“Mae?” Kizu asked. The last he’d heard, they’d been taking the whole witch apprentice situation relatively well. From what he’d gathered from both Anata and Mae, the Kitsune girl liked Stina quite a bit and they’d been taking their studies slowly, with Stina wishing for her apprentice to build a foundation of basic knowledge before delving deeper into the mysteries of witch covens. “Is everything fine? Something I need to know?”

“As I stated at the start of our conversation, my apprentice is more than adequate. While she fails spectacularly in any subject she lacks interest in, thankfully my lessons have not been among those disinterests.”

“Then what do you need to speak to me about?”

“Let us first converge on the dining arena. My body requires subsistence.”

Kizu didn’t know why the witch referred to the dining hall as an ‘arena’ but decided against questioning it. Originally, he had planned to forgo lunch in favor of visiting Comet’s stable with Anata. It was meant to be a surprise visit though, so his niece wasn’t expecting him. Anata wouldn’t be able to leave the schoolhouse during lunchtime without him to escort her. He decided that her spending a bit of extra time with her classmates might do her some good. He vainly hoped that more exposure might mend a few of the bridges her clique had burned with the rest of the class.

Today’s menu came from Edgeland. Many of the students were tearing into ribs slathered with sauce, the thick liquid dribbling down their chins and hands, staining their hands. Kizu, upon searching through the menu, selected a chicken pie. It appeared before him, steam curling off it.

“I trust the last favor dealt for you and your soul companion went as expected?” Stina asked. She selected roasted horse meat for herself. After muttering something that sounded backwards to Kizu, the meat in front of her suddenly split into finger-sized chunks.

“Yes. The girl’s plants were cursed and she turned against Emilia,” Kizu said. “But don’t call Ione my ‘soul companion.’ That makes it sound like you’re talking about Mort.”

“Ah, yes. I oft forget of the existence of your familiar. Forgive this oversight on my end.” Stina slowly chewed some of her horse meat. She appeared to be savoring it.

“What is it you wanted to tell me about Mae?” Kizu asked. “Are you worried about her studies at the local schoolhouse?”

Stina continued to chew for half a minute. “No.”

Rather than let his irritation with the conversation show, Kizu decided to exercise patience. Stina asked him here, she’d get to her point eventually. He instead used his knife to cut into the meat pie, increasing the steam from the dish tenfold. He cast an air elemental spell to blow on the food, cooling it off.

“My mentee’s mother is an issue,” Stina finally said. “She clams up like an eclipsed moon on some topics involving her progenitor. Other times, she will insist on sharing what she believes her mother’s opinion on something would be. Always at the most inconvenient times.”

“Her mother is missing,” Kizu replied around a chunk of carrot that was still too hot and burned the inside of his cheek. To neutralize the heat, he took a swig of a tart crabapple juice offered with today’s dishes. It stung his burned cheek.

“May I ask where she disappeared to? Did she vanish in the night like a shadow into darkness?”

“She was abducted and brought into the World Dungeon.” Kizu needed to be careful about what he revealed. Stina was a friend, but these secrets were a burden to place on someone.

“I see.” Stina tapped her sharp, black nails on the mess hall table. “I understand why she might find that distressing.”

“So, yeah. She’s not in the best place right now.”

Kizu took a sliver of information out of his compartmentalized mind and used the knowledge to heal the inside of his mouth. While Zayne had never been a healer, the warrior had thoroughly studied human anatomy after his experiences with the Genie. He felt the wound smooth over.

“Will you offer me further insight into my pupil’s mind?” Stina asked. “Or am I to flounder about, like an eye-less sailor lost in the breaking waves of the sea?”

“I’m… losing track of these similes.”

“Fine. For the sake of clarity, I will shift my vernacular to that of my common peer in this academy of magic.” Stina leaned back, crossed her arms, and met Kizu’s eyes. When she spoke again, her voice lacked Stina’s usual grandiose boom. It sounded almost… sad. “Do you have plans to do anything about Mae’s missing mother?”

This was a subject Kizu had been putting out of his mind for the last few months. He rationally knew that going down into the World Dungeon in an attempt to rescue Kumiho would be a death sentence. He’d certainly grown in power over the last year, but not to the point where he felt confident facing Otochi in the Blood Lord’s lair surrounded by bloodspawn. He still remembered how helpless he’d been in his past fight with Anata’s father. Perhaps it was irrational, but there was a part of him that thought he’d only escaped with his life because Otochi had allowed him to do so. And then, of course, there was his doppelganger down there with an arsenal of summons and hexes Kizu was utterly in the blind about.

And yet, if he didn’t do something, who would? He was the one equipped with the bell, necklace, and atlas. No one else had those tools. They were powerful beyond belief, and yet the bell and atlas were simply gathering dust in the trunk under his hammock on Owl’s Respite.

Kumiho might not even still be alive. If she was dead though, who else could be relied on to avenge her? He decided it was time to stop putting off difficult tasks.

“I will handle it,” Kizu said.
