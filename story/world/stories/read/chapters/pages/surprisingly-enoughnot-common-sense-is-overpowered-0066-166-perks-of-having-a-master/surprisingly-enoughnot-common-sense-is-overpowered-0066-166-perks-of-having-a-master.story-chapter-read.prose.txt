“Now that we have that out of the way, let's talk about alchemy.” Ling Xin settled into her seat and looked at Ning with interest. “How much do you know about it?”

“I purchased a First-Grade, Low-Tier alchemist inheritance some time ago, Master, so I'm familiar with the basics. As for actual practice, I've only occasionally helped an alchemist friend with his work.”

As he spoke, Ning retrieved the inheritance jade slip from his storage pouch and handed it over.

“Oh, this one?” Ling Xin glanced at the jade slip and nodded in recognition. “It's a fairly basic inheritance, but it covers most of the theoretical knowledge a beginner needs. How much have you learned?”

“I've memorized everything, Master.”

“Good, good. Then we can move on.”

Ning blinked. “Master, aren't you going to test me?”

“Eh? Test you?” Ling Xin looked genuinely surprised, as though the possibility had never occurred to her.

Ning stared at his new master for a moment before remembering that this was her first time formally teaching a disciple.

...Should I be worried?

After all, he'd spent countless hours studying that inheritance, and he'd expected at least a few questions to confirm his understanding. But looking at Ling Xin's expression, he had a feeling that if he insisted, she might actually start asking him how a master was supposed to conduct a test.

“Never mind, Master. Please continue.”

“Alright!” Ling Xin smiled, apparently satisfied with how smoothly her first lesson was progressing.

She studied Ning for a moment before continuing.

“Hmm, your spiritual attributes are varied, but the alchemy I practice mainly relies on Fire qi. Will that be a problem?”

“No, Master. But are there other methods of alchemy?” Ning asked curiously. “The inheritance I studied only mentioned refining pills using spiritual flames.”

“Of course there are. Fire-based alchemy is the most common, but there are methods that rely on Water qi, Wood qi, and even other attributes.”

Ling Xin seemed pleased by his interest.

“Water-based alchemy, for example, is particularly suited to large-scale refinement. It takes much longer than ordinary fire refinement, sometimes several months or even a year for a single batch, but the resulting medicines generally have gentler properties and much lower toxicity. The yield can also be very impressive.”

Ning nodded thoughtfully.

A year to refine a single batch of pills sounded terribly inefficient at first, but if the method could produce enough medicine at once, perhaps it wasn't such a bad arrangement.

“Wood-based alchemy is even more interesting,” Ling Xin continued. “Some ancient inheritances describe cultivating special spiritual trees that bear fruits with effects similar to medicinal pills. Unfortunately, those inheritances are extremely rare. I've never personally encountered one.”

Ning's interest immediately increased.

Growing pills on trees?

Now that was something worth investigating.

“As for Metal and Earth qi, there are records of alchemical methods associated with them, but most of those inheritances have been lost over the years. With the changes in heaven and earth, many ancient techniques have gradually disappeared.”

Ning listened carefully, finding the subject far more interesting than he'd expected. His purchased inheritance had taught him the basic principles of pill refinement, but it had barely touched upon the wider possibilities of alchemy.

“Anyway, Little Ning, there's something I'd like to ask you,” Ling Xin said once she'd finished explaining.

Ning paused.

Little Ning?

His lips twitched slightly at the unfamiliar nickname.

Perhaps noticing his expression, Ling Xin coughed. “Don't look at me like that. Despite my appearance, I'm actually much older than you, you know. It's just that I cultivated rather quickly, and my appearance ended up staying like this.”

She glanced down at herself and sighed in disappointment.

Ning was momentarily speechless.

This was the first time he'd encountered someone who sounded genuinely dissatisfied with having cultivated too quickly.

Truly, the troubles of talented people are beyond my understanding.

“Anyway,” Ling Xin continued, seemingly eager to change the subject, “would you mind if I used my divine sense to probe your body?”

“...Probe my body?”

Ning froze.

For a brief moment, several rather unpleasant possibilities crossed his mind.

Under normal circumstances, he wouldn't have immediately suspected his own master of wanting to conduct human experiments. Unfortunately, their first meeting had been quite explosive, pun intended.

It was difficult to blame him for being cautious.

Seeing his expression, Ling Xin hurriedly waved her hands.

“It's nothing strange! I just want to see whether you possess a special physique. I've never encountered someone with all five spiritual attributes before, so I was wondering whether you might have something like the Five Elements Spirit Body.”

“Oh, I see.”

Ning relaxed slightly.

Special physiques were another form of cultivation talent, separate from spiritual roots. Some people were born with bodies naturally suited to particular Dao paths, allowing them to cultivate certain techniques with unusual ease or develop abilities that ordinary cultivators could only obtain through years of practice.

Ling Xin herself possessed the Pure Fire Spirit Body, which gave her an exceptional affinity for Fire qi and greatly increased the power of her fire techniques.

There had even been debates over whether the abilities granted by such physiques should be considered innate supernatural powers.

“Then please be careful, Master.” Ning extended his hand.

“Don't worry. It'll only take a moment.”

Ling Xin took his wrist and gently sent a strand of divine sense into his body.

“Hmm?”

Her expression gradually became thoughtful.

After examining him for several breaths, she withdrew her divine sense with a faint frown.

“Strange. Your body is quite robust for someone at your cultivation, but I couldn't find any signs of a special physique. With your spiritual attributes, I thought you might possess the Five Elements Spirit Body.”

“I-is that so?”

Ning allowed a trace of disappointment to appear on his face.

Of course, he already knew the answer. His panel had never indicated that he possessed any special physique, but since his master had gone to the trouble of examining him, he could hardly respond as though he'd known the result all along.

Ling Xin seemed to mistake his expression for genuine disappointment.

“Don't look so discouraged! Special physiques are rare to begin with, and having one isn't the only way to become powerful.” She patted her chest confidently. “Besides, you're my disciple now. I'll make sure you don't fall behind anyone!”

“Thank you, Master.”

Ning smiled.

Whatever else could be said about Ling Xin, she certainly didn't lack confidence.

“Now then, you're only at the beginning of the sixth stage of Qi Condensation,” she continued, returning to the subject of alchemy. “Without spiritual sense, you'll have difficulty handling more complicated refinement processes, so we'll start with Low-Tier pills.”

She paused, apparently considering where to begin.

“Oh, right! I noticed that you don't have a proper flame-control technique suitable for alchemy. Why don't I teach you one first?”

Ning's eyes brightened. “That would be great."

This was precisely why having a master was so valuable. Apart from providing guidance and protection, they could also pass down techniques that ordinary disciples might never be able to access.

And most importantly, it was free.

“Good!” Ling Xin placed her hands on her hips, looking rather pleased with herself. “Then I'll teach you the signature fire-control technique of our Spiritual Flower Peak, suitable for Qi Condensation cultivators.”

She turned toward a nearby pile of jade slips and began rummaging through them.

After a moment, she triumphantly pulled one out.

“Found it!”

Ning accepted the jade slip and examined the name recorded within.

Crimson Flame Control Technique.

“Read through it first,” Ling Xin instructed. “I'll take care of the mess from my earlier experiment while you familiarize yourself with the contents.”

Before Ning could respond, she disappeared in a flash.

He glanced toward the ruined laboratory, then lowered his attention to the jade slip.

The more he read, the more surprised he became.

From the overview alone, he could tell that the Crimson Flame Control Technique was no ordinary beginner's art. It contained five distinct stages of cultivation, each building upon the principles established by the previous one.

A technique with such a complete progression was almost certainly at least Spirit Grade.

“So generous...” Ning muttered.

Apart from Pure Eyes, whose grade remained unknown, this was the first Spirit-Grade technique he'd ever obtained.

And his master had simply handed it over during their first proper lesson.

Having a powerful master really is wonderful.

A short while later, Ling Xin reappeared.

Her clothes were still perfectly clean, and when Ning glanced toward the laboratory, he discovered that the entire mess had vanished.

She'd simply stuffed everything into her storage pouch.

...I suppose that's one way to clean.

“So, Little Ning, what do you think?” she asked cheerfully.

Ning decided to ignore the nickname for now.

“It's quite impressive, Master. Rather than simply producing flames, the technique focuses on controlling their shape, intensity, and circulation. It can be used for pill refinement, but the principles seem applicable to refining materials and handling spiritual herbs as well.”

“Oh!” Ling Xin's eyes brightened. “You've already understood that much?”

She straightened her posture and cleared her throat. “Ahem. Then allow me to explain the five stages properly.”

Ning nodded attentively.

“The first stage is called Ember Awakening. The cultivator gathers Fire qi and nurtures a stable flame seed, establishing the foundation for all subsequent transformations. Once formed, the flame can be sustained and adjusted without constantly recreating it.”

Ling Xin raised a finger, and a tiny vermilion flame appeared above her palm.

“Think of it as giving the flame a foundation of its own.”

Ning studied the flame thoughtfully.

The description in the jade slip had been filled with references to awakening the sleeping fire and nurturing its spiritual nature, but the practical purpose was straightforward enough.

So the first step is establishing a stable flame that can be maintained throughout refinement.

That made sense. If an alchemist couldn't even maintain a consistent heat source, there was little point discussing more advanced techniques.

“The second stage is Twin Flame Manifestation,” Ling Xin continued.

The small flame above her palm divided into two, each moving independently.

“At this stage, you learn to divide your flame while maintaining control over both portions. It becomes especially useful when refining ingredients that require different temperatures or need to be processed separately.”

Ning watched the two flames circle one another.

Independent heat control, huh?

It reminded him of managing several cooking fires at once, except that each flame had to respond directly to the cultivator's qi.

Naturally, that would require far more concentration than maintaining a single flame.

“The third stage, Thousand-Layer Flame Seal, builds upon that principle. Instead of dividing the flame into only two portions, you learn to form multiple layers, each with a different intensity.”

Several thin rings of fire appeared around Ling Xin's hand.

“Some medicinal ingredients are delicate and must be refined slowly, while others require intense heat. With sufficient control, you can handle these differences within the same furnace.”

Ning nodded.

That was particularly useful. One of the difficulties he'd noticed while helping Su Fan was that different ingredients didn't always respond well to the same temperature.

So this stage focuses on precise temperature distribution.

“The fourth stage is Vermilion Sun Fire Wheel.”

The rings began revolving, forming a miniature wheel of fire.

“By circulating the flames continuously, you can distribute heat throughout the furnace and prevent it from accumulating unevenly. Of course, the circulation must remain stable, or you'll only make things worse.”

Ning watched the rotating flames with growing interest.

Controlled heat circulation...

He could already imagine how useful that would be when refining pills that required long periods of steady heating.

“And finally,” Ling Xin declared, looking rather proud of the display, “the fifth stage is Heavenly Core Convergence!”

The revolving flames suddenly contracted, gathering into a single brilliant point above her palm.

The surrounding air shimmered from the concentrated heat.

“At this stage, the cultivator brings the scattered flames together, concentrating their power while maintaining complete control over the resulting fire. Once you've mastered it, even a small flame can possess tremendous intensity.”

Ning stared at the brilliant point of fire.

So the final stage is concentrating the flame's energy without losing control of it.

That sounded simple enough when expressed in those terms, but he could tell from the demonstration that achieving it would be anything but easy.

After all, compressing spiritual fire into a smaller space would increase its intensity, and any instability could have rather unpleasant consequences.

Especially inside an alchemy furnace.

...I should probably make sure my furnace is sturdy enough before attempting that.

Ling Xin dispersed the flame and looked at him expectantly.

“When a cultivator reaches this level, their flame begins to approach the essence of true spiritual fire. Of course, the technique can be cultivated even further, but that's something you can worry about in the future.”

Ning nodded, mentally arranging what he'd learned.

The names were certainly grand, but beneath the poetic descriptions lay a surprisingly systematic method of controlling spiritual fire.

Whoever created this technique really knew what they were doing.

“So,” Ling Xin suddenly asked, trying to look wise, “My dear disciple, what have you understood?”

She remembered Ning mentioning earlier that masters were supposed to test their disciples, so she immediately applied the idea.

She was very clever like that.

Ning ignored Ling Xin’s slightly smug expression and calmly explained his understanding.

As she listened to his explanation using straightforward and practical terms, Ling Xin blinked repeatedly.

Why did it sound completely different from what she had just explained?

Yet, when she thought about it carefully, the essence of the matter seemed to be the same.

For a moment, she was speechless.

Why had her majestic explanation suddenly become so mundane when spoken by the little disciple of hers?

“A-ahem.” She cleared her throat. “Very good. Your comprehension is quite impressive.”

Fortunately, she wasn't the sort of person to dwell on such minor matters.

“Now then! Your first task is to master Ember Awakening. Once you can maintain a stable flame and control its intensity properly, we'll begin practicing pill refinement.”

“I understand.”

“And after that...” Ling Xin leaned forward, lowering her voice mysteriously. “I'll teach you the super-secret, incredibly awesome legacy of our alchemy lineage!”

Ning raised an eyebrow.

Super-secret, incredibly awesome legacy?

He looked at his master, who seemed quite pleased with how mysterious she'd managed to sound.

Well, considering that she'd casually handed him a Spirit-Grade technique during their first lesson, whatever she intended to teach him next was probably worth looking forward to.

“Thank you, Master. I'll work hard.”

“Good, good!”

Ling Xin smiled proudly.

Her first teaching session had gone wonderfully.

As expected of me.

Just as she was congratulating herself, Ning suddenly remembered something.

“Oh, right, Master. I almost forgot. I prepared a small gift for you as thanks for accepting me as your disciple.”

He retrieved a neatly packed box from his storage pouch and handed it over.

Inside were several varieties of sweets and cakes he'd prepared the previous night.

As someone who followed the Dao of Preparation, Ning had naturally made inquiries about his new master after hearing the rather ominous stories circulating among the senior disciples.

That was also why he hadn't been particularly surprised by Ling Xin's youthful appearance.

After all, this was a cliché xianxia world. Elderly cultivators who looked like little girls were practically a genre tradition.

What he hadn't expected was to be greeted by an explosion the moment he arrived.

No amount of preparation can account for everything, I suppose.

Between the chaos of their first meeting and everything that had followed, he'd almost forgotten about the gift.

“Oh!” Ling Xin's eyes immediately lit up when she opened the box.

“Sweets!”

She picked up one of the cakes and examined it curiously.

“I've never seen this kind before. Did you make these yourself?”

“Yes, Master. I hope they're to your liking.”

Ling Xin took a bite. Her expression brightened almost immediately.

“Mmm! Delicious!”

She quickly reached for another.

Ning watched her with a satisfied smile.

As expected. My information was correct.

His new master had an undeniable sweet tooth.

And seeing her happily enjoying the cakes while looking like a young girl, Ning couldn't help thinking that the whole scene suited her appearance a little too well.

Naturally, he kept that observation to himself.

After all, there were some things even a filial disciple shouldn't say to his master.
