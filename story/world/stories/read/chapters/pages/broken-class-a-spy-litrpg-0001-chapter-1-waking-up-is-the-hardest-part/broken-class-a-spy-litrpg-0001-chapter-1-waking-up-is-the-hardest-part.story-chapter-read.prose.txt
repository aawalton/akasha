For centuries, Earth stood alone.

No signals from the stars. No alien life. No second Eden. Just a fragile blue dot, in a dead, echoing void.

To us, it was a miracle.

But one man saw through the illusion of a prison dressed as a sanctuary.

A cage with just enough sky to keep the hope alive.

The wars, the poverty, the heartbreak were no accident. It was all part of a hidden framework designed to keep humanity suffering. Somehow, human emotions were being harvested. Pain, more than anything else, was drawn out and converted into energy. That energy was funneled into something still hidden from view.

He saw the evidence in black-site archives. He watched feeds from satellites that officially didn’t exist. He found machines buried deep beneath cities, machines that were harvesting grief.

And in one of those machines, beneath a condemned hospital in Bucharest, he found something real.

A living fragment known as the Heartshard. It was part of a hidden construct that kept Earth cut off from the stars. This artifact absorbed pain and unrest, using them as a fuel source to maintain the planetary seal.

He stole it.

He ran.

He killed four agents on the way out, escaped three drones, and crossed two borders in forty-eight hours with no sleep and a bullet in his shoulder.

And he still believed Rachel would help.

She met him at a private airstrip in the Austrian Alps, snow falling as her soft eyes met his and her voice trembled with something close to regret.

“You don’t understand what you’ve done,” she said.

“I do,” he replied. “I am breaking the cycle.”

Then the gunman stepped out from behind the fuel truck.

Too late.

He crushed the artifact in his palm.

The world howled, the sky unraveled, and the veil between dimensions gave way.

And something slipped through the gap.

His body collapsed, but he was already gone, pulled beyond the walls of the cage.

For the first time in human history, Someone got out.

For several long, painful minutes, he couldn’t think, couldn’t move, couldn’t even groan dramatically. All he could do was lie there and exist as a meat sack full of throbbing pain.

His head pulsed like it had its own heartbeat - slow, heavy, and nauseating. A low ringing screeched at the edge of his awareness, rising and falling in waves. Every breath sent a flare of pressure behind his eyes, like something inside his skull was swelling and pushing outward.

Then slowly, methodically training took over. Not the kind that teaches you to bake cookies or ride a bike. No, the fun kind: advanced torture resistance, courtesy of the Agency.

Breathe. Center. Push past the agony.

He focused on the basics. Count the seconds between breaths. Wiggle a toe. Flex a finger. His tongue felt like it had been rolled in dust and left to dry, and the sour taste of bile burned in his throat. He parted his lips and let out a faint, cracked groan. Proof that his vocal cords still worked. Okay. Not paralyzed. Not dead. That was a start.

Eventually, he forced his eyes open.

Mud floor. Dirt walls. Air that smelled like sweat and crushed dreams. Definitely not a hospital. Definitely not dead either - unless hell had gone through budget cuts.

Light stabbed into his eyes. He flinched, blinking hard. Everything was too bright and too blurry, like someone had smeared grease on the lens of his reality. Shapes shifted. Shadows danced. He clenched his jaw and waited for the world to settle.

Worse, his body felt... wrong. Gone were the muscles honed by a decade of espionage and near-death workouts. In their place was a frame so frail and malnourished it looked like someone had put a skeleton in a skin suit and called it a day.

He flexed his hand. Thin fingers. Calloused. Weak.

Well, shit. This is new.

The last thing he remembered: slipping into a condemned hospital in Bucharest, uncovering a machine powered by grief, and stealing a living fragment called the Heartshard. He’d found proof that Earth was a cage, and that someone was feeding on the pain. So he destroyed the Heartshard before they could kill him. It was his final act to destabilize the seal and give Earth a chance to escape its fate.

A voice interrupted his spiral - gentle, worried, female.

“Arthur… thank the stars, you’re awake.”

A middle-aged woman rushed into the hut and dropped to her knees beside him. “We were so worried. You wouldn’t wake up… and your breathing, it kept slowing. We thought we were going to lose you, son.

Your father… he was with you every moment he wasn’t down in the mine. He never stopped worrying. I told you to stay away from the Guardians, didn’t I? Why didn’t you listen?”

He blinked.

Not because of what she said. But because somehow miraculously he understood her. The words weren’t in any of the thirty languages he’d mastered. But the meaning came through crystal clear, like the world came with auto-translate and zero regard for privacy settings.

New world? He didn’t know yet. But something was definitely off. The body swap, the foreign-yet-understandable language... it all felt suspiciously familiar. He’d read enough isekai novels to recognize the checklist.

Still, jumping to conclusions wouldn’t help. For now, he’d pretend this was normal.

He didn’t recognize her face. Not even a flicker of familiarity. But the relief in her eyes, the way she clutched his hand, trembling - told him she knew him.

He swallowed, throat raw. “I… I’m sorry. I don’t know who you are.”

Her smile faltered. “Arthur… It's me. It’s your mother.”

He blinked, confusion painted on thick. “I… my name is Arthur?”

Her breath hitched.

“I don’t remember anything,” he said, softer this time, like admitting it hurt. “Not even… me. Everything in my head feels scrambled.” He hesitated, then glanced at her with wide, searching eyes. “Please… tell me what’s going on.”

He added a few well-practiced tears for effect. Training had covered emotional manipulation, too.

She started sobbing. “Oh… my sweet boy. I’m so sorry. We couldn’t afford a healer. Your father and I, we tried to get help. We begged Rajak, but he wouldn’t hear a word. Said you weren’t worth it. That your work didn’t matter.”

Arthur filed that name away: Rajak. A man worth punching, apparently.

“He made your dad take your shifts,” the woman continued, “said if we didn’t, he’d have you thrown out of the compound. Left to die like an animal.”

Arthur’s throat tightened. He didn’t speak right away. Instead, he reached out and placed a trembling hand over hers.

“Please… just tell me what you can. Everything. Maybe it’ll help me remember.”

So she did. Her name was Mary. Her husband was Paul. Both were born into slavery here at a copper mine called Darvaza - a miserable pit of labor and suffering. Arthur was their only surviving child. Nineteen years old. Beaten nearly to death for daring to look a Guardian in the eye while speaking.

Rajak, apparently, took offense to eye contact.

Arthur let her ramble while he absorbed the situation. Eventually, he feigned exhaustion and closed his eyes.

Inside, his mind was a whirlwind.

He had escaped one prison, only to wake up in another that felt even worse.

But one thing was certain: he wasn’t going to stay a slave.

He’d already died once trying to break free. Freedom wasn’t just a value to him. It was the cause he gave his life for. He had done it to free Earth from its unseen captors and liberate humanity from a prison built on pain.

Now, by some impossible twist of fate, he had a second chance. He would fight again. Not just for freedom, but for the life he never had the chance to live.

With that thought steadying him, Arthur let himself drift into sleep, already planning how to shatter his chains.
