Chapter 182 / 27: To the Heart of Deadness

The wayfinder reminded Joe of a three-dimensional compass. Instead of being in a disk, the magic item was an orb the size of a billiards ball. Floating in the middle of the clear glass sphere was the needle, which Joe expected. What was new was that, unlike a normal compass, which had a fixed target, this item was more like a GPS navigation tool. The little arrow pointed in the direction they had to travel towards next. It was not locked on their final destination, leaving the small team to figure out how to get there.

They crept along in their customary formation. Yuk was spread both front and back. Then came Joe, under the faint blush of their new [Glowstone]. The last one hadn’t needed to be recharged yet, but no one wanted Joe or Yuk to have to spend the time and mana to refill it. Margen, as usual, brought up the rear, but on this leg of the journey, he feasted as he walked. Joe couldn't imagine what the base camp was thinking as the mountains of food they had been throwing into their end of the [Paired Pouches] kept vanishing at an alarmingly quick rate.

After twenty minutes, their magic pathfinder brought them back to the blue stone walls of the ziggurat. In front of them stood one of the heavy stone doorways, but this one had been jammed open. The temperature dropped again as they stepped through the opening and into the dressed stone of the fey prison. Following the arrow, the trio slipped down the corridor until Yuk called out a squad of approaching wights.

They had to fight six times within those dark hallways. For five of the six fights, they stuck with their practiced battle tactic. Joe surrounded Margen in dual [Halefires] and stood just before the man. The ghasts locked onto Joe, both being outside the undead-consuming flames and robust with life-energy. Margen employed his surgical strikes while Yuk, Joe, and the rat packs whittled away at the wights. These battles were savage, painful, and tiring, but they were fights they could easily recover from. Nothing fast or flashy, but the strategy was one that they could keep up pretty much indefinitely.

Then they hit a whole different configuration of roving dead. Instead of being predominantly wolf or worg marauders, this force had three of the boarish chargers, backed by a dozen of the vulture-mages.

Fields of damaging spell effects landed on the team: acidic black tentacles, sickening auras that burned the lungs, spectral bony hands that clawed and grasped, along with bolts of death magic, lightning, or fire. Yuk’s rats evaporated almost as quickly as he could summon them. [Grimoiriguard] blocked whatever Joe could get it in the way of, but the gas attacks flowed around the shield, and the tentacles shooting up out of the ground were almost impossible to intercept.

Margen had a much bigger health pool and a higher spell resistance, but he was also much harder to repair. Joe did not want to have to spend an hour fixing the results of the damaging area attacks.

If it were just the casters, they would have pounced on the casters, but the boar's physical might was too much for Joe to ignore. He focused [Morphic Form] into his muscles, pushing his effective Strength to 15. This allowed him to match blows with the boar-headed brutes, but it took all his focus. He had to leave the vultures to Yuk.

‘Joe, on my command, get you and Margen under your hide.’

‘[Bug Bomb]? Are you sure?’ Joe sent, while at the same time, he sent a claw to touch the hide and will it to expand. He noted Margen’s confused exclamation, but Joe was too busy to explain.

‘It will be smaller this time. We panicked last time. And we have stam potions too now, so I can rebuild faster. Ready?’

“Margen! I’m incoming. Hold it open,” Joe shouted.

He jumped up and grabbed the shoulder of the central monstrosity before swinging his body so his feet were against the chest of the right-hand bruiser. Kicking off with all his skills, Joe swung himself and the middle porcine brute around, dragging his target over onto the wight on the left. The two tripped and tumbled into each other.

‘Set!’ Yuk counted.

Joe tumbled backward as acrobatically as he could. He was still clipped by a blow from the right ghast. It knocked him slightly off target, but Margen read the situation perfectly. The swordsman stepped forward; his huge mitt yanked Joe under the magical pelt, which was now the size of a decent-sized tarp.

‘Blow,’ howled the telepathic cry of exploding verminist.

The hide flapped in the powerful whump of the [Bug Bomb]. At least one of the boaresque brutes was thrown on top of Joe and Margen. Joe willed the hide to open for them. Before he could move, DoomHerald decapitated the gibbous charger against the pelt.

The other two were in bad shape but were determinedly dragging themself back up onto their hooves. The only sign that there had been any carrion canters was a few fluttering charred feathers spinning towards the floor.

‘Nice job bud.’ Joe thought to what was left of the youth, while forcing a yawn to pop his ears.

‘Thanks. Now, can you pour some of those stamina draughts onto us, please?’

As he upended three of the yellow-filled bottles, Joe realized that Margen needed some ranged attacks. While he commanded the [Hide of the Hunter] to reform into a hauberk for the big man, Joe added waist loops and handed the big guy his throwing hatchets.

“We can get more from the pouch if you need ‘em.”

“Good idea,” the large blade master agreed.

They suffered more overall damage in that one fight than any of the other five combined, but thanks to Margen’s constant feasting, Joe had far less work to do on the founder post-fight than he expected.

It was not long after that last skirmish that they spotted the stairway up to the level where the Hellions had been extracted.

“Alright, Myllo, we’re almost there. How do we activate the gatestone?” Joe pulled a bronze shape out of the pouch. It looked like a large, fat chess piece, with a flat top.

“Easy, you just need to inscribe a symbol onto the top plate. Doesn't matter what the symbol is as long as it's the same as the one we inscribe. We can send you something through the pouch, but Vex says it would be more stable if your end, the anchor end, used a mark with some significance to you. Is there any symbol you know perfectly that you have a connection to?”

“Err. Would one of my wards work?”

“He’s making a face at me for some reason,” Myllo remarked. “What else have you got?”

“The only symbols I know by heart are from my old world, Myllo. I’m ninety percent certain that Vex isn’t going to know the band logos for Twisted Sister or Van Halen. And I don’t know any from Illuminaria.”

“Use my symbol,” the big warrior suggested. “I have made my mark on countless orders over the years. I could do it in my sleep.”

Joe recalled seeing the sign all over the place during Founder’s Day. It was a wide M with a chevron above and below the middle angled line.

‘So could we. We’ve doodled Margen’s sign a thousand times.’

‘I thought you couldn’t use items,’ Joe thought back.

‘I can’t wear magic items. I can still affect the physical world, you dope. You’ve seen me wrap cloaks around myself and lift a tankard. I can touch stuff and move it around, including a pen. I can also activate items, like wands or gatestones. I just don’t have enough connected mass to wear magic items.’

‘Ok, yeah. I was being dense. You keep three different conversations going and see what dumb ideas you trip over.’ Joe switched his focus to Myllo. “We have a winner. We’ll use Margen’s symbol.”

“Perfect,” the artificer replied.

They snuck up the steps, listening. Joe pushed [Morphic Form] to his Perception, trying to pick apart every sound and scent coming from above his team.

He immediately caught traces of something vile and demonic, which had to be Naragash. Zaebos left a lot of stink behind, as well, but Joe would take smelly dog over icy demon any day. Reven’s burn and Broon’s rot were layered with the other two. Of Morty, Joe found nothing definitive. Of course, all of these scents were mixed in with the ancient reek of moldering flesh and dry smell of {Death} magic.

Focusing on his ears, Joe could pick out the murmur of garbled growling and the swishing clack of the loom. Both seemed far above where he was crouching beside the stairway. He was almost certain the dead and the loom were two levels away. As for just up these stairs, Joe heard nothing.

Your skill [Whisperstep] has increased to rank 20.

Silently, Joe and Yuk ascended the steps. Yuk spread out across the floor and skittered away while Joe waited. After a minute, they confirmed Myllo’s description: three rooms and another set of stairs. No other undead around. The ceilings of this floor gave off a dim glow, enough that the [Glowstone] could be put away, hiding their presence further.

Joe gestured for Margen to join them, and the trio slid into the first room. This hall had almost surely once been dedicated to Autumn, but it was hard to be certain given how heavily it had been defaced and defiled. Only hints of the forest trees remained; everywhere else, claws, hooves, or objects had shattered the carvings. Black ichor and foul fluids were splashed across the walls. Hundreds of creepy black beetles skittered across every surface. Considering how much of Yuk Joe had been wearing all day, these scarab-like insects shouldn’t have bothered him in the slightest and yet the swarming mass gave him the willies. There was just something disturbingly off about these black-shelled vermin.

The next chamber was equally ransacked. It, too, was bug-infested, with just enough visible hints to know this room had once honored Summer.

The third room was not only unvandalized; it was pristine and laden with offerings to Morrigu. A dark statue of a ravenesque-looking woman stood in the middle of the floor. Surrounding her was a thigh-high pile of jewels, artwork, coins, and magic items. Joe identified a few of the items and found not a single magical offering with less than a rare rarity. Joe now knew why someone had touched that hoard.

The loom and murmurs were clearly coming from above. While the team gazed at the massive mound of wealth, the upstairs growling voices took on a more purposeful tone. As footsteps slapped against the floor overhead, Joe, Margen, and Yuk pasted themselves against the wall of winter’s hall, keeping out of sight. A squad of twenty or so dead dashed down both flights of steps and off into the ziggurat, completely unaware of what was lurking on the other side of the wall from them. The feral voices calmed again as the loom ceaselessly clanked away.

“Ok. The way up is just ahead,” Joe whispered. “Do we want to make the symbol here?”

“Nothing against this Myllo fella,” the old warrior said questioningly, “he seems a very competent leader, but I have been thinking. Sounds like they only have a bit of extra temporary mental defense. Whereas the three of us are inherently better equipped. I think we should see how much damage we can do first. Those Hellions you mentioned weeded down the number of deader, and we have taken out a hundred more already. If we take out another chunk of them here, then the guild team should be able to help us mop up without fear of enthrallment.”

“I don’t know. Do you really want to chance it? What if something goes wrong? Haven’t you been trapped in here long enough?”

“There is no rest for me until this threat is vanquished, son. I’ll die a hundred more times or once for real before I let that rotting bastard into my head again. You sound like you’re brain-warded to the hilt. Hells! Your familiar doesn’t register to ‘em. Better to bring in the cavalry while the enemy is wounded and engaged than when it can organize a defense.”

‘Um. Joe. We agree with Margen. We’re back to full mass. If you lure a wave down, we can have a big [Bug Bomb] waiting for them. You could have fully charged [Grit Razors]. We could do a lot of damage and get their focus, before calling in the rest of the guild.’

“Alright. We’ll do it,” Joe agreed, keeping his voice pitched as quietly as he could. “Let’s prep the stone first and let the base camp know the plan. Margen, you will be some unknown ally. Then we power up the gatestone. If still nothing has noticed us yet, we’ll grab something off the pile to kick the hornet’s nest.”

“Solid plan, soldier.”

‘Stop looking so worried, Joe. This is going to work. It’ll be awesome. We’ll be heroes.’

‘Says you. If not, this is going to suck very, very badly.’
