[Cerpital has judged Levi Tate to be safe from immediate harm. Please check your system logs for rewards and breakdown of recent events.]

He'd been moments away from dashing into the veil of darkness at the other side of the hallway when the message had popped up in his field of vision. It was the same type of message that had popped up after he'd successfully synced to Cerpital. A blue, semi-transparent box, with letters arranged to form the message.

Huh, with everything that has happened tonight, I'd almost forgotten about my system.

Cerpital had claimed he was safe from immediate harm, an observation that unnerved him a little bit. As far as he could tell, Cerpital wasn't in the dungeon with him, which made him skeptical. Curious as he was about the system log and whatever rewards that Cerpital held in store for him, he wasn't going to risk checking it out in his current position. Thinking quickly, he decided to return to his starting position—the walled entrance. At least that way, he could put enough space between himself and the darkness.

And even if I do get attacked, I should have enough time to react.

With his mind made up, he swiftly walked back there, occasionally looking back to confirm he wasn't being set up. He didn't trust anyone and anything, Cerpital included. He wasn't sure if the system was trying to feed him to the monsters in the F-ranked dungeon since he was stupid or brave enough to attempt soloing it. Truth be told, he wasn't sure of anything, and he didn't have any plans of taking any additional risk that he didn't have to. That was why when he eventually reached the walled entrance, he turned to face the darkness ahead, one eye on Cerpital’s message, and the other looking past the semi-transparent blue text box and into the darkness.

Alright, this is it.

System logs.

[Warning: You have lost six health.

Congratulations, you have leveled up!

Congratulations on your first Level up, you have automatically received a point to the three root attributes of your rogue class for free.

The three root attributes for the rogue class are perception, dexterity, and stamina. All three attributes will automatically receive a free point each time you level up.

You will also receive two unused points per level gained to spend as you wish.

One unused point allocated to health will grant you +10 health.

One unused point allocated to mana will grant you +10 mana.

For each multiple of five level up you achieve, you shall be offered a list of free skills to choose from.

You have received two unused points for reaching level two!

Congratulations, you have leveled up!

You have received two free points for reaching level three!

Hint: In the future, make sure to check your system logs for the latest updates.

Good luck, synced.

]

His fingers automatically brushed against the cut underneath his eye as he read the first line of the system log. The clawed attack had cost him six health, and as insignificant a loss as it was in the grand scheme of things, he instinctively knew that he couldn't afford to take many hits like that. After all, a dozen or so cuts like that would kill him just as surely as a claw to the throat. He couldn't afford to get sloppy; he couldn't afford to get hit.

The lack of a healer on hand made his situation more dire than he'd expected. There would be no saving him if he got severely injured, and Cerpital forbid he gets his eyes gouged out. What then would he do? A grimace appeared on his face at the mental image of him without an eye, trying to fight off whatever creatures remained hidden at the side of the hall, obscured by darkness.

I only suffered one hit, though. That has to count for something.

It had to, or at least it should've, but for someone like him who was obsessed with perfection, it didn't count nearly as much as it should have. His entire schtick depended on him being unseen; it depended on him being unhit. This synced business was taking that away from him; it looked like there would be no perfection going forward, only survival — and best believe that Levi had every intention of surviving, by any means necessary.

Grakking hell, what else is in this system log?

A thin smile replaced the grimace on his face as he went through the rest of his system log. He'd leveled up twice, and he'd gotten some free points to boot. The points he'd gotten from levelling up would definitely help him to fight whatever was up ahead. So far, it looked like he had already started to reap the benefits of hitting the dungeon. His eyes glossed over the system log again, coming to a stop once he saw the only detail he'd yet to analyze — the allocation of unused points to health and mana.

The explanation given to him about allocating unused points to either his health or mana stat had been very straightforward. One point equated to a plus ten bump to whichever of the two stats he picked. All in all, the new information he'd just received wasn't hard to dissect and digest; in other words, it was straightforward, which was the way he liked things.

Alright, time to allocate my points.

Status

[Status

Rank: F

Class: Rogue

Sub-class: Unavailable

Level: 3

Stats:

Strength: 4

Constitution: 5

Intelligence: 8

Perception: 9

Dexterity: 9

Stamina: 8

Unused points: 4

Health: 94/ 100

Mana: 110/110

]

A glance confirmed the expected; his root attributes had been updated alongside his level. His unused points had also received a bump in line with the number of times he'd leveled up. The information in the system log had been correct to a tee, much to Levi's satisfaction. With one eye on the blue screen and another watching the darkness beyond, he let his mind take center stage. He needed to allocate his points as fast as possible, and he needed to do it correctly, as a point to the wrong attribute could cost him his life.

Which attribute should I invest in?

That was the question. As far as he could tell, all options were open to him; Cerpital wouldn't stop him from allocating points to any of his stats, and so the decision of which stat he'd improve lay on his shoulders. He could easily rush the decision, but he chose to take his time, thinking deeply but still vigilant to his surroundings as he considered all he'd learnt so far.

For starters, his perception could do with a bump. The darkness lifted a little, but it was a pitiful improvement. His gaze still couldn't pierce through the darkness, and he doubted that anything less than two or three points to the stat would provide tangible improvement to the stat. Shifting his thoughts away from perception, he focused on the two odd stats on the screen — health and mana. Health, he could definitely see himself improving, mana, he did not.

He didn't doubt that mana would be an important stat later on, but what he knew for sure was that he had to plan for the present; he had to plan for the now. Unfortunately, that meant mana would take a back seat. Until he could figure out its use, the stat was inconsequential —at least to his immediate survival.

With his options narrowed down, Levi had an easier job of picking and discarding stats until he was left with the only three stats that he felt were worth improving immediately. The first was his health — for obvious reasons — then his perception, which had somehow managed to return into contention, and lastly his strength. The rest of his attributes clearly dwarfed that of his strength; he'd been assigned a measly four points to the stat by Cerpital. An arrangement that wasn't necessarily wrong, as he often preferred finesse to brute force. His begrudging acceptance of his strength level wasn't enough to cleanse the sour taste it left in his mouth.

Alright, let's get this over with.

Two points to strength

One point to perception

One point to health.

[ Allocation of unused points confirmed.

Improved stats below;

Strength: 4 —> 6

Perception: 9 —> 10

Unused points: 4 —> 0

Health: 94/100 —> 104/110]

He felt energy flowing through his body, and on some instinctual level, he could actually confirm the improvements that had been made to his body. He felt his muscles suddenly expand and contract, his eyesight become a little bit clearer, and as for his health, Levi simply felt more alive. It all took place within a breath, and soon enough, everything normalized.

"Okay, that should be all about that," Levi said as he willed the screen to disappear with a thought.

The prompt execution of his commands by Cerpital had left him impressed, but he quickly refocused on his priorities — the darkness beyond the glowstones' reach. The allocation of points had distracted him from the savagery that had taken place during the night, but he still had to remind himself that he wasn't done. The monsters at the other side would eventually come to him if he continued to stall. So Levi had a choice: he could either bring the fight to them or let the fight come to him.

Taking the fight to them was his choice; he couldn't risk the possibility of multiple gremlins assembling themselves to attack him at once, no. He'd rather bring the fight to them, taking out individual groups rather than the entirety of the dungeon at once.

With his mind made up, Levi began moving towards the darkness. His daggers bared towards that direction as he readied himself for any sudden attacks. His ears strained to pick up any sounds, and his improved eyes were vigilant for any sign of movement. His movements were careful as he stepped over the dead bodies of the gremlins littering his path to the darkness beyond the reach of the glowstones.

The first thing he noticed was the smell, or lack thereof, of one, apart from the intense smell of iron emanating from spilled blood and the odor wafting up from the stinking corpses that littered the dirt behind him. Levi couldn't smell anything. He also couldn't hear or see anything within the darkness, even though he was but three paces away from it.

What happened?

It was a good question: what exactly had happened between the time he'd walked back to check his log and right now. Why had the other gremlins he sensed at the border of the darkness suddenly disappeared? Why had their scent simply vanished? Lots of similar questions flooded his mind, but Levi couldn't dwell on them. Besides, he already had the sinking suspicion that remaining gremlins had regrouped somewhere deeper within the darkness while he'd been attending to his system logs.

Shite.

He couldn't even blame himself for taking a break to check his system log, which had been important. He still felt it was, but the sting of a missed opportunity had him in a bit of a mood. A grim smile appeared on his face as he reluctantly accepted that killing the other gremlins would be a bit harder than he'd hoped.

With a grunt, he took a step and another, and then another until he was fully on the other side of the hall — his frame and features easily consumed by the darkness. He stood for a moment, once he'd crossed into the darkness, fully expecting to be attacked, but the attack never came. Neither did he hear anything suspicious. So, he took a step forward, and then another, before the two glowstones at the other side of the hallway suddenly went dark.

Levi didn't have a moment to contemplate what had happened before another dim red glowstone appeared in front of him. This time, the stone wasn't to the side of the walls, but rather it was above the door. The door, which had been surprisingly left ajar, was a few feet ahead of him, a dim red glow emanating from behind it. As cautiously as possible, Levi made it to the door and quickly slid in to the other side. Once he was inside, he quickly scanned the place, concluding that it was a small room. At the sight of the occupants, illuminated by the red glowstones spread around the room, a bloody smile bloomed on his face.
