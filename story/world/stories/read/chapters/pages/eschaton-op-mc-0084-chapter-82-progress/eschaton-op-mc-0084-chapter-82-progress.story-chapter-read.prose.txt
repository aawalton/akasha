After we exit the Fracture and return to the Guildhall to divide up the loot, I’m blown away by just how much everything is worth. Even my own relatively meager portion is worth in the hundred million dollar range.

The Cerulean Guard’s portion, counting the Fractal Shards, was just shy of a half a billion dollars. Very, very few people are capable of clearing Cat-3s, and even fewer are capable of clearing the aquatic ones that form in The Deep. The government places a large bounty on Fractures that scales with strength and difficulty. Just by clearing the Fracture the Cerulean Guard earned fifty million dollars.

There’s a reason Empowered have rapidly become some of the wealthiest individuals on the planet. Not to mention why plenty of Typicals make an effort to clear Cat-1s. Even the meager worth they have can still set a person up for life with just one or two dives. They try despite the risks and the gruesomely high mortality rate.

Of course, the Cerulean Guard might receive just half of that at best. The government has rapidly jacked taxes up on everyone and everything. The money to pay for the bounties, the rapidly expanding military and the massive defensive infrastructure projects has to come from somewhere. The defense budget effectively tripled overnight. Congress is debating doubling that, too.

No expense can be spared when the alternative is extinction. A war of annihilation has been forced upon us. One that will never end.

The Cerulean Guard refused to let me waive my own portion of the bounty. They split it 50/50, leaving me twenty-five million dollars. More money than I ever expected to make over my entire lifetime made in a single day.

Morgan has plenty of people who handle money for a living and I shove it all at them. I have more important things to think about than investments or charitable donations or funding private research. I don’t really like the idea of being paid for work like this. It feels like it cheapens it, but Morgan’s words return to me.

‘Treat yourself like you do others.’

I wouldn’t lessen the effort the Cerulean Guard has shown because they’ve been paid for it. Danny even got his magical hot tub, and I wouldn’t begrudge him a heated indoor pool. Not after seeing what he willingly goes through. To use a cursed weapon? To eat pain for breakfast and take the hits for his team? I have nothing but respect for a man like that.

The suspicions about why so many cargo ships have disappeared have finally been confirmed. Both rogue magical storms have swallowed them up, and far more terrifyingly Krakens have been sighted. A cargo ship was broken in half by a squid nearly half a mile long. A patrol aircraft watched it happen from above, and the video went viral.

Not a single ship crew is willing to get out of sight from the coast line now. The only reason the George Cobb is willing to leave the coastline behind is because of me. I asked, and they answered. They’re willing to die because I said I needed their help.

An honor I’m not sure I deserve. But one I will try to live up to.

And so the days become weeks as we clear a Fracture every few days. I check in with Morgan and Warden at least once a day, much to their growing annoyance.

“Mach, buddy,” Morgan sighs. “We’re working as fast as we can. These Hearts of Growth are, well, weird. They greatly accelerate plant growth, and while I know why—the specifics aren’t important here—I’m having trouble converting that into glyphs. At least with the capability we need.”

“You’ve got something functional?”

“Only sort of,” she grumbles. “It’ll work if we throw enough power at it. I’m talking like a 3 Amp shard a day per arcology. More depending on the size. It works, but not well enough. At least we’ve made more progress on the Arcologies themselves, but those have problems of their own. Good news though, the kelp samples and the aquaculture has been working out.”

Unfortunately, the aquatic Fractures out here haven’t produced too many useful materials for the assumedly freshwater based hydroponic systems the Arcologies would be using. That’s not to say it’s been a complete waste of time.

Between the Morgan, Hashimoto, and their teams, they’ve come up with a hydroponic system variant capable of growing kelp through land based farms. Morgan has started working with Hashimoto on the more technological side of things. While technology is going to do the heavy lifting here, magic will still play a crucial part. The technology we have is just not quite there yet when it comes to artificial aquaculture with such scale and complexity. Magic will be needed to bridge the gap.

Unfortunately, due to the amount of water they need, the kelp farms are not viable for skyscraper usage and are only possible on the coast. It also needs a dedicated offshore pump to pull in enough seawater for it. But they’d be capable of not only farming kelp but also fish, crustaceans, and bivalves like calms and mollusks. A test facility has already broken ground in Monterey. It’s currently run as a joint research project by Morgan, the California Academy of Sciences, and the Monterey Bay Aquarium. We finally found our way to buy more time. If it works, anyway.

“This is an amazing development,” the President says.”This could be the breakthrough we’re looking for.”

“It’s not the solution we need,” Morgan cautions over the group call. “And we don’t even know if it’ll work yet. We need to do at least some experimentation first, but it does look promising. We can’t afford to start mass building a failed design. Fortunately, the Monterey Bay Aquarium and the Academy are taking the lead on this. It’s almost entirely mundane technology at this point. It won’t take away any serious time from my study of the Hearts of Growth.”

“I’ll keep a close eye on it,” President Drayson promises. ”I’ll see about getting them some more funding, too. Forty percent of the US population lives on the coast. If we can feed them off aquaculture, that’ll buy us a good six months.”

Warden’s news on Pythia is less than encouraging, unfortunately. It took two weeks before she was even able to get out of bed, and she’s far too weak to attempt another divination attempt. No one wants to find out what happens if she pushes herself too hard. No one knows if Ascended can die, and what happens if we do. We contain so much raw energy it’s terrifying. Enough to rearrange regional geography if we try hard enough.

The Empire has certainly shown they can do that, having dug a few canyons as “examples”. They’ve restrained themselves from scraping more cities off the map, as the rebellion General Guan Yu has really started to kick off. He was right in that the tighter they grip the more they lose. It appears they’re trying to hold what they can with the grip they currently have.

At least they’ve slowed their expansion. Still, by this point they have at least half of Africa under their control to varying degrees. Everything north of the Congo and east of Mali is firmly under their control—to a degree that’s shocked even the good General. Getting rebels in the heartland has been near impossible. Still, chewing away at recent conquests and the edges of their territory does a lot.

I try not to think about the costs of such a war. I hate it, I hate it so much. The constant death and slaughter, the throwing away of so many lives simply to slow them down. I burn with anger every time I think about what the three Ascended that rule the Empire are doing.

They’re next. After I’m done with the Arcology. They’ll get what they deserve.

Myself and the Cerulean Guard are able to keep up, just barely. At least for a little while. The time we bought is well spent. The National Guard has seen the largest influx of recruits in the history of the United States. Not even during the heights of WWII did so many people volunteer for military service. Second Squad was brought to the Bay Area with McKinely and Captain Rains, and they’ve spent their time training up new recruits. They’re some of the most experienced monster slayers in the National Guard, and that experience is valuable for the huge influx of volunteers.

Additional coastal defenses are under rapid construction as well. The near constant attacks made it difficult to complete them. By clearing so many Fractures, we’ve cut down on the number of attacks and the size of them significantly. Enough that the lower Starred Empowered land-based teams can handle the defense. Hundreds of construction crews and dozens of vessels are working around the clock to build large concrete structures and gates underwater that will force larger monsters closer to the surface of the water.

Once on the surface, torpedo launchers fire a mixture of Mark 48 heavy weight torpedoes at bigger targets and lighter Mark 54s for smaller ones. On top of that, the Navy has removed the main guns of some of their older destroyers and turned them into makeshift coastal guns. The mundane five-inch rounds might not be enough to kill the bigger monsters, but the smaller ones are very much not happy about it. The Navy has also been experimenting with adding infused materials as a fragmentary jacket on their shells. Expensive, but nothing else compares when it comes to trying to kill magically durable monsters. Especially the kind that come out of the Cat-3 Fractures.

Several artificial islands have been constructed, mostly through magical means. They now house the torpedo bunkers below the water line and the coastal guns. Additional nets and blockades have been constructed ahead of the Golden Gate Bridge to protect it from any monsters that might attack its foundations, though so far none have targeted it.

Point Bonita Lighthouse, on the northern point of the Bay’s entrance has been rapidly renovated into a listening post and sonar emplacement. Combined with a newly built station by Mile Rock Beach on the southern point, they work 24/7 to make sure nothing larger than a seal is able to sneak into the San Francisco Bay. Nike Missile site just north of Point Bonita, a relic of the early Cold War, has been brought back online. It was given a Vertical Launching System literally ripped out of a first generation Arleigh-Burke class destroyer.

The USS Curtis Wilbur was a Flight I destroyer that was damaged during a frantic defensive battle. Between its age as one of the oldest active-service destroyers in the fleet and the heavy damage it sustained, it was considered unrecoverable. While its hull may have been broken beyond repair, many of its other components were still intact and useful. Its VLS cells were still operational, and were installed in the reactivated Nike Missile site to act as a long range surface to surface defense emplacement. It can even fire modified Mark 54 lightweight torpedoes using the RUM-139C Vertical-Launch Anti-Submarine Rocket. I had no idea you could fire a torpedo like a missile.

I bet the Navy would stuff a helicopter into a VLS if they could.

Between myself working the Cerulean Guard and the Pacific Raiders putting in some overtime, we’re able to prevent all Cat-3 ruptures, at least for a time. It’s an unsustainable pace and everyone knows it. It’s simply a matter of time until we’re all forced to slow down and one ruptures.

A month and a half after I cleared my first Fracture in the Bay Area, we finally miss one. There’s so many so close together it’s easy for one to get lost in the mix of signatures and emanations from them all.

Rather than charging straight for the nearest coastline, the bigger monsters from ruptured Fractures almost to tee make for San Francisco Bay. As for why, no one knows. Most people simply assume the most common answer of the day: magic. It makes coordinating and building defenses much easier though.

Of the five artificial defensive islands, I stand on the central one watching the battle unfold. A Moby Whale is forced to breach the surface, the heavy concrete wedges under the water giving it no other choice. Angry red eyes peak up from just above the water line, and the monster’s white blubber is pock mocked with scars from harpoons, anchors, and cannon fire.

A heavy torpedo hits it right on the head, and the 650 pound warhead blows a chunk out of it. Blood pours out of the gaping wound, though it powers through the injuries. It bellows in pain and rage. Monsters fight until they die, and they don’t die easy.

The five inch gun mount on the central island lets loose with a five round burst. They hammer home, and the infused fragmentary rounds show their worth. Each shot causes the monster to shudder, and with the fifth impact, the Moby Whale dies.

A million dollars a shot. At least they’re getting their money’s worth.

Rear Admiral Halsey, the commanding officer of the Bay’s coastal defenses grips his binoculars so tightly his knuckles go white. When the Moby Whale dies, its white flesh floating on the surface and red blood staining the water, he relaxes.

He orders the mortars to fire, and they let loose with a salvo of depth charges. They’re not as effective as torpedoes, or as accurate, but they’re cheap, simple, and most importantly, easily mass produced in the thousands. They don’t need advanced components like the torpedoes do, making them a preferred option for weaker fodder monsters caught in the nets and traps.

The defense is short lived and one sided. The time myself and the Cerulean Guard bought was expensive, but worth every ounce of blood and sweat. Once the shooting stops, and the monsters are all dead, the contractors are sent out. Using retrofitted fishing vessels, they haul the larger monsters away to revived fishing districts to be broken down into materials and even food. Smaller ships trawl through the water, doing their best to recover every last bone fragment.

Halsey puts down his binoculars with a sigh of relief. The Bay Area is safe. At least for now.
