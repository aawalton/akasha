“Welcome everyone. Who wants to go first?” I ask, looking around expectantly.

We’re still in the meeting room off my cabin, though I expect this to be the last session we hold here now that the command building has been constructed. Outside, it’s still dark – the sun won’t be up for a little while longer. At least waking up early doesn’t feel so bad with a higher Constitution.

Breakfast is set out on the table, though only traces remain in the serving dishes – we don’t have enough supplies to over-cater. Even the nobles recognise that, however unhappy some were about the reduction of choices. There’s little fresh fruit on the table – after the long voyage, our supplies of that are scarce – nor are there any of the pastries I ate with Nicholas in the Titanbend estates. However, the food is still perfectly palatable – porridge-like cereals with fresh milk from the livestock we brought with us, and seasoned with spices and honey. While we were eating, we shared some of the more social developments from the past few days, but now it’s time to get down to the substance of the meeting.

“I feel like my report should be fairly short, so perhaps I should go first,” suggests Josiah. “As you know, I’ve been organising the patrols in the bay area. Most of the beasts that were originally in this bay when we arrived were either driven out by your Earth-Shaping or chose to attack and were killed for it. By this point, many of those who fled have started filtering back in. Mostly Tier ones, but some Tier twos have been spotted.”

“Do they seem to be aggressive?” I ask warily.

“Cautious – for now. But that could change at any moment, especially if a Tier three comes along.” Josiah warns. “My Water Mages are of the mind that we should keep the entire bay area clear of anything above Tier one. I’m inclined to agree. That’s how my father kept our people safe at home and I’ve continued the policy ever since taking up the seat.”

Before I have time to think about that suggestion, Layton leans forward.

“That’s an excellent idea, Josiah. In fact, I was going to suggest that we do something similar in the area around our settlement – if we rid the forest of anything above Tier one, our people would be a good deal safer when moving around.”

Elarion scoffs.

“Do you really think that would work? Perhaps you are too used to your desert territory, but forests tend to have a far higher density of beasts. Remove all the Tier twos from the environment and see far more conflict as those in the surrounding areas move in to fill the vacuum. It would be a boiling pot of beasts – more dangerous than maintaining the current state of affairs.”

“Respectfully,” Valence adds with a dip of his head to Layton, “I would agree with Lord Elarion. In addition, I’m not even certain that we would be able to clear the area of Tier twos, let alone Tier threes. We just don’t have enough skilled people.”

I frown and interject at that.

“Really? Our people seemed to defend against the Tier three that attacked us on the beach well enough.”

Valence half-inclines his head in partial agreement.

“My scouts have been working hard to draw conclusions about the area around us and its differences with what we’re used to. Something they have discovered is that the relative level of beasts is a lot higher here than back home and this has had several important effects on the hierarchy.”

“Like what?” Layton asks sharply. As he’s in charge of the patrols, I’m not surprised he’s paying full attention to this.

“Well, as you know, in Moriax there is generally a single Tier three – or mated pair of them – that rule over a territory as Beast Lord. Here, that same Beast Lord might only be powerful enough to be one among multiple subordinate Tier threes to the true Beast Lord. The beast that attacked us on the beach, though certainly a Tier three, would only be one of those subordinates. If we compare to the hierarchy back home, that beast would not be classed as more than a peak Tier two in relative power.”

I’m not the only one to take in a sharp breath. The difference between a Tier two and a Tier three is dramatic. As we’ve seen from every Evolution to Tier three, and most recently in Bastet’s, it usually leads to a drastic increase in mana, health, and stamina, particularly in whatever area has been chosen as the focus.

The Tier three plant-controlling bull that attacked us was relatively easy to take down, but only because of how many fighters we had able to combat it at a moment’s notice. If most Tier threes are significantly stronger than that…we might be in trouble.

“Then the beasts my patrols have been fighting…” Layton trails off with a hint of horror in his voice.

Valence inclines his head.

“Are on the lower end of the scale? Yes. In fact, based on reports of my scouts, some of them may have been deliberately sent at us by the local Beast Lord to test our defences. At least, we have found some traces that a strong Tier three has been watching us at the time they attacked, though we have been unable to track the beast or even spot it in place.”

I lean forwards.

“Any signs that it’s intending on massing beasts in its following and attacking us?” I ask him urgently.

Valence hesitates, then slowly shakes his head.

“No overt signs of such a movement, no, my lord. However, I strongly believe we should prepare for one. Evidence is strong that the Beast Lords defend their territories fiercely.”

Layton makes a vaguely amused sound.

“Perhaps they defend them almost as strongly as lords in the Marble Chamber defend their political positions,” he comments dryly.

Snorts – more or less elegant – respond to his comparison.

“Please, Layton,” Josiah comments with mock-disdain. “That comparison is insulting. No beast could ever match the viciousness of Lord Rockfall and Lady Ironhand when debating mining rights in the Dragonstail mountains.”

They exchange mirthful glances, that quickly vanish when Elarion clears his throat.

“If we could return to the topic at hand….”

Pinking slightly, his expression turning serious once more, Layton leans forwards.

“Of course – my apologies. Actually, I was wondering why the beasts are so much more powerful here than the ones at home – relatively speaking. Does anyone know why?”

“Perhaps the Energy density?” Valence suggests thoughtfully. “I cannot be the only one who has noticed how much faster I’ve been accumulating Energy to the next level.”

Several considering looks cross the faces of those around and I suspect that a similar expression is blooming on my face too. It’s certainly true that my Energy absorption has almost doubled since arriving on the Lost Continent.

Thanks to spending some time every day meditating with a Core or Energy Heart, as well as the regular attacks, I managed to increase my level twice during the journey. However, in the last five days, I’ve already gone up four percent towards level thirty-four, and that’s without anything more than my natural Energy accumulation aided by the Light Meditation that I tend to keep permanently active.

It makes me wonder whether I should reconsider my approach to keeping Meditation to myself – though it would be a commitment to create some Skill stones, if it could mean that my closest allies advance faster, the investment might be worth it.

But as Elarion leans forward, I tuck the thought away for later.

“The Energy density is probably partly the cause, but I suspect the local plants have a large impact as well. What Valence is describing ties into my own report about the strength of the local flora and the difficulties it’s posing for farming.”

My eyes flick to him.

“I’m certainly interested to hear what you have to say about the farming, but let’s tie up this topic first.” I look back at Layton. “It appears that creating and maintaining a clear zone isn’t a good idea in the forest. Not now, at least. We can revisit the topic later.” I turn to Josiah, hesitating for a moment as I consider the question.

I’m reluctant to call for extermination. This, after all, is their home. We’re the ones who have invaded it. If the beasts aren’t attacking us, then shouldn’t we live and let live?

But I’m not unaware of the potential risks. There will always be more danger to my people if there are beasts around than if the area is kept clear. And, unlike the forest, the bay is a more restricted space; the harbour even more of one.

“Keep the harbour area clear of anything above Tier one,” I decide finally. Hopefully the beasts will get the message. And if they don’t, I suppose that it’s at least good training for the fighters and mages on the ships. “And monitor the bay area for anything above Tier two.”

Josiah shifts in place.

“That could potentially put our ships in danger when leaving the harbour,” he points out.

I raise an eyebrow.

“Really? We’ve just crossed the ocean, facing all manner of threats. From what I remember, our ships did fine against anything below a Tier three.”

“You have a point,” he admits after a moment of silence. “And I suppose the harbour area would be easier to keep clear than the whole of the bay area,” he agrees with a hint of reluctance.

I nod.

“I know your Water Mages will appreciate a lightening of their workload.” That earns me an amused glance.

I turn to Elarion.

“You had a report about the farming. Would you like to give it now?”

“Certainly,” Elarion agrees. “As directed, the farmers, Druids, Plant Mages, and I have been working hard to establish our farms. We have cleared several large patches of ground in fertile areas. However, we are encountering several challenges that are significantly impacting our efforts.”

“Go on,” I invite, tiredness settling on my shoulders. I wasn’t expecting it to be plain sailing – especially when the actual sailing wasn’t easy in the slightest – but we need those farms.

Magical enchantments have made preserving provisions so much easier than it might have been, but after six months of sea travel, even those are running thin, especially after the sabotage that spoiled some of our stock. Our healers are starting to struggle to keep people healthy when they haven’t had access to the full range of nutrients they need.

I’m not too worried that we’ll starve in the immediate future – not as long as we have access to the sea for fish, and our hunters are bringing in a good bit of food. However, it can’t be denied that our food situation is precarious. Hearing about ‘challenges’ is not encouraging.

Elarion leans forward, steepling his fingers as he rests his elbows on the table.

“The land here is significantly different from what we’re used to on Moriax,” he reports. “Everyone agrees on that. The plant life is completely foreign, and even the earth itself contains a drastically different balance of elements than we – and the plants – are accustomed to. Additionally, the potions that we have been using just do not have the effects we are expecting. In short, although I would not abandon all hope of farming our plants from Moriax just yet, we’re going to have to take much more time establishing farming than we expected.”

I narrow my eyes.

“I can understand the difference that the soil makes, but why is there an issue with the potions? Shouldn’t they work better in an environment that has more Energy?”

Elarion nods briskly before continuing.

“Logically, yes. However, that is in itself part of the issue: sometimes they don’t function at all; sometimes they function too well. Additionally, the local flora is far more aggressive than what we’re used to on Moriax. Our crops struggle to establish themselves because the native plants often absorb the Energy from the potions that do work.

“So far, our most successful batch of crops has only grown to about a quarter of the size we expected and hasn’t produced any seeds. Our other batches have either failed entirely or produced fruit that is poisonous to us.”

I listen to this report with growing dismay.

“What about the local plants? Have you been able to determine whether any of those are edible?”

Elarion inclines his head, though not in agreement; in acknowledgment of the question.

“We have been testing them. So far, the results are not positive, and I’m sure you would agree that we should not introduce any plant to the general populace until we are certain that it will not have deleterious effects on both Classers and non-Classers alike.”

I forcibly keep my sigh inside, instead inclining my head to him.

“You make a very good point there, Elarion, and I completely agree with you. However, perhaps we should determine what the most cost-effective solution is. Should we focus on testing what might work for the plants we have brought over from Moriax, or should we focus fully on determining which plants in the local area might be edible and capable of being cultivated by our farmers?”

The Ice Druid contemplates the question deeply, staring at his steepled fingers for a long moment. Finally, he sighs and looks up.

“Given our constraints, at this stage, I would suggest we focus on discovering which plants in the local area can supplement our meat supplies. I believe this will be the most time-effective route, as I cannot be certain how soon we will overcome the challenges involved in adapting our Moriaxan crops to the local environment.”

I nod sharply.

“Then let’s have the majority of your farmers focus on identifying edible local plants while a small team continues experimenting with the imported crops. I don’t feel like we should abandon that effort entirely.”

Hopefully the still split approach won’t slow down testing too much. I’m aware that the food situation will only become more pressing over time. But I’m reluctant to give up entirely on the adaption of imported crops – there’s no guarantee we’ll find anything edible here, even if it seems likely.

We continue with the reports – first from Layton, concerning the encroachment of beasts into the area, and then from Sarran about the expedition’s resources and future plans.

It’s while we are bent over the city map, debating energetically about where to position the most important buildings and how best to establish the sanitation network, that a sudden knock shakes the door.

An urgent voice calls through the wood.

“My lords – the settlement! There’s something you need to see!”
