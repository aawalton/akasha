It turned out I hadn’t seen the final form of the Deep One.  This was no great surprise to me, and I suspected these Akira-baby abominations were just the first step on an ever-evolving path of Lovecraftian horror.

Mountainous blobs of shadow-flesh reared above us in the distance.  Metamorphic pseudopods lashed out, claws and all-too-human fingers forming along their lengths as the nightmares tried to connect with their targets.

I flicked the rainbow shield vertically in front of us, catching the top edge with one hand, and a startled dwarf by the scruff of his armour with the other.  Bone spikes bounced off my fingers where they stuck over the lip of the former flying carpet, which did not improve my mood.

“Let me go!” wailed Granvil.  He looked down and changed his mind.  “Don’t let me go!” he yelled even more fiercely as he wrapped his arms around my leg.

My mage hands were bugger all use against these kinds of monsters.  Five of them formed a semi-circle around the only tunnel leading onwards.  Midnight tentacles flailed and lashed at my minions, who rolled and dodged while pouring cannon fire into the core and slashing at any tendril that got close ot them.

Nonetheless, as I lowered the rainbow shield back towards the ground and offloaded my unhappy passenger, I used the glowing blue hands to try to assist my borgs in their battle.  It mostly came down to shoving one of them aside as a limb crashed down, or yanking them backwards to avoid being pancaked.

Lances of crimson light burned holes straight through the enemies, but the surrounding flesh simply contracted and sealed the wound.  How the hell did you deal with gigantic metamorphic monsters?  Well, there was one tried and tested method, but I didn’t have access to an orbital laser, and it wouldn’t reach this far underground anyway.  I was trying not to think about the thousands of tons of stone and earth floating above my head.

I was tough and might even be able to portal out before I got squished if I had to, but I had no desire to take the risk.  So I opted for plan B.

“Take this,” I said, shoving one of the oddly laser-centric weapons I’d stolen, ahem, requisitioned from Bulb’s storehouse.  It was a long spear that made the dwarf look even shorter as he clutched its two-and-a-half metre length.

“What do I do with this?”

“Aim the pointy end at the bad guys and activate—for fucks sake!  Try and miss the bunnies!”

Even with the glare from the blast of light half blinding me, I’d seen two brave and noble bunnyborgs flash into ash as the weapon discharged.  He ducked back behind the shield, and his moustache moved due to what I suspected was a savage grin beneath it.

He’d burned a nice, six-foot wide hole through the nearest of the monsters, though, so at least the bulk of the damage had been inflicted on the enemy.  His manic grin did not fill me with confidence that he would use his new power responsibly.

“Why don’t I just take tha—”

Granvil whirled to the side and ran off, cackling, blasts of energy from his spear punching holes in the monsters until he ducked into the cover of a nearby rock.  I was keeping my mage hands busy helping out Bighorn, who had assumed a heroic stance and was standing still, slashing at the base of the nearest beast.  The holes that Granvil had stitched up the monster's main body were all that was keeping the idiot minotaur alive as he bellowed and mooed while his axe flashed again and again.

The pain and loss of mass weren’t leaving the creature much room for forming limbs to smash at the horny guy; they were mostly occupied in trying to fend off the blasts from the robobunnies' mana-cannons. Another barrage of lightbolts from Granvil hit the thing, and I hurried towards him, moving the shield along with me.

These bodies, the constructs of the Deep One, were constantly shifting.  Faces, fingers, arms, even limbs and torso boiled up out of their surfaces and melted back in as they moved.  The faces were the worst.  Expressions locked in agonised screams melting back into the amorphous blobs would haunt my dreams for months to come.

On the positive side of this eldritch horror show, they were chunky enough that it would be almost impossible to accidentally kill one of them with a single shot.  The downside was that if I did accidentally land a killing blow, I would likely end up looking like a scaly version of them with all the biomass I’d get.

Bearing in mind my desire not to become a big fat wobbleslob of a dragon, I selected my weapons carefully.  The joys of an interdimensional pocket dimension in which to store my shit meant that I always had the tools for the job.  Vanity preened in my mind and sidled towards Lust, who was cowering in a corner.  The visages of the shapeshifting monsters were enough to kill any sense of desire, despite the occasionally impressive boobs being formed by the creatures.

Barcott’s Nose Pickers were a pair of twinned daggers made of filigreed steel.  They were more air than metal, not intended for stabbing at all.  They could serve as an improvised cheese grater in a pinch, I supposed, as I turned the blades in my hands.

I leaned around the side of my shield and sent a blast of fire, dripping spots of burning acid onto the stone between the baddies and me, and pointed the daggers, one at each of the two nearest nightmares.

The breath attack hit the first one and splashed off, coating it down one side and filling the air with a sharp tang of rot.  The daggers… I might have miscalculated with them.  They were the least impressive of the various weapons I’d liberated from Bulbs depository.  But not in terms of the enchantments, I had just learned.

When an organism experiences pain, it reacts.  Snatching a hand away from a hot stovetop when you’re an infant is the first step along the path that leads to not signing up for military service.  The foot-wide column of light that shot from the end of each of my daggers wasn’t a blast.  It was a sustained beam, and as the monsters moved, they carved lines through their body, destroying tons of biomass as they tried to escape the ravening fire.

I cut off the spells and blinked as the glowing lines refused to fade from my eyes.  A spray of bone spikes from the ones I hadn’t attacked pinged off the shield as I ducked back behind it.  I’d not only carved chunks out of the monsters, but I’d also cut neat glowing holes that stretched as far as I could see into the stone behind them.

Bloody gods and their bloody trinkets.

Another gout of holy illumination shot from where Granvil was pointing his spear over the boulder he was using as cover.  He wasn’t even looking, just doing the whole hide-and-hose-down-the-enemy bit, so I lost a pair of bunnyborgs to his efforts.  One of them was the musically inclined individual, and the singing suddenly stopped.

“Fucks sake, Granvil.  At least try and bloody aim!”

The surviving borgs took the opportunity to pounce on the closest of the monsters.  The one I’d doused with fire before carving up like a Sunday roast.  Mana-cannon beams flashed out, cutting it into chunks that desperately tried to extend psuedopods to each other, and they tried to pull themselves back together.  Wrist blades rose and fell as my robobunnies leapt and flipped between the chunks, keeping them from reforming.

I launched more beams from the daggers at the next closest shadow monster, moving them in circles to chop the thing up as much as possible.  They cut off as the walls behind them collapsed onto the “heads” of the three behind it.

A pair of portals opened up, and two more squads of bunnyborgs spilled out and charged into the fray.  Granvil managed to vapourise two of them with his blind shooting before I snatched the spear from his hands and stowed it away in my possum pouch.

“You can’t be trusted to play with your spear.  You’re making a fucking mess,” I grumbled as he blinked up at me.

“Have you seen those things?” he snapped.  His eyes flicked around wildly.  “If they’re here, then they’re probably here as well!”  He began to carefully back away from the boulder he was using as cover, nervously looking at the walls.

“MOOOOOOO MOTHERFUCKER!”

Bloody Bighorn.  Fortunately, in this case, this wasn’t literally true.  The monsters didn’t bleed red, but the tips of his horns were stained black with something that slowly oozed down their length.  I dispatched mage hands to try to wipe them off, causing the minotaur to fly into a rage.  I didn’t want that creeping biomass to tough his flesh.  I had visions of that scene in The Thing occurring at some point when I thought we were safe.

“No stroking me horn!” He flailed his hands about above his head, swatting away the glowing blue fingers of my spell.  Fortunately, he blindly stumbled away from the ravenous mountains of ruinous flesh, his hoof caught on a loose stone, and he fell face-first just before a midnight tentacle swept through where he’d been.

“Granvil!  Learn to aim for the love of god!” I yelled, moving forward and keeping my shield in front of me.  My mage hands were working together to drag the punch-drunk cow-dude away from the dying monster.

I raised my shield and grabbed him by a horn, eliciting a noise that I really hoped was pain, not pleasure, and hauled him easily behind my shield, letting it drop down and cut off the barrage of spines that were raining down around us.

“Time for reinforcements, Horny.”

I ignored his moans as he rolled back and forth, opening another pair of portals and letting a group of BB-209s stomp forward to unlease their rotary mana-cannons on the now retreating abominations.

I threw Bighorn a heal and dragged him upright with one hand.  “Granvil!  Get your arse over here!”

“You disarmed me!  I’m not going anywhere!”

“I took away the toy I—not that kind of toy, Bighorn!—gave you because you kept vapourising valuable Bob Co. property like the cowardly little buttplug that you are!  Get over here, or I’ll make Bighorn carry you to the next battle up his back passage.”

“Not my usual thing, but I’m ok with it!” the minotaur added helpfully.

“I am not going quietly into that stanky night!”  The dwarf hurried over despite his complaints and hunkered even further down next to me.  I really shouldn’t be constantly making short jokes about him in my head, but my tolerance for bullshit at the moment was lower than his knees were from the ground.

“There won’t be any need if you just do as you’re told.  Now, where the hell is this Damp Passage you were talking about?”

“Three more caverns, Bob.  Three more caverns and then you face the Moistness.”

“The what?”

“You’ll see.”  This time I could read his expression, but only because all he did was raise one very fluffy eyebrow at me.  So I punched him in the face and charged after my minions.  Minions that I could feel dying like flies.
