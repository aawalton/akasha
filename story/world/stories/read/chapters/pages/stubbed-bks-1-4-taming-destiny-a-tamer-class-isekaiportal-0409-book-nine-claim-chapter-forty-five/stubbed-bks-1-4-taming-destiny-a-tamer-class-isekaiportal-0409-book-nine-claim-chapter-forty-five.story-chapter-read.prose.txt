“Land ahoy!”

The call from the crow’s nest meets my ears even inside my cabin. I was half awake in the early dawn light, but the words make me sit bolt upright. Ninja, Trouble, and Fenrir jerk awake with my movement. When they realise there’s no threat, Trouble rumbles a complaint and then lies back down, putting his head onto his paws once more.

“The scout said there’s land in sight!” I defend myself against Ninja’s accusing gaze – and Bastet’s judgemental one from where she’s curled up on the carpet. One downside of her Evolution is that she’s now too big to cuddle up with me on the bed. Instead, she and Lathani often curl up together. Right now, though, they’re both awake and staring at me.

Pushing myself from my bed, I quickly dress. By the time I’m ready, Lathani, Bastet, and Ninja seem to have given up on sleep. In contrast, Trouble has just taken over my spot and Fenrir, after sending me a quiet question, has shifted closer to him – if there’s danger, the lizog is the first to pad by my side; when there’s no danger, he prefers to sleep in.

I head out of the cabin with two pony-sized felines and one lion-sized raptorcat. My cabin is on the second deck and near an exit, so it’s not long before we emerge into sunlight. Since my cabin is in the stern, I climb up to the quarterdeck instead of walking all the way to the prow, eager to catch my first glimpse of land in more than five months.

“Lord Markus,” the man at the steering wheel greets me as I reach the top of the stairs.

“Captain Waveborn,” I reply with a nod. Though I regret that we lost my original choice of captain in the first month of our journey, Tavalis Waveborn has proven a worthy successor in the time since. He has staunchly directed us through storm, beast attack, and inexplicable magical phenomenons. The lines are more deeply carven on his face than when he started, and I’m sure there’s more white in his hair – the consequences of stress – but he faces threat and peace with the same stoic determination to carry out his responsibilities as best he can. I admire him for that. As do the crew, both those who signed on with us in Whalehost and those who have replaced the crew members killed.

“You heard the lookout’s call, I take it,” Captain Waveborn comments.

“I did,” I agree. I scan the horizon ahead of us but don’t see any indication of land. “Where?”

Captain Waveborn takes a hand off the wheel to point. I strain my eyes but see only the faintest smudge breaking the horizon. It’s not surprising I didn’t see it before – it looks like nothing more than a cloud.

“Here, my lord,” Waveborn offers and I turn to see him holding out a telescope.

“Thanks,” I say gratefully, taking it from him.

Holding it to my eyes, I search for the ‘cloud’. There. I feel blindly for the carven runes on the side of the telescope and, with ease of practice, recognise the one that magnifies the image.

I keep my finger on the rune until it reaches its maximum focus. Like this, even the slightest twitch of my hand moves the view drastically so I do my best to stay still. I even pull out my wings and rise a couple of inches above the deck of the ship so its sway doesn’t cause issues. Like this, I can very clearly see that this is no cloud: I see massive trees, white cliffs, and waves lapping at them.

Though the way the water laps at the cliffs is like any other similar coastal formation, the cliffs themselves have a pinkish shimmer I don’t recognise. Some metal deposit? Or is this a magical phenomenon? The trees, too, are different again from those I’d started to get used to on Moriax. Once more, I’m somewhere new – and the trees make that very obvious.

My hand starts to shake and I quickly lower the telescope before I make myself feel nauseous with how much it’s moving.

Land. Finally.

I knew it would take five to six months to reach the Lost Continent, but I hadn’t fully appreciated how long that would be. Sure, we’ve made good use of the time, but I’ve grown sick of the same wooden walls and decks, the same faces, the same routine – except when intermittently broken by a battle with beasts.

Suddenly, having seen actual land, I find myself longing to stand upon it, alien though it might be. I yearn for scents that aren’t laced with salt and the smell of the sea. For something other than fish to eat.

I glance at my companions. They have obviously picked up from me that land is indeed within sight as all pairs of eyes are fixed on the horizon and longing far deeper than my own reflects back to me.

Bastet turns her head to gaze at me, her eyes filled with such relief that it almost takes my breath away.

“We’ve made it,” I exult quietly, holding her gaze. She doesn’t respond verbally, but I feel the surge of fierce gladness that emanates from her even as she returns her gaze onto the horizon.

I follow her example, my mind being inexorably drawn back to thoughts of the journey.

There have been so many moments over this voyage when I’ve feared we never would arrive – or not intact. The encounter with the Tier five early on – thankfully, not repeated – is one time I was almost sure we would perish, but it’s not the only one.

There was that time the massive Tier three fish rose and tried to eat Sor’az’s ship in a single gulp – we still have steaks of tuna-like meat from that beast. It learned too late that we were prey that would bite back.

Or another when a powerful storm hit and ripped our sails to shreds – again – and almost capsized Josiah. I thought we’d surely lose at least one ship before we arrived, but we survived it. Thanks to Sor’az’s soothsayer, we even managed to avoid the magical storm she warned us of.

And now, as I look around at the fleet, I feel so much pride that I could almost burst. Our ships are ragged – the damage has outpaced our crafters’ ability to repair and so they’ve had to prioritise what is most necessary for our ongoing ability to sail. We’re far slower than when we started – along with the damage to the physical aspects of our ships goes damage to their enchantments – and our shields are almost non-existent by this point for multiple reasons.

But we’re all still here. Sor’az continues to lead us and my ship sails behind his: no longer a maiden; by now she is a dowager – worn by the hardships of life but still proud and upright in bearing. On either side sail Valence and Elarion. Behind, sail Josiah and Layton. Finally, bringing up the rear is Garous and his now-experienced crew of both slaves and freemen.

I breathe in and out deeply, feeling my fluctuating emotions settle into something harder and stronger, like molten metal cooling into steel. Because getting there is only the first battle; the rest is still to come.

We’ve pored over the maps that we have available, and have identified several spots that might be good places to create our first settlement, but there’s no telling right now whether we’re anywhere near those.

What little we know tells us that the Lost Continent is vast and we have certainly not followed the route planned for us exactly – the Tier five and the storms all sent us significantly off-course and we cannot be sure whether we regained our exact heading. Especially since the last few nights have been cloudy and it’s been impossible to use the stars to determine exactly where we are. The sun’s movement at least tells us we’re still heading in the right direction and haven’t been turned around.

In fact, it’s entirely possible that this land isn’t even the Lost Continent but some other landmass. I recall how Columbus thought that America was actually the other side of Asia because he wasn’t expecting there to be a whole other continent between Europe and Asia.

Scouting is an absolute must and I know just who to ask.

Sirocco? Noir? Ivor? Are you up for some scouting?

I am already in the air, Sirocco tells me. My eyes immediately seek out where she is in the sky, our Bond giving me a direction in which to look. Finally, I spot her – a dot in the sky, already moving towards the land.

My lips curl up in a smile at her proactivity.

Thanks, Sirocco. Just, be careful, alright? We don’t know what threats might be between us and the land.

So far, we’ve managed to avoid her getting eaten by anything, though it was close when we were attacked by a flock of Tier two birds led by a Tier three. I lost sight of her in the flock and only knew she was in danger when she cried out along the Bond. I used Bond Teleport to pull her to my side, but it took long enough that her wounds were almost fatal, and the cost was high enough that I barely had enough mana left to keep her alive. Worse, my Energy channels were still inflamed from facing the Tier five, so I didn’t dare take any mana potions.

By the time I’d managed to stabilise her and I’d got some mana back, the battle was over.

Sirocco clearly remembers that time as she sends back grim acknowledgment of my warning.

Meanwhile, Noir and Ivor have both materialised from where they were meditating in my spatial tattoo. They each hand over a Core for safekeeping. Ivor found high compatibility with the Cores from the same flock of birds that almost killed Sirocco, so he’s been steadily making his way through the ones I kept from that battle.

Noir, on the other hand, found that one of the fish Cores that the fishermen harvested really suits him. He’s still working on maxing his stats at level one, but he’s close to doing so – when he reached ten points in Intelligence, his ‘feeling’ about his stats resolved into an actual table like my own. His three physical stats are already at twenty, as is his Wisdom. All that’s left is Intelligence and Willpower, each at nineteen points.

Can we practise later? he asks me hopefully even as he takes off into the air.

We’ll find the time, I promise him. The sooner he’s up to twenty points, the sooner he can start actually levelling up again. And, I know you don’t like it, but you should spend some time with Tiria and Keshaka. Though he has absolutely valid reasons for disliking Tiria, the fact is that those two mages are the most knowledgeable on this ship, and among the most knowledgeable across all the ships, not only in magic but general knowledge. Based on how quickly his Intelligence went up when he started talking regularly to the mages, and those two in particular, that’s what he was missing.

Following my directions, Ivor and Noir both fly at different angles to Sirocco – the more ground we can cover, the better. I send them the same warning I sent the bird, though it’s probably unnecessary since we all know by this point to be cautious of everything.

Markus? Elarion’s mental voice reaches along a different pathway to the one my Bonded use – our Alliance Bond. My captain reports that land has been spotted.

I sense that the other members of our Alliance are paying full attention now.

Yes, I agree. I’ve just sent out some scouts. Let us convene in a mark to see what they have to say – and to make our plans.

In person, or through the Bond? Layton asks briskly.

I hesitate for a moment, then nod unconsciously.

In person. We’ll need to look at and annotate maps, and plans will be easier to make if we’re all in the same room.

We’ll make plans to come to your ship shortly, then, Josiah agrees, Valence seconding his words.

I’ll have the galley prepare some celebratory snacks, I promise. Unease spikes through me at the realisation that now land is in sight, the plans will very soon be more than simply theoretical. But I push it away. This is what we’ve been fighting for – to get here. I even have some wine I’ve been saving for a special occasion.

That meets with full approval from my Allies – understandably since the last of the wine otherwise was consumed a month ago.

Pulling away from the communication and feeling the others do the same, I reach out to Loran and update him on the situation. Then, lifting the telescope to my eye once more, I examine what I can from here. The more information I can gather before we speak, the better. Who knows what little detail might make the difference between success and disaster?
