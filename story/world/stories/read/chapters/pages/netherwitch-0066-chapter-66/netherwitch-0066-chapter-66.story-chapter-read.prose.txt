“Ten years ago, I arrived here in the Cloud Island Wilderness,” Sylvia said, her voice amplified by ki. “There’s a little town to the east of Starport where I first set foot. They call it Sylvia’s Landing. I’m sure nobody has ever guessed the history behind it.”

A muffled chuckle went through the crowd.

Sylvia stood behind a podium on a raised stage which overlooked more than a thousand gamers. Most had plain faces, Lucifer’s free character customization having long since expired. Behind the asteri was an array of better-looking men and women in suits. Mostly men, whether by prejudice or self-sorting she didn’t know. Sylvia herself wore a fancy, white dress, layers of ruffled skirts pooling around her ankles. The top of her dress was dark silver complemented by gold embroidery.

Today was the Festival of Light.

Once upon a time, giving a speech before so many would’ve left her nervous and awkward. Now? Well, both of those things held to an extent, but not nearly to the same level. Sylvia wondered how much of that was driven by her familiarity and how much was the result of her exceedingly high spirit attribute.

“Many of you know the story of what came after. Starting from Starport, I made my way to Axis by way of the Snake River before finding a pass through the northern mountains,” Sylvia continued. The witch smiled. “Then, with a little help from my fiancée, Esmeralda Vallentfelt, I laid the foundations of our future.”

The silver-haired asteri paused for a moment, before adding:

“It’s been an adventure. For me, as well as you. I never expected to be appointed governor. I made mistakes. We struggled. Yet, I’m proud of what I accomplished. I’m prouder of what we built together. So I hope all of you will forgive me for being a power-hungry tyrant,” she joked. With a sly smile, she continued. “Luckily for you, I know how to step down. Which is why I’m happy to introduce our new, duly-elected governor, Mr. Joshua Sanchez!”

Gesturing behind her, Sylvia surrendered the podium.

The crowd roared. Dozens of gamers waved political signs. The energy and enthusiasm were beyond anything Sylvia had ever earned or experienced. A dark-haired man walked forward, a friendly smile on his boyish face. Governor Sanchez waved at his supporters, unlicensed music blasting in the background.

Gamers had come from every corner of the plane to witness the inauguration.

Starport. Windroost. Mountain Point. The Beginner’s Village. Some had flown in on airships. Others had sailed up the Viridian River on more conventional boats. A few had completed the hike on their own two feet. Most of those gathered, however, lived in Viridian City itself.

“Thank you. Thank you,” Governor Sanchez accepted. Rather than his unforgettable hakama, Joshua wore the classic suit and tie favored by modern political figures. “It was only with your support that I could stand here, on this stage. But before I begin, allow me to apologize. I will never be as beautiful as our former governor, Sylvia Swallows.”

Laughter rippled through the crowd as Mr. Sanchez gestured in her direction. Sylvia’s smile turned pained, her right eye twitching. Politely, the silver-haired asteri clapped before retreating to the line behind them.

“It’s a shame,” Emmy said, her expression schooled and her voice sweet. The lady gently took the other witch’s hand. “If you’d only made the attempt, you would’ve easily won reelection for governor.”

The emerald-haired woman was wearing an elegant dress of blue and white. Nearby were Esmeralda’s former retainers, Mayor Marshall and Mayor Sable. Not far from them was the sour-looking Natalie Ward, who had once been part of Sylvia’s class. The new parliament was on the opposite wing. Political parties were still in their infancy, but if Sylvia were to judge, the witch’s faction had clearly lost.

In fact, Natalie had been crushed 53.2% to 44.5%.

Not that it’d make much difference on the local level. The mayors of the various cities weren’t up for election for another four years.

“Power comes with a promise,” Governor Sanchez continued. “And my power comes from my promises to you. Everyone here has a dream. A dream for our future. A dream of what our burgeoning nation should become. It is my solemn duty to make those dreams into reality. And so, I promise you this: the Cloud Island Wilderness will rise into a new, greater era! Today, our nation consists of eleven thousand. Ten years from now, we will reach one hundred thousand!”

…

Sylvia wanted to facepalm.

For the past six years, the plane’s population had been doubling every two. Forget one hundred thousand. If current trends held, they’d cross three hundred thousand before Mr. Sanchez’s term was out. What a ridiculous promise. If that was Joshua’s goal, they could’ve put a monkey in charge.

Clapping, as was polite, Sylvia replied to Emmy’s comment.

“They only love me so much because I wasn’t running.”

Sylvia had a strange, fanatical following. To a certain extent, she understood her attraction. She was a very pretty girl. Furthermore, she was the founding ‘mother’ of the plane. On top of that, nearly a third of all the inventions and technologies were in some shape or form related to her or her actions. Add to all this the fact she was the System’s number one ranker on both level and merit points, and it’d be strange if she weren’t popular.

But seeing Joshua work the crowd made Sylvia aware of what she lacked. Charisma. Governor Sanchez had a charisma which inspired others to follow. There was a kind of energy in the atmosphere she could never rouse.

Though, maybe that was because she’d never tried.

“Today, airships carry goods from Starport to Axis. Tomorrow, we’ll open corridors across all of Pyrinas!” Joshua Sanchez shouted, his tone matching the excitement. “I have always believed in freedom. The freedom of the people. The freedom of society. That’s why I’ll support the right to resurrect and defend free enterprise! More importantly, I’ll grant freedom from the unfair taxes placed on those living on the east side of the Viridian River!”

Ay, ay, ay.

Well, if Viridian City turned into a mess after this, it wasn’t her problem. Mayor Marshall, however, looked less than pleased. They’d only gotten started on cleaning up the slums in the last year, and now that hard work would be undone.

Watching it all from the sidelines was going to be amusing.

The government of the Cloud Island Wilderness had one fundamental difference from all others. Here, the System stood as a ‘neutral arbiter’ above everyone else. If politicians ran afoul of System Law or the constitution, there was no need to involve the courts. Their bills would be slapped down instantly.

And the System wasn’t above gamifying politics either.

Why, just this morning, the System had happily graded Sylvia’s term as governor before handing out merit points. She’d even earned a +0.25% advantage in her next election. She didn’t know how the System would interact with parliament, but she could easily imagine the Devil gleefully screwing every politician who’d made campaign promises they had no intention to keep.

“Many of these things will take time to realize,” Governor Sanchez continued. “But I don’t want anyone to think I’m all talk. That’s why, this very morning, I convened parliament to pass two simple bills. First, the Festival of Light is now an officially recognized holiday. Merry Christmas and happy Hanukkah to everyone!”

Joshua raised a hand. Behind him, City Hall lit with colored lights. While the crowd oohed, three witches flew overhead. From glass bottles, the ladies dispensed a potion into the sky.

The magic of law unfolded.

Warm, unseasonable air suddenly cooled. As the chill swept over the city, the bright sky turned gray. Slowly at first, then in increasing number, fluffy white snowflakes fell onto the world.

Sylvia smiled.

She had prepared the concoctions three days earlier. It’d taken five hours to brew all the edicts and stick them into a bottle. The result, however…. She gazed at the awed gamers. Some looked up. Others stuck out their tongue or extended their hands, capturing the little crystal flakes. Most of the players in Axis hadn’t seen snow. Not after entering the netherworld. In the Cloud Island Wilderness, the chill of winter could only be found in the high mountains or on the Frost Pole far to the west.

Cheerful Christmas music played in the background. Surely, decorated pine trees were in their future. Though, it wasn’t yet the holiday season on Earth. Which was no surprise. Earth time would never properly align with that of the netherworld.

So they may as well use the Festival of Light as a substitute for the traditional holiday season.

“Our second bill involves a matter of great interest and intense debate,” Governor Sanchez resumed. “So we decided to submit it as a referendum. Rather than explain the contents, I think it’d be better if you all looked at them yourselves.”

Ding!

Joshua released the System task to everyone.

“Voting will be settled by the end of next week,” Mr. Sanchez reminded. “Now, enjoy your holiday. For those who are working, you’ll receive double-time pay and merit points.”

…

As the governor stepped away from the podium, Sylvia’s pastel pink eyes turned toward her betrayer. Her gaze was utterly dead, like that of a suffocated fish.

“Do you regret giving up your governorship now, my love?” Esmeralda whispered, looking very smug.

“I’ve seen through your vile plot,” Sylvia accused dully. “You delayed our wedding until after I retired, all so you could marry a princess.”

“What a dreadful allegation,” Emmy replied, wearing a smile befitting an evil imp. “If things are as you insist, then I must’ve hatched this plan when we exchanged rings three years past.”

Sylvia’s eyes narrowed. The silver-haired witch summoned a blue window before accessing the forums. With her digitized soul, Sylvia scratched out a quick post. This madness had to be nipped in the bud. If she allowed her fan club a moment, who knew what absurdity they would conceive.

“I think you started this scheme when I returned from Tartarus,” Sylvia sneered, typing with her mind. “But don’t worry. I’ll put an end to it.”

After all, who’d be crazy enough to give her the title of princess? Most of the players in the Cloud Island Wilderness were good American folk… mostly because that’s where the interdimensional gate was located. They’d never go for a royal lording over their heads.

“I’ll admit, I’ve dreamed of marrying a princess since I was a child,” Emmy said shamelessly. “And by all means, do your worst. I hired a group of men and women to subtly seed the forums six months ago. If you can turn public opinion this late, I’ll admit defeat.”

…

Shouldn’t little girls dream of being a princess?

Shooting the woman a glower, Sylvia turned her mind back to the battlefield. On the forums, she found despair. A deep, dark pit of endless despair. Her fan club had already gone wild. There were a dozen topics posted already. Some, to her horror, held solid arguments, endorsements from important figures, or pasted notes from DustyLibrarian laying out the historic, netherworld precedent.

Sighing, Sylvia’s gaze turned up. The world was bleak, gray, and cold. Why was it so cold?

“Chimera.”

The word left Sylvia’s lips without giving the Devil a chance to speak. The silver-haired witch was seated in the System’s virtual conference room. The walls were gray steel, their angled shapes exuding a sci-fi feel. Fluorescent lights provided stark illumination, the cold glow recalling strange nostalgia.

“You know that isn’t why I called you here,” the blond man retorted. Lucifer was dressed, as usual, in his red tie and white suit. “But by all means, princess, indulge me with your drivel.”

Sylvia’s jaw tightened. She resisted the urge to respond to the Devil’s barbs. To do so would mean surrendering her initiative.

“Chimera has many meanings,” Sylvia continued. “In alchemy, a chimera is a creature assembled from multiple beasts. Souls can also be chimeric, should they be polluted by the nether code of phantasmal beasts. In Greek mythology, a chimera is a creature with a goat’s head, a lion’s body, and a snake’s tail.”

The asteri’s pastel pink eyes shimmered with starlight, a fractal universe reflected within.

“But when you gave me that word, you weren’t referring to any of those,” she continued. Sylvia paused then pronounced her verdict. “The chimera you spoke of was medical.”

“Oh?” The Devil sat up. For a moment, something other than derision glinted in his blue eyes.

“Medically speaking, a chimera is a creature which contains the cells, tissues, or organs of another being,” she finished. “For instance, during pregnancy one twin might be absorbed. The cells of two children combined into a singular being.”

Sylvia let her words hang in the air. Slowly, Lucifer brought his hands together in a mocking golf clap.

“Congratulations, you’ve grasped the truth of your origin,” the Devil jeered. “As you suspect, Eric Swallow left the womb a chimera. Don’t be surprised that you didn’t know. This condition is quite rare and ofttimes non-obvious. Unless a doctor takes a sample from the right piece of flesh, the secret may be held until death.”

Smirking, the blond man stood. Lucifer strode across the metal room, unleashing his villainous monologue as he paced.

“But what you’ve neglected is the spiritual significance of your mortal condition. Biology is not just a matter of the body. Flesh forges souls,” the Devil explained, proud and eager. “When life quickens, it creates a spark. A frail and fragile light. A proto-soul is what we call it. When one twin swallows the other, well, that’s where things get interesting. Usually, the spirits merge, but in rare cases they divide into layers. A halocline. Fresh water and salt water side-by-side, neither mixing.”

Gleaming blue eyes turned in Sylvia’s direction.

“And you. You were the rarest of specimens,” Lucifer continued. “For ten years, the soul of your fraternal sister remained suspended in your flesh. Ten years of maturity. Ten years of growth. Ten years where the spark brightened, untainted by thought or emotion. What fortune to stumble upon such a treasure before the storm of puberty ruined the delicate balance.”

Prometheus grinned, a mad smile on his lips.

“The souls of mortals pale before those of the void, polluted by the hungers of humanity and weakened by individuality. Only the proto-soul of a chimera retains the purity of an unborn child and the maturity to endure without flesh. A perfect foundation on which to build my System, don’t you think?”

A single scientist could cast a shadow over the world. Ideas were larger than men. They could shape entire civilizations. Here in the netherworld, the giants could stand so much taller. Demons held superhuman power, and this was not limited to physique. Sylvia’s mind moved four times faster than a mortal’s. A shallow assumption would be that she could do four times as much mental work.

Wrong.

Attributes multiplied on each other. When Sylvia invested in spirit, she firmed her resolve. A more durable mind could focus better and longer. The speed of her intellect was multiplied by this expanded period of efficient labor. Thus, four times became eight.

Wit didn’t just cover awareness. It also enhanced capacity, the breadth of the mind and the clarity of memory. High capacity allowed her to hold complex ideas firmly in her head without having to break them down into parts. For simple problems, it helped little. For large ones, the gain was transformative.

Hence, eight times approached twenty.

Yet, her calculation still undersold the accumulated greatness. Where would a company find twenty mortal engineers who worked together without flaw? Communication took time and introduced errors. The skills and knowledge of one worker would not mysteriously leap into the head of another. A team of twenty introduced huge inefficiencies.

Sylvia was not a team. She was a single person. Thus, the twenty could be compared to fifty.

In certain senses and situations, Sylvia could literally do the work of fifty engineers. This was not absolute. For some problems, she might outperform two hundred. For others, she might struggle against a team of ten. Prometheus, the ancient god of science, technology, and civilization surpassed her in every measure. It wasn’t wrong to say the man could rival the Silicon Valley giants.

The silver-haired girl drew in a breath. “I was the first,” she summarized.

“No, you were the fifth. But the soul of your sister became the kernel on which everything was built. Thirteen souls drawn from thirteen chimeras, all melted into a single pot,” Lucifer explained, gesturing toward the windows. The blond man retook his seat. The Devil leaned forward, hands steepled. “But I must have left too much of its soul inside of you. Pieces the System longs to take back. How interesting.”

Sylvia didn’t find it interesting. She found it creepy and sad.

Finally, she knew the truth. Why did she have flawless talent? Because her existence was the one closest to the System’s origin and therefore its nature. The System, in some senses, was her twin sister. A lonely girl who’d been with Eric for a full ten years, never heard, never felt, and never seen. Even now, in Sylvia, existed a part of itself. A piece it longed to embrace.

Was this why she’d obtained digitized soul? To heal that lonely existence? If so, the Heavenly Will had chosen the wrong person. Sylvia wasn’t that nice.

“Did you ever consider what would’ve happened if your System evolved a human consciousness?” she asked scathingly, her pastel pink eyes like ice.

The Heavenly Will was born from a void soul for a reason. To create an omnipotent god that possessed human feeling and human folly was a recipe for catastrophe. As for how Lucifer had treated her twin’s soul? Sylvia didn’t much care. Though the System was in a sense her sister, this was a sister who’d never been born and had never truly existed.

In fact, if she looked at things from a different angle, the Devil had saved her sister’s soul from certain destruction.

“Then you’d be a puppet to a selfish, petty tyrant,” Lucifer sneered, arms spread wide. The blond man leaned back in his chair, left leg crossing over his right. “For a brief time, anyway. Then the Heavenly Will would scour us from this world. An acceptable risk for a grand creation. But, I didn’t bring you here to discuss trivial possibilities. I brought you here to discuss our future.”

...

On second thought, it was clear that the Heavenly Will was using her to clean up after this apple hat’s mess. Sylvia Swallows, cosmic maid, following behind the universe’s greatest scumbag. Her cynical mind insisted a conclusion so horrible had to be real.

“If you want to talk about the plane’s future, you should’ve called Governor Sanchez,” she said in retort.

Sylvia was now, officially, a retired old lady. Politics was behind her. The world was a burden to carry on her shoulders no longer. Her new duty was to run around playing as she pleased, completing quests only when they caught her eye.

“Children playing house,” Lucifer scoffed. “I have no intention of revealing myself to this new government. You know my name and my image have been twisted by that mockery you call religion. To speak with my own mouth would only invite trouble. And why should I, when I have a ‘princess’ and an ‘apostle’ to speak for me?”

This booger, putting the p-word next to the a-word. Sylvia had accepted she was a girl, but that didn’t mean she wanted to be a princess!

“Remind me, when did I agree to be your apostle?” the asteri said blandly.

“Whether or not you agree, it makes no difference to those who believe,” Lucifer snapped back. “In the eyes of the netherworld, you are already my apostle. And since that is the case, we had best make use of that fact.

“A storm is coming. Before, the Cloud Island Wilderness was a small candle in the dark. A tiny flame, easily hidden by the leaves and branches of the forest. But now, our numbers swell and so too does our fire. Soon the forest will be alight.”

Sylvia let out a breath. “I’m aware,” she said. “That’s why I called in Governor Sanchez last week and explained to him why we trade with Jeolsung and the Fey Federation, avoiding Tartarus altogether.”

Trade accelerated development. The benefits were worth the risks. But there was no reason to be trading with the plane on which the Inquisition had cast its eye. The netherworld was a big place. There were plenty of partners.

She’d also informed Mr. Sanchez of the ‘why’ behind all of this. Not the exact details, of course. Rather, she had explained that it was the rise of the System which made all the fate compasses in the netherworld shift one notch toward discord. The rest? Well, Joshua wasn’t a stupid man. He’d figure it out sooner or later.

“That alone is not enough,” the blond man dismissed. “Our secrets cannot be hidden forever. What we need are closer, brighter, more threatening fires to steal Heaven’s eye.”

Her eyes narrowed. “You want to hide chaos behind chaos.”

It was a bold move. And a cruel one, because whoever was on the front line would be the first to feel the Inquisition’s ire.

Lucifer’s answer was a smirk.

Sylvia leaned back, contemplating. Strategically, it could work. And she wasn’t some ditsy, pure-hearted, magical girl princess who insisted her every move had to be morally flawless. Reality consisted of mud and dirt more than anything else. Those who wished to accomplish anything of meaning were destined to get their hands dirty. And she was a lady who preferred actions that had meaning.

“If we wish to build enclaves on other worlds, we’ll need to plant dungeon cores.”

Without them, the local System client implanted in a demon’s soul would have to speak to the System directly. In practice, this meant their communications would be routed through Earth or the giant antenna called Yaalon. Which was a problem, since any form of communication could, theoretically, be traced.

Dungeons would provide local processing resources, obscuring this indication. Better yet, unlike the soul of a gamer, dungeons were disposable. By routing communications locally and through other planes, the Cloud Island Wilderness would be obscured.

And this chain could be improved further with a network root.

Pastel pink eyes narrowed.

“You want me to focus on Cybernetic Cores.”

Recently, Sylvia had been marking off objectives in the quest A Perfect Core. Every time she did, a new side quest was unlocked. Cybernetic Cores was created after Sylvia successfully fabricated a core capable of supporting 5000 users.

“Don’t bite off more than you can chew, little girl,” Lucifer sneered. “I’ll alter the code used by your classic dungeons to better hide the bread crumbs. Finish your current research. When your Astral Observatory is complete, progress in other domains will proceed more smoothly.”

Sylvia’s current thrust was processing nodes. They needed a good one to run simulations in the Astral Observatory. She was pretty close to producing something functional, but Sylvia wasn’t going to call her product a real processing node until she eliminated the need for Yaalon’s fruit.

“If that’s all, then why did you bring me here?” Sylvia asked, irritated.

“To convey the importance of your task,” the Devil answered. “And to remind you that you are an important piece on my board. You’ve already reached the fourth consolidation. Why do you foolishly dither? Don’t waste time. Transcend.”

“You do realize that it’s only been ten months, right?” she growled.

“I don’t know what mush others have put into that fool head of yours, but the second grand mutation is easy,” the blond man sneered. “Just pick an ideal then sever everything that gets in the way of your ambition. Or are you stuck on some fantasy that you get to pick your providence? The Heavenly Will has chosen your route forward, girl, don’t delude yourself otherwise.”

…

Wow. What sterling advice. Sylvia was going to file Lucifer’s suggestion neatly in the cabinet labeled: ‘things to never do’. But, if Prometheus had severed everything that got in the way of his ambition, that’d explain his friendly personality.

“I’ll duly consider your words of wisdom,” she said dryly.

“Do not consider for long,” Lucifer said. “Perhaps you cannot grasp the importance of power while you are here in the Cloud Island Wilderness, but in the netherworld only ‘great men’ and ‘great women’ command respect. And only the gods get to decide destiny.

“Now, away with you. You understand your purpose well enough.”

“Mmm.”

Emmy let out a soft hum, her arm slung over Sylvia’s waist. Her breath whispered across Sylvia’s neck, a warm, comforting trill. The silver-haired girl drifted for a moment, feeling the rise and fall of Esmeralda’s chest. Even in death, the habits of life remained, a gentle contentment.

She indulged for a moment. Then, slowly, Sylvia slipped from underneath the covers. Emmy’s arm curled, grasping for the absent body.

“Sylvie,” she murmured. “Don’t… go.”

With an affectionate smile, Sylvia gazed upon her fiancée. Here, in the dark, with all her defenses set aside, Sylvia could see Emily’s adorable sweetness on Esmeralda’s mature face. Drawn by the expression, Sylvia leaned across the bed and kissed her lover lightly on her forehead before adjusting the covers.

“I’m just stepping out for a moment,” Sylvia whispered.

“Mmm,” Emmy sounded sleepily.

Quietly, the silver-haired witch left the room.

The halls of their house were cast in shadow. For an asteri, this was no obstacle. Sylvia’s pure starlight eyes caught the glow of ether, rendering the dark with faint ambiance. Guided by this weak light, she pattered down the stairs, the gossamer layers of her nightgown floating around her.

She paused beside the door to her office. She entered. When she left, a small locked chest was held in her arms.

She stepped out into their backyard.

Even this late, the jungle air wrapped her in its muggy embrace. The heat ran in sharp contrast to the winter’s chill cast by her potions in Viridian City. The sky gleamed with stars, the heavens divided between the constellations of Duo Pacha Ayu and the chaotic twinkle of the starry void.

Overhead loomed the large blue moon, its shape a waning gibbous. Its smaller, silver cousin gave chase, showing only a sliver of its light. Draped in this cobalt glow, Sylvia strode through the short grass. Cold water touched her toes, the day’s humidity condensing into dew. This wasn’t a scientific phenomenon. Rather, it was an echo of the memories from which formed the logic of this world.

Sylvia set the chest on a table. Then she took a wicker chair.

A storm was coming.

She’d known this for a decade. Lucifer, Sylvia believed, had slipped into her dreams to remind her of this fact. The danger and the stakes hadn’t become smaller as the days turned. Instead, the horror was creeping closer. Sooner or later, this beautiful peace would break. Celestial ships would fill the horizon.

She closed her eyes and imagined the devastation. The flash of calamity cannons, their beams ripping across the beach. Houses shattered. Starport a flaming wreck.

She let out a breath. Not tonight. Tonight there was only silence and beauty.

“I suppose I shouldn’t delay things much longer,” Sylvia said, patting the box.

The silver-haired witch leaned back in her chair. The seat rocked gently as she called forth a blue screen. The virtual glow was a companion that had stood beside her for almost thirty years now.

She had been following a 4-2-1 distribution for her attributes, recently. Perhaps, she could optimize things further, but life wasn’t a video game. Reality was never simple. The jack of all trades was ofttimes better than the master of one. Trying to maximize her combat power appealed to her gamer heart, but life demanded breadth.

Besides, the higher her level, the fewer the foes she fought.

Fourth consolidation was a milestone. Only one in twenty-thousand would ever reach this level of strength. For ordinary demons, those who crossed the fourth consolidation were invincible. On the colonial planes, they might even become kings – as was the case of Vilhelm Codrin. On the major planes, they held noble positions like Viscount Malik Nychta. Even when such persons avoided politics, they exerted great influence as with High Witch Yvonne Taubert.

Yet, for Sylvia, none of this had meaning. In the Cloud Island Wilderness, she’d long been the strongest. What purpose did levels serve except to ease her labor? Sylvia’s most useful attribute was wit. As things were, the rest hardly mattered.

She opened a window, reviewing her most recent traits.

Mystic attunement was best described as a raw multiplier of her magic attribute. The overall effect was minor. It did let Sylvia put a little more umph into her spell. Which could be nice under the right conditions, but Sylvia was already strong enough to maximize the majority of her primordial magics.

To get real use out of the trait, she would have to improve her grimoire.

And that was no small task. Combat magic demanded extreme efficiency in terms of runes and mana. The high magics had to consider common counters as well. At her current level, throwing down laws, erecting wards, or slipping into the void was all too easy. If her spells could be defeated by just that, they’d be useless.

Ironically, it was the last bullet in the System’s description which Sylvia found most useful. The ability to sense intent.

The netherworld was full of magic, whether natural or artificial. While Sylvia’s senses were sharp, it was hard to differentiate useful information from the background noise. Intent was a separator, one that made subtle spells like traps, divinations, and curses half again more distinct.

As for Deep Reservoir, she had no strong feelings about it. It’d been a long time since she’d run herself dry. Nevertheless, she was sure the trait would prove its worth eventually.

Finally, Sylvia viewed her secondary attributes.

“I’ve come a long way.”

She could still see it in her mind’s eye, a young witch speaking the rune voya for the first time. The thrill of her first skill book. The moment magic opened up to her, revealing the first of its mysteries. The day Sylvia had truly set foot onto the path of the witch.

“And now it’s time to go one step further.”

She opened the box. Inside lay a blue fruit, its presence like a whisper. She could feel the psychic energy murmuring in her mind.

What she had in front of her was a psychic fruit. Sylvia had been planning to eat one for years, hoping to make her memories fresh. Yet, she’d kept putting it off. The reason for this was her skill book list. Once eaten, a psychic fruit restored and solidified roughly ten years’ worth of memories. Memories, not experiences. Therefore, the effect was wasted on the Waltz of Flowers and obviously she wouldn’t direct its precious energy toward junk books like All About Wind Blade.

Hence, she’d waited until her accumulation was sufficient to extract maximum value.

Today was that day.

“Let’s find out what you taste like,” Sylvia said eagerly.

She licked her lips then took a bite. Flesh crunched between her teeth. Tart juice flowed out, rolling over her tongue. It was sweet, delicious, and oddly nostalgic. Strange, foggy energy poured down her throat. The essences melted into her blood before infiltrating her brain.

Her eyes grew heavy. She found herself drifting on the edge of sleep. Hurriedly, the silver-haired witch devoured the rest, afraid she’d fall into slumber and let the remnants evaporate into the wind.

She dreamed.

There, she saw an adorable brunette peering down at her, shyly clutching a spear. Emily, cute and fragile. Color seemed to brighten as the details of this precious memory filled in. She gazed into the umbral green of Emily’s eyes. She noted the arch of Emily’s brows. An image was burned into her mind as though a photograph augmented by sound, smell, and emotion.

Pictures of that same girl teased her heart. Emily’s tears when she was confronted in Orasul Lunii. Emily on their porch, seen for the first time with emerald hair. Esmeralda and Emily sharing a hug, looking so much like sisters.

Sylvia let those memories flow for a long moment. Then, cold and rational, her digitized soul took control, directing the dream according to her greater agenda.

Suddenly, she was in an empty room. In front of her was a book. She turned a page. She could feel the paper, rough and crisp between her fingers. On the left side was depicted a rune in its natural form then as a glyph. On the right were the details and description.

The Lesser Codex as seen when she’d used her first skill book.

The false Sylvia turned the page again, a machine reading through the tome. Each image was made sharper and clearer, burning into her brain. Her first skill book was followed by another. She absorbed the Great Codex again, memorizing all 3000 runes. After that came the seven-book compilation called the Encyclopedia of Runes, which introduced 4000 more. Then Sylvia read through a scattering of small texts, the Runic Errata compiled by the Collegium Magicae.

Still, the sweet psychic flavor lingered on her tongue. With cold calculation, her digitized soul directed her to a different set of memories.

Math.

Eric, counting numbers on a line, a small pudgy boy struggling with multiplication and division. Middle school came after, introducing exponents, algebra, and geometry. Slowly, the memories transformed. It was no longer Eric studying these subjects, but Sylvia grasping calculus, statistics, topology, and differential equations in a System-simulated classroom.

Classes on math blended into those of arithmancy. Arithmancy flowed into spell theory. Spell theory became alchemy, a subject rich with memorized facts. After alchemy came rune logic. A decade of memories passed through her mind, real and artificial. All were made crisp and clear, strong and enduring.

Golden light fell upon Sylvia’s face, rousing her from her slumber. She blinked, raising a hand to shield her eyes from the sun. Her limb caught on a set of sheets. The witch gazed down in confusion. Then she turned. A head of emerald hair rested against her shoulder. Emmy’s eyes turned up, twin galaxies of umbral green overshadowed by sparkling nebulae that surpassed the pillars of creation.

“Hey ya,” Sylvia sounded.

Esmeralda rose. Their lips touched. An impish smile grew on the lady’s face.

“Good morning, my adorable, darling princess,” Esmeralda said sweetly.

Sylvia sighed. “Why did you have to bring the p-word into this?”

Emmy’s eyes were teasing. “How crass of me. Did I swear in front of my princess?”

There was only one answer to this affront. Summoning all her emotions, Sylvia fixed her paramour with an exaggerated glower.

“Oh my, have I displeased her highness?” Esmeralda tittered happily. Her eyes drifted to the empty box. “I see what brought you out here, but what inspired you to eat it last night?”

“He visited me in my dreams.”

Esmeralda’s lips pressed. “What a rude man.”

“Very,” Sylvia commented dryly. “A crime without redemption. I say we chain him to a rock and have Windscourge peck out his liver every evening.”

The emerald-haired girl leaned against Sylvia once more, her gorgeous eyes fixed on the brilliant hues cast by the rising sun. The wicker chair rocked. Except, it was a wicker chair no longer. Sometime during the night, Emmy had transfigured their chairs into a double chaise lounge.

“That seems excessively cruel,” Esmeralda commented. “We should, at least, allow him weekends to recuperate.”

“Your benevolence knows no bounds,” Sylvia said blandly.

“As an advisor to the princess, it is my duty to dissuade your tyrannical whims,” Emmy returned.

Esmeralda was far, far too fond of the p-word. If Sylvia didn’t love the emeraldette to bits, she’d never put up with it.

Side-by-side, the two of them rocked together for a few minutes.

“When do we get married?”

“Mmm, allow me a year to prepare the venue and the dresses,” Esmeralda replied.

“I’ll leave it all to you then,” Sylvia said boldly.

Emmy’s eyes turned from the rising morn. The emerald-haired witch studied her companion for a long moment. “I see there is still a spark of boy inside of you.”

“How dreadful,” Sylvia commented, deadpan. “I suppose you’ll have no choice but to remain by my side until you can crush this infestation thoroughly.”

“Indeed,” Esmeralda agreed. “Such manners are quite unbecoming of a princess. I must diligently correct them.”

Sylvia turned to see Emmy’s umbral eyes. She was enjoying this too much. Sylvia wondered how Emmy would deal with the pain when her evil plot met its inevitable end.

But for now, there were words she needed to let free from her heart.

“I love you.”

Are Dark Guilds Out of Control?

By Tiffany Lowe

A cool wind brushes over my face, the wide Viridian shimmering in front of me. Beneath the river are twinkling, sapphire lights, the cores of the famous emerald crocodile that lurks in these green waters. The phantasmal beasts pay us no mind, having little interest in the broad vessel passing over their heads.

This is the common experience of those traveling from Viridian City to Mountain Point. Over the last four years, traffic along the Viridian River has tripled. This waterway has become an important artery of trade, bringing wood, stone, and other precious resources into Axis. For most of those who ply these waters, it’s a peaceful life interrupted only by the occasional beast. But, last week, this very river became a place of terror.

“They came out of nowhere then stabbed James through the chest. After throwing his body overboard, they took everything. Everything! They emptied my entire hold. I was lucky to make it out with my ship intact.”

These are the words of Harry Young, a long-time member of the farming guild Harvest Doom. His voice is just one among many. Whether it’s the path to Mountain Point or the skies around Windroost, dark guilds can be found everywhere. None of these wilderness territories are ‘PvE Only Zones’ as designated by former Governor Swallows. That means players are free to kill or steal from other gamers as they please.

“The dark guilds are a major problem,” Harry Young continued. “Dealing with the wildlife is already a risk. If pirates are taking my goods, how am I supposed to run a business?”

They aren’t the only ones asking themselves that question. As Governor Sanchez takes the reins of power, everyone is reminded of his promise to protect free enterprise. But the answer won’t be an easy one because the challenges along the Viridian River are nothing compared to the problems found in the skies around Windroost.

“They come at you from above while you’re fully loaded,” a hunter working in the Windroost area explained. “They’re geared for aerial combat, you’re not. A three sisters roc can hit 800 km/h when it dives. By the time you see them, you’re already dead. We’ve tried hiring guards, but it didn’t change a thing.”

In order to reach the sky islands or the meadows below, gamers rent mounts from the local dungeon. When hunting parties are attacked, these mounts are a dark gamer’s priority target. Because of this, the cost of a failed venture can be ruinous. Several small guilds have gone bankrupt already after encountering the worst.

But not everyone agrees that the dark guilds need to go.

“PvP is just part of the game. The fact that the System created special rules for it is proof of that. The Cloud Island Wilderness isn’t supposed to be a carbon copy of the real world. It’s supposed to be fun. Dark players and dark guilds add to the sense of adventure.”

When I asked him about Harry Young, he added:

“Look, obviously there have to be restrictions on PvP along the major trade routes. That’s just good economic sense. But guilds like Harvest Doom also have a responsibility. Hiring guards drives the economy and player-on-player conflict builds combat experience, both of which are important to the future of the Cloud Island Wilderness.”

The System Seeks Agents For Earth

By Liam Martin

Governor Sanchez has confirmed the recent rumors. Several gamers have been offered a chance to return to Earth. We spoke to the Governor on this topic.

“The quest is offered solely at the System’s discretion. The Cloud Island Government has no ability to interfere on this matter,” he told us. “What we can say is that all returnees will be agents working for the System. It’s a job, not a vacation, and one that comes with tight restrictions.”

Obtaining this quest isn’t easy. Gamers must have a core level greater than 500 and a lifetime merit of 10,000 points to even be considered. But what comes after is the real limit. The System mandates that all agents must first obtain the requisite set of knowledge by purchasing a series of skill books.

Spots are also limited. As far as anyone can tell, the System is only offering five.

“To live on Earth demands a compatible mortal body,” Governor Sanchez explained. “From what we know, these bodies come from patients who were left in a permanent coma. If your soul isn’t compatible with any of those bodies, then you won’t be selected for this quest no matter your qualifications.”

Nimbus Weekly knows this is a subject of great interest, so we will be following it closely.

Say Hello To Cloud Princess Sylvia Swallows

By Tiffany Lowe

On the stroke of midnight, House Bill J-01001-002 passed 63.8% to 36.2%, a major victory for those who supported Governor Swallows’s coronation. Parliament was quick to formalize her highness’s station, awarding Sylvia the official title of Cloud Princess.

Sadly, the newly minted princess was unavailable for comment. Instead, Nimbus Weekly reached out to Jennifer Ross from the Astral Observatory to answer the burning question on everyone’s mind. How long will we have to wait before we have a matching prince?

“Obviously, it’s mathematically impossible for any governor to earn the title of prince or princess this century. In fact, it’d take a minimum of 140 years, as governors cannot serve for more than two consecutive terms. Statistically speaking, it’s highly unlikely that the same governor would win so consistently. I’d estimate it’d be another 500 years before anyone is titled. One thousand, if we consider a prince in particular. Of course, all this assumes our laws remain static.”

For our disappointed readers, we can only offer one solace. The coin bearing her highness’s face will now and forever be known as a ‘princess’.
