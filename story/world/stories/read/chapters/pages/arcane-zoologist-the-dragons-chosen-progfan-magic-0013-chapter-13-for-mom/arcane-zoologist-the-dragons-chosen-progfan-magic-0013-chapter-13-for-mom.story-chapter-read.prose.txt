The sparkling, tingling energy stirred within her as Callie stared at the dandilynx plant. Could she perhaps make it grow bigger? After all, the dragon had grown a beautiful flower out of barren ground. Surely making a flower bloom when it was not too far from blooming wasn't such a stretch.

Callie focused her eyes on the dandilynx plant in front of her. She took a deep breath and thought about it growing bigger. That's how magic worked, right? You thought very hard about the thing that you wanted to happen, and then it happened?

The energy swelled within Callie, and once she felt like there was enough of it, Callie let it go, trying to aim it at the plant. Aim was a generous term. The energy moved of its own accord, and she felt like she didn't really have much control over it. But surely the magic should have understood what she wanted, right?

However, as Callie released the energy within her, it burst out in the form of fire, which burnt the plant to a crisp. Callie yelped, jumping back from fright.

"Callie, did something happen?" Dad called.

"It's fine!" Callie hurriedly kicked some soil onto the smoldering plant and stomped it down. "Just saw a snake slither by."

Thankfully, Dad was some distance away and couldn't see exactly what was going on in front of Callie.

"Don't pick it up!" Dad warned.

"I-I won't…" Callie replied with a strained voice. Much to her relief, Dad didn't seem to notice that, instead going back to his search.

"Where's the snake?" Callen appeared a moment later, scanning the surroundings.

Callie pulled on his sleeve, making him bend down. When he was low enough, she whispered. "There is no snake. I accidentally burned a plant…"

"What?" Callen hissed, but thankfully remained quiet enough for Dad not to hear him.

"I wanted to use magic to make it bloom. But instead it… just burned," Callie said, looking at the spot on the ground where the dandilynx plant had been.

"B-Burned…" Callen blinked, then crouched down. "Okay, I think this magic of yours is a bit out of control at the moment. You can hear animals talk and burn stuff. Can you try not to use it?"

"I've only heard Wiggles talk. But I can control it… to an extent," Callie said. After all, she had released her magic on purpose this time, even if the result wasn't what she wanted.

"Okay. Please try to keep it contained. We don't know anything about it and currently… We just need to find a way to save Mom," Callen said, a hint of desperation leaking into his voice.

He still had his strong "Big Brother" face on, but Callie could tell how truly worried for Mom he was. As was she. Callen was absolutely right. Magic, arcane beasts, and dragons could wait until Mom was better.

"Let's keep searching," Callie said with a nod.

As the search continued and the sun slowly crept beyond the horizon, Callie's heart sank. She was tempted to try using her magic again, feeling the familiar swell of energy rise within her as her frustration grew. But she held the energy back this time. What would be the point of setting the forest on fire?

Eventually, Dad urged them to return home. He wanted to remain in the forest to continue the search, but Callen and Callie managed to talk him out of it. Even Callie wouldn't be able to recognize the plant in the dark. What hope did Dad have?

However, even at home, sleep didn't come to Callie. Her mind was filled with worry for Mom. Part of her was angry at the arcane beast who dared to hurt her Mom so badly. But another part was angry at Mom. She undoubtedly provoked it in the first place. In an attempt to protect the village so the arcane beast didn't hurt Callie, Callen, or anyone else.

But surely there was a better way to do it than just killing it. A way to get even a dangerous arcane beast to leave the village in peace without anyone suffering in the process. Maybe if she could talk to it like she talked to Wiggles?

First, however, they had to find that dandilynx flower. Mom couldn't lose her arm. That would be… No, it couldn't happen. Callie would find a way to get those dandilynx flowers to bloom. Perhaps there was a place in the forest where they were already in full bloom. Like near that cave where Callie met the dragon.

An idea appeared in her mind. Wouldn't the dragon be able to make the dandilynx flower bloom? If it could grow a flower out of nothing, then making a little flower bloom would be easy.

That gave Callie hope. Finding the dragon wouldn't be easy, and she couldn't let Dad find out about it. But maybe Callen could distract Dad for long enough for Callie to slip away. Sure, Dad would be mad afterwards, but if she got the blooming flower for Mom, that was all that mattered.

With her mind set, Callie was able to finally fall asleep.

The next morning, the whole family was up at dawn. That meant that neither one of them had gotten much sleep with how early the sun rose during the summer. But nobody complained, not even Callie, as they all hurriedly ate breakfast of eggs, flatbread, and tomatoes.

After that, they all headed back into the forest to search for the dandilynx flower.

"Dad, I think we should search deeper in the forest. It's unlikely we'll find a blooming flower in the same place," Callie said.

"Do you have any ideas?" Dad asked.

"Um… that time I got lost in the forest, I saw many different plants in the area. We could try searching there," Callie offered, hoping that Dad wouldn't be able to somehow figure out her intentions.

He narrowed his eyes at her suspiciously, but eventually nodded his head. "I feel like you're not telling me something, but fine. If we can find that damned flower, then it's all that matters. Lead the way!"

Callie had only been to the cave once, but she had nonetheless remembered the path. All she had to do was take the forest trail leading to the matricaria leaf grove, which went past that giant mossy rock that looked like a nose. After that, they stepped off the trail to the left and headed towards the densest part of the forest.

"Are you sure you know where we are going? There's no trail here," Dad said suspiciously.

"I am sure," Callie confirmed. The area was rather unique, so Callie had remembered it well. The first giveaway was the wet soil, which had made walking rather difficult during the night. Upon closer inspection, Callie saw that there were bits of clay peeking through.

Before long, she noticed the rocky outcroppings. Somewhere, there was the entrance to the dragon's cave. Callie's heart thundered with excitement. While it was more likely that the dragon wasn't there, returning to this place was making Callie feel closer to it.

"Here it is," Callie announced, stopping some distance away from the cave. It wouldn't be smart to lead Dad too close and have him run into the dragon.

"It looks rather murky in these parts. Are you sure any flowers can bloom here?" Dad asked skeptically.

He was right that the vegetation was actually sparse here, consisting mainly of moss and ferns. Oh, please don't let Dad figure out her plan!

"We searched everywhere else yesterday. I figure trying a new place would be better…" Callie said meekly. Oh, how she hated lying…

"Okay, we'll search here for a few hours. But if we don't find the flower, we are going somewhere else. I'll search in this direction. Callen, you search there. Callie, always stay where either Callen or I can see you!"

"Okay," Callen and Callie agreed.

As soon as Dad was out of earshot, Callen turned to Callie. "What are you planning, little sister?"

Geh! Her brother had seen right through her. It wasn't really that surprising. Dad and Callen both likely knew Callie was lying—they just didn't have enough time to care about the reason why.

"Could you distract Dad for a little bit?" Callie whispered to her brother.

Callen blinked. "What? Why?"

"I'm going to try and find the dragon," she said.

Callen's eyes went wide, and he clearly struggled to hold in his voice as it came out strained. "Wh-Why would you do something so dangerous?"

"The dragon could grow a flower out of nothing. Making a little dandilynx plant bloom would be easy for it," Callie said.

"Callie, that is way too dangerous. What if it hurts you? It's a… you know…" Callen stammered, not quite daring to utter the final word.

"It is NOT a monster," Callie snapped. "Do you want to save Mom or not?"

"Of course I do." Callen looked offended that Callie would even dare to consider otherwise.

"Then give me time to find the dragon. There just aren't any dandilynx flowers blooming right now. It doesn't matter if we search the whole forest. The dragon might be our only hope."

Callen sighed. Then he ran his hands through his hair, pursing his lips all the while. It looked like he was having quite the internal struggle.

"For Mom!" Callie urged him.

"Alright, fine! I'll keep Dad distracted. But be back within an hour, two at the latest!" Callen said.

"Yes! Thank you, Brother!" Callie said. She slowly, deliberately made her way closer and closer to the dragon's cave, keeping close to the ground, sticking close to trees, so in case Dad threw a glance in her direction, he wouldn't notice her sneaking away.

Eventually, she made her way to the dragon's cave. Her heart thundered in her chest as she touched the stony wall. A dragon had been here. She had known that already, but it was so exciting. She hadn't really realized how amazing it had been the morning she woke up. All the events had made her a bit dull.

But now, seeing it in a clear mind, Callie couldn't help but feel amazed. Was the cave the preferred place for dragons, or had it been there purely by accident? Hiding from something, perhaps? No, that was silly. What would a dragon need to hide from?

Hunters. Like her Mom.

Callie's heart grew heavy. This never-ending dispute… if only there were a way to make others see reason. But it wasn't the time for that. Callie needed to get that flower for Mom. After that, she could worry about other things.

She descended into the cave, slowly, to avoid falling. Her heart beat faster with each as she peeked down, hoping to see a sign of green scales or any movement. But soon, her heart dropped as she saw the cave empty, the same as the morning when she woke up.

"Stupid! You're so stupid, Callie! Why would the dragon be here when it wasn't there when you woke up?" Callie said aloud, tears welling in her eyes.

She had been so fixated on the fact that the dragon could grow the flower that she didn't consider whether she could even find it on a whim. Of course, she couldn't. She wasn't so special that a dragon, the king of all arcane beasts, would show up whether Callie wished for it.

"Stupid, stupid, stupid!" Callie chastised herself, trying and failing to hold back her tears. What about Mom? What if she lost her arm? How could she get the flower to grow now?

Why you cry?

A soft, slightly girlish voice spoke into Callie's mind. She whirled in surprise, searching for the source, but couldn't find anything. Could it be the dragon? It sounded too… gentle to be a dragon, though. Although what did she know about how dragons sounded?

Up here.

Callie turned towards the cave exit and saw the glimmerhare sitting there, looking down at Callie with its bright blue eyes. She gasped. She could hear the voice of the glimmerhare.
