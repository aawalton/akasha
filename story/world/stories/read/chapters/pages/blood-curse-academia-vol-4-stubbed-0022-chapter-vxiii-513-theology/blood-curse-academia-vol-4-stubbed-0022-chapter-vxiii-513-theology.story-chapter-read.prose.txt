Chapter  V.XIII (5.13) - Theology

“What are Genies?” Kizu asked. He asked the question of his own free will, having exited the unasked for vision set on him by Zayne.

Unlike his previous experiments with the soul potion, Zayne remained visible after the return of Kizu’s consciousness. The spearman from a distant land and time stood in front of the skeletons playing cards.

If the skeletons comprehended the presence of his soul, they showed no sign of it. One of the ones with charred bones from Inari’s attack placed a card and the next one, clearly cheating, countered it. It was possible that they did know of Zayne’s existence but didn’t care enough about it to react.

Mort, on the other hand, who sat watching on the other side of the table, clearly had sight of Zayne, likely through his connection to Kizu’s soul. The monkey watched everything with golden eyes. Not for the first time, Kizu wondered how much of a connection there was between his familiar and Zayne. Both were connected through his soul but neither seemed to interfere with the other's connection.

“We used to play a similar game with strips of dried leather,” Zayne recalled, still examining the skeletons. “As I see more of the world through your eyes, the more memories of the past world open up to me. If you had asked me about Genies a month ago, I would have struggled to express coherent words to define them.”

“But now?” Kizu pressed.

Zayne swiped down at one of the skeleton's hand of cards. His hand passed through the bones without resistance.

“Now I can tell you that Genies once existed as powerful entities from my homeland. They were said to aid humans. However, I knew of none who experienced that so-called kindness first-hand. The rumors said they were supposed to be a bit like their cousins, the Kitsune. And like their cousins, they also originated from a Titan.”

“A…Titan?”

“Behemoth? Great One? Originator?”

Kizu shook his head slowly. He knew of titan as a word for something large. Nothing more. “Are you implying that The World Dungeon is a Titan?”

“Yes. You should study more theology.”

“I’ll add it to my list of subjects,” Kizu replied dryly. “I don’t suppose you can embed that information in my mind like you did astronomy?”

“No. I am no theologist. My father taught me the stars and of the wonders of worlds beyond as a way of connecting and bonding with me. Theology would never serve as a substitute for that. It is grim and repressive in its nature.”

“Then what can you tell me about the Genies?”

Zayne considered the question. “I think it’s better I show you again. When you and I both have the time. I still need to organize my memories, but it is all far clearer than before. The longer I spend as your companion, the more individuality returns to me. The time will come.”

The time…Kizu jolted, as if shocked by lightning. He scrambled over to the side of his hammock and pulled out his scrying orb. He let out a sigh of relief. He still had half an hour until his daily dinner with Taroe and Anata. Not enough time for another soul journey. But plenty for finishing up a bit more studying and tidying up.

Kizu looked up from his scrying orb to ask Zayne a final question, only to find himself alone with the undead skeletons and Mort watching him from the other side of the table.

“I don’t suppose you know anything about what a Genie is?” Kizu asked his familiar.

But of course, unless it lived in the Hon Basin or could be eaten, Mort was both unknowing and uninterested.

Kizu closed his eyes and thought back to all the information given to him that preceded the memory of Zayne speaking with Lahel. Despite receiving them earlier that same day, the memories of the astronomy lessons weren’t fresh in his mind. But they were there. While the exact details of the lessons, especially the earlier ones when Zayne was young, were clouded by the fog of time, the basic information remained. It reminded Kizu of learning arithmetic with Anna when he was young. He didn’t recall the exact lessons of addition and subtraction. But he did still retain the basic knowledge that was later built upon. And, of course, he remembered that the lessons had occurred as well as other details involving the class structure. Like that the burned man had been Zayne’s father. But, curiously, Kizu lacked the insight of where those burns originated.

“It was never discussed during their astronomy lessons,” Kizu realized after some reflection. “I know he was Zayne’s father because that’s how he addressed him both verbally and internally.”

Kizu shuddered as he realized he currently felt more love for Zayne’s father than he did his own. And, while that was a low bar for affection, it still disturbed him. As Kizu took on these memories, he felt some portion of him melding with Zayne.

“Well, I have the astronomy information at the very least,” Kizu declared to Mort. “We’re one step closer to finding my sister.”

Mort hummed. A noise of acknowledgement, rather than one of approval. Kizu could feel his familiar’s hesitance to encourage his actions. It was Kizu’s soul the familiar had bonded with, not Zayne’s.

“You’re bonded to my soul,” Kizu stated, wanting to address an earlier thought. “Then…Mort, can you sense the soul fragment of me that broke off? The renegade piece that serves Otochi. I know you’ve never personally encountered it before, but can you sense it at all?”

Mort cocked his head. Kizu felt the monkey concentrating through their bond. Mort’s examination and scrutiny of him slipped away a moment later as it sought something beyond him. Then Mort flinched and Kizu felt the sudden burst of pain between their bond.

Kizu jumped out of the hammock and was at Mort’s side in an instant right as the monkey collapsed on the table. No longer attempting to connect with the other entity, the pain left Mort nearly as swiftly as it had descended on him. But the memory of the jolt made Mort’s muscles quiver and unable to support his weight.

“I’m so sorry, Mort.” Kizu gently scooped up the monkey into his arms.

He felt Mort try to protest and put forward a strong front, but he felt him trembling. Thankfully, the sensation lessened every second. In under a minute, Mort was rattled, but otherwise uninjured.

“Do you know where he’s at?” Kizu asked as he set Mort back on the table.

The monkey nodded, then gestured down. Below the table. Below the underground lake. Below the stone. And into the World Dungeon.

Kizu had known the soul fragment was among Otochi’s retinue of followers. It only made sense for him to be down below with them. And yet, that piece of intel also served as a massive relief. A weight lifted from his shoulders. Because, if the corrupted piece of his soul was down there, it meant he wasn’t somewhere else, impersonating Kizu.

That nagging fear had constantly needed to be pushed to the side ever since discovering the existence of his doppelganger. It would take very little effort on his soul fragment to utterly ruin Kizu’s name and reputation. Taroe and Headmaster Ballarfulur knew of its existence, but he doubted his excuse of an evil twin would do much to rebuild any other bridges the soul fragment decided to burn.

At least, in a way, the situation helped him relate to Ione. He wondered if the evil Kizu would have any interest in dating Sene.
