Chapter 32

“There is a Black Madonna in the crypt of Saint-Victor that is seven hundred years old. There is also something in the crypt that is made of seven people who went in last week. Both of these things are in the same room and tomorrow we’re going into that room.” - The Ether Atlas: Field Notes of Steven Parr, Marseille

“The last time I walked this route,” the scout with the crossbow said, “four of my friends were alive.”

His name was Julien and he’d been a postman before the sky changed. The route to Saint-Victor used to be his Tuesday delivery. The other scout, Marc, a fisherman’s son with a sharpened boat pole and careful eyes hadn’t said anything since they’d left the harbour.

“How far?” Donna asked from the front of the group.

"Ten minutes. The approach is clear. The last two hundred metres, that is where the creatures start."

“Start what?” Steve asked.

“Appearing. All day, all night. Like they’re drawn to it.” Julien checked his crossbow’s string tension without looking down. "Don’t know why. It is not normal, this place. They don't leave."

"Steve," Donna called back. "What's the abbey like? What am I walking into?"

“Fifth-century fortress-church, built over a fourth-century tomb, built over a Hellenistic necropolis. Walls are about two metres thick at the base. One main entrance. Possibly secondary access through the south cloister, but Rémy’s scouts used the front.”

“Single entry.” Donna repeated and they could hear the displeasure in her voice. “If things go wrong inside, we’re retreating through the same chokepoint we entered.”

“I know.” Arthur said.

“Just making sure, that's all.”

Marc spoke to Julien in quiet French. Steve overheard it.

“What did he say?” Arthur asked.

“His cousin was one of the seven.”

Nobody spoke for the next thirty metres. Archie’s geometry drifted beside Arthur, dim and silent. Twelve hours now. The entire harbour fight, the compound meeting, the night, the dawn. Arthur had stopped expecting a response and at some point, in the early hours of dawn, lying on the warehouse floor, he’d made peace with it. Archie might come back. He might not. The crypt was still there either way.

He came back between one step and the next.

Without warning the dim lattice snapped to full luminescence in a single pulse. New geometries rotating through the structure, the light sharper and more complex than anything from London.

“Sector authorisation recalibration complete.” Archie said. The voice was the same and yet he sounded more confident. “Operational parameters updated. Regional guidance mode active.”

Arthur jumped. "Archie. Where the bloody hell have you been? Twelve hours." He was surprised at how happy his voice sounded.

“The transition between operational sectors requires architectural recalibration. Processing duration was within expected parameters.” Archie paused angled himself towards the abbey and continued. “The structure ahead registers Ether density consistent with an active Major Node. Significantly above the surrounding baseline. I would advise heightened caution on approach.”

Steve was smiling as well.  "Good to have you back. And was that actual tactical advice? I'm rather touched."

Donna smirked from the front of the group. “Don’t encourage him.”

Julien was staring around with wide eyes. “Vous parlez à qui, là?”

“I keep forgetting how disconcerting this could be for others,” Steve said. "Julien, it's all right, don't worry about it."

“Steve, tell him Arthur talks to his notifications sometimes,” Donna added, smiling.

“"Oh, sod off. Steve, don't you dare," Arthur protested. But Steve was already translating.

Julien looked at Arthur. "Peuchère," he said in a sympathetic voice. "You're talking to the air."

“Welcome to our life,” Donna said, chuckling.

***

A boar rushed them from the collapsed wall at the abbey’s southern approach.

It was bigger than the harbour creatures. This one had lived in the node’s shadow, soaking in the Sanctum-level Ether for days. The chitin was darker and thicker. Its legs pumped with furious force as it barrelled forward.

Julien fired, the bolt hit chitin and skidded sideways with a sound like a stone skipping ice. “Merde.”

Arthur stepped in and raised his shield. The charge hit the Bullguard and his boots slid four centimetres on the stone, something that hadn’t happened at the harbour. He braced harder and the creature’s tusks scraped across the shield face hunting for an edge to hook.

Donna flanked from the left. Her first strike bit shallow, the chitin absorbing what should have been a killing blow. The boar wrenched its head towards her, tusks sweeping low to gouge. Donna leapt back while Arthur moved in again.

He rammed the shield boss into its snout and hooked his hammer head over the nearest tusk, wrenching the skull back towards him. The neck opened on Donna’s side, and she didn’t waste the opening. She adjusted, her left shoulder hitching on the backswing, and the blade punched through on the second hit. The creature dropped.

“These are tougher,” Donna said, examining a chip in her axe’s edge. The harbour boar hadn’t chipped the blade.

“Proximity to the node. Higher density, bigger creatures, harder armour. It won’t get any friendlier inside.” Steve said.

***

The abbey doors were wedged back. Eight people had wanted to be able to run; only one had.

"Bon. This is where we stop," Julien said in a steady voice, trying to mask his unsteady hands. “Rémy’s orders. We hold the entrance. If you’re not back by midday, we report to the harbourmaster.”

Marc looked at the open doors and said something.

“He said, ‘Tell them I’m sorry I wasn’t here.’ He means the scouts. His cousin.” Steve said.

Arthur met Marc’s eyes and nodded and got a nod back. A contract between two men who carried the names of people they couldn’t save.

The crypt stairs spiralled down into gloom and cold. They found a dropped torch on the third landing and a single boot with its laces still tied. Arthur stared at the boot. Laces tied meant it had been pulled off a foot that was still wearing it. Donna saw it too and adjusted her grip.

Steve had stopped on the last step. "Early Christian," he said. "Late fourth century, early fifth. Some of the oldest Christian tombs in France. I've only ever seen these in photographs."

"Whisper or shut up," Donna said.

The crypt opened below them and Donna tossed in a couple of chem sticks. As the light settled, they found themselves surrounded by sarcophagi in alcoves carved into living rock and now illuminated by a green glow. At the far end stood the Black Madonna carved from dark wood.

Steve dropped his voice but kept going. “This crypt was originally the church. The first one, built beside the martyrs' graves. In the eleventh century the monks built a bigger abbey straight on top and this became its cellar."

Arthur had been reading the vaulting since the stairs. Squat pillars and thick arches, carrying a thousand years of abbey. "So, everything above us is sitting on these."

"Most of it."

"Better not hit the pillar then."

Donna shone her chem stick into the alcove carved about halfway into the crypt. The sarcophagus lid in it sat askew, and the scrape marks through the old grime was fresh.

"That's half a tonne of marble, give or take," Arthur said quietly. "It didn't shift itself."

And as if on cue something moved behind a sarcophagus.

***

It pulled itself from the shadows like a nightmare coalescing in the dark. The movement was slow at first, another of the dozen shadows in the chamber and then it straightened. The nightmare manifesting fully.

Human bodies and old bones were fused into a single mass by Ether that had rewritten their biology without regard for where one person ended, and another began. Limbs connected to torsos they didn’t belong to. Hands arranged along its flanks like the oars of a galley, reaching and pulling. Heads sat at angles where no spine supported them. And then there were the faces, they were recognizable as human faces, but the features had been rearranged. A jaw fused to a brow fused to a mouth that opened sideways.

Archie rotated next to the party. “Human-derived Ether construct. Biological fusion event leading to involuntary fusion of biomass. Guardian-class entity but not a Guardian. Threat assessment: significant.”

Not a guardian? Involuntary recombination. Arthur looked at Steve. The colour had left his face.

The guardian reached for them in a wave of limbs extending from the central mass, covering the crypt floor in grasping hands. It was a flood, and for a moment they were left gaping at what was happening in front of them.

“Tight! Behind the shield!” Arthur raised the Bullguard and planted.

Six hands hit the shield at once; the pressure distributed across the entire surface. Arthur braced and tried to push back but the hands didn’t stop. They flowed around the edges, reaching for gaps. Fingers scraped his forearm guard, and more hands came from below, groping for his ankles. It was like trying to hold back a tide of hands.

Arthur triggered structural Reinforcement and the ground cracked beneath him. He held the centre, but the hands kept coming. He couldn’t move, couldn’t reposition, couldn’t do anything except be a wall while the flood found the gaps around him.

Donna went right and triggered Reflex Acceleration, gaining two seconds of terrible clarity. She read the mass and found the seams. The joints where bodies met imperfectly, where tissue had fused without proper integration. Her axe found the first seam and something that resembled half a leg separated from a torso. The abomination screamed with three mouths.

Then Donna watched in horror as the entity adapted.

The seam she’d struck sealed and rearranged itself. Tissue flowed across the wound, and the hands on that side retracted to cover the vulnerable joint. The thing had felt where the blade found weakness, and it was protecting itself.

“It’s closing the gaps,” Donna said. Her voice was tight. “I can’t hit the same point twice.”

Steve drove the Reacher into the guardian’s left flank. The sensory feedback telling him things he could have done without knowing - human tissue, warm, dense, and recognisably muscle wrapped around a core of crystallised Ether. He twisted the blade.

A hand shot from the mass and gripped the Reacher’s shaft. It was followed by a second, then a third. The three hands gripped the polearm and started pulling the weapon and Steve towards the fused mass. He hit a sarcophagus and the impact knocked the air from his lungs. The Reacher was being drawn into the guardian’s body and Steve was still holding on to it, his hands locked in the involuntary death grip of a body that had decided in panic that the weapon was the only thing between it and death.

“Steve! Let go!” Donna screamed.

Steve was staring wide-eyed at the writhing construct of flesh and bone.

Arthur grunted, dropped Reinforcement, and swung the lump hammer into the three hands gripping the Reacher. Bone, chitin and fused tissue cracked under steel and the hands released the pole. Steve fell backwards, gasping.

But Arthur had opened himself and the creature used the opening.

Without the shield in the line, it surged forward. Its full mass moving at once. Arthur tried to get the Bullguard up, but the angle was wrong and the mass hit his left side. Hands gripped his stab vest and pulled. Without Structural Reinforcement anchoring him his feet went out from under him. He crashed onto his back and the guardian’s weight pressed down.

Arthur realised in horror that the abomination wasn’t attacking him. Instead, the hands were pulling at his armour, his limbs, trying to draw him into itself. To engulf him and make him a part of the writhing mass.

Arthur felt human fingers close around his wrist and begin to pull his arm into the mass. The pressure was enormous. The wet cold of the fused tissue pressed against his skin and it was the worst thing he’d ever felt. Pure wrongness, the sensation of touching something that had once been alive and was now something else entirely.

His arms were already covered with writhing flesh. Unable to swing his hammer, he started doing the only thing he could. He gripped the flesh and started to pull it apart. If this thing wanted to consume him, he would tear it from within and crawl back out.

Donna barked an order. “Steve, start cutting, forget about joints, just cut.”

Then Donna’s axe was in the mix. Her second Reflex Acceleration triggered, and she came in with three strikes, each one finding a different seam. Steve was moving the billhook about like a scythe.

The monster howled from multiple mouths, each at a different pitch. It was spread across the floor, trying to consume and defend simultaneously. It couldn’t protect every point at once and now it was beset from two sides and from within.

Donna kept hacking, both Reflex Acceleration bursts were used up. This was raw speed and fury of a woman watching her friend disappear under a wall of human wreckage.

Arthur had managed to rip his right arm free while Donna and Steve were cutting. And as he made a fist of his left inside the body of the monster his hands gripped something round and slick. He could feel the core pulsing, the ether keeping the flesh and bones fused. One of the limbs was on his throat now, inching up towards his nose. Arthur screamed and pulled. The core came away and he fell back.

Without the core fuelling the fusion, the amalgamation came apart. The bodies separated and dropped onto the stone like a structure losing its supports in sequence.

Arthur lay panting on the ground, gulping the stuff air in great lungfuls till Donna came and offered her hand.

He took it and stood. His ribs ached where the weight had compressed against his chest. His left wrist was bruised, the skin already darkening. He was alive but the margin had been uncomfortably small.

Steve was sitting against the wall. White-faced and breathing shallow. The Reacher was across his knees; his hands still locked around the shaft.

"It's dead, Steve," Donna said. "You can let go."

"I know," Steve said. "My hands don't."

Donna crouched in front of him and took his right hand. She worked the fingers off the shaft one at a time, starting with the little finger, and then did the same with the left. She stood and propped the Reacher against the wall beside him.

Arthur looked at the core he had torn out. It was slightly larger than the last Guardian but unlike other cores this had red veins running over its surface. He put it in his pack.

Archie’s geometry contracted briefly. “Human-derived Ether signature. Residual biological patterning. I have insufficient comparative data on cores of this origin.”

Steve didn’t say anything. He was looking at the seven bodies on the crypt floor and writing something in his guidebook with hands that wouldn’t stay steady.

As they waited for their hearts to stop hammering, Marc ducked into the crypt. He must have left Julien at the entrance when the fighting started, orders or no orders. He was kneeling beside one of the separated bodies, holding the dead man’s hand.

“Merci,” he whispered, and Donna put a hand on his shoulder.

Arthur took a deep breath and looked around. He desperately wanted to be back above ground, and they were still not done with the chamber.

“Let’s take thirty and then we need to figure out what ‘Touch the anchor where the shadows fall back’ is all about,” he said, taking out dried fish from his pack. “I want away from this bloody place as soon as possible.”
