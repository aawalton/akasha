Xavier took a reflexive step back, eyes widening as he stared at the glowing red eye embedded within the stone wall of the Void Dungeon.

The glowing red eye that had just proclaimed, in a startlingly loud and chilling voice, that it was the dungeon.

Xavier swallowed. He’d felt the rage of this place pointed at him before, but this was different. This was more acute. Stronger.

Far stronger.

And now the damned thing is talking to me? That can’t be right…

Derrida, the ancient Wanderer who’d dropped him here then vanished with barely a word, had certainly not mentioned anything like this.

“You’re… the dungeon?”

“YES. GOOD LISTENING.”

Xavier blinked, asking his Personal System to identify this thing.

I have no information about this. However, this does not appear to be a lie or misdirection. Utilising the strength of all your senses and the knowledge I possess, there is nothing to say this isn’t genuine.

I suppose that was at least somewhat helpful…

Xavier hadn’t even considered if this thing might not be the genuine article. He’d already felt enough of the dungeon’s hatred to know that this was the real deal.

The question was: Why was the Void Dungeon speaking directly to him? Did it always do this, or was this something unique?

Xavier ran a hand through his hair, suddenly feeling awkward. Then he straightened. Why did he feel awkward? He knew the dungeon was sentient, and he hadn’t come here to make friends with it. He didn’t need to feel sorry for the beasts he’d just slain.

“Why are you talking to me, Dungeon?”

“YOU ARE NOT WELCOME HERE.”

Xavier blinked. That threw him off base again. “I’m not?” He scratched the back of his head idly. “And why might that be?”

“I KNOW WHAT YOU ARE. WHO YOU ARE. YOU HAVE THE SMELL OF THE BEAST ON YOU. DEEP INSIDE YOU. YOU REEK OF ITS MALOVELENCE.”

The smell of the beast…?

Xavier was taken aback. The first thing that came to his mind was the Interdimensional Dragon—the devourer of universes that had come from a different version, actually several different versions, of Xavier Collins.

Could the Void Dungeon somehow know? Xavier wasn’t about to give that one away if it didn’t.

He waved a hand in the air. “You want to kick me out of your dungeon because I smell?”

There was a silence.

“THE BEAST DESTROYED MY WORLD. MY UNIVERSE. I WAS PLUNGED INTO THIS UNENDING ABYSS. TO THIS LIFE THAT IS NOT LIFE. TO THIS AGONISINGLY SLOW DEATH. DO NOT LIE AND SAY YOU DO NOT UNDERSTAND OF WHAT I SPEAK, DRAGONKIN. I CAN SMELL IT ON YOU. YOU KNOW.”

Xavier definitely did know. The problem was, he was pretty sure that he and Derrida were perhaps the only beings in the entirety of the multiverse—except for the Interdimensional Dragon itself—who did know.

So how could the Void Dungeon have figured it out?

He narrowed his eyes. “Surely other versions of me have walked these halls before,” Xavier mused aloud.

There was a pause. The red eye blinked. The glow did not dim. Xavier, not having expected it to be able to blink, stopped himself from taking a step back. His wings twitched, and he had the sudden urge to be far, far away from this dungeon.

As much as he wanted to complete the dungeon, he knew he wasn’t anywhere near ready for that.

What if this thing teleports me to a deeper floor? A floor I’m not yet strong enough to handle?

It wasn’t as though Xavier hadn’t been thrust into such positions in the past. The System back in his corner of the multiverse was particularly prone to throwing him deeper into the Tower of Champions than was strictly fair.

“THEY HAVE.” The massive glowing eye embedded in the stone blinked again, then narrowed, still staring directly at Xavier. “BUT THEY DIDN’T HAVE YOUR STINK. YOU ARE MORE CONNECTED TO THE BEAST THAN ANY OF THEM EVER WERE. THOUGH LOOKING BACK, I SEE NOW THEY WERE CONNECTED TOO. HOW I EVER MISSED IT, I DO NOT KNOW.”

Xavier let the words the Void Dungeon told him sink in. Something in his stomach twisted.

More connected? How? And what could that mean?

And how did this thing know?

Xavier wondered if he should admit the truth, or if that would be the biggest mistake he could make, but something told him this Void Dungeon would be able to sense if he lied to it, and lying… that might be an even bigger mistake than telling the truth.

“You speak of the Interdimensional Dragon, do you not?” Xavier wanted to be sure they were on the same page.

“YOU KNOW THAT I DO.”

Xavier released a breath. He produced an armchair from his Storage Ring and sunk back into it, relaxing his body and mind. Then he leant his elbows on his legs and, clasped his hands together, and leant forward. “I could lie to you, but I won’t. You are right. I am connected to it.”

The glowing eye flared a deeper red and grew in size several inches, narrowing further as it continued to stare at him. Xavier felt the rage wafting of it in waves.

He raised a halting hand. “But that does not mean what you seem to think it might. The Interdimensional Dragon came from a version of me, yes. From… several versions of me, actually.” Xavier eyes changed, turning deeply serious, and deeply angry. “But I can guarantee you that I wish for its death perhaps more than anyone in the multiverse. Not only that, I’m on the path toward destroying it, once and for all.”

“YOU?” The Void Dungeon’s voice sounded baffled.

Xavier inclined his head, exuding as much confidence as he could muster. It wasn’t hard. This was his path. He had more than just hope that he would achieve it. He had steel-forged determination. “Me.”

“YOU ARE NOTHING BUT A WEAK CHILD, STUMBLING IN THE DARK, KILLING THE MOST MEAGRE BEASTS I CREATE. YOU CANNOT POSSIBLY KILL THE INTERDIMENSIONAL DRAGON.”

Xavier nodded. “You’re right. Right now, I’m close to nothing. Hell, in the grand scheme of things, I am nothing. And compared with that brute of a beast?” He shook his head and gave a chuckle. “I don’t stand a chance.”

“I SHOULD KILL YOU WHERE YOU STAND JUST FOR SMELLING LIKE THAT THING. AND IF ANY MORE VERSIONS OF YOU STUMBLE INTO MY DOMAIN, THEY SHOULD SUFFER THE SAME. A SWIFT DEATH. IT’S MORE THAN YOU DESERVE.”

Xavier’s heart skipped a beat. If the Void Dungeon truly wanted him dead, he was quite sure there was nothing he could do about it. Something told him the temporal cage wouldn’t work on the dungeon itself—it was too powerful, too large, to succumb to someone as weak as him.

But if it wanted him dead, wouldn’t he be dead by now? Or at least fighting hordes of Void Dungeon-created beasts, well on his way to becoming dead?

Xavier opened his hands in a sort of shrug. “You’re right. You could kill me. I dare say I’ve probably done something to deserve it. I haven’t exactly left a lot of beings alive in my wake. But I am not the Interdimensional Dragon, and I am not responsible for its actions. If you hate the Interdimensional Dragon as much as I think you do, then the best course of action for you is to leave me be, to let me keep delving deeper through your floors. To treat me just as you would any another who has entered your domain.”

“I TREAT OTHERS WHO ENTER MY DOMAIN WITH DEATH. YOU WILL NOT REACH MY HEART. YOU WILL NOT CLAIM MY SOUL. AND I WILL NOT ALLOW YOU TO ESCAPE.”

Xavier pursed his lips. Something told him this wasn’t strictly true—that the dungeon didn’t merely kill all those who entered. He was sure there were plenty of delvers who’d left this place, though Xavier didn’t have the means to do so, Derrida certainly did. As far as he could tell, she was able to come and go as she pleased, though he wasn’t a hundred percent sure of that, she had to have some plan for how to take him back out of here when she deemed him ready.

Reach my heart… claim my soul…

The Void Dungeon must be talking about its core. Was its core its soul? Xavier had long discovered that souls were also cores—so he supposed it shouldn’t surprise him if that was the case. Just because none of the Void Dungeon-created beasts possessed souls didn’t mean the dungeon itself lacked one.

Xavier stood, depositing the armchair back into his Storage Ring. “I will not die here.” He sounded surer than he felt. Doubt didn’t creep into his voice, but it crept into his heart. He wasn’t sure where it had come from.

Was he truly afraid of the Void Dungeon?

No… It’s not the Void Dungeon I’m afraid of.

The Interdimensional Dragon. That was why the doubt had crept into him. The Void Dungeon somehow knew of his connection to it. Felt a stronger connection from him than any other version of him… but how could that be?

How could he be connected to it?

Because I want to kill it… Because I know it’s me…

But neither of those reasons rang true to him.

Because I’m more like it than any other version of me has been before?

That didn’t quite ring true either, but there felt like there was more truth to it than the other reason.

Again, he couldn’t help but worry about what might happen after he defeated the Interdimensional Dragon. And not just for him, but for the countless other versions of Xavier Collins, the different unfoldings, that would be fighting by his side during the conflict.

What happened if one of them, or more than one of them, turned evil?

What if they wanted more power?

What if the united Xaviers became something far worse than the Interdimensional Dragon ever could be?

What if they joined together…?

Such power…

Xavier dispelled these thoughts, as he’d done so many times before.

That isn’t who I am!

His whole body tensed, every muscle flexing, the anger evident on his face.

The glowing red eye embedded into the stone wall of the Void Dungeon blinked.

“I WILL NOT INTERFERE WITH THE NORMAL RUN OF THIS DUNGEON, XAVIER COLLINS, BUT NEITHER WILL I LET YOU LEAVE. ONLY ONE OF US WILL SURVIVE WHEN THIS IS OVER. TRUST ME WHEN I SAY NO ONE EVER LEAVES THIS PLACE WITHOUT MY ALLOWING IT.” The eye blinked. “NOT EVEN THE WANDERERS.”

The glowing red eye dimmed and shrank until it disappeared completely, and no matter how hard he looked for it—no matter what senses he used—he couldn’t find it again.

Interestingly, he noted that while the glowing red eye was very similar to that of the runes he’d discovered on the walls of this place, it had lacked something they’d all had—a putrid, rotten smell.

Curious, he thought, running his fingers on the stone where the eye had been. Very curious.
