As Rex rubbed sleep from his eyes, he spotted Andy out stretching and hopping around to get his circulation going. Dew still clung to everything, and mist was thick in the air.

“Man, you’ve got more energy than I do,” Rex groaned as he got up, hugging a looted fur closely for warmth.

“No time like the present, or at least that’s what my old chief used to say.”

“I guess that means you’ve recovered?”

“Yeah, more or less. The worst wounds are still a little tender to the touch, but look,” Andy said, pulling his pants up. “They’re looking pretty crusty, huh?”

“Man, gnarly. And you’re not in any pain?” Rex said, looking at the scab-covered legs.

“Eh, a bit. But nothing bad enough that it’ll stop me.” Andy proudly thumped his chest.

“Good to hear,” Rex said and turned to look at Ernie.

They had given Ernie most of the furs they found around the gnoll camp, but he was still shivering and looking a little pale. And the two of them looked back at each other with a bit of worry.

“I’m sure he’ll be okay,” Andy said.

Worrying about Ernie slowed the morning more than they would have liked. They ate, and afterward, Ernie got to his feet. He was weak, but not immobile.

“I’m fine, guys. I can move. Stop worrying so much.”

“Well, if he’s good, we should probably make the most of the day,” Andy said, way too spiteful. “There’s no guarantee Credo won’t show up.”

“You’re going to be able to keep up? We can’t exactly leave you here,” Rex said.

“I think I’m–oww”

“Here, grab hold, Ernie,” Andy said and propped him up over his shoulder.

“Ow, ow, ow!”

“Sorry.”

“No, it’s fine.”

“You sure?” Andy asked.

“Maybe? I can bear it. I think.”

With Ernie wincing on every other step as they made their way back toward the farm, they got moving.

Since Rex was the only person with a stealth-related Feat and far better at restraining his aura, it made sense for him to be the only one to go close to the farm. The other two picked a good hiding spot not too far away with a good vantage, and hunkered down.

“Try to keep Slimer from following me if you can. Or at least slow him down.”

“I’ll try,” Andy said.

“Wait, Rex.”

“Yeah, buddy?” Rex turned to Ernie. “What’s up?”

“Take this,” Ernie said, handing out the protection ward.

“Whoa, no way! You can barely defend yourself. At least this might give you some chance.”

“Exactly,” Ernie insisted. “I can’t do anything in this state. What’s saving me from one hit going to help? You take it. You might actually be able to make use of it.”

“You got your jabbing stick. That’s why we gave it to you,” Rex said, pointing at the shoddy gnoll spear.

“Seriously, Rex. Maybe I can defend myself from a blind goblin. But if I get in real trouble, this amulet is not going to help me. But it might help you. And if you die, we’re all screwed.”

“He’s got a point, Rex,” Andy said. “Nobody's got sneaking down like you do.”

“Fine. You better not die on me, though,” Rex said and took the amulet. “Digging a grave sounds like a major pain in the ass.”

And with that, Andy gave him a salute, and he was off.

Rex wasn’t planning on attacking the farm. It was just a scouting mission. Once they had some information, they could come up with some kind of plan.

But when he reached the edge of the treeline, all he spotted was the burned-out remains of the farm. There were no guards anywhere. In fact, there wasn’t a soul in sight.

But he couldn’t be reckless. He took his time scoping the farm out. Vigilantly looking for any signs of life or traps. But there was nothing, and his senses were too keen to be so utterly fooled, or at least he thought they were. If there were people waiting for him, he surely would have found them by now.

I wonder if they even know what happened? If they didn’t see us escape, they might not know what started the fire.

He realized that Jango and all his men had been taken out at once, and then the entire farm had burned to the ground while chaos reigned. Would they even suspect that he was capable of such a feat?

As he considered that, Rex looked over to the farm. The roof had collapsed, and it was in ruins. But if they had reached the cellar and discovered their missing corpses, they would probably suspect their hand in it, one way or another.

But if not?

I need to get closer.

He hesitated for a moment longer before taking the plunge and creeping out into the farm.

There were several things he noticed. Wagons were missing, and most of the ripe or nearly ripe vegetables had been hastily harvested, with quite a bit of wastage.

Don’t mind if I do, Rex thought as he scooped up a few pieces of veg and stuffed them in his pack. He preferred deer meat, but it was probably a good idea to have some fibre in his diet.

It also looked like most of the tools had been taken, except for the heavier and harder-to-carry stuff.

It looks like they left in a hurry.

While he hadn’t expected this, he supposed it made sense. He had killed their boss, alongside most of his closest men, and burned the farm down. Until they knew how it had happened, it was probably a good idea to be cautious.

I doubt they fled through a Gate, though.

It was one thing if Mulvaney and his hunters were scared off by someone killing Jango. But would Credo be? That seemed unlikely. All the information pointed to Credo being some kind of gang boss on Carsus. And Rex highly doubted some gang boss would be able to maintain their reputation if they were frightened off so easily.

Then where? Where did—the mill!

Rex had almost forgotten about the mill. Not only did they already have men out there, but it was a full-blown outpost. He also knew that the caravan guards stayed there between trips, so they obviously had extra accommodation.

That makes a lot of sense. It also means they might not be scared of us, but rather, they might be regrouping.

He knew he needed to go investigate, but since Ernie had already been there, it was best to go and check in with him first and gather whatever information he could.

But first, he had to check the cellar and the farmhouse for any loot that might have survived the fire.

It looked like people had been digging around the ashen remains. But since the roof and second floor had collapsed in, they hadn’t gotten far.

Doesn’t look like they reached the cellar.

If they didn’t even know where Jango was when he died, they might even believe he simply perished in the fire.

This is good news. We might still have the element of surprise. Hopefully, they also think we’re dead.

Unfortunately, the rooms were unrecognizable, and he didn’t spot any artifacts. He really hoped Freyja had escaped with his loot. Losing his first artifacts was too much to bear.

Ulysses’ armory was also completely trashed, partially buried, and had nothing of value to loot.

However, he did find something interesting. It was a glass jar with a cork lid, but the way it caught light stole his attention.

Mana Catcher [ Rare]

Crafted from bluerock, this jar is both incredibly sturdy and ideal for transporting magical components.

I wonder what they were using this for?

Curious, but unsure if it was useful, Rex pocketed the jar and kept searching. Unfortunately, he didn’t find anything else. If anything of value had survived the fire, it was buried.

Leaving the farm behind, and pocketing a few more pieces of veg as he left. But before making it all the way back to where the others were hiding, he bumped into Andy, who was desperately trying to stop Slimer, blocking his path and swatting at him with a branch.

“Get back, ya little bastard!”

“Hey Andy,” Rex waved.

“Oh, hey. And you! I said, halt! I mean it, you’d better stop rolling right now!”

Slimer completely ignored Andy, dissolving his branch, and wiggling over to Rex, and then turned to follow as Rex walked.

“Ha… wha—why? Why does he just follow you like that?”

“Slimer? Dunno,” Rex shrugged. “Likes me for some reason?”

Andy stood there, glaring at Slimer.

“Are you coming?”

“...Yeah, right behind you.”

Ernie was very glad to see them when they arrived, waving down at them from several branches up.

“You put Ernie up in a tree?”

“Yeah? I had to stop your little pet, didn’t I?! What else was I supposed to do?”

“Hmph. Fair enough. Anyway,” Rex continued, explaining what he had found after helping Ernie down.

“The farm was empty, and you think they think we’re dead?” Andy asked.

“Maybe? I mean, as far as I can tell, they have no evidence to believe otherwise. Unless someone spotted us escaping. Which is possible, but based on how frantic everything was, it’s also quite possible they didn’t.”

“Hmm, that is advantageous, if you’re right,” Ernie agreed. “But the mill is…”

“The mill is... what?” Rex probed.

“Err, it’s odd. They watched us the entire time we were there, and we were strictly forbidden from leaving the main building where we stayed, except for when we went on guard patrols. And the farm—or should I say, mill hands? Those guys, they didn't speak at all. They looked even more miserable than the farmers.”

“Why didn’t you mention any of this earlier?”

“Oh, I dunno,” Ernie threw up his hands. “It kind of slipped my mind with the whole getting captured, escaping a burning building, and almost dying. But if not for that.”

“Okay, okay. Fair enough. Do you have any idea why they were so particular?”

“Nope. None. Like I said, I didn’t see anything.”

“Okay. What about the surroundings? You said you went on patrols? Do you know the area?”

Ernie nodded. “Yeah. Kinda. They didn’t want us nosing about in the buildings, but they were happy to use us to patrol the surrounding forest. I mean, it was only a few days. But remember some of it.”

“Better than nothing.”

“What are you thinking, Rex?” Andy asked.

“I’m thinking we scope the place out. But first, I have something that’s been bugging me that I want to try.”

Rex pulled out the jar and looked over to Slimer.

“Hmm, how’s this going to work?”

It was a bit of a stretch, but Slimer was a mana slime, and his jar was a Mana Catcher; it seemed only logical to test them together.

“I wonder if I do this…”

Rex took a piece of jerky, dropped it in the jar, and placed it on the ground.

Slimer wiggled, and a slimy edge poked out from its round form, as if producing a little head. Then, it rolled over to the jar and curiously poked around at it.

“Come on, there you go. You know you wanna get inside.”

Rex almost gasped as the slime stuck a bit of itself into the jar and was sucked up into it in a near-instant.

“Whoa! Did you see that?”

“What’d you do to the little guy? I thought he was your friend?” Andy said.

“I dunno,” Rex shrugged, picking the jar up. “Are you okay? Slimer?”

He tipped out upside down, and shook, and a second later, Slimer plopped out.

“Huh.”

Even more curiously, when he eyed the jar, there was no sign of Slimer’s signature damage.

“How did it even fit inside that jar?” Ernie said. “There’s no way that jar holds enough volume for that slime.”

But even more interestingly than any of that, Slimer didn’t seem bothered by his jar-prison, and when Rex placed it down again, Slimer wiggled on in with a little coaching.

“Oookay, that’s strange,” Rex said. “But it is also super helpful.”

He placed the cork on and looked inside, but it just looked like it was filled with a blue liquid.

“Um, Ernie?”

“Yes, Rex?”

“So, what were you saying about patrols?”

Rex’s thoughts had drifted. As important as foiling their little slaving operation was, he was very curious to find out why they had the strange jar in their possession.

“I see. Interesting. Thanks, Ernie. That gives me a lot to work with. Time to go investigate the mill.”

“But, but—what about the—”

“Don’t worry about me. They won’t even see me coming. Although I do desperately need to get some arrows.”

“J-just be careful, Rex.”

Rex helped Andy move Ernie closer and set up a new camp hidden amongst some thick vegetation, and then he set out.

“Keep your heads low and out of trouble. I’ll be back soon,” Rex said, stashing his new jar filled with blue liquid into his belt and disappearing into the forest.
