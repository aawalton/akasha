Before he could execute his plan, Arthur first had to prepare. The mask experiments had left his mana dangerously low. Inside the hollow of the great tree, he reached into his ring and pulled out a handful of monster cores. He swallowed them one by one, feeling the familiar warmth spread through him as he guided the raw mana into his depleted spark. When his pool was full again, he focused his mind, picturing the face of the man he had chosen as his key to Copperpine. The invisible mask on his face tingled as it molded his features into a perfect copy. The transformation was complete. He then pulled out a set of the man's clothes and gear from his storage ring, which he had stolen during his days of observation, and quickly donned it, completing the illusion. Now, he just needed to find the original.

He moved through the undergrowth like a whisper, a shadow detaching itself from other shadows. Finding the adventurers wasn't difficult. Even a competent crew left a trail: broken branches, scuffed earth, the lingering scent of a campfire. He was no longer just a participant in this world. He was a predator and the forest was his domain.

His search ended at dawn when he found the party resting in a sun-dappled clearing. Arthur settled into a thicket of ferns a hundred yards away, his breathing slow and even, his presence fading into the background noise of the forest.

The leader, a formidable man named Gunnar, was sharpening a heavy battle-axe, his movements filled with aggressive confidence. The tank, Torvin, sat impassively, his massive tower shield resting beside him like a tombstone. In the branches of a nearby tree, the ranger, Elara, kept a silent watch, her longbow resting across her knees. Near the center of the camp, the mage, Fen, nervously cradled his simple wooden staff, his posture radiating a nervous energy that marked him as a non-combatant.

Then there was his target, the man whose face Arthur now wore. He sat slightly apart from the others, cleaning a short sword with a practiced, methodical rhythm. He was the picture of professional detachment. Medium height, unremarkable features, and an aura of quiet competence. He was a flanker, a role that required him to operate on the edges of a fight, exploiting openings rather than creating them. He was perfect.

Throughout the day, Arthur shadowed them, a ghost at the edge of their vision. He studied their dynamics with an intensity that bordered on obsession. Gunnar was the heart of the group, his roars and commands dictating the flow of every battle. Torvin was the anchor, a living wall that absorbed punishment without complaint. Elara was their eyes, her arrows striking with deadly precision from afar. Fen was the protected asset, always shielded by the others.

And the flanker, Zane, was the scalpel. He moved with a fluid grace, his short sword and dagger a blur of motion as he bled their enemies dry. He was skilled, but his style was standard, efficient, and devoid of personal flair. He rarely spoke, offering only brief, professional acknowledgments to Gunnar’s orders. He was a tool, a highly effective one, but a tool nonetheless. This detachment was the weakness Arthur could exploit.

The plan formed, elegant in its simplicity. He wouldn't just create chaos; he would create a problem that would force the team into their pre-defined roles, naturally isolating his target.

Later that day, their hunt successful, the party began the trek back toward the city. Arthur went to work. He found what he was looking for a mile away: a nest of Scrabbler Fiends. The fast, insectoid creatures were fiercely territorial and hunted in swarms. He didn't need to fight them. He just needed to annoy them. Using a handful of pebbles, he began to pepper the entrance to their nest. It took less than a minute before a chittering shriek echoed from within, and a tide of clicking, clawed bodies poured out into the night.

Arthur was already gone, sprinting back towards the adventurers' camp, leaving a clear trail for the enraged swarm to follow.

He circled around and took up his position as the first sounds of the rampage reached the camp. The adventurers reacted instantly.

"Incoming! Swarm!" Gunnar bellowed, his axe already in hand. "Torvin, shield wall! Protect Fen! Elara, find a perch! Zane, with me! We flank and bleed them!"

It was exactly the kind of chaotic brawl Arthur had anticipated. Torvin slammed his shield into the ground, creating an immovable barrier in front of the terrified mage. Gunnar charged headfirst into the swarm, his axe a whirlwind of destruction. From the trees, Elara’s arrows began to rain down, each one finding an eye or a joint with pinpoint accuracy.

This left the flanker, Zane, to do his job. He drew his short sword and dagger and moved to the left flank, his movements quick and precise as he began to cut down the fiends that tried to get around Torvin’s shield. He was separated, focused, and playing his part.

The battle was winding down. Most of the Scrabblers lay dead or dying, their chittering fading into pained clicks. Gunnar was about to call for the cleanup.

This was Arthur’s moment. From the shadows, he released a single, large Grubtusk Devourer.

A heavy, crashing noise cut through the clearing from the far side. The Grubtusk, big, angry, and unexpected, burst from the trees, trampling the remaining Scrabblers underfoot with its tusks lowered in a blind charge.

Gunnar swore, spinning to face the new problem. "Where did that come from?! Torvin, intercept! Elara, its eyes!"

The sudden appearance of the new monster, just as they thought the fight was over, threw the party's focus. Torvin moved to block the charge, and Elara shifted her aim, her attention now split.

With everyone distracted, Arthur circled wide around the chaos, using the trees as cover to approach Zane from his blind side. The maneuver brought him to the edge of the clearing, right behind his target. The flanker, professional to the last, sensed the new presence instantly. He spun, his blades coming up in a defensive posture.

There was no time for words. Arthur, his own body enhanced by [Mana Body], closed the distance in a blur. Zane’s blade met Arthur’s looted short sword in a sharp parry, but the flanker had committed to blocking the main attack and was completely unprepared for the follow-through. Using the opening, Arthur’s dagger, held in a reverse grip, slammed into the side of Zane’s head with the force of a hammer. The flanker’s eyes went wide with shock, then rolled back as he crumpled to the ground, unconscious.

Arthur didn’t hesitate. He touched the unconscious man’s shoulder, and with a silent command, Zane vanished into the stasis of the storage ring.

He jogged back toward the battle, arriving just as the last of the Scrabbler Fiends fell and the Grubtusk, riddled with arrows and bleeding from a dozen cuts, finally collapsed.

Gunnar, breathing heavily, turned and clapped him on the shoulder. "Good work, Zane. That was a messy one. Thought that Grubtusk came out of nowhere."

Arthur gave a noncommittal grunt, a perfect imitation of the man now sleeping in his ring. He looked at the carnage, his face a mask of weary professionalism.

The hunter was gone.

Only the flanker remained.
