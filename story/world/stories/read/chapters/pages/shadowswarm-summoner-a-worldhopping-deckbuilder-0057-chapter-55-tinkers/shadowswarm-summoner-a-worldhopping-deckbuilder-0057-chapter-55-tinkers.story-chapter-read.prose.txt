Chapter 55: Tinkers

Jessica didn’t know how things were going for the others, but it was probably boring in comparison. Dexi’Lynn proved herself to be a capable guide, but also incredibly chatty and colorful about it.

“Over there’s Ritchie’s Diner, which is fine if you don’t mind shitting yourself silly in just a couple hours. Good fried food. But if we’re gonna be sucking down greasy grubs, there’s a place two more levels down that’s even better. But that’s a bit out of our reach for the day, I guess.”

The kobold spoke a mile a minute and walked about as fast. Jessica and Raeleq had no issues keeping up with the speedy little thing, but it was getting a bit hard to keep track of their surroundings and listen to their guide at the same time.

“That’s all great, Dexi,” Jessica said breathlessly, “but what about where we’re trying to go? We’re not here to have fun. We’re here to get a Levi-Box.”

Dexi’Lynn whirled around, nearly causing Jessica to plow into her. “Oh, yeah. Right. Well, what about afterwards? It’s not like it’s going to take us six fuckin’ hours to get your box and then meet up with the others. We can have a little fun too, right?”

Jessica frowned. They’d talked about it the day before, how they were going to go and get it done, and then maybe they’d be able to talk about it. “After we have the box. We have to get this done. You’re not going to flake on us, right?”

“What? No, no, of course now. Well, no, but.”

Jessica groaned and looked at Raeleq, who shook his head with a soft smile. “We have to stick to the plan.”

“I get that, but consider this: it’s gonna be a complete pain in the tail to haul the box around for hours on end, right? Why not get a drink first, maybe some food and a smoke before we do anything serious? We got time. We got so much time.” She grinned, showing pointy teeth.

“I don’t think it’s a good idea,” said Jessica, biting her lip nervously. “We have to find one first, and then make sure we can actually afford to get it. So no, Dexi. Guide us to a Levi-Box like you promised.”

For a second, she wasn’t sure putting her food down was a good idea. They were at the kobold’s mercy now, and although she’d been nothing but friendly up until this point, there was always the chance that she could turn on them. A chill went down Jessica’s spine as she considered the possibility.

Dexi’Lynn took a long, deep breath and let it out. “Yeah, I guess you’re right. But I swear to whatever gods are out there, you’re buying me a smoke afterwards.”

“That seems reasonable,” said Raeleq. “After all, you’ll deserve a reward for your hard work.”

“See? The cute one gets me.” She winked at a clueless Raeleq, who couldn’t stop looking around.

“Are you doing okay?” Jessica asked.

Raeleq nodded after a second’s hesitation. “There’s a lot of people here. It’s loud, crowded, and I don’t like it. I’ll be okay, I just want to get it over with.”

It was true. Even with them rushing around, there was never a point where they had peace or real privacy. Molekin and kobolds darted around everywhere, pushing past them when needed. Even now, stopped, people flowed around them, some shooting dirty looks at the obstruction in the road.

“Okay. Then it’s just down one more level, okay?” Dexi got moving again, a little more slowly this time, deliberate.

The engineering block wasn’t what Jessica expected. Half the level was basically a big trash heap. Scrap metal as far as the eye could see, broken machines, and furnaces and fires everywhere. Between the magma below and the fires all around, it became a bit hard to breathe.

“This is where a bunch of people do the building and selling of parts needed to operate here in Stonskarr,” Dexi explained. “It ain’t glamorous, but it’s got a lot of good shit if you know where to look.”

The buildings here resembled a bunch of shacks piled on top of one another. Some were squat, ugly boxes, while others were tall, three or four stories, but narrow. They wound around a man hammering a sheet of metal and into what looked like a ship that had crashed and been torn open. A counter of sorts separated the frizzy-haired woman working on something she didn’t recognize.

“Hey Gemma, how’s the hammer hanging?” Dexi called cheerfully.

“Low and heavy,” Gemma replied, looking up with a small smile. “What do you want? I’m not sharing my stash this time. Not after last time.”

“Relaaaax,” Dexi crooned. “We’re just here for a Levi-Box, if you got one. Wait, was there anything else?”

Jessica shook her head. SNAC had assured him that he could handle a lot of the repairs with supplies they already had on the ship, they only needed something to help keep the Wanderer airborne long enough to take off and leave. Memories of the way the ship had fought her and sunk haunted her a little. Handling the ship at home or in the Aetheric Expanse had felt as smooth as a dream, but the sense of heaviness and the inevitability of their crash…

“Just a Levi-Box, please,” she said, repressing a shudder. “We’ve got cards and can pay whatever price is needed.”

Gemma’s eyes lit up, even as Dexi groaned. “Don’t tell her that shit! We want a good price, and now she’s going to gouge us. Great job, Jessie.”

Again, she had to remind herself that she wasn’t at home, where people would bend over backwards to give her what she wanted in exchange for a kind word or a favor down the line. She winced, but Raeleq’s comforting hand on her shoulder kept her from showing any more weakness.

“Don’t worry, little ones,” said Gemma. “I’ve got you. We happen to have one Levi-Box ready, and another two in production. It all depends on the cards we get in, of course. You a crafter, by any chance?”

“M-me?” Jessica asked. “Uh, no. But it runs in my family.”

Her grandfather was as famous for his crafting cards as he was for being powerful. The Wanderer was a testament to his skill and expertise. And her own mother had continued on that legacy, focusing on making small, lightweight weapons. There had been several times in her childhood when they’d tried to push Jessica into doing the same.

She’d never wanted it, focusing instead on pushing her body and cultivating discipline and control. A sharp mind and a sturdy body appealed more, though Jessica couldn’t deny curiosity and a love of learning. Tinkers tended to need to have an expanded knowledge of many different kinds of cards and how they’d interact when consumed to create a magical artifact.

The idea of consuming cards to create something new scared her, in some ways. Her family would never run out of cards to use, but what if she got it wrong? What if she was wasteful, and still weak, even in that area? Jessica was already a disappointment magically, but failing in her famous grandfather’s field would be impossible to live down.

“Well,” said Gemma, shaking her from her thoughts, “Levi-Boxes are necessary here, and we get plenty of people who approach the world wrong and end up a smear. Did you get towed here by another ship, or…?”

Raeleq laughed, sudden and booming. “We nearly ended up a smear!”

Gemma nodded, chuckling as well. “I knew it. Does that mean you’ll need other supplies as well? How do you plan on paying?”

Dexi motioned for Jessica to get out her purse. She pulled out a deckbox of cards, about half of what they collectively had, filled with the ones they thought they could spare. It included some of the cards they’d stolen from Vanderborn’s office, like the graduation cards.

Gemma whistled at seeing some of them. She quickly pointed out three in particular.

[Stolen Effort: Rare. Let your opponent do all the hard work for you.

Spell Effect: Counter an enemy’s spell, and cast a spell from your hand with the same cost or less for free. 5 Mana]

[Rend Metal: Uncommon. Through sheer elemental might, bend metal to your will and break it.

Spell Effect: Destroy weapon, armor, or artifact, or metal object weighing no more than a hundred pounds. 4 mana]

And

[Detect Metal: Uncommon. Reveal all sources of one of three metals within a thirty yard radius for ten minutes. Choose from Iron, Gold, Copper, or Silver. 5 charges remain. Resets weekly.]

Her choices puzzled Jessica, and that curiosity must’ve shown on her face.

“Why these? Simple. Synergy. What do you know about crafting?” Gemma asked her.

Jessica shrugged, all the information flashing in her mind. “Crafting cards are a form of ritual that consumes other cards in exchange for creating either a new object, or imbuing an existing artifact with new magical qualities,” she recited from memory. “And it can take anywhere from three to ten cards for a single craft, with power and complexity determined by…Well, a lot of things.”

Gemma laughed, nodding as she went. “Very true, and very complex. What do you think you would get if you combined the last two together? What do you think that would make?”

Jessica put the other cards back in the box, and took a look at the two metal-focused ones in front of her. It seemed fairly obvious to her, which must’ve meant there was a trick to it.

“Would you be able to maybe choose a metal within a certain range and break it all?” she ventured.

Raeleq made a supportive noise, like it made sense to him, and Gemma smiled. “That’s a possibility. The thing with crafting cards, is you get a certain sense of what will work and what won’t. Combine those two with another Primal card that affects the earth or magnetism, and you might be able to attract all the metal of that kind in an area. Or with a Wild affinity and you could convert wood to iron, or even gold. Think of how valuable that’d be.”

The crafter took the three cards laid out and slipped them into her pocket. “Levi-Boxes aren’t especially cheap, but that combo you were sitting on, it’s potentially more valuable. I’d call this a fair trade, wouldn’t you?”

They walked out of there, Levi-Box carried in Raeleq’s broad arms. It was about one foot tall and three feet wide, forty pounds, and came with instructions written on the side. That was handy. And more than that, they got their task done in one try, and now had hours and hours to kill. Which may have been a problem.

“Alright, we got your Levi-Box, and without costing much,” Dexi’Lynn said almost as soon as they’d left the shack. “I’m begging you, I need a smoke or it’s gonna send me over the edge.”

Jessica looked around the rest of the engineering block. It was an ugly, inelegant place where people worked wonders. And now, for the first time in years, she found herself curious about crafting and what it might do for her. Part of her wanted to poke around, see if there were any basic crafting cards she could cut her teeth on.

Even if that would’ve meant her grandfather and mother had been right about it being a good idea. They were just far enough away now that Jessica thought she might be able to live with it.

“Pleeeaaase?” Dexi’Lynn moaned dramatically, attracting some attention. “I’ll be your best friend.”

They had time before they returned to Crag. And so far, they were basically invisible in the city. No one cared who they were, everyone was too busy. She turned to a frowning Raeleq. “What do you think?”

“I think it’s a bad idea,” he answered honestly. “But it’s either that or be bored. And I have a feeling a bored kobold is a bad thing.”

She grinned fiercely. “You’d be right. C’mon, let’s get some good leaf, maybe a couple of bottles of something strong for the road, and we’ll meet up with the others in like an hour or two. What’s the worst that could happen?”

Famous last words, but Jessica couldn’t fault her. If Dexi was going to be one of them, they couldn’t treat her like a servant or ignore her needs.

“Just for a little bit,” she said, regretting it already.
