It is often hard to realize that even seemingly eternal existences such as Realms themselves have an end. That they are finite. For, in our cosmos, nothing can truly escape an end.

But one thing must be considered at all times before the finality occurs.

Who stands to benefit from the end?

Preface

Catalogue of Ends

Vikion V.

Ryland couldn’t find it in himself to be concerned about everything going on in Rhel. There was a great deal occurring at the moment after the Demiurge’s determination to free everyone from the grip of his own Soul Glyph. There was so much chaos going on everywhere, it was a wonder the whole Realm of Rhel wasn’t on fire.

People were leaving in droves. A generational divide had split up Rhel after the incident they were calling the Serration.

The vast majority of the youth, who hadn’t been subsumed by the Rhellian way of life for more than a decade or two, failed to see their future on Rhel. They refused to entertain their full lives on a world that was open about controlling their very souls. Ryland couldn’t blame them at all for their perspective, no matter what excuses the Demiurge made.

But the older folks weren’t so up in arms about it. They understood that it was a precaution their ancestors had taken upon themselves. It was something passed down through the generations, a way of life in truth.

To them, the whole act wasn’t as tyrannical as others might think at a glance.

Nevertheless, the youth were winning. Combined with the outrage from the other Realms—just barely mollified now thanks to the Demiurge’s decision to remove the Soul Glyph from others—and the fact that most parents were loath to part with their children, the pendulum was swinging the other way. Rhel was, slowly but surely, being abandoned.

Of course, this didn’t mean everything was smooth sailing. There were little conflicts springing up at a lot of places for a whole host of consequential reasons.

In some cases, people were debating just what they were willing to leave behind. Many were looking to sell off their properties and valuables, though with everyone seemingly departing Rhel, there wasn’t exactly an active marketplace for abandoned wealth.

To somewhat pacify the supreme despondence of those who believed their entire lifetime of work was being invalidated, the Rhellian government—both local ones and the overarching administration headed by the Demiurge—had created a relief fund for the departees. It was certainly better than getting absolutely nothing.

On top of that, this fund was in hard gold and silver. Several Realms had started dealing with banknotes, and even Rhel had such a system prevalent on their world, but with Rhel itself collapsing, their currency no longer held any worth on any Realm.

As such, the Rhellians were emptying their coffers and carrying away bags of precious metal and gemstones wherever they could.

Those would never lose their real value, after all.

Other conflicts were even messier. A not insignificant number of people were choosing to remain on Rhel despite the revelation of the Demiurge’s powers. Somehow, they still believed in him and all that Rhel had stood for this entire time. Their faith was almost magical, even in the absence of magic.

This led to others trying to force them away. Sons and daughters arguing with dead-set parents, siblings fighting amongst each other, marriages tearing apart as spouses considered their young children’s needs over their lovers, and so on.

Yet again, the Rhellian government had to step in. They had instantly banned any forceful actions enacted by any member of their society when it came to leaving Rhel.

Naturally, this had not gone down well.

“I was hoping you would enact this sort of change when I invited you here, Ryland,” Yulkaviel said. “Although, I had never imagined it would grow so dire.”

Ryland considered things for a moment. They were in a sea of deceptive calm inside the whirl of danger and devastation that Rhel threatened to erupt into any moment. The collapse of an entire civilization was rarely a smooth transition.

Even now, on this little balcony in the institute, they could spot at least three columns of fire in the distance.

Multiple someones were not happy.

“I certainly didn’t choose to be the vector of such chaotic times,” Ryland said. “But admittedly, I did predict something of the sort would occur centuries ago.”

Yulkaviel nodded knowingly, almost as if he had been present then. “When you were last here.”

“Indeed.” The mannerisms of Ryland’s conversation partner finally brought to fore the thought he had been contemplating. “Tell me, Yulkaviel. Were you always aware of Mavar’s Soul Glyph branded onto your manasoul?”

“Awareness is a difficult thing to consider,” Yulkaviel said after a moment. “Sometimes, things felt like a dream. At other times, things felt—no, were—undeniably right. Yet even that might have been a trick. I can’t say for certain, I’m afraid. Yet, the reason why there hasn’t been backlash is because, in some integral way, we all know it to be true. Knew it for a long time.”

Ryland nodded. He had suspected as much. There just hadn’t been a significant amount of evidence before for those suspicious enough to overcome accusations of paranoia. And for the rest…

Well, they were still fine with it, as evidenced by their reactions.

“Now that you’ve succeeded,” Ryland said. “Where will you go?”

Androvia had opened its borders to accept Rhellian refugees, as had Jure, and several other Realms were both cognizant of the situation and also welcoming all affected by it. Yulkaviel had options.

“I’m not certain,” the old elvhenan said. “Somewhere far away. I’ll probably gather up Solverfel and get away to a place where we won’t be bothered.”

Ryland nodded again. He hadn’t heard about the Mage Knight who had challenged him at the academy, but it seemed the younger elvhenan was more than just a government-mandated guard to Yulkaviel. Ryland also noted that the former Second High Adjutant hadn’t specifically answered him.

Which was fine by him. Yulkaviel more or less wanted to retire. No one could blame him for such a decision.

Ryland got to his feet. “In fear that we may not meet each other again, I’ll take my leave for one last time, Yulkaviel.”

The old elvhenan got to his feet too. “Thank you for everything, Marionys Ryland. I fear you haven’t heard those words often enough.”

Ryland smiled. “I don’t mind. Keeps me from getting a big head. [Translocate].”

He went to check up on the Arcoryx contingent for now. Having received official commands to return, they had all but taken the final step into the still-functioning Teleportation Circles.

The only reason they hadn’t yet was because not everyone was accounted for.

There were a couple running late. Sint was finishing up research. She was terribly mad at everything at the moment and it was hard to tell her to hurry, especially since the whole thing was not at all her fault. Her loss in the tournament had certainly not improved her mood, and no one could stand against her intention to come away with something worthwhile from Rhel.

She had Tyra’s backing too. The High Academ was assisting her research, mostly by shifting tack so that they could end the rest of it on Androvia. Something the others greatly appreciated.

But Sint wasn’t the only one missing at that moment.

“Did you know it’s conducting Living Mana, sir?” Viren said. “The Crypts we went to. It’s not just storing manasouls. Or maybe it is. I don’t really understand it. But we went there again, and Acrypha found it was actually storing and channelling Living Mana too.”

There were a lot of implications from that Ryland could pick out. He initially went with the one Viren had mentioned last. “Do you understand the impossibility of that from a historical standpoint, Viren? Rhel has famously more or less eradicated all Living Mana on the Realm.”

“I’m not lying, sir! She’s still there, still investigating it.”

“I never said you were lying or that you were deceived. We have ample proof that things on Rhel are… not quite what they might seem at first. So it’s eminently possible that certain facts that have been accepted as universal truths are not what we initially assumed. Now, what is this about Acrypha investigating things over there?”

While Ryland had asked the question, he was already formulating his own answers. Of course Acrypha of all people would be highly intrigued by Living Mana.

The Pure Manifestations of her own parents were constructed from Living Mana, after all.

“…and the priestess said she sensed the Living Mana inside her,” Viren said. “From that incident, I think. So, she said she’d answer Acrypha’s questions if Acrypha went with her.”

That was concerning, especially since Acrypha was yet to return. It was now long enough that the Arcoryx contingent had sent in a few people to the Crypts to look for them.

“Linsby and Academ Jules have gone ahead to retrieve Acrypha,” High Academ Tyra said. She had been listening in on Viren’s account patiently, even though as the leader of the contingent, she had to be tremendously busy with departure preparations. “But they haven’t returned yet…”

Another point of concern.

“Fear not, High Academ,” Ryland said. “I will be heading there myself, shortly.”

Tyra’s carefully controlled mask broke just a little then. Just enough for it to be visible only to Ryland. “Androvia gave us a specific time by which to return, Ryland. I would dearly like to hold myself to that deadline.”

Ryland nodded. “I will return with them all in no time at all. Please be patient till then.”

Viren stepped up as Ryland’s manasoul started spiking with magic. “Sir, please take me along with you. It’s all my fault. It was my idea to go to the Crypts. If I hadn’t pushed it, this would never have happened.”

“Yes,” Tyra said a little harshly. “I’m glad you recognize your fault in the matter.”

“High Academ Tyra,” Ryland said. “Since Viren intends to make up for his gaffe, allow him to accompany me as punishment for his mistaken decision.”

The High Academ took a deep breath, then sighed it out. “I’m not so sure that accompanying you of all people is a punishment really, but I’ll leave you to handle it. He’s not a bad lad, all things told.”

“I’m right here, High Academ,” Viren grumbled.

Ryland nodded. “Thank you, High Academ. We will return in no time at all.”

With that done, Ryland decided to layer some extra protection on Viren just in case things spiralled out in ways he couldn’t predict. He wasn’t certain anything untoward would occur, but it was best to remain cautious.

A simple [Translocate] took them to the Crypts in no time.

“Yes…” Viren was looking around at the dank, gloomy tunnels and distant, lightless rooms. “It felt just as abandoned the last time we were here, sir.”

Ryland could sense an air of neglect. There should have been more priests and priestesses in the passageways and rooms. Beyond that, there should have been those manasoul fragments which had tried possessing the Rhellian students when they had conducted their ill-fated field trip here.

Now, it all felt empty. Devoid of the magic that had flowed through the conduits in their walls.

Except for one small thing.

“You’re right,” Ryland said. “There is Living Mana here.”

He had missed it the last time he was at the Crypts. There had been far too many distractions. The possessions, the discovery of mana being conducted through the walls of the Crypts, the piece of him as well as the presence of a Flourinus Order mage.

It almost felt like the distractions had been placed on purpose. Like Ryland had been led away from discovering the existence of Living Mana existing in a suppressed state.

Why?

He couldn’t quite fathom the reason. Certain puzzle pieces fell into place, but that still didn’t reveal the full picture.

The fragments of manasouls were born from the Living Mana. Having already received proof in how Living Mana was willing to possess anything resembling people, including people themselves, the earlier possessions of the Rhellian students made a lot of sense. Especially when their manasouls were hollowed out by the Demiurge’s actions.

It also made sense that the Rhellians weren’t eliminating the Living Mana on their world so much as sweeping it under a rug called the Crypts. Much easier to hide something when it couldn’t be destroyed easily.

Yet, Ryland couldn’t shake off the feeling that there was something deeper going on. Something that he was missing.

“Do you know the priestess, Viren?” Ryland asked. He hadn’t brought Viren along just as some sort of punishment, after all. The boy would be useful. “As in, you should be able to recognize her, yes?”

“Yes, sir.” Viren couldn’t stop looking around dubiously, like he was seriously considering whether the Crypts were actually haunted or not. “But I don’t know where we might find her.”

“It won’t be difficult for me. But before we go after her, I would like to investigate the Crypts itself. That it possesses Living Mana to this degree suggests there is more to this place than meets the eye.”

“Do you think they were keeping the dead here to… facilitate the Living Mana somehow?”

That was an insightful point Ryland hadn’t fully realized just yet. “Yes, well done, Viren. There is certainly a puzzle here that we can’t quite make out.” He paused for a moment. “But I think I shall revise my intentions. First, let’s find Acrypha.”

Viren nodded. “Yes, let’s go, sir.”

Ryland closed his eyes for a moment. When he opened them again, they were covered by [Omniscience].
