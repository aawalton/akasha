Borevin Shirazi had been deep in prayer when she began sensing faint vibrations within the earth. Her forehead pressed against the prayer rug—transmuted snow, actually—and her knees pressed together as she whispered the evening recitation.

"Guide me on this winding path, this path you have chosen specifically for me..."

The vibrations grew stronger, and Borevin frowned in annoyance. Even in this cursed realm, far from the dunes of Calharlcan—her home clan—she would not allow anything to interrupt her communion with Tihamun.

The desert god demanded discipline above all else, and she had not survived fifteen years as a priestess by letting worldly distractions pull her from worship.

Some fool was probably using earth manipulation to tunnel deep within the ground—another example of the reckless abandon that had overtaken her fellow captives in this frozen wasteland. This was what happened without faith in Tihamun. People inevitably lost their way.

Shifting a bronze strand of hair from her face, Borevin gritted her teeth and continued her prayers with stubborn determination. "Hold my heart, lord—" The vibration spiked beneath her, making the prayer rug shudder. She forced the words out through clenched teeth. "Strengthen it that I may prevail in this trial you have brought my way. Let me not—"

The presence rose through the earth itself, moving with unnatural speed toward the surface. Her eyes snapped open, and in that moment, she realized that this was no earth manipulation but a form of electrical essence moving through stone as if it were water.

It pulsed with a consciousness. With intent. And it was heading straight for her.

She jumped to her feet immediately, hands moving instinctually to her prayer beads—not to seek comfort, but in battle preparedness. Three beads snapped free from the strand, and the bronze sand within them poured out, liquefying mid-air before solidifying into bronze javelins that hovered above her, poised to strike down whatever blasphemous force was surging beneath her.

And then the earth erupted—not with violence or destruction—but with the shocking sight of a humanoid form materializing from the ground—stark naked like a newborn goat.

He couldn't have been more than the age of seventeen, more than a decade younger than her. Had she been less guarded in her youth before becoming Tihamun's priestess, she could have birthed him!

Scowling in judgmental fury, Borevin's gaze strayed downward before she could stop herself, and heat flooded her cheeks in mortification. Fifteen years of disciplined service, and this dust-licker had made her falter in mere seconds.

"Pervert!" she shrieked in scandalized fury, spinning away and shielding her eyes with trembling hands. Her fingers brushed against her prayer beads, seeking the familiar comfort. "Shield your nakedness, you imbecile, before I do something horrible to you. I was Tihamun's priestess before all this madness!"

The man's eyes widened in shock, and a cloak of storm instantly appeared around him.

"I'm very sorry," he spoke, raising his hands to placate her. "I didn't mean… I didn't realize…"

"You vile tempter!" Borevin spat, keeping her gaze averted. "Rising from the earth to corrupt me with your indecency. It will not work!"

"That's not—"

"Do you know how many have tried to break my faith?" She cut him off. "I have always prevailed. Always. You will not succeed where others have failed."

"I have no intent of seducing you!" the man shouted, his voice like the boom of thunder as he glared at her. "This was a mistake. I apologized already. I am sorry."

Borevin scowled, but before she could speak, a chorus of shouts echoed behind the man.

"Aodhán!" "Aodhán!"

The man's comrades burst through the tree line behind him, weapons drawn, eyes scanning for threats. Borevin glanced past the man, and her eyes widened as her gaze landed on a creature she hadn't expected to see in a realm like this. A creature formed from storm and chaos.

A familiar.

Borevin stumbled backward in instinctive panic. Familiars were the pets of mythics and, sometimes, the allies of powerful champions. What could such a creature be doing here?

Who could—

The familiar roared as it flew toward the man before her, growling softly in what was obviously a show of concern and affection.

"I'm fine, Varéc." The man whispered as he caressed the dragon's neck. He turned his gaze to the group of people Borevin assumed were his comrades and smiled apologetically.

"I apologize for worrying you guys. I just got a little carried away."

A man with a cosmic bloodline manifestation glared at him. "You have some explaining to do. But for now—" his gaze shifted to Borevin. "Who are you?"

"I am Borevin Shirazi!" Borevin announced proudly. "Junior priestess in the priesthood of Tihamun. Who are you?"

Magda bowed low. "Esteemed priestess Shirazi, I am Magdalene Al-Rasheed. Please forgive my comrades. "They do not know the value of the position you hold."

"I can see that." Borevin sniffed derisively as she glared at the man and his comrades. "But that still doesn't dismiss their behavior. However. I will not hold it against them." Her voice softened, and she let out a small smile. "Raise your head, child; it is not you I am mad at."

Borevin's gaze strayed back to Aodhán. "It is this man… this dust-licker, that had the gall to cut my prayer short, rising from the pit of hell like a demon."

"I am not a demon!" Aodhán sighed, tired of apologizing at this point. "Like I said earlier, it was an honest mistake. I am very sorry."

"He is very sorry," Magda repeated with a small smile. "By Tihamun, please find it in your heart to forgive him, priestess."

Borevin snorted. "Only if he tells me what he was doing so deep within the earth. I thought he was going to possess me."

"I was conducting research underground," Aodhán gritted out in frustration. "I was searching for the dragon's core, and I found it."

"What dragon?" Borevin interrupted, confusion momentarily overriding her indignation.

The others, however, did not share her confusion. They all gasped, suddenly tense.

"You… you found it?" Brennon stuttered, fear obvious in his tone. "Where did you find it?"

Aodhán smiled at the illusion awakened. "We are standing right above it."

Everyone jumped back immediately, eyes wide and shuddering as if the very thought of standing over the dragon made them sick. Even Baxter, who was usually so composed, jumped, his eyes wide as he stared at the ground Aodhán was standing on in fear.

At this point, Borevin's confusion had morphed into fear, and with a stutter in her voice, she asked again. "What dragon are you all talking about? Have you all lost your marbles?"

"We should be asking you that question." Baxter glanced at Borevin worriedly. "Have you slept at all since you arrived in this realm?"

Borevin shook her head, weariness obvious in her features for a moment before she pushed it away with a frown of determination. "Sleep weakens the mind. I don't need it."

Brennon's eyes widened. "You haven't slept? At all? Not even once?"

"I just said—"

"You didn't get the message, then," Baxter interrupted grimly. "About the creatures."

"What creatures?" Borevin demanded, her hand instinctively moving to her prayer beads.

Magda quickly stepped in, arms wide in placation. "Esteemed priestess, please. Allow me to explain."

She darted a warning glance at Brennon before proceeding to explain the whole situation to her—from the dream itself to all they had found out so far.

When Magda finished speaking, the javelins hovering over Borevin had fallen to the snow beside her. Her composure had cracked, and fear was obvious in her expression.

"Tier 75 creatures?" She stuttered, the words seemingly tearing themselves from her throat. "By Tihamun, how are we supposed to survive that?"

"That's what we are trying to figure out," Aodhán explained softly. "People have begun gathering at the twin-peaked mountain. They intend to fight together to improve their chances of survival."

"And what about you people?" Borevin asked hesitantly. "Do you not intend to join them?"

Everyone glanced at Aodhán, and he grimaced. “We do. Eventually.”

“But you have something to do first.” Borevin understood immediately. She glanced at the ground. “Something that has to do with finding one of these creatures.”

It wasn't a question but a statement of fact, one that was confirmed by their expressions.

Borevin took one more look at Varéc, thought deeply, and said. "Then I shall join you. If your plan works, then good for us. But if it doesn't, we can go to the twin-peaked mountain together."

"That would be great," Magda beamed and turned to glance at Aodhán. "Right?"

"I have no problems with it." Aodhán shrugged. The woman was at the 41st tier of advancement, making her the fourth highest awakened he had seen so far. Adding her to the group would only boost their overall power.

Destra, however, wasn't so comfortable with the woman's presence, and she pulled Aodhán to the side. "Can we trust that she isn't an agent or resurrected?”

Aodhán grimaced. "I don't know, to be honest. I know she isn't resurrected because I can't sense any aura of death around her." But I can’t say for sure that she isn’t an agent. She's strong, though, or at least strong enough to make it to the portal on her own."

“Oh,” Destra’s eyes widened at that, and she nodded. “That makes sense, but I still suggest we stay cautious before trusting her completely."

"Naturally," Aodhán agreed, glancing at Borevin from the corner of his eyes. Aside from being powerful enough to survive independently, she seemed too devout to even consider cultist bargains.

Still, Destra was right. They needed to keep an eye on her until they were certain.

Destra sighed, not liking the situation, but she nodded anyway. "So, is Varéc going down immediately?"

"Yes," Aodhán nodded. "There's no need to dally. Hopefully, I haven't ruined our chances by letting the dragon sense me."

Destra's eyes widened as she glanced sharply at him. “How did that even happen?”

“I don’t know,” Aodhán whispered, not wanting the others to hear, because yes, he had been reckless just as they had all feared.

Destra stared at him for a moment and just shook her head. With a sigh, she waved a hand, clearing the snow off a wide area of ground in preparation for Varéc’s descent.

While she did that, Aodhán turned his gaze to Varéc and whispered. "Are you sure you want to do this, buddy?"

Varéc growled, anticipation rushing through their psychic bond.

Aodhán smiled. Varéc was perfectly fine. Excited even, but he couldn't help but worry.

The fact that Varéc couldn't die eliminated the greatest danger of the mission, but that didn't mean the mission still wasn't dangerous. Because, while Varéc couldn't die, he could still be captured.

He was about to meet a real dragon—creatures known for their incredible power and tendency to hoard shiny stuff. And Varéc was very shiny.

Aodhán grimaced, rethinking this whole thing. He wished he could see through Varéc's eyes; that way he could at least get front-row seats to whatever happened and would be able to tell Varéc the exact moment to get out of there.

But he couldn't do that. And so, he would have to rely on Varéc's good sense to get him out of danger.

"Be careful," he whispered quietly. "The dragon is very powerful. If you think you can't convince it to see reason, leave there immediately."

Varéc growled again, the rumble of thunder reassuring.

"We are ready for you, Varéc." Baxter called out from behind them, but Aodhán wasn't ready to release Varéc yet.

"A few minutes, please," Aodhán called out. "I need to go over the plan again with Varéc."

Varéc cocked his head in confusion, but Aodhán ignored him and continued speaking. "The Dragon's chamber is four hundred and fifty meters deep, so just keep going down till you reach it. No need to go sideways."

Varéc nodded in understanding and began moving, but Aodhán held him back. "Wait, I'm not done. At our current location, you'll be entering the chamber directly above the dragon. So, make sure you're careful. More likely than not, the null runes in the chamber will dampen your strength a little, so take that into account too."

Varéc growled again, a hint of impatience entering his voice. Aodhán ignored it.

"There will be spatial tears all over the place, so be careful of that. Also, make sure to watch out for the dragon's spawns. Arkhan didn't mention seeing any, but we should work with the assumption that the dragon has at least a few underlings in its service."

Varéc growled again, his tone exasperated.

Aodhán glared at him. "You better listen now. If anything happens to you down there, I cannot save you. So, listen."

Varéc grumbled but settled in to listen, and Aodhán continued. "On no account should you fight any of its spawns. I don't know dragon culture, but I doubt that'll get you into its good books."

"Aodhán, I think Varéc's got it." Destra whispered from behind him, and Varéc growled in agreement.

Aodhán sighed, still holding tightly to Varéc's neck before reluctantly letting go. "Just be careful. And keep me posted. I want to know every single detail that happens in there, got it?"

Varéc nudged his head with his snout and growled in comfort. Aodhán smiled and then nodded. "Okay, you can go."

Varéc perked up and growled as if to say, "Finally."

He activated {Lightning Body—Silver}, drew a ton of willpower from Aodhán's core, and without hesitation, he dove into the earth, blurring down even faster than Aodhán had been able to.

His lightning tail was the last thing Aodhán saw before Varéc suddenly vanished from his senses, hidden behind several layers of static interference.

Behind him, Borevin recreated her prayer mat and began to pray—this time, uninterrupted, though the threat beneath them made the communion far less peaceful than before.
