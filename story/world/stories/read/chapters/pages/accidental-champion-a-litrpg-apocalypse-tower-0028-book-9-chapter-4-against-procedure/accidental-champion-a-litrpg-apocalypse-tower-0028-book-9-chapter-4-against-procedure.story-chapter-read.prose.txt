Xavier floated through the portal, leaving Alica’s corpse behind to drift in space above the currently deserted planet Batrah.

Using techniques the former Empress Larona had once used on him to cloak his identity, Xavier had performed a Class Graft on himself, having one of his drones do the procedure. Now, whenever anyone scanned him, he would appear to be a Level 485 B Grade Master of Time—or rather, a Mistress of Time.

That was a strange thought.

While Xavier didn’t feel any different, he looked different. In fact, he looked exactly like Alica, the dead mage he was now impersonating. The Class Graft hadn’t been enough for him to imitate her appearance. That had required other spells, ones he’d manifested on the fly in order to get the job done.

His appearance, voice, and outward identity to scanning had been completely disguised. Not only that, when he’d been mind-controlling Alica, he’d managed to store as much of her memories in his own mind in the timeframe that he’d had as he could. In doing so, he was able to layer what was essentially a simulation of her mind over his own, allowing it to lead his speech and actions.

Stealth had never been Xavier’s forte, but when it came to saving his friends, he didn’t want to limit himself. Despite how it might appear, he wasn’t above admitting when he wasn’t ready to face an enemy.

The instant Xavier arrived, he found all eyes on him. He’d been monitoring Alica’s Communication Stones’ channels right up until he’d forced her to withdraw all the stones she possessed from her Storage Rings and hand them over to him, so there had been at least half a second when Alica—or Xavier acting as Alica—hadn’t been able to reply.

Then there’d been the short few moments when he’d undergone the procedure.

The twin Denizens—Dybal and Lydad—were staring at her, frowns marring their faces, but they didn’t say a word.

Another Denizen, a broad-shouldered man wearing a black suit of armour, peered at her with unrestrained contempt. “You’re late, Alica.” He stepped forward as Xavier took in the room, which looked exactly as Alica’s memories had shown.

A large room with thick steel walls and a ramp leading up to where the portal stood. At the other side of the room was a wall about twice his height with a balcony overlooking the portal. It looked like an observation area, with a force field instead of tinted glass shielding it from his view. The layout reminded him of the gate room in Stargate SG-1.

Except for the massive, magic-infused guns—for he didn’t know what else to call them—that were installed all along the walls, and every single one was currently pointed at him.

“Yes,” Xavier said, in Alica’s persona, raising an eyebrow and pointedly glancing at the guns directed toward him. “By about two seconds. Sir.”

The black-armoured man, Striker, took a few more steps toward her, his hands crossed behind his back and his face starting to redden. “Is that anyway to respond to your superior, Agent?” He tilted his chin up. “Do you require another round of re-education? Old habits die hard, I hear, and your habits… well.” He opened his hands and looking around the steel-walled room. “They got you here.”

Xavier straightened. He saluted, though he wanted nothing more than to punch Striker in the face. “No, Sir. Sorry, Sir. I was worried we might have missed something and performed an extra scan of the planet. I didn’t want to leave any stone unturned when it came to a target like Xavier Collins.”

“A target like Xavier Collins? From that backwater sector? He was nothing but an insect with delusions of grandeur,” Dybal quipped.

Striker rotated at the waist and stared heavily at Dybal. He didn’t say a word. His expression was blank. Dybal straightened and diverted his gaze. Striker’s attention returned to Alica. “You did not report your actions; this goes against procedure. You will be penalised and have an extra 100 years of service added to your contract. See Sigmad at the end of the week to have your contract altered. If you don’t show, you’ll earn yourself double. Understood, Agent?”

Xavier did his best impersonation of Alica’s contrite look. He lowered his head in what appeared to be shame and said in a weak voice that shouldn’t have belonged to a peak B Grade, and certainly didn’t belong to Xavier Collins: “Yes, Sir.”

Striker stared at her for a long, searching moment, before he gave a curt nod and addressed the three of them as a whole. “Congratulations, Theta Squad, on a successful mission. Management will be happy with your success. You may avail yourselves of the Social Rooms then return to your quarters at your leisure. Your next mission will be bright and early tomorrow morning.”

He turned, walked to a door beneath the observation balcony, and shut it heavily behind him. A lock clicked.

Dybal let out a long whistle. “Well, that was fun. Seriously, why were you late? You weren’t responding to our communications. That isn’t like you, Alica.”

Lydad raised an eyebrow. “Come on, don’t you start on her now. It was a successful mission. Let’s celebrate.” There was a twinkle in the man’s eye.

“Yes, let’s celebrate our continued servitude to…” Dybal sighed. “Whoever Striker’s bosses are.”

Lydad clapped his twin brother on the back. “Maybe stop talking like that in front of the weapon systems.” He flicked his gaze up to the magical guns on the walls, which now faced Dybal instead of Xavier, as though they had a mind of their own. “Or, you know, stop talking like that completely.”

Dybal sighed. “You always were the cautious one.” He scrunched up his face. “At least, I think you were.” He glared at the observation deck. “I can’t actually remember.”

Xavier looked at the observation deck as he followed the others out into the hall. He knew the layout of the base from Alica’s memories. At least, he knew about as much as she did. What he didn’t know was where they were holding Palini, because Alica hadn’t known they were holding Palini—hell, she didn’t even know who Palini was.

As he walked down the hall, following the twins, and brought more of the information from Alica’s memories to the forefront of his mind, he was beginning to wonder if coming here might have been a mistake. While he wasn’t about to doubt himself or hold onto any regrets for what he’d done to Alica, he was starting to feel sorry for the Theta Squad.

They didn’t even know who they were.

If he could have gotten here a different way, he would have let the woman live. He stared at the back of Dybal and Lydad’s heads and wondered what crimes had gotten them here, or if indeed they’d committed a crime at all.

Impersonating Alica had certainly been a good way to get into this base undetected, but her access to the base was heavily restricted. Xavier activated The All-Seeing Eye, but it was no use. The walls weren’t merely made from steel. From what he ascertained with his spiritual sense, the walls weren’t one massive slab of steel but several layers, or sheets, of metal fused together. Each sheet of metal had a separate set of protective runes etched into them.

Even his spiritual sense couldn’t see all the way through the walls.

The hallway outside of the Mission Room lacked the same magic-infused guns, but it didn’t lack surveillance. Several crystals on the ceiling stared down at him. His every move was being tracked.

Every door he passed was locked, and no matter how hard he pushed his spiritual sense he wasn’t able to see to the other side.

Where the hell are you, Palini?

~

The Social Rooms were several connected bars and lounges within the base’s compound where drinks and food were freely served and the different agents from the different squads were able to mingle. Xavier made small talk with the other members of Theta Squad and members of a few other squads. He tried to probe answers out of those he spoke to, but every time he did, they gave him a questioning look and glanced up at the crystals on the ceiling.

Speaking freely in this place clearly wasn’t the done thing. Neither was asking questions.

Inside, Xavier was gritting his teeth.

He was wasting time. This was all taking too long. When he’d imagined coming here with this identity, he’d expected to have the freedom to search through different rooms inside the base until he found Palini, then simply teleport or portal out.

Things were far more complicated than that.

After only a few minutes in the Social Rooms Xavier wanted nothing more than to blow through the different walls around him, find Palini, and get him the hell out of this place.

That is, if they haven’t already killed him.

He tried to use the Communication Stone linked to Xavier Prime—a Communication Stone that was powerful enough to work across universes—but it was blocked by the protective runes. He could alter those runes, but he wasn’t sure how to do that without being detected…

“You okay?” Lydad slipped onto the stool beside Xavier at the bar, a drink in hand and a frown on his face.

Xavier looked at the man—a man who’d been sent to kill him, a man who thought he was talking to his dead squadmate. “You ever think about—” He glanced at the crystals on the ceiling. Shut his eyes. “Taking a vacation.”

Lydad gave him a steady look. “Every single day. Be careful.” His gaze found his brother. “You’re starting to sound like Dybal. I don’t think Management would like that. You already got another 100 years of service.”

“You don’t really think they’re ever going to let us go, do you?”

Lydad blinked. “No. I guess not.” He wouldn’t meet her eye as he pushed off the bar and walked away.

Xavier drummed his fingers on the bar and sipped the heavily alcoholic drink Alica had favoured.

How damned foolish had he been to come to this place?

As he looked around the room, seeing at least a dozen other squads like Theta Squad, and knowing there were far more currently off on missions, he knew stealth wasn’t going to be how he got out of this mess.

What he’d do to simply blow through the walls and kill anyone who got in his way…

But Striker wasn’t the only A Grade around. His spiritual sense might not have been enough to get through the walls, but careful probing allowed him to ascertain the grades of those in the room without being detected.

Am I strong enough to take on Striker, let alone all of these?

And who else would I have to fight inside this damned base?

Just as he was thinking about how to find Palini—hoping the man was still alive, because what did they need with him now they thought Xavier was dead?—and get the both of them the hell out of there, a man walked into the Social Rooms with two others beside him. A man with a stony expression and a hand-and-a-half sword at his hip.

He was grinding his teeth, glancing about.

There was a lull in the chatter around the room as people looked over at the new arrivals.

“Looks like they put together a new squad,” Xavier heard Dybal mutter to his brother. They were a few steps away at a table nearby “They don’t even know where they are.” He clapped his hands together. “Want to be the welcoming committee?”

Xavier controlled his reactions, stopping himself from shooting up off the stool and widening his eyes in shock.

It was Palini.
