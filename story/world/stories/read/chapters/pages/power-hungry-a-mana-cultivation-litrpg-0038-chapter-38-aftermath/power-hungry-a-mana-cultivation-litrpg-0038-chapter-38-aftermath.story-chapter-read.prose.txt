Darius listened to Fiara and Cale while they gave him the after-action report. Fiara did most of the talking, describing things in a clinical manner from her own perspective. Darius tilted his head when Fiara mentioned Cale’s new sword. After the explanation he turned to Cale with an unreadable smile on his lips.

“Anything you want to add?” Darius asked as he crouched next to Cale.

“No,” Cale said quickly. “I think she got it about right.”

“And how exactly did you manage to defeat three Mana Circuitry cultivators?”

“They were unarmed?”

“Yeah,” Darius said and chuckled. “Turns out you weren’t.”

Cale took an instinctive side glance at the other recruits. They were busy resting and drinking water.

“Didn’t I tell you to keep your True Integra a secret?” Darius asked, his intense eyes boring into Cale’s. The moment lingered. As if Darius was waiting for a reaction. Cale was too tired to provide one.

Darius burst out laughing and clapped him on the shoulder. It was the good one, but it still stung like hell.

“I can’t believe I’m forced to say this, but I’m proud of you kid.”

“I’m proud of myself as well.”

“I’ll make sure Ravia knows how your progress is going. Speaking of which, did you manage to get any cultivation in during this mission?”

Cale grinned. “I’m at peak of six.”

Darius’s jaw dropped and he scoffed. “You’re shitting me.”

“I expect the Gray Lotus will be happy to provide me with advancement materials when we get back,” Cale said. “And then some.”

Darius gave him an appraising look. “You’re learning to play the game, and it isn’t only bluffs you got in your deck anymore.”

“You told us to make ourselves valuable. How am I doing so far?” Cale said.

“Kid, I could almost kiss you right now. You really came in clutch. You’ll absolutely be compensated. If I can’t persuade Ravia to show you some love, I’ll take it out of my own bag.”

“Don’t let her hear that.”

Darius chuckled. “Just because I’m growing a soft spot for you, doesn’t mean I’m stupid. Or self-destructive…”

“What am I going to do about these guys knowing about Aura?”

“What’s your relationship with them?” Darius asked, lowering his voice.

“Varies.”

“That Madia kid is a pain in the ass, but I think the rest can be worked with. I’ll talk to Ravia about it. Maybe we can work out a deal with them, as they’re getting rewards anyway.”

“Non-disclosure agreements?”

“Some of them will talk. Slip it out by accident or seemingly so,” Darius said. “But we won’t be able to hold a lid on your case anyway. We just need to make sure you’ll get to the academy.”

Cale gestured towards the dead Sullied. “I like my chances.”

“I’m starting to like them too,” Darius said and clapped him on the shoulder again as he got up from a crouch. “But don’t think for a second I won't run you like a dog once we get back to the HQ.”

“Wouldn’t want it any other way,” Cale said and nodded.

“Oh, and one more thing,” Darius said, pointing a finger at Cale. “You’re going to tell me and Fiara everything about that sword of yours. Everything.”

Cale swallowed. That secret might be too big for even Darius’s upgraded paygrade.

*

Under Darius’s leadership, the recruits collected their things and left the spire. With the floating platform now empty, Darius allowed them all to lie down on it as they started making their way back towards the closest Sullied fortress. The recruits all popped their anti-Citadel pills and slept. Cale saw no reason to struggle awake further, and he also let the sweet oblivion take him.

Once they were back, Darius had a long and hard talk with the leader of the Sullied about the betrayal. He barked and bellowed with a rage that Cale hadn’t realized had been simmering. Cale found he wasn’t quite as trusting of the Sullied anymore. Whether their situation and the rage stemming from it was justified or not, Cale could no longer pity them. Distant and wary indifference is what he felt.

Trust is expensive in this world.

The recruits were mostly avoiding Cale. Madia and Galvon were straight up avoiding him, but Cale caught them both throwing unfriendly glances at him. They must not have liked Cale stealing the spotlight. Artan and Risto were mostly indifferent. But in the few exchanges Cale had with them, he could sense there was a fascination. They wanted to know more, even though they didn’t outright say it. At least Risto had stopped testing him for reactions. Not that Cale would have any of it anymore.

Fiara stuck close. She didn’t say much, but Cale found she was always hovering about, sitting next to him to the point of leaning when they ate. They spent two days in the citadel, mostly just resting and eating and idly chatting about their experiences. Everyone was too tired for anything that Cale classified as ‘classic cultivator bullshit.’

It was nice. They talked about the upcoming academy entrance exam. Everyone was going to attend. Only Fiara was quiet and pensive whenever the topic was brought up. Cale wasn’t surprised. It was not like she was here as a recruit. She was here as Darius’s direct assistant.

But Cale felt like he would miss her if he went to the academy. He told her as much. She snapped something at him and stomped away, leaving Cale confused.

The recruits would skirt around the topic of Cale’s Integra, leading with questions, making provocative comments. So their rest wouldn’t be entirely without games, how thoughtful. Cale simply stopped the lines of questioning by stating that the less they knew the better. This especially pissed Madia, who had a moment of outburst asking Cale who the hell he thought he was, acting all special. She didn’t sit with them in the mess hall for the rest of their stay.

Finally two jets came to the landing platform and they were ready to leave. Fully rested, bellies full of spagon, Cale and the recruits were happy to leave. When Cale sat down across Fiara in Darius’s private jet, he felt at ease. He felt like he was finally getting the hang of this cultivator business.It was a hard business. Brutal, cunning, unforgivingly difficult. But Darius had a point. It was the game. The ultimate game. The ultimate way to test who you are and how far you could climb, using whatever you can.And Cale found he liked being talented at it.

The looming shadow of the secret legacy of the Nevani cast a dark thundercloud in the back of his mind, but for now he had to push it away. The world was not in immediate danger. And even if it was, right now Cale could do nothing about it. He would need to get stronger and find more Nevani sites to keep upgrading Aura and learning more.

His path was clear. It was laid straight ahead of him. It would be a rocky path, so steep and high in thin air, that it would require desperate crawling forward.

But Cale resolved to crawl, so that some day, he could fly.
