Dammit. Luke had more questions about the void being, among a host of other things, but he supposed he should be grateful for the time he did get with Guide. The way that talk ended didn't sit right with him, and he needed to walk around a bit to get rid of the nervous energy that had taken hold.

Once back to his trusted bench, Luke took a moment to make sure Smudge's injuries weren't too bad, and fed him a couple of monster cores afterward. Finding no injuries, he settled on petting the companion, marveling over how soft his... void was. What a thoroughly strange creature. Unable to help himself due to curiosity, he extended a thread into his friend. At first, there was nothing there, just a pitch-black void of nothingness. Luke knew that couldn't be it, though, so he focused and pushed with his will, trying to force whatever lurked inside to reveal itself. There had to be something there, or void creatures couldn't be alive. There had to be... Ah! There it was. Smudge whimpered. Luke scratched under his chin to calm his companion, but looking that far into Smudge's void made the little bastard uncomfortable, and he slipped into the Stable before Luke could stop him.

"We're not done with that," Luke admonished his friend, sending some nice thoughts to go with the sentiment.

With some time on his hands and no obvious threats looming over Earth, Luke knocked out the remaining enchantments needed to reach Rank 4. To his great disappointment, reaching that goal awarded him no more than a single new enchantment. A useless one. For him, anyway.

Enchantment of Blades: Adds a slight increase in sharpness of melee weapons.

Maybe Elijah and Ray would want that, Luke supposed. A profession wasn't just something to add to his own power. That concept wasn't difficult to come to terms with, but with how little time he had to work on it, he had still hoped to get something useful for himself. Perhaps once leveled up to Rank 5. Upon reaching Rank 4, a message had appeared as well.

Your enchantments grow more powerful.

That wasn't something he'd seen when getting to a new rank before. A general improvement of all enchantments wasn't something to sneeze at, even if it was minor. In Luke's experience, small leaps in power accumulate into big things over a long enough period of time. He considered starting his journey to the next rank in enchantment right away, but decided against it. While grinding the hours away in a semi-conscious state while staring at socks sounded like a pleasant enough time, he needed to check in on some people.

Jumping through the weave to the first one who came to mind, Luke choked and immediately left, heading to his sister, who he sure hoped wouldn't be in the same sort of situation.

"Did something happen to you?" Milla asked, a worried expression on her face. She was standing out on the street together with Lana, the two of them in the process of packing up the guild headquarters, the plaque hovering in the air before them while Lana worked some skill on it, her hands glowing as she twisted them this way and that.

Warmth spread through Luke's face, and he did his best to erase the scene he had just happened upon from his mind. "Nothing, nothing. Was just checking in on Lemira and Elijah."

Lana burst out laughing.

Milla raised an eyebrow. "I'm guessing you found both of them?"

"Yep," Luke said, clearing his throat.

"And what have we learned?" his sister asked, talking as if she were chastising a child.

Luke drooped and looked down at his feet. "I should stop teleporting to people without asking first."

"You should stop teleporting to people without asking first," Milla agreed.

Shaking off the embarrassment, Luke did his best to act as if he had moved past it. "You knew about those two?"

"Sure," Milla said. "It's been going on for a while."

"I didn't know."

Milla scoffed. "You're off saving the world or whatever." She took Lana's hand now that she was done with the plaque. "Most of us still need a little closeness."

"Yeah..."

"Speaking of which. How's Hannah?"

"Fine," Luke answered, with more than a little suspicion in his voice. "Why?"

"She's just worried about her little brother," Lana said with a smile. "And you're not the best with women." She caught herself and raised her palms. "Her words, not mine!"

"You're such an asshole," Luke muttered, directing his comment to Milla.

"Tell me I'm wrong," Milla said, now more than a little amused. "And that's Guildmistress Asshole to you."

Luke shook his head, exasperated. "I'm leaving."

"Why did you come? Not that I mind the company, but we've got work to do. Got a plane waiting for us."

"I was fleeing. Didn't think much about the destination," Luke grumbled. "Wait. A plane?"

Milla shrugged. "I've got connections."

"Of course you do. Well, I'm off. I'll meet you over there later, then."

"You'll ask whoever you're going to first, right?"

Luke walked off without an answer. Of course he was going to ask. He did not want a repeat of what he had just seen. Who knew you could have horns there?

Milla: "By the way, it seems like the US government is starting to get on its feet again, DIA included, and there's a lot of progress being made toward the monster core as power thing. We might even get internet access again soon."

Luke had lost his cellphone somewhere along the way, and he wasn't so sure he would welcome it back even if he found another one. The distraction-free life was kind of nice. Yup, no distractions at all.

Luke: "Ella. How are you doing?"

It took a while before he got a response.

Ella: "I don't want to do it."

Ella: "What if I run into something again?"

Luke: "There's always a risk of that happening."

Luke: "But it's no different from before. You could get run over by a car going to school."

Ella: "I biked through a park to get to school."

Luke: "You get the idea."

Luke: "Things happen. I'm around to heal you, and there are no walls in the weave. No physical ones, anyway."

Luke: "Can I come over to you?"

Another long pause.

Ella: "Fine."

Catching hold of her echo thread, Luke jumped through the weave and ended up in a room where the window overlooked a pasture full of cows. There were posters on the wall with young guys with dark make-up around their eyes, most of them reaching toward the camera. One said 'A1'. A desk stood against the wall, and a large Barbie house had been crammed into a corner to make room for a drum kit.

"Hej," Ella said.

Luke whirled around to find her sitting on a narrow bed full of stuffed animals and fluffy pink pillows. "What is this place?" he asked.

She looked around, and faint color spotted her cheeks. "My parents' house. This was my room. Is, I guess, since they never redid it after I moved out years ago."

"They have cows?"

"The neighbors do. We just get a lot of flies."

Luke nodded and stepped over to the bed. "So, what do you say?"

"About?"

He grinned. "You stop running and start, well, running. We could use all the help we can get."

"I don't know," Ella said, hesitation clear in her eyes. It took her a moment to speak again, and when she did, her eyes turned to the bed, and her shoulders drooped. "No. I don't think so."

"But-"

"I said no!" she shrieked, taking Luke off guard. Tears had formed in the corner of her eyes, and her expression was almost feral, like a wounded animal backed into a corner. "I don't want to die!"

Luke backed away and raised his hands as he cast about for words that would change her mind. None came to him, and perhaps it was for the best. The young woman in front of him was in no state to fight anything by putting her will into it. "Okay," he said. "Okay. I'm sorry, okay? I'll go."

He turned and reached for the weave.

"I'm sorry," she whispered, just as he went.

Not wanting to make that previous mistake again, he had made sure to check on the person who was his intended destination and got the go-ahead.

Ray and Curtis stood on a protruding cliff halfway up a mountain, looking down on a valley below. The two guys weren't alone. Luke nodded a greeting to Martin, smiled at Priyah and Sam, and thumped a fist against his chest to copy Kenny's greeting. "What are you guys up to?" Luke asked, coming up to peer over the edge.

"Leveling," Ray said. "Not all of us have nukes and nuke-like substances we can just pull out of our asses and get a hundred levels, so we make do with dungeons." The half-grin on Ray's face betrayed that he was just messing around, and not as angry as he sounded.

"My, my, aren't you sassy today?" Luke asked, shaking his head in disappointment.

Curtis pointed up and to the side, along the mountain, where snow gave way to stone. "We started up there. Going down is a pain."

"There are shamans down there in need of killing," Ray said. "And I'm closing in on Level 35."

"That's an impressive leveling speed," Luke said. "But don't you have a plane to catch?"

Curtis ignored the comment, and Ray fidgeted a little before answering. "I'm not sure I'm staying with the guild."

"Why not?"

"I'm going," Priyah interjected. Both Kenny and Martin nodded in agreement, but Sam just stood there, expressionless.

"I don't see the point," Ray said. "What good does being in a guild do?"

"How about working together to make sure Earth is taken care of?"

"Isn't that what you're doing out there, in the void?" he scoffed.

Luke couldn't help but glare. "I mean here on Earth... Political stuff, helping people. Stuff like that. Coordinating against dungeon breaks, making sure we don't have a Singer uprising. Doing bigger dungeons. That sort of thing."

"Back in the good old days before the orb, being in a guild, while gaming, I mean, was necessary to do raids and whatever. That's not how it works here. I don't care about all that stuff," Ray said, shaking his head as he got going. This was something he'd been thinking about for a while. That much was obvious. "I like being alone or in small groups. I didn't become Integrated just to go to meetings and do whatever someone tells me to. The Band was a mistake."

Luke looked over at Curtis, who gave a resigned shrug that seemed to indicate both their minds were made up.

"So you're quitting?"

"The guild?" Ray asked. "Yeah. Never liked being bossed around by your sister."

"Fine," Luke said.

"Fine," Ray repeated.

Martin stepped between them with a nervous chuckle. "Heh. Now this became awkward, didn't it? How about we just go deal with the shamans?"

"Fine," Ray nodded and went into stealth, and Curtis stepped back and leapt off the cliff, plunging toward the ground below.

"They'll come around," Martin said.

Kenny agreed. "Don't want to start bleeding talent. I suck at going solo."

"Yeah," Luke said. "Maybe. Anyway, aren't you going to Europe?"

"Milla, Lana, Elijah, Lemira, and Josh are going first. Josh is going to come back for us after," Priyah said.

"He's that good now?" Luke asked, remembering when the Jumper had trouble getting himself and Luke to New York. Transporting that many over an ocean would be a lot more difficult, he figured.

"I guess," Priyah said, glancing down into the valley.

"You go ahead. Don't let me keep you," Luke said. Joining them would just ruin their experience gain and not give him much at all, so there wasn't much reason to.

Unsure what to do next, Luke reached out to Penny but found out she was on her way to Hannah and Popov, riding a storm. Joining her up in the clouds would not be a good time, so they decided to meet up later. After notifying Milla of Ray's and Curtis's intentions, he set off. In the far distance, high above his current altitude, he could see the faint glow of a portal. Since he didn't feel like talking with Milla again, he decided it was preferable to climb. It would give him some time to clear his head and have a good think, too.
