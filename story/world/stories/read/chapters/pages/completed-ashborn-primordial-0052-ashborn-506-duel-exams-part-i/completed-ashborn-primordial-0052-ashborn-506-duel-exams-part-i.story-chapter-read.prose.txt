As Vir walked unsteadily down the same musky and oddly cold underground halls he had explored a week prior, he wondered how this fight would go.

The professors had, of course, informed them of the nature of the battle exams beforehand. This being the first exam of the year, it would consist of a single duel against a single opponent in a private setting.

While leaderboards would be published after the fact, no details of the fight would be shared publicly. No doubt those who excelled would ensure the entire Academy knew, but at least this way, the losers could save some face.

Not so for their later battle exams. Vir had heard later exams involved everything from squad battles where one had to defend territory for a set period of time to others where teams had to steal the opponents’ flag. The later battles were broadcast to all of Alt Ashani, and had become something of a spectator sport. There was even quite a healthy betting scene.

It all reminded him of the Bairan Tournament, and under other circumstances, he might have enjoyed lingering to experience it all. Sadly, he had no such luxury to dabble.

His opponent was a classmate to be picked at random from their batch, which came as a blessing. Considering the many hundreds of students in their batch, it seemed likely that Vir would be matched against someone he had never met or likely even seen.

It wasn't his performance or grades he cared about, of course. Such things were irrelevant, and performing poorly here would scarcely lead to expulsion. No, it was simply putting on a convincing enough performance that he was allowed to stay in the Academy. Winning was the last thing on his mind.

If Maiya’s tattoo failed, or rather, when—given its construction, it seemed all but inevitable—Vir’s loss was all but guaranteed. The fights were designed to be won only with magic. Putting a katar up to his opponent’s neck would simply reset their positions, forcing them to go again.

At the very least, he hoped he wasn't paired against Mina, Tia, or Princess Ira. If it was Ira, he at least had some confidence he might win. She seemed as sickly and frail in this universe as she had in theirs, and he was honestly shocked she had managed to gain admittance, though he supposed princesses in any universe received special treatment.

As for the tattoo, Maiya had taken six of her opportunities, using up every last drop of prana-infused blood Vir had prepared.

The first two attempts, the inscription hadn't worked at all, burning away the instant Vir tried to pour even the smallest trickle of prana into it, and Vir began to worry his entire theory behind the inscription had been incorrect.

The third time, however, allayed that fear, as Maiya grew more proficient with the art of inscription and Vir was able to convert the tiniest amount of prana.

The charging rate was less than half of what he could actively do already without burning his soul, but the crucial difference was that his new prana was of the Lightning affinity. A novel sensation, to be sure, but the amount was nowhere near what was required to power a single C Grade Arc spell.

Both on account of Maiya’s… commendable but crude workmanship, and because of the fact that Vir could not cycle the immense prana locked within his body to speed up the process. Not consciously, anyway. Prana was naturally bound to blood, and only Vir’s active efforts overrode that process. As such, prana was continuously cycling through his body, infusing his muscles with vitality.

That very same slow cycling gave Vir the fuel he required to power the inscription. It was more akin to opening a tap than channeling his prana, but it worked, and the Lightning prana that re-entered his bloodstream was his to freely manipulate.

Still, just as Vir was getting used to the sensation of having a different pranic element within his body, the tattoo burned away.

“Not again!” Maiya cried, looking as guilty as she did embarrassed.

“Hey,” Vir said consolingly, squeezing her hand. “It worked! That’s a huge accomplishment, Mai. It means we’re close. And we still have three more tries.”

Setting her mind to the task, her fourth attempt was better, and the fifth improved upon that.

The final iteration had proven promising enough that in the few hours since, he’d managed to accumulate enough for at least a handful of charges. It was difficult to tell exactly how much an Arc spell took, having never used the orb before.

At least Prana Vision works, Vir thought to himself as he approached the door to the dueling chamber. He’d activated it only briefly back at their home, keeping it off to conserve prana. That short invocation had revealed that powering the ability with Lightning prana only allowed him to see prana of that particular affinity, which was curious.

He'd chosen Lightning because, ironically, Vayren wasn’t completely wrong in extolling its virtues. It was efficient, fast to cast, difficult to avoid, and against a single opponent, ideal for ending a fight quickly.

If only I had more time, he thought. In a few days, he could convert the ocean of prana within his body. He could convert them to any affinity he wished.

Alas, reality was not so forgiving, and Vir arrived at the arena he was assigned. It was a large space with high, three-story ceilings. To accommodate aerial combat, he assumed.

“Oh, you're here. Darsh, yes?” the proctor, a tired-looking cleanshaven black-haired man in his mid-thirties said. “Please stand over there and wait for your opponent to arrive. We will begin as soon as he comes.”

Vir did as he was told, wondering how difficult the professors’ lives must be. In addition to teaching, they’d been tasked with defending the city against the never-ending Ash Beast incursions. The stress of watching helplessly as their world was slowly consumed was something Vir could very much relate to. Lately, it seemed like all he’d done was damage control. Staunching the bleeding of a dying realm.

Vir didn’t mind the wait. He savored it, in fact, as every minute he spent was more prana he wrested under his control.

It was interesting converting his own prana into a usable form. He wasn't gathering it from the environment, nor was he burning his soul in order to gain it. This was an entirely new mechanism devised entirely by him.

He’d nearly forgotten how much he’d missed that. Tinkering with prana, expanding his pathways. Actively working to better himself. It was a feeling he sorely missed. Perhaps, once he’d met Janak, once the immediate threat of the Ash Tears that plagued his universe was gone… perhaps then, Vir could return to more enjoyable pursuits.

He had to admit, the thought of delving into the secrets of magic, inventing new and wondrous contraptions alongside the greatest Thaumaturge ever to have lived had piqued his interest.

He often envied Aida, who’d taken something of an apprentice position under the Thaumaturge, despite her initial reluctance. A reaction shared by many, for it had not taken long for the realm to recognize the Thaumaturge’s genius, what with him churning out invention after invention that visibly bettered the realm.

Vir’s thoughts were suddenly snapped back to reality when he realized something had gone wrong with his inscription.

A quick check confirmed his fear. The tattoo had begun to fray at the edges.

Vir reduced the amount of prana he channeled through it, but it was too late. The damage had been done, and the tattoo only continued to unravel.

Vir sighed. It wasn’t Maiya’s fault. For someone with no experience in inscription tattooing to be able to make something even halfway functional after only a half dozen failures was nothing short of miraculous. And Vir couldn't be more thankful to Maiya, even if she beat herself up over how lackluster she perceived the tattoos to be.

It would have to suffice. Whatever prana he had was what he would have going into the duel.

Minutes later, his opponent finally arrived, and Vir froze.

The boy across from him smiled. “Ah, Darsh! Fancy seeing you here. It seems you are to be my opponent for the duel.”

“It seems that way,” Vir replied glumly. It would also seem that these matchups were perhaps not nearly as random as they seemed.

For standing across from him was none other than Vayren Vastav. Vayren, son of the man who’d killed Vir’s father in another universe. Whom Vir had thoroughly humiliated in full view of a professor and a class of a hundred pupils… and who was currently giving him a malicious glare full of glee, no doubt salivating at the prospect of obtaining glorious revenge.

Vir groaned, running a hand through his hair. If there was any doubt the very fabric of the world conspired against him before, it could be set to rest.

Bracing himself, Vir began channeling his prana.
