The great outdoors chirped with birds and the wind whispered around the hills, quite quiet compared to the screaming cave Naiad and Triangle had left. Being outside on the foothills and realizing they had to walk away from the city did not bring peace to Naiad’s mind. Because they went down the pacifist path with the goblins, they gained zero experience for leveling up. The mountains were still a level five-plus zone. Now on the foothills, she pulled out her bow and watched as Triangle brought the jingle bomb out.

“We only have the one,” she reminded him. “Save it for the sheep.”

“What am I going to do if we run into monsters? I want to fight.”

Naiad chewed her lip, then put her empty hand out. “Double chocolate crumble.”

“Frosting and sprinkles,” he whispered back, giggling.

With the pass-phrase completed, she summoned from her inventory the new knife they got earlier. They agreed never to mention this to the parents or any adults. This would be something they had to put back when done, like Dad did when he stole more chocolate cookies than he should’ve or Mom using the last of the sprinkles. They thought they'd snuck it from the kids, but Naiad noticed the pitiful lies.

“Don’t get too good at fighting, or an NPC will show up to give you a quest,” Naiad teased.

Triangle pretended to slash and added laser sword sound effects. “Pishew, pishew. I got this! Let’s go get some sheep.”

Naiad rotated around until she found the positioning that matched her recollection of Unkgil's map. Thanks to Dad’s lessons, she learned which way was East and West. The drawing was fairly accurate. There were six large peaks near the city. They walked to the closest one, keeping the city to their left. Triangle continued practicing his fighting moves.

“My fighter's name is ‘The Point’, sharp and quick to it. Stab, stab, stab! Woah!” He stumbled and tripped on the uneven rocky mountain.

The knife fell out of his hand. When he stood back up and went to dust the knife, he cut himself. He lost a single health point. “Ow. Ow. Ow. Ow. I need a potion!”

Naiad shook her hand in annoyance as he mistreated her new knife with no care. “It will stop on its own. We can’t risk a potion. Dad will check.”

“But it hurts! Please!” he cried louder.

Then he let out that annoying whine that caused a ringing sound in her ears. “Stop! Fine, you can have it. We need to make enough coin to buy a replacement. And don’t tell Grandma. Sprinkles still continue.”

“Sprinkles still continue.” He echoed the pass-phrase as he snatched the potion and chugged the deep liquid that looked similar to some fruit juice.

She heard sheep baaing nearby and a sheep bell. “That was quick. It’s only been twenty minutes.”

Up higher on the mountain, there was a small shelter that looked similar to a port-a-potty stall. Someone slapped together the shelter with leftover wood trim, causing it to lean to the right a bit. The roof was large branches and thatching. With how small it was, it would barely keep any rain out, just the sun. A young man sat inside it, smoking a long pipe.

He waved down to Naiad and Triangle. “Hello!”

He stood up and walked down to them with a shepherd’s hook in his hand. “Don’t see strangers this way much. That was a nasty fall. I’ll give ya a potion if you do something for me.”

Triangle's eyes moved a bit, signaling that he had received a quest. “Yes.”

Of course he received a quest, because he only had to exist in the game to get one. Naiad and her dad had to go looking for them in rats' nests. Triangle had luck.

“Great,” the shepherd beamed as one heart floated above his head. “I lost a few sheep down in the streambed. Dumb animals. They aren’t drowning as there hasn’t been rain, but I hate digging them out of the mud. Get them out and stop them from getting into the streambed, and the potion is yours.”

“On it!” Triangle said, letting go of the half-finished potion in his hand and letting the glass shatter. The knife was out as he chased after the lost herd. “The Point is to the rescue! Baaa!”

“Wait!” Naiad called out, but he was already jogging down the mountain slope.

She grumbled as she followed along. A few bushes and tall grasses decorated the sloping hills along with a few boulders. It created an open area, making it easy to see if there were any monsters hiding in the area. But small rodents could still be in the fields below or flying predators in the sky.

While kicking stones down the hill, she complained out loud, “We should’ve asked him for information about the goblins. What he knows. What monsters are in the area? What-”

Triangle didn’t hear him as he skidded on the rocks and slid down the streambed to find forty black and white sheep in the area. He ran over and hugged a sheep that was as tall as his character.

He tossed Naiad’s knife aside and tried to lift the sheep up, declaring, “Sheep! The Pointer is here to save the day.”

Annoyed at her knife being thrown, she grumbled and did not help Triangle figure out how to get the flock of sheep up and out of the ditch. There was less grass in the area as it was a source of water. The mud stuck to her boots as she slid down into the streambed and picked up her knife.

“Don’t throw my knife. What if there was a monster down here?”

Triangle was digging his fingers into the rough, clumped-up wool of the sheep. “The game didn’t make them fluffy. Maybe that’s why there are no monsters eating them? They're too rough.”

Naiad rolled her eyes and didn’t bother to answer. This far down in the ditch, the shepherd could not see them. She pulled out the shears and started trimming a sheep. The sheep bucked at her and baaed as she came near before trotting off further down the ditch. “Hold it still!”

Triangle laughed at her attempt. Naiad moved through the mud and went for the one he hugged. “No, not Spongey!”

“Yes Spongey! Why did you name it after an item?”

“Because he felt like the rough side of a sponge.”

She ignored him and started clipping. As she got near the stomach and Triangle’s hands, he let go, and the sheep kicked and head-butted Triangle’s chin for another hit point.

He fell with a splat in the mud, adding a layer to his dried coat. Mud spotted his red hat and face. The half-shorn sheep got away.

Naiad put the shears away and picked up the bit of wool she had successfully removed so far. Cutting the wool from eight sheep was going to take too long. This was not the time for arts and crafts.

“Should we click the sheep five times or test out that jingle bomb?” Naiad suggested.

“Not on Spongey!” Triangle panicked and went chasing after the sheep he declared now as his own pet.

“Fine, there are a lot of other sheep we can test them on. We can get Spongey out of here for the quest you have.”

The slopes of the ditch were slightly taller than her. She wasn’t certain how they were going to get out either, with the muddy walls. The shepherd must’ve climbed along somewhere or used his crook to pull up the sheep.

Without shovels, there was no way to dig a proper hole. The game suggested using the jingle bombs, so it would be fine. Neft said they were friends with the shepherd. He probably knew all about the bombs. It should be a simple quest: use an item given to solve the quest.

“Let’s make sure we only get eight sheep,” Naiad remembered Grandma’s original bag count request.

Triangle moved Spongey up out of the ditch first because Triangle declared Spongey liked his dirty wool coat. It was not worth the argument. There were about twelve sheep in the ditch near them and the rest of the herd moving away. Naiad shoved five up over the wall. It was originally only four, but the second sheep tumbled back down. The remaining eight sheep were further away from them, mostly grouped up, tearing out the last bit of vegetation popping out of the soil in the ditch.

Naiad loved the outdoors; however, farm work was too busy and loud. As she leaned against the wall, catching her breath. Her base stamina stat went up with the sheep hauling, and it still took five minutes to recover it.

Triangle was fine and ready to go. He pulled the jingle bomb case out of his inventory, carefully removed it from the box, and threw it without further warning.

Naiad was hoping to check the properties out before it went up sailing in the air. She was curious if it was a popper level, firework, fireball, or a mushroom cloud-making explosion. She was about to find out as it chimed a few times and bounced right next to a small group of six sheep near the wall of the ditch. They turned their heads, and the curiosity vanished from their cute little faces.

The noise was so loud, and the ground shook beneath their feet as mud, grass, wool, and hooves went flying.

Spongey was up on the ditch looking down, and now sported a red coat.
