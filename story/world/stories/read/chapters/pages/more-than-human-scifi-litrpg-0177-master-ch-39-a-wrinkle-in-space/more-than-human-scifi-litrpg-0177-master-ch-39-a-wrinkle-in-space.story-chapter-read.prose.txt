The only sound in the command bridge was Max’s harsh breathing as the world twisted.  The familiar pain of processing the extra dimensions of the Shadowverse assaulted him, but now that pain felt right.  His thoughts weren’t constrained to his head as more pathways for thought presented themselves.  He relished the feeling of freedom and growth.  He felt his father’s collective already forming into a tight weave.

He was about to ask a question when the bridge’s virtual displays snapped back on, showing the dark planet behind the ship against the starless void beyond.  The Valkyrie's command deck fell into absolute silence as father and son witnessed the death of a world.

The Promise, two kilometers of twisted metal and angry resolve, struck the alternate Earth's surface at a quarter the speed of light. The relativistic impact released energy equivalent to the detonation of a thousand gigatons of TNT, a force that dwarfed humanity's most powerful weapons. The initial flash bleached every sensor white before automatic filters kicked in, revealing an expanding hemisphere of superheated plasma that vaporized everything within a thousand-kilometer radius in the first microsecond.

Bill's multiple consciousnesses processed the destruction through dozens of sensor arrays, each clone-mind contributing to a collective understanding that no single human perspective could have grasped. Engineering-Bill tracked the shockwave propagation. Tactical-Bill analyzed the energy release patterns. Navigation-Bill monitored their position relative to the expanding devastation. But it was his prime consciousness that felt the weight of what they were witnessing, the murder of an entire world.

"Holy shit," Max whispered, his enhanced senses momentarily overwhelmed despite the ship's filtering systems. The Keldranith artifact in his grip had gone dormant; its twisted lines no longer moving and dead against his claws.  He could feel its energy expanding into a new direction.  Its unheard voices were muted.  Bill shifted the virtual screens to zoom in on the damage for himself and Max.

The shockwave raced outward at hypersonic speeds, flattening mountains and boiling oceans. Where the impact had occurred, a crater nearly two hundred kilometers across began to form, its edges glowing white-hot as the planet's crust liquefied and was flung into the upper atmosphere. The alternate Earth's rotation actually stuttered from the impact, its angular momentum disrupted by the catastrophic energy transfer.

Through the chaos, Bill's sensors detected movement in the orbital space where Luna should have been. The swarm-ball of dark dragons that had occupied the gravitational well recoiled from the planet's death throes, their writhing mass contracting into a tighter sphere as waves of superheated debris began reaching their position. Even creatures born in the lightless void of the Shadowverse seemed to recognize the magnitude of destruction below.

"The solar flare damage," Tactical-Bill observed across their shared mental network, highlighting regions of the planet's surface that still glowed with residual fire. "It was already damaged…but this…this is complete destruction now."

The remnants of the stellar eruption that had bathed this alternate Earth in lethal radiation were still visible as aurora-like curtains dancing through the upper atmosphere. But Bill's enhanced sensors detected the fractured remains of something else: a planetary shield system of impossible complexity, geometric patterns that hurt to contemplate directly. The entity below had diverted the solar flare's energy, channeling it through dimensional conduits to power the breach gates it had planned to use for its invasion of Earth.

"Look at those energy readings," Systems-Bill reported across their shared network. "This creature, Cthulhu, was using the solar flare to power its breaching gate. Dr. Parks' suppressors never had a chance against it.  It turned its own sun's fury into fuel.   But it’s kindling now, all the transmission lines are broken."

Through the clearing plasma, the planet's surface revealed its true horror. Amidst the rare pockets free from magma and ash, a glimpse of corpses could be seen.  It had been a living nightmare populated by creatures that would have driven H.R. Giger and M.C. Escher to madness with twisted biomechanical horrors with impossible geometries that crawled through cities that folded in on themselves. Bill shuddered, remembering the cannibalistic swarms of things that were part insect, part machine, part something that had never been meant to exist, as they tore at each other in endless feeding frenzies.  Gone now, as the shockwave reduced them to superheated ash.

Max's clawed fingers tightened around the artifact as he watched entire mountain ranges get swept away by the expanding ring of destruction. "How many of those things do you think were down there?"

"Based on the bio-signatures and thermal readings of our earlier reconnaissance?" Systems-Bill ran quick calculations, his voice carrying a note of disturbed fascination. "Billions of entities. Most weren't recognizably biological—geometric predators that existed in folded space, living architectures that fed on dimensional energy, swarms of things that were simultaneously one creature and many." He paused. "Were," he corrected grimly. "The shield system may have protected them from the solar flare, but there won’t be much left after that impact."

Science officer – Bill harrumphed, “Don’t count your dead until they stop moving.  Those bastards are resilient and adaptable.  They’re dug deep in the ground and the sea.  Their collective might be shattered, but there will be survivors.   Maybe even intelligent ones.”

A sound reached them then, not through any physical medium, but carried along the many-angled pathways between the dimensions they could perceive.  It made the Shadowverse bigger, darker, and strangely closer. It was a scream of pure rage and agony, words in an ancient tongue that predated human language. The Enochian syllables burned through their minds like acid.

"K'yarnak ph'nglui mglw'nafh fhtagn! Uln'gth syha'h ymg' bthnknyth!"

Max jerked in his seat as his powerful chthonic block skill rebuffed the verbal assault.  Bill's prime consciousness staggered as every clone-mind recoiled simultaneously.   The organic Bill’s of the crew reeled and gripped their heads.

Max felt the powerful thoughts encroaching around them, applying pressure as he fought back.  The extra dimensions here mingled the thoughts of those who could use them, like Bill and himself…and the powerful entity far below on the dark planet.

Max could feel the powerful thoughts and emotions.  Amusement at Max as he encircled Bill’s more fragile mental threads and rallied to rebuff the intrusive power of the monstrous voice.  Max felt the twist as the being tried to suborn him.  Max recognized the godlike presence from his dreams.  Chthulhu, an ancient and godlike being that dominated the other monsters here.  Max shored up his defenses.  He hadn’t been idle and accepted the alien skills blindly.  He’d modified them as his understanding of how they worked grew.  He’d improved them.

He grinned at Cthulhu’s surprise when he didn’t cave under its pressure.  Bill sensed Max’s help and assisted instinctually, as best he could, by filling in the gaps of Max’s more powerful screen to resist the massive pressure.  Max spoke first.

"That was... that wasn’t a random rage scream.  The thing was targeting us. It seems to have recognized something about the device’s portal signature."

Max's enhanced intellect, sharpened by viral uplift and exposure to Shadowverse dreaming, parsed the meaning with disturbing clarity. "It roughly translates to 'Death to the light-bringers! The ancient enemy returns to reclaim what was stolen!' The entity down there, I think it recognized this.  The Keldranith technology beneath the Labyrinth."

The screaming voice briefly shifted in register to abrupt pain before being abruptly cut off completely as the expanding plasma cloud reached the massive tower node, located about where Yellowstone was on the parallel Earth.  The twisted cityscape was vaporized along with its dreaming god in temperatures that briefly rivaled the surface of a star.

They watched in stunned silence as the shockwave continued its relentless expansion, turning forests to ashes and cities to rubble. Oceans boiled away, leaving exposed crust that cracked and glowed like broken pottery in a kiln. The planet's magnetic field fluctuated wildly as its molten core was disrupted by seismic waves beyond any scale in recorded history.

"This is all my fault," Bill finally said, his voice carrying the weight of collective guilt across all his bodies. "I brought that thing to our dimension. If it had stayed in normal space, Earth would have been-"

"Dead," Max interrupted firmly. "Earth would have been dead, along with everyone we care about. That ark ship wasn’t stoppable.”

“Mmph.  You’re the one that shifted its course, not me.  My tech only put us all in danger.”  Bill said.

“Bullshit.  Apex knew what needed to happen.  It lined us all up like little soldiers to do our parts, no matter how big or little.  You did the heavy lifting, Dad.  I just got lucky.  Your work saved our world and billions of lives.  This is the best outcome, perhaps the only outcome if we believe Apex really can predict the future.  There would never be any safety with those creatures, only a breach away."

Bill's prime consciousness gestured at the screens showing the dying planet with regret and then turned to Max. "And I almost lost you in the process. I should never have let you enter that Labyrinth. Not when I began to suspect what was coming."

Max turned from the displays to study his adoptive father—or rather, fathers. The man he had known for years was still there in the prime consciousness, but distributed across dozens of identical bodies, each carrying a fraction of the whole while remaining undeniably Bill. The change was profound and unsettling, but somehow familiar. Max's own transformation had been no less dramatic.

"You couldn't have stopped me," Max said with certainty. "And honestly? I'm not the same person who went into that place. The uplift virus, the Shadowverse exposure, the things I've seen and learned.  I understand now why you take the risks you do and why you sacrifice so much to protect others.  Seeing things more clearly means that you’re the first to act when there isn’t any time."

Bill's multiple bodies exchanged glances, a gesture that looked bizarre when performed by a dozen identical faces. His eyes kept drifting to the artifact in Max's hands. "That device...and that gate.  I’d say it defied physics, but such a comment would be hypocritical coming from me.  I've never seen anything like it. But those markings on it look familiar."  Bill's collective gaze went distant for a second with shared memory.  Understanding bloomed, and he continued.

“When Alexie and I were on Phobos, he was adamant about the ruins on Phobos being alien. Just kept muttering about 'the key to everything' and 'voices from beyond the veil.  Those markings are similar, but I proved the ruins were false!  But this device proves everything was a lie.  He seemed so earnest, but when I presented my evidence, he blamed the Outsider group as untrustworthy.  That evil bastard lied to me!"

Max hefted the sphere, its surface now mercifully still after the chaotic geometries had ceased their maddening dance. "I’m not sure he was evil.  The thing got into his head and drove him insane.  It’s the reason he built the Labyrinth. It really is aliens.  They call themselves Keldranith, and this is technology.  A sort of multidimensional gateway controller and calling card in one. Alexie could hear their voices but couldn't understand them like I can. The artifact was trying to establish contact between us and their Cooperative, to offer a contract that would give them access to our solar system…to aid us in combating the spreading darkness."

"That being down there, Chthulhu, recognized it.  They must be enemies with these Keldranith.  And they want to cleanse the corruption," Bill said slowly, understanding dawning across his shared consciousness. "And they would help to seal the breaches?"

"Exactly. There’s an avatar or AI in there, along with a whole string of sealed gates, who tried to sell me on their contract.  They’d have helped, but not for free.  I dug around in there and found evidence that any help would come with a world-sized price tag.  I don’t trust them." Max's grip tightened on the device. "At least these dead monsters were honest.  I’m not sure if the Keldranith are as benevolent as they say."

Bill's multiples exchanged odd glances.  It was a gesture that looked bizarre when performed by a dozen identical faces. "You're different, Max."  He mused. "Smarter. You’ve always been a sharp pup, but now…it’s amazing.  More dangerous. But also..." He paused, searching for words. "Also, more isolated. I can see how you process information, how you analyze threats. Your enhancements came with a price.  You used to be happier. Are you still certain your uplift was the right thing?"

"I wouldn't trade it for anything.  Ignorance isn't bliss…it's dangerous," Max replied, his enhanced cognition already analyzing their current situation, calculating orbital mechanics and energy requirements for their return journey. "My isolation isn't the same as being alone. I have family. I have people worth protecting."

The Valkyrie shuddered slightly as debris from the impact began reaching their altitude—chunks of superheated rock and metal that had been flung into space by the unimaginable forces below. Navigation-Bill adjusted their position with brief thruster burns, maintaining a safe distance from the expanding cloud of destruction.

"Speaking of protection," Tactical-Bill interjected, his sensors detecting movement from the dragon swarm, "we should consider our exit strategy. The local inhabitants seem to be taking an interest in us."

The mass of dark dragons was indeed expanding, individual creatures breaking away from the main swarm to investigate the strange ship that had appeared during their world's death throes. Each dragon was easily the size of a city bus, but their forms seemed to absorb light rather than reflect it, making them appear as moving voids against the stellar backdrop.

"They’re distracted and our cloaking is working for now, " Command-Bill observed, his enhanced senses reading their electromagnetic signatures. "But that won't last long. These things are always aggressive and hungry.  I think they know we’re here, but I’m not sure how."

"Then we leave now?" Max asked.  Command-Bill nodded with a smile, his prime consciousness already issuing orders to his engineering bodies.  He looked down at the device in Max’s hand again.

"I think our own shadow gate should be sufficient, Max.  No need to risk using that thing again.  Opening a comm portal back to normal space now. Full power to shields and weapons. This place is done.  Let’s get back home.  Vector for a polar orbital for when the Earthside ShadowGate opens for us.”

As the Valkyrie's portal generators spun up to full power, both father and son took one final look at the devastation below. The alternate Earth was still burning.  The ash-filled atmosphere would rain down eventually, and some creatures might survive, but for now, Earth might get a respite.

"No more suicidal heroics," Bill said quietly. "Either of us. We've risked too much already."

Max nodded, but his enhanced intellect was already analyzing potential threats, calculating probabilities, and preparing for the next crisis. In the depths of his viral-enhanced mind, he knew that promise would be impossible to keep. The universe had too many horrors, too many innocent people who needed protection.  He could see the fractal shadows shifting over the surface of the dim sun.  There were more like Chthulhu on the dark side.

But for now, it was enough to be heading home.

Bill took control of his portal back to Luna with a stern look at Max.  Soon, the SG2 station opened a shadow portal, dim starlight shown through like a beacon against the absolute darkness of the Shadowverse sky. The Valkyrie accelerated toward the breach, leaving behind a dead world and the hungry things that fed on its remains.

The portal snapped closed, leaving the darkness behind.  The dark dragons circled and watched with ancient, patient eyes.  With the light gone, they refocused on each other…predators and prey both.  Already, the brief visitors who had brought such interesting destruction to their realm were fading from their memories.
