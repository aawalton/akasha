It was a random morning, a day like any other.

The butterfly and the spider had spent most of the morning hunting, they had returned to the fort town with their game, and they had managed to escape the fortification without Adon being pulled into another meeting.

On such days, their afternoons were almost always consumed with one thing.

“Show me what you can do now,” Adon said, looking Goldie in the eyes.

They were both in semi-humanoid forms, standing in the forest, no one else around. Goldie had extra pairs of legs—giant spider’s legs—adding additional stability alongside her human legs. Adon had bits of exoskeleton on his arms, additional eyes on the back of his neck that mainly detected motion, and a few unusually long hairs on his head that were actually Venomous Spines. Transformation had become more and more key to their fighting styles as both mystic beasts grew stronger.

Now they seldom fought or hunted using their base forms, unless it was simply to keep in condition.

It was possible to do much more damage with transformed body parts, and they could also endure more brutal attacks.

“How serious do you want to go this time?” the spider asked.

“Pretend I’m an actual enemy,” Adon replied. “We’re close enough to the fort that we could get back quickly if there was an injury we couldn’t heal on our own somehow.”

“The High Priestess shortens her lifespan every time she performs a miracle, remember,” Goldie said quietly. “Do not underestimate me, Adon. You have not seen everything I am capable of doing.”

“The same goes for you.”

Even though they hunted together every morning, there were always things that neither invertebrate needed to pull out for the limited opponents they fought. That was why sparring against each other was important.

Rosslyn was too busy most of the time to participate herself, and there were few others who would make adequate opponents by this point in their respective evolutionary development. Goldie had improved more quickly than Adon since the dungeon, so he estimated she was just a little bit behind him in power.

“Let’s go, then,” Goldie said.

Adon stepped forward, but as he moved, the spider moved behind a thick tree trunk and instantly vanished.

She’s not just hiding, he thought.

He used fire magic and unleashed a thin disc of flame that chopped through the tree diagonally like a hot knife through butter.

The tree slid sideways along the line Adon had cut and then tipped over and fell.

The humanoid form of the spider did not appear behind it.

Hm.

The one disadvantage Goldie consistently had, if Adon chose to press, was that she could not truly practice stealth with Adon’s Telepathy. Her power was enough to keep her thoughts from being read, but no one Adon had ever met could conceal that they had mental activity completely.

He reached out and felt for her presence.

She’s not in front of me, she’s—

Adon wheeled around just in time to see a long, spear-like appendage swing down at his back. He threw himself to the side, and the point of the sharp exoskeleton spear stabbed into the ground.

Did you make yourself invisible? Adon sent.

He was already transforming himself, growing exoskeletal spikes on his arms and legs and a plate on his back that would cover his neck and head.

There would be no sneak attacks, or at least no effective ones.

For just a moment, I snapped back to my normal form, Goldie replied. I’m sure you have noticed how that use of Transformation is faster than anything else we can do with it. Then I launched myself across the space that separated us.

The spider was moving as she communicated, pulling her body up into the heights of the trees. Not just answering Adon’s question but preparing to strike again. Neither of them had called a time-out, after all.

But it was the butterfly who made the next move. He could still pinpoint Goldie’s exact location, even though she had placed leaves and branches in between them. He extended one arm, pointed it at the spider’s place in the trees, sprouted a dozen spines from his wrist, infused them with mana, and then fired them as projectiles.

Then he was moving, not waiting to see if they struck.

While she was reacting, he would set himself up for the next attack. He grabbed hold of the nearest tree and started rapidly climbing. With his chest against the tree and his back covered in exoskeletal armor, he would be a hard target until he reached the height he needed to attack her more directly.

He heard her crashing through branches as she moved to get away from his spine attack.

So she didn’t snap back to her small form again, he noted.

Then he sensed her shift her position to directly above him.

Ten feet or so still separated them, but the proximity made his blood run cold. He could sense an attack coming.

He threw himself to the side just in time, as she unleashed a burst of fluid that rushed past where he had been, melting the tree where it touched the branches and trunk.

Acidic venom… or is it just acid at this point? Adon thought. Either way, Goldie wasn’t holding back.

He reached up with his mind and gently pulled at her feet with Telekinesis.

The spider slipped and began to fall, but she quickly caught onto a branch with one hand and then shot another burst of acidic venom in Adon’s direction. He slipped around the side of the tree he was clinging onto and then allowed himself to slide down.

All right, new plan, don’t get closer to her after all. He was out of her sight. He would have to take advantage of this moment. She is definitely tracking my location with Telepathy at this point, too, though.

Adon snapped back to butterfly form for a moment, used Shed Skin, and began another Transformation coupled with a more intensive use of Telepathy.

—

Where did he go? Goldie wondered. Just a second ago, he changed shape, and now…?

The spider nervously shifted on her branch and wondered if she should have let Adon climb further up before she attacked. She had assumed he would keep coming at her despite the acid bursts, and she was counting on it, in fact.

Overhead, she had created a miniature network of webs using her slenderest threads.

They were strong but almost invisible, and they might not be capable of binding Adon for long, but she was confident that they would hold him for a sufficient time to give her an advantage in the spar. No form he could shift into could completely disregard something that had hold of his flesh, as far as she knew, though there were times he got close to being literally the wind.

But there was no way to plan for fighting the wind. Delicate threads were the closest she could come to a truly airtight attack.

There he is. Somehow she had lost his position for a moment, but even with Adon’s level of skill, it was impossible to completely hide your presence from Telepathy. She couldn’t see him, which probably meant that he had reverted to a smaller form. Hopefully not his actual butterfly form, which would be difficult for her to attack without doing potentially permanent damage.

We’re both taking this seriously, so he wouldn’t let me hit him in that form, she told herself. He’d form some sort of thickened shell around himself or something.

The spider tried to quiet her own presence as much as possible, enhanced her lower legs with Transformation, and finally took a running leap across the branches to place herself directly above Adon’s new position.

She still couldn’t see him.

She concentrated for a moment to make the particularly acidic brew of venom she had been working on that little bit stronger, then spewed it straight down where the butterfly should be.

It burned through layer after layer of tree branches and leaves, until it finally revealed Adon. He was in butterfly form, leaning against the trunk, unharmed, seemingly waiting for her.

Did he just block it with Telekinesis, or did I aim too far from the trunk?

You had better dodge this, she sent telepathically.

She fired another burst. Then the sun seemed to appear next to the spider.

Goldie felt a burning sensation spread through her abdomen, accompanied by only a single thought.

I got hit…

—

Sparring over? Adon sent.

Goldie took a moment to respond. She was visibly burned from chest down to her waist, and although Adon knew she could and would heal, the sight made him nervous.

We’re done, yes, Goldie sent after a moment. You were… She tilted her head to indicate where there still appeared to be part of a butterfly, attached to the tree but partially dissolved by the bit of acid venom that had hit it.

It was just an exoskeleton, the skin that Adon had shed earlier. But from a distance, it was, of course, a very close likeness to the butterfly.

I was there for a minute, until I wasn’t, Adon sent.

Yes, but… Goldie didn’t finish the thought. The spider had begun pushing healing mana throughout the injured places on her body, and she had to focus on that to guide it. At any rate, Adon knew exactly what she wanted to ask.

I figured out how to project my thoughts from one place to another, the butterfly answered. It works well as a kind of camouflage.

He didn’t mention that he had recognized how to do this by reference to when he had essentially projected his entire mind into Samson’s body in the latter’s final moments.

Maybe he would tell Goldie that if she asked very directly, but he preferred not to remind her about Samson’s death unless there was a very good reason. She had begun to act more like herself again after days of grieving.

So, I cannot reliably track your location with your mental activity, Goldie thought. Useless Telepathy!

They shared a quiet laugh.

Very useful for me, Adon replied. You would have ambushed me more than once otherwise.

“And your attacks are getting more lethal,” he added out loud.

“No choice for me but to grow,” she said breathlessly. “I’m being hunted by a deadly species of butterfly. I must adapt to survive.”

“What were you doing up above me?” he asked. “You stayed up there for most of it.”

She told him.

He nodded.

“I need to learn to stop trying to follow you up there. Maybe if I got up into the heights first, and you didn’t have any time to prepare… But even though I can fly, the treetops are really your domain. As long as you have your silk, I’m in danger anywhere but the open air.”

You almost got an acid bath, Goldie agreed telepathically. Not to mention the venom the silk itself is coated in. It would have shredded your wings if you flew up there.

“That’s why I make sure not to stay a butterfly,” Adon replied.

“Do you ever miss the days when being a butterfly was the strongest version of yourself?” Goldie asked.

“It still is in some ways,” Adon said. He paused. “But yes.”

The spider nodded. Her side was almost fully restored now, and she returned to a more nearly fully human form.

I miss being strong as a spider, too, she sent.

“Ready to go home?” she asked.

“I was just waiting for you.”

The pair descended the tree, carefully avoided stepping in any of the acid that had pooled on the ground or touching the trees that had been lit on fire by Adon’s attacks, and moved back toward the fort.

Another day, another little bit of training. Another step closer to the level of strength they would need to avenge Samson and help defeat the Empire.
