Nature’s Creation: Sacrificial Potato

Jinyo pulled out 10 kilograms of Exploding Potatoes and assembled them into a Sacrificial Potato. The round, plump creature that looked completely harmless appeared before him once again.

[Sacrificial Potato. Name: Salt Flavor. Level 11 (1 day)]

“Potato.” Salt Flavor looked up at its master. It seemed like it wanted to say something, but its body’s mechanism prevented it from speaking human language. It ran to pick up a stick and scratched out letters to communicate instead.

What kind of name did you give me! Your naming sense is absolutely horrendous!

“Guh.” Jinyo was cursed out by his own familiar and choked on his own breath!

“I didn’t mean to. I just couldn’t think of anything and blurted it out.”

Couldn’t think of anything and blurted it out? Would you like to be named Salt Flavor? This name is stuck with me for life. You’re capable of everything except coming up with a decent name. Your brain is made of toilet paper. If your next creations end up named Seaweed Flavor or Seafood Flavor, would you like to know what I’m capable of?!

Salt Flavor scratched out letters venting its fury. The stick in its hand snapped two or three times before it finished writing. It was genuinely furious! Since it couldn’t change its own name, it could only drag its future companions into hell together.

“Hahaha. Don’t you feel sorry for the ones who’ll come after?”

NO!

Salt Flavor showed no hesitation in its answer. The letters N and O were enormously large.

To calm its rage, Jinyo promised Salt Flavor that if he ever named other potato spirits in the future, he would name them all after potato chip flavors and accept all the blame himself. It would probably make Salt Flavor feel better knowing its companions had equally terrible names.

“That’s amazing!” Luciria praised Jinyo for Gretel to hear.

“Isn’t it! As expected of the man who shared his blood to bring me into this world.” Gretel puffed out his chest and soaked in the compliment fully. Even though at that moment he still didn’t understand what was so amazing about it.

“In twelve years of life, this is the first time I’ve seen someone get dominated by their own familiar.” It was a truly rare sight!

“…” Gretel deflated. Somebody please give him back his pride.

Jinyo released the Otherworldly Domain to restore his Planar to full capacity. The reason he had used magic to summon Salt Flavor was to prepare in case they encountered an Eltharok so he could react in time. Salt Flavor’s explosion had enough power to nearly kill one instantly. As long as it created an opening for Jinyo to sever its neck during a moment of weakness, the Sacrificial Potato would have fulfilled its purpose.

Jinyo had used only 10 kilograms of Exploding Potatoes and just 1,000 Planar. Half of what he’d used before, but still enough destructive power to be genuinely useful. Truthfully, Jinyo wanted to create a second Sacrificial Potato, but creating one required another potato spirit.

Right now Jinyo only had Salt Flavor. It held the broken half of a Carrot Sword as its personal weapon, marching at the front as a suicide vanguard. Its order was simple: if it encountered an Eltharok, charge straight in. Without the Otherworldly Domain to dispel the fog, everyone felt suffocated again because no matter which direction they looked, visibility was limited to a hundred meters.

Then Salt Flavor suddenly froze mid-step. Jinyo gripped Sonneteer and plunged into the fog. He saw a pumpkin-headed human swinging a machete with its massive arms. He raised his sword and disarmed it, knocking the machete from its grasp. Another pumpkin-headed human brought an iron rake crashing down with full force. Jinyo retreated backward to dodge.

Eye of the Sage displayed their names.

Cursed Villager, Level 10–15

Compared to the Eltharok, these things were practically adorable. Their strength was considerably greater than ordinary humans but not quite terrifying. There were about four or five of them—unlike Eltharoks, which didn’t gather in groups.

But the feeling of facing them was different. At least the Eltharok was clearly a monster. The Cursed Villagers wore human clothing, had human-like bodies—only their heads were pumpkins. Jinyo felt a moral sting having to decapitate creatures that resembled humans. But they were still monsters regardless.

“Lady Luciria, two on the right. I’ll handle three on the left. Agreed?”

“Y-yes, I mean…” Luciria stammered before Jinyo charged to the left. Intense Sword Spirit surged from the blade. He displayed flowing forms that scattered the sweet fragrance of peach blossoms. Luciria drew her sword but stared at the pumpkin-headed humans approaching with rakes and axes.

They swung their arms and weapons at Luciria, forcing her to dodge. She countered with sword slashes. At her skill level, killing low-level monsters shouldn’t have been difficult at all.

The sword she wielded moved like water waves. The high-quality steel blade—purchased at great expense—cut through the Cursed Villager’s neck until the head separated from the shoulders. Yet even after killing the monster, Luciria’s face didn’t look well.

Jinyo killed the Cursed Villagers with ease. He watched the girl who had been agile, sharp, and fluid when sparring against older children—fierce and powerful when tested against combat dummies—but now wobbled like a child learning to hold a sword for the first time.

“Lady Luciria, your skill is more than this.”

“I know…”

“I understand everyone has their problems. But if you can’t perform at your full ability, it’ll be difficult. Not for me—but for you.” Jinyo could have killed all the Cursed Villagers by himself. But the system wouldn’t give Jinyo experience points. Better to let Luciria get the kills and gain experience to increase her level.

“I’m sorry. I’ll try not to be a burden to you.”

“Don’t overthink it. Even if you don’t fight, I’ll protect you regardless.” But if you can fight on your own, that would be wonderful! Because he’d rather save his energy for the big enemies than waste time killing low-level monsters. Jinyo didn’t say it, but Luciria could feel the pressure. Her heart trembled with fear for reasons she couldn’t explain.

Gretel walked along behind his father with a scowl, swinging his arms. Along the way, they fought nearly a hundred Cursed Villagers. Jinyo deliberately let one in ten slip past for Luciria to fight at the rear. She tried to prove herself useful but still fell below the standard she was capable of.

Gretel also noticed something. Every time they passed the corpse of a human from the training center, he would see Jinyo make a pained expression as if every death was his own fault. The boy didn’t know what it meant. He just kept following close to Jinyo wherever he went.

The further they walked, the more frequently they encountered Cursed Villagers, while Eltharoks became almost nonexistent. It should have been good news that they’d escaped the danger zone. Eventually, the gray fog covering the forest gradually faded. The sky that once had some light filtering through was slowly replaced by darkness.

Jinyo climbed a tree. He wove vines into a hammock sturdy enough to bear their weight. He jumped down to carry Gretel and Luciria up onto the tree where—though dangerous—it was still better than sleeping on the ground where who-knew-what might come prowling.

Jinyo distributed the rice porridge he’d prepared earlier for the children to eat and stave off hunger. Luciria glanced at him as though she wanted to say something. But perhaps it was the opposite—she wanted Jinyo to speak first.

“You… aren’t disappointed in me, are you?”

“Disappointed? About what?”

“I’ve been called a genius. But I can’t be of any use to you at all.” If she were scolded, she might actually feel better.

“Yeah, Sis Lucy is supposed to be really skilled, but when fighting monsters, she’s completely useless. I don’t understand,” Gretel spoke up loudly. It was as if he’d discovered some great secret but couldn’t keep it contained any longer.

“When I have to fight monsters, my head goes completely blank. I’m scared and confused. I can’t do anything right.” Luciria hugged her sword tightly, as if it were the last thing that could protect her life.

“It’s not that complicated. Just swing and kill them—that’s it. If I had power like Sis Lucy, I’d use it to kill every enemy. I’d protect Mom and everyone.” Gretel declared loudly. He was frustrated by Luciria’s timidity. She was older than him and could do so many things. Her swordsmanship was excellent. She had everything Gretel wished he had, yet she acted as if she were weak. It was completely irrational.

“If you’re not going to use that sword, just give it to me. I’ll cut off all their heads! Stop being so wishy-washy. Coward.”

“I’d like to give it to Gretel, but you can’t even lift it. The sword’s length alone is almost as tall as you.”

“!!!” Was he being mocked for being short?!

Gretel’s face turned red. He absolutely hated it when people mocked his height. Even if it was the truth. But being shorter didn’t matter—ever been punched by someone smaller? No—he had to be polite to women. Mom taught him that.

“If it were me, I’d definitely do better than you,” Gretel grumbled.

“But I don’t think anyone can perfectly take another person’s place, Gretel. You’re still young. You don’t yet understand Lady Luciria’s position. The person who has to hold the sword and fight is different from the person who just talks. And it’s very rude to call someone who saved your life a coward.”

“Why? Why are you taking her side? I’m your son! How can you side with an outsider over me? I’m telling Mom.” Gretel raised his voice at his father. Inside, his heart ached with resentment. Or was it because he didn’t call Jinyo “Father”? The boy turned away. If he could, he would’ve run off somewhere, but he couldn’t climb down from the tree by himself. All he could do was huff in frustration.

“I’m sorry,” Luciria bowed her head, feeling guilty. The relationship between Jinyo and Gretel was already awkward, and now she’d made the rift between father and son even wider.

“It has nothing to do with you. Gretel is still too young. Don’t take it to heart.” Jinyo smiled gently but kept his pain buried deep inside. Gretel was still just a seven-year-old child. His emotional maturity wasn’t sufficient yet. No matter how much he tried to please him, giving in on everything would only spoil the boy for nothing.

Before long, Rina would have to return to duty. If Jinyo didn’t learn to discipline Gretel, he’d feel guilty toward Gretel’s mother if he couldn’t raise him well.

“I’m not a good father. I’ve never raised children before. But this boy’s mother told me herself not to take his side on everything. If he’s wrong, tell him he’s wrong.”

“But Gretel was right. I am a coward.”

“I’ve never heard of a coward who puts their own body on the line to protect someone else. I think it’s worthy of praise, even—being that scared yet still fighting for someone you have no connection to. ‘Genius’ is just a word people use to praise us on our good days. But in the end, a genius is just an ordinary person. We make mistakes like anyone else. I understand how you feel.”

“Has someone as skilled as you ever made mistakes too?”

“Countless times. I wasn’t born talented either. Especially…” Jinyo turned to look at Gretel, who had his back to him. When Jinyo reached out to place a hand on his son, Gretel swatted the arm away. Jinyo gave a thin smile, but it was especially warm.

“We just need time to adjust, and someone to guide us on the days we stumble. I used to have Grandmother, Mother, and my younger sister. Today, I have a son and a daughter—two more people who remind me of what I’m still capable of.”

“Someone to guide you…” Luciria heard that sentence and felt something stir inside her. She was afraid—afraid of defying her grandfather’s orders. But she didn’t want to have regrets anymore. A life where she couldn’t choose anything—not even the person she wanted to learn from. There was only one person who could guide her. The man right in front of her.

“Mr. Jinyo. Please—will you accept me as your disciple?”

“About that…”

“It has nothing to do with my brother or my grandfather. This is my own request. I want to choose the teacher who will teach me how to live—not a teacher who just makes me follow textbooks. I know it’s shameless, but please teach me how to fight. As for tuition, someday I’ll find a way to pay. Please—accept me as your disciple!” Luciria gathered her courage and voiced her true desire. She bowed her head in reverence even before receiving an answer. These were words refined from the depths of her heart. She wanted to be taught by Jinyo even if her family wouldn’t permit it.

[Luciria Halfrin wishes you to be her master. Will you accept?]

“I understand. I’ll accept you as a temporary disciple. But until we have your guardian’s permission, we’re only half teacher and student.” Because without guardian consent, it would be master and disciple in name only.

“Yes, Master!”

[You have accepted Luciria Halfrin as a temporary disciple. Training guidance system unlocked.]

[Guide Strength (Strength)]

[Guide Training (Planar)]

[Guide Movement (Reflexes)]

[Guide Mind (Psychic Power)]

[Guide Knowledge (Intelligence)]

[Guide Bearing (Charm)]

[Select one skill you possess to pass on to your disciple. When you guide that discipline, the disciple’s comprehension of the taught skill will increase twofold. The guidance buff ends when the disciple’s skill proficiency equals yours. You will gain skill proficiency each time the disciple understands what you teach.]

Six training icons appeared on the system window. Jinyo could see the flaws and the most suitable training methods for Luciria—more than two approaches. It wasn’t just about guiding her forms or power; it also revealed what she was worried about.

Her terror of monsters and the emotional block preventing her from overcoming it—primarily rooted in the fear she’d experienced facing the Locust Demon. If she could conquer that fear, she could unleash her full potential.

Her mind was too fragile. She needed Psychic Power training to become stronger.

Jinyo selected Psychic Power guidance. Luciria’s Psychic Power training schedule appeared in his mind. But it was too brutal for a twelve-year-old. He just needed to adapt it to suit Luciria. And now wasn’t the appropriate time.

As for the skill he wanted to pass on—since she was his disciple, Jinyo wanted to teach Luciria Song of a Hundred Flowers. But Luciria had a complicated background. He couldn’t pass it on without her guardian’s approval. So he chose the skill in which he had the highest proficiency.

[You have chosen to pass on the skill: Sword Spirit. Luciria’s comprehension of Sword Spirit will increase by 100% when you guide her.]

“I’ll teach you Sword Spirit.”

“Sword Spirit?”

“You’re looking down on it, aren’t you? Wondering why it has to be Sword Spirit. Even though Sword Spirit is a basic skill every swordsman can use, it runs extremely deep.”

“No, Sword Spirit is fine. I watched you fight using Sword Spirit. It was incredible.”

“I’ll explain Sword Spirit to you. Just close your eyes, meditate, and listen.” Jinyo began the guidance immediately. Guiding Sword Spirit didn’t just increase skill proficiency—it also helped develop the mind.

“The sword is a reflection of the soul. When the mind is weak, the blade grows dull. When the mind is strong, even a wooden stick can cut through steel. Imagine a sword in your mind. Control your breathing in and out. Before you stands something that must be struck. Steady your resolve. Don’t rush to swing. Just still your heart until it is unshaken. Focus only on what must be cut.” Jinyo began his lecture. Every sentence resonated deep within Luciria’s heart. The system’s power helped her absorb the lecture. Her understanding of Sword Spirit increased.

[Your disciple, Luciria Halfrin, has benefited from your lecture. Sword Spirit skill proficiency increased. You have gained a slight increase in Sword Spirit skill proficiency.]

[Your son, Gretel Erindel, has benefited from your lecture. Swordsmanship comprehension slightly increased. Your own swordsmanship comprehension slightly increased.]

???

Of course. One of the four core skills. A teacher is a child’s second parent. It didn’t just affect disciples but also his own children. If Jinyo could teach Gretel to understand, Jinyo would benefit as well—it was just that until now he’d never unlocked the guidance function, so it had never shown any effect.

Jinyo smiled quietly. Even though Gretel was sulking right now, the boy was obediently absorbing every word Jinyo spoke. As a father, he was overjoyed. Jinyo continued his lecture until some time had passed and something unusual occurred.

“Hm?” Gretel felt something strange. By then, he had already forgotten his dissatisfaction with his father.

“Look—look over there!” Gretel pointed to a spot in the distance. Jinyo and Luciria—who had just completed the temporary master-disciple ceremony—turned to look at what had startled Gretel. It was peculiar. In the midst of the pitch-dark forest, there was a faint glow and white smoke rising up into the sky. But it was so small it was almost invisible.

Someone was making a fire over there!

“There are people there!” Luciria was excited. This was the first time since entering this insane world that they’d seen any sign of other survivors.

“Let’s go! Mom might be there!” Gretel was desperate to find his mother. Only being with Mom made him feel safe. Jinyo thought about traveling through the forest at night. From his experience, the forest at night was far more dangerous than during the day.

But the nighttime forest didn’t have fog obscuring visibility. This was their only chance to find other survivors. It was risky, but there were no other options. Jinyo used his sword to cut the vine hammock and tied Gretel to his back with it. Gretel’s eyes went wide as goose eggs. He was like a baby monkey clinging to its father during a flood evacuation. How utterly embarrassing.
