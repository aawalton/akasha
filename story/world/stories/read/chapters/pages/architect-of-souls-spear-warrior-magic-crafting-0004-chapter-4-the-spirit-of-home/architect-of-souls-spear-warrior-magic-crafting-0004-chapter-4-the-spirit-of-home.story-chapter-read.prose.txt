Looking back, Ferris could still barely believe he'd become a cultivator that night. People claimed ‘cultivation has as many paths as travelers,’ but he hadn’t heard of anyone else succeeding by throwing a tantrum, crying, and giving up.

He thought it all happened after he truly accepted he would never be a delver. Ironically, this was the very thing that rekindled his dream.

No matter how it happened, once someone found their soul for the first time, every time afterwards was easier. All it took was a little more meditation.

Laying on his new bed, he let the memories play through his mind, then drift away. Each thought was acknowledged and released. In time, he felt the familiar yet still new presence.

Ferris slowly reached out, letting it encompass him. His soul: that indescribable piece of himself. His entire life he had been told it existed, but words could never do it justice. Once he’d felt it for the first time, he could always find it again.

Opening his eyes, Ferris found himself inside his soulspace.

He’d heard this place called many things over the years, but Uncle Winton said it was his spiritual foundation. A space where his soul reflected reality, where he could cultivate and improve himself.

Gaining access to one’s soulspace was considered the first step on the path of cultivation. It felt like an entire world that existed within him. Although so far, his was mostly empty…

Ferris stood and looked around. In here, his body wasn’t real. It was a mental reflection of how he saw himself. He still unconsciously expected his missing leg to be there, and so in here his body was whole.

He stretched his legs, reveling in the simple pleasure of walking.

In front of him burned a healthy, joyous fire. It was surrounded by a stone hearth, protecting and containing the flames. This was Ferris’ Heartfire. As long as it burned he would live, and as long as he lived it would burn. Every moment of his life the Heartfire burned a little bit of his lifeforce away. When his lifeforce was gone, the fire would go out, and Ferris would die.

Near the Heartfire, there was a small pool. Well, more of a puddle than a pool, but someday it would be a pool. Eventually, it would hopefully be an ocean. This puddle was Ferris’ Qi Pool. It held all the qi energy that Ferris had painstakingly gathered, working every day since unlocking his soul. Right now, it could barely fill a bathtub, but each day it grew larger.

Both his Heartfire and the Qi Pool had a subtle presence to them that was hard to describe. It was like a vibration in the air around them that he could feel in his heart, but only if he stopped to concentrate.

Propped up against the hearth was a simple spear. Ferris had created the spear himself, mentally shaped from the qi he had collected. There was a constant compromise a cultivator had to make: should they use qi to fill their pool or to craft objects. A large Qi Pool was necessary to grow stronger, but there were many other uses for it.

The spear, for example, helped Ferris protect himself as he gathered qi. The soulspace reflected himself, and everyone had their personal demons. On his first visit to his soulspace, small imp-like creatures swarmed and ate him alive. They were blood red, with snouts like a wolf, horns, and three sharp claws on each limb.

Luckily, dying in the soulspace did not cause death in real life. It did mean he had to reform his mental body, which took several days. Even if it wasn’t fatal, it was still painful. After that, Ferris was more cautious. He stayed close to the Heartfire when possible and didn’t explore too far without reason.

Ferris had cultivated, working in his soulspace every day since becoming an Adept. Today, he was surprised by something new. A life-size stone statue of himself bound by chains stood next to his Heartfire. The statue was missing its left leg, cut off right below the hip—just like Ferris in real life. Its hands were dramatically reaching up towards a stone leg floating just out of reach. Most striking of all, stark white chains wrapping around everything.

Studying the statue, Ferris realized this was the oath he had made to Elder Harper. It really did bind to his cultivation, physically appearing in his soulspace. If he broke the oath, the statue would turn into a real problem. On the other hand, if he was faithful, it would be a boon: the statue would turn into a representation of his honor, with benefits to his cultivation.

For now, there was nothing he could do with the statue. He gripped his spear and looked around. The demons usually stayed away from his Heartfire, but sometimes they waited at the edge.

His goal was to gather qi, so he prepared to set off. Besides his spear, he needed two things: a sack and a light. The sack was simply something to hold anything he found, but the light was trickier. Ferris’ soulspace had no natural source of light. It wasn’t like the real world, where the barrier to the Wild lit up everything. Illumination was rare beyond the Heartfire or glowing Qi Pool, so he needed something to take with him when he explored. The spear and the sack would last him a long time—as long as they didn’t break. The kind of light he could make, on the other hand, would only last a short time.

Sitting at the edge of his Qi Pool, Ferris placed his hand into the liquid. He closed his eyes and visualized what he wanted: a small stone disk with a hole through it, which would shine brightly. Pulling his hand out of the pool, he now held a ‘lightstone,’ which he tied to his belt. This was the simplest, and weakest, way to craft with qi. It made what his uncle called qi constructs.

The word ‘construct’ was generally used to mean a ‘mental construct.’ It was anything that was created here in his soul, rather than the real world. Since it was made of qi, it was a qi construct.

The glowing blue spears that Dan and his uncle had shot out at the clawling in the rift were also mental constructs, created in their soulspaces. They had used an ability to manifest the constructs into the real world, but only after making them in their soul.

Creating the lightstone used up some of his Qi Pool, but it was worth it as it helped him gather more.

Heading north, spear in one hand and sack in the other, Ferris reveled in his ability to walk. The freedom to simply take a step was fulfilling in a way he had never appreciated before losing it. He had to be vigilant not to let the sensation distract him.

Beyond the safety of the Heartfire, Ferris’ soulspace was a barren wasteland. The rocky ground was mostly flat, and the lack of any vegetation or life made him feel unwelcome in his own body. The lightstone didn’t shine very far, but at least he could see.

It didn’t take him long to reach his destination: a long trench that cut through the ground in a giant circle. The trench wasn’t very deep—it usually came up to his waist—but stretched far. It ran farther than he could see, bending as it disappeared into the darkness.

There was a somewhat magical effect inside it. While outside the air was calm, within the trench there was an unnatural current of air. The wind’s direction flowed back and forth with every breath he took. Every time he inhaled or exhaled it immediately matched, almost like he was unintentionally copying the pace of the wind. In reality, the wind followed him.

More than simply an unusual location, this trench was an ability in his soul called Air Passage. Breathing was an essential part of cultivation. Special breathing techniques could have a profound effect on the soulspace, but simply moving air in and out of the lungs was important. Each breath carried qi from the outside world. Most would be lost as he exhaled, but not all. That qi would collect here, in his Air Passage.

Looking down into the trench, Ferris was shocked. There was a thin layer of water along the bottom. The water was currently still, but it looked like a flood had flowed through the passage. There was also more accumulated qi than he’d ever seen. Glittering chunks of crystalized qi lay scattered on the trench’s floor. They looked like small shards of quartz, but glowed with their own iridescent light.

All this, just from walking through a rift? Ferris could hardly believe it. Of course, the air in a rift contained more qi than typical. Killing monsters could also release qi into the air, which also must have contributed.

Even so, he was shocked. There was more than ten times the usual amount in his Air Passage. As a beginner, Ferris was limited by his Air Passage’s efficiency rather than lacking qi in the air. Why had more qi in the air had such a dramatic effect, if he couldn’t filter it all?

The water, he thought, it must be from the water breathing potion. No one told him that it would flood his Air Passage, although in retrospect it made sense. He had been literally breathing water, and the soulspace reflected reality. The water had even corroded away at the walls, enlarging the trench. That was great, since now he could gather more qi from each breath.

Stepping down into the Air Passage, Ferris confirmed that it was indeed deeper. Instead of coming up to his waist, now he could barely see over the edge. The usual stream of air pushed him back and forth in a steady flow, with no identifiable source of the wind. The Air Passage was a large loop. It usually took Ferris about half an hour to walk the entire distance, collecting qi.

The Air Passage gave off the same subtle vibrations in his heart as his Heartfire and Qi Pool, but there was another layer on top. It almost felt like he was falling backwards, although not as disorienting. Uncle Winton had explained that this was due to the ability being placed over an open meridian.

Meridians were flows of spiritual energy, which connected the real world to the spiritual. While things in the soulspace could naturally affect Ferris’ body, to interact with the world around him required a meridian. The Air Passage took in air and filtered out qi, connecting to his real lungs by flowing energy through the loop in this specific spot in his soulspace.

Reaching down into the water, he picked up a piece of crystalized qi. While his Qi Pool was liquid, this was hard as a rock. Ferris placed it in his sack and started down the passage. He liked to walk with the wind of his exhale. Either direction would loop back to the start, so it didn’t matter.

As he walked along, his sack started to fill up. A bit after halfway it was completely full. He’d never filled it before, never even come close. Delvers risk their lives in the rifts, but there were advantages. This bounty of qi was a prime example.

There was nothing to do but make a trip to his Heartfire to empty the sack. It would take some time, but the lightstone should last. Meditation did not substitute for sleep—at least at Ferris’ level—so he didn’t have all night. Still, this was worth it, even if it made him miss a meal.

Halfway back, Ferris spotted several shapes moving at the edge of his light. He dropped the sack and readied his spear. While he may not have bonded a spear like his uncle, he had grown up training to use one. He took a balanced stance, gripping the weapon in front of him.

Three demonic blue frogs hopped into view. They were about the size of a dog, although they leapt about on powerful back legs. Like the other demons he had seen in his soulspace, these had brightly colored horns. Each of the sharp points were swirled with lines of blue, standing out against the demon’s darker hide. Ferris had never seen this type before, which was concerning. The soulspace reflected Ferris, and the same was true of the demons. New heart demons meant he had new problems.

Ferris started circling to the left as the demons made deep croaking sounds. He believed he was fast enough to skewer any that attacked, but he was afraid of being counter attacked by the other two. In a fight against multiple enemies, he thought, the trick is to force several one-on-one encounters. Pack monsters were common, so Ferris had been trained for this.

The closest demon frog leapt at him. Ferris dodged backwards, watching the other two. As expected, they both jumped shortly after. Digging his feet into the ground, he stepped to the side and attacked the left frog from the outside. The farthest frog was still in the air, while the first hadn’t recovered. This gave Ferris the space to fully commit to the stab.

His spear pierced the demon in midair, sinking deep. The blue demon gave a ragged croak while black blood gushed from the wound. Ferris pulled back, swinging the spear to free his weapon. The frog stubbornly stuck tight, weighing him down.

The first frog leapt again. Ferris was out of position for a stab, and he didn’t have time to dodge, so he tried to block instead. The spear tip was still embedded in a demon frog, so he dragged it back and braced with the spear pointing down. The beast slammed into the shaft, horns almost reaching him. Ferris pushed it back, taking the opportunity to step down onto the skewered frog, pulling his spear free as his foot held the corpse down.

Just as he freed his weapon, the last demon slammed into him, sinking its pair of horns into his side. Screaming, Ferris ignored it and attacked the enemy in front of him. It was too close to stab, so he swung his spear down on its head so hard it broke the shaft in half. A better construct wouldn’t have broken so easily, but this spear was made with the most basic of qi crafting techniques.

Gripping the remaining half, he stabbed it down into the frog still sticking into his side. The wood was sharp enough to impale the thing, pushing it off him to fall dead to the ground.

Breathing ragged, Ferris held one hand to his side. He reached down with his other hand and picked up the top half of his spear. The last demon was still stunned as he impaled it, finishing the fight.

Gingerly, Ferris tore his shirt into strips to wrap his side. His mental body was not real, but it bled and felt pain. The wound would probably have incapacitated or killed him in the real world, but the effects were lessened in the soulspace. His body was a mental construct, not real flesh. Ferris only bled because he believed he should. With a conscious effort, he focused on moving.

He retrieved the sack and gathered the three demon frog’s bodies. They would be valuable crafting material, but scavengers would steal them given the chance. He couldn’t fit them into the sack, but he was able tie them on its side.

Ferris staggered back towards the Heartfire, barely able to carry everything. He kept the top half of his spear for protection but abandoned the rest of it. He didn’t have the skill to repair it, but it was better than no weapon.

Luckily, the remaining journey was uneventful. Back safely, he untied and dropped the three demon corpses next to his Heartfire, they would be as safe here as he could get.

Lifting the sack, Ferris took it to his Qi Pool. He unceremoniously dumped the crystalized qi into the pool. Given time they would liquify and grow the pool. It was another oddity of this place: left anywhere else, the rocks would stay rocks.

Gingerly, he poked at his wound. He didn’t have the strength to harvest the second half of his Air Passage tonight, nor could he defend himself if he tried. Better to wait for tomorrow, he thought, than risk it. Ferris sighed. Closing his eyes, he exited the soulspace and mentally returned to the real world.

Ferris opened his eyes to his room. He felt a pain in his side, a weak side effect of being injured in his soulspace. Wounds in the soulspace may not be real, but somehow they still hurt.

Stretching, his side felt odd. Like there was a disconnect between the real pain and lack of any physical evidence. Like how his leg often felt, after he had lost it.

Reaching down, Ferris tried to itch the leg that wasn’t there. Walking in his soulspace was always a dream come true, but waking up after…

Laying back down, he sighed. While he hadn’t harvested the entire Air Passage, his Qi Pool was larger than it had been yesterday.

He would need every bit he could get to help regrow his leg.
