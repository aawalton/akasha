Done with testing Jade’s Consume Pool, Ferris woke up to eat his own lunch.

The rice balls Mother Lily had prepared were good, with the pickled filling giving just the right balance of salt. They were a bit messy, with grains of rice sticking to his hands.

After Jade finished off the leftovers, the two of them returned to refining spirit gems. It was another yellow one next. As simple to refine as the one from yesterday, but still tiring. Ferris could see why this wasn’t the most popular job. It was turning out to be both laborious and boring.

The last gem of the day was a vivid green. Compared to Jade’s dark green crystal, this gem contained a brighter, livelier shade. Not the same, but he was still excited at the similarities.

Inside the refinement device, the gem contained a field of tall grass along the ground beneath the crystals. Jade was overjoyed, and immediately started systematically rolling around, eating out a clearing.

Watching the slime work, Ferris considered this latest environment from the spirit gems. He knew monster cores had aspects, like water, fire, acid, and… plant. The type could be almost any concept—as far as he knew—although you had to find a monster with that aspected core type to acquire it. Certain concepts occurred more frequently, and rarity didn’t directly relate to power. Ferris also knew that the same aspect from two different types of monsters would also have differences. Clan Bluereef always collected water cores from a very specific type of monster for consistency.

It was fairly obvious that these spirit gems also had aspects. This was yet another similarity to cores, along with the crystals and pollution. Ferris knew spirit gems were valuable, and rarer than cores. Every monster had a core, but the same was not true of spirit gems. He supposed he also knew they needed refinement, now. Beyond that, they were a mystery.

If the blue gem had a water aspect, and the red gem was fire, could this green be plant? What type was the yellow gem? Did acid gems exist? If they did, would he need to wade through acid to refine them? Were there gems that needed special abilities to refine?

This green gem went slower than the others, but only because Jade insisted on clearing out the field of grass as they went. Thankfully, the grass was only around the crystal formations and didn’t fill the entire walled-in area as the water had. Even with the delay, Ferris and Jade finished the last gem even sooner than yesterday due to their teamwork.

They probably had enough time to refine at least one more gem, but Ferris wanted to see if he could find a new food source for Jade. He returned the refinement device with the clear, vivid green gem. Then, they quickly left Ox house and headed for the sixth district.

Each branch of Hollowfield had a specialty, and the sixth was in charge of food production. The slime ranch where Ferris had gotten Jade was a single part of the process the sixth branch used to produce enough food to feed the entire clan—and more—every day.

The fifth district smelled as bad as ever, but then gave way to the abundant flowers of the sixth. The main pathway through the sixth district was dominated by restaurants, cafes, and small specialty shops. Near the center, however, Ferris found a large grocery. The place was busy, with many people coming and leaving. There also appeared to be an area for carts to load and unload along the side.

Inside, Ferris and Jade found a large open space filled with rows of boxes. Each large box was piled high with various fruits and vegetables. People collected various items, paying for them as they left.

Exploring, Ferris compared prices, looking for the most volume for money. Growing up, he had often gone to a large open market like this with his family. Clan Hollowfield had a lot more variety than he was used to—likely because everything had to be imported back home—but the atmosphere was nostalgic.

He saw a help stand along the side, and stood in line to speak to someone. Soon, he was greeted by a man wearing a green shirt with an apple embroidered on it. Ferris had seen the same symbol above the doors on his way into the grocer.

“Hello,” the man’s eyes flickered to Jade, who was being carried in Ferris’ arm, “Adept, and welcome to the sixth branch provisionary. I am Louis Hollowfield, how may we help you today?”

“Greetings Louis, I am Adept Ferris Bluereef, retainer of Hollowfield. I am interested in pricing and availability for a recurring bulk order,” Ferris said. If Jade could turn food into qi, he needed a steady supply.

The man nodded, likely a common request, and pulled out a form.

“May I get an idea of what food types and amounts you require?” Louis asked, already beginning to fill out the form.

“I am not sure the exact amount yet. What is the least expensive commonly available option you have?” Ferris just needed food, the cheaper the better.

“We have a couple of options; would you be more interested in the cheapest overall, or ingredients with more nutritional value? Or perhaps prepared rations?” Louis asked, looking up from his form.

Ferris considered for a second. He didn’t need the food ready to eat—Jade could eat anything raw—so that only left deciding between size or quality.

In theory, the Consume Pool absorbed food to make qi. Ferris wasn’t sure if ‘better’ food could produce more qi, but it would make sense. On the other hand, the ‘worse’ food could be cheap enough to win overall… so long as Jade could eat it all without the Consume Pool overflowing.

“I’m not sure,” Ferris said, “I want either the volume or the density. I may need to test the same cost of each on different days.”

Louis looked a bit confused, so Ferris thought it might help the man to have more information.

“I am looking for food for my familiar, Jade. They eat a lot.”

It was like a light had appeared in Louis’ head, and the man nodded.

“Yes, we can certainly help with that.” Louis glanced down at Jade, and asked, “If I may be so bold, is your familiar a picky eater?”

Ferris shook his head. No, Jade was anything but.

“If we are discussing inexpensive bulk food, would you consider animal feed? It wouldn’t be as appetizing, but it is nutritious.”

Ferris doubted Jade would mind. The slime happily ate anything they could, shoes included. If it were a problem, he could just change the order.

Louis discussed a couple of options the provisionary had for different animal feeds. Livestock was not common, but some animals were raised either for important products or specific purposes. Or to show off.

The cheapest option was large bundles of wet straw. After a harvest at the farms, there was a great deal of leftover plant material. Most of it went to the slime ranch to make fertilizer, but a limited amount of the stalks of plants like oats and other grains were bundled up for livestock.

The farms had a full harvest every day, and space was limited, so everything needed to either be disposed of or sold quickly. Since there wasn’t any room or time to dry the stalks, it came still freshly cut.

The smallest bundles came tied together in a brick larger than his head. Ferris doubted he could carry one while using his crutch.

The grocer didn’t have any stock on hand, but Louis was able to accept an order for the following day. Apparently, the grain harvest would only set aside enough bundles to meet that day’s orders. Ferris wanted to set up a weekly order, but he wasn’t sure how much Jade could eat in a single day.

For now, he decided to do a smaller test. He made his best guess on one day’s worth of food, and then purchased twice as much. Any excess food would just get eaten eventually.

Louis presented Ferris with the bill, which took a massive chunk of Ferris’ mundane currency. He could afford this once, but not beyond that.

Typically, a cultivator could easily afford any common mundane items. However, bulk goods had two considerations: supply and the Empire.

For the supply, Ferris was dependent on Clan Hollowfield. A cultivator could theoretically acquire enough mundane wealth to purchase a clan’s entire food supply, but the clan would simply say no. Clan Hollowfield grew and sold the food for the sake of the people living in the clan. No amount of mundane coin would be worth selling off the entire stock.

Of course, mundane currency was also regulated by the Empire. There were many ways an Adept could abuse a local market. But if there weren’t any kind of common currency, it would disrupt trade too much. If the Empire didn’t impose rules, most groups would have to resort to barter just to protect themselves from being manipulated by their opponents.

Thankfully, Louis assured Ferris that the amounts he wanted per day were allowed, although only because he was a retainer of the clan. That was what a clan did: come together to provide better things together than any of them could do alone.

To pay for this long term, Ferris would need to acquire more mundane currency. Delvers could usually find some less-valuable resources in the rifts to earn mundane coins, but that wasn’t an option yet for Ferris. The money from Uncle Winton wouldn’t be enough to cover Jade’s food after today.

The easiest, and least desirable, solution was to simply exchange merit for mundane money. While mundane currency could not be changed into merit, the clan was more than happy to do the opposite—Ferris had seen the generous exchange rate when he visited the first branch’s merit store.

Typically, no one would be crazy enough to do it. Merit could directly increase a cultivator’s power, while mundane currency was only useful for mundane purchases.

Only… for Ferris and Jade, this food would convert to qi, which could be worth it. After a quick calculation, Ferris found that a single merit point could purchase nine days—at Ferris’ current guess for amount—of the food Jade needed. If Ferris did an extra day of work every other week, he could afford it. Of course, that was one less potential merit point spent on abilities and techniques that could save his life.

He decided to spend the merit point—and work the extra day—to get him started. He could keep looking for anything else to feed Jade, but this was a short-term problem. Eventually, they would be delving, and Ferris expected to find plenty for Jade to eat in the rifts.

Louis offered to have the order delivered for a small fee, pointedly not looking at Ferris’ missing leg. Not that Ferris could have easily carried this much food even with two legs. He accepted the offer gratefully.

Ferris looked at Jade. Mother Lily prided herself on her meals and keeping them fed; he wasn’t looking forward to explaining the food. It also wouldn’t fit in his room… was there a place to leave the deliveries? He hoped there was a storeroom, or he would be sleeping on bundles of wet grass.

Feeling accomplished, and a bit poorer, Ferris carried Jade back to Lily House. Jade was unhappy they left without eating anything, but was soon mollified as they arrived just in time for dinner.

Mother Lily had prepared a hearty soup, chock-full of beans and vegetables. Warm, yeasty rolls were provided on the side, along with a smooth, creamy spread to smear on the bread. No birds were harmed in the making of this meal, Ferris chuckled to himself, enjoying the break from the meat. Still, some fish would be nice.

All the residents of Lily House were present. While eating, Brix was excitedly talking with Karl about her plans to start delving. Both Cyrus and Brix had an evaluation test for joining clan delving groups the next day. She’d been preparing for it since before Ferris had joined Clan Hollowfield—although that wasn’t that long—but she still pestered Karl for advice.

“Which rifts have a better chance of lizard monsters, the Volcano or the Hanging Tree stone?” Brix asked.

Ferris knew rifts would always have some randomness, but rift magic had certain resources that could influence it. The most common way to get targeted rifts was to use a cornerstone, or ‘stone’ for short. Each would have consistent patterns and attributes, which delvers would use to make up a nickname—like Volcano or Hanging Tree. Somehow, they had a natural stabilizing effect on rift magic.

Some cornerstones had better attributes than others, but all were valuable. Clans—and other organizations—would hoard them to send their delvers to farm resources. Cornerstones appeared very rarely, usually as a stable rift that would draw in delver groups until it was found. The map on the wall was made by Mother Lily’s husband as his group attempted to find and bring a new stone back to the clan.

“Volcano, for sure. Unfortunately,” Karl shook his head, “neither are an option. Even after you pass the test, they will send you on Red Tower runs for at least a month.”

“A month!” Brix exclaimed. “They said I could queue for any stone as soon as I passed.”

“Yes,” Karl said with a grin, “but they never said a group would pick you from the queue. You need a higher rating to lead your own group, and it is understood that only Red Tower groups accept the newly bonded.”

Brix fumed a bit at being called newly bonded, but Ferris could see the point. Karl had been a cultivator for over half a decade, while both Cyrus and Brix had less than half a year since they were mundane.

“What are the requirements for the test?” Ferris asked. He would need to take it eventually as well, although he probably had to wait until his leg was healed.

Karl glanced over, but didn’t answer. The man had been standoffish—albeit polite—since Ferris had bonded. Ferris was interested in the man’s experience, but not if he was going to be arrogant over Jade.

Luckily, Karl wasn’t the only source of information, and Cyrus was happy to explain.

“Well,” Cyrus began, “they mainly care about four things: rift magic, an offensive ability, a defensive ability, and mobility.”

“Rift magic is a deep and treacherous field, but we only need to skim the surface of it to qualify. We don’t even need to be able to open a rift. The clan simply requires all delvers to be able to quickly exit a rift in case of emergency.”

Ferris remembered Valda opening the rift that took him from Bluereef Bastion to here. What Cyrus described was a common ability that let a delver return to the bastion from anywhere in the rift. It would be foolish to enter a rift without one, especially if the rest of your team had it. Rift exit abilities had many restrictions, and they commonly caused backlash or other problems when used, but they were definitely worth it.

“The offensive and defensive abilities are usually tested individually,” Cyrus continued. “Mainly, they want to ensure you can hurt the monsters and survive. The Red Tower rifts are usually safe enough, but they won’t let anyone delve who would die from a single hit.”

“Lastly, mobility.” Cyrus shrugged. He didn’t say it, but this would be Ferris’ largest challenge. “Delver groups have to be able to move quickly together. The clan has an obstacle course, with a time requirement to pass. It is highly encouraged to have a mobility ability, but not strictly required like the others.”

Karl and Brix had resumed their conversation, and Ferris considered Cyrus’ words. He was considering between Grow and Assimilate Object as his first offensive ability to build, at least until other options appeared. Everything decent from the library required an open meridian, so they wouldn’t help.

Grow was simple, and would be straightforward to develop offensively… but if Jade was large and slow, the slime may also need to develop a movement ability like Jump to keep up. Plus, they needed separate defensive abilities for both Jade and Ferris.

Assimilate Object could integrate a magical weapon, maybe even giving a suitable offensive ability. It would also leave Jade small enough that Ferris could continue carrying the slime, not requiring an additional movement ability. Although this meant purchasing a magic weapon to assimilate.

Assimilate Object could also be considered a defensive ability—but only for Jade. Assimilating the chain cage to protect Jade’s core would do nothing to protect Ferris. Grow would help protect Jade a little, but not enough that Ferris would bet his life on it. In both cases, he needed something more…

Ferris spent the rest of dinner considering what defensive abilities he could try for. The library had a few options, but none of them appealed more than the others. Long-term, he needed to wait for the Core Tree to give seeds for Acid Resistance, Acid Dismantle, and Acid Reconstruction. While waiting, he could choose to work on either Grow or Assimilate Object—unless something better appeared.

After the meal, Ferris was able to speak to Mother Lily.

“Excuse me, Mother Lily, but could I have a quick word?” Ferris asked, while Jade was ‘helping’ Mother Lily clean up. The slime had realized that sometimes the other residents left food behind, and insisted on helping dispose of it.

“Yes, dear, what can I do for you?” Mother Lily was stacking bowls, after scraping each one upside down over Jade.

“I have a delivery coming tomorrow, and I am not sure if it will fit in my room. Is there a place it could be stored?”

Mother Lily stopped and considered, “There is some room downstairs. How long would you need to store it?”

“Well,” Ferris put a hand on Jade and said, “I am practicing a new technique with Jade.” He considered how to phrase it, such that Mother Lily wouldn’t think she was underfeeding them. “We are experimenting with what happens if Jade dissolves large quantities of raw material regularly. If it works, I was hoping for a permanent space with weekly deliveries.”

“Hah!” Mother Lily said with a smile, “It is about time you fatten up that slime. I can’t do it all by myself, you know. The clan gives me a generous food budget, but it has limits. You can’t keep neglecting the poor dear.”

Rather than being offended, apparently Ferris was now in trouble for not feeding Jade more.

“Jade loves your food, Mother Lily.”

“Yes, the little dear certainly does. Now, flattery aside,” she said with a wave of her hand, “I will clear out a space down in the basement, now run along and let me clean up.”

“Thank you, Mother Lily,” Ferris said, taking Jade and heading back to their room. Brix’s excitement for the delving exams had him looking forward to his own. He may be behind her, but he was on the path.

And tonight, his next step would be to finally retake the chain fort.
