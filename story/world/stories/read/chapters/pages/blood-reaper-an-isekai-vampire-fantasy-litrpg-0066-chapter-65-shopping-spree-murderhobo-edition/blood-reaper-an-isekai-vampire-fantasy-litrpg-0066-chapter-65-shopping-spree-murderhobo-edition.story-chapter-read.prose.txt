Given how well-dressed the vampire bandits had been, their loot was impressive—and their blood  had served well in refueling everyone’s Soul Meters and HP.

The aftermath of their bloody battle lay scattered across the clearing. Violet sunlight fractured through the canopy in shattered rays, catching on crimson droplets that clung to leaves. The metallic tang of blood saturated the air, embedding itself in Ace’s senses and coating his tongue with each breath.

Ace’s team had quickly made their way through this graveyard without even a moment’s hesitation. Their bodies carried a fluidity now—predators rather than prey—shoulders squared against the weight of necessary violence, eyes scanning with calculated efficiency. Each of them had inspected the fallen with the detachment of those who had learned to survive. They had gathered their loot with deliberate purpose, searching for anything that might keep them alive another day. The quiet and methodical nature of their looting stood in stark contrast to the frenzy that had preceded it.

The fight had been brutal. Quick.

Efficient.

Shadow energy lingered like grief, and dark tendrils from his brief foray into the Shadow Realm dissipated slowly into the morning air. The forest floor had become their canvas, their story written in boot prints pressed deep into yielding earth and arterial sprays that painted fallen leaves.

In the spreading pools of blood that hadn’t yet been absorbed by the thirsty soil, Ace caught glimpses of his team’s faces—fighters that had been forged in combat, tempered by loss, and bound together by the desperate need to not face this nightmare alone.

“Well done, team,” he said, his voice carrying easily through the clearing. “Anyone hurt?”

“Nothing I can’t fix,” Tara replied, the crimson light of her magic fading as she finished tending to a deep cut on Marcus’s bark-covered forearm.

The healer stood, brushing dirt from her new armor—a gleaming suit of leather armor that hugged her curves and reflected the afternoon sunlight in ripples across its surface. The metalwork and binding were a much-needed replacement for the tattered black scrubs she’d worn when they first met.

Ace found his gaze lingering longer than it should have. He forced himself to look away, uncomfortable with the way his chest tightened at the sight of her. Whatever they were to each other—friends, soldiers, or something more—the lines blurred further with each passing day.

Everyone had gotten decent loot this time.

Ace had gotten daggers, the silver-haired leader’s dual-bladed staff, a fresh shirt, clean pants, and some iron cubes—which were apparently another type of currency in this world.

Tara had received something called the Echo Staff, and the beautiful mahogany and gold weapon had metal adornments on both ends that were sharp enough to slice into trees. It had some impressive properties that she still needed to study, but the essence seemed to be that it dealt insane levels of damage to shadow beings. She had also replaced her Forgeborn Tactical Combat Harness with something called a Nightingale’s Shroud—a set of armor that offered buffs to her defensive stats, along with a boost in her shadow magic. It also highlighted her figure better than her tattered old scrubs, and Ace found his gaze lingering on her hips more than once.

Marcus proudly wore his new amulet, which he had traded Olivia to get. It enhanced his Mind Leech ability, and it offered some protective buffs. He’d also gotten slick new leather armor with small spikes along the spine, as well as a pair of sturdy boots.

Olivia had gotten Hydra’s Bane, a curved dagger that seemed to absorb the shadows around it, along with a flowing black-and-crimson dress that complemented her dancer-like movements.

“These people were well-equipped,” Olivia said, her voice carrying the same detached appreciation she might’ve used to discuss a particularly striking painting. She twirled, her new armor-clad, black-and-crimson dress flowing around her legs, and did an experimental spin of her new staff. “Look at this gear! Beautiful. I feel almost human again.”

Marcus snorted, still examining his new gemstone pendant as it glowed with subtle purple energy. “Human? Speak for yourself. I’ve got bark growing up my arms.” He pushed back his sleeve to reveal the woody texture that had spread since their encounter with the Thornhide Behemoth. “Though I will say, the new threads are an improvement over blood-soaked rags.”

Ace nodded absently. With a fluid, practiced move, he reached into his Abyssal Armory and summoned the weapon he had received in his own loot—the silver-haired leader’s dual-bladed staff. Though he had never held something like this before, he was already smitten.

It was beautiful.

The weapon hummed as his fingers closed around its shaft, as though greeting a long-lost friend. Shadow energy rippled along its surface like water, and Ace felt an immediate connection to it.

“Hello, gorgeous,” he said under his breath as he gave it an experimental twirl.

It hummed in response.

The balance was perfect—each blade on either end complementing the other, designed for spinning attacks that could slice through multiple opponents. The weapon felt natural in his hands, more like an extension of himself than a tool.

“That suits you,” Tara said as she stepped closer. “Better than those daggers you got.”

“Can’t argue with that,” Ace said as he spun it again.

In a rush of black smoke, Ace tucked it away in his Abyssal Armory once more. The Shadow Realm storage space had become second nature now, a pocket dimension that held his growing arsenal.

“It feels so good to get rid of those scrubs,” Tara said with a relieved sigh. “They were basically ribbons.”

“We certainly look the part now,” Marcus agreed.

And look the part they did.

Gone were the ragged survivors who had stumbled through their first days in this nightmare world. In their place stood four predators dressed in armor and weapons that reflected their growing power.

Ace ran a hand through his hair, surprised to find it clean and smooth. The grime and sweat of battle had been replaced by the strange magic of this world’s loot system. His new black pants and shirt felt light yet strong, moving with him rather than restricting his motions.

Everyone went silent for a moment, and it took Ace a second or two to realize why—the System hadn’t shown up to taunt them. The silence felt wrong, somehow, and her absence was almost as unsettling as her presence.

Almost.

“The System must be busy,” he muttered, scanning the clearing for any sign of their childlike tormentor.

“Right? I was thinking the same thing,” Marcus said, his gaze sweeping the trees as if she would show up any moment. “Anyone else find it weird she’s not here to gloat? This feels like exactly the kind of moment she’d pop in with some backhanded compliment.”

Everyone tensed, as if expecting her to materialize at the mention. When nothing happened, the silence felt heavier somehow.

Ace shook his head, adjusting the straps of his Blood-Bonded Thornplate Armor. “Let’s not invite trouble. We should move before scavengers arrive.”

“Scavengers?” Tara asked, her brow furrowing. “You mean like... vultures?”

“No,” Ace replied, his enhanced senses already detecting movement at the edges of his awareness. “The kind that walks on two legs and would happily add us to this body count.”

“Ah,” she said with a knowing nod.

Not fazed. Not bothered. Not even a little bit afraid.

Predators were simply the norm in this world.

“Shall we address the obvious?” Olivia asked. She shifted her weight onto one hip and set her hand on her waist.

Marcus glanced down at the scattered bodies on the ground. “You mean why the System hasn’t shown up to take their essence? Or—”

“No,” Olivia said with an apathetic shrug. “The hivemind.”

Ah.

“That was…” Their Blood Dancer pursed her lips, and her eyes briefly glazed over in thought. “How do I word this?”

“Unnerving,” Tara answered for her.

“But useful,” Marcus added.

The group gathered in a loose circle, their new gear gleaming in the scattered light that filtered through the forest canopy. Ace flexed his fingers, still feeling the ghostly connection that had flowed between them during the fight—the unspoken commands, the shared awareness, the perfect synchronization that had turned their ragtag team into a lethal fighting unit.

“It was wild,” Marcus continued. He tapped his temple with his index finger. “I could feel what you wanted me to do without you even saying it. Like having someone whisper in my ear, except the voice was in my head.”

“Like I said,” Tara replied. “Unnerving.” She adjusted the straps on her new Nightingale’s Shroud armor. The black leather fit her perfectly, reinforced at vital points while allowing full mobility. “It wasn’t just commands, though. I could sense where everyone was positioned without looking. I knew Marcus was about to get attacked before I even turned around.”

“So,” Olivia said, her eyes narrowing as she studied Ace’s features. “Perhaps we should disband the party.”

“Fucking what?!” Marcus asked.

Olivia huffed and cast him a sidelong glare. “I don’t appreciate having my mind invaded like that. I refuse to be controlled.”

“It’s not mind control,” Ace clarified, wanting to establish that boundary immediately. “From what I can tell, you all have free will to ignore what I suggest. The hivemind just communicates intent. It’s no different than me barking out orders.”

Olivia frowned, her crimson lips curving downward in a sultry pout. “I don’t know, Ace. It’s a step too far for me.”

“Well, what are the limits?” Marcus interjected. “Can you read our thoughts? See our memories?” A flicker of genuine concern crossed his face. “Because there are some things in here I’d rather keep private.”

“No, I don’t think so,” Ace assured him. “Just your physical state, your position, and basic emotional responses. Enough to coordinate a battle, not enough to invade privacy.”

“Range?” Tara asked, her paramedic’s mind naturally categorizing and analyzing.

Ace shrugged. “We’ll have to figure that out as we fight.”

“Why can’t I communicate with the others?” Marcus pressed. “I tried during the fight, but no one seemed to hear me.”

“I think only the alpha can hear us,” Olivia suggested, her gaze sharpening.

“Let’s not make it weird,” Ace chided.

Tara laughed. “Weirder than a silent psychic hivemind link between our party members? Is that possible?”

“We did fight a tree,” Marcus pointed out.

“Fair point,” Tara admitted.

“We need to focus,” Ace interjected. “We shouldn’t waste daylight. Let’s get going.”

“Yeah, I guess you’re right,” Marcus said. “The Crucible awaits,”

“How far away is it?” Olivia asked.

“Three days, maybe?” Ace answered. “Depends on whether or not we encounter much opposition on the way. We should avoid fighting, if possible. I’m getting the feeling we need to preserve our HP and SM.”

Tara and Marcus nodded.

Olivia, however, remained silent, her gaze fixed on the trees.

“I won’t make you stay,” Ace said flatly.

Though she didn’t otherwise move, the Blood Dancer’s gaze shifted toward him. Her chilling gaze snared him, and for several moments, no one spoke.

“Hmm.” Olivia pursed her lips in thought. “If you’re right that there’s no mind control, then I will stay. We work well as a team.”

“That we do,” Ace agreed.

With a glance at the fallen vampires who had attacked his team, Ace gestured for the others to follow him. Silently, they headed out into the woods, ever aware of the possible danger lurking between the trees.

And whatever came for them, they would be ready—with razor-sharp steel and a healthy dose of brutality.
