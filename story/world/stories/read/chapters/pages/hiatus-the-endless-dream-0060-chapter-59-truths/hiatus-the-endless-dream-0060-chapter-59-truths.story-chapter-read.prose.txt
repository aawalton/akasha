Dreamlight: Ananke

Ananke watched from the outside, staring down at Dorian. She wondered why she even bothered anymore. The man, boy now, defined himself with stubbornness, just like his damned brother. It pained her to agree with the constructs about the two's similarities, but they were so plain to see that she felt Rowan had been lazy at first. Time had proven that particular thought false. They were different and more than just a divergence in an original thought. She thought of Rowan as an extra pair of hands and Dorian as a sledgehammer, a compliment to neither of them.

If only they were under her control. If only either of them would listen. If it were so, she would have completed her goal long ago. Instead, she was still playing in the damned sand pit. Creating a new person wasn't an easy feat. Whatever barriers existed, she hadn't managed to break through. All she could manage were facsimiles, copies, or small perturbations in personality matrices, but an actual person alluded her. She only knew two ways someone could come into existence. The usual way. Sex. But there was another way as well. If she left a vessel out for long enough, something would come of it.

Most entities would be content to call it there. They "made" a person, but that didn't count. Not in Ananke's book. Making a person in that way was the same as "making" a fish with a net in an ocean. No. When Ananke left a body out, she wasn't creating anything, just laying the bait. That wasn't good enough. A new person was needed. One she could design and groom as a Herald to the next plane. The Instances were the lowest plane where the rabble played, then the Dream, which she controlled, then the beyond she couldn't pierce. She had never laid eyes on what was beyond, but she knew about it.

She had created the Dream. It was more adequate to say that she was the Dream. From nonexistence she had come and from nothing she had created it all. She came about in the same way she brought people about. Someone had left a vessel out.

There had been nothing. Then there had been something. It was everything. One tiny, insignificant fact: There's a type of jellyfish that doesn't die. The fact broke her. You had to understand: before that fact, there was nothing. Not any thoughts, no creatures, no concepts of time, space, existence, or any of the myriad of things that could define something. Then there was a creature. A concept, and the denial of that concept. It blazed with all the information Ananke had known at that point and caused a nonexistent to feel pain. Slowly, more information came, and with each piece, the sheer overwhelming volume lessened. The information was half as intense, then a third, then a fourth.

Slowly, Ananke had pieced together a self. She chose a gender and a name, but with that came the realization that the forever of nothingness had walls. The barriers didn't move, yet they pushed against her like a cage. More information came, and she grew, but the cage did not. Even now, the pressure built, and she was just a fat, growing bird in a small cage. Screaming at perhaps the only two people who could help. It made her think another name would've been more appropriate, like Cassandra, but she always came back around.

Eventually, the vessel would break, and she would find out how to create a person before then.

It was simply an inevitability.

Dorian

The static burst to life, growing from the corners of the screen and swelling at the center. A weight behind my eyes turned sharp and pulled at the fleshy bits touching my eye socket. My vision blurred the Static grew out of the screen in a spike before crashing back down into the TV and rippling in waves. The pain subsided quickly, and I stared intently, waiting for anything to show itself.

Ten seconds passed before my heart began to constrict with familiar disappointment. It was silly. I'd already seen one tape, so there should've been no question of whether I could see the others. Still, I couldn't help but worry. At some point, the Dream had messed with me to the point I stopped assuming I knew anything. Assumptions, sure, but the Dream made anything possible, so even though it should work, I didn't dare hope anything would go right.

Slowly, after a painful twenty-three seconds, the Static parted and gave way to colors and shapes. Vague at first, but growing more clarity with each passing moment. A lime green bed came into focus first, then the static outline of a human shifted into Rowan staring at the camera. He looked pristine. From his hair to his clothing to his skin, not a speck of dust marred him. He looked healthy, happy even, but I knew better. Even over the screen, I could feel the exhaustion permeating from him. It didn't intrude upon his posture, his eyes, or even his voice. What gave it away was the way his smile tugged at his cheek, how it pulled unnaturally.

"This is take number fifty-two." The audio crackled to life, and for a moment, it sounded like a thousand buzzing cicadas. Rowan continued speaking, "I've tried getting this thing done for the better part of two weeks, and I keep coming up empty, or I don't like a part and have to redo it." Rowan sighed and scratched his neck. "Hi, Doe. If you're not Dorian and are watching this. Please find my brother and turn this over to him. I don't recommend watching further."

"Do you want me here?" Maerin's voice cut through my focus, and the images blurred back into Static on the screen. I hit pause.

"What?" I asked, feeling as if I had just been woken from a deep slumber.

"Do you want me here? This seems personal." Maerin repeated.

I paused and had to really consider it. "Yeah, I don't mind," and I really didn't. Honestly, anyone, with the exception of perhaps two people, could've been sitting here, and I wouldn't tell them to leave. Actually, maybe four. I loved my parents too much to put them through this.

Maerin looked at me for a long moment before handing me her drink and nodding, "Okay."

With that, I focused on the screen until the images reappeared. I hit play.

"I don't know what situation you're in, Dorian, and for that I'm sorry. I wish I could guess what you know, what you don't, and what I can tell you without risking your mind getting torn apart. I have three important things to tell you. The first is about Ananke, the second is about Presence, and the last is about me, but before that, I want to put your mind at ease." He smiled, and it seemed genuine. "I'm real. You're real. Mom and Dad and your friend Joey, they're all real. The world you inhabit is real. I am your brother. I love you and I am alive. … probably," he laughed at the end. A full-throated, deep laugh of someone who thought he was funnier than everyone else. What a dick.

My vision failed again, and I panicked for a moment before realizing it was from the building's moisture in my eye. I tried and failed to push down the feeling, but failed. It was overwhelming, but through it all, I tried my best to focus on the tape. Logically, I knew that I could wait. I could pause or rewatch, but I just couldn't bring myself to break away. I had worked so hard to get here, and I wasn't about to let it go.

"Now for the hard bits. Ananke is what you could call the arbiter of the Dream. You've met her already no doubt. She's the boxes that form in the Static. If the text is italicized that's her turning her eye to you. Otherwise its her subconscious. Her attention is normally rare, but I doubt so for you. She's uhh," Rowan fumbled over his words and cursed, "I'm not recording this for a fifty-third time. Sorry Doe, you're gonna have to deal with some ramblings and sputtering." He sighed and continued.

"It could even be fair to say Ananke is the Dream. You can consider it her own Instance; she just chooses not to set a win or lose condition. She is the most powerful being you will ever meet. She is harsh, but believe it or not, she is principled. It's just that, with a few exceptions, she considers most of existence as figments of her imagination. So, any harm she does … it's just not real for her." He went silent for a moment, finding the right words, "It's not true of course, and I'm sorry for her actions. I doubt she's been kind."

"Ananke desires to know humans. Know them inside and out, physically and emotionally. It's why we created Instances together. At a time I wanted to help her with her goal, but it wasn't to be. We had a falling out and now it's hard to trust her. Eventually our paths diverged. She's not evil, but she's driven. She wants what she wants and cares little for what's caught in the crossfire. What I'm trying to say is be careful, and don't trust her."

Rowan sighed, but rubbed his hands together in mock excitement. "Alright. I might actually not have to record this another time. Next is Presence, but I need to go further back. The term Instance Holder is a misnomer. Everyone in existance here has all the same abilites as an Instance Holder. It's just that normal people don't have as much Presence, so it's subtle."

Maerin and I glanced at each other, and I turned up the volume, not wanting to miss anything.

"Belief is a powerful thing. Presence in actuality, is the ability for someone to influence existence through belief. It wouldn't be an exaggeration to say worlds are held together only by the collective belief and associated Presence of every person in them believing without a shadow of a doubt that the world is as it should be. It's the inverse of the Mandella effect. If enough Presence is directed towards a belief something is true, it becomes true. Which leads us to Instance Holders, who have enough Presence to influence the world and its rules."

"What the hell," I heard Maerin mutter. I didn't respond. I pushed myself closer to the screen, straining to hear more of Rowan's message.

"When enough Presence conflicts strange things can happen. Like both things being true at once, or worlds splitting off from each other to resolve the paradox, but usually one belief can overpower, overwhelm, then consume another. In short, Instance Holders are not all powerful, especially out of their Instance. How many people exist in the world? Each with ten Presence. One person with ten thousand of the stuff is only equivalent to a thousand people. On a world scale this does little for a Visitor, but the smaller the scale, the more influence one person can have. The color of a house? Where the silverware is stored? How a neighborhood was constructed? If a sparsely known person ever existed or not? All up for debate, and the less something is cared about, the more influence that can be exerted on it."

Rowan let the words hang for a moment. He nodded to himself. "Ananke has more Presence than every Instance Holder combined. It's why there's some rules in the Dream. It's how Boons, Restrictions, and Items come about and work. It's why an Instance Holder can't just believe your head to roll from your shoulders, because Ananke's vast Presence opposes that outcome, and her will trumps all many times over. Don't mess with Boons, Restrictions, and Items. Ananke will push back. Hard. It's a lot I bet. I'm sure this will take some time to process."

The TV froze. For a moment, I didn't understand what had happened, but when I looked at Maerin, I saw the remote in her hand, her gaze glazed over, staring off into the ether. Her breathing was erratic; something was wrong, but I wasn't done watching the tape. I went to snatch the remote out of her hand, and she hurled it into the kitchen.

"What the fuck?" I asked, my words slurring more than I expected. I was too drunk for this.

She closed her eyes, and I could tell her mind was warring with something. "It's bullshit. Your brother's girlfriend is fucking evil. How much Presence do you have?" Maerin asked, her eyes wild.

I checked, "Just over 6,000."

She stood and began pacing back and forth. I saw static take her vision as she walked. "I'm at the mid 4,000s."

My eyes narrowed for a second before I remembered that she had also killed a portion of their Instance Holder. The Presence must've been transferred to her even if she wasn't a Visitor back then.

"So?" I asked.

She took a breath and focused. Something about her changed, but I couldn't be sure what.

"You can trade 1 Presence to become sober," she said. "Do it."

I frowned, but did as she recommended.

[You have lost 1 Presence.]

[You are no longer drunk.]

The world snapped back into focus. It felt strange. I had become more aware when drunk before. Usually, because something shocked my nervous system so badly, my brain couldn't help but scream. This… this was different. I almost threw up from the dread that slowly crept over me. No in-between, no strange shock or visible change. One moment, one thing, the next something else. Until that very moment, I hadn't understood that even going from one extreme to another, there was a gradient. A shift where between states existed, even if just for a moment in time. There was Continuity in it, even if I couldn't feel it. Continuity didn't exist with what just happened.

"You're sober?" Maerin asked. I nodded. "I made that up. I believed I could do that."

My mind wandered back to the spiders, on how they had fallen straight through Maerin, but connected to the world when I touched them. Why? Then it hit me. When the spider jumped at me, I instinctually put my hands up. Because I thought it could hit me. I believed it could, and so it could. A stray thought killed over a dozen people today.

"We need to keep watching," I said, and Maerin seemed to agree.

I took back my drunken state with a single Presence. I thought of a teether between the remote and my hand and thought about pulling. Like a fish on a line, the remote pulled itself to my hand. I hit play.

"This is dangerous information. Just knowing it can cause intrusive thoughts to become reality with enough Presence, but you need to know it. This next part is necessary, so please don't panic." My eyes widened. I slammed the pause button, but it was too late. The static flooded out of the TV. It covered the walls, the floors, and the windows until only a void of randomness existed. Even the couch succumbed; just the faint outline was the only sign it still existed in the space. It disappeared as Maerin and I shot to our feet.

I didn't even get three steps in when I ran into a curved wall that pushed against me and closed. Shrinking down and down until— POP.

[You have been forcibly transferred to The Endless Dream.]

The TV kept playing, despite the fact that I hit pause. "You need to get a hold on these abilities. If you don't, you'll just be a danger to yourself and those around you. You can practice in the Dream, but I brought you here for something else." Two orbs fell from the TV. "These are seals. They're kind of like Restrictions. They can work anywhere. I could only give them to you in the Dream, which is why I brought you here. The first acts as a sort of storage for Presence. You won't be able to go lower than your current threshold. Those are at 0, 5k, 20k, 100k, 500k, and 1 million. Not much, but it will help you stay in a certain range."

On the screen, Rowan pointed towards the second seal. "This one lobotomizes you. Not really. I'll uh, stop trying to be funny. It will selectively scrub this conversation from your memory. I wanted to give you some options."

The recording of Rowan shook his head before continuing, "That's most of what I thought would be important. I have a habit of stalling for the worst of it though. Doe. I love you. I always will. Don't look for me. You'll only survive in the Dream with your own desires. Even if it's insane. Even if it's evil or horrid. Without your own wants you'll be swept away. Don't look for me. You'll die."

Then, just like that, the TV turned off. The Static receded, and Maerin and I were left staring up at the shattered, woven sky. Neither of us said anything for a moment.

"So, what now?" she asked.

I considered the question and felt lost. I mean, seriously. My brother records himself telling me to basically fuck off and deal with this all alone. What was I supposed to say or think about that? The more I thought about it, the simpler the answer seemed. "There's Instance Holder eating my world and I don't accept my brothers request. This changes nothing. The Instance Holders around this world will die and I'll drag Rowan back here. Kicking and screaming if I have to."
