The crops were coming along nicely, and fields had spread across the greenhouses. Wheat, rice, and various root vegetables were sectioned out and had already grown several inches.

Still, they were some time from harvest were issues with their food production. Hunting had grown scarce quickly, and the game was escaping deeper into the tunnels. The hunters were ordered not to follow the giant worms unless food became a dire issue.

It wasn’t just the possibility of losing skilled hunters to the depths of the tunnels, but what trouble they might stir within those tunnels.

Luckily, fishing had proven bountiful. Buckets of fish and crustaceans were brought into the settlement every day. Fishing wouldn’t sustain the population alone, but it was enough to greatly subsidize their rations. And what they had lost from the poor hunting results was at least temporarily relieved by the huge carrion wyrm corpses.

Elsewhere, the huge mushrooms provided good starter fuel for fires but didn’t burn long. Carmack knew they needed a replacement. They likely would have already lost people to the cold if not for the warm air traveling into the chamber from below. But now they were struggling to find fuel for cooking, let alone anything else, and Carmack wanted to get the city’s industry going again, which meant fire for forging and other crafting uses.

Sitting in his room, Carmack meditated, sending his mana in search of resources. The spire had been rather easy to find; its size and myriad characteristics required for raising a settlement made it stand out like a beacon, his mana easily directing him to it. But searching for veins of minerals was not such a straightforward task.

However, in time, he found what he was looking for. He located many minerals close to the spire, such as copper, gold, iron, lead, zinc, silver, coal, and most importantly, athridium.

Athridium, as it was spoken on Avalock, was a rare and valuable metal that conducted magical energy. Apparently, none had ever heard the word athridium here, on Kaedon, but finally, he pulled the information he needed free from their lips. Most here simply referred to athridium as simply magic ore or, as the scholars called it by the metal’s dwarven name, minithrel.

Carmack sensed the minithrel deeper than the other ores, hidden in a chamber far below.

For now, he put aside thoughts of rare magical metals and concentrated on what they needed. As much as the treasured metal would aid them, he needed to get the craftsmen working, which meant acquiring the fundamentals.

He could sense that a few of the richer ore veins were nearby and connecting tunnels, and surmised that with a few additional walls, they could be secured for exploitation.

The job took several days to complete, but by the end, he had built three additional bridges connecting the spire to sections of the cavern wall that adjoined the desired tunnels. From there, he located valuable mineral veins and erected walls to protect miners.

Once the most accessible metal was mined, the mines would require redevelopment to reach deeper metals. But in their current state, they needed to be cautious. So, Carmack parceled off small sections for mining, closing them off to the deeper sections of the tunnels.

While there had been mines around Lydrus, there hadn't been any within the city itself, so very few miners escaped into the underground cavern when it fell. However, plenty of laborers and other craftsmen were currently out of work and could fill roles within the mines until more suitable work became available.

Coal, metals for tools and furniture, handles and latches, etc., were prioritized—coal being the most important. Metal wasn’t much good without the forges to work it in, and there were many other needs requiring reliable access to fire.

Several small mining camps were setup in quick order. Their harvest was limited, but it was enough to get primoritized forges and furnaces operational.

With the fuel situation mostly solved, the fear of being forced to eat raw meat was also alleviated, giving Carmack time to focus on other issues.

Namely, their flawed security.

Carmack had relied on dirty tactics to trick the dragon. While he was not bothered morally—his conviction to save the settlement was entirely justified—he knew such tactics couldn’t be relied on in every situation. They needed a military strong enough to defend themselves.

For his part, Carmack was leveling whenever he had time. But he couldn’t rely solely on himself, even if he did possess great advantages below ground. There was effectively a small city under his control, and he needed to empower the people to defend themselves. The three schools of magic—Spell Scribing, Witchery, and the Holy Power of Lydria could manage their own affairs. But the rest of the city left much to be desired.

The citizenry was essentially defenseless, and the guard had been trained to deal with common thugs, thieves, and petty monsters like goblins. Carmack wasn’t a combat trainer, though. The young commander, Gorn, impressed him, but he lacked the experience and accolades to resolve these issues. He knew that outside assistance would be needed if the guard were going to rise to the job of defending their settlement down here in the Undercavern.

They won’t be hunting goblins down here. We need to get serious, or these men will get themselves killed sooner or later.

Calling Managra, Carmack turned to the fallen city’s High Priest for advice on how they might recruit help.

“Hmm, troublesome indeed,” Managra scratched his chin from the court throne. “The truth is, most of our greatest warriors fell with the city. As far as we know, not just the guard commander but the entire Knight’s Order. They were Lydrus’ true military power. Even the warlocks lost their leader and entire inner circle.”

“Huh? I thought Madrix was–”

“That pipsqueak?” Managra chuckled. “No, Madrix was just some meddling middle-tier warlock before the fall of Lydrus. He was ordered to take command of the apprentices and help watch over the civilians as the heart of the warlock order assisted in protecting the walls and gates against our invaders. Sadly, they are likely all dead now.”

“I see. So, the College of Warlocks is now just a bunch of apprentices?”

It made sense why Carmack hadn’t sensed much power in the warlock. He had thought that perhaps the city lacked strong magical talents, but they seemed like they had once been more capable than he realized.

“Exactly. Still, even novices they’re a valuable asset. But be wary of their political ambitions. The warlocks have always had a hand in Lyrdus’ politics.”

“Understood. Perhaps we should look beyond those the government employed to fix our problem.”

“Hmm, interesting proposition,” Managra nodded thoughtfully. “There is someone who comes to mind. But I’m not sure if they fit our needs.”

“And who would that be?”

The Adventurer’s Guild was a catch-all, combat-oriented guild that provided manpower for coin. Anything from clearing out a den of goblins to forming a mercenary battalion to fight alongside an army was within their purview, and they had long held a presence in Lydrus.

Gathered in the court, Carmack and Managra waited for the guild representative after being summoned.

The guardsmen stepped aside as the no-nonsense woman strolled into the chamber. Aphexia wore a permanent scowl on her face and a scar across her left eye, but was otherwise quite attractive, with big eyes and thick brows. Her hair was cut short, and she was more muscled than most men. She was covered in patchy plate armor that covered her right arm, shoulders, and legs like scales, but left much of her toned flesh exposed like a Colosseum gladiator.

“Lord priest,” she bowed. “Hero of Lydrus.”

Carmack could sense the powerful aura radiating from Aphexia. He couldn’t be sure who would come out on top in a real fight, but he knew she was undoubtedly a higher level and more powerful, at least on paper.

“Thank you for coming so soon,” Managra said. “It is always a delight to receive the guild.”

“Guild, hmph,” she chuckled and looked around. “Where are they?”

Well, she’s got character at least.

“Perhaps they’re invisible. Anyway, it’s not like there’s much else to do around here,” Aphexia shrugged. “What do you need?”

“Bored are you? Hah,” Managra chuckled. “I wish I shared such a predicament.”

Stepping forward, Carmack scrutinized the adventurer with an unyielding gaze. “Managra tells me that you were a senior member of the guild before Lydrus fell. Is that correct?”

“Senior? If the Lord Priest says so. Well, I was, or am, a gold-ranked adventurer. I suppose that’s kind of a big deal in a backwater city like Lydrus. Why do you ask?”

“How are you finding employment since the city’s fall?”

“What do you think?”

“Right, well, we have many jobs around here and few with the skills to do them. You must be able to find some work.”

“Ha, the hero is a funny one. Do I look like a farmer or a fisherman?”

“No, I can’t say you do. We were hoping to revive the Lydrus Adventurer’s Guild, and I even have a contract on my mind.”

“Reopen the guild? You’re aware I’m the guild’s only surviving member? You know, one person isn’t much of a guild.”

“I’m aware. What about the contract would you be interested in, Aphexia?”

“Contract? What does it entail?”“Training. Training Lydrus’ Guard, to be precise.”

“Don’t they already have people for that?”

“Indeed, they did. But we’re making do with what we have now. Besides, the guard was never designed to be the city’s main fighting force. And since we don’t have anything else to replace it, that is going to have to change.”

“Fine, if it pays, I’m in,” Aphexia shrugged. It wasn’t like she had a lot of options. Returning to the surface alone was too dangerous, which left her shoveling rocks or hunting worms to earn her rations.

“Good. Now, it seems the guild is operational again,” Carmack grinned.

“Right,” Aphexia rolled her eyes. “Whatever gets you off.”

“Oh, I’m quite serious. In fact, I was hoping you could be our guild master, at least until we have some stability around here.”

“Look, I don't want to burst your bubble, but that decision is about me. I’m just an adventurer. And besides, I don’t think that lacking a guild master is your problem. Have you looked around recently? We’re kinda underground. How do you plan for adventurers to even get here?”

“Oh, through caravans and trade routes,” Carmack raised a finger as if it was obvious.

“Are they hiding under a rock?”

“Baha,” Carmack chuckled. “She’s hilarious. But no, I intend to secure trade routes with surface cities once the guard has been strengthened and the city is suitably secure. I have already found an exit leading up to the surface. It is, however, some distance away and no doubt dangerous.”

A little feisty, but I do like her.

“An exit to the surface?” Aphexia’s eyes widened.

“Indeed.”

“Can’t we just leave then?”

“And go where? The kingdoms above guard their territory jealously, and the journey is perilous. We have everything we need here; we just need to develop it.”

“If you say so,” Aphexia sighed. “Fine, call me whatever you like and make sure I get paid on time. But don’t hold me accountable for whatever happens. ”

“Oh, and why do you say that?”

“Look around, big guy. We’re a few thousand refugees hiding away in a cavern and trying to survive on scraps. Why in the world would the guild risk the investment of starting an outpost here? And who would come? Usually, the cost of a guild contract is directly related to the danger involved in completing it. Excuse my elvish, but we've already dealt with a fucking dragon, and we’ve barely lived down here for a month. So, unless you've got giant chests filled with gold hidden somewhere around here, I doubt you're convincing any adventurers to relocate. Even now, I plan to get the fuck out the moment I can. And I actually liked Lydrus. Spent four years living in the city; that's a record for me, including my childhood.”

She seems honest. I already told her I found a way out. She could have just nodded and used her position to escape to the surface.

“Understood,” Carmack nodded thoughtfully. “It is quite the ask. But I have faith in what we’re building here. Should things go to plan, I believe we’ll have much to offer enterprising young adventurers.”

Carmack had already spotted enough potential revenue streams around their new settlement. While none had been developed yet, he saw it as only a matter of time.

“I don’t think you understand. It’s bloody dangerous down here. I felt the mana of that thing the dragon killed, and it sent shivers down my spine. Look, gold rank might not mean that much in a big city, but we’re still talking about elites. These people expect to get paid well, and I don’t think there’s much room for anybody below that level down here. Maybe there are a few contracts you could offer Iron Ranks, but that’s about it. Now tell me, how are you going to convince a bunch of elites to move to a little town filled with refugees?”

“Hmph, good point,” Carmack grunted. “I suppose I’ll have to make the contracts worth their while.”

“Stop making it sound so easy! If that’s all it took, every city would be filled with Gold Rank and above adventurers.”

“I’ll keep that in mind. Here, take this,” Carmack passed her the scroll. “It’s our contract.”

“Do you listen to anything?” Aphexia shook her head and took the scroll. Unravelling it and scanning over it, her face scrunched up.

“What is this?”

“Our contract, like I said,” Carmack smiled.

“You’re paying me in rations?” Aphexia reddened.

“For now,” Carmack corrected with a raised index finger. “If you keep reading, you’ll see that I have offered much more.”

“You never mentioned any of this.” Aphexia’s brow twitched. “Take the reins of the Lydrus Adventurer’s Guild and recruit members? Did you listen to anything I just said? There’s no way you will recruit people who can survive down here. It’s impossible. It doesn’t matter what you offer me if I can’t complete the task you set for me!”

“I see; I suppose there is no way around it. I guess we’ll have to find an alternative. Excuse me, though, what is that gemstone encrusted into your belt buckle?”

“This?” Aphexia said, gripping the emerald buckle between her hands. “A greater emerald. Why?” She narrowed her gaze furiously on Carmack, clearly uncomfortable with her treasured belt being pointed out. From her expression alone, Carmack could tell she had fought a battle or two over it during her life.

“Yes, I can tell that it’s an emerald. My knowledge of gemstones is actually quite proficient. But I meant the enchantment. What does it hold?”

“You looking for a fight, hero? I don’t care who you are; I’m not going to just roll over and let you rob me.”

“Pardon me. I intended nothing of the sort,’ Carmack waved dismissively. “I was just curious about the powers it held.”

“Body enhancement. Strength, durability, and a nice little healing perk. Saved me more than once, and it’ll save me again,” she gritted her teeth, unconvinced of Carmack's sincerity.

“Ah, I see. Invaluable, no doubt. I wonder, though, would you still be a Gold Rank without it?”

“Excuse me?”

“Pardon me if that came out insulting. I just meant to say such a valuable item must help quite a bit when adventuring.”

“It does,” Aphexia hissed.

“Say, what if Lydrus were to offer similar artifacts tied to positions here in the city? Positions such as guild master and other senior posts within the guild?”

“Huh?”

“I mean, do you think that might persuade some adventurers to call our little hole in the ground home? And perhaps make them strong enough to survive what is down here.”

“And what's to stop them from stealing your items and running back to the surface?”

“Curses,” Carmack said matter-of-factly. “I’m told the warlocks are actually quite good at them.”

“Heinous,” Aphexia scrunched up her nose. “You think adventurers would agree to that for some trinkets?”

“No, I doubt that. But life-changing artifacts that possess the ability to change their fortunes? I dunno, maybe?” Carmack shrugged.

“And you’re telling me you’ve got artifacts like that?”

“Not yet,” Carmack admitted, pulling the monster core from his robes. “I have, however, already procured this and will have it turned into an enchantment once I procure a suitable gemstone. And this is only the start. This world's Undercavern is teeming with powerful monsters. I'm sure I'll collect more in a short time.”

Aphexia’s outraged expression softened a little. It was obvious she sensed how powerful the core was.

“Okay, fine, you've got a plan that might work,” she crossed her arms. “Still, it's a long way to travel, and I don’t see any wagons heading out.”

“I intend to deal with that problem. For now, tell me, Aphexia, will you take the contract?”

“Are you planning to give me the item you craft from that core?”

“I intend to do everything I stated in that contract.”

Apheix sighed, weighing over her choices. There were two things warlocks were unmatched at: summoning demons and writing curse contracts. If she agreed to the terms and conditions, there would be no means to break the curse bestowed on her if she were to break the contract.

Her eyes glazed over as she looked into the powerful monster core held like a marble between Carmack’s fingers. It was undoubtedly more powerful than the core used to make her belt. If a suitable gemstone were found, it would certainly make for a more powerful item.

Aphexia’s brow bent amusingly.

Carmack wondered what was so special about this woman he liked and respected. Was it that she was trusting? No. Something else. Ahh, it’s pride, isn’t it? Aphexia had pride, but not the self-absorbed kind. She possessed the kind of pride that came with honor. If this woman accepted a contract, she would see it through for her name's sake.

“How about this: same contract, no curse.”

“Huh? You don’t even know me. Why would you trust me?”

Carmack smiled in amusement. “You wouldn’t break a contract, now, would you? Not when you agreed to the conditions set out. Such a dishonorable act is beneath you, isn’t it, Aphexia?”

Aphexia’s eyes widened. She had always worn her roguish facade; it was good for business. But honor and pride had always been core to her tenets. Unfortunately, those traits could be taken advantage of, so she did her best to hide them. But this man had seen straight through it.

“It's a good thing, really. It makes me want you as our guild master that much more.”

“What are you going to make me?” Aphexia's gaze narrowed.

“Oh, the magical item? I’ll have made whatever you wish.”
