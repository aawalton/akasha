Sally held up three fingers. “High-fantasy Othea, Sci-fi Othea, and what I like to call Grimdark Othea.” She flexed the extended digits. “Earth is a little more difficult to assign different genres. Based on word-of-mouth intelligence, one we can designate as Supernatural Earth. One where hell and other phenomena exist.”

Theo nodded briefly and adjusted his position in the chair. “That’s not the one we’re from.”

“Correct. Other than our world having fewer ‘travelers’, there isn’t much solid evidence it differs from the other Earth.” The zombie paused slightly and lowered her fingers. “Nadine, our Sea-scryer, says there’s the shape of a shadow over one of these worlds, but that doesn’t tell us anything.”

“Then there’s the Sea itself, containing every System-world.” The vampire rubbed the bridge of his nose. “Plus the new dimension I apparently found, which we are calling The Beyond?”

“You can think of it as a three-by-three grid, with the STAR Gateway in the center.” With a wave of her hand, a screen appeared.

[Supernatural Earth] [The Beyond] [Sci-fi Othea][Contemporary Earth] [STAR Gateway] [High Fantasy Othea][Shadowed Earth] [The Sea] [Grimdark Othea]

“This isn’t super important for our meeting right now, but you missed out on a lot of exposition when you were off ignoring due process.”

Theo gave the zombie a tired smile. “Remind me not to use my Avatar right before needing to have an active brain.”

“Eh.” Sally gave him a pat on the leg. “You pushed Ren into getting her Ultimate, and scared most of the audience half to death. Energy well expended.”

The circular meeting room in the tower had never been so busy. Chuck had considered finding a larger venue, but there was nowhere more secure and central than this room. It was iconic, in a way. A digitized display hovered in the middle of the table, displaying the gathered group, as if they didn’t know each other already.

[Architect: Chuck][Outsiders: Archie, Sally, Theo, Max, Ren][Guardians: Dent, Wolf, Norah, Edward, Chapman][Sanctuary’s Bastion: Humphrey][Logistics & Welfare: Lana, Jackie, Lucius]

This was the designated Council to save the world. Of them, only Chapman was someone Sally knew only in passing. A proficient caster from one of the newer areas of Sanctuary. Other than that, it was a veritable who’s-who of the most seasoned or dedicated people the world had to offer.

Not exactly an army, but there were thousands of combat-worthy individuals out in the world who would lend their strength when the invasion arrived. Thanks to the STAR+ even the monsters and bad actors within Sanctuary would fight back against the threat, rather than being idle.

After a brief low conversation with Dent, Chuck stood up from his chair and tapped the table to call everyone to attention.

“I’ll try to keep this brief and concise,” he began. “The amount of… personality in this room is suboptimal. We are nearing the time that the entire world will be plunged into war. Sanctuary is no stranger to dire times, but this is unprecedented. The events of the last two months have bolstered our chances by leaps and bounds. Whether through new allies, reclaimed power, or our new STAR+. But, I want to make some things clear.”

He gestured toward the hologram in the middle of the table, and it switched to a rotating globe.

“I am not a god. My position as Sanctuary’s janitor has been further solidified by the implementation of the new System. Were I to involve myself in the fighting side of the war, I’d make as much difference as any of you here. Just like before, I am unable to increase your power, grant you overpowered items, or alter reality to make this a walk in the park.” Chuck sighed as he looked around the room. “That is not to say I haven’t tipped the scales, of course.”

Sally had her brow furrowed in concentration, her lips pursed so that she didn’t interject. The Architect, done with his introduction, then went on to detail several key points about the invasion.

The first revolved around letting the Radochs onto the planet. Originally, the plan was to prevent them from piercing Sanctuary’s bubble and have the fleet in ‘orbit’ to work out how to invade by themselves. The issue there was that the potential methods the aliens might employ could be devastating for the world. It was much better to leave the front door open rather than allow them to break it down.

They could slow how many spaceships entered the atmosphere, but inviting them in put the Radochs at risk as well. The vessels could be destroyed or boarded.

“Our anti-air capabilities only cover around fifteen percent of our skies,” Dent added. “Due to our recent increase in our landmass. Of that, probably half is sustainable long-term, or has the strength to bring down ships repeatedly.”

Not great odds, but it put a dent in the invading force.

Chuck gestured, and the turning Sanctuary image swapped to a model of a Radoch spaceship. “To our benefit, the aliens are here to steal our former respawn technology. As they have no idea how this technology is represented, they are unlikely to want to destroy Sanctuary outright. The Radochs are collectors at heart, and may deem everything as assets to steal… until they learn the truth, at least.”

Resources would be stretched thin. Even with unlimited teleportation, it would be difficult to patrol and guard the entire world.

While Sally was deep in thought, Archie stood to address the room.

“Based on our brief experience against the Radoch, they have a particular way of invasion. First, a mass of weaker troops to soften up resistance and control cities and important hubs of the world. Then they send more specialized and elite, but smaller, squads to apprehend targets or collect what they deem important.” He nodded at the hologram in the center of the table. “The leaders and most powerful tend to remain on their ships, unless forced to act.”

After shooting the Architect a polite smile, he sat again and allowed Chuck to take over again.

“Bearing this in mind, our current plan is to restrict the amount of power that meets the populace. We’ve identified seven areas around Sanctuary where our armies of adventurers will gather. Half of Sanctuary’s Bastion will bolster these groups, while the other half will patrol back and forth, providing support where needed.”

Chuck exhaled through his nose and looked over to Sally. “The Outsiders will be the boarding party. The more vessels they can bring down or hobble before the second phase begins, the better. I… hesitate to bring this up so bluntly, but there isn’t such a thing as a war crime when it comes to what methods the Outsiders use. Winning is our only concern.”

While a grim statement, there wasn’t anyone in the room who displayed concern over the directive. It was, after all, a war for their survival. A lone Radoch had managed to kidnap several members of the former Outsiders. They wouldn’t pull any punches against a much larger threat.

A dry smile crossed Chuck’s face. “You may all be wondering about the locked gift boxes everyone received during the STAR+ global holiday. It was the only way the System let me give someone out, with certain caveats. Once the Radochs arrive, they will open. Inside are several potions, a necklace, a set meal, and several party favors… but most importantly, a single-use self-resurrection idol that will take you to the staging area of the final celebratory event of the holiday period.”

“Ah.” Edward rolled his eyes. “I wondered what you were doing sniffing around down in Hell.”

“It’s the most out of sight place on Sanctuary.” The Architect seemed pretty pleased with himself for one-upping the System, if not exhausted from the act. “It will give everyone a second chance to make their mark in the battle, and demonic portals will allow travel anywhere necessary. Resurrection beyond this will be sparse and not guaranteed.”

Sally raised her hand slightly. “Technically, if only a single person survives this, it will be a win?”

“While unrealistic, I suppose so.” Chuck tilted his head and shrugged. “Assuming no structural damage to the System, every soul is stored here and could be brought back with enough time.”

She returned to her thoughts as the meeting progressed into a more detailed account of supply lines, advantageous terrain, battlefield tactics, and other things that would surely slide right off her brain even if she were paying attention. Sanctuary had little need for resurrection scrolls or other items with the previous STAR. She hadn’t found any since the change, as she had been trying to learn a spell herself - to no avail.

If she pointed Theo toward farming them, it would only end up with them handing them out to others. Maybe. The Outsiders were the more powerful and capable, so bringing them back to life could be more impactful, but they were the ones least likely to die as well. Something to discuss later, perhaps. After several years, her Inventory was quite a mess of items - some more useful than others.

Normally, she’d have plenty of confidence about the looming battle. After seeing the odd technology and resourcefulness of the aliens, she knew it wouldn’t be a walk in the park. Even if the defenders’ win was decisive, it would leave a deep scar on Sanctuary. Despite her lingering concerns, it was probably for the best that Chuck had taken away immortality.

Not only to make them less of a target, but to ensure their mortal enemies remained just that.

“Plans after this?” Theo asked her under his breath, while Lana went on about the distribution of resources.

“More abilities?” It was a reasonable way to improve their power. Sanctuary didn’t really have any items they could want, although… “Or sifting through storage.”

The grimace on the vampire’s face was enough of an answer, but she saw the merit in it. There were possibly hundreds of items she’d stored away in the bank over the years. Who knew how useful some of them could be? Or if they’d changed on the switch to the STAR+. It wasn’t the most exciting job, but a productive way to spend their free time.

Archie told the Outsiders that he wanted to meet them after this talk, as he had something for them. She was hoping for pancakes, but it was probably related to the war. Shame.

His eyes snapped back to the Architect as he paused mid-sentence. The color drained from his face right before his human flickered and faded away, leaving him as a blue wireframe man once more.

Dent stood from his chair and put a hand on the hilt of his sword. “What is it?”

“The Radochs.” His eyes looked up and to the side, unfocused. “They’re now within a distance where I can track them. There’s… a lot more than I expected.”
