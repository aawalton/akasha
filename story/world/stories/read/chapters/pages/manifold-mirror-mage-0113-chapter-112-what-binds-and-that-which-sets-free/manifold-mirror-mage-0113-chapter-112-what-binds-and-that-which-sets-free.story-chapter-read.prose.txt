As the door closed behind Minette, Gio allowed his domain to fall. This also allowed the strange moth from the back of the door to begin fluttering lazily away into the tapestries.

The strange creature trailed dense streams of knowledge-aspected mana, which emitted a scent of mildew and damp pages. Gio was enraptured by the hypnotic, deep purple patterns on its black wings and the shimmering dust-scales that shook free during its flight, momentarily forgetting himself and where he was.

However, the moth did not make it very far before a titanic stone tongue lashed out from the statue of a frog, grabbing the insect from the air and brutally crushing it between its moss-covered lips. A trickle of water ever-presently bubbled from the frog’s head, like the centerpiece of a decorative fountain.

Gio stood still in shocked silence under the gaze of the massive statue, suddenly very aware of the panopticon of statues that surrounded the door, each likely representing a different manifestation of the spirits of the school.

Among the court of spirits, Gio caught sight of the familiar wooden form of the Owl, Librarian of the stacks. He also spotted a figurine of a salamander with two pinprick flames for eyes. The lizard’s back held hundreds of lit candles, each dripping a steady stream of wax upon the warm rocks it sat on. There was a great butterfly wrought of stained glass that shone with an inner light, as well as an elephant that appeared to be carved out of a living tree.

But another statue drew his attention most of all. Because it was rendered in bone-white ivory, it took a moment for Gio to make sense of the shape. He came to realize that it was a highly detailed carving of a bisected whale.

The gruesome statue was cut straight down the middle, with markings drawn to annotate the function and labeling of its internal organs. It reminded Gio of the anatomy dioramas present in his preparatory school’s health class in a highly unsettling way. As he traced the organs with his eyes, the cleaved halves of its bone-white heart pulsed.

Is that… the new Vivisection?

Gio thought of the horrible creature from Baphelus’s vault that had previously worn that name. He briefly thought of the forbidden book and the temptation of awful knowledge. He shivered.

A stone-cold hand placed gently on his shoulder shook Gio out of his stupor. Gio turned around to face Saint Vaught.

“A touching display… but I will have to ask you to return to your seat now, Mister deGloria.” Saint Vaught solemnly said.

“Um… yes, I-I’m sorry, sir. I mean, sir… uh… Saint… sir….” Gio muttered, suddenly aware of the weight of the entire room staring at him.

Gio quickly hurried back to his seat. He was met with Papyrus and Vito blocking his path, with complicated expressions on their faces. Vito especially looked like he was fighting back some intense emotions. Chandrika stood behind the boys, wearing a perplexed expression.

“What happened?” Vito quietly demanded. “What did you say to her?”

Gio understood why Papyrus and Vito would be upset. He was also upset and didn’t enjoy the emotions that Vito was projecting in his direction. He tried his hardest to keep an edge from his voice, mostly succeeding.

“I… tried to relate to her. I wanted to stop her, but she had already made up her mind.” Gio replied.

Before Vito could respond, Papyrus put a hand on his shoulder.

“Thank you, Gio,” Papyrus interjected. “For even trying. Minette often spoke of the similarities in your situations… and I think that your words may have meant more to her than ours in the moment.”

Vito wrestled with something internally, grasping a bracelet on his hand like he was trying to rub it off.

“Sorry,” Vito apologized, “I just feel so…”

“Helpless.” Gio finished.

Vito and Papyrus both nodded.

“All we can do is hope that she succeeds. For what it’s worth, despite the dangers… I think she knows what she’s doing more than we do.” Gio said.

“I hope that you’re right. She was supposed to graduate with us. We only got a few months together, but she feels like family to me.” Vito replied.

Vito’s words punctured Gio like a sharp knife.

Chandrika wordlessly placed a hand on Gio’s, offering a reassuring squeeze.

He didn’t have anything that felt worthy to say in return to Vito, offering a nod and a wan smile as the four students made their way back to their seats, not wanting to further test the patience of the ongoing ceremony.

Saint Vaught captured the attention of the room with a flicker of magic. A singular star-like mote of magenta-tinged mana emanated from his cupped palms, floating up to the rafters and hovering there. Gio did not immediately recognize the color of the mana.

Gravity, perhaps?

The small ritual was not something that Gio was intimately familiar with, but when the other professors followed suit, Gio pieced together the significance. Already, some of the other students had sent out their own motes to accompany the professors. A rainbow of different lights swam in the air above, each held aloft by some invisible force.

This was a Mage’s Salute. A small, magically insignificant gesture of unity. Gio sent up a gleaming silver mote that reflected the light of the others.

A shaking Vito bitterly threw out a black star that instead threatened to consume all light around it. He then began to hastily head for the exit of the room, clearly having no desire to stay for the rest of the examination.

Papyrus followed Vito, but not before offering up two motes. One, a beige star that appeared almost as if it were a paper cutout with crinkled edges. The other… was a tiny green thing that smelled like pollen and greenery.

Saint Vaught collected the green mote, nodding to Papyrus. Vaught’s dry voice sounded out to break the solemn silence.

“In accordance with the old rules, Minette Pedini has chosen to take on the yoke of challenge bearing nothing, sparing the sweat of her own brow. We, who collect under the auspices and shelters of the pacts of heritage, may in return offer her nothing, spare our hopes and well-wishes. She shall bask in the light of her successes alone, and thusly, too, the loneliness of her singular defeats. May this message reach her ears in a time of safety. We salute you, Minette. Though you shall never return to us wearing the mantle of student, perhaps if fate is kind, she shall queerly return you to us in another guise. Go forth and raise your sword.”

The stone-skinned Saint cast forth the green star that represented Minette, and all others followed it as it passed through the carved door's wood, carrying the message into the mysterious depths of the school. Only the spirits would know if the message ever reached her.

Gio sat among classmates whom he didn’t know particularly well, dispassionately watching the domains on display. He occasionally thought of Minette. Mostly, his eyes glazed over in a state of numbness, and the peace of gentle dissociation.

Chandrika had left shortly after testing resumed, citing a desire to contact her grandmother and get some rest before their evening dinner plans after the exam. Jean would later inform Gio that he tried to make it back to the testing room, but was turned away at the door by a stodgy inquisitor who shamed him for ‘not taking the examination seriously enough- this isn’t some social event.’

In the coming days, Gio would struggle to recall why he didn’t just leave with Chandrika. He had no reason to stay in the stands. All of his close friends had passed the examination, with the glaring exception of Minette’s journey into the depths of the dungeon.

In his fugue state, Gio couldn’t seem to focus on anything.

Abigail Kosmas, a girl from Gio’s class that he had previously had harsh words with, coincidentally, over the subject of Gio’s misunderstanding with Minette, had just finished displaying a rather striking display of light with her domain. The tall girl’s imperious features sneered derisively in Gio’s direction with pride as she soaked in the applause of her admirers and stoked her internal, one-sided rivalry with the object of her infuriation, and indeed, repressed infatuation.

But, unfortunately for her, Gio’s eyes focused on something else entirely.

Like a strand of spiderweb illuminated by afternoon light, a strand of gold rode the waves of invisible air, trailing off into obscurity. Gio followed the strand out of his reverie.

The strand landed on a boy approaching the professors. Gio recognized the boy… a little. He was an athletic young man of average height, with a model-worthy face, and a fashionable yet ‘low effort’ hairstyle that would not look out of place on a mannequin in some middle-of-the-road boutique that aimed to please customers of all demographics. The young man wore a beaded turquoise smock of some kind over his uniform and had an intense expression on his face as he approached the stand.

Gio had seen him around the school a few times, actually. On the first day that he’d set foot into the Green Expanse at the beginning of the semester, Gio somehow vividly recalled watching this student blast himself in the face with water.

That was the same day that I watched Jean fly for the first time. I think that I almost introduced myself to both of them that day. I wonder how different this year would have gone if I had met Jean first instead of Chandrika. Would they have started talking again? Would… would Rika’s Sage skill still have warned us about the dragon-lich?

"Mister Sterling Sundleah," Professor Cazlitine announced. "You may proceed."

The blue-garbed student seemed to look elsewhere.

Gio absent-mindedly tracked the flight of yet another golden strand.

Gio sharply inhaled as he watched it turn blue. It joined a bundle of coarse, ephemeral cyan ropes that seemed to anchor onto Sterling’s back.

“[Grave of the Naiads.]” The boy invoked.

A bit of foul-smelling water had begun to bubble up from the floor, bringing with it a great deal of what might have been sediment, like the silt from a riverbed.

More and more of the ephemeral strings began to fall from the rafters. Gio watched as they pulled taut, like all of the slack had been let out of a fisherman’s rod as he reeled in a big catch. Something was happening.

A geyser of stale water burst forth, showering the floor with stale water and grime. Some of the muck got on the young man, and he set his jaw as he made eye contact with the proctor.

“Saint Vaught… I would love to show you what little remains of my Clan’s domain. ” He said. His deep voice rang with a sneering note of challenge that felt wildly inappropriate when referring to a saint, to Gio’s sensibilities.

What is he doing? What’s going on?

A pale white figure floated up from the grime, and Gio’s stomach soured near-instantly. The smell was horrid, like rotting fish and salt. Gio stared in shock, even as his gorge began to rise.

As if ripped from a storybook just to be murdered, a mermaid floated in the pustulent murk. Bits of gelatinous substance clung to her skin, her face locked in a grimacing death mask, like she was eternally gasping for air. Her scales, which once might have shimmered with an iridescent sheen, were now mottled with decay and falling loose into the waters around the young mage.

From the dead spirit, more of the ominous strands cascaded, like thick bundles of cable thrown into the air. They coiled and writhed like snakes, attaching onto anything that they touched. Gio grimaced as he watched a particularly large cable magnetically latch onto Decadence’s face.

Well, it seems that I’m still the only one that can see them. But… why?

One of the ropes shot at Gio, who nearly jumped out of his skin. As he put his hands in front of his face, the rope erupted into grey flames, burning down to the root. The neighboring strands all violently seized, ballooning away from Gio… almost like they were scared of him.

“Young man,” Professor Anaximenes began. “If you’re trying to make a statement, now is not the-”

“That’s quite alright, Alexander.” The Saint hummed.

Saint Vaught descended into the water, allowing the foul fluids to coat his stone form. He approached Sterling, standing with him in the center of his domain. Where the saint walked, the strands rippled.

“Young man… I know who you are. I must confess that I do not believe that I am the true object of your anger, am I correct?”

Each and every strand in Gio’s vision snapped fully taut.

“I wonder… if you threatened a professor of the school for calling the integrity of a Saint into question, what would happen to me if I said that I wanted revenge against one?”

Sterling Sundleah, a first-year student of average academic standing and no particular talent, defiantly glared at one of the most powerful wizards to have ever lived.

Saint Vaught stood perfectly still. After a protracted silence, Sterling looked even more upset than he had previously.

“Am I not even worth your words, Saint Vaught?” Sterling demanded.

“What would you like me to say?” Saint Vaught responded.

“Anything,” Sterling cried. “Any acknowledgement that my family mattered at all. I would like even the tiniest bit of accountability on behalf of Saint Arbalum.” Sterling replied.

“I don’t believe that there is anything that I could say to alleviate your pain. Only time can-”

“Time? Time?? Time won’t bring my mother and father back from the dead. Time cannot undo the death of my culture.” Sterling spat.

“I hope to never be so callous as to disregard the suffering of one who is hurting… but you must understand that you are not the first orphan that the Cloud Castle has made, nor will you be the last. If Saint Arbalum had not diverted Atmos when he did, many of your fellow students wouldn’t be here today. Look around you. Are you bold enough to claim that the lives of your family are worth more than the combined worth of the people of the entire City of Rings?”

“Don’t do this.” Sterling spat. “Don’t reduce my life to some bullshit trolley problem. A whole city of mages, versus a poor farming community. Imagine that? No, I don’t believe you.”

More of the strands began falling. At this point, besides the circle around Gio, most of the room had been covered. Gio was starting to have a hard time seeing out beyond the rain of blue thread.

“Arbalum pushed the storm to my village because it was convenient. The Ringed City would have survived- you have indestructible walls. He chose to annihilate my people for no reason other than that the nobles of the Gold and Silver Rings would have complained about lost revenue.” Sterling ranted.

“Almost none of what you have said is correct. You’re speaking about machinations that you have no true understanding of. I have sympathy for your situation… but I must confess that even my patience has limits.” Saint Vaught volleyed.

“So what are you gonna do about it? You gonna kill me over it? I imagine that it would be easy for you… all of you saints are basically just killing machines, right?” Sterling said. He was frothing at this point, his face beet red.

Gio caught sight of something out of the corner of his eye. He turned.

Sybil was being wrapped up like a cocoon. The disgraced Ataraxian princess sat unknowingly within her cocoon, looking politely disinterested in the dramatic display occurring on the testing dais.

Looking around, Gio saw that the vast web had formed into several odd shapes. Some students, including Sterling himself, were strung up like puppets with strings that seemed to be attached to each limb. Other people, like Professor Fast-Bloodsheen, were seemingly only blindfolded. Even Professor Anaximenes’ psychopomps were seemingly gagged, having their skeletal mouths sewn shut.

Gio thought to himself for a moment.

This has to be abnormal. Why aren’t the spirits reacting? Am I really the only person that can see this? Should I do something?

Gio thought back to the mirror. The shard that had coincidentally embedded itself into his spellbook. Or was it a coincidence at all?

Well… if these threads don’t like chaos…

Gio quietly placed his open palms in his lap. He summoned the smallest sliver of mirror that he could, a tiny fleck of silver no larger than a grain of rice.

And then, he let the flower bloom.

The grain of mirror began to multiply, quickly spilling out of his hands with a sound like beads clattering to the floor, which had the unintended side effect of completely ruining whatever notions of discretion that he might have entertained.

“Dude… can you like, not stand to be the center of attention for two minutes?” Decadence quipped.

Gio ignored her because his plan worked. The threads surrounding him burst into puffs of psychedelic fuzz, unraveling into wisps and then disintegrating before his eyes. As soon as her cocoon began to unravel, Gio met Sybil’s eyes.

The Ataraxian princess had an incredulous look to her. She stared down at Gio’s hands before clasping her fingers under the folds of her dress. She stared into his eyes as she also discreetly cast a spell.

A white flash. Beneath the gauzy black fabric of her gown. It burned into Gio’s memory, resonating deep within him, the hum of the magic landing in the same place that the spellflower sat. It sang one resonant word to him.

SEVER.

The sharpness smoldered inside Gio, like the ghost of a razor pressed against his neck. Gio didn’t have an opportunity to contemplate the nature of whatever spell she had just cast, because he was too busy watching the fallout of it. Like locks of hair falling from a barber’s chair, cut threads began to peel away from everything. She had severed them all.

“Excuse me,” Sybil politely interrupted.

Madam Stradivari pursed her lips from across the room, looking like she was contemplating stopping Sybil from interjecting.

Sterling, who was still fuming, and Saint Vaught both turned to regard her.

“Now is not the time, Miss Quinta.” Saint Vaught tersely replied.

“I apologize, but unless I am mistaken, that student seems to be under the influence of the [Fatebond] curse.”

A few gasps whispered around the room.

The what curse?

Professor Fast-Bloodsheen snapped her fingers, and a bloody mess slapped down on the table in front of her. She reached into the mangled corpse of… something, dragging out a few sinewy arteries. She manifested a blade from somewhere, cutting away at the gore. As she did so, the blindfold of blue threads simultaneously fell away from her eyes.

“Well spotted, girl. It appears that someone has attempted to, as they say, ‘pull the wool over our eyes’. The question would then be… whom?.” The professor crowed.

She flicked a hand in Sterling’s direction, and he dropped like a rock, or perhaps like a puppet whose strings had been unceremoniously snipped.

Sybil looked curiously in Gio’s direction.

“Become a professor at the magic school they said,” Professor Steamcrest murmured. “It’ll be good for your career.”

Sterling’s domain began to fade.

“He passes. Summoning dead house spirits is impressive enough, though I doubt that he learned how to do it on his own. Quite frankly… I don’t have the time to delve into this mess. It’s not my problem, and the spirits won’t care… or, at least they won’t care today. I’ll leave the intrigue to Eudoria. Someone wheel the poor boy out of here. We still have all the [Wizard’s Domain] students to power through before the day is done, and I’m growing tired of all this excitement. The next person- be it student or faculty- to cause some kind of dramatic upheaval will be polishing my meteorite collection for a month, on this I swear.” Saint Vaught declared.

At that, the room seemed to quickly regain some sense of normalcy as students shuffled back into their positions. How many oddities had Saint Vaught handled over his long life to be able to shrug off something like this so quickly?

Are all the saints like this?

“Weird timing,” Decadence whispered, in a singsong voice. She had appeared over Gio’s shoulder and was playing with one of the mirror-like beads that he’d conjured to invoke the spellflower, holding it up to the light in a way that made Gio twitch with anxiety. Gio’s fingers still had a faint prismatic sheen to them that would hopefully fade soon, before anyone noticed.

“That’s gonna be a tough one for your friend to explain, huh?” Decadence continued.

“What do you mean?” Gio asked.

“Well… that curse, [Fatebond], if it really was that curse, is pretty famous from what I’ve heard.” She explained. “Back in ye olde dark ages, they used to use it to doom people to horrific deaths… or to make them do things against their own will.. allegedly.. Pretty scary stuff, as the curse is supposed to make you think that you want to do those things. I’ve even heard tall tales from people who believe that it actually changes the victim’s fate, if you believe in that kind of stuff. Little far-fetched for my tastes… I don’t believe in destiny, much less that there would be magic that could mess with destiny.”

Fate… but wait, hold on.

“They?” Gio urgently asked. He still didn’t know who decadence was referring to. “Who is ‘they’? Who used that curse? I’ve never heard of it before.”

Decadence blinked.

“Well, the witches of Ujjbala Sūrya, duh... Chandrika Chakraverti’s family? You guys are close, aren’t you?”
