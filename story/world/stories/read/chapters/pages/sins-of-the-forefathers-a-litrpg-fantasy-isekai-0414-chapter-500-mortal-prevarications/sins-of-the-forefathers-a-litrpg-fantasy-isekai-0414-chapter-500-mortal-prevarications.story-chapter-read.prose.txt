The question became then, of course…

How do I do it? If I was going to commit to this path, how exactly does one kill a Great Spirit? Normally, that would be the grand question. After all, these were existences that could reasonably be compared to the gods they so despised.

That was, if in her madness, Neris hadn’t just told me how one of the former Great Spirits had been murdered millennia ago.

The Vereden.

Ul-Korveth and Nehushtan had told me that the ancient starship was a weapon of some kind, but I hadn’t had time to consider just what kind of weapon it was. Apparently, it had some kind of capability where it could physically cross into the Concord, like I had been drawn into it during my confrontation with Rhazal. Then, it had targeted the true self of the long-dead Vaelis and…did something to him. Hell, for all I knew, the ship had some form of capital laser that did it.

Considering what I knew about the Netherim, I wouldn’t put it past them.

There were…problems with this plan, though. Not the least was that I was essentially allied with three of the surviving Great Spirits, congenial with one of the two who was already dead, and…it would probably antagonize the last one. From what Neris was telling me, Orus had essentially given half-hearted help to her scheme of vengeance before rolling back to sleep, letting her conspire with Anguis to doom Vereden to a monster-filled apocalypse. But I had no way of telling if he would remain so lethargic, especially if I murdered the other third of the trio that had been Sea, Sky, and Mountain.

What choice did I have, though, but to risk it? Even if I got Neris to back down here, and that was a monumental if, she was never going to stop. She would try and try and try again to get her vengeance against those she saw as responsible for the death of her brother. I wish she had thought to come to me first, before concocting this genocidal plan. I had already been confronted with the possibility of needing to kill Fynneas before she had even been approached by Anguis, last year. I…probably wouldn’t have been eager to slay what was apparently the controlling mind behind a starship in the form of Nehushtan, but I would have been much more enthusiastic at the prospect of deicide if I’d had a near-divine backer that could give me more substantial help than Anima could.

I couldn’t allow Vereden to lose the Vereden. If I, if we, could gain control of a true space-faring vessel, that represented far too much for the future of the planet. I know that Grey would have agreed with me on that.

The prospect struck me, then, as I started into the swirling crimson depths of Neris’s eyes that I…might not have turned down the possibility of becoming her Champion. I wasn’t the most stable of people at the best of times, myself. I had my obsessions and my madnesses, my faults and my griefs and joys. I had already been coming to love the seas and the open oceans during the Construct War.

God. What an odd thought, and I couldn’t help but mourn the possibility. I allowed my face to visibly droop as the future started to crystallize before me.

What the Hell was I going to tell Anima? ‘Sorry, but your sister had to go?’ I couldn’t contact her right now anyway, to try and beg for a little guidance from the person I had entered into a relationship we had called being a ‘pseudo-Champion’. Just like when I had been in the presence of Hermus, being here in the Nexus between Sea and Mountain had blocked that connection.

I was going to hurt her doing this, and yet…I would do it. I would do it anyway, because it meant I would help protect not only the future of Vereden, but the future for my daughter.

My tired grief wasn’t lost on Neris as I stood in front of her, now certain of my path. My guilt only spiked when, irrationally, an expression that could almost, but not quite, be mistaken for concern crossed over it. “What troubles you, Precursor?”

I met her eyes, and…only partly lied. “I’m tired, Neris,” I said quietly, to the person I was intending to murder. “I…understand your desire for vengeance, I truly do. But it’s caused a great deal of grief and tragedy for us, the mortals of Vereden. A lot has happened for us because of this phenomenon we’ve named the Skyfall. Neris…we, I, had already committed to the need that Fynneas should be put down. I suspect that you even know the true purpose behind Precursors as to why. I wish…I wish you could have just come to me, and we could have done something about this together.”

Neris simply stared at me uncomprehendingly, there in the middle of a cavern full of captives frozen under her power, and I knew then that she was truly lost.

You couldn’t reason with a person this far gone, this calcified in their beliefs. And I truly did believe that Spirits were people.

But for the good of Vereden, no matter the consequences, no matter the damage it might well do to the Concord, no matter the chaos it might cause for the Spirits…

She had to go.

I took a deep breath and shook my head. “Never mind. It doesn’t matter anymore. Neris, I’m assuming this whole ritual is designed to break the shield around Nehushtan? Did you order the Korvoks to abduct all of these people?”

I didn’t even need to try to hide any of my disgust for this entire apparatus anymore. At this point, there…just wasn’t a point.

Neris simply didn’t care. The only affront she seemed to take was when I explicitly mentioned people she personally had a grudge against. “Yes, that’s generally correct,” She said, her tone of voice rapidly becoming bored as she waved a disinterested hand in their direction. “Those that survive are free to go once the shield has collapsed, and I have avenged myself against that perfidious serpent. It shouldn’t take much longer.”

Alright. I basically knew enough now.

Time to get this show on the road.

I deliberately let my eyes drift over to Ul-Korveth for a moment and allowed a mirthless smile to cross over my lips.

The Korvok merely raised an almost anticipatory eyebrow back at me.

I saw what was really going on here. For now, I could tell…

My goals might just align with those of the Korvokhan himself. He wanted me to do something. This was the entire reason he had dragged me down here in the first place. Holding the prisoners over my head like this was nothing more than an excuse.

I didn’t appreciate that. I would play along, but I wasn’t his puppet.

I turned away from him to see that Neris had still been disinterestedly watching the frozen forms of the captives. “Did you know, Lady Neris,” I said pleasantly. “That one of your brother’s Champions should be among the very prisoners being drained?”

Out of the corner of my eye, I saw the cheer drain out of Ul-Korveth’s face.

Slowly, the swirling black waters of the Great Spirit’s avatar turned to face me. The blankness that had settled over its face was, somehow, a bit satisfying. “Excuse me?”

I placed my hands behind my back and respectfully approached her, aware that unblinking crimson eyes tracked my movements with every step. I was thankful that Fade played along with me, as he sauntered alongside me. When I reached her side, I pivoted on one foot to direct a raised eyebrow at Ul-Korveth. “Oh yes, did the vaunted Korvokhan not inform you?” I tsked and shook my head. “The current Champion of Lord Tarus was in residence when he attacked the Hold just next door to this one. Recovering him was one of my primary objectives when it came to my visit to this…interesting demesne. Presumably…” I slowly turned to regard Neris with an earnest expression. “He should be in this very room, in fact. Can you truly not tell, my Lady?”

The ancient Great Spirit examined my face for a moment, as if she could read truth from the soul beneath.

Good luck with that. I’d had a long time to gain complete control over every aspect of my facial muscles.

Neris’s head didn’t move away from me, but her eyes did cut away to fix Ul-Korveth with a distinctly unimpressed stare. “Is this true? You laid your hands upon the chosen mortal of one of my siblings?”

No fool he, Ul-Korveth picked up on the very much implied hostility in that question. “Absolutely not.” The asshole had the temerity to lie with a straight face. “I would never be so brazen as to insult House Spiritus so. It is certainly possible that such a mortal was among those we procured at your request. After all, there are thousands of them.” He chuckled politely at his own joke.

Neris was unmoved.

Actually, that’s not quite true. She did move, physically, by pivoting her avatar’s entire body to face the Korvokhan, all the while her eyes neither moved nor blinked.

She remained silent.

Perhaps it was my imagination, but I swore I saw a drop of sweat drip down the side of his face. An utter impossibility, of course, considering his sweat glands had been flayed away long ago. But I enjoyed watching him sweat metaphorically.

Even more so when I smiled pleasantly at his expertly hidden, but not expertly enough, panic. “He’s lying, of course,” I faux-whispered into Neris’s ‘ear’. “He boasted to me only minutes ago how he captured the Envoy Azarus at the same time he did the leader of Karag Varla, one Jorvik the Defiant. I even know from another source that our dear Korvokhan was present to capture them personally.”

“Of course I was present!” Ul-Korveth unexpectedly barked, sounding affronted. “I was needed to counter the strength of the legendary Defiant himself! Yes, there might have been another…unexpectedly capable Dwarf with him, but if,” He stressed. “If his companion was the chosen partisan of Lord Tarus, I had no way of knowing!”

I scoffed. “I find that hard to believe. Envoy Azarus is visibly touched by the hand of Lord Tarus, and battles in a manner that displays his allegiance most evidently.”

Ul-Korveth was starting to look just a bit harried. “I-”

“ENOUGH.”

I wanted to grit my teeth, as the unexpectedly loud word echoed not only through the physical realm, but the Aetherial one as well. That was soul-speak, something I only ever experienced from powerful existences.

The anger within it was enough to cause the foundations of my soul to shudder.

“I tire of these mortal prevarications,” Neris seethed, visible waves crashing upon the surface of her avatar. Her hand abruptly lashed out at both Ul-Korveth and me, lengthening in an unnatural way to reach the Korvokhan. Though the hand was made of black water, it still clamped upon my throat with unbendable strength. I choked upon the words that immediately rose to my lips as I rose into the air, suspended by Neris’s grip. Ul-Korveth rose with me, scrabbling at the watery fingers crushing his and my windpipes.

Perhaps I…pushed her a bit too much.

“Do you think me naïve, fools?” Neris sneered at both of us. “Am I not meant to see the animosity that exists between you? You flesh creatures are so blatant. Even if I didn’t have centuries of dealing with your wretched kind, I could still read your intentions from the clouds on your meager, physicality-limited wisps you call souls. I am not your attack hound, Precursor.” Her head whipped around to fix me with a furious stare. “You are the blade here, not I. If you wish to slay this mutilated Dwarf, then do so yourself. And you,” She shook the choking Ul-Korveth like he was a disobedient pet and she was a poor owner. “You should know better than to try and lie to me. I can taste your deceit on your forked tongue, thou false servant. After everything I’ve done for your misbegotten tribe…you disappoint me. Both of you. And so…you shall make it up to me.”

She abruptly tossed both of us away from her, to land harshly upon the stone of the cavern. I rolled with the impact, crouching cautiously as I regained my footing.

“With blood,” Neris decreed in a vicious, bloodthirsty, regal tone. “Duel for my amusement, and whosoever emerges victorious shall have the honor of both serving me and retrieving this supposed Champion.”

My eyes snapped away from the Great Spirit to land on the form of Ul-Korveth barely a dozen meters away from me. The Korvokhan slowly stood to his feet, eyeing me all the while, and I did the same.

There was a look in his eyes as he considered me. Something familiar to me.

Cold, calculated, bloodlust.

“Well, Nathaniel,” Ul-Korveth said quietly. “We both knew it was always going to end this way. I must admit, though, I didn’t expect it to happen like this.” He chuckled softly, shaking his head. “I suppose the time has come to drop pretenses.”

The leader of Clan Korvok slowly sank down into a familiar, bare-handed combat stance. I’d seen it dozens of times from not only the Karvokar, but the Rendarchs as well. To his credit, though, I had to admit his stance seemed more refined, somehow.

“I’m going to tear your head from your shoulders, Nathaniel,” Ul-Korveth promised calmly. “Bathe in your blood, and fashion a steak from your butchered corpse.”

I snorted softly as I drew Terractus and sank into my own stance, palm upon the hilt and blade pointed right at the Korvok’s heart.

“You’re welcome to try.”
