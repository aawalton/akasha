Chapter LXII (62)

Mitsuko’s pain tolerance increased tenfold by the time she ascended up to the fourth floor of the pyramid. She’d been poisoned, burned, crushed, sliced, and even boiled. Only Mend kept her in one piece.

“While I admit it serves as excellent practice for casting my spell and advancing in levels, you really should exercise more care in regard to your health,” Sterling chastised her. “Should you damage your heart, the entirety of the Prismatic Spiral will cease to function. Your soul will be the final to escape before you doom all to an oblivion frozen in time.”

Mitsuko bit back a retort. Not for the first time, she found herself wishing that she could be as deaf to the sage’s words as everyone else on the archipelago. Her entire body throbbed with phantom pains from the memory of a hundred different wounds. She was not currently in the mood for Sterling’s judgmental banter.

On the third floor they had encountered a room covered in bioluminescent moss, which Mitsuko had proceeded to scavenge and now used as a light source moving forward. Of course, she first had to fight off a swarm of acidic parasitical worms that lived inside the moss, but she had emerged from the skirmish triumphant and with spoils that now dimly lit her path.

A path that suddenly crumbled underfoot. She attempted to scramble back, but it was too late. The stone fell into pieces all around her. Sterling leaped to the side, nimbly avoiding the pit trap.

Mitsuko fell.

A second of panic, with her heart skipping a beat, then she slammed into the stone below. The impact cracked her back and sent her head spinning. The good news was that after a flash of pain, she no longer felt any pain below her neck. A normal child would spend the rest of their life as a cripple from an accident like this. But Mitsuko wasn’t a normal child.

She Mended her body and the bones popped back into place, sealing together once again. Then she rose and looked up. It looked like the pit trap had dropped her all the way back down to the first floor.

Now with the moss illuminating the area around her, Mitsuko found the winding hallways of the first floor to be far less threatening. Almost…drab. But she wouldn’t be down here for long.

Unlike a normal victim to the trap, Mitsuko did not need to travel through the maze of corridors to relocate the staircase leading back to the second floor. She simply cast Mend on the rubble scattered around her and rose back up to the fourth floor with ease.

“You’re very fortunate there were no spikes at the bottom,” Sterling said as she joined him. “Or lava. Lava would have been very bad. Your reaction time with Mend could certainly use improvement. Your injuries as a whole could have been entirely avoided if you Mended the floor before you finished your fall. It’s not like you didn’t have the time on hand.”

“I’ll keep that in mind for the next time I trigger an ancient jungle temple’s trap and fall to my near death.”

“Very good.” Sterling nodded his head. “I’m glad that you are absorbing valuable information from my lessons.”

Mitsuko meant for her comment to come off snarky, but as she spoke the words, she realized this very well might be another situation she’d find herself in. At least with her current streak of luck. If each of the sages was hidden away in a temple then that meant traps.

“Why didn’t Escara have a temple? Both you and this sage have one.” She asked the question without expecting a straight answer from Sterling.

“He did,” Sterling replied bluntly. “It had simply been looted and neutered in the thousand or so years since we locked ourselves away.”

“Wait, you’re saying the clock tower itself was a temple at one point?”

“Certainly. There were several fascinating traps and even some enchanted clockwork creatures constructed as a means of protection. Sadly, it now is nothing more than a hollow shell of its glory.”

While interesting, Mitsuko’s heart still fell. That meant there were three more temples she needed to clear before freeing the sages. Maybe she’d get lucky and they’d be gutted like the clock tower. But again, her and luck weren’t on the best of terms at the moment.

“Your temple wasn’t actually where you were at though either,” Mitsuko pointed out.

“The temple was the entrance. And the challenge the city itself. In our original design it was a different sort of hurdle designed for our Champion. A navigation of the city itself, laced with different sorts of traps than the more traditional features you see here.”

“The Champion who failed,” Mitsuko said.

“Yes.” Sterling turned his tail to her, the light of his green eyes disappearing as he walked away.

She hadn’t pressed Sterling on the topic. He was cagey and uncomfortable whenever the previous Champion was mentioned. Even more than other subjects involving the Prismatic Spiral. She was starting to think that maybe she’d be better off using a question on the topic with a different sage. But, then again, it might be a bit of a sore spot for all of them. Regardless of what happened, it was clearly a mark of their past failure.

Mitsuko created a sword and used it like a walking cane to test each brick she stepped on before committing her full weight. She triggered one trap that burst green acid across the hallway. With a quick cast of Mend, the green goop retracted itself from in front of her before barely more than a few drops began to sizzle on the stone. It replaced itself in the hidden compartments and Mitsuko nimbly crossed the trap without triggering it.

“Were these temples designed for your Champion?” she asked, catching back up with Sterling.

“Of course not. They were meant to hold great treasures. However, we decided that there was no greater treasure than the information and magic we stored within our blood and souls. So we commandeered them for our own purposes.”

“Does that mean that they have like real treasure stored within them as well?” Mitsuko dodged and sliced as a vine shot out from its place on the ground. It landed on the floor, chopped in half and now still.

“‘Real’ treasure?” Sterling huffed. “Absolute ingratitude! You are offered power and immortality on the same plate, but instead you bemoan the scraps we feed the dogs!”

“Hey, hey, don’t get all worked up,” Mitsuko waved her non-sword hand placatingly. “You’ll trigger something and get us killed.”

“I am hardly the one most likely to trigger our deaths.”

Maybe that Champion comment earlier stung Sterling more than Mitsuko had thought.

They reached the stairs leading up to the fifth floor. Mitsuko took one step, and the sound of a flute washed over her like a dam bursting.

Suddenly she was back in the Hon Basin as a child, chasing butterflies with a net in hand. Her mother stood off to the side of the clearing, laughing as Mitsuko jumped into the air, trying to reach a golden butterfly. It flew just out of reach, taunting her. But even still, Mitsuko felt completely whole in that moment. A blissful bit of childhood memory.

“Mitsuko!” Sterling yelled. “If you don’t respond I am ending this loop and reverting your memory. Mitsuko!”

She blinked. The memory shattered all around her. Then she was back in the hallway with Sterling with one step on the stairs. The music continued to pour down from above, but it wasn’t quite as potent as the first wave.

An oppressive sadness took root as Mitsuko’s mother disappeared from in front of her.

“I’m fine,” Mitsuko said. Her voice quivered slightly so she cleared her throat.

“As I advised before, you shouldn’t face this enemy until after you level up Retrospection.”

“Could you end the loop now if I asked?” Mitsuko asked. “Like you just said you would.”

“I—yes…I mean, no! Not on request!” Sterling replied, flustered. “Not unless I deem it a danger to the Prismatic Spiral as a whole. So memory alteration. Or if I see a direct threat to your soul or heart. Otherwise I risk overstepping my role as your guide.”

That was a small comfort. At least that meant it wouldn’t always be her judgement call. But it also removed a bit of her already limited autonomy which Mitsuko was not entirely happy about.

“So if you decide I need to die, you can kill me with a mere thought?” Mitsuko asked. “But if I want to reset the loop, I need to stab myself in the neck or throw myself off a cliff?”

“There are…other means in which to die. Less painful options,” Sterling said, not meeting her eyes. “But that’s not important at the moment. I advise we retreat. Or perhaps use this opportunity to gather more information on the other four floors of the temple. You are not prepared to face the guardian and I’d rather if you didn’t lose all your memories, completely resetting your knowledge of Verdant Island.”

Mitsuko understood the rationality of Sterling’s suggestion. She listened for a minute as the melodious song continued to wash over her from above.

She raised her blade and examined its edge. Tapping a finger on it, she felt the cold blade cut through the soft, uncalloused flesh of her fingertip. Blood welled up on the cut and dribbled down her finger. The pain was comparable to that of a papercut. A moment later, she Mended the small wound.

“A sword cannot slice through a melody,” Sterling said.

“No.” Mitsuko closed her eyes and raised her sword higher. She rested the cold blade on her cheek and took a moment to enjoy the music’s call.

Then she sliced through her ear.
