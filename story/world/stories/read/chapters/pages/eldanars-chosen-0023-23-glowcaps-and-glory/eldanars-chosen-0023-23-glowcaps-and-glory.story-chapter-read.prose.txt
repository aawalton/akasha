The cool blue glow of Brett’s spell bathed the tunnels in a steady, ethereal light, but even with its reach, shadows pooled in the cracks between jagged rock formations. The air was damp, carrying the faint mineral tang of deep stone, and the rhythmic drip-drip-drip of water echoed somewhere far ahead.

“Alright,” Brett said, holding his staff aloft as the blue light flared a fraction brighter. “If I forget to refresh this again, someone hit me. Preferably not with a sword, though, Josh.”

Josh smirked, his storm-grey eyes scanning the dark periphery. “No promises.”

Carcan shook her head, but a small smile tugged at the corner of her lips. “If you’d all stop talking and keep your eyes open, we might avoid walking straight into another drake’s nest.”

“Where’s the fun in that?” Perb said. He crouched low to inspect a clump of faintly glowing fungi growing in the crack between two heavy boulders. “Ah, jackpot.” He carefully cut a mature one free and tucked it into his satchel. “More glowcaps. We’re nearly finished with that collection quest already.”

“Are they poisonous?” Carcan asked, stepping past him to keep watch down the tunnel.

“They’re not poisonous,” Perb said with a dismissive wave. “They’re… mostly edible.”

Brett raised a brow, pushing his non-existent spectacles up his nose out of pure habit. “Mostly?”

Before Perb could answer, the sharp crunch of talons on stone came from ahead. A shadow shifted in the gloom, just out of the light’s reach.

“Company,” Josh murmured, his grip tightening on his shield.

A Rock Drake slid into view, its thick scales mottled grey and brown to match the cavern walls perfectly. Its long, low body hugged the ground as it hissed, a forked tongue tasting the damp air. It didn’t lunge immediately; instead, it paced in a slow half-circle, its heavy tail scraping the floor.

“Looks like it’s sizing us up,” Josh said, raising his shield and moving squarely in front of his group.

It struck without warning. The drake darted forward with a sudden burst of speed, aiming low for Josh’s legs. His shield met the blow with a jarring metallic clang, but the sheer impact still drove him back half a step. Josh pushed his shield forward, creating space between his sword arm and the beast. His blade met tough hide, and although it cut through, the drake twisted away with surprising agility.

Brett’s voice rang out, firm and clear. “Hold still!” A flare of magic shot from his staff, striking the drake with a stunning burst of bright light. The creature staggered, blinking blindly, which gave Perb the perfect opening to launch a rapid volley of arrows.

The monster reared, attempting to drive towards Josh in a blind frenzy, but its jaw smashed violently into a hastily cast magical shield that Carcan had created to hinder the beast.

It took longer than expected to bring it down. The creature’s hide was incredibly thick, and its movements were unpredictable, forcing the group to constantly adjust their strikes. When Josh finally found a gap between the plates and drove his sword into its neck, the drake hissed weakly before collapsing.

Perb let out a low whistle, lowering his beautiful elven longbow. “Stubborn thing.”

“Good teamwork,” Caistina said quietly from the rear, her amethyst eyes glancing at each of them in turn. “No one overextended, and Carcan, your shield placement kept its focus exactly where we needed it.”

Carcan gave a small, proud smile. “We’re learning.”

They pressed on, weaving between narrow walls and over jagged stone shelves. The tunnels widened in places, revealing small caverns where pale clusters of glowcaps clung to the walls like stars against a night sky. Perb and Brett collected what they could, stuffing the caps into their sacks until the scent of earthy, damp fungi clung to all of them. Along the way, they also found another mottled drake egg tucked away in a shallow rocky nest.

Two more drakes found them before long. The first came without warning, bursting from a narrow side tunnel in a blur of stone-coloured scales and snapping jaws. Perb’s reflexes were lightning-fast; his arrow darted forward to drive the creature back while Carcan’s shield magic flared into being, blocking a vicious rake of claws that might have shredded Brett's unarmoured clothing.

Josh darted in low, his heavy plate clinking softly as his blade flashed. He carved a shallow gash along the beast's underbelly, the sound mimicking tearing leather. It took all of them to finally bring it down, the last blow a precise firebolt from Brett’s magically-imbued staff that sent cracks of searing light spiderwebbing over the drake’s hide before it collapsed.

They barely had time to regroup before the second made its move, stalking them in complete silence until Josh caught the shifting shadow on the wall.

“Behind!” he barked, spinning heavily to meet the lunging beast.

Perb’s arrow flew first, striking the drake perfectly in the shoulder and making it rear back with a shriek. Josh swept in from the side, slamming his shield forward to pin the heavy lizard against the cavern wall, allowing the others to hammer it safely with their magic and arrows. The beast thrashed wildly, its heavy claws scraping bright orange sparks from the cavern floor, but Josh held firm, planting his boots and using his heavy gambeson and plate armour to anchor himself.

When it finally fell, its tail twitched in the faint blue glow of the mushrooms before dissolving into shards of fading magic. Brett leaned heavily on his staff, breathing hard. “I swear these things are made of solid stone.”

“They live in stone,” Caistina replied smoothly. “Over time they have evolved, absorbing magic from the environment. It makes them much harder to kill, yes. But you’re doing well.” She gave an approving nod before adding, “Just… try not to get cocky. The ridge’s deeper tunnels will have more than just drakes.”

Perb cursed softly under his breath. “That’s encouraging.”

Josh tightened his grip on his sword, feeling the reassuring weight of the pommel. “Then let’s keep moving. The sooner we find what we came for, the sooner we’re out of here.”

With that, they stepped deeper into the glowcap-lit dark, heading down the side tunnel that the first drake had ambushed them from.

The cavern quieted again. The only sounds were their own steady breathing and the faint drip of water somewhere deeper in the stone. The soft, ghostly blue-green light of the glowcaps scattered along the walls seemed almost comforting now compared to the chaos of battle. Josh crouched beside a dense cluster sprouting from a crack in the rock, carefully cutting the caps free and tucking them into his satchel.

“That’s… what, fourteen now?” he asked over his shoulder.

“Sixteen,” Brett corrected, peering into his own pouch. “And they’re still weirdly warm. I'm not sure how I feel about that. We only needed twelve, so we can stop collecting them now. Unless we earn more for bringing extras back?” He looked to Caistina, who simply shook her head.

Perb stepped ahead, his bow held loosely in hand, his luminous silver eyes scanning the uneven floor. “You’ll feel better when we’re out of here and not surrounded by the things that eat these mushrooms.” He tilted his head towards a low shelf of rock ahead, his gaze latching onto a shadow. “There’s something up there.”

He scrambled up the rocks with lithe agility to retrieve whatever it was. “Oh, it’s another drake nest!” After a moment, he held something round and mottled in his palm: another drake egg, the shell patterned in swirls of grey and green.

Josh joined him and spotted another tucked firmly against the cave wall. “One more to go,” he said, lifting it gently. “That makes five.”

Carcan was looking around, genuine concern showing on her aristocratic face. “Either we’re getting close to a nesting ground, or these things lay them everywhere and we’ve been very unlucky.”

Brett snorted, shouldering his staff. “Unlucky would be finding out they’d hatched in our bags halfway back to town.”

“Thanks for that image,” Josh muttered, carefully stowing the egg that Perb had passed to him.

They moved on in relative silence, each step crunching softly on grit, the glowcaps’ dim light pooling on the damp stone like captured moonlight.

After a short while, the tunnel opened into a truly wide cavern, a massive bowl of stone cupped by jagged stalactites and slick, wet walls. Glowcap light painted everything in a sickly blue-green; the air was intensely damp and tasted faintly of old metal. In the middle of the chamber, piled into a rough, steaming mound, lay a nest of scraped earth, shed scales, and broken bones.

The thing guarding it was enormous. It was a low-slung drake the size of a warhorse, composed of corded muscle and stone-hard scales, its belly mottled with paler, softer plates. It rested with one heavy foreleg draped protectively across two leathery eggs, its lidless eyes watching the tunnel with a slow, animal patience.

It wasn’t a flyer, much like the smaller ones they’d seen earlier. It was low and long, every inch of it a slab of mottled stone-grey hide. Muscles rolled under thick plates as it shifted, heavy claws scraping the earth. Its head lifted, flat and clever; its tongue flicked out, tasting the air while its nostrils flared. When it turned towards them, the party could see exactly how fast it could move despite its bulk. It was an apex predator built to surge and strike at ground level.

They’d hoped to catch it unready, but they hadn’t been that lucky. However, it appeared completely unwilling to leave its nest to chase them. They dropped back into the tunnel's shadow and quickly formed a plan. It was blunt and ugly.

Brett would open with a restraining weave to slow the beast’s massive legs; Perb would rain arrows into the exposed joints; Josh would close the distance to pin and punish the drake while the ranged assaults dug in; and Carcan would use her magic to shield and heal Josh as needed. Caistina would stay at the lip of the cavern, her eyes sharp but her hands folded, watching quietly as always, only stepping in if she absolutely had to.

They moved like a well-oiled machine. Perb crept to the left, an arrow nocked and drawn, his breathing slow and perfectly even. Brett moved to the right and whispered a count, quietly chanting to himself, visualising his magic: chains growing from the rocky ground to bind the beast. Josh prepared to move into the cave; he leaned into his shield and flexed the thick leather straps, every muscle coiled for the first slam.

Perb didn’t wait for Josh to slam into the monster. He let his arrow fly, not to kill, but to force the drake’s reaction. It struck high on the beast's flank with a loud thunk. The creature's head snapped around, emitting an ugly sound like stone grinding together.

That was the signal.

Brett released his binding spell: pale ropes of magical light shot out of the ground, twining tightly around the drake’s haunches. For a heartbeat, nothing happened, and then the cords tightened with a vicious snap, tethering the rear legs like a hunter’s noose. The drake reared, furious, but the bindings bit deep into the earth, and the beast found its range of motion suddenly stripped away.

Josh exploded forward. Shield held high, his boots hammering the stone, he ran straight for the drake’s head, determined to draw all of its attention. He slammed the heavy boss of his shield into the creature’s snout with a wet, stony impact. Scaled teeth clacked loudly, and a hot, rancid breath washed over his face. Pain sang up Josh's arm from the blow; it felt like hitting a solid brick wall.

The hit rocked the drake sideways and sent a spray of slobbered dust into the air. Josh didn’t give it time to recover. He twisted, used the shield’s momentum to shove the creature slightly off balance, then stabbed up under the edge of a flank with a short, practised strike. His blade found soft tissue between the heavy plates, and yellow drake-blood hissed violently where it met the air.

The monster retaliated with raw, animal fury. Its head snapped sideways, its massive jaws trying to clamp down on the shield and forearm. Metal rang sharply as steel met chitin, and the drake’s remaining teeth bit deep into the metal rim and leather strap. Josh felt his forearm jar with the immense force; pain flared, a hot white line that rattled his teeth.

An arrow suddenly thudded into the drake’s cheek, piercing through the beast's dense muscle. It let out a screech of pain, momentarily letting go of its hold on Josh’s shield.

Brett tried to be a conductor in the chaos. When the drake tried to twist for a flank attack, he poured another folded loop of mana outward: a sticky, dark chain that wrapped around the beast’s haunches and yanked them taut. The drake staggered, its tail whipping free but significantly slowed as the magic knotted tightly.

The drake lashed with its tail, a heavy, clubbing sweep that smashed squarely into Josh’s shield and rocked him, sending his heavy boots skidding backwards in the chitin-strewn mud. He let the shield take it, his feet digging back in, then shoved forward, using the momentum to ram his sword in under the shoulder plates. The blade didn’t slide clean; it bit, sank with an ugly resistance, then found something that snapped. Josh felt a sharp ping vibrate right up the blade. He twisted hard, feeling the blade catch and tear. The drake lunged forward with a snarl that physically shook the cavern, but it dragged its front leg clumsily as it moved.

Brett loosed a firebolt, but it didn’t follow a straight line. He bent it with the new trick he’d practised, curving the crackling flame neatly around Josh’s shielding bulk and hammering the drake’s exposed underbelly. Flesh steamed and cracked where the fire bit, quickly beginning to look like a dried riverbed after a drought.

Perb danced on the absolute edge of the fight, pinning precise shots into joints and softer parts. He was doing his best to slow the beast down and to distract it. An arrow buried into the flesh under its left eye sent the creature rearing, its injured leg nearly collapsing beneath the immense weight. He let another arrow fly, finding a home cleanly between its thick ribs. Each impact slowed the drake’s coordination; the great lizard’s movements grew noticeably more jagged and less precise.

Carcan didn’t strike, but without her, their battle would have been vastly different. She kept a low, melodic hum on her lips and created shimmering magical shields at critical points, protecting Josh’s exposed flanks. It was a dome of light that flickered and reformed as heavy blows landed. Twice the drake’s claws smashed into that ward; the magic cracked like a loud bell but held firm, eating the blow and splintering into hot motes of light. When a rear leg clawed aggressively at Josh’s knee, her hands flared and a shield snapped tightly around Josh’s hips, steadying him enough to stay on his feet. She moved with a quiet, intense focus, sealing the seams in their defences and letting Josh safely take risks.

This unfortunately meant that Josh became slightly overconfident, leading him to take a glancing blow that knocked him sideways, slamming his shoulder hard into the cave wall.

The beast lunged for Josh’s exposed flank, its massive jaws opening to crush muscle and bone. Josh pivoted, planting his heavy boot, and with a desperate heave drove the hilt of his sword forward under his shield, striking the creature’s throat right between scaled segments. At the exact same time, the drake’s jaws closed around Josh’s shield. The blade bit home with a wrestling grunt; the drake choked out a terrible, splintering sound.

For a terrifying second, Josh’s world narrowed to the small space in front of him. His shoulder muscles burned, his arms trembling violently from the sheer strain of holding the beast’s heavy head as it snapped and floundered, its teeth grinding horribly against his shield's rim. The beast's hot breath washed over his face. As Josh looked around his shield, he could see clearly down the drake's throat, the glint of his own steel catching the faint blue light.

It was close and terrible. Teeth ground painfully against metal, and the monster started to wrench backward. Josh felt his arm pulled sideways, the thick straps biting deeply into his palms, his ribs jarring against his breastplate. He planted his feet and drove his body weight down, using his hip as an anchor to keep from being dragged across the floor. He twisted his sword brutally inside the wound, cutting through thick meat and drawing more foul blood.

The drake's claws lashed forward in a blind panic, cutting into Josh's exposed leg below his armour. Carcan’s voice immediately cut through the chaos as she chanted a potent heal over him. Warmth knitted deeply into his legs where the claw had grazed flesh, and repaired the torn, exhausted muscles in his arms and shoulders. The sudden relief from the pain eased him enough to push again.

Brett saw the opening. He forced his voice, barking a magical cadence he barely understood; Perb answered with a tight volley of arrows that hammered the drake’s cheek and shoulder, blinding its remaining vision. Brett gathered the very last of his control and poured a heavy knot of binding into the stone at the drake’s rear: not to trap, but to root. The ground around its hind legs flared brightly, clinging to the scales like wet, hardened clay.

The beast threw its heavy weight, trying desperately to fling Josh off. Its tail swung in a savage arc that gouged the dirt where Brett and Perb had just stood. Carcan’s shield took the indirect blow, blue magic sparking brightly into the air, but it held steady.

Josh twisted the sword again, not to pull it free, but to work the blade into a rending angle right between the drake’s thickest neck plates. He felt something give: a vital tendon, perhaps, or a seam between the scales. The drake bucked, and Josh used the violent motion. He stepped hard and shoved the entire length of his body weight into a final, punishing strike.

The sword sank in up to the hilt at the base of the skull. The tip pushed through brittle tissue; he twisted with absolutely everything left in him.

The drake gave one almighty, choking roar. It was more animal than beast, a sound of complete, furious refusal, and then its heavy limbs simply collapsed out from under it. The great body convulsed twice, shuddering violently, and then lay entirely still.

For a long moment, there was only ragged breathing. Dust settled slowly in the blue light. The drake’s body twitched reflexively, scales grinding softly against the rock, and then it stopped. When the dust finally settled, the cavern felt enormous and painfully sudden in its quiet.

Breathing was heavy and loud. Josh slid off the drake’s flank and sank to one knee, his chest heaving, his sword left abandoned in the beast's throat. He tasted salt and iron on his tongue. Around him, the others staggered in, exhausted smiles on their faces, immensely glad that they had overcome this enemy without a single casualty.

They stood there together for a long minute, panting, hands trembling as the damp cave air cooled the sweat on their skin. The drake’s body lay humbly defeated, its ragged breath gone. The fight had been raw and uncomfortably close, but they’d truly functioned as a team.

Josh felt a familiar prompt at the back of his brain, looking up just in time to see a blue message flash across his vision.

[Congratulations, your party has slain Level 6 Rock Drake!]
