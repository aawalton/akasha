Zane made his way through Command in silence. There was silence all throughout the center—Haxorax caught his eyes and nodded, and he crossed paths with a few more folk he recognized on his way. But they seemed to recognize his state of mind, and they let him be.

It'd been six hours since he'd arrived. In that time the Scryer's Guild confirmed the speculation.

Malzareth's army had nearly been annihilated.

The bulk of the Greater Endbringers and Primes had escorted their Master back, which meant the rest of the army was trapped in Dragonspire, with little way to void-walk out. The next several hours saw Dragonspire's finest finishing off entire legions of lesser Monsters.

There was a fraction of the army—something between 10% and 20% of its original size—that'd made it back to the Superdungeon, where they'd likely take up defensive positions.

The rest were annihilated.

Dragonspire might've taken heavy losses, but it now had the clear numbers advantage at all levels of firepower, except possibly at the very top.

The next step was quite clear.

In Command he joined in on a few discussions as Reina debriefed the Dragonspire Grand Council—discussions with all of Dragonspire's top elites, including the ones who'd just taken part in the strike on Malzareth.

It was widely agreed they'd need an assault on the Superdungeon, but the question of timing remained in debate.

Malzareth's army was permanently damaged. That much was certain. Even with years to recover it couldn't replace a Prime, and definitely not the losses it'd taken in the Greater Endbringer ranks and below. A Scryer's Guild Councilmember added that with the depth of the damage the Sage had inflicted, it was extremely unlikely Malzareth could recover to its full capacity without many weeks, if not months of uninterrupted effort. They knew Malzareth had taken soul damage before—chiefly via System punishments and divine tribulations—and it was clear its soul wasn't so different from everyone else's. It'd likely heal fast, but they estimated they'd have a window of at least a few weeks where it'd be significantly limited.

***

Right after the meeting Reina made a point to catch up with him privately.

"I was the one who drew up that attack," she told him, with a look of slight guilt. She was quite straightforward with him. “Zane, if anyone should take responsibility for the Sage's condition—”

"It was his choice.”

She didn't manipulate the Sage into it, and she also didn't neglect to mention the option to take that elixir; she'd just given the Sage the choice if he wanted to take it. It was what Zane would've wanted as well—the choice to make the difference.

“If I’d just—”

“In my mind, you didn't do anything wrong," he said. “And the Sage will be alright, which is what ultimately matters.”

He looked her in the eyes, so she could see he genuinely didn’t blame her. “Let’s focus on what comes next, alright?”

She looked conflicted, but she acquiesced.

They were back to addressing the field of play.

She interviewed him about his Supreme, making sure she catalogued and noted all its key properties—his fights had been quite difficult to scry, given they'd taken place first in the void, and then in nowhere. A few of his descriptions made her blink and pause.

"I'm just surprised it could make up that much of a shard-gap that easily," she told him. "We estimated Gilgoroth's shard count at around 650, but... in your case, it really does seem your quantity's a quality unto itself. That's..."

She seemed to have a thought. "We'll have to check that it works with created Souls as well—the ones used in Summons," she said. "It shouldn't be difficult to verify. If it does, we'll have much less to think about."

He got what she was getting at. "We can try it when the Skill resets," he told her. She nodded, and they continued on.

She was also quite interested in how that super-charge effect worked—normal Skills couldn't simply have more essence pumped into them, and 'overclock' as his did, but something about his Skills in his Supreme—she thought it might be his silvered essence—altered them fundamentally, if only for the duration of the Supreme.

Plans as to the precise nature of the coming attack were still rather up in the air. She was still understanding his role, which seemed to be changing in her mind almost by the minute.

"For now, you'll have at least a few weeks to work with," she said. "I'd estimate the earliest we'd have the capacity to attack is in two weeks."

They still needed to address their own—it'd been an exhausting few weeks. Not just the battle but the siege leading up to it—the bulk of their elite fighters were actually working with Destruction injuries that'd never gotten the chance to fully heal. Now they'd get that chance.

It should've been a relief, having a bit of a respite like this, but he still didn't quite feel it.

He just kept thinking about his promise to the Sage, and that distant golden point in that tunnel of void.

She asked him what was on his mind, and he told her.

"You should go train," she said with a slight smile. "It'll be good for you—clear your mind."

She was off to check up on a few of her wounded Winds.

"You should rest up too," he told her.

"I will," she promised. "I need to clear my head too. There's just... a lot to consider."

***

She left to finish her tasks as general and to try to piece together Dragonspire's future plans, and Zane took her advice. He left to train.

He was capped Destruction shard-wise, and there were only Creation shards to go—but going from 300-ish to 400-ish Creation shards wouldn't significantly change his fighting strength.

So it was only logical to go all-in on his System Store.

This brief stint of training quickly turned out unlike any he'd done before. He started off taking in a few Creation shards, and then quickly decided to focus entirely on dreamsteel.

He'd always paired taking in dreamsteel with something else—Destruction shards or Creation shards, which meant he'd never quite hammered them as efficiently as he could.

But for 10 real-time days—and significantly more in his Time Chamber—he essentially did that exclusively.

At the end of those ten days—just 6 real-time days before they were scheduled to leave for the Superdungeon, according to the newly finalized war plans—he was rewarded with a notification.

Friend of the System V unlocked!

And there, with some anticipation, he considered his prizes.

1 use: 4k Time Dilation Machine [??] Cost: 500 Credits per use

A replica of the 4k Dilation Machines of the Celestial Imperium. This Dilation Machine is powered not by a Galaxy Core, but by the System's remaining reserves.

The '4k' variant of the Dilation Machine dilates time by a factor of 1:4000. Each 'use' lasts 1 real-time hour, or 4000 dilated hours. Note: due to System reserve constraints, uses are limited to once per 24-hour day.

This treasure grants a cubic mile's worth of space for personal use. External non-spatial items can be brought into the machine, provided they are under the grade of Origin Mythic.

It was the least expensive new addition—an upgraded version of an existing resource treasure—and it was one he'd expected from browsing the section before. He welcomed it, but its usefulness definitely depended on what else the Section offered.

1 use: Universal Prism [??] Cost: 2,400 Credits per Instance

The Universal Prism is a perfected scrying tool, useful for a wide range of purposes, but especially for Law understanding. It is able to filter out all information except specific Law insights and scope the Universe for instructive instances of Law, capturing 100% of the Law across an instance of spacetime. The Prism can be used to capture Instances of any Law and Concept under T9.

Purchasing this treasure grants the user a single instance of spacetime, to be viewed with the Universal Prism.

The Aeons Titanbone [??] Cost: 6,000 Credits

He blinked as he took in the descriptions. The costs for these next few items were incredible—he'd been wondering, for quite a while, just what could possibly justify them. His 100-million-year Bone, he remembered, was only 200 Credits—and this Bone cost 30 times more?

As far as he was aware, no one in Dragonspire had any point of reference for what that could look like.

At the end of the description—

Note: grants Unique Cornerstone Skill, Last Will of the Titanborn

There was an accompanying System store note: Integrating this Bone automatically grants an unlimiter of the 3rd Edict.

Half an hour later, he showed Reina the list. She was already visibly stunned when she got to this point—the System allowed her to open up another notification and read exactly what the Skill did.

"Zane this is..." She trailed off as she read the next notification. "The 3rd Edict?"

She seemed puzzled as to what it meant.

"It's not done," he told her.

Unlimiter of the 1st Edict [??] Cost: 6,000 Credits

Another obscenely expensive item, but the description—

A System-exclusive service treasure granted for exceptional service, which merits an exceptional reward. Grants the Unique passive Skill 'Universal Field' to the wielder's Reality Distortion Field, which endows it with the base properties of the Universe.

The System writes an exception into the 1st Edict, allowing the wielder to bypass their 512 True God shard cap for Destruction or Creation shards.

Silence from Reina.

Unlimiter of the 2nd Edict [??] Cost: 5,000 Credits

A System-exclusive service treasure granted for exceptional service, which merits an exceptional reward. The System interfaces with the wielder's Supreme, unlocking any prerequisite-based conditions in Fate.

The System writes an exception into the 2nd Edict, allowing the wielder to take on more than one Supreme while still under the defined limits of half-step Overgod, provided his Title offers such a Skill, and the corresponding conditions are met.

Unlimiter of the 3rd Edict [??] Cost: 2,500 Credits

A System-exclusive service treasure granted for exceptional service, which merits an exceptional reward. The System writes an exception into the 3rd Edict. Grants the wielder the ability to bypass the 512 True God body cap for Creation shards, given a suitable physical base.

She didn't say anything for almost a minute.

He knew her expectations had been quite high coming in—she'd thought the new Section would give him enough to get to that pinnacle tier in Dragonspire, given a little time. But regardless of its offerings, the shard caps still were what they were.

With how the store valued these items, she'd thought it was even possible that he might not be able to make use of several of them while he was still True God.

She seemed to be coming to the same conclusions he did the first time he read through the items.

Finally she met his eyes. "Zane—you said you had 36,000 Credits."

"Yes."

"...We'll need to make some changes," she said. "To the final plans."
