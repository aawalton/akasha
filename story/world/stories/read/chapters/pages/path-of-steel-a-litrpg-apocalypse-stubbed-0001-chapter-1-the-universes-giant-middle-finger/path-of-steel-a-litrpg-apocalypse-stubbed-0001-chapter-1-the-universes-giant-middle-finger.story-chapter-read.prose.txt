“Oof,” he said, covering his face. “I really should open these more often…”

The sun shone through the open curtains, illuminating a neat but tiny office space. A cheap desk covered one end of the room, right below the window, furnished with a top-of-the-line gaming chair and a computer full of colorful LED. Behind them was the door.

This was where Gary spent most of his day, every day—working for eight hours and playing games for the rest. It was a quiet life. As his eyes adjusted and he gazed over his office, he remembered why he kept the curtains shut.

“Is this all there is?” he muttered. The window split the world in two. One half contained a bright day, tree-covered streets, and hurrying pedestrians. The other, a computer. As Gary looked outside, however, he saw the people rushing to and fro, heard the incessant honking, noticed the still but revving engines beneath his window. People shouted. A drill buzzed in the distance.

“Yeah, I’m good.”

He fell back into his chair, the sun reaching up to his neck. Sunlight was necessary occasionally, but he’d close the curtains once he got hot. The screen flashed with notifications, his supervisor requesting more changes to the code. Gary sighed and rubbed his hands over his face. Dragging his fingers to the mouse, he brushed by a near-empty coffee mug. Salvation.

“Gotta get more,” he said, changing his online status to Busy.

The door opened into a dim corridor and eerie silence. Gary lived alone. Had been, since he moved here two years ago. He’d expected to be used to the silence by now—yet, sometimes, it still surprised him.

At the end of the corridor, the kitchen and living room lay to the right, and opposite that was Gary’s bedroom—both rooms as cramped as his office. A half-filled coffee machine waited for him in that kitchen, yet something stopped Gary from pacing over. With a sharp turn, he stopped in front of the apartment’s door and opened it, putting on the shoes he kept outside. He placed his mug on top of the shoe rack.

“Be right back,” he said, hearing his own excitement.

Already dressed, as he always liked to work, he hopped down the stairs and left the building. The sun blasted him again, but he was prepared. Wading through cars and pedestrians, he crossed wide streets to arrive at a cute coffee shop only two blocks away.

Not a person glanced at him on the way, nor did he look at anyone.

The door jingled open. Gary entered a bright if tiny space, only two tables and a counter.

“Welcome!” a voice called out.

“Hey Clara,” Gary replied. “How’s it going?”

“Pretty good, how about you?”

“Hanging in there!”

Clara smiled, then turned back to the coffee machine. She zoomed from one end of the counter to the other with persistently excessive energy. Blond bangs hung in front of her slightly angular face, while her voice brightened up the place. She was strikingly attractive, in the sharp way slavic girls tend to be. Her energy was contagious—just by being near her, Gary felt ready to work another shift and laugh all the while.

He knew, without a shred of a doubt, this was a girl he’d never be able to get. They were several leagues away. Yet, he enjoyed the fact that, by some blind miracle or capitalistic inevitability, she’d come to work within walking distance of his house. It felt like the only contact he’d ever have with someone like her.

Gary wasn’t handsome, or athletic, or charismatic. He was just average. A little smart, sure, but how far could that take him?

He made himself comfortable on a stool.

“The usual?” Clara called out.

“For sure,” he replied, leaning on his elbows.

He knew he didn’t stand a chance. However, he didn’t want to be a coward. Gary yearned for change. He’d prepared, and practiced, and mustered his courage.

“Do you ever feel smothered?” he asked.

“Smothered?” Clara replied, pausing for a second to let her eyes flicker over him—as if acknowledging his presence anew.

“Yeah. Like life is empty, somehow.”

“Do you feel smothered?” she asked, returning to her work.

“A little. I’ve done everything I had to, yet I’ve gotten nowhere. I studied hard, aced every exam, and finished university. I got a good job. I make a decent living and can live by myself. Yet, every day, I can’t help feeling unfulfilled. Is this really all there is? Am I supposed to be happy yet?”

Another pause. She glanced back, olive eyes meeting his.

“I work at a coffee shop, Gary, and I live with three roommates. I don’t think I can weigh in on your first world problems.”

“Uh… I didn’t mean it like that. Sorry.”

“Don’t worry. I may not be able to weigh in, but people say I’m a good listener. So? What do you think you’re missing?”

Gary was off script now, but that was alright. It was bound to happen eventually.

Improvise. Adapt. Overcome.

“Fun, I guess,” he replied. “Life feels so set and boring, you know. I can predict my day to the t. I feel stifled.”

She placed three coffees on the counter. One was his—the rest had a tag indicating them for delivery.

“You sound super self-aware. If you know what’s missing, what’s stopping you from getting it?” she said, standing across from him. With nothing to do, the weight of her full attention made Gary sweat.

“I, uh, I’m going to. I mean, I’m trying. It’s just a little hard to shake what’s already built.”

She cocked her head. Her eyes narrowed, and the ends of her lips rose. Gary was certain she’d seen through him, yet instead of cutting off the conversation, she leaned forward.

“Do it anyway,” she said. “You want fun? Grab your friends and go get it, tiger.”

“I don’t have many friends,” Gary admitted, realizing it was the wrong thing to say. “I mean, I do have friends, but most are back in Dalton. It’s a little hard to meet people when I work from home.”

“I wish I worked from home. Do you know how many creeps come in here every day?”

“I can imagine,” he blurted out without thinking. His eyes widened. “I mean, I can see it happening because—”

No, Gary! Be brave! This is your moment! Shoot your shot and fail with glory!

“—you’re just so hot,” he finished.

She cocked an eyebrow at him.

“Okay,” he said, “wrong word. Pretty is better. Beautiful?”

Her stare sizzled him, but he fought the urge to look away. He was definitely blushing.

“Look, I’m sorry. Just ignore that. You, uh, you’re from Europe, right? Did you face the same issue when you came here? Not finding friends easily, I mean.”

On his collapse, her stare finally broke into a chuckle. “Can I tell you a secret, Gary?” she asked, leaning in to whisper. Gary leaned in as well.

A shiver ran through him. The world rippled like the surface of a still lake in which someone had thrown a boulder. Clara’s face froze, then it warped unnaturally, bursting like a flesh balloon and shooting hot blood all over the place, including Gary’s face. He was paralyzed, staring at the red stain where Clara used to stand, sensing the burning liquid on his face. He could still hear the pop.

“What the fuck?” he muttered, falling off the stool and on his butt. “What the fuck?” He looked around.

There are times when life is great. Good thing happen out of nowhere. Then, there are times when the universe sees you on a roll and pulls out its giant middle finger to rub it in your face—and boy, what a finger.

More bloodstains littered the street outside the glass window. Bodies and clothes were gone, but accessories—smartphones, handbags, briefcases—remained on the pavement. Blood had sprayed everywhere. A car shot past Gary's field of view, its windshield completely red, then came a string of booms.

“What the fuck?” Gary repeated. He could see no other people. A stain was at the cafe’s door, a delivery man who’d been about to enter. He was alone. His face still burned.

“WHAT THE FUCK?” he yelled.

A screaming woman ran past, then locked eyes with him through the window. She was insane. So was he. A blue screen popped up before his eyes, making him jump back and hit his head on the foot of the stool. The pain shook him awake, somewhat. The blue screen persisted, hiding half his vision.
