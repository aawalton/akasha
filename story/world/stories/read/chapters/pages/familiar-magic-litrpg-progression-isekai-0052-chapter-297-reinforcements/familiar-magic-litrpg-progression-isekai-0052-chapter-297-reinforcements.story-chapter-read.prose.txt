Ethan, Selina, and the Nator hastily disembarked from the Leviathan. They had stopped as close as they could to the gathered army, and all stood watching as the scorpiursi finished freeing itself from the ground.

“It must tunneled here,” Selina said, “otherwise Steelbinder would have seen it.”

Ethan nodded, trying to retain his calm as he stared down the Champion rank monstrosity. He’d faced countless monsters and demons since becoming Bonded, but this one held a special place in his nightmares. It had been the last truly terrifying threat he’d faced as a normal human, and he’d yet to encounter anything half as vile since.

The enormous pincers were pulling the creature into the wide gully surrounding the plateau that housed the rift, but they were the least intimidating part of the beast. Its double-pronged stinger loomed ominously, and was so large it could likely strike at anything in a half-mile radius.

The writhing legs of an arachnid made Ethan’s skin crawl, but it was the head that seemed born of the deepest recesses of hell. It looked like the head of a bear, except, instead of a lower jaw, the whole skull was split vertically. It clapped open and shut, revealing a combination of bladed teeth and writing feelers inside.

That maw could swallow a bus with room to spare, and Ethan couldn’t imagine a much worse way to go. He actually found himself somewhat grateful for the thin coating of liquid black that covered it. While that made it abundantly clear that it was under Follower control, it still hid some of the gruesome details.

As he inspected their foe, however, something else caught his eye.

“Some of its legs are missing,” he said.

“You think it needs more than it has?” Selina asked. “There must be twenty or more. I hate bugs!”

“True, but you can see the stumps,” Ethan continued. “They end in shattered stone.”

There was a brief moment where Selina struggled to follow the logic, then she swore. “The Basilisk,” she said, and Ethan nodded.

“The Emperor must have used him to weaken the creature enough to take over, just like we did with the other Champion.”

“How unfortunately clever,” Selina said, glaring at the titanic demon. “So what now? Is it a race to The Throne? Do you see it?”

Her head whipped around as if searching for a suspicious chair, but Ethan sighed. “Not quite. I can feel it–can feel Dusk, I suppose. This is the place, but there’s two contenders for the power. Neither one of us can seize it while the other one has enough of the corruption under our control.”

Selina shuddered. “So we have to kill that…thing?” She looked up at the Leviathan. It was like looking up at a living skyscraper. “Well, boy? Are you up for it?”

“Not a good idea,” Ethan said.

Apollo jogged up behind them, having been coordinating with the Nator. “Did you say we’re not trying to fight that disgusting thing? As plans go, I love it.”

“Sadly, we still have to fight it,” Ethan said. “We just don’t need to kill it. I don’t honestly know who would win in an all-out battle between the scorpiursi and the Leviathan, and I don’t like risking the fate of three worlds on two demons with no stake in this battle.”

“So…we get our guy to fight defensively?” Apollo prompted.

Ethan nodded. “And we focus on our real enemy.” He pointed, just as the Emperor leapt from some unseen place on the Champion’s back.

The leader of the Followers didn’t seem to look any different from the others of his kind, at least in terms of proportion. He was obviously tall, and had the same pale grey skin, covered in carved runes, but only a strange cloak wrapped around his body marked him as any different from the generals hovering over the battlefield.

“We kill him, and The Throne is ours,” Ethan said.

“I’m not arguing, Ethan,” Selina began, “but how do you expect to do that? Even if he wasn’t Master rank, there has to be nearly a hundred Twilights down there.”

She lowered her voice and moved closer. “I’ll fight with my people, and we’ll fight until the end, but we’re not strong enough for this. Especially if you’re busy with the Emperor.”

She politely left out the fact that the Master rank Emperor would likely tear Ethan apart. He’d been worried about the same thing, but…

“There’s something off about him,” Ethan said. He could sense the other man now. It was like they were connected, now that they were this close. The corruption bubbling up inside them was all really coming from the same being, and Ethan had a surprisingly clear sense of the Emperor’s power.

“He’s wounded,” Ethan said. “But there’s more than that. I don’t think his ability to control the Champion is as strong as our own. We have the benefit of the Fable Train as an intermediary, but him… I think that as long as the scorpiursi is distracted, the Emperor will avoid taking part directly.”

“That’s a lot to gamble on,” Apollo said.

“And it doesn’t solve our other problem,” Selina added. “We still have to face an army of Twilights with nothing but Dusks.”

Ethan smiled then. “Well, as long as we’re gambling, I have one last roll of the dice for us.” He withdrew the gleaming core from his inventory, holding up the blue-white orb before them.

“What is that?” Selina asked. “Demon core?”

Ethan nodded. “This was always a long-shot,” he said. “Even with Steelbinder locating a demon with dimensional affinity, this was never going to be a guarantee. We could never figure out the targeting problem.”

“What are you talking about?” Selina asked. “And who’s ‘we’?”

Ethan turned a grin on her. “Just think lucky thoughts.”

He moved a short distance away, still eyeing the Follower army. Evidently arriving with the Leviathan hadn’t left them in the mood to make the first move. The Emperor now seemed to be strategizing with another of his kind, pointing violently at Ethan’s group.

Ethan ignored them for the moment, placing the orb on the ground, and reaching inside himself to ask for Deevee’s help. Thankfully, there were a number of advantages here he’d been counting on.

The nearby rift was like an enormous beacon of dimensional energy. Even if Ethan couldn’t draw from it, he could use it as an anchor. Earth was likewise easy to locate. He didn’t understand the specifics, but it somehow occupied the same space as Potentia and Nexum, essentially, separated by divine magics beyond comprehension.

But divided or not, it was home to Noctem, a god with the same dimensional affinity Ethan needed. As soon as he felt that connection–distant, yet somehow near–he pulled energy from the demon core, and tore open a rift.

The actual effort wasn’t too far removed from something he’d done once before, when he’d stranded the Basilisk here. The blue-white portal that appeared was tiny compared to the one left behind for Dusk, but it was still large enough to drive a car through, and Ethan stepped back from it, quietly hoping.

“Where does that lead?” Selina asked, moving to stand next to him.

“This same spot, but on Earth,” Ethan said. “A city called Golden, deep in the Rocky Mountains. I’m hoping it will do more than just freak a lot of people out…”

At that moment, a figure walked through, peering around cautiously, then relaxing and coming forward.

“It worked,” Michael Bishop said in a bland tone.

“Michael!” Selina said excitedly, then rushed over to gather the man in a hug.

The career soldier accepted the hug dutifully, a tiny smile tugging at one corner of his lips, but he actually barked a laugh when Ethan joined.

“Good to see you, brother,” Ethan said, stepping back. “I wasn’t sure if you’d be able to figure out where to go. I’d hoped this was where The Throne was, but I couldn’t expect you to wait around for a year.”

Michael shrugged. “We got some intel that confirmed time and place.”

Ethan raised an eyebrow. “Intel?” he asked, but Michael didn’t have a chance to answer.

King Rothavaro rushed through the portal. “You’re out of your damned mind if you think I’m waiting a heartbeat longer, Bishop!” The king barely glanced at the assembled army, instead rushing right up to Ethan. “Where is my son?” he roared.

Ethan wished he had better news to share, or even some way to soften the blow. He didn’t.

“The Emperor has him,” he said flatly. “I don’t think he’s been transformed, thank God, but he’s wrapped in one of their Familiars.”

That made the king’s eyes finally stop boring into Ethan’s, as Rothavaro quickly scanned the gathered forces. “I don’t see him,” he whispered.

Ethan took a risk, putting a hand on the larger man’s shoulder. “I have a plan,” he said. “I wouldn’t have come here if I didn’t.”

That steely gaze shifted back to Ethan. “A better plan than me rushing in, and taking back my son?”

Ethan sighed. “Yes. I promise you, yes.”

“And can you guarantee Calevaro will be brought back to me?”

The question was a demand, but Ethan could see the pain in those penetrating eyes. The pain and desperation of a father.

“I guarantee that I’ll do everything in my power to save him. I’ll even do what shouldn’t even be in my power.”

Rothavaro watched him for another long moment, then gave a single nod. He turned back to the portal then, and called out in his commanding voice. “Banshee! Get over here, there’s work to be done.”

The Banshee, former member of the Broken Covenant, and current pupil of the king, came through the portal with some hesitation. Her pale face grew paler as she took her first real look at Potentia. She opened her mouth to speak, but Rothavaro was already shepherding her away, pointing at the Follower army and barking commands.

Selina watched the two go, and Ethan could see her struggling to look hopeful. “Three more Twilights–congratulations, by the way, Michael–three more is fantastic, but I’m sorry Ethan, I just don’t think it’s enough.

Before answering, Ethan looked back at Michael. His stern-faced brother’s single nod was enough to make a smile spread across Ethan’s face.

“Don’t worry, Selina. You’ve seen what a few Bishops can do.”

Michael raised a small radio to his lips. “It’s clear, move in,” he said, voice flatter than Rothavaro’s, but equally commanding.

Moments later, people started flooding through the portal, marching in pairs. They wore paramilitary garb, like Michael’s, but their weapons were an eclectic mix.

They were clearly soldiers, every one. But not just any soldiers.

“Like I said, Selina, you’ve seen what a few Bishops can do. Now you’ll see what a few hundred Bishops can do.”
