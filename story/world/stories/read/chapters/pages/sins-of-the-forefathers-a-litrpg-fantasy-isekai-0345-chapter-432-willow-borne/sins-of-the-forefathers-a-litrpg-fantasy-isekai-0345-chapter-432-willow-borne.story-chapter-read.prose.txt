Darkness.

At this point, it was becoming an old friend of mine. I was very well acquainted with the void of unconsciousness at this point. It was…familiar.

And I could easily recognise when one of my Lattices had succumbed to it.

It was hard to describe the way my mind worked to people. I considered the strange orientation of my Lattices to be my closest hidden secret when it came to my abilities. Yes, there were the obvious things, like my transformation, or my cloak, or the simple theming of my abilities. But when it came to the internal mechanisms of them…I had only fully disclosed it to one other person.

Sylvia.

I hadn’t even read Grey in on the full mechanics of my mind. And even then, Sylvia hadn’t really been able to truly understand what it meant to be Nathaniel Hart, these days.

I’d tried to help her, of course, by answering her questions.

Were there three Nathans that existed in my mind?

No, we were one person expressed in three different thought lines.

Were you an insectoid like hive mind? Were decisions made by community vote?

No, we were one person, experiencing the world through different perspectives, but still unified. All decisions, all true thoughts, were experienced and decided upon all at once.

I think it really only made things odder for her the way my clones and I acted when my Lattices were occupying them. From an outside perspective, I suppose it did look like they were acting independently. They weren’t, but I could understand it.

Still…

There was one caveat to my existence. I hated to say this, but my Outer Lattice was preeminent when it came to the control of our body. Not the autonomic functions of it like Core, or the grip on our mental state that Middle had. But the locomotion and movement of my body belonged solely to the Outer self that Nathan. Control over our abilities as well. Core and Middle had no grasp on my Mana or Skills.

Honestly, I…think it was an oversight from the System. It was possible, as I’d learned. The System wasn’t perfect, and my existence as a multi-part mind was granted to me by it. But I honestly didn’t think the System had understood what it was doing when it first granted me Ringed Mind, before the Skill had evolved. Even my Familiar Bond with Fade was explicitly linked to Outer. The others couldn’t hear a word from him unless Outer was present to broadcast it to them.

Thus, I had found, when the Outer was rendered unconscious…Middle and Core still remained awake.

Just…unable to reasonably act.

Outer had been defeated in battle by Archmage Daffyd, and the other two-thirds of my consciousness could do nothing about it.

Nothing but wait in silence, while I awaited our fate. For all that I knew, I could be seconds away from death. The way Daffyd had outright crushed my collarbones, partially ripping them out, I think, was a death sentence for most people. And it’s not like I expected him to stop there. The werewolf was utterly convinced that I was the person who had explicitly murdered his daughter.

Frankly, it was entirely possible he would have torn my throat out with his fangs immediately afterwards. But I couldn’t know that, and so…all I could do was sit…

And wait.

It’s not like this would be the first time.

And then…I was a bit surprised. Something happened then that I was…only somewhat surprised about. This was far from the first time. I had another link to my soul, after all. Something that was a connection to the whole of me, and not just Outer.

My link to Anima.

As if from a great distance, I heard a voice. It was almost as if it were approaching me, slowly wading through the murk of unconsciousness, casting a spotlight of purest emerald light as it did so. In a state of pure unconsciousness as I was, I had no eyes in which to shield, though I wanted to. Instead, all I could do was focus on the voice.

“…nathan…”

In a purely reflexive move, I tried to breathe in so I could call out. I felt like a fool moments later and instead concentrated. I had existed in states like this before, in the past. I had even communicated with spiritual beings when I myself was little better than a soul. I knew how to do this; it had just been a while since I’d needed to.

My words, echoing across the vast darkness, were far quieter than even the faint, whispering voice. And yet, I managed to vibrate my spirit in such a way that they still came out.

“…over…here…”

Instantly, even with the faint, weakened state I felt myself in, the spotlight fell upon me. Rapidly, the focal point of the light grew nearer to where I…existed? Floated? I had no way to tell. I didn’t even feel like I had a body right now.

And yet, within moments…

I felt cool, gentle hands reach down and pick me up. They felt larger than any hands I’d ever encountered before, greater in proportion to myself in the same way a mouse would be to a man. They cupped the indistinct, near formless mass that was my spirit, and drew me up and up and up.

Until I was held against what seemed to be their chest. Then, I felt it.

A heartbeat. Strong, steady, and most of all…

Strangely familiar to me.

I knew this rhythm. I felt it every single day. Every time that I reached out with my senses, any time in the last year, I had heard and felt this beat. The cadence was as familiar to me as the taste of clean water on my tongue, or the laughter of my daughter, chasing Fade through our garden.

I named it in a breath.

“…vereden…”

I felt, more than saw, the feeling of a gentle shake of a massive head. “No,” I was corrected gently. “I am not. Nathan…you must wake up.”

A sudden jolt. It was like lightning was being forced down a spine that didn’t exist. Suddenly, I could feel the consciousness of Outer waking up, but only the thought line. My physical self was still unconscious. Whatever had happened to us, it had been meant to bring me to full awareness, but not back to the realm of the living. My form coalesced from an indistinct mass of pure consciousness, a blob held together by nothing more than will, and into something far more familiar.

I was a man, once more, standing upon two cupped hands of enormous size, cradling me with surprisingly tenderness.

In the flash of a moment, I suddenly realised just what was happening…

And where.

That…only deepened my confusion, though.

The self that currently existed in this place looked up…and beheld a god.

No…that wasn’t quite right, was it?

A Great Spirit.

Anima.

She was…massive. Easily thirty feet tall, in comparison to myself, and more importantly, humanoid. Her form wasn’t quite that of a true woman, but she was close. Her flesh was a mottled brown and reminiscent of tree bark, smooth and uninterrupted by the crags and wrinkles so common of dendritic flesh. It was soft, judging by the pliancy of the hands I stood upon, and warm in a way I would expect a human’s to be. The same pulse that I had mistaken, somehow, for that of Vereden’s heart thundered through the flesh of the being who cupped me. They were…quite obviously female in nature, judging by the curvatures of their frame, but I didn’t focus upon that.

Instead, her head, and more importantly, her face, captured me. A pang of unexpected heartsickness overtook me then, and I drew in a sharp breath with lungs that didn’t exist.

I…hadn’t seen such motherly affection directed at me since…

I shuddered and pushed the thought away.

She was…homely. I wouldn’t call her beautiful in the traditional sense. She didn’t possess an otherworldly, impossibly precise symmetry that I would have expected the very orderly nature of Spirits to skew toward. Instead, I would call her features kind, like that of a weary, weathered mother, battered by the vagaries of life, and yet unbowed. From her enormous head flowed a mane of hair reminiscent of a weeping willow. It was as if her crown were that of a particularly stately example, countless flowing stands of interlocked, minuscule leaves that fell past her shoulders, and down below her knees, brushing whatever counted for a floor in this place. Two, patient, kind, knowing eyes of an emerald green, hauntingly familiar to me, sat recessed in her sockets, staring down at me.

That was the same shade of green as mine and Aveline.

And yet, for all of her otherworldly appearance, it wasn’t her eyes that had produced the spotlight which had found me.

It was what hovered behind her head.

I could only call it a mandala. A scintillating circle of pure, flowing emerald Aether, glowing through the darkness of our surroundings like a verdant, miniature star. It perfectly framed her head, and within it, I could see life.

All forms of it imaginable. From the smallest insect, to the swiftest birds, to the stoutest hound, to the strongest bear, to all forms of the biosphere. She was not the font of life…

But she was its steward.

“Anima,” I breathed, knowing who this was as intensely as I knew my own name. “How…why…is this…the Concord?”

A small, sad, small smile crossed Anima’s lips, then, and she shook her head ever so slightly. Still, the movement was enough to cause her willow-like mane to sway.

I couldn’t help but stare.

She was equally as titanic as Rhazal had been…and yet I wasn’t scared of her in the slightest.

“No,” Anima finally spoke, in a manner familiar to me. This was the soul speak I had encountered from many of the great existences of Vereden. A method similar to the spoken word, only with meaning conveyed not through the whistling of lung-borne wind, but by the spirit itself. “It is not. Look around, Nathaniel, and I suspect you can divine our location.”

Furrowing my brow, I did as she asked…

And beheld the wall.

Off to my left existed an edifice of such astounding, terrifying size that it threatened to swallow my mind to behold it. There was no end to it that I could see, and no beginning. It dominated the entire horizon, stretching off into what seemed to be infinitely that it almost seemed to curve around unto itself. The geometry of it was so alien to my understanding that just looking at it almost gave me a panic attack. I nearly questioned how I could possibly miss the structure, but I hadn’t been all there until Anima had awoken Outer.

It was composed of something…familiar. An endless expanse of impossibly dense grey cloud, hardened to such an extent that nothing could penetrate it. It was hard not to hate it on sight, and why should I not? I saw it every day, now.

Those were the clouds of the Skyfall. Only…without the frozen lightning.

I shuddered and looked away from the wall. “We’re not inside the Concord,” I licked my oddly dry lips. “We’re outside of it. Directly outside it.”

Anima’s smile grew, and she nodded at me approvingly. “Well done, Nathaniel. You are correct. What you see before you,” She glanced over at the wall with visible disdain on her enormous face. “Is that which separates the Concord from the physical realm. They exist within both, and they are mirrors of each other.”

“Only…Fynneas’s Mantle is in one place,” I said slowly, considering the matter. “It exists fully within the physical, and is being…filtered in the skies above Vereden.”

“I…suspect you are correct, Nathaniel,” Anima sighed.

“But how are we even here? Why are we here? The last thing I remember…” I paused, suddenly, and my hands flew up to grasp at my collarbone. I don’t know what I was expecting, but it wasn’t to feel perfectly intact flesh.

No injuries at all.

I blinked, staring down at the…admittedly, spiritual approximation of flesh and bone I had on this form. I sighed. “Ah. I’m fine here, but in real life…”

“You died,” Anime confirmed sadly, sending the bottom of my stomach falling away. “But only briefly. Long enough for your spirit to be cast from its shell. My understanding is that you are in a coma, in the waking world. I cannot know what is happening with your physical self, but I felt it the moment you came so close to dissipation. I came to find you.”

I was silent for a moment, absorbing that. So.

I suppose I had finally met someone or something that was beyond me. I’d gotten a bit cocky, I suppose. I was strong for my level; I had no doubt about that. Strong for my level of advancement.

But I wasn’t strong enough to stand toe to toe with an Archmage. Even with Bleddyn by my side.

And my arrogance had nearly cost Aveline her father.

Fool.

My eyes wandered shamefully over to the wall before I winced and averted my gaze. “How did I end up here? And…how are you here? I thought you were trapped in the core.”

Anima’s enormous eyes followed mine, and she scowled at the wall separating her from her home. “We are here for a reason. I know not how you have been incapacitated in the short time since we spoke, but…I have been busy. So close to the bounds of the Concord as we are, the nature of reality is more…malleable. This is how I have been communicating with you, by essentially bouncing my being off of this monstrosity. My voice reaches you along that line. I…” She sighed, then, sounding deeply frustrated. “I have…made contact with another, whom I thought I could avoid, and invited them here to a meeting between us. Normally, it would have been quite difficult to establish communication with them. But I know…intimately just where this existence hides itself. I wished to avoid this if at all possible…but if what you told me about the machinations of this Dwarven mortal is true, then I must…”

“Must what?” I heard a sudden voice hiss from out of the darkness. “Must lower yourself, oh She Who Breathes? Must beg for assistance from an old friend?”

My lips parted as I saw an immediate scowl grow on Anima’s face. In the darkness surrounding us, I was starting to see faint, almost slithering movements, highlighted by the emerald mandala that rotated behind her head.

I…I recognised that voice. Hell, I recognised the form slithering just out of sight.

“You are no friend of mine, old one,” Anima said scornfully. “You are an ancient coward, hiding in a hole for fear of powers greater than you. If not for what you know, and where you are, I would never have-”

“Never have what?” The sibilant voice hissed, from somewhere off my right. “Never have come crawling on bent hands and knees to save you and the rest of your band of upstarts? I doubt it. This was inevitable.”

Finally, an enormous triangular head, clad in jet black scales and crowned by gnarled, ancient tree roots emerged from the darkness. Glittering amber eyes first looked at Anima, and then visibly dismissed her to land on me.

The mouth of the odd, snake-like Spirit that had called itself ‘Nehushtan’ before smiled at me.

“Hello again, failure. You’ve certainly bungled up things once more.”
