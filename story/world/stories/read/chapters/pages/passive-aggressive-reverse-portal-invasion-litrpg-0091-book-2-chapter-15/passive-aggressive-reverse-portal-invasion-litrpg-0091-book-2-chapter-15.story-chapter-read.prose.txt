Movies lie. Anime especially. When somebody tosses a spell at you, there’s no time to monologue, no time to quip off a clever one-liner. There’s definitely no time to activate a magical girl transformation or go through some prolonged Kamehameha of your own.

I managed two things. I ducked my head beneath the edge of my shield. Then, having spotted a hint of orange, I activated my Fire Resistance ability that’d let me heal from fire for thirty seconds.

Of course, that didn’t help me any. The orange hallucinated by my mind was the combined red and yellow background of the walls, not any effect from the actual talent.

The earthen missile hit me a moment later.

At first there was just pain. Pain mixed liberally with chaos and disorientation as I hurtled backwards, spiraling entirely out of control. When I finally got a measure of control, I found myself sliding down the steep hill, and everything hurt.

Afterward, I had to count myself lucky, that I hadn’t hit any magical traps on my way downhill. Yet, as I fought my way back to my feet, there was just one sensation in my mind. Pure, uncontrolled fury. I started walking, then running, back up toward the gate. I checked for debuffs and saw a newly emerged trio of debuffs. Without even looking properly, I used my skill to remove the highest-Tier one of them.

My arm stopped feeling like it was getting torn out of its socket. Nifty. The rest of my body still felt like it’d been thrown down a goddamn cliff! Steve and Victoria met me halfway, skidding and stumbling as they fled down the slope.

“Dios, Liam, I thought-“

I didn’t stop. In fact, I sped up even further, my legs taking me up and past her in long, loping steps. Both Victoria and Steve shouted at me to turn, to stop. My mind was too filled with rage to even think about it.

When I came closer, I did slow down. And I started yelling. “Who fucking does that? I said hello, you bloody wankers! Who attacks somebody over that? This is the second time that’s happened! Is it my face? It’s my face, innit?” I slowly realized that I might be a teeny weeny bit out of it, and tried to reel myself back in a bit. With a growl, I directed my voice at the wall again. “I came here in peace. To talk. But so help me, if anybody attacks me again, I will tear your heads off and talk to those!”

I ground to a halt, twenty meters from the small gate, eyes still fixed on the battlements above. “What will it be? Do we talk, or do we fight?” I exhaled hard through gritted teeth. “I did come as a representative of my kind, but I’ll admit, right now, I could do both.”

For ten seconds, twenty, nothing happened. There was still nothing to be heard, not a single word, except for a shuffling noise that might be the scuffing of booted feet up above. Finally, ever so slowly, the gate slid open silently. A tall, thin Hiscariat stuck out its head, sporting emaciated drawn features and dark brown skin, like leather left in the sun. Then, the rest of its thin body followed as it stepped out entirely, draped in a gray and purple robe. It said nothing, but the features looked nervous.

I glanced up to see if I was being set up for an ambush from above. Nothing.

Then a voice appeared. Not from the Hiscariat. Within my mind. A cautious, halting voice made itself known, as if I was wearing a set of invisible headphones. “Hello. We are so sorry.”

The next few minutes went by in a flash. Once I realized that: A, I wasn’t going to be attacked and, B, I was hurting all over, my body decided to macrodose me with an overly generous serving of adrenaline. It was all I could do to keep my composition and not flop over right on the spot.

Fortunately, Steve and Victoria were right on my heels. They backed me up, Victoria with a string of Spanish curses, Steve with a hand on my shoulder, steadying me.

The Hiscariat kept talking inside my head. Judging from the exclamations from the others, inside their heads too. “I will repeat. We are terribly sorry for the incident. The Mages at their posts made a horrible judgement call and will be reprimanded, heavily. This was very much not intended. I wouldn’t want you to think that all Hiscariat are violent unthinking brutes. That is not the case at all. Usually, we prefer diplomacy above all.” She was rambling.

Heh. First time I ever experienced telepathy, and it was somebody pissing themselves, rambling and out of control. I steadied my hands, tried to fight through the adrenaline and focus. “Okay. Let’s assume we’re willing to put aside the fact that you guys tried to put half a mountain through my skull. In order to get there, why don’t we do this. I will act as if nobody tried to murder the crap out of me, and you try to welcome us as if we were esteemed diplomats. What would you do then?”

She blinked. Then she spread her arms-

I took a half-step back before I caught myself.

She spread her tentacles. The robes were entirely purple. The gray came from the two long, tentacles affixed to the body, a bit lower than where arms would usually be, extended in what I assumed was supposed to be a welcoming gesture. “Welcome to the Telvan Outpost. The Hiscariat appreciates you making the journey and hope that you will enjoy your stay.”

Steve smoothly accepted the welcome and answered kindly. ”So do we. We have looked forward to talking with you and long to learn more about your kind.”

Five minutes later, we were inside the wall. Not on the inside, but in the wall itself. It turned out that the wall was wide enough that there was room for several rough chambers before emerging on the other side.

The Hiscariat, who went by the name Mind-Aloft, took care of us. After that first welcome, they very much changed their tacks.

The tiny chamber we sat in was sparsely furnished and utilitarian. It was also clearly used as a meeting room of sorts, because it held a large wooden table and comfortable, if tall, chairs. Once we’d been offered a drink of something traditionally Hiscariat, (Warm and savoury too. Like a blend of herbal tea and beef broth.) Mind-Aloft noticed just how hurt I was and insisted that she’d send for a healer and help me improve before they’d show us the city. Until then, she’d answer any questions we might have.

Admittedly, there were quite a few injuries. I hadn’t truly taken in. The singular attack, as well as the ensuing high-speed contact with rocks, dirt and whatever else came in my way, removed forty percent of my health. It also sprained my right pinkie and granted me another debuff that made me chuckle in between the stinging, pulsating pain.

Skin Abrasion (medium)

Your body has suffered a lot of friction damage and a significant part of your outer and inner skin has been removed. It will require a period of rest to regrow.

-15 to Pain Tolerance skill.

-5 to Disease Resistance skill

35 hours, 59 minutes remaining

“That is ugly!” Victoria exclaimed with badly disguised excitement, as she squatted, examining the raw, oozing skin on my hand and… well, pretty much any piece of skin that hadn’t been covered by armor. My ass too, since the friction had pushed my protective leather halfway down my buttocks. “You should probably not try to flirt with the locals until that’s nice and healed up, güero.”

I chuckled. “I’ll try to keep it in my pants. But hey. At least, I finally got proof that my old PE teacher in Germany was wrong, and also a sadist. Road Rash is an actual injury!”

Leaping to her feet, Victoria asked. “Hey, Mind.”

“Mind-Aloft, please.” She bowed her head. “There are more than fifteen Hiscariat with primary variations of ‘Mind’ in their name.”

“Yeah, Vic.” I winked.

Rolling her eyes at me, she moved up close to the Hiscariat, grinning at the seated woman.

I was impressed by how she didn’t seem fazed by her form. To me, it was the uncanny valley effect of her. The body was well hidden by the voluminous purple cloak, but looked human enough. Same for her upper body and head. Her stubby, afro-like hair was thick enough it almost looked like something apart from hair, and she was about a head and a half taller than myself. Even so, she could sort of pass for a basketball player who’d recently been sick. Until you saw the tentacles. The gray-and-flesh-colored appendages moved constantly, almost as if they were independent of her control, and they proved her to be very and truly inhuman.

That being said, she was by far the nicest person I’d met on Ademia to date. She hadn’t tried to kill me once!

“Tell me, Mind-Aloft.” Victoria practically beamed, placing her chair backwards right in front of the Hiscariat and plopping down. “Tell me about the telepathy!”

“Telepathy? You mean – oh.” The voice in my head cut off abruptly, before resuming. “I forgot. We rarely have visitors of other species.” There was a clicking sound, not unlike the sound of somebody clicking their pen, which my mind automatically associated with the clearing of a throat. “To us, the Hiscariat, it is not telepathy. It is merely speaking. One of the first skills we learn as children.”

She started speaking out loud instead. “Of course, we do have vocal folds. Yet, nearly all Hiscariat prefer mental speech, as it is less… taxing.“

The mental switch was super weird, as her spoken voice was a lot fuller and deeper than the mental one. Also, there was an emphasis to her point, as if there was more behind her point than what she said.

Victoria didn’t care. ”Yeah, that’s not what I’m asking. How do I learn it?”

I blinked. That absolutely should’ve been my own first thought. If that was a possibility, it would make our lives so much easier.

There was that clicking noise again. “I honestly do not know. Usually, those not of the collective do not have the capabilities or the mindset to learn.”

“But you said your children learn. And we’re already listening to your voice in our heads.” Victoria tapped her own head. “I’m a quick learner. How do I go from listening to speaking?”

Mind-Aloft leaned back on her chair. Her tentacles wafted airily. “I- How does one breathe? I’m not sure I can explain. Mental speech is an intrinsic part of our society, of our being. It defines who we are, how we act. It-“

“Wait.” Victoria cut her off, frowning. “If listening to mental speech is something passive that anybody can do… how do you go about having a private conversation? Or is mental speech like normal speech, that it only goes so far?”

She sighed out loud. When her voice appeared in our heads again, it had a different lilt. More exasperated. “This wasn’t how I expected my first ever meeting with an outsider to go.”

“Ahuevo.” Victoria said. “We defy expectations.”

“…That you do. Now, as to listening, anybody can do that. But with concentration, practice and higher Mental attributes, you can listen really, really well. This is what makes Hiscariat society something singular. Everything we do, everything we say, it’s all out in the open. As such, we rarely communicate anything that we would not want others to overhear.”

I reeled at the implications. “So, if I got a dodgy kebab on my way back from the bar and spend half the night on the can with watery bowels, crying that it hurts… anybody will be able to hear that?” I paused, then added, with increasing panic. “Did everybody hear me say that?”

She tittered out loud. “No, Liam. Nobody heard that, because you aren’t communicating mentally.”

“Oh. Right. I knew that.” I rubbed my temples, fighting the blush that was threatening to reach my ears.

“However, the point is accurate enough. We modulate our mental speech. Yet, you can expect anybody out there to be able to hear your mental speech as long as you are within city limits. There are a few exceptions, privacy talents that some people choose,” Her voice carried enough disdain there was no discussion what she thought about those people. “But we strive for honesty and truth in all our dealings.”

Steve was the one who reached the finish line first. “What you’re saying is that anybody who decides to talk to us out loud instead of mentally might be wanting to hide what they’re doing?”

“That’s exactly what I’m saying. There are people – underhanded, horrible people out there who will not raise their voice to the heavens, but speak the language of secrecy.”

I frowned. “Wait. Does that mean that you’re all horribly prejudiced against all other species who can’t speak mentally?”

Her tentacles fidgeted at her side. “Of course not. You are incapable of doing better. That is not your fault.”

I nearly snorted, but stopped myself. That was an absolute yes.

“Mental Power or Control? Which one influences listening most?” Victoria asked.

“Both?” Mind-Aloft smiled. “Mental Power will influence the range at which you can listen. Mental Control will help you decipher and separate what is there.”

Steve nodded happily. A second later, I felt it, as my mind… improved. I checked to see, and yeah. He’d buffed Mental Control for all of us.

“Okay. That’s done then. Is there anything else we need to know before we go out there?” Victoria jabbed a thumb in the direction of the city.

“Nothing much. Our healer should arrive in seconds. Then, we will be going straight to the palace for your introduction.”

“To the… palace?” I blinked, looking down over myself. What few parts of my body weren’t currently oozing or bloody were most definitely dirty and reeking. “Don’t we get a chance to, like, clean up a bit first?”

“Not needed. The Hiscariat place very little importance on the physical side of things. It is much more important that you conduct yourself with grace and control.”

I snorted. Yeah. Of course. Nothing I did better.

“There’s just one thing. I do believe that, maybe, you should be prepared for when you leave this room. You might find that you’ve attracted a bit of attention.”
