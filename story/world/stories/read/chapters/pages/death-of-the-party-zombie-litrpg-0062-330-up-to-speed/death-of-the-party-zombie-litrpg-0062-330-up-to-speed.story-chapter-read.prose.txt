[Sally Danger - Human, Zombie][Class: Necromancer][Awakening: Avatar of Life][Affinities: Stamina+, Power, Vitality][Group: Outsiders]

The STAR+ kept things simple. Affinities were based on your personality and life experiences, with most adventurers having two bonuses. These Stats determined what sort of abilities you could learn, which in turn gave you your class. There was still plenty of vague psudeo-magic to it, but it felt like a natural extension to what was there previously.

Sally closed down her Stat window and stretched out, her feet on top of Wolf’s fuzzy stomach as he slept in the arena stands. Dent was a genius for bringing them here for training. While the dusty gladatorial pit had seen little use since the sword tournament, the System imposed a very important rule in this small area.

If you died in the pit, you would be resurrected outside.

Naturally, this made it the prime location for the Outsiders, Guardians, and Sanctuary’s Bastion to go all out and train against each other. The downside was that it had to be a structured and limited fight. They had to take turns.

Other than the large bear, who lay upside down wedged between rows of seats with his tongue lolling out, the rest of the Outsiders sat with her in this area of the otherwise empty stands. Max idly cut through a pack of cards one-handed. Ren had an eagle-eyed glare focused on the pit occupants. Archie was busy writing in his own arcane tome, barely paying attention to the proceedings.

In the oval ring, a dozen feet down from the raised sands, Theo faced off against Humphrey. The rest of the Guardians—aside from Wolf—were currently having a pep talk with Dent in one of the staging areas. Dotted around the rest of the seats were several members of Bastion, but they were mostly keeping to themselves or in small groups.

Theo stood casually, one hand in his pocket while the other held his sledgehammer on his shoulder. The sunlight reflected off his glasses, making them appear pure white. By contrast, the Death Knight stood with his massive greastword at the ready, crimson flame lapping at the back of his helmet. Empty eye sockets regarded the vampire with thinning patience.

“If you’re hoping things will go the same as in the tournament, I’m afraid things have changed.” Theo grinned.

“Better yet, I am looking forward to humbling you in a real fight.”

Theo narrowed his eyes and used Confirm Identity.

“Aha, you managed to acquire your own Awakening.” Theo grinned again. “I knew something was off when you suddenly turned down the Guardian offer.”

Humphrey grunted. “I should have expected you to have some way of spying on my details. Such an insidious ability suits you, bloodsucker. Are we to battle, or does this new information give you pause?”

“The only pause I’d enact is to yawn. How about this - round one, no abilities?”

Humphrey's skeletal face grinned. “A display of pure martial prowess? Yes, let's proceed.”

“This should be interesting.” Max stopped shuffling his cards. “Theo has been rather coy about showing off his power in front of us so far.”

Ren gave the barest shake of her head. “He is being solely defensive here. If he is on the back foot, it is only because he chooses to be.”

“Still,” the magician continued, “I've never seen such quick movement from a knight-type Class.”

The former Outsiders hadn't been sitting idle while the rest were out on other worlds. It was impressive that Humphrey had earned an Awakened Class in such a short time, but in a way, he was built for it. Ever since he was just a floating Observer he had tried to protect Sally, and now he put all that care into keeping Sanctuary safe.

Theo slid back as the Death Knight struck him with the pommel of the greatsword. “Oof. You're not going easy on me, are you, old man?”

Humphrey grunted and changed stance. “It's not often my training dummies talk back to me. Will you start putting in some effort too, or are you hoping I'll tire early?”

“I don't want to use up all of my energy before the more worthy opponents have a go.” He shrugged, driving the nail in further. “I'll give you time to buff up to make it worth my while.”

The flames at the back of the Death Knight’s head grew wilder. “Your arrogance will be your undoing.”

A wash of yellow light covered Humphrey before a second green one followed.

“Iron Defense and Greater Fortitude,” Theo noted. “Next you'll do Weapon of Judgement and Compel Duel me.”

The greatsword burst into golden flame as the knight pointed it out toward the vampire. “No,” Humphrey intoned, his voice deep and emotionless.

Theo's sledgehammer bounced softly on the sandy ground. “Oh, that's new.”

The magician was on the fence. It was certainly plausible. Theo had seemed competent and powerful when helping on his world, and here in Sanctuary he was much stronger. A simple plan, as was often the best when stakes were high. After agreeing, they prepared themselves and gave Theo a nod.

She could sense Max in there, and see the occasional dim flash of purple. Wolf’s roaring was unmistakable as well. Firing blind wasn’t ideal, but the chances of hitting were never zero. As she calmed her breathing, she sent her dove off into the darkness so that Theo couldn’t appear and take her out easily.

The ground shook. Above the sounds of battle rose a terrible, guttural sound. Ren scowled as the dire horn vibrated the air, aching at her elven ears. Even after what she’d endured in the past, there was something about this that chilled her. Max should have dispelled the darkness by now, but maybe they were making a retreat to recover first.

A shape emerged from the edge of the magical fog. Despite knowing it could be an ally, Explosive Shot+ loaded into the rifle.

The deafening knell resounded once more, as something pulled itself into view. A dragon, although its snout was short, with large leathery ears sitting atop its head. It had no skin, and the giant wings were nothing but tattered scraps of decaying flesh over pointed bone. The creature looked more like a golem than a true skeleton, with blood seeping through the cracks of hundreds of mismatched bones. Two bright crimson eyes glared down at the elf.

“Avatar of Death,” she whispered. “Theo really wasn’t messing around.”

That probably meant the other two were dead. All previous strategies out of the window. Judging by how quickly the vampire had taken them out, he wasn’t about to go through the usual motions of testing strength and working on improvement. He had determined nothing was to gain from their duel. Hitting him with any of her abilities would only end up with her in the staging room in a matter of seconds.

Things had happened too fast. Unexpected. She needed control of the battlefield. To be back on normal footing. What she needed was…

The flow of realization felt like a clean river flowing through her head. Even as the macabre Avatar loomed closer, Ren’s body language relaxed and her focus narrowed once more.

With a dry grin on her face, she stood and placed the butt of her sniper rifle into the ground.

“Avatar of Order,” she commanded.
