It took all of that day and most of the rest of the next for the wildfire to finally burn itself out from around the lake.

However, it was far from completely extinguished. Just moved on. Alice saw it raging on as a bright line of orange that crawled up and down the far hills.

She had a difficult time sleeping that night and only hoped she had not put anyone in danger.

Natura assured her that the fire was racing through dead parts of the forest—and if it was dead, that meant it was likely host to nests of Tree Wraiths. No one could live among them.

The fact that Natura, who loved all things natural, seemed to accept the fire’s destruction went a long way to making Alice feel better. Either way, the fire was out of her control.

Iggy and Spark were in charge of collecting their meals that first long night. There was no sign of the blood eels in the lake waters, but it wasn’t exactly a robust ecosystem, either. (Natura’s words, not Alice’s.)

However, Iggy did find a few frogs for himself, Prim, Spark, and Aconite to eat. Natura was happy to locate some tasty roots in the cooling ashes by the lakeside. Meanwhile, Numi had her stash of coins. Alice made herself content with some fish that Iggy managed to scrounge up—though they were hardly bigger than minnows.

Using his fire, Iggy roasted everything but the coins in mud bricks. For his effort, he received another level in his Fire Breath skills.

“It seems that you receive your levels not only for experience, but when you learn to use your skills in different ways,” Alice said to Iggy, who was puffed up with pride.

“We should strive to remember that,” Prim added. “These early levels are supposed to be the easiest to achieve. They grow harder to level as we gain experience.”

Numi hiccuped around a silver coin she just bit in half. “Sounds like a good problem to have. By the time I get my Appraisal skill high enough to worry about, I’ll be quite adept at appraising.”

Alice agreed. “And not to mention leveling also unlocks access to even more skills which you then have to level in turn.”

“Yes, it is a never-ending cycle,” Prim said, and relished the thought of that by biting into a raw frog.

Alice shuddered and looked away.

Late afternoon, Iggy flew to the lakeside to check the status of the ashes. The fire had burned out from their area hours before, but he was worried about pockets of embers that lay just under the ashy surface.

His Ultimate Armor provided him with protection from the heat, and he stomped and snorted and rooted around and generally did his best to try to get burned. When he returned, gray with ash, until he dipped into the water to clean himself, he announced that the land was safe enough to walk on.

Alice couldn’t wait. She hadn’t been looking forward to another cold evening out on their muddy lake island. Besides, she could swear that the dark egg was calling her. It wouldn’t be long until it hatched.

“Let’s go,” she said, and plunged into the water.

Traveling back through the rest was an odd experience. Everything had burnt to the ground, leaving upright charcoal pillars in place of what had been mighty trunks.

Natura flicked out her tongue to taste the ashes. She nodded to herself. “This will be very helpful in renewing the soil. No doubt there will be a great deal of erosion this winter, and some mudslides of course. But new seeds will have blown in by spring and will start to take root. In a few years this land will look very green, indeed.”

“Yes, but it will take many more years for the trees to grow and for the forest to truly return,” Alice said, slightly out of breath. She had increased her pace to a fast walk, almost a jog. That wasn’t easy with deep ash and unsure footing underneath.

“Yes,” Natura agreed. “Which is why those Tree Wraiths were evil, evil things.”

Numi scuttled along at Alice’s heels, plowing her own path in the ashes. But at Natura’s words, she cocked her head and asked, “Why are the tree wraiths here? And those blood eels?”

“I don’t know, but they are not from our land.”

“Yeah, you said that before, too. How do you know?” Numi pressed.

Natura shrugged. “I can feel that they are not native here. It’s part of my elemental manipulation. It tells me what does not belong... and those tree wraiths, blood eels, and death beetles most certainly do not belong.”

“Death beetles?” Alice asked.

“Oh yes, they bore under the barks of trees and emerge when a new corpse begins to rot. Then they eat the food, multiply, and bore back into the trees. This kills the trees and keeps anything else from harvesting the meat.”

Alice shuddered and redoubled her efforts to not trip and fall. The ash coating everything looked like snow, hiding dips and holes. She did not want to sprain an ankle out there.

Natura seemed happy enough with the topic and went on. “The rot of the forest was spreading. It would have reached that town with Alice’s shop within a few decades. No, this fire was really for the best.”

“I’d like to believe you, but this ash is almost knee-high in places, and we need to move faster!” Alice said, feeling a sudden surge of urgency. Though she couldn’t quite say where. She only knew that if they kept at this pace, they wouldn’t make it in time.

The pull of the egg was growing stronger.

She increased her speed until she was at a run, heading directly toward where the egg was calling her.

Distantly, she heard her dragons cry out their own warnings. Iggy was worried about any tree wraiths that might have survived the fire. Prim reminded her to please be wary of pockets of hot coals. Those would be invisible under the ash.

Alice ignored them both.

Let a tree wraith come, she thought with unusual fierceness. That was what she had Iggy and Spark for.

As for the coals... well, that was a danger, but there was nothing she could do but forge forward.

She did not need Natura’s directions on where to go. Her heart told her exactly where the egg was, and it screamed at her that she was needed now.

Reaching it at last, she fell to her knees and clawed through the soft ash. Her fingers grazed the shell, and she pulled back, shocked. The egg was so hot, it was a degree or two from burning her, like hot candle wax.

The other dragons gathered around her. All fell silent, watching.

Biting her lip, she plunged her hand in and pulled it out. There was already a long, jagged crack down the length of the shell.

She had made it in time. Just.

Alice activated her Aspect Token.

“Yes,” she said, and had a moment to wonder if she would be rejected by the hatchling. After all, this was not an egg that Prim had laid for her. Like Natura, this was a dragon who would have to accept her.

She needn’t have worried. With a feeling of dark joy, the little hatchling inside accepted.

Something that was more thought than voice slithered through her mind: I see your power.

Before Alice could process that, the eggshell shattered in her hand like glass to reveal her newest dragon. She was like a black diamond, hard and so dark that for a moment it was hard for Alice to tell where her curled limbs were.

Then the little dragon blinked equally black eyes and Alice picked out her general shape. She was somewhere between Numi and Iggy—lizard-like but without Iggy’s ropy length. Her head was different from them both, flat with a shovel-like skull with eyes and nostrils on top.

Like a grave digger's shovel, Alice thought.

But it was the wings that really caught the eye. The dark dragon was more overtly magical than any of the others because her wings were made entirely of black smoke with lighter gray edges, almost like ash.

The dark dragon raised her head and nodded respectfully at Alice. “I greet you,” she purred with a voice both smoky and dangerous.

“Hello,” Alice said and reached to stroke one finger down her back. There were tiny bumps along her spine, the beginnings of spikes.

Like Natura, her skills were already locked in. It seemed this dragon knew exactly who she was.

Skills:

Smoke Form

Curse of Ash

Burnt Sacrifice

Alice blinked. “That is... quite an impressive set of skills.” Though privately, she thought they sounded extremely ominous.

“Yes, my powers are made to serve She of Many Dragons.”

“We are less about service and more like a family,” Alice said, feeling increasingly more awkward by the moment. “What should I call you?”

The dark little dragon blinked her eyes in confusion. She had two sets of eyelids, one going up and down, the other side to side.

“What is your name?” Alice asked, clarifying.

“My name is whatever you decide to call me.”

That was different, too. With the exception of her sub-aspects, the others all knew their name. Strangely enough, the only thing Alice could think of was: Destructor.

But... that was not the tone she was trying to set.

At a loss, she looked at Prim.

Her first dragon knew her mind better than anyone else. Perched on Alice’s shoulder, she sat up. “I think her name should be something beautiful.”

The little dragon was a bit oddly shaped, but she was no doubt beautiful.

“How about Bellanyx?” Numi said. “It means dark beauty.”

“Bella for short,” Alice agreed. “What do you think?”

The dark dragon blinked again. She seemed as much at a loss as Alice had been before. Clearly, she had been expecting something fearsome. “If that pleases the master,” she said.

“It would please me if you did not call me master. My name is Alice. This is Primordialis or Prim, Numismatis or Numi, Igcendiorum or Iggy, and Natura. And of course, these are my two sub-aspects, Spark and Aconite.”

“I have a sub-aspect too,” Numi said. “Her name is Fixie and she’s helping at Alice’s shop a long way away from here.”

Bella nodded, taking everything in with solemnity. “And what is this place?” She turned to look around at the ashy land. “I sense you were the one who caused all of this delicious destruction?”

“That is a long story,” Alice said. “But I believe it’s past time we found our way out. I’m afraid it will be a long journey.” She sighed. “While we walk, why don’t you tell us about your, uh, skills?”
