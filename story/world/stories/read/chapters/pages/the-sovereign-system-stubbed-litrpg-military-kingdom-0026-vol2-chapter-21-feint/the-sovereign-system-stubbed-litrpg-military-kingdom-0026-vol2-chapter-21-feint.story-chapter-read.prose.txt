The middle-aged man seemed momentarily taken aback, but his smile returned a few seconds later—though this time, it felt forced.

“Very well,” he said shortly, flipping open the book to a random page. “Among the five needs of the common people—”

“None is so constant as water,” Luke interrupted smoothly. “To guide it upward from river to field is to grant life to dry land. Page 137,” he added matter-of-factly.

The man’s eyes narrowed. He quickly turned to another page, this time angling the book away to obscure its contents. Luke, stifling a chuckle, waited patiently.

“To master the way of wood and—”

“And water, is to serve the people—and so the stars themselves may take notice,” Luke finished once more, his tone calm. “Page 293.”

He kept his expression neutral, careful not to show smugness, though inwardly, he was quite pleased with the man's reaction. From the moment he’d noticed him in the library, Luke had already guessed the man’s identity.

The book snapped shut with a sharp clap, the sound echoing through the empty floor.

“It seems I was mistaken,” the man said, his smile now more genuine. “I apologize if I interrupted your reading.”

Luke gave a respectful bow. “It’s no trouble, Head Librarian. Thank you for checking on me.”

The man raised a brow, his green eyes appraising Luke with renewed interest—as if he'd just heard something particularly curious. “So, you guessed who I am. I’m not sure whether to be impressed or concerned.”

“It was just a guess, sir. Forgive my impudence, but you carry the air of someone both knowledgeable and important,” Luke replied, his tone deliberately courteous.

“Oho, you do have a way with words, Luke Drakon,” the man said with a crooked smile. “From the stories I’ve heard, I expected someone a bit more... inflammatory. It seems the rumors didn’t do you justice.”

Now it was Luke’s turn to be caught off guard. He hadn’t expected the Head Librarian to know who he was—especially given that this was their first meeting.

Before he could respond, the man continued. “I heard you arrived not long after the siege of Clayton City… My uncle died in that battle.”

Luke’s gaze sharpened. For the first time, he studied the man’s face more closely—and now, he could see it: the resemblance.

“Your uncle was Master Gale?” he asked, trying to temper his surprise.

“Mmm.” The man nodded. “He was my father’s brother. We weren’t exactly close in recent years.”

“He was a great general,” Luke said softly. “And a brilliant teacher.” The memory of Master Gale falling atop the city walls—skewered by the enemy general’s spear—flashed unbidden in his mind.

“But a lousy uncle,” the man added with a chuckle. “I’m Tristan Gale, Head Librarian of the Royal Academy. A pleasure to meet you,” he said, cupping his fist in a formal greeting.

Luke mirrored the gesture and added a slight bow to show deference. Though his rank as Viscount didn’t require it, he understood that small gestures of respect often paved the way to deeper connections.

“The pleasure is mine,” Luke replied. “I have classes this morning, but I’d very much like to speak again—if that’s all right with you?”

“Of course,” Tristan replied, waving a hand casually. “You can find me on the tenth floor. Perhaps we can exchange knowledge sometime. I’m still curious about the story behind your invention of gunpowder.”

Luke gave a polite nod, expressed his farewells, and made his way down the stairwell to the ground floor, where the lectures were held. Inwardly, he was brimming with joy.

He had been hoping to meet the Head Librarian at some point during his time at the Royal Academy. The mystery of what lay on the library’s upper floors had gnawed at his curiosity from the beginning—and now, the door to those secrets had been cracked open.

Even better, he had made a good first impression. Not only had he shown proper respect, but it turned out they were related—through Master Gale, though the man had passed.

But that could wait.

For now, Luke had lectures to attend.

He entered the first lecture hall, only to find it completely empty. His brow furrowed as he tried to recall the day’s schedule.

“Ah… it’s a practical class today,” he muttered, quickly turning around and heading out.

Like the academy in Clayton City, the Royal Academy divided its curriculum between theory and practical classes. While most lessons focused on battlefield tactics and administrative duties, the nobility insisted their children also learn the art of war firsthand—even if most of them would never step foot on a battlefield.

Luke left the library and took the nearest path toward the training grounds, which lay nestled within one of the academy’s expansive gardens. A wide courtyard soon opened before him—pristine stone tiles stretching nearly the length of a football field.

Master Hail stood at the center with a group of students already assembled. The lesson had clearly begun.

The older man twisted his drooping mustache between two fingers as he spotted Luke approaching. Without missing a beat, Luke cupped his fist and offered a respectful bow.

“Apologies for being late, Instructor.”

He braced himself, half-expecting a reprimand.

But Master Hail only smiled.

“Just in time, Luke,” he said. “I was about to demonstrate the effectiveness of feints. Would you care to assist with the demonstration?”

Luke’s stomach dropped. He knew all too well that Master Hail was no ordinary instructor—the man was a true master swordsman and had even offered Luke private instruction, something he hadn’t yet found the time to accept.

Is this payback for being late? Luke wondered, his palms beginning to sweat.

“Yes, Instructor,” he replied calmly, forcing down the unease rising in his chest.

“Excellent,” Master Hail said with a grin. He strode over to the weapons rack at the edge of the courtyard and selected two wooden swords, handing one to Luke a moment later.

Seeing that they’d be using practice weapons, Luke let out a quiet sigh of relief. Though the system had granted him thirty years' worth of sword proficiency, his body still hadn’t fully synchronized with that knowledge. There was a real chance his reactions might lag behind his instincts.

Getting humiliated in front of the entire class wasn’t exactly on his agenda today.

He glanced to the side and caught sight of Kayson and Sebastian watching from the edge of the courtyard. Their presence made the pressure even worse.

Luke offered them a small smile, hoping to reassure not only them—but himself.

“Alright,” Master Hail announced, turning to address the students. “We’ll be sparring with these wooden swords. I want all of you to pay close attention to how I use feints—and how Luke reacts.”

As soon as the man entered his ready stance, a prickle crawled up the back of Luke’s neck. His instincts flared like an alarm bell.

He stared at the simple wooden sword in the instructor’s hand, feeling a mix of disbelief and apprehension.

Despite the casual pose, Luke couldn’t find a single opening. Every angle he considered—high, low, wide, center—was sealed off. It was as if the man had no weaknesses at all.

Is this what a master swordsman looks like? Luke thought, his throat suddenly dry.

“If you won’t attack,” Master Hail said, his lips curving into a playful smile, “then I’ll come to you.”

The instructor surged forward without warning, shoulder rotating like a coiled spring as if to thrust straight through Luke’s heart. On instinct, Luke raised his sword to intercept—but his eyes widened a moment later.

It was a feint.

But not just that.

The tip of the wooden blade was now resting lightly against his neck, hovering an inch above his shoulder.

If it had been a real sword, his head would be rolling on the tiles.

“A simple feint can decide the outcome of a duel,” Master Hail said casually, his blade already lowered. “You can throw them in at any time, but the key is mixing them with genuine attacks. That way, your opponent can’t tell which is which.”

Luke nodded, slightly embarrassed. He had known feints would be part of the demonstration. And still, he’d been caught clean on the very first move.

It wasn’t that he was a bad swordsman. Far from it—his system-enhanced skill placed him leagues ahead of most. But in this exchange, he had relied too much on instinct over technique. Worse, the instructor’s speed was unlike anything he’d ever seen.

He makes Michael Ring look like a child, Luke thought, swallowing hard.

“Shall we try again?” Master Hail asked, raising a brow.

Luke nodded silently and stepped back, forcing down his frustration. He resumed his stance, eyes locked onto the man in front of him.

Again, Master Hail looked relaxed. Unassuming. And yet, every line of his body radiated danger.

This time, Luke moved first.

He lunged, quickly closing the gap between them and launching a swift thrust with the wooden blade. But Master Hail parried from below with minimal effort. The impact nearly tore the sword from Luke’s grip.

Gritting his teeth, he transitioned into a compact horizontal swing aimed at the instructor’s left flank. It was fast—clean and tight—but the strike was stopped just as easily.

Then, without warning, the counterattack began.

A blur of thrusts, slashes, and deceptive movements came flying at him. Each strike was measured but fast, like a tide rolling over him. The feints were the worst—Master Hail wove them into the flurry so fluidly that Luke couldn’t tell what was real anymore.

It felt like drowning in deep water.

His stamina plummeted. Each breath came heavier than the last. His arm burned with fatigue, his footwork growing sluggish.

Then came the final blow.

A straight thrust aimed at his navel.

Luke’s eyes barely tracked the motion. Desperate, he flung his wooden sword across his body in a shaky attempt to parry.

But the strike never came.

The sword stopped short.

By the time Luke realized it, it was too late. Once again, the wooden blade came to a stop against his neck—this time from the opposite side.

“Feints can be small, or they can be large,” Master Hail said, lowering his weapon with a smile. “Incorporate them into your swordplay, and your opponent will be left guessing... until the moment they fall.”

With a fluid motion, he flourished his sword and returned it to his side as if the entire sequence had been choreographed.

“Thank you for the demonstration,” Luke said, cupping his fist and dipping his head respectfully. Though part of him still burned with embarrassment, he would take that feeling over arrogance on the battlefield any day.

“You did well, Luke. Your instincts are sharp, and I can already see the vestiges of greatness in your form,” Master Hail said, his voice sincere. “I look forward to training you further.”

Luke bowed once more and returned to stand among the other students. As he settled in, a heavy hand clapped his shoulder.

He turned, only to find Kayson’s smug face staring back at him.

Luke’s knuckles twitched. A single punch, and that expression would be gone.

“That’s too bad, brother,” Kayson whispered, his tone full of playful mockery. “Maybe give up on the sword? You’re better with a spear anyway.”

Before Luke could reply, Master Hail addressed the class.

“Alright, I want each of you to find a partner for sparring. Give yourself room in the courtyard—and remember, I want to see how well you incorporate feints into your swordplay.”

At those words, a slow smile tugged at Luke’s lips.

He turned to Kayson, resting a hand on his shoulder while locking eyes with him.

“Let’s spar,” Luke said, his hazel eyes gleaming with mischief. “If you can beat me, I’ll drop the sword. Just like you said.”

Kayson’s playful expression faltered.

“And if you win?” he asked cautiously.

Luke tapped the tip of his wooden sword against his chin, pretending to consider the question—though he already knew exactly what he wanted.

His eyes flicked toward Sebastian, who stood a short distance away with the other academy servants. A grin spread across Luke’s face.

“If I win… you’ll double your training with Sebastian.”

Kayson’s face paled instantly.
