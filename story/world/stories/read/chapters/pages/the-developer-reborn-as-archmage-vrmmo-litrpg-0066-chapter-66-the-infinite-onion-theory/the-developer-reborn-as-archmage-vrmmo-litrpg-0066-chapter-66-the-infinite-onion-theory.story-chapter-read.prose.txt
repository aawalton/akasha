Did I just fucking die?

It was a long while before she had another thought. At least that’s what it felt like when she finally had it.

It was the strangest way she had ever woken up. It actually felt like she was suspended in jelly. Thinking in this state was a recently unlocked ability. She felt as if a hundred years had passed. She had no idea why.

Where the hell am I?

There was no way to answer this question. She had felt exactly like this at the very start when she was resurrected as Darya Altazark. Back then, she had been able to perceive some blurry shapes and lights. This time, she was truly in the void.

It didn’t feel like the /dev space either. That at least had the basic representation of space. It was a place. This was nowhere.

“Hello?”

Nothing. Only the void. She didn’t hear her own voice either. She wasn’t sure if she even had a mouth.

“Well, shit. Now what?”

That was a scarier question than she thought. She was a consciousness trapped in a void. What if it didn’t end? What if this was death? Just thoughts in nothingness for an indeterminate amount of time. Thoughts, and boredom.

She wondered what would happen next. How long before she went insane? Would she even go insane? What would she be thinking about that could last forever?

What would she dream about? How to kill time when time was unlimited.

Perhaps she could just dream. Endless, increasingly elaborate hallucination. She could give infinite attention to everything. Wait. Maybe that was how everything worked. Maybe that was God, and she had always been a figment of his imagination. Now she could dream her own figments.

Maybe one of those figments would also work on her universe’s equivalent of a VRMMO; she could end up terminally afflicted (if that existed in her universe), she could die at thirty. (in her own interpretation of time), and…

She could already see it all happening.

It was figments of imagination all the way down.

Really, it was just layers of an infinite onion. Earths inside Vainfalls inside Earths and Vainfalls.

In conclusion, she had a profound realization. She actually said it out loud.

“I’m so fucking high. What kind of shroom did I eat?”

She woke up. It was dark. She couldn’t tell where she was, but it sure as hell didn’t look or feel like underwater. She was lying on her back somewhere. It took a while for her eyes and ears to adjust.

First, she saw a blurry luminous blob moving everywhere. It was really close to her. She could feel its movements on her neck, chin, and collarbone.

Sound became clearer. It was familiar. The gentle sound of creaking wood. She was lying on a bed—her bed, in the princess room of her princess yacht.

She opened her mouth, trying to raise her hand. She couldn’t move any of her limbs. They felt heavy, as if they were made of iron. Something heavy lay across her.

Seris?

She couldn’t see anything. That wasn’t the only weight pinning her down. Something was on her waist. Another was on her shoulder.

The blurry luminous blob appeared right in front of her. She felt gentle tickles on her nose. Darya felt a sneeze coming. She inhaled.

“AchoO!”

Something flew off her face. A piece of cloth, or some membrane. Now it made sense why she couldn’t see anything.

Her sneeze also sent something else flying. The glowing ball was floating high above.

“Enta!”

One of the weights on her shifted with a mumble. She heard clattering and fumbling. A light fell on her face.

She let Seris fuss all over her, squeezing her like a toothpaste tube.

“Guess I didn’t die then…?” Darya said meekly as Seris nuzzled her face, planting kisses all over the side Enta wasn’t covering.

She heard other mumbles in the dark. More weights shifted. She saw two glowing balls over Seris’s shoulder. There was no sound.

Lurim landed on her as if he fell through the ceiling. He buried his face on her chest, clinging onto dear life like a koala on a falling tree.

“Mother Goddess, I feared the worst!” Darya heard Sarka’s voice somewhere in the dark.

The mermaid fell across her, wrapping her thin arms around her neck. Darya was buried in a mess of her long dark green hair. She was unusually dry for a mermaid, and she smelled like flowers.

The rest was a bunch of incoherent whispers, sobs, and laughs. It all reduced to sighs of relief that tickled her. Now it seemed as if everyone went back to sleep while hugging her.

Suddenly, she heard footsteps on wood. The door opened with a loud creak.

“Wh-what was that noise?! Is everything alright?!”

“I’m down here,” She said, her voice muffled under all the bodies lying on her.
