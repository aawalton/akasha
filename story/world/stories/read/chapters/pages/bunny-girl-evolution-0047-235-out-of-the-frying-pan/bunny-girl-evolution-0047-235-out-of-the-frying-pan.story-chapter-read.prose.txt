“Wait!” Elise shouted. “We don’t want to fight!”

The Revolutionary draugr had spoken. It implied some level of intelligence, and Elise hoped that he could maybe be reasoned with. Her hopes were dashed when he ignored her pleas, instead screaming in a more draugr-like way, and calling the ordinary undead to attack her. After that first word, he seemed to have reverted back to an ordinary monster.

“Stop!” she commanded again. “Let’s talk.”

The Singer stopped again, as did the few ordinary draugr close enough to be in range, but the Revolutionary was again unaffected.

“No,” it repeated.

Again, the spell was broken. The other draugr started moving forward unabated while Elise and Charlotte prepared for battle.

Is that the only thing it can say?

She tried to get a read on the Revolutionary’s aether. Aside from being stronger than the other draugr’s, it didn’t seem much different. None of the draugr had proper emotions, as far as she could tell, and he seemed no different. His eyes lacked intelligence, and his aether lacked complexity. As much as Elise wanted to resolve this without bloodshed so she could get to safety and heal herself, it was now clear that wouldn’t be happening.

She needed to take out the Revolutionary. If he was gone, everything else could be handled somewhat easily. Her commands would work on the other draugr if the Revolutionary wasn’t there.

“Charlotte focus on the one that just–”

Before she finished her sentence, the Singer screamed. It was not the {Power Word Kill} that Elise had been fearing, but it was still a powerful soul attack on its own, and it made both Elise and Charlotte stagger midair. It also seemed to empower the draugr on the ground, who were getting close enough that some were visibly getting ready to jump.

Elise responded by flying higher and firing an aether spike at the Singer. Its soul defenses were much better than the average draugr’s, but lacking compared to the Knight’s, so Elise could tell she had done some real damage. A few more and it would probably be dead.

The issue was that between the fight with the Knight and the failed attempts to sway the draugr, she was running low on aether. She could kill the Singer, but then she wouldn’t have enough left to both kill the Revolutionary, and command all the other draugr to leave. And even if she did have enough aether, she didn’t know if she had enough time. By the time she finished the Singer off, the horde would be upon them.

Fortunately, she had Charlotte, and Charlotte was actually much better at killing things than Elise was.

“Kill him!” she said, pointing at the Revolutionary. “I’ll handle her!”

“Okay!” she heard Charlotte shout.

Elise didn’t turn to make sure that Charlotte was doing as she asked. She was still wary of the Singer having {Power Word Kill}, and wanted to make sure it wasn’t going to go after Charlotte. Luckily, she needn’t have worried about that in the first place. Just like the Knight, the Singer was fixated on her and her alone, and didn’t spare a glance when Charlotte zipped past below.

Good.

Elise fired another aether spike, which made the Singer scream again, raising her arms to hold her head. Two more would have finished her off, but Elise wanted to make sure she saved her aether for when she truly needed it. Casting spells still cost a little bit of aether, but it was so negligible that it was essentially nothing. She recovered aether faster than she expended it when spellcasting, and while it was weaker for her, now that the Singer had been partially incapacitated, it would be manageable.

While Charlotte cut through ordinary draugr on her way to the Revolutionary, Elise fired spell after spell at the Singer. None of them were strong enough to do serious damage, but the piercing variety of her homemade {Magic Missile} seemed to work best. They were at least drawing blood. She fired a few at the Revolutionary as well, though he was a little too far away to take real damage.

Killing the Singer wasn’t her real goal anyway. Even killing the ordinary draugr with mana was difficult for her, and the Singer was a full Tier higher, and clearly had more going for her. But as long as she held the elite draugr’s attention long enough, Charlotte would be able to kill the Revolutionary, and Elise would be able to end the whole battle in an instant.

The Singer slowly began to recover her wits, and Elise debated hitting her with another aether spike, but ultimately decided not to. She could sense that the Singer’s soul was already quite damaged, and that even if she started fighting again, it wouldn’t be at full strength. The bigger threat was actually now the ordinary flying draugr who were now attempting to dive bomb her. Elise could dodge them for now, but if too many more showed up, things would get difficult.

She spared a glance down at Charlotte and saw that she was right in front of the Revolutionary. The other elite draugr’s attention was still clearly focused on Elise, but unlike the Knight, he wasn’t completely ignoring Charlotte. When Charlotte reached out to grab him by the neck, he stepped to the side and tried to run her through with his spear hand. Charlotte dodged, thankfully, but it still freaked Elise out a little bit.

Maybe I should head down…

No sooner did she think that did the Singer finally recover enough to do another screaming attack, hitting her soul with another wave of violent aether. Elise’s defenses held easily, but it immediately killed any thought of going down to join Charlotte. The Singer’s attacks were omnidirectional, and affected by distance. Going down would just bring another element of danger, and Charlotte’s soul defenses were not quite as robust as Elise's.

Be careful, Charlotte.

A few seconds later, the thing that Elise had been worried about happened. The Singer used {Power Word Kill}.

Wait, that was it?

Elise had heard the word. Just like when she herself used it, she could not remember it clearly, but it was still the same, and she felt the aether crash against her soul, more intense than any of the Singer’s other attacks… but that was it. It didn’t seem particularly special, and it didn’t cause any adverse effects.

The same could not be said for the nearby draugr. None of them died, since the Singer had not been targeting them, but they all stopped their motion, momentarily keeling over in pain. Elise looked down toward Charlotte, worried, but the arachne had not been visibly affected in any way, and was still trying her best to kill the Revolutionary.

She had successfully hit him on the shoulder with a gob of acid, and it was gradually melting through, disabling his more human arm. There was a thin, net-like web over his spear arm as well. Still, he was far from dead, and seemed to be getting agitated, and he was still looking up at Elise. She had a bad feeling about him still, but tried to push it aside. Less than a minute, and Charlotte would have him dead, and it would be over.

Her side throbbed, and her head was starting to pound too. Not from the attacks, but the constant strain of keeping up her aether defenses, fighting, dodging, and keeping track of Charlotte was getting to her. She wanted to go lay down. She wanted it all to be over so she could go home.

A flying draugr breezed by, one of its tentacles slapping against her leg, taking a bit of skin and leaving a stinging mark. She blinked in surprise, then shook her head to clear it. Another draugr swooped in, and she barely avoided it. The Singer used {Power Word Kill} again, and her soul defenses trembled slightly. It gave her a brief reprieve from the attacking draugr as they were all stunned, though, which gave her time to get a bit of distance as she fired a few more missiles at the Singer.

Come on, Charlotte.

She looked down again. The horde had arrived. There were hundreds of them, and while most of them seemed to be focused on Elise there were enough focused on Charlotte to pull her attention away from the Revolutionary.

No, thought Elise as a pair of Draugr landed on Charlotte’s back.

Then, Charlotte used a Skill that Elise had never seen her use before. She tensed her entire body, and the next moment jets of acid sprayed outward from all over. The draugr on her back were torn and melted, and the ones nearby didn’t fare much better. With the corpses still on her back, Charlotte tensed again and took a flying leap straight at the Revolutionary. Elise felt a split second of fear as the elite draugr raised his spear-like arm to intercept her, then cheered aloud as Charlotte fired a web from her abdomen to snag the spear and pull it down.

The arachne landed directly on top of the Revolutionary, her spider fangs sinking into his torso as her human hands reached for his throat. At the same moment, the flying draugr started closing in around Elise, but she barely cared. Just another moment, and the Revolutionary would be dead, and it would be over. She could stop them all in her tracks.

“Uvrky.”

The world seemed to stop as the Revolutionary spoke. It was {Power Word Kill}. As he said it, Charlotte’s form suddenly went limp. Her hands still reached the draugr’s neck, but they did not grab it, and the only acid that hit was the acid already on her hands. Elise watched in horror as the Revolutionary’s spear arm rose, ready to pierce Charlotte in the head.

“Stop!”

Elise rocket toward Charlotte as all the draugr froze around her. Even the Revolutionary stopped, though his arm was still raised. Charlotte was still alive. Unconscious, but alive. She wouldn’t survive another {Power Word Kill}, though, and she wouldn’t be able to defend herself either.

I need more aether. How can I get more aether?

The Revolutionary cancelled her command, but Elise spent the last of her aether once more to stop everything again. She needed time. And aether.

Her desperate mind came up with a half dozen half-baked options and dismissed them all instantly. How was she supposed to take aether from the draugr? How was she going to command them to help if the Revolutionary could just cancel it out.

If I had just killed the Singer. Why didn’t I think of that? A few levels and I would have had more!

A sudden thought struck like a lightning bolt. She almost stopped midair. More levels. She had more levels. She had been saving them up for an entire year, condensing the soul fog she got whenever she killed something and storing it. She had an entire hot tub-sized basin full of it in one of the rooms in her soul realm.

In front of her, the Revolutionary opened its mouth again. She didn’t have time to think further. While she flew through the air toward Charlotte, her soul form flew back to the house, diving into the hot tub. She could feel the power immediately. The liquid was being absorbed by the form directly, and even while it did, she gulped in more, drinking so much she started feeling bloated.

Outside her soul, her body was suddenly flowing with aether. Rather than send another command, she sent a soul spike at the Revolutionary. It hit as he canceled her command, so she commanded the other draugr to stop again, burning through aether without a care.

She slammed into his outstretched arm, bringing the spear away from Charlotte’s neck, then got to her feet, firing another aether spike. The Revolutionary screamed in pain and rage, but the sound was quickly cut off as Elise sent another, knocking him unconscious.

Those three soul spikes alone were almost enough to kill him. She could feel his soul leaking aether even more than Erun’s. The draugr would be dead in minutes on its own. She didn’t want to leave anything up to chance, so as she turned to face him, she readied a finishing blow.

Though he was unconscious, his eyes were still open, and he had still been looking at Elise when she struck. Her eyes met with his, and she felt a wave of what almost felt like nausea.

I know him, she realized.

For the third time, she recognized one of the faces here. And this time, it came with something else.

I didn’t like him… But who was he?

For a few seconds, she just stood there, staring, trying to make sense of it all. All around her, nothing moved. The draugr might as well have been shadows, and the only movement from the airborne ones was the flapping of their wings to keep them up. Then, beside her, she heard a scraping on the ground as one of Charlotte’s legs twitched.

The sound brought her back to reality. She remembered why she had even dived in to attack the Revolutionary in the first place. Charlotte was in danger. She couldn’t afford to be spacing out like this.

She fired a final aether spike into the Revolutionary, then turned her attention to Charlotte. The arachne’s soul was damaged, but not too badly. It was less even than when Elise tried to withstand the soul storm without protection. She would be fine in a day. But they needed to get out.

Elise hastily pulled her soul form out of the pool of condensed soul fog. More than half of it was gone, and she felt extremely bloated. In a bad way. She had taken in too much, and her soul was having trouble taking it all. But that was something to worry about later.

“Charlotte,” she said. “Charlotte, wake up.”

Charlotte stirred, but did not wake immediately. Elise flew over her head, raining fairy dust to help speed up her recovery and bring her back to alertness. Charlotte soon woke up, but she was groggy, and her words were slurred. It took a few minutes just to get her thinking clearly enough to understand what Elise was saying, and a few minutes more for her to recover enough to fly.

In the meantime, Elise maintained control over the draugr. Her aether was full, and draining only very slowly as she maintained her presence. She almost just told them all to leave, but instead, she called the Singer down toward her. Elise looked into the undead woman’s face, trying to get any sense of familiarity, but there was nothing.

I didn’t know her.

The Singer looked back blankly, as did all the other undead under her command.

“E– Elise?” said Charlotte, limping forward. “What’s– What happened?”

“…I’ll explain later once you’re recovered,” Elise said. “C’mon, let’s head back.”

Elise turned away from the Singer, taking Charlotte by the arm, and guiding her into the air. Without looking back, she fired her strongest aether spike yet, killing the Singer. Then, she did the same to another couple dozen draugr as they flew away, returning to the shelter in the tunnel.

Charlotte barely recovered enough to defend herself from the next soul storm, and immediately afterward, she fell into a deep sleep. She was visibly doing better, though, which made Elise relieved. And without needing to worry about Charlotte, she could focus more on her own issues.

She had gone too far. She was trembling, both in body and soul. It felt almost like she was about to burst.

I definitely hit the Evolution threshold. And then some.

She was terrified to reconnect to the System, worried that it would force her to evolve right there or something. If that happened, she was bound to die, but even without it, she wouldn’t be able to put it off for long. It was obvious that being in such a state was bad for her.

But what could she do? She was not yet good enough to keep her soul safe during evolution, and she couldn’t afford to leave. Not when there were so many things she still needed to do here. There was too much history—too much of her history—to just leave, no knowing if return would ever be possible.

While Charlotte slept, Elise stayed awake running through possibilities. Maybe she could get Oberon to find some way to make a teleportation scroll that she could anchor to this place. But was there enough time? Perhaps she could trick Rayna into a fey bargain, taking some of her strength for her own. If that was even possible. Maybe she could brave the storm anyways, hoping that she could somehow stay conscious enough to keep herself safe.

If only she had some way to protect herself. Some kind of shield. Something robust enough to last the entire duration of her evolution.

Maybe Erun has something that can help, she thought.

She brightened up at that thought. The man had somehow survived thousands of years in this place with a soul so damaged it couldn’t even handle ordinary aether pulses. He had to have something like that. There was no way he could handle the soul storms on his own if he couldn’t even handle some light aether pulse practice.

The sarcophagus! she realized.

It suddenly made complete sense. His sarcophagus kept him safe. It stabilized his soul, and kept him from being damaged by the storms. She had never stayed in the castle during one of the storms, but it would explain a lot.

And it was also a problem. Erun needed the sarcophagus just as badly as she did. Taking it over to evolve in would be essentially sentencing him to death. She couldn’t do that to him.

But maybe…

She glanced over at the wall of their shelter in the direction of the draugr-filled cave. If there had been someone like Erun there, they were most likely dead by now. And if they were damaged like Erun, they would have needed a sarcophagus too.

We need to get to the castle. And fast.

From how her soul felt, she knew she only had a few days before she would either be forced into an evolution, or would start to suffer. It wasn’t much, but it would have to be enough. No more taking their time to train. They just needed to barrel straight toward the castle. But that was well within their capabilities at this point. As long as nothing went wrong, and as long as she didn’t accidentally connect to the System–

Wait, she suddenly realized. The System… How was I commanding the draugr without the System?
