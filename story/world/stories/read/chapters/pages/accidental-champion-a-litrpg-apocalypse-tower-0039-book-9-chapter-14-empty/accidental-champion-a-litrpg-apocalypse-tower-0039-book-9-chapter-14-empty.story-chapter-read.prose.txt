“This is where I leave you,” Striker said.

Xavier stood on the planet Batrah, where this whole mess had started. Every one he’d liberated from the base was gathered on that same, grassy plain where the fake rendezvous with Palini had been supposed to happen.

The base, along with the uninhabited moon it had been built upon, would inevitably perish. Though time hadn’t caught up to it quite yet. The explosion likely hadn’t even reached the invisible barrier that had surrounded Arrack. But it would soon enough.

“Are you sure you don’t want to join the cause?” Xavier asked.

He’d shared, with all of them, memories showing that the universe would one day come to an end. That this was what he was fighting for—that this was why he wanted to unite everyone.

The A Grade squinted at the planet’s twin suns. “I owe you a debt, Xavier Collins, for breaking my contracts. But what I don’t owe you is fealty. With the escape of Arrack’s soul, it’s only a matter of time before Jhanku has me—and everyone from that base—in his sights. If he doesn’t already. And he’s not going to contract us again.”

They’d made it to the Memory Room, restoring the memories of all the agents and staff that were on the base at the time, before their attempted interrogation of Arrack. The interrogation had become mostly moot by the time Xavier had returned to that circular steel room.

He’d learnt everything he’d needed to know from Striker.

That was why he’d risked letting Arrack move within the time dilation field—he’d wanted the man to see his defeat. Wanted him to taste it.

Another mistake.

He hadn’t expected Arrack’s soul to escape.

Half the base’s active agents had been stuck off world somewhere as Xavier had freed the others. With the help of Striker, the base’s portal mages, and Xavier’s drones with active time dilation fields, they’d been able to retrieve the agents from their missions, free them from their contracts, and return their memories.

Five hundred agents. One hundred base staff. Thirty A Grades among them.

Every one of them grateful—every one of them terrified of Jhanku.

And now Jhanku has all the more reason to take me out, especially after what I did to his great-great-grandson.

“So, what, you’re going to run and hide?” Xavier asked.

Striker smiled. “The universe is a big place. Plenty of places to disappear. Unless you can guarantee our safety in those two young sectors of yours?”

Xavier touched his Storage Ring, shook his head.

“If you are what you say you are, what you’re trying to be, maybe that will one day change. Though I suspect that will be a long time from now.” Striker summoned a Communication Stone. “But if you ever need anything—anything not too insane, mind—I’m a man who pays my debts.”

The portal mages from the base raised their staffs and portals started materialised around the clearing.

Before the agents departed, they each handed Xavier a Communication Stone. Only the A Grades handed him a universe-wide stone. The B Grades only had Communication Stones that covered a 50-sector radius—stones that would ping a notification when they were in range.

Xavier watched as, one by one, the Denizens disappeared. Striker held back, hands folded in front of him. The man had been sending these people on missions, even if enforced ones, for many years. It looked like he wanted to see them off one last time.

When everyone else had left, two near-identical mages shuffled over. The twins, Dybal and Lydad, looked a little sheepish for peak B Grades. Xavier noticed, for the first time, the differences between them beyond their attire—Dybal had green eyes, where Lydad’s were blue. He wondered if that was something they’d done on purpose, altering their appearance just enough to distinguish between them, or if it was some quick of their heritage.

Dybal ran a hand through his hair. “We’d like to come with you.”

“Even with the threat of Jhanku hanging over me?”

“He hangs over us all. Besides, that old bastard is the reason we were slaves for the past six-hundred years. If you’re wanting revenge, then we’re coming with you,” Lydad said, voice hard.

Striker looked at the two mages. “You two sure about this?”

“We won’t run,” Dybal said. “Not that it isn’t the smart play.”

Striker sighed. “Well, I’m done giving you two orders. I just hope you aren’t throwing your life away. Jhanku is one of the most powerful Denizens in the inner sectors.” He turned his gaze on Xavier. “It’s hard to say, but he’s probably in the top ten of all the old monsters in the universe.” He glanced at one of the portals. “But I’m not going to stick around and convince you.”

The A Grade left without another word.

Xavier looked at the two men. “You know I killed Alica, don’t you?”

Dybal’s eye twitched. “Yes.”

“We figured that much out.” Lydad looked away. “But we’d been sent to you—and you could have killed all of us. Hell, you could have left everyone to die in that base when Arrack exploded himself.”

“Alica’s death isn’t on you,” Dybal said. “It’s on Arrack and Jhanku, and all Denizens like them.” He kicked at the dirt, uprooting a clump of grass that flew all the way to the edge of the time dilation field.

“Did you know her before?” Xavier asked. “Is she in your memories?”

Dybal and Lydad exchanged a look. Lydad cleared his throat. “For six-hundred years we only knew we were brothers because we look alike, and because our classes align.”

“And our names are kind of similar…” Dybal added.

Lydad smirked. “Yeah, there’s that.” He sighed. “All these years, what we didn’t know was that… Alica was our sister.”

Xavier took a beat to process that. “Then I’m surprised you don’t want to kill me where I stand.”

“As I said, it’s not on you.” Dybal cracked his neck. “But don’t think I haven’t considered it.”

“Dybal!” Lydad replied.

“What? Haven’t you?”

Lydad turned his head.

“I don’t blame you,” Xavier said. “Trust me.”

“We do,” Dybal said. “Trust you, I mean.”

“That’s why we want to come with you.”

“The crap you’ve managed to pull off…”

“Not quite the backwater Denizen you thought I was, huh?” Xavier said flatly, then gave a smirk.

Dybal laughed awkwardly. “No. Far from it. The things you did—”

“I can teach you two, if you wish,” Xavier replied.”

The twins’ eyes widened in unison. “We wish,” they said simultaneously. “We definitely wish.”

Xavier chuckled, his gaze turning to the portals and the empty plains.

“So, what happens now?” Dybal asked.

Xavier touched a finger to his Storage Ring. “I have a few things I need to do before returning to my sector. And I’ve been gone longer than I expected.”

“It’s been like… three hours since we were last here?” Dybal looked at the suns. “Maybe less.”

“A lot can change in three hours.”
