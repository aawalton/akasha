It smelled like rotten eggs.

That was the first, and least pleasant, sensation that hit me. One moment I was nestled in a gray quilt, yawning in the aftermath of my carefully brewed sleeping tonic and counting the lines of wood grain patterned on the ceiling.

Next thing I knew, I was surrounded by the stench of rancid eggs. That and pearly walls, stone workbenches, and cabinets lined with beakers, bottles, jars, and pouches. Dusky lavender light trickled in from beyond great glass windows and a vast sky hued like ripe plums. Hanging glass globes, nearly-opaque but glowing with furls of blue smoke, lit the space even further.

“You’re early.”

I froze. Closed my eyes. Took a moment to brace myself for… him. This involved taking a deep breath, which was a mistake, and all composure was shattered as I hacked and gasped and nearly toppled a wooden shelf. My movements jostled glass jars full of crushed powders in vivid shades, withered plant bits, and what looked like pulverized bone. I steadied myself, and the shelf, with trembling hands.

Velidan stood at a long table, watching me with one brow arched. But, for once, the Fae himself was not what commanded my immediate attention.

Rather, it was what lay dead on the table. Some kind of scaled, heavy-looking creature, as long as I was tall, with a goat-like head and six stubby limbs ending in curved talons. Blood, both dried and fresh, smeared the stone beneath it. The creature’s body was ripped open in the middle, with bones and scales and bits of red flesh propped out haphazardly. Velidan wore a dark, short-sleeved vest. His lower arms glistened with blood and viscera

“I’m here on purpose,” I managed, coughing against my palm and eyeing the scene warily. “I… I wanted to speak with you.”

“I’m flattered.”

“Don’t be,” I muttered, steadying myself against a table. Creeping closer revealed a pile of slimy-looking organs in a basin near his feet. “Saints, what are you doing?”

He grimaced and, with a gut-wrenching squelch, tore a long strip of rust-colored flesh free from bone. It plopped into the basin. “Harvesting a baliker. Open that jar.”

I did as he asked, then covered my mouth. “Why?”

Gristle-slicked fingers deposited a small, dripping black something within the glass. I closed the lid quickly, horrified by the appetizing scent that wafted up.

“They feed on sprites and wisps. Transient creatures. When a baliker is fully grown, their very bodies are bloated by fragments from those more whimsical Fae.” He glanced up, took in my doubtlessly baffled expression, and snorted. “Baliker flesh is well-suited to fueling goblin magic.”

“Oh,” I said, as if that clarified everything. I didn’t know if I should be intrigued, confused, or even more horrified. Perhaps all three. After another moment spent watching, wide-eyed and fighting the urge to retch as he tore part of the creature’s spine in half, I rasped, “Can we… talk?”

One black brow rose. “Are we not?”

“I m-mean… um, maybe somewhere less… distracting.”

Another bone snapped with a sharp crack, making me flinch. He raised one gore-spattered arm. “My next order of business is a bath, but I fear you’d find that even more distracting.”

I bristled. “Even more horrifying.”

He smirked. “I’ll leave the choice to you.”

“Fine. H-here, then. I… look, I want to… I’d like… a little while ago, there was an attack in the city. Right in the middle of the marketplace. In the middle of the day, no less!”

He looked curious, but unsurprised. Bone fragments clattered to the stone.

I began pacing, hands knotting at my tunic and eyes fixed on the polished floor. “People died. Innocent people, because of this Embriel, who I don’t know anything about except that I remember you said he and you were on opposite sides of… of whatever this all is. And it sounds like… salt, I don’t even know! But it sounds like something much worse is going to happen soon.”

The sounds of thick, wet flesh being ripped fell silent. “Explain.”

I repeated the flaxen-haired woman’s message, word-for-word, watching Velidan’s expression closely as I did so.

His brows drew together and he set a torn limb down, frowning thoughtfully.

“You said… well, you’ve said lots of things. But if the part about you wanting to stop these other Fae is true, then help me. Please. I’m with the Thalessians right now, and-”

“Brin,” he practically snarled my name, voice thick with anger.

“I haven’t given them your salted weapon!” My fists curled even as I trembled. “I believed you, okay? I get it. You’re a monster and you’ll kill anyone who… I get it! I’m trying to keep people safe, not get them killed.”

“Good.” Dark, glittering eyes narrowed. “Keep it that way.”

“I will if you help me. Look, you… earlier I watched people murdered in the streets! A child nearly died in my arms! Help me keep more people from dying.”

A portion of slimy, rancid organ meat fell to the basin. “I already am. All you need to do is remain in the city.”

I paused midstep. Squinted at him, gnawing my lower lip. “So you can come get the shard. And then… what? Are you going to stop this… ritual? Go kill this Embriel before he hurts anyone else?”

He examined a handful of oily-looking viscera. Pungent, acrid sulfur hit my nostrils and made my eyes water. “Truthfully? I have very little idea what to expect. I’ve not been privy to my peers’ plans until very recently.”

“You haven’t? Why?”

“Because I’ve shown no interest in claiming humans, or human territory. I’ve cautioned others against it and refused to be a part of their little… incursions into your realm.”

I thought for a moment. “What changed?”

The Fae’s gaze flicked up. Caught mine and held it as full, red lips curled into a wicked smile. “Why, you, Brin. You made quite a scene the other night, and I’ve… capitalized on a misconception.” Broad shoulders shook with laughter, making the velvet vest rasp. More gore plopped to the ghoulish pile. “Hand me another jar.”

I paced closer, covering my mouth against the stench, and found an empty bottle. “I don’t understand.”

He methodically ripped out and dropped jagged black teeth into the glass. Each fell with a soft ting. “Human blood was smelled in my garden, Saerith mentioned you running around the estate in a nightdress, and for the grand finale, you showed up battered and bound in the middle of a party. It was assumed I’d been…” The smile sharpened. “Having some fun with you.”

My heart stuttered. I sank back to one of the workbenches, thighs pressing into cool stone. “So now the other Fae-”

“Think I’ve had a change of heart after finding a plaything of my own. Seen how fun a human can be.” Soft, silky laughter snaked along my skin. “I suppose they’re not completely wrong.”

I squeezed my eyes shut against the cruelty behind those words. Scraped my nails against the stone. Breathe. Focus. This isn’t about me, it’s about protecting people. “But you…” my breath hitched. “You s-still want to stop them, r-right?”

“I do.” He watched me for a moment, then reached for a scale-covered limb. A loud snap rang off the walls.

The sound nearly broke my composure. “You… you said what they’re doing will get you killed. But if you don’t even know what they’re planning-”

“I know that my kind are violently attacked when we grow too bold with you mortals.” He grimaced. “Just think of the circumstances around the shard.”

“Attacked by who?” He offered no answer and I shook my head, hands spread wide. “I still don’t understand. Why don’t the other High Fae think they’ll be stopped? Why have they decided to be so bold?”

Velidan braced both hands against the table, smearing dark blood along smooth gray stone. He regarded me silently for a long while. Then, every word measured, “That question requires a very complicated answer. It has quite a bit to do with magic, and even more to do with history. I don’t think we have time for a lesson at the moment. But I do have an… idea.”

The blood drained from my face. There was something very predatory about the look being leveled at me. I shifted back, curling my fingers into my hem.

“Now that I’m cooperating,” he spat the word, “I’ve been invited to an… event. One that I know very little about, other than it begins tomorrow and culminates in some very powerful, ritualistic blood magic. I’d wager that’s what his lackey hinted at with you and your Thalessians today. I’ve been told it will lead to, in Embriel’s words, ‘the hunt of the century and a city on its knees.’ I’ve been planning to attend, learn what I can, leave early, and collect you before… well. Imagine ten creatures like me on the loose within the walls of Sedgewick Vale.” He chuckled, teeth gleaming beneath the pale light.

“Saints.” My mouth went dry. “What do you mean, collect me?” One brow rose, as if the answer should have been obvious. “No. No. You can’t… If other Fae are coming, I don’t want to run! I have magic, I want to help-”

“A very noble sentiment, but pointless.”

“But the city-! My friends are there! You can’t just take me away and leave everyone to die!”

“No?” He gave me a patronizing look. “And how do you propose to stop me?”

I glanced around, helplessly searching for an answer amidst the shelves and jars. “I… I…”

“I told you to warn your friends. That was a kindness, and if they failed to listen, well…” broad shoulders lifted in a shrug. “Although, who knows? Perhaps in the bloodshed and frenzy I’ll find some opportunities to actually test the shard.” He laughed, as if delighted by the idea.

“But we need to stop all this!” I lunged towards him, jostling the table. Several jars went clattering down and rolled along the stone.

“It’s difficult to stop something I know nothing about, Brin. However… well. I told you I had an idea. There may actually be a way for both of us to get something we want out of all this.”

“All I want is to keep people safe.”

“And I want to stay at this event, learning what I can and keeping up appearances, rather than leave early to find you. Crossing the realms is incredibly difficult and leaves me… ill-suited for much else.” He grimaced. “The murder of my peers, in particular.”

My heart hammered madly as, piece by piece, I began to see where he was going. “You… you can’t mean…” I paled, inching back. “Are you s-saying you want me to come?”

“I think you should.” He searched my face hungrily. “If you were seen so publicly at my side, then found during the hunt, you’d be left alone. I would be free to stay among my kind and find you at leisure, rather than make it a priority. And I’d be much more capable of… well. As I said, I’m hoping to find some opportunities for bloodshed.”

I gaped at him. The idea was horrifying. No, beyond that, it was insane. “But won’t I be in danger if I go? Won’t your friends… ashes, eat me? Or worse?”

He actually laughed. “No. They wouldn’t dare. There’s some… etiquette, when it comes to our belongings. Although…” the laugh cut off abruptly, his face darkened, and before I knew it he’d stalked around the table to stand before me. Several drops of blood slid down his fingertips, spattering onto the floor. “Your antics will not be acceptable.”

“My antics?”

Full lips parted in a sneer. “Yelling at me. Threatening me. Running away from me. Stabbing me.”

My spine stiffened. “I-”

“If you behave as such, I’ll be expected to punish you.” Endless pinpricks of silver glittered, catching and reflecting the pale light with an unsettling glow. He drew closer, voice low, and promised, “And, mark me, I will. I think I’ve made it very clear that your wellbeing comes second to mine.”

The threat sent me backing away, one hand clasped at my throat. “Why would I ever agree to this?!”

“I imagine you’ll overhear quite a bit. Perhaps we’ll even discern what Embriel’s ritual is in time to prevent it. Information is very often the equivalent of power, Brin. And, once you wake up, you can share what you’ve learned.” He tilted his head, obviously pleased by this idea. “Believe it or not, I’d like the human city to be as well-prepared as possible. The more my peers are thwarted, the happier I’ll be.”

I sagged against a table. My mind raced. My eyes darted along the polished white tiles, searching for reason and answers and… finding nothing save dread. Finally, through lips quickly growing numb, I rasped, “And I’ll… I’ll be safe?”

He leveled a stern look at me. “So long as you behave.”

“Behave.” I nearly gagged on the word. My knees felt like jelly. “And what… w-what exactly does that mean?”

Velidan paced, boots making slow clicks against the tiles. “Mostly, you’ll just need to look lovely, seem frightened of me, and be silent.” The Fae’s voice lilted, threaded with amusement, as he added, “You’re very good at two of those things. I’ve yet to see you remain quiet for long.”

A manic, breathless laugh choked its way up my throat.

“And you’ll need to wear… hm.” He looked me up and down, and I almost imagined a wicked gleam in those endless eyes. “Something.”

“I should hope so,” I croaked, earning a chuckle. Clammy arms curled around my middle, lashes lowering as I took a deep breath. “I… I…”

“Think it over. Decide tonight.”

“And if I don’t want to?” I whispered.

“I’m not going to drag you unwillingly.” He scoffed. “As I said, I’d need you to behave. But,” he stepped closer, casting me half in shadow, “If you want to try and prevent a massacre, this is your answer.”

My mind whirled. My stomach roiled. And above all the panic and uncertainty, one thing screamed itself over and over again.

He tricked me before. He misled me, fed me things I wanted to hear. What if this is all some… elaborate lie? Some awful new game?

I stared up. My heart thrummed like a trapped bird.

A softer, sadder thought chased the others. Whispered itself as I examined the cruel figure towering over me.

What would be the point?

Why bother with lies? Why try to trick me? I was already trapped. I was already a prisoner. If he wanted to drag me out on display before other monsters, what could possibly stop him? If he wanted to appear in my bedroom, promising to kill people if I called for help, what could possibly stop him?

If he wanted to keep ripping his way into the mortal realm, leaving towns in flames and women bleeding at their husbands’ feet, what could possibly stop him?

My gaze trailed down. Past the carved jaw, past the curve of a tapered collarbone disappearing beneath hazy velvet cloth. Down to moonlight-hued fingertips slick with blood. I watched a fat drop snake its way down his palm. Tracing a grim path through the gore.

“You have to stop.”

The Fae blinked. Clearly, that hadn’t been the expected answer.

I straightened. Sniffled. Rubbed my eyes and squared my shoulders. “I… I wanted to come see you even before what happened in the marketplace.” My voice quavered, and I fought to steady it. “You need me. I don’t understand… almost anything, but I’ve figured that out. Stars know I don’t understand why, but the shard seems determined to stay with me. And I saw your face this morning, when I used the rune. You were thrilled. You couldn’t believe what I’d done.”

He flashed that wolfish smile. “I won’t deny it, Brin. You’re quite valuable. At the moment.” The last words were casually, so casually, laced in steel.

I didn’t let the threat dissuade me, though. “You want the sword. Fine. You want me to hold onto it, help you forge it. Fine. But you have to stop hurting people.”

“You’re in no position to be telling me what to do.”

“I’m asking.” My heart stuttered, but I didn’t back down. “H-here’s my… my offer. If you really are trying to stop other Fae, I’ll cooperate. With the relic, with h-helping you, but only if you stop hurting humans.”

The Fae sighed, gaze lifting as if he hoped to find patience in the ceiling. “I’ll remind you that I’ve hurt very few-”

“Those people last night-”

“Are perfectly fine,” he scoffed. “The old woman-”

“Her name was Resa!” I snapped, stepping forwards. “And she wasn’t fine! She was frightened and hurt, and her husband was scared out of his mind! They might lie awake every night, terrified of you, for the rest of their lives! You could have healed her, could have… I don’t know, explained things to them, reassured them, instead of-”

“I’m not in the habit of explaining myself to insects. She was alive. That’s a far kinder state than most Fae would-”

“Not good enough! You want me to cooperate, right? Use Faer again? Hold onto the shard instead of, I don’t know, throwing it in the lake? Well, that’s my price. It’s a simple one. A bloodless one.”

Velidan’s carved features were impassive. The eyes holding me were dark and heavy. Once again, I wasn’t sure if I had crossed a very dangerous line… or was somehow managing to walk it perfectly.

The answer came a heartbeat later when, with all the unhurried grace of a predator whose prey was already caught, the Fae stepped closer.

“Most of my kind,” his vest brushed my own tunic and I jerked back, practically crashing into the table. Panic spiked at the unwanted closeness.

“Wouldn’t tolerate,” blood-slicked hands settled beside my hips. Casually trapping me. I slammed my eyelids shut. Fought to breathe beneath the press of fear. Hated how quickly my resolve broke before him.

“A fraction of your insolence.” His voice was soft. Barely a murmur. A thin covering over a deeper threat. A quiet reminder that I had no power here; not over him, not over his actions, not over anything.

I counted my heartbeats. Managed to look up at a cruel face that was far too close. Then, rallying the last of my courage, slowly reached for a nearby bowl. My knuckles were white around the smooth wooden rim as I shifted it between us. A meager shield, but… all I had.

One fist delved into a pocket. And, with a shaky breath, I began scooping handfuls of seeds and mostly-crushed herbs into the bowl.

Velidan blinked.

My mouth was bone-dry, but I somehow stammered the words out all the same. “This is m-my…. t-token of good faith, I s-suppose. I didn’t know w-what else to… you s-seem like you already have everything you could possibly… I… I thought you’d like them.”

The room seemed to grow still. Or perhaps it was just him.

“I don’t know what any of the s-seeds are,” I admitted, ducking my head. Brown hair swept down, swishing against the rim and providing a much-needed barrier. “They were labeled in bins and pouches, but everything got scattered during the attack. But I still thought… I mean, there’s bound to be a tomato in here, somewhere. And I bet you don’t know what zucchinis or pumpkins or radishes are, r-right?” I gnawed my lower lip, peering up.

His face was unreadable.

Heat crept up my neck. I began to feel very foolish, but forged on. “And you can strip the herbs. If you want, I mean. You’ll have to trim the stems and find some glassware to keep them in. With water and soil, until they grow roots, and then you can transplant them to the ground. Or I can, I suppose, if you don’t want...”

Still no answer. The silence was deafening.

I fidgeted, glancing around uncertainly. Still trapped, still shivering. “None of it w-will be as nice as… well. As anything here. But I thought… I don’t know, I thought… y-you seem to enjoy… I suppose it was a foolish-”

The room trembled. Carved, impassive features swirled. Hazy lavender and blue twisted the edges of my vision and I gasped, fingers going slack around the bowl. It fell, and my little spark of hope fell with it.

As the world began to fade I closed my eyes, braced for the hollow clatter of wood against polished tile.

It never came.
