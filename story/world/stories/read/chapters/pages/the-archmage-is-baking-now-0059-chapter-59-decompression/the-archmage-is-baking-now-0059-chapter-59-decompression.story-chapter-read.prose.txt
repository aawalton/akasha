The ride to the quarry was quiet. Jane spent most of it staring out the window, watching the reddish-brown landscape roll past.

About halfway there, Dave’s voice drifted down from the driver’s seat. "Did you make any progress?"

"I might have." Jane leaned out the window so her voice would carry better. "I have something to try, at least. If it works, that's great."

"And if it doesn't?"

Jane considered the question.

"Then the main goal is to ensure that I don't make things worse. As long as I don't break anything more than it’s already broken, we can keep trying other things. Other approaches.”

“Sounds reasonable.”

"I hope so," Jane said. “To be honest, nobody has ever tried what I’m going to try. It’s hard to predict everything.”

The quarry came into view a few minutes later. Eustice was waiting for them at the rim, slapping his hands on his legs. He looked impatient, though in an adult, restrained way. As soon as they disembarked from the carriage, he ushered them all to the lift and down into the quarry.

Once they were at the lowest level, he led Jane to a tiny hole she never would have found by herself, about an inch across.

"I'm sorry it isn't wider,” Eustice told her. “We prioritized depth."

"That's perfect. Width doesn't matter much for what I'm planning. Depth is what I need. How deep does it go?"

"Nearly forty feet."

"Oh, that's excellent. Thank you.”

Jane knelt beside the hole and placed her palm flat against the stone surrounding it. She could feel the compression immediately, that same wrongness she had sensed before. But there was something else too. Far below, past the affected layers, she could just barely detect the natural signature of uncompressed stone. The drill had gone deep enough.

She stood up and looked at the small crowd that had gathered. Eustice and his workers were clustered nearby. Dave, who had asked to accompany them into the quarry this time, was hanging back by the lift. Bella stood beside him, watching with the patient confidence of someone who had seen Jane work before.

"I need everyone to stand back,” Jane directed.

Once everyone was at what she judged to be a safe distance, she turned back to the drill hole. Pulling a piece of chalk from her pocket, she began to draw on the stone, scribing a simple circle around the opening. It wasn't anything fancy, just a basic amplification pattern meant to project her magic further and more precisely than she could on her own.

The circle complete, she knelt in its center and placed both hands flat against the stone.

Here goes nothing.

She pushed her magical senses downward, following the narrow channel of the drill hole into the depths. She sank deeper and deeper, past layer after layer of compressed rock, feeling the pressure increase as she went.

And then she was through.

Below the compressed layers, the stone felt different. Looser. More relaxed. This was how the rock was supposed to be, its natural state before years of alchemical blasting had squeezed it into something wrong. Jane anchored her awareness there, in that natural stone, and began to study the boundary between the two states.

It was sharper than she had expected. There was a clear line where the compression began, a border where the stone went from right to wrong over the span of just a few inches. That was useful. A gradual transition would have been harder to work with. A sharp boundary gave her contrast.

She filled her magic with intent. The stone needed to move, she told it. It needed to decompress. To relax back into its natural form. The stone on this side of the line needed to be like the stone on the other side. She focused on that idea, that desire, and let it flow down through the drill hole into the depths.

She didn't try to force anything. She just provided power and direction. If she was lucky, that would be enough. The compression was unnatural, a state the rock had been forced into against its fundamental nature. All she had to do was give it a path back to where it wanted to be.

She started at the boundary, that sharp line between compressed and natural. She fed power into the stone there, an unfocused energy meant to loosen the bonds holding the stone in its wrong shape. She expected to feel some slight movement, maybe a few inches of stone relaxing back into its proper form.

What she got instead was an earthquake.

The ground heaved beneath her, throwing her off-balance. She lost her connection to the drill hole as her concentration shattered. Little clouds of rock-dust rose from the surface of the stone as she tried to keep her feet. All around her, people were shouting in surprise and fear.

Then, as quickly as it had started, the shaking stopped. It had lasted maybe five seconds in total.

Bella's voice rang out, sharp with concern.

"Jane!"

"I'm fine!" Jane scrambled to her feet, coughing dust. "Is everyone all right?"

Voices called out from around the quarry. Nobody was hurt. Nobody had even fallen, as far as Jane could tell. Eustice was already running toward her, pale-faced and clearly frightened.

"What happened? What did you do?"

Jane held up a hand to stop him. "Give me a moment."

She knelt again and pressed her palms to the stone. Her head was pounding from the effort of the initial work, but she pushed through it to send her senses back down into the depths.

The compression was gone. The stone below her felt natural now, loose and relaxed all the way down to the bottom of the drill hole and beyond. Whatever she had done at that boundary layer had cascaded. The entire mass of compressed rock had decompressed at once, like a spring being released.

She probed outward, checking the areas around the quarry. The same thing had happened everywhere. The wrongness she had felt that morning was simply not there anymore. It was an accident, mostly, but a happy one.

Jane stood up and found herself smiling.

"Well," she said, looking at the small crowd of worried faces gathered around her. "That went better than I thought. Everyone, I think the problem is solved."

—

The celebration started almost immediately. Jane tried to explain that they should wait, that the water might not actually come back just because the stone had decompressed. There were still unknowns and variables and all sorts of things that could go wrong.

Nobody wanted to hear it. Word spread through the quarry in minutes, accompanied by raucous cheering. A runner was dispatched to town while Jane and her companions were still riding the lift up to the surface,

"You might want to brace yourself," Bella warned her as they climbed back into Dave's carriage. "I have a feeling this is going to be a long night."

She was right. By the time they reached the inn, the common room was already filling up. Miners had finished their shifts early, shopkeepers had closed up for the day, and everyone wanted to see the archmage who had saved their town.

Jane tried to slip upstairs to her room, but Eustice caught her by the arm and steered her toward a table that had been cleared in her honor. Someone put a drink in her hand. Before she knew what was happening, she was the center of attention in a way that made her deeply, deeply uncomfortable.

"Just smile and nod," Bella whispered in her ear. "They need this. Let them have it."

So Jane smiled and nodded. She accepted congratulations, answered questions about magic as best she could, and drank the ale that kept appearing in front of her. All the while, she tried her hardest to smile and be everything the town needed in that moment.

The announcement came a few hours later, when a man Jane didn't recognize burst through the inn's door. He was breathing hard, like he had run the whole way from wherever he had come from. The room went quiet as everyone turned to look at him.

"The pumps," he gasped. "They've started producing again."

The cheer that went up nearly deafened Jane. People were hugging each other and actually crying with relief. Jane hadn’t really noticed how tense the whole town had been until now, when the tension was gone. There was hope in the air. Its presence helped everyone to breathe easier.

Despite her previous reluctance, Jane found herself swept up in it. Maybe it was the ale, or maybe it was the genuine joy on the faces around her. Whatever the reason, it was hard not to celebrate.

Maybe I actually did it. Maybe this really is the end of it.

She had no idea if such a fast result was normal. Should the water have returned so quickly once the pressure was released? Would it stabilize, or was there another disaster coming? Her training had covered a lot of things, but it couldn’t have covered this. All she could do was hope and try not to worry.

Hattie found her sometime after the third ale, or possibly the fourth. Jane had lost track. The old woman appeared at her elbow like she had materialized out of thin air. Which, given Jane's slightly fuzzy state, was impossible to rule out.

"Thank you." Hattie's voice was quiet. "What you did today saved this town."

Jane shook her head, trying to clear some of the ale-fog from her thoughts. "Nothing is sure yet. The pumps are better, but that doesn't mean they'll stay better. I don't know how permanent any of this is."

"Oh, hush." Hattie waved a hand dismissively. "Of course it's sure. The water is rising. That's more than anyone hoped for when we sent word to the academy. If you had come here and told us the problem was unfixable, we would have believed you. Instead, you fixed it in two days. Stop doubting yourself."

"Even if that's true, I have no idea how permanent it will be once you go back to using the quarry. The blasting is what caused the problem in the first place. If you keep doing it, the compression might build up again.”

"We won't.” Peter had appeared at the table. Greeting Hattie with a nod, he pulled up a chair and gave Jane a reassuring smile. “Keep using that quarry, I mean. The three of us talked about it when Eustice got back from the pit. We're starting a new one, on the other side of the hill. And we'll never go that deep again. The stone we get from closer to the surface isn't quite as good, but it's good enough. We’ll be just fine.”

Jane felt some of the lingering tension drain out of her shoulders. That was something, at least. If they weren't going to repeat the behavior that had caused the problem, then maybe her fix really would be permanent.

“That’s good to hear.” She took another sip of her ale and sighed contentedly. "That's really, really good to hear."

The mayor was silent for a moment, watching the celebration swirl around them. Then he leaned in close, lowering his voice.

"I have a question for you. Would you like a ride back to Glenfall?"

Jane blinked, surprised by the offer. "That's very kind, but it would take days. I couldn't impose like that.”

Peter’s eyes moved across the room to where his son stood near the bar, laughing at something one of the miners had said. Dave looked different than he had when Jane had first met him, she realized. More relaxed. More confident. Just more, overall, in a way that was hard to define.

"I'm sending him away," Peter said simply. "He needs more opportunity than he can get here. This town is fine for people like Hattie and me. But Dave? He's been restless since he was old enough to walk. I've never seen him as alive as he's been this week."

Jane thought about how eager the young man had been to help, and how quickly he had adapted to each new situation he’d found himself in. He had taken to the work like he had been waiting for it his whole life.

"Are you sure?" Jane asked. "Sending him away is a big decision. For both of you."

Peter’s voice was firm. "I'm sure. If just having someone to transport made him this happy, then he needs a bigger world than he's ever seen. Glenfall isn't huge, but it's bigger than here. More opportunities for someone like him. I want him to have that.”

Jane thought about it. She thought about the long walk back to Glenfall, the blisters and the dust and the endless miles of road. She also thought about what it was like to be in the wrong place, and to have a chance to find the right one. She had some experience in those matters.

She nodded slowly. "All right. If you're sure, then yes. I'd like a ride back.”

“And we'll help him get settled once we're there,” Bella added. “We can introduce him to people who might need his services."

"That's all I ask." Peter gave the two girls a grateful nod before his gaze returned to his son. "Just keep him safe until he finds his feet. After that, he's on his own. He's old enough to make his own way."

"Of course," Jane told him. "I promise."

Hattie was beaming. She smiled at Jane and Bella as she stood, pulling Peter with her.

"Good. Now finish your ale and get some sleep, Jane. You've earned it."

Jane was obediently savoring her last sip of ale when Bella put an arm around her shoulders and squeezed.

“See?” Bella gestured around at the joyful scene with a broad grin. “I told you we’d find a way to make your birthday special.”
