“That’s a unique trick you got there,” Nolan said casually, but the look on his face was anything but. To be honest, he appeared focused, like he was trying to make sense of my unique gift. “No one has ever had the ability to activate dungeon plots.”

‘Well, how often have you encountered dungeon ruins?’

“They aren’t ruins. Like I said, those weren’t there before. Those monoliths were precursors to dungeons. They are essentially what form a dungeon. These rare finds don’t normally come up when I travel, and are said to vary in appearance, but after seeing one in person for the first time, I’m curious as to how it was triggered by you.”

‘What usually activates them?’

“A dungeon core.”

‘And what activates a dungeon core?’

“A god.”

I squinted and looked at Nolan funny. ‘Are you indirectly calling me a god, gunslinger?’

He scoffed. “I’d be damned if a tiny dragon was a celestial being.”

I giggled. ‘Well, it sure sounds like that’s what you were saying. Regardless of how I activated it, it’s alive! And inside is the last stone I need to evolve!’

His face turned less serious before he turned to me. “You’re right. So let’s exterminate some bugs and level you up.”

I gulped. ‘Um, bugs?’

He looked down at me funny. “It’s a Spider Queen’s nest. Granted spiders are arachnids, but these types of crawly dungeon types are filled with all sorts of multi-legged freaky shit. Didn’t you read the pop up?”

‘Ha. Yeah, I knew that,’ I said, trying to hide my squeamishness with a forced laugh. ‘I was just making sure you did.’

“Right.” It was impossible to tell if he was being sarcastic or sincere. Until he asked me, “Are you sure you’re up for it? It looks like you’re two seconds away from passing out.”

Admittedly, I overlooked that pop up text! My eyes trained themselves to immediately look down at the rewards. I saw an evo stone and got excited, and didn’t bother to read much else about the dungeon. I even overlooked the fact that it was a tier three menace—the monsters inside this one were higher leveled than me, right?

And so were the others who crossed my path, I reminded myself. Honestly right now, I was just making up excuses not to go raid it! I was deadly afraid of crawling critters, and Nolan must have noticed it. But that wasn’t going to stop me from getting what I wanted.

‘One hundred percent sure,’ I told him anyway. ‘I have you as backup if things get… hairy.’

He cocked his eyebrow at me and studied me for a moment. “Really?”

‘Honest! Now, let’s quit wasting time out here and dive in.’

“Don’t you want to take care of that spider on your back first?”

I yelped, spun in a circle, and knocked the imaginary bug off with my tail. Nolan was hunched over laughing when I regained my composure and shot him a dirty look. I scowled at him, already plotting my revenge. ‘Not funny,’ I said, only to be met by more chuckles.

“You’re right. It was absolutely hysterical,” he said as he wiped a fake tear from the corner of his eye. “That phobia will be the death of you. I was going to suggest ditching this and finding another dungeon, but I’d be doing you a disservice if I went that route. Instead, we are going inside, and you are going to face your fears. You got that?”

I nodded hesitantly.

He chuckled. “Hey, don’t worry, kid. We’ll take it slow if we need to.”

I trusted Nolan.

If the mood outside the entrance was ominous, what waited below those scary stairs redefined the word. The air grew thick and dense, a sticky, oppressive cloud that made each breath feel like a struggle. We descended further into the depths of the dungeon, until we reached a straight path on leveled grounds. The tunnel was narrow, branching out into an even narrower opening that connected to another path and a fork in the road. The spider queen’s lair was a bit different than the first dungeon we’d encountered. Our first experience was pretty straight forward for the most part. This, however, was a sprawling network of caves and tunnels that weaved through patted earth, stone, and clay.

We eeny-meeny-minnie-moed a route and kept it moving. The glyphs along the walls were beginning to fade the further we explored, and I was losing confidence in my sense of perception the darker things got. I tried reading them, thinking that my skill, [Multiverse Adaptation] would kick in and help, but when I realized I couldn’t read the walls, I took my loss and kept it moving.

Besides, my head was preoccupied with something else anyway. I was getting uneasy, expecting an ambush of oversized arthropods. I wish I had some crystals to shine our path as it grew darker. I could use my flame breath, but at such close proximity to Nolan, it was a risk. Not only that, I didn’t want to disturb anything around us. These walls looked like they were alive….

I didn’t know how to describe it. I just felt feedback from them. Tiny web strands stretched into intricate patterns from within the walls. The further we went into the dungeon, the thicker and denser these webs became. They filled every crack and crevice, glowing ever so slightly, as if they were part of some living organism.

“There is something off about these walls,” Nolan said after studying them for a moment. “I think they’re made of flesh.”

I gulped. ‘Flesh?!’

He pointed at the surface, which were covered with tiny hair-like fibers that I hadn’t noticed before. “Looks a lot like tissue to me. But hey, we can call it clay if it makes you feel better.”

I didn’t have anything smart or sarcastic to say back to him because the thought of walking through a living dungeon was one of the most disturbing things I could think of. And that was saying a lot, considering how twisted my imagination was.

It was getting even darker now, the light from the glyphs nearly nonexistent. We were forced to slow down our pace. Eventually, we reached an area where we had to crawl on our hands and knees. Nolan went first, trying to go under the low horizontal hunk of stone and clay. He could barely fit, but he squeezed through with a grunt, making good use of his arm as a sweeper for all of that webbing that was blocking our path. The tight space made me even more paranoid, and I was worried that if I poked around down there and touched something I shouldn’t, that I might alert a swarm. Or worse….

Nolan shot up on his feet and dusted his knees off before insisting, “Come on. Your turn.”

I looked down at my tiny feet and quickly noticed how much softer the area around that underpass was compared to the rest of the dungeon. Now, I was no tomb raider, but this felt like a trap waiting to happen. While my reasoning was valid, given my observations, the fact that Nolan didn’t set off anything would mean that it was safe to pass. Right?

Right….

All I had to do was dip my big head through and slide to the other side without imagining an ambush of creepy critters.

Nolan extended his hand and pulled me up. “You alright?”

I nodded. ‘Yeah. Feel a little sticky, but I’m good otherwise.’

Of course, I spoke too soon. As soon as those words fled my thoughts, I felt something on my tail. At first, it was subtle, not so subtle for me not to yelp and jump, but subtle enough to think that something small just crossed my scales. But then, a tug, no, a suction, like I was being dragged down. My eyes ballooned up when I noticed I was being kidnapped by a pulsating sphincter in the ground!

‘I’m being sucked into the ass of the dungeon! HELP ME!’ I screamed, Nolan rushing in to help. He crouched down and grabbed me by my short arms before he yanked as hard as he could to pull me out. But then I heard a swarm of nasty buzzing heading our way!

‘KILLER WASPS!’ I shouted, my heart freezing at the sight of those giant buzzing bastards. Wasps with red eyes and stingers the size of daggers zoomed in our direction, maneuvering with crazy speed.

‘Dusty!’ I cried out internally, trying to summon my dire wolf for help. I forgot I had to [Summon] him the proper way through my codex, but even then, it didn’t work.

What was going on? Why wasn’t my obedient dark wolf emerging from the depths of hell and leaping to my aid?!

‘Nolan, I can’t summon my dog!’ I panicked.

Nolan glanced over his shoulder and mumbled, “Oh shit,” through clenched teeth. He gripped my arms even tighter and gave me one more pull, but I was still pinned down by the pull of the floor’s relentless orifice.

‘DON’T LEAVE ME!’ I cried helplessly as he drew his gun and pointed it at the swarm. His precious Scarlet shot with crazy precision, blasting through the air as he fought off the assault. Unfortunately for me, they were coming at us from both directions, their levels making me pale-faced:

[Dire Wasp, Level: 30]

[Dire Wasp, Level: 32]

[Dire Wasp, Level: 33]

They looked even bigger and deadlier up close as they ambushed me from behind. I swear I was going to pass out from fear when they got dangerously close. Wasps were scary enough; oversized dire wasps were downright terrifying! Nolan was preoccupied with the swarm, fighting them off as fast as he could. Despite how high-pitched I yelped, I couldn’t depend on him to save my hide. I had to fend for myself, and fend for myself I did. Swallowing my intense fear of bugs, I fought back. When one of them curled its back to sting me, I used my flame breath to roast it. Simultaneously, using my flame breath also loosened up the grip the ground had on my tail. Perfect—this fleshy pit had a weakness to fire. The more of it I used, the freer I became. Until I was able to wrap my tail around one, holding tight and squeezing the life out of it as it desperately tried to sting me.

I was in full panic mode, burning and flailing like my life depended on it. Because honestly? It did! In a wild, adrenaline-fueled moment, I escaped the sphincter trap by using a combination of [Fire Breath], resilience, and the sheer fear of being the result of a reverse bowel movement. Finally, the ass of the dungeon spat me out, launching me into the air like a catapult. I hit the fleshy wall with a thud before I dropped down carelessly onto the ground. But it didn't matter how ungraceful my landing was; I was finally free!

With no time to waste, I scrambled for my life. While I was out of the dungeon’s butt hole, I was not out of trouble. More of them swarmed in, their buzzing echoing through the tunnel. I didn’t have time to be afraid, so I planned my route of attack. They were relentless and tricky, shooting darts at me, straight from their stingers, forcing me to dodge and duck like crazy. I needed to get close enough to use my special skills or they wouldn’t land. Unfortunately, one grazed my scales, making my side tingle and burn. I had a feeling the sting was venomous, but I pushed through and fought back, knowing I didn’t have a choice. When I was close enough, I unleashed one hell storm of a [Meteor Strike].

Barbecued dire wasps rained down like fireworks. They all crashed into the walls, scattering in tiny explosions as they dropped like flies. A couple of them even blew holes into the fleshy surface, exposing a network of webbed innards that vibrated and convulsed. My exhaustion and relief were short lived, though; before I could even catch my breath, more of them showed up as if to avenge their fallen comrades.

In this endless mosh pit of flying nuisances, I was tapping into my mana reserve more than I ever did. I wasn’t sure if this was a good thing or a bad thing. The good part about it was I was leveling up bit by bit with these added experience points. The bad part about it was these bugs didn’t drop any loot to replenish said mana.

“This is a burner trap,” Nolan said after he fired his last round, his shots dispersing sweet sizzling waves of pain through those nasty bugs. He downed seven of those dire assholes with one shot before he turned over his shoulder and continued, “This part of the dungeon is meant to burn through our resources. More specifically, any item that restores mana. Hence, why these flying bastards aren’t dropping any loot. Any gullible guild would cap this point to max out EXP, but by the time they reach the next segment, they are assed out of resources.”

‘So what do we do? Leave?’

“Yes. Strategically, of course.”

Eventually, when their numbers were significantly lower, we made a run for it. We dashed down the tunnel, pushing through where the swarm kept spawning from. Unfortunately, there was no other route for us, and we dove head first into an even larger swarm. Without hesitation, I triggered my [Cyclone Shield], swallowing those bugs in a mini tornado before I blasted them outward in a burst of energy. That trick gave Nolan and I enough time to escape and find a fork in the tunnel’s path.

We staggered into the new corridor, panting and out of breath as the rest of them chased us. I didn’t know how much longer I could keep going without collapsing. But we kept at it, pushing forward while I could still muster enough energy to not pass out mid-run. Being this tired was unlike me, but I was so pumped up on adrenaline that I didn’t realize it, not until my body forced me to.

All of a sudden, I started feeling numbness all over. First, it was just my arms, but then that tingling sensation expanded throughout my entire body. It was the venom! In my rush to escape the swarm, I forgot to down an antidote from my inventory.

Before I could fetch it, my limbs jumbled up underneath me and I crashed to the ground, helpless and paralyzed.

“Shit!” Nolan shouted as he immediately slid to a stop. I could barely feel him scooping me up and cradling me, the sound of buzzing growing louder. His voice became muffled as he shouted at me. While faint, I could still catch the desperation in his undertone. My body was succumbing to the poison. I felt so stupid for being caught off guard like that, leaving Nolan to fight the swarm alone.

Eventually, his voice faded completely. Everything felt distant, cold, and blurry. Was this how it ended for me? Outplayed by a group of oversized flying creeps? At this point, I could barely keep my eyelids open, all sensation trickling from my bones. Before I knew it, it was lights out for me, and there was nothing left to do but succumb to the swallowing darkness.
