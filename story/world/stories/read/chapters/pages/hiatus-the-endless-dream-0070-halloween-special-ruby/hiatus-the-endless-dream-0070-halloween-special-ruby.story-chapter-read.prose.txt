Dreamlight - Ruby

The room was a perfect eight-foot cube. Each Cube was embedded in a wall with eighty other cubes, and there existed ten walls in total. By the pale-eyed woman's count, that meant there would be anywhere between eight hundred ten and one thousand six hundred twenty people in the world at any given time. Sometimes a specific cube would hold more than one person, but never more than two—even then, not for more than a minute—and a cube would never be empty. That was the rule. That was the only rule.

On the fifth wall, in the Cube, three down and seven across sat the woman with lightless, pale brown eyes. There was nothing behind them, not even the contemplation of the next action. She would stand, watch, sit, pace, sew, sleep, breathe, flail, or scream. The sum total of the woman's existence was summarized with nine actions. Until the day ended, there would be nothing else. Then, for one glorious minute, her options would double. Until then… she opted to watch.

She looked out from the Cube's translucent internals. Nothing else existed besides the walls, cubes, and people, and the cubes never moved. A vague, dull relief pricked at the woman whenever she people-watched; thankful was she that her wall was located in the center and not at one of the two ends. Her eyes drifted to the sixth wall and the cubes she called "The Team," located four across in the top corner—a block of four connected cubes.

The rules still applied to them, just as they did to everyone, uniquely. Or it could be more accurate to say it applied to the Cubes more than the people. One person per Cube, two at most, and not for more than a minute. Those four had been there for years, and they had been inseparable. Before then, those cubes had been a constant bloodbath with a rotating cast. The pale-eyed woman stared with unbridled envy so deep her face slightly twitched, and the scene on the opposite wall came into focus.

One of the guys—she called him Bee, having no way of getting his actual name—left his room to kiss his friend. Just a little peck. They never had time for anything else. As soon as the majority of Bee's body left the threshold, a new person, confused and wary, sat up from the bed in the corner. She recognized the freshly created person as Rachel. For some reason, that woman rarely appeared, and when she did, she acted erratic. Would cry and scream and yell, and annoy the other four people in the connected cubes. She did that for forty-five seconds before Bee walked back into his room with a face-splitting grin. The man was on cloud nine, barely remembering to take a nearby knife and stab Rachel in the throat. Her body vanished before it hit the ground.

Bee sat on his bed and continued talking to his friends.

The pale-eyed woman wished she were down there. She wanted anyone else to talk to. It would take a year for them to not hate her after she killed to take a cube, but it had happened before. They'd get over it.

They had it best.

The only other one that might have had it better was the one on the fourth wall, 6 down, 8 across. A barrier split the room. One person would spawn on the empty side, and the original would be on the other side. They could talk for fifty-nine seconds before the original would disappear. The old would become the original, and the cycle would repeat. A short existence, but there were others at least.

She had not decided which Cube had it worst, and she would like to think that if another had been here, they could have convinced her of a choice. There were just so many options she thought were bad. Any nudge would be sufficient to decide, but there were no outside influences that could reach the woman.

One Cube seemed alive and breathing. It would expand ever so slightly, and the person inside would be buffeted on all sides, slammed into walls and the floor as if by a cyclone. Then it would contract, and the person would shoot out of the room into nothing just as another entered from nowhere. One had sped up time, in which each person would grow up and give birth to their replacement, and die in sixty seconds, both men and women. She was unclear about how the males gave birth, but it seemed painful.

Pale eyes flicked over to that room to see Thomas being born. She knew everyone's face by now. There were only about a thousand of them that would cycle. Thomas was the most common by far, with three in sight from her own Cube.

This was her everyday routine as she waited for her room to be activated. She had long since stopped preparing for it. She would get lucky or she wouldn't. She would die or she wouldn't. She never remembered dying, but it was impossible for it to not have happened.

The chime from a far-off cube told her it was about to start. Her breathing didn't even hitch as she became blind and disoriented. It was time. When her vision returned, pale eyes met pale eyes. No fear. No regret. Pure apathy was directed at the other, but internally, they both wanted to be the one who lived.

Forty seconds later, one of them was dead and the other was alive. She'd never know if she was the newcomer or the person who had been before. The memories were the same. They only diverged in the moment they split, and only for the time it took to kill the other. She didn't know what would happen if she didn't kill the other within sixty seconds. It had never happened.

The woman sat down. Breathing hard. She couldn't even cry about this anymore if she wanted to, and she certainly didn't. She had no memories before coming to this place—and she suspected there wasn't anything to remember—but she remembered everything since her first day in the box ten years ago.

It had been nine years since she stopped crying every day. Seven years and two months since she stopped crying at all. Two years and eight months since she stopped begging to be let out. She had thought the begging might work. She was special after all. There had been a million Thomas' and Bee's over the years, but there had only ever been one of her. That had to mean something. That had to be important.

The man in the top hat shattered that dream.

He had looked her in the eye and broken the news.

"You are never getting out of here."

Why. That was too cruel. This was all too cruel; it made no sense at all.

"That's the rule in this world. Each Cube must always have one person."

Who decided that? Who decided that! Free me!

"Even if I wanted to there'd be no point. Where would you go? What would you do? You can't even fathom it can you? When you imagine freedom what do you see? Just another box. A nicer box, perhaps. With other people and affections? That's what grounds you right? Physical touch."

You don't know that. You don't know any of that!

"I do know. Because that's how I made you. It's how I made the world. You weren't supposed to be conscious, but there's nothing to be done about that now. When I've gotten all I need from this experiment---playing with this rule---the walls will come down, and you will cease to be. Please, just hold on a little longer."

She wanted to scream. To yell that she didn't want that. She wanted to exist. She wanted to be free. So, she made a plan.

Reality slammed back into the woman as the past fell away. She stared at her own dead body. It wouldn't be long until it faded away, but the body had to go somewhere after it vanished. She believed that with all her heart. With everything she had.

She tried to kill herself quickly, so she had time to send a message—stitched or carved onto her dead body's back—"Help," it read.

She had sent one thousand nine hundred eighty-nine bodies out with that message.

This one made one thousand nine hundred ninety.

Soon, the body disappeared, and she stood and continued her routine. She stared out at the other cubes.

Today, though, something caught her eye.

Bee was getting greedy. He left his room again, and in his place, another person formed on the bed. One she didn't recognize. Tall, dark skin, large and muscular. He smiled as he started talking to the four others in the "Team" cube. They ignored him. They scowled. The new man—she named him Caleb for the time being—left the room, another person spawned, a Thomas.

The woman with pale eyes frowned. That wasn't supposed to be possible. She felt her brain straining and breaking as she tried to understand what was happening. Two people in a room was all she had considered, but now there were three. Which meant that there could be one thousand six hundred twenty-one people in the world at that moment. It wasn't possible. One thousand six hundred twenty people were supposed to be the cap. Right? That's how it had always been.

The "Team" attacked Caleb. The newcomer didn't move. Every attacker just vanished, and new people replaced them. She couldn't hear what he was saying, but he was waving his hands wildly, rotating his finger in a circle. He started running through the four rooms, gathering people as he went. More spawned. More joined him in running.

One thousand six hundred twenty-five people

One thousand six hundred twenty-nine people

One thousand six hundred forty-three people.

They continued to run through the Cubes. More spawned. More were created until eventually no more people were created. A room was empty.

Something in reality strained. It tried to complete its rule, but there was nothing for it to pull from. The world wanted one person per room, but didn't have the means to enforce it with what the man in the top hat had given it. There was no one else to spawn. A local paradox was created, and the world shattered because of it. For the first time, the pale-eyed woman saw what was beyond the white void. Stars. Beautiful cosmos.

She wasn't awake for the rest of it. The blowback from a world breaking too much for her to endure. She came to quick enough, though. The man in the top hat was bound in ethereal chains. He looked sickly and broken. The one she had dubbed Caleb kneeled down close to her. He asked her name. She did not have one.

"Found your bodies strewn around the Dream. This guy was using time dilation. A whole mountain of them fell. Stirred quite the trouble. Let me track you down and find this guy. You got guts." He had laughed after that, having come up with a joke, "real diamond in the rough, but if I go around calling you Diamond, people will get the wrong idea. So, let's call you Ruby instead."

He had then turned to the man in a top hat. She barely understood anything he said. The words she recognized, but the order they were in was odd. They talked of rules. Of constraints, and of how the Dream fulfilled set rules as it saw fit. The man in the top hat begged. Claimed it wasn't his fault. That it was the Dream who did the awful things. It did not help him.

The man made a miniature world---a compression of reality so dense she could not see the bottom of it----and in that miniature world he had one rule, "The man in the top hat must suffer".

That was the first time she met Dorian. The True Child of the Dream.

She would meet him three more times, and each time reality broke.
