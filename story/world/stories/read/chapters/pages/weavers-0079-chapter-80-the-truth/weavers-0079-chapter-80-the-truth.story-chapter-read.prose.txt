Chapter 80 - The Truth

High above the colosseum, Kana leaned against the back of the seat in front of her, one elbow propped lazily on the rough wooden edge.

"Oi mate, sit down! I'm trynna watch the fights."

"Fuck off and sit somewhere else then. Not like you're gonna see much anyway. Trees are blocking most the view for here." Kana replied coldly.

The professors seemed to murmur quietly among themselves. Occasional nods of approval or shaking heads denoting criticism appeared as they watched from their special seating area. Kana was only half watching, her attention sat focused on one man.

Below them, the battle raged on. Dust plumed from the ground where spells collided, and the roar of the crowd pulsed like a living heartbeat through the marble stands. The air shimmered faintly with magical residue, refracting the torchlight into ghostly hues of blue and green.

Kana barely noticed. Her eyes weren’t on the students.

They were on him.

Professor Aric stood a few seats away, just at the edge of her peripheral vision. His dark grey cloak trimmed with silver thread hung perfectly draped across one arm, not a crease out of place. From a casual glance, he looked as composed as ever: a scholar among scholars, the very image of authority and respect.

But Kana knew better. Why he wasn't with the other professors, she had no idea. But all the better for her. It made him easier to watch.

Her Mind threads stretched outward delicate, invisible filaments that shimmered in the unseen spectrum of thought and emotion. She cast them like a fisherman’s net, light and careful, brushing along the surface of the stands. She wanted to sneak a peek at Aric's mind, maybe see if Kaelin was all talk. But as she pushed closer, Aric seemed to catch on. He looked around suddenly as if he were looking for the perpetrator, causing Kana to reel her threads back in quickly.

That was when she felt it.

At first, it was no more than a subtle pressure in her skull, like the tension before a storm. Then it deepened, spreading through her mind like oil through water. A static hum, growing slowly, becoming more of an ache. The constant pressure giving her pain.

She gasped, out of breath from trying to hold back her screams.

Looking back up at Aric, she though his face might have held a small smirk, but before she could read into it, he looked back at the match, showing no emotion bar the expected interest of any other spectator.

Kana’s breath settled.

Kana thought to herself, trying to keep composure.

She watched in uneasy fascination. Beneath the disciplined rhythm of breathing which he held close, a nod to his training, the energy flickered between unease and impatience.

Maybe he has somewhere to be. But where?

And still, the others noticed nothing.

Professor Reed was focused entirely on maintaining the terrain below, her hands weaving through the air as new trees erupted from the earth in response. The other professors all watched with intent as some scribbled roughly on their notepads. Around them, the spectators cheered, the noise swallowing any sense of danger.

Only Kana saw it.

Only she felt it.

Her heart drummed faster as she sent her Mind threads back out. This time, she stretched them thinner, hoping to avoid Aric's watch. The closer she got to him, the worse it felt. Similarly to before, the pain started as an ache, slowly growing with her threads' proximity to him.

A jolt of psychic pain lashed through her skull. She gasped, flopping back in her seat, the dizziness affectingher vision. Suddenly she saw some words in her mind. They had been placed there, presumably by the same person as who attacked her.

Aric.

Stop what you're doing. Don't try again, this is your last warning.

Kana looked back up at Aric as her vision returned to normal.

She wasn't imagining it, he stared into her soul, eyes like daggers. He wasn't playing around, and that was no empty threat.

Kana steadied her breathing, pulse racing. “Fucking hell” she whispered through gritted teeth. “Kaelin wasn’t lying."

Her eyes narrowed. Her fear was easily removed. So what if he was powerful, they had numbers. And if Kaelin wasn't lying, then it just meant that more exciting stuff was to come.

Then Aric’s hand twitched.

It was a small movement, barely even visible, but with the combination of Kana's focus and her sharpened senses, it was thunderous. His fingers spasmed once, twice, before curling into a fist at his side. His lips began to move. Slowly at first, then faster. She leaned forward.

He was talking to himself.

The sound didn’t carry, but she could read his lips every so often when he turned. It was only a few words at a time though, and so ultimately useless. His head tilted slightly, as though listening to a voice just over his shoulder.

Kana’s skin prickled.

The whispers grew steadier, more fervent. His breathing hitched. The tension in his shoulders built until he was shaking.

She rose to her feet.

And then, suddenly, his head snapped toward her.

Their eyes met across the short distance, cold, hollow, and inhumanly spaced out. For a split second, she felt the weight of something vast pressing against her mind, heavy and suffocating. Her pulse thundered in her ears.

Then, just as abruptly, the fog rolled back over his gaze. His face slackened, calm again. Without a word, he turned and began to walk away.

He didn’t excuse himself. Didn’t acknowledge any of the students who had sat beside him, nor asked them to make way. He simply left, pushed his way to the nearest exit, and left.

Kana blinked, breath catching in her throat. Her heart felt like it was hammering against her ribs.

She had to keep with him.

She couldn't let him run free.

The edges of his cloak disappeared into the light as he stepped down the well thought out ladder-exit down from the heightened stands. The instant he was gone, the oppressive weight in Kana’s mind eased, like a storm cloud passing over the sun.

“Aria’s not gonna like this.” she muttered, forcing her feet to move.

***

The stands vibrated with the crowd’s energy as she hurried down the ladder. As she reached the ground and kept running, her boots struck the marble in quick succession, echoing faintly beneath the sound of thunder above. Rain drummed against her arms, each drop bursting into a silver tear as it slid down.

Kana thought to herself.

Kana pressed a hand to a railing which stood on her left. She closed her eyes and reached out with her threads, feeling for the mark she had placed on Aria when they had met up in the warehouse before the colosseum battle.

There.

Across the arena, on the opposite tower, Aria stood tall at her observation post. Even at this distance, she radiated poise: back straight, hands clasped behind her, eyes focused on the chaos below. Her violet hair caught the light like liquid silk.

Kana sent the thought with the precision of an arrow:

He’s leaving. Kaelin was right, we need to follow.

For a moment, silence. Then the familiar, cool tone pressed gently back into her mind:

You sure?

If what I saw wasn't it, there's nothing else out there which could convince me.

Alright. You go, I'll head down from here and catch up. Just do it quietly.

Kana’s lips quirked despite herself. “Quietly,” she murmured. “Sure, I can do that...”

Across the way, Aria tilted her head almost imperceptibly. Then, without breaking her calm composure, she turned and began her descent. Each movement was deliberate, her robes brushing softly against the seats as she passed them, before she jumped down the ladder with one quick motion.

No one noticed her leave.

Kana’s chest tightened. “Right then,” she whispered. “Time to see what else the old man’s hiding.”

She pulled her hood up, letting the dim lamplight slide off her features as she strode toward the exit of the academy gardens. The wind had grown fiercer, trees flexed like fishing rods in some mad struggle. A low rumble of thunder followed her as she snapped onto the mark she'd managed on Aric, leaving her current position in pursuit.

For a heartbeat, she glanced back at the towering arena.

Kaelin was still fighting, darting between trees and splintered rocks, her movements fluid but strained. She sat, watching from above as she had for most of the fight.

“Hang on, Kaelin,” Kana murmured. “We’ve got your back.”

Then she slipped into the darkness of the night.

***

The roar of the crowd faded behind her as she descended into the tunnels beneath the academy. The air changed at once. Cool, damp, and heavy with the scent of damp rock. Water dripped from somewhere unseen, echoing softly in the silence.

Torches lined the corridor, their light flickering weakly. Shadows shifted along the walls, long and crooked. Markings were carved into the stones, remnants of both recent and old alumni. They came together to create a canvas of thoughts and emotions. Those of these kids, down here without permission, skulking around, both hoping not to get caught, and enjoying the thrill of the possibility.

Kana’s footsteps echoed softly, quickly swallowed by the vastness.

She followed the lingering trail of Aric’s presence, her marking growing weaker as he increased the distance between them. It led her deeper, down toward the old archives.

Far above, muffled through the stone, came the cheers and shouts of the battle still raging. She tried not to think of Kaelin amid it all, both unaware of what was happening just below, and possibly one of the most knowledgable.

Her pace quickened. “Just please be okay up there.” she muttered, voice swallowed by the dark.

The corridor split ahead - left toward the school archives, and right into the forbidden archives. She hesitated only a second before turning right.

The air changed again. A low hum built gradually, resonant and physical, making her teeth ache. It wasn’t sound, not exactly. More like a pressure, spreading deep throughout her entire body. Similar to that which she had felt before, only less targeted.

Then came the voices.

Two of them.

She slowed her steps, pressing herself against the cold stone wall and peering around the corner.

There, framed in torchlight, stood Aric. His cloak hung perfectly still despite the draft. His posture was rigid, like a robot which had only been programmed to do this one thing and nothing more. His hand twitched once, then again. He was murmuring to himself, words spilling out unevenly, rapid and unintelligable.

Then, impossibly, another voice answered.

Well, not exactly another voice. More like if a different person tried using the same one. It had different mannerisms. Different methods of both enunciation and pronunciation.

Kana’s heart hammered. Every instinct screamed that she shouldn’t be here.

At the far end of the hall, heading down another corridor, a lanky figure appeared.

Aria.

Their eyes met across the corridor. Aria nodded once, her fingers twitching with tension.

Wait, how did she find her way down? I though she could only use Nature magic... Kana stood, deep in thought despite the situation.

They had Aric cornered.

Or so they thought.

Aric’s voice stopped. Slowly, his head tilted, and his words came soft but clear, cutting through the resonating hum like a well-sharpened blade.

“Did you really think I didn't notice you?"

Kana froze.

He hadn’t turned. How did he know? Was she being too loud? What was it!? What had she done wrong!?

Aria’s eyes narrowed, and her hand ghosted toward her threads. “Careful,” she mouthed silently.

Kana tensed, clenching her fist around the weapon which she had gotten from the warehouse. Not perfect, but it would do. Something's better than nothing.

But Aric only laughed. The sound was low and erratic. “You’ve no idea,” he murmured, “how long I’ve been waiting for this.”

And with that, the torches along the wall flared, then blew out one by one, each extinguished flame sending curls of smoke into the halls.

Within seconds, the corridor was swallowed by darkness.

Kana muttered under her breath subconsciously, "Shit."

The only thing which remained was the echoing laughs of a madman.
