I woke facedown in a tangle of black leaves and torn silver bark.

There was nothing in my stomach, so my retching was just the miserable, breathless convulsions of gasped-up bile and seizing muscles. Acrid heat burned at my throat as I shook against the fallen witchwood.

‘Tell her you belong to a High Fae.’

A dreadful thrill had followed those words. The idea of someone so beautiful, so strong, declaring me as his… Something in me trembled at the idea. My heart ached at the very thought.

That I was precious. Worthy of protecting.

He didn’t mean it. It was a… a suggestion. A threat. Something for me to say to dissuade the other monster. Because he wants me alive, because he needs the shard, not because he cares.

It was just words. Empty words.

Frosty air brushed at my hair. Horace’s voice slithered along the breeze.

‘It’s considered, ah, poor taste among our kind to hunt another’s prey.’

Somehow, the thought that I was prey- intended to be killed, devoured, torn apart- was almost appealing right now. If the Fae wanted me dead, then there didn’t need to be any second-guessing at motives, or daring to hope that he might actually be good.

He just wants me to trust him with the shard. That’s what this is. All of it. All the… the charm, the supposed kindness. Healing me. The garden and the jewels and… he’s trying to manipulate me. He’ll probably tear my throat out as soon as I fall for it. As soon as he has what he wants.

Words. Just words. A threat to wield in the face of death.

All the same, my heart wouldn’t stop hammering.

Strong hands on my waist. Endless, glittering eyes. Fingers threading through my hair.

Focus. Find Teela. Nothing else matters. Not now.

I gripped the cool, once-smooth bark and dragged myself upright. My legs were shaking. My limbs felt as though all strength had been drained out of them.

Got to find Teela. He said there’s still time.

I staggered a few steps away, then froze with a sharp intake of breath. Something behind me had hissed. Barely louder than a whisper, or than the rustle of wind through grass, and yet… a sound I’d know anywhere.

I turned, hardly daring to hope.

For a moment my heart fell. The tree was still split savagely in half, still torn and ravaged. Then… twitching through the rotted grass like the heads of frightened snakes… velvet leaves.

Reaching for me.

I knelt beside the branch with a sob, stretching my fingers out to touch the feather-soft black. The leaves whispered, twisting around me with aching slowness.

“You’re alive,” I moaned, laying my other hand on the glassy bark. “You’re alive, you’re alive…”

Was this this rune? Luridel? Did I… revive it? Heal it?

The witchwood was still ashen and ravaged, but… there, in some of the cracks and holes, I could see substance that hadn’t been there before. I peered closer, sliding my shaking fingers into the wounds.

It was wood. Not glassy, not silver, but supple and shaded like a bruised cherry. The red wood was sparse; just shadowy sections knit deep within some of the tree’s tears. I could make out lush red fibers stretched taut, almost bound to the glassy witchwood flesh. It was beautiful… and a bit eerie.

The leaves curled against my fingers, and I could feel the thin velvet shivering against my skin. Their movements were so slow it made me want to weep.

“I’m so sorry this happened to you.” I pressed my forehead against the bark, feeling almost guilty. Any wounds I’d mended were pitiable when compared to the overall damage, but I couldn’t afford to use the rune again. I loathed the idea that I’d urged some hope, some fight, into the fallen tree when I had to then turn and leave it helpless.

“I’ll come back.” Ancient pines and firs loomed overhead, bearing witness to my vow. I shivered, rising. The leaves wound around me, clinging… then drooped. “I’ll do more to help you, I promise. But right now someone else needs me more.”

Trying the rune again was out of the question. Teela needed me to keep moving, and I didn’t have it in me to face him right now. Not after what had just happened. And whatever fight I’d coaxed back into the branches was far too feeble to consider using it as a weapon. I flinched away as the silver and black went limp, crumpling into the dirt.

“I’m sorry,” I whispered again, then turned and hurried back towards Snowmelt.

I caught a few glimpses of the overcast sky as I walked; enough to guess it was not yet mid-morning, which meant I hadn’t been unconscious for long. Next I managed to force myself, very woodenly and with no enthusiasm, to gnaw on a seed cake and some dried meat. I would be of no use to anyone if I kept fainting away. And I felt as though I was reaching the point of physical weariness that all it would take was a strong wind, rather than a burst of divine power, for me to collapse.

‘Tell her you belong to a High Fae.’

His deep, soft voice chased me along the path. Captivating and cruel, tempting and terrifying. I tried desperately to leave the words in the fallen leaves and rustling pines, but they loomed like shadows in the back of my mind.

Teela. Only Teela. Everything else can wait.

But, try as I might, my determination quivered like the green canopy high above. Bitter air nipped my skin and I pulled my cloak tight, shaking from more than just the cold.

Five days. It really has felt like a lifetime, but it’s only been five days since I left Fellbrook. I never thought I could miss something so much. Durst, Royce, Marion... My home where I had people who loved me and a good, peaceful life and… and…

Where, in my moments of solitude and trembling quiet, there was light. Hope. A vow.

Beloved.

“Did you see this, too?”

I stopped, turning my face skyward. The question had come out almost thoughtlessly.

I dared my next thought in a voice so soft she probably couldn’t have heard it, even if she had seen this moment.

“You set me on this path. Told Durst to leave me alone in the house, told him I had to run away by myself. You said… s-said you loved me. But now… all of this… how can everything possibly be alright? How could you have wanted all of this to happen?”

There was, of course, no answer. The forest creaked and swayed around me.

“And I… I failed. Saints, five days, and I already failed! I didn’t bring the shard to Pinnacle, I gave it to Renner. But I had to, I couldn’t… I couldn’t just leave Teela!”

‘You belong-’

“I didn’t do anything wrong!” I shouted, curling my hands into fists.

Had there been birds or insects nearby, they doubtlessly would have flown away. But, in this nearly-lifeless place, the only response was my own voice echoing off stoic trunks.

I didn’t. Royce was dying, and I did the only thing I could to save him. I didn’t know what it all meant. I didn’t know it would bind me to him. And giving the shard to Renner… ashes, how could I have done anything else? I couldn’t very well fight him, try to hurt him, and Teela’s life is worth more than some divine hunk of metal!

“I didn’t do anything wrong.”

The truth of it all quickened my steps, urged some heat back into my trembling limbs and brought steel to my spine.

And it became my mantra once again as I forged through the forest. I ground it out over and over again between chattering, clenched teeth, daring the memories chasing me to say otherwise.

They did not.
