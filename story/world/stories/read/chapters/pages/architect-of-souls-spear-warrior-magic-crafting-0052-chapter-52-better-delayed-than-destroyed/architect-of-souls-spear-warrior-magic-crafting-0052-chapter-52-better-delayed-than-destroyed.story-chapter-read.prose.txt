The last day of his enforced break, waiting for his mental body to reform, passed quickly. Ferris was eager to return to cultivating that night, but he took the chance to relax until then.

He visited the bathhouse and spent a lazy afternoon soaking in the pool. On the way out, he learned they had special rooms for cultivators to purge impurities, just like his uncle had predicted. Now he just needed to figure out how to actually do it.

Dinner was a quiet affair, in contrast to last night’s large gathering. Both Brix and Cyrus were missing, which left Ferris eating alone with Karl. Neither spoke much over the meal. Jade didn’t appear to mind or notice the awkward silence, happily asking for more food and giving Ferris an excuse not to engage with Karl.

Afterwards, Ferris excitedly took Jade back to his room. It’d been just over two days since Ferris’ spiritual body had died, melted away in acid, and he should be able to cultivate again.

Lying down, holding Jade in his arms, Ferris sought the calm of meditation. Being idle the last two days was frustrating, but also a guilty pleasure. Lazy mornings and quiet afternoons reading at the library were more fun than breaking rocks to refine spirit gems, after all. At least he’d been able to catch up on some sleep.

Opening his eyes in the soulspace, Ferris was happy to find his mental body was fully recovered.

After grabbing the axe as a backup weapon, Ferris took Jade to first investigate where he had died. As expected, there was no sign of the bone spear or Ferris’ body. In fact, there was no sign that anything strange had happened. The area appeared the same as the rest of the slimespace, all evidence washed away by the wave of acid.

In better news, the Digest River was full of qi. Every clam he could see had a qi chunk ready to harvest, and Ferris wasted no time beginning to collect. Jade and Ferris’ coordination fishing out the chunks had improved, efficiently hooking and pulling the qi from the river. Ferris still had to reforge the chain several times, as it eventually dissolved in acid, but he had plenty of crafting material.

He’d had some time to think about Nyoka’s advice to leave Jade’s qi alone. She claimed it would ‘support a larger population’ of spirit animals. He’d originally assumed the strange ants, clams, and birds were spirit animals. Now, he wasn’t so sure. Nyoka had implied they should look like slimes.

In the end, he’d already emptied the Digest River many days in a row without seeing any adverse effects. It’d also been full for days, and nothing had touched it. Ferris decided to keep collecting the qi, but watch closely for any problems. It was just too useful to leave sitting around.

Seeing the Qi Pool rise back up from almost nothing was a good feeling. Uncle Winton had told Ferris that a larger Qi Pool would help in many ways, but it felt like he could never save any. If Ferris could gather this much every day, he might actually be able to progress.

But first, Ferris needed to replace his weapon. He had plenty of bones from the chain demons, so he decided to try to recreate the imprint bone spear.

As he prepared the same mixture of melted bone and qi as last time, Ferris’ eyes were drawn to the legacy chest. Inside were several more ‘imprint’ weapons, promising to teach him the skills of his clan.

The first bone spear he’d made had been a pure joy to use, helping him reach a new level of mastery. He wanted a new one.

But what good is it, Ferris asked himself, looking down at the ground, why keep practicing a spear style built around the Water Jet ability? How far can I take Bluereef Surging Spear Art before hitting a wall?

Was he just wasting his time chasing a dream that would never happen?

With a sigh, he refocused on the task at hand. He needed a weapon, and a spear would aid him more than any other.

Placing his hand into the cooled mixture, Ferris focused his mind on the concept of a spear: the strength and flexibility of it in his hand, combined with years of practicing various forms and movements. Countless lectures and endless hours spent holding the weapon flashed through his mind.

Pulling his hands out of the pot, Ferris drew out a new spear. Visually, it appeared the same as the last one, but the surreal feeling of guidance felt more intense.

Practicing with the weapon, Ferris easily fell back into the same meditative trace he had last time. It was reassuring to know that the first bone spear wasn’t a fluke, and he was happy to confirm he could recreate it. The new spear used about a fifth of the Qi Pool, but he had at least learned more—and made a better weapon.

Going over to the legacy chest, Ferris unlocked it and removed the first imprint weapon from it. He tried the same practice forms as before, but couldn’t even get through the first couple of motions. Everything felt clumsy and awkward. He knew, on some deep level, that everything was wrong.

Sighing, Ferris returned the spear to the legacy chest and relocked it. There wasn’t time to practice it now, as he had too many other priorities.

It also wasn’t the right time to worry about having spent half of his life learning a style he could never truly master, but that wasn’t so easy to stop.

Picking up his new bone spear and crossbow, Ferris and Jade then collected qi from his Air Passage. There was much less qi here than Jade’s Digest River; it was both slower to accumulate, and the demons were still raiding it. They encountered two of the ragged, cloaked demons, but the creatures quickly ran away each time. He did find enough qi chunks to fully replace what he used on the spear.

Afterwards, Ferris sat down in front of his Heartfire and considered his next steps. His uncle’s advice had given him a lot to think about over the last couple of days, and he had plenty of downtime to make plans.

There were many things Ferris wanted to experiment with, but he had limited time, qi, and materials. The full harvest of qi from Jade’s Digest River and the pile of corpses from his hunting gave him something to play with. Ferris had decided to try solving three problems that night: create a backup weapon, protect the two ability seeds in the slimespace, and collect the impurities from the chain demon fort.

Ferris had previously neglected making a secondary weapon, but several fights where he had lost his spear had made it a priority, especially considering the grasping nature of the chain demon’s attack.

This was also a chance to test out a new material: the red lizard corpses Ferris had collected from the spirit gem refinement. They weren’t from his soul, so he was excited to see what they could do in qi crafting.

Ferris filled his cooking pot with equal amounts of qi and red lizard, and then heated it over his Heartfire. The lizard cooked down and merged with the qi, similar to the demons.

After it had cooled, he reached in and visualized a dagger. The red lizards had been small, quickly darting in and out of attack range. Their teeth were short but sharp, able to slice through skin and cloth. The weapon he imagined would be used the same, with fast strikes to bleed the enemy. Ferris had never been trained to fight with a dagger like he had the spear, but he had used basic knives often for domestic tasks. He threw those memories in as well: food preparation, childish carving, gutting fish, and more.

Ferris pulled a red-tinted dagger out of the pot. The handle and the blade were all one material, but the grip had the texture of the red lizard’s scaly hide. It looked and felt like a solid weapon, but there was nothing special about it. The blade lacked the extra mental weight of the bone spear. No ‘imprint.’

Although disappointed that he couldn’t repeat his success on another type of weapon, Ferris was happy with it as a backup option. It also confirmed that he couldn’t add that special intent to just anything he wanted. He assumed he needed more experience wielding a dagger if he wanted to make an imprint weapon of one.

He couldn’t tell any discernible difference using the red lizards compared to the demon corpses. Once Ferris had more qi, he wanted to test the durability of several different materials, but that would need to wait. He made a simple sheath for the blade with plain qi, and tied it to his belt.

Done with crafting, Ferris made a trip to the slimespace. After watering the Core Tree with qi he looked around to see how vulnerable the ability seeds would be if Jade was injured, and a wave of acid came down through the dome’s wall.

Neither seed had sprouted above ground yet, but he was worried about losing them to an unlucky injury. Should I dig the seeds up, and store them for later? He wondered. Then I could replant them once I find a way to protect them.

That would slow down Ferris’ development, and waste most of the liquid qi he had been watering them with, but it would be better than losing the ability seeds completely.

Nearby, several of the grey ants were entering one of the sandstone pillars. Most entrances to the pillars were high up, although some were lower down. The higher ones would help keep acid out. It wouldn’t help if the entire dome disappeared, but it was better than nothing.

Looking back at the Core Tree, it was atop a high tower. If Ferris could build an acid-resistant tower or very high wall for each ability, would it be enough to protect them from the waves of acid? It would need something to keep acid from getting in from above as well. In fact, the Jump ability was already slightly protected underground until it sprouted.

A wall or tower might help protect the Jump ability—depending on how much flooding happened with large injuries—but the Grow ability was even more vulnerable. Ferris couldn’t think of any way to protect something inside the dome when the dome itself would be cut open.

He briefly considered moving the Jump seed to one of the two towers, but his understanding from eating the seed’s fruit was that the ability needed more room than was available, eventually growing into a massive tree.

There was also a risk that digging up the seed after it had been planted would harm it. If it had started to put down roots, would it die if the roots were pulled out?

With a sigh, Ferris decided to dig up both the Grow and Jump seeds. It was painful to pause his progress, but he didn’t see any way the abilities would survive long-term where they were. Jump had some protection until it sprouted, but Ferris didn’t think he was close enough to being able to build a wall around it.

It was better to risk digging the seeds up than to spend more time and qi developing them, only to watch everything melt away in a wave of acid. Even if this ended up destroying them, it was better now than after investing more into a sunk project.

When Jade lived in the slime ranch, they had been cut daily to harvest slime fertilizer. Despite this, the Core Tree was able to grow and produce fruit. Ferris would need to learn exactly how injuries in the real world affected the slimespace, and how to protect his abilities through them.

Before he developed any abilities in the slimespace, a proof of concept would need to be done. He would pick a spot and attempt to protect it from any damage that could happen.

Committed to the decision, he considered how to remove the seeds as safely as possible. Ferris would never have typically risked this, but he felt it was better than leaving them vulnerable. While digging them up might hurt them, doing nothing would condemn them to be drowned in acid.

Abilities grew out of a seed, but they weren’t the same as plants in real life. There may be similarities, but it was important to understand that not all the same rules applied. Unfortunately, real-life plants were the only basis Ferris had.

He decided to treat this like transplanting a tree. He would make a pair of pots, and then move everything around the seeds with them to preserve any roots they may have created. He felt this had a good chance of saving the progress he’d made watering them so far.

This raised questions, like whether the seed would continue growing in the pot. If Ferris moved the pot into his soulspace, would the ability grow to affect his body instead of Jade’s? Could you make movable ability structures with big enough pots?

He didn’t know, but with a quick trip to his Qi Pool, Ferris had two barrel-sized pots created. He grabbed one and took it to the slimespace. After approaching the spot where he had planted the Grow seed, Ferris used his shovel to carefully extract the seed—and anything around it—into the pot.

Some of the material around the seed was the dirt Ferris had packed in on top, but most of it was the strange, hard translucent material of the dome. A large hole was left in the wall after he was done—although he had been careful not to dig through the wall to the other side. Ferris sat nearby as he considered whether he should try to fill it.

Every once in a while, one of the grey ants would approach the hole and feel around. They didn’t do anything to repair it, but did seem drawn to investigate it every time one passed by.

In the end, Ferris decided this was an opportunity to learn more about the dome. He would leave the hole there and check on it later to see if it healed naturally. Given that nothing had attacked Ferris for digging into the wall, he also collected a few samples of its translucent green material to experiment with. He hoped it would be resistant to acid.

Holding the potted barrel with the Grow seed, Ferris decided to keep it in his soulspace. There were risks everywhere he could store it. The soulspace had thieves, but the slimespace had tidal waves of acid. In the end, he liked the symmetry of storing it next to the Assimilate Object seed, which was placed inside his core pedestal in the soulspace.

The planted pot was the largest thing Ferris had moved between the slimespace and the soulspace, but he had no trouble returning to the soulspace while holding it. He just closed his eyes and imagined leaving the slimespace with it, and it was done.

Taking the other pot, Ferris repeated his actions with the Jump seed. He placed its pot right next to the Grow seed in the soulspace. He decided to continue watering them inside the pots. It might not be necessary, but even with qi being limited, he would rather do everything he could to keep the seeds alive. Qi was replaceable, but the seeds were based on random chance.

It was getting late. Ferris knew he would be getting less sleep that night. It was inevitable that work had piled up after spending two nights unable to cultivate.

He had one task left, but he knew it would take the longest. It would also be the most dangerous.

Uncle Winton had said the four burning towers at the fort would attract more demons. It was past time to do something about it.
