Chapter 188 / B3-33: Bearing a Blossom

As the trio hiked up the high wall of the vale, Joe found himself trying to argue his way out of being a contender for the Spring Crown.  He had never been a fan of politics when he was on Earth; he sure as hell didn’t want to derail his new life here with them.  Yet no matter what angle he came at it from, Hawking was unwavering.

One of the interesting things Joe discovered during his futile debate was that the rulers of the Feylands were not the same beings Arawn had first appointed.  Turns out the mantles could be and had been passed on.  Since being fey meant their personalities and memories were essentially immortal, Joe had assumed that each ruler was just an incarnation of their former selves.  Yet over time, each of the monarchs had been replaced in one fashion or another.

There once had been a Titania, like the fairy queen from Shakespeare.   Centuries ago, after suffering a tragic loss, the fey queen could no longer embrace Summer’s edict of bounty and growth.   She had abdicated, passing her crown to the current queen, Gloriana.

Maab the Mad, the first queen of Winter, had not been insane at first, but after tampering with magics from beyond the two worlds, she encountered something in the Void that shattered her mind.  Supposedly, she was also locked away.  In her case, it was in a tower made of iron.  Joe wondered why Maab had been replaced by Morrigu, while the Erlking, who was similarly unfit, was left with his crown.  According to Hawking, the answer was more politics.  The bound king could not contest the will of the queens, leaving the female pair in a position to seize rule of the Feylands.

The Oak Baron, or Oakbaron was a singular entity, just with a slight name change and the physical transformation from an ent into a talking tree.  Before him was the first king who was actually named Oberon.  That first king died in battle against the great dragon, Salmara’roog.  Turns out the greatest bane to the fey is not iron.  It’s dragons.  Having been utterly destroyed, unable to be reborn, it was his death that broke Titania’s spirit.

Then there was the first Lord of Spring, known as the Alder King.  His mastery was in shapechange rather than enchantment.  The Spring Lord so loved the form of songbirds that he spent more time in that shape than he did as a humanoid. At one point, he simply vanished from the Gossamer Lands, never to be seen again.  How the [Mantle of the Spring King] came into the Erlking’s possession was a mystery that to this day remained unanswered.  Given how cagey Hawking's replies were during this part of the discussion, Joe was almost certain the ethereal entity knew the real truth.

Joe finally pulled himself out of his head and realized that the three of them would already be up at the base camp if he stopped moving at Taylyn’s pace.  Time to play taxi service again.  He willed the pelt to form a kangaroo-like pouch on his upper back for Taylyn and had Yuk climb fully aboard.  He then took off like a squirrel, launching himself up and across the steep hillside, aiming for the path the team had used when they entered Glen Cumha.  Due to the precipitous angle of the slope, Joe ended up using hands and feet to half-climb, half-bound his way upward on all fours.

A few wights lurked in the woods, but they were weak cousins of those they had found within and beneath the ziggurat.  Joe let Yuk’s rats overwhelm most of the undead hunters. [Brawler] again proved to be fun in combat, allowing Joe to trip or overrun any of the ghasts that managed to stay in his way.

He could have easily slain these lesser wights, but Joe only needed about a thousand experience to level again, and he had just gotten a trait from Margen.  He had to hold off leveling for a few days before he could use [Anyone] again.  This meant he had to be careful about how much experience he gained while he waited for the cooldown to clear, or he’d miss his bonus trait for level seventeen.

In no time at all, Joe cleared the crest and jogged through the ward-stone barrier, approaching Myllo’s clockwork pavilion.  As he put Taylyn down, a flurry of frantic barks preceded Mojo by bare seconds.  Before he could even get his arms around the happy little pup, he was tackled by a second body.

“Oh, Murrcee may. I was sure we had lost you,” Kenda declared, her face pressed into the shaggy pelt.

Just the feel of their warm bodies against his was a greater balm than any spell. Joe had fought dozens of battles of mind and body.  He had been cold and clawed, overwrought and terrified, for ten hours straight. While Yuk had been his sanity-lifeline, the one thing his strange new friend lacked was the ability to offer physical contact.  Joe squeezed them both and held on for a few seconds before he felt centered enough to quip.  “Did you notice we found somebody you folks lost?”

“I KNOW!  Margen!  Nobody can believe it!” the guild’s skill-savant shouted, thrusting herself back with both arms, her hands still clasping his shoulders tightly. She looked him in the face, beaming with wonder and pride.

Her eye then slid to her limbs, which revealed that her embrace had transferred a significant portion of Joe’s chitinous teammate to her body.  She had Yuk everywhere: chest, neck, cheeks, hair. A brief look of panic flashed across her sweet features before realizing she was not covered in just any bugs but a specific skittering ally.

“Oh, yuck! Yuk! Come on! Get off!” Joe had become pretty much immune to the idea and sensation of his crawling compatriot. Poor Kenda was clearly not a fan of insects, regardless of how much she adored Yuk.

“Ssssooooooryyyyyy,” a chorus of voices from all over her body chattered. That didn’t help at all. Joe watched her face pale and her body wince as the small army of vermin slithered off her and back onto Joe. ‘I’ll go talk to Myllo and debrief while you two get reunited. Have fun,’ Yuk sent with an exaggerated leer in his mental tone.

‘Shoo fly,’ Joe jibed back as Yuk’s multitude scampered down his body. And then, for the first time in what felt like forever, Yuk was gone.

Your party has been dissolved. [Parasitic Bond] has ended.

A shiver ran down Joe’s back. He had become used to Yuk’s thoughts bumbling around with his in the hive-mind they had been sharing. He felt suddenly smaller without his odd new ally sharing their collective headspace. It was strange how quickly he had become comfortable and a bit reassured by his connection to the raggedy guilder.

That loss of closeness was swiftly replaced by a far more physical connection. Kendell pulled him close, kissing him soundly before returning to her bone-bending hug. Joe squeezed her back with one arm while smooshing Mojo’s neck. Without trying to, he felt his body shift slightly, melding to her. His collarbone moved enough to be out of the way while his ribs bowed, so her breastplate was no longer pushing two of them apart. Kenda sighed and held him tightly, relishing in the closeness before her sharp mind caught on to what Joe’s form was doing. She leaned back again, this time releasing him fully, watching as Joe’s chest reformed to its natural state.

“Wow. You have some serious instinctive control over that skill now. I can’t wait to share notes,” the skill savant in her exclaimed. “And you bulked up, too,” she added, giving his biceps a squeeze.   “I can’t wait to hear everything, but first you need to report in with the boss,” she sighed wistfully. “I’ve got to get this back to Puq,” she stated, easing the white ribbon off his forehead. She kissed him once more before taking his hand and leading Joe and Mojo to where the guilders had gathered.

“I told them you’d be fine,” breathed the white-skinned huntress as they came up to her. “The winds spoke of wildness and wonder, but not of woe.”

“And somehow, we were supposed to be heartened by that cryptic aeromancy,” droned Howling Voice’s defender, in his flat metallic manner. “Though, after the insanity you pulled against Tusked Tarz,” Tezeno continued,  “I was inclined to believe her. Glad to see she was right.” Tez grasped Joe’s forearm in a warrior’s clasp. Even though the sentinel never sounded emotional, the steely-skinned archon bore a wide smile.

“Thank the Wode you are! I would never have forgiven myself if you died to save me,” the elf lamented. Tears welled up around her wide, green eyes. When they spilled down her petite cheeks, the drops looked enormous, causing Joe’s sympathy reflex to jump into overdrive.

“Hey, hey, hey,” he crooned, scooping her into a hug, which was immediately assisted by a shaggy shug monkey.  “You saved my butt with the badboons more than once.”

“You … you know that’s different,” she stuttered. “What you did …”

“Is what any one of us would have done for each other,” he asserted earnestly, cinching his embrace around her a bit tighter. “We’re a team.”

“He’s not wrong, Earcellwen,” Tezeno professed. “I am by no means trivializing Joe’s outstanding bravery, but if I, or Hah’roo … or you were the one best suited to the task, you know we would have been the one to undertake it.”

The archon continuously surprised Joe with his odd mix of strict rigidity and general compassion.

“I can see you debating a Wyrd Oath,” the justicar continued. “I would bet my shield that such a vow would be the last thing our idealistic companion would want from you.”

“That sounds about right,” Joe agreed. Leaning sideways towards the archon, “What the heck’s a Wyrd Oath?” he uttered out of the side of his mouth.

“It is part of elven culture,” the small voice against his chest answered. “When someone performs an extreme act to save another, the one saved vows to become their savior's vassal until the debt is paid. I would be …”

“Are you kidding me? No frigging way are you swearing yourself into servitude to me, Wen!” Joe blurted as firmly as he could without snapping harshly at the teary-eyed archer. He stepped back. “You are one of my best friends in the world. There is no way I’d ever want … or allow you to become my vassal.” He met her eyes with his. He could see she was not convinced. Joe took a chance and added, “What does your dad say about friends?”

“True friends and circles are as one,” she uttered almost remotely.

“Meaning.”

“They are seamless without beginning or end; equal in all things be it station, rank, level … or debt,” the archer admitted.  Her tone had shifted, and Joe was no longer afraid she would make some insane pledge of indebtedness.

“Exactly,” he assured, grateful that his gamble had not stumbled into a dad-quote about obligations trumping friendship. “There is no debt between true friends, ok?”

“Agreed,” she breathed with a heavy sigh.

“And I have got to meet your Dad someday soon,” Joe tossed in, giving her shoulder one more squeeze.

“It is a long way to where my clan resides in the Kingswald, but I would love to introduce you.”

Joe slipped out of his teammates, leaving Mojo with Wen, and approached the captain of the Adventurers Guild, who had the founder at his side.  Margen looked even better.  Though his body was still mottled with leprous-looking patches of skin, his aura was a lilac shade.  The mummy rot still tinged every part of the man, but he glowed with a modest presence of health.  They had found him a shirt and new trousers, which Joe guessed Nard must have passed through a [Paired  Pouch].  Even Mortalius was not as barrel-chested as the ancient general, and the rustic, duck-canvas-brown outfit seemed more fitting for the guild’s gardener than the black-garbed cleric.

Myllo looked Joe over from head to toe.  At first, his face was filled with concern, yet once he determined Joe was whole and seemingly untraumatized, his bearded face lifted into a proud smile.  “Sure has been a day of surprises, wouldn’t ya say, Joe.  Surviving down there with just you and Yuk, finding Margen, and now an aspirant for the Spring Throne.  You planning on breaking the odds on anything else today?”

“Nope.  At this point, all I want is about fifty showers and a dozen burritos,” Joe sighed.

“Showers, we can get you,” the korrigan artificer proffered.  “And you ain’t kidding.  Be’in downwind of you is a real challenge.  As for the other, what’s a burrito?”

“I know!  You guys have so many comparable foods from Earth.  How the heck have you not figured out the best one yet?”

As the rest of the teams packed up the campsite, Joe gave an abbreviated summary of the day's events.  After which, Myllo sent Joe on ahead to the guildhouse.  As soon as he exited Vex’s portal. he headed straight for the locker room and stripped off everything he could.  Using the cleansing wands, he erased the filth from his hide, magic items, and gambeson.  Everything else he was happy to burn.

In the shower, as he was scrubbing away at himself, Joe uncovered something new on his right forearm.  It was another mark, sitting just beside the golden-moted Thirteenth Omen.  The new tattoo was a blue flower, but it never seemed to be the same flower each time Joe looked at it.  At first, it was just a simple forget-me-not.  Yet with the next glance, it had shifted subtly to look more like blue flax.  When it cycled through completely different flower shapes, such as iris,  cornflowers, and thistles, he knew it was not his imagination but the mark itself.

You have received the [Sign of Spring’s Aspirant].  Your pattern is bound to the Court of Spring.

While Joe was feeling a bit grumpy about this development, the [Mark of Death] seemed to be quite pleased with its new neighbor.  There was a smug sense of satisfaction emanating from the swirling prophetic tattoo.

Almost an hour later, a very pruny Joe emerged from the jetting water, finally free of the feel of layers of oily rot, pus, and chunks of things he really didn’t want to know.

As he scrubbed himself dry, his head ran through all the obligations he needed to attend to: the Spring Mark, waiting achievements, open attribute points, Margen’s health, Margen’s quest, a full debriefing, and more.  Yet after a quick bite, Joe tossed all those tasks on the back burner for some mental health time.  For the next few hours, it was just him and Mojo, on one of the training fields, playing ball.
