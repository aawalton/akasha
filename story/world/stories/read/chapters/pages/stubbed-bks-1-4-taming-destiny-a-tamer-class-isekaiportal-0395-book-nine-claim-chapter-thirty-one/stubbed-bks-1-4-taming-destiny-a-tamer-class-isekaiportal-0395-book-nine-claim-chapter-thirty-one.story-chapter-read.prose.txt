It takes a moment for his words and their implications to sink in. And even when they do, I don’t want to accept them. We survived a Tier four. We could surely survive a rift. But a Tier five?

The problem is, it fits too well. The domain that hit us that made the roc’s feel like a breeze, and Kalanthia’s like a kitten’s intimidation. The odd behaviour of the water. The sheer mountain of a creature that looms over us despite being at least a couple of hours’ sail away – more if we use what we can to slow ourselves down.

I can’t help but curse my luck. Tier fives are very rare in human-controlled lands – intentionally so since any Tier four who is discovered is swiftly made into a target by the most powerful Classers due to the threat they pose. And what are the chances of just wandering into the territory of one? They’re usually immobile, and with clear domain markers that our instruments should have picked up!

Unless, I think uneasily to myself, the instruments were damaged by the roc in some way…. Though wouldn’t the other ships’ instruments have worked nonetheless? Another problem, but one for later.

Despite their rarity – or perhaps due to their danger – there are some records of Tier fives in the histories I’ve perused in Nicholas’ library. One commonality to them is that they’re able to use their domains to directly influence the world in a way that doesn’t seem to use magic at all. The reason for the Water Mages’ arguments is clear: some of their Skills no doubt read the domain’s influence as magic while others didn’t. If only we’d considered the possibility sooner!

I breathe deeply, pushing my Intelligence stat to make my thoughts move faster. Time is of the essence.

“I need to speak to the other lords,” I decide. Now we know more about what we’re facing, we can make a plan to combat it – though I don’t think that literally fighting it is on the table. The roc was difficult enough; I don’t want to imagine how much worse a Tier five will be. And we’re already weakened after the earlier fight. But what can we do?

“We need to get the ship as ready to sail as possible. If the lack of the captain or his first mate is causing so much chaos, I’ll have to ask you to take over his organisational duties. I know you’re not an expert in sailing, but I trust your ability to command in a crisis. Can Loran take over your normal role?”

“He should be able to manage temporarily,” Sarran agrees with a sharp nod.

“I’ll get him to bring the mirrors and then send him to you for instruction," I decide. Then, just before I drop the Seal, I look at Sarran firmly. “It’s clear we should have paid better attention to the warnings we were given, but we didn’t. I don’t know if there’s any move we can make that keeps us alive, but, if we do nothing, it’s certain we’ll die. Are you with me?”

“I am, my lord,” Sarran confirms after breathing in deeply. The fear is still deep in the lines of his face, but the light of determination in his eyes has rekindled.

I pat him on the shoulder and then stride towards the quarterdeck, signalling for Loran to come and meet me there. Though there is no captain at the repaired wheel, it’s still the best place for me to be out of the way but still be able to see everything going on.

On my way, I’m blocked by Kalanthia standing in my path, her bulk creating another obstacle for the sailors to move past.

Markus, that’s a Sovereign! Her voice is filled with more fear than I’ve ever heard before. I feel a ripple of fear going through the others in the Bond, though not much surprise.

That’s the next tier above Immortal, right? I check with her – for whatever reason, beasts have different names for the tiers than humans do.

Yes, Kalanthia agrees, shivering slightly, her ears back. Even her domain feels weaker than usual, like it’s hiding rather than out and proud – the apex predator has instinctively recognised something that considers it prey.

Oddly enough, it’s actually reassuring. I’ve already faced the possibility of it being a Tier five – what I didn’t want to find out now is that it’s a Tier six. Though, to be fair, it might not make much difference – dead is dead, whether crushed under a brick wall or under a building. But the show isn’t over until the fat lady sings.

Can you jump in the tattoo? The sailors need the space, and I need to get to the quarterdeck.

Kalanthia hesitates, but then presses close and vanishes. I keep on moving towards the quarterdeck while continuing our conversation.

What are the chances we can get out of this situation? I ask her privately, not sharing the conversation even with the others.

We can’t fight it, Markus. We can only run, Kalanthia warns, that same fear growing even stronger.

I wasn’t planning on fighting it, I assure her. So you think we can run?

If it is not paying attention to us.

I frown.

But why wouldn’t it be paying attention to us?

Even as I speak, my mind lingers on the way its domain pulsed out – but didn’t remain constant. The roc maintained its domain throughout the battle – a Tier five can surely maintain it even longer. So why pulse it and then drop it? If it had kept the aura going at the same strength as the first pulse, it would have pinned us in place helplessly until we were devoured.

Then I realise something that makes my eyes go wide.

It has no eyes – it can’t see us! It pulsed its domain to work out where we are!

Not us, Kalanthia replies with certainty in her mental voice.

My frown returns even as I nod absently at Loran and we start setting up the mirrors.

Then who…?

We are too weak to offer a Sovereign much interest. The carcass of an Immortal, however….

It finally clicks.

I curse myself and the other lords and everyone who influenced our decision to stay and harvest the carcass – it might have seemed safe enough at the time, but the Tier four has clearly been acting as a beacon for something we really should have stayed far away from.

But there’s no point wasting time in recriminating past decisions right now. And if the Tier five is focused on the roc’s carcass rather than on eating us, we might be able to slip away without the Tier five noticing – or, at least, without it caring.

Do you have any suggestions for how we can run away? I ask Kalanthia to finish off our conversation even as the mirrors begin to activate and show the pale and sweating faces of my allies.

On water, no, she replies, helpless frustration going through her. I can empathise. But what lies beyond the blackness is inescapable.

Thanks, Kalanthia, I tell her, even though I could have concluded the last for myself. I step forward as the final mirror flickers on, Captain Sor’az’ furious face filling it.

“Me soothsayer told ye we had te get out! An’ this is why!”

“Yes, yes, we know,” Elarion replies sharply, his usual mask and composure in tatters as fear fills his face. “Reminding us is hardly helpful.”

“A Tier five,” Valence whimpers, his face like milk.

Josiah, too, is looking sick, his tanned skin more the colour of old parchment.

“I’m sorry,” he says in barely more than a whisper. “I…I didn’t know.

“A Tier five,” Valence repeats, fear seeming to have made him unable to think.

“Is that the only thing you can say?” snaps Elarion, then he looks at me. “Markus, what do we do?”

He’s not the only one to look to me for answers – every one present turns to stare at me, whether with frightened, demanding, or angry eyes.

I take a deep breath, glancing at the looming mountain that has crept noticeably closer even in the time since it first appeared. Elarion has hit the nail on the head and I still don’t have much idea of what to do beyond the obvious. I need more information.

“Captain Sor’az. What have you found?”

The captain’s furious expression softens slightly. He’s still understandably not happy, but he appears willing to cooperate, at least.

“T’ current is weaker away from t’ bird. No’ weak, mind, but tha’s clearly t’ target.”

I nod – it’s good to have confirmation of the theory.

“If we all set sail now, do you think we can reach the edge of the current before we are pulled into the mouth?” I ask him urgently.

He takes a moment to consider the question and I have to force my fingers not to fidget in my impatience, my eyes straying to the calm current that whips us onwards against our will.

“Nay,” he replies finally. “No’ all o’ ye. Us, yes. Ye fastest an’ least damaged ships, mayhap, but…no’ ye ship, Markus,” he replies with a regard that’s not entirely unsympathetic.

I clench my jaw. Elarion’s ship might survive, then. Josiah’s probably. Layton’s and Kyrian’s former vessel too. But Valence’s and mine definitely not, and the others aren’t definites either – they all took a beating in the fight with the roc, a beating that we still carry the scars from.

We can afford to lose one ship, perhaps – Kyrian’s has added an extra one to the fleet. But half the fleet? And all the resources on them besides? We might be able to evacuate the people if everyone works together, but we can’t evacuate them and the resources. But losing those could be a death sentence for so many of us – either during the journey or after we reach the Lost Continent.

There has to be another way.

“It wants the roc carcass – why don’t we just give it to the beast?” Layton asks. He’s pale and there’s sweat on his forehead, but otherwise he’s composed; clearly his brain is still working.

“Even if the roc carcass is its priority, do you think it will pass up the opportunity to snack on the rest of us?” Elarion demands irritably. “I don’t think we can bank on the possibility of it closing its mouth just before the rest of us go straight in. And that hole is big enough to take the whole fleet in at once!”

I can think of some ideas that would probably make it want to, but I’m wary about actually drawing the Tier five’s active attention. Our only hope is to go unnoticed. But Elarion’s derisive words have sparked an idea.

“We can’t assume it will close its mouth, no,” I agree. “But what if we can block the opening?”

That earns me more than a few stares, most questioning; some hopeful.

“What do we have that could possibly block something that massive?” Layton questions, his eyebrows high on his forehead. “Even the roc carcass will slip inside without touching the edges!”

“Do you have something in your Inventory we don’t know about?” Elarion asks suspiciously.

“No. I’m thinking about the ice you’ve made in the last few battles. Can you create something big enough to block it?”

That earns me an incredulous stare.

“Big enough– Markus, you’ve seen the size of that thing, haven’t you?”

“Elarion, we don’t have time for this. Is that a yes or a no?” I ask tensely.

Elarion immediately shakes his head.

“Without any sort of anchor point, help, or far more time than we have, no. No, I can’t do it.”

Time I can’t help with, but as I consider the options, I consider that maybe the idea isn’t dead in the water.

“And what if you could have both an anchor point and help?” I ask.

Elarion goes still.

“What kind of anchor point? And what kind of help?” he questions, the suspicious note returning to his voice. That he hasn’t shot down the idea immediately makes hope flicker in my breast.

This is going to be tricky, but maybe it’s our best chance.
