Gary Steel and Feng Shen stared at each other, spears drawn. They were measuring. Waiting. Neither moved, but in their minds, the battle had already started.

“Fight!” the crowd cheered. Their voices rose. Banners flew.

“Baldinator!”

“Feng Shen!”

Chanting and clamor rang freely. The tension made people shiver from excitement. Some laughed. Others bit their nails.

The sand below Feng Shen pulsed. He was a human drum—beat by beat, the sand jumped higher, grains falling over each other. A yin yang diagram slowly appeared beneath him, formed by the sand.

Black aura rose from Gary, flying an inch over his body. The sand also pulsed beneath his feet, but every grain that rose was burned, never to rise again. All that appeared below him was a black circle.

Despite the phenomena caused by both fighters galvanizing their aura, neither moved.

Until they did.

In an instant, Gary’s and Feng Shen’s spears met. An explosion filled the arena. Sand flew everywhere, covering a wide area where a hundred sparks appeared as they clashed repeatedly. The crowd roared. Bursts of aura crashed into the energy walls.

“Gary Steel! Gary Steel!”

“Feng Shen! Feng Shen!”

Gary was fighting fresh. He had achieved new heights and stats since the battle against Sui Duang, and his mind was rested, letting him utilize everything he had. He flowed from move to move, gradually accelerating.

Feng Shen was a very different opponent from Sui Duang and all the previous people Gary had faced. Sui Duang had been aggressive, overbearing, flooding Gary with his water techniques. Oracle Jay had used his extreme aura sensitivity to infer Gary’s next moves. The Lord of Pot had used a flying pot. Most other spear-wielders had also used their vibrations and special powers to try and gain an edge.

But Feng Shen was like Gary. He possessed rock-solid fundamentals, which served as the basis of his fighting style. His vibrations were only secondary.

Gary had practiced the spear moves tens of thousands of times each. He’d trained early in the morning and deep into the night until his palms bled. He could thrust unconscious if he had to. That fanatical devotion to the basics had given him an edge in every fight, even when his technique was otherwise equivalent to his opponent’s.

Facing Feng Shen, however, Gary sensed a kindred spirit. This man had trained as much as Gary. He could see it every shadow of his strikes, every maneuver, every clash. His thrusts were identical to each other, like a well-oiled machine—like Gary’s. His transitions were smooth. His mastery, evident.

There were no tricks in their battle, no odd techniques, no pots or clairvoyance or streams of water. It was purely spear. Gary felt himself relaxing as he sank deeper into the battle trance, all his training finally coming to the fore. It felt right. Everything clicked. He accelerated, his body falling into familiar patterns, the moves flowing effortlessly out of him. The spear was an extension of his body. He was soaring.

He caught himself grinning.

Gary had always been considered a hard worker in the clan, and he’d heard the same words levied at Feng Shen. It was true.

“What a splendid display of spearmanship!” Rollo Frollo exclaimed. “Can you see them, everyone? I barely can! They’re still accelerating! Out of everyone who could have made it to the final, this is the best possible match-up. What a celebration of the spear! What a display of mastery! What honor to the clan!”

Gary wasn’t listening. He couldn’t. Every fiber of his being was invested into the spear, and his soul was soaring, and his heart found purpose.

This is it, he thought. This is why I live.

In that moment, he experienced ecstasy. The world was golden. Strike after strike, hundreds after hundreds of exchanges, he was growing. His potential was drawn out in real time. He could move better than ever. All his training made sense. Everything he’d practiced but not quite understood suddenly clicked. He was having a blast, and it didn’t matter that he was pushed back.

Gary’s recent stat increases put him far ahead of most Birth of Aura cultivators, but Feng Shen remained superior. He was stronger and faster than Gary. Gary had counted on endurance, but he found himself panting before his opponent did. His spear grew heavier. The gap between them grew.

“Gary Steel is pushed back! Watch, everyone, he’s about to reach the wall!” Rollo Frollo exclaimed.

Gary frowned. As fun as this was, to keep it going longer than necessary would be cowardice. Everything had their time. “Black Thrust!” he yelled. Black lightning blossomed on his spear, met immediately by the black and white move he knew to expect. Feng Shen’s aura slash, superimposed upon the thrust of his spear, was heavier than Gary had imagined. The thrust itself felt like a mountain being shoved in his face. It met his move equally. The slash carried on, almost slicing him in half before he thunderstepped out of the way.

“And he’s out!” Rollo Frollo yelled.

Feng Shen’s aura erupted like black and white ribbons around his body. He hadn’t been like this against Lin Xin. The previous exchanges must have heated him up. His eyes were fierce.

Gary’s aura also erupted, a torrent of clouds and arcing black lightning, but he could barely register when Feng Shen fell upon him. His spear was a blur. Black and white slashes were mixed in his strikes at random, and he moved at a speed Gary could barely comprehend.

Gary defended on instinct. Black lightning enhanced his speed and reflexes, and it tried its hardest to block Feng Shen’s attacks, but even like this, the only way he could survive was by constantly thunderstepping away.

“Feng Shen is chasing Gary across the arena! The gap is becoming clear!” came the commentary.

Shut up, Rollo Frollo, Gary thought, receiving a deep thrust in the arm for his distraction. Feng Shen was fierce. His pressure was unreal. It felt like being preyed upon by some wild beast, where all Gary could do was delay the inevitable.

But he could see it now. He could see the slashes. He could see the aura, see the yin yang splitting the air just before the attack came.

It pushed him to the limit, and defending was barely possible, but he could defend.

Can I win? he dared to hope.

Trapped in the afterimages of spear strikes, Gary forced his eyes to stay wide open. He roared, accelerating further, his own arms becoming blurs. It was pure instinct now. He had no time for anything else. Black and white split and merged in the blink of an eye, producing powerful slashes at almost point-blank, but Gary’s speartip shattered them. Their spears intertwined. Black arcs zapped at the yin yang, disturbing its formation. For a moment, Gary could hold his ground.

“It’s balanced again! Gary Steel grew faster! He’s adapting in real time!” Rollo Frollo yelled as the audience thundered. The sound of their voices mixed around Gary, becoming a liquid he drowned in but simultaneously enhanced him. He lived in the battle. This was his spotlight.

He roared harder. His spear battled forth. Feng Shen was no kindred spirit anymore, no peer, but a target he needed to ruin. He’d slaughtered Gary’s planet—so Gary would triumph. The lightning grew thicker. Its blackness grew deeper. This state of complete focus wasn’t something he could maintain for long, but just for a moment, he found a breather.

He pulled back his spear, and the clouds he’d been conjuring moved. A terrifying black thrust shot out alongside his spear, shattering the black-and-white slashes like brittle glass. Feng Shen dove out of the way, letting the Golden Bolt sail past and nail itself in the far wall all the way to the hilt.

“Fourth Song!” Gary yelled as he sprinted after his spear, taking advantage of Feng Shen’s ducking to bypass him. Strikes rained after him. He took one to the leg, but thankfully, Thunder Step was just fast enough to reach the spear and rip it out of the wall in time to block.

I need to learn how not to throw my spear! he thought, cursing inwardly. He was cornered again. Feng Shen’s strikes rained down, and the brief lull had let him get into a rhythm, accelerating massively while Gary grew slower. He was overwhelmed by strikes. He had to let some pass and graze him. One mistake could cost him a limb—or worse.

As he met Feng Shen’s eyes, however, he only saw battle. The number one disciple wasn’t playing. There was no mercy there.

“Flood of Black and White!” he roared, his strikes tearing the air asunder, creating a wide, black-and-white field which completely submerged Gary. Every strike was cataclysmic, and they came from all directions. Gary was suddenly glad he was cornered.

“Fan the Spear!” he yelled, spinning his spear like crazy, but it could only mildly affect the incoming attacks. They sliced at him like hail, grazing his arms and legs, tearing his robes to pieces. Deep red lines appeared on his skin—if not for his high Vitality, he might have been chopped to blocks. Moreover, every injury contained the energies of yin yang, pulling the flesh apart so it couldn’t heal.

Just as the strikes were running out, Feng Shen span his spear, redoubling the attack. “Enter the Sea of Sin!” he yelled.

Gary could no longer protect himself. Fan the Spear wouldn’t cut it. Roaring over the pain, he thunderstepped out, accepting the heavy injuries in return for his life. The wall where he’d been standing was sliced to pieces, raining down as tiny cubes. Gary stood to the side, panting heavily, blood running down his exposed torso from various wounds. One of his eyes was shut, blood flowing from a tear just above it. A chunk of his hair was missing.

“He took the Baldinator’s hair! What an insult—and what a last-minute save! If they aren’t careful, they really might kill each other!” Rollo Frollo yelled.

Gary couldn’t care in the slightest. These were very light injuries compared to what could have happened if he didn’t locate the one direction in which the attacks were less concentrated. He had the suspicion that Feng Shen had left that gap on purpose.

Even after this, Feng Shen was still ramping up. He pulled his spear back. The world split into black and white and converged on the tip of his spear, then again, and again. A black-and-white sphere of light hovered right there. He thrust it at Gary. The world went still. Only that ball moved.

“Final Convergence!” Feng Shen yelled.

“This is the move that defeated Lin Xin!” Rollo Frollo yelled. The audience held their breaths.

Facing the ball which seemed to move slow but was actually near-instant, Gary held his ground. Feng Shen may have been superior to him in every aspect, but the one field that he especially excelled at was speed. If Gary dodged, he’d make this about speed, and he would lose. His own strong suit was destructiveness. A single, ultimate move.

Gary pulled his spear back. Clouds gathered in front of it, half merging into the spear, half shooting ahead. He lifted the vacuum. The spear exploded forth, much stronger than the previous, hasty strike. It shot straight for the ball.

“Fourth Song: Lightning!” Gary yelled.

The two strikes met. The world collapsed. A sucking power tugged at Gary’s robes, and he hurried to make some distance as a massive explosion filled the arena, shaking the stands, making the energy walls vibrate. Exclamations of wonder rose from the crowd.

“Incredible!” someone shouted.

“How are these Birth of Aura cultivators?” another lamented.

At the point of impact, black and white slashes unraveled against a dominating black strike. The Golden Bolt refused to stop. Even as the last bits of lightning fizzled out, Goldie roared with power, and the spear smashed through the ball’s last vestiges. Feng Shen was no longer there—the spear rammed into a wall again, spreading a web of cracks for dozens of feet around it.

Gary looked up. Feng Shen hovered high in the air, almost as high as the energy wall. No, he wasn’t hovering—he was gliding, a yin-yang circle under his feet somehow slowing his fall. Gary had no time to ponder. He rushed to retrieve his spear.

I beat his strike! I can do this! There is a path to victory! he thought.

“You truly are strong, Gary Steel! This is my greatest technique. If you can take it, I’ll forfeit the battle,” Feng Shen declared from the sky.

Gary grabbed his spear and turned to face him. His eyes went wide. Black and white energies covered the air over the arena, slipping around each other like rivers, yet never touching. The energies spread and arranged themselves into a circle almost nine feet wide. As more and more aura converged, the circle turned clearer, revealing it had precisely the pattern of yin-yang. The black and white energies carried tremendous animosity towards each other, similar to Gary’s positive and negative charges, yet some iron-clad power kept them still at only inches away from each other. There was black in the white and white in the black. The power on display made Gary’s hair rise.

“You asked me about Kyle Mercy!” Feng Shen exclaimed, floating over his move. “This is the technique I devised after losing to him! I would challenge him when he returned, but it was not fated. Since you made it this far and you use the same technique as him, you can be the first person to ever face it!”

On the sands below, Gary was barely listening. He was rapidly blowing through techniques.

“First Song: Mud!”

“Second Song: Life!”

“Third Song: Rain!”

All three of his techniques flew at the circle and disappeared before even touching it. They’d been obliterated by the sheer potency of Feng Shen’s aura, but it was fine. Gary hadn’t put much into them. They were just the prequels to his real attack, placing his aura in the perfect condition to launch the fourth song. Already, clouds were forming, as many as Gary could maintain. They struggled against his will. He pushed even harder, knowing that without surpassing his limits, resisting this attack was impossible.

Feng Shen raised his spear, then slashed it overhead.

“Yin Yang Collapse!” he bellowed. The circle flew for Gary. Sensing its approach, how the ambient aura bowed in its wake, Gary felt weak in the knees. He considered abandoning his attack and running, but the impact of the circle against the sand would release such powerful aura that its energies would flood and maim him no matter where he was in the arena.

He conjured more clouds. He had one shot. One attack to rely on. As the circle fell on him, he bellowed and thrust.

“Nine Songs of the Spear, Fourth Song: Lightning!”

The Golden Bolt flew out so fast it ripped the skin of his palms. The clouds were pulverized. The greatest strike Gary had ever used, the absolute peak of his power, flew into the sky. It clashed with the circle. A terrible impact rang across the arena. The walls groaned. The sand was pressed flat. The energy wall shuddered. People screamed.

Gary’s ears rang and his hair flew wild with his robes. He watched as his spear dove into the yin-yang diagram, releasing a torrential amount of all-destructive black lightning. The black and white parts of the circle interacted, collapsing into a singular blast. The lightning was annihilated. The spear was flung away. Half the circle’s power was used up, but the other half continued towards Gary at a speed far superior to before. He had no time to even blink. It was upon him.

A terrifying explosion razed the sand, shattered the walls, made the energy wall vibrate wildly. The Empyrean Steps shook to their foundation. People gaped and stared wide-eyed. Even those at the VIP seats had glinting eyes.

The explosion ended, and there was absolute silence. A chunk of stone tore free from the wall, agonizingly slowly, and tumbled hollow to the sand.

As the sand settled, Gary Steel was revealed, lying flat on the ground. Only then did his spear land, somewhere far away. Gary had eyes wide, panting heavily. The patch of sand below him was untouched. Feng Shen finally glided to the ground, sheathing his spear.

In the silence, Gary spoke. “Thank you…for your mercy…”

Feng Shen smiled and bowed. “You fought well, Gary Steel. You bring honor to your name. I look forward to facing you again.”

The crowd erupted in clapping and cheering. “Feng Shen! Feng Shen! Feng Shen!” everyone chanted, even Gary’s supporters, and the sound of their excited voices rained over the two finalists.

Gary stood up. He didn’t know how he felt. Towards the end, he’d really started to believe he could win, but he could only shake his head and accept his defeat. Feng Shen was truly strong. He’d tried his best.

Gary walked up to Feng Shen.

“Where I come from, this is how we show respect for each other,” he said, extending a hand. A moment of surprise later, Feng Shen shook it. “Congratulations, Feng Shen. I have no regrets from this battle—I tried my hardest, and you were better. You’re an amazing cultivator. You absolutely deserve this. I will return to challenge you when I’m ready, but until then, I hope you reach for the heavens. Show the world that I, Gary Steel, did not lose to a random genius.”

Feng Shen smiled wide. “I swear.”

“What a display of sportsmanship from Gary Steel! What a graceful loser! Of all the years I’ve been presenting the Heaven Reaching tournament, this is the greatest final duel I’ve witnessed. Everyone, give it up for Feng Shen and Gary Steel, the strongest disciples of their generation, and the rising stars of the Heaven Reaching clan! Give it up for all the disciples who fought and bled on this sand, for their sweat and tears, their hopes and aspirations! Long live the heavens until we pierce them! Long live the clan!” Rollo Frollo yelled.

Clapping cascaded on the sands. Everyone would have red palms for the rest of the day, but who gave a shit? After such a battle, who wouldn’t clap!

“You’re amazing!”

“I admire you both!”

“Please, at least one of you marry my daughter!”

“Ew, Dad, you’re gross.”

The other disciples had stood and were clapping, also receiving the respect of the audience.

“Do you think we’ll ever receive applause like that?” Cog asked.

“Honestly? No…but we’ll try our best!” Alicia replied.

“That’s the spirit! And, look how hard they’re clapping; these guys must love us!”

“With the final over, it is my honor to announce the conclusion of this year’s Heaven Reaching junior disciple tournament,” Rollo Frollo said. “A short award ceremony will follow, and after that, a round of complimentary cocktails will be offered at the arena exits.”

The audience cheered even harder.

“Nevermind,” Cog said, laughing. “They don’t love us, they just love themselves. Who gives a shit about applause? I will cultivate for my dream, to free my cousins, and to reach the heavens! Long live the clan! Long live, uh, me!”
