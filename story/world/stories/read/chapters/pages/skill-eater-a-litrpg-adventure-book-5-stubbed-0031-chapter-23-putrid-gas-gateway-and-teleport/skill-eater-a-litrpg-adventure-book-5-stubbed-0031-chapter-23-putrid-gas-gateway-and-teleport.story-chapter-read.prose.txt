Edge was pleased that his Companion had chosen caution instead of charging straight in. Forsaking the element of surprise would make it difficult to exterminate the rodents without letting some escape, and it would be foolish to engage opponents above his stage in a straight-up brawl regardless.

Rue hid beneath a pile of rubble while concocting a plan of engagement. He sniffed at the air to acquire more intel, sampling the scentscape until he was confident there were twenty rats in living in the rooms lining both hallways. He thought he could defeat them by eliminating one chamber at a time, but he was worried the clamor of conflict would alert the other beasts. When they sensed his presence, the remaining rats would either make their escape or try to rescue their brethren and catch the fox in a pincer attack.

Fortunately, Edge had a solution sitting in his vault, or half of one at any rate. He’d decided to purchase one of the privacy devices Sakura and Trapper were using. They were vital for investigating the Ancient without giving themselves away and were certain to come in handy in other situations too. He placed the device along one side of the intersection and switched it on.

A bubble of blackness expanded to fill the hallway. They couldn’t see through it unless they were inside, but the dome would block light and sound from passing through. The rats would notice it eventually and realize there was a problem, but it would let the fox begin the hunt without revealing his presence to the rodents laired in that direction.

Satisfied that he had prevented a worst-case scenario, Rue walked to the first doorway and stopped before the threshold. As requested, Edge examined the contents using Gaze’s combined mode. In addition to copious mounds of detritus, the chamber was home to four yellow rats.

Judging from the size, they were all stage one. Three were asleep and one was nibbling on a piece of bread Rue had set out as bait, which must not have been a desirable prize since the critter had the whole slice to itself. When he used his Companion bond to convey his findings, the beast asked Edge to watch his back and went in for the kill.

Rue ignited his core and Blinked. The grain-gnawing rat sensed the skill’s mana and raised its gaze, but the fox was already gone. Before the beast could sound the alarm, a pair of furry jaws closed around its neck, ending its life before it had time to squeak. After that, it was a simple matter of sneaking up to the remaining rodents and culling them in their sleep.

“Not bad,” Edge whispered. “Four stage-ones down, and you only used a single skill and didn’t announce our arrival. Keep up the good work.”

Rue’s tails wagged as they moved on to the second chamber. Once again, Edge used his magical echolocation to peer around the corner without stepping past the threshold. All three stage-one rats inside were awake, but Rue wasn’t worried. He Blinked three times in a row, slaying two rodents in a flash. He had to use Warp Claws to finish the final critter off, taking a glancing blow from an electric attack in exchange.

After making sure Rue hadn’t been harmed and the colony hadn’t sensed the mana from his skills, they approached the final chamber at the end of the hallway.

Thus far, the fox’s ambushes had gone off without a hitch. His third attempt wound up being… somewhat less than perfect. The beasts were gas rats this time around, two stage-ones and a stage-two that was about half Rue’s size. After stepping into the privacy bubble to make sure nothing was coming down the other passage, the golden vulpine was ready to tackle his first stage-two target.

The rodents were sleeping, so the fox crept into the debris-covered chamber, judging that he was less likely to wake them if he didn’t ignite his core. The first stage-one died without knowing what hit it, but when Rue’s jaws closed around the second, it opened its eyes and let out a terrified squeak. He ended its existence by breaking its neck as the stage-two rodent sprang to its feet.

Both beasts activated their skills at the same time. Rue Blinked behind the gas rat with his Warp Claws poised to strike. The varmint didn’t see him coming and would have died without completing its spell if the magic required a target. However, the skill in question didn’t rely on precision. As the fox’s rift-clad claws severed the creature’s spine, its mana assumed material form.

Half a heartbeat later, they learned why the guard had referred to the rodents as fart rats. The blast whipped Edge’s hair around his face as gas flooded the chamber. While the substance wasn’t poisonous, it smelled like they were standing in front of a kaiju’s sphincter—ground zero as the behemoth passed wind in preparation for a major bowel movement.

In short, the stench was fucking awful. Even holding his breath, it stank to high heaven. They ran like the wind, emerging from the noxious cloud while heading for the stairs. The expression on Rue’s face was hysterical. Edge kept trying to laugh, which resulted in breathing rank air and pinching his nostrils while the fox snickered back. He won the exchange by pulling his gas-mask out of his vault, sighing in relief when the magitech device neutralized the abominable odor.

Safe from the fallout of further errors of judgment, they waited until the air was clear and Rue was ready for round two.

They were lucky. The gas was a powerful deterrent, but it wasn’t directly dangerous. “If something like that happens again, teleport into you den instead of running away. That will limit your exposure, and I can use Rejuvenation to purge any poison I breathe before my mask is in place.”

It was a valuable lesson, and Rue had learned from his mistake. Thus far, the hunt was shaping up well. The fox put down the rat before he retreated, and the privacy device prevented the other beasts from noticing the fight. Refilling its aether would cut into his profits, but Edge had plenty of Credits and this assignment was proving to be an excellent experience for the young beast.

On that note, Rue had refined his tactics while waiting for the air to clear. For the second half of the hunt, the fox asked Edge move the privacy device in front of each doorway in turn. It wouldn’t block sound conducting through the walls, but it would lower the odds that the creatures laired in the other rooms sensed any scuffles that ensued.

He was proud that his partner had come up with the idea on his own. He repositioned the privacy bubble as requested and scouted the chambers ahead. The first room was empty. The second was home to a pair of stage-one volt rats the fox eliminated without any problems. After switching off the device, they were ready to tackle the final chamber.

This enclosure contained the fox’s toughest challenge yet—two beefy volt rats that were well into two-stage and a half-dozen stage-one gassers. None of the beasts were asleep, and they seemed to have realized something was wrong, which meant this situation was considerably trickier than anything the vulpine had tackled thus far. His Companion asked Edge to stun the little rodents, so they didn’t have to worry about another smelly surprise while he dealt with their lightning-flinging cousins.

Edge didn’t mind pitching in under the circumstances, although had some reservations about letting Rue fight both stage-twos at once. Since the fox could retreat to his den if he got in over his head, Edge was willing to let the beast try his luck. Before they made their move, he pulled back long enough to activate Warlord’s Mantle. Rue would have the aura buffing him most of the time anyway, and it would shield him from a nasty shock if the rats managed to land a hit.

“Ready?” He whispered, and Rue nodded in reply.

With that, Edge stepped past the threshold, igniting his core while issuing an Intimidating Roar. The aural attack paralyzed the little rats, freezing in place as primal terror invaded their minds. He excluded the volt rats from the skill’s effect, hanging back while the late stage-one fox took on two mid stage-two rodents at once.

Their fur crackled as they got ready to fire, casting shifting shadows across the floor. Rue watched closely as he circled, waiting for an opening to appear. He expected both rodents to let loose at once, but that wasn’t how events played out. The rats used the chamber’s corner to protect their backs, limiting Rue’s options as the first critter unleashed a sizzling arc of electricity.

The bolt spread as it surged forth, extending to form netlike tendrils that made it difficult to dodge. Rue was surprised, but he Blinked out of the way… which put him straight in the path of the spell the second rodent cast the instant he reappeared. Oh shit. These rats have way more combat experience than the others.

Edge thought his Companion was in trouble, but he was in for a surprise of his own.

Blink was an amazing skill, but it suffered from two key limitations. The first was it required Rue to have a direct line of sight of where he wanted to appear, which meant he couldn’t teleport through walls, but a window wouldn’t get in the way.

The second was the casting time. It took two seconds to complete the spell, which wasn’t that long in the grand scheme of things. However, in the heat of battle, even a single second could split the difference between life and death, particularly when trying to evade an incoming attack. As Rue evolved and gained more skills, he would acquire options for dealing with various problems, but with only two rank-one powers at his command, there wasn’t much he could do. Or so Edge thought.

His jaw dropped when the two-tailed fox teleported twice in the blink of an eye, reappearing beside the rodent or the right before it could fire again. He swept a Warp-bolstered paw through the rat’s midriff, quadsecting its spine and killing the creature instantly.

Before Edge could process the astonishing turn of events, the battle arrived at its climax.

In a desperate bid to live through the day, the final volt rat unleashed a skill combo. The first power flooded the room with electricity. It wasn’t strong enough to do any damage, although it made his body tingle. He wasn’t sure what was happening at first, but his skill senses provided the details.

That spell is some kind of wide-area targeting magic. It just locked onto everything within its range, ensuring the rodent’s next attack lands on target. Sure enough, the volt rat sent a tremendous bolt of lightning Rue’s way. The fox double Blinked, but the skill adjusted its course, following his path like a heat-seeking missile.

Edge wanted to rush in and protect his friend, since it seemed inevitable that Rue was going to get hurt. He forced himself not to interfere. The fox had chosen the direct approach—which was always risky when fighting an opponent above your stage, let alone two—and he needed to understand that his decisions had consequences.

The bolt wouldn’t kill him with Mantle running, and he had to experience the suffering that accompanied life-and-death combat, or he wouldn’t be prepared for the pain while battling stronger prey. Besides, the device embedded in his collar will activate if it senses a fatal blow.

There wasn’t time to Blink again, and dodging the old-fashioned way wouldn’t work either. As matters stood, the fox was fated to lose with the limited tools at his disposal.

Thus, both he and the electricity-flinging rodent were flabbergasted when Rue stopped running. He stood his ground and activated a skill Edge hadn’t seen before. A shimmering rift wavered into existence a bare instant before the bolt landed. The sparking surge entered the field and vanished without a trace, only to emerge from the second portal that had materialized above the rat’s head.

Rue let out an enthusiastic yip as the beast was struck by its own attack, flesh frying as it lost control over its limbs. Apparently, while the rat was resistant to lightning, it wasn’t immune, and eating its strongest spell was sufficient to stun it. Before the critter had time to recover, Rue Blinked beside it and activated Warp Claws. One decisive swipe sheared the rat’s head from its shoulders, ending the fight in a flash.

Since the pelt was ruined anyway, the fox helped himself to the remains, enjoying the crunchy sensation of gnawing fried meat as Edge walked over to congratulate his Companion on the accomplishment. The stage-one rats were out cold from his Roar, so he could deal with them in a minute.

Instead, he took a moment to process the unexpected development. The double Blink was clearly the result of a rank-two upgrade. That move looked premeditated rather than spontaneous, which means Rue had already ranked up his teleport and decided not to tell me. Sneaky little bugger wanted to save the big reveal for a dramatic moment.

It was a smart play to pick an upgrade that mitigated Blink’s biggest drawbacks. Visual obstructions won’t limit his options nearly as much going forward, and he can reposition unpredictably to catch his enemies off-guard. He effectively doubled the range of his teleport too, which makes this a solid improvement across the board. I think he learned that new skill in the heat of the moment, driven by the pressure of the emerging situation

When Edge pulled up his profile and looked at Summon Companion, he learned that Rue’s newest power was a Rare called Gateway. He didn’t receive a vision since it wasn’t his skill, and the full description wasn’t provided, which meant they needed experiment to discover the details.

“How about it, boy. Up for a few tests before we call it a day?”

The fox was game, so they took Gateway through its paces. At rank one, the first portal had to be within six inches of Rue’s body and the second had a range of ten feet. Its maximum size was a circle with a two-foot diameter, which would let Gateway intercept a wide range of projectiles.

He could only keep the portals open for a few seconds, and the mana drain was intense. Spells and physical objects could pass through, but when Edge tried tossing one of the unconscious rats in, it passed through the field without activating the portal. Finally, there was a limit to how much mana the rift could handle. It dispersed after three Vacuum Blasts, or two if he flared the skill, and could divert a water saw for about two seconds before being overloaded and destabilizing.

All in all, Gateway was incredible addition to Rue’s skillset. It offered incredible defense against a wide range of attacks, and as the fox had demonstrated, could be used to turn his opponent’s powers against them. We could also use it to land my skills from unexpected angles. Once he masters its dynamic, Gateway will be an amazing asset in battle and a nightmare for his foes.

Edge was sure the crew would have some suggestions on how to make the most of Rue’s powers, and he was excited to see what they would come up after putting their heads together. He needed to get a move on if he wanted to meet up with Mel in time for tonight’s brawl, so they put the rest of the rats down, gathered the bodies, and made their way to the beast hunters’ society.

Along the way, he closed the cellar door and used Shadow Play to erase his tracks, keeping this discovery their little secret until he learned where the blocked passage led. I can’t wait to see what’s down there, but we have bigger fish to fry.
