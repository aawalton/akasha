Eola woke up the next day looking forward to all her classes.

Breakfast was her favorite apple-chunk porridge, and she ate heavily. It wouldn’t be wise to run out of energy on her first day. Last year, she hadn’t put much—if any—stock in most of her classes. The real learning was always going to be the library, and three of the four had just been busy work to her. Only Magical Dueling had, for the first month, been useful.

This year, she had a good reason to be in every class but Patrol Duty, and she wasn’t going to walk into Extinct Tongues or Ensorcellment with no energy. More importantly, every single class would get her closer to her goal.

A familiar face spotted her from across the cafeteria as she was finishing—Jeanette Ferrin. The mousy young woman had nearly beaten her in the final exams for Magical Dueling last year, and she was by far the better mage, at least when it came to Ordered Magic. As Eola carried her tray to the wash line, Ferrin caught up with her. “Did you read that book, Lemiene?”

Apparently, they were on a last-name basis, even though they’d given each other first names last term.

“Dualiste Principalis, right? No, I haven’t gotten a chance to collect it yet,” Eola said. “I’ve been busy with some personal projects over the term break. Are you in Spellsword Tactics too?”

To her surprise, Ferrin’s eyes narrowed. She eyed the empty tray. “Yes. You ate that much, huh?”

“That’s my usual breakfast—when I remember.”

“Well, it might be wise to forget about breakfast in the future, at least until you know what’s coming. Lieutenant-Librarian Fringe is a stickler for, um, conditioning.”

“Conditioning? What do you mean, Ferrin?”

Ferrin ignored her and started walking as Eola hurried to keep up.

“What do you mean?”

She found out what Ferrin meant twenty minutes later, in the combination gymnasium and dueling center they’d had Magical Dueling in last year. Lieutenant-Librarian Fringe was a yellow-skinned gnome—the color of daffodils, in fact. He arrived in a full set of armor, staring at the ten students. A bushy eyebrow rose, and his equally bushy mustache waggled as he addressed them. “You believe yourselves fighters because you’ve danced the Taron-Li. That elven blade school is the core, but it’s nothing without three things. First, control. You, you, and you, First Guard. Hold it until I tell you to stop. Do not deviate. Second, focus. You four over there, staring contests. Loser does twenty push-ups, then change partners. And third, discipline. You three are running laps.”

Eola tried not to groan. Ferrin shot her a sympathetic look from where she stood in First Guard, sword out at full extension. The porridge sat like a lump in her gut as she started to run.

“Now, the Taron-Li is the core. Maestro Yarrowbloom will be teaching that part of the class. His job is to turn you into skilled combatants with spell and sword. Second-Day and Fourth-Day, he’ll teach you blade-work, footwork, and spell-work.” The gnome stood in the center of the echoing room as Eola rounded the first corner. “My job is to take those skills and teach you how to apply them. Spellswords aren’t just duelists. They’re strike-mages, trying to get to the other side’s siege mages. Or they’re defenders, trying to protect their own. Magical combat is a brutal affair, and you’ll need to know your role.”

A shock of blonde hair caught up and matched pace with Eola, and she did a double-take. Catrine Andrese didn’t make eye contact as she slowly passed Eola. In fact, she didn’t even look her way. It was an incredibly aware effort on Catrine’s part to ignore Eola so completely.

The running continued as Lieutenant-Librarian Fringe lectured on what he’d accomplish. It didn’t stop for the entire hour, and by the end of it, Eola’s legs were less than jelly, and her body was less running and more staggering as it tried and failed to fall. When he finally allowed the exhausted students to stop, she collapsed to the stone floor gratefully, sucking in air as best she could.

“First-Day and Fifth-Day are conditioning days. Be ready. You may want a light breakfast. And Third-Day is when the learning happens. You’ll see what I’m talking about then—both Yarrowbloom and I will be here. You’re dismissed.” The gnome paused and scratched his mustache. “Unless your next class is Patrol Duty. In that case, you’ll be sweaty for a while longer.”

Eola was too tired—and her stomach too riled up—to care that she was dripping with sweat. She stayed in her spot on the floor and waited for the next class to start.

To her surprise, Lieutenant-Librarian Fringe’s entire personality seemed to shift as the other twelve or so combat-track students joined the spellswords. Patrice and Colin both stared at her, and the tall girl started to say something before Eola shook her head. “Later,” she said.

“Good choice, Miss Lemiene. If you just got here, I’m Lieutenant-Librarian Fringe. The spellsword class will try to convince you I’m a sadistic monster. I am, but there’s a good reason for that.

“Now, why do we perform Patrol Duty?”

The question hung in the air. None of the spellswords had the energy to answer it. Ferrin massaged her arm; she’d sat in First Guard for close to the entire class.

Fringe nodded approvingly. “Good. None of you are foolish enough to guess. When Saint Y’aer first gave the world magic, they also gave it monsters. Ever since, we in Alemia have balanced on the edge of a knife. On every side, dangers. Monsters. Rogue Archmages and Great Mages. Other magecraft holds and cities. There were countless threats to every pocket of civilization that managed to cling to existence. Any of them could tip the balance and send a hold or city crashing down.”

“Varin changed that. The Great Mage tipped the balance the other direction, making magic accessible to anyone and everyone. Ordered Magic has made the world a safer place in every regard. Safer, but not safe.”

He paused and touched his mustache again. “My main role in the library is as chief historian. In that regard, I can tell you about the bloody battles as Ordered Magic pushed the world’s dangers back. I can explain the reasons schools like this exist at all. But I can also point to those very monsters living in our own library, and to the ones making the Spine Mountains their home, as proof that Alemia is not a safe continent.”

“And we’re learning how to protect ourselves, then?” A young man asked.

“No, Mr. Skagget. Magical Dueling taught every student at Varin’s Academy that.” Fringe shook his head. “You’re learning how to protect others.

“This will be a practical class. After the first few weeks, we won’t meet formally. Instead, you and a partner will sign up for shifts. You’ll help either the librarians or some of the nearby villages with monster problems. Instead of accompanying a more experienced mage, the two of you will need to work together to solve those problems yourselves.”

The gnome drew a rune, and part of the floor shimmered and blackened into a chalkboard. “Gather around so you can all see. Library duty will be more frequent. Each pair of you that signs up for this will take a two-day shift once per month. Six shifts before the mid-term and seven after it. During that time, your other classes’ practical lessons will be forgiven, but theory and homework will still be required. You will enter the second or third floor and clear a section of it, then patrol the other cleared sections.”

Eola all but salivated over the thought of going into the library with the express purpose of hunting monsters. She and her friends had done alright for themselves in the Library Exam, but this would be different. Self-driven. Self-planned. And with no one and nothing to fall back on. She wanted it. She wanted it badly.

“The other option is six longer, three-day to one-week patrols through the Spine Mountains.” Fringe nodded thoughtfully as he wrote the requirements across the floor. Eola had no idea where he’d gotten the chalk from. “You will visit the villages around Varin’s Academy, discover the locations of any threatening monsters nearby, and eliminate them as threats through whatever means necessary.

“Now, what’s the difference between a monster and an animal?” Fringe finished.

No one spoke. Then an elven girl raised her hand. “How it interacts with the ecology of an area and those living in it?”

“Elaborate.”

The elf cleared her throat and continued. “Well, if a pack of wolves hunts a section of the mountains, they’ll take a few elk, but generally not enough to wipe out the elk population. And they may take a few cows, but unless something’s gone wrong, they won’t attack a farm or village. If a Fiendwolv takes over that pack, though, they’ll hunt an area until there’s not an animal to be seen, and they’ll siege a village, kill everyone in it, and wait for visitors.”

“Good answer. Incomplete, but very close.” Fringe nodded seriously. “Some of my colleagues, in different circumstances, would be monsters. If you’ve ever seen a rogue hamadryad in action, you probably look at Instructor Clearance a little side-eyed, as an example. We’ll be discussing exactly what makes a monster a monster, how best to handle different situations while on patrol, and the realities of patrol life over the next three weeks. Next First-Day, you and your partner will sign up for one of the duties. When you’ve been properly trained and prepped, you’ll sign up for more specific missions. You’re dismissed early today. Spellswords, get cleaned up before your next classes.”

Eola didn’t need to be told twice. She limped from the gymnasium, trying to figure out what patrol to take with Colin.

And then, before she’d even eaten lunch, it was time for Extinct Tongues.

Extinct Tongues, at least, promised to be a sane class.

Instructor Tarik provided a long, long list of reading material.

A Briefe History of the Alemic Tongue

A Coastal Elven Primer

Introductory Orantic Dictation and its Shorthands

Field and Vine: Untranslated Southron Legends

Historie of the Schism: Two Elfin Cultours and their Languajes

Blunt Roles, Chaos Rules: Gnomic Cipher

“The first five are required. We’ll divide the term into equal fifths and spend two months on each book—and on the supplementary readings on the back of this list,” Instructor Tarik said from the lectern in front of the classroom as Eola mentally noted each of the languages and their books. She was pretty sure four of the five existed on either the first or second floors of the library; if she was quick after Ensorcellment, she could get a head-start and churn through all four before—

“I recommend you focus on only the topic at hand,” the professor said. Eola met his eye, and he held eye contact for long enough that she knew he was talking directly to her. “The final text will be a two-week unit at the end of the term, and will be optional. However, it will be on the exam if you choose to learn it.”

The class continued with a solid forty-five-minute lecture about the different languages of Alemia and how Alemic had superseded them all a thousand years before Varin’s time. The rise of Ordered Magic had actually resurrected three entire languages, it turned out. Old Alemic and Forsooth Script had both been extinct as well, save for their use in magic. Now, though, there were sizable regions of Alemia that used one or both as their main tongues. And Emerald Script had also made a comeback—although in its case, that was as much a deliberate act of resistance to Alemic among the elves as it was for its use in Third Order and higher spells.

Jeanette Ferrin took copious notes as Tarik lectured, and Eola spotted Garreth Skagget again. The young nobleman was sitting in one corner, near the back and as far from one of Catrine’s two lackeys as he could. Eola noted that. He and Ferrin might be acceptable study buddies, since none of her fellow apprentices were taking Extinct Languages.

“Our time is almost up,” Tarik said after far too long, “But before you go, note that while every supplemental text is available in both translated and untranslated versions, the expectation is that you do the work yourself. That’s what you’re taking the class for. I’ll know if you use the translated copies—they have clear tells hidden in them.”

As Eola left, the professor caught her eye again and mouthed a single phrase. It took Eola a moment to parse it, and by the time she did, she was out of the door and on her way to Ensorcellment.

He’d wished her luck with Lannith.

Atta accompanied Eola to her final class.

It was in the library. Specifically, it was behind a silvered gate on the first floor. She’d noticed the gateway before—the delicate latch and lack of lock or handle. A handful of other students stood outside of it, waiting. Eola didn’t recognize any of them by name, though they were a mix of second and third-year students. She scratched her head, then raised her badge to the gate. It creaked open.

Ominous. A badge-locked gate meant only one thing: danger lay beyond it.

Lannith hadn’t been present at the apprentice-choosing last term. Eola hadn’t noticed then, but as she proceeded down a stairwell so narrow she had to turn sideways to squeeze between bookshelves and the temperature dropped until she was shivering, she started to wonder why their instructor hadn’t shown up herself. Had she been sick, maybe? Down this far, and in this wet cold, it wouldn’t surprise her.

The stairwell eventually opened into a wide, dark workshop. In the far corner, a half-dozen person-shaped shadows stood near a burning forge. There was no light aside from that, and when Eola tried to cast a Bright Ball, the Mana drained from her soul without ever reaching the rune. She shrugged and hurried to join the cluster of students. Next to the forge, a suit of impossibly slender armor sat on a rack, unmoving.

Atta shivered. Her eyes met Eola’s, and she reached for her smallsword reflexively.

It wasn’t there. She’d left it in her room.

“Welcome to my atelier, students.”

The voice wasn’t a person’s. Eola’s heart stopped, then started to pound out of control, as the suit of armor stood. The Mana drain made a sudden, horrifying amount of sense. She scrambled for her wand. Around her, a handful of other students were doing the same. Roth, however, sat perfectly still. Eola opened her mouth to say something, then saw the hatchet sitting in his lap.

He’d been more ready for a trip to the library than any of the others—including Eola. But even he wasn’t ready for a demi-Srilla.

“You won’t need your weapons, students.” The demi-Srilla’s voice was simultaneously grating and melodic, male and female, lilting and crashing. It changed with every syllable, and somehow never felt like more than a whisper. “I assure your safety in this workshop. The conditions of my…tenure…at Varin’s academy are that any student willing to learn honestly and wholeheartedly is welcome here. I do not wear this form as a threat to any of you, but rather, as a way to protect you from me.”

Eola stared at the thin, tall armor. As she did, her heartbeat slowly slowed. Lannith was a demi-Scrilla—a shapeshifter similar to the monster she’d encountered during the Library Exam. If a Srilla fed enough and grew enough, it would eventually metamorphose into a Demi-Srilla, with significantly more intelligence and cunning. There were plenty of rumors about a Demi-Srilla in the library's first floor somewhere. Eola just hadn’t expected them to be true.

More importantly, the monster…their professor…had willingly placed itself in a chromite-and-silver shell. The suit of plate left few gaps, and it’d be painfully obvious if Lannith attempted to extricate itself from it. There’d be plenty of time to run, and the narrow stairwell was too low-ceilinged for the armor to make it up without a squeeze. This wasn’t Eola’s first monstrous professor, either—and Clearance was safe enough.

“You see,” Lannith said, pointing at Eola. “Now, the work. I have been interested in ensorcellment for over three hundred years, since my metamorphosis. I have taught this class for…seven years now. Ensorcellment is the practice of using ideograms to add magic to items, either permanently or temporarily. If you are in the wrong place or have no interest in this, leaving now is important. Honesty and wholeheartedness will be necessary to learn from me.

“This will be a practical course. You will each pick a project and spend the first half of the term working on it. Your test will be a practical one as well. If you create a tool, you will use it in your craft. If you create a weapon, you will use it in battle against me.” Lannith’s voice almost sounded mirthful about that part.

“Safety is of the utmost importance, behind honesty. I will teach you safety, techniques for ensorcellment, and the tools we use. Then you will get to work. There will be no scheduled classes. Instead, you will create your ensorcelled item, with your own hands, from the raw material components. Please have your first project’s proposal finished by our meeting on Fifth-Day of this week.”

Atta glared at the demi-Srilla, back arched, as it continued to lecture, rolling into the various Child’s Magic and First Order runes most commonly used in ensorcellment. Eola stroked her familiar’s back, but the cat refused to calm down until, after their hour was up, Eola finally climbed the stairs and passed the silvered gate.

She barely noticed the cat’s anxiety by that point—a million different project ideas flooded through her mind, one after another.

But only one stuck.
