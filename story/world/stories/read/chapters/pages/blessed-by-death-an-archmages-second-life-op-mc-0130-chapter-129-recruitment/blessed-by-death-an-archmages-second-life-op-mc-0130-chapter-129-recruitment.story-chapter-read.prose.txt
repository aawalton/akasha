Terminus stared at Levi incredulously. “Are… Are you trying to headhunt me?”

“Yep.”

“Why?”

Levi shrugged. “It’s either this, or your actual head.” Terminus didn’t seem convinced, so Levi sighed. “You are not a… completely terrible person. As objectionable as the Crucible’s plan is, you are right about one thing: at least you guys are trying. Misguided, but with good intentions. I can work with that.”

Terminus pursed his lips, looking apprehensive, but he didn’t immediately refuse. “What are your specific goals?”

“I wish I could tell you, but honestly, I don’t fully know yet,” Levi said. “Let’s just say that I lack certain knowledge of the world.”

Terminus blinked. “Oh. Are you a time traveler?”

Levi frowned. “What? No.”

“Reincarnated mage from the Age of Heroes?”

“No…”

Terminus paused. “Alive mage from the Age of Heroes who used some sort of stasis preservation spell?”

“I’m not that old…”

“Inter-dimensional traveler, then.”

There was a moment of silence.

“You’re not denying it,” said Terminus.

“... eh, I guess now’s as good a time as any,” Levi said. “Yeah, I am.”

He’d expected Terminus to react with shock or disbelief. Instead, Terminus cursed. “Phantasm was right? How does that bastard keep winning?”

“Winning? Did you two bet on my origins?”

“The entire Crucible did,” Terminus said, his voice hollow. “It was a fifty-iridium-coin pool too… I don’t suppose I can convince you to tell them that you’re a dragon in disguise?”

“That’s what you thought I was?”

Terminus nodded glumly.

“Huh.” Levi considered it. “You deserve to lose.”

“It made more sense at the time…” Terminus said defensively. Then, he grew serious. “Why are you here?”

“Officially, I was sent to this world to eliminate the Devourer and Chorus. I reincarnated roughly two months ago, just in time to join Liliya Volkov on her Ascension Trials.”

“Where you immediately came across our operation… Hah. Quite the coincidence. Two months ago… That’s rather new. So that’s why you don’t know what you’re going to do? You’re not familiar enough yet with this world?”

“More or less. I’m not really in a rush. Exploring this new world has been… fun. Almost like an adventure. I’ll learn the ropes eventually. Until then, just know that everything I do, I do for one reason.”

“And what is that?”

“To save people,” Levi said. “You see…” He smiled. “Once, I really wanted to be a hero.”

Terminus inhaled sharply. “Because of the Age of Heroes?”

“No. We had no such thing in my old world.”

“Then why?”

“Because…” Levi’s gaze became slightly distant. “I don’t know. There are quite a few reasons. But at the crux… I guess it’s because I don’t want anyone else to suffer like I once did.”

“I see,” Terminus said. He inclined his head. “Admirable. I’m glad you were able to become one.”

“Maybe,” Levi said. “The System certainly thinks I did, since it gave me that Class, but–”

“No,” Terminus said. “You did, Class or not.”

Levi was caught off guard by the sheer certainty in the man’s voice. Then, his gaze softened. “Thank you. That means a lot.” He exhaled. “That’s why, when you say you want to save the world? I do too. And unlike the Crucible, I can do it in such a way that won’t result in the deaths of millions.”

“How can you be so sure?”

Levi tilted his head, considering it. Should he? Ah, he had already come this far. Might as well.

Levi Ironwood closed his eyes, and Levi opened his. Immediately, Terminus stiffened. Though Levi let not a drop of magic leak out, and though his expression remained calm as ever, Terminus nonetheless looked deeply unnerved, even alarmed.

“Because before I was a hero,” Levi said, “I was a servant of Death.”

“... you’re serious.”

“Dead serious.”

Terminus opened his mouth, then frowned. “Was that a pun?”

“No,” Levi lied. “For ten years, I was sent after all manner of terrifying beings. Forces so powerful, so aberrant, that they would make even the Three Pillars look like bumbling amateurs who can’t find their way out of a paper bag.”

Terminus stared. “And you defeated these forces?”

Levi smiled. “I’m here, aren’t I? I’m not saying they were easy victories. The opposite, in fact. My missions were… difficult, more often than not requiring some insane, nigh-suicidal plan to succeed, and even then I was left with more scars than I can remember. But in the end, I lived, and my enemies did not.”

“Simple, yet poetic,” Terminus murmured. He examined Levi, his stare piercing into Levi’s soul. Finally, Terminus gave a single nod. “Well, alright then.”

Levi blinked. “Alright?”

“I’ll work for you,” Terminus said before chuckling. “Not like I have a choice in the matter. If I don’t say yes, I’ll die, won’t I? I already prepared for this eventuality and have multiple contingencies in place, and by all rights, my calculations should be flawless. Yet despite that, my instincts are telling me that if I try to run, I’ll die, and if I try to fight, I’ll be in such pain that I’ll want to die.”

“... for the record, Death never had me torture anyone–”

“Ah, I think my instincts were referring more to the fact that you’ll probably get irritated and have Ravenbane use his enhanced interrogation protocols on me afterward.”

“Oh. Okay, yeah, that’s fair.”

Terminus nodded. “Anyway. Am I wrong?”

For a moment, Levi didn’t respond. Then, he gave a knowing smile and set his hand on Terminus’s shoulder. Though Terminus had plenty of time to avoid it, he stayed still.

“A word of advice: you really shouldn’t have used [Domain Creation],” Levi said. “You may have thought that I was trapped in here with you, but…”

Levi’s magic flared outward and sank into the artificial boundaries of the domain. With his True Sight, he could tell that it was virtually impossible to break or escape from the domain. That was fine, though. He didn’t need to. Instead…

The edges of the domain flickered before settling. Terminus drew in a sharp breath, dread washing over his face. “What did you just…?”

Levi grinned. “You’re the one trapped in here with me.”

The domain operated on two rules: 1) Only the entities known as ‘Terminus’ and ‘Levi Ironwood’ were allowed inside, and 2) once inside, the entity ‘Levi Ironwood’ was not allowed to leave. Levi couldn’t delete that rule; the god of Limit’s magic was too powerful, even for him. However, he could certainly add to the rule. Like a name, for example.

2) Once inside, the entities ‘Levi Ironwood’ and ‘Terminus’ were not allowed to leave.

“You just… altered my Conceptual Skill,” Terminus said in disbelief. “What? How?”

“So, yeah,” Levi said. “You weren’t wrong.” His smile turned more genuine as he released his magic, the domain returning to normal. “I’m glad you decided to agree.”

Terminus huffed. “Was the demonstration really necessary?”

“Yes.”

“Fair enough. Unfortunately, I don’t speak for the rest of the Crucible. I can attempt to convince them, though.”

“How many members are there?” Levi asked curiously.

“Seven. We’re a small organization. I can call a meeting and convince them to hear you out.” Then Terminus’s eyes narrowed. “Also, I need to have words with Phantasm. If he were here, then we’d actually be able to escape. He’s the one with all the fancy stealth and transportation techniques.”

Levi raised an eyebrow. “I thought you didn’t want to escape?”

“Yeah, but it’d be a lot better for my blood pressure if I knew I could. Ah well, lesson learned. At any rate, I can’t take you to our headquarters – they’d assume that I was compromised if I did. I’ll have to speak to them first, if that’s alright with you.”

“Yeah, that should work,” Levi said. “I’ve been away from–” His breath caught–“home for a while,” he finally said, an odd note to his voice. “I want to head back to Luminarche first.”

He needed to check up on Liliya – did she manage to resolve her situation? – and Ayla. And Andevar and Guildmaster Abode – did the patients with Proliferation develop any post-operation complications?

Levi had been away for weeks. He didn’t know how long the Crucible situation would take, and he would prefer to rest up and prepare first before he potentially had to fight six unknown extremely powerful individuals.

“Don’t take too long, though,” Levi said.

“I’ll get in touch by the end of this week,” Terminus promised. He hesitated, suddenly looking unsure. “Do you… want to use a magical contract or something?”

Levi shook his head. “No. I am not a fan of magical contracts.”

“Oh? May I ask why?”

“An alliance formed by a magically binding contract completely removes the element of trust,” Levi said. “I want to trust my allies, and I want them to trust me. Otherwise, there’d be no point.”

“That seems… oddly impractical and sentimental of you,” Terminus said carefully.

“I have my reasons,” Levi said, and for a second his eyes flickered with something like regret.

Terminus looked puzzled, but accepted it. “Very well. Thank you, then. I appreciate it.” He cocked his head. “What will you do if the Crucible refuses?”

“... we’ll deal with that when we get there.”

Terminus winced. “Right. Just for the sake of transparency, I don’t know if I can help you fight them,” he said apologetically. “I’m sorry if that’s going to be an issue, but they have been my only companions for quite some time…”

“That’s fine,” Levi said. “I don’t expect you to. Just stay neutral, and we’re good.” He smiled thinly. “I can take care of them by myself.”

Terminus nodded before his expression turned pensive. “Earlier, when you spoke of what would be my fate if I continued the Crucible’s plan…” His gaze pierced into Levi. “Your words didn’t sound hypothetical. Were you speaking from experience?”

Levi let out a long exhale. “You’re perceptive,” he said. “Yeah. I was.”

“What happened?”

For a moment, Levi was utterly still, his grey eyes clouded over. It was like he’d turned into a stone statue. Then, in a voice softer than a whisper yet harder than iron, he spoke.

“To slay the Emperor, you needed to kill Yuki and all her relatives. To slay my enemy… I needed to destroy a million souls.”

Terminus froze. “You… You mean kill a million souls, right?”

“I said what I said.”

Terminus’s eyes filled with genuine horror. “Soul magick,” he breathed in realization.

Levi nodded. “I did what I had to do.”

A long, heavy silence fell over them.

“Thank you,” Terminus finally said.

Levi blinked. He’d expected condemnation or accusations of hypocrisy or just general disgust. Not gratitude. “What for?”

“You found another way to kill the Emperor,” Terminus said. “And in doing so, you helped me avoid that fate. Your fate. That is why I thank you – because you may have saved Yuki, but you saved me as well.” He smiled. “See? I told you the System wasn’t wrong.”

Levi stared at him, at a complete loss for words. Then, he laughed. “Heh. Maybe you’re right.”

“I know I’m right,” Terminus said. “You said that you were cursed by the ideals that you once thought beautiful. And perhaps that’s true, perhaps they did curse you. But be that as it may… You still find them beautiful, don’t you?”

Levi closed his eyes, and when he opened them again, it was Levi Ironwood looking back at Terminus. Grey eyes that were sad, solemn, but devoid of the miasmic guilt and self-hatred that had once consumed them. In their place was something clear and strong, nameless yet unmistakable.

“Yeah,” Levi said, smiling. “I do. That is why I will forever strive to be a–”

Then be one.

Just like how a Hero would.

For that, even if no one else will, I thank you.

“[Hero].”

Terminus smiled. “I’m happy to hear that.”

“That is also why I can never join the Crucible,” Levi said. Terminus furrowed his brows. “You want to create heroes by hurting innocents, when instead you should be striving to become the heroes yourselves.”

Terminus went rigid as though he’d been shocked. Then, slowly, he nodded.

“Ah,” he said. “Maybe… Maybe you’re right.”

A silence fell over them, each lost in their own thoughts.

“Well,” Levi said. “We should probably get out of here before Qorbin decides to start setting up booby traps around the domain.”

Terminus snorted. “Good idea. It has been an enlightening talk. I have much to think about and reconsider. I’ll see you in a week, Levi Ironwood.” He paused. “Oh, and one last thing. A parting gift, if you will.”

“Hmm?”

“Phantasm told me that you’re Mythmaker Heimler’s lab assistant?”

“Yes. Why?” Levi got a foreboding feeling. “Is there something wrong?”

Terminus hummed. “You asked me why the Crucible never took care of Qorbin Ravenbane, right? Well, you’re not going to like the answer…”

“Tell me.”

Terminus told Levi. He was right.

Levi didn’t like it.

The domain flickered and shattered, normal space flooding back in. Levi and Terminus reappeared on the roof of the mansion. Instantly, Qorbin Ravenbane was rushing forward, his dark blade streaking through the air. It met a solid wall of magic, bouncing off with a reverberating clang.

“It’s fine,” Levi said. “Terminus is cool.”

Qorbin narrowed his eyes. “The sparrow sings…”

“But the raven croaks,” Levi said, completing the passphrase they’d established months ago as a preemptive measure against possession and imposters. “It’s me, don’t worry. Terminus agreed to join our side”

Qorbin blinked. “He what now?” He turned to stare at Terminus, who shifted slightly under his gaze.

“Levi Ironwood made a convincing case,” Terminus said simply. He tried for a diplomatic smile. “We’re on the same team now, Ravenbane.”

Off to the side, Yuki looked briefly surprised before relief washed over her.

“Huh…” Qorbin said. Then he shrugged. “Alright, fair enough.” A gleam appeared in his eyes. “If we’re on the same team now, then I’m sure you won’t mind answering a few of my questions–”

“We’re also not interrogating him,” Levi said.

“Aww,” Qorbin practically pouted. “Not even a little?”

“Even if I said yes, I doubt Anastasia would let you…”

“Bah, that’s not fair. She has no problems with the Church Inquisitors devising some of the most fucked-up infernal torture methods known to man-and-demonkind.”

“That’s not true,” Anastasia protested. “I’ve always been against their methods.”

“More importantly,” Levi interrupted, a somewhat grim tone to his voice, “we need to get back to Luminarche immediately.”

Qorbin and Anastasia both sobered up. “Why?” Qorbin asked, his tone becoming brusque and business-like.

“I’m sorry, but I can’t tell you,” Levi said. “Not yet. Just know that it’s important.”

Qorbin scrutinized him for a second before nodding. “Well, alright then. Shall we take a swim?”
