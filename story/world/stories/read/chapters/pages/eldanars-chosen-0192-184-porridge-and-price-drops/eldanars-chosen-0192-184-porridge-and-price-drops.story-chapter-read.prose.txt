The harsh, grey light of early morning filtered through the frosted windows of the Bull's Head Inn. Josh groaned softly, his spine clicking in protest as he rolled off the thin straw pallet. The wooden floorboards had been remarkably unforgiving, but after days of sleeping in freezing mud, being indoors still felt like an incredible luxury.

He sat up, rubbing the sleep from his eyes, and immediately noticed a distinct change in the atmosphere.

Usually, mornings at the Bull's Head were heralded by the mouth-watering scent of sizzling sausages, thick-cut bacon, freshly baked pastries, and fried eggs. Today, the air simply smelled of boiled oats.

Josh stood up, his heavy joints popping, and walked over to the hearth. Garrik was standing behind a massive, battered iron pot hung over the flames, stirring the contents with a long wooden spoon. The old fighter looked incredibly tired, his bald head gleaming in the firelight.

"Morning, lad," Garrik offered, though his usual booming cheer was entirely absent. He tapped the spoon against the rim of the pot. "Grab a bowl. It is not much, but it is hot."

Josh looked into the pot. It was a simple, watery porridge. There was no honey, no dried fruit, and certainly no meat. It was the absolute bare minimum required to sustain life.

"I am sorry, Garrik," Josh said quietly, taking a wooden bowl from a stack nearby. "We are used to a bit more of a spread when we stay with you. Is it truly this bad?"

"The rationing is absolute," Garrik sighed, ladling a generous portion of the grey slop into Josh's bowl. "The city council sent guards around yesterday to inventory the cellars of every tavern and bakery. Fresh meat is practically gone. Flour is strictly controlled. We have enough oats and dried beans to last a few weeks if we are careful, but after that... things will get very ugly. This is the best we can offer anyone right now."

By the time Josh sat back down at their table, the rest of the party had begun to stir. Brett sat up, his blonde hair a chaotic bird's nest, and looked at his bowl of porridge with an expression of profound disappointment. Bhel simply grunted, shovelled the oats into his mouth in three massive, unceremonious bites, and wiped his beard. Perberos and Carcan ate in silence.

"We need a plan for the day," Josh stated, keeping his voice low so as not to disturb the other sleeping refugees scattered across the common room. "We have a massive bag of gold from the kobold bounty. Sitting on it does us absolutely no good. If Vane was right about an undead legion massing in the northern woods, we need to be prepared for a siege."

"Gear maintenance is the priority," Bhel agreed, tapping the scarred iron head of his left axe. "I need whetstones, oil, and perhaps a new grip wrap. The sweat and mud have ruined the leather."

"My breastplate is heavily damaged," Josh added, carefully prodding his right side where the bandages peeked out from under his shirt. "The acid chewed straight through the leather strapping and melted a hole right through the padding of my gambeson. If I take a solid hit to the ribs right now, the armour will just fall off."

Brett finished his porridge, pushing the bowl away with a slight shudder. "I also require an upgrade. Since my magic evolved, I no longer require a wooden staff to focus my mana. It is actually acting as an abrasive bottleneck. I need something more direct. I am hoping to find a skilled enchanter who can weave a set of focus gloves, and see if that boosts my power at all, or at least offers a bonus of some kind.”

"And we are critically low on supplies," Carcan noted, her silver eyes serious. "I need healing and mana potions, along with some antidotes if I can get my hands on them. Perberos also needs to resupply his quiver."

"Right," Josh nodded decisively, appreciating the clear objectives. "We split up. We cover more ground that way. Bhel and I will hit the armoury district. Brett, you take the arcane quarter. Carcan, Perberos, find an apothecary and a fletcher. After that have a look around town and find what ever else you might want. We meet back here before sunset."

They left the quiet, sombre atmosphere of the inn and stepped out into the freezing morning air.

If Ashenfall had been crowded the night before, it was an absolute madhouse in the daylight. The streets were an impenetrable sea of desperate humanity. People were huddled together on every corner, begging for scraps or arguing loudly with overwhelmed city guards.

As the party navigated the chaos, moving towards the commercial districts, a bizarre and highly disturbing economic reality began to reveal itself.

The usual rules of commerce had completely inverted. Outside a small bakery, a frantic merchant was offering a beautifully crafted silver ring just for a single loaf of stale bread. Food, clean water, and basic medical supplies had skyrocketed to astronomical, extortionate prices.

However, as they neared the market stalls selling adventuring gear, the opposite was true.

Weapons, armour, and heavy tools were practically being given away. Desperate merchants, realising that a steel sword could not be eaten and was entirely useless for fleeing the city, were aggressively dumping their stock. They wanted light, transportable coins to buy their way onto the last few protected caravans heading south.

"It is a deflationary apocalypse," Brett observed, shaking his head in sheer disbelief as a frantic trader tried to sell him a masterwork iron broadsword for three copper pieces. "The economy has completely collapsed under the weight of primal survival instinct. It is fascinating, and utterly terrifying."

"It means our gold is going to stretch a very long way today," Josh noted pragmatically. "Stick to the plan. Stay sharp, keep your coin purses hidden, and do not get drawn into any fights."

They split off at the main intersection, melting into the dense crowds.

Josh and Bhel pushed their way towards the armoury district. The air here was usually thick with the deafening, rhythmic ringing of hundreds of hammers striking anvils. Today, the noise was sporadic and subdued. Many of the forges were cold, their owners having already abandoned the city.

They found a small, soot-stained workshop that was still operating. An older, heavily muscled human smith was frantically throwing iron ingots into a wooden crate, preparing to flee.

"Shop is closed!" the smith barked, not even looking up as Josh and Bhel stepped into the dark, smoky room. "I am leaving on the midday convoy. Try the guild."

"I have solid gold," Josh said loudly, dropping a heavy handful of coins onto the nearest anvil. The bright, musical clatter of the metal instantly stopped the smith in his tracks.

The man looked at the gold, then up at Josh's massive frame. He wiped a dirty hand across his brow. "What do you need?"

Josh quickly unbuckled his damaged breastplate and laid it on the workbench, followed by his ruined, acid-burnt gambeson. "I need the metal on the right flank re-forged and smoothed out. The acid compromised the structural integrity. I need new, thick leather straps riveted on, and the padded gambeson needs a heavy, reinforced patch over the ribs."

The smith ran a critical, expert eye over the damage. He poked the melted edge of the steel. "Nasty spit. Right. I have a spare patch of hardened troll-hide I can stitch into the gambeson. It will offer better resistance than plain canvas. I can beat the steel back into shape and temper it. I have some thick bull-leather straps ready to go." The smith looked at the gold on the anvil, clearly doing the mental math. "It will take me two hours. It will cost you three gold pieces."

It was an absolute bargain. A week ago, custom repair work with troll-hide would have cost ten times that amount. The desperation in the city was driving prices into the dirt.

"Deal," Josh agreed without hesitation.

While the smith immediately went to work, firing up the bellows to heat the forge, Bhel wandered over to the racks of discarded weapons. The dwarf picked up several heavy throwing axes, testing their balance and grunting in mild disapproval at the human craftsmanship. He eventually settled for purchasing a set of high-quality diamond whetstones and several pots of heavy, rust-preventing weapon oil, tossing a few silver coins onto the bench.

Across the city, in the arcane quarter, Brett was experiencing a similar buyer's market.

The magical district was usually a place of quiet, arrogant exclusivity, filled with wealthy mages and expensive reagents. Now, the shops were frantically boarding up their windows. Brett slipped into a small, cluttered enchanting shop that smelled strongly of ozone and dried lavender.

The proprietor, a nervous-looking half-elf with ink-stained fingers, was sweeping scrolls into a leather satchel.

"I am seeking a very specific, custom focus piece," Brett announced, stepping up to the counter and projecting his most authoritative, scholarly aura. "I no longer require a staff. My elemental affinity has evolved, so that I can channel pure flame through my mana veins, and out of my hands. I need a pair of gloves designed entirely for thermal transference and mana amplification."

The enchanter looked up, his eyes darting nervously towards the door. "I do not have time for custom weaving, sir. I am trying to secure passage out of Ashenfall."

Brett casually placed a small, incredibly pure silver bar onto the counter. The enchanter stared at it, swallowing hard.

"I believe you have the time," Brett suggested smoothly. "I want them made of something highly heat-resistant. Dragon-hide is likely unavailable, so heavily treated salamander leather will suffice. I need them embroidered with focus runes, using raw fire-silk thread. They need to boost my aura and handle the raw output of concentrated plasma without burning my own hands to ash."

The enchanter picked up the silver bar, weighing it in his hand. The sheer value of the metal clearly outweighed his immediate panic. "I have a pair of reinforced drake-scale gauntlets in the back. They were meant for a high-tier spellblade, but he died in the Crypts last week. I can re-thread the runic arrays for pure elemental fire channelling in about an hour."

"Excellent," Brett smiled, looking incredibly pleased with himself. He spent the next hour browsing the discounted utility scrolls, picking up several fire spells for a fraction of their usual cost, hoping that maybe some of them will give him some inspiration.

Meanwhile, on the eastern side of the market district, Perberos and Carcan were navigating the crowds with quiet, efficient grace.

They found a fletcher who was desperately trying to sell off his bulky stock. Perberos mercilessly negotiated, using his intimidating, silent stare to drive the price down even further. He purchased three hundred high-quality, steel-tipped arrows, stuffing them into the spacial sack that Brett had leant them.

Carcan had equal success at a nearby apothecary. The owner was terrified of the impending siege and practically threw bundles of dried healing herbs, rolls of sterile linen, and six vials highly concentrated blue mana potion at her in exchange for a handful of gold coins.

With their bags significantly heavier and their coin purses only slightly lighter, the elven twins stepped out of the bustling thoroughfare. Perberos guided them down a quiet, shadowed alleyway between two tall stone warehouses, far away from the shouting crowds and the crying refugees.

The alley was damp and completely silent.

Perberos stopped walking. He leaned against the cold stone wall, crossing his arms over his chest. He looked at his sister. His pale, luminous silver eyes were entirely devoid of their usual cool detachment. They were sharp, focused, and intensely serious.

"We have exactly what we need," Perberos stated quietly, his voice barely a vibration in the damp air. "We have potions. We have ammunition. We have the gold to buy two fast, sturdy horses from the stables near the southern gate."

Carcan froze, her hands tightening around the small wooden box of vials she was holding. She looked at her twin, a sudden, cold dread pooling in her stomach. "Horses? Josh said we are staying at the Bull's Head until the guild organises a defensive line."

"Josh is a human, Carcan," Perberos replied, pushing off the wall and stepping closer to her. "His perspective is entirely limited to the walls of this city and the people inside it. But you heard what Mich said. You heard the panic from the scouts. The borders of the safe zones are collapsing across the entire continent. The deep-woods predators are fleeing the dimensional mana. The undead are massing in the north."

Perberos paused, letting the heavy reality of his words sink in. He looked deeply into her eyes, pleading with her to understand.

"Briarwood is completely exposed," Perberos whispered, speaking the name of their ancient forest home. "Our kin live entirely without stone walls. They rely on the natural borders of the deep woods to keep the monsters at bay. If the ecosystem is truly fractured, the forest will be overrun. We have a duty, Carcan. Our family needs our skills. We must return home."

Carcan felt the breath leave her lungs. She looked away from her brother, staring down at the muddy cobblestones of the alleyway.

Briarwood. The sprawling, ancient canopy of silver-leaf trees where they had been born. The quiet, melodic singing of their elders. The safety of the high branches. She loved her home with a fierce, profound devotion. The thought of their elegant, peaceful kin being torn apart by desperate, starving beasts or rotting undead horrors made her heart physically ache.

But then, she looked past the alleyway, out towards the bustling, terrified human city.

She thought of Josh.

She saw him in her mind's eye, standing tall and resolute in the freezing mud, his heavy plate armour sparking with raw, untamed lightning. She remembered the way he had stepped directly into the path of danger again and again, risking his own life without a single second of hesitation simply to protect the people behind him. She remembered the quiet, profound moments they had shared, the unspoken understanding that had passed between them in the steamy bathhouse.

Her secret feelings for the human fighter had grown from a seed of quiet admiration into a deep, undeniable, and entirely consuming affection. He was brave, he was fiercely loyal, and he anchored her in a world that was rapidly spinning out of control.

The idea of leaving him behind, especially now, with a potential army of the dead marching toward the city gates, felt like an unforgivable betrayal. It made her physically sick to her stomach.

"We cannot just leave them, Perberos," Carcan finally whispered, her voice trembling slightly with suppressed emotion. She looked back at her twin, her eyes bright with unshed tears. "We fought beside them. We bled with them. They are our party."

Perberos sighed, a long, sad sound. He was intensely perceptive; he had known his sister for what felt like centuries, and he saw the conflict tearing her apart. He reached out, gently placing a hand on her shoulder.

"Is it the party you cannot leave, sister?" Perberos asked softly, his voice gentle but probing. "Or is it the shield-bearer?"

Carcan flinched, the colour draining slightly from her pale face. She opened her mouth to deny it, to hide behind the professionalism of being their healer, but the lie died on her tongue. Perberos saw right through her.

"He is a good man," Perberos conceded quietly, offering a rare, genuine compliment to the human. "He fights with honour. But his life is a fleeting spark compared to ours, Carcan. The humans burn brightly, and then they are gone. Our blood, our kin in Briarwood... they are our forever. We cannot let our forest burn to save a human city."

"We do not know that Briarwood is burning," Carcan argued desperately, clutching at any logical lifeline. "We only know that the borders are shifting. The elven rangers are the finest in the world. They can hold the trees."

"They cannot hold a continent-wide surge!" Perberos hissed, his stoicism finally cracking slightly, revealing his own deep-seated fear for their family. "If Ashenfall falls to the undead, the horde will simply march into the woods. We have to help fortify the ancient groves."

Carcan closed her eyes, a single tear slipping free and tracking down her cheek. She was entirely torn in two.

"Then we ask them to come with us," Carcan pleaded, opening her eyes and looking at her brother with fierce, desperate hope. "Josh, Brett, Bhel. They are incredibly powerful. Brett’s fire, Bhel’s axes, Josh’s shield. They could help us defend the forest. We ask them to abandon Ashenfall and journey to the elven lands with us."

Perberos looked at her with profound pity. "You are asking a human, a man whose entire class and psychological makeup is built entirely around defending the weak, to turn his back on a city filled with thousands of helpless refugees right as an undead army arrives at the gates?"

The words hit Carcan like a physical blow. She knew Perberos was absolutely right. Josh would never leave the city now. It was entirely against his nature. If they asked him to abandon the terrified civilians they had just spent days escorting to safety, he would refuse. He would stay and fight on the walls, even if it meant his death.

"So, what is our choice?" Carcan whispered, her voice breaking. "Do we abandon our home to burn? Or do we abandon our party to die?"

"I do not know," Perberos admitted softly, dropping his hand from her shoulder. The normally decisive, calculating scout looked utterly lost. "But we must decide soon. If the undead legion arrives, or another horde of beasts, the city gates will be sealed. We will be trapped here. We must choose our path before then."

The damp alleyway fell into a heavy, suffocating silence. Around them, the terrified city of Ashenfall continued to scream, completely unaware of the heart-breaking choice tearing the elven twins apart.
