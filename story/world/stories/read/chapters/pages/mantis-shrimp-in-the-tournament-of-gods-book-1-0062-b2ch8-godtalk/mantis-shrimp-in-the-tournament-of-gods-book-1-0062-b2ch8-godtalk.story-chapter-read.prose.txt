"Finally we can talk my ass! Who are you? Show yourself!" I screamed, looking all around me and seeing the same void no matter where my eyes pointed. It was hopeless, and I knew that no matter what I had tried, I wouldn't be able to stand here in a position of power.

I didn't fucking care.

I screamed, swung my arms around, cast skills one after another, not the ones that had long cooldowns, but I did cast as many of them as possible.

And none of it helped. After about twenty minutes of doing everything and achieving absolutely nothing, I stopped for a minute, trying to regain my breath.

"Are you done, you dumb little crustacean?"

"No, just..." I huffed, finding the words hard to get out. "I just need a minute. I'll be back at it, don't worry."

"Well, try to get on with it. Time flows differently here, but soon enough your companions will try to figure out where you've gone."

I didn't even think of my companions until now. It was then that I realized that this was the first time ever since my new life I was completely without Mark's presence near me. It felt strange, and not in a good way.

That struck me deeply. I didn't need that guy... but... I've gotten used to him, and he was helpful when he tried. That didn’t mean that often, and oftentimes he stuck his nose where it didn’t belong, but still…

And perhaps he could have figured out how to hurt this damned god that made all of those not funny descriptions.

Light suddenly appeared, my vision adapting quickly. Closing my eyes, I let this light in through my eyelids before slowly opening them again. I saw a light person. It was strange; it definitely had the physique of a human, yet it was so bright and so large that I knew that it wasn't a human immediately. Not that I needed more convincing after hearing the voice.

I also felt a huge presence. The dragon was dangerous, and some other things I've seen on the first floor of Exercia were definitely far stronger than I was. None of them had even come close to the same solar system as this being had. I knew immediately that it was a god. Well, I knew that before, but now there was no doubt left in my mind.

"Well, why did you call me here?" I asked, feigning more confidence than I actually felt. I knew that this being could sweep me away from existence like I could sweep off a butterfly, but I wouldn't let him know that. I wouldn't give him the damn satisfaction.

"I came to talk to you, little one." He said, his grin the only thing I could see from his face. He didn’t have eyes. "I have an offer for you."

"Offer? Is that even allowed? Seems like cheating to me. Surely your boss wouldn't like this that much?"

"No, he wouldn't, but it's a good offer, I think, one that would serve you very well."

Serve you just as well, I suppose, I said in my mind but didn't speak, waiting for the god in front of me to continue.

"My name is Tevannas, the God of Words. You don't need to remember my name; it will be of no matter to you, no matter if you win or lose the tournament. I came to speak to you because I feel like the future’s flow will revolve around you a lot. Truth be told," he grinned even more widely, as if thinking of a fun memory, "your item and skill lines have been the most fun to write out of all the players."

"I knew it was you, you fucker," I mumbled.

"Of course it was! I fill out the items for all of the players. It's sometimes a bit more fun than Earth was, but you in particular have managed to become something of a spectacle. A lot of gods watch you these days, though they still are shy about granting many boons. You're not the strongest player."

"I doubt that. Have you seen my fight at the end of the last floor?" I grinned, trying to mimic his own confidence.

"I have. It was quite spectacular, if I can say so myself, but it was only the second most interesting fight going on. Maybe the fifth overall from the first floor."

"You're just trying to provoke me."

"No, no, no. That's not true, but let's just say that the top three players won't like each other that much if they bounce into each other in the future."

That was information. I definitely should store that for later. I tried my best to commit it to my memory. I seriously did, okay? Definitely didn’t forget a moment later.

"Okay, you had a point that you wanted to talk about. Out with it so I can refuse you and go back to the dungeon already."

"Oh yeah, the request." He said, connecting his fingers in front of his chest as if thinking how to say it in a way that I understand. I didn't even know why I knew that was what was going on through his head, but something in my whole being just wanted to punch this god with all the power that I had.

"I cannot tell you exactly what the wish is." He said, and I interrupted him before he could continue.

"So why call me here in the first place if you can't even tell me? I can’t read in between the lines, man!"

"I know, but know that while you might hate me for the descriptions, I am nowhere near the worst of the gods. I write them that way because it’s my job."

I had a hard time believing that, but I held my tongue for once.

"My ask of you is simple: when the time comes on the eighth floor, I need you to take it all."

…

"What the fuck does that mean, Tara-whatever?"

"It doesn't matter for now. You will know when the time comes."

"How the hell am I supposed to remember it until the eighth floor? We are just on the second, and already it feels like too much has happened for me to remember what happened at the beginning. I won't remember what we talked about here when we reach the eighth floor. Should have just taken me here when we reached it." I pointed out all around us at nothing in particular.

"I wouldn't be able to. Already I am risking too much."

"Why?"

"Can't explain. Dammit, you little crustacean, can't you just listen for a second? I am trying to help you, no matter what you believe. I am not as omnipotent as you or your guide believe, and I need you to take it all when the time comes."

Another rebuke fell on my lips, but he raised his hand, stopping me.

"I couldn't ask you this later, because as the players die and you continue doing your exploits, you will get more eyes and you will get his eyes. After that, there will be no talk between us. I will only be able to leave hints in skills and other items you find, but those will be far more cryptic than this."

"So you just want me to take something?"

"Saying more will catch his attention. Just remember this:"

For the first time ever since this all began, I actually saw the god as not something that sneered at every death, not something that seemed to want me to die at every opportunity, but I saw an actual being.

He looked scared.

"What about the other gods? Are they as kind-hearted as you are?" I scoffed, failing to keep the poison out of my voice.

It actually got a chuckle out of that god.

"We are all different, just like humans are, just like any race we have visited in the thousands of millennia. To answer your question, it depends."

"That's the most frustrating answer you could have given me."

"Farewell, Shrimpie. I'll be hoping for your success."

The darkness started disappearing around me, replaced by walls of rock. The same architecture I expected when entering the dungeon.

"Oh, and last thing, you cannot mention this to anyone else. The gods are watching, remember? Keep your mouth shut or you will die."

With that, the darkness disappeared completely, and I was standing at the entrance of the dungeon once again. The other players were walking forward, not noticing my shock as I stood in one place. I had far too many questions, more than I had just moments before.

"You alright, Shrimpie?" Mark asked, concern at the edge of his voice.

I looked at him. The conversation from a few moments ago still fresh on my mind, yet somehow receding to the back of my memory, as if forcefully changed into being an event that happened weeks ago. A strange feeling, yet I remembered all of it nonetheless. I remembered the threat of what would happen if I shared what I found with anyone.

"Yeah, all is fine," I said, swallowing my saliva, trying to keep my hands from shaking. "Let's go."
