Satan Jr.’s body grew and grew. He rose to twelve feet tall, about the same as the puppeteer standing on its tentacles, and he met it in the center of the battlefield. Punches flew. He rained straights, hooks, and uppercuts on it, his form sharp and experienced. He dodged the swipes of tentacles. Every punch that hit deformed the puppeteer’s black head like a balloon, pushing it left and right. It was an onslaught.

“He’s winning! Go Satan!” Goldie cheered from where she and Gary had retreated.

“Does it look like he’s dealing damage?” Gary asked.

“Of course! Krow-pow! Krakaboom! Spinning kick! Look, Gary, he’s kicking its ass!”

People all across the gymnasium army cheered. However, Gary shook his head. “It’s not that simple, Goldie. It looks like he’s connecting, but in reality, he’s dealing no damage…”

“What!? How do you know?”

“I can see it. Come on, let’s take our friends and get the hell out of here.”

Feng Shen and the champs had left already, and the brothers had left even earlier. The only ones remaining from the previous battle were Boulderus Johnus and the Slime King Monom, duking it out somewhere inside the puppeteer’s base. Gary glanced at the open doors.

He wanted to go in there and tear that thing apart for using his mentor’s face. He wanted to ask it things. About him. About itself.

“Do you want to go?” Goldie asked softly.

“Now’s not the time,” he replied. “It’s just a monster. We have to save our friends.”

Everyone had been battling for some time now. Exhaustion was taking its toll—Gary saw many climbers just sitting, panting heavily. This is where his high Vitality came into play. He was tired, but not exhausted. He could go on. Things would only get better for him from now on.

The recovering climbers were almost exclusively enemies. They had a massive advantage in this battle, which is why they had that luxury. The Gymnasium forces were constantly pushed back, not allowed a second to rest. People were falling like flies. Field bosses rampaged on the backlines.

Thankfully, the Legion’s main forces had arrived while Gary was inside the base. They’d stabilized the center of the Gymnasium army and inspired discipline onto those around them. It really was a clan made for wide-scale warfare. Gary allowed himself a moment of fantasizing about campaigning into unknown lands alongside these brave men and women.

An arrow slammed into him from the side, sending him spinning. He gasped. The arrow was made of hard metal and had dug between two of his ribs. He ripped it out as he saw the assailant, a woman in tribal leather armor flanked by three hyenas. He was debating whether to attack her or try to escape when a pack of hellhounds leapt onto the hyenas, drawing them into a bloody battle of tooth and nail. The woman immediately helped her beasts. Another woman, a witch, arrived to help the hellhounds.

Gary tore his eyes away and kept going. He’d been deep into enemy lines, and he had to take a wide circle around the floor bosses. It would take some time.

Meanwhile, Satan Jr. was no longer kicking the shit out of the puppeteer. The tentacles had multiplied, and they slapped at him or slithered in to grab his limbs. As easily as he shook them off, they were many. He smashed a fist into the puppeteer’s face, and he received a tentacle slap in the nose. Two more tentacles wrapped around his horns, trying to snap his neck backwards, but he slipped out of their grasp, arrived behind the puppeteer, and suplexed it into the ground. The tentacles writhed in the air like medusa hair.

“Show yourself!” Satan cried out, pummeling the puppeteer. It maintained an eerie silence. Its body rippled with every punch but held.

Meanwhile, the horror army’s advantage only grew. As they pressured the gymnasium forces, chimeras stood just behind the front line, raising any who fell. Their army grew as the gymnasium’s thinned. The pressure increased. The weight plate-dispensing treadmills were firing smaller plates now, as the heavier ones already littered the battlefield. So did corpses. Most originated from the gymnasium side but still had tentacles.

As Gary rushed towards his friends, he saw familiar spear-wielders strewn across the ground. He recognized some of them from the tournament, or from random chats around the clan grounds. He said good morning to some of these people. He’d fought one during the early rounds of the tournament.

They were accompanied by fallen legionnaires, witches, beef boys, pros. Everyone had their hopes and dreams. Everyone had worked hard to reach this point. They were dead now. Everything they’d poured into themselves went right down the drain.

There were enemy corpses, too. Fewer, but still a lot. Monsters, zombies, climbers. He kept fearing he’d see some of his friends lying there, but it never happened.

The most populous group of all, however, was the rogue climbers. By far. These poor people were weak, disorganized, and helpless. They were fodder. Coming to this event had been a great risk on their part, throwing away their lives for the small chance of rising from the ashes of so many fallen. Of claiming an opportunity that other people had been born with. They’d walked into hell for it.

Rogue climbers had a high chance of dying. Clan disciples had strength, allies, and the occasional life-saving treasure. They were safer, but still in danger. Geniuses were much safer. They probably wouldn’t die regardless of who won the war. As far as Gary knew, the only truly powerful climber who’d died was the rogue woman sector boss assassinated by Jin.

To the weak, war was hell. The stronger one was, the more it started to seem like a game.

Like life.

A terrible stench covered the battlefield. A mix of blood, sweat, and spilled intestines. It was so heavy Gary thought it would never leave his nose. The stench of a nightmare.

He finally reached the front lines. Legionaries held their shields up, retreating orderly, while the remaining clan and rogue climbers covered the sides. The gymnasium monsters fought with zeal, but everyone else clearly intended to flee. Gary already spotted some climbers clashing with the pros surrounding the battlefield.

He was on the wrong side of the front line. He saw the backs of tentacle monsters, swordsmen, wizards, and beast tamers. Spells rained overhead. Even he couldn’t get past this.

Someone noticed him. “Spy!” they yelled, and heads turned.

“Shit,” Gary said, thunderstepping away. A weight plate whistled past his head—it easily weighed half a ton. An errant spell dug the ground beside his feet. A javelin came for his chest, barely deflected. He spotted the tribal-looking field boss monster who’d attacked him before, as he escaped the orchard. It was already drawing another javelin.

Gary thunderstepped away, circling the battlefield from the outside until he located a thinner point. He broke through the front line there, taking a few glancing strikes, then tumbled over the allied shield wall.

“He’s ours!” a voice came, narrowly saving him from the guarded legionaries.

Gary nodded at Aurelius Pratius Lutius, who apparently led this part of the wall. “Are you escaping?” he said.

“We don’t need to escaped. Once Satan dies, we’ll be kicked off the floor.”

“What if they keep him alive on purpose to hunt us down?”

Aurelius gave him a long glance. “Yes, we’re escaping,” he said. “Men! Double time!”

Gary went deeper into the allied forces, pushing past the very angry men and women who held the line. He located Torga Ba, his tall, dark body easily visible, and made it over. He was pleased to find not just the Earth Alliance, but also his friends, mercenaries, and summons.

“We’ve lost half our people, Gary! This is terrible!” Marla said.

“Welcome to the tower,” he replied.

“Boss!” Osteo cried out.

“I thought you’d close the door,” Asim said.

“It was impossible. I think they were cheating.”

“How?” Cog asked.

“There was this thin gray light that kept the door ajar. We couldn’t close it. We were pulling and pushing from both sides, uncontested, but nothing.”

Cog fumed. “I hate cheaters.”

“Weren’t you selling illegal Muscle Sap?” someone from the Earth Alliance said.

“That’s just rumors,” Cog replied.

“Hey, where’s Evil Shrek?” Gary asked.

“He fell. The donkey tried to hump a dead monster, and a field boss came and killed them both. It was instant,” Asim said.

“I wish someone would kill me instantly,” a voice came from inside the Egyptian’s robes.

“What did we say about sarcasm, Edgar?” Asim said.

“It’s only sarcasm if you don’t mean it, Master,” the voice replied.

“Why are you still talking? We need to get out of here!” Chloe said.

“Right,” Gary said. “I can cut a path out for us. Follow me.”

“No,” Torga said—the first word out of his mouth.

Gary paused. “No?”

“No. The gymnasium monsters will die anyway, but all these climbers won’t. We won’t retreat alone. We stay in the company, and we cover everyone’s retreat.”

Gary considered it. “You’re right. Let’s do that. I will move to the front lines and help with the defense. You guys just stay safe.”

“We’re rotating to the front lines. We’ll do our part,” Torga said.

“That’s fine too. I won’t rotate. When you get to the front line, make sure you’re nowhere near me.”

The veteran raised a brow but nodded.

“What? Why?” Cog asked, but Gary didn’t reply.

He left them, charging towards the battle again. Getting through his army was far easier when going this way. He arrived at where the rogue climbers were barely holding the line and unleashed a Nail Gun, immediately taking off some of the pressure.

“It’s Gary Steel!” someone cried out.

“Hang on, guys. We’re in this together. They won’t break us!” he said.

“Yeah!” the people cheered, immediately reinvigorated. More enemies fell to Gary’s spear. The rogues beside him could take a breather.

The retreating army had stuck their backs to Satan Gym by now. They’d vacated most of the battlefield, leaving Satan Jr. surrounded. The poor guy was trying his best. He slapped away field bosses like mosquitoes and stopped the regular monsters and climbers by sheer aura. They weren’t all, however. The brothers had joined that battle, carefully whittling down Satan while the puppeteer handled the brunt of his attention. Jin wasn’t there, though. Neither was the Slime King. The leaders of each climber clan were also present, harassing Satan as they could.

The puppeteer itself was a worthy opponent to Satan. Given everyone else harassing him, he was riddled with wounds, panting heavily as fire burned in place of blood. Admirably, he held on.

“This is a grave insult,” he roared. “I will remember all of your faces!”

Suddenly, a devil appeared beside Satan in a flash of fire. It was the referee, whom he’d previously killed. He held something in his hands. “Mr. Satan Jr.! I’m here to help— Ack!”

A stray attack struck the devil as he appeared, skewering his chest. The devil fell forward very theatrically, accidentally tossing the item in his hands towards Satan Jr., who happened to be turning. The thrown item—a galss ball—made a beautiful curve as it dove directly into Satan’s only pocket.

“Oh no!” Satan Jr. yelled. “Not my pocket! That’s where I keep the Soul Powder!”

The attacks slowed down as the clan leaders looked at each other in confusion. The brothers were already running away. Satan reached into his pocket to reveal a glowing white powder, fizzling as it somehow slipped through the walls of the glass ball. Black dust was inside the ball. The glow came from where the powder met the dust, quickly rising in intensity until it was bright as the sun.

“What a terrible accident! That clumsy devil’s annihiball fell onto the Soul Powder I can legally carry! The interaction will result in—”

“Run, you idiots,” the puppeteer growled. Liu Dong was already running. The beast tamer and church leaders were a beat too late.

The ball in Satan’s hands exploded. A massive, white-and-black shockwave blasted through the battlefield. Oddly, no dust was raised, and Gary didn’t see anything flung away. It was as if the visible shockwave was just an illusion. Even from this distance, however, he felt a tug in his soul—a wholly uncomfortable feeling.

As the shockwave passed, the brothers were completely fine. Liu Dong stumbled but regained his balance. The church and tamer leaders collapsed like puppets with their strings cut. As for the puppeteer, which had been at the epicenter of the explosion alongside Satan, its black head had melted away, revealing the figure of a slender woman inside.

Her eyes were purple, and her hair pitch-black. Her skin was nightmarishly pale. Twisting horns emerged from her forehead—resembling not a goat’s, but a devil’s. She wasn’t moving.
