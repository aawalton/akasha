Chapter 22

"The moment a candidate manifests directed Ether manipulation, the evaluation shifts from 'survive' to 'capable.' The distinction is not semantic. It is the difference between a species that endures and a species that fights." - Codex of the Long Watch, Operational Directive

Arthur woke before dawn; he had felt the technique all night. A door in his body unlocked and waiting.

Steve was already awake, sitting cross-legged with the guidebook arranged in front of him. He was holding his hands six inches apart and staring at the space between them.

"Steve."

"Shh."

Arthur watched for ten seconds while nothing happened. Then there was a flicker of a flame. A steady, blue-white flame the size of a large candle, hovered between Steve's palms without fuel or wick. It gave off quite the heat for such a small flame, Arthur could feel it from two metres away, and real light, casting sharp shadows across the office.

"Ether Flame," Steve said. His voice was careful and controlled, the voice of a man holding something fragile. "External Ether manipulation. I'm pushing energy out through my palms and exciting the air molecules between them. It's thermodynamics, accelerate molecular movement and you get heat. Enough heat and you get combustion."

"How long can you hold it?" Arthur asked.

The flame wavered and shrank. Steve's face tightened with concentration, and it stabilised again. "Not nearly long enough, maybe fifteen seconds before I start to feel the drain." He let it die and his hands dropped to his knees. "This is costing me something, I can feel it, like I've been sprinting, but in my head instead of my legs."

He looked up sharply at Archie. "Am I losing Integration?"

"Technique utilisation does not consume Integration," Archie stated. "Integration is permanent biological restructuring. Techniques draw from Ether Reserve, a regenerating energy pool."

Steve was writing in the guidebook before Archie finished. "Right, so Integration is the permanent engine, which only goes up. Reserve is the fuel tank, goes up and down and refills over time."

"The summarisation is within acceptable approximation parameters."

"How big is the tank?" Donna asked. She'd woken during the exchange and was sitting up against the wall.

"Reserve capacity scales with Integration level and Constitution."

Steve looked at his hands and Arthur watched the realisation arrive.

"Constitution," Steve said quietly.

"Constitution," Donna’s voice was smug. "The attribute you tried to skip last night. The one I had to force you to take one point in. That's not just how hard you are to kill, Steve. It's how much fuel you carry. Every Constitution point you skipped is a fireball you can't throw."

Steve opened his mouth, but nothing came out.

"I'm not going to say I told you so," Donna said.

"You're literally saying it right now."

"I'm observing a factual correlation between your allocation choices and your operational capacity. That's not the same thing."

"You've been spending too much time with Archie."

Arthur almost smiled. “What about us, then? Archie, Steve’s got the flame. What have we got?”

"Techniques are assigned based on accumulated behavioural data. Each integration profile reflects the dominant survival pattern observed during Levels One through Five."

Arthur looked at the door inside himself and reached for it with intent. The same internal focus he used for attribute allocation but aimed not at a stat but at a capability.

The door opened and he felt like he became a building.

Ether flooded through his Nadi Matrix and into his musculoskeletal system, and every bone, every tendon, every joint locked into a configuration he recognised instantly. His entire frame was calibrated to manage load.

His feet pressed into the carpet and through it, the floorboards beneath creaking as his weight seemed to double. His legs were foundation pilings driven into bedrock. His arms were crossbeams. His spine was a column carrying the full compressive load of a structure that had decided not to move.

And he couldn't move. For three seconds he was rooted and nothing short of his structural demolition could have shifted him. But he was also locked. A wall doesn't walk. A foundation doesn't dodge.

Then the Ether flow cut, and he staggered. His body had been stone for three seconds and now it was flesh again, and the flesh didn’t handle the transition well. His joints ached and protested like a structure loaded past its design tolerance.

He caught himself against the wall and breathed.

"Structural Reinforcement," he said, once his hands stopped shaking. "I can channel Ether into my frame and everything locks. My body feels dense and rigid like an immovable object. I’ll have to test it, but I can hold it for three seconds, maybe five if I push it. The problem is that I can't move while it's active, and the recovery takes a while.”

"A wall that doesn't move for five seconds," Donna said. Arthur could see the scenarios running behind her eyes: Arthur locking down in front of a charge and absorbing an impact that would send anyone else flying, buying her the seconds to circle behind. "You become the terrain. The chokepoint isn't the corridor any more. It's you."

"That's the idea."

"That's rather more impressive than a candle flame." Steve said unhappily

"Your turn, Donna," Arthur said.

Donna closed her eyes and did not move for some time. Then her eyes snapped open and seemed different. If attention had a quality, you could say hers had improved. When she looked around it was almost like her eyes were scanning.

Suddenly her head turned sharply left and the axe came up. Then she relaxed.

“Mouse,” she said. "In the wall cavity. “It’ll come through the crack by the skirting board in about three seconds.”

Arthur and Steve both looked. One, two, and then a mouse pushed through a crack in the plaster, saw them and bolted.

"Reflex Acceleration," Donna said, her thumb pressed against her temple. "It's like my brain sped up. I felt the movement in the wall before I heard it: sound, vibration, probable size, and speed. All processed before the mouse finished deciding to come through." She flexed her fingers and shook out the fine tremors in her right hand.

"Two to three seconds of accelerated perception. Then it cuts and the headache arrives."

"It’s fading already but I wouldn't chain two bursts without a gap." She looked up.

"So, in combat you could trigger the burst, read the incoming attack and exploit the gap. Two seconds of seeing the future." Arthur was nodding his head, “That’s going to be handy.”

Steve was staring at both of them. His flame was impressive in isolation. But next to Arthur turning into an immovable wall and Donna predicting a mouse through a wall, it was a candle at a fireworks show.

"So, Arthur gets to become an immovable fortress," Steve said. "And Donna gets a precognitive combat sense. And I get—" he held up his palm and produced the flame, “—a slightly impressive lighter."

"Arthur’s technique reflects his survival pattern. Sustained physical defence and the choice to absorb impact," Archie stated. "Donna’s technique reflects her own combat pattern of threat identification and response. Your technique reflects a behavioural contradiction that the Nadi Matrix has not fully resolved."

Steve blinked. "What contradiction?"

"Your attribute allocation prioritises Ether processing capacity. Your combat behaviour prioritises physical-range engagement with a polearm. The resulting technique assignment reflects the system's best approximation of a profile that does not yet have a clear operational classification."

"You've confused the system," Donna chuckled, before Steve could say anything. “Well done, Steve. You’ve even managed to confuse the Nadi Matrix.”

"I haven't confused it. I'm building towards something it hasn't seen yet. The flame is the foundation—"

"The flame is the Matrix hedging its bets because you've been speccing for wizard and fighting like a pikeman."

Steve looked at Archie. “It does get better, doesn’t it? As my Acuity increases, the external manipulation gets stronger?”

"Acuity directly correlates with external Ether manipulation magnitude. At the current threshold, sustained flame is the maximum viable output."

"And at higher Acuity?"

Archie's geometry flickered. It almost seemed like he was deciding how much unearned information is too much unearned information.

"Archie?"

"At Acuity twenty-five, the bandwidth supports directed environmental manipulation at combat-viable magnitudes." Archie replied finally.

"That means I point at something and the air around it catches fire." Steve translated for Donna.

"At Acuity twenty-five. You're on twenty-one."

"Four more points. Two levels if I commit."

"If you survive two more levels made of paper."

"That's what the one point of Constitution was for."

"The one point I had to force you to take."

Steve had no comeback for that. He held up the flame instead, casting his face in a sharp light. Sub-combat, it was barely a party trick. But it was external Ether projection at a sustained output that nobody else in the party could produce, and in two levels it would become something that changed the shape of every fight.

"I’ve gone from using a billhook screwed to a pole to this," he said. "The trajectory is upward."

“With that output, I daresay you’ll be using the billhook for a while yet.”

Donna looked at the flame for a long moment. Then she shook her head.

"Put that out before you set off a smoke alarm that doesn't exist anymore, we've got a church to break into."

***

They packed in silence. The morning routine was automatic now, check supplies, check equipment and gear, secure everything tightly. The solicitor's office returned to emptiness, the cold cup of tea still sitting on the desk.

Arthur paused at the door. A couple of days ago they'd been playing D&D in a pub and now they were a party of an immovable tank, a pre-cognitive DPS and a budding mage. The thought should have felt absurd, it didn't and that felt even more absurd.

He flexed his hands and thought about William Marshal, the Greatest Knight, carved in stone, who protected a child and kept an empire together.

Steve was still writing in the guidebook as they formed up. Arthur caught a glimpse of the margin: Three techniques. All active, all costly, all different. Arthur reinforces. Donna accelerates. I project. The Matrix gave us what we already were, only sharpened. The system or the Nadi-Matrix watches how you survive and then help you survive better in exactly the way you were already doing. No menus or skill trees. Just you, amplified.

"Temple Church," he said and they moved.
