It looked like a dream image of a unicorn, if the dreamer was stark raving mad. Or on drugs.

Clawed white hooves ground great gouges from the earth.

“What the-”

I ran, as easily half a tonne of furious red-eyed horse-monster galloped after me. Leaping for the nearest overhanging branch, I scrambled up the tree, desperately trying to hoist myself to safety.

Fanged jaws snapped, narrowly missing my swinging bare foot. I snatched it up, balancing it on the oh so skinny, preciously bobbing branch. Since when did unicorns have teeth like fucking velociraptors? What the fuck!

The unicorn bucked and reared, whinnying and snapping wildly. “Fuckkkkkkkkkkkkkk!”

Higher, I needed to get higher. Suddenly I was regretting not putting more points into strength. I scrambled up, clinging to the trunk desperately, staring down at the insane [Night Mare].

Maybe it will go away? Why isn’t it going away?

Its movements were erratic, jerky, its eyes staring everywhere all at once. Foam dripped from its lips. The fact that I could not see its level was not encouraging. And I only had enough mana for one solid [Curse].

Before I could open my mouth, the unicorn threw back its head, letting out the most appalling noise, half scream, half whinny. It galloped headfirst into the trunk, slamming its head into it. The whole tree jolted, shuddering violently. Its horn impaled in the bark, the unicorn screamed with rage, shaking furiously beneath me. The world tilted as the tree took damage. One of my scrambling feet made contact with the unicorn’s snout as I slipped, and there was a solid crunch.

“Fuck off, you creepy undead My Little Pony wannabe, fuck all this fantasy bullshit, fuck the system, and fuck you, you piece of shit fairy tale knock off—” The mana dribbled away, before my words ended - but I had had the foresight to target its ankle. Hopefully a point of weakness in the fairy tale crazed tank.

For a brief second, I saw veins of thick black blood snaking beneath the unicorn’s skin, the outline of bone within. Then it was gone. My mana spent like a drop in the ocean. With only a single point left, I had no choice but to go full barbarian. Swinging wildly, I clubbed downward with the remains of my branch, missing the unicorn as many times as I hit. Pieces of twig flew everywhere. It was like beating a rhino with a piece of straw.

The [Night Mare] finally wrenched its horn free of the trunk under my ineffectual barrage.

For a hopeful second, I thought it was leaving, but no, it was merely backing up for another run, one hoof scraping the ground, horn lowered. It seemed as enraged at the tree as it was at the woman dangling from it. I scrambled closer to the trunk as it galloped. There was a horrible crack, as it impacted the trunk once more.

This time the wood split, the tree groaning as it started to give, not all at once but slowly, agonisingly slowly, tilting over with a series of sickening cracks and pops.

“Ahhhhhhh!”

I fell to the ground in a heap of twigs and leaves.

A high-pitched whistle pierced the air, slicing through the night with shocking clarity. We froze in tableau; the unicorn’s head held high, me crouching under my pile of debris. It hurt my brain. Like brain freeze, but not the nice kind you get after eating ice cream too fast, but the kind that makes your brain want to leak. A thin trickle of blood dripped from my nose.

I clapped both hands over my ears, trying not to scream. Despite the pain, I had to keep my wits or I’d be horse food. I grit my teeth and tried to force myself to focus, to ignore the splitting headache and formulate a plan.

Then suddenly, it eased a little, as if someone had plopped thick earmuffs over my ears.

[You have acquired the Skill: Mental Fortitude lvl 1! Your mind is your own! Fight to keep it that way! This passive ability helps shield you from unwanted mental attacks, psionic threats, and psychic manipulations.]

The screaming in my head retreated, but I was still listening, still entranced, unable to focus fully. My eyes fixed on the unicorn, on the wild staring eyes, on the single glob of gleaming saliva trickling from its jaw, so close to my face.

I fought to prepare my muscles to run, bunching them up, but then – then - the unicorn was running away, galloping wildly through the trees in a flash of silver and white.

It was gone. Gone, gone. As if it had never been, and so was sound that had driven it away. I sagged onto the ground, my nose an inch away from the sod. The smell was comforting.

[Congratulations! You have survived an encounter with an insane Night Mare!  Really pushing your luck there, ey, kid. You have reached Witch level 3. 3 stat points are available.]

I clambered to my feet, scratched and dazed, looking around warily, not entirely sure what had just happened, but I was keen to avoid a repeat experience. Time to leave. Definitely time to leave. Suddenly I was beyond tired.

I put all three points from my level up into Endurance, and the blue lines across my arm eased a little. The skin turned less blue. I think - it was hard to tell under all the layers of grime and dried blood.

The forest was exactly as it was before. Pleasant, dark. More menacing now, although clearly it was me that had changed not the forest. I slapped the side of my face, and my ears popped. Ugh.

I turned and walked over to the splintered remains of the oak’s trunk. Where the unicorn had gored the wood with its horn glittered with soft, silvery residue.

[Unicorn Dust. Quality: excellent. Anti-Poison.]

Sounded useful.

My legs were suddenly rather noodley. I felt a compelling urge to scream? …Sing? Oh dear. I think I might be in shock. It had been a weird night.

“Ninety-nine bottles of dust on the wall, ninety-nine bottles of dust…. Unicorn dust…” I hummed as I scraped it off into a leaf. “Take one down and pass it— uh. What would happen if ah um… bother. I’ve forgotten the words.” It was not the most scientific way to carry unicorn dust, but test tubes and lab equipment were in short supply around here. The idea was rather humorous for some reason, so I had myself a good giggle.

The powder was very pretty. I stared at it, the fragments collected on my leaf, sparkly, sparkly, then, in a whim, daubed some on my eyelids. I could do with a bit of shimmer. Jazz things up around here.

It felt weirdly cooling. I blinked and the glade was suddenly sparkly too. An improvement? Maybe. My legs dragged. I was exhausted. It seemed [Endurance] had a limit after all, and I was rapidly reaching it. Which was too bad, because I wanted to collect more sparkles

I stumbled through the forest, blinking through the glitter. I needed to go… somewhere safe, but there were only more of the damn trees. So many stupid trees. I needed to find somewhere to sleep, if I slept in the open, the unicorn might come back.

Though that might also mean more sparkles. I liked sparkles… not dying through, that was no fun. Lucky something had whistled. The thing… whatever it was that had made that noise might come back and explode my head like a splattery, silvery, messed up, pretty witch-melon. Decidedly not fun. It was around this point that I decided I was definitely in shock.

Think, Rachel.

I dunked my head in the brook. That was better.

Now, to business. Finding a place to sleep without undead unicorns or mind-melting whistles. There had not been anything nearby that resembled a town or a settlement nearby, when we had flown in. The lights I had seen had not shown on the map. I opened my map to make sure I had not missed anything, examining the foggy corners. Nope. Nothing. Dejected, I turned, and trudged back to my gargoyle, who had not moved.

“Did you see the insane unicorn?” I demanded, when I reached him. There was the barest hint of a stony nod, although the eyes slipped, as if unable to help themselves, to the diminished pile of eggs. “Much good you were,” I muttered. Perhaps there is a little guilt in his expression? Perhaps I was imagining it. Regardless, I fed him another egg and then picked up my collection of unappetizing soggy leaves.

A thought occurred to me.

“Was there a reason you brought me to this particular spot?” I asked him. “Or was it just random? I asked for somewhere safe.”

No response, just the slow crunching of eggshell. Crunch, crunch.

Then the slow grate of stone against stone. The gargoyle slowly, dramatically lifted one arm and pointed away through the trees. I peered after his finger, but there was nothing to see but greenery.

“This way?”

He nodded.

Too tired to be excited, I set off in the pointed direction, keeping a sharp look out. I found a new stick, and kept walking, making sure I was headed in a straight line by judicious checking of the map. All I found was more and shrubs and mushrooms. And bugs. Too tired to bother with [Inspect] or [Forage], I kept going.

One tick of mana later, I found my way blocked by a thick tangle of briars.

I stopped and looked at them thoughtfully. These were not just briars. These were the kind of briars briars wanted to be when they grew up. The grandmother of briars, the prehistoric queen of sharp pointy things. Briars with a plethora of thick, vicious thorns. Here and there pale roses sprouted among the masses, doing nothing to make the bushes look less edgy.

[Sleepy Hawthorn Briars, poisonous, venomous.]

There didn’t look like there was a way through, and it was just… quite spectacular rose bushes. Kinda pretty if you were into that sort of thing. Was this what the gargoyle meant? I stifled a yawn as my eyes roamed over the thicket.

What was that? Something was hidden between the greenery. Just a hint of stone, at tree height. I moved slightly, and it disappeared. Was there… was there a building hidden within?

Excitement filled me with fresh energy, and I paced the boundary of briars, seeking a way through that wouldn’t leave my skin a lacerated flesh mess.

In the end, I found a small, barely Rachel-sized hollow at ground height and managed to crawl my way in on my stomach. After a certain amount of calculated squeezing and pushing, I found myself looking up at an oddity.

[Ôstarmânoth.]

Enlightening.

It was a place, certainly. A stone structure hidden in the forest. Not a cottage, more… I tilted my head. A gatehouse? But a gate to where? My eyes travelled up. And up. It was oddly majestic for a small building, although it actually wasn’t that big. What was it? A folly? A shrine? A tomb? A shiver ran down my spine at the thought, as I carefully wormed my way through the thicket.

There was no sign of skeletons, or vampires, or bandits camping, it appeared to be just an overgrown ruinous structure in the middle of a thorny thicket. There were no lights, no movement.

Still lying on my stomach, I mentally clicked open my map, I saw it was now marked with [Ôstarmânoth], and a tiny, crudely rendered X. I closed it and searched for an entrance, and more clues to its function. It was not a house, it did not give off house vibes. The architectural style would be much more in keeping with a much larger structure. Like… a gothic cathedral. The small size made it weirdly cute. Like it was the tip of something far larger protruding from the ground.

The building reached two storeys high, although most of the height was a sort of arched spire, with four twisting columns, supported by four flying buttresses. Half the roof was missing, although it was hard to see it clearly with all the shrubbery and vines and branches in the way. Judging by the state of it, no one had been here in a long, long while.

After several near run-ins with the thorns, I located the doorway, which was heavily barricaded. On the outside. I blinked at it, mind moving sluggishly. Weren’t doors usually locked on the inside?

Like the rest of the building, the door was out of proportion to the structure, giving that same weird out-of-scale grandiosity. Mostly intact, it was heavy and rotting gently in one corner. I heaved the door bar aside. Apart from that, it was not locked, although there was a single rusty keyhole, sans key. I pushed, using all of my nice new muscles, and the door swung open with a soft creak.

A wave of musty air washed over me.

The interior was completely dark, quiet, and hung with shadows. Some uninvited brambles had crept their way in through cracks in the masonry.

Beyond was a room. A closed room – some sort of entrance way. Stale, unexciting. Safe?

I stepped forwards, hefting my trusty branch. Stone floor, a few pieces of old wood strewn about. Furniture, or the remains of furniture. A pile of rags in one corner. An old bird’s nest. Beyond was another heavy door which I pushed open likewise. A mouse or some other small creature dashed away chittering and vanished. Here was a larger chamber, this one partially open to the night where the roof had fallen in. The interior was just as ornate as the outside. Nothing of obvious interest, just a big mess of stone and wood.

Whatever it was, it was clear no one had been here for a long time. But it had four walls, and a door. So I closed myself in, barricading the door from the inside this time, and lay down to rest.

Just for a moment.
