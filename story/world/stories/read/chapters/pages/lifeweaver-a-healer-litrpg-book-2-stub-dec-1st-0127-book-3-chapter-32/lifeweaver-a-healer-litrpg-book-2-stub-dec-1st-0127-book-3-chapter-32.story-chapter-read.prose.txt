It didn't take long for them to reach Peter's office. There was something about emerging onto a floor where other people worked, and where his office wasn't some large chamber of opulence, like every time Luke met with Alan. The circumstances inspired at least a little confidence in not only the man himself, but also in the new direction ISG was taking.

"This is me," Peter said, gesturing for Luke and Lemira to sit in a pair of orange chairs in metal and plastic. They stood opposite a small desk and looked as if they'd been plucked from an Andy Warhol-inspired yard sale.

Luke sat down. "So you're in charge now?"

"No. It doesn't work like that anymore."

"Then who's the guildmaster?"

"That position is, if you strip away its glamour, an administrative one, so we assigned it to an administrator. All decision-making power is in the hands of the board."

Luke was about to ask, but Peter preceded him. "Yes, I am a board member."

"Sounds corporate."

"That is the idea. Mr. Schmmidt's core principle of running a guild like a corporation was not a bad one. In truth, it was genius. We will see a lot more of that in the future, I'm sure."

Luke raised an eyebrow at that answer.

"The execution of his vision was a little on the extreme side."

"Evil," Luke corrected.

Lemira looked a little confused but didn't ask. Luke would have to fill her in later.

"You mentioned news?" he asked.

"Yes, of course," Peter said, grabbing a manila folder off his desk and handing it over. "The authorities have decided to pursue legal action against you despite our best efforts. I'm afraid you're a wanted criminal."

Luke took the folder and looked inside, sighing. It was some sort of document detailing what they accused him of. Murder was at the top of the list. Great, just great. "I notice there aren't any cops around here."

"The New York Police Department has a lot on its plate at the moment, as I'm sure you understand. Same with the FBI. Mr. Schmidt was not a family man, so you won't have to expect revenge for patricide, at least."

"Phew," Luke said.

Lemira leaned forward and glanced down at the paper. "You stand accused of the death of this 'Mr. Schmidt'?"

"I do," Luke said. "I killed him. But it was for good reason."

She didn't seem too shaken up about that piece of news. "I see."

"What about the guild? You looking for revenge, Peter?" Luke asked.

Peter handed over another piece of paper, one he'd seen before. A guild application. "We want you to join Integrated Solutions Group again."

"Not happening," Luke said, refusing to take the offered paper.

Peter gave a quick grin. "Worth a shot."

"Why did Alan want me here?"

"There is no simple answer to that difficult question. I'm afraid we don't know. Not yet."

"Really?"

"Well, yes and no. We know why, but not the core of it. Let me explain. I'm sure you've noticed the state of this city?"

"Tons of portals, monsters in the streets, people hiding or fleeing?"

"Yes," Peter said.

"That's the reason?"

Peter nodded while giving the impression he wasn't quite sure. "It's getting worse. According to our statistics, portals are growing more frequent by the day, almost meaning more monster breaks and more difficult challenges for Integrated and non-Integrated alike. And New York is not alone in this."

"Where else?"

"A lot of major cities throughout the world. Los Angeles, Houston, Berlin, London, Stockholm, Moscow, Beijing, to name a few. Even New Brussels, despite its low population. We think Integrated concentration might have something to do with that last one."

"Major cities, but not Chicago?"

"We're seeing an uptick there too, but not at the level of many other cities."

"Why?" Luke asked.

Lemira whispered a single word, but Luke didn't quite catch it. "Sorry?"

"Event," she said.

"Event?" Luke asked.

Peter leaned forward on his desk, staring at Lemira with an intense gaze. "You know something?"

"A period of intensifying upheaval, followed by a system event."

"You make that sound ominous," Luke said, giving Peter a side glance before looking back at Lemira. "So you've heard of this before?"

"Yes."

Unsure if they should discuss this in front of someone he wasn't sure he trusted just yet, Luke decided not to question her further before they got out of there.

Peter saw through it right away but didn't comment or ask questions of his own. Instead, he shared more of the guild's knowledge. "New York is the epicenter of this. If an event is to take place, it will do so here. Or Beijing. Perhaps both."

"The increase is growing faster here?"

"That's right," Peter said. "Figures from Asia are confirmed only through satellite, so they are ballpark only."

Luke grunted. "Huh."

"This is also the reason we at ISG have decided to keep as many Integrated here as we are able. To capitalize on what is happening. I thought it best to inform you in the spirit of cooperation and transparency."

"Thank you," Luke said. "Do you have any idea if it's close to hitting its peak?"

"So far, it has been building at a linear rate. We can't be sure when it'll happen."

"Okay. Thank you for sharing these things with me. Is there anything else?"

"Well," Peter said, leaning back in his chair. "I was hoping you would want to continue the arrangement that you and Mr. Schmidt worked out together."

"The healing?"

"Yes, the healing. Our funding has not diminished with the passing of our former guildmaster. Quite the opposite. We have much to offer."

"What about Dot?"

Peter looked a little apprehensive at the mention of the guild's own Lifeweaver. "She is a wonderful young woman and a capable healer, but her low level and slow progress means Dr. Marlene cannot take on the more advanced cases. Most of the requests we get from potential partners. stakeholders, governments, and so on are edge cases where we have little room for error."

"You should tell Dot you need her to level up, but I'll do it."

"Really?" Peter said, unable to stop himself. "I'll be honest, I didn't expect that."

Luke held up a finger. "I'll help you out once, that's it, and I will need something other than money."

The joy in Peter's expression bled away a little, but he still looked optimistic. "We have a candidate selected. He is a high-level Integrated. One of our top performers."

"Martin?" Luke asked.

"Who?"

"Nevermind. What's wrong with him?"

"Dungeon run gone wrong. A monster struck him, and our healers haven't been able to make much progress in his condition."

"I'll take a look," Luke said. "About the cost. I'm not going to lie, it'll hurt your wallet."

"Like I said, I'm sure we can come to an understanding. Can you see him now?"

Luke nodded, grinned, and explained what he wanted in exchange for his heal.

Following the somewhat pale-looking Peter down a stairwell, Lemira leaned over to whisper in Luke's ear. "You are sought after in matters of healing to this extent?"

"Usually, it's to deal with non-system-related illnesses. My class. Lifeweaver. can fix those. Other healers can't."

She nodded, understanding. "I see. That would be sought after in my system as well."

Luke shushed her. It wouldn't do to give away her secret in a place like ISG headquarters. "Let's talk more about it later."

They made it down a couple of floors, then exited the staircase into a corridor, which they followed until it felt like they should've hit the end of the building. There, Peter brought them into an unassuming elevator, pressed his thumb to a fingerprint reader, entered a long string of digits on a pad, then even leaned forward to scan his eye.

"That's a lot of security," Luke noted.

Peter just shrugged. "We have a lot of projects that we aren't ready to share with the world just yet. Not as many here as in Chicago, but some."

"Like an Integrated in need of healing?"

"You'll see," Peter said. "It's more about what's keeping him alive."

The elevator ride down was long. Alan had sure liked his lairs. Once they were far underground, and they emerged into a place that resembled The Gauntlet quite a bit, Peter brought them away from that area and down another corridor, and then another, until Luke's head was spinning.

"You're not going to try to kidnap us, are you?" Luke asked, the joke obvious in his voice. They knew what would happen if they tried.

"Almost there," Peter said.

Luke stopped dead in his tracks when he sensed a presence, one he should've noticed the moment they stepped out of the elevator, if not sooner.

"She's here," he said.

Lemira leaned forward a little to get a good look at Luke's face, frowning. "Who?"

"A girl I know."

Peter stopped and turned around. "I didn't know she would be here, nor that it would be a problem."

"Nevermind. It's fine," Luke said, steeling himself as Peter opened another door and ushered them through.

The inside looked a lot like a sterile operating room, because, he realized, it was one. Surgical equipment, monitoring machines connected to the patient beeping away, men and women in gowns, face-masks, and even hair coverings. The non-medical people stood out. Healers, Luke figured, seeing as Dot was with them.

She opened her eyes when Luke entered, but didn't lose control of the thread of mana connecting her to the patient. The healers surrounded the table in the center of the room where the patient lay unconscious, each of them with a hand on a small outcropping covered in small script and symbols, spellweaves hovering over.

"Hey," Luke said, looking at Dot.

"Hi, Luke." She smiled back at him, but it was a sad little smile that did little to alleviate the awkwardness of the situation.

Lemira's eyes were fixed on the patient, which Luke could understand. It was something, alright.

"How is that man still alive?" Lemira asked, shaking her head in disbelief.

It was a fair question, seeing as most of his head was gone. Caved in might have been a more apt description. Stuck through by what looked like a massive claw would also have sufficed. The poor guy's entire face was gone.

"The table funnels healing mana from the healers and enhances it before distributing healing to the patient," Peter said. "It's what's keeping him alive at the moment."

"Infection is almost a guarantee," one of the medical staff, a tall woman with gray hair, said. "Even with all these... interventions, Mr. Shields is not long for this world."

"Well, he has a massive claw through his head. I'd say he's doing well under the circumstances," Luke said, stepping up to the table. "I'll take a look."

Peter followed to stand right next to Luke, while Lemira settled herself on a chair in the corner, drawing a look from Dot.

"Who's she?" she asked Luke.

Luke looked over to her and got a smile from the half-demon. "A friend."

"Oh," Dot said, her face expressionless. "There's nothing I can do to help Jeff. This is too big."

"You need to level up, Dot. With Weaver's Renewal, there's a chance."

"Luke, his head is almost fully gone."

He shrugged. "Won't hurt to try."

Peter broke into their hushed conversation. "I didn't expect to see you here, Dr. Marlene."

Dot looked up at the taller man, her boss. "Fayez sent out a guild-wide request for healers."

"Fair enough," Peter said. "But you can disregard those in the future, Dr. Marlene. If we have a need for your specific talents, we will reach out to you. Also, you'll be pleased to hear I've received reports of many ill and injured waiting for you back in Chicago. You can retire for the day and prepare for travel, if you wish."

Dot shook her head. "I'd rather stay and see this. I might need to do something like it in the future."

"I understand."

Extending a thread of mana to Jeff, Luke found rather narrow and weak mana channels and a small meta-heart, which told him he was dealing with a fighter of some sort, rather than a spellcaster. Healing mana rose into his body from below, entering Jeff's body before surging to his head. And what a mess his head was. A high Vitality had saved him. That, and a heap of healers nearby. It must've been a real effort to get him out of whatever dungeon he'd been hurt in and all the way back to ISG headquarters.

There was no activity at all in the still-surviving mana channels in Jeff's head. No regular brain activity either. Grim. Dot kept a thread of mana wrapped around the patient's heart.

"Did his heart stop?" he asked.

She shook her head. "No, but I'm afraid it might."

"Oh, it will. Be ready to squeeze."

Turning his attention from the patient's body, he searched for and found Peter standing against the wall by the door.

"We'll need to pull that out of him," Luke said. "Got any strong Integrated nearby?"
