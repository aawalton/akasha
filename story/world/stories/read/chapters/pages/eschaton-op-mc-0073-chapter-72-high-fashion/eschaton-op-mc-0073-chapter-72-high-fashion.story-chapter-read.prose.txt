“Morgan!” I hiss once we get back to the compound. “What are you thinking?! A party!? Me!?”

“Oh, calm down you big baby. It’s a party, not a warzone. You’ll be fine,” she says, rolling her eyes. She lounges in the air, laying back on nothing, hands behind her head. She kicks one leg idly.

“But a party? In the middle of an oncoming famine? This seems… wrong.”

“And given that the solution to said famine is expensive research, this is just another part of the solution,” Morgan says with a shrug. “It seems counter-intuitive, I know, but I promise this is an important event and not just an excuse to have a good time.”

“I meant I could just go kill the monsters myself.”

“Uh huh, yeah, sure. For how long can you do that? And don’t say as long as it takes, because that’s not true. You’re overstretched as it is. You can’t afford to take on even more responsibilities.”

“So what’s the plan with the gala, then?”

“The point is to connect the Academy with like minded people. Either wealthy private donors who want to do something good with their money—or get tax write-offs, take your pick—or maybe independent Empowered who just need to be shown a way to help.”

“Doctor Lam said they don’t have the budget to compete with big corporations.”

“True, but how many teams would like to help but don’t know how? Do you even know how Empowered sell things they recover from Fractures? What if they don’t even know that the Academy is doing research, or needs research materials? Not everyone has instant access to all human knowledge like you do.”

“Oh. I guess that makes sense? But how do we help with that? I’m sure they could host the party on their own.”

“Mach, you big lug, you’re great, but you could use a little work on the social skills, huh?” She sighs melodramatically.

“Look, I was a single kid who went to college for engineering! I didn’t exactly have a lot of ‘socializing’ going on.”

“Well, have no fear, Morgan is here!” She says cheerfully. “Socializing is one of my many skills! So, first off: why us? The answer is simple. We bring legitimacy and a draw.”

“How could the California Academy of Sciences not be ‘legitimate’?”

“Oh, they are, for the crusty upper class looking for tax write-offs,” she says, waving a hand dismissively. “For independent Empowered teams, who at this point are used to action and combat, maybe not so much. They might see it as a museum they went to as a kid, not a lab. They definitely don’t see it as a ‘thing’ Empowered do. If we attend, and then make sure it’s known to teams that we’re attending, then they’ll take the Academy seriously as a part of the growing industry around Empowered. Or they’ll show up with the hope to talk to us. Either way works, honestly.”

I internally shudder, my form not really suited to such movements.

“Morgan. I’m not going to be good at this. What if I say the wrong thing?”

“Mach. You’re a big stoic robot. Just don’t say anything if you don’t really want to. Most people are just going to assume you’re enigmatic, deliberative, and/or quiet. You don’t have the same body language cues normal squishy people do. If you stand there, stock still, maybe with a head tilt or two, they’ll just think you’re not a talkative guy.”

“Surely they’ll think that’s weird.”

She laughs. “Mach, you’re powerful enough that you’re not weird, you’re eccentric. That’ll let you get away with being a little different when it comes to galas or social events.”

“I guess. I can’t say I’m happy about this.”

“Well, too bad. With a single party you could do more work than a week of blitzing through Fractures. You can’t carry this world on your shoulders, like you keep saying. The biggest thing you can do is to show everyone else how it’s done. This is one way.”

“So the plan is to get Empowered to come to the gala hoping to talk to us, then guide them towards helping the Academy?”

“Exactly!” Morgan says cheerfully. “A good few hours of socializing, schmoozing, and general diplomacy and the Academy will be drowning in resources.”

“This feels manipulative.”

“Yes, it is,” Morgan says, rolling her eyes. “That’s not a bad thing. I know that word has a bad connotation, but that doesn’t mean it’s a bad thing. They need a reason to show up, we are that reason, and once they show up, we can convince them to do something else.”

“Fine,” I say with a sigh. “I make no promises about my own performance.”

She pats me on the arm. “That’s okay. Let me do the talking! I’m good at that.”

“Clearly,” I say dryly, and she laughs.

“Look, my family taught me all about this stuff when I was the designated heir to the corporate throne. I may not have liked most of the wheeling and dealing that came with that, but parties? I love parties.”

“So when are we doing this?”

“Probably a few days, Benny’s gonna need some time to get it all set up and send out the invitations. This is going to be pretty last minute, a lot of these things are planned out months in advance.”

“That gives me some time to clear a few Fractures,” I say a little more energetically.

“Haha, no. We’ve got to get you some fashion! I did a good job on the cloak, but there’s still more to do, you know.”

“I have no idea how to dress for a formal occasion,” I tell her sadly. “I was a band-tee kinda guy.”

“I am not surprised.”

Morgan apparently has a personal tailor, and portaled her in just after our conversation. She circles around me, muttering to herself.

“Normal clothing won’t work, no, no. Makes it seem like there’s something to hide. That’d be awkward. Cape? No, no, too tacky. Cloak? Yes, but this won’t work. Too similar. Hides too much. Hmm.”

“The cloak makes it so he can walk indoors,” Morgan points out. “I don’t have time to make another that can do that. Modification of it isn’t possible either, you’ll break the enchantments.”

“Ah, Ms. Buchanan you press me so. Cloak will have to work, hm. Perhaps a formal sash? Hmm. Machina. Show your sword.”

“Uh, alright?” I summon my sword, the entire thing nearly three feet longer than I am tall. It seems to hum, though not audibly. Its presence draws the attention of those around, and it has weight. There’s still so much I have yet to learn about myself.

“Too big! Much too big. Make it smaller.”

“I don’t think I can,” I tell her.

“Why not? You made it.”

“It just doesn’t work like that.”

“Bah, fine, fine. Prop sword won’t work, fakes easy to spot. Hmm. No weapon then. Just sword arm uncovered? No, hm. Would get tangled if you drew a sword. Yes, we keep scabbard side uncovered, much better idea. Empty scabbard? No, would be silly. Never used a scabbard before. Formal sash? A scarf would be silly. Or would it? Hmm…”

The tailor, Coraline Arnault, trails off while still circling like a hungry shark. She snaps and points at a ladder, then at one of her several assistants. They scramble to haul it into place, and she steps up. Despite the added height of the short ladder, she still is barely eye level with me.

“Hmm, yes, scarf. That would work. Sashes are tacky,” She says, clicking her tongue. “Will also wear the cloak differently. You cover too much!”

Normally the cloak covers most of my body when I’m standing still, like I am now. It softens my form and hopefully makes me less imposing. I’ve noticed from time to time how nervous some people get when I get close.

“Uh, alright?”

“Yes, leave left side, scabbard side, uncovered. Cloak should come to here,” she says, pointing about half way over my arm. “And cloak on sword side should come to here. Just short of where your sternum would be.”

With a little bit of intent, the cloak responds, and it shifts into the positions Mrs. Arnault indicated.

“Ahhhh, yes, much better. Scarf across the neck, covers left shoulder. Should be a contrast color. Blue would work. Matches your eyes. Well, you know what I mean.”

“Why are you moving the cloak around? I thought maybe I’d be too imposing. I don’t want to loom.”

She snorts.

“Imposing? Looming? A cloak won’t change that, Machina. You are tall, wide. Big. Cloak or not. Besides, if you hide yourself, that signals you are ashamed. Are you ashamed of what you are?”

“No, of course not.”

“Then do not hide.”

She then takes a measuring tape from one of her staff, and starts rattling off a bunch of numbers.

“Good, good. How long do I have to make this?”

“Three days,” Morgan says sheepishly. “It was set up very last minute.”

“Three days!? Three days, good grace, Ms. Buchanan. You try me dearly. Bah, fine, fine. This is why you pay me.”

“Isn’t the cloak I have iconic? Or at least, becoming that way,” I mention. “Why do we need to add extra pieces?”

She waves her arms dismissively, which seems to be her default response to what I have to say.

“Iconic? Bah! Maybe for soldiers and the average person, but not for those you will be talking to that night. Iconic, he says, hah!”

“Well, what’s wrong with it? I rather like it.”

She huffs, but doesn’t respond to me.

“What about myself, Mrs. Arnault?” Morgan says.

“We know who you are, Ms. Buchanan. It would be silly to think magic powers changed that. We already made something for you. Now, we must work quickly. Much to do.”

The days pass quickly, and the scarf is finished the day of the gala, mere hours before we’re slated to arrive. I don’t think Mrs. Arnault or her staff have slept. She gives a jaw-cracking yawn.

“Hmm. Yes. Good enough. Not our best work, but for three days? Very good.”

“I’m surprised a scarf took so long to make,” I tell her. She glares.

“Tsk. You have little understanding of the nature of fashion and tailoring. To make something like this? Difficult. Time consuming. Even for someone as good as myself.”

The scarf is a bright blue, the same color as the fire in my helmet. It matches with the inner lining of the cloak that Morgan made for me. Onyx black thread is embroidered on it in complex patterns that remind me of armor plates. It’s made of the same material as the cloak as well.

“The material was difficult to work with. Needed Ms. Buchanan’s help in making some needles capable of working with it.”

She steps up a ladder and wraps it around my neck and left shoulder. She spends several minutes adjusting. Even to my perception, the changes seem miniscule.

“Ah, there we go. Very good, hm. Sometimes I even surprise myself.”

“This is easily the nicest thing I’ve ever worn,” I tell her honestly. Mrs. Arnault sniffs haughtily.

“Of course it is. You must have worn dress shirts at most. Maybe even a rented tuxedo, bleh.”

“I wore a nice suit to my parents’ funeral.” Morgan winces, but Mrs. Arnault merely waves a hand, undeterred.

“Bah, nice is relative. Off the shelf but tailored is the bare minimum. Well, now you have good clothing. Good luck at the gala. I am going to bed. Ms. Buchanan, if you could send me back?”

Morgan quickly paints a portal to New York, and Mrs. Arnault and her support staff exhaustedly trudge through it with their equipment.

“Right. Now to plan for the gala itself. A bit last minute, but it’s been a busy few days. The gala starts at 7:00, so we should get there at 8:30 or so.”

“Uh, Morgan, that’s like an hour and a half late.”

“Yes, that’s the point, we’re the main event, the guests of honor, the big cheeses. We’re supposed to be late. Ever heard of fashionably late? Well, here you go.”

“Still, that seems a bit rude.”

“Fine, fine. Does 8:00 work for you?”

“Alright,” I grumble. “fine, I guess. I don’t like being late to things.”

“Oh, you’ll be saying a few words. Here, memorize this. I’m sure it’ll be easy for you.”

“WHAT!?”
