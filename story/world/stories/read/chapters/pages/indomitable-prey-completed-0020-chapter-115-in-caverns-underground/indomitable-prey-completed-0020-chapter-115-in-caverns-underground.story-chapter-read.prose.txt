I was wrenched from my thoughts by the duke asking how it was I came to be here. I'd explained, in a rough outline at least, my journey; how I'd been caught by the Crimson Lions in Colchet, how I'd journeyed with Francis, Sven, Rank, and Shavkat into the Dragon-Spines, how we'd been set upon in a barbarian raid—the screams in the night, the blood splashing everywhere, the terror.

I'd done my best to channel the experiences of the townsfolk in the Riverlands that I’d witnessed. I'd tried to imagine how terrifying it must be for someone with very little power to stand up for themselves. It wasn’t a hard thing to do while in the presence of the duke, honestly.

He felt like Vera did during our spars; all hard edges that would cut me if I got too close. Where she made the effort to turn off whatever threat she put out into the world most of the time, I picked up no such courtesy from the duke and felt myself constantly walking a knife-edge around him. A prickling reminder that death lurked nearby.

He'd asked at one point how I'd got to Colchet to begin with, but luckily, he was easily distracted. I knew there would be questions, but I hoped that I could avoid them for now. I only needed to distract them for a day or two, after all, and they clearly had bigger things on their minds currently.

I soon understood why when we arrived at our destination. I got the sense we were no more than four or five miles from the castle but deep underground, as all my worst adventures tended to be.

The earthen tunnel had given way to what was clearly a more reinforced structure. Thick wooden slats bracketed the sides and roof, and mage lights hung at regular intervals to light the way. It then ended abruptly, and without any time to prepare, I found myself at the top of a quarry of some sort. An enormous cavern opened out before me, with the ground dropping precipitously away but the roof continuing on to loom above the darkness below.

This open space must have been thousands of feet wide and long at the least, reminding me eerily of the tunnel I’d encountered Alvorak the Broken within, and the Subakir that wore her guise so poorly. That brought no comfort though, feeling more like a dark omen than a shining portent of good things to come.

The cavern extended into a dark void so that I had no real idea of its true scale, but my less earthly senses told me it was large beyond imagining. As I looked down, I saw what appeared to be slabs of symmetrical rectangular rock, clearly cut by humans with intention. They were stacked, one on top of the other, reaching towards us such that I felt I was standing at the top of a pyramid of some sort.

Perhaps quarry was a better term. Wooden scaffolding descended from my position at the roof of the cavern, all the way down to the base some thousand feet below. The duke wasted no time in descending, Estan following after him, seemingly unconcerned with the idea of me running. To be fair, I doubted I would get far before he caught up even were I to break the amulet’s effects and use my full speed.

I followed them, criss-crossing my way down the wooden planks scaling the side of the pyramid structure, and it was only once I was close enough to the rock to touch that I realised it was obsidian. Black glass, formed from volcanic struggle deep in the heart of the earth, spilling to the surface only after millennia of battle. I wasn’t even aware that it could be formed in such large shapes before, but I suppose that was likely the least surprising thing about these ruins.

I reached out with my stone-sense, now far stronger and more fluid with Shatter Point than Faultline had ever been, but I came to an abrupt stop only an inch or so into the glass slabs. Something impenetrable blocked me, and it wasn’t the type of force that could be pushed against. There was simply no way to delve deeper beneath whatever shroud shielded the structure from my view.

“Don’t bother,” the duke called over his shoulder from below, not bothering to look over.

I knew he could feel mana to some degree—we all could, and as a powerful 3rd tier his sense was likely much keener that my own—but it was still surprising for him to pick up such minute shifts when in such a thoroughly magical place as this. The air was saturated, the ambient mana high enough to affect how often I could use my skills.

It wasn’t just a magnificent structure of volcanic glass within a massive underground cavern, either. There was something else here, too. I didn't know how I knew, but there was a certainty deep within my bones that something lurked below me, like a predator lying in wait.

There is a feeling in deep water; an understanding that you are out of place and out of your depth, that creatures could be lurking beneath, that their jaws are racing towards your feet even as you float unaware above them. It is present at night in the forests, too, and all humans have felt such fear before. The desire to look over your shoulder takes hold, and once you given in—once you chance a look back to make sure nothing is there—it takes you. Whether or not you see anything, the fear takes hold because you have already acted on its whims.

That was the feeling I had in that moment; that I needed to leave. That there was something below waiting for me, something hungry, and I needed to get out. I shivered in fear and then felt a snarling heat within my soul in response.

“You feel it, boy?” The duke asked.

His words helped ground me, and I was reminded that the true danger here was not from the ruin. I tried desperately to contain the urge to let my pathbound skill roar its defiance at whatever aura was trying to convince me to leave. The first of many traps and defensive mechanisms that this ruin possessed, no doubt.

“The Ashkanians were a remarkably advanced civilization, and this is one of their legacies. Stable aura manipulation by inanimate objects that persists long after such magic should normally fade,” he explained casually. “Impressive, isn’t it?”

I jerked a nod, still fighting my own instincts. As well as the sinking feeling of dread from the ruin, I struggled against my own body. I felt my heart starting to pound within my chest, beating faster, stronger, with an urgency as if it could protect me from whatever this feeling was.

I fought back desperately, willing my body not to turn and run, and also equally willing my soul not to reach for the mana that even now swirled within my core. Activating End Of The Hunt would be a mistake, for while it was not necessarily the most powerful skill, the defiant nature of it would definitely clash with the image I was trying to put forward.

I then felt a heavy blanket settle around me, spiritual rather than real. Nevertheless, it weighed me down slowly, a subtle pressure around my shoulders demanding subservience but at the same time promising that so long as I was to serve, I would be protected.

The duke wandered over, seeming to observe me for a time. “This vault is warning us away,” he remarked, as if out for a stroll in a field beneath a pleasant sky rather than miles underground in an ancient and decidedly ominous ruin. “Well, God-Touched; now is the time for you to prove your utility. Come.”

I followed him as he strode straight-backed into the darkness. “We believe we have isolated the mural that holds the key. If you can truly comprehend ancient Ashkanian, you should have no issue deciphering the instructions and opening the way to the vault.”

I nodded, though I didn’t make any promises. A more interesting question occurred to me that I couldn’t help but ask first. “Forgive my ignorance, but is Ashkanian not a commonly spoken language in some academic circles?”

He answered as we walked further into the cavern. I saw the evidence of excavation all around. Pickaxes and slabs of obsidian moved about haphazardly. Tools used for more careful and precise work; small handheld drills, delicate brushes and chisels, mage-lights and candles placed at areas of particular interest.

“Ashkanian was not a single language, though they made great strides in political and cultural centralisation. I am given to understand that there are many dialects and sub-languages, especially so for the written word, within Ashkanian history. Unsurprising, given their longevity—it would be strange for two cultures separated by nearly a thousand years to be too similar, no matter their contiguity… or so I am led to believe by what little I’ve read on the subject.”

I saw Estan role his eyes as his father droned on, though I couldn’t see why—it was a fascinating topic, and even if one wasn’t inclined towards history, when walking through such a place as this, it would be hard to remain aloof unless one was profoundly worldly, or completely ignorant. I suspected I knew which camp Estan fell into.

“Regardless, most who do speak the ancient tongue speak High Ashkanian, supposedly from the 2nd Era at the height of their civilisation. This vault is not High Ashkanian.”

We came to a little cluttering of lights near a desk, filled with scrawling diagrams and images that reminded me somewhat of some of the scrolls I had seen Nathlan peruse on occasion. I did not have the theoretical and magical knowledge to understand anything hidden within them, but it was clear that this was a site of scholarly inquest.

“Here,” Duke Ryonic said imperiously, gesturing to a single massive slab, roughly six feet wide and half as high. “We believe this holds the details. Can you read it?”

“I’ll need some light,” I said, as I gazed at the strange patterns woven within the obsidian block. They were like charcoal lines on a black surface—just barely visible if you strained, but at a quick glance appeared to be nothing more than a trick of the light.

“I have already sent word to Varice. She will join us momentarily,” was the only reply I received, and so I spent my time squinting in the dim light, a blank book in my lap in which I tried my best to note down the swirling patterns.

Without seeing the whole thing, I couldn’t decipher it, but I could likely make a passable drawing of it over time and then read from that. Perhaps. The duke loomed behind me, a stalwart weight holding back the darkness of the ruin, but also a blade poised above my neck at the same time. Suffice it to say, it wasn’t the most pleasant working environment.

Varice arrived soon though, and that is where my real troubles began. She was an unexceptional woman in many ways—average height, somewhat frumpy build and features that wouldn’t look out of place in a half-silver mine or serving drinks at an inn. Her eyes were somewhat dramatic though, containing a faint hint of purple light. I found myself unable to trust anyone that looked too normal in such an exceptional place.

She wasted no time with introductions, instead waving a hand before the obsidian stone, which lit as if from within with a deep purple. The light bubbled up from the centre until it shone through the stone, highlighting the swooping patterns carved within the glass itself.

I took a step back, my eyebrows climbing my forehead, but I began to read aloud before the duke even turned toward me. The words were violent, filled with glottal stops and choking sounds rather than the lilting, musical language I had expected from an ancient, advanced civilisation.

At least, that is what the faces of Varice, Duke Ryonic, and Estan seemed to say when I turned to them. We stared at one another for a few moments before Estan muttered in exasperation.

“Well? What does that mean?” he asked.

I blinked a few times, before realising that I had been speaking Ashkanian rather than Escriban—the most common language in the Sunsets. The block I’d read aloud described a surprisingly simple mechanism for disabling the aura that permeated this primeval ruin. It was actually a shockingly simple design, and I suspected that it was only a defence mechanism in that it may warn off wild creatures and perhaps other sapients that were not Ashkanian from attempting to access the vault.

Any who could read Ashkanian would be fine, since the block described in simple terms how to deactivate the aura. In such simple terms, in fact, that I suspected accessing the rest of the ruin and disabling any traps would be a trivial thing to do. Too easy, too quick.

I thought fast.

“Oh. Yeah, so it roughly translates to ‘find the repeating pattern of 3’. Not entirely sure what that means, but I would guess we have to look around and find—”

“Lies!”

The cawing of the phantasmal raven was like a whip cracking through the air, making me and Estan flinch. Varice, on whose shoulder the purple raven of dripping shadows was resting, seemed no worse for wear despite the echoing noise emerging from next to her ear, and reached up to scratch the creature. It nuzzled its head into her hand and made a little crooning hum followed by some very unbirdlike clicking.

The duke hadn’t taken his eyes off me and took a slow step forward. He seemed to glide across the ground, wooden planks covered in a carpet of black dust from the excavations making no groaning noise as he stepped, despite their constant flexing prior. It was as if he floated, more phantom than human, and his pale, ghostly eyes drew me in. The knife above my neck, that ever-present aura of threat he emanated, sharpened.

“Now boy,” he said, and suddenly he was directly in front of me, looking down with those merciless eyes. “Why would you do a thing like that?”

I backed away a step, but the duke kept pace, filling my vision and constricting what space I might try to find.

“Sir,” I protested as the duke continued to walk me down. “I don’t know what’s going on, but that’s what it says, I swear!”

I could see that my words were having less than no effect, and my strategy of delay would be useless with a human—or avian, as it were—lie detector shadowing me. More important was to keep the trust of the duke, or at least not strain it to breaking, for a while longer. It was looking increasingly like I would have to grant access to the duke to this vault in the end, but that wasn’t so bad. He could empower himself a little, maybe find an artifact of note, but without weeks to absorb the insights and power from this place, he’d not pose any special challenge to Jorge and Vera, when they eventually broke in.

It might even help them, to have the duke distracted in the ruins down here. Give them a few more days to plan and execute things. They might need it, given how the plan had already changed with the castle’s military readiness and my companions’ orders not to leave.

So, keep them onside and open the vault. I again let my racing mind apply itself to the task. Last time I tried thinking fast I got myself caught in this lie, so perhaps it was a stupid idea to try again, but I was in it now, with a merciless 3rd tier bearing down on me. Time to pile lies atop lies until that fucking raven couldn’t keep up.

“By three they come. By three thy way opens. Nine by nine by nine as the sun rises, and the pale moon quests forth on a shadowed night,” I began to quote. Nonsense, of course, but perhaps they didn’t know that. “The waning of our empire is at hand, but a seed planted in darkness can grow to offer shade during daybreak. Let Ashkania’s gentle light bless you traveller, and should you be worthy, match the—”

The duke slowed, sending a questioning look Varice’s way. She looked to her raven, which cocked its head and locked a beady eye on me.

“Lies!” it croaked once more, and the duke turned with true anger on his face now.

“That was a poor choice, young man,” he said, but the pleasant words sent a shiver of dread down my spine.

“No, listen!” I pleaded desperately, finding the acting easy and natural given the circumstances. “It’s meant to be a lie!”

“Explain,” the duke commanded, though he did at least stop his ominous approach.

“It’s a riddle. I can’t get all the context—that’s not how my title works—but there’s a riddle in the seal. It’s not about finding a pattern; it’s about cutting through the lies! Look, just let me…”

I trailed off, taking a breath and stepping past the duke, pushing him aside with my shoulder and hurrying to the stone slab we were originally standing next to. I’d backed away a good ten feet in my retreat, and everyone had turned to face me. The duke, thankfully, seemed to buy the act and moved aside at the last moment. I’m not sure if I’d have kept my nerve had he stopped me.

Those icy blue grey eyes… Something about them unsettled me. They reminded me of Sadrianna’s description of the Deep Lakes; sapphire holes in the earth itself, with dark titans swimming within. Scale, perspective, power. That is how the duke felt to my senses, and not just because of his aura. I’d met a few soldiers and guard stronger than I since entering castle Ryonic—not many, granted, though enough to be a problem—but they didn’t fill me with a sense of threat in the way the duke did.

He was lethal, poised to kill at a moment’s notice, and bore the air of a man long familiar with violence. Not sadistic like Shavkat had been, but just well enough acquainted with death that it held little interest to him any longer. The moment I was useless I’d be dead, and that was that.

I made it to the obsidian block with my thoughts swirling, and spent a moment to focus on the patterns of ghostly light emanating from within. I interpreted the words of the ancient civilisation easily and was relieved when I once more realised how simple the process was. A subtle application of mana, channelled through specific parts of the block in sequence.

“I need to channel mana—don’t kill me for this,” I said, and then followed the instructions.

It was anticlimactic. No flash of light, no grinding of stone as ancient wards unlocked. Just an immediate cessation of that ominous aura. The darkness below us became just plain darkness. An absence of light, nothing more. I still trembled slightly beneath the lethal aura of the duke pressing down on my back, but that was the only threat in this ruin now.

Crisis averted. For now.

I looked up, sighing in relief. The duke raised an eyebrow and looked to Varice, who just shrugged.

“Seems to have worked,” she said noncommittally.

“See?” I said with faux cheer. “Show me the entrance, and I’ll get you in.”

The duke turned, heading for the stairs behind us. “We shall see.”

We followed after in a procession, and I found myself now trapped between the duke and Varice. Seems they weren’t content to let me tag along at the back any longer, their trust in me diminished. There were a few seconds of silence before the duke spoke again, his voice hard as a frozen tundra.

“Play games with me again and I shall push you from this ledge and let you fall to your death.”
