“I think we should split up. That way, we can cover ground faster,” Kouki said as his eyes locked onto the countdown. Less than fifteen hours remained.

“No. That would be dangerous. What if we got surrounded?” Andrew said. Unlike James or Kouki, Andrew was not confident adventuring solo. Spears were excellent weapons. They had good reach and could even be thrown, but once one got tangled in an opponent’s corpse, you were done for. That meant spear users could only kill one monster at a time. In tight corridors like this, it would be hard to maneuver his spear in battle.

“This is a tight corridor. How could—ah, you’re right. My apologies.” Kouki was about to deny Andrew’s claim, but he remembered monsters could literally spawn from the walls here.

James continued walking, his sword still in his hand. Two hours later, they found another chest. James tapped it with an ice spear. The chest sprang open, spitting acid toward his face. Luckily, he was a good five meters away and had ample time to form an ice shield.

Inside the chest was another orb, along with six hundred coins as a prize. James split the reward and moved on.

“Two down, one more to go,” Andrew said. His eyes were still on the countdown. Twelve hours seemed like a long time, but there was no guarantee they would be done after opening the door. The quest asked them to kill the Hive Queen. That meant there would be another boss behind it.

After five hours and three more engagements with larvae, the group found their final chest. James struck the chest, and just like before, it sprung another trap. This time, it lobbed a grenade at them.

“Fuck, these chests were all booby-trapped…” Andrew frowned.

“This should be the last one. We’ve found them all. There were only three slots on the door, right? Andrew, do you know the way to the door?” James asked.

“Yeah, I remember. It’s this way.” Being the smart one, Andrew had learned some cartography skills and memorized the path they took. From his memory, they had explored almost all the corridors. Only the door remained.

The journey back took more than an hour.

“Great! We have returned! If it were not for your great memory, we might have been trapped here for days!” Kouki smiled. His smile soon faded when he found not one but two massive cockroaches. The cockroach they had defeated earlier had not only recovered but had split its body. James, who had expected it to recover after seeing the system protect it earlier, drew his axe.

“James… on your mark.” Andrew said, James raised an eyebrow when he saw Andrew ready to throw his spear, but he decided to go along with him. Using his jet thruster, he leaped toward one of the cockroaches and used his axe skill on it. Before the axe could even spread its effect, a golden meteor slammed into it, shattering the head of the massive cockroach.

James turned to the other cockroach. Kouki was somehow dual-wielding his swords. One glowed red while the other glowed golden. James hesitated, unsure of whether he should help. He noticed that whenever Kouki slashed it, the cockroach healed. The battle was at a stalemate. But as he was about to step in and deliver a fatal blow, Andrew yelled.

“James! It is resurrecting itself!” Andrew said. James turned and saw the cockroach regenerate the head that was supposed to have been smashed to pieces before. He abandoned his idea of saving Kouki and went for the other cockroach.

“Damn it. How are we going to defeat it then? We smashed its head!”

“Give me the orbs. We will escape through the door!” Andrew pulled back his spear and ran for the door while the massive bug chased after him, despite still missing half its head. James bombarded it with lightning bolts and ice spears to attract its attention. Its armor prevented damage from normal attacks; only weapon skills could get through.

Andrew leaped toward the door as James threw the three orbs to him. He juggled them in his hands before slotting them into place. The door lit up. Gears ground as it creaked open, while James and Kouki inched closer with two cockroaches in tow.

Then, from the far end of the wall, another cockroach was born, then another, then another. Before long, there were a dozen of the massive cockroaches swarming toward the group.

“Hurry up!” Andrew said. The horse, the raptor, and the dragon that had been waiting a bit further away leaped in at once. He then snatched the three orbs and went inside. The door groaned as it slid back, then began closing on its own.

“On it.” Kouki and James leaped inside. The cockroaches also charged, but their massive frames smashed into the door in vain.

Andrew eyed the pedestal on the inner side of the door, with three cavities similar to the outer side, and sighed in relief. He didn’t plan on going back there, but having an escape route was always a good thing.

“So, where is the hive queen?” Kouki asked. The chamber inside the door was empty.

“Great. More walking,” James muttered as he saw another path across the room.

“Damn it. "We have only eight or seven hours left…” Andrew cursed.

“Hurry up.” Kouki began jogging.

James wanted to rest. They had been exploring this dungeon for almost a full day, and exhaustion was beginning to catch up with them. But James understood what was at stake here. Once that countdown reached zero, not only might the five hundred residents inside the town perish—they might as well. It was a race against time.

After another hour of jogging, they met two giant mantises standing guard before a large stone door.

“Out of the way!” Kouki leaped at one of the mantises as a red glow flashed around his sword. The mantis’s sword arm glowed golden. James’s heart skipped a beat when he realized they could use sword skills.

Elemental attacks had lower force than normal skills. James expected the mantis to cut him apart, but Kouki slid his sword against the blade. He slipped in, then with a second hit, his blade went for the mantis’s soft flank. The cut was shallow, but since it was an elemental attack, the mantis burst into flame.

Kouki took a deep breath as fatigue crept in. He didn’t see the other mantis sneak up behind him.

Luckily, James wasn’t idle. Knowing he wouldn’t make it in time, he used his axe skill. He threw his axe at the other mantis. The mantis that was about to chase Kouki noticed the blue meteor heading its way and blocked it with its scythe. The scythe froze over. A blue meteor soon followed, bounced off its chest and froze it solid. Andrew followed with his spear throw.

In less than two minutes, the battle was over. When Kouki was about to open the gate, James stopped him.

“Wait. We should rest.”

“But we’re pressed for time!” Kouki said.

“No. James is right. We should rest for at least thirty minutes,” Andrew said. “Do you think we’re prepared to fight a boss? It worked with the mantises, but I think you lost your cool there. If James hadn’t thrown his axe, that mantis would have gotten you for sure.”

“…Damn it. All right. I’ll listen to you.”

Despite resting, their nerves were still strung tight. Their hearts still drummed in their chests. There was still time, but the countdown was getting to them.

Andrew then opened the door and found a massive queen bee the size of a truck in the throne room.

“…Why is there a throne room here?” James muttered as he saw the queen bee laying egg sacs in front of the throne. The cockroaches then transported the eggs like worker ants and piled them beside it. Multiple larvae squirmed as the eggs hatched, birthing grotesque larvae. These were all normal larvae and cockroaches, each no bigger than a thumb. Their small size made the scene more grotesque.

“This tree probably grew right under a palace or a castle or something, and the tree swallowed the entire throne room before destroying the castle,” Andrew said as they entered. The bee and all the cockroaches and larvae born from it snapped their attention toward them at once.

“There are so many of them,” Kouki reeled back. His eyes twitched as he saw the squirming larvae and cockroaches.

“Not for long. They are fusing,” Andrew said. The queen bee roared, flapping its wings. The tree trembled as its host roared, and the larvae and the cockroaches scrambled toward each other, biting and clawing. Scenes of gore continued as their tiny bodies fused, merging into a caterpillar and a beetle. Then, from the wooden walls of the throne room, three treeman knights emerged.

“Shit!” James cursed. Andrew and Kouki frowned. They were outnumbered.

“Dodge!” James yelled. A golden glow shone from the treemen’s spears. Kouki and Andrew leaped away as the golden meteors chased them.

“Kouki!”

“I’ll handle the left flank!” Kouki leaped to the side.

James went for the queen, throwing flying slashes at it. He thought the queen would defend itself or ignore his attack, but the cockroaches and larvae moved toward it instead. They disregarded their own safety, trying to shield the queen bee with their soft bellies. The treemen also stopped engaging Kouki and went for their queen. They threw their spears, all aiming for James’s life.

James activated his jet thruster and leaped away as multiple spears and jets of acid went after him. The treemen extended their arms, regrowing the spears they had thrown. The entire room pulsed as the queen bee grew agitated. The ceiling and walls of the throne room collapsed, and more monsters poured in. The throne room expanded—now it was as wide as a football field. Four mantises, three more massive cockroaches, five more larvae, and another dozen treemen appeared.

Kouki was stunned. His resolve wavered. Andrew, however, saw a silver lining in this predicament.

He called out to James.
