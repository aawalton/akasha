Adabelle had always been hard to rouse in the morning. No matter when she went to bed, waking up and leaving the fog of sleep behind wasn’t easy. It had gotten to the point where she could even make breakfast for three and still not be awake thanks to a lost bet.

Trevor, on the other hand, was consistent. He woke up a few minutes before his alarm would go off almost every morning, and was generally ready to go from the moment he opened his eyes.

Neither of them even so much as stirred as they held each other into the late morning. They were perfectly content to rest after the excitement of the previous day.

It was Adabelle who stretched first, her fingers lightly digging into Trevor’s chest. She was halfway draped over him, and blinked the sleep from her eyes. Her movement woke him, and he wrapped his arms around his wife.

“G’morning,” he mumbled before yawning.

Adabelle nestled her cheek against his shoulder. She had slept so long that she didn’t even feel her usual morning fog, allowing her to relish the moment of waking next to her husband.

“Good morning, Tre-EH!” the noblewoman squeaked as she was suddenly pulled over her husband as he shifted but refused to let her go. When he settled, she was on her back, and he was resting his head against her chest.

“Mmm, that’s better.”

“Is it, now?”

“Much, much better,” Trevor confirmed as he looked up at her. An effortless smile graced his features. “Hey there, beautiful.”

Any fight Adabelle might have put up at being manhandled like that, playful or otherwise, was immediately dashed as she melted at his expression. “You’re lucky you’re cute,” she told him, feigning a sigh.

“Darn right, I am,” he readily agreed with a chuckle, closing his eyes as he basked in his wife’s warmth. “I wouldn't be here otherwise.”

The demonkin tilted her head to kiss the top of Trevor’s and ran her fingers through his hair. A small note of contentment escaped her throat as she laid there. Her husband was all wrapped up around her, and her tail was likewise around his waist. She was perfectly content to stay in this moment forever.

Until her stomach loudly protested that it was well past time for breakfast. She quickly placed her hand over it as if that would quell the sound. Predictably, it did no good.

Trevor, however, saw a problem and immediately offered a solution. Raising his hand from where it was gently stroking her, he summoned a chocolate covered strawberry near Adabelle’s mouth. It was still chilled from the magic plate they had found it on.

“There’s more?” she asked. “Trevor, are you holding out on me?”

“Nah. I wanted to save one to surprise you with, and it looks like I succeeded,” the courier answered with a smile. She took a bite, leaving only the stem and leaves behind. Those were sent back into his inventory without a second thought. “How about I whip us up some breakfast real quick and we eat out on the balcony?”

“I could make it, if you want,” she offered.

“How about we alternate? I’ll take today, you’ll take tomorrow, and we switch whenever it's relevant.”

Adabelle kissed the top of his head. “Deal. You go on up, then. I’ll be there soon.”

Instead of leaving right away, Trevor moved so that he was over the noblewoman. His hand cupped her cheek as he kissed her. There was a brief moment where neither of them were sure if he was actually going to get out of bed, but then his stomach growled and, with a laugh, they parted for the moment.

The kitchen was fully stocked with everything Trevor needed to make something spectacular. He paused in front of a selection of aprons, eventually picking one that had a stack of pancakes on it. That seemed like a delicious choice to him, and so the courier put it on and got to work.

Eggs, sausage, hash browns, and, of course, pancakes with blueberries. He had been momentarily confused when he opened the cupboard to get some flour and found a plate of warm, fried mozzarella sticks. The enchantment on the dish was the same as, though opposite to, the one with the chocolate covered strawberries.

Lora hadn’t been kidding when she said she had tucked away snacks for them. Amused by the discovery, he popped one in his mouth before adding them to their plates. They didn’t really fit the theme of breakfast, but they were good and there were no rules for taste. If Adabelle didn’t want any, he’d toss them in his inventory for later.

“Now that’s a good view. You look good in an apron, son of Ander,” the demonkin said as she entered the kitchen, coming up and hugging Trevor from behind.

“Just for you, lady,” he replied as he flipped the eggs one last time.

Satisfied that they were done exactly the way they liked them, he brought the whole frying pan, food included, into his inventory. After some mental gymnastics to move things around, he retrieved the clean cookware and set it down to cool. He would dispose of the grease and everything else that remained in the other space later.

“Hungry?” he asked.

“Famished.”

“You’ll have to let go of me if we want to eat on the balcony.”

Adabelle pouted as she patted his bare chest under the apron. “Why must you force me to make the hardest decisions so early in the morning?”

“Then… Hop on, I guess?”

“That sounds horribly undignified.”

“And?” he asked, waggling his eyebrows as he looked back at her.

There was a slight pause as the demonkin contemplated his words before deciding that the best thing to do, obviously, was to heed them. Wrapping her arms around his neck, she jumped up onto Trevor’s back. Her legs wrapped around him, and his hands immediately went down to help hold her up.

“Onward, then, to the balcony,” Adabelle commanded, pointing towards the doorway. Her tail smacked him on the butt, causing him to jump into motion.

“Hey, careful with that thing,” he said, enjoying every moment of her giggling.

The courier carried her out onto the balcony, stopping for a moment to enjoy the view. The sun was only an hour or so away from its apex and the Wildlands below basked in its light. It was just as breathtaking the second time around as the first. His stomach quickly reminded him that scenic sights weren’t enough to sustain him, and he walked to one of the nearest tables.

When Trevor summoned two plates full of food, Adabelle decided she was hungry enough to leave him be. She was light on her feet as she dismounted and quick to take a seat. He moved across from her before bringing out four glasses, two with juice and two with water. They both sat close enough to the railing that they could rest their elbows on it.

“This place is beautiful,” Adabelle absently remarked as she started digging into her eggs.

“It really is,” Trevor agreed as he looked out over it again. “I especially like the…”

He trailed off and frowned. The Wildlands were different today, though he couldn’t put his finger on how or even why he felt that way. His eyes searched for what was different as he ate.

Adabelle, it seemed, also picked up on this. She was quiet as she ate and observed the land below. It got to the point where she picked up her plate so she could face the Wildlands and eat more easily.

The center plains were just as lively as they were yesterday with herds upon herds grazing, playing, and generally meandering about. There was a lot of movement around the lake, which surprised neither of them, but it hadn’t changed either.

Finally, Trevor’s eyes fell on the crystalline trees. The red chimpanzees were in full force today, coming and going with great speed. He frowned as he looked them over, even going so far as to summon his binoculars from his inventory. They had been his favorite part of the Wildlands, but had they been so close last night?

“No, that can’t be right,” he said as he watched them.

“Did you think of something?” Adabelle asked curiously.

“It’s probably just a trick of the light, but it looks like the crystal forest over there might have moved,” he answered as he scratched his chin in thought. “Not very far, though. That’s why I’m not sure.”

“May I have my pair?” she requested, and he summoned the second set of binoculars from his inventory. The noblewoman set down her plate to use them, focusing on the trees. She observed them for a minute before nodding. “No, you’re right. That’s a good catch, Trevor.”

“Wait, so they did move?” he asked incredulously, putting the binoculars down to look at Adabelle.

“It’s a little hard to tell from this distance, even with these, but you can see where the leaves on the trees they were around before have been broken,” the noblewoman explained, gesturing in their direction. “It doesn’t look like they’ve made it very far, maybe a couple hundred feet, but they have moved. You’ve got good eyes, Trevor.”

“Huh, that’s neat,” he said, looking at them one more time before setting the binoculars down so he could resume eating. “I wonder what they actually are. Probably not plants, right? Some kind of crystal monster?”

“We can always ask the Quest Master,” Adabelle said before taking another bite out of her pancakes.

“Or…” Trevor glanced inside for a moment, thinking things through before standing up. “Hold on a minute.”

“Where are you going?”

“Gonna look at something real quick.”

Heading for the lounge, Trevor stopped at the bookcase. It held a large, impressive collection, as if anything in one of Klar’s getaways could be anything but, and he scanned the titles. Some of them were in Minotauran, which he had learned to read thanks to the Language Fish Trinket, but they weren’t what he was looking for.

One of the rows was dedicated to the Wildlands. There were a plethora of books ranging from what scholars had been able to unravel behind its history, the flora and fauna and how it differed from the countries around its boundaries, and even the founding stories of the nations that have tried to claim it for themselves. It was an extensive collection.

He quickly found what he was looking for. Satisfied with the books on the plants and animals, which were separated into two volumes, he brought them back to the balcony. Adabelle looked at him curiously as he returned.

“Find what you were looking for?” she asked as he set the books down.

“We could put the question to the Quest Master, sure,” he said before excitedly tapping on the cover of the book on Wildlands fauna. “Or, we could do our own research and try to figure out what they are on our own like proper scholars. You know, if you want to. We could just ask if you don’t feel like doing homework on our honeymoon.”

“Quite the contrary, it sounds interesting,” Adabelle said with a smile. Setting down her fork, she reached over and put her hand on top of Trevor’s when he gave her an uncertain look. “Truly, it does. Have you ever known me to shy away from a little homework? I’ll enjoy learning more about the Wildlands. It’s not a topic that comes up very often.”

Trevor returned the smile. It wasn’t what he had expected to do today, but he knew her enthusiasm was genuine. They had often solved puzzles together as a way to both bond and show off. She usually got the better of him when it came to trivia, though he had moments where he would shine when it came to out of the box thinking.

“But,” Adabelle continued, pushing the books away. “Only after we’re done with breakfast. I won’t allow any grease or errant food to get on Klarkartar ’s literature.”

“That’s fair,” Trevor agreed as he looked back at his plate. In his excitement, he had left it mostly full. “After breakfast, then. I look forward to nerding out with you.”
