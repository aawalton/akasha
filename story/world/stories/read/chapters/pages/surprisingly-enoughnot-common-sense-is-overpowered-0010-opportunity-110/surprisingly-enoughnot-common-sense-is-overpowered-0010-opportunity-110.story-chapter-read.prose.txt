After the lecture ended, Ning couldn't wait to return home and digest all the insights he had gained. Although Elder Lou Ran hadn't gone into much detail, his explanations had been concise, with plenty of useful advice about what to do and what to avoid.

Unfortunately, that would have to wait, because today was also the first proper gathering of the Mutual Aid Association.

The group soon found themselves in a teahouse, the closest thing this world had to a café. The place smelled faintly of roasted tea leaves and sandalwood, with waiters moving between tables in robes that looked one step short of ceremonial attire.

I miss hoodies.

The clothes here were cumbersome. Robes looked elegant until you had to sit, stand, or bend over. That was when you realized the sleeves were traps, getting caught on practically everything.

Wei Zhusang naturally took the lead, ushering everyone toward a long table by the window. Once they were seated, he clapped his hands together.

"Since today is our first proper meeting, let's make it official with introductions!"

Ning gave him a sideways look. This was sounding more and more like the corporate icebreakers back on Earth, minus the doughnuts.

He missed doughnuts.

One by one, the members began introducing themselves.

Wei Zhusang, Zhang Feng, and Lang Rulang went first. From the glances exchanged around the table, it was clear that most of the members already knew who they were.

Then came Su Fan.

"Su Fan. Hmph!"

That was it. A name and a disdainful snort.

His attitude naturally irritated several people around the table. After all, not everyone was as easygoing or worldly as Ning.

"This—"

Wei Zhusang smoothly interrupted before anyone could complain. "Brother Su Fan is a man of few words. He works in the Alchemy Hall."

He paused before adding, almost casually, "His sister is a core disciple."

That settled things.

Ning raised an eyebrow at the new information.

As far as he knew, the sect's disciples were divided into several ranks: peripheral disciples, outer disciples, inner disciples, and core disciples.

Inner disciples were generally considered promising candidates for Foundation Building, while core disciples were those with the potential to reach the Purple Mansion Realm.

As for outer and peripheral disciples, they were essentially workers, laboring like horses and mules to support those above them.

A few murmurs spread around the table, followed by glances of envy and awe. Su Fan seemed to enjoy every moment of it, his chin rising a little higher.

At least the foundation supporting his arrogance is thick enough.

Ning couldn't help feeling a little envious himself. With a background like that, Su Fan's cultivation journey would certainly be smoother than most.

A few newer members introduced themselves next.

A skinny youth spoke nervously. "L-Liu Yuan. I'm a junior clerk in the Records Office. If you ever need something copied or checked... I can help."

Another young man, broad-shouldered but quiet, added, "Chen Mu. Beast Taming Hall. I'm still just feeding spirit chickens, but... I'll improve."

A girl with bright, attentive eyes spoke up next. "Qiao Mei. Spirit Clothing Hall. I'm still learning how to comb spiritual silk."

When his turn came, Ning kept his introduction simple.

"Ji Ning. I work in the spiritual fields."

As the introductions continued, Ning noticed that most of the members came from relatively humble backgrounds, though their professions were surprisingly diverse.

He resisted the urge to give Wei Zhusang and his two friends a meaningful look. Gathering disciples from different professions was already a promising first step toward building one's own faction.

Still, his attention was soon drawn to something more interesting.

"Brother Wei, what did the elder mean by a great change?" Qiao Mei asked softly, her gaze lingering on Wei Zhusang a little longer than necessary.

Ning almost wanted to give the girl a thumbs-up. That was an excellent question, and it saved him the trouble of appearing too curious himself.

Wei, Zhang, and Lang exchanged brief glances before Wei Zhusang spoke.

"The great change concerns our chances of reaching Foundation Building. It involves both our generation of disciples and the one before us."

Zhang Feng leaned forward, a grin tugging at his lips. "Basically, all these incentives, resources, lectures, and special treatment are meant to help us reach the late stages of Qi Condensation faster. With decent spiritual roots, getting there is manageable. Breaking through to Foundation Building is the real problem. That's the wall."

"An ugly wall, too. The kind you'll have to bang your head against for years," he added, drawing a few chuckles from the table.

Wei Zhusang nodded, seemingly accustomed to Zhang Feng's antics.

"In five years, there will be a sect-wide competition. Those who rank high enough can become inner disciples directly, even without reaching the late stages of Qi Condensation. That would make the path to Foundation Building much smoother."

Lang Rulang finally spoke, her voice cool and direct.

"So practice hard. Otherwise, you'll be left behind."

Ning narrowed his eyes slightly at the implications.

The sect was offering quite a few incentives to encourage rapid cultivation. It seemed they were preparing for something, though what that might be was still unclear.

For now, he'd have to wait and see.

...

After returning to his humble abode, the first thing Ning did was pull out his notes.

He had noticed during the lecture that many disciples hadn't bothered taking any. But as someone who had gone through compulsory education, he knew better.

You might think you'd understood everything during class, only to discover that half of it had vanished from your head the moment you stepped outside.

Notes were lifesavers.

Flipping to the meridian diagrams, he began reviewing the elder's instructions.

If he was being honest, Ning was still wrapping his head around the fact that the human body in this world was different from what he had known on Earth. Spiritual roots, dantians, meridians... these weren't metaphors or flowery diagrams.

They were real.

Calming his thoughts, he straightened his posture, placed his palms on his knees, and drew in a slow breath. Qi stirred within his dantian before he guided it upward through the pathways described in the Pure Qi Sutra, directing a portion of it toward the Heart Meridian.

At first, it felt like forcing water through a clogged hose. The qi bunched up, sluggish and uncooperative, threatening to scatter whenever he tried to move it forward.

Elder Lou Ran had advised them to visualize the flow for smoother circulation.

Easier said than done.

Until one reached higher realms and developed the ability to observe the inside of one's body, much of the process remained vague and abstract.

Alas, if only I could borrow a book or two.

Unfortunately, peripheral disciples weren't allowed access to the Sutra Pavilion's miscellaneous texts.

Ning took that as another reason to work harder.

To be honest, he was still a little lost about what he actually wanted to do in this world. He had no idea how transmigrators in novels managed to find some grand goal worth pursuing almost immediately after arriving.

Wasn't that a little too fast?

He'd barely gotten used to living here, let alone figured out what he wanted to accomplish with the rest of his life.

For now, he was content to go with the flow, finding small goals to work toward while slowly figuring things out.

With that thought settled, Ning returned his attention to cultivation.

He imagined a steady stream of water trickling through invisible channels. Having encountered similar concepts in his previous life, he found the visualization easier than expected, and gradually, his qi began to move more smoothly.

Warmth spread through his chest before traveling toward his armpit and down the inside of his arm, producing faint sensations at several points along the way.

The names from his notes began to match what he was feeling. Jiquan beneath the armpit, Shaohai near the elbow, Shaofu in the palm, and Shaochong at the tip of his little finger.

He could feel a dull throb at his elbow, warmth gathering in his palm, and a faint tingle near his fingertip.

It wasn't painful, though it wasn't exactly comfortable either. The sensation reminded him of the pins and needles that came when blood returned to a limb that had fallen asleep.

Once the qi reached the end of the Heart Meridian, Ning carefully guided it through the remaining pathways prescribed by the Pure Qi Sutra.

The first circulation was slow and awkward, but the second went more smoothly. By the third, he had begun noticing small obstructions where his qi snagged like cloth caught on thorns.

He carefully eased it through, remembering Elder Lou Ran's warning never to force the flow.

Damaging a meridian was no small matter. In some ways, it was like damaging a blood vessel back on Earth. The consequences could be severe, and repairing it would be far more troublesome than simply taking his time.

As the qi completed another cycle and returned to his dantian, Ning noticed that the circulation had become smoother than before. With fewer interruptions along the way, he could maintain a steadier rhythm and refine his qi more efficiently.

An hour passed almost unnoticed before he finally brought his cultivation to a stop, allowing the circulating qi to settle within his dantian.

He opened his eyes and exhaled a faint stream of turbid breath.

[First Stage Qi Condensation: 2 → 3]

"Those insights really are useful."

Ning mentally fist-bumped.

Progress achieved.

However, his satisfaction didn't last long before his thoughts returned to the association's discussion about the great change.

He was reasonably confident about reaching Foundation Building. His spiritual root was decent, and with enough effort and resources, he had a fair chance of making it that far.

Purple Mansion would be much harder, though he wouldn't call it impossible.

Beyond that, however, things became increasingly uncertain.

Without sufficient opportunities and resources, he would eventually hit a ceiling. Cultivation also demanded an enormous amount of time, and every year spent struggling at a bottleneck meant less time available for the realms that followed.

Still, the sect's recent changes presented an opportunity.

If Xiao Fan truly was the chosen one, as every cliché seemed determined to suggest, then perhaps the smartest move was to ride the dragon's tail and take advantage of the opportunities that came his way.

Of course, that would only work if he was strong enough to keep up.

Ning leaned back with a sigh.

"If you don't progress, you regress," he muttered. "Looks like it's time to change the plan."
