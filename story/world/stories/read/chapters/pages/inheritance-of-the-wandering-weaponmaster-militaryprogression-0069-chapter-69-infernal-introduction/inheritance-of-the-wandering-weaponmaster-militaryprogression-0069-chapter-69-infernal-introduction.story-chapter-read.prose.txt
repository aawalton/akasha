Ezra fought to keep his attention on the fight that was approaching him, but he couldn’t help but have his attention yanked away by the ghost's exclamation. He glanced towards Jormun, but only for a split second before he looked back at the goblins that were approaching.

Fortunately, it wasn’t hard to see what it was that had gotten his mentor's attention. He pointed towards the center of the battle, where Michel stood and fought. Ezra caught a flash of light, but couldn’t continue to watch for long. He turned back to his own battle and, with a few swift cuts, dispatched the goblins that were actively attacking him. Then, with no more goblinoids within at least fifty meters of him, he turned back towards Michel.

The Pseudo-Knight had obviously activated his shroud, and his inner world's affinity was blindingly obvious. Literally. The large man was enveloped in bright light, and every swing of his sword sent out a ray of light that carved and scorched everything in its path. In front of him lay two twitching corpses, Dragonkin with limbs chopped off. Evidently, Michel was stronger than Chloe, or at least better at killing Dragonkin quickly. Another Dragonkin was in the process of fighting the elite soldier, and the fourth, the final Dragonkin, was already a corpse and lay a handful of meters away from the man, its chest pierced through by a dozen arrows.

Regardless of Ezra’s expectations, the four people-shaped draconic monsters were obviously not what Jormun was concerned with. Though they each varied in appearance from one to the next, he had no eyes for them. Instead, he stared at a surprisingly small creature that hurried forward. At least, surprisingly small for the immediate and obvious effect it had on the battlefield.

The creature, apparently an infernal, was perhaps just a bit taller than Ezra himself, a fair amount shorter than two meters tall. Like Michel, it glowed, though not with aura. Instead, it had skin that seemed like stone, cracks appearing between its joints where fire leaked out and scorched anything nearby. If there was such a thing as burning stone, Ezra didn’t know what that was, but he would guess that it would look just like the infernal. Where the fire cooled, stone grew, but mostly, the beast that Jormun was so concerned about seemed to be on fire. No, to be fire itself.

Ezra couldn’t ask him what was so concerning about this creature, but seeing its approach, Michel's body flared with light and his blade flickered through the air in a quick pattern of five cuts. Evidently, whatever he’d done drew heavily on his inner world's reserves, but as a result, the final Dragonkin fell to the ground, dismembered into a heap of limbs.

“Here’s the aberrant!” Michel shouted to the surroundings. “Stay clear.”

He didn’t say anything more as he closed in against the creature. Michel was much larger than this monster, but even with his shroud empowering him, the infernal matched him blow for blow. His blade couldn’t catch the creature, even as he pushed himself by drawing more heavily on his shroud. The two grew faster, step by step, each trying to outmaneuver and strike the other down. Even so, as fire exploded from one of the infernal's attacks and lit the forest surrounding Michel on fire, they seemed to be equally matched.

The fucker is turning the environment into one that favors it more, Jormun said. It’s not going to get tired as fast as he is, either. The more fire that surrounds it, the stronger it’ll be.

Ezra desperately wanted to ask what he meant, how Jormun was so familiar with this strange creature, but he couldn’t. Instead, he had to pull his attention away for a few seconds as the infernal screamed at the goblinoid army that served it. With its shout, a faint red haze burst out from it and made the cool spring air hot on Ezra’s face. The skin on his forehead tightened as his sweat quickly evaporated, and the enemy army roared as they threw themselves forward with greater ferocity.

Somehow, what the creature did strengthened the goblinoids. Fire seemed to pulse around them as they rushed forward, and the goblins that closed in on Ezra had sparks flying from their eyes as they jumped forward. Their skin darkened from green to an almost gray, continuing to grow darker by the moment. More, each of the goblinoid’s weapons now drew trails of embers through the air, and when Ezra braced his shield against them, the strikes were twice as strong as they had been before.

The longer this fight goes on, the stronger it and its thralls will get, Jormun said, his voice taking on a tone of urgency. You’ve gotta kill it and its supporters as fast as you can, or it’s going to light this entire forest on fire and use that to make more infernals.

“What am I supposed to do with that?” Ezra asked back, no longer thinking about not drawing any attention to himself.

Without saying anything else, he stepped aside from Artemis, just enough to give Telemachus a good shot at one of the goblins. The spear whooshed past him and seemed to hit some resistance with the goblin's skin, which was gradually blackening, but still pierced into its chest. There, the Adept twisted his weapon and ripped it out, and the monster fell to the ground, dead as its blood pooled on the ground.

“This is an aberrant,” Artemis shouted by way of explanation as she shifted her stance. “These fire ones are some of the more troublesome that we’ve heard of. Focus on killing the goblinoids. Michel will take care of the aberrant.”

“I don’t think I could enter that fight if I wanted to,” Ezra replied.

“They’re about a half step stronger than most Dragonkin, so no,” she said back, pushing forward more aggressively. “We need to wrap this up as fast as we can, or else we’ll be fighting in the middle of a forest fire.”

Hearing Artemis echo what Jormun had said, Ezra wasn’t sure how to react. He hadn’t thought that the ghost had been making things up, but evidently he knew exactly what he was talking about. With that in mind, he followed Artemis's lead to close in on the next fight, an orc and a handful of goblins.

“I haven’t used much of my inner world's reserves,” Ezra said. “I’m going to deal with this one faster, take a good amount more of what I’ve got to do it.”

“Go ahead.”

Hearing Artemis's agreement, Ezra hustled forward and, pushing more aura than he’d used in this entire fight so far into his axe, wound up a heavy overhead swing. Then, pulling on the speed of the swirling storm inside, Ezra stepped forward surprisingly quickly, attacking the orc. It raised its weapon in defense, but Ezra’s axe couldn’t be so easily stopped. Pushing the weapon closer to the orc and forcing it down, Ezra smashed through the quick defense. The head of the axe reached the monster's chest, cutting a three-centimeter-deep line straight down the middle of the orc, the line uninterrupted from sternum to groin.

With the descent of his weapon, a tempest of frigid air smashed down on the center of the small squad of enemies. The goblins were sent tumbling in every direction, their small, light bodies unable to withstand the force of Ezra’s yooperlite-enhanced attack. As they rolled across the ground, Ezra didn’t pay them any mind, instead focusing on the orc. The giant creature roared in pain at Ezra’s attack, the axe blade cutting deep while the wind spread open the wound and exposed the monster's entrails to the wind.

Disemboweled, the orc fought to keep its feet, screaming at the goblins to come protect it. They rolled to their feet, disoriented, but generally unwounded. Unfortunately for the orc, Telemachus and Artemis were quickly killing the disorganized enemies. Only then did Ezra’s target seem to realize that it was in serious danger and started to try to retreat. It was far too late for it to have a prayer of escape, and Ezra continued forward, bashing his shield into the orc's side and knocking it into a sprawl. It rolled, pain obvious in its movement, but Ezra didn’t let it suffer.

Two steps forward, and with a final swing of his weapon, the orc was no more.

Looking around, most of the rest of the enemy goblinoid army was dealt with. Every orc and bugbear had been slain with a few scattered goblins remaining and being killed without much resistance. All the while, the fight with the infernal was reaching a climax.

The infernal creature drew its arms through the air in a series of cuts, sending flashing lines of burning stone at Michel. He weathered the blows under his shield, but the infernal's attacks grew faster and stronger. Around the Pseudo-Knight, the ground was starting to morph from soil into undulating molten stone that destabilized the large man's footing. His knees constantly bent as the ground under him flexed and threatened to swallow him up. The monstrous creature shrieked in excitement as it threw itself forward at Michel.

Fire exploded in every direction as it scrabbled madly at his glowing shield. Then, as Ezra’s own fear clenched in his chest, Artemis seemed to relax. Michel raised his shield above his head and, as the burning creature held on, the shield exploded with light. The infernal quickly understood that it had made a mistake, and Ezra realized that Michel wasn’t specializing in the use of his sword, but his shield.

Bright light somehow burned away the flaming creature, and it attempted to escape as Michel roared, turned with his whole body, and slammed the infernal into a solid section of the ground. Trapped between his shield and stone, it writhed and screamed, but a scorching light grew ever brighter as the shield and the older man's aura destroyed the aberrant.

Just like that, the burning presence that had filled the air disappeared, and Ezra could breathe more easily as the leader of the army was killed. The dryness in his throat was still there, and he longed to pull out his waterskin and drink deeply from it. Instead, he looked around to see if there were any other goblins nearby. For now, there was nothing, and Telemachus stepped up next to him.

“Is that what a Dragon-Touched can do?” the other man asked, looking down at his spear. “Maybe I should get into the Dragon Wilds, find an untouched pool of true dragon blood, and baptize myself.”

“I don’t think that’s a Dragon-Touched,” Ezra shook his head.

“You don’t think?” Telemachus said, cocking his head. “Seems like it to me. But if it is, wouldn’t it be awesome to be that powerful? To be able to keep people from telling you what to do?”

“Sure,” Ezra chuckled, “but I don’t wanna be a flaming rock person like that.”

“I guess that’s true,” Telemachus shrugged. “But if I could find a way to do that without turning into a monster, I’d do it.”

Ezra waved him off, walking closer to Jormun, as he desperately tried to push the ghost to tell him what was going on. Fortunately, with the death of the so-called infernal, Jormun had turned to Ezra. His face was twisted in a rictus that Ezra hadn’t ever seen. His teeth were bared, and his eyes wild. Ezra had to fight to keep himself composed as finally, the old man answered Ezra’s unspoken question.

An infernal is a demon. I’ve killed tens of thousands of them. I know what it is. Somehow, there’s fucking demons in your world. And if you don’t kill them, if you don’t stop them from coming, your world will end the same way that mine did.

Lord Dorian

Looking in from the outer plane, Lord Dorian absentmindedly stroked the pattern of still-burning, weeping wounds across his chest. Time between planes and within worlds was an insubstantial, ethereal thing, but there were certain things that transcended his limited control over time. An explosion of pure aura, of a nearly transcendent tier from point-blank?

Yes, that was certainly something that could continue to bare its teeth after a long time.

Too bad he never knew the wrinkled bastard’s name. To have overloaded one of his Mastered weapons at the sacrifice of his own life was an admirable final stand. Having watched him detonate so many weapons all at once, Dorian had hardly believed that the human was capable of it. After all, there had yet to be, across the dozens of worlds whose fall Lord Dorian had witnessed and participated in, a single person who had reached Sainthood with more than a single weapon.

Well, it didn’t matter, not really. Never again would there be someone so foolish and stubborn as to hold themselves back from power simply because there might be a different peak to summit.

As he thought it, Dorian stretched a hand out, his magic reaching out and grasping a tormented soul. This was a particularly fine specimen, a fifth-circle sorcerer who had, in the midst of sacrificing their connection to the world, been caught in a time dilation. Were Lord Dorian to be honest with himself, it was pure luck that he’d caught the woman like that, but he was the instrument of his own luck. And wasn’t that just a testament to how he was favored by the universe itself?

With a broad smile across his face, he infused the strength of the soul he’d taken into his being, and some of the wounds that still wept across his chest faded. With only three or four more like that, he’d be back up to his peak performance. Still, dozens of wounds remained, and it might well be millennia before he gained access to another vessel so pristine and fine as that little treat.

No matter. Quantity was a quality of its own, and some of his agents had started to finally cross over the border of this newest world. As they continued to lay a foundation and give him his sacrifices, he would find himself whole once more. With a sigh, he sat back into his throne as his mind stretched forth once again, trying to connect with some of his worshippers who had been sent as his vanguard. Though he could only see fleeting glances, there was so much in this new world that was so interesting. It’d been a long while since there’d been true dragons in a world he was conquering, even if they were long dead.

Once he was back in fighting condition, he, alongside the other eight Lords of Ultz’akara, would be able to set foot on the material plane once more, to gorge themselves on the many races of this New World. After all, a few years, or even decades, were just a blink of an eye to the so-called demon nobles.
