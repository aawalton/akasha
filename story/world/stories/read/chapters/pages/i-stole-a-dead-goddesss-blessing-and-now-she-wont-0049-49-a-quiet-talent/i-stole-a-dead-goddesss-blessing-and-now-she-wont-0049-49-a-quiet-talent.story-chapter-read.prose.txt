“And that’s why the fall of House Asteris led to House Delonis taking over its citadel,” Magister Calista explained, chalk in hand as she sketched the territorial shifts on the writing slate on the wall. “After that, it was renamed the Great Citadel of Delos, and all of Asteris’s old territory, Aura Cay included, came under their dominion.”

Magister Calista turned to face the class. “This change took place right after the War of Ashen Vain, about twenty-one years ago. Make sure to write this down. It’ll be on your exam.”

Anadis sat in class, dozing off as the magister went over the topic for the third time that day. He’d already memorized all of this. Grandma Anna had drilled it into his head multiple times so he could be ahead of his peers; that was her reasoning. For some reason it was a topic she felt strongly about—incensed, even, at times. Perhaps she believed the citadel had been better governed under the old house of Asteris than under Delos.

Well, nothing she or anyone could do about that.

A glimmer of light twirled beside him. Hel unraveled from a glowing ribbon into her tiny form and perched on the edge of the marble-topped desk, one leg draped over the other as she idly swung them. “When did this world become so… mundane?” she murmured. Her gaze drifted across the room. “It once held far greater wonders…” Without warning, she sprang upward like water from a fountain, as though venting centuries of frustration, before slowly spiraling back down. “History is so monochromatic now.”

Mundane? Monochromatic?

How could she feel that way about Igindor’s history? Well… Gaelora’s history, since Igindor was just a continent. And the Shrouded Continent surely held its own history, far richer than Igindor’s. Learning about the way history had unfolded in Gaelora had always sparked Anadis’s interest. Gods, Blessings, Grulls, the Fourbloods… even Draconic people, though they were now extinct. Well, he supposed that from the perspective of a goddess who had lived for millennia, what had transpired in the last fifteen hundred years could somehow look mundane at best. He wished he could ask her about so many things—about each god, what their realm was like, why they had slumbered for so long… but then again, she couldn’t remember any of it.

Anyhow, now that Leander’s memories had returned, he could compare Gaelora’s history to Earth’s and see how different it was. And yeah, Gaelora’s was far from dull. He rubbed the back of his head uncomfortably. Every time he thought about his first life—Leander’s life—the same question surfaced, bringing a strange sense of alienation: Was he an Earthling or a Gaeloran? Which world did he truly belong to? He’d lived in Gaelora for years, growing up just as naturally as he once did on Earth, yet that only made it harder to understand. Maybe he never would.

The Learning Hall’s sonorous bell rolled through the classroom like a wave, snapping him out of his reverie. Instantly, kids practically leaped from the marble benches, feet shuffling as they gathered their things, excited to head out.

“Remember, this will be on the exam!” Magister Calista said in one last attempt at relevance.

“You were hoping to see her today, I assume,” Albus asked beside Anadis.

“Yes, but I guess it makes sense she didn’t come in today either. She hasn’t been here in more than three weeks.” Anadis had hoped his father would bring Selene straight to the Learning Hall after they left the house early that morning, but it made more sense to take her straight to her parents. And he couldn’t even go there to check on her. He was grounded. Funny how a Blessen who had lived two lives and was technically hundreds of years old still abided by such rules.

Mom. That’s why.

“Let’s just head home,” he said to Albus, who nodded.

Hel darted in front of his face as he walked and pressed a finger to his nose. It felt sharp, almost like a mosquito proboscis digging into his skin. “Get to your house,” she ordered. “I’ll teach you the basics of shard purification today. There is little else worth doing.”

“Or I can have Nora teach me the basics instead,” he replied telepathically.

Hel pouted, puffing up her cheeks, then shot him a glare as sharp as broken glass. “You know what… do as you please, mortal. You’ll come begging eventually.”

Nora was stupid.

Or at least that was what she’d called herself, over and over again for the past two days—when she bolted from the dinner table the moment her father finished speaking. The moment he told them the truth: he had been a Luminous all alone.

Honestly, she still couldn’t say what had made her snap. Was it the truth itself? The fact that they had kept them all in the dark for so long? Or was it that they had told Noe but not her?

That was it.

There was no point pretending; it stung that they still saw her as an irresponsible kid. And honestly, could she even blame them? She had run off while Aelia, Albus, and Anadis stayed and faced the truth. She, the oldest, had fled.

She had already apologized to both her parents. They told her she had nothing to apologize for, that it was normal to feel betrayed. They were good parents. She knew that. She just wasn’t a good daughter. Or so it felt. All she was good for was playing the clown, never taking anything seriously.

Sure, she could feel faint tingles through the soles of her feet when most people felt nothing, but that was something she had felt for as long as she could remember. A gift she was born with, not something she had earned through work.

Wildflowers flattened under her weight as she lay spread-eagled in the grass, rolling a flower stem between her fingers and staring up at the sky. Both suns shone brightly, with only a few lazy clouds drifting overhead. A breeze brushed across her skin, fresh and cool, the kind of perfect weather that defined the month of Goldturn.

Despite it all, Nora felt tired, despondent, and even distressed.

Even with everything unraveling inside her family, one question haunted her more than anything else: Will I ever get a suitor?

Truly, would she? Nora was seventeen, and while most girls became betrothed between the ages of sixteen and twenty, she knew she wasn’t exactly late. Still, most had already received several propositions by now—or at least had a decent idea of who they might marry. Nora had received nothing. She knew of no one.

She knew she wasn’t exactly a great catch. She was loud, unladylike, and loved going barefoot everywhere—another decidedly unladylike trait. For as long as she could remember, Nora had always been bold. Coquettish, if she was being generous. Loud, if you asked anyone else.

When she was younger and first started attending the Learning Hall, if she liked a boy, she wouldn’t hesitate to tell him. Why would she? If she wanted something, she went after it. That was how it worked… or at least that was how she had thought it worked.

Apparently, it didn’t work that way with guys.

The dumb boys would usually freeze the moment she confessed. They’d sweat, stutter something incoherent, or simply stop talking to her altogether. The worst were the ones who ran. It had taken her a while to understand why: boys didn’t like girls like her—loud, rough, straightforward, the kind who spoke over them and made them feel small and less manly.

Boys liked girls like her sister Aelia. Kind. Gentle. Soft-spoken. The type who wouldn’t speak unless spoken to first. The kind who made them look more manly simply by how modestly they carried themselves, even while doing something as ordinary as eating.

Nora had once watched Aelia nibble at a pastry with such delicate, measured bites that it took her twenty minutes to finish it.

Spare me… The excess sugar would make me vomit.

There was a time when she had tried to act more like Aelia. She minded her manners, kept her back straight, and fussed over her hair. It was exhausting. She didn’t understand how it came so naturally to her sister. In the end, her efforts bore no fruit.

At one point she had stared into the mirror and asked herself, Am I hideous?

But she promptly shook her head. No way. Of all her siblings, Nora most resembled her mother and had inherited her father’s striking blue eyes. Yeah, she was beautiful. In no world was she ugly—not in a million years.

Still, it gnawed at her that Aelia was only fourteen and already received at least one confession every month, even from Gold-class nobles. Who knew—she’d probably end up marrying a Diamond-class noble someday.

Or that boy Sevran? Hmph… he had been following her around like a lost puppy ever since he moved into our house.

Nora, meanwhile, hadn’t gotten a single confession in her entire farcical life. And to make matters worse, Noe, her ridiculous older brother, was getting married to the only other girl in Aura Cay who could hold a candle to Aelia’s beauty. And get this—she was an elf.

She thought back to how she used to mock Noe relentlessly for chasing the elf, only to be crushed by her rejection every single time. Yet he never gave up. He was a Kalonis, after all. Then one day, lo and behold, he surprised everyone by bringing her home. They had actually become a couple. Nora still remembered the stupid grin that had spread across his witless face.

To this day, Nora was convinced Noe had threatened the elf. That had to be the only explanation. Or maybe the elf just had bad eyesight.

But no… she had to be fair. Noe had always been popular with girls. It was just that the one he truly wanted hadn’t liked him at first. Now, years later, they were only days away from getting married.

Letting out a defeated exhale, Nora rose gingerly from the grass. The moment her palms touched the ground, a barrage of rippling energy surged through her body. Leywaves flowed in one steady, unified direction, like a river following its natural course. She was like a living junction where the Leywaves ran in and out. Nora didn’t even bat an eye. To her, this was as ordinary as the air brushing her skin. Even if her life depended on it, she couldn’t say when she had first felt these pulses. They had simply always been there, part of her.

When she was little, wearing sandals made the pulses feel distant and wrong, leaving her head spinning. If she had to describe it, it was like walking with her ears plugged—everything still worked; she could see and move normally, but she was missing a vital sense. Direction, balance, something essential. It simply felt off.

That was why she’d always hated wearing sandals. Well… she still did. But according to her mother, her grandmother, and society at large, she couldn’t go through life barefoot. So she didn’t. Sadly.

After years of training with her master, she’d learned to control and guide the Leywaves at will, even while wearing sandals or anything else that dulled the pulses. Still, that was the only thing Nora felt adequate at. She wasn’t as well-mannered as Aelia, didn’t attract any suitors, and remained painfully immature.

“Nora!” a voice cut through the meadow. She glanced over her shoulder and saw the twins, Anadis and Albus. Her face lit up.

There was no helping the grin that spread across her face every time she saw them. Anadis—they had almost lost him. She had almost lost him.

“H-Hey,” Anadis said between ragged breaths.

“How are my precious handsome little brothers?” Nora cooed, unable to help herself as she pinched both their cheeks. So smooth. “Huh?” She frowned slightly. That was unusual. Normally, Anadis would have slapped her hands away by now or grumbled something like Leave me alone.

“You… want something,” she said, narrowing her eyes.

“How can you tell?”

“I just can.” She crossed her arms. “What is it?”

“I want you to teach me and Albus how to purify a Grullshard,” he said with a cheeky smile.

“What?!” She stared at him, then burst out laughing. “That’s impossible. You need to be an Attuner for that.”

He tsked and wagged a finger. “Oh, don’t give me that lie. I know Blessens can attune as well. It’s just not common knowledge.”

She gaped, utterly befuddled. Attuners followed a golden rule—a silent agreement—to keep quiet about the fact that Blessens could also feel Leywave pulses, albeit faintly. So faintly, in fact, that most couldn’t even sense them until ascension, and even then they had to train relentlessly to master the skill. Of course, the knowledge slipped out now and then, especially when speaking of higher-ascension Blessens, but it remained a well-kept secret.

“How… do you know that?” she asked, her voice cracking slightly.

“Don’t worry about it… Just teach us. Please.”

“No—” They were geniuses. If she taught them, they would surely pick it up far easier than she ever had. She didn’t want that. This was her thing, the only thing she was truly decent at, and if they took that from her—

Anadis hit her with the biggest puppy eyes. Albus simply watched her with a flat, expectant poker face.

She shut her eyes, hesitated, then cracked one open and tried to resist. She couldn’t deny them, even if it meant giving away the only thing she was good at. “Argh! Fine…”

Anadis beamed, then hugged her. Albus joined in. Nora felt like she was melting in their embrace.

“Don’t tell Aelia,” Anadis whispered, “but you’re better than her.”

“Oh, I know.” She let out a smug snort and forced herself out of their arms. “Still, don’t think I don’t know what you’re doing. There’s one condition.”

“One condition?” Anadis echoed.

“We need to ask my master for permission. If she disagrees, I won’t teach you.”

The twins exchanged glances. “That’s going to be hard, but sure.”

“Alright, we’ll head to her house now,” Nora exclaimed. “Actually… you’re grounded, so we’ll need to convince Mom first.”

“Ugh, I’m not looking forward to it,” Anadis murmured.

“Okay, hand me your shard,” Nora said, extending her hand to Anadis.

He obliged.

The three of them stood in the backyard of her master’s house—well, mansion was a more fitting word. It hadn’t been easy to convince both their mother and their master. Mother hadn’t even wanted to hear about it; she was furious at the thought of Anadis out of her sight. Only after Nora reassured her multiple times that both she and Albus would stay with him did the woman finally relent.

As for her Attuner master, Nora couldn’t be one hundred percent sure, but she suspected the woman didn’t like Anadis much. The moment Nora mentioned that he already knew Blessens could attune, her master’s face had turned thunderous, a vein nearly popping in her forehead. Still, Nora had begged until the woman finally agreed.

If Nora was being honest with herself, part of her wished she hadn’t pushed so hard. It would have been the perfect excuse to keep attuning as the one thing that made her feel a little more special than her siblings.

Aelia was a stunning beauty with impeccable elegance, turning heads wherever she went—and now a Blessen on top of it. Noe was on his way to becoming a key political figure in Aura Cay. Impulsive and clever in equal measure, in a way only she seemed to understand.

Albus was a literal prodigy, the only person she had ever heard of blessed with a white core. And Anadis… oh, Anadis. Though his core was black, he was every bit Albus’s equal. Since childhood, he had been frighteningly brainy, learning to read and reason even before entering the Learning Hall, and relentlessly dedicated to his training.

They’ll probably get it on their first attempt… Nora thought, letting out a heavy sigh.

“Well, I’ll show you both what I do. Just watch,” she said, meeting their eyes.

The twins nodded.

“This is an Unhallowed shard, so it’ll be harder to purify than an Abominant one,” she said, wiggling her toes in the soft grass. She had insisted her brothers go barefoot too—it always worked better that way. “You have to take deep breaths and clear your mind. Let go of every thought.” Nora’s lungs filled to the brim. She held the breath, then exhaled slowly. “Doing this attunes your body to the rhythm of the waves beneath the ground.”

She shifted her stance. “Leywave pulses carry a steady, rhythmic energy that flows everywhere beneath Gaelora’s surface,” she explained. “Grullshards are the opposite—chaotic, jagged, and unstable, as if cursed by their very nature.” Closing her eyes, she continued, “Now watch. I guide the Leywaves into my body, channel them toward the Grullshard in my palms, and bring it into resonance.”

Within seconds, the shard’s cursed energy began to entrain with the planet’s pulse. The shard contorted slightly in her palm, and small wisps of crackling black smoke rose from it like water hissing on a hot pan.

This was the part she hated.

“Next, I allow the cursed essence to drain from the shard, pass through my body, and sink back into the ground,” she said. “This is where amateurs get hurt. You can burn vessels, tendons, even bone—so don’t lose focus here.”

They nodded. “Nora, I’ve never seen you so focused before,” Albus said quietly. “Or maybe just this serious. It fits you.”

“You think so?” Nora scratched the back of her neck, a little sheepish. “It’s my first time teaching this.” She smiled, then let out a slow breath, rolling her shoulders to ease the lingering tension. “Okay, now it’s your turn. You try it. To summarize: I allowed the Leywaves to enter my body, wove them into the shard, and then the Leywaves, which became divine essence inside me, pushed the shard’s cursed essence out of it and through my body. I slowly guided it back into the ground. Basically, one foot is the entrance, the other is the exit. A one-way road.”

She wiggled her toes deeper into the grass for emphasis, feeling the steady pulse beneath her soles. “Got it?”

Hours slipped by as they trained, and Nora could hardly believe it. “It’s been forever, Anadis!” she groaned, rubbing her forehead in frustration. “You too, Albus! What do you mean you both haven’t felt a single pulse yet?”

“I don’t know what you want from us,” Anadis shot back, kicking at the grass with his bare foot. “How are we supposed to feel a pulse from the ground?! There’s no logic to that.”

“It’s a matter of feel, not understanding,” Albus said, stroking his chin thoughtfully, “and yet that is the hard part.”

“You both aren’t emptying your minds well enough,” Nora tried to explain, pacing a few steps. “Just clear your head. No thoughts. Nothing at all.”

“That’s not possible!” Anadis exclaimed, throwing his hands up. “How can someone just not think?!”

She grunted, tossing her arms in the air before dropping heavily onto the grass. The pulses flooded her body at once. Her lip curled up—just a tiny bit—and she let out a soft chuckle only she could hear.

So… they’re not complete geniuses after all. She wanted to laugh boisterously, to yell it to the world that they couldn’t do it… Well, they would eventually, but not as fast and not as well as her.

As Anadis groaned and failed his fifth attempt, Nora felt something warm settle in her chest. She crossed her arms, a slow grin spreading across her face. Perhaps she was no Blessen and would never get married like Noe. She didn’t have dozens of admirers lining up like Aelia. Gods, she didn’t even have a single guy friend—but then a thought struck her.

Who needed any of that? Who needed a man when she had attuning? What she had always possessed and always would: a special connection to the planet right beneath her feet, one most Blessens couldn’t even feel. Yes. This was hers. She was unique.

Resolute, she pushed herself to her feet, brushing grass from her palms, and looked at her brothers. “Alright, let’s do this one more time. You’ll get it this time, I’m sure,” she said brightly.

“Why do you have such a smug smile on your face?” Anadis asked, narrowing his eyes.

“Oh, nothing…” She shrugged, still grinning. “I just realized I also have something special.”

His expression turned strange. “What does that even mean?”

“Don’t worry about it. Just focus on attuning!”
