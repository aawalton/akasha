Asher had expected the team to spend the next couple of days training with one another as they planned their next steps. Now that they’d truly fought beside each other in order to take down the abyssal creature, a giant swarm of demons, and even a greater demon, he had no doubt they’d all pinpointed where they could best work to improve their cohesion as a team and possibly even come up with some new ideas.

However, Rulfar had other plans.

Not long after they returned from their attack on Zygrul’s base, Rulfar ran off into the nearby forest and vanished. After he’d been gone for nearly two full days, Samantha tried her luck at tracking him down, and actually managed to find him. The bloodhound had been sleeping peacefully like an animal in the woods, and had been none too happy about Samantha waking him up. Apparently, his ability to maintain his powerful battle form for so long was actually one of his blended skills, the skill related to both Strength and Bear. While it did allow him to keep up his half-bear form for far longer than most transformation-based skills, the cost of doing so was effectively borrowing from his future strength. In practice, that meant Rulfar was utterly exhausted and far weaker than his massive bulk should be until he finished paying off his accrued debt.

Asher knew Rulfar had stayed in his battle form for quite a while, from the moment they started fighting the abyssal creature until they left Zygrul’s base, but Rulfar had been sleeping for days now. Seeing such a drastic cost to using a blended skill filled him with just the slightest bit of worry over his own new skills he’d get from the Stage of Bleed.

Of course, he still had to hit the stage first.

“I think I’m going to go assassin hunting,” Asher said the next morning, catching Samantha off-guard as she looked up from her crocheting. The temperature had been dropping more and more lately, so her latest project was a pair of gloves.

“Any reason why?” Samantha asked, nimbly continuing her work without even looking down at her fingers. “Nostalgia?”

“No,” Asher chuckled. “Though those were some fun days. With Rulfar sleeping and Moxy doing her best to kill the captain of Donvath’s royal guards, we’re not really doing a lot of sparring lately. I want to stay at the top of my game. Though more importantly, Judgement is sitting pretty at level 19, and I need one final push to get the skill up to the level cap. Stronger auras definitely help level the skill faster, and senior assassins have some of the strongest auras around.”

“If you wanted to power-level it, why not just go sneak into one of the noble districts and start people-watching?” Samantha asked, pointing at him with one of her crochet needles. “Sounds like it would be more efficient.”

“Okay, yes, I’m also feeling a bit nostalgic, as morbid as that sounds,” Asher admitted. “I don’t regret putting our team together, and I certainly don’t regret sparring with you. But there’s a difference between facing down powerful opponents entirely on my own and doing it in a team. When the greater demon grabbed me by surprise and isolated me, it sparked that hunger for battle within my chest that had dimmed over the past few weeks of working as a team. There’s just something about relying solely on one’s own skills and abilities… You know?”

“No, I don’t,” Samantha said, giving him a small smile. “Though just because I don’t share your same desire to throw yourself into deadly situations and claw your way out of them all by yourself, doesn’t mean I don’t understand it’s important to you. Not to mention Moxy claimed the other day that fighting all on one’s own actually pushes your skills to level up faster, so it’s even beneficial. Just be careful, alright? And don’t stay out too late killing assassins, our day with Brian is tomorrow.”

“And the Grand Auction is selling the new greater demon corpse the day after,” Asher nodded, tapping his head. “Don’t worry, it’s all up here! I Incorporated your calendar the other day.”

“I’d argue that’s cheating,” Samantha giggled, leaning forward and giving him a quick kiss as he stood up. “Which city are you planning to hunt in?”

“Whikoga and Poltar are still without the Guild at the moment, so not them… Maybe I’ll check out Dormaul. The Guild went to ground when I ran around killing their senior assassins before, but that was weeks ago. I can’t imagine they are willing to stay quiet forever, and I actually know the location of a few of their cells from when I hired a seeker to help me find them. Hell, I might even get lucky and get a visit from the Finger in charge of the city!”

“Try not to antagonize the Head any more than we already have,” Samantha sighed, shaking her own head as she went back to her current project. “Just because they’ve been quiet ever since you took down all the Knuckles, doesn’t mean they’ll stay quiet forever.”

“No promises!” Asher winked, Recalling away to Dormaul before Samantha could call him an idiot. Having only just chatted with Helpha the prior night, he wasn’t surprised in the slightest to find the store dark and empty, and he slipped into the astral, stepping out the front door and into Dormaul’s third level. Taking in a deep breath through his nose, he let it out his mouth, smiling wider than he had in ages.

Ah, just like the good old days. Time to hunt!

Switching his own class back to assassin, Asher left the alleyway behind, strolling through the third level as he started putting together a plan.

Most of the vacant cells we managed to track down before were on the fourth level, hidden in plain sight among the throng of tourists and warehouses deeper into the city. Might as well start there.

Grabbing the first zipline he could find, Asher shouted excitedly as he shot from the third level down to the fourth. With the sheer speed and angle of descent of the many ziplines dotting the city, he still couldn’t believe that using them was just a totally normal, ordinary part of citizen’s lives in Dormaul. Every time he witnessed an elderly person snag a zipline and step off a level, he half expected them to simply plummet to their deaths as their grip gave out.

Once he was on the fourth level again, it didn’t take him long to track down the different cells that the seeker he’d hired had led him to long ago. Having a map of Dormaul Incorporated into his head helped with that, and Asher used Astral Dip to peer through the outer walls of the different buildings. The first three cells he checked were all busts, clearly having been abandoned for a good couple of weeks now.

But the fourth one was a winner.

Asher stood within the astral, his face pressed through the wall of what looked like it was supposed to be an ore processing plant, based on the massive furnaces lining the walls. Only rather than smelting ores, he’d caught the sole person within the building red handed.

Dumping bodies into one of the large furnaces.

[Assassin] - 2/3 elements

The assassin wore ordinary work clothes, dirty leathers covered in coal and ash. His aura was rough and jagged, like a small storm of broken blades swirling around him, each one covered in innocent blood as they worked to lash out at anyone who dared get close. The man whistled a jaunty tune as he continued pulling one body after another out of a large wheelbarrow, heaving to lift them up enough to dump into the furnace. From his casual clothing and bored expression, if it weren’t for the stack of dead bodies, Asher could have believed he was watching someone just plugging away at a nine-to-five.

Tossing the last of the bodies into the furnace, the assassin wiped some sweat from his brow, smearing a fresh streak of ash across his forehead. Stretching his back, he yawned, smacking his lips as he grabbed the handles to the wheelbarrow and began bringing it away.

Well now, looks like he’s almost bored over there, doesn’t it? Asher thought to himself, pulling Nail out of his rift and grinning at the gleam on his refurbished blade. Let’s fix that, shall we?

Stepping fully through the wall, Asher returned to the material realm, watching the man walk for a few seconds before he called out. “Need any help?”

“Hm?” the man asked, turning and narrowing his eyes as he took in Asher standing there. To Asher’s surprise, the assassin wasn’t only completely unfazed at his sudden appearance, he still looked more bored than anything. His eyes swept over Asher from top to bottom, before he shook his head. “Nah, I’m good. Thanks though.”

Asher could only blink as the man turned and continued walking, picking up his whistling once more as he rolled the wheelbarrow across the floor. Expecting him to spin around and launch some sort of surprise attack any second, Asher waited. But as the seconds passed and the man plopped the wheelbarrow against the wall and turned to tug on a coat, he finally broke first.

“You are a senior assassin with the Guild, aren’t you?”

“Nah, not anymore,” the assassin said, not even looking at him as he focused solely on doing up his buttons. “Decided to retire. Some maniac, I’m assuming you, wiped out a whole third of Dormaul’s Guild a few weeks back without breaking a sweat. I’m not about throwing my life away for no reason, so I quit. I’m just doing this for fun now until they catch me. You can’t exactly quit the Guild without them coming for you.”

“For fun?” Asher repeated, staring at the man in shock before he realized exactly what he was looking at. He’d yet to actually encounter one to quite this degree, but he had no doubt he had the assassin pegged. “…You’re a sociopath, aren’t you?”

“Fancy term,” the man said, tugging on a pair of leather gloves and fixing his coat. “I joined the Guild because killing made me actually feel something, and I was good at it. But I didn’t want to die, so I left. Now I kill people on my own time. Trying to kill as many as I can before I’m caught. The Guild abandoned this building because it was compromised, but none of the guards knew about it just yet. Been using it for myself, seeing as nobody else is.”

“Okay…” Asher said, still wondering how on earth he’d gotten himself into this situation. He wanted to throw himself into the blaze of battle, not chat with a sociopath and learn his life story. “…You do realize I just saw you dumping a wheelbarrow full of dead bodies into a furnace, right? Even if I didn’t already know you were an assassin, I can’t just let you walk away.”

“You could,” the man shrugged, finally giving Asher his full attention now that he’d run out additional clothes to put on. “I told you, I don’t want to die. I don’t particularly want to go to prison, either, but I suppose that’s preferable to death. I was planning on turning myself in to the guards in a few days anyway, before the Guild managed to pinpoint my location. I could probably find a way to kill other prisoners here or there if I’m careful.”

“Why should I let you live after what you’ve done?”

“I can give you the location of the other cells that haven’t been abandoned,” the assassin offered. “If you turn me in to the guards, I’ll confess everything, and you’ll get the location of all the other assassins still operating within the Guild. Good odds you’ll even be able to find our Finger. From one killer to another, it sounds like a good deal if you ask me.”

Asher could only continue to stare at the assassin. He was still on the balls of his feet, just waiting to slip into the astral at any moment. But as far as he could tell, the assassin didn’t look like he was planning any sort of trap. The man simply stood there, looking bored as he waited for an answer. Sighing, Asher finally shook his head.

“You really don’t want to fight me? Wouldn’t that be more fun?”

“I don’t have fun fighting,” the assassin shrugged. “I have fun killing. I highly doubt I can kill you, so I see no point. Do we have a deal or not? I suppose you could just attack and kill me instead, but I’m not going to put up a fight, and I can tell you won’t like that one bit. Not to mention if you do, you won’t know where to find the others.”

Frowning, Asher shoved Nail back into his rift. He didn’t like letting someone like this go, but it was true that this was the last of the places he knew to check, and he’d need a new lead after this. And it wasn’t as if he was just letting the man walk away. He had no idea how the justice system of Dormaul worked, but if the man was truly going to admit to everything, he was undoubtedly going to be locked away for the rest of his life.

“Alright, you have a deal,” Asher said, wondering how easy it would be to infiltrate Dormaul’s prisons and finish the job later if he had to. It could be a fun side-activity some day at the very least. “Where can I find your old boss?”
