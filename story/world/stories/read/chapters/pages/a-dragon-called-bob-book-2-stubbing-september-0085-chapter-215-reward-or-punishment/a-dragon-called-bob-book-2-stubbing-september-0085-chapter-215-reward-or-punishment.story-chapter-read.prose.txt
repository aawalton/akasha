“I’m impressed,” Doris de Laney, Imperial Spymaster extraordinaire, said flatly.

“You don’t sound impressed?”

She was sorting through paperwork at a lightning pace, signatures scribbled, and documents moved like she was dealing cards.  The gems on the shoulder pads of her sci-fi jumpsuit were dimmer than last time, and her smooth skin looked ruddy.

“We’ve got a lot on our plate at the moment, Baron,” Lady Artington added from the seat next to me.  “We appreciate your putting an end to the Skeldrak invasion and sowing chaos in their society.”

“I don’t think I—”

“You started a civil war among them.  The preykin are uniting, and half the predkin are at each other's throats.  The return of Moon Shiver would be a nightmare for us, if there wasn’t one tiny factor in play as well,” Doris said absently.

“They’re at war?” I asked.  I’d figured Ali would stamp on any rebellion.  I had left her a bunch of bunnyborgs to act as enforcers, and the not-teenage ninja anthro-babes had sworn loyalty to her.

“Not yet, but it is only a matter of time.  The rats, of all things, seem to have come into a supply of Imperial-grade enchanted weapons, somehow.  Know anything about that?” Doris fixed me with a glare.  She was remarkably good at glaring for a human.

“No idea.”

“Hmm.  Our intelligence suggests that there are as-yet unmet conditions on the peace between them and us?”

“It’s a matter of… heads.”

Doris raised an eyebrow at me and intensified her glare.  She’d make an impressive dragon.  She lay down her pen and steepled her fingers under her chin.  “Whose?”

“Well, that’s the billion-gold question isn’t it?”

“No member of the imperial family will be accepting responsibility or offering any body parts,” Artington said quickly.

“Not even if they were involved in the trade?  And it wasn’t for the arena, or not mostly, at any rate.  They were taking young, attractive women with fluffy tails.  It was pervy nobles.”

“Which nobles in particular?”

“Well, first of all, there’s Hateskale.”

“Kill him.  We don’t care.”

“He’s only been in power for a little while, Doris,” Artington said softly.  “He may not have been directly involved.”

“Either way, I’m killing him at the Battle of Hairy-Bulge or whatever stupid name the big fight is going to take place is called.  He’s an easy one.”

“Agreed.  Next?”  Doris raised the other eyebrow to match her first.

“Dimpleworn.”

“That’s… more problematic,” Doris said, shaking her head slightly.

I leaned back and admired the antique artwork that lined the walls of the spymaster's study.  Long dead men and women, dying heroically.  Or at least in a bloody and violent fashion.

“Nobles die all the time.”  I shrugged.  “Why is this one a problem?”

“Lord Dimpleworn is currently the head of the small council.  He is functionally the head of state.”

“He’s Lozenge’s boss?”

“Not exactly.  He is the head of the noble faction, the counter to the imperial faction.  And since the Madson’s father earned something of a bad name, the small council has essentially been running the empire.  He is also the leader of the shadow supporting side in the civil war.”

“What about the people’s faction?” I asked.

“The what?  United, the nobles are more powerful than the imperial faction and prevent His Majesty from taking any major decisions that they disapprove of.”

“Thanks, Jemima.  Was that a hint of annoyance I heard in your voice at how it holds your boss back?”  I sighed.  “So it’s two party system.  Those are always great.  The guy was involved in the furry girl trade, so the furries want his head.”  I shrugged.

“His Majesty cannot in any way be involved in the removal of a troublesome noble,” Doris said flatly, watching me closely.

“So you want me to do your dirty work for you, again?”

“Hardly, Bob!”  Artington slapped my arm hard enough that I felt it.  “But we won’t attempt to interfere if you do decide to… take matters into your own claws.”

“Hateskale is fine, Dimpleworn, I won’t get any help, but you guys won’t help me,” I muttered, clenching my fists against my legs as my mind ran through my possibilities.

“Oh, we just can’t be seen to help.  Information is power, Baron.”  Doris slid a file across the desk.  I picked it up and leafed through.

“This is weirdly thorough.  Why the hell did you check… Oh, sweet baby Jesus, there are pictures!”  I slammed the file shut.  “I’ll kill that dude for free; it’s not often murder is a karmic positive.”

“If you want to clean up the nobility, I’d be happy to provide you with some more files.  Dimpleworn won’t be easy to reach, but there may be some things I can help with there as well.  Who is the next target?” Doris asked coolly, as though discussing assassinations was par for the course.

For her, it probably was.  This petite woman of indeterminable age held the reins to one of the largest information networks on Helstat.  Behind her bland face and cold eyes lurked a mastermind, one that I did not wish to piss off.

“The third one is just some nobody.  One of the mercenary captains who worked with the bears.”

“If you give me the name, I can—”

“Thanks, Doris, but I’m good.  Already got a bead on him.”  It was going to have to be the fleshsculptor.  Making an enemy of the Imperial household was not on my bucket list.  As offensive as the thought was, I couldn’t deal with creatures like Doris or the emperor's immortals.  Yet.  Maybe one day.  The thought of what the imperial treasury might look like flashed through my mind, and Greed began… being Greed in my mind.  Bad dragon.  Not yet.

“Now, what about my rewards?” I asked, smiling broadly.

“What did we agree to?”

“Erm… I don’t seem to recall any definitive terms?”

“I will make Mrs Sanderson available to your pixie friend.  She will do wonders to streamline your operation.”

“She’s your spy!” I accused.  The only logical reason for a spymaster providing me with staff would be to insert an agent into my midst.  Or my team's midst.  Whatever.

“She’s an adherent of She Who Must Be Thrice Blessed And Stamped.  No priestess of Karen betrays their legitimate authority,” Doris replied.

“Aha!  And what higher authority than the imperial house is there in an empire?”

“Baron, Bob, if I wanted to insert a spy in your service, the last thing I would do is propose them myself.”

“It’s the perfect cover.”

“It’s moronic.  I’ve already got spies in your dungeon anyway.”  She shrugged, ignoring my level nine glare.  “I keep tabs on everyone; it’s nothing personal.”

“She is very good.  You’ve never seen bureaucrat magic in action.  It’s impressive,” Artington added.

“I’ve seen it.  I killed a High Priestess of Karen when I was clearing one of the early floors in my dungeon.  It was horrific.  Practically a warcrime.  I hate paperwork.”

“And she will take it off your hands. Or Lady Ekaterina’s hands, at any rate.”

“Fine.  Graciously accepted.”  I ignored Doris’s sceptical expression.  “What else?”

A piece of card was slid across the table, which I snatched in annoyance.

“Who the hell is Narble the Novice and… Longlens the Leerer?  Look, I know Kat mentioned porn last time we were here, but that really wasn’t my plan.”

“Narble is an upcoming mentalist.  He can read minds and transcribe what he finds onto recording crystals.  Longlens… Farseers are rare, and he’s the only one you’ve got a shot at hiring.  We looked into your movie concept.  Spoke to a few outremondes in the Immortals.  We’re interested in supporting this venture, in exchange for some considerations on your part.”

“No subtexts criticising the administration?”

“Exactly.”

“I don’t give a shit about the administration.  No offence.  These guys can help?”

“They will be your camera and film.”

“You have done your research.”

“Baron, I keep tabs on thousands of nobles, murderers, entrepreneurs, criminals, mercenary groups, mages and statisticians.  This didn’t really count as research.

“Why did statisticians make your list?” I asked.

“Can’t trust ‘em, Bob.  Can’t bloody trust them.  People who love patterns that much are… strange.”

“Well, if you’ll excuse me, I’ve got some more meetings to attend to this afternoon.  I’ll see myself out.”

“Good luck, Baron.  Please let us know when you’ve settled affairs with the Skeldrak.”

“No worries, Doris.  Lady Artington.”  I nodded to each of them, then stepped backwards into a portal that opened into the courtyard outside the Long Horn.  I looked around at the pub, the whitewashed walls and freshly repaired roof felt like a home away from home for some reason.  I looked at the piece of card in my hand and grimaced.

What to do first?  Plan to assassinate a head of state or go and see a man about a head.  Or go and have a drink?

I pushed the doors aside and entered the smoky gloom of the main bar.  I received a few nods from some of the locals, but none of them made me feel anymore welcome than that.

Beville smiled from behind his counter and reached for the good stuff he kept beneath the bar, pouring a generous measure into a crystal tumbler that was pushed towards me as I parked myself upon an unnecessarily plush barstool.  I ignored the flush of irritation I felt at being reminded of Esme’s spending spree.

“Congratulations!” the barlord intoned, raising a glass of his own.  We clinked, and I gave him a confused look.

“What for?  I’m in a bloody pickle, Beville.  All my choices are risky at best, and I need to do some unsavoury things.  Maybe I should hand the movie idea over to Kat and let her run with it while I sort out the shitty stuff.”

“Your egg hatched?”

“Oh.  Yeah.  Thanks, Bev.  He’s a good kid.  He likes the booze, but he doesn’t swear too much.”

“The baby?”

“Yeah.  Dragons are born with old souls.”

“But… well, what’s his name?”

“Morris.”

“Morris, son of Bob.  A good name.”  Bevel nodded sagely.

“I don’t think he wasn’t my surname?  I don’t know, Esme and Kat have been wrangling him most of the time.  Little bastard is fast, though, I’ll give him that. If he gets a dimensional storage space, I’m going to have to lock down half the dungeon.”  I smiled faintly.

“Well, I’m glad it’s working out for you, boss.  I’ve been meaning to ask, any chance of a bump in the budget?”

“Mr Robertson, please don’t answer that question until I’ve had a chance to go over the company’s books.  Mr de Sackville, I am Mrs Sanderson.  You may call me Mrs Sanderson.  A third stage Initiate of Our Lady of the Paperwork.”

I turned my head slowly.  A chorus of “blessed in triplicate” had gone up from the people who heard her declaration, and many Holy Stamping gestures were made.

“Mrs Sanderson.  I hope the disagreement between myself and your former employer won’t be an issue.”

“Of course not, Mr Robertson.  My lady, may she forever have ink and stamp, has assigned me a challenging quest, but I will see it through.”

That sounded ominous.  She’d said it in a completely neutral tone of voice.  Everything about the woman was neutral.  Hairstyle, beige clothing, minimal makeup, even the heels on her shoes were a neutral half-inch.

“And what task is that?”

“Bringing the good word of paperwork to a soul as lost as yours.  We will require weekly catch-up meetings once things are settled, but to begin, I’m afraid I must insist on a three-week program to ground you in the basics, then we will look at specialist programs for your weaker areas so we can expand your love and knowledge of correct filing and proper formatting.”

“Speak to Kat.  She’s your girl.  I’ve got people to murder for the furries.”
