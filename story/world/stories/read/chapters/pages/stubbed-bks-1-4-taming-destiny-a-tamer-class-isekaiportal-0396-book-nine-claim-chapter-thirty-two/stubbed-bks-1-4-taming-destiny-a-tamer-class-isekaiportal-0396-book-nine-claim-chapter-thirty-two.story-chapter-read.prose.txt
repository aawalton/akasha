Are you sure this is going to work? Loran asks me fretfully. At least he keeps it private – we don’t need fear taking hold among our Bond network. There’s enough trepidation as it is; only the knowledge that this is the only chance any of us have is keeping us all going.

It has to, I reply grimly, refusing to look at the space between us and the waiting behemoth – the space that’s all too rapidly diminishing. Just make sure those on the ship are doing their jobs, and we’ll be fine.

He’s not convinced, and he can no doubt feel my own uncertainty, but I feel him send an acknowledgement and draw his attention away from the Bond. That frees me to once more focus entirely on my own task.

Controlling water under a Tier five’s influence is significantly more difficult than normal – only the fact that I’m not trying to separate one section of water from another allows me to succeed. It would be significantly easier if I were in contact with it, but I’m not willing to risk being trapped.

I lean forwards and peer past Noir’s shoulder to see what we’ve done so far – and what we still have to accomplish. Shifting my weight backwards again, apprehension settles heavily in my stomach. Not enough, is my answer.

But none of us could think of any better plan, not one that didn’t involve sacrificing one or more ships anyway. Fear that we’ve made the wrong decision gnaws at my gut – better to lose a ship than to lose all of our lives – and to a beast to whom we are probably no more than a snack. That, though it grates, is what we’re banking on to survive this.

If a Tier five wants to consume us, there’s nothing we can really do to stop it, not in the middle of the ocean. But if the Tier five is targeting the carcass of the recently-slain Tier four roc and we’re just extra toppings…. It’s a chance. And it’s all we have.

Frustratingly, our assumption that the Tier five’s focus is on the roc means that I don’t dare take its Core for fear the behemoth will sense its absence and pursue us. Still, I didn’t think it would do much harm if I allowed Bastet to meditate with it in the meantime, especially since there wasn’t any way of her helping in the current situation – fire is the last thing we need right now.

Kalanthia was disgruntled not to be able to do the same, but she was needed elsewhere. Just a little ahead of Elarion and I, to be exact.

I need to get closer, I urge Noir, and my flying mount dives towards the leading edge of our last-ditch effort.

Layton and Josiah, along with half of the Classers we found who are capable of controlling water, ice, or earth, are packed into a couple of the most intact rowboats from the ships. One is at the head; the other one is at the talons.

Glancing sideways, I glimpse Elarion’s expression, his pressed lips and the lines of strain on his face telling their own tale as he stands in his own row boat with his hands hovering only inches away from the surface of the water.

Freezing the domain-touched water is no doubt even harder than redirecting it, but he’s a powerful enough Ice Mage that he’s succeeding, especially with the help of the rest of us to cool the water and move it into the right positions. Then Ivor dives to once more breathe ice-shards onto the construction ahead of us and I lose sight of the other Great Heir.

But I can’t fail to see his efforts. Ice roots spear upwards from the thick spike of rock Kalanthia has grown out from the head of the roc. Forming tree-like shapes above the water, I know that the roots extend just as far below. Behind us are more trees, the ridge of icebergs appearing like a hedgerow chiseled out of crystal. I’d be stunned by the beauty if I weren’t so concerned about the speed of our efforts.

I look over my shoulder at the feet-end of the carcass. There floats the second rowboat that contains the rest of the Classers capable of controlling water, ice, or rock. They’re attempting to do the same job as we are on this side, but my stomach tightens when I see how much slower they are.

Anxiously, I spare a glance at the water that remains between us and the sucking mouth and the knot in my stomach tightens further.

“We’re not going to have time!” I call to Elarion. “I’m going to the other side.”

He barely spares me a glance, but it’s enough to tell me he’s heard – and that his hopes of this working are dropping as quickly as mine are.

Noir, take us to the other end of the roc. Fast.

The alcaoris sends me back wordless, grim acknowledgement, but twists in the air and shoots to the far end of the roc’s carcass. We considered trying to turn it so its wings could create the barrier we need, but we decided against that – we need a bulwark more than just a barrier and its body offers a better anchor point than its wings would, for all they’re longer.

It’s still more than twice the length of any of our ships; so it will have to do. To speed our journey, I tap into my Air-Shaping to reduce air resistance.

On our way past, I glance at the ships, trying to assess how close they are to their assigned positions. I have to force my teeth to unclench before the tightness of my jaw risks cracking one – that’s the last thing I need. At least they’re moving, if not fast enough. Given how many vessels are struggling with sailing or steering, we decided to separate into two groups. Those that can sail will pull those that can’t.

I’m relieved to see that my own ship is almost in place – without a proper captain to organise the efforts, I know Sarran has had his hands full.

At least I can see the effects on the current of water below – the flow on our side of the ice wall is slower and weaker than that on the other side. Of course, it’s not gone completely – that would make it too easy. But I can already see how it’s helping the ships shift faster into the right positions.

Glancing to the other side of Noir and at the roc’s body itself, my stomach rises into my mouth as I see the enchanters and alchemists still at work. They were supposed to be done already! I think to myself with dismay. There must have been a snag somewhere – or the Tier four’s meat is more difficult to freeze without spoiling the meat outright. After all, as tempting as it is, if we damage the Tier five’s meal, we’ll probably end up on the menu in its place. But in its current, flexible state, it’s no use to us. I can only hope they get back to the ships in time.

Time…our most precious resource right now – and the one in shortest supply. I swallow as I see how close we are – within a candlemark I would judge. And that’s with everyone who isn’t otherwise occupied working hard to slow the speed of our forward progress, mage or sailor alike. Even the mage who signed up at the same time as Vellus is there, using her songs to encourage and speed people as they work. And Aingeal is powering the hot air balloon, the angle of the rope pulling back on our ship. But given how much we still have to do….

The only good thing is that my fear the Tier five would become impatient and increase the pressure of its domain has proven itself groundless – it seems that the Emperor is willing to be patient as long as it will eventually be fed. That dovetails rather nicely with its apparent hunting strategy – to draw its prey towards it.

I also feared that the Tier five would notice the other measures we’ve taken, but Kalanthia reassured me on those grounds. Her opinion was that as long as it doesn’t feel under threat and there’s progress, it won’t care. And so far, nothing has happened that has contradicted her words. Nothing here is likely to hurt it, or do more than delay it for more than a few minutes. But if we’re lucky, if this works, then a few minutes will be all we need.

Reaching the other side of the roc, I direct Noir to dive closer to where the group of Mages are working. The wall of icebergs on this side is far less impressive or beautiful than on the other. It’s unsurprising: there have been fewer Mages working here, and less powerful ones besides.

Quickly working out where I would be most useful, I pull out a mana potion and prepare to throw it back as soon as my mana pool hits half-empty. It’ll be my fifth of the morning. Sera Kerst, River’s mentor, provided us with cleansing potions that are awful to use, but that purge our bodies of residue from the potions and allow us to take more of them – within limits, she warned.

Sinking my awareness into the spur of rock that the nearby Earth Mage has created, I pour mana into it. This, at least, isn’t affected by the Tier five’s domain, making it easier than Shaping the water.

Though it costs more than limestone, given how important this spur is in anchoring our bulwark, I grit my teeth and focus on creating granite. It’s even more expensive mana-wise to create than lava would be, but I dismissed that option ages ago – heat is the last thing we need right now.

I empty my mana while watching the spur of rock grow. As soon as I hit rock bottom, nausea curdling in my stomach and a headache throbbing behind my eyes, I throw back another mana-recovery potion to keep going.

I grimace as I see how much is still to be done – I’ll need to take another cleansing potion, I think. But we don’t have time for me to take a fifth – if I even dare do so. The cleansing potions are damaging in their own way and I can already feel the strain.

As always when I’m focusing deeply, time slips by in odd ways, marked only by the distance between us and the cavernous maw waiting to consume us that creeps ever closer.

Markus! I hear, almost a shout. Loran.

What? I croak, even my mind almost too tired to form the message.

The spurs on both sides extend far enough, he tells me and I send back grateful acknowledgement, switching tasks with relief. It’s not a break, but at least it’s something different – and visible progress. Now we just need to increase the depth of the ice attached to the rock spurs and to the carcass of the Tier four.

As expected, Elarion’s side is far further ahead on that than we are on this end. Revulsion going through me, I pull out the tar-like cleansing potion and hold it for a moment, trying to summon up the will to swallow it.

Closing my eyes, I flick the cork out with my thumb and tip it back. Though its taste is only vaguely unpleasant – something between charcoal and slightly-off fish – it coats my tongue and throat thickly enough that it feels like it might prevent me from breathing.

Then, worse, its equally-thick magic sinks into my body and pulls at the remnants of the other potions. Pain shoots through me at its ungentle tugging and then nausea is all that I can feel.

Leaning over Noir’s shoulder, I vomit everything that’s inside me, black tar matted with all sorts of other colours pouring out of my throat and into the open sea beneath.

Even when the convulsions have stopped, a thin film of disgusting liquid seems to coat my mouth and throat, one that refuses to move even when I suck back some water from my flask. But at least the throbbing behind my eyes has reduced.

Grimly, I consume another mana-recovery potion. What does it matter if I feel like death when actual death awaits us if we don’t all push ourselves as far as we humanly can?

Though I can’t make ice directly, I tap into Fire Shaping to draw heat out of the water around the rock spur, making it easier for the Ice Mage to freeze the water.

It feels like forever, and at the same time only minutes before there’s the call for all mages in boats to return to their ships. At least that’s easier in the lee of the ice wall.

I’m not in a boat so I continue even when everyone else departs. I’m not an Ice Mage, but my method of pulling out heat is pretty effective, though much slower now I’m doing it all myself rather than just cooling the water down to allow an actual Ice Mage to do their job.

-kus! Markus! You’re too close! Please listen to me!

Loran’s frantic voice cuts through my focus with the desperation of someone who’s already given the same message multiple times with no response and is starting to fear he won’t get through before it’s too late.

I blink groggily – my brain hurts like something is inside it with a hammer. But as I look around, my eyes widen. The behemoth’s maw is all I can see – I’m almost inside it.

Even this close, I still can’t see anything beyond a certain point – it’s like the water disappears into nothing. As if it is unmade rather than consumed.

Something, my soul, perhaps, shivers and shrivels up inside me. This close, the beast’s domain is pounding on me, threatening to make me freeze once more – not because it is trying to cow me, but simply because it is that intimidating, even without trying.

Noir, quickly – we need to get back, I manage to instruct the alcaoris. My faithful companion needs no more urging, twisting on his tail and shooting back towards the ships as fast as his wings can take him.

Though, as I feel the air cling at us in a mimicry of the stickiness of the water below, I pray we haven’t left it too late already like we did the escape for our ships….
