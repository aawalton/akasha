To Ace, the silence was deafening.

A small fire crackled in the center of their makeshift camp, casting long shadows across the tense faces of the three other surviving heroes in Ace’s team. Gray tendrils of smoke twisted upward into the predawn darkness, carrying the burning char of pine into the still air. The forest loomed around them, the trees little more than a wall of black silhouettes standing like sentinels at the edges of their tiny clearing.

Above, a cluster of clouds passed in front of the moon, and its sudden absence plunged the surrounding woods into even deeper darkness. Each breath fogged in the chill, and each slight movement was amplified in the unnatural stillness of the forest. No birds called, no insects chirped—just the occasional snap from the fire and the soft sound of four people breathing, each lost in their own thoughts about what awaited them in the Crucible, each wondering whom they could truly trust when only one could claim the prize.

This was it.

The moment of truth—either they would stick together, or greed and mistrust would rip them apart.

“I’m staying with the team,” Tara said finally. "We’ve survived this long together. I’m not abandoning anyone. Like I told you before, I’ve tried being alone. It’s not for me.”

"Agreed.” Marcus nodded. “Whatever happens with the Keystone, we’ll face it together when the time comes.”

There was a flicker of doubt that radiated from their telepath, but it quickly faded. Once again, he poked at the fire with a stick, lost in thought.

Ace shifted his attention to Olivia, who remained motionless for several seconds before offering a single nod. “The Fang Gang remains intact.”

For now.

The thought echoed through him in her voice, as if she had spoken only to him, but her gaze drifted down toward the fire.

Holy shit.

He had no idea how he was sensing these things.

“Good,” he managed to say. “Then we stick to the plan. Enter the Crucible together. Watch each other’s backs. As a team.”

Across the fire, Ace felt a flicker of anxiety flutter through Tara. He had the inkling she was about to speak, and he glanced up at her before the words could leave her mouth. She faltered, her eyes scanning his face, but ultimately plowed ahead.

“What the hell is wrong with you?” she asked

He let out a dry laugh. “Can you be more specific?”

From his spot beside the fire, Marcus chuckled.

“You’re on edge,” Tara pointed out, ignoring Marcus. “Tense. You keep looking at everyone right before they speak, as if you know when they’re going to talk and what they’re going to say.”

Ace rubbed his temple, inwardly debating whether or not to answer that—or, rather, how to even answer that. This was a new and utterly foreign sensation, something he didn’t even understand himself.

How to explain it—well, he had no fucking clue.

He stared into the fire, watching the flames dance along the burning wood and casting light that never seemed to push back enough darkness.

“Something’s... happening to me,” he finally said. “Since the Alpha Slug. I can feel emotions that aren’t mine. I can sense action and thought before it happens.”

Olivia shifted slightly, and a brittle kind of curiosity brushed against his consciousness as she stood taller. Before she even leaned forward, he knew she was about to speak. The sensation felt like frost spreading across meadowgrass—delicate, beautiful, and dangerously cold.

“Define ‘things,’" she said, her voice clinical despite the predatory interest he sensed beneath it.

“Emotions. Physical states.” Ace looked directly at Marcus, whose knee had been bothering him since their mad scramble up the cliff wall during the slug battle. “Your left knee has been grinding since yesterday. Every time you put weight on it, there’s a spike of pain."

Marcus stiffened, his hand instinctively moving toward the joint before he caught himself. "I didn't tell anyone about that."

Ace shrugged. "You didn't have to. I just… know."

“Well, that’s not creepy as hell or anything,” Marcus muttered.

“That’s the Party system at play,” Tara said with a casual flick of her wrist. “We can all sense each other in some way.”

“Not like this.” Ace shook his head, still trying to find the right words. “This is… this is potent. I’m not sure how else to put it. This transcends the sensations I felt before.”

A wave of unease rippled through the group. Ace could feel their reactions like changes in the air pressure—Marcus's alarm spiraling outward, Olivia’s detached curiosity narrowing to a laser focus, and Tara's concern kicking in as she studied his face with newfound intensity.

Their healer shifted around the fire, inching closer, her curiosity apparently overriding her wariness.

“Your pupils are dilating strangely," she observed, leaning in to examine his eyes. "Like they're responding to stimuli none of us can see."

As she scrutinized him, Ace experienced the peculiar double awareness of seeing her concern and feeling it simultaneously, like viewing the same landscape from two different angles. Her proximity sent another sensation through him—worry layered with something warmer, more complicated.

“Explain what you’re feeling,” she said gently.

“I’m not sure I can.”

“Well, try.”

He paused, frowning as he racked his brain for ideas on how to word this.

"It’s like echoes," he finally said, struggling to put the experience into words. “Not thoughts exactly. More like... I don’t know. Are they impressions, maybe? Ripples. It’s as if the Party connection has gotten far stronger, somehow. Before, I could feel a flicker of anger here or a moment of surprise there, but now? Now it’s intense. Visceral. Kind of… raw.”

Marcus’s brow furrowed. “Like the slug hivemind? I mean, remember how they moved? A perfect unit. Different bodies, one awareness.”

"Interesting,” Olivia stated, her tone flat despite the fascination Ace could sense churning beneath her composed exterior.

Hivemind.

The term landed like a stone in still water, sending ripples of recognition through all of them.

“How is that even possible?” Marcus asked.

“Maybe it’s the Party system.” Tara tapped her finger against her bottom lip as her eyes glazed over in thought. “We’re already connected, and Ace killed the slug alpha. Maybe the hivemind is an EXP Solute.”

“It’s possible,” Olivia admitted with a shrug. “Can you sense anyone outside the party?”

Ace gave her a half-hearted salute. “If we encounter anyone trying to kill us, I’ll let you know.”

She chuckled.

He opened his mouth to continue, but something changed in the air.

The shift was subtle—a disruption in the forest’s natural rhythm that Ace might have missed if not for his enhanced gear. His boots detected movement where there should be none, weight distributions that didn't match the forest’s natural settling and scuttle. The vibrations traveled through the earth in patterns too regular and deliberate to be anything but footsteps.

A prickling sensation crawled up the back of his neck.

They were being watched.

This awareness felt different from his emotional connections to his teammates—sharper, more immediate, tied to his physical senses yet somehow enhanced beyond them. Through the corner of his eye, Ace scanned the treeline.

There—a subtle disturbance in the underbrush approximately twenty yards away.

He raised his hand slightly, and the others went instantly silent. It was a rapid response, faster than it would’ve been if they’d simply noticed his movement, and he yet again wondered what the limits were on this new hivemind ability of his—if that really could explain what was happening to him.

The others froze in place, their bodies instantly alert. His attention shifted toward Olivia, mainly because she was his fellow melee fighter. When their eyes met, he tapped two fingers against his thigh—their signal for potential hostiles.

“We have company,” he whispered, voice barely audible. “Don't make any sudden movements. I can sense three, maybe four, people circling our position."

“How do you know?” Marcus whispered.

“The boots. They track vibrations.”

“That’s fucking awesome,” their telepath whispered.

In unison, everyone else shushed him.

Careful not to make it obvious, they all inched closer to each other so that they could easily form a defensive circle should the need arise. They’d practiced this maneuver plenty of times before, and it was slowly becoming instinctive.

Come together.

Unite.

Fight as one.

“Options?” Olivia asked quietly, in a voice that was almost impossible to hear even this close to her.

Before Tara could speak, Ace felt her thoughts forming—not through words, but through impressions of movement, escape, territory. He knew with absolute certainty what she was about to suggest.

“You think we should keep moving?” he asked, turning to her. “Use the darkness as cover to reach more defensible terrain?"

Tara’s eyes widened. “God, this new ability of yours is fucking weird.”

“Thanks,” he said dryly.

The snap of a twig silenced them instantly. Whatever was hunting them had drawn closer, and they had to focus.

Marcus extinguished their fire with a handful of dirt, plunging them into darkness. Ace’s enhanced vision adjusted within seconds, the world shifting to shades of gray and muted blues as he scanned the gloom. The forest around them transformed—shadows had gained depth, branches had become sharp silhouettes against the star-speckled sky, and the faintest movement became visible just at the edge of what he could see through the towering oaks and aspen trees.

“Alright, listen up.” Ace drew Moonfall. The sword hummed in his grip, its silver surface reflecting the dying embers of their fire. “Right now, we need to—”

He stopped mid-sentence as his boots detected a change in the vibrational patterns shooting through the soil. Their pursuers weren’t merely circling anymore—they were closing in with a coordinated effort.

The movements were too organized and too strategic to be random predators.

"They’re hunting us,” he realized aloud. “And they know exactly what they’re doing.”

"Form up,” Ace whispered. “Triangle defense. Marcus at center.”

They all instantly obeyed. Tara and Olivia flanked Ace as they formed a protective perimeter around Marcus. The telepath’s powers made him their most vulnerable member in close combat, but also their greatest strategic advantage if given time to focus.

Ace let Moonfall hang at his side, the blade humming with quiet anticipation. In his peripheral vision, he caught Tara palming something from her medical pouch—not one of her healing vials, but something darker that he hadn’t noticed her use before. Olivia held her makeshift staff in front of her, holding it at an angle that would allow for instant strikes in any direction.

A peculiar sensation washed over Ace—Marcus’s fear pulsing outward like waves from a stone dropped in still water. Before the telepath could speak, Ace turned to his left, eyes fixing on a shadow that seemed to move against the natural flow of darkness.

“I see it too,” he said, answering Marcus’s unvoiced alarm.

Marcus’s confusion brushed against his consciousness. “I didn’t say anything."

“You didn’t have to.”

Ace sensed Tara shifting her weight, her head following something in the forest that the others hadn’t noticed yet. He adjusted his stance to cover the area she would temporarily expose as her shoulders pivoted slightly. The synchronized dance happened without verbal coordination, drawing a sharp glance from Olivia, whose cold expression he could feel even without seeing her face.

“Your perception is expanding,” she observed quietly.

Before Ace could respond, his boots detected multiple vibrational patterns converging on their position—no longer circling but approaching. The signatures didn’t match the heavy tread of Forgeborn hunters or the chaotic movements of the monsters they had encountered thus far. These vibrations carried a different quality—light, controlled, and disturbingly methodical.

“They're coming," he whispered. "Multiple targets, synchronized. Something different."

"Different, how?" Tara asked, voice barely audible.

A wave of inexplicable dread washed over him from Tara. “Organized. Professional.”

Movement flickered at the edge of the clearing, too quick to track. Then it came again, from a different angle this time. The forest fell completely silent—no insects chirping, no animals moving in the underbrush, as if the natural woodland itself held its breath in anticipation.

“Marcus,” Ace murmured, “can you—"

“I'm trying,” the telepath hissed. "Can’t get a lock. They’re... shielded somehow.”

Moonlight suddenly streamed through the canopy as clouds shifted overhead, bathing their small camp in silver light. The newfound glow revealed what darkness had concealed—they were completely surrounded.

Seven figures stood in a perfect circle at the edge of the trees. Unlike the ragged Forgeborn or feral predators they’d encountered before, these vampires wore elegant attire that seemed untouched by the wilderness. Their aristocratic bearing and imperious expressions marked them as something far more dangerous than common hunters.

At their center stood a woman whose presence dominated the clearing without effort. Her white-blonde hair cascaded like liquid silver in the moonlight, framing a face of such perfect symmetry that it crossed the threshold from beautiful to unsettling. Her eyes glowed crimson, not with hunger but with the patient anticipation of a predator who never doubted the outcome of the hunt.

This was clearly a woman who had won plenty of battles before.

As she stepped forward, Ace experienced a bizarre sensation—emptiness. Unlike his teammates, whose emotions radiated like heat from flames, he sensed nothing from her. No fear, no anticipation, no malice—just a perfect void where emotional signals should exist.

Interesting. If this hivemind ability really was evolving for him, it only seemed to apply to his team.

The silver-haired woman’s lips curved into a smile that held neither warmth nor mercy as her gaze swept over their small band. Her fangs flashed in the moonlight as her grin widened. She inhaled deeply, as if savoring their scent, before her attention fixed on Ace.

“Look at all these baby vampires,” she purred, her voice musical yet laced with venom. “And with so much precious loot.”

Her companions shifted, their hands grabbing their weapons with the casual confidence of those unaccustomed to defeat.

“A Moonfall blade, some Blood-Bonded armor, and… what’s this?” Her eyes drifted over Tara, hesitating a moment longer than necessary. “You found yourselves a healer? My, my, what a fascinating little group this is.”

Ace frowned, bristling protectively as he angled himself in front of Tara.

“And a brave leader to top it all off.” The woman’s gaze returned to him. “So many treasures wasted on mere fledglings.”

“What do you want?” Ace asked in a flat tone.

“Your gear,” she said simply. “And your healer. I could use some more EXP Solutes, but I’m feeling generous today. So, you have two choices. Option one is to surrender everything—along with your lovely little medic—through a trade and walk away alive.”

“Or?” Olivia challenged in a dark voice.

“Or choose option two, get slaughtered where you stand, and lose it all anyway,” the woman said with a careless shrug. “Makes no difference to me.”

The tension in the air thickened. The swish of metal on leather cut through the air, and Ace glanced through his periphery as one of the enemy vampires drew her sword.

“Hmm.” The silver-haired woman snapped her fingers impatiently. “Choose quickly, little ones. My generosity has limits, and my appetite does not.”

Ace tightened his grip on Moonfall, feeling his team’s readiness through their connection. In that silent exchange of determination, he found his answer.

“Let’s see what you’ve got,” he said with a mischievous grin. “I could use some EXP Solutes, too.”
