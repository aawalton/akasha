Hel laughed—not loud or boisterous. Anadis doubted she was even capable of that. Instead, she let out soft little chuckles, as if she were trying her best to maintain her dignity.

Anadis sighed, then grunted, drilling his index fingers into his temples. He paced around Eljudnir, the shallow water rippling with each shift of his legs, faint wisps of souls brushing against his skin and leaving a lingering chill in their wake, though he was already getting accustomed to them.

“This isn’t a laughing matter,” he grumbled, looking down at himself. “We’re back in Eljudnir, and I’m in my damn jammies.” He grimaced at the cloudy painted designs Aelia had added to them.

“Well, that’s what you get for being so inattentive,” Hel retorted, still chortling as she hovered in front of his face.

“This is actually your fault, you know,” Anadis shot back. “I was about to set the return point with the blue anchor when you interrupted me.”

She gasped dramatically, tiny hands flying to her cheeks. “You dare blame your own oversights on me? Unbelievable.” For once, she didn’t look or sound truly insulted. A mischievous glint sparked in her eyes as she darted forward and landed lightly on the stone slab. “Perhaps there is some good in this after all. This is an excellent opportunity to teach you how to absorb essence properly and strengthen your Divine Core.”

“I suppose,” Anadis groaned again. “It was a good thing I had my satchel on me, or we wouldn’t even be able to do this much.” He sat on the stone slab and spilled the Grullshards out of the satchel.

“Is this the one you were absorbing?” Hel asked, hovering over the greenish shard that belonged to the beetle Grull.

“Yes. I was working on it for about two hours, and it gave me—”

“You don’t have to tell me,” Hel said, palm open. “I can tell exactly how much essence you gained and how much is left.”

“Really?”

“Of course. I’m part of your soul, so I know these things even better than you.” She picked up the shard, glanced between it and him, then continued, “Let’s see… you didn’t extract too much essence from the Grullshard, and your conversion rate is about 9.6%.”

“I believe so. I couldn’t take any more—my body felt like it was rejecting it.”

“I’ll explain what it all means. Pay attention. I don’t tend to repeat myself,” Hel said, her tone crisp and deliberate.

“I understand.”

“First, the Essence Capacity is how much essence a Blessen’s Divine core can accumulate. The fuller it is, the stronger you’ll feel and the closer you’ll be to ascending into an Emanant.”

“The Divine Voice said mine was ten thousand,” Anadis explained. “Is that how much I need to ascend?”

“Yes,” she said, nodding and folding her tiny arms across her chest. “Every Blessen’s Divine core has the same maximum accumulation of ten thousand, regardless of core color. That is the threshold for ascension. Understand?”

Anadis nodded.

“Good. Ascension progress is how far you are from ascending. It needs to be at one hundred percent. Simple enough, right?”

“Mine is at 0.024%. That sounds like it’ll take me years to reach Emanant and impossible for anything higher than that.”

“That’s how it usually is,” Hel agreed, her expression stoic and offering no comfort. “Next, we’re going to discuss the Conversion rate.”

Anadis groaned, already anticipating how bad she would rate his. “Albus and Sevran already told me theirs: 96% and 30%. Mine’s only 9.6%.”

“That’s right.”

“What makes mine so much worse than theirs?”

“Your Divine core, of course.”

“Explain.”

She eyed him with mild condescension.

“Please,” he added, smiling.

“Good boy.”

Anadis shook his head.

Hel gestured toward the beetle shard. “Tell me—when you absorb essence, where do you first feel it go?”

“Into… my core, I guess.” He frowned, trying to recall the sensation. “Where else would it go?”

“Wrong. It goes through your soul thread first. The thread filters the essence before it ever reaches your core.” She tapped his chest firmly. “A white core has one thick, unbroken thread. Essence moves through it almost without resistance. Your thread—” she paused, something flickering behind her eyes, “—is different. It’s like eyesight: some people are simply born with better vision than others. Your black core works, but not nearly as well. It can’t handle much essence, and what does get through has to fight its way past a hundred tiny fractures instead of one smooth path.”

“So basically, when I absorb a Grullshard, I… can’t drain all of its essence. Is that it?” Anadis asked, brows furrowed as he tried to process her explanation.

“Indeed.” She lifted the beetle Grullshard. It looked comically large in her tiny hands, nearly a quarter of her size. “This shard contained a total of twenty-five essences. Out of that, your core was only able to take a little over two.”

“That’s it?”

“Yes. If it were your twin, he could take almost 100%. Your sister, around 80 to 95 percent. A red core is about 50 to 75, grey 25 to 50, and black 5 to 25 percent,” Hel told him, as casual as if she were reciting a grocery list. “That’s the reason most black-core Blessens never ascend. They effectively need to defeat thousands of Grulls just to absorb enough shards, and most die somewhere in the process.”

Anadis didn’t need a mirror to know his face must have been a blend of defeat and amazement at just how terrible the hand he had been dealt was. “And how can I get past this? Is there even a real way?”

“Of course there is. I’ve been telling you that for a while now, haven’t I?”

“Really?” He couldn’t help but smile a little at that.

Hel nodded at the shard in her hands, saying, “Eat it.”

He stared at her for a long moment, then scoffed and waved his newly healed left hand dismissively. “No, but seriously—come on. I didn’t take Goddess Helphrena for the type to make jokes.”

“And you shouldn’t. I don’t make jokes,” she replied, violet spirals swirling in her eyes as they fixed on him. “Eat it.”

Now he turned his stare to the shard. It was about the length of his index finger but twice as thick—green, porous, and definitely not edible. Not even honey could make something like that palatable. “No… but seriously?”

“Yes. Seriously,” she said, the faintest hint of mockery in her voice.

“Can you explain the whole idea at least?”

“Very well.” She hovered upside down while explaining. “When your stomach digests the shard, its essence will want to go somewhere. Some will travel through the thread, but since so much essence is released at once, the rest will have nowhere to go but straight into your core, engulfing it as it enters.”

“How come more Blessens don’t do this? Actually, how come it isn’t well known if it’s so easy to absorb one hundred percent?”

“First, it’s not one hundred percent, but close to it. Also, eating a shard can be… unhealthy.”

He blinked. “Unhealthy?”

“A Blessen’s body is usually strong enough to digest it, though there is always the chance yours isn’t. But that is not the dangerous part. Flooding your Divine Core with that much essence at once can be lethal if it cannot withstand the surge,” Hel explained. “As for why it isn’t well known, I couldn’t tell you. Perhaps it was lost in history, or the powerful are deliberately withholding the knowledge.”

“I don’t think I should do this,” he said, eyes twitching as he stared in horror at the shard.

“You have me. I’ll ensure the essence enters gradually enough not to harm you. The digestion, however, is all on you.”

Gulping audibly, Anadis grabbed the shard from her hands and held it just above his lips. He glanced at the still water around the round ruins, walked over, and crouched at the edge. Hel’s wings buzzed incessantly in his ears.

With one hand, he cupped some water and then whispered to himself, “Just do it.”

He popped the small shard into his mouth. It tasted earthy and stony, neither horrible nor pleasant. Swallowing, however, was awful. It felt like the shard was grinding against his windpipe the entire way down. He gulped the handful of water, then cupped another, and another, until the shard finally dislodged and slid into his stomach. Anadis let out a long breath of relief, silently thanking Amatheris that the worst was over.

“Good. Now it’ll take about twenty minutes for your digestive system to start doing its thing,” Hel explained. She interlocked her fingers and popped them as she stretched, then transformed into a ribbon of golden and violet light. Iridescent wings left sparkling motes in her wake as she seeped into his chest.

“I’ll be in your soul, ready to regulate the first waves of essence,” she said through their mental link.

“Sure…” Anadis replied. “Should I start absorbing another shard in the meantime?”

“No! Are you stupid?” she snapped. “I’m talking about regulating the flow, and you want to bring in more essence? Of course not! Besides, I’m sure you won’t be able to once the acid reflux hits you in a couple of minutes. Just sit still.”

Burning.

Anadis felt like someone had dumped battery acid straight into his guts. Hel had not been kidding. The shard dissolving in his stomach was worse than any spoiled food he had ever eaten. He had already thrown up twice and suddenly felt like he had been vomiting far too often lately.

Yeah… yesterday I vomited too because of Sevran’s—

That thought alone made him vomit again.

However, for one heartbeat, he felt it—both the shard’s essence and his own, sitting inside his core. He could sense Hel imposing order within the walls of his Divine core. It was working. It was painful, but it worked. The two streams of essence met, clashing violently before finally blending and flowing together. Still, the burning continued relentlessly. It was a full hour of agony—retching violently, then desperately drinking stale water from a puddle in the ruins. He didn’t even stop to think about how old or foul that water must have been… He just kept drinking. Souls drifted around him, brushing against his skin as the agony finally receded.

Hel appeared once more, wiping beads of sweat from her white brows. “Well, that wasn’t so bad,” she said, huffing. “How are you feeling?”

He belched, his head spinning. “I don’t feel so good.”

“Perhaps you need some motivation,” Hel said with a sly smile curving her lips. “Why don’t you check the Divine Voice?”

Releasing another burp, Anadis did as she suggested.

Divine Core Status

“My conversion rate! It went up so much, and my Ascension progress is already that high?” Anadis blurted, suddenly feeling much better. His Ascension progress was now almost ten times what it had been before.

“Yes. The conversion rate will fluctuate when you are absorbing versus digesting a shard,” Hel replied.

“Hmm,” he hummed, not really listening. He grabbed another shard and was about to drop it down his throat when Hel slapped it out of his grip.

“Ugh…” She pinched the bridge of her nose. “Must you rush everything? You can’t simply consume another one. You need to let your stomach and Divine Core rest. You have to absorb as much of it as possible first and then eat it. Otherwise, the burden on both your stomach and core will be far greater.”

“That makes a lot of sense,” he whispered. “I got carried away.”

“It’s all right, I suppose. Here’s what we’ll do: rest for thirty minutes, then absorb as much as you can from the next shard before eating it. After that, we’ll rest for another thirty minutes before moving on to the last one.”

“There’s still the Unhallowed shard.”

“That one is still unpurified. Unless you’re eager to visit Elandris ahead of schedule, I wouldn’t eat it,” Hel advised.

Anadis raised his eyebrows at her. “Got it—no eating unpurified shards.”

“Good. Then that’s the plan.” Hel clasped her hands.

“My eyes!” Anadis cried out as he emerged from Eljudnir. It took a moment for his vision to adjust, but he felt the welcome warmth of the twin suns on his skin despite it being autumn. The wind on his face did carry the seasonal chill. The musty, stale air of the ruins washed away, replaced by the crisp scents of the wild, making him feel alive again.

The runes shimmered behind Anadis as Eljudnir’s entrance closed. “You know, it’s kind of amazing how some words written the right way can do something as incredible as literally allowing access to another realm.” He sighed. “Can I really learn how to work them? Runic Etching even if I’m not an Attuner?”

“Yes. Why would I lie about that?” Hel’s wings fluttered once. “You’ll learn in time. As for that Unhallowed shard, it can be purified as well. Any Attuner can manage it, and so can a Blessen. It simply requires far more effort.” She paused. “Far more.”

“Wait, we can even purify shards?” Anadis shook his head. “Then what’s the point of Attuners?”

“As I told you, the process is vastly more demanding. Most Blessens cannot manage it until they ascend to Luminous. For an Attuner, it’s nearly second nature.”

“Yes, that time I was prepared with good gear, food, potions, and boots—not in a sleeping gown and bare feet.” Anadis waved a hand over his pajamas. They weren’t really like the pajamas from Earth; they were more like a thin, simple linen tunic he always wore to sleep.

He shook his head. That was beside the point. What wasn’t was that he was still barefoot!

“Walk. You’ll manage.”

Groaning, Anadis followed a few paces behind the fleeting goddess. Sharp stones stabbed at his bare feet with every step. He scowled—this was going to be a long, long trek.

Then Hel spoke. “You’re stronger now, vessel.” Her tone was earnest as she tossed her ethereal hair. Her body swooped forward, sparkling as it caught the sunlight. “Even if only slightly.”

“You bet I am!” Anadis beamed, then conjured the Divine Voice just to hear his stats once more.

Divine Core Status

His Ascension progress had jumped all the way to two-thirds of a percent, and his Conversion rate had risen to eighty-five percent after absorbing all three shards—the beetle and the two wolves he had killed. Well, technically he had killed all four, but Aelia had protected him and Sevran from the Unhallowed, so she deserved the other two.

Anyway, his stats were finally starting to look a little closer to Albus’s. He just had to endure way more pain… way more. It had taken almost four hours and seven more bouts of vomiting, but it had been worth it even if it wrecked his stomach. If Albus had taken the first step on that staircase, Anadis would have been right behind him, with Sevran bringing up the rear, which felt quietly satisfying. Before, he had been staring down the barrel of slaying thousands of Grulls just to reach Emanant—something that probably would have killed him long before he got there. Now, with this new method and assuming most Abominant Grulls gave around thirty essence on average, he was looking at just a couple of hundred.

However, Hel had said the more he did this, the better his Conversion rate would become. Anadis also needed to learn how to control his essence and his core better, to the point where he wouldn’t need her help as much. It would take years, but everything truly good in life did.

After what felt like an eternity—but was really only about three hours—of trekking while avoiding Grulls, both Unhallowed and Abominant, Anadis had no desire to fight another one, especially not while dressed in pajamas and barefoot. Hel suddenly halted and stared ahead. Anadis followed her gaze, wondering what had stopped her. “Is it a Grull, Hel?”

She did not answer.

A sharp cry echoed through the forest. It startled Anadis, and Hel flinched, appearing just as startled.

“Was that a girl’s scream?” Anadis muttered, his voice faltering with apprehension.

“Yes, just up ahead,” Hel said, pointing with her tiny finger. “Use Soul Sight.”

Anadis quickly reached into his core. Cold surged through his body and into his eyes.

He looked past the ghostly outlines of trees, rocks, and distant mountains. In the distance, he spotted two floating cores beyond a group of Grulls. He couldn’t discern their colors from so far away, but he knew they belonged to humans.

“I see two cores—clearly people, not far from here. Maybe a mile or so,” he said, massaging his eyes.

“I still need to make sure. Those are people, and after that scream… Let’s go. We need to save them—maybe a Grull is attacking them.” Anadis didn’t wait for Hel’s response and darted toward the cores.
