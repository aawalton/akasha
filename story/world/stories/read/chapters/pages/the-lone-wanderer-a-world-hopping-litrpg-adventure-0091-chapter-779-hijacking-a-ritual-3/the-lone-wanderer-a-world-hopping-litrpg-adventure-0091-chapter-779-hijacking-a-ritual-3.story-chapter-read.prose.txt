As far as Percy could tell, he had two options. The safest one would be to increase the dosage slightly again, as he’d done from the start of the ritual, to boost his new mutation as much as he could by the time he exhausted the ingredients.

If he did that, he would still walk out of the vat a winner – since he was already much stronger than he’d been when he’d entered it – but the last few minutes had proven that his Ancient Remiorian Physique didn’t have much room for further improvement.

The riskier option would be to go all out, and push his mutations to merge into something even better.

So, really… this had never been much of a choice.

Percy emptied the secondary ingredients of the original ritual into the vat: the Conflux of a Thousand Toxins and the Abyssal Moss. He hadn’t expected to need them, but it honestly felt good to not leave any leftovers wasting away inside his seal.

The new ingredients reacted with the demi-human potions and whatever Heartblood was still swirling within the bubbling water, churning the magical soup even more violently. The mixture seeped into Percy’s pores again, trying to subject the interior of his body to a similar fate.

His flesh resisted the changes at first, but only until he injected himself with the rest of his Violet potions.

All of them.

He’d still had a couple doses of each type and knew that consuming them slowly wouldn’t be enough to jolt his mutations into action.

Percy’s arguably reckless move finally elicited a reaction from his body.

The potent wave of trollsfury ink tore his heart in half. Electricity flooded his nervous system, rattling his brain and causing his whole body to convulse. His organs shifted, and his muscles practically started eating themselves.

The external changes were just as concerning.

Percy’s body swelled to double its size, as his stomach instantly liquefied any food that entered it to fuel the transformation. Random patches of skin turned green again, but that was just the start of his problems. Unlike the smooth form that Remior’s projection had shown him in the canyon, a series of warts and granules started to bubble their way through Percy’s flesh, making him look like a swamp troll. At the same time, other patches flaked off entirely, revealing a faint scale-like pattern.

Percy’s irises alternated between the pale-yellow glow of the wood goblins’ eyes and the unsettling crimson colour of the Thess’kalans’. A pair of long tusks grew from his lower jaw, seemingly competing against the newly formed pair of curved fangs pointing down from his upper jaw.

“Ganpa. I hing naou wud be a gud time,” Percy said with a wince.

Archibald needn’t be told twice. Sticking his hands into the vat, he pressed his palms against Percy’s shoulders, pumping enormous amounts of White life mana into his grandson’s body.

Doing his best to hold his organs in place with his willpower, Percy allowed the patriarch to mend his injuries as well as he could. Unfortunately, the potent healing only accelerated the reactions occurring inside his body, causing his mutations to lash out with even greater fervour.

Percy decided to get rid of the external changes first. Keeping his body pink-skinned and human-shaped was non-negotiable, so he fully gave into the instincts afforded to him by the Mimicry trait, trying to impose order unto chaos.

It wasn’t easy.

His fourth trait had never struggled so much to adjust his appearance but, then again, he’d never asked it to turn what could only be described as a hulking abomination back into a regular human – at least not without access to Micky’s shapeshifting abilities.

Percy repeatedly pushed his mutating flesh inwards, trying to shove it back into a more reasonable shape – almost like somebody stuffing a messy pile of clothes into a small bag. It took several attempts, but he eventually managed to shrink his muscles into a more manageable size, and to return his teeth and skin to their original appearance.

A part of him was afraid that suppressing the external transformations would weaken the ritual and the resulting mutation, yet he soon realized that his worries were unfounded. The maelstrom of blood and potion only intensified upon being bottled up.

Percy’s heart was already in four pieces, held together by his willpower and barely pumping blood due to Archibald’s constant healing.

That was no longer enough.

Gritting his teeth, Percy had no choice but to finally tap into his Clear mana, summoning the first droplet directly inside his heart. His secret weapon honestly wasn’t that much more potent than his grandfather’s mana, because the patriarch’s first core had already reached White thanks to the Cradle, and had been empowered further by the external component of his boosting art. Archibald’s mana was also being guided by centuries of experience instead of being allowed to run wild.

Still, having two excellent sources of healing working together was definitely better than one. The problem was that neither Archibald’s mana, nor its divine counterpart were going to last long at this rate, and the same was true of the potions. By injecting all the remaining Violet doses into his organs, Percy had managed to push the ritual to the next level, but he’d also left himself with nothing to keep feeding the reactions.

‘The Blue potions will have to do,’ he decided, pouring enormous quantities of the five substances both inside and outside his body.

There was a limit to how much the weaker potions could stretch the ritual, because they would inevitably start diluting the effects of their Violet counterparts. Even so, this was better than doing nothing. Percy couldn’t allow the bath to end before he was done reforging his body.

Once he deemed that his condition had stabilized, and knowing that he was quickly running out of time, Percy focused his sensory domain inwards, trying to figure out what was wrong with him.

With every painful throb of his shattered heart, his muscles pounded against his skeleton like hammers on an anvil. For the first time since the start of the bath, Percy felt his reinforced bones cracking and rebuilding themselves, as ruby-coloured grains got knocked out of their structure and spilled out of his wounds to mix into the human soup.

Percy was glad to see that, but other body parts weren’t doing so well. For example, his muscles weren’t healing properly. Their fibres kept twisting themselves into knots again, undoing his previous efforts of painstakingly combing through and smoothening them.

He wasn’t sure why, but his best guess was that there was a pair of opposing impulses pushing his musculature in two different directions. His Remiorian mutation wanted the fibres to run parallel to one another, and to form simple flat strips that were further layered onto dense blocks of muscle.

His Greater Thess’kalan Physique, on the other hand, was trying to enforce a strange crisscrossing pattern that would probably lower Percy’s overall strength in exchange for greater flexibility and snappiness.

Neither option was bad, yet the two impulses kept fighting against one another without agreement, pointlessly wasting Percy’s limited resources. He had to somehow satisfy both mutations, though he didn’t have time to sit and carefully design the perfect shape for his muscles. The problem reminded him of the time he’d woven his Cloak, and had to resolve the conflict between the Weaving and Scribing traits…

‘The Weaving trait…!’ he thought with a gasp as his eyes widened. ‘Isn’t it the perfect tool for the job?!’

What if he looked at the muscle fibres as loose strings, and allowed the Argalios’ instincts to take care of the rest?

Combining a simple linear shape with a slightly more complex crisscrossing pattern should be laughably easy compared to the challenge of weaving countless tiny runes into his silk without compromising its strength.

Percy barely had a chance to complete that thought, when his trait woke up from its slumber and sprung into action. It seized control over the transformation, showing him how to please both physiques.

The trick was to fuse his fibres into wide but flat strips, and to knit those into an even larger, higher-ordered crisscrossing pattern. This should allow his muscles to preserve most of their strength without losing any agility or flexibility.

Spreading the new pattern across his body thankfully didn’t take long, because Percy had already removed most of his previous impurities. He also didn’t have to deal with it manually, as he could just offload the task to the Weaving trait and move on to the next challenge.

The strain on his heart was already more manageable, though the organ was still in pieces and beating irregularly. He’d almost exhausted the ingredients, too. He’d have to get his heart healed before the ritual ended or Archibald’s mana ran out, otherwise he would die.

The problem was that there were more body parts interfering with his circulation.

His body was trying to shuffle his organs around, to modify them in various subtle ways, and even grow a whole new set of alien organs – probably to accommodate the Thess’kalan mutation.

The first two parts were acceptable, but the last one was out of the question.

Percy didn’t know what the new organs were supposed to do, but he had no intention of turning himself into a snake person, nor did he think that the extra body parts were necessary. They probably performed similar functions to his human organs, and he’d rather just modify those into meeting the new requirements instead of introducing redundancies.

He was more than happy to extract whatever benefits he could from the alien physique, yet he wanted to keep not just the exterior, but also the interior of his body distinctly human.

This time, he leaned openly and heavily in favour of the Remiorian mutation, nipping the unwanted changes in the bud.

He still allowed the Greater Thess’kalan Physique to transform his body, but he forced it to work with what it had, by slightly rearranging or applying minor adjustments to his original organs.

Once he was done, he felt that his blood was circulating more easily and the strain on his heart had lessened even further.

But it wasn’t enough.
