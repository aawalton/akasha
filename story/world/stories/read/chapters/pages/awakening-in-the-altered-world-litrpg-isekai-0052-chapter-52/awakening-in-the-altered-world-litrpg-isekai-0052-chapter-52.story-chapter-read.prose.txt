Stella embraced Alex, and he froze.

When she stepped back, Stella still had tears in her eyes, and it felt like a very hard shard of emotion in Alex’s chest melted a little.

“Alex, it wasn’t your choice that you ended up here,” said Matt, meeting Alex’s eyes again; it seemed like he’d also made a decision. “It wasn’t your choice that you ended up with us twelve years ago.”

He smiled a bit. “I get it’s not your style to be this sappy, but—if you’ll have us, well, we’d like to have you as well.”

“Of course,” said Alex thickly.

“Then we don’t have anything else to say, I think?” said his uncle. His eyes glinted. “Except welcome. It’s a little weirder here. You might just like it.”

It wasn’t like all the complications that came from being an Outworlder magically went away; it was kind of Matt to kind of imply as much, but Alex wouldn’t delude himself into thinking it. And Alex was sure there’d be complicated feelings. He was certain his godparents might still need time to fully process things.

But at that instant he was flooded with relief; he finally let himself think—maybe things will be okay.

“Want to let us know what you’ve been up to?” said Matt, with an easy grin. “If you’re up for sharing.”

“Yeah,” said Alex, trying to readjust mentally. “It’s been kind of an eventful week, actually...”

It felt like so much he wasn’t even sure where to start.

Alex regaled them with how it’d gone, starting with his somewhat unusual Awakening ceremony, with the failed Awakening Stone, and the two successful Stones right afterward. He talked about the talents he’d Awakened.

The two of them weren’t as shocked as he’d expected—at least, they weren’t as shocked at his talents as he’d been.

“I practiced in the Training Center for a few days,” said Alex; they were all walking at a slightly more leisurely pace than before, listening to the seagulls as they went. “I tried a few v-Duels, and—”

He went on to talk about his rift—he gave them a bit of an abridged version.

He was a bit wary, and sure enough—

“Alex, you signed up for a rift with two days’ experience, with barely any gear.” Stella seemed incredulous.

“Well,” said Alex, wincing a bit. “It gets worse.”

Then he talked a bit about what happened in the rift, including their decision to try the Boss.

“Alex, what’s gotten into you?” Alex was worried Stella would be angry, because it was a very silly decision in retrospect, but she seemed bemused, as well as a bit concerned. “It’s been four days, what’s the rush? You’re an adult now, it’s your life—and it was the same with Bella, she could take on these kinds of risks and be fine, because she was what she was.”

Alex wasn’t sure how he felt about the comparison with Bella—Bella was definitely in a different category, even at his level—but he let her continue.

“You’re acting like you don’t have a choice. Like there’s a timer on you, when there isn’t.”

“Well, the rift was a once every six-months thing,” Alex began. “And there’s only four Rising Stars Cups a year, and by the next one, I would’ve been aged out.”

“But you could’ve taken Association-led rifts,” said Matt reasonably. “Is what Stella’s getting at, I think. You could’ve worked your way up to being able to buy full rifts, and gone from there. Sure, it might’ve taken a little longer, but you’re barely starting out. You should have all the time in the world.”

Alex wasn’t sure why, but he really disliked this framing of it; he tried to articulate why.

“But if I have this chance to be an F1 driver—is that a thing, still?—and I have this car that can go 200 miles an hour, how am I going to make it, driving it at half-speed? You guys get it, I mean, it’s obvious. I have to take chances.”

“Alex, this is going to sound annoying. I’ll apologize in advance for that,” said Matt, giving him a glance. “You said you wanted to follow your interests, and explore, and see what you could do with magic, right? And you would’ve said that before you transmigrated. But what you just said isn’t that, it sounds like what Bella might say. You never used to talk like that.”

“Guys, it’s not mutually exclusive,” said Alex, pursing his lips. “I can’t push what I can do with magic if I’m not really high-level at magic, right? And of course I want to push things a little now. I have this potential now.”

“Alex, you had the potential before,” said Stella. Alex was turning away. “Guys, you don’t need to—it clearly isn’t the same.”

His godparents exchanged a complex glance.

“Your talents—Cultivation, A-rank, with borderline Exceptional and Very High affinities—they’re almost the exact same as Bella’s,” said Matt.

Which genuinely surprised Alex.

“Different affinities,” his uncle clarified. “She’s more storm and water, but yes. And you won’t like hearing this—okay, I’ll stop anticipating what I’m saying. Alex, you never saw this, but to us it was clear as day. You were always as talented as your sister, you were just interested in other things. And that was always fine with us, genuinely.”

“Alright,” said Alex, lips quirking.

“You just had this mentality, like—your sister isn’t a god,” said Stella. “She has her flaws, like anyone.”

Alex, of course, knew Bella wasn’t flawless; he’d seen her psychology up-close. The way some people idolized her amused him. But what she was was ridiculously talented, which wasn’t just in his head, obviously.

I feel like this point might’ve hit harder if I didn’t just see a reel of like 10 elite Guild talents all terrified of her.

And if the point is, my talents are the same as hers—well, it took me an Outworlder transmigration, and a super-sized soul, just to get the same level of magic talent.

People are born different, and that’s fine. We don’t need to like—whatever.

He changed the topic; “So what’s with that giant water-dragon Bella’s riding?”

His godparents exchanged a glance, but relented.

It was, apparently, a variant of Flood Dragon, specializing in storms and seas.

As they went, his godparents told him about how Bella had tried out for about a dozen elite Guilds, including X1, Echelon, the Institute, Templar, [ newgen ], Fairfax & Co, etc.—pretty much every recognized gobal elite except ‘Parallax,’ a tinier, but apparently still elite Guild Alex wasn’t aware of, which Bella didn’t qualify for.

Essentially she had her pick of Guild, and selected to become a Templar fast-track talent. And while all elite talent contracts were standardized, there were technically handshake agreements you could make, besides contracts; in any case when this incredibly rare variant Flood Dragon rift appeared, Templar managed to purchase it, and by then Bella was first-ranked in her talents Class, with suitable affinities.

It was a ridiculously expensive investment; they’d had to get multiple specialists to stabilize the rift as a pocket dimension, and then there was always the risk that Bella would be unable to bond the legendary creature via specialized Skills and treasures, that it wouldn’t recognize her and accept her. But Bella was Bella.

“And now she’s in the Antarctica Tower?” mused Alex. Likely with between 50-100 other elite talents across the globe. “It’ll be an interesting challenge for her, at least.”

These Towers might be regularly cleared, but there were always challenges within; certain insanely difficult instance dungeons within certain floors, especially the highest floors, had never been cleared before—and so their reward items were never released to the world. By contrast, plenty of the lower-difficulty instance dungeons were so well-known, the exact % of receiving every potential reward was available online.

Alex also talked about his thorny money situation, and his thinking on Guilds—and to his surprise, his godparents were pretty understanding.

“I’d be careful as well,” said Matt easily. “Sign up if you’re clear-eyed about it, but I like your thinking about your time and your freedom. I’d be aware of what you’re signing away implicitly. Guild contracts for newbies, even talented ones are pretty much always going to be heavily weighted to the Guild’s side of things.”

Alex was realizing, now that he was a little more hyperaware of their relationship in this meeting, his focus on being clear-eyed about things, and maybe even his tendency to try to consider things carefully, might’ve come partially from how Matt went about life.

“You always have a choice,” Stella agreed. “If the benefits seem convincing, then that’s great! Bella always did well with people to push her, and she always gushes about the quality of the teachers Templar brings in. It always seemed like the people, and okay—getting on the shortlist for chances like Tower expeditions probably helps. But it does seem to help to be in the right spaces, with the right people. Everything else, the gear, the rifts—everything on the money side’s a little less urgent to me.”

She gave him an uncertain glance. “But if it suits you better trying things solo, I’m sure that’s also okay?”

“That won’t be necessary,” said Alex, thinking. He did get why they’d think that, given he was always a bit of an introvert, but there was something really helpful, almost intoxicating, to being surrounded by peers. That was very obvious by this point. “I just wish there were a way to get all the benefits, without the restrictions? At least, with fewer restrictions.”

This, Matt and Stella were less sure of, and they were in Professions, which didn’t have Guilds, more workshops, which was different. “I might speak to Bella when she gets out,” said Stella. “But that is in two or three months.”

“I’ll figure it out,” said Alex, wincing at the idea. Bella was strong-willed, to put it kindly. And she apparently also had a freaking legendary dragon.

He definitely felt better after this meeting, but Stella and Matt were one thing; Bella… that’ll be interesting.

“And about that money side,” his aunt continued. “Bella’s taken in, it has to be tens of thousands of credits of investment from Templar?”

She and his uncle exchanged a glance.

“Alex, money… really doesn’t need to be an issue for you,” said Matt, looking him in the eyes. “Stella makes staffs for some of the best Guilders and independents in the world—she’s been underselling herself a little. She’s as quality as it gets in New York City.”

“The best Staff-makers are all D-ranks,” Stella protested, blushing. “But I’m—financially, yes.”

“And I do just fine myself,” Matt continued. “If you want any support, just ask.”

Which forced Alex to consider his choices.
