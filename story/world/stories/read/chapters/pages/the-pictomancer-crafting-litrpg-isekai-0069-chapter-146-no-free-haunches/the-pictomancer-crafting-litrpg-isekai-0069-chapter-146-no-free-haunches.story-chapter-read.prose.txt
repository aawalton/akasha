As they moved deeper into the village, Clive began to notice the people.

Or not only people. A broad figure with lupine ears and grey-furred forearms ducked under a low doorway ahead of them, straightened on the other side, and kept walking with a sack of grain over one shoulder. He glanced at Jill as they passed and gave her a short nod.

Jill returned it without slowing.

Two children came around a corner at speed and nearly ran into Jill. They pulled up short, long rabbit ears swiveling independently.

"Moon Mother." The taller one grabbed Jill's sleeve. "Are you playing with us today?"

"Not today."

"You said that yesterday."

"I did," Jill agreed.

The smaller one had already lost interest and was staring at Azura instead. Azura looked back at her with equal directness.

Then they were gone, vanishing between two buildings with a shade-ling rolling after them in lazy pursuit.

"Popular," Clive said.

Jill tugged her sleeve straight. "They'd follow anyone who gave them attention twice."

Clive let it go and looked around instead. Wolfmen, bunnyboys, catgirls. A woman with a long white tail was hanging laundry from a second-floor window. Marblehaven had been entirely human. This place felt like its own ecosystem by comparison.

"Is it always this diverse?" Clive asked.

"Most settled towns are this way," Jill said. "It’s part of the philosophy of Vandiel. Their motto is that difference is no reason for separation." She watched a catgirl slip between two adults without breaking stride. "The people out here are descendants of those touched by the old plague. It changed them. Their children were born changed. Most places turned them away. Vandiel didn't.”

"That's… inclusive," Clive said.

It was an undersell and he knew it. He'd spent enough time in San Dioral hearing Vandiel spoken about in a derogatory tone.  The demon king's realm, the corrupted lands, the enemy at the edge of every map. He'd took it at face value.

But the shape didn't match what was in front of him. A wolfman dozing on his steps with a shade-ling curled against his shin. A catgirl getting scolded by her mother in a doorway across the lane, ears flat, tail lashing. Ordinary life, going on ordinarily.

He was starting to think that maybe San Dioral had been wrong about a few things.

They entered an inn called the Waning Gate — burned into the sign in letters that had been re-carved at least once, the older marks still visible beneath. The door was heavy, strung with dark ribbon charms that brushed Clive's shoulder as he ducked through.

The room beyond was warm. Not hearth-warm, though there was a hearth — shade-lings had colonized every corner of the place, clustering under tables and along the ceiling beams and in the gap between the bar and the wall, their ambient glow giving the room a dim, bruised light that the candles had to work against. It felt like drinking in a lantern. Clive found he didn't mind it.

The innkeeper looked up from the bar. She was lean, reptilian, with scaled forearms and red eyes. She took in Jill and straightened.

"Moon Mother." She pressed her hands flat on the bar and inclined her head. "You grace us again."

"Thank you." Jill set her hands on the bar. "Is Maelus here?"

"Arrived this morning. Said to expect you." She reached under the bar and produced three keys on a single iron ring, setting them on the wood between them. "Rooms are secured. He's waiting for you upstairs — last door on the left." Her slit eyes moved briefly to Clive, then to Azura. "I'll have food sent up for you and your guests. Any preferences?"

Jill looked at Clive. Clive looked at Azura, who was still examining her surroundings.

"Azura."

She looked up. "What."

"Food. Do you have preferences?"

"Meat," she said. "And nothing that smells like the grey powder you put on things in San Dioral."

"Salt?"

"That one."

"Meat, then," Jill said. "Whatever's hot. We're not particular."

"Bread," Clive added. "If there is any."

The innkeeper nodded and disappeared through a door behind the bar without further comment.

They headed upstairs. The last door on the left was open, light spilling into the corridor, and Maelus was inside, sitting in the room's one good chair with his boots off and a cup of something in his hand.

He stood when they came in. His eyes went immediately to Azura and stayed there.

"This is the dragon?" he said.

"This is Azura," Clive said.

Maelus looked her over with an unreadable expression while Azura looked back at him with the patience of something that had once been large enough to eat him and hadn't forgotten it.

"Hm," Maelus said, and sat back down.

The food arrived before anyone had to decide what came next — the innkeeper's girl, a quiet bunny-eared teenager set the tray on the table without making eye contact and left just as quickly. Bread, a clay pot of something stewed, and a plate of meat that Azura identified and claimed before anyone else had finished sitting down.

They ate. The fire was low. For a while no one said much.

Then Jill set down her cup.

"So what now, Clive?"

Clive paused. He wasn’t entirely sure. He'd walked away from the prince's service, attacked him even. It wasn’t the type of bridge-burning that could be undone by a good excuse. Whatever that cost, it was coming eventually.

And Lucia was still with them. He kept coming back to that. Whatever consequences landed, they needed to land on him. She'd had nothing to do with any of it. She'd followed him in good faith and he'd led her here, and she was innocent in all of it. He hoped above all else that the prince would think similarly.

Clive made a mental note to check up on her. Whatever else happened, that was a debt he owed. But that was a problem for the future. Tonight he could barely think past the weight in his legs.

"I don't know," he said.

"Then don't decide yet." Maelus refilled his cup. "Stay here for a while. Rest. The prince may have sneaked past the borders but they won't be able to stay here long. This deep into Vandiel territory, they’d have to move an army through contested grounds if they want any meaningful operations.”

Clive looked at Jill. She was watching him with an expression he couldn't quite read, waiting to see what he'd do with the option.

After everything he’d been through, resting for a few days didn’t sound so bad. He’d been through the wilderness, trekked a mountain, and fought dragons. Now, the idea of sleeping in a real bed without one ear open felt appealing. His body had opinions about this that his pride had no good argument against.

"Alright," he said. "A few days."

"Good." Jill set down her spoon. "The village could use the extra hands while you're here. There's always work."

"I can work," Azura said, from the corner, where she had finished her meat. She looked up. "What kind of work?"

Jill considered her. "Can you carry things?"

Azura looked at her own hands. "I carried a mountain once."

"We'll start smaller," Jill said.

The next morning, Clive was still half asleep when Azura appeared in his doorway.

"I'm hungry," she said.

"Good morning to you too."

"I'm very hungry."

He sat up. She was leaning against the doorframe with her arms folded with eyes begging like a puppy.

Clive sighed. Although she may look like a human, her appetite was still decidedly dragon.

He got dressed.

Jill was already downstairs, finishing her breakfast. She took one look at Azura and stood up.

"Come on," she said. "I know where the butcher is."

The butcher's shop was three buildings down from the inn, announced by a smell that hit Azura at the threshold and stopped her in her tracks. Three haunches of boar, smoked, hanging from iron hooks along the back wall. The shop was otherwise modest — a low counter, a cleaver block, sawdust on the floor — but Azura had eyes for nothing else. She stood in the doorway and looked at the hanging meat with an intensity that made the butcher take a small, involuntary step back.

"She's fine," Clive said.

The butcher, a heavyset wolfman with a grey-streaked muzzle, looked unconvinced.

Jill stepped up to the counter. "Morning, Greymane. Three haunches."

The butcher relaxed visibly at the sight of her. "Moon Mother." He reached for the meat, then paused, glancing at Clive. "That'll be fifteen dross. Plus the smoke tax — two iron-marks per haunch, so six total. Nineteen dross and six iron-marks, or just round it to two silverthorn if you'd prefer."

"Ermmm—" He stopped. "What's a silverthorn?"

The butcher stared at him.

"Standard coin," Jill said. "Vandiel mint. San Dioral currency isn’t good yet."

Clive kept silent. He didn’t have any San Dioral currency either. He was just as broke in this world as he was in their old world. But she didn’t need to know that.

The butcher looked between them. Then at Azura, who had drifted two steps closer to the hanging boar without appearing to notice she'd moved.

"I can put it on the Moon Mother's account," he said. "She's done enough for this village."

"I can't," Clive said.

Jill looked at him. "Clive."

"I mean it." He didn't say the rest of it. That he wasn't going to be the man who let his wife's reputation feed him, who coasted on her standing in a village that had nothing to do with anything he'd earned. He may be broke, but he still had principles. "Is there any work you need done around here?"

Greymane studied him for a moment. "You know anything about meat?"

"I know which end of a knife to hold."

"That's a start." Greymane lifted the counter hatch and jerked his head toward the back. "I've got a boar came in last night that needs breaking down. You help me work it and I'll call the three haunches more than fair."

“Fair.” Clive rolled up his sleeves and went through.

Jill watched him disappear into the back room. Then she looked at Azura.

Azura was still watching the hanging haunches.

"He's going to be a while," Jill said.

"I know," Azura said. "I'm waiting."
