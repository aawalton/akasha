Air, clean and pure, filled my lungs. The coughing woke me.

I stared up as a blue sky stared right back down at me. A blue sky, the first I had ever seen. The place where I lay was soft, so soft. I turned my head and saw the green grass stretching around me, bleeding into a sea of trees. I was in a forest, then. A human forest.

The light that beat down upon me was not scalding, simply soft and warm.

"Finally," I said as something in my chest loosened. I raised a single hand towards the sky, just to reach for it. Just because I could.

I was in the human realm, and that meant that my mad gambit had worked. I laughed. For the first time since I had taken my Role, I laughed. It started as something small and foreign, and then it wouldn't stop. I laughed until my stomach hurt, and only then did I lie there, letting a smile creep across my face.

"You stupid Gods and your stupid rules," I said. "Even us pawns can bite, you bastards."

A warm breeze slowly filtered over the clearing where I lay. It felt pleasant on my skin. Evidently, I was still wearing my battle armor, [The Darkness Binding]. That was convenient, as it would be hard to find its like again in this world.

I finally rose, getting into a sitting position as I glanced around. There was no way to tell where I was, or which part of Enreth my spell had taken me to.

"[Map]!" I said, my right arm extended outwards. My mana whirled within me. I expected a translucent blue screen to pop open in front of me. Instead, nothing happened. I stared at my hand for a second, perplexed. It was like trying to move a limb and having it refuse to move.

"[Status]!" I said again. Nothing happened this time either. My confusion gave way to annoyance.

"[Inventory!]" Nothing again. The annoyance suffocated under the weight of uncertainty.

I turned my head and stared at the nearest tree. "[Inspect!]"

….......

Something cold settled inside me. I was the Demon Queen. If I demanded something, it happened. This was as true of my subordinates as it was for the World itself.

"Fine then," I growled. I decided to use my strongest spell -if subtlety would not work, force would.

"[Oblivion's Decree]!" My mana swirled.

This time, something answered. My mana surged against the world and the world pushed back. A weight settled over me, pressing down on every part of my body at once. My power clawed at the fabric of existence and for the first time, existence refused to tear. My mana settled.

I wiped sweat from my brow, feeling faint tremors in my hands. My heart was beating fast. Was I...out of breath?

"Damn it," I muttered, finally rising to my feet. Mana still coursed when I called it, and it settled in whatever limb I wished. That, at least, seemed to work just as it had before.

I had the tiniest fraction of my power. That was what I had. I hadn't felt like this in a very long time. I did not care to name what this feeling was.

"Well, let's see," I said thoughtfully. Information had to come first.

I channeled mana into my limbs and ran for the tallest tree in sight. It had been a long time since I hadn't used [Flight] for something like this.

I made my way up the tree, my head cresting above the canopy of leaves. My breath caught as I stared in every direction at once.

I had thought the sky beautiful from the ground. Now, I could see it in full. It was blue as far as the eye could see, and a setting sun bled orange into halcyon. The trees stretched all the way over to the horizon.

For a few moments, I forgot the entire reason I had climbed this tree at all. For those moments, all I did was take it in.

"Damn it," I said, reaching to wipe my eyes. It was a sniffle, nothing more.

I saw a reflection of light somewhere to the right, a river that cut through this large forest. That was as good a place to go as any. I made my way down. Mana reinforcement had never been my specialty, since you needed a feel for it I had never possessed.

I landed on cool grass. It was odd that my shoes hadn't transferred with me, but the ground felt pleasant.

"I don't feel them," I finally said, after a few minutes of walking.

The invisible strings that had haunted me for centuries, the burdens of my Role in this world. That was why I did not despair when my mana did not answer my call. That was why the System's absence mattered even less.

As I walked, I occasionally saw a squirrel scamper up a tree, or a rabbit duck and hide from view. Birds flew overhead, cooing and cawing. In the Demon Realm, there was no such thing as an animal that wasn't a Beast. It was odd, to see something living and not have to kill or subjugate it.

"So I wasn't imagining it," I said.

I had been trying to draw ambient mana into my body. I was succeeding, but one thing was clear: even the mana felt different. It was thinner. It filled my core easily enough, but there was less force behind it, like breathing air at the top of a mountain. It was simply lesser.

A few oddities with me I could accept. The spell had been a mad gambit. A mad gambit by a genius, perhaps, but still a mad gambit. This was something wrong with the world.

"Serene, a curse upon all you stand for!" I waited.

The Goddess of Peace didn't answer. Or if she did, I did not feel her gaze. The Gods weren't watching me. No, it seemed they could not watch me. My role was truly at an end.

"It's not my problem, then," I said.

I walked for so long I almost wanted to climb another tree and check again, just to make sure I hadn't wandered off course. That's when I finally heard it: the sound of running water, somewhere in the distance.

I ran through the trees and underbrush. Branches broke under my weight and deer startled from my path. A boar bolted when it saw me. The sound grew louder until I finally broke past a line of trees and saw the river before me.

I strode forward and knelt down. The water was pure, and so clean it didn't look like anything at all. I leaned down and drank greedily. Cool liquid drove back the dryness that had been clawing at my throat.

"I never knew it could taste this sweet," I said when I'd finally had my fill. There was no need for mana to keep it from burning my throat.

I stared at my reflection and froze.

My features looked similar, and yet not quite the same. My face was a tad darker, a tad younger than I remembered. My eyes were bright crimson now, with hardly any white to them at all. At least my two jutting horns were the same at first glance. They were much darker, and I could not shake the feeling they were thinner.

I was still a Demon. That much was certain, but this face was close enough to mine to unsettle me in a way a stranger's never could.

"Did I do this?" The spell was meant to reincarnate my body in the future, not change it. Any alteration would and should have destroyed me in the process. Yet here I was, alive and of sound mind.

No answers came, no matter how I turned the problem over. It would have to wait.

My stomach grumbled unhappily, and I winced. Even my near-divine body had still been mortal in the end, and now it was closer still.

Growl.

There was a sound behind me, one low and guttural, one that vibrated in my chest. I turned and saw the beast standing at the edge of the clearing, where the trees thinned. The beast was a silver wolf, though wolf seemed too small a word for it.

The creature was as tall as I was and at least twice as wide. It had three heads, each set of its jaws were opening and snapping shut again. I knew only one beast with that many heads. A cerberus, but none of them had looked like this. The beast's six eyes focused on me.

"[Balefire]!" I cried out, one arm extended. My mana swirled, and yet the spell did not come.

The beast lunged for me. It was fast, and I was on the ground before I knew it. Its weight threw me to the floor, pinning me against the ground. The central head lunged for me. I blocked it with two hands, flooding my arms with mana. My fingers sank into its fur, and I only just kept those teeth -each one as long as my fingers- from finding my neck. Its other heads thrashed against my [Darkness Binding], but did not draw blood. My armor could still manage that much at least.

Its spit ran down my arms. Its breath was rancid. My arms were shaking, something they had never done before. "You damned beast," I hissed. "You think the likes of you can- can stand against me?!"

There was fury boiling inside me, and yet my spells would not work. All I had now were my hands, against this weight that was far greater than my own. Against a beast I should have been able to kill with just a thought.

My hands started to buckle. I pulled my right back and punched the beast in the throat with as much mana as I could. It was basic mana reinforcement, the kind any demon learns almost as soon as they learn how to walk. Something shuddered under the weight of my blow, cartilage or bone perhaps.

The weight on top of me shifted, and then I was free. I rose to my feet quickly, but the beast was already up, almost before I was.

All three of its heads were low, and all six of its eyes were focused on me. The beast was slowly moving forward. Every instinct told me to stand up and fight it -instinct born from centuries of fighting, one that demanded I show this beast the difference between predator and prey. My legs chose for me. I ran. I, Lysanthia, The Strongest Demon Queen who had ever walked Enreth, ran from a petty beast.

I felt it race to catch me, and I could almost feel its breath on my back. I channeled mana into my legs. Even without looking, I knew this creature was faster than I was.

Curse this body!

I could hear it behind me. Its breathing was getting closer and closer with each second that passed. More mana poured into my legs, but that did nothing. I was still not fast enough.

Damn it.

There was a slope in front of me where the ground dropped. It was so sudden that my feet slipped out from under me, and then I was sliding down. I rolled to my feet the second I hit the earth. I looked up the slope and decided.

If I kept running, in the open then this beast would catch me. I had escaped this creature once, I would not be so lucky again. My only chance was here on lower ground, where it would have to lunge at me. A lunge was a predictable motion, one that I could dodge. The beast crested the slope.

All three heads looked down at me. Its teeth bared. I set my feet and bared my teeth right back. The stupid mongrel lunged. Its body drove down the slope, and the three maws spread wide for the kill. I stepped left.

The beast's mass thundered past me. One of its jaws almost found me but didn't. It started to turn, but I was already on its back, my arms locking around the central neck.

I squeezed hard, as hard as my arms would allow. The beasts other two heads tried to bite at me, but the angle made it impossible for them. They could just barely not reach me. I pushed every ounce of mana I had into my arms until every muscle in my arm was screaming.

The beast did not go quietly. It thrashed, and when that did not work, it twisted. It shoved itself to the side, and I went right with it. My vision was spinning until the world become nothing but dirt and fur and then dirt again. It was almost enough to make my grip slip. Almost. The Darkness Binding took the impacts, but each one still threatened to knock the air from my lungs.

There was only one simple way to kill a Cerberus. Kill its central neck, the one that controlled the body, and the rest of the beast would follow. This beast was not quite a Cerberus, and yet I staked my life on this beast being close enough.

I screamed at the top of my lungs and held on. It didn't sound like the cry of a Queen or the battle sound of a warrior, but it left my throat anyway.

Something inside the beast snapped. I felt it in my arms as much as I felt it move through the central neck. The beast thrashed harder at that. When it growled, the sound came out pained. Perhaps it knew the danger.

A second snap followed the first, and this one sounded softer. The central head bulged against my arms. There was a third snap, and this one almost sounded delicate. With it, the beasts entire body spasmed against my arms even as its weight collapsed against the ground with a small thud.

I still held on. For five more seconds I held on, just to be sure. Only then did I finally let go. My body was shaking, and not just from the exhaustion. I did not recognize the sound of my own harsh breathing. Once, ten thousand beasts such as this should have died at just a thought. Now, just one had almost killed me.

I stood over the corpse of something I had nearly failed to kill, in a body I barely recognized. I waited for the frustration to come. It never did.

My arms ached. How long had it been since they'd ached?

I smiled, a rarer sight in this world than Wraithsteel. It was odd that effort should taste sweet.

My stomach growled again. There was no use waiting. Other races might balk at this, but demons simply ate.

I leaned down and sank my teeth into the beast's flesh. The meat tore easily under my teeth. Warm blood flowed down my throat, and the chewy, raw meat tasted perfect to my starved self. I bit past bone and ate muscle, fat, and organs alike. When I was done, there was a large hole in the beast's back. There was still plenty of meat left. I licked more blood off my fingers, finally feeling satiated.

There was blood on my uncovered skin, which was hardly a surprise. There was also blood covering my battle dress, which was a surprise.

"Tsk," I grunted and moved over towards the stream, sinking down into it.

Water slid off the [Darkness Binding], taking the blood right with it. It didn't turn damp as any other piece of clothing would have. That was the least of its powers but the most useful now. It was also, I couldn't help but suspect, the only use it still had besides its base durability.

The water felt cool on my skin. In the Demon Realm, water was always either scalding or poisoned. Neither of the two had mattered to me, but this freshness was still something I had never felt before. I found myself basking in it as I stared up at the sun.

Questions lingered, but for now they didn't matter a bit. Tomorrow, I would have to try to find something that could speak, and I would wring the answers right out of them.

"Eek!"

I froze and turned, staring at the person who had just wandered in behind me.

She had long golden hair, a heart-shaped face, bright blue eyes, and a fit body. She was covered in resplendent green armor, the kind that bore markings I knew well.

The girl stared at me. I stared right back at her. She pointed.

"You Demon!" The girl roared. "Who are you? Where am I?" Her hand was already on her broadsword, the [Hero's Sword].

What? Why? How?

Why was the Hero who had killed me also here?
