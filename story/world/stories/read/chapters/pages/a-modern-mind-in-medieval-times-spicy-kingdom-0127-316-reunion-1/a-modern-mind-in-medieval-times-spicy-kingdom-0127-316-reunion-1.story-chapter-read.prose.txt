As the orange sky began darkening, the ship veered left towards the coast.

The mists surrounding the coastline thinned, revealing a forest of crowded shanties, covering every single inch of the waterfront. Beyond it rose a weathered gray ribbon of stone; the Outer wall. A taller and whiter version far behind it poked its head above. A lone mountain dominated them all, on whose peak sat Castle Hohenburg, like a crown of stone.

Aldersthron; a city of contradictions. Full of poverty and opulence. Opportunity and misery.

Home.

Leaning against the railing, Reshma heaved a sigh of relief.

Even the bloody winds favored Jack. Waiting for them to turn, it had taken the Royal ship almost five days to reach the capital, instead of the usual three.

She looked up at the tall tower rising above the cliffs and the walls; the keep of Hohenburg. The seat of the Queen of Nahnon.

Her heart fluttered with excitement, anticipation and dread.

I’m going to be in so much trouble, she thought.

Jack, being Jack, had done what he wanted, and in the process managed a rare feat; making enemies out of nobles, the Church and the guilds, all at the same time.

She, who was supposed to control him, had failed spectacularly. No amount of begging on her knees was going to assuage Laira’s anger this time.

Then there was the secret.

The bastard had left the decision to her.

Whether to tell Laira about his other-worldly origin or not.

Damn him.

He knew perfectly well that Nahnon and maybe even Sindhu would stand or fall on the strength of his weapons and ideas, so she had no choice but to act on his behalf, whether she wanted to or not.

The keep’s windows began to glow golden-orange as the ship slowed down and turned into the harbor. The thick smell of brackish water carrying the city’s refuse and rotting seaweed overpowered the salty fragrance of the open sea.

Laughter, arguments and drunken songs drifted over the water. As the ship approached, the dilapidated buildings that blanketed the coast became more than specks.

The sky began to darken, but not every establishment had closed shop. Many, in fact, flourished at this very time of the day.

Reshma remembered with disdain how naive she had been as a Princess. A fool, ignorant about the world. She hadn’t even known about the people who lived in such places, much less thought about them. Not until she had almost become one of them.

She shuddered, but not from the descending chill.

She gave thanks to God for madame Bertrun noticing her in time. If not for her, she might, no, would have surely ended up as a resident of one of those buildings, bathed in the dim, flickering light of red lamps. Selling herself everyday for survival.

Bile rose up in her throat.

Perhaps that was why she hadn’t fought against Jack’s reforms as hard as she ought to. Even the memory of the fire in his eyes when he talked about how much better the common people’s lives could be made her heart flutter.

Or maybe it was just love.

Her hand instinctively settled over her stomach.

“My lady, we’re here.”

Sir Grimhart’s deep voice pulled her out of her thoughts.

She caught the railing, just in time, as the ship struck the royal berth with a solid thud. It rocked once, twice, then finally came to rest.

She stretched her neck while the men shouted, throwing ropes down and lifting the gangplank.

Time for her reckoning.

A carriage was already at the docks, waiting for her. Her own would take time as the horses acclimated to being on land again.

Let’s hope it’s here because she is eager to meet her lover, and not a failed aide, she thought as she climbed inside.

As she took long breaths to steady herself, the carriage lurched forward, rattling over planks, then cobblestone.

The motion felt… wrong.

It shook too much.

Guess I got too used to his unique carriage.

And his presence.

As the carriage went through the Outer Wall’s gate, another worry sprouted within Reshma.

How was Laira going to react when she found out about them?

There was no guarantee that the desire that made her melt seeing the two of them together would also extend to…

No.

Laira had known it could happen.

Or maybe she had been blind in the throes of passion.

While Reshma dealt with doubts sprouting within her like weeds, the carriage reached the castle. She only noticed it when the large gates groaned open to let it inside.

She climbed out gingerly into the castle’s courtyard.

Night had fully descended upon the city.

Valmar, the old goat, was waiting for her. His silver hair gleamed under the flickering orange glow of torches.

“My lady,” the butler said, bowing sharply.

She inclined her head. “How have you been, Valmar?”

“Well, my lady.”

“And Her Majesty?”

“Well,” he replied cautiously.

She didn’t miss it.

“What happened?” the question tore out of her.

“I… should not be the one to tell you,” he replied slowly, then gestured to the keep.

Not waiting for him, she began running.
