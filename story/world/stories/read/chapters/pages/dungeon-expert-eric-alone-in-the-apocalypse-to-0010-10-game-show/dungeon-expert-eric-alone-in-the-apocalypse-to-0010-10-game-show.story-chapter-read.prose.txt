The theme music quieted, and a few advisories appeared, informing viewers about the game. He didn’t know if it was for the benefit of the Audience or some other first-time watchers out there, wherever there was, but they just covered things he already knew.

And then a disembodied voice chimed in. “Is everyone ready? Let’s go! The eagerly anticipated… first show!”

“As a reminder, no, you can’t subscribe to any individuals yet… but remember their names for later! When subscription opens next week, I’m going to want to see at least a thousand subscribers for each of our rising stars, okay? Now! Let’s see… our dazzling trainees!”

The show cut to a near-indescribable number of people standing inside an equally indescribably large warehouse. At the orders of someone offscreen, they moved through what looked like a set of kata or martial arts moves. That scene replayed over and over, with people holding swords, spears, hammers, every weapon imaginable. As the camera panned, it would occasionally zoom on one person or another, highlighting their name on-screen.

And then it cut. The video feed split into dozens of smaller screens, each one showing drama in some sort of dorm, usually involving one of the people it had shown earlier. All the audio played at once, overlapping over each other. It was far too much to follow, but Eric had the distinct feeling that this wasn’t for the human audience. Or Audience, as it were. He watched one of the scenes, but couldn’t pick out which audio stream belonged to it, so could only watch as three unfamiliar people argued over something without understanding why or what they fought over.

The voice-over cut on again, silencing all the various dramas. “Remember, even if you can’t subscribe yet, you can always pick a Player and watch their stream or play back the VOD! Streams are on during all active hours… yes, without exception. I know you perverts out there can’t wait, huh?”

Eric grimaced. Gross. Then again, the streams were only up during “active hours,” which meant they could do private things during break hours—night, if he had to guess. Sex work was work too, so if someone wanted to engage, he couldn’t really fault them. It was just the part where they waited until now to mention that felt exploitative.

Who am I kidding? This whole thing is exploitative with a capital E.

“We’re blacking it out for now, of course. But starting next week, it’s No. Holds. Barred! If our Players want to play to you, we won’t stop them!”

That’s… a little better, I guess? Not much, but at least it wasn’t completely icky. At least they warned people, as long as the people watched the broadcast and remembered it. Not the greatest situation, but whatever; these people were literally running a death game, so did he really expect high morality from them?

“After next week, if a Player wants to black out, well, they’d better earn that in-Game!”

Eric looked up at that. Wait… that’s something I can buy? Black-out hours where no one can watch me, even during active hours? He didn’t know when he’d need that, but that sounded like something essential to know. He did a quick search through the System, but came up with nothing. No reference to black-out hours or anything. The only place he could have found out about it was the broadcast.

No wonder Joe told me to watch it.

He fiddled with the ultrarares in his inventory. He desperately wanted to open them, but if they were like the shop token—one-time-use—and he accidentally used them without thinking while he was watching the broadcast, he’d never forgive himself. So he left them in his inventory, to pull out once the broadcast was over.

The twelve-fold screen refreshed several times before the drama segment ended, and what followed was a clip show of battle. Most of it featured the Players in the Tutorial fighting a single small imp monster, with varying success. Blooper reels featured high in the clip show, along with snuff. The bloopers were mostly palatable, the usual pratfalls and silly mistakes, but every now and again, the TV-MA rating spiked to NC-17. After the second time the little imp disemboweled a Player and cackled directly into the camera about it, Eric swapped to watching the screen out of the corner of his eyes.

Toward the end of the clip show, the clips started focusing on the people the broadcast had highlighted at the start. A woman with a katana neatly sliced the head off an imp; a man with brass knuckles pummeled an imp’s body until it was nothing but a smear on the dirt; a handsome man with an overly-polished jawline called out “360 noscope”, spun, and pierced an imp through the eye with an arrow; a kid in an anime t-shirt did a backflip, clumsily beheaded an imp, and gave the camera a big grin and a thumbs-up.

Eric pursed his lips. Mentally, he categorized the clips: Hot woman, ultraviolence, hot man, cute kid. It was a truly boiled-down version of what humanity had to offer, like browsing a social media site for ten seconds without letting the algorithm get ahold of the feed—and without any sort of censor. If anything, they seemed to delight in the lack of censor, lingering on the imps’ bodies and replaying the killing blows on loop.

More and more, dozens, maybe even hundreds of clips, the screen once more segmenting as they reached the less popular (Eric assumed) Players. Most of them were shots of the Players trying (or failing) to kill the imp, or conflicts between Players. One of the clips, inexplicably, was a loop of a cute dog spinning in circles. Another, for some reason, featured a girl with overdone nail art playing with slime. Yet another featured a group of kids doing a dance with the regimented lines of Players in the background. All the videos, even the dog and the slime, were clearly shot at the Players’ barracks, but were almost normal brainrot instead of the Great Game’s violence highlight reel. Eric frowned. Did some people get to bring their personal influencer setups into the Game? Where the hell had that girl found that slime, and how were her nails still flawless? He had so many questions, and so few answers.

One image in the top of the grid caught his eye. Instead of the beige tones of the barracks, lush greenery filled the background of the shot. From behind, he watched an unfortunately familiarly dressed man battle with black snakes. The man stepped forward, and yet another snake lunged at him, rearing up to hiss at his face. The man whipped around and hissed back.

That’s me! Eric jolted, then tilted his head in confusion. He’d done so much fighting, and that was what they picked? The time he hissed back at the snakes in annoyance? Not the giant boss snake?

Maybe it’s coming up next. He settled in to watch, but the rest of the clips were set securely in the barracks. There wasn’t a single shot of his takedown of the snakeman camp or his battle with the giant snake.

“That’s all for tonight, folks! See you back here same time tomorrow! Or, if you’re eager to get in on the action, you know where to find the live streams!”

The screen went black. Eric sat alone in the gas station, staring at the blank television. “Huh.”

“Disappointed?”

Eric glanced over his shoulder. A very pale, very tall man loomed behind him, dressed in black, his hair dyed pitch black, and a dozen piercings in his face. Tattoos scrawled over every inch of his skin, including a spiderweb that encased his entire neck and a spider that descended from the outer edge of his right eye. He wore the gas station’s uniform polo over his black ripped longsleeve shirt, and it looked ridiculous clashing with his over-the-top goth attire.

“Hey, Joe.”

“Hey, kid. This guy’s boring. A total straight-edge except for being into pain. Not much of that around here, so I gotta just sit around like a loser.”

TMI. He gestured at the television. “They didn’t use any shots of my fight.”

“Well… yeah. Hate to inform you, but you fought an invisible snake. Invisible. It doesn’t make for great television.”

“Why’d they put the invisible snake out there, then, if it doesn’t make for good watching?” Eric asked, exasperated.

“It’s only bad television when you win. It plays great when it’s eating Players, ambushing teams one member after another, the spreading terror, the haunting screams…” Joe shrugged. “There’s people out there that pay mega-bucks to watch compilations of every time that monster eats someone. The POV of each individual player vanishing into the giant maw… well, you guys have the concept of vore, right? It’s not not that.”

Eric considered it for a moment, how horrifying it would be to see a friend vanish into the maw of an invisible beast, for it to pick off people one at a time while he struggled to locate it. He shuddered. “Those people are messed up.”

“Most people who enjoy the Great Game enough to buy recordings are messed up by your culture’s standards. Hell, even by my standards. But hey, you gotta play to your audience.”

“I don’t want to play to that audience.”

“That is an excellent point. Pick your audience wisely before you start blindly pandering.”

“How can I pick my audience? I don’t even have an audience. I killed a giant snake, and all I got was—was a ten second clip of me hissing at a snake! A normal-sized snake!” Eric pointed out. “I thought I was supposed to have an advantage here.”

“You do. You got airtime. Do you know how many humans there are?” Joe pointed out.

Eric opened his mouth, then shut it. Ten billion or so--call it five billion, what with the Audience, and how many had appeared in the show? And he had appeared. That already put him in an elite group of a few hundred, maybe a few thousand if he was generous and included the people in the backgrounds of shots. Once the Game started properly, he was sure those billions would decline quickly, but for now, the competition was just as fierce as it had been on his smartphone—no, maybe even more fierce. Everyone and their mom was trying to get famous now, after all. Literally. Fame had gone from fun-to-have to a matter of life and death. People who had never had any interest in the internet suddenly had a strong incentive to care about making it big.

He took a deep breath and let it out. He was frustrated because he’d almost died and gotten almost nothing for it, but what had he really expected? A sweeping panorama of his battle, all of the running around and ambushing and so on, when the show only spanned an hour or so? They would have had to devote an inordinate amount of time to him if he featured that much, and even he had to admit; the battle wasn’t that good. Besides, as Joe had reminded him beforehand, the Tutorial episodes were more about introducing the various audiences to the Players than showcasing individual battles. He got an introduction. That was already money in the bank.

Fame wasn’t something they were going to hand out easily. If he wanted it, he’d have to fight for it tooth and nail, just like everyone else.

And then he put a hand to his chin as a thought came to him. “All those people, everyone in the Tutorial… they’re not being prepared for what they’re actually going to face.”

When Joe had said people left the Tutorial at level 10, he’d assumed it was a very slow levelling system, but he’d gotten two levels from the snake alone. Sure, he’d beaten a boss, but he was going to beat more than one boss in a year if he’d already beaten one by now. But instead, in the Tutorial, the Players were walked through basic fighting form and given one monster to kill at the end of their day. At the rate of one monster a day, how long would it take for him to level up? Assuming that imp is about as much EXP as a snakeman… He’d cleared an entire dungeon, but the boss had been the one to push him over from 2 to 3. Sure, it’d then given him enough to push him all the way to level 4, so he probably hadn’t been far from level 3 when he’d killed it, but still. He grimaced. It could easily take a year or more to hit level ten at that rate.

That, and when they landed in the World Dungeon, they’d have no idea what was about to hit them. They were blind and naïve, led to believe that killing one unarmed monster meant they were masters of their chosen class, unaware of the actual state of the world, where they’d face mobs of enemies at once while also kicking back snakes and stomping cockroaches. And not only that, but Joe had implied that it would get worse as time passed, that this was a weak state for the dungeon.

He shuddered. “When they get here, they’ll get slaughtered.”

“Hmm? Oh, yeah. Well, that’s built in, isn’t it? The first few days after the mass release will have higher viewership and ratings than anything for months afterwards. Sometimes even years. Sometimes even for the whole Game. The people want to see blood, and boy, will they.” Joe grinned and rubbed his hands together in anticipation.

Eric narrowed his eyes at him.

Joe threw his hands up. “I mean, oh no! What horror! I can’t believe they’d do that to you bald monkeys!”

“Apes,” Eric corrected for the second time. “Bald apes.”

“You’re lucky I know what a monkey is.”

“I was thinking,” he interrupted, before the conversation could get further off track, “that I should help them. Do something to try and… I dunno, inform them of what’s actually waiting for them?”

Joe shrugged. “Play to the home audience? Sure, why not? It’s a good starting point. I wouldn’t end there—your home audience isn’t going to buy you a whole lotta goodies, if you know what I mean—but getting a big following at home is the first step to getting a big following where it matters, for a lot of people. Those people they highlighted? Most of them have a big following here, at home, too.”

“Really?” Eric asked. He hadn’t recognized any of them. To be fair, he watched fairly niche streamers, not the top twenty or so. And that was just among English-speaking, mostly US-based streamers. When it came to the planetary top twenty, he didn’t even know where to start. In the first place, were they even on the platforms he watched? Or were they on short-form video platforms, or even old-fashioned traditional platforms? He wasn’t surprised he didn’t recognize all of them, but he was a little surprised he didn’t recognize any of them.

“Big is relative. And it’s not just the top twenty like you’re thinking. There’s also a… je ne sais quoi that they’re looking for that makes someone a big star in the wider market. Being big at home is ultimately a bonus, but it does help them narrow down the search from the starting point of several billion to a few million.”

Eric stared at Joe for a moment. The gothed out, punked out, grunge tattoos man saying a French phrase was almost too much for him to handle.

Joe raised his bedazzled brows at Eric, then tapped his temple. “This guy had a big vocabulary, believe it or not. Don’t judge a book by its cover.”

“I guess not,” Eric allowed. “But that’s not the point. I was thinking—”

“I approve. Get at it.”

Eric blinked. “I didn’t even explain my thoughts.”

“You’re going to try and show the folks at home what dungeons are like, right? It’s a good idea. Gets you hometown sympathy and doesn’t ragebait anyone else too badly. You’ll probably get ignored, but hey, you’ve gotta start somewhere. Once you make your first attempt or two, that’s when it’s worth it for me to step in and make recommendations. Right now, whatever you do is good enough. You won’t do it well enough for it to matter, anyways.”

“I love the optimism,” Eric deadpanned.

“Eh. When you’re in the biz as long as me, you see some things. Either you pop off and unicorn with this, in which case, I need to play support and let you do your thing, or, more likely, you fail wildly but learn enough from this to understand the basics of what you need to do to succeed. At that point, my advice is far more valuable and you’re more likely to listen to me, because you’ve learned you don’t know how this Game works. It’s a win-win in my book.”

“You really have a way with words.”

“Thank you.”

Eric ran his hand through his hair, not sure what else to say, then glanced at Joe. “Why’d you tell me to clear that dungeon today? The reset at midnight?”

“Yeah. Dungeons don’t stay dead forever; they reset on a certain timeline. What that timeline is depends on the dungeon; could be minutes, hours, days… years, even. But that’s just a little reset. That’s not important. What matters is that every month--at least for the first scenario, anyways, there’s a big refresh that rolls out and rebalances the dungeons. The first big refresh is a bit shorter than the rest so it lines up with the first episode. I figured, you get a better chance that they pick a shot of you and you get ahead of the reset. That being said… you’re probably going to have to move on.”

“Oh, yeah?”

“Yeah. All the dungeons get harder when the big reset comes around, but the ones that are cleared the most get reset to be the hardest. The number one most cleared dungeon in the world becomes the most powerful regular dungeon in the world, and so on and so forth down from there.” He waved his hand. “For the most part, it doesn’t do much, since by the time you hit the fifth or sixth most cleared dungeon, you’re in the chaff where hundreds, if not thousands, of dungeons have been cleared the same number of times. Rather than being concentrated in one dungeon, the difficulty spike penalty is spread across every dungeon at the same clear rating, which at thousands of dungeons makes it almost meaningless, no more than the usual difficulty bump. It’s meant to penalize campers and people who grind one dungeon repeatedly without bothering to interact with any other dungeons. There’s no thrill in watching the same guy do the same thing he’s already done a thousand times; the viewers want excitement. They want blood.”

“But right now, only one dungeon has been cleared. Which means the dungeon I cleared will become the strongest in the world,” Eric realized aloud.

Joe pointed finger guns at Eric. “Bingo.”

Yep. Can’t clear that if I barely beat an average dungeon. “Thanks for the heads-up.”

Joe shrugged. “If you walked there, it’d warn you at the door; that’s why I was able to tell you. I just saved you a short walk.”

“Still… thanks for saving me the hike.”

Joe waved dismissively. “You’ve got some ultrarares to open, don’t you? Better get to that. I’ll see ya later.”

Right, nearly forgot. He went to wave Joe away, then paused. “What about when there aren’t enough people left?”

“Hmm?”

“This is a death game, right? People die? What about when there’s more dungeons than people? When there aren’t enough people to clear the constantly-refreshing dungeons?”

“You’ll find out when you get there. I can’t say.” Joe made a hush-hush gesture and smirked.

“Yeah, yeah.” Eric saluted, and Joe vanished in a puff of smoke. Hopping down from the counter, Eric turned to his ultrarares. It was finally time to open his rarest rewards.
