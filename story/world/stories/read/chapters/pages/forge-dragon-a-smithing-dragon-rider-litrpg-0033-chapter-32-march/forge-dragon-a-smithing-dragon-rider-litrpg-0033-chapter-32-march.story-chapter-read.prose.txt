Once confident Kelia wasn’t about to die on him, Caleb moved to make her comfortable in sheltered outcropping that was the wyverns’ nest. Within this outcropping saw a massive nest—made more from branches and logs than sticks and leaves within which sat a wyvern egg.

Well, what good judgement told them all was a wyvern egg. None of them had ever seen one, but the large egg the size of Kelia’s torso couldn’t likely be much else.

They briefly debated eating it, but decided against it. The empire was in the village and they’d likely pay them handsomely for it. The army did employ wyvern riders and if this one took after its cold-aspected parent, this would be doubly valuable.

While the boys had come unprepared to spend the night, Avis caught them a few rabbits, dropping them before Caleb with disdain. The bird apparently didn’t like that her master was dependent on the filthy Caleb, and to be honest, Caleb didn’t like it either. He was used to Kelia taking care of his own negligence to his health, and being the one to take care of her felt backwards.

Then he felt a bit shameful at that, and tried to push the thought aside.

“Caleb!” Grisim’s voice called urgently from outside the cavern. “Come look at this.”

Caleb ran out, half expecting this to be a joke on his brother’s part. Grisim wasn’t the best actor but he seemed to have a skill that increased his proficiency in all things two fold when pranking his brother.

Unfortunately, the call wasn’t a joke, and Caleb immediately saw what the problem was.

A snow storm was blowing into their village down the valley of the ice volcanoes. While normally a blizzard was no strange occurrence, this one was coming from the wrong direction. Natural blizzards were storms bellowing in from the south west that chilled as they rose into the mountains. This one was heading in from the north east, out of the glacial basin. There was only one thing that could cause this—at least that the brothers knew, and much like the wyvern egg it was a safe bet.

“The Frigidi are attacked,” Caleb said, somehow certain this was somehow his fault.

They ran back inside to tell Kelia, and she quickly scrawled a note onto Avis’ leg and sent her home to warn the village.

“We have to go,” Caleb told Kelia.

“I can’t,” she told him. “I’m not dying, but I wont be able to keep up with you two. I’m exhausted.”

Caleb knew the truth to her words and didn’t argue it.

“Take this,” she said, handing him her ingot of +2 constitution. “It will be more use for you against their ice magic than strength.”

Caleb didn’t protest, and suddenly felt bad once more that she was the one trying to take care of him.

He reshuffled his stat boosting equipment until he had a +6 to constitution and a +2 to strength, only realizing then that he’d leveled, allocating his stats to his mental ones across the board. He noted that his capacity for stat enhancements had increased to 9 from 8, and quick math suggested that the boost really was simply one tenth of your total attributes.

Grisim and Caleb emptied their bags of everything but what they’d need, and began their mad dash home.

This skill was the codification by the system of all his work earlier in improving his quenching technique. Even with the pressing matters at hand, he was eager to see how much of the quenching steel he could use now, aided as he would be by the system in maintaining the integrity of the part.

These choices were interesting. None of them were immediately helpful, but still, they were interesting. Not Quick Strike though, that was a complete waste.

Mounted Warrior had implications for when Bog was larger and for the short term. The skill had no mention of Caleb needing to actually be riding Bog for the benefits to work, though he suspected the sharing would be limited if they weren’t touching. This could potentially let him work for even longer in the forge—though he’d need to learn how to actively draw upon his stamina. As it was he could already work all day. With this, he could work all day at a higher rate.

The Resist Aspect (metal) though called to Caleb in a way he couldn’t articulate. The description was short, the name simple, but the implications great. Resist metal. He didn’t know what the mechanics of resisting metal would be, but he knew this would be invaluable.

He almost picked it then before he considered. He’d never fought enemies wearing or using metal of any kind outside of training.

Do I need this? I want it, but when will I fight men?

The Frigidi he was running to face now didn’t use steel, they fought with steel strengthened ice.

He ran with Grisim, thinking it over, and even asked his brother’s opinion who gave some surprisingly sound advice.

“Do you think you could figure out the sharing thing or resistance on their own?”

Caleb thought about it, and quickly probed his bond with Bog—who they were running along side, not wanting to face a horde of Frigidi without their iron dragon—decided quickly that the bond as it was offered no insight into the skill.

Then he considered the resit metal. He’d sort of already done that with Immutable Metal.

Could I do the same but with myself?

That seemed much more likely. So, taking his brother’s advice to heart, he pushed aside the skill that called to him, vowing to figure it out himself before he was inevitably re offered it at level 10, and selected Mounted Warrior.

The mana within was passively being pulled in by his acuity and held there by his willpower. Caleb always had a sense for this constant subconscious effort that his status screen conveniently gave numbers too, but if he tried to guess he would be very close to those numbers just from this vague sensation.

Caleb suddenly felt access to a much larger pool though if the normal pool he sensed felt like his fingers, nimbly under his control this mana was like stickers tied to them. He could still manipulate it, b ut it was crude. The mana would only be useful for directly fueling skills
