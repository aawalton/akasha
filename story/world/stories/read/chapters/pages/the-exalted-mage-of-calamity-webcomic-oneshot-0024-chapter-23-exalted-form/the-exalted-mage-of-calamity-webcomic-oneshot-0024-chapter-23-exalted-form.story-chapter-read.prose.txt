Finn ran with all his might.

Branches tore at his sleeves. Roots caught beneath his shoes and snapped apart. Smoke burned his throat with every breath, but he didn’t stop. He couldn’t.

His sides hurt from the way the man had carried him. His face was swollen from being punched. His arms were sore, and his teeth ached from biting down as hard as he could on the man’s arm. The taste of blood was still in his mouth. Maybe it was the cultist’s. Maybe it was his own.

Nevertheless, he had gotten away. He knew the cultists always carried smoke pellets in their back pockets. He’d spied on them enough to know their tricks. He had even snagged a few extras just now, just in case.

He just couldn’t believe that even with the envoy here, they still needed more sacrifices. Especially children. That was all he could manage to hear as his mind swam back into consciousness.

Finn kept running. The only thing left spinning through his mind was getting back to town. Finding Veronica. Maybe alerting the guards. Contacting the steward or even Captain Luthen.

As he swept branches and leaves out of his way, a thought came to him.

Hopefully Old Thom is alright…

It was only a coincidence that he had spotted a cultist sneaking around. He had been about to turn around and warn Veronica, but that was when the cultist spotted Old Thom.

The cultist tried attacking him, and Finn wasn’t sure whether to join in or alert the guards. But once that knife was pulled out… Finn made the decision to jump in. He was too late, though. The cultist still managed to stab Old Thom. And all Finn got for it was several punches to the gut and face before being knocked out.

“Just have to make it back to town,” he muttered between panting breaths.

Luckily, it was just a few grown-ups and a scary-looking demon monster that seemed too bulky to give chase. He was confident in his ability to escape.

He heard an explosion behind him. He didn’t turn. Luckily, he had already begun running and had a head start. Whatever they wanted to do, he could outrun it.

That was until the forest behind him pulsed with light. A roar like an erupting volcano shook the air. Finn flinched; he stumbled and fell, scraping his palms against the dirt. He tried to scramble back up, but when he looked over his shoulder, he froze.

The demon’s chest was burning. Through the few trees between them, Finn saw the light. It was hard to miss.

Fire pooled in its throat, molten cracks spreading through its armor-like skin. The blast was meant for him.

Finn’s breath caught. He tried to get up, his mind screaming at him to run.

He’s really far away… there’s no way, right?! he thought. But if he’s going to attack me anyway, then that means…!

His limbs wouldn’t move. He was trapped in his own thoughts. Would it reach him or not?

Seconds passed as his throat grew drier. His palms began to sweat as he froze like a deer before torches.

Move… move…!

His thoughts were cut short as a figure suddenly appeared between him and the walking inferno called a demon. The earth before him exploded into dirt and small twigs, scattering in every direction and showering over him.

At first, it was only a silhouette, blurred by flame and smoke. But he saw the hair, long and wild, gleaming faintly beneath the rising moon.

She raised her hands. A shimmering wall of light and force billowed outward, spanning wide. Wide enough to cover her, but also him.

The world became fire. An endless torrent of heat rolled over them.

The blast consumed everything. Trees shriveled into cinders, dirt blasted upward, and the canopy was ripped away as if the sky itself had fallen. Finn hit the ground with his arms over his head, but the searing heat still licked around him.

It felt endless. A furnace swallowing the forest.

And then… silence.

Finn slowly cracked his eyes open.

The forest hadn’t vanished completely—just most of it. Directly ahead of him, the trees still stood, their bark unscorched and the grass still green. But to his left and right stretched a cone of annihilation.

The blast had carved a path of ruin that fanned outward like a giant’s claw. Entire trees were nothing but charred spires, their bark blackened and crumbling. Branches collapsed into cinders at the faintest touch of wind. The ground was stripped bare, reduced to ash and glowing embers that still smoldered where roots had once been.

Finn froze. He sat at the very tip of that cone. Had Veronica not stood where she had, had her barrier not bent the flames… he would have been nothing but ash among the trees.

Relief burst through him. He laughed once, breathless and almost giddy. “You… you blocked it,” he whispered. “You actually blocked it! I knew you were strong!”

Excitement surged in his chest. Veronica wasn’t just some traveling mage—she was powerful. Powerful enough to stop a demon’s fire. He was sure she’d be the one to solve Greystone’s mystery.

But then he looked again. Closer this time.

Her silhouette wavered in the haze. The barrier was gone now, leaving only her body outlined against the ruin. She still stood, but smoke trailed from her arms. Her shoulders hunched as if every breath weighed more than she could bear.

Finn’s smile faltered. The rush of excitement drained from his face, replaced by a sharp, piercing stab of realization.

She hadn’t emerged unscathed. She had held it all, taken everything head-on.

“…Veronica,” Finn whispered, his voice cracking. Was she injured because of him?

Before he could say or think anything else, she turned around.

For a moment, he thought she was about to yell. To reprimand him like the others in town. Tell him that he should have called an adult. That he shouldn’t have been so reckless and gotten in the way. That calling for her would have been better than taking things into his own hands.

But instead, she crossed the few steps of distance between them and pulled him into her arms.

Finn froze.

“H-hey—”

Her grip tightened around him.

She was trembling. Her fingers curled into the back of his shirt, as if she needed to make sure he was really there. That he wouldn’t leave.

“Thank Nidhogg,” she whispered. “You’re safe.”

Finn stared over her shoulder, his eyes wide with confusion. He didn’t know what a “Nidhogg” was, but he couldn’t find the words to ask. Instead, his gaze lowered.

Smoke curled from what remained of Veronica’s sleeve. Black soot streaked her arms, neck, and cheek. Parts of her clothes had been burned away, and beneath the ash, patches of skin looked blackened or scarred.

“Veronica?” Finn’s voice shrank. “H-hey… you’re covered in ash.”

“It’s okay.”

“You don’t look okay,” he said.

“I’m fine,” she said, her tone firmer this time. Her arms tightened once more, and the trembling began to fade.

She let him go.

She stood tall and turned back toward the demon and the remaining cultists. The smoke was clearing, and they all saw one another through the fading ash.

“Run,” she said.

Finn swallowed. “What?”

“Go back to town. Warn the baron and the viscount. Tell them the cultists are after Claire and that Greystone may be attacked.” Her voice steadied with every word. “Find Hadrian, Elise, Luthen—anyone who will listen. Get as far from this place as you can.”

“But you—”

“Finn.” Her tone was stone-cold. “Go. Now.”

Finn’s lips trembled, but he forced his mouth shut. His fists clenched at his sides. Then, with a stiff nod, he turned and bolted. Tears streamed down his cheeks as he vanished into the tree line.

One of the three cultists coughed, still recovering from inhaling the earlier smoke bomb. His arm was bleeding, with suspiciously child-sized bite marks on it.

He spotted Finn making a run for it. “Oh no, he doesn’t!” He lunged after Finn, intent on following him.

Veronica’s head twitched upward just enough to reveal one glowing eye through the curtain of her hair. Her arm rose. Light gathered in her palm. Brilliant… and merciless.

The cultist never made it another step. His body detonated in a white-hot blast, erased from existence as a brilliant beam tore through the air, leaving nothing but half a cloak and a pair of boots. The beam continued, striking the tree behind him and toppling the entire thing backward.

The remaining cultists staggered back, fear flashing behind their masks. The demon, however, did not flinch. It leaned forward, its molten eyes narrowing. Its voice grated like stone dragged over gravel.

“You… who are you?”

Slowly, Veronica lifted her head. Her hair fell aside, revealing her face smeared with ash, yet unbroken all the same. Fury sharpened her features in the dark, but a faint curve of a grin—smugness—tugged at her lips beneath the moonlight. Her eyes blazed, violet light burning from within.

Her heart rate returned to normal. The thumping in her ears stopped, replaced by a whirring echo deep within her body. Her vision had returned, and she no longer felt like she was choking. She could take a full breath again.

The panic attack was gone. She was back in familiar territory. Her mana was like a relentless torrent, but it was fully under her control. Still, it was a far cry from her reserves at the 10th tier, but at least now she didn’t feel like a broken twig.

“Me?” she said, her voice calm, steady, almost demanding reverence. “I’m Veronica. Just a normal mage passing through.”

Her Exalted Form.

The reason people once called her the Exalted Mage had nothing to do with being a 10th-tier mage. It was never about raw rank. It was a technique—one only possible for someone with two mana cores.

Years of study had gone into it. Painful years, and very careful ones. Most of them had been spent alongside Maeve, her mentor and teacher, dissecting her condition piece by piece, searching for something—anything—that could be turned into an advantage.

Maeve had been the one to name it.

The name of the technique still embarrassed Veronica, regardless.

But she couldn’t deny the truth. It was powerful. And it was the only good thing that had ever come from Medusa’s curse. It was a technique she had only learned at the fifth tier. She had never used it this early before.

Intermittent use, then? Phasing in and out of my exalted form for this fight… How annoying.

Veronica exhaled, slow and steady. She pinched and rubbed her fingers together. Ash crumbled off in small flakes.

Her outfit was practically ruined; half of it now clung loosely to her body. The right side of her clothes was intact, while the left side had been completely burned away. What fabric remained only served to frame the upper black strap and curve of her bra, which stood stark against the black soot streaking across her skin. The flames had also carved small holes that still smoldered, exposing her side, which was now also veiled in black ash.

She clicked her tongue. “Tch. I only bought this set a few days ago. And now look at it.” Her eyes flicked down to her body. “That’s the last time I buy expensive, unenchanted clothes.”

The cultists stared at her. “How did you block that attack?” one of them shouted.

Veronica ignored them.

She pulled off what used to be the left sleeve of her dress, now only a rag. She slowly ran her hands across her skin, brushing away the soot in long streaks and wiping her shoulders, neck, and sides as best she could.

Although she had been consumed by the fire, the moment her Exalted Form activated, nearly all of the initial burst of mana she received was converted into excess mana solely to power a form-fitting barrier. This was the excess mana reward she had received.

Thanks again, Sage.

She wouldn’t have died from the fire, but at least this way, she avoided much of the damage and pain. Her clothes had, too—at least enough of them to keep her modest. The money she had spent on them, however, had practically—and literally—gone up in flames.

One of the other cultists found his voice, seeing the nonchalance of Veronica in front of them. It was like she was mocking them, more concerned about the dirt on her than being in their presence. They couldn’t handle the silence any longer, seeing that the demon wasn’t making a move yet.

“If you think sending that boy off changes anything…” He pointed a trembling hand at her. “You’re delusional. That one strike nearly killed you. You’re too weak to stop us, even if you took care of the assassins we sent. We know your capabilities.”

Veronica chuckled. It was soft at first, then low and dark as she tilted her head, still brushing soot from her collarbones and shoulders.

“You got two things wrong,” she said.

The cultist tensed. “Wrong?”

She raised a finger, as if lecturing a child. “First—you assume what you saw me do against those assassins was the limit of my power.”

The cultist said nothing, but from his body’s movements, there was clear hesitation. He slowly reached behind himself, ready to attack.

Veronica’s second finger lifted, calm and deliberately slow. “Second—you think I sent Finn away just to warn the town? That was part of it, sure. Maybe he’ll save some lives if they listen to him. But the real reason…” She paused, her lips curling. Then she flicked her hand upward in a clawing sweep, two white wings flashing ever so briefly.

The air howled sharply.

The two cultists flinched, grabbing hold of the daggers at their waists—but it was too late.

A whirlwind erupted beneath them. They were both yanked screaming into the air before slamming hard against the trunks of the trees behind them. A sickening crunch rang out, splitting bark and bone alike. Blood sprayed across the wood around them. Both crumpled, their bodies involuntarily coughing up more blood, their masks broken and falling away.

They died a few seconds later.

Veronica straightened, ripping one last strip of ruined cloth from her hip. Now she looked like the war-born, apocalyptic mage of the past, as if she had run through a battlefield of flames—a hint of what she would become again in the future.

Her violet gaze sharpened, fixed squarely on the last towering figure. The demon stared back at her, growling low. It didn’t seem too concerned by the fact that the cultists beside it had just met their demise.

“The real reason,” she murmured, almost to herself, “was so I wouldn’t get distracted by him getting hurt. Can’t exactly fight at my best while protecting someone.”

Light gathered in her palm; spectral wings flared into being, sharp and white.

One wing… two wings… three wings. Her hair lifted as mana and power formed an aura around her body. Her clothes fluttered as the mana within her could no longer be contained.
