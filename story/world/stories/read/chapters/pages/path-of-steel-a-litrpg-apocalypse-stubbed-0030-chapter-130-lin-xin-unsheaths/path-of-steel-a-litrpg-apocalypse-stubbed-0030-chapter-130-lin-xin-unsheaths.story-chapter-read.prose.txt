As the situation still calmed down, Rollo Frollo rushed to speak. If he gave that clan any more seconds, they might start fighting again.

“Let’s present the tournament rewards!” he said, drawing attention as treasure always did. “For making it through the preliminaries, the sixteen finalists have all earned a whooping ten drops of Muscle Sap, along with one thousand aura stones!”

Wondrous exclamations rose from the ground. Gary didn’t know whether to laugh or cry. Is that so much? I’ve already gotten, like, ten times that…

Cog nudged him as if reading his thoughts. “That’s great wealth to the average person, Gary. Even Birth of Aura cultivators can only earn a few hundred aura stones per month, so this is multiple wages, plus the Muscle Sap that’s impossible to acquire otherwise.”

“I guess my perception is skewed,” Gary said.

“However, these rewards are only the beginning!” Rollo exclaimed. “The fighters who win today and make it to the top eight will receive another ten drops of Muscle Sap and a thousand aura stones. Those who make it to the top four will receive another two times that, for a grand total of forty drops and four thousand aura stones!”

The audience was heating up now. Even Gary was getting excited. Ranking in the top four, which he might with a little luck, would give him half as much as the entire Muscle Sap operation so far—without any risk.

“Starting from the top three, however, there will be additional rewards. Are you ready to hear them?” Rollo asked, and the audience collectively cried out, “Yes!” Gary almost replied himself.

“The disciple who ranks third will receive a Flying Fools Card—an exceedingly rare life-saving amulet! When they’re in a pinch, even if pursued by cultivators an entire realm higher, they’ll be able to crush it and escape without a problem! Moreover, everyone will know they possess it, so who would dare challenge them? There is no greater threat than a genius you can’t quite put down!”

“Ohhh,” the audience muttered.

“The second-ranked disciple will receive a Bulb of Enlightenment—a priceless item that can only be found, not bought! When the bulb is lit, it is said to increase the comprehension of anyone who sits under it, accelerating their progress in understanding vibrations!”

“Ahhh!” the audience exclaimed.

“Holy Mother of Metal! A comprehension treasure!” Cog said, gears spinning fast.

“Is that rare?” Gary asked.

“Incredibly so! People like me would never see one in our lifetimes. Comprehension is the hardest thing to increase, even more so than talent! Although, I’ve never heard of this bulb. Its effect must be minimal, otherwise it wouldn’t be a second-place reward. Actually, it wouldn’t be given out at all…but increasing your comprehension even a little is a massive advantage!”

“Is that so?” Gary asked. Back in the third floor, when he’d taken the inheritance trial, his talent had been barely sufficient to pass. His comprehension, however, had been praised.

He didn’t know anyone else who’d fused two vibrations during Birth of Aura.

“The rewards are much better than usual. Is it because of the double clan selection? What could be the reward for first place…” Alicia muttered.

“Are you wondering what’s in it for first place?” Rollo asked, drawing out the answer. “Are you ready to hear it?”

“Yes!” the audience replied.

“The disciple who ranks first in all of their generation will receive a treasure so valuable, so precious, so rare and highly sought-after, that it doesn’t need an explanation. They will receive…a vial of Temper Serum!”

Everyone in the audience collectively gasped.

“A Temper Serum!” Cog explained. “Feng Shen is so damn lucky!”

“If only I could get it…” Alicia said, clenching her fists.

Gary was also lost for words. He’d already received a vial of Temper Serum as a reward for the Floor of Trials’ boss ball quest. That didn’t mean he scoffed at it. Quite the opposite—the Temper Serum had been pivotal in his future journey. If not for it, he would have died at the third floor’s inheritance trial, and even if not, his entire path would have been slower. He’d never have reached these heights. He’d never have won all those marginal battles.

Talent was one of the greatest attributes of a cultivator. It affected the rate at which they gathered aura, but also their aura control and the speed at which they could improve it. It made a cultivator’s moves sharper, stronger, and more efficient. A person with low talent could practice for months for what a person with high talent could achieve in days. It was, in a way, the speed at which a cultivator progressed.

Only vibrations weren’t included in that. They were covered by comprehension, an equally important attribute which took the lead in the higher realms.

Gary’s talent had been only decent. Even after he ingested the Temper Serum, he was nothing special. His talent was rated as low earth. Feng Shen was high earth, and Lin Xin was low heaven. The differences between them were massive.

Even though Gary trained all day every day, his true strengths lay in his stats and vibration. His aura control had always been a weakness. The only reason it hadn’t bottlenecked him yet was because he practiced really really hard, barely keeping up with others who could put in half the work for double the result.

If he got another vial of Temper Serum, however…

That might change.

Greed lit up Gary’s eyes. I must win first place.

No, wait. I must beat Sui Duang first. Talent means nothing if I’m dead.

He glanced at his opponent again, but Sui Duang wasn’t looking. His eyes were turned at the arena—filled with greed as Gary’s had been. The only difference was, Sui Duang thought he’d win.

Gary shook his head.

“All rewards will be given after the tournament. For now, let’s focus on what’s ahead of us: a day of wonderful fights between the strongest disciples this generation of the spear has to offer! I present to you—the finals!”

Under the crowd’s cheers, Rollo leapt from the sands to a special platform above the brackets board. “In the first fight, welcome a woman of special renown! Having trained for only a few months in the clan, she’s already caught up with the older generation—but has she surpassed them? Wielding ice, a legendary spear, and a low heaven-grade talent, please welcome: Lin Xin!”

The crowd cheered as a woman leapt on stage. She wore the same white robes as usual, contrasting her long black hair. Her beautiful features were obstructed by the distance, but a projection appeared over the arena, focusing on her face. Many men in the audience sighed. She gave a curt bow.

“Facing her is the prodigy of this floor’s northern swamplands. A boy who grew into a man by choking a python. I present to you, Roooooy “Snake Boy” Baaaaane!”

A small man landed opposite Lin Xin. A green-and-black robe covered him from the neck down, while his long-sleeved arms held onto a spear carved to resemble a python. The snake’s head ended just before the head, making the spearhead seem as if it was jutting out of the snake’s throat.

That was no Spear Hold spear. It had been custom-made for this man. When the projection gave him a close-up, his face was dark and expressionless. His eyes whispered of horrors.

A high-ranked deacon jumped into the arena to officiate.

“Please give me guidance,” Lin Xin said, bowing lightly. The boy nodded. They raised their spears.

“Fight!” Rollo said, and the two fighters rushed at each other. Their spears blurred. They exchanged a dozen strikes in an instant, each showcasing speed and technique at the highest levels. While they were evenly matched, however, their moves were entirely different. The snake boy slithered around the stage, always on the move, spear biting at unexpected angles. His thrust was sharp. Lin Xin, on the other hand, held her ground. Her moves were steady and left no gaps. Her attacks forced the snake boy to reposition, as he couldn’t or didn’t want to face her in a direct clash. Their spears danced for a time. The projection magnified their battle for all to see.

“Vile Venom!” Snake Boy yelled. Every bite of his spear spat venom at Lin Xin, surrounding her from all directions.

“Snowstorm!” she shouted back. A coat of aura was expended from her body, spinning around her like a snowstorm. The poison aura that entered her range wasn’t just frozen—it fell and shattered to the floor like a real, physical thing.

“Wow,” Alicia said. “They’re both so good.”

“I’m almost glad we didn’t make it to the finals,” Cog said.

“This the first bracket. I’m in the second. I won’t meet any of them until the final,” Gary said.

“Isn’t Feng Shen at their bracket?” Cog asked.

“Yeah.”

“You aren’t meeting them in the final either.”

Spears clashed below. The sand was coated white where Lin Xin stood, while green streaks ran over it in places. Where there was poison, the sand boiled.

Snake Boy ducked under a strike and jumped away. He remained expressionless. Suddenly, his robes flew up, revealing a slim and wiry body beneath. They hid no concealed weapons; instead, his robes themselves became an asset, twirling around his hands and hiding their movement. His spearplay turned trickier, biting at Lin Xin like a snake from the underbrush.

She frowned and stepped back to assess the new threat, but her eyes changed. Green streaks of poison covered the sand around her. She was lost in a minefield, with no time to look down. The snake bit at her from the front. Steam rose in every other direction.

Finally, Lin Xin was pressured.

“What a sinister plan!” Rollo Frollo exclaimed. “Snake Boy masterfully coated the sand in poison to box her in. Lin Xin was none the wiser! Her inexperience shows! This tribesman is more than a brute—he’s wickedly intelligent! A master hunter!”

The audience cheered at the high-intensity battle. Sparks flew where spears clashed. Snow met venom. Chanting rose to the heavens.

“Lin Xin! Lin Xin!”

“Snake Boy! Snake Boy!”

Gary doubted whether the fighters could even hear the cheers. An instant of distraction could prove lethal. They accelerated until their spears were only blurs, Lin Xin surviving the onslaught by pure spearplay. Finally, however, the opponent’s spear slowed.

“Oho! Tired already? Someone forgot to invest in Vitality!” Rollo joked.

“Everyone has a weakness,” Cog lamented.

“No, that’s not it. It’s frozen,” Gary said.

“What!?”

Snake Boy jumped back, and the truth became apparent. He wasn’t panting. His wiry body favored endurance, anyway. Ice had caked parts of his spear, making it heavier and messing with its weight distribution. Melting it would be as simple as pouring aura into the spear, but he couldn’t afford that. Lin Xin stepped after him. She pressed on. It took everything Snake Boy had to defend, but he was forced to retreat, and the ice barragged him. More of it caked his spear, until it was more white than green. The snake head got covered. His moves grew slow.

Snake Boy was trapped between a slow defeat or a ray of hope. He chose the latter. Jumping back, he poured a river of aura into his spear, melting all the ice, but that was an instant where he wasn’t defending. Lin Xin erupted as if she’d waited for precisely that. She slashed out, her spear not reaching Snake Boy but unleashing a violent snow storm that swallowed him whole. Whooshing sounds filled the arena. Lin Xin dove into her storm, and white flashes were all anyone could see.

Only the projection gave a better view, piercing the snow storm to show the people inside. Snake Boy was trapped in mid-air. He was fighting desperately, but his venom was frozen the moment it appeared, and though his spear was free of ice, his whole body was caked in it.

The snow storm condensed. A single point of absolute frost erupted on Snake Boy’s back at the same that Lin Xin’s spear came from the front. He couldn’t block both. A full-powered thrust met him square in the chest, sending him flying like an arrow, nailed hard into an arena wall. Stones tumbled. Sand arose. Through it, Snake Boy lay on the ground, motionless, but never leaving his spear.

In the middle of the arena, Lin Xin bowed. “Thank you for your guidance,” she said, panting.

“And skill beats planning! Victory goes to Lin Xin!” Rollo shouted, at the same time that the audience erupted in cheers.

“Lin Xin! Lin Xin! Lin Xin!”

The stadium shook from the roof to the foundation, and Lin Xin bowed four times, one in each direction. Her bow towards the VIP seats was longer and deeper. Snake Boy finally stood up, holding his chest, and hobbled towards the disciple seats.

“Let’s give a round of applause for the brave warrior, Snake Boy!” Rollo said, and the audience clapped wild.

“Good job, Snake Boy!”

“You fought masterfully!”

“Keep it up!”

If he was happy by the cheers, he didn’t show it. He jumped to his seat with the same expressionless face as always and sat alone. Lin Xin followed.

The first battle had been sharp, intense, and climactic. The audience was fired up. This excitement carried through the next duels, each more impressive than the last. Sui Duang dominated his opponent. Feng Shen drew his spear for the first time, but his victory came easy; Gary still couldn’t see that black-and-white strike, only its aftermath. Two people fought a hard, balanced battle where victory was determined by a hair’s width. They both gave it their all. Oracle Jay, the mind-reader Gary had fought before, defeated an underdog without getting hit once.

Finally came the eighth and final battle of the day.

“And now, the moment you’ve all been waiting for,” Rollo Frollo said, winking towards the section preparing a banner. “The Lord of Pot versus Gary “Baldinator” Steel!”
