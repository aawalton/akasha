Risha squinted at the tiny hand-writing of Elder Oltak. Her letters were getting better, but she still had to parse the script little by little.

It was good news about the Academy, and it made her curious to go there herself at some point. Several mages from across her burgeoning Empire had volunteered to be teachers. Even the spiders had sent one of their own.

She set the paper down, and looked up. Elder Kulos looked out the window of her home, studying the movements of the monsters below.

Risha stretched, pulling her arms above her head as she tipped her chair onto its back two legs.

Kulos looked away from the window at her movement, a small smile on his face. “How is that you find a fight every time you leave, Mother?”

Risha frowned at the goblin, then jumped out of her chair. “Risha is a perfectly responsible goblin.”

A snort from the other side of the room drew Risha’s gaze to Sun-Chaser who was looking over her own documents. The fox didn’t look up as she scribbled something into her notes.

Kulos grinned, then looked back out the window. “Things have been changing behind the scenes during your absence.”

Risha blinked, “What?”

“The Dragon.” Sun-Chaser answered for the goblin, “She’s been putting her hands into every movement of the council, nothing malicious, but…” She paused as she frowned at one of her papers, then set it aside, “She’s been redirecting resources to the Scaled city, and had several meetings with the Alphas who still oppose you.”

Risha hummed, her gaze narrowing on the home where she’d last met Elderoa. She closed her eyes, replaying the conversations from several weeks ago. They felt like so long ago now. “Help her move the resources. And send more of our people to assist the Scaled city. I want our farmers, healers, and builders making them better than they ever were. Show them they are better with us than without.”

Sun-Chaser paused, and looked at Risha in surprise, “Yes Mother.”

“As for the meetings…” Risha closed her eyes, “What do you think Kulos?”

The Spymaster didn’t respond right away, instead looking over to where Sun-Chaser was writing Risha’s words into paper. “She is drawing power to herself, and I don’t know what that power is for.”

Risha nodded, “Risha will talk with her.”

A knock on the door made them pause, and a moment later a Goblin Warrior peaked her head into the room. “Mother, Deathbringer Diel is here to speak with you.”

Risha smiled wide at the familiar name, and a moment later, a familiar Orc Warrior stepped into the room, a small scar marked his face above his eye from the battle where she’d captured him from the forces of Chieftain Loklok

Diel knelt on the floor in front of Risha, “Mother Risha, you are as radiant as ever.”

Risha smiled, then walked over to the orc and gently laid her hand on top of the Orc’s head. “Diel, Klaz’zks tells me that you have served her well.”

The orc lowered his head further, “I am honored by her words.”

Risha brought her hand to the orc’s arm, and had him stand. He followed the direction in a smooth movement.

She motioned for him to sit near Sun-Chaser, but he shook his head. “I am here to deliver a message to Arish.”

Risha paused, then looked over to a confused Sun-Chaser, and decided not to clear up the misunderstanding. Her secret identity would remain protected! “A message?”

The Orc nodded, “The Elf, Alalio, wishes to meet with her. He wouldn’t tell me why, but he implied it was important.”

Risha looked at Elder Kulos, and he bowed, “I will fetch Mist-Prowler.”

Risha nodded, then turned to Diel, only for a questioning tone to interrupt her.

“Arish?”

Risha looked over to the Kobold, then looked at the ceiling, “A very good name for a goblin!”

Sun-Chaser snorted, then returned to her work.

Risha looked back to Diel, “It’s important, the Elf’s request, right?”

Diel nodded.

“Tell him we’ll meet him at the tavern in an hour.”

The orc bowed his head, then swept out of the room.

“Arish.”

Risha ignored the kobold, as she made her way to the singular sword that hung on her wall next to Scarlet and Crimson. The spirits of the chainswords reached out to her, but they were too recognizable as hers.

Risha pulled the Sword Of The Calithi down from the wall, the same sword she’d taken from the first Overseer she’d killed.

Minutes later, Mist-Prowler arrived in her goblin form, the blue-stone piercing on her lip flashing in the sunlight as she walked into the room.

Risha smiled at the coyote, as she pulled out the same colorful fox-mask Risha had worn last time.

Sun-Chaser looked up as Risha put on her disguise, then did a double take at the mask Risha was wearing.

“Is that me?”

Risha and Mist-Prowler burst into laughter, but neither one answered as Risha continued to put on a more normal looking cloak.

One last dagger tied to her thigh, and Risha felt ready to explore the city as Arish.

***

Misty led the way through the evening light of the city. Risha found her eyes moving to the many demons that walked through the city. They were working with the humans to expand the territory of the city into the hills, and forest. Sun-Chaser had worked hard to divide land to those willing to work it, and small villages were beginning to sprout between the major cities of Risha’s territory.

The capital city, where Risha was now.

Rishaton, the city that used to be Risha’s old village.

Another orc city on the river simply called Riverton.

Finally, the Scaled City in the mountains.

The Kobolds also had their own home in the hills, but there were less of the other races that made their home there.

Risha was proud of how her city looked, and she made a mental note to look at the others when she made her way to the ocean.

Lanterns, a gift from the cities of the depths, were lit by a High Goblin Laborer who ran down the street at a speed impressive to Risha.

A troupe of entertainers enacted a play that centered around a High Goblin woman in a crown and an impressive older orc who bore a striking resemblance to Klu. Risha had to stop for a second as the woman back-handed a second sniveling orc who held an ax and had a paper crown on his head. The crowd, several of whom were orcs, cheered and laughed.

Misty grabbed her hand and tugged her further into the crowd. Risha followed slowly, her body twisting to continue watching as the orc who looked like Klu walked up beside the Goblin woman, and said a witty line to the sniveling orc that made the crowd laugh even harder.

Risha would have to watch this play in the future.

A mixed group of young monsters walked through the street chatting loudly. Risha noticed the symbol of a spear covered in vines sewed into their robes’ shoulders. It was the same symbol that was on the piece of paper Elder Oltak had sent her. They passed Risha, a young human among them giving Risha a confused look before they were lost with the crowd.

Misty and Risha turned into an alley with several bustling taverns, eventually turning into one of the quieter ones.

Soft music rolled over Risha as they entered the building. A familiar older Orc worked in the back, pouring liquor for those who waited at the bar. At the front, a Dark Elf played a large stringed instrument that Risha hadn’t seen before.

Risha looked over the tables until her gaze settled on a secluded booth in the back. Diel, and the elf waited there drinking from large mugs as they watched the Dark Elf play her music.

Risha took the lead, Misty falling into step behind her as they approached the table.

Alalio straightened at the sight of the two of them and set his mug down, “Arish, Misty, I’m glad you answered my call.”

Risha slid into the booth opposite the Elf, Misty following her a second later. “Arish is happy to be here. Diel told us it was important.”

The Elf nodded, his gaze turning serious, he leaned forward. “You are close to the Goblin Grand Mother, aren’t you?”

Risha tilted her head, and blinked.

“Or at the very least, you’re important. Diel holds your names highly, and you handled yourselves against two Fae assassins. Not to mention that moments after I received a Deathbringer as a guard, the other diplomats received their own protection.” The Elf paused, “I need your help, and your protection.”

Risha shared a look with Misty. The kobold bowed her head, letting Risha make the next move.

Risha hummed as she turned to the Elf, then smiled wide, showing all of her teeth. “What do you need, Alalio?”

“The Ancient Mother is testing the waters by sending me here. There are many Tenders who oppose my presence, who would rather see me dead than have any relation to the nation of monsters.” Alalio tapped his fingers against his mug. “My mission here means more than even I realized, because what you’re doing here, what you’ve accomplished, I want it for my people. I want us to see beyond the term ‘monster’.”

“What is your mission?”

Alalio took a deep breath, then let it go. “To establish an Elven grove in your nation.”

Risha frowned, “What does that mean?”

“A home for my people. I’ve been working slowly with the older trees near the river. But two things are stopping me. Something powerful has bound the forest near your city to it, and breaking those bonds takes time and energy.”

“You said two things.” Misty spoke up, her eyes studying the elf with careful scrutiny.

“Yes.” The elf took a long drink from his mug, then set it down. He leaned back, folding his hands together, “Whoever wants me dead has established a contract with assassins from the fall court. I need to negotiate with the fae to have that contract voided. That’s where you two come in. I need someone to guard me through the entrance into the fae realm.”

“You have Diel.” Misty said.

“And he is strong, but fae are sensitive to death. Taking him through the portal with me wouldn’t be wise.”

Risha looked into Diel’s eyes. She wanted to know if the elf could be trusted. Diel’s gaze was strong and unclouded. He gave her the smallest of nods, signalling his trust in the elf.

Misty opened her mouth to respond, but Risha motioned for her to hold. The coyote in disguise turned to her.

“The Grand Mother is renegotiating her peace with the saplings of the forest.” Risha said, “How are these trees bound? Do they want to be free?”

“They would welcome me if they could.”

“I will speak with the Grand Mother, and she will give you your grove.” Risha ran her tongue over her teeth, “Where is the entrance to the fae realm?”

“In the forest south of Rishaton.”

Between Rishaton and the ocean. It followed the path that Risha already planned to take. “Then Arish and Misty will guard you through their lands.”

“Arish-”

Risha shook her head at Misty. Risha trusted her instincts, and her instincts told her that this Elf’s mission was important.

She would enter this Fae realm herself, and speak with the Fae Mother if she must. If worse came to worse, she had the power to travel through her Void Star to safety.
