Darien’s room in the clinic was sparse, containing only a bed, a small chair tucked into the corner, and a flower vase by the window.

Despite its emptiness, the room felt calm—soft lightning flickering along the walls, drawn curtains muting the outside world, and the faint hum of stabilizing runes etched into the structure itself.

It was peaceful.

Or at least, it was—until their gazes landed on the unconscious young man lying on the bed.

His left side looked intact. Bandaged, bruised, but intact. His right side, however…

Aodhán looked away, unable to stomach the sight. Beside him, someone sobbed softly, drawing the attention of the young healer dozing in the room.

She jerked awake, eyes darting to Darien first before turning toward them, her expression immediately apologetic.

“Excuse me,” she said quietly, pushing herself to her feet. “I’ll give you all a minute.”

“Thank you,” Sophran nodded, his gaze heavy.

For a brief moment, they all stood at the entrance, watching Darien from a distance until finally, one of them moved.

It was Rathis Cloudrend. Darien’s underling.

With shaky steps, he walked to Darien’s bedside, giving the others the strength to follow. Aodhán was the last to enter, shutting the door behind him and inhaling deeply as he moved to stand beside Darien, his eyes already pricking with tears.

His gaze drifted once more to Darien’s right side—to the flesh and bone that had come apart—and his vision blurred.

Aodhán wasn’t a wuss. He had seen burn victims. He had seen injuries terrible enough to expose bone. He had seen heads severed and limbs hacked apart. He had seen soldiers reduced to boiling tissue and ruin from his own attacks.

Yet somehow, the sight before him felt worse.

Darien’s body hadn’t burned. It had unraveled.

His flesh was coming apart into glowing fragments, like shards of existence peeling away from reality itself. Nearly half his face was in that state—eyes, flesh, cheekbones—all splitting into splinters of light barely held together by threads of energy.

And it wasn’t just his face.

His right arm, chest, and leg were the same, barely holding form like a painting being scraped from reality.

Had the space Calamity who saved him arrived even a second later, Darien would have been gone. Reduced to nothing but light and drifting particles.

Aodhán shuddered, and when he finally looked up, he realized there wasn’t a dry eye in the room. Even Halo seemed subdued. He glanced toward Nymera, expecting her to speak, but she shook her head slightly, silently handing him the reins again.

Aodhán sighed and nodded. He was the one with the Path of Authority after all.

He looked at Darien again, his gaze lingering for one second. Then two.

Then he spoke.

“Dry your eyes, everyone. We’ve not come here to mourn… but to heal.” He turned toward Sophran. “Do you think Halo can do this?”

Sophran hesitated before glancing at Halo, who stared intently at Darien with luminous, glowing eyes.

“Can you do this?” Sophran asked quietly.

For a moment, Halo seemed uncertain. Her eyes dimmed slightly.

Sophran’s expression fell. He looked up at Aodhán and began to shake his head—but before he could finish the motion, Halo yipped. Her eyes flashed once more as she leaned forward, raised a paw, and placed it directly on Darien’s unraveling chest.

“What’s she doing?” Rathis asked sharply, glancing at Sophran.

“I…” Sophran hesitated. “I’m not sure.”

“She’s going to destabilize him—”

“Let her work,” Aodhán snapped, glaring at Rathis.

The boy froze instantly, fear flashing across his face, and Aodhán realized he’d let a thread of his aura slip free.

He leashed it immediately.

“Let her work,” he repeated more quietly. “Be patient.”

And so, for the next few seconds, they simply watched Halo stare at Darien’s body, her eyes glowing and dimming unevenly.

Seconds stretched into a minute.

The fragments along Darien’s face trembled—then drifted further apart.

A minute turned into ten.

And with every passing second, it became increasingly obvious that whatever Halo was attempting… wasn’t working.

When the silence stretched too long without change, Sophran finally spoke, unease creeping into his voice.

“She’s trying to restore him—but she can’t lock onto him properly. He’s… off. Misaligned.”

“That’s because she’s trying to make him perfect,” Su Tianan said for the first time since entering the room.

Everyone turned toward him.

“Mirelle did a lot of deconstruction and reconstruction of constructs,” he continued. “If she were here, she’d tell you to take it one piece at a time. One fragment of existence at a time.”

Halo paused. Her glow flickered once. Twice.

Then steadied.

And the moment it did—she exploded with light. Blinding radiance poured from her body, engulfing Darien in a wave of liquid starlight.

Beside her, Sophran huffed sharply as though punched in the gut. Aodhán smiled faintly. He knew the feeling well.

She was drawing willpower from him. And she was drawing a lot of it.

The light spilling from Halo intensified, washing the room away until brightness was all anyone could see.

Sophran groaned again, his reserves draining rapidly, but though Aodhán couldn’t see, he could still speak.

“Control the flow, Sophran,” he commanded. “Remember—you’re giving. Control what you give. You mustn’t bottom out.”

Sophran didn’t answer, but a few seconds later, Aodhán felt the pull on his core lessen slightly. Halo, however, didn’t seem affected in the slightest.

She glowed brighter and brighter until her entire body transformed into strands of searing light.

And then—after what felt like hours, though it couldn’t have been more than minutes—the radiance finally began to fade.

Slowly—reluctantly—the blinding radiance thinned, receding in uneven until the world was no longer drowned in light.

Aodhán opened his eyes at once, impatient to see the result.

Seconds passed as the light continued to subside, then finally, after a few long moments, the radiance receded completely to reveal Darien bathed in the ghostly blue brilliance Aodhán was beginning to recognize as the mark of Halo’s powers.

And he was… complete.

“By all the gods,” someone whispered beside him as they all leaned forward, eyes wide as fragments of existence that had once drifted freely—held together only by threads of energy—now stood restored, stitched back into flesh like a mosaic painstakingly rebuilt from ruin.

Darien’s chest rose and fell. Shallowly, but steadily. His arm rested at his side, no longer unraveling.

His face… restored. He hadn’t awakened yet, but he felt tangible again. Real. As though a stray breeze wouldn’t simply scatter him apart.

He wasn’t perfect. Not yet. But he was whole.

Aodhán smiled then, tension bleeding out of him all at once.

“She did it,” he breathed. “She fixed him.”

For half a heartbeat, nobody moved.

Then Halo yipped excitedly and began licking Darien’s face, and like a pressure valve snapping loose, the emotions in the room burst free.

Relief—pure and unadulterated.

Rathis let out a shuddering breath, his hands hovering over Darien’s as though afraid touching him would wake him from a dream.

“He—he’s…” His voice cracked, and he laughed shakily. “He’s actually whole.”

“Yes,” Sophran breathed, sagging where he stood as relief and exhaustion crashed into him all at once. He stretched his arms toward Halo, who bounded into them happily. “You did it. I can’t believe you actually did it.”

“I can,” Nymera smiled, stepping closer.

Her luminous fingers brushed lightly across Darien’s restored arm, searching. Confirming what Aodhán could already sense.

A second later, her smile brightened—and another wave of excitement swept through the room.

The underlings hugged each other, fresh tears flowing freely while Aodhán, Nymera, and Sophran shared a quiet smile of gratitude between themselves.

Aodhán allowed himself to bask in the relief for a moment before hurrying out to call the healers, quickly briefing them on what they had done.

Suffice to say, even the healers were stunned by the effectiveness of Halo’s powers and didn’t hesitate to voice their disappointment that Sophran didn’t share any of her abilities.

“Maybe it’ll come as your bond deepens,” one of the healers suggested as they finally opened the curtains, allowing natural light to stream into the room.

Sophran crossed his fingers with a grin. “I can only hope.”

They all laughed, excited by the possibility that with a few more days of care, Darien would most likely awaken. Whole. Restored.

Aodhán glanced down at Darien, a broad smile tugging at his lips as he whispered softly, “I can’t wait to see you walking again, buddy. I can’t wait to see you alive.”

Joy sparked in his chest at the words. Yet as his gaze lingered on Darien, the realization struck him that under different circumstances, this could just as easily have been someone else lying on this bed.

Very soon, this could be Crixis. Or any one of the other underlings.

His smile vanished immediately.

The war was still ongoing, and he had no idea what the enemy alliance was planning or how the next few months would unfold. He had been too busy these last weeks to properly train Crixis, and if he wasn’t careful—if he didn’t help the boy grow stronger soon enough—this could very well happen again.

Only Crixis wouldn’t be unconscious.

He would be dead.

And it would be Aodhán’s fault.

The excitement inside him hardened instantly into resolve, and after a few minutes of thought, he said his goodbyes to Darien before turning toward Crixis.

“Let’s go home, buddy. It’s high time we discussed your new Path, don’t you think?”

It had been several hours since they left the clinic, and Aodhán had spent most of that time speaking with Crixis about his new Path.

The Path of Ruinous Inevitability was an excellent one—far stronger than those of the underlings. Perhaps even comparable to Darien’s or Seamus’s… or Mirelle’s before her death.

But it still wasn’t on the level of his own Path, or those of the other transmigrants.

Even so, it held immense potential, and Aodhán intended to squeeze every ounce of strength from it that he could.

He would make Crixis strong, one way or the other.

And the first step toward doing that was teaching him his techniques, beginning with the Perfect Imbuement Technique.

He simply hadn’t had the time since the war began. More than a month had passed already, and he’d kept waiting for the right opportunity.

But the realization he’d had earlier that evening made him understand something important.

There would never be a right time.

Crixis needed guidance now, and if Aodhán neglected him any longer, there was no doubt he would regret it.

Aodhán did not intend to regret it.

So he sat within his cultivation chamber now, storm essence roiling densely around himself and Crixis, who sat opposite him sweating despite the cool atmosphere.

“How goes the technique?” Aodhán asked, opening one eye to glance at him.

“Brutal, Senior Brother,” Crixis gritted out. “Four threads of perfect imbuement so far.”

Aodhán raised an eyebrow at the overly respectful tone, but remembering Nymera’s words, he let it slide this time.

Instead, he nodded.

“Good. Inevitability requires immense control—far more than most Paths. It means you are predictable, yet impossible to stop. It means your enemies will see your attacks coming… yet still fail to evade them. Fail to escape.”

He paused, remembering the duel they’d had an hour earlier. “But you’re not inevitable yet. You failed to land even a single attack on me.”

“You were too fast,” Crixis argued through gritted teeth, struggling to maintain the energized strands within his spirit while still speaking.

“And you were not inevitable,” Aodhán replied with a small smile. “You need more control. There are ten hours until dawn. Triple those strands before then.”

“What?!” Crixis exclaimed, his control shattering instantly. “That’s twelve!”

“It is,” Aodhán replied calmly. “And if you fail, we shall duel again.”

“But—”

“Do it, Crixis. It’s for your own good.”

Crixis relented with a groan, and Aodhán sighed before closing his eyes to resume his meditation.

He had gained a great deal during the four weeks since the war began.

He had perfected many of his skills, evolved others, gained confidence in his Path, improved his control, and advanced nearly ten tiers in an incredibly short period.

Compared to the man he’d been before the war, he was almost unrecognizable.

His skillset had changed drastically. His reaction time had sharpened. His coordination with Varéc had deepened. His instincts had become something almost feral in their precision.

Like he said, he had grown immensely stronger physically.

Spiritually, however… he had barely changed at all.

He hadn’t gained any new seals. He hadn’t evolved his understanding of the Origin Plane. He hadn’t improved his control over chaos, nor discovered why he was suddenly unaffected by the twisted effect.

He hadn’t improved the Energy Enhancement Technique. He hadn’t refined his Aspect. He’d gained no inspirations. No insights. No nothing.

He had simply been advancing physically—and that was dangerous.

Though he wasn’t alone in that. The other transmigrants were the same. They had all been too busy fighting for their lives to truly gain anything from the war beyond survival itself.

Aodhán intended to change that.

No doubt, the Origin Plane had been calling to him throughout the war. He had simply been too busy to listen.

Now, however, he had ten uninterrupted hours and a mind overflowing with half-formed inspirations.

Hopefully, he would gain a seal or two from this.

Hopefully.

Far away from the Storm Sect, on a battlefield torn apart by blood and lightning, the Earth wept crimson through fractured stone where bodies had been erased rather than slain, their absence leaving wounds in reality that refused to close.

Glasslike craters stretched for miles, their edges warped and curling inward as though reality itself had tried—and failed—to cauterize the damage.

Lightning still crawled across the surface in thin, dying veins, flickering without source or direction like nerves firing inside a corpse.

Abyssal essence cloaked everything, spilling endlessly from the colossal crater at the center of the battlefield like the souls of the dead rising toward the surface.

The land was dead.

But the sky… the sky was worse.

It hadn’t healed.

Space hung in jagged layers like shattered panes of invisible glass stacked unevenly atop one another. In some places, it folded inward, bending horizons and refracting light into impossible angles. In others, it had frozen into razor-sharp stillness, entire stretches of reality trapped mid-moment.

Void bled through the fractures, sliding down the heavens like sludge and devouring the fabric of existence itself.

Even the air felt corrupted—thick and viscous like the inside of a wound that refused to heal.

Nothing was alive on that battlefield. Nothing could have survived it.

And yet, for the briefest instant—

The world stilled.

The fractures tightened. The warped space shuddered. The looping moments snapped into alignment as though something vast and unseen had forced reality itself to inhale.

And in that infinitesimal moment of correction—

A scream echoed across the battlefield.

“Let me out!”

It tore through the ruined world—raw, furious, and unmistakably alive—before the moment shattered apart once more.

Time slipped.

Space fractured.

The Abyss surged.

And the scream vanished as though it had never existed, dragged backward and trapped once more two seconds in the past.

It had been four days since Seamus began his race to catch time.

He was still running.

And he was still two seconds behind.
