Chapter 280

The canopies of trees swayed in the wind. In the distance, Michael could see the heaps of materials waiting for him, gathered by the Fae under Drullkrin’s command. The other notable change was a massive dirt road, wider than a six-lane highway, winding across the whole Valley. It connected all the access points that they knew of–stairs leading up and down, of which they knew two pairs now–to the Fae city and to other points of interest.

He turned around, leaning with his back against the wooden railing of the treehouse’s balcony. Inside, he saw Icarus getting dressed.

“Are you ready?” he asked.

“Coming!” the AI-elf said. They hurried into their clothes, fixed their hair, and rushed outside.

Michael shook his head. Icarus could just refresh their body, since they were only a projection, but he knew full well why they didn’t do that. He took their hand and the two descended down the steps along the great trunk of the tree at the center of the Fae city, entering the forest proper.

“My lord,” Drullkrin greeted him from below. Then the goblin nodded at Icarus, greeting them by name.

The landing was close to where Michael had once been kept hostage by the Forest King while the Fae discussed all the ways they could make him dance inside the fire. Not much had changed at first glance, except now the village had grown into a sprawling city built in the forest.

After the greetings, Drullkrin led the two on a tour, showcasing all the projects he was overseeing.

“As you can see, now that we know big changes to the Valley are permanent, we are beginning to undertake massive projects under Johanne’s guidance.”

“I noticed,” Michael said. “Does the road mean we are finally ready to connect the Valley to the surface?”

“Indeed, my lord,” the goblin general said. “Everything is ready. If you wish, we can head there immediately.”

“Sure,” he said. “Lead the way.”

As they walked, Icarus sent a message to Johanne asking her if she wished to join them. She appeared in a swirl of magic right as they arrived at their destination.

“Wow, that’s quite big,” Icarus exclaimed. “Seeing things in person is always different from the schematics.”

Michael saw the playful grin on their face, recalling when the same had happened with the inner space schematics.

Before them, a massive ring of steel rose into the air in the middle of a flat field of barren earth. All around, the area was a busy construction site filled with all sorts of Fae and human workers digging, pouring concrete, and taking measurements.

“Bringing Valley time dilation to zero,” Icarus announced.

Michael and Johanne looked at the massive ring with their arms crossed. Drullkrin barked the last few orders to his people.

“Ready,” Icarus said.

“Fire it up,” Michael ordered.

The ring began to glow as a thinner band of blue material at its center started spinning. With his senses, Michael could see the flow of magic coming from buried infrastructure beneath the ring, gathering from all over the Valley to power the portal.

Then, finally, the build-up of magic reached its peak. The portal connected to a twin device on the surface, somewhere in a suitable spot within Site 00. The sight was of space slowly dissolving, reducing the distance between the two points to zero.

A secondary set of devices also activated at the same time, flooding the area with time and gravity elements.

“Portal physics is very delicate, my lord,” Johanne explained. “Portals such as this are different from the temporary portals we summon with our magic. They actually connect two points in space together by bending space-time, allowing the passage of matter, energy and even gravity.”

“You should see the mathematical models!” Icarus said with a giggle. “They are quite the mess. Did you know that two horizontal portals in a normal gravity field, one above the other, generate twin gravitational singularities at each side of the opening?”

“I had no idea,” Michael said. “I saw you working on that in one of the data rooms and stayed the hell away.”

Johanne adjusted her glasses. “Well, my lord, this is actually a different scenario. One of the two portals is inside the dungeon, while the other is not. I would like to experiment with that.”

“Not with this big-ass portal, I hope,” Michael said.

“Of course not,” she said a bit too quickly. “I will be conducting experiments in a controlled environment first.”

As they hammered out some of the details of her experiments, with Michael spending most of the time vetoing her more absurd ideas while Icarus seemed to have the time of their life, the first pieces of heavy machinery began to flow into the Valley through the portal. Travis was with them, leading the way.

“Oh, we are going to do so much with this place,” he said as he took a deep breath of the fresh Valley air, pretending not to notice the plumes of dark smoke coming from the machines nearby. “Why are you smiling?”

“Nothing,” Michael said. He had followed Travis’s movements. The man had been sitting at the Origin System’s Central Dashboard, in the inner space. It would have been easy to just let him step out of there and join them, but instead he had been forced to take the portal out, emerge underground in the control tower’s lower floors, take the elevator, lease a teleportation skill through the Origin System with his privileged access, teleport to where the portal had opened on the surface, and finally step through it just so he could pretend to casually stroll through.

“Travis,” Johanne said immediately, her voice stern. “I sure hope you are not planning to keep using all those diesel-powered machines and contaminate the Valley air, are you? You do know my lord hates the smell of pollution.”

“Relax,” the man said calmly. “Icarus, tell her.”

“Me?” Icarus pouted. “You tell her!”

“Fine,” Travis said. “Icarus gave a quest to a few of our engineers through the Origin System, asking them to develop mana engines. They are working on it with zeal.”

“The quest reward unlocks the Engineer class,” Michael added. “As soon as they saw the class benefits and skills, they went insane.”

“That is good,” Johanne said stiffly. “Indeed, the kind of intermitted ratio reward on a variable schedule promoted by the System is proven to be the most effective at motivating people.”

“What?” Travis was at a loss for words.

“Gamification, Travis.” Icarus explained. “Don’t you love our dystopian methods? They are so effective that gamification is illegal in Europe.”

“Ah. Then yes, keep doing just that. As for Europe, we have to work on it.”

Icarus’s face lit up. There was a light pop of magic, and suddenly there were two of them.

“Yes! Let’s take over Europe!” the second Icarus said with glee. “Come with me!”

Michael watched Travis get taken away by the AI’s second manifestation. “You are too unserious,” he said to his Icarus.

“Only when you are around,” Icarus replied cheekily.

Sixth floor of the dungeon. The room of the four pillars.

The first pillar was glowing. As Michael got close to it, his face was buffeted by the currents of Qi coming from it, but instead of hurting him or trying to push him away, they were like a warm wind caressing his skin. Touching its surface, he felt the gentle heat coming from the stone.

The heat slowly left the pillar, becoming tangible energy that gathered in the shape of a wolf, then of a person.

“Master Yu!” Michael exclaimed.

“Arrogant brat! Is this how the disciple greets his master?” the man said, but while his voice was stern, there was an amused smile on his face.

“Apologies,” Michael immediately said, bowing his head. “Disciple greets master.”

Master Yu chuckled. “Still arrogant, but much better.” Then, looking at the pillar and the glowing characters on it, he added: “You have finally taken the first step in the path of the Dao of Interwoven Fates. It was high time.”

“Yes, Master Yu.”

“Tell me, what insights into the First Embrace allowed you to finally take the step?”

“I learned to extend trust first, Master.” Michael said. “I could not begin my games with the white stone, but I tried to not let my distrust of others cloud my judgement. I learned to trust the people around me, to let them help me rather than trying to control them all the time. Together, we achieved much more than I could have ever achieved alone.”

“You speak the truth,” Master Yu said as he nodded gravely. “Then you have also taken great strides toward understanding the teachings of the other pillars, especially the third pillar, the Open Hand.”

“Thank you, Master,” Michael said.

“I can see that you wish to speak more, disciple. Something troubles your mind, which I see has become particularly crowded since the last time we met. Will the other entity come out and greet me? Last time, I let it slide but this time, I will not.”

Icarus suddenly manifested. They were wearing normal clothes, for once, and even their perfect elven features were somewhat muted.

“There you are,” Master Yu said. “What is your name, strange entity?”

“I am Icarus,” they replied. “Do not call me a strange entity again, please.”

Master Yu blinked. His demeanor suddenly changed. “I apologize. You are indeed as vast as the tapestry of Dao itself, more inscrutable than the infinite tapestry of the sky, deeper than the deepest oceans, grander than any doctrine. Everything is clear now. However, I wish for my disciple to speak his doubts out loud rather than us merely implying their existence.”

The two turned toward Michael. He cleared his throat, words struggling to come out. “You see, Master Yu…” he began.

“Speak freely, disciple. Do not fear retaliation for your words, for there will be none no matter what you will say.”

“Thank you. You see, Master Yu, I don't think this Dao is the right one for me. I learned to trust Travis, and David and the others but… I don’t think I’ll be able to open with a white stone with the pope. Not after what he’s done. In fact, I already opened with the black by ignoring his summons. I opened with the black with the President, exploiting him after saving him. Now the Origin System is a giant black stone, and I feel like this is not how I am supposed to behave if I am to progress on the path of the Dao.”

“Does it feel against your nature to act as you are acting?”

“It’s hard to tell. What even is my nature?”

“If one could know, then one would always be in harmony with himself. Yet, most people do not even ask the question.”

“I feel like I am taking steps in the right direction, but the Dao you tried to teach me is too restrictive. I have other desires and aspirations.”

“Yes. I can see it in your mind. Limitless potential is one, but not even it is your true path. Likewise, the Dao of Interwoven Fates is not your path either,” Master Yu said. “Perhaps, it once was, or perhaps it never has been. You are not the same man who has been sent here, lost, broken, consumed by taint, in hopes that I would help them find themselves again. I will take my part of the blame. When I was sent here, I failed to see the true path you were walking, thinking you merely had the making of a great king or emperor. I know well that nobody’s destiny is set in stone, but alas, in my old age I have begun to act as if it was. I taught countless disciples, and as the ages passed, my ability to see their path grew until I no longer failed. In my complacency, I did not see and yet I thought I was seeing. I beheld the moon from the bottom of the well and did not stop to ask why the rest of the sky was missing. The Dao of Interwoven Fates is a great Dao. It is my Dao. It is fit for kings and great immortal heroes–the XianXia. You are something more. The pillars will never be enough for you.”

“The altar,” Michael said.

“It will help you plant a seed,” Master Yu said. “However, you will have to bear the weight of charting your own path, for there is no sacred text in the records of history that will ever teach you a path only you can walk.”

He looked at Icarus, and sighed. It made him look beyond ancient and, for the first time ever, Michael saw the weariness behind his features. The light in his eyes seemed to dim, as if the man had realized that he could do no more.

“You are not alone in your journey,” he said. “That is the most precious thing one can ever ask for. Treasure it, and even though the teachings of the Game of the Hungry Ghost might not shed light into your Dao, they will help you live a more fulfilling, meaningful life. Now, it is time for me to leave this mountaintop. Look for what remains of me, deeper in the maze of existence that is this place. Perhaps, if the Dao wills it, we may speak again.”
