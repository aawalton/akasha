“What was that?” Zigfried asked, a mixture of surprise and irritation in his voice.

He rubbed at his chest gently like he’d just suffered something unpleasant, but perhaps on the order of a bee sting for a pre-System human.

“I believe someone just tried to kill you with magic,” James said in a deadpan tone.

“Well, I kind of noticed that,” the dragon replied. “And I figured it couldn’t have been you. I saw you stop when I called time out, and none of my other senses perceived you continue moving forward. You might have used some sort of illusions, but I think I would have noticed you casting them. So, there’s another invisible person around, huh?”

Good figuring, James thought approvingly. Don’t blame me for things I actually didn’t do!

“Yeah,” he said. “I didn’t think it was a common ability, but there’s at least one other person with Invisibility.”

The dragon tilted his snout up and inhaled a big whiff of the air.

“Ah, now I have the scent,” he said. “You won’t escape me!”

The monster took a step forward.

“Wait!” called a voice from the middle of seemingly empty air. It was a familiar voice. James took only a moment to place it, since he had heard it recently enough.

The Assassin King.

The invisible man stepped into view, standing in the air like it was a pane of glass.

“I have no quarrel with you, dragon,” he said. “But you were in the way of the assignment I must complete, eliminating the Fisher King.” He turned to James and grinned. “My clients have pledged to pay double if I can accomplish this matter before the end of the week, and I saw that you were using Soul Magic to make good your killings. I guess that’s a way around the rules?”

“If you had no quarrel with Zigfried, why did you try to use Soul Magic on him?” James asked.

“That, um… I wasn’t…” The Assassin King swallowed, saw Zigfried was glaring at him, took a step back, and reactivated his own Invisibility.

The dragon, seemingly heedless of the Assassin King’s disappearance, leaped forward and wrapped himself around something that perfectly blended into the air—but had only managed to move a few feet in the moments since vanishing.

Clearly the enemy had not made good his escape.

Why not? Doesn’t he have some movement Skill that would let him make a quick exit? Or did he already use it for the failed sneak attack? Or maybe he was hoping to get in another surprise attack when I thought he was gone.

James heard the sound of ripping and tearing, then a loud sound of protest from the dragon. Zigfried stepped away, jaws open wide, streaming blood from an eye that had a dagger sticking out of it.

“Good one, invisible man!” the dragon exclaimed. Then he pulled the dagger from his eye and tossed it over his shoulder.

James tracked the weapon with his eye and saw it vanish into thin air.

He used Warp Step to move to where it had been and swung his fist—at empty air. Then he threw a kick. That struck something.

There was a muffled grunt as an invisible shape that James guessed was the Assassin King struck the ground.

The man made himself visible again. James noted that he had a big, bloody bite mark going through his dark clothing, across his hip and thigh.

“Fuck it,” the Assassin King grumbled, sweat beading his face. “I can’t coordinate my own moves properly when I can’t see my hands and feet anyway.”

Zigfried suddenly dropped beside the other two, at the same moment that the Assassin King sprang to his feet.

James took a step back.

How is this going to play out?

“Look,” the Assassin King began. “My job here—”

“Hey, the Assassin King is getting in the way of our fight,” James interrupted. “You want to just kill him really quick and get back to it?”

“Oh, right, death isn’t final here,” Zigfried said. “Yeah, let’s kick his tail!”

The Assassin King looked between James and the dragon, opened his mouth as if he wanted to argue, then shook his head, grimaced, and raised two daggers to defend himself.

James lunged with Meteor Strike wrapped around his fist. Zigfried leaped forward with a silver aura wrapped around his claws as well.

The next several minutes were ugly for the Assassin King.

He defended himself with great ability using his two daggers and a barrage of different Skills.

When James and Zigfried launched their initial attack, the Assassin King teleported a short distance away. The Fisher Emperor and the dragon could react swiftly enough to avoid injuring each other, though. James leaped off of Zigfried’s back, caught the Assassin King in the midst of turning back to face the two of them, and struck him in the abdomen with Meteor Strike after all.

Then the Assassin King sank one of his daggers into James’s arm, but had to leave it behind to try to avoid a bite from Zigfried. Unsuccessfully.

The fight went on like this. James and the dragon repeatedly cornered him, he would teleport away, and they would catch him. The injuries stacked up on the Assassin King, but it was an incredibly slow grind. He was surprisingly durable for someone who had probably begun his Orientation as a Rogue or a Light Warrior.

Though his passive self-healing wasn’t on James’s level, it was competitive with the dragon’s.

Whenever it felt like they were about to rip his head off, he showed some new trick.

His daggers could explode and reform themselves.

Using an exorbitant amount of Mana, he could create a flaming black dragon as an offensive attack. That ended up being completely wasted when Zigfried stood his ground and sucked the black flames into his maw like it was a spaghetti noodle with lightly spicy sauce.

After that, the Assassin King avoided using flashier moves for several minutes. James and Zigfried pummeled him while he waited and recovered Mana.

Then the Assassin King escaped from what would have been a decapitating bite from the dragon, replacing his own body with a life-sized statue of himself made of ice.

Again, he had just teleported a short distance away, so Zigfried and the Fisher Emperor pursued him without delay.

James hadn’t been showing any of his unique Skills during this part of the fighting, preferring to let Zigfried do the heavy lifting and keep the Assassin King in the dark as to what his actual target could do.

He did not plan to actually kill the Assassin King in this engagement, even if he got a chance to strike a blow with Soul Magic. Zigfried would probably be angry, and there wasn’t much upside to murdering a hired gun when James was doing so well at killing off the clients.

Hopefully that was the sort of restraint that would preserve whatever reputation James had in the international community at present.

Rather than fighting to kill, he was simply trying to observe as many of the Assassin King’s Skills as he possibly could. Zigfried was a big help in that regard. As James had done earlier, the Assassin King was trying everything he could come up with to counteract the dragon’s difficult to damage hide, strong resistance to magic, and occasional bursts of fiery breath.

He’s mostly spamming that teleport Skill, whatever it is, though, James thought. Maybe I need to put an end to this. I’m not sure I’m learning much anymore. There’s just one thing I don’t understand.

The Assassin King teleported again, and while Zigfried kept his eyes peeled for the enemy reappearing in any direction, James simply looked behind himself.

Yeah, there he is. That confirms it.

Over the last few minutes, the Assassin King had used teleportation to evade attacks to such a degree that it was almost like he didn’t know any other method of dodging. But he had never used his Skill to try and escape the fight.

At first, James had thought there must be some sort of range limitation. Now, though, as he watched the Assassin King launch himself at the slightly off balance Zigfried, James knew that there was something else at work.

Why does he keep teleporting within the same range?

Whenever the fight seemed like it was moving in one direction for too long, the Assassin King would teleport the opposite way, guiding his opponents back closer to where they had started fighting. Given that James had just predicted where the Assassin King would reappear—and not for the first time, either—he felt confident that his understanding of what had been happening was correct.

The Assassin King was herding them, keeping the fight within a fairly tight thirty foot range of the starting point. In theory, that was large. But in practice, it was much too small to be coincidental, given the vast range of the arena.

Why? Is he worried about running into other fights?

As James thought that, the sky turned dark, and a hunter made of stars came to life in the blackness above. It fired several arrows at a target beyond the nearby mountain, and then the constellation pursued the enemy outside of James’s sight. He was aware of that struggle continuing at the edge of his perception, but he tried not to pay too much attention to it.

The Constellation King was here, but he wasn’t affiliated with James’s faction or the enemy’s, so he had to be ignored for now.

Just like most people are ignoring fights that they’re not in, unless they’re trying to snipe people who are distracted. But the Assassin King can turn invisible, and if he teleported in the middle of a fight, we’d have a really difficult time pursuing him. Why isn’t he trying that?

The only conclusion James could come to was that the enemy did not want to get away. That made his apparent weakness—he seemed like someone who might give James a long fight if they were one on one, but not someone who James would actually worry about—a ploy.

I need to take him out right away, James thought. He’s still trying to assassinate me, or he’s doing something I can’t even think of without deeper knowledge of his Skills. Playtime is over.

As the Fisher Emperor thought this, Zigfried leaped through the air, that silver aura around his claws again. The Assassin King raised an arm to defend himself, but the metal that had deflected the dragon’s attack before failed this time. The arm dropped to the side, chopped into two neat pieces, and the Assassin King writhed in pain.

Zigfried opened his jaws to breathe fire on the Assassin King, but the human managed to forestall his fate with a quick toss of a smoke bomb down the dragon’s gullet. The air between them filled with smoke, and James could hear the dragon coughing as the Assassin King bent to pick up his arm.

But James wasn’t going to give him the chance to recover fully. He could see through the smoke and sense exactly where the Assassin King’s presence was. It was more than enough location data to use his planned finishing move with precision.

Proficient Silk Fabrication. Warp Step.

A web of James’s strong spider silk formed directly behind and around the Assassin King, and the man froze for a moment, aware that he was caught and clearly trying to think about some countermeasure.

James did not intend to give him time for that. As he raised his foot to take the single step that Warp Step required, however, James saw a gray aura appear around his lower legs, slowly rising up his body.

Is that Space Magic? Is this what the Assassin King’s trap was? A last minute self-defense move?

He completed his step, but his body didn’t move.

Wait, this isn’t Space Magic. He could sense this Mana wasn’t a type he was familiar with at all. It was similar to Space Magic in some difficult to describe sense.

Why can’t I escape with Warp Step?

As James tried to understand what was going on, the world around him faded away.
