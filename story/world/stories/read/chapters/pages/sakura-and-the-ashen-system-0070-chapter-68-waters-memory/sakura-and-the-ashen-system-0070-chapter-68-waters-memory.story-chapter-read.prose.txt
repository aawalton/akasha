The obsidian beneath Faerd’s palm began to grow again. First, a thin black crust appeared, then a sharp edge stretched out from it. Faerd was breathing heavily on one knee, staring at his own hand as if he had no control over it. He really was confused, but that did not mean he was no longer dangerous. If anything, a wounded, disoriented predator could be the most dangerous kind of enemy. They could not forget that.

"The threat must be..." he rasped.

"Faerd, please," Sakura called to him.

The man squeezed his eyes shut. A muscle twitched at his temple, but the obsidian kept spreading across the ground. Ater did not show himself again. He only shouted from the station’s broken window:

"The powder bought you some time. Don’t waste it!"

Gilbert looked sharply toward the window.

"Very useful advice. Maybe you could also explain exactly how we’re supposed to wake him up?"

"If I could do it myself, I already would have!"

Pampli remained beside Lir, growling low at Faerd. The beast was clearly ready to charge again, but Sakura briefly raised a hand, stopping him from interfering.

Sakura barely listened to them and kept watching Faerd. Not long ago, he had fought as if he did not even know the meaning of the word pain. Now he looked different. And a single word had nearly terrified him. Someone’s name...

"Faerd, who is Maev?"

His eyes flew open, and the obsidian shot upward. Gilbert grabbed Sakura by the shoulder with his good hand and pulled her back, but the spike stopped before it had even reached half its usual length. For a moment, she thought Faerd was looking at her with hatred, but there was something else in his eyes. It took her a second to understand that it was fear. That same almost animal terror.

"Don’t say that name."

This time, his words did not sound like someone else’s order.

"So you remember her?"

"Shut up."

"Who is she?"

"I said shut up!"

Several obsidian plates rose around him with a crunch. They grew at random, colliding with one another and breaking apart. Compared to everything he had done before, it looked sloppy, but it was still dangerous. Gilbert moved closer.

"Keep going. Just don’t push too far. If he loses control completely before you break through to him, it could bury us along with half the forest."

Sakura wanted to ask how exactly she was supposed to judge this wonderful line between enough and too much, but then her attention caught on the water. The station stood almost directly beside the spring, and the explosion had cracked part of the stone channel. Water now spilled over the edge and spread across the ground in thin streams. One of them had nearly reached Faerd’s feet, mixing with the blood running from his wounds.

An old phrase from Earth surfaced in Sakura’s mind: water remembers. Back there, it had always sounded like a pretty theory, but in Veyr, water actually spoke to her. It could even think and lie. The water had already found Faerd through connected streams before he ever reached the station.

And now his blood was in the water.

Sakura dropped to her knees beside the stream and immediately winced. The cut on her thigh burned when she bent her leg.

"What are you doing?" Gilbert asked.

"I don’t know."

She lowered her palm into the water. Cold immediately ran over her fingers, and the voices followed. After the recent fight, there were too many of them. Fear and anger seemed mixed together with images of obsidian and blood. Sakura closed her eyes and tried to separate everything she did not need.

"I need him," she whispered. "Faerd."

The water stirred around her fingers in a small, sluggish whirlpool.

"Show me what is trying to break through his memory right now. Maev... Who is Maev?"

Sakura had no idea whether this would work at all. She was acting mostly on instinct, trying to ignore the skeptical voice of reason. For several seconds, nothing happened, and then someone else’s memory crashed over her.

A small house appeared before her under a bright summer sun. A wooden porch, a window, pale fabric drying on a line. A woman stood near the door with loosely gathered dark hair. Sakura had never seen her before, but the name came with the memory itself. Maev. There was irritation on her face that did not match her smile at all.

"You’re late again."

Sakura could not make out Faerd’s answer, but along with the memory, she felt his exhaustion after the journey and his relief at finally being home. The smell of good food drifted from the house, and his gaze kept returning to the open window.

The memory suddenly changed. A little girl, maybe four or five years old, was running toward him through tall grass. She tripped, fell to her knees, immediately got back up, and kept running, laughing loudly.

"Dad!"

Sakura sucked in a sharp breath. In the next fragment, small hands were already holding onto Faerd’s head, and Sakura could feel the girl’s weight on his shoulders. She saw and felt it all from his point of view, as if she herself were Faerd inside the memory. Maev walked beside him, talking about something, and Sakura heard his own laughter. She had never heard Faerd laugh before and could barely recognize the voice as belonging to the man who had tried to kill her only minutes ago.

The water whispered another name: Tinli.

Everything in that memory was filled with happiness. Happiness that smelled like a warm home, fresh baking, and grass beneath the sun.

Sakura opened her eyes. The real Faerd stood before her again, bloodied, filthy, surrounded by black debris, and utterly alone. The realization of how she would have to use what she had just seen made her feel sick, but she knew no other way.

"Maev waited for you at home," Sakura said.

Faerd jerked.

"Shut up."

"And Tinli ran out to meet you."

The obsidian around him burst into a spray of small fragments. Lir instinctively covered his face. Gilbert raised his good hand, preparing to strike with magic, but Sakura quickly stopped him.

"Don’t."

Faerd suddenly grabbed his head.

"You don’t know them."

"I saw the house. Your porch. Light-colored laundry was drying on the line. Maev was standing by the door."

"No."

"Tinli ran through the grass. You put her on your shoulders. You were laughing."

"No!"

His voice broke. Sakura flinched, but continued:

"She called you Dad."

Faerd doubled over, and black lines appeared along his neck. They did not look like normal veins. They were too even, too unnatural. The lines climbed from beneath his collar toward his temples, forming a thin pattern. Gilbert noticed them at the same time Sakura did.

"There it is."

The faint cold echo of the water still lingered in Sakura’s fingers after the foreign memory. When the black lines reached Faerd’s temples, pale text flashed before her eyes.

EXTERNAL MAGICAL INFLUENCE DETECTED.STATUS: UNSTABLE.

Sakura read the message quickly, surprised that the usually secretive System had suddenly made itself known at a moment like this. It was as if they had touched something truly deep. Something even the System could not simply ignore.

"The System sees the influence. It’s unstable."

"What exactly?" Gilbert asked. Apparently, he could not see the window.

"The compulsion."

Faerd raised his head. The black lines on his face pulsed.

"The threat must be eliminated."

The phrase sounded dull and lifeless again, nothing like his recent "Don’t say that name."

"Faerd, look at me."

He looked at her.

"Who is Maev?"

"The threat must..."

"Who is Maev?"

"Stop."

"Who is Tinli?"

His face twisted.

"Don’t touch her."

Sakura stepped closer. Her injured thigh ached sharply, but she did not stop. Gilbert swore quietly behind her but did not interfere.

"Then remember her yourself."

Faerd shook his head.

"I don’t..."

"You carried her on your shoulders."

"No."

"She ran toward you, remember? She was so small."

"Shut up."

"She called you Dad. You were her father."

Faerd cried out. Obsidian rose around him in a wall, but almost immediately a wide crack split down its entire height, and the wall broke apart in both directions. The black lines on his neck grew brighter.

Sakura lowered her fingers into the water again and tried to call up more of Faerd’s memories. Something that might let her reach him. This time, she felt the weight of Maev’s head on his shoulder and saw Tinli sitting on the floor in front of them, drawing something with charcoal. Then another fragment came. The door of the house stood before him, Maev very close, her lips brushing his cheek before he left.

The warmth of home ended abruptly, replaced by a white stone room. Faerd’s hands were strapped down, bright light burned into his eyes, and somewhere nearby an unfamiliar voice calmly said:

"Again."

Then came sharp pain.

Sakura yanked her hand from the water. Nausea rolled through her. Faerd stood in front of her, trembling all over.

"What did they do to you...?" she breathed.

His eyes met hers, and understanding appeared in them, even if only for a few seconds.

"I... don’t know."

"You do."

"No."

"They made you forget it. The house. Maev. Tinli."

"The threat must be..."

Faerd clenched his teeth, and the phrase broke off.

"No," he rasped.

The black lines flared. Faerd screamed and collapsed to his knees. An obsidian spear grew from the ground in front of him, and he grabbed it with both hands.

Sakura prepared to step back, expecting another attack, but Faerd did not point the weapon at her. His hands were shaking badly, and then he slowly turned the point toward his own chest.

"Faerd!" Gilbert shouted.

Sakura rushed forward. Pain sliced through her wounded thigh, forcing her into a limp, but it was too late to stop.

"Don’t you dare!"

Faerd froze. He was still on his knees, barely holding the spear.

"The threat... I can’t stop." He stumbled over the words, his voice growing hoarser.

Sakura understood what he was trying to do. If the order in his head took control again, Faerd would try to kill them all. And the only way he could see to stop that from happening was the simplest one.

She caught his wrist before he could drive the spear forward.

"They made you do this. You won’t fix anything by just dying!"

Faerd raised his head and looked into her eyes. None of his old indifference remained.

"Tinli," Sakura said more quietly. "If you can still remember her, then they didn’t take all of you..."

Faerd’s fingers slowly opened. The spear fell to the ground and shattered.

"Maev," he breathed.

Then, almost soundlessly:

"Tinli."

The black lines on his neck twitched and began to fade. The obsidian stopped growing, and the plates already surrounding Faerd cracked and started crumbling into black grit.

Text appeared before Sakura’s eyes again.

EXTERNAL MAGICAL INFLUENCE TERMINATED.

For a second, she wondered if all of this had something to do with her talent. Was that why the System was showing her these messages that no one else could see? Or was it because her magic was obsidian too, related to Faerd’s magic, giving her some kind of special connection to him? There was no answer. And right now, Sakura had more important questions.

Faerd opened his eyes. The emptiness from before was gone. For several seconds, he stared at Sakura, then looked down at his own bloodied hands, the ruined station, Gilbert, and Lir. Horror slowly spread across his face.

"What..." His voice barely came out. "What did I do?"

Sakura did not know what to say.

Faerd looked down at his hands again, then went limp and collapsed to the ground.
