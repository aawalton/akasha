Veric stood outside Jin's room with a cigarette burning between his fingers and a gnawing feeling in his gut after getting thrown through a magical door to check on his boss.

Five hours since Jin had sent a message asking for updates on the recruitment operation, the bastion repairs, and a dozen other things Veric had dutifully compiled and sent back. Five hours since that message, and then… nothing, nada from Jin. No follow-up questions. No new orders. No sarcastic one-liners and no responses to his questions.

At first, Veric hadn't thought much of it. The boy—Jin was technically still a 17-19 year old while he was double his age—had fought a fucking Underlord and won. That kind of exertion would put even seasoned veterans into a coma for days. Sleep was expected. Hell, he would encourage his boss to sleep even.

But five hours of dead silence when Jin looked fully in control after the briefing didn’t sit well with him.

He was tired, yes, but this… was something else, or at least that’s what his gut was telling him.

And so Veric had made inquiries. Quiet ones—he was good at quiet—checked with Vera first, then Hans, then the twins. Nobody had heard from Jin since the initial burst of messages. Rudy was deep in training with his father. Reyana was still recovering in her quarters.

It wasn't until he'd tracked down Joe that the picture shifted.

The man had been elbow-deep in bastion repairs when Veric found him. Joe was a complicated man—much like Jin Winters in that regard—but where Jin hid his true face behind the cold mask of that skill of his, Joe hid his behind a permanent grin and easy charm.

The difference was that Joe's mask never slipped. Not once in the hours Veric had observed him. Jin at least only wore that mask when going to battle.

Frankly, Veric didn't give a shit who fancied what or what demons they carried in their hearts, as long as they could complete their tasks effectively. And both men were more than capable.

So he'd given Joe the full rundown: recruitment numbers, aptitude screening results, resource allocation, Illiana's repair timeline, the whole operation Jin had them running. Joe had listened with that easy smile, nodding along, asking sharp questions that betrayed a mind far more calculating than his demeanor suggested.

Then Veric had mentioned Jin going dark on the radio.

"Does he do this often?" Veric had asked, keeping his tone casual. "Go silent for hours?"

The smile had faltered.

Not much. Just a fraction of a second where something raw and worried bled through the cracks before Joe sealed it back up. But Veric had built his career on catching fractions of seconds, and that one told him everything he needed to know.

Something was wrong.

The next thing Veric knew, Joe said something about how he had faced a mind mage and had snapped his fingers, a door had materialized out of thin air, and Veric had been hurled through it like a sack of mail.

He'd landed—graceless but upright, because he still had some dignity left—in the corridor outside the room he recognized as Jin's assigned quarters.

If the man could send him here instantly, why hadn't he come himself?

Veric sighed, brushing dust off his sleeves. Underlords and their eccentric personalities. So much good could be done if they actually moved and stated ancient customs of doing work only in the range of thier power levels.

Well, whatever's going on, it's time to see what our acting commander is up to. He is the lynchpin in our survival after all.

Veric didn't think Jin was the type to indulge in anything unsavory. The kid was too focused, too driven, too wrapped up in saving lives to waste time on vices.

Which made the silence worse.

He took a drag from his cigarette, exhaled slowly, and knocked on the door.

Nothing.

He knocked again, harder this time. "Sir Winters? It's Veric."

Nothing.

A third time, loud enough that the sound echoed down the corridor. "Jin. Open the door."

Silence.

Veric frowned and tapped his comms, keeping his voice level. "Vera, is Jin spotted anywhere else in the bastion?"

"Negative," came the response. Vera's voice was clear and professional over the line. "No sightings since he returned to his quarters. Although a request came in from his room a couple of hours ago, it was a message… asking for stacks of paper, notebooks, and pens. We sent them up. No contact since."

"Papers and pens," Veric muttered to himself. "How odd."

"Is everything okay?" Vera asked.

Veric considered the question. Considered lying. Decided against it, Vera was too sharp to be fooled, and if things went sideways, she'd need context.

"It's probably nothing," he said, and the words tasted like ash alongside the cigarette smoke. "But if possible, send either Mr. Rudy or Miss Silvers to Jin's room. Call it a precaution."

A pause on the line.

"Also," Veric added, "I'll need a card access override to Jin's room."

"I... I can do that. Just a minute." The sound of fingers tapping against a console. Then: "Done. Your access card should work on his door now."

Veric scoffed, the sound dry and humorless. "That easily? You just handed me the override codes to our acting chief's personal quarters without a second thought."

The line went quiet.

Then Vera spoke, slowly. "You're... not planning to hurt him, right?… Wait, actually, you're old… I don't think you'd be able to hurt him, much less hold him down."

Veric choked on cigarette smoke. "Wha—"

He cut the call before she could hear the coughing fit, cursing under his breath about the new generation and their complete lack of respect for their elders.

Old? OLD? He was in his forties, not eighties.

He stiffly rotated his neck and placed his palm flat against the door.

His mantle stirred.

Just a gentle pulse of awareness, the investigator's instinct he'd honed over decades of reading rooms before entering them. The essence flowed outward, through the door's surface, mapping the space beyond.

Jin's presence was there.

Odd…

Jin always kept his presence controlled as far as he could remember.

Right now, his essence signature was radiating freely into the corridor like an open wound to anyone with the means to perceive.

“So either he is undergoing a breakthrough, or something is really wrong…”

How odd indeed.

Veric pulled his access card and held it against the scanner. The light flashed green with an affirmative beep.

He pushed the door open and stepped inside.

◈◈◈

The first thing that hit him was the stench of Iron. It was sharp and unmistakable, layered under ink and something else… something acrid that burned the back of his throat.

Ugh, what is this feeling?

Veric moved through the short entryway on full alert, his footsteps silent on the tile, decades of training keeping his body loose and his breathing controlled.

He reached the main room and stopped. The whole space had been transformed.

Every wall—every single surface from floor to ceiling—was covered in papers. Pinned, taped, stuck with whatever Jin may have found, overlapping in layers that turned the room into a cave of text. Some sheets were covered in fine blue handwriting, neat and precise. Others bore a bold, shaky red scrawl that Veric recognized instantly… it was not ink.

Blood.

The writing was in a language he didn't understand. The characters were foreign, angular in some places and flowing in others, mixing scripts in ways that suggested someone writing in multiple systems simultaneously or someone whose mind couldn't settle on just one.

Veric's gaze swept the room with the methodical care of an investigator cataloguing a crime scene. Broken pens littered the floor in pieces. And the blood—it was everywhere. Smeared on the papers, dripped across the desk, pooled in small puddles on the tile where it had fallen from—

Jin.

He was crouched in the center of the room, hunched over more papers spread across the floor, writing with a finger that dripped red. His back was to Veric, and his posture was wrong—too tight, too coiled, like a spring compressed past its limit—every few seconds his hand would jerk across the paper with frantic speed before pausing, trembling, then starting again.

Veric kept his expression cold and blank.

He'd seen this before. Not exactly this, but close enough. During his years as an army investigator, he'd walked into rooms where soldiers sat in their own blood, carving names of the dead into their walls. He'd interviewed prisoners who'd written confessions in languages they didn't speak, guided by things that had crawled into their minds during interrogation.

He knew what broken looked like.

And he knew what cursed looked like.

The question was which one he was dealing with.

The Underlord priest was a mind mage, Veric reminded himself. Jin killed him, yes. But mind mages don't always die clean. Their final acts are often the most dangerous—last-ditch curses, psychic anchors…

A mind mage possessing another person through death wasn't unheard of. Rare, but documented. And the scene in front of him—the obsessive writing, the unmasked presence, the total dissociation from his surroundings—could easily be the signs of an internal battle for dominance.

Or it could be a curse. A very nasty one.

Either way, it was clear Jin Winters was not in control and that he had to proceed very, very carefully.

He couldn’t subdue Jin on a normal day, and he definitely couldn’t subdue a crazed Jin.

"Sir Winters?" Veric kept his voice calm, measured. The tone he'd used a thousand times with suspects and trauma victims alike.

No response.

Jin didn't pause. Didn't look up. His finger kept moving across the paper with the repetitive urgency of a man running out of time.

Veric swiftly raised his hand to his comm, speaking in a much quieter voice. "Vera?"

"Yes, I'm here, sir. Everything—"

"Get down to Winters' assigned room along with Dr. Campbell." Veric's voice was steady, but the words came out clipped. "And keep this down."

"What! Is everything—"

"Keep it down!" Veric chided, lowering his voice further. "ETA on Miss Silvers? And relay a message to Joe. Exact words: Need you here, something's wrong with Winters. Likely mind mage. Convey those words exactly."

A pause on the line. Veric kept his eyes trained on Jin's figure, noting every movement. The rhythmic scratching of his finger against paper. There was a slight hitch in his breathing every few seconds. The way his shoulders hunched tighter with each passing moment.

So far, Jin was completely dismissive of Veric's presence. As if he weren't even there.

"Miss Silvers is almost here," Vera said, her voice now professional and taut. "I've relayed the message to Joe, but he's working on the pillar. The message didn't reach him directly—I sent it through one of the volunteers."

"Vera..." Veric breathed. "If things go wrong in here, I need Joe to contain the damage."

Silence. Then, quieter: "Understood. I'll reach Master Illiana to convey the message directly. I've got Dr. Campbell with me. Maya too."

"Get here soon."

The line went dead.

Veric very slowly bent down and unlaced his shoes. He pulled them off one at a time and placed them into the spatial ring he'd recently acquired—courtesy of his generous boss—and straightened.

One step toward Jin. Silent on the cold tile.

No response.

Another step.

No response.

Another.

No response.

He picked his way carefully across the floor, stepping over and around the scattered papers with the deliberate precision of a man navigating a minefield. He'd seen cases like this, especially after wars. People came back broken. Some tried their best to suppress the pain, the anguish, the trauma, but it was only ever a matter of time before everything exploded.

But this wasn't just trauma.

Veric was sure of it. Knowing Jin's personality—the iron control, the tactical mind, the stubborn refusal to show weakness—if this were simply a breakdown, Jin would have locked the door, called Reyana or Rudy, and handled it internally. He wouldn't be sitting on the floor writing in his own blood like a man possessed.

Something had taken hold.

And whatever it was, it was forcing Jin to remember.

Remember what? If it were the mind mage, it could be trying to get more about plans or the quest…

He reached Jin's side—standing just close enough to look over his hunched shoulder without disturbing any of the papers—and panned his gaze across the sheets.

The writing was frantic. Overlapping. Multiple scripts and foreign languages crammed together, some in neat blue ink, others in blood that was still wet enough to smear. Diagrams sat beside dense blocks of text. Names beside numbers beside crude maps beside what looked like character descriptions.

But after a certain point—maybe twenty or thirty sheets in—the variety stopped.

The same three words. Over and over. Written in every available space, cramped into margins, scrawled across the backs of sheets, growing progressively shakier with each repetition.

HELP LOOSING MYSLEF

Veric's jaw tightened. He didn’t know what language or what those words meant.

Not trauma. This is something else entirely.

He took a deep breath, leaned on every year of experience he had in handling cursed soldiers and interrogating the broken, and raised his essence.

Just a whisper. The barest thread of power, directed at the papers on the floor beside Jin's knee.

The sheets fluttered.

Immediately, pressure descended on the room.

Veric held himself steady, feet planted, spine straight, as the air thickened around him like water closing over his head. The pressure wasn't directed—not an attack, just a reflexive response—but it was heavy. Heavier than anything an Order III should be capable of producing.

Jin paused mid-stroke.

His head turned—not toward Veric, but toward where the papers had been disturbed—and the movement was wrong. Jerky. Twitching. His neck rotated in small, uneven increments that resembled an animal tracking prey more than a human turning his head.

Veric observed every detail. The way Jin's right hand reached toward the disturbed papers, fingers splayed, and the air around that hand flickered. Space itself warped and bent—just for a heartbeat—before Jin's left hand shot out, grabbed his own right wrist, and wrenched it back down to the paper.

His finger resumed writing.

HELP LOOSING MYSLEF

The same characters.

Internal conflict, Veric noted. One part of him is trying to do something, and another part is fighting to keep writing. To keep remembering.

He bit his lip, deliberating.

Then reached out with his essence again, this time toward the papers pinned to the far wall, and yanked.

The sheets ripped free with a sharp, tearing sound that cut through the room.

The reaction was immediate and violent.

A growl—low, guttural, bestial—tore from Jin's throat. The pressure that had been merely heavy became crushing, tenfold stronger than before, slamming down on Veric's shoulders hard enough that his knees buckled.

Jin's right hand rose again.

His left couldn't stop it this time.

The fingers clenched around something invisible in the air, and Veric felt the pull… felt Jin's hand close around the traces of essence he'd just used, holding them like a man catching a fly.

Oh shit.

Jin turned toward him.

Veric's heart leapt into his throat.

Jin's irises were solid white. Not silver-blue like when he used that skill of his. White. Colorless and empty.

And from those white eyes, dark tears poured in thick streams—viscous and black as oil—running down his cheeks and dripping onto the blood-soaked papers below.

Those terrible eyes latched onto Veric, and immediately, he saw the room warping at the edges of his perception, walls bending inward like the throat of something swallowing, and then the visions came. Not gently. Not like memories surfacing. They crashed into him like a battering ram through a wooden gate.

Faces. Dozens of them.

The young soldier he'd sentenced to death during the purges, nineteen years old, crying, swearing his innocence while Veric signed the execution order, knowing full well the boy was telling the truth.

The woman in the border camps who'd begged him for food, and he'd walked past because stopping meant compromising the mission.

His partner. His oldest friend. Left behind in enemy territory because extraction was too costly, and the empire valued assets over people.

Every regret. Every sin. Every choice he'd made that haunted the hours between midnight and dawn—all of them dragged into the light and amplified until they screamed.

Veric fell to his knees.

Blood poured from his eyes—not dark, not corrupted, just the ordinary red of a man whose mind was being shredded—and through the haze of agony, he felt a hand close around his skull.

No.

The grip tightened.

Get a grip!

"DOWN!" Veric roared.

His mantle erupted.

It wasn't much, but what he lacked in power, he made up for in precision. His mantle was built for one thing and one thing only: compelling obedience.

The compulsion slammed into Jin like a hammer, and for one precious second—one single, beautiful, life-saving second—Jin's body obeyed.

He dropped.

His hand released Veric's head. His knees hit the floor. His body pressed flat against the tile as the compulsion forced compliance through every fiber of his being.

Veric threw himself backward, scrambling across the blood-slicked floor, putting as much distance between himself and Jin as the room would allow.

The compulsion broke a heartbeat later.

Jin rose and snarled, “⏁⍙⍜ ⌇⊑⏃”

Veric did not recognize the language but registered the sharp increase in pressure bearing down on him.

The strongest Veric had ever felt in his life, and he had been in the emperor's presence… this was similar.

His knees crashed back onto the floor, tile cracking beneath him, and the weight drove the air from his lungs. His vision darkened at the edges. Blood vessels burst in his eyes, painting his sight crimson.

And Jin—no, whoever this was—took a step toward him.
