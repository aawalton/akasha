The trail of qi that wound through the eastern mountains was something I had been staring at for a while now, and there was this feeling of dread that kept growing in my stomach no matter how much I tried to push it aside and focus on what was ahead of us.

The red sun energy inside of me had gotten somewhat more stable than before, but my body still felt terrible, my muscles were aching, my meridians were burning, and there was a headache behind my eyes that wouldn’t stop no matter what I did.

I reached into my storage ring and took out a Qi Recovery Pill, it was smooth and jade-green.

I rolled it between my fingers for a moment before putting it in my mouth and swallowing it despite the bitter taste that hit me right away, I could feel it working almost immediately as the qi from the pill began spreading through my body.

"You should take a Blood Replenishing Pill as well," Azure said to me. "Your physical essence levels are still fluctuating."

I didn’t argue with him and just took one of the crimson pills out and swallowed it, this one tasted like iron and cinnamon, which was at least slightly better than the first one even if it was still not something anyone would ever want to taste.

"I have always wanted to know," I said as we kept walking and following the qi trail through the mountains, "why is it that cultivators can’t make medicine that don’t taste like crap."

"The texts explain that the taste comes from the ingredients needing to keep their spiritual properties intact," Azure replied. "The more potent the pill is, the worse it tends to taste."

"That sounds like something someone made up so people would feel better about having to eat terrible medicine."

Yan Ziheng appeared at my side not long after that, and I had a feeling he had noticed the look on my face.

"Are you okay?" he asked me directly. "That fight with the Stormwing Raptor took a lot out of you."

I tried to smile at him. "I’ll be ready to fight by the time we get to the shrine," I told him. "I’m just thinking about everything that happened."

"I’m not worried about whether you can fight," he said. "I’m worried about you."

That me completely off guard, it wasn’t something I expected from a teammate.

"That technique you used..." he started, then paused like he was trying to figure out how to say it without being rude about it. "Is it... is it actually safe to use?"

Calling the Overclock Rune safe would have been a very generous way of describing it, but telling him the truth about that wasn’t going to help anything.

"It has costs that come with using it," I told him, "but I’m able to manage them fine."

He didn’t look like he fully believed me, but he nodded anyway and said, "If you need someone to rely on during the investigation just stay close to me, I’m not a combat specialist but you put your life on the line to save mine, I’ll do the same for you."

"Thank you," I said to him. "I really appreciate that."

Even though the situation was urgent, our group was moving through the mountains at a slow careful pace, which wasn’t what anyone would normally expect, since most people in this type of situation would just rush straight to the shrine as fast as they possibly could, but Liu Chang had been the one to insist that we move carefully and not rush.

"We just fought through a major beast wave," he had explained when Shen Xuanyu questioned why we were going so slowly. "It’s better to arrive there at full strength than to get there quickly but have nothing left when we do."

It gave everyone time to recover their qi and deal with their wounds, and given how eerily lifeless everything around us had become, a cautious approach seemed more and more like the right call anyway.

As we walked, I started to notice that something felt wrong about our surroundings and it took me a moment to actually figure out what it was, there was total silence everywhere around us, no birds, no insects, not even the wind seemed to be moving through the leaves of the trees.

"Master," Azure said, his tone much more serious than before, "I am detecting no life signatures anywhere near us."

He was right, and looking around I could see that everything, from the smallest blade of grass all the way up to the tallest trees, had been drained of all life and now looked like replicas made out of paper.

I reached out and touched one of the nearby bushes and the leaves crumbled apart between my fingers and dissolved into tiny points of qi that drifted east toward the shrine, joining all the other energy already flowing in that direction.

"It’s like walking through a painting, everything looks real but nothing is actually alive," Su Yue said quietly from beside me as she watched a butterfly, or what had at some point been a butterfly, fall apart into qi when she touched it. "Even the most aggressive qi-draining formations I know of wouldn’t be this complete in what they do."

"Is that what happened to Chu Feng?" Yan Li asked. "He was killed by some kind of formation?"

"It looked like he was working for someone and wasn’t able to do what they needed him to do," Shen Xuanyu spat out.

Zhang Wei shook his head slowly. "But who was he working for, and what was the actual purpose of it all? He had more than enough chances to sabotage the mission during the beast wave if that was his goal."

"Maybe sabotage was not the point," Bing Lan offered. "Maybe he was trying to find something specific."

"The shrine," Liu Chang cut in. "He was very eager to go look at the shrine when we first got here, but then his whole attitude changed after we had actually been there."

I thought back to how Chu Feng had stayed behind while the rest of us explored, and at the time I had thought it was just caution on his part but now thinking about it again, it seemed more like he had been looking for something and hadn’t found it there.

"Whatever he actually was," Yan Li said, "the qi signature coming off of him at the end was not fully human."

"Could he have been possessed by something?" one of the female disciples asked in a small and frightened voice.

Liu Chang thought about it for a moment. "It is possible, but I think it is unlikely, possession alone would not cause his body to end up like it did."

"I think what we are actually looking at is some energy conversion process," I said slowly, because the different pieces of what had happened were starting to come together.

Everyone turned to look at me when I said that.

"The beasts, Chu Feng, all of the plants in this area, they are all being converted into pure qi and then flowing toward the shrine," I continued, and I was also remembering how the beast waves hadn’t actually been targeting the village the way we had all thought at first but had been heading for the shrine instead. "It’s as if something is drawing them all there in order to collect their energy."

"Collecting energy on a scale like this...," Yan Ziheng murmured under his breath.

"But why would a qi collection formation that large need something like Chu Feng as part of it?" Su Yue asked, frowning. "A human catalyst specifically?"

Yan Ziheng nodded slowly. "Old formations from ancient times often required what were called living links, meaning sentient beings who could guide and control how the energy moved through the system. If Chu Feng was serving that role..."

"Then possession was not what happened to him," Yan Li finished. "He was connected directly to the formation in some way, and when the formation activated fully, it consumed him along with everything else around him."

"But why was he begging for another chance right before he disappeared?" one of the Heavenly Jade disciples asked. "He was pleading with someone for more time."

I thought back to Chu Feng’s final moments and frowned. "It was like he had been given a task and had failed to complete it. Whatever is controlling this formation, whether Chu Feng was working for them by his own choice or not, they were the ones he was answering to."

"And now the formation is pulling in all of the qi it can reach," Bing Lan added, and she sounded grim about it. "Including the agent it had been using."

A cold feeling settled over everyone who was standing there, and without anyone really thinking about it, our hands had moved closer to our weapons as our eyes kept scanning the dead and silent forest that surrounded us on all sides.

"If a formation of this size actually exists somewhere in these mountains," Yan Li said after a pause, "then it is even more important than before that we investigate it thoroughly and bring a full report back to the sects."

Everyone murmured that they agreed, though some of the disciples looked less confident about the idea than their voices suggested, and honestly I couldn’t hold that against any of them because I wasn’t exactly feeling enthusiastic about it myself, but I also couldn’t leave because there were people here that I needed to make sure were protected.

My parents had been evacuated to the western mountains along with the rest of the people from the village so they were safe for now, but how long would that actually last if whatever was causing all of this decided to move west instead of continuing east.

Part of me wanted to just leave and convince them to get as far away from these mountains as possible, but my father was an extremely stubborn man whose family had lived in Floating Reed Village for generations and getting him to leave willingly wasn’t going to be simple or easy. And abandoning my team was also something I wasn’t willing to do, not because of what the sect would think about it but because that was simply not who I wanted to be as a person.

Liu Chang fell into step beside me at some point and kept his voice quiet so the others wouldn’t hear what he was saying. "I understand that you are worried about the village," he said. "But we can’t turn back now, we need to investigate what is happening here."

"Even if whatever we are investigating turns out to be beyond what our cultivation level can handle?" I asked, and I didn’t quite manage to keep the sharpness out of my voice when I said it.

He looked at me for a long moment with an expression I couldn’t quite read. "We can’t go back to the sect without being able to explain what happened to Chu Feng," he said finally. "And if the threat here is something beyond our ability to deal with ourselves, then it is better that we find that out now so the sect can send people who are capable of handling it."

I held back what I wanted to say and just nodded, because he wasn’t wrong.

If something ancient and powerful was waking up in these mountains, the sect needed to know about it, but at the same time without the safety of the time loop I had relied on back in the Two Suns World, I couldn’t afford to do anything reckless here.

If what we were dealing with was an Elemental Realm threat we would have to retreat, and we would have to do it fast.

Though the more I thought through it carefully, the more I felt like a true Elemental Realm entity probably wouldn’t have bothered waiting for the qi condensation stage 9 beasts to actually die in combat before collecting their energy. And cultivators at the Stellar Realm and above generally didn’t concern themselves with something as low level as Qi Condensation beast waves since it would be beneath them.

It seemed more likely that we were dealing with something at the very peak of Qi Condensation or possibly something that had just barely crossed into the Elemental Realm not long ago.

But with five Stage 9 cultivators in our group, including Yan Li with his imperial qi and Liu Chang and his Immutable Titan Scripture, we at the very least should be able to get a real sense of what the threat level actually was before making any decisions about retreating.

If we couldn’t win a direct fight, we could still gather information that the sect would find valuable. I still needed to stay careful though, because even something at peak Qi Condensation could kill me if I made one wrong move at the wrong moment.

The pills were continuing to take effect and I no longer felt as exhausted as I had earlier, so I took the chance to check on my inner world and was relieved to find that the red sun was slowly getting back to its normal size and intensity even though it was clear I wouldn’t be able to use my runes as freely as I normally could for a while still.

The blue sun was clearly working harder than usual, its cooling energy moving through my inner world steadily.

"I have been going through the original Ke Yin's memories of this area," Azure told me while I was checking on things internally. "There is something strange about the way the villagers dealt with the shrine over the generations."

"Strange in what way?"

"The offerings they brought changed over time. In the oldest memories they were mostly food and wine, standard traditional offerings you would bring for worship purposes. But as the centuries went on, they started becoming more and more personalized."

"What do you mean by personalized?"

"In the more recent period, people would bring things that had sentimental meaning to them, a child's first practice sword, locks of hair, pieces of clothing, things that had a strong personal connection to the person giving them."

That was strange to hear, because standard spirit worship almost always used the same type of offerings, things known to be useful or appealing to spirits, and bringing personalized things like that pointed toward something very different.

"The actual objects didn’t matter," I said, thinking it through, "but the connection between the object and the person who gave it was what was important."

"That is right, Master. And there is another thing that does not fit. The shrine went through periods where it seemed to be dormant, sometimes for decades at a time, and then would suddenly become active again."

"Cycles," I said quietly, thinking of the piece of text I had partially translated earlier. "Sleeping and then renewing itself."

"Form up," Liu Chang ordered as we came around a bend in the path. "Standard formation, defensive posture, nobody uses active techniques until I give the signal."

We got into position following his instructions, Liu Chang and Bing Lan taking the front, Su Yue and the Heavenly Jade disciples on both sides, Yan Ziheng and myself in the protected center rear position as the formation specialists, and the Yan Clan team taking up the rear with Yan Li's golden qi faintly visible as he held himself at the edge of being fully ready to act.

It was the right approach for walking into something unknown, the strongest fighters up front to take anything that hit first, ranged people on the sides to react quickly, and the members who provided more support protected but still positioned to contribute.

The path got wider as we came closer to the Sacred Grove.

I was expecting to see the ancient trees that had been so full of spiritual energy when we had passed through there before.

What I actually saw made me stop dead in my tracks.
