I’m down in the hold again, playing with the hatchlings. Over the last couple of weeks, it’s become one of my favourite ways to pass time between training sessions. I use small, harmless bits of magic to keep them entertained: shaping flickers of fire into moving figures for them to hunt, ruffling their spikes with gentle gusts of wind, and letting cool threads of water curl around their limbs until they chirp and wriggle in protest.

They’ve grown noticeably in the weeks since hatching – almost double their birth size. Samurans grow fast, though they’re still very much babies. None of them can speak yet – not even in a way that Animal Empathy can translate. Instead, they communicate with chirps, grunts, and an enthusiastic willingness to tear into any carcass I bring them. No milk for these hatchlings and they eat a lot.

In a village, broodmothers would handle their care until the hatchlings were around two months old and roughtly half the size of an adult. Only then would they be taken into the forest, taught the basics of hunting and survival, and the survivors indoctrinated with the need to support the village. On a ship, though, the traditional approach isn’t an option.

Choice cuts of meat are always good motivators, but there’s the question of exactly what kind of training we should give them. Right now they’re spending most of their time eating, sleeping, and playing increasingly violent games, but, even at only two weeks old, they’re already physically capable enough that keeping them confined to the hold has become increasingly challenging.

Samuran hatchlings are curious, persistent, and remarkably talented at getting into trouble – as I experienced myself after the last hatching. My companions do what they can, but everyone has duties, which means rotating supervision. One adult samuran watching nearly thirty hatchlings is not ideal.

After the third hatchling managed to slip out and cause chaos elsewhere on the ship, Bastet coordinated with Artemis to station one of our companions at the door at all times. Since then, there haven’t been any successful attempts – and the hold has been a great deal quieter.

I raise my head in alarm as I feel the ship suddenly change direction. That shouldn’t have happened. I’m just debating with myself whether to go and find out what’s going on or to remain here and see if anything else odd happens when the sound of the door opening draws my attention. I turn my head to see a sailor bowing politely.

“Yes?” I invite slightly tensely.

“Lord Markus, Captain Tolly is asking you to come to the quarterdeck.”

Not good.

“Please tell him I’ll be there shortly,” I reply as calmly as I can while my thoughts race. It’s not necessarily a threat, I tell myself. There are other reasons for course correction.

Though not many that wouldn’t be able to wait for me to agree to it first, a little voice reminds me grimly.

“Yes, my lord,” he acknowledges, and then quickly turns around and exits the room.

I gently brush away the three samuran hatchlings who are currently trying to use me as a tree and push myself to my feet. Their grunts are disappointed and they snap their jaws in annoyance. I stroke their crests and brush my fingers against the underside of their jaws affectionately to soothe them.

“Sorry I have to leave you – duty calls,” I apologise to Iandee who’s currently on duty.

“No worries,” he replies happily. “We’ll be just fine here.” Out of all the adult samurans, he seems to enjoy spending time with the little ones the most while Happy is the least comfortable. In her case, I think she’s worried that she’ll accidentally use more strength than she intended. Iandee, however, seems to thoroughly enjoy the little games the samurans play – like trying to touch his tail without being caught.

It’s good to see him so carefree. It reminds me of the samuran I met rather than who he turned into after Murmur and Sleeps were killed by Raven.

“Alright. Hopefully it’s nothing too important and I’ll be able to return,” I say with false optimism, stroking the head of the hatchling closest to me – and quickly withdrawing my fingers when he snaps playfully at them.

Since Iandee is enraptured again with the samurans – who are incredibly cute, in a reptilian way – I just smile at him and leave, careful when I open the door to make sure that no samuran tries to slip out with me. That’s how the last one escaped.

Ninja’s the one on door-duty right now, and I caress her head briefly in silent thanks for her service as I go past.

It takes me a good few minutes to make it to the quarterdeck – I was almost as far away from it as is possible in this ship – and when I get there, I see what has Tolly worried.

“What is that?” I ask in concern. It looks like a storm cloud but…not. First of all, it’s moving faster than a cloud would naturally, and secondly, it’s not nearly as puffy – more of a blur.

“My money is on a beast o’ some sort,” Tolly replies grimly.

“Is it targeting us?” I ask, my eyes narrowing. Now I know what I’m looking at, I see what’s led Tolly to that conclusion.

The waves are unnaturally smooth below it, but the effect is causing a bow wave and wake as if it is a sailing ship rather than flying. Though I’ve heard of odd phenomena on the ocean, this definitely looks more like a beast. Sirocco, if you’re around, can you take a look at that thing? But be careful – it’s clearly able to affect the air around it.

“‘Spect so.” Tolly replies at the same, unaware of my silent communication. “It’s got closer and changed its route when we did.”

That’s the reason for the course correction I felt even in the hold, then.

“That’s worrying news,” I comment uneasily. A creature that size….

“Aye. Especially, as that’s no Tier three.”

My eyes dart to the Captain at his implication even as my stomach drops.

“You think it’s a Tier four?”

“Aye,” he agrees grimly, the lines tight on his face. “Look at the size o’ its domain an’ how quickly it’s moving. Suppose it could be a powerful Tier three on the brink o’ Evolution, but my money’s on it already bein’ Evolved.”

My jaw clenches. Maybe I shouldn’t be surprised. There's a reason why few people try to sail across the ocean – and fewer survive it if they do. But I’m determined that our fleet won’t be on the list of casualties.

“Have you communicated with the other captains?” I check.

Tolly nods.

“Aye, first thing I did when I realised it was more’n a cloud. Sent a message to the Steward too – he’s organising a meeting o’ all the ships.”

“Good thinking,” I praise. “He’ll be coming here shortly, I take it?”

“Aye, my lord,”

Just then, Sirocco gets back to me. She had been sitting in the crow’s nest – her favourite place on the ship, for obvious reasons – but took off and flew towards the blur at my request. Her eyes are good enough that she doesn’t need to get close to see more details.

“It’s a giant bird,” I tell Tolly after a few moments of examining the creature. I feel more than see his attention sharpen on me.

“What colour?”

“A mixture of greys and blues. She says there’s something strange about its patterning too – it keeps changing.”

Tolly’s lips tighten.

“Its beak – hooked or straight?”

I wait a few moments for Sirocco to catch sight of it.

“Hooked,” I say finally. “And massive – the beak takes up half the bird’s face. Like a parrot’s, if that means anything to you.” There’s no guarantee that he’ll know what a parrot is – I have no idea if they or an equivalent exist in this world.

Tolly curses, so I guess it translates well enough for him to understand what I’m saying.

“An’ how big is it?”

Again, I have to ask Sirocco for her best estimate.

“Body length is close on the size of this ship; its wingspan is more than double that,” I tell Tolly finally. “But it doesn’t seem to really be beating them – it’s soaring on the currents which are turbulent for everyone else.” Sirocco herself is hesitant to get closer – the way they’re moving makes her unwilling to get caught in the turbulence.

I call her back – I don’t want her getting caught in it. Not if this is likely to be a Tier four when she’s only a Tier two.

Tolly is cursing again, even more fluently this time. His fingers tighten on the steering wheel and his weather-beaten skin has turned pale.

“That all means something to you, I take it,” I prompt.

Tolly nods grimly.

“Aye. It’s a giant roc. Awful birds. We get them close to shore, but those are never more’n Tier three. An’ those are bad enough. Lost a cousin to one down near Nymphbay. They tend either to storm, lightning, or wind an’ focus deeply in whatever they choose. Saw Lord Softrain – the previous one – fight a Tier three Storm Roc when I was just a lad. He were at least level sixty, an’ a powerful one at that. Was still a close fight an’ caused a lot o’ damage. No offence to the current Lord Softrain, but I don’t think he’s at his father’s level yet. Nor is anyone else we have with us who might stand a chance. No offence to your lordship neither.”

“None taken,” I say absently, my mind turning over his words. Now I can see why he’s so worried. He’s right – none of our mages are level sixty or above, especially not those who might be able to match it blow for blow.

“Do you think we could outrun it?” I ask Tolly hopefully. Our goal is to get across the ocean in one piece, not fight Tier fours. A fight is likely to put lives at risk – people we will need on the Lost Continent to face all the dangers there. Worse, we’re trying to get across this ocean without attracting the attention of one of the leviathans from down below – or one of the Sky Emperors. A fight with a Tier four is likely to be far too loud – both magically and literally.

Tolly hums for a moment, his eyes examining the shape in the distance.

“We can try,” he agrees pessimistically. “I don’t see any lightning, an’ Storm Rocs tend to have more clouds around them, so I suspect it’s a Wind Roc. Lightning Rocs are faster but windies are more’n capable o’ stopping their prey from escaping most o’ the time. But…if it ain’t hungry enough to put much effort into catching us…."

I nod briskly. It might not be much hope, but it’s better than just assuming a fight straight away.

“Then let’s do that.” I nod at Sarran who’s climbing the stairs quickly with a familiar brown briefcase in his hand. “I’ll communicate with the other nobles and get them to do the same. And then, once we’re moving, we can figure out contingency plans for if we can’t shake it off.”

“Aye, my lord,” Tolly agrees. While he starts shouting at the sailors to prepare for increasing to full speed upon my word, I help Sarran prepare the stand for the mirrors. Josiah’s gem is already pulsing – he’s clearly trying to get through to me. And, since he’s the lord of a coastal city, I have a good idea why.

As soon as I accept his call, Josiah’s face appears, pale and sweaty.

“Lord Markus, we have a problem.”

“I know,” I tell him grimly. “I’m getting the others on the line. Get your ship ready to move at full speed – we’re going to try to outrun it.”
