I was expecting it, but the sheer force with which the Korvok in my arms suddenly tensed and started to struggle still surprised me. Even in the split second before Fade reached it, the Rendarch quickly proved far too strong for me to handle with just Might of the Wyrdwood active. I had to immediately transform into my fully scaled self to keep from being bucked off.

Even then, though, I think the Rendarch would have still managed to escape from me. Through our close proximity, I felt a rapidly building charge of sickening, dense Mana building up within his form. Oddly enough, through the entire half-moment struggle, the Rendarch didn’t once make a sound.

Still.

It didn’t matter in the end.

Fade’s barred fangs closed around the throat of the struggling figure, and the moment they did, he stilled. All of the fight went out of him in an instant, and he hung limp in my arms. I nearly overcorrected from how suddenly it happened.

I blinked my reptilian eyes. “Did…it work?”

I could practically taste the smug satisfaction rolling off of Fade. “Yup, I’ve got him. As long as I have his life in my jaws, the Judgement will make it so he can’t act. This is an old Working that Taran taught, from the days that Spirit Beasts were more common among mortals. In those days, the Mynnyd people sometimes turned to Taran and other Spirit Beasts as judges of the guilty, and ironically enough, truth needed an easy way to be weeded out from lies. So, this was born. While I have him by the throat, he’s compelled to answer questions; he can only tell the truth, and he can’t fight back. I can hold this for…maybe fifteen minutes. After that, he’s free to contest his ‘trial’. So, uh…maybe do something about him before that, yeah?”

I cocked my head. “So, to summarize for everyone else…” I said slowly. “He’s helpless while you have him; he can only tell the truth, and he has to answer us?”

I understood perfectly well. I just wanted everyone else to, too.

And judging by the smirks now growing across the faces around me, they did.

I nodded sharply. “Alright. Meia, keep track of the time. As soon as fourteen minutes pass, I need you to take him out before he causes any trouble.” I took a deep breath and relaxed my hold on Vis Maledicta Exactoris, shrinking back down to my normal, humanish height. “Fade, can he hear me?”

“He can. He’s even fully aware of what’s happening,” Fade answered, a faint smirk in his mental voice. “Go ahead, Nate.”

I leaned in closer to the point on the flesh hood where this thing’s ear likely was. “Now…what is your name, abomination?”

The head that I still couldn’t see underneath the hood moved, as if it’s jaw had opened.

And yet, no sound emerged.

“Ah, shit,” Fade mumbled across our bond.

I frowned. “Fade? Why isn’t he speaking?”

“Well…” My Familiar sounded distinctly irritated. “Minor problem. Turns out, this guy’s name is Serekh the Quiet. As part of his vows as the ‘Rendarch of Memory’, he got his vocal cords cut out. So, no speaking for him. Ever.”

“What the Hell drove him to do something like that?” I snarled. I was getting fed up with the bizarre self-mutilations that the Korvoks engaged in.

“Hart?” I heard Meia ask sharply. I just shook my head instead of answering, as Fade did instead.

“First, he’s laughing at you now,” Fade replied, sounding just as irritated as I was. “Second, it’s apparently tradition for the post of Rendarch of Memory.”

I wanted to curse this asshole, but instead…

I paused.

Something had just occurred to me.

This was the second Rendarch we had encountered that was a study in contradictions. I hadn’t thought much of it at the time that ‘Rhogann the Veiled’ had literally been wearing multiple veils before I ripped them off. Now here was an apparent archivist, someone meant to catalogue and relay knowledge, who had had his vocal cords removed.

Once was coincidence.

Twice was a pattern.

There was something important there, I suspected.

“Keep the questions coming, Nate,” Fade told me, sounding a bit impatient. “Twelve more minutes.”

Enough; I could contemplate this later. I needed to get to business.

“Where are you keeping the captives from Karag Varla?” I asked sharply.

“I’m not keeping any captives, he says.”

I grit my teeth. “Where are the captives that were taken from Karag Varla being kept?”

“He says, in the Heartspire.”

Confirmation. My heartbeat picked up.

Heartspire, eh. That had to be the name of the central tower. A bit on the nose, but then again…most location names were.

“Does that include Jarl Jorvik and a Velancian that fought with him named Azarus?”

“It does. Both of them are being held with the rest of the,” Fade paused for a split-second. “Sacrifices.”

My eyes narrowed. Unfortunately, I wasn’t even surprised. Djarn had spoken all about the purity of the Red Truth, and how they were going to convert all of the people stolen from Varla.

It was entirely unsurprising that these bastards were intending to sacrifice them instead.

Hypocrites.

“Have any of the captives already been sacrificed?” I growled.

“No, to the best of his knowledge. Preparations were still being made.”

I relaxed just a bit. Not enough to release him, but enough that I wasn’t about to snap his shoulders.

Well. If I was strong enough for that. Who knew with these weird mutants.

“Alright,” I breathed. “How do we get into the Heartspire?”

“He says you don’t.”

The Hell did that mean? All buildings had an entrance.

Wait.

‘You’.

“How do you get into the Heartspire, Serekh?”

Fade chortled across our bond. “Oh, he really didn’t like that one. He says that only the Rendarchs and favored members of the Karvokar can enter the Heartspire. If one of them approaches the spire from the northernmost position, a bridge and an entrance will appear.”

There it was.

I directed a cold, calculating look at the back of this monster’s head. “Ask him if he needs to be alive for this path to open.”

For the first time since this interrogation had begun, I felt the frozen form of the Rendarch I was restraining actually tense. Slowly, haltingly, his arms twitched, and I had to tighten my grip on him.

Over his shoulder, I watched as Fade clamped down harder on the throat of Serekh, the mint colored mist flaring brighter for a moment. The Rendarch grew still once more, though I could still sense a tension in its muscles.

“He didn’t want to, but he said that just his body would work,” Fade said, a note of tension in his voice. “Nate, hurry. I can’t hold this much longer. You got like one more question before you need to deal with this guy.”

Out of the corner of my eye, I could see Meia move closer. At first, I thought she was merely moving into position to handle Serekh once the fourteen minutes were up.

That was, until she leaned down closer to my ear and whispered into it. “Hart, what we discussed earlier?”

Ah. I understood.

That was a good question to ask.

I gave her a slight nod to show I understood, and Meia backed off.

And then I leaned in closer to the Rendarch’s ear, close enough that the disgusting flesh leather of its robe nearly brushed my lips.

“Serekh the Quiet…” I breathed into its ear. “What is beneath the blood lake? What is the purpose of the ritual?”

The struggle within the bound muscles of the monster in my arms abruptly cut off. The entire time we had been interrogating this creature, there was a sense of resistance. Not just physically, but in it’s Mana and in its actions.

That was gone, now.

Slowly, softly, I felt the lungs in its chest inflate.

And despite its lack of vocal cords, it formed words. It wasn’t true speech, not really. Instead, it was as if it were merely whistling in a certain way, blowing air through shaped lips and tongues to form facsimiles of words.

But even through the intense slur that resulted, I could make them out.

“You’ll…never…know…”

An odd chill ran down my spine at the words

Strangely enough, Fade suddenly spoke up once more, but there was an audible note of alarm in his voice this time. “Nate, he just bit through his tongue! I don’t know what’s happening, but his Aether is acting odd-”

I didn’t need him to tell me that. This close to the Rendarch, I could feel what was happening inside his body. Serekh’s Mana had suddenly started to boil in a manner that reminded me of a madly bubbling pot with the lid locked down. The roiling was increasing with every passing second, and I swear that I could almost taste the mad glee within it.

My eyes widened in alarm before Fade even finished his sentence, and I acted.

First, I let go of the stranglehold that I had on Serekh’s shoulders, and reached around him to rip Fade’s muzzle from the Rendarch’s throat and bodily tossed the Spirit Wolf over my shoulder. I ignored the wild spray of blood that issued from the sudden, no doubt ragged wound, spraying in a wild arc before me.

Then, I fell backwards, chambering my legs beneath me as I did so. The instant the bottom of my feet had aligned with Serekh’s back, I kicked with all of the strength available to me in both legs.

The mad, mutilated Dwarf rocketed away from me, arcing away to slam into the opposite wall of the archive room we were in.

But I knew that wasn’t enough.

“MEIA!” I roared, stumbling to my feet. “AEGIS! EVERYONE, GET BEHIND US NOW!”

Far calmer than I, Meia Itzelan came to stand shoulder to shoulder with me, and extended one heathery grey arm. The loose iron bangle upon its wrist clanked…

And a broad, room-spanning pane of shining blue Mana sprang into being, dividing the hall in half.

The Aegis Spell.

While Abjurists were, for the most part, concerned with the more ritualistic aspects of Magic, there were active component Spells within the school. Beyond Wards and Stones, Ajuration possessed Spells in its repertoire that allowed to you cast short term, fixed position shielding Spells. Relative to the strength of Wards, they weren’t all that powerful.

But that was like comparing the sturdiness of a castle wall to the stopping power of a knight’s shield.

Both were necessary, if you wanted to survive.

The Aegis Spell was one of those Spells. It wasn’t the strongest shield Spell in existence. But then again, neither of us was the strongest of Magi.

But it was the fastest Shield Spell you could conjure in a hurry. It was also something that greatly benefited from layering, by combining your Shield with that of another Abjurer’s. There was a specific way you were meant to do that, and Meia, someone who had probably learned this Spell years ago, knew that just as well as I did.

Layer one was the flat angle. It was thick, and rigid, and it was meant to defuse as much of the force directed toward you in horizontal directions. But it was fragile all the same, and meant to be broken in that configuration.

The layer behind that was always meant to be shaped differently. A rounded shape, typically a half sphere in front of the caster. The Aegis was more durable in that form, deflecting force around the rounded head of it. It lasted longer, and yet in absolute terms it was considered a weaker active defense.

And it was the more important of the two, in the layer. A working team of Abjurers depended on the sphere Aegis caster to be competent, because it was more taxing and longer to cast, if only by fractions of a second.

Meia had left that to me. In that one gesture, I read a level of trust and confidence in my abilities that I didn’t think the Half-Orc was capable of extending. It touched me in a more personal way than I was expecting.

I wouldn’t betray that trust.

I shaped my Mana in a way I hadn’t needed to in nearly half a year now, and thrust a closed fist out in front of me.

A solid half sphere of glowing blue Mana formed behind the pane of Meia’s own Aegis, as mine was conjured into being. As all of my soldiers scrambled to obey my hurried orders, I braced for what I knew was coming, clamping my left hand over my right elbow tightly enough to bruise the bone.

We nearly weren’t fast enough.

Nearly.

Across the room, in the shadows of the wall I had kicked Serekh into, I saw two glowing points of crimson light.

The mad Rendarch’s eyes watched us for a single moment more…

Before he erupted into a violent, self-sacrificial explosive Spell.
