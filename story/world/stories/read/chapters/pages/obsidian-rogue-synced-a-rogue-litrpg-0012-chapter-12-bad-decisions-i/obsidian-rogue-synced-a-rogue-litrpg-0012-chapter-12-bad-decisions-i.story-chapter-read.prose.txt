As he walked to the exit on the far north of the saferoom, Levi made sure to keep an eye out for the mage. It wouldn't do him any good for it to seem like he was stalking her. Considering how far away he was from Gantus, he could afford to relax a little and stay out of unnecessary problems. The kind of problem that could arise from his actively trailing a mage whom he'd bundled over on their first time meeting.

"Stupid, stupid, stupidddd," Levi kicked himself. The rogue still embarrassed by the earlier situation.

It didn't really help his case that the mage was cute. She looked... Nice, really nice. Although he'd have to say that anybody not from Gantus probably looked nice. He chuckled at the face she'd made when he'd first tackled her; surprise mixed with confusion had been prominent on her face. Shaking his head in embarrassment, he dispelled the errant thought, choosing to focus on more important ones.

His hands slipped into his pockets, where his newest acquisition lay. It was a pouch that he'd lifted from the mage he'd collided with. It wasn't like he'd meant to rob her, but he'd ended up doing so anyway. It was simply muscle memory on his part; his life as a thief had conditioned him to react in certain ways to specific situations.

To be fair to himself, it wasn't a bad skill. In fact, it just showcased how good a thief he was. During the earlier days of his thieving career, when he'd been eight, stumbling around in the slums of Gantus, he'd managed to steal a loose pouch from a drunk passerby just by bumping into the man and catching the pouch before it hit the ground. Of course, the man, even as drunk as he was, had noticed the missing weight and had looked at the unkept child who was apologizing profusely in suspicion before walking away, too far gone to figure out what had just happened.

He'd been young that day, inexperienced, but it was also the first day he had earned his first coppers, and from then on, he practiced and practiced bumping into people and stealing from them unnoticed, until the skill became second nature. And that had led him to bumping into Killian, the taskmaster, who had caught him that day, but rather than hand him over to the patrol guards, he'd employed Levi. And those events had led him to this point, him on the run, with only his clothes on his back after the damned taskmaster had sold him out to die.

He'd had to have known that I wouldn't work for him anymore, maybe not for sure, but to sell me off to die, he had to suspect something.

Levi huffed in annoyance. He should've seen the betrayal coming. He might've worked for Killian for years, but at the end of the day, it was nothing more than business. No relationship had been built between them; no grandfatherly care. It was all just business to Killian, and the taskmaster had made sure to squeeze out every coin he could from him.

It wouldn't have taken much for the taskmaster to realize that Levi wouldn't work for him much longer after getting synced without a contract. So the taskmaster had decided to cut him loose before he could, and the old bastard had earned himself a few gold in the process, at the expense of the rogue's life.

If the mage is right about the kind of person Ghost is, then I hope the rogue visits Killian tonight.

A smile blossomed on his face at the thought of Killian dying at the hands of the rogue. The mage had claimed that Ghost didn't like to leave loose ends, and Killian was definitely a loose end, one that the rogue shouldn't have too much trouble tying up. Which meant the taskmaster would get everything that he deserved.

Guess you're going to die first, bastard.

Levi chuckled at the thought before pulling his hand out of his pocket as he neared the exit. He had no intentions of drawing any attention to it, just in case the mage he'd taken it from was outside, waiting for him. He made sure his hood was secure, kept his eyes on alert, and listened for any sounds coming from outside.

"... The lady sure is in a foul mood today," an old male voice said.

"... Of course she is, their type are always in a mood when with us," another, belonging to someone much younger replied.

"... Can't blame her, we've been standing here all day, we probably stink"

"... We? Speak for yourself, Mantos. I for one had my bath t..."

The chatter stopped once Levi emerged from the safe room. The source of the noise — two guards, one at each side of the door — gave him a once-over before staring him down. Both were dressed in light armor with a peculiar draconic insignia on their right chest. One of them—an old and aging guard —turned and openly sniffed at him, before spitting to the side in disgust. His counterpart, a much younger and vibrant man who could only be a handful of years older than Levi, stared at him equally in disgust, but he didn't spit. Not that Levi would blame them; he'd spent the better part of the night running like a beast, as well as fighting multiple monsters. For his troubles, he was soaked in monster blood, sweat, and gremlin odor. He probably stank worse than the slums of Gantus.

"What rotten part of hell did you escape from?" The older guard asked, making a show of waving a hand in front of his nose. "The stank wafting off of you might send me to an early grave, boy."

"Mantos, be nice, he's obviously coming from a dungeon," the younger guard replied, before Levi had a chance to do so himself.

"Be nice? I don't have to be anything other than me, I'm old Zen! Old!" Mantos harrumphed. "Besides, his stink might actually kill me before my time! I bet that he is the reason why Lady Xara was in such a foul mood."

"We don't know that," Zen argued.

The pair got into a back-and-forth, and he let them work it out. His thoughts wound into a spiral as he considered what the older guard had said earlier. The guard, Mantos—if he was correct —had called the mage, Lady Xara. At least he believed the guard was talking about the mage he'd attacked earlier. Besides, he hadn't seen anyone else when he appeared in the saferoom, so it had to be her. Was she nobility? Was she someone important, or more importantly, was the mage even the lady Xara the guard had been referring to? With incomplete information, he couldn't pursue the thought to a reasonable conclusion, so he let the matter die down, focusing on the two guards who were still bickering. On a whim, he decided to analyze the pair.

Both guards glared at each other fiercely before Zen turned to face Levi again. The apologetic look on his face was no doubt there to placate him due to how his partner had behaved earlier. It was touching, but ultimately inconsequential, as the rogue was already accustomed to such things.

"I apologize on his behalf. Please don't report us to the city council." Zen begged.

"Don't sweat it," Levi said.

"Five coppers, I can't afford that," Levi lied. Yes, he did have the mage's pouch on him, but he had yet to check out its contents—for obvious reasons—and he couldn't open the pouch in front of these guards as well.

"Yeah, the gutters, I'm sure you're used to it," Mantos spat.

Zen said, pointing in the direction that Levi was supposed to go. The young guard then dipped his hands into his pockets and tossed Levi some coins. "And here's three coppers, for your trouble here. It should be enough to pay for a hot bath."

More bribes, sweet.

Levi nodded to Zen before walking in the direction that the guard had pointed at. The path was laid down with cobblestones, and the place was covered with streetlights spaced at regular intervals, lighting up his path and making it easy for him to find his way. As he walked the street, he noticed that the houses and other buildings lining the street were well-made, at least on par with the houses in the affluent area of Gantus.

Who would've thought that the houses that he'd admired back in his little town would litter the city, like it was nothing? The idea began to make more sense to him the longer he thought about it. Those buildings of the rich in Gantus were simply the houses of regular folk in the city. His mind raced at the marvelous architecture that awaited him in Pelossi.

He loved buildings, so much so that he'd once considered becoming a synced architect by choosing the non-combat class, but he hadn't. His whole life, he'd been a thief; no sense in risking things and picking a class he wasn't sure of. Besides, he could still see all the buildings he wanted to be as a rogue; he could get up close to them as well. Perhaps in another life, things would've been better for him, but right now, he was a rogue, and he didn't hate his job. What he hated was his current circumstances. Having to run away from a highly skilled synced without a plan was a recipe for disaster; he needed to prepare accordingly as soon as possible. His thoughts came to a halt as he finally arrived at the broken torch.

Although it looked drab and ageing, it was definitely better than his old place in Gantus. A testament to how rotten a hellhole the slum he'd been forced out of was. Shaking his head, he walked in through the doors of the establishment. The sound of raucous laughter, clacking mugs alongside others, assaulted his ears as he stepped in. The place was more lively than it had looked outside. The broken torch was also a tavern as well as an inn.

Levi walked to the counter of the tavern where a middle-aged woman stood cleaning a mug. "Can I get a room?"

"Sure, five coppers a night. Six with a cold bath, eight with a hot one. Ten and we'll do your laundry." The barkeep said without looking up.

"I'll take your offer for eight coppers," Levi said.

"You'll need to deposit something." She said, eyeing him up.

Levi responded by dropping the three coppers Zen tossed him on the table. "Will this be enough?"

"Yes, here you go." She said, dropping the mug she was cleaning to the side, scooping up the coins, and replacing them with a single key. "Go upstairs, your's is the last room on the right. Your hot bath will be run in a minute."

Levi nodded before grabbing the key and heading upstairs. The staircase was surprisingly sturdy for an ageing building. Within a few movements, he was in front of the door he was directed to. He slotted in the key, slid into the room, and locked the door behind him. His gaze went over the neat arrangement of the room. The only things in it were a single-person bed, a drawer, and a nightstand. His gaze stopped at the door to the bathroom, and with relief, he walked towards it, eager to have a bath and give his body the rest it deserved.
