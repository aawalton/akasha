The Adventurer’s Guild in San Francisco takes up more than just the Ferry Building. It actually includes the piers behind and to the side of it. They knocked down the northern building on Pier 1, converting it into a refrigerated warehouse. It was not the only thing to be changed. Pier 41, on the Ferry Building’s south side, had its E and F Gates filled in with earth and then paved over. Now, it’s a parking lot.

Clearly the personal vehicle ban didn’t extend to the most powerful and wealthy—of course it wouldn’t—which in this day and age are the Empowered. Luxury supercars fill the small lot. An extremely well-dressed 1-Star clambers out from an Italian supercar.

That one cost 2.2 million dollars. Not counting the cost of shipping. They made just six hundred of them.

“Those are some very expensive cars. I thought personal vehicles were banned except in case of emergency.”

“Our members do quite well for themselves,” Guildmaster Carrigan says noncommittedly. “We arrange auctions and sales between interested parties. I would not be surprised if they’re capable of paying the light fine the city places on them. A thousand dollars a day sounds like a lot, until you realize just how much money Empowered can make. I’m sure you pull quite a pretty penny.”

“It’s not about the money,” I tell her and she laughs.

“Everyone says that, but it never hurts. Though we aren’t nearly as mercenary as that makes us out to be. I promise, while the Empowered who use our services are well paid, they are working for it. You should see the casualty statistics on 1-Stars,” Carrigan says darkly. “I wouldn’t begrudge them a few luxuries like this. Half of them won’t see the end of the year at this rate.”

“That bad?” I whisper, leaning in.

“While the Bay Area looks like it’s doing well, the answer is we’re on a precipice. We’ve got a truly massive amount of land and coastline to cover and barely enough Empowered to do it. That doesn’t even count what pours out of The Deep. We provide some truly critical services that keep our Empowered equipped with the best gear. Please, follow me. Even you might find something useful.”

She guides me into the mall, clearly been recently renovated. The marble floor sparkles with infused magic. The walls are decorated with tiles carved from multi-colored coral. Wood and metal make up accents, and every bit of it is infused with Potentia.

“When I purchased the building, I had it renovated with materials I acquired from some Fractures I had dived. An unpleasant experience, one I have no desire to repeat, but well worth it. The Guildhall is second to none when it comes to the interior design.”

Crowds of people are shuffling around. They shoot glances of awe and shock in our direction, and scurry away. My glacial pace gives them more than enough time to clear the space. More from the second floor look down at us.

“It’s certainly very interesting.”

“Thank you. Our services are the real draw, however.” She points at one store, the sign above claiming it to be ‘Smith’s Smithery’. “The idea of a blacksmith might seem anachronistic, but good armor is gravely important to an Empowered. Magical constitution only goes so far.”

The back half of the store is separated by a privacy curtain, the fabric enchanted to block even supernatural senses. Set outside of the curtain on racks and shelves are example armor pieces. Every single one glows with infused Potentia. They’re not enchanted or converted into magical metals, just made of materials retrieved from Fractures, which makes them inherently magical. An important difference, or so Morgan keeps telling me.

“This is just one example of what we do. We give skilled innovators and entrepreneurs a space to sell their work and centralize it so those who need and want it may find them. There’s a reason the Bay Area’s Empowered are the best equipped in the nation, possibly even the world.”

The first floor has a wide variety of shops. Armor and weapon smiths make up the bulk of them. There’s even a tailor on one end, and they’re making brisk business.

“What’s the point in being good if you don’t make it look good? Magically enhanced materials are all the rage when it comes to high class fashion these days, anyway,” the Guildmaster mentions when I glance at it.

An acrid smell wafts out of another storefront. Guildmaster Carrigan purses her lips.

“That’s the alchemist shop. I’ve tried to tell him to keep a handle on the fumes, but the ones he sells here are very strong. It’s one of the reasons he’s here, he is one of the best. I’ve tried to get him to make his wares elsewhere like everyone else does but he refuses to do so. His skills make him too valuable to lose, so unfortunately we must grin and bear it.”

“What can the elixirs do?” I ask.

“All kinds of things. They’re as varied as mundane pharmaceuticals. Why don’t we go talk to him? He can make some time for us.”

We enter, and Carrigan casts a small wind spell. A muttered word and a magic circle of the size of a quarter blinks into existence over an extended finger. The tiny wafts of purple smoke rush for an open window. The smell vanishes quickly after.

Boxes of whisky-shooter sized metal bottles are arranged in neat rows on shelves. All of them have labels, and we make our way towards the back. I can see over the shelves. A bald, clean shaven elderly man hunches behind a counter. An Empowered in front of him is gagging and coughing. A puddle of violently blue liquid drips off the counter and onto the floor, with wafts of more purple smoke drifting away.

“You damned fool! You’ll have to pay for that, you know. Mana elixirs are expensive! Not easy to make, no, no. Agh, here, take this,” he shoves a rag into the man’s face, who takes it. “And clean up your own damned mess.”

“Mister Doxiadis, I have a guest who’d like to speak with you.”

“Ah, yes, yes, be right there.” He glares at the man who spilled the pungent elixir, who scrambles to wipe it up. His eyes are still tearing up. “I told you. It’s too strong for you. You are a 1-Star. An Elixir of the Binary is too much for you.”

The man shuffles out, embarrassed, refusing to look at us.

Guildmaster Carrigan is floating high enough to be at eye level with me, and Alchemist Doxiadis grumbles as he looks up at us.

“Must you make an old man crane his neck so?”

I take a knee as to tower over him less, but I’m still a few feet taller than him. I doubt he was a taller man even in his younger years, and age has taken several inches from him.

“You must be that Machina boy, aren’t you?”

“I don’t know of any other,” I tell him.

“The Guildmaster sings your praises every chance she gets, telling everyone you’re a big Hero, capital ‘H’.”

“I try my best, though I don’t know how often I succeed.”

“A humble man, too. Maybe the Guildmaster was onto something. Well, how can I help you? Can you even drink an elixir?”

“No, I was just curious about them.”

“Ah, trying to steal an old man’s secrets now are you?”

I hold up my hands up defensively. “Oh, no that’s not what—” He cackles.

“Ohohohohoh! I got you, my boy, yes I did! Ohohoh. No, it’s quite alright, there isn’t much to say. It’s much like cooking, you see. I used to be a chef, many years ago. One of the best in the Bay Area!”

“Where does the magic come from? You’re a Typical,” I say as kindly as I can.

“Ah, the only magic I need is the magic of my kitchen, and of course, the ingredients. Empowered bring me interesting things of all sorts.”

He hands me one of the metal bottles that decorate the shelves and the cases underneath the counter. The label on it reads ‘Mana Elixir of the Trinary’. I unscrew the cap, and the smell of a thunderstorm bursts out of it. The Guildmaster wrinkles her nose, but otherwise seems unbothered. It must be overwhelming for others. Overstimulation hasn’t been a problem for me, thankfully. Alchemist Doxiadis seems unfazed, strangely enough.

“Typicals have a harder time smelling it,” he tells me. “Ah, something to do with the magic in the elixir and enhanced senses. That right there is fit for a 3-Star, and would recharge their mana when low. A lower ranked Empowered could drink it—but not recommended, no.”

“How do they work?” I ask, and he chuckles.

“Ah, you’re one of those, yes. That’s fine. The truth? I don’t know. Not really. You see, reality used to be very harsh on what was and wasn’t possible. The laws of physics and what have you were more than rules. They were the table the dishes were sat on. To be outside of them meant nothing, because existence did not allow for that to happen. But now? Now the dishes can float. They don’t need the table, not anymore.”

“Or so I think,” he says, shrugging. “I am not a philosophizer. Or a physicist. Just an old chef. So could I tell you how they work? No, not at all, not like a chemist or a doctor could. I know how to make them, I know how the ingredients blend together, I can even make new ones—with experimentation, of course. But I am no scientist.”

He turns another Trinary mana elixir in his hands, clearly thinking about something else. Age lines trace his face, and tiny oil burn scars on his hands and arms tell a long and storied history. Calloused fingers whisper over the aluminum can.

“Tell me, my boy. Are you and your girl Morgan truly working on a farming tower? The rationing measures were not a surprise, to tell the truth. Times are hard.”

“We are, and she’s not my ‘girl’,” I say awkwardly. “How did you hear about it?”

“Guildmaster Carrigan loves to share the latest gossip,” he says. The Guildmaster slightly cringes, though the movement was so slightly only an Empowered with their enhanced senses would have caught it. “Ah, here, take this.” He hands me the Trinary mana elixir.

“Maybe it’ll be useful for you at some point. Or maybe that Morgan can do something with it. Ah, and take this one too.” He plucks a second canister off the shelf behind him and hands it to me.

‘Vitality Elixir of the Trinary’ it reads.

“Good for the body. Heals up wounds. A life saver, or so I’m told.”

They both disappear in a flash of light.

“Thank you. How much do I owe you? These can’t have been cheap.” He waves it off.

“Pfft, hah. This is just this old man doing his part. Many people are only looking out for themselves. They see this time as a chance to make money. Not me, not me! Besides, I make plenty. I can take care of myself, have no worries for me, my boy.”

“Well, thank you again, Alchemist Doxiadis.”

“Now, both of you need to scram,” he tells us, not unkindly. “A team made an appointment with me. I have yet to prepare, and it is soon. Go, go!” He shoos us out, and after we leave a shutter closes behind us with a clatter.

“While Alchemist Doxiadis is certainly one of the stars of the Guild,” Guildmaster Carrigan says. “His services are far from the only thing we provide. We also include an auction. Unfortunately, the next one is scheduled for tomorrow. We also provide a space for healers to offer their services, and private areas for members to recuperate, though that isn’t here. We converted the hotel across the road into a private medical center.”

“That can’t have been cheap for it to have been done so quickly.”

Guildmaster Carrigan smiles. “Like I’ve said before, we do quite well for ourselves here. The network and community we’ve built gives us plenty of options when it comes to magical applications as well. If you have need of our services, we’d be happy to count you as one of our members.”

“I think I’m alright for now. I’m mostly just looking for the Cerulean Guard right now. I came here as I figured this would be the best place to find them.”

“And you’d be correct! The Cerulean Guard are a regular of the Guildhall. I’ve been told they’re currently in closed-door negotiations, and aren’t available currently. They’ll be out soon, I’m sure.”
