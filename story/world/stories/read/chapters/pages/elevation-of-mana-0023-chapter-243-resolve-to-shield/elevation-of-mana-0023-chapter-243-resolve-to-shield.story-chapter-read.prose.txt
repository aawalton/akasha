“You coming little Op?” one of the men in our group, big Op, asked me. Yes it was confusing, but that was how we were named. I wasn't sure if he was my father, mother liked him, that I could tell, but there was nothing like marriage in our little tribe, at least not as far as I could work out.

The women, if they wanted to, would go out to a field to meet the men as they returned from hunting. From that I knew little else. I'd not been allowed to go out there, instead staying behind with one of the older members of our camp, or whichever woman decided she wasn't interested that day.

Did they have partners they kept? Did they have some arrangement? I didn't know, the group itself lived in a sort of socialismesque form. There were requests of others, shared food, shared sleeping arrangements, but also private things. People owned stuff, and certain ones demanded rather than asked. The older ones had more power, as did the better hunters like big Op, who got most of what he wanted.

I waddled along behind him. “Yeah, coming.”“Good, have to teach you to hunt one day yeah?”“Yeah,” I confirmed.

He was being more interested, they all were now. All the men, and even the women, were more concerned with what I wanted. My suspicion was that they suspected that I was a shaman, and I was, but I wasn't sure I wanted that confirmed just yet.

“Oof, this isn't as easy as it used to be,” old Pa complained, her bones sore as the pack on her back, smaller than the rest of ours, was still causing her problems.

“Come, give some of your weight to me, or the dogs,” mother said, helping her redistribute some of her gear.

Everyone had a harness of some kind on, even me, even though mine was mostly empty excepts a small packet of meat. It was more to show that I was part of the group, not separate from it. Half the time I wasn't even walking. The dogs too, the whole pack of them, each had a small harness made of twine and rawhide, carrying a small load.

Our group was relocating. This was apparently a thing that had to be done every few years, as the game in one area became scarcer, and we worked our way through the local fauna for the easy to get food. Perhaps if there were more, or it were of better quality we could stay, but no, move we must, so move we did.

We were going inland, far inland, and had been moving for several days now. Our old site was abandoned, not destroyed but left in such a way as to minimize rot and damage, and then left. Perhaps someone else would come across it, perhaps not. Again, I was still very small and had no real knowledge of these things. This was after all my first move.

This of course was hardest on our older members. Pa and Ro had had a long, deep conversation about staying behind. She was struggling, huffing and puffing, and he, well he showed no weakness, but his crutch had clearly worn into his arm, and his face was hard, it was clear he was in pain.

“Good Ro?” I asked as we came to a break.

“Good Op, just old. This will be my last travel,” he said. “Pa's too if I see right.”

“Last travel?”“Yes,” he said, pulling me to sit beside him. “We're too old now, too weak. When the group goes we will stay behind next time.”“What about food?” I asked, concerned.“With only two of us we'll have enough food, I can make traps and Pa knows berries. We are old, we will stay there until we are done with this life. It is not a bad thing, it is the way it must be.”Ro wasn't that old, sure, he was older, but not as old as Pa, and it made me sad. We were leaving our elders to die. A child might have mistaken it, but I wasn't one on the inside. They would die there, either one at a time, or shortly together, no defense from the group, the easy food taken from the land. Death, death was the sentence for getting too old.

“You look sad little Op, but don't look sad. I have lived a good life, so has Pa, and we have a few years more before we must stay behind.”

“Want you to say,” I said, keeping my sentence simple.

“Haha, I want to stay too little one, but this is the way the world is. Men are born, we get old, we die. I can no more change that then I can change the sun rising and falling. No more than I can make the rains fall upon command.”Old Pa must have heard us and she too gave me a weak if brave smile. “Little Op, you are a good boy.”

The others too seemed to agree with them, this was just the way of the world. The old, the infirm, the ill, they would be left when they could no longer travel with us all, when they became too much of a burden. It wasn't immediate of course, injuries would be treated and attempted to be helped, but when you could no longer keep up with the clan, you stayed where you were.

Of course I'd come from a modern society, so this rankled me. We didn't abandon our people. Perhaps we'd not been perfect, but we'd sought to see to it that the elderly or the injured had a way to live. Maybe they didn't live well, maybe they didn't get half of what they actually needed, but left behind in a field to rot? It shook the moral compass of me.

Could I fix Ro's leg? I didn't know, I certainly wasn't good enough at magic now. If I did though would I be leaving old Pa to herself at the end, alone in whatever shelter we left her, left to die either from beasts in the woods, or her own slow starvation? That didn't seem good either. Of course she might not even live that long, so I wasn't sure it was a thing to worry about.

No, right now I needed to learn what I could do, so that if there was a way I could help them. Even if my magic never taught me to fix his leg I would do what I could.

In that moment I made a promise to myself, an oath with all my resolve. I would do what I could to protect them all. I would stand against the darkness in the night and save any who were possible to save, the weak, the innocent. I would be the shield for my people, for as long as I could be.
