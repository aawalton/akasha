Asher was pleasantly surprised to discover that getting what they needed to go up against a lich who specialized in magical plagues was much easier than he’d first expected. He was prepared to have to give Opal a complicated order, paying out the nose to try and have some version of Earth’s quarantine suits reconstructed here in this world. As it turned out, something like that already existed.

“While one half of Dormaul is entirely open and exposed, the vast majority of the city is nestled deep within a mountain,” Opal explained, her award-winning smile never wavering for a moment as she waited for one of her guards to bring her what she’d asked for.

Just like every other time Asher had swung by her shop, the woman wore a dress that straddled the border between sultry and entirely inappropriate. If Samantha hadn’t been by his side, he was certain she would have been pressing the attack far more than she currently was in an attempt to get him to focus on her figure over the price tags in her shop.

“As you would imagine, that comes with a whole host of problems we’ve had to work around these past few centuries,” Opal continued. “Things such as a distinct lack of air flow, waste disposal issues, and pest control problems.”

“Which sounds like the perfect grounds for a plague to start up,” Asher muttered, easily picking up what she was putting down.

“Precisely,” Opal smiled, batting her eyelashes at him despite the death glare she was currently getting from Samantha. “Magic and skills can do a lot, but you would be surprised at the speed of which a plague can spread. Usually, by the time anyone realizes anything is even wrong, it’s already gotten its roots dug into Dormaul and extreme measures have to be taken. It doesn’t happen too often, but every few years, I’m asked to create a couple more of these.”

A large, dangerous-looking man returned from the back of the shop, nodding as he placed what appeared to be a modified motorcycle helmet onto the counter between them. The helmet was thick but streamlined, made almost entirely out of clear glass, with what appeared to be a small diamond set roughly where the mouth would be.

“Created with reinforced glass from one with a Glass element and enchanted in order to provide the user with clean air regardless of how long they wear it, this is Dormaul’s answer to how they protect their healers who go around attempting to tackle any plague,” Opal explained, laying a dainty hand on top of the glass helmet like it was a used car. “If you’re planning on going into a disease-ridden area, this is what you want.”

“That’s it? Just a helmet?” Asher asked, frowning as his years of sitting through meetings on the importance of personal protective equipment sifted through his mind. PPE was incredibly important if one wanted to be a mortician, so he knew full well that a fancy helmet wasn’t enough. “What about the rest of the body? Cuts or abrasions on the skin? A disease could easily get in through countless other avenues!”

“I’m sure a warrior as skilled as yourself can avoid getting any sort of wound easily enough,” Opal winked. “These helmets are used for people entering quarantine, not an active warzone. The glass is strong, but it’s certainly not indestructible.”

“Our sea serpent leathers are pretty durable,” Samantha pointed out. “Not to mention they cover almost our entire bodies. Maybe we could see about picking up some gloves to go with them?”

“Maybe… though that poor tailor already hates me something fierce,” Asher admitted. “Still, if this is the best this world has, I suppose it will have to do. How much?”

“Sourcing the reinforced glass isn’t easy, and I don’t make these very often,” Opal said, slowly trailing her fingers back and forth along the top of the helmet as if it were the chest of one of her lovers. “But seeing as you are a returning customer, and I do just love it when you pay me a visit… I could part with them for five hundred thousand shards each.”

“Five hundred thousand shards?! If you don’t make these very often, then it sounds like the demand is functionally nothing,” Samantha said, cutting Asher off before he could say anything. It was clear the high price point had struck a nerve with the frugal hunter, and Samantha narrowed her eyes as she glared at the enchanter. “I’d be willing to bet these have been sitting in your backroom for years now, just taking up space. How long has it been since the last plague? How many years do you think until the next one hits and anyone actually wants to buy these?”

“Demand may be low, but supply is as well,” Opal countered, her lazy, gentle expression turning a bit harder as she turned her focus toward Samantha. “I only have a few of these helmets, and you won’t find them anywhere else in Dormaul.”

“Fair enough,” Samantha nodded, taking Asher’s hand as she started leading him out of the store. “Asher, can you teleport us back to the capital? We should shop around and see what the artifact shops outside of Dormaul are offering.”

“Wait!” Opal said, wincing as her easy-going, seductive aura was dispelled in an instant. Letting out a small sigh, she gave Samantha a much more genuine grin. “I’ll admit, threatening to teleport a few hundred miles to the capital to price-check me is a new threat I’ve yet to experience. I suppose The Reaper is a bit more of a tough customer than I realized.”

“You know who I am?” Asher asked, wincing as he realized the scattering of nobles who had been wandering the store were now staring at him with wide eyes.

“I had a hunch,” Opal smirked, winking at him as she slid almost effortlessly back into her usual persona. “I’ll admit I was pretty taken aback when I put the pieces together, but it made sense how you originally knew almost nothing about artifacts, not being from our world and all. Naturally, I wasn’t planning to out you by any means, but if your lady friend here is willing to spill the shards, then I’m more than happy to see about getting The Reaper a better deal. You are a loyal customer here, after all.”

Asher didn’t miss how Opal’s voice increased in volume as she talked about him being a loyal customer, nor the way the nobles in the shop whispered among themselves. Some of them even darted out the door, no doubt eager to be the first ones to tell their friends that they’d come face-to-face with The Reaper in the flesh and lived to talk about it. Asher didn’t doubt that by the end of the day there’d be rumors of him having entire conversations with practically each and every noble in the room.

Hell, with the way the rumor mill worked, he’d be lucky if there weren’t stories about him assassinating Opal floating about by tomorrow morning.

“…You goaded me into revealing who Asher was,” Samantha realized, her face turning red as she realized she’d been played. “Was that your intention from the very beginning?”

“I was hoping for it, but not counting on it,” Opal admitted, turning a touch more serious as she looked at him. “I wasn’t lying when I said I wouldn’t out you just for the sake of drumming up business. You’d have to be a rather unique brand of moronic to willingly annoy a man who is rapidly gathering fame as the world’s deadliest assassin. But I didn’t miss how your companion’s eyes widened the moment she looked at any one of my price tags. I figured she’d have approximately that reaction.”

“Impressive as always,” Asher said, shaking his head in awe at the woman’s ploy. Opal put any shady used car salesman to shame. With how heavily she thrust her sex appeal in her customer’s faces, it was easy to miss the cunning glint of intelligence in her eyes.

“Asher, I’m sorry,” Samantha winced as a few more nobles left the shop, excitedly gesturing to their friends as they did so. “I know you prefer to keep a low profile.”

“It’s fine. I sort of thought this was where the conversation was going, and I chose not to throw up a Spatial Lock to keep the conversation to ourselves,” Asher admitted. “I can’t say I’m a giant fan of the idea, but with what’s to come, it’s high time I started trying to interact with the nobility of the Noala Kingdom a bit more. Maybe this will give me some sort of in I can use.”

“Letting me use your friend, in order to use me?” Opal purred, looking like she was trying remarkably hard not to step forward and start stroking his chest instead of that helmet. “The Reaper does not disappoint.”

“I’m not that politically savvy,” Asher snorted as he shook his head. “More like I’m just trying to look at the bright side. Though while you may have drummed up a bit more attention and business in your shop, I hope you realize your little plan does have one slight drawback. Like Samantha said, I like to keep a bit of a low profile. I don’t exactly appreciate you spreading word of me shopping here without asking.”

“Which is precisely why these are on the house,” Opal smiled, gesturing toward her guard as he placed three more identical helmets down on the counter beside the first one. “Your lady friend there wasn’t wrong. The next plague probably won’t hit for at least another decade, and I’d much rather apologize and maintain good relationships with The Reaper than drive him away because of my games. I hope all is forgiven?”

Asher could only laugh as Samantha stared at the dangerous woman with her jaw hanging open. He couldn’t exactly blame her though. Samantha had spent her entire life dealing exclusively with merchants from a traveling caravan that passed through her small village roughly once a month. While she’d gotten a little bit of experience interacting with merchants in Whikoga and the capital over the past few months during some of their shopping trips, those regular, ordinary merchants were nothing like Opal. He supposed dealing with uppity nobles on a daily basis and knowing you were the best in the city tended to have that effect on a person.

“It’s a start,” Asher said. Reaching through a compressed tunnel of space, he picked up the first of the helmets, making it vanish into his Personal Rift. Opal’s eyes widened slightly at the blatant displays of his Spatial element, as if only just realizing precisely who she was playing her games with. “I imagine someone like you probably has a good number of connections. If I ever wanted to get in touch with a particular noble, is there any chance you could help me set something up?”

“There you go showing your ignorance yet again,” Opal giggled, smiling coyly at his confused expression. “Asher, you are The Reaper. If you want to discuss something with one of the nobles, all you need to do is enter their portion of the city and begin walking toward their estate. I guarantee you before you’re even halfway there they’ll have the welcome mat rolled out for you. The nobility play their silly games, perhaps a bit too often, in my opinion. But greater powers play by their own rules. You’ve got power. Don’t be afraid to flaunt it a little.”

“I’m not really one to go about flaunting anything,” Asher admitted as he tucked the rest of the helmets into his rift. “...but I’ll try to keep that in mind. Despite the fact that I came here for some artifacts and ended up putting on a show for your customers, I do appreciate the helmets. Thank you.”

“Always happy to help a loyal customer,” Opal smiled, licking her lips as she looked at him and eliciting a dangerous noise from Samantha. “I can’t very well have my most famous patron run off and perish because some lesser enchanter sold him a faulty artifact. Wherever it is you’re planning to go, I wish you the best of luck. I’ll be waiting for your safe return!”
