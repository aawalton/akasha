Chapter 116: Hypothetical Trap Mastery

Bo zoomed in a camera for Jack, already meant to be pointed right at the target zone. Jack reached out to his field, definitely not moving it even slightly, but poised, like controlling fine hairs to deliberately bristle themselves. He collapsed his memorite into the mass of metal in the sand.

They passed right over the zone, Chime never detecting anything, perhaps because he needed motion — that or a lot more of some strange material to tip him off. It ultimately wasn’t much mass down there, after all. Just metal shavings buried in some earth. Nothing special!

A mass of sharp needle darts exploded out of the ground under their feet, the audio feed quickly picking up shocked screams from the sims. Jack deliberately swayed the blast toward Délight, and Defiant was covering her with his body almost instantly, preventing any frontal damage. However, Jack wasn’t even focusing on that — it was just a slight bend to things. Instead, he was focused on a follow-up, coming up from behind Chime. A controlled stabbing blade going right for his throat…

And he was cock-eyed off. Totally off! Working through the camera had messed up his aim entirely, and he had to stall and slow the blade just before it whiffed into Chime’s peripheral vision. Chime was already diving at that point, though, and Jack’s awkward second try missed utterly again.

It was still threatening, but then Hiccup flashed from one position to the next, kicking the knife into the sand and already moving evasively.

Damn it! I didn’t think the camera-strike would be like this! So many things to practice, so little time. And I can’t spend sim-time practicing this type of shit. We have to use the sims while we can, with the rest coming after.

Jack opted to pelt them with a secondary volley, his AoE maneuvers evidently much easier through camera sight than single-target shenanigans. He’d diced up everyone to some degree, but especially Chime’s legs and Délight’s back and… buttcheeks.

It’s AoE. What can I do?

Defiant’s sim was unhurt by the AoE, but they had no clue how his shit worked, so it was moot. Guesswork. It either would or it wouldn’t. If he had to guess, needles were just considered multiple single-targets, by most definitions.

By this time, they were running down the corridor to get around the fortress, injuries not severe enough to stop them, though the two more injured in the party were not moving as fast.

Jack next reached out to any lodged metal on Chime, spending a careful, focused period to pinpoint small items across the distance with Interpret. Boom. He found a needle still lodged in the side of his leg, unremoved and not doing much, as small as it was and stuck at an oblique angle in the skin. He’d feel that, but it was likely he didn’t think Jack could manipulate that acutely at that distance.

Wrong!

Jack gently slid the needle out, turned it into a blade, and tried to slide it around over Chime’s skin to that juicy femoral triangle, as he’d already practiced doing when testing Nettle Storm viability. It was supreme guesswork finding the spot, and he had to move quickly, but the actual movement wasn’t too hard. He could feel the metal pressing against skin on one side, the suit on the other.

Indeed, even as he was moving and quickly stabbing into the flesh as hard as he could, Chime was yelling out and tripping right into the dirt, going into a roll.

Because the simulation was fully simulating the PACCs rules at the moment, he didn’t actually get to dive deep into the leg, but he quickly got the sense he’d done something nasty from Chime’s pain and immediate struggle. Nonetheless, he kept himself together enough to clack his sticks together right over his leg, and Jack felt the blade break into pieces as sound shattered it. Chime did it again, and the metal went even finer. Jack couldn’t keep focus or hold on to it.

“Shit,” Jack said out loud.

“What?!” Bo asked, stepping forward, her hands clenching. “Should I block that?! Should I have already blocked it?!”

“Uhh. Probably should have. I think? But no point now.”

“Damn it! Warn me next time, Ferro. I’m not going to waste energy, but if you need it-”

“I got it, I got it! Quiet now.”

“I verified I can’t do a Sandy, by the way. Need a direct line of sight.”

“Okay. Quiet, please!”

Chime’s leg had clearly been injured. Hiccup had put her arm around him to help him up and to walk. Limp! Oh yes, a good wound had been simulated. Possible heavy bleeding if he so much as nicked the right shit.

Chime pulled out his xylophone and was already banging on it, producing many complex sounds…

Jack launched a new volley of needles from the roof, centering and focusing them on Chime and perhaps his current tool. Predictably, Defiant moved in to block, leaving Délight to dodge behind all of them with Defiant front-facing. Needles just marred themselves, hitting the tanky bastard and bouncing off.

Huh. Not a bad tactic if you know the direction.

On the plus side, he’d buried lots of material into the ground that he was trying to track as much as possible, for follow-ups.

Perhaps I should’ve used small spheres to fire from here. It isn’t as if they would be surprised to find metal on the roof. And who says that’s easy until they clear it? I can probably go heavier.

Chime’s efforts suddenly resulted in him and Hiccup stepping out, and Chime brandished his tool, plonking it and making a very sharp, focused sound. Boom — their best camera on the enemy team went out.

“Looks like the jig is up,” Jack said. As he spoke, he worked quickly, connecting metal under the dirt, unseen and unlikely to be seen, with what Chime was focused on.

“Yeah,” Highfive said. He’d been pacing and suddenly stopped. “I guess we shouldn’t rush out to them. Right?”

“Right. There’s not much they can do about the debuff on Chime. If he’s bleeding badly internally in that leg, he’s only got so much time. They gotta come for us. Or run away and apply a tourniquet? Fat chance a simulation will do that, though.”

Inkblot offered, “I think there is some nerve damage being simulated based on his impairment. His mobility isn’t going to get better.”

“Perfect…”

Because the enemy was in a row, Jack found metal on either side of them and blasted them with needles of varying states of condition. Once more, Defiant obviously had orders to guard Chime. Jack didn’t care. On the other side, he launched what he’d been working on: a thin cord put together from buried needles and other metal bits. Using the weird camera angle they had left, he launched it for Délight’s ankles. He didn’t need to be precise here. It was long, and ‘close enough’ was fine.

Predictably, she tried to jump over it at the last second. Bad move — he knew she would. The metal cord adjusted and tangled her legs even as she dropped. He got a wrap quickly, mostly by feel.

Defiant reacted quickly, moving like lightning and grabbing the cord, trying to pull it free. Jack effortlessly ‘liquidified’ the metal where he grabbed, making his hands pass right through it, at which point the metal re-solidified. He had plenty of practice at this sort of thing.

Meanwhile, Chime was switching from his xylophone to his sticks…

Jack tightened his hold over every inch of the cord at once and then slung Délight into the nearby wall as hard as he could, going for an overcharged output of 110%. She screamed as she was hurled.

Defiant somehow reacted in time, seeming to teleport in the feed from the camera, interposing himself and ‘catching’ her. However, he couldn’t seem to use his powers in a way that protected Délight by suddenly arresting her. Instead, he let himself get slammed into the wall, dulling the force ‘gradually’ but interrupted by the impact.

As such, Délight took a serious clock that must’ve stunned her, from the way she flopped in Defiant’s arms.

Chime was in the process of being turned toward Délight to use his sticks…

“Disrupt his arms or hands, Bo,” Jack said.

“Ha!” she exclaimed. “Easy.”

Chime looked like he had a brief seizure in his arms and hands, dropping his tools immediately.

Jack focused the cord in a certain way around one of Délight’s ankles, leveraging two sections. “Block this, motherfragger.” Jack put his full output into ripping the ankle two directions at once.

Defiant did bend down abnormally fast and grabbed her foot with a hand. But he couldn’t stop the leverage created that way, as Jack was pulling two directions at once. He stopped some of it. Not enough.

Délight screamed in agony as her ankle was literally broken.

Jack’s eyes widened. It was just a sim, but it had not just ‘simulated’ the broken ankle. There may have been some mitigation, but not much. “Wait, that’s allowed?”

“Yeah, bro, of course,” Highfive said, as if the most obvious thing in the world. “This is the PACCs. A broken ankle ain’t life-threatening, and we got healers.” He suddenly grinned and had to stop himself from clapping. “You broke that witch’s ankle! Hell yeah!”

Jack could feel Bo glaring at him. “Ferro, you are not going to hold back on her.”

“I won’t, I won’t!” Jack insisted, suppressing a sigh.

Hiccup was moving over to Délight, leaving Chime on the ground, who had apparently found the other camera in the vicinity right before the distraction, as he was looking and ready to ‘hit it’ with his xylophone.

“Who to disrupt!?” Bo asked urgently.

Jack wanted to say ‘Defiant,’ but he knew better. Stopping him was an order of magnitude harder for her. What he did was not powered merely by physical movement. He had to disrupt his powers directly, and a Conceptual powerset was among the hardest to do it to.

“Hiccup,” Jack said. He was certain she could grab the cord, displace faster than him with it in her hands, and disconnect it.

Hiccup tripped into the sand face-first. She displaced herself slightly, but it didn’t seem to help due to her temporary disorientation. It might’ve been a quick, aborted’ power-use; he wasn’t sure.

Jack leveraged Délight’s other ankle in a cloned maneuver, aiming to clone his previous break and cripple her even more. This time, Defiant had draped Délight over his shoulder and grabbed her foot and ankle with both hands, cradling it.

Hrm.

Chime blew out the camera just then, blinding them in the area, but Jack didn’t exactly need it with a cord already wrapped around an enemy. Interpret worked just fine for a decent-sized object.

Jack created what leverage he could in the area, in between the bracing hands of Defiant. It couldn’t be perfect, right? He couldn’t actually lace ‘defiance’ over her bones.

Hmm. Two bones, right there. The smaller fibula, and the, er… other one. Okay.

Judging by feel, he could likely very easily exert intense, sudden, one-way pressure on the fibula and crack it. So that’s what he did.

He didn’t know how effective he was due to the lack of a nearby audio feed. She was probably in shock and pain enough not to scream again.

An instant later, he felt the metal hurling through the air.

“Ugh,” Bo exclaimed. “Sorry. She did that really fast, and I couldn’t see.”

“No worries,” Jack replied. Jack busied himself collecting the metal and making a stronger cord to bring up behind them, possibly for a follow-up. It wasn’t as if he didn’t have more traps ahead. “Save your energy and focus on disrupting at the key point in time.”

“Roger.”

The enemy team was apparently fed up with remaining stationary. They were caught sight of coming around the corner to the front of the fortress, Delight riding piggyback on Defiant and Chime half-carried by Hiccup. Chime was already working like crazy on his xylophone and almost immediately wiped out the camera, which they more or less allowed.

They were slowed down again, though, as Jack took more needle potshot blasts at them, whittling them down. They fought off any follow-up tricks, as Jack had accuracy problems without the camera, despite the fact that he could dish out AoE practically all day long. Délight’s team was not terribly strong against it, but Chime was pulling double-duty, blocking and demolishing large swaths of metal ammo. They also remained super paranoid about needles getting inside their suits, Chime or Hiccup removing a couple each before Jack even focused on them.

Jack had them bleeding and harried. Délight was at a real disadvantage without any enemies to affect, but she was not useless, clearly using her powers to push herself and her team through pain, fatigue, and ordeal. Morale was no joke in this situation, and she was making them tenacious in their drive.

“Did you see Chime’s lower leg?” Inkblot asked. The rest of the team just looked at him, so he continued. “Wet. Blood seeping through as the suit allows it to permeate. The suit won’t let it pool under the sealed system. He’s bleeding fairly badly, which means it’s even worse in simulation. He’ll be weakening.”

“Nice!” Highfive exclaimed.

As soon as the enemy team caught sight of the heavy wooden gate, Chime was ringing his cowbell and battering it, instantly causing cracks. This was far enough away, the camera wasn’t even on them, and all they saw — and heard — were the results.

Ah. Good test and a note for the match. Need a long-distance camera from the other side, angled on that spot.

Not that it mattered too much. The best trap was immediately inside the door. They had to go through that chokepoint. Odds were, they would have trouble scaling to the top. That was the key issue for them.

Jack shot heavier darts from out of the sand, more or less to keep up appearances and nickel-and-dime them more. He got no coup de grace on anyone. He hadn’t intended to. Meanwhile, Bo didn’t use her disruption, allowing the fortress door to be gradually destroyed as Jack pelted them with ammo for the privilege.

In all honesty, considering it was a sim, he wasn’t even trying very hard. He wanted to lure them to the trap and see it work. They already had a massive advantage in this particular sim. When it finally became a direct altercation, they would wipe their enemy out.

Inevitably, they blew through the heavy wooden door. The team darted toward it, with Defiant in the lead.

Jack had all the metal in the portcullis gate nearby to work with, jutting out from the wall directly across from the blown fortress gate. Mostly, it would look suspicious not to use it. Despite the fact that they were moving fast, the enemy team obviously hesitated in passing close to it.

Simple and stupid, eh? Jack pulled the bars out and launched them like spears, one after the other.

Chime turned to deal with them, battering them out of the air with sound. To Jack’s eyes, he was clearly moving with more labor; his arm motions with his instrument slowed.

Meanwhile, Defiant ran through the doorway. No doubt, he wanted to do that alone, and would, if not for his ‘girlfriend’ having two broken ankles. Mostly. She could probably put weight on one of them, just not without pain.

The thinking was probably to get eyes on their opponents with Délight, to begin some sort of offense to deal with. Even if Bo disrupted her, that would free Chime somewhat. It was doomed, but what else could they do?

Things were pretty much too perfect with Chime being distracted and having to cover them.

As soon as Defiant ran over the ‘spot,’ Jack was ready, grabbing the metal it was entirely composed of — despite appearances — and collapsed it into a block on one side. Because the floor was otherwise gone, this dropped the two lovers down into the dungeon area.

There wasn’t jack shit they could do about it. They dropped and fell, Defiant landing with a thud on his feet and poised for a jump.

The ceiling was already closing again, though. Aside from that, Defiant couldn’t make the damn jump if he wanted to!

They were sealed over before he could even try it — or any other shenanigans, for that matter.

Bubbles of excitement and satisfaction came out of the others in the sealed room Jack was in, as they watched on their internal monitors.

“Defiant can’t teleport, right?!” Highfive asked.

“No!” Bo said gleefully — with a little hop and her fists thrust out.

Jack was too busy to pay attention or respond. Hiccup and Chime immediately began toward the opening, but Jack made this difficult by picking up the shrapnel that was embedded in the remains of the door and blasting it at them.

And then Jack opened the wall of the room they were in. “Go. Play. Have fun.”

His team immediately burst outward. Well, he kinda sorta could tell Inkblot did, despite that he was already invisible.

He was right on their heels, eager to start getting physical eyes on his foes for once. He’d mostly just said what he did because it was funny, like he was the ‘mastermind’ unleashing his minions. Oh, he’d certainly never be admitting that to them, but he could enjoy it internally!

It occurred to him… he’d been doing all this to their enemies, massively weakened… and they were in complete shambles, separated and useless. Defiant would not be getting out of there anytime soon. Not soon enough, certainly.

Fraggin’ awesome. Well. Might as well enjoy it. We clearly have to make this damn sim much harder.

🌑 🌒 🌓 🌔 🌕

The team mopped up the weakened enemy sims with tragic ease. Defiant was currently being simmed as ‘slightly weak’ to AoE, so despite that Bo was available as a ‘final sacrifice,’ they first tried out the hypothetical weakness. Eventually, Defiant was whittled down. Supposedly, he would forfeit anyway when his team was wiped, but that was useless for training and couldn’t be assumed. Maybe it depended on circumstances.

They also ‘rewound’ their enemies and tried out Bo’s sacrifice, just to test, and because they could spare her energy this time. Naturally, it needed to be keyed to not wipe her quite as much, so both her ability output and his assessed resistance were toned down.

Otherwise, it worked well. When his power was disrupted, they wiped him out immediately. A clean one-for-one. Sort of — it wasn’t like it was ‘killing’ Bo to wipe out fatigue-wise. To finish a match? Totally acceptable.

After a brief rest, they dived back in, making minor logical adjustments, revising strategies, and discussing how things might go differently. Jack was adamant that they force new approaches from the enemy team each time, so as to be ready for as much as they could. Who knew what curveball was going to come?

Underneath it all was an anticipation, glee, and maybe even awe at what Jack could do before the enemy even touched them. Their core strategy was a definite go — if anything, he had to curb their inclinations to make more traps. There wasn’t time, and it was best not to overdo it, if they could set up ‘the big one.’ Gutting the floor was hard and time-consuming enough.

All too soon, the sim time was up, and their guesswork would face the final test the next day.

Jack was hesitantly looking forward to it. One way or another, the answers would come. No matter what, they were ready.
