We hammered into the God of Thunder.

His spear lashed out like a rattlesnake in the desert, and Stormrunner was barely enough to keep me from joining Tanat. I used Lightning Dance for the extra security, then went on the offensive. Laminar Blade to cut through his armor. Stormfall to attack almost as quickly as he could defend. The Storm’s Blade and his spear—the same one Stormlord Nalthelas had carried—met, and sparks crashed down around me.

And as we fought, my Mana emptied and was immediately refilled as the combination of Shadowstorm Battery and the Eye of the Storm went to work. Ellen was draining me as fast as she could, her spells empowered by Voidwalker, but no matter what she did, the Eye of the Storm worked faster. The Shadow Cell slammed shut around the SS-Plus Paragon, only to be shattered before it could bore into his armor. She followed it up with Black Hole, and Abyssal Storm that ravaged the room but didn’t even crack his armor, and an Echoing Black that filled the room with Darkness.

The God of Thunder danced between the barrage of spells. It was a thing of beauty—or it would have been, if not for Tanat’s body flailing and the gasps of agony. She was long past screaming, without enough air to express her pain. Twice, I had to pull my attacks when the God of Thunder put her between him and me.

As we danced, sword and spear cracking the sound barrier with each blow, I pushed Lightning Dance to the limit. One attack from behind him. Another from—

—the side. And another—

—from right above.

But every blow was met with the spear, and every attack only resulted in another wound. A puncture to my thigh that left me limping slightly as Stamina poured in and my skills got to work fixing it. A painful cut from scalp to lip that missed destroying my eye by millimeters. Bruises even through my swirling storm armor.

“I see. You’ve grown, kid. It won’t be enough, but you’ve grown.”

I howled in rage.

My shadow flickered. Then it disappeared. All around me, the world shifted as even the lightning against the black clouds lost its contrast. Ellen’s hands rose over her head, and shadow pooled around them in a whirlwind of pure black that grew and grew, reaching up to the Eye of the Storm. It made contact, and the blue-white lightning all around us went green-black.

“Interesting. The Shadow Witch has some tricks.” The God of Thunder’s spear hung between him and me as the cyclone crashed down on us. Liquid shadow scoured away at the God of Thunder’s body and armor like a raging river. It ripped plates of Stormsteel away and threw them across the Lightning Tower. And green-black lightning pulsed around us.

And I used Stormbreak. Exclusions on Ellen, Tanat, and the flying serpents—who were both vomiting sunbeams and lightning at the God of Thunder to little avail. The flower that bloomed this time was different. Each was either black or green, and when they pulsed, they pulsed with the same shadowlightning energy pouring down from the Tides of Darkness-infused Eye of the Storm. The pulses went on and on, fed by the storm overhead. I had been enough to put the Placid King on his back foot.

But the God of Thunder only laughed. His armor smoldered as Stormbreak ripped into it, and the Tides of Darkness tore plates away, but underneath it, his lightning body was untouched.

“Your partner’s a powerful mage, Kade Noelstra. She might be more of a threat than you.” He jammed the spear into the floor, peeled off the wreckage of his armor, and dropped into a boxer’s stance. I’d never seen him fight without either his dragon form or his weapon, and I couldn’t predict what’d happen next.

Then he moved. Faster than anything I’d seen before. Like lightning.

His fist slammed into Ellen’s chest, and she doubled up from the impact.

Ellen understood, intellectually, what was happening.

The God of Thunder wasn’t like Bob. He couldn’t be. People, to her father, weren’t people—they were tools. Pieces on a game board. That was all. But she’d seen the same thing Kade had in the Hall of Thunder, and she understood what lesson the God of Thunder had taken from it. It was the same lesson she had, but corrupted and perverted. Or, maybe…maybe even more pure than hers. She couldn’t tell.

She’d come away from Bob unwilling to ever be in another person’s debt, at least until Kade, Jessie, and their friends showed up. She wouldn’t owe anyone anything, and no one would owe anything to her. That way, she’d be free from people like him. The God of Thunder had clearly thought the same thing. He’d never be dependent on his pupils or cult, unlike his former master, and he’d only hunt one of them at a time.

So, as the God of Thunder’s fist smashed her left kidney into paste and she desperately poured Stamina into it, she understood exactly what the God of Thunder was doing.

He was teaching Kade a lesson.

The pain ripped through Ellen’s body. The next blow caught her in the chest, cracking a rib somewhere below her collarbone. The next one dislocated her hip. She cast Shadow Puppeteer, and a shadowy version of the God of Thunder threw itself into the fight. It lasted almost three seconds—an eternity, at the speed everyone was fighting at. Then it was back to the beating.

Ellen had never been okay with pain. Right from the moment Kade had first worked on teaching her how to direct her Stamina—and, in fact, from the first time she’d fallen and scraped her knee in Bob’s garden—she’d feared pain more than anything.

Another blow. This one had to have been at half the God of Thunder’s strength; it caught her on the chin, snapping her head back and to the side hard enough that she saw stars, but her neck wasn’t broken. She slammed The Shadow Cell closed, then echoed it with Arcane Resonator over and over, trying to cause enough damage to break free from the SS-Plus monster’s assault.

And the pain built up, higher and higher.

She wanted to break. To scream, or to lose consciousness. To beg for the beating to stop. S-Rank she might be, but escaping the God of Thunder’s assault was impossible. And, worse, he was doing it one-handed. Tanat’s limp form still hung from his off-hand, whipping around like a ribbon as he moved.

Could she get it to stop by begging? By pleading for mercy?

No.

No, the pain was something to get through. Not to avoid. She couldn’t avoid it.

Cheddar and Pepperoni’s beams of sun and lightning ripped across the God of Thunder. They burned her arm through her tattered Portal Tyrants uniform, and the sheer novelty of that pain brought her attention back to the fight.

She had an option. It wasn’t a great one. She hadn’t thought about it in months, because her other skills were all powerful, A and S-Rank mage skills or representative of her bond with Kade.

Ellen used Dash. The burst of movement felt slow in comparison to the God of Thunder, but it caught him off-guard, and in less than a second, she slid to the floor behind Kade.

Attack.

Attack.

Attack.

The battle trance had me. I was so far beyond my limits I didn’t remember what they were. All I knew was to attack. To fight. To hurt the bastard who’d impaled Tanat, then used her as a shield and thrashed her around. To make the God of Thunder suffer for the beating he’d given Ellen. To end him. Preferably quickly.

As Ellen broke free, I finished my Hand of the Thunder God combo. It burst out in green-black, the beam of lightning washing over the God of Thunder like pure hate.

When the beam stopped, he’d changed form. His long, serpentine body smashed through the side of the Lightning Tower and curled around the building as he flew through the Eye of the Storm. Thunder boomed. “You were my most interesting disciple, Kade Noelstra. I loved you in a way I hadn’t loved a student since Tanat. But even so, you’ve failed to learn the most important lesson of the Stormsteel Path. You still rely on your friends. On your partner. Even on me. And in the end, the only way to walk its final step is alone.

“You’ve seen how I learned that, kid. You visited my darkest memory. So, you know it’s the truth.”

I didn’t say anything. I couldn’t. The battle trance gripped me too tightly for that. The God of Thunder loved me? I glanced over my shoulder at Ellen’s swelling face and collapsed thigh. She nodded from the floor and started, once again, pouring power into her Tides of Darkness.

I kept casting, too. Another Hand of the Thunder God. It caught the God of Thunder mid-flight, boring a hole through his long torso lengthwise, but the hole filled in instantly. His Health was simply too much to overcome.

“There’s no shame in dying to a superior predator, Kade Noelstra.”

No, there wasn’t. But there was shame in breaking my promises. Ellen was still down behind me, even as the Tides of Darkness built around her. Jessie was back at the Portal Tyrant’s fortress, and so was Jeff. I’d promised him a chance to fight alongside me, and I hadn’t delivered yet. Then there was Dad.

I summoned the Stormkin Avatar and threw him into battle against the God of Thunder, who only laughed. “You still haven’t learned the lesson, Kade. You. Cannot. Rely. On. Others. It isn’t just that they’re weak. It’s that, at the end of the day, either you betray them, or they betray you.”

Nothing I did stuck. The Stormkin Avatar hacked away at the God of Thunder, but the sword’s cuts healed almost the moment they appeared. He was playing with us, like a cat with a mouse.

Tides of Darkness. It built up around Ellen, then rushed in a torrent toward the God of Thunder. I didn’t expect a different result than the first time, but I was out of options. I used Stormbreak again.

And this time, the lightning was a familiar blue-white.

It pulsed. The Eye of the Storm fed it until it collapsed. My vision went blurry from the strobing assault, even through eyes squeezed closed. Ellen moaned in pain behind me as the crashing, booming cacophony gradually came to an end.

The God of Thunder didn’t laugh this time.

I’d excluded Ellen, Tanat, and the serpents. The power that had coursed through me should have killed me—if not the first time, then definitely the second. And yet, I still stood, and the God of Thunder still flew, hovering just out of sword range, Tanat gripped in a massive, electric claw.

“I understand, Kade. You don’t yet understand. Worse, you refuse to understand. I’ve been around for millennia, and in that time, every Paragon who believes in allies either is betrayed by them—like this Tanat woman’s Retan—or betrays them himself. The Placid King was working on it, and Stormlord Nalthelas betrayed me.”

“I don’t care,” I ground out through gritted teeth. Every fiber of my being screamed to keep fighting. We’d hurt him, at least a little bit. Instead, I kept talking. “I trust Ellen, and I trust my sister. And I trusted Tanat. She fought you instead of betraying me.”

“We’ll see.”

Before I could respond, the massive storm dragon’s length unfurled behind him as he turned and flew for his portal. I fired an un-empowered Hand of the Thunder God, but it didn’t even clip the SS-Plus-Ranked Paragon. Then he vanished into the portal, Tanat in his claw.

The God of Thunder was not a monster.

As he arrived in his red-and-blue-skied portal world, he wove between the massive pillars that housed twenty thousand years of Mana, then landed on the wide, flat platform he rested on. The dark-eyed, long-limbed woman’s body was slowly healing already, but he nevertheless pushed electricity into her in carefully controlled bursts, sealing the wound that had pierced her from hip to kidney, as well as the worst of the rest of her wounds.

After all, he wasn’t a monster.

He laughed bitterly. Thunder rolled. His instincts swelled inside him. There was a delver—a helpless delver, and one who wasn’t sworn to him—on the hard surface of his portal world. It was time to silence the voice that screamed at him to kill and kill until everything around him was dead.

He was a monster, after all. It was in his nature to kill the Tanat woman, no matter how helpful she’d been to his former pupil.

But while a monster he might be, he wasn’t a sadist. Pain held no particular interest to him. In fact, the entire time he’d fought against Kade Noelstra and his core-bonded partner, he’d taken great pains to shield the unconscious delver. She had to be alive for his plan to work, after all.

Kade Noelstra was the key to everything.

The kid knew exactly what the God of Thunder was. He understood, now that he’d seen the Hall of Thunder, what drove him.

The God of Thunder planned on weaponizing that understanding. The thin swordswoman had been the first step in that process. By attacking Kade’s allies, he was trying to isolate him. Tanat had been savaged, first by her futile attempt to resist the God of Thunder, then by the inadvertent beating she’d suffered during his fight against Kade and Ellen. That was perfect. Beaten, at death’s door, but alive. “Simply perfect.”

His assault on the shadow mage had been deliberate, too. He could have killed her. Instead, he’d left her broken, but alive. He’d been watching the two, and he understood Eleanor Traynor. She was afraid. Afraid of being in debt. Afraid of her own pathetic excuse for a father. And afraid of pain.

It had been simple to inflict it on her—not too much, but enough that she’d hesitate the next time Kade went to battle. She’d wonder if next time, the God of Thunder wouldn’t hold back.

More importantly, though, Kade would think that.

The God of Thunder had never played chess before. But even so, he understood enough of the game Kade loved.

“Check. Your move, Kade.”
