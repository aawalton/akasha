Ezra grunted as he braced his shoulder behind his shield and defended against Telemachus's attack. The spear slammed into his defence, but Ezra twisted his grip to have the weapon slide off the wooden shield over his shoulder, then swung forward and struck a hit on his opponent's left hip. Even though Ezra was wielding a padded club and smacked into the other man's thick practice armor, Telemachus staggered back, hissing out a long breath of pain.

“Don’t give up. Keep going!” Chloe commanded. “All of you, when you're in a real fight, the pain doesn't matter! Only winning does!”

Telemachus took an extra hopping step back, trying to get space between himself and Ezra, which he only halfway managed to do. With that first solid hit, Ezra cemented enough of a lead in the fight to maintain his position as the eventual winner for the fight. When finally Chloe called an end to all of the fights about a minute later, both men stepped back, fumbling at the straps under their chins to pull off their helmets. Then, with gasping breaths, they stepped to the edge of the ring and fell onto their stools.

“I got you this time,” Ezra huffed out a laugh, pulling off his heavy gloves and reaching a fist out to Telemachus.

“Yes, you're up to seven to four now,” the other man laughed, pouring a ladleful of water over his head and letting his black hair run down his face, “but I'm getting closer to evening out that score.”

Ezra couldn't argue with that, and instead set about pulling the most uncomfortable bits of padded armor off. The man who had swiftly become his best friend in the garrison was right, and in the three weeks since they had returned from the extermination expedition, he had done well in following Ezra's suggestion to constantly draw on the influence of his inner world. Even when he wasn't using fire, Telemachus's movements were more explosive, vicious, and overwhelming, and his results were showing it.

“That's it for today, right?” Ezra asked, deliberately raising his voice for Chloe to hear. "Just dinner, then some meditation?”

“For most people, yes,” she said, walking over. “For you, we have a quick meeting with a new assignment after you eat your dinner. Meet me in my office.”

“Yes, ma'am,” he snapped out a quick salute.

The fist-fighting Pseudo-Knight nodded at him and gave quick counsel to Telemachus regarding his footwork. She didn't wait for an answer, continuing to walk around the four pits where people had been sparring. She frequently liked to push the trainees into duels when she was controlling a training session, making them push not for points or single hits, but instead treating it like a full fight, usually for four or five minutes. That was more than long enough to exhaust them all, and it sure was better than the conditioning that Michel liked to push the trainees through.

Ezra grunted as he finished pulling off most of the training armor that he could while still sitting, and then, pushing off of his knees, he stood up. As he stripped off the rest of his armor to take it to the stands along the edge of the courtyard, Telemachus mirrored his movement.

“Any idea what this assignment is gonna be?” the fire-affinity man asked.

“No.”

“Do you think it'll have something to do with the private meetings you have with her twice a week?” he insisted, not trying to hide the suggestive tilt to his eyebrows.

“I've told you, those meetings are training in manners,” Ezra ground out. “I don't know why she makes me do it instead of you, and I would prefer that you take the spot.”

“Bedtime manners?”

“Shut the fuck up,” Ezra shook his head, reaching out and punching Telemachus in the shoulder.

They both grinned as they got to the racks for their training weapons and armor. Dirk, Rose, and Miriam were all waiting for the other two commoner trainees there, and Ezra nearly melted in gratitude when they helped him remove the rest of his armor. It was soaked in his sweat, and, now over a month after his arrival here in Brayton, spring was in full force. This much farther north than his hometown, the spring was genuinely warm instead of just no longer cold. These training sessions in the afternoon on the sands that had been baked by the entire day's sun were painfully hot, and Ezra wasn't looking forward to the summer when it came.

“When you have a minute,” Dirk said as he untied the line of cinches holding the quilted armor tied to Ezra's body, “I wanted to ask you a couple questions about the way you move the aura into your inner world. Maybe during meditation tonight?”

“Mr. Important over here won't have time tonight,” Telemachus said, turning around to give Miriam easier access to the straps along the back of his neck. “He's getting an assignment from Captain Chloe tonight after dinner.”

“Don't speak for me.” Ezra grumped at Telemachus as the last of the armor was shucked off his body. “It is true that I'm meeting with the captain after dinner, but I'd imagine that she isn't going to have me there for the entire two hours until it's lights out. If there's any time tonight, I'm happy to do my meditation with you and see what I can help you with.”

The rest of the conversation shifted away from that as the group of five commoners made their way toward the dining hall. A part of Ezra found it strange that he'd basically immediately stepped into the position of the leader of the five, even though he'd only arrived about a month ago, while the next most junior member was Telemachus, who had arrived in early autumn of the year before. Rose had joined the squad about a year before Ezra, with Miriam and Dirk arriving at the same time about six months before that.

Regardless of Ezra's thoughts on it, the reality was that he was the strongest of the five and had the best handle on aura and his inner world. They were all in the trainee squad because they had potential to continue to develop their inner worlds, and when choosing between arbitrary seniority and skill, nobody had any complaints in prioritizing skill.

They walked toward the mess hall, steering clear of the other trainees but no longer having to walk as carefully as before. Now that the five commoners had two Adepts as well as the victor and runner-up of the tournament among the trainees, they didn't have to walk so quietly and keep their heads down to avoid the anti-Duke arseholes. Instead, the trainees who were not officially among the anti-Duke faction treated the commoners well, and the anti-Duke faction remained antagonistic, but stayed away unless forced to.

It was nice to be able to simply enter the mess hall and get their dinner without worrying that their meat servings would be reduced or their rolls replaced with days-old ones. Stranger than Ezra somehow becoming the leader of his little squad, or not having to worry about his rations, was what he couldn't say to anyone. Through Jormun, he was reasonably certain that there was a demonic incursion occurring within the Dragon Wilds. He didn't know what exactly was happening there in the mountain range populated by beasts mutated by pools of dragon blood and draconic remains, but it was bad. The Dragon Wilds were a constant looming threat, one of the main reasons why the four countries found around the continent largely avoided all out war.

The valley between the mountains, said to be made from the corpse of the last true dragon, was populated with hundreds of thousands, perhaps millions, of beasts, all of them mutated and maddened by the draconic essence that filled the area. What could Ezra do about this demonic invasion anyway? He couldn't fight against the infernal that Michel had killed, to say nothing of whatever they were working to summon. And how could he explain to people that he was sure these “aberrants” were actually invaders from another land that would stop at nothing to destroy everything and set the very skies on fire?

He and the grizzled ghost had had dozens of conversations over the past weeks, trying to figure out what they might be able to do to take care of these problems, but they hadn't come to any real solutions. Instead, finally, they'd come to the decision that they would have to tell somebody and trust them to do something, and sooner rather than later. How Ezra was going to attempt to justify it was a mystery to him even now, but he figured that his meeting with Chloe tonight would give him plenty of opportunity to at least broach the topic.

For now, Ezra enjoyed the meal with his friends, each laughing around the table where they sat, the jokes only half of the conversations they shared. Tips on how to change their stance or approach with different weapons filled the air just as much, and, with them being granted a full hour to eat and wash away the sweat from the day, they took about thirty minutes to eat before they each retired to take a well-deserved bath. The sweat and dust from the day ran down in brown rivulets until finally the water was clear, and Ezra was clean.

At that point, he soaped himself up, still amazed at the small luxuries that were provided to him as a trainee that he'd never had growing up. Scented soap, the good stuff, was provided free of charge, and as much as he could possibly use. Not the stuff that would leave his skin red and irritated, and he also just enjoyed the time in the warm water.

When a good long while had passed, leaving only about ten minutes for Ezra to get to Chloe's office, he left the baths and toweled off. The towels provided were soft, better than the rags that he would've used at home, and he continued to laugh at how soft he himself had become as he entered his own room. Less than two minutes later, he walked out again, his hair curling softly across his forehead, still damp.

Over the past weeks, he'd been meeting with Chloe regularly, and the path toward her office was a well-trodden one. Going up the stairs, he found the hallway where the Pseudo-Knight offices were all located. Supposedly, Dominick, the sole Knight in Brayton, had an office here as well, but he spent most of his time in the Duke's manor, staying close to the man to whom he had pledged himself. As such, it was the Pseudo-Knights who effectively ruled here, along with the commander of the garrison, an older man named Gray.

At the door, Ezra knocked twice and fell back into parade rest. He found it strange how much he'd changed over the past weeks, so easily acting like an experienced soldier. He carried himself more formally, with greater poise, and it was just… weird. The thought crossed his mind frequently enough that he’d commented as much in the letters he sent to Tilly. He hadn't received any back yet, but he'd decided that sending one every two weeks was enough to make sure she was staying in contact with him and he wasn't bankrupting himself doing so.

“Come in.”

Ezra opened the door and closed it behind himself, falling again into parade rest as he looked at Chloe. She sat behind a simple desk, the top scarred from what had to be many years of use. An array of papers was spread across her desk, and she held her left hand up in front of him as her right finished writing something. Then, with practiced movements, she folded the letter and began dripping red wax on it. Pulling a seal from a drawer, she finished preparing the letter and stamped the seal into it.

“Thanks,” Chloe said, sitting back and looking more casual than he usually saw her. In public, she always conducted herself as Captain Chloe, happy and generally kind, but stern. “Both for waiting, and for coming. At ease. Actually, take a seat.”

Ezra nodded and obliged, sitting down in one of the two chairs in front of the desk while continuing to look at her. She'd summoned him; he wasn't going to make any demands of his commanding officer.

“Sometimes, I miss the unaware boy you were just a short while ago,” Chloe said, breaking the silence. She smiled as she leaned against the table, steepling her fingers together. “My training on our way here and until now has borne fruit, though.”

“Thank you, ma'am.”

When Ezra didn't continue, Chloe nodded her head in acknowledgment, his further display proving that she was correct. Finally, she got to the point of her summoning him and looked far less relaxed as she sat up straight.

“You've been here for a while. The question at the core of this, before I even mention what this assignment is going to be for you, is what your opinion is regarding the Duke and the anti-Duke faction.”

“I don't have much of an opinion regarding the Duke himself,” Ezra replied immediately, the words flowing freely and almost unbidden off his tongue. II've never met him, though from what I know of his subordinates, he seems to be a good enough ruler. His people, even Mikhail, who apparently doesn't agree with him wholeheartedly, respect him. They're loyal, which goes to show that he treats them well. He hasn't come in here to start giving commands, so he seems willing to leave things to people who know what they're doing instead of trying to insert himself. On patrol, I can see that while there are poor people, crime is relatively under control, and I don't see children starving in the streets, which is a good thing. That said, I haven't been to every part of the city, and I'm sure there's worse places in Brayton. So, while I don't have any strong judgment on the Duke, I'd say I have a higher estimation of him than I do for other nobles.

“As for the anti-Duke faction, I won't attempt to hide the fact that I think they're a bunch of arrogant assholes who think that money is worth more than a life, and I wouldn't mind never having to interact with them again in my own life. If I had to make a choice between the two of them, which seems to be what your question is, I wouldn't hesitate to choose the Duke over the anti-Duke faction, with what I know now. That said, I don't have enough information to make an absolute decision just yet.”

At that, Ezra's lips pressed together as he realized that, in accordance with the incessant training that Chloe had put him through, he had reported just as she would have wanted him to. He narrowed his eyes at her, and she smiled widely as she leaned back in her chair.

“Considering who you are, I'd call that a pretty good answer,” she said, nodding as she pressed both hands flat against the table and stood up. “Now, follow me.”

“Yes, ma'am,” Ezra said, standing up as well. “Is this for me to receive my assignment now?”

“I'm not the person giving you the assignment,” Chloe said, looking over her shoulder and cocking a smile at him. “For now, it's time for you to meet the Duke.”
