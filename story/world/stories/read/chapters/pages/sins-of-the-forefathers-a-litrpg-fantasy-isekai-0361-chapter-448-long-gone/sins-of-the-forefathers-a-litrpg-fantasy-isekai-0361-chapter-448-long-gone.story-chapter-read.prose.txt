I stared at the glowing stone for a moment with bated breath, wondering if I was suddenly about to hear a familiar voice echo in my mind. However, nothing happened, no matter how hard I held my breath and tried to strain my senses.

No sarcastic, uncaring voice suddenly revealed itself to me…and so I let out my breath, and furrowed my brow.

Alright, this was…odd. What, exactly, did this mean?

This staff had belonged to Tlazo. It had been created by him, apparently. I very faintly recalled that he had told me that the capstone had been created from the same material as his phylactery had been. That had been the given reason he was able to even speak to me when I was in the Concord.

Apparently, the Lich’s essence and soul had been banished back to the spiritual anchor that sustained him when Rhazal had reduced his physical self to dust. That being his phylactery. Once I had finished with Rhazal and recovered from my ordeal, I had spoken to Honoka about the entire matter, which is where I had discovered her past with the now Lich.

At the time, the guess was that it would take him several years to reconstitute himself after being so thoroughly defeated. Honoka had even implied that she had a good guess as to where her erstwhile husband’s phylactery was. I was inclined to believe her on both matters, honestly. The two had, from all accounts, been married for centuries before the man known as Rafael had faked his death to become the Lich known as Tlazo. Who knows what he had told his wife about his craft, in all that time?

Including where he kept his most precious of treasures?

Was he…here? Was this the place that Honoka had hinted at as the hiding spot of Tlazo’s phylactery? A small, hidden chamber, within an absolutely enormous underground caldera, only reachable through previously collapsed paths in the long defunct Dwarven Long Road?

At least, that’s what it had been. Neither Honoka nor Tlazo could have predicted that the caldera would be breached in the future through the work of Anguis and his men. They would establish a plantation and town above it, and construct an elevator to transport goods. I doubted that the Savoy had found this specific nook, but…

That sounded…plausible. This was an incredible hiding spot, rife with one of the things that helped to sustain a Lich and their work.

Death Aether.

What better location could there be to hide a phylactery and help one recover?

Well.

Only one way to find out.

Now…where to begin. Carefully raising the staff and curious if it would do anything, I channeled the slightest amount of Mana I could into the capstone. The glow brightened, and a soft amber light spilled out over the room around me, joining the warm glow of my Skill. Flickering my eyes around, I considered the various paths I had access to.

Presumably, if the staff had only lit up once I had reached this second level, then it was possible that Tlazo might be down one of those tunnels.

If my wild theory guessing was correct, of course.

Which one to start with, though? I suppose it didn’t really matter. I’d have to search each one either way, either for the possible Lich or a way out of here.

Eenie, meenie, miney, mo…

You.

I crossed over to the south-westernmost tunnel from where I stood, only to stop in my tracks once more.

As I approached the tunnel, the glow from my staff had waned slightly.

Curious…

Testing a hunch, I returned to where I had entered the second level, and sure enough…the light intensified.

Alright then. Built in seeker mode, I suppose. This was getting more and more certain as time went on.

One by one, I approached the mouths of each tunnel, testing for a reduction in the light from the glowing capstone upon my staff. It didn’t take me long to find one that reacted differently.

At the north-western tunnel, the light coming from the hunk of amber grew stronger.

Standing before it, I contemplated the darkness of the tunnel that stretched out before me. I couldn’t see anything out of the ordinary from where I stood. This tunnel didn’t look any different from any of the others I had searched on the first level. But…I did realize something curious as I stood there.

This tunnel led in the approximate direction of the dais in the room below. If I went far enough down it, I would pass right over the giant skeletal mole and its mummified acolytes.

Hmm.

Probably should have thought of that earlier.

Oh, whatever. Firming my resolve, I stepped foot down the tunnel that my inherited staff was directing me to.

With every step that I took, the light that came from the craggy hunk of crystal grew ever so slightly stronger. Not enough to grow blinding, of course. But certainly well enough to better illuminate my surroundings.

I was extremely gratified to discover that, finally, after all of the near identical stone tunnels I had investigated, with their veritably duplicated stone cells, this one was different. No more matching, near monk-like living spaces.

No, instead, I found much larger rooms, carefully carved out of the rock. For some of these rooms, it was hard to parse just what the purpose of them was. What exactly was I meant to parse out of a stone room with no identifying characteristics, roughly twenty feet by twenty feet, with nothing in it?

Others, though, I had a guess. And it was enough to give me an inkling as to what this wing of the complex was for.

One large room had long stone tables spanning the length of it, with stone benches on either side of it.

A dining room, I’m sure of it.

Another one had large stone bookcases lining the walls, stretching from the floor to the ceiling. To my enormous disappointment, each of the seven tiers in the bookcases was empty of anything but dust.

Of all things, a library would have been the most exciting. Maybe then I would have gotten some cultural knowledge about these people.

I sighed in regret and moved on.

Until, finally, I reached the end of the hallway. To my complete unsurprised, it terminated almost exactly over where I thought the lower, main chamber’s dais was below me. Not only that…

But it was an almost complete copy of that structure. And in its own way, it was just as ominous.

Before me was a room that seemed to be sized to the exact diameter and circumference of the circular dais below. If I had to guess, the edges of it would match the edges of the platform below exactly. This place, though, was an oddly perfect dome, and its roof wasn’t quite as tall as the cavern of the first floor. Still tall, but not monolithic in scale. None of that mattered, though.

What did was what was inside of it.

In the exact center of the room, in the spot where the enormous mole’s skeleton sat in eternal tranquility, was an altar. A great stone edifice of carved rock that thrust from the floor of the room, it was nearly as tall as an average Dwarf, coming up to roughly chest height on my frame, and twice as as long. The edges of it were straight and sharp, in stark contrast to the roughly hewn nature of everything else I had seen so far. The impossibly precise nature of not only the engineering of the altar, but the room itself was so complete, I immediately pegged it for what it had to be.

This, more than anything else, stank of the Netherim to me. It had to have been machined in some way. And yet, the aesthetics of the entire structure were still non-standard to what I understood of Netherim construction. Because not a single solitary surface within this dome was bare of decoration.

Spanning not only the walls, but the altar itself, were runes. An impossibly dense, impossibly complex schemata of runic print in such a scale that I was immediately struck dumb at the sight of it. My vision swam as I tried to make sense of what I could see all around me, on every surface in sight.

The floor, the walls, the ceiling, the altar…even the doorframe was inscribed with so many runes that I was simply not capable of comprehending them all. It was to such a small scale, as well, that I refused to believe it had been done by hand. I was only able to tell that the markings were runes, and not just tiny engraving, because I leaned in and shined my glowing amber staff right next to it. They were all so precise as well. I’m not sure even I could do such a thing with my Aetherial Melding. It had to have been done with a tool of some kind. Laser engraving, perhaps? Such a thing would have been possible to the Netherim, who I was now absolutely certain had something to do with this place.

But…I couldn’t parse this runic scheme. For the first time since I had learned the runic language, I was completely lost. It was almost like…

Stop. Think for a moment, Nate.

The runic language was strange sometimes; you knew that. There was the common parlance that you were used to. The thing that ninety-nine percent of all Magi and Cultivators used for their more involved mystical works.

Then there were the variants. I had seen some small examples of what the Orcs used for their magic, but only fragments. Nothing to the extent I’d be able to reproduce it.

Most importantly, though…I had seen examples of Divine runes. The Netherim usurpers that called themselves ‘gods’ had, supposedly, once upon a time, ‘gifted’ the runic language to mortals so they could affect mysticality as well. Only, they’d had a stronger, more complex version of it, they had kept to themselves.

This was almost like that. Only, it read like it was older somehow. The easiest way I could parse it would be like…like…

If modern runes were modern English, to my Terran sensibilities. And then Divine runes were Middle English, and this? Whatever this was?

It was ye olde English. The type of English that the only way you were going to understand it, after literal millennia of linguistic drift, was if you were specifically educated in its use.

But for this, I wasn’t. And so I didn’t understand a damn word of it.

Had the ‘gods’ stolen even that? Did the runic language have a different origin that they had stolen a fucking language from?

I let out a frustrated, shuddering breath at the implications.

Still…while that was a bombshell of its own, these revelations weren’t why I was here.

I was here for what was on the altar in the center of the room. After all…

It wasn’t empty.

I couldn’t quite tell what was lying upon it, considering the surface of the altar was draped in a dingy white cloth, but just from the shape alone, I could make an educated guess. The shift was thin enough that there was a general outline visible through it.

I shook my head and stepped through the doorway once I was…relatively sure it was safe to do so. Whatever those runes were meant for, it wasn’t for a trap of some kind. It was just too complex for something as simple as that.

This had been used for ritual magic on a scale I simply couldn’t comprehend.

My footsteps echoed loudly in the almost alien-seeming room as I crossed it’s carven surface. I think it was only through long exposure to truly powerful entities, in the past, that I was able to maintain my composure as I did. This place, whatever had happened here…it had left an imprint. Both in the Aether, and in something deeper. There was a weight to the ritual room I had rarely experienced, and if I was being truthful, I’m not sure I ever had before. Not even being within the hard of Kyronkar, with its full war wards deployed, had felt as heavy as being in this room did.

At last, when I came to stop before the altar, I leaned on my inherited staff and looked down impassively at the surface of it.

And the covered Human remains that lay utterly still beneath their shroud.

I contemplated the shape of them for a moment, unable to truly make anything out through the cloth.

And then snorted at the drama of it all. “Wake up already, you bag of bones,” I said irritably. “We don’t have all day. I know you’re not really asleep. You would have felt my coming.”

Beneath the dirty white shift, light suddenly flared into being, coming from two separate places. The first was at roughly heart level upon the figure. An extremely familiar, amber glow of a purity and intensity that put my staff to shame began to shine through the cloth.

But more importantly…

Two eerie blue flames flared into existence upon the obscured head of the figure, right where the eyes would be…

Before anotherworldly voice I hadn’t heard in over a year reached my ears.

“The youth these days…” They grumbled, the formerly still figure on the altar suddenly shifting in place. “Absolutely no sense of decorum at all.”

And the Lich formerly known as Rafael, but whom I knew as Tlazocuauhtli, rose from the dead once more.
