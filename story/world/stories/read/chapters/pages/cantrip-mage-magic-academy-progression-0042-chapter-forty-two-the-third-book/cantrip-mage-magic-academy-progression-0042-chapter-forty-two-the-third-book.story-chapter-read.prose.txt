When they’d lifted the shelf back into its place, Eola searched the empty book covers under it until she came up with an unlabeled, leather-bound journal, covered in cracks. There it was—the first journal of the Great Mage Varin, written when he was still an apprentice. Her heart rose, only to drop as she opened it.

It was all but empty.

Yes, she’d hoped for at least a single page. But the scrap she’d been left with was still disappointing. The handwriting matched the scrawl on the page Atta had ‘gifted’ her, but there were no runes, no notes on magic—nothing of use.

—agecraft is about finding the best solutions, not the most realistic ones.

Cut away the fetters of—

—elan says this a lot, and I think I’m starting to understand what she mea—

That was it. Three fragments of sentences, without any context. Eola stared at the page, flipped it to the other side, and encountered…nothing. The yellowed, crumbling paper stared back at her, ink-free and unspoiled except by time—time, and the monster that had attacked her.

So, she’d gotten what she wanted—a page from Varin. She couldn’t be too mad about that. Could she?

Yes, she definitely could. She shoved the ruined notebook into Colin’s satchel—he narrowed his eyes but didn’t say anything. Then she sat down at the edge of the room, feet hanging over the void of the library dungeon, and pondered.

The quote…she’d seen it before. The whole thing escaped her, but it was always, always, attributed to Varin. It had probably been something her parents had told her. Yes. That sounded right. It had been something they’d told her when her attunement manifested and the reality of her situation set in. She kept thinking until a long, curving stairwell connected, then held up a hand. “Not that one. It doesn’t go anywhere we need it to. Trust me.”

“You’re pretty sure of yourself on this,” Roth said.

“Trust me,” she repeated, and went back to thinking.

‘Magecraft is about finding the best solutions, not the most realistic ones. Cut away the fetters of preconceptions, believe the world is what you can make it, and magic will meet you there.’ That was the whole quote. It had powered her through the rough years between ten and thirteen where she hadn’t fully understood just how much her attunement had betrayed her desires. It had been the reason she’d enrolled at Varin’s Academy in the first place. If she could cut away her own perceptions about magic, she could make it what she needed it to be.

But she’d always assumed that the Great Mage Varin himself had said it. Instead, he’d heard it from his master, and who knew who Frelanne had heard it from?

And what did it really mean? The more Eola thought about it, the more confused she felt.

The next stairwell arrived suddenly, from above. It slammed into the room’s side, shaking the shelves and knocking Colin on his bottom. He picked himself up, wincing. “Is this one fine?”

“Should be,” Eola said. She stood, drew her smallsword, and stepped onto the short, wide stairs. They only led up maybe twenty feet; a carpet covered most of them, but dark, stained wood peeked out from either side, and there were no guard rails. As the others climbed on, Eola sat down and pushed her free hand’s fingers under the rug. “I’d hang on if I were you.”

Colin didn’t waste any time, gripping the carpet with white knuckles. The others joined him—just as the stairwell rocketed up and to the side. Wind whipped Eola’s hair, and Patrice’s high-pitched scream barely registered over the rush of air. The stairwell rushed sideways across the library’s third floor, then clicked into place between two rooms.

“Thoughts?” Eola asked.

“Down matches a book location,” Colin said.

“Good enough. Go down!” Patrice tried to shout, but she was still breathless and panting, and the words came out as more of a sigh than a yell.

They hurried off the stairs and into a lobby that looked almost identical to the library’s first floor. A few dozen chairs, some pulled around desks and others up against the walls. A silvered gate embedded in the marble wall. Towering bookshelves. Tile worn shiny from countless feet. And a smell…

The smell and the tile tipped Eola off. “Monsters,” she said. There was too much wear in the tile floors, and the whole, gigantic floating room smelled like…rot. Rot and emptiness.

A half-dozen other stairwells led up and down from the lobby, but the one behind them disappeared, zooming silently off into the library to connect two other rooms for a few seconds.

“Battle plan?” Patrice asked.

Eola glanced at the other stairwells, then at the gate. Then she relaxed. “We’re either perfectly safe or we’re in an incredible amount of trouble. Are there several gates down to the second floor or not?”

“No. Instructor Clearance says the librarians only use one entrance to get to the third floor,” Colin said.

“But does that mean there’s only one entrance? Because that is a gateway between dungeon floors,” Eola said, pointing. “If we’re not sure, let’s assume that we’re looking at the one between the third and fourth floors. The monsters we find here could be pretty dangerous.”

“What about that?” Roth asked. He stared at a section of library that was roped off. Eola took a look at the sign. Danger: Ensorcelled Floor.

“We stay away from that at all costs,” she said after a moment. “Let’s get moving—and stay close. We’ve definitely got monsters here.”

They started searching the shelves, but nothing was labeled. Not a single book on the entire left side of the room had a label, and Eola wasn’t about to start pulling every one of the probably thousands of books off the shelves to check. She did glance at a couple, though—just enough to see, in ink, the poorly-drawn sketches of Child’s Magic runes. The Old Alemic cantrips were faded, the pages yellowing, and not one of the books she looked at included more than one or two First Order spells.

“These are all spellbooks,” Roth said after a minute. “We’re searching a few thousand Y’aer-cursed children’s spellbooks for what?”

“The Water War, Notes and Fragments: Willowsong’s Sonnets—that one’s in Emerald Script, and The Love Letters of Dame Brandwell and Duke Iverly,” Colin recited without digging up the list.

“None of those are spellbooks, Colin.”

“You’re right, Roth. Eola, we need to check the right side.” Patrice shut the spellbook in her hand and shoved it back on the shelf. Eola was pretty sure that she’d misshelved it, but it wasn’t worth the argument. “If we’re smart about it, we should be able to cross that roped-off section, right?”

“Right,” Eola said, but she stared at the velvet barrier like it was a dagger pointed right at her. Atta gave her a disbelieving look. Nevertheless, she ducked under the ropes and stepped past the sign warning about ensorcelled tiles. She’d just said they needed to stay away from it, and now here she was, pushing into it as if her life depended on it.

Her life didn’t depend on it, of course, but her parents’ lives? They might. She needed to pass this test, and so far, she hadn’t done anything notable to help her teammates. The puzzle in her head kept turning over, but she wasn’t making any progress with her modifying marks, and now she had to add Varin’s journal to the mix.

The others crossed into the ensorcelled section. “The book’s got to be around here somewhere,” Patrice said.

The floor vanished and they fell through it.

Eola screamed as, for the second time this year, she plunged through the third floor’s Mana-heavy air.

Atta twisted mid-air, shooting her an ‘I told you so’ look.

Colin started casting something—a ritual, most likely—but there was no way he’d finish before—

The four apprentices and their familiars hit the floor below in a tangle. More accurately, they hit a long, curving, leather couch that wrapped itself around a room below the lobby they’d been exploring. Eola disentangled herself from the pile, checking for broken bones. Nothing, but she’d have some monstrous bruises all across her side, from shoulder to bottom, when they got out of here.

If they got out of here, that was.

“This place is awful,” Roth moaned.

“You can say that again. At least my trinkets took the worst of it,” Patrice said.

Eola took stock. She was right. Her magical defenses had done good work. She looked around, smallsword in hand and wand at the ready.

Then she swore. “Y’aer’s curse...”

Mana Mites were, quietly, one of the most dangerous monsters on the third floor. They were the size of a single coin, with tiny fangs that couldn’t even pierce bare skin. Silvery shells, six legs, and no eyes or ears—they didn’t need them, instead relying on a sixth sense that was a lot harder to trick. Mana Mites could detect fluctuations in the ambient Mana around them, and could hone in on it unerringly. There were a few other predators with similar abilities, but unlike Cortrices, Mana Mites relied only on their sixth sense, and unlike Attunement Flowers, they were mobile.

In small numbers, they were a pest. In fact, Eola’s suite had a handful; she routinely killed them when she found them, but they weren’t worth the effort to have another student cast the necessary trap spells to get eradicate them as long as she and her suitemates kept the population in check. But if they swarmed…if they swarmed, things changed. Instructor Clearance’s lectures hadn’t been about the individual silver-colored bugs. It had been about how their behavior shifted when they grouped together—how they went from scavengers to predators when they hit large enough numbers.

And, of course, they’d dropped right into a swarm of them. A big one.

Eola barely noticed the shredded shelves around them or the tattered, empty book covers littering the floor. Her side hurt, but she ignored it. She grabbed Colin’s arm and yanked; he yelped in pain but got up. “We need to leave. Now!”

But how?

There was no way out of the room they’d fallen into, and already, Eola felt the Mana in her soul coming under assault as the swarm approached. She backed down a narrow row of books. The shelves glowed a purple-blue; dozens of unweeded books had started to go feral. Patrice and Roth ran to catch up, then pass her. Colin was already casting, sketching his fire ritual in the air at the end of the hallway.

That’d help. It wouldn’t be enough, though. There had to be hundreds of Mana Mites. Maybe even thousands.

“They’re…I can feel them eating my Mana,” Roth said. He looked as sick as Eola felt. So did Colin. He was actively casting, and even though he was farther away, using his Mana made him stick out like a sore thumb. Patrice looked terrible; she had more of her Mana left than any of them, and they were probably targeting her.

They had to keep moving.

Around a corner, then down another dark hallway. Colin’s ritual finished, and flames vomited down the hallway they’d just left. The pressure on Eola’s soul lessened, then slowly grew again as more mites pressed into the burning gap. A feral book emerged from a shelf. She and Patrice attacked it, and a whip-like modified Spark Shot finished it off at the cost of Mana she couldn’t afford to spend. The swarm followed, a tide of shining gray Mana Mites eager to feed.

Then, Eola saw it. A stairwell.

“Go!” she yelled. Her friends broke into a staggering sprint. She turned to follow them. Something caught her eye, and she sheathed her smallsword. Her hands closed on the spine of a book, and she pulled it off the shelf. The pressure on her soul grew, and an unplaceable pain rippled across her as her Mana turned into the mites’ meal.

Her feet caught the bottom of the stairs, and Patrice grabbed her arm as the stairwell moved under her. “Got you! What took you so long?”

Eola didn’t say anything, but in her hand, she held Notes and Fragments: Willowsong’s Sonnets. She’d known just enough Emerald Script thanks to her translation work earlier in the year to recognize the title. It even had most of its pages, and only one chunk torn out of the hard, oversized cover.

“This counts, right? I’m not going back for a different copy.” She handed it to Colin. He added it to his overflowing satchel without complaining.

Then she promptly vomited as her stomach rebelled from her empty soul and the mites’ horrible, violating assault on it. She was out of Mana.

The stairwell brought an exhausted Eola—whose mind was on performing the ‘purification’ process as best she could and refreshing the Mana in her soul—and her friends to the largest room they’d found. She’d found herself leaning on Colin a little more than she wanted to, but he didn’t complain, and she was too busy refilling her soul to stop.

An entire section of a library, study rooms, work desks, and shelves, had been lifted away and put here. Sconces covered the walls, with actual candles lighting the space instead of the Bright Balls she and her friends were used to. The flickering lights cast shadows up walls three or even four floors high, with tapestries hanging from bars between the thin shelves, but no walls behind them. Rolling ladders lined the walls, and other than the tiled first story’s tile, every walkway was made of polished wood and beams sticking out from nothing.

They’d arrived in the middle of a pile of comfortable-looking chairs and cubicles for people to read without bothering each other, while all around them, a half-dozen doors on the first floor offered even more private study rooms.

“Any chance this room’s one we need?” Patrice asked. She yawned. “It’d be nice to wrap this up soon.”

Colin nodded quietly. “It would.” As he skimmed the parchment, he shook his head. “We’ve gotten Recorded Musings of Apostle Antoinnene, A Briefe Historie of the Alemic Tongue, and Notes and Fragments: Willowsong’s Sonnets. That leaves The Love Letters of Dame Brandwell and Duke Iverly and The Water War. Both are in small rooms. The first has a fountain with two nude gnomes dancing—awkward—and the second is a crescent-shaped room with entrances at either side.”

“Y’aer save me! I need a nap.”

“You always need a nap, Patrice,” Colin said, but he looked sympathetic. Eola felt much the same. Their encounter with the Mana Mites had taken a lot out of her, and she was more than ready to be done with this, even if her soul was feeling a bit more full. She probably had five or six bars of Mana.

“Do you think we’re at least close?” Patrice whined.

“Yes. We’re close,” Colin said. “Let’s, uh, find some stairwells and keep moving?”

“Sounds like a plan,” Eola said. Her stomach growled. She stared at Colin’s satchel for a moment. If she’d been smart, she would have packed snacks. Of course, if she had, there was a good chance they’d all be eaten by now.

She climbed up one of the rolling ladders, stepped onto the second floor’s wooden walkway, and started walking the room in a long circuit. The sooner they were moving again, the better—her legs were starting to get tired from climbing up and down the dozens of floating stairwells, and they still had two books to go for the exam.

Atta hissed. Her fur stood on end. “What is it?” Eola asked. The cat had been jumpy for a while now. In fact, ever since they’d returned to the room with Varin’s journal, she’d been on edge, nervous, and constantly checking over her shoulder.

“Something’s here.”

“What’s here?”

“I’m not sure,” her familiar said, voice soft and low. “Whatever it is, it’s been following us for a while, but it’s kept its distance. Now that we’re here, though…you should gather your friends, girl.”

Eola didn’t miss the omission of either ‘stupid’ or ‘silly,’ but before she could respond, she caught a purple glow reflecting off a brass plate on the shelves, and spun, sword up in First Guard.

And a familiar set of claws hammered into the blade, inches from her face.
