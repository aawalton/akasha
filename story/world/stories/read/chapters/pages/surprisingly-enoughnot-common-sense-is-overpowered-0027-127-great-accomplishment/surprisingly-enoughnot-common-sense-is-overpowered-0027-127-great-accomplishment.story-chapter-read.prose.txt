Wearing a straw hat, Ning knelt behind the plot of land.

He was currently planting four acres of basic spiritual grain. For now, he planned to dedicate an acre to Frozen Breath Grain. Once he became more proficient, he could gradually convert all the crops to Frozen Breath Grain, greatly increasing his yield and income.

Ning gently brushed the soil with his hands. He opened the small satchel containing the seeds, their crystalline husks glinting faintly in the sunlight. Each seed radiated a faint chill.

"Alright," he muttered to himself. "Let's do this properly."

He began the meticulous process. First, he lightly tilled the soil, loosening it without disturbing the spiritual energy too much. Then, he sprinkled a mixture of crushed used spirit stones, cinnabar wood ash, and composted spirit-beast manure, exactly as the guide had instructed. The soil absorbed it eagerly, its spiritual energy brightening subtly under his careful touch.

Planting the seeds was no simple task. Each one needed to be positioned at a precise depth and spacing to allow its ice attributes to flourish. Ning worked slowly, measuring, counting, and double-checking every placement. His fingers, nimble from experience, made the process smooth and precise.

Once the seeds were in the soil, he stepped back and surveyed his work. Every seed was perfectly positioned; every inch of soil infused with energy.

Now came the most crucial part: nurturing the seeds with the Small Cloud Rain Technique.

This spell was the foundation for any competent spiritual farmer. It called upon the spiritual energy gathered by the formation and guided it into rain infused with Qi. The better one controlled it, the more refined and potent the rainfall became. At its peak, it could nourish acres of crops and even work as a fertilizer.

Ning took a deep breath, centering his spiritual energy. Normally, casting this spell required a lengthy incantation:

“Spiritual essence of heaven and water, gather upon my call…”

Ning had repeated those words enough times that he was thoroughly sick of them.

More importantly, he no longer needed them.

Great Accomplishment represented more than simply becoming better at casting a spell. At that level, a cultivator understood the technique well enough to begin adapting it to themselves, simplifying certain steps or altering the casting process without weakening the final result.

For Ning, the incantation was the obvious place to start.

Words were only a tool used to guide the mind. If he could reproduce the same mental process without speaking them, then there was no reason to keep reciting an entire paragraph every time he wanted it to rain.

So Ning replaced the words with something simpler.

Images.

In his mind, the chant became a simple equation: Moisture + Spiritual Qi = Cloud Formation. Each line of the incantation became a visual trigger, a hand seal, a breath pattern, a mental cue. Where others needed a full sentence, Ning required only a brief thought. With the countless hours he had spent on this technique and his improved mental stability due to the inner demon, this became possible.

The effect was immediate.

Thin wisps of vapor coiled above his palms, gathering faster than ever before. The spiritual energy in the air responded eagerly, like how Goku pulls up suddenly when hearing someone is strong.

The chant continued silently in his mind, rhythmic and precise. Qi flowed upward, and the air shimmered faintly. Wisps turned into fog, fog into cloud. Within moments, a gray mass hung over the field, dense with spiritual energy.

Ning formed the final seal, pressing two fingers together.

“Small Cloud Rain Technique.”

A soft drizzle began to fall, droplets glimmering faintly with spiritual light as they sank into the freshly tilled earth. Beneath the soil, the newly planted seeds quietly absorbed the spiritual essence.

But Ning didn’t stop there.

He refined the circulation further, feeding more Qi into the clouds. The drizzle thickened into a steady rainfall, rippling outward like a heartbeat, ten meters, thirty, fifty… a hundred.

As Ning maintained the technique, he gradually noticed something different.

The spell felt easier.

The individual steps that had once required conscious effort now flowed together naturally. He no longer needed to deliberately think about gathering moisture, guiding the qi, forming the clouds, and maintaining the rainfall as separate processes. After using the technique countless times, he had made those steps so familiar that he could adjust them almost instinctively.

Ning experimented a little, adjusting the cloud density and shifting rainfall from one part of the field to another. The response was noticeably smoother than before.

A smile slowly appeared on his face.

He understood.

This was Great Accomplishment.

Only then did Ning glance at his panel.

[Small Cloud Rain Technique: Great Accomplishment (0/400)]

As expected.

“Finally. My first spell at Great Accomplishment.”

It was fitting. Of all the spells he had learned, this was the one he had practiced the most.

Now, he could finally start taking advantage of that mastery.

“Now, we have less than four years until the Outer Sect Competition,” Wei Zhusang announced. “Anyone who ranks among the top ten can enter the inner sect immediately, even without reaching the usual cultivation requirement. So let’s work hard, everyone.”

“Yes!” The others responded.

Ever since Wei Zhusang and the others had become outer sect disciples, the association’s meetings had been reduced from once every fifteen days to once a month. Everyone had their own cultivation, missions, and responsibilities now, and finding time when all of them were available had naturally become more difficult.

Most of the association’s members were still at the third stage of Qi Condensation, however, and as Ning looked around, he could already feel the subtle difference his advancement had made.

A few members who had barely spoken to him before were noticeably warmer now, while others became more respectful the moment they saw his outer sect robes. Aside from Wei Zhusang, Zhang Feng, Lang Rulang, and Su Fan, Ning had been one of the fastest among them to reach the mid stage of Qi Condensation. Since most of them possessed similar spiritual roots, his steady progress naturally stood out.

No wonder every xianxia protagonist is so desperate to raise their cultivation, Ning mused.

Protagonists in those stories usually started at the absolute bottom, mocked by relatives, servants, random passersby, and occasionally people who seemed to have woken up that morning with the sole purpose of insulting them. After enduring that for years, watching the same people suddenly become respectful-or outright terrified-the moment their cultivation rose must have been incredibly satisfying.

Perhaps repeated face-slapping really was beneficial to the Dao Heart.

Ning, fortunately, had never suffered from that particular problem. He wasn't especially close to most members of the association due to his frequent absences, but he wasn't isolated either. He was a chill guy. If someone wanted to talk to him, he would talk. If they ignored him, he would simply go about his day.

Once the meeting ended, Ning quickly made his way toward the Wei trio. If he waited too long, Wei Zhusang's suitors or one of the other disciples would inevitably occupy their attention.

“Brother Wei, Brother Zhang, Sister Lang.” Ning approached the three. “I wanted to return part of the spirit stones I borrowed from you.”

“Hm? Already?” Zhang Feng asked in surprise. He knew exactly how much they had lent Ning.

“Yes, though I can only return half for now.”

Ning had no intention of taking their generosity for granted. The three of them might not care when he repaid the debt, but relationships needed some degree of balance. Accepting help when he needed it was one thing; making no effort to repay it when he was finally able to was another.

“But you might still need those spirit stones,” Zhang Feng began.

“No need, Feng.” Wei Zhusang interrupted him. “If Brother Ning is ready to repay them, then we should accept.”

He then turned toward Ning with a smile. “And congratulations on reaching the fourth stage so quickly.”

Wei Zhusang was genuinely impressed. Ning didn't have the resources or family backing that the three of them enjoyed, yet his cultivation had progressed steadily. The fact that his first thought after earning some spirit stones was to repay his debts only improved Wei Zhusang's opinion of him.

“It’s thanks in part to your help,” Ning replied with a smile.

Zhang Feng and Wei Zhusang smiled back, while Lang Rulang merely gave her usual hmph.

Ning had long since grown accustomed to her attitude.

“I'll repay the rest when I can.”

He handed fifty spirit stones to each of them.

His mission at the Scripture Pavilion had earned him a hundred spirit stones. It wasn't much considering the mission had come from a Foundation Building elder, but Elder Shen practically lived paycheck to paycheck and spent most of it on booze.

Combined with the income from his latest harvest, Ning finally had enough to repay half his debt while still keeping some spirit stones aside for emergencies.

Besides, he wasn't in a hurry to clear everything at once. A small debt between friends wasn't necessarily a bad thing; as long as neither side abused it, it was another thread connecting them.

“Oh, and I made some cakes as thanks.” Ning produced several small boxes. “Please don't refuse these.”

“I won't.” Zhang Feng accepted his immediately. “Those cakes you made last time were pretty unique. I've been wanting more.”

When Ning had originally borrowed the spirit stones, he hadn't gone empty-handed either.

“I'll accept as well,” Wei Zhusang said, taking his box.

Then all three looked toward Lang Rulang.

She hesitated.

Ning already knew she liked his cakes, but apparently admitting that openly remained incompatible with whatever prideful swordswoman image she had constructed for herself.

Under everyone's gaze, Lang Rulang finally took the box.

“I'll consider it interest.”

The others laughed.

Before Lang Rulang could become annoyed, Ning wisely changed the subject.

“By the way, have any of you seen Brother Su Fan? There's something I wanted to discuss with him.”

Wei Zhusang immediately seemed to understand. “You've reached Great Accomplishment in the Small Cloud Rain Technique?”

“Yes.” Ning nodded. “I was lucky.”

“As expected, your talent with spells really isn't ordinary.” Wei Zhusang's eyes brightened.

“That's amazing,” Zhang Feng added. He himself had yet to bring any spell to Great Accomplishment.

Even Lang Rulang looked at Ning with visible surprise.

Reaching Great Accomplishment in a little over a year was impressive by any reasonable standard.

Ning merely smiled. He didn't mind letting them know about his progress. His talent with spells might be noteworthy, but the three of them had backgrounds and resources far beyond his own, so there was little reason to worry about provoking envy. If anything, showing that he was worth investing in would only strengthen their relationship.

“Unfortunately,” Wei Zhusang continued, “I heard Senior Brother Su Fan left on a mission with his sister. We haven't seen him recently. If I manage to contact him, I'll tell him you're looking for him.”

“Then I'll have to trouble Brother Wei.”

Ning cupped his hands in thanks.

Later, at home, Ning held a jade slip in his hand, the one given to him by the Elder.

Infusing it with Qi, he unlocked the contents.

He had a lingering suspicion about the so-called “supernatural powers,” and now he wanted to confirm it.

The slip contained details similar to what the elder had explained, followed by a ranked list of supernatural powers. The stronger the power, the higher its position.

Ning scanned the first few entries.

[Eyes of Heaven]: See through Yin and Yang. Those born with this power can learn spells and martial arts at a single glance. Possessors often lead their era, their insight allowing them to surpass others quickly. It is said they can even control space and time.

[Supreme Bones]: Grants inborn divine skills and abilities. The wielder can use these powers anytime, anywhere, unrestricted by cultivation level or spiritual energy. Such individuals excel in combat, even against far stronger foes. These bones are said to be the legacies of ancient cultivators.

"As expected of a cultivation world, these powers are ridiculous," Ning murmured. The elder had warned that most descriptions were exaggerated, but there was surely some truth to them.

He continued reading.

[Void Tongue]: Allows communication with spiritual beasts and even elemental spirits. With this ability, one can negotiate, tame, or summon creatures with ease, often turning the tide of battle.

[Bodhi Mind]: Greatly enhances comprehension. Such people are born wise and grasp truths instinctively. They can master any technique with ease.

Finally, around the twentieth entry, Ning found the supernatural power of the sect leader.

[Sword Bones and Intent]: Born to wield a sword. Can learn most sword techniques with half the effort and double the effectiveness. Possesses a unique sword intent even at the Qi Condensation stage, enabling cross-realm combat. Doubles cultivation speed for sword-related techniques. Sword intent also aids in resisting inner demons. Allows the possessor to develop their own unique sword heart.

"No wonder the sect leader sounds so badass," Ning muttered. "This power really does make someone unrivaled in their realm."

He scrolled further, reaching more modest powers.

[Ape Bones and Snake Tendons]: Increases a cultivator’s physical strength and flexibility, making them suitable for body refinement arts.[Quick]: Doubles movement speed.[Four Arms]: Grants four fully functional arms.

Unlike the higher-ranked powers, these could be imitated through martial arts or spells.

Finally, in an inconspicuous corner, Ning found one that caught his attention.

[Know Thyself]: Grants real-time awareness of one’s physical and spiritual condition. Seems to have some effect on maintaining one's spirit.
