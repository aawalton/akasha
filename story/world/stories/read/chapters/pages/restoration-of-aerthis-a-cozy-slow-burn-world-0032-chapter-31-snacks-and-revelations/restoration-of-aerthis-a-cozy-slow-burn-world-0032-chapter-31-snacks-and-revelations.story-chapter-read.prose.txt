The day began with pancakes!

Actual, fluffy, sweet pancakes!

Still warm, stacked too high on too small a plate, with syrup that tasted faintly of cinnamon and that weird fruit she had recently from the market stall.

Elynara blinked at the sight of them, halfway through the hall entrance, and only believed it was real when Pufflet nearly headbutted her thigh trying to leap onto the bench beside her.

Mina beamed from across the table.

“Father Alaric said we deserved a reward.”

Elynara didn’t argue. Why would she?

Instead, she simply devoured the first two in five bites total, enjoying the blissful sensation. She actually stopped to chew the next few bites, before eating the rest at a much more measured pace.

They were really good. And Elynara regretted not having more to eat right away. But she had work to get to.

And the spark of joy the food gave her making the day already feel like a blessing.

Even if the errands that followed didn’t quite match the magic of breakfast.

But at least they passed quickly. A wood shipment needed checking, a flock of confused birds had taken to nesting on the bakery’s awning – nothing a bit of magic couldn’t fix, and someone lost a key in the vegetable garden again. She retrieved it after a thirty-minute dig through beet roots and not one, but two surprise toads.

By late morning, she returned to the guild with dirt on her sleeves and a mostly empty stomach again.

Alicia was at her usual post, a half-dozen papers already stacked in order. She raised her eyes as Elynara approached, slid a fresh slip across the counter, then waited just long enough to make sure no one else was listening.

“I have something for your ears only,” she said quietly.

Elynara paused, her curiosity piqued.

“Yes?”

“Not now,” Alicia replied, sharp but calm. “Tomorrow. Come early.”

“What is it?”

The question barely left her mouth before Alicia’s expression closed up again like a well-sealed envelope.

“Tomorrow.”

Not a flicker of extra information. Not even a twitch of amusement.

Elynara frowned, tapping the edge of the paper in her hand.

“That’s it? You’re just going to leave me hanging like that?”

“Yes.”

And that was that.

Elynara actually stood and stared at Alicia for a good moment before shaking herself into action. Namely – more pestering!

But nothing worked. Hardly even made a dent. And Alicia came to simply ignoring her asks after the third one.

Fine then, she will wait. And try to figure out what the smug receptionist is hiding this time.

And just then Alicia signed off the errand slip, handed her a second biscuit from the mysterious snack supply under the desk, and returned to sorting requests like the conversation had never even happened.

The gall of that woman!

Elynara stepped outside again with half a biscuit in her mouth and her brain turning over half-formed theories.

Maybe it was a new job. Maybe a big one.

Or maybe something weird was going on and Alicia wanted to drag her into it with maximum suspense.

She never would have suspected her to be into theatrics either.

…It would feel better to learn that if it weren’t at her own expense though.

Either way, she was hooked.

The rest of the afternoon blurred into routine. She cleared two tasks, waved to Marcus as he passed through the guild on his way out again, and finally made her way back to the church with aching arms and a stomach rumbling louder than Starpuff’s hover buzz.

Dinner that night was thick vegetable stew with bread and a little salted cheese.

She ate slowly, more out of habit than hunger. Most of the children were already getting quiet by then, either tucked into corners with toys or finishing up their meals with sleepy energy.

Mina sat by the wall brushing Pufflet’s fur with a worn cloth. The little animal twitched with every pass but didn’t move away.

Elynara smiled, finished the last of her bread, and carried her plate to the washroom before retreating upstairs.

In the quiet of her corner, she finally sat down and opened her notebook.

Her daily tally – which she started doing after getting back to Oltenar – came first.

Six jobs today. Nothing major. Out of them, only the mysteriously dripping ceiling was worth noting.

Which really just turned out to be a bucket of water hidden for “revenge purposes” by a seven-year-old with too much free time.

Elynara smirked at her finding there.

The other requests weren't quite so fun – or glorious.

However, they came with unexpected benefits.

The System’s blinking dot told her as such.

Fitness +1

Huh.

A stat increase that wasn’t in mana?

Elynara was weirdly excited about that. It was proof it was possible. And already she could feel her body ‘lightening up’ a little.

She smiled, her thoughts turning to weird places.

Lightening up, yes, but at what cost?

If all it took to gain Fitness was hauling endless wood and goats around, she was going to end up as Oltenar’s first bodybuilder mage!

‘Fire Queen with Abs,’ whispered in taverns everywhere. Wonderful. Absolutely not what she asked for. Though knowing her friends, they would be thoroughly entertained by the idea.

Then – very briefly – Elynara considered what led to that gain, before dispelling the nonsense.

…She did an awful lot of walking and lifting heavy things recently, didn’t she?

It really was just that simple, and silly to ponder over.

Really!

That grand ‘mystery’ solved, she turned to her next target.

The drawings.

Elynara turned to the page she had marked the night before and studied the spirals again. Circles, corkscrews, double loops. Starpuff had been trailing them in the air more frequently now. Not random like before.

Patterns.

Intentional ones.

She had tried to copy a few down earlier but gave up after the fourth attempt started to look like a noodle vortex. It was harder than expected.

But she would get better, eventually. It just needed some time and effort.

And now, under dim light and with the room mostly still, she drew slower.

One simple loop at a time.

The shapes weren’t spells, at least not the kind she recognized. But they weren’t nonsense either.

She chewed the end of her pencil.

It was too precise for nonsense, and too nonsense for spells.

Which meant… what exactly?

Secret code? Puffball mathematics? Inter-sprite dance language?

If she solved this mystery, she would either come another step closer to understanding the world of magic… or discover her companion’s lifelong hobby was drawing noodles in the air.

She hoped not. It felt too purposeful to mean nothing.

…Probably.

Or Starpuff was just messing with her, the little bully.

It floated innocently at her side – pretending it wasn’t watching. And likely seeking an opportunity to boop her on the nose as suddenly as it possibly could.

Or so she assumed when it accidentally planted itself into a wall while circling her. Then immediately pretended like it totally meant to do just that before resuming its loops around her.

Silly puff!

She glanced at it sideways.

“You know I can see you, right?”

The sprite trilled softly and spun once in place, then curled lazily around itself like a ribbon unwinding.

Elynara narrowed her eyes and tried to flick it lightly.

The puffball dodged without effort and wobbled away just out of reach.

“Cheeky.”

She leaned back against the wall, pencil tapping lightly against the edge of her book.

And then her thoughts drifted back to Alicia’s words.

‘Something for your ears only.’

That phrasing wouldn’t leave her alone. It was far too deliberate and weighty. Though she had little idea what it could really be. If it were just another request, why the mystery?

Elynara couldn’t tell if she was excited or anxious at this point.

Probably both.

She closed the notebook and set it beside her bedroll, tucking the pencil between the pages. Starpuff settled at her side, nudging her slowly, shifting in small pulses of color in a rhythm she wasn’t quite meant to follow.

Elynara curled her arms beneath her head and stared at the ceiling.

Tomorrow couldn’t come fast enough.

…But when it did, Alicia chose to be a bully again!

Elynara marched into the guild with her notebook under one arm, spine straight and expression set. Her steps clipped across the floor, her boots tapping with intent.

The line she had practiced all morning perched at the edge of her tongue – sharp, direct, impossible to wriggle out of.

…Yet Alicia didn’t even give her the chance to utter it.

Without so much as looking up, the woman slid a stack of requests across the counter as soon as Elynara drew nearby. The parchment landed with a clean tap.

“…Really?”

“Really,” Alicia said, already scribbling something down in the logbook.

Elynara stared at the stack. It wasn’t even big. Just five slips. No bulk bundle, no folded-in lecture, nor even a suspicious footnote. She narrowed her eyes.

“Too easy,” she muttered.

Still, her fingers closed around the stack with practiced ease. She turned on her heel and headed for the door.

“I’m onto you, woman.”

Alicia didn’t respond, probably on purpose.

Alright then, she would let Alicia keep her secrets.

For now.

Elynara marched back out, Starpuff following, any thoughts of the mystery behind her now. She had requests to do, and idle thoughts were a distraction she didn’t need.

The first request she got to was to help deliver smoked fish to the bakery.

She double-checked it, in case her eyes had made a mistake. Fish. For a baker. She sniffed the wrapped bundle out of reflex. Smelled decent, actually.

The baker looked just as baffled when she handed it over.

“Did someone mix up the slips again?” he asked.

“Nope. Yours.”

He shrugged and tucked the package under his counter like this wasn’t the oddest thing he had ever received.

Aaaand that’s one request done.

Her next job was hauling kindling across three streets for an older widow who absolutely insisted she didn’t need help, then gave Elynara two slices of sweetbread for the effort. Starpuff tried to steal a nibble out of one immediately.

She denied it. It was her snack, earned by right. A reward from her own two hands doing the work, all while Starpuff happily chased after itself, floating merrily.

It bonked her head in retaliation and pouted from a streetlamp for three whole minutes.

…Elynara was pretty sure it nibbled some off anyways when she wasn’t looking.

Not that she minded. But it was fun to mess with it sometimes.

Then came the goat request.

Weird.

But as she was learning by now – a lot of adventurer requests were weird.

She shrugged and trudged on.

At least it promised to be fun, if nothing else.

The problem with it became apparent as soon as she reached the request location

Apparently, the goat had decided a barrel of apples at a back porch storeroom was now its lifelong treasure. Elynara coaxed it off the porch using a trail of fruit slices, then got dragged across half the street when it decided to charge back and reclaim its bounty.

A younger boy helped her catch it the second time.

“Third time this week,” he said cheerfully, gripping the goat’s horns.

“You all just let it roam?”

“It’s got opinions!”

…Right.

By the time she wrangled it back to its pen, her arms ached and her braid had come loose.

Yet somehow a smile would not leave her lips.

The work kept her moving.

And was weirdly fun.

Even pleasant at times, if exhausting.

Elynara still caught herself bristling now and then – especially when she remembered Alicia’s clipped tone – but most of the irritation drifted off as the sun climbed higher.

Early afternoon light spilled warm across the cobbles. A few clouds tugged at the sky, low and pale, but the breeze carried the scent of baked bread and clean water.

She enjoyed it for a while, just taking in the pleasant smells as she relaxed between the jobs.

It wasn’t a bad day.

But Elynara still didn’t have her answer yet!

So when she returned to the guild, sweat drying on her collar and dust smudged down one side of her sleeve, her patience had thinned to thread.

She pushed open the door and made straight for the counter, requests already tucked under her arm.

Except, Alicia wasn’t there.

Marcus was.

He looked up from the rack of half-filed reports and raised an eyebrow.

“She’s in the back,” he said before she could ask.

Elynara gave a tight nod and waited, arms crossed.

After a moment, he added,

“You hungry?”

“I’m fine.”

“You look like a winded sparrow.”

“I said I’m fine.”

He shrugged, then passed her a wrapped bun from behind the desk anyway. She didn’t refuse.

And when Alicia returned a few minutes later, there was no new stack in her hands.

Just the same look she would recognize anywhere – professional, composed, and utterly unreadable.

Elynara stepped up.

“You said you had something for my ears only.”

Alicia nodded.

“I still do. But not yet. Tomorrow.”

“You already said that yesterday.”

“And you came back, didn’t you?”

Elynara opened her mouth. Closed it.

And with no clever retort at that simple admission, simply took a bite of the bun and chewed.

Starpuff landed on her shoulder and flopped across it, pretending to be melting.

She stared at Alicia for another full breath, then turned and walked out.

The sun had dipped slightly, and the shadows stretched longer than before. She made her way back to the church, weaving between stalls and nodding to a familiar merchant who waved a skewer of grilled vegetables in her direction.

The noise in the streets faded once she crossed under the stone arch. She entered quietly, dropped her satchel by her cot, and joined the others for the evening meal without fuss.

Later, once the children had drifted to their blankets and the hall settled into its usual hum, she opened her notebook again. Her fingers traced the edge of the paper. Today’s entry was brief, with just a few job notes, a mention of the goat again, and a small sketch of the pattern Starpuff had flown earlier.

A double spiral this time.

She watched it floating in the air beside her, blinking innocently.

“You’re not helping. Really.”

Starpuff spun once, slowly, then zoomed up right up to her face! She tensed up at that.

Then it simply settled just above her shoulder.

This prankster!

Elynara closed her eyes. Inhaled slowly. Exhaled slower.

Then tapped her pencil once against the page.

Alicia’s voice still echoed behind her thoughts. But she really was no closer to solving a mystery she had no clues to. The only thing she knew was…

Tomorrow.

Always tomorrow!

Elynara exhaled, shut the notebook, and leaned back against her bedding.

Fine.

She would wait one more day.

Elynara pushed open the guild door, staff in hand, and ready to demand justice.

Then froze halfway through the threshold.

Marcus stood near the far table, arms crossed, a grin already spreading across his face like it had been waiting there all morning.

Alicia sat with perfect posture at the counter, hands folded. And between them, on the polished wood, rested–

“Snacks?”

And not just a single plate either. That Elynara might have still expected.

Instead, it was an arrangement full of them.

Sweet biscuits dusted in sugar, dried fruits glistening in shallow bowls, small nut-cakes drizzled in amber glaze. There was a jug of juice beside them, already sweating slightly in the morning warmth, with three cups set out.

Her righteous fury stalled. Just a little bit.

Elynara looked from the treats to Alicia’s face. That far too calm face. Too composed. She was definitely planning something. And worst of all, she had her hooked.

“You can’t bribe me with food.”

Alicia slid one of the cups closer.

“Juice?”

Elynara hesitated. Then took the cup. Then a sip. Then another. Marcus very gentlemanly slid over a piece of honey-treat snack for her to pair up with juice.

No. She wouldn’t give in. She would stand tall. She would not let sugar defeat her.

Except the glaze smelled like roasted nuts. And the jug of juice had condensation sliding down like a siren call. She hated how her fingers moved before she even told them to. And how Alicia definitely noticed.

“…Darn it.”

Starpuff dove across the counter, shining excited brightly in her magic vision. It latched onto a nut-cake and started nibbling with contentment.

All smug and satisfaction.

With none of the guilt!

Marcus chuckled.

“Guess she’s got you cornered there.”

“I’m not cornered,” Elynara muttered, already reaching for a cookie. She took a bite, then narrowed her eyes. “You made these, didn’t you?”

“Of course,” Alicia replied.

It was almost insulting how good they were. Light crunch, soft center, just the right amount of sweetness. She hated how much she liked them.

She pouted harder.

Alicia leaned forward, her voice softening.

“Thank you, Elynara. For everything you’ve done these past days. The requests. The extra work. You have helped keep things running while the guild was spread thin.”

The shift in tone landed harder than she expected.

And she probably did a poor job of hiding it, given Alicia’s growing smirk.

Elynara glanced down at her half-eaten cookie, then at her ‘tomorrow’ adversary’s face again.

“…Oh.”

“We’ve managed to catch up. Most of the adventurers are either back or expected to return soon now.”

Marcus nodded.

“Quite a few rolled in late last night. Smelled like swamp and looked even worse.”

“So,” Alicia said, fingers tapping a new stack of parchment, “those were your last errands yesterday. You’re officially off duty.”

Elynara blinked.

“Wait. Off duty?”

“Your next assignment,” Alicia continued, “is to rest.”

She stared at her.

“What?”

Marcus snorted.

“You heard her.”

“You’re telling me I came all the way here for snacks and a vacation?”

“Exactly that,” Alicia confirmed, utterly serene.

Elynara’s mouth opened, then closed again. She glared between the two of them.

“This is your revenge, isn’t it. For when I made Marcus apologize to Mina.”

“I would never stoop to that,” Alicia said without blinking.

The twinkle in her eyes told another story.

Elynara sat down, utterly defeated, letting her staff rest against her knees.

“You’re enjoying this far too much.”

“We are,” Marcus said.

Alicia didn’t deny it. She took a sip of her own juice, then set the cup aside and pulled one final slip from the folder.

“Of course, there is one more thing.”

Elynara leaned forward at that.

But it was like they were just waiting for her to become invested. The twinkle in Alicia’s eyes confirmed it. She enjoyed watching her squirm.

And when she finally deigned to explain, she did so with deliberate calm to boot.

Ugh!

“The adventurers who just returned were on a long emergency assignment. They won’t be leaving again soon. Which means–” she tapped a parchment folder, “–there is work that only you can do now.”

Elynara straightened, staff across her knees.

“Work?”

“Out of town,” Alicia confirmed. “Villages further out. Nothing dire, but requests need to be done. And it’s a perfect chance to check on the spirits along the way.”

Her heart kicked faster at that revelation.

She would get to draw an even bigger map. And have plenty of new places to visit, search, help, and learn about. If nothing else, it would be an opportunity to see more of the world.

Who knew what she would find?

“But most importantly–” Alicia’s smile sharpened as she slid the final parchment forward. “The furthest village reported something unusual. Ruins. Hidden, deep in the forest, right at the edge of the Deadzone.”

Ruins? Her fingers twitched over her notebook.

Marcus folded his arms.

“Not your average old stones either.”

“The only ruins in those parts,” Alicia said, “would belong to the magitech civilization. Centuries dead.”

Elynara’s breath caught at the revelation.

Magitech ruins from a centuries dead civilization? She read nothing about that so far. But it wasn’t surprising. The collection of books Oltenar library had to offer wasn’t all too varied. Or even particularly organized.

She would know.

Still, her giddiness burst like sparks in her chest, utterly impossible to hide. She practically squeaked at the possibilities.

Not that she had any clue of what to expect there.

Marcus laughed. Alicia leaned back, smug as ever. They were watching her face like hawks, savoring every ounce of her reaction.

She tried to scowl at them, yet failed miserably.

“…You two are the worst.”

They smirked at her harder.

“I’ll need time to pack,” she said quickly. “And I’ll need a fresh notebook. And pencils. And–”

Marcus laughed.

Alicia smiled, lips curling.

“You leave in three days.”

Starpuff zipped upward in a sharp spiral, light pulsing green and gold as it twisted midair.

Elynara grinned wide.

“You two really are the worst.”

“No,” Alicia said, still perfectly smug. “We’re just well-prepared.”

Elynara bit into another cookie, too full of giddiness to argue further. Her foot tapped against the leg of her chair as the tasty treat worked its wonders. It kept her fingers from immediately reaching for her pencil just that little bit longer.

She would need to prepare to expand her map tonight.

And maybe bring more juice.

The village better have ruins worth the walk.

And as she looked up, intent on getting another snack – perhaps of those honeyed nuts – she caught them staring once more.

They were both watching her still, waiting for the next outburst. Her scowl didn’t last.

She was far too excited to be mad.
