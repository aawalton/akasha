There was an enormous cracking sound, followed by an equally enormous plume of dust that erupted from our point of impact. The only thing visible through the cloud, even to me, were flashes of bright yellow lightning and motes of rapidly dissipating, crimson red thorns. The entire Heartspire seemed to shiver in the aftermath of our strike, and for the first time in a while, the Calamity’s heart beat.

A shower of blood from the enormous punctured heart rained down along the sides of the tower. When it reached us, it weakened the cloud of dust, causing it to dissipate and reveal the fruits of our labor.

A slight smirk crossed my scaled face. I knew it was arrogant of me, but from this angle, nobody could see me but Bleddyn. And my werewolf friend looked just as smugly self-satisfied as I did.

We’d done it. There was now a hole ripped open in the side of the Heartspire, jagged and crumbling, big enough for three men to march through shoulder to shoulder. It wasn’t quite as wide as the bridge that had brought us here itself, but it was good enough.

However…

The light finally penetrated through the dust cloud we’d caused on the interior of the tower, allowing us to finally see into it.

And the smile on my face disappeared.

There was someone in there. They stood just beyond the furthest edge of the dust cloud, revealed as it settled. Whoever they were, they waited patiently in the middle of a plain, unadorned hallway that extended past the entrance we had created, their hands clasped behind their back, utterly still. There wasn’t a single inch of skin, or otherwise, visible on the figure. They were covered from head to toe in a frame concealing black robe, cinched at the waist with a dull red belt whose tassels drooped all the way to their knees. Their hood was up and filled with an unnatural darkness so thick I’m not even sure a flare could penetrate it. There was only one, singular thing I could discern about them.

Their height.

They were, approximately, Dwarven sized. True Dwarven, and not the abnormally tall, mutated height that most Korvoks seemed to possess. They were entirely average as far as one of the Dwarves came. And yet, something told me that this wasn’t one of the captives from Karag Varla, incongruously standing in wait for us.

No, this was a Korvok, I was sure of it.

And they were waiting for us like they had expected the wall to be broken down. That…sent a chill down my spine.

Even through the darkness of their hood, I could tell that they were examining the inhuman forms of Bleddyn and I, standing before the ruined wall of their fortress. Somehow, I got the impression that they were pleased.

That was only reinforced by the fact that, before the frozen form of either my friend or I could act at the presence of an enemy…

They bowed to us. The Korvok unclasped their apparently gloved hands from behind their back, reclasped them in front of their chest in a disturbingly familiar gesture of prayer, and bent at the waist in our direction.

I blinked, and they spoke. Even their voice was as inscrutable as their appearance. It was incredibly androgynous, although extremely, oddly polite.

“Be welcome in these hallowed halls, oh honored guests,” The Korvok said softly, voice drifting our way down the hall. “Ul-Korveth greets you as friends, and bids you to join him in his viewing chamber.”

Silence. Utter, dumbfounded silence. And not just from Bleddyn and me. More than a few different people, Sylvia, Fade, and Renauld included, had jogged up to gape through our shoulders at the unexpectedly polite host who had greeted us.

Bleddyn was the one who gathered his wits first. “And…you’re not Ul-Korveth?”

The still unnamed Korvak shook their head from side to side in a negative. “Oh no, we could never be the honored Korvokhan. We are merely a steward of these halls.”

A steward? The leader of the Korvoks had sent a steward to, what? Welcome us in like we were guests for Sunday brunch. I almost felt a sense of vertigo overcome me at the sheer insanity of the situation. I ruthlessly squashed it with a rare use of my Middle Lattice’s emotional control.

“And does ‘this steward’ have a name?” I asked cautiously.

They bowed to us once more, although at a shallower angle. “We are known as Korvakh, though the honored guests may refer to us as they wish. Please, dispel your worries. No harm shall come to you, whilst you are regarded as Merciful Ul-Korveth’s honored guests. No blade, nor force of arm, nor working of Aether can be bent against you, without his blessing.”

I absorbed that.

Honored guests. I…didn’t know what to make of that. We had slaughtered literally hundreds, perhaps thousands of Korvoks to reach this point. Through our actions, we had slain more than a few of their leaders, the heads of their faith. We had even caused, in some unknown way, the nearly complete collapse of the intricate ritual with the spires.

And here there was a lone, oddly unmutated Korvok, who bore a name that was suspiciously similar to that of their entire people, welcoming us in like old heroes.

“Nate, this is freaking me the fuck out,” Renauld whispered urgently from behind me.

Honestly, same.

But…

I could also see the benefit to something like this. I…wanted to play along. If nothing else, we would be entirely unmolested as we were literally led to the leader of these freaks. It was entirely possible that we would be led to all of their captives as well, if we accepted.

I made my decision.

I stepped forward into the hall, raising a hand behind me to stop the almost instinctual hiss of protest I heard come from Sylvia’s lips.

I had this.

…probably.

“We would be happy to accept the Korvokhan’s gracious invitation,” I started, releasing my hold on both Vis Maledicta Exactoris and Might of the Wyrdwood halfway through my words. “However, I am accompanied by quite the large retinue presently. I must insist that they be allowed to continue their escort of our persons.”

I undoubtedly felt incredibly ridiculous, dragging out the courtly manners I had honed during my time in Blutstein. I was tired, wired, and covered from head to toe in blood, just like the rest of my ‘retinue’. My hair was matted with the crimson liquid, my clothes stuck to my skin beneath my armor, and I was ready to shift right back into battle at a moment's notice. I don’t think I would have been laughed out of Wenzel’s court, back home, if I tried to present myself like I was.

The Regent probably would have killed me himself.

But if I was going to be addressed in a familiar manner, I was going to respond in a familiar manner.

Oh, I still fully expected that I was going to be fighting this ‘Korvakh’ to the death in the fullness of time. He or she probably expected the same thing. But that wasn’t really all that different from the going on’s of the upper nobility.

The fact that the Korvoks, of all people, were approaching us like this…

It was admittedly a bit interesting. But more than that?

It was safer.

So.

Here we go again.

Korvakh the ‘steward’ tilted their head ever so slightly. “Of course they may come,” They said calmly. “The Wise Ul-Korveth’s invitation was for all of those present to come and bear witness.”

“Bear witness to what?” Bleddyn stalked forward to join me, also having released his transformation. I was quietly grateful he was letting me take the lead on this.

“Unfortunately,” Korvakh bowed their head in what seemed like genuine apology. “We are forbidden from saying. Know only that it is a grand and great gift that Gracious Ul-Korveth is choosing to bestow upon you and yours, honored guest. Besides, you shall shortly find yourself in familiar company. After all…”

When next the steward raised their head, even if I couldn’t see their face, I could read the satisfaction in the lines of their body.

“Your comrades are already waiting for you.”

I almost instinctively thrust out my arm to stop Bleddyn from taking another step forward. Good thing, too, because the leader of the Unchained was very tense just from those words. It may not have been his people that had been captured, but my old friend had fought along Olag, Maria, and all of their men just as long as I had, on this long expedition.

When Bleddyn gave his loyalty, he meant it.

Luckily, my gesture was enough to stop everyone else at my back from charging forward as well. But as I regarded Korvakh coolly, I could hear more than one grumble. I appreciated the trust, though.

Well, well.

What obvious bait. It couldn’t be more blatant if they tried. Still…

That was confirmation enough for me. It only cemented my desire to meet with the Korvokhan.

After all, that was the thing about bait. It was all too easy for the hunter to become the hunted.

I smiled with no warmth behind my eyes, motioning my forces forward as I did. “Lead on, Steward Korvakh. I am very much so eagerly awaiting our audience with the Korvokahn.”

For a variety of reasons

“Excellent. Then, if you wouldn’t mind following me…?” Korvakh turned and gestured further down the darkened hallway. They didn’t seem put out at all that the path itself was nearly pitch black.

The steward just started leading us down the hall, and I and my forces…

Followed.

Right into the den of monsters.

…………………………………..

I made sure I was close enough to our guide that I could nearly reach out and touch them. Even though I suspected they weren’t a pushover, it was nice to know I could react instantly to whatever trap this probably was when it sprung, and tear their head off of their cannibalistic shoulders.

While Korvakh himself didn’t seem to need any kind of illumination to see in here, we weren’t the same. In moments, a multitude of light Spells, Skills, and Arts brightened up our surroundings. The steward themselves didn’t seem to care, so I took that as implicit permission.

Not that I would have accepted a denial. If they had told us to quell the lights, I would have told them to shove it up their ass and die.

In a much more polite manner, of course. But he didn’t

It didn’t matter much, though. There wasn’t really anything to see. This was, by far, the single most unadorned Korvok structure I had either been inside or seen so far. The walls around us were flat-out blank. Simple, unadorned stonework lay on either side of the hall with nothing upon them. No carvings, runic or decorative. No depictions of their faith. Certainly not artwork. They didn’t even have any of the flesh that clad the spires of the Hold beyond the lake.

You would have thought that the literal central pillar of these fanatic’s faith would have been more…ceremonial, so to speak. Just a bit more extravagant, if only to praise their Red Truth.

But no. If anything, the inside of the Heartspire was the exact opposite. It was almost like this place was vestigial, if anything.

Abruptly, the single long hallway that Korvakh had been leading us through ended. But not even that was richly decorated.

Sure, the resulting room was fairly large…

But it was empty, like everything else.

This, I think, was the center of the Heartspire. Turns out, it wasn’t wrong to think of it as being essentially needle-shaped.

Because the entire thing was hollow. I…think the hall we’d just spent the last several minutes walking down was essentially the thickness of the walls. This space looked to stretch far up and away above us. So far that in the darkness of the central hollow, I couldn’t actually see anywhere near the tip of the Heartspire. None of our lights reached the distances that this space possessed. Every step that every person took into the central hollow echoed loudly, but that wasn’t the sense I was focusing on the most.

Instead, the moment I had set foot in here, my Aetherial sense had gone wild. To the extent that I had briefly stopped, just to try and calm the blood pounding through my ears.

This hollow might have been empty of anything material…but it was absolutely filled to the brim with Aether. It was to the extent that I could nearly taste the power in the air. I nearly felt like I was swimming in raw, unrefined power.

And I could hazard a guess as to where it was coming from. I stared up at a distant roof that I couldn’t even make out, through the murk.

“It’s a syringe…” I muttered to myself in astonishment. I shook my head at the sheer gall involved.

The Korvoks had built a giant syringe tall enough to pin a Calamity to the top of a mountain, pierced its heart, and then started to suck it dry of its Aether.

I…couldn’t help but be a bit impressed by the ambition. Even if it was a monstrous affront to all decency, it was still an extraordinary achievement.

I shoved the feeling down, steadied my senses, and continued my following. My astonishment may have only lasted for a few moments, but I still caught an almost knowing tilt of Korvakh’s head. I paid no attention to it.

Instead…

I considered the goal they had led us to.

Right in the center of the enormous hollow…

Was a single set of stairs that led downwards.
