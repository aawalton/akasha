Rowan sat on the hard stone floor, legs crossed and eyes closed. His mana churned throughout his channels, a familiar weight that settled his racing thoughts.

He let his mind wander, trying to find connections between things he knew and things he suspected. The inn. The Rift. The Demons inhabiting it. All of it swirled around in his head as his Core steadily refilled.

Rowan exhaled, a slow, deliberate release of breath, the tension pressing down on his shoulders slowly growing fainter.

The gate separating him from the next chamber called to him, the runes etched into the stone a poignant reminder of what he faced here.

Demons. Lesser and Greater. Monsters that wouldn’t stop, that wouldn’t falter. Not unless they were made to. Not unless they were forced to.

“A fucking Demon Prince,” Rowan muttered, running a hand through his hair. “How am I supposed to deal with that?”

It was one thing to go up against monsters of a higher level than him. His father had taught him that a difference in stats meant nothing in the face of pure mastery. Being fast, or strong, or hard to kill was only a foundation. One that needed to be built up with hard work and dedication.

But that sentiment only went so far.

There was a gap that no amount of skill could overcome, and while Rowan wasn’t sure where exactly it lay for him, he was pretty damn sure a Demon Prince was over that line.

His parents, both Archmages of great renown, both skilled, and talented, and hardworking, and both of them had lost their lives against the Archdemon of Pride. It had taken the collective might of Eiseylth to finally put down that threat, causing untold destruction and a death toll in the thousands.

A Demon Prince might be a step down from an Archdemon, but from Rowan’s perspective, both of them were opponents he couldn’t hope to match.

At least not yet.

One step at a time, he told himself. He’s not here yet. And if I play this right, there’s a chance he never will be.

Rowan didn’t know how those responsible managed to corrupt this Rift, or how they managed to connect it to the Nine Hells. But then again, you didn’t need to know how something worked in order to dismantle it. And he had a feeling that clearing this Rift would go a long way in foiling their plans.

His fists clenched, a heavy sigh leaving his lips.

“They could have done so much good,” he muttered angrily. “They could have been hailed as Heroes of the Kingdom for this discovery, and instead, they chose to destroy. To sow chaos and pain.”

Being able to create Rifts would have propelled the Kingdom of Vandral into a golden age the likes of which they hadn’t seen since the reign of King Alaric the Just. It would have allowed for the systematic training of scions, for the gathering of resources not found anywhere besides the Far North. And above all that, it would have been a catalyst for change, for triumph against the ever-encroaching Wilds.

Rowan clamped down on the frustration bubbling up inside him.

“Evil people, evil deeds,” he whispered, letting out a slow breath. “Like blades of grass in a field.”

Rowan shook his head. “No point in thinking about this. Not until I have more answers.”

His thoughts shifted to the moment he’d translated the runes adorning the gate, to the image of Azrathul that assaulted his mind. But instead of focusing on that, Rowan recalled the sense of control that washed over him.

His Soul had stirred in response to the Demon’s visage appearing in his head, empowering [Iron Will] to a level he hadn’t thought possible. It allowed him to force it out—to overpower it.

It’s been happening more often, he thought. I’m using Spirit. Draining my Wellspring. Even without meaning to.

He couldn’t be certain, but Rowan felt the sudden progress with his trait had something to do with gaining another one. [Storm Touched] took his already substantial magical talent and ramped it up to another level entirely, allowing him to command his mana like a conductor would an orchestra.

Every minute twitch of his Intent resulted in exactly what he wanted, whether it be control, or power, or speed. And while at first that didn’t seem like it’d be connected to [Immortal Soul], Rowan had some guesses about how Spirit worked.

Somehow, it’s interpreting my desires and trying to make them reality. During the fight against the Wyrmlings, I wanted them gone, and [Immortal Soul] made that happen. Then against the Demon, I wanted to end him, and it empowered my [Fireball]. And now, when Azrathul’s image assaulted my mind, my trait dealt with it by boosting [Iron Will] to force it out.

He sighed. “That’s all well and good, but I still haven’t managed to consciously use it. I have this ridiculously sharp sword, and it keeps slipping out of its scabbard.”

Rowan spent the next few minutes deep in thought, letting his Core refill as his mind went over a dozen different things.

In the end, he came to the conclusion that what he really needed was a month or two of relative peace. No goblin tribes, no Demon incursions, no nothing. Just him and his magic, working towards understanding the tools he had at his disposal. But with the way things were looking, Rowan doubted that was in the cards.

I have two more weeks before the team arrives. And before that, I have to clear this Rift, make it to the Storm Crown, and hopefully find a safe place to call home. At least for a little while.

A bittersweet smile tugged at his lips as his thoughts turned to the Grove. He wondered what they were doing, if they were safe, if they missed his company as much as he missed theirs.

Rowan shook his head. They’re fine, you idiot. Focus on yourself.

It took another few minutes for his Core to fully refill, and he wasted no time getting to work. Rowan stood up, dusted off his coat, and walked back to the gate. After all, this Rift wasn’t going to clear itself.

His fingers traced the runes engraved into the stone, and Rowan didn’t try to push down his growing excitement. As dangerous as his current situation was, it was also a chance for him to grow, to test the limits of what he was capable of.

His reserves were full, his mind focused, his heart eager.

“Alright,” Rowan whispered. “Let’s do this.”

He tugged at his mana, casting two whips and a shield. There was no telling what waited for him on the other side, so Rowan decided to err on the side of caution.

With a steady hand, he pressed against the cold stone.

Slowly, the heavy door groaned open. A fine layer of dust billowed into the air, swirling in the dim light and catching the faint glimmer of Rowan’s spells. But what awaited him on the other side wasn’t a horde of Demons vying for his blood. Instead, his eyes landed on a vast hall with four separate gates arrayed around the room.

Directly opposite from the door he’d just passed through stood the largest one—its surface an intricate tapestry of dark metal and stone, sealed tight by three runes that pulsed like a heartbeat.

Something about the gate set Rowan’s instincts on edge. He didn’t need to translate the runes to know what lay on the other side. There was only one thing it could be, but it seemed like getting it open wouldn’t be as simple as a shove or a spell.

Around the chamber, three smaller gates were nestled into alcoves, each door different. One bore a pattern of entwined thorns and flickering embers, another was inscribed with cold, angular symbols that seemed to shiver in the low light, and the last was almost organic in design—dark, twisted flesh that pulsed with a slow, steady beat.

A notification appeared.

[System Notification]!

Corrupted Rift: [The Blackened Keep - Tier 2]

Objective: Clear Three Trials (0/3).

Reward: Gate Key, ??? [Unstable Rift: Rewards may fluctuate]

WARNING: Rift Anomaly Detected.

Rowan slowly exhaled, the tension leaving his shoulders. “This would have been a slightly better place to mediate,” he muttered, looking around. “No burning carcasses at least.”

He let his spells drop.

It seemed this Rift was slightly more organized than he’d first expected.

Rowan had been ready for a castle filled with Demons, but he had to remind himself that this wasn’t his reality. This was a pocket world with its own rules.

His lips quirked into a smile, followed by a feeling of childlike giddiness.

Rowan spent most of his life thinking he’d never have this. The System, his magic, strength. And while he thought he’d gotten over that after everything he’d done already, there was something about delving into a Rift that made it tangible.

He walked up to the first gate, reaching out to touch the smooth stone. As soon as he did, another notification appeared.

[Trial of Pride]

Rowan jerked his hand back, eyes narrowing as he registered the words.

Pride was a sin he was intimately familiar with. In more ways than one.

Walking over to the next gate, he repeated the process.

[Trial of Wrath]

Then he did the same for the third.

[Trial of Envy]

With the trials revealed, Rowan finally made his way to the largest gate, hesitating for just a moment before placing his palm on the behemoth of metal and stone.

[Shattered Throne]

His hand fell away as pieces of the puzzle clicked into place. The clues he uncovered so far forming an image of his current circumstances.

He’d half expected for the notification to read Throne of Ash, but considering it didn’t, Rowan felt safe in assuming the Demon’s plans weren’t yet close to bearing fruit.

“Shattered Throne, huh?” he said, stepping back and taking in the towering gate. “Now I just have to make sure it stays that way.”

Rowan looked around the room, knowing what he needed to do but unsure how to go about it. All three of these trials needed to be cleared in order for him to progress. Now it was just a matter of choosing which one to do first.

Out of the three, Wrath seemed like the safest option. Not because it wouldn’t be dangerous, but because it was simple.

Demons of Wrath weren’t complicated creatures. They thrived on chaos and wanton destruction, leaving nothing but death in their wake.

Rowan wasn’t sure what this trial would involve, but he felt it was a safe bet to assume it wouldn’t stray too far from that core principle.

“Chaos and mayhem,” he whispered, coming to a decision.

With steady steps, Rowan stepped up to the first gate. He reached out, pressed his hands against the stone, and pushed.
