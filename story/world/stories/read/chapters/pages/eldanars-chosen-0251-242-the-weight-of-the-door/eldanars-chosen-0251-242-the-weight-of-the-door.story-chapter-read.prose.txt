Josh sat perfectly still on the freezing cobblestones of the inner staging area. He stared at the colossal, closed oak doors. His storm-grey eyes were completely blank, unblinking as the thick dust from the archway above settled onto the dark steel of his greathelm.

The brilliant, blinding white light of Torin’s final, overcharged core detonation was permanently burned into his retinas. He could still feel the heavy, iron-plated shove against his breastplate. It was a phantom impact, a ghost of the veteran fighter forcibly rejecting Josh's protection and claiming the sacrificial right for himself.

Josh was a Guardian. His entire class, his entire purpose in this brutal world, was to be the shield that kept his allies breathing. Yet, he was sitting safely behind a locked gate, and Torin was on the other side. A suffocating, poisonous knot of pure, unadulterated guilt began to coil tightly in the pit of his stomach. He should have been faster. He should have anticipated the shove. He should have grabbed the old man and thrown him through the gap instead.

A flurry of hurried, splashing footsteps broke through his dark, spiralling thoughts.

Carcan dropped to her knees in the mud right beside him. The elven healer did not look relieved. Her aristocratic features were twisted into a mask of absolute, terrifying fury.

She did not speak. She simply grabbed the thick, dented iron rim of his breastplate with her slender hands, her knuckles turning stark white, and violently yanked him forward.

"Take your helmet off," Carcan commanded. Her melodic voice was trembling with a raw, unfiltered anger that Josh had never heard from her before.

Josh slowly reached up with numb, trembling gauntlets. He unclasped the heavy chin strap and pulled the master-crafted steel greathelm from his head, letting it clatter to the cobblestones. The freezing winter air bit instantly at his sweat-soaked, ginger hair.

Carcan pressed her hands firmly against his chest piece. Her amber-topped staff, resting in the crook of her arm, flared with a brilliant, blinding golden light.

The healing magic poured into his body, seeking out the cracked ribs and the deep, bruising trauma left by the Bone Goliath's concussive shockwave. The holy energy forcefully knitted the bone back together, pushing the swelling down and clearing the dark, pulsating spots from his vision.

But her touch was not gentle. It was rigid and punishing.

"You absolute, unforgivable fool," Carcan hissed, her silver eyes blazing with tears she refused to let fall. She pushed harder against his armour, the golden light flaring brighter. "Do you have a death wish? Do you actively seek out the very jaws of the grave simply to test your own metal?"

"Carcan," Josh started, his voice a hoarse, broken rasp. He looked down at the cobblestones, unable to meet her furious gaze. "I didn't..."

"Do not speak!" she snapped, cutting him off completely. Her hands moved to his left shoulder, repairing the torn muscle fibres where the Wights had battered his shield. "You promised me. You gave me your word that we were leaving the wall together. And then you simply turned your back on the gates and walked into an ocean of monsters. If Torin had not followed you, if he had not thrown you back through that door..."

Her voice finally cracked. The terrifying, furious battle persona shattered entirely, leaving only the profound, suffocating terror she had endured while watching him fight from afar.

"You would be dead, Josh," she whispered, a single tear slipping down her pale cheek. "And I would have had to watch."

Josh slowly raised his heavy, steel-clad hands. He gently enveloped her trembling, pale fingers, completely ignoring the grime and blood coating his gauntlets.

"I am sorry," Josh rumbled, his voice thick with a heavy, profound sorrow. The internal anger he felt towards himself completely overshadowed the physical pain of his wounds. "I should not have stepped off the line. But Torin... he took my place. He made the choice for me, Carcan. I let him die."

"He made a choice," a nearby city guardsman interrupted softly, offering a grim, respectful nod towards the locked oak doors. "He bought the city time. Do not dishonour his final stand by drowning in guilt."

Outside the heavy timber doors, the muffled, chaotic sounds of the undead horde seemed to be losing their frantic intensity. The shrieking and the scraping of iron against the wood were slowly dying down. The horrific, explosive force of Torin's core detonation had clearly obliterated the immediate vanguard, creating a temporary lull in the meat grinder.

Josh took a deep, steadying breath, allowing Carcan's magic to finish sealing the micro-fractures in his arms. He squeezed her hands gently, offering a silent promise to do better, before releasing his grip.

"Thank you, Carcan," Josh murmured, picking up his greathelm and sliding it back over his head.

Before the elven healer could reply, the relative quiet of the staging area was violently shattered.

BOOM.

The impact was absolutely deafening. It sounded as though a falling meteor had struck the exterior of the gatehouse. The colossal, thirty-foot-tall oak doors groaned in sheer, structural agony. The heavy iron dropping bar wedged into the cobblestones jumped violently, striking sparks against the stone. A thick cloud of ancient dust and loose mortar rained down from the archway above, coating Josh and Carcan in a fine, grey powder.

The Bone Goliaths had arrived at the chokepoint. The massive, towering monstrosities did not care about the narrow gap. They were simply going to batter the entire gatehouse into splinters.

BOOM.

The second strike hit the right-hand door. A massive, jagged crack appeared in the solid oak timber, running entirely down the centre of the massive log. The heavy iron hinges shrieked, threatening to tear completely free from the stone masonry.

Carcan stared at the fracturing wood, her luminous silver eyes wide with a sudden, crushing despair.

"All that sacrifice," Carcan whispered, her melodic voice completely hollow. "Torin gave his life to close those doors, and it was all for nothing."

"Clear the kill zone!" Captain Aris roared, sprinting down the stone stairs from the upper ramparts. The commander of the city guard was waving his broadsword frantically. "Fall back to the secondary wall! Those gates are going to shatter in minutes! Move!"

The terrified guardsmen and the remaining adventurers in the staging area did not hesitate. They turned and fled the enclosed space, sprinting towards the massive, semi-circular barricade of packed earth and heavy rubble that had been hastily constructed earlier that morning.

Josh grabbed Carcan by the shoulder of her pale green robes, physically pulling her away from the buckling oak doors.

"We need to move," Josh ordered, keeping his dark steel heater shield raised to protect them from the falling masonry.

They ran across the short expanse of open cobblestones, reaching the base of the towering secondary wall. The crude barricade was easily twenty feet high, constructed entirely of loose paving stones, overturned merchant carts, and massive mounds of packed dirt. It was ugly, desperate engineering, but it was incredibly thick.

A series of heavy, crude wooden ladders had been hastily lashed against the rubble to allow the retreating defenders to climb over.

Josh ushered Carcan up the ladder first. He followed closely behind her, his heavy steel boots slipping dangerously on the slick, muddy rungs. The physical exertion of the climb made his freshly healed ribs ache with a dull throb, a sharp reminder of the sheer punishment his body had absorbed.

They reached the flat, packed dirt at the top of the rubble wall.

Josh turned, looking back down at the staging area they had just vacated. The massive iron portcullis hung like a dark shadow behind the bulging oak doors. The thick timber was literally bowing inward under the relentless, apocalyptic pounding of the Goliaths outside. It was a terrifying sight, a visual representation of a dam mere seconds away from a catastrophic failure.

"Down the other side," a sergeant barked, pointing towards a series of ramps leading down into the city streets. "Keep moving towards the inner district!"

Josh and Carcan descended the dirt ramps, leaving the immediate shadow of the gatehouse behind them. The atmosphere within Ashenfall was completely apocalyptic. It was no longer the frantic, rushing panic of a city trying to organise a defence. It was the sombre, terrifying reality of a city preparing to die.

The streets around the gatehouse were choked with desperate, crude barricades. Every single alleyway and main thoroughfare had been blocked with a chaotic assortment of domestic junk. Heavy oak dining tables were stacked atop overturned wagons. Massive iron anvils had been dragged from the local smithies to reinforce the wooden frames.

Standing behind these makeshift walls were the remnants of the civilian populace.

They were not soldiers. Josh saw an elderly man with snow-white hair, his hands shaking violently as he sharpened a rusted butcher's cleaver on a whetstone. He saw a young mother sitting on the cold cobblestones, rocking back and forth as she clutched two terrified, weeping children tightly to her chest. Local tradesmen stood with pitchforks and heavy hammers, their faces pale and drawn tight with pure, unadulterated terror.

They were waiting for the roaring monsters to break through the walls and flood the streets. They knew they stood absolutely no chance of survival, but they were refusing to surrender their homes without a fight.

The grim, silent determination of the doomed citizens made Josh’s chest physically ache. The heavy steel of his armour felt completely pointless. He was a Guardian, but he could not guard an entire city against thousands of walking corpses.

They navigated the crowds of people, moving slowly towards the base of the massive timber walls flanking the central gatehouse.

They had established a makeshift command post in a relatively secure, enclosed courtyard located not too far from the wall. The courtyard was filled with exhausted, bleeding runners delivering frantic updates from the various flanks.

As Josh and Carcan entered the courtyard, a familiar, broad-shouldered figure caught Josh's eye. Standing near the corner of a heavy stone building, partially obscured by a stack of supply crates, was Bhel. The dwarven fighter was still stripped of his upper armour, leaning heavily on his iron axe, standing a fierce, silent vigil over a makeshift canvas stretcher.

Lying on the stretcher, completely unconscious and wrapped tightly in his shimmering crimson cloak, was Brett.

"Bhel!" Josh called out, his heavy boots clanking against the stone as he hurried towards his friends.

The dwarf spun around at the sound of the voice. His dark eyes widened in profound shock. He dropped his heavy iron axe onto the cobblestones with a loud clatter, completely ignoring the weapon.

Bhel did not offer a gruff greeting or a sarcastic remark. He closed the distance in three massive strides, throwing his thick, muscular arms entirely around Josh and Carcan simultaneously.

The dwarf squeezed them into a massive, rib-crushing hug. He buried his bearded face against the cold steel of Josh's breastplate, his broad shoulders trembling.

"Mountain and stone, you stubborn fools," Bhel rasped, his gravelly voice completely breaking with raw emotion. "I saw the Wights close the ring around you. I saw Torin shove you back. The gates slammed shut, and I thought... I thought you were dead in the mud, Josh. I thought I had lost you both."

Josh wrapped his heavy arms around the dwarf, returning the fierce embrace. "It takes a lot more than a closed door to keep me out, Bhel."

Carcan rested her cheek gently against the top of the dwarf's head, her own eyes shining with unshed tears. "We are safe, Bhel. The Light provided a path."

Bhel finally pulled back, wiping his eyes roughly with the back of his calloused hand. He coughed loudly, instantly attempting to reassert his gruff, stoic persona, though the attempt was entirely unconvincing.

"Right. Well. Good," Bhel muttered, picking his axe up from the cobblestones. He gestured towards the sleeping mage on the stretcher. "The scholar is still out cold. He has not moved a muscle since they carried him down here. The magic is completely drained from him."

"He needs time," Carcan confirmed softly, looking at Brett's pale, peaceful face. "Time we might not possess."

Josh turned his attention away from his sleeping friend, looking towards the centre of the courtyard.

Caistina Tharistrar was standing over a large wooden table, surrounded by the surviving guard captains. The elven mage trainer looked absolutely exhausted. Her immaculate sapphire-blue robes were covered in black soot and mud. The brilliant, pulsing light of her white staff had faded to a weak, dying ember.

Josh walked over to the command table, his heavy presence causing the shouting captains to fall silent.

He looked down at the large parchment map of the city. The beautiful, intricate drawings of the districts meant absolutely nothing now. The entire northern perimeter of the map was covered in dark, chaotic ink stains, representing the crushing tide of the undead horde.

"What is the plan, Caistina?" Josh asked, his deep voice carrying a quiet, desperate hope for a tactical miracle. "Torin bought us a few minutes, but those gates are failing. The secondary rubble wall will not hold against the Goliaths. Where do we establish the final chokepoint? How do we counter-attack?"

Caistina slowly raised her head.

She looked at the towering human Guardian. She did not offer her usual, commanding aura of absolute certainty. The sharp, brilliant tactical fire in her amethyst eyes had completely burned out, leaving behind a cold, hollow emptiness.

Captain Aris stood beside her, his scarred face completely broken. The veteran commander looked like a man who had already accepted his execution.

"There is no counter-attack, Josh," Caistina answered, her melodic voice dropping into a devastating, defeated whisper that carried across the quiet courtyard.

She reached out, her elegant fingers resting lightly on the edge of the parchment map.

"We have expended every single arrow in the armoury," Caistina explained, stating the horrific logistics with blunt, pragmatic cruelty. "The pitch trenches are smothered. The high-tier mages are either unconscious like Brett, or suffering severe mana sickness. The militia is broken. We have absolutely nothing left to throw at them."

She looked back up at Josh, her eyes filling with a profound, terrifying sorrow.

"There is no plan," Caistina admitted, the sheer weight of the failure crushing her posture. "When those oak doors splinter, the horde will wash over the rubble wall. They will flood the streets. Ashenfall will fall within hours."
