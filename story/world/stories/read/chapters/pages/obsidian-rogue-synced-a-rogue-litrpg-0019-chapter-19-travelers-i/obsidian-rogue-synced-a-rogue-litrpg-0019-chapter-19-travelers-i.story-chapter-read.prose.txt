Levi smiled at the text on the blue box that popped up. The rogue had gotten to level five after clearing his first dungeon. It wasn’t much, but he’d managed to improve from being a level one rogue. Progress like that was a lot appreciated, even as little as it was. He’d risked his life to clear that dungeon alone, and now he reaped the rewards. He'd gotten a new skill as well as points to level up his skills.

Speaking of points, now is as good a time as any to decide how I am going to distribute my points.

He stared at the screen for a minute. Trying to decide how exactly he was going to share his unused points. He had four of those to spend, and he knew that he’d have to spend them wisely. Thinking back to his fights in the dungeon, he tried to figure out what exactly he'd done wrong. From what he could recall, he’d barely made it out of there alive. His quick thinking and luck had saved him from becoming the latest fool to die trying to solo a dungeon.

Levi shuddered involuntarily as a hand went up to his right shoulder. He’d received a nasty blow from the chief gremlin's cudgel on that shoulder. It’d been the most painful sensation he’d had to endure, and even though the injury was healed, it stayed imprinted in his mind as a reminder of the hazards that came with dungeon diving.

Pulling his hand away from his shoulder, he turned his focus to analyzing his fight. As far as he could remember, his major issue lay with his inexperience with dungeons. Monsters were different from the humans that he robbed in Gantus. They were much more feral, with little to no regard for their lives. He also had difficulty with group fights. The gremlins might’ve been monsters, but they had also been smart enough to use their numbers to their advantage.

Some gremlins had been used as decoys to create openings for their kinfolk; the most memorable decoy he’d fallen for had been the one that had led to his shoulder getting popped by the chief gremlin's cudgel. He wasn’t sure which stat he was supposed to blame his inefficiency on; was it his perception, intelligence, dexterity, or constitution that was at fault? He couldn’t pinpoint it, but two things were certain. He’d been too slow to react to the cudgel making contact with his shoulder. In fact, if he’d been smart or fast enough, the cudgel wouldn’t have met his shoulder. That aside, he received a hefty health loss for that one careless movement. He’d lost twenty-eight health, which meant his constitution wasn’t good enough to dull the effects of such hits.

He paused for a moment, weighing his options. From what he’d been able to learn from his fight in the dungeon, he could say that his constitution was clearly lacking. His health pool had prevented the hit from the chief gremlin from being life-threatening, but he knew the truth. It'd only have taken him getting hit by the cudgel on his other shoulder to make him completely useless in the fight, and otherwise spell his death in the dungeon. That made improving his constitution a priority, and he’d have to assign at least two points to the stat since it was so far behind the others.

Dexterity, intelligence, and perception were the other stats that held weight at the moment, but he wasn’t sure what exactly intelligence entailed. Would it make him smarter, or was it a way of qualifying him for something? He wasn’t sure, but regardless of his uncertainty, he’d assign a single point to the stat, just to find out its effect. That left him with only one point to spare, and it was either going to his perception or his dexterity. He couldn’t really single out the stat that would’ve made the most difference in the cudgel incident, and in the end, he decided to go with his perception since his dexterity had already received a boost from his new cloak.

So perception and intelligence both get a point, and constitution gets two.

He grinned as the system informed him of the update to his stats. With a thought, the rogue dismissed the screen, turning his attention to the mage, who was now clothed in a new robe. Her old one lay discarded on his bed. Levi stared at the two robes, and his ears perked up at the difference in quality; the one she now wore was of an inferior quality to the one that lay on the bed. Aside from the quality aspect, the new cloak also had another noticeable difference: the dragon insignia, which had been on the discarded robe, was absent from the new one. Come to think of it, he’d seen that same insignia on the guards stationed outside the safe room. He’d been too busy to remember that tiny detail at the time. The rogue's mind whirred at the implications of such a thing.

She's abandoning her house Insignia? Or is she just a servant of the house disguised as the heiress of the Noir house?

He shook his head at the thought. The guards back at the safe room had referred to her as ‘Lady’ even in her absence, and they couldn't possibly have known that he was on his way from Gantus. Of course, it wasn’t entirely impossible that the mage had told them to say something like that as she walked away, but it was a bit of a reach. At this point, it was evident that he was grasping at straws; the mage might just give up her entire life for him. No, not for him, but to travel with him.

“Are you done?” Xara asked, jolting him from his thoughts.

“Yeah. We should go.” Levi replied.

The mage nodded in agreement, and within a heartbeat, all the equipment they discarded for new ones vanished into thin air. No doubt the mage had moved them into her storage ring. Levi whistled internally at the use of the ring. It was an artifact that would make their journey a lot easier. He wanted to ask about the ring in detail, but kept his lips sealed; too many questions in a short amount of time would showcase his incompetence to the mage.

“We’ll take the window,” Levi said.

“Why?”

“For our safety.”

“Okay.”

The mage agreed to leave the room through the window; getting her to jump out was the hard part. As expected, she hadn’t done anything of the sort before, and so he’d had to reassure her that she wouldn’t break a leg from the jump. At least Levi was sure he wouldn’t, as he’d judged that he wouldn’t be at risk of breaking his bones. As experienced as he was with jumping out of buildings via the windows or off the roof, he didn't think the jump from the room's window and onto the street would do more harm than a simple jolt to his knees.

“Oompf,” Xara breathed as she landed on her feet on the cobblestones of the street. Levi had eventually managed to convince the mage to jump out the window with him.

“You okay?”

“Yeah, I just need a minute,” she breathed.

“Sure,” Levi said.

His eyes scanned for threats as people milled about the streets, totally unbothered by the two people who’d just jumped out of the window. The window had been to the side of the inn, and where they'd landed was more of an alleyway than the main street, as the front doors of the inn faced in that direction. Alleyway or not, there were still people milling about, and so he had to keep his eyes sharp while Xara caught her breath. It wouldn’t do them any good to be caught off guard. Besides, he actually wanted to know if they were being followed, and by the looks of it, they weren't, or at least he couldn't spot anyone interested in them.

“Alright, we can go,” Xara said. “Follow me.”

The rogue wordlessly followed the mage, his eyes constantly scanning for threats even as they moved from alleyways into the main street. The mage led them to their means of escape. And as they moved, Levi couldn't help but marvel at the size of Pelossi. They’d been walking for what seemed like moments with no end in sight. The mage was in the lead, and her movements were confident and sure. She moved the way he imagined those of nobility would—short but confident steps. Head and shoulders raised high.

While that was a pretty sight to most of the common folk, it was also a style of walking that attracted the wrong type of attention. Petty thieves lurked within the streets even as early as this. And Levi caught more than five unsuspecting victims being liberated of their valuables, and he couldn't help but smirk. From what he’d seen, the thieves in Pelossi came in all sizes and shapes. Male, female, young, and old. Every corner of the street seemed to have a thief who’d blended in, and they did a fine job of keeping their activities quiet; besides, most of the pickpockets he’d spotted were finely dressed, and the few who weren’t were sufficiently dressed to fit in with the common folk.

The big city sure does have a lot of thieves.

It wasn't much of a surprise to him because it made a lot more sense. The streets he walked were filled with synced and unsynced alike. Mages, rangers, priests, warriors, rogues, and non-combat synced moved about in their droves. The big city was a place teeming with lots of marks for thieves who knew their stuff, and unfortunately for Xara, a lot of those had marked her as an easy target. A couple had weaved their way around the crowds to manufacture an accidental bump on the road, but all it had taken most to dissuade them from making a move on her was a glance from him. Of course, not all thieves had been deterred by his faceless stare; some had tried to make a move to which Levi responded by flashing the blades of his daggers. The soft glint from his weapons was enough to dissuade the rest from trying anything funny.

He wondered how the mage usually dealt with such pickpockets. Of course, she’d said that the bulk of her coins were deposited in her system account, but at the end of the day, she did move about with a lot of coins. Twelve gold might've been a paltry sum to her, but Levi knew better. He endured true poverty. But his background wasn't the issue he wanted to think about now.

Or was it the robes?

She’d had the dragon insignia on her former mage robe, which marked her as a member of the house Noir. Surely that would’ve been enough to dissuade petty thieves from trying to steal from her. Only someone as ignorant as he’d been would attempt to lift items from the mage without knowing what the insignia meant. Then again, the mage hadn’t really cared about the gold, had she? No, if it weren’t for the mark on his forehead that she’d glimpsed, she probably wouldn’t have tracked him down.

Then again, there was no telling if she would’ve. Rich people were the most unpredictable of the lot. He might even call them crazy. Sighing at the absurdity of it all, he turned his attention to the road and kept playing the part of a background bodyguard for the mage. The hood sufficiently hid his face from the public and made him a bit of an enigma, but it mattered little, as there were other rogues milling about, some covering their faces with hoods and others not.

“We’re here,” Xara said, breaking him out of his thoughts.
