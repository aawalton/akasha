Redberry brooded and sulked alone in the inner chambers of her mindscape house, refusing all questions and requests — in fact, she basically ignored her realm, leaving it to her Servitors. She knew it was small of her, but the failure she’d had on the battlefield was a shame that would sting for weeks, if not months. In any case, her realm didn’t particularly need her. Samantha might eventually need another fighter, but there wasn’t any urgency there.

All that Faith Energy invested in the Avatar, down the drain. I’ll need to save up for the upgrades…

She felt a poke at her mind from an attempted direct communication. Zadkiel. He’d tried a dozen times since the battle, even while still in it, technically.

On impulse, she accepted it. “What do you want, Zadkiel?”

Relief saturated his response as he said, “To hear you’re doing alright!”

“I spoke to Samantha.”

“Yes, I heard it was mostly, ‘I’m fine, and everyone had best leave me alone.’ Does that sound about right?”

“Yet here you are.”

A devilish smile translated from him. “I’d rather be where you are than whatever ‘here’ counts as.”

Redberry scoffed. Before she could reply with something acidic poised on her tongue, she felt another impulse grip her, intense enough to wipe her response out of her mind. “Fine. Come.”

She abruptly disconnected and sent the invitation to him, which he instantly took advantage of to arrive. Smart.

Where she resided right then could not be described as accessible to the area guests had access to. She let him stew for a while.

Am I doing something with this, or continuing the tease? I don’t really care for chatting right now.

She shapeshifted herself into her typical, natural dryad form, popping up in the bedroom and inspecting herself briefly in mirrors. She ran a hand over her head, where the scar used to be. It still amazed her not to see it, to feel it, to be free of the pain.

No matter what, I’m better off than before. Something to remember. I remain healthy, despite the recent destruction. With health comes the impulses of the body, reminding me that I live…

She extended her perceptions to peek in on Zadkiel. He did not appear impatient. He had helped himself to some wine that he’d found, and was looking at a sculpture in the living room, calm and poised, sipping wine, contemplative, brooding, big, muscular, desirable…

Hmmph — enough of this in my system!

From the floor of her bedroom, she created a mass of vines to shoot out, opening doors and extending down the hallway and into the living room, finally firing across the distance for Zadkiel. He growled and spun around to grab the first couple out of the air on impulse, the wineglass tossed to shatter on the floor.

How cute.

Doubling her efforts, Redberry quickly wrapped him up in a multitude of vines, but Zadkiel had stopped struggling, eyes wide and looking at them with new consideration.

The look on his face was priceless. By no means did it offend her — she adored it when prey surrendered themselves into her power. He didn’t need to tell her where his mind was, because she felt it in his body, sensed it intrinsically, tasted it through the vines. Beyond that, they’d played this game to this — his preferred conclusion, more or less — for years and years.

She knew what he wanted. He’d get a morsel of it, at least.

Wrapped up tightly, she pulled him into the bedroom. Just as he arrived, his wide eyes taking in the existence of things few men could claim to have seen — with clear excitement — Redberry covered his mouth.

“No talking, demon, not until I grant you the right here, in my private domain,” Redberry commanded. “Do you understand me?”

He nodded quickly.

“Good boy,” she muttered, and then moved him to the bed.

After the destruction of Matrazere, ‘difficult’ was not the word for the days that passed for Samantha and her realm. ‘Grueling’ might suffice, but the work was too satisfying on top of things to allow that, either.

Busy. That was the word.

There was almost no activity from the enemy in the region, as the Dominion’s leadership had a huge vacuum without the Unbroken Fortress there, controlling everything for the length and breadth of a duchy at the least. The raids on supplies along the river to Rivermount evaporated like smoke. South of all the blighted terrain in Matrazere, Samantha built what was at first a large camp near the end of the river where a previous town had been destroyed (by Matrazere). Owing to the ease with which she and her people built actual permanent structures, though, it soon became obvious that it could and should be more.

She founded the town ‘St. Deikmorn’ there, surrounded by reinforced and enchanted walls. She assigned it a governor and charters and instituted trade, and it grew rapidly. All signs and the available support structure behind it pointed to its likelihood of becoming a key city for the region, assuming sufficient time allowed.

The funeral and burning of the body of Deikmorn Brakka was conducted with all honors in the town named after him, and was attended by what was effectively an entire army, and then some. Very sparse words were spoken by a priest, loudly announcing Deikmorn’s titles and accomplishments and the heroism of his final moments. High figures from his clan, as well as his lover, Nyomel, then lit the pyre collectively.

Nyomel’s grief had been consoled by Samantha’s efforts to arrange her access to Heaven and Deikmorn Brakka’s ‘captured’ spirit. Deikmorn, for his part, had adjusted with ease to Heavenly life, quickly jumping into the wealth of ‘retirement’ activities. He got more and more relaxed, as if shedding a long life of toil and fighting was just what he wanted.

The armies of the alliance were not truly idle for long, however. With no challengers for a vast swath of territory and time, they took advantage by splitting the army into many independent groups and expanding their conquest in all directions, including territory northwest of Rivermount. Little fighting transpired as Dominion citizens surrendered and quickly swore allegiance to the invaders, their own soldiers already fled or dead at Matrazere. Farming communities, towns, and even cities were occupied on the daily.

Military recruits were continuous, too, young men especially tempted by the promise of coin. The need was great, as they could never have enough token garrisons just to maintain order and act as sentries to potential Dominion shenanigans.

Terrorism — community sabotage where they’d already taken territory. Samantha knew it was coming, and it was just a matter of time, there in the chaotic and unstable conditions she could not avoid creating with war. She had the Dominion reeling, but they’d inevitably get their shit together to some degree before she could reach the Capital. In fact, she had quite a few things still to resolve before she got there. This was both a tangible understanding and a feeling that she still had to grow in power.

Community-wise, that was blowing up exponentially yet again. She had gone from not only The Goddess in Rivermount, to her full and iconic persona being widely proliferated as the central head of the church and the existing Pantheon…

The Goddess Samantha was fully public, and to say it was spreading like wildfire was to undersell it.

She went with it, shifting from the Puck temples she’d developed in the southwest, such as at Merrington, to vague replications of her cathedral at Itha Ranon, spreading in former Dominion territory. She moved her best architects up to work with them directly and rapidly forged a couple of beautiful actual cathedrals in St. Deikmorn and Rivermount, mostly making the frames and leaving the inner, inspired, artistic elements to others, under the guidance of the Angel and actual artist, Dina.

By now, Dina was leading others, including Angels Samantha had never even intended as artists, as well as cleromages specialized in creation and physical alteration magics.

Regardless of facts about the core public deity and her prominence, those sub-entities were not made obsolete. In the Land of Naug and amongst the Naugites in general, Puck was still favored under the umbrella of Samantha’s authority. So were Greenleaf and Stillwater in their respective areas. In Geirkos, it became a mix of all of those in the Pantheon, though the nobility were quick to convert to favoring the ‘true mother goddess,’ especially with certain figures finally coming out publicly to endorse her. Her core Followers, after all, had known the name in secret, per the cultic origins.

The cultic aspects simply adapted and evolved, without that technical big ‘name’ secret. The hierarchy, while not necessarily important for military ranks, still held strong, and the leaders continued their secret rites and consultations with each other. Did they sometimes make it a sex thing?

Of course they did.

More interestingly, the tarot deck had become a key element to cultic life in Geirkos and Catboro, the ‘interpreters of Fate’ leading people to inspired decisions.

Well. Samantha did smile on those who used the tarot, after all. How could she not?

Knock yourselves out, mortals.
