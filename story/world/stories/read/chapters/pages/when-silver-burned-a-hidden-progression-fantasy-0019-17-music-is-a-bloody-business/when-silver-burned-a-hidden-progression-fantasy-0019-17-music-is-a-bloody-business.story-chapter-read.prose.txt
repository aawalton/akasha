The fossegrim didn't look like a mythical being out of a legendary tome of otherworldly monstrosities. His face was that of an elderly man with long, gray hair and knotted beard, but from the chest down he was sporting a body that was tall and wired with muscles, resulting in an odd, unsettling appearance. His white shirt was crisp clean, and with matching trousers he might've been called fashionable in many cities of the Republic. He was holding a harp in the crook of his arm, the wooden instrument large enough to reach below his knee.

Like assessing a new toy, the fossegrim stared at Kalsin for an eternal second, then the wrinkles around his eyes stretched as he revealed a row of perfect teeth. "You don't know primball 'bout music, do you?"

The stories were greatly exaggerated, because his voice was rough and scratchy from disuse. Kalsin didn't think long about the answer, seeing no reason to lie. "Nah. I'm more of a pen-and-paper sort of fella myself."

"That so," the fossegrim said, sitting down with his legs crossed. He gestured for Kalsin to follow suit. "We'll remedy that in just a spell. That's the reason the sons of mankind call upon my services, neh? For talent to kindle talent."

Kalsin mirrored his position. He'd found it's often easier to seem agreeable to people if he copied their body language, acting in a similar way. Though he wasn't facing a human this time around, he figured it couldn't hurt. He opened his mouth to make conversation, but the forming words died upon seeing the instrument, which was now sitting in the fossegrim's lap.

Finely crafted, rich golden wood framed and held dozens of strings stretching from top to bottom at a slight angle, the elegant lines almost mercurial as the sun illuminated their surface, sparkling. The fossegrim brushed a finger along the harp’s curved, wave-shaped neck. "What, you never seen one of these before," he said, a statement instead of a question.

Kalsin had met a handful of bards during his travels, some with harps, but he'd always paid more attention to the person than the instrument. Needless to mention, he wasn't particularly fond of those experiences. Musicians tended to be an idealistic bunch, a pinch too grandiose, a touch too eloquent for his tastes. The type of people who, if given the choice, would save a thousand strangers instead of a single friend. Or so they liked to make others think they would.

After all, idealism is nothing more than loyalty to a cause rather than loyalty to the individuals in one's life. A twisted kind of mind-cockery, that.

Kalsin said, "Seen harps, yes. Held? Don't rightly remember. Played, not damn likely."

If the fossegrim found his attitude problematic, he didn't show it. He strung a chord, eliciting a pure sound way louder than it had any right to be. For that matter, Kalsin realized his voice was somehow staying above the waterfall's rumble without any effort. "Only those willing to learn can do so," the fossegrim said, whispering. He then snorted, looking up at Kalsin. "What a load of cowpiss. I'll have your fingers dancin' the time of their lives before the sun falls below the canopy." He extended the harp in his direction, inviting.

Kalsin, his cynical thoughts giving him pause, hesitated for a moment before reaching out and taking the harp in his hands. He pushed down the unbidden memory of Cinder's hand falling off like a discarded appendage when trying to strike a deal with a mythos.

No. This was a different time, a different mythos, a different situation. If he wanted to get out of this alive, he needed to follow his instincts. And his instincts were telling him to play along. Bide his time. Stall, stall until events fall in place and opportunity presents itself.

"Where do I start?" Kalsin asked, looking at the instrument like a snake about to bite him.

The monster wearing the old man's skin spoke with a surprisingly gentle tone then. "You don't. It started millennia ago, and it will continue until we're dust."

But Kalsin wasn't paying attention to the fossegrim anymore, because his fingers started gliding along the strings on their own. At first, they plucked at a string tentatively, almost fearfully, before moving on to another with a grain more confidence than before. With each passing minute, his awkward gestures smoothed out and became a steady, though still unskilled motions. And through it all, the harp remained completely silent, as if the instrument was full of noble dignity, waiting for hands competent enough to wield it.

The fossegrim nodded along to the unheard music, head lolling forward and back in a trance. Had Kalsin not been so baffled by his own body, he would've clocked the creepy fucker right then and there.

Minutes stretched into an hour, then another. Kalsin's fingers were sore from the constant playing that had quickened considerably by then, moving between strings with great agility. The spray of water had soaked his back entirely, and sweat was attempting the same around his neck. He tried to speak, only to realize he couldn't. Instead of words, the piercing chime of the harp came out of his mouth.

To say the next minutes were spent in a panicked struggle would've been true and false at the same time. Inside, Kalsin was screaming and thrashing as if his future depended on it, but to the outside observer he was singing an angelic cadence without words. There was no conscious thought to the performance, as if his body decided to travel abroad and absorb years of study in a renowned academy without bringing his mind along for the trip. The fossegrim squinted an eye open, and Kalsin swore he saw the tip of his tongue sneak about in preparation for a filling dinner.

Two things happened then. Kalsin’s fingers started bleeding, dripping down his skin and coating the strings in red as he played. He tried throwing the harp, arms extended as far away from him as possible, but his hands remained gripping the wooden frame and strumming the strings, dancing along without missing a beat. He felt no pain, as if the bleeding digits had different lives of their own.

And at the same time, Jack came rushing into the glade in the corner of his eyes, stopping at the lake’s shore with the azTéls a step behind. Kalsin turned toward her and shook his head, conveying all the confidence he didn't quite feel. But she had a message of her own as she glanced behind her with a grim expression, gripping the hilt of her sword.

Kalsin smiled, and apparently that was enough for Jack to let the next stage commence. She slowly retraced her steps into the forest, finding a place to hide nearby with the others.

Now, Kalsin just had to hope the fossegrim hated untimely interruptions. Preferably before his fingers became minced meat.

He didn't have to wait long until there was another sound different from the harp's symphony, which, interestingly enough, had been hushing the crash of the waterfall entirely. Leaves rattled, branches crunching under several pairs of feet, drawing closer and closer. A heavyset man stepped out from the forest, and judging from the mace in his hand, he didn't come to appreciate the birth of a magical bard. And he didn't come alone. Three men, an all too familiar straight-backed and slender woman, and finally Collector Quin arrived at the lakeside. Similarly to when Jack had appeared, the fossegrim didn't as much as glance at the intruders, still staring at Kalsin.

Not until Quin, without wasting time to utter a greeting, raised his crossbow and launched a bolt in their direction.

The fossegrim spoke a word, almost like singing a note, and the bolt tumbled to the side to disappear in the roiling waters. Staying seated, he set his eyes on the collector—and in that moment Kalsin finally got the first glance at the monster hiding under the skin of the old man. There was no humanity there. No rage or hatred promising retribution, no fear for his life; there was no emotion at all. But there was a great deal of finality. It wasn't a question for whom.

One does not become a collector without a great deal of perception. Realizing who was the more dangerous of the two figures sitting on the small slab-island, Quin tossed the empty crossbow at the ground and aimed a new one taken from one of his men directly at the fossegrim. But before he pulled the trigger, he laid his suddenly brown-flecked hand on the bolt.

Even from a good fifty feet away, Kalsin could see the bolt harden, swirling earth gathering, then rotating around it as if drawn by a powerful magnet. It was his power as a spark, a physical manifestation of the surge attuned to each person that only an overworlder could use.

The fossegrim wasn't idle in the meantime. Another word left his mouth, this one sharp as a knife. There was no fanfare, no projectile. A red, vertical slice appeared across the collector's chest. But not before Quin managed to launch his enhanced bolt over the lake. The quarrel sailed through the air, leaving a brown trail in its wake and when the mythos tried to deflect it, only pebbles scattered to the side. The bolt hit the fossegrim square in the shin. The earth around the bolt latched onto the flesh it touched, creeping over the fossegrim's lower body and fusing his crossed legs together like a living statue.

Taking that as their cue, Jack, Erazza, and Áron fell upon the disoriented hunting party, their cries drawn out by Kalsin's still ongoing song. He'd only now noticed his playing had become faster and faster, the music taking on a frantic speed with no regard to his body's ability to handle it. Dozens of paper cuts littered his fingers, spraying specks of red upon the rock surface as strings bit into his skin, catching on his flesh, straining and releasing pent up tension.

He focused all his willpower to make his fingers stop, but like begging the clouds to bring a downpour, it accomplished nothing. His legs were numb from sitting in place for so long. He could only turn his head and watch as Jack fought against two men at once, deflecting blows and delivering strikes simultaneously. Áron was pushing the swordswoman, Xil, back toward the forest while Erazza was locked into a melee of her own.

The only positive note was Quin, who had gone down looking like shredded bacon as he choked on his own blood, coloring the shore red around him.

Unfortunately, still sitting next to Kalsin with an impassive face, the fossegrim took that as an opportunity to look for his next target. Who happened to be the one closest to them, fighting ankle-deep in the lake. Erazza.

Kalsin redoubled his efforts to wrench back control over his body from the inanimate damn instrument. But nothing answered his call. Like with the surge all those years ago, he could do nothing but strain with useless effort while the people who depended on him suffered for his own failings. Only his arms moved, legs numb and fingers playing. He was useless, again. Defective, again.

Kalsin raised his arms, the harp with it, and decided he might as well continue the tradition of ruining everything he touched.

Crunch!

He brought down the harp on the fossegrim's head, the wood colliding hard with his skull. Kalsin gave him no opportunity to regain his senses as he connected another hit, this one glancing down his forehead and bashing into his nose with a sick snap. Each connecting hit was loud and clear, because Kalsin had stopped playing the moment he stunned the fossegrim.

His fingers weren't yet back at their full capacity, but he didn't need them as he clobbered a supernatural musician into a bloody paste of torn meat and brainmatter with his own magical harp. The wooden instrument couldn't withstand the rough treatment for long, splintering, breaking in two as the strings went slack, some tearing with a series of off-tune pangs. Strands of white hair stuck to Kalsin’s arms. Still he kept at it, taking out all his bitterness on the fossegrim’s no-longer so ominous carcass.

Destroying the harp and killing the mythos, both acts were just about equally satisfying.

He tossed away the ruined pieces of wood with disgust and took one hasty look through the fossegrim’s pockets. A successful, though messy job, all things considered. He couldn't see the deep-pearl ring anywhere that Cinder had mentioned a few days ago, but being alive was enough of a reward for him. Two breaths and a slimy brow wipe later, Kalsin deemed himself in good enough condition to brave the swim back to the shore, cleaning off the filth in the process.

Things had died down on the lakeside by the time Kalsin reached it. Unmoving bodies were sprawled across the glade, rivulets converging and coloring the waters a rustic red. Jack sat on a trunk, catching her breath as she gazed toward the sky with her eyes closed, sword and shield discarded as if she had lost the strength to hold them for a second longer. Instead of bugging her, Kalsin approached Áron who had Erazza tending to a nasty gash on his cheek.

"You missed one," Kalsin said.

Áron replied with something that sounded an awful like a hissed curse, scoring a discontent look from Erazza and a sharper pull of the sewing than necessary. "Your skill at music is nearly as good as your ability to state the obvious," she said without looking up from her work.

"Clenn swordslady got lucky," Áron said. "I had her, then she shoves her boot in my face like some gods-forsaken combat ballerina. The coward. She fled before I could regain my wits about me."

It was Kalsin's time to curse. If someone reported back directly to the Civil House, that would greatly accelerate the timeline of their wanted posters going public.

He was pacing back toward the lake when a chortling laugh interrupted him, coming from none other than the collector just a couple of feet to the side. Kalsin whirled around, but shouldn't have bothered; Quin was lying flat on the shore, his own fluids leaking from the countless slashes all over his body and caking him in the mud. He tried propping himself up, but all he managed was the feeble flailing of a grounded fish.

Kalsin knelt at his side, putting a calming hand on his shoulder. He didn't do it to grant some show of respect for a dying man, but in hopes of finding reassurance, for his own peace of mind. Quin might've been a proper bastard, but Kalsin had never intended for things to come this far. "Easy. Easy now, friend. You can rest. It's over."

"Oh no," Quin said with the intake of a wheezing breath. "Far from it… friend. There'll be no place to grant you refuge. No gloves will soften the blows the Republic is going to rain down on you and those you love. Not even the last crumbs of justice will stay reserved for your kind." His smile was adorned by blood seeping through the cracks of his teeth. "Long before this is over, you will have embraced misery."

Kalsin stared down at him. "You're probably right. You were before, you know. When you told me about the Republic tearing itself apart if a cure was discovered. If people caught word, if they discovered some power with the ability to cleanse the Scarrd, it would start a war so large and so monstrous in its destruction, the heavens themselves would weep for the next thousand years to come."

Kalsin leaned closer to the collector, whispering. "Only... I find myself out of fucks to give. I'll find a cure for myself so I can be finally reunited with my sister. I'll find the cure for Jack, because she damn well deserves it more than any dirt I've ever met in my life. And to do it, I'll trudge through waist-deep paste of the collective rotting corpses of both the guilty and the innocent."

The collector was still enough to make Kalsin think he was gone already, but he roused one last time. "Forward my greetings to the lead torturer of the Civil Hhh.."

He choked on the last syllable.

Kalsin watched as life departed from a man who, for however little time, hunted him based on nothing but principles created by his own circumstances. Kalsin understood him. He even sympathized with him, to a small extent. But his melancholy was short lived, because he knew Quin was just another piece out of the long line of obstacles standing between him and his goal.

Speaking of his goal. He had a pair of heretics to finally give some much needed answers.
