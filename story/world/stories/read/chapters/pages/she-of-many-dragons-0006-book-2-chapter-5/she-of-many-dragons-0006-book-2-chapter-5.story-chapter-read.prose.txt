Alice's emotions were in a storm. She exited the city, walking so fast that she was almost running.

She had always known that her class—the class that had brought her darling dragons and friends—was powerful. She had never let herself dwell on it, but now she could see so very clearly how her class could be used for evil. Her dragons were utterly loyal to her, and their abilities mirrored what she needed at that exact moment. What if she ever needed something vile?

"Alice—" Prim began.

"I don't want to talk about it," Alice said.

She felt Prim shrink back at her sharp tone, and that only made her feel worse. The worst part was that Alice longed to voice her fears with her dragons, but they would only reassure her that they loved her. That was very nice, but the last thing she needed was reassurance.

She needed truth.

"Alice," Iggy rumbled after a bit, "what shall we do about Orris?"

Orris? Her thoughts screeched to a halt, and she looked back at the city. From here, it was hard to spot the buildings within the overgrown vegetation. It simply looked like a split canyon that had risen out of the landscape.

Should she go back for the blind dragon?

Alice reluctantly shook her head. "No, he made his choice clear. If he will not join us, I will not push."

Strangely enough, voicing that decision out loud made her feel a little better. She wasn't completely evil... was she?

Perhaps she could share some small portion of her worries with her dragons.

She sighed. "If we have learned this about the... the He of Many Dragons, others may have too, in the past. Or it may be in libraries, history books." She shook her head in derision. “Maybe it's something common that all children learn in school. I wouldn't know."

She could feel herself starting to catastrophize and forced herself to stop.

"Alice, if it were that widely known, we would have heard about it by now, surely," Prim said.

"Oh, I know!" Numi said. "Perhaps you can create a sub-aspect for me who could learn these things for us—someone who could do research for you."

That earned her glares from the other dragons.

"If anybody is to get a sub-aspect, it should be me," Prim said.

They were not about to have this argument all over again. Besides, Alice had to admit that Numi wasn't wrong. "There are no libraries here, but perhaps once we visit one in another city..." she said, then shook her head. "No, my concern is who knows what and how they may act once they learn of my class."

Now she was doubly glad to be away from the Protectorate Guild, though she was aware that they could find her if they ever chose. Could they take her dragons away from her?

That thought stopped her breath in her throat.

"Alice?" Prim asked, pecking her cheek lightly.

Alice closed her eyes. Then she made a decision.

"Iggy, you must fight to grow in levels." This was not a question, though the red dragon nodded. "Go," Alice said, "seek out any dangerous creature—"

Natura squeaked out a protest.

"Ask Natura what is most destructive to the forest," Alice amended, "and then eliminate it."

She turned to the little green dragon. "Numi, I want you to range out and see what treasures the forest may have hidden."

"Really?" The little dragon acted as if Alice had just given her a wonderful present, and Alice felt momentarily guilty for keeping her—and all of them—so very, very close. She had to trust them to grow strong without her.

"Really," she said, "appraise everything, and find what secrets this forest has."

She turned to her newest dragon. “Natura, work with Iggy to identify what can and should be destroyed, but I also want you to focus on every skill that you can." This forest on the other side of the city was not thriving, and many of the trees had browned branches, and some had yellowed completely as if an early fall were coming. “I have a feeling that this place needs some care.”

"It does," Natura agreed, and put a bark-like front paw on one of the nearby trunks. "There is a beetle that is decimating these trees, and the river… it needs help.”

"Then go out and do what you can for it," she said. Then she turned to her last dragon. "Prim, I—"

"I will stay with you," Prim said fiercely. "I grow stronger by giving you more dragons and managing the others, and you've set them to appropriate tasks. Besides, I don't think that you should be alone."

"Spark will stay with you, too," Iggy said.

She had a feeling that Iggy would not be talked out of that, and she had to admit that she felt a little better with the lightning-elemented sub-aspect by her side.

She looked at them all. "Anybody who knows of Orris’ story will be our enemy. If this He of Many Dragons has any descendants, they will certainly be our enemy as well. I need you all to grow stronger, my darlings. Quickly.”

Iggy stared down at the wriggling mass of eels in the hole before him. Scarlet red and faintly slimy, he had never beheld anything so disgusting before.

He looked from them over at Natura, who sat nearby. Her calm expression didn't change, even when one eel flopped out of the hole and tried to nip at one of her toes. She merely flicked the creature away and tossed it back in the hole, to the outraged hisses of the others. She was the one who had uncovered the mass of eels for him.

"What are they?" Iggy asked at last.

She startled as if coming out of a daydream. "Oh, it's not obvious?"

"No," he said, "but I can tell that they are not good to eat."

"They are quite toxic," she agreed. "These are blood-sapping eels. Once introduced to a stream, they eat every living thing within it. This stand of water is all but sterilized." Another wet flop as another eel tried its luck, followed by an angry hiss as she curtly knocked the eel back into its hole again. She continued, "Except for the eels, of course. Once they eat everything else, they turn into cannibals to sustain the population. But as you can see, this is breeding season."

Iggy gave another disgusted look into the hole. "Is that what they're doing?"

"A mating ball, yes." She looked at him with expectation. "Feel free to set fire to them at any time. I certainly won't mind."

Iggy used one hind foot to scratch the back of his neck. "It is only... It doesn't seem very sporting to slay the helpless."

She bristled. "Did you miss the fact that they have killed this river system?"

Iggy looked around. Come to think of it, nothing, not even grass, grew along the river's bank, and every tree more than five feet out from the water was gray with death. No bugs even skipped along the surface.

While he was silent, Natura built up her indignation. "Animals can't even drink around here. The eels jump and bite at their faces and mouths, and if it is small, or exceptionally unlucky, the eels haul the creatures in and feast."

At this, Iggy perked up. "Are they venomous or poisonous?"

She thought for a moment. Natura was young, but smart. "Only poisonous," she decided. "Their bite is not special. The danger is that where one leaps, others will as well. Enough that latch on can drag even a small deer under the water."

"Pah, I am no deer! This is a perfect opportunity to practice my ultimate armor!"

She rolled her eyes. "Before you do that, exterminate these vermin first. They're not even supposed to be here. They are invasive," she added, on a plea.

"Very well," Iggy sent a breath of fire out, and the eels caught at once as they seemed exceptionally flammable. They died with squeals and a terrible stink.

Iggy turned toward the river. "Now that's done, I will go fight—"

"Not so fast." Natura gestured, and the mud thinned in places up and down the bank to uncover at least a dozen more balls that were hidden just under the surface.

Suddenly, the idea of a deer being dragged under wasn't too far-fetched. There could be hundreds or more in this small section alone.

"Ah, there are quite a few," he allowed.

"Better get started," she agreed.

Numi was ecstatic to be sent into the forest to explore all on her own. Though she doubted there would be many coins, one never knew. She would love to find a delicious—er, bountiful—cache that had somehow been untouched by the elements for all these years. Perhaps there were other hidden treasures too, like the journal.

Thinking about it, Numi let out a sigh. She regretted that what had been written inside the journal had made Alice sad.

Prim certainly wasn't happy with her and had pulled mean faces at Numi whenever Alice wasn't looking. But she also knew that knowledge was a kind of treasure, too. Alice missing out on that would have been a shame.

Perhaps she could improve Alice's mood if she found something truly special.

With that in mind, Numi took the opposite track of Iggy and Natura, not wanting to get in the path of either an obsessive fighter or someone who constantly preached about the virtues of the forest.

Striding away from the water and to the east, she traveled in a grid-like pattern to make sure that she missed nothing. However, there was no sign of an old civilization, no secrets or delicious coins or rare metals. Just the scent of disgusting rotting wood and mud.

Right up until there wasn't.

Her nose caught something vaguely familiar, and yet she couldn't quite place a claw on what it was.

The scent was coming from the other side of a large oak tree—one of the few that looked healthy with a broad trunk and a leafy crown. Coming around it, she found that the scent led her between two large roots.

She dug, happily throwing clods of dirt in all directions. A few feet down, she found...bone? Ooooh, lots and lots of bones.

Not interesting, sadly, or particularly exceptional at all, except that they were human and rather small.

She dug more carefully as she did not wish to throw the bones anywhere. That might attract predators.

Then there, resting tucked up against the skeleton's ribs, was an egg.

A dragon's egg.

Numi reared back, blinking her big yellow eyes.

"Um..."

Quickly kicking leaves back over the hole to cover it, she turned tail and scuttled back to tell Alice.
