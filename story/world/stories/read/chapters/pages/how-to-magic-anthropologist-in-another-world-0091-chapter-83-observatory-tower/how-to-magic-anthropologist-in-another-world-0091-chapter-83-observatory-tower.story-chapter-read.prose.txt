Feeling relaxed after I visited the salon, I returned to the top of the observatory with another teleport and immediately flopped onto my chaise lounge.

Above me, the endless night of the Sunless Reach glittered with countless stars. Every few minutes, another shooting star crossed the heavens, filling me with Star mana. I decided to take a thirty-minute nap after all the teleporting I had done today.

When I woke up, I stretched lazily and glanced around the rooftop.

"Huh."

I had expected Calr to be waiting for me by now.

Instead, the rooftop was completely empty.

The tower was probably one of the greatest surviving relics of the ancient Telepaths. If anyone was going to lose track of time exploring it, it would be Calr.

I smiled to myself. Maybe I should offer him a room once I renovate the place, but that would mean I would have to teleport him in and out of Hano every time.

"Yeah, nah, I'll pass," I grumbled. "That would be far too inconvenient, especially in a world without phones."

Rather than searching floor by floor, I decided to save myself the trouble.

"I wish I was next to Calr."

The world shifted, and a heartbeat later I found myself standing on the tower's ground floor beside the buried entrance.

Calr was kneeling beside the collapsed doorway, brushing dirt away from something buried beneath centuries of rubble.

"Oh." He glanced up briefly. "Alice. You're back."

"I am." I looked around at the piles of broken stone surrounding him. "What are you doing?"

"I got curious."

I stepped closer and saw that he had cleared away a surprising amount of debris. Beneath the collapsed masonry lay the top half of a massive metal door that had once sealed the tower's entrance before the ceiling above it had collapsed.

The ancient metal was dark with age but showed remarkably little rust.

"Ready to go home?" I asked.

"In a second."

He didn't move.

"Before that, I wanted to ask you something," he said.

"What is it?"

Instead of answering, Calr pointed toward an engraving carved into the stone floor just before the doorway.

"Have you ever seen this symbol before?"

I crouched beside him.

The carving immediately caught my attention.

It was a septagram enclosed within a perfect circle. Unlike the other carvings throughout the tower, this one wasn't merely etched into the stone. The grooves had been carefully inlaid with tiny colored crystals that still shimmered faintly after more than three thousand years.

It reminded me of the crystal sun in the observatory's heliocentric model. Although here, each point of the seven-pointed star held different color crystals.

Red. Orange. Yellow. Green. Cyan. Blue. Purple.

The colors formed a perfect rainbow inside the circle.

I studied it for several seconds before slowly shaking my head.

"I don't think I've ever seen it before."

I looked back at him.

"Why?"

"I have."

"You have?"

He nodded.

"Well..." He scratched the back of his neck. "Kitame did."

I blinked.

"Oh."

"I saw it through one of her memories."

My curiosity immediately grew.

"Where?"

"On the cover of a book." He hesitated. "A book that was in Donas's hands."

Donas was the leader of the Old Realm cult, a former freelancer who had betrayed Hano.

"That sounds important."

"It is."

Calr's face grew noticeably more serious.

"The book originally belonged to Taka Agame."

"Oh, Raik's father."

"The Savior of Hano." He nodded.

For a moment, neither of us spoke. I found myself staring at the colorful symbol again. I really needed to find a history book about Taka someday. His name kept appearing everywhere. Yet the only time I tried asking Raik about his father, he quietly changed the subject. It was obvious there was a painful story there.

"I wonder what was in the book."

"I've wondered the same thing."

An idea crossed my mind.

"Want me to teleport and grab it?"

Calr's head snapped toward me.

"What?"

"I mean..." I shrugged. "If the book still exists, I could just wish to appear next to it."

His eyes widened in genuine alarm.

"Don't even joke about that."

"I wasn't joking."

He stood up immediately.

"Alice."

His voice carried far more weight than usual.

"That book is almost certainly still in Donas's possession," he said as he took a step toward me, alarm written across his face.

"Or locked inside one of the most secure vaults in the Contested Realm," he continued. "If it's with Donas, you're teleporting directly beside one of the strongest people alive."

"If it's in a vault, there could be curses, soul-binding restrictions, alarm enchantments, or any number of magical defenses waiting for thieves."

"If it's that dangerous, I doubt my wish would take hold. I'm not suicidal," I said, defending my stupid suggestion.

"Why even risk it?" he asked. "It's not worth it."

I shrugged.

He continued staring at me.

"Fine." I sighed dramatically. "I won't do it."

Calr kept watching me for another few seconds anyway, trying to decide whether I actually meant it. Eventually, he relaxed.

"Good."

He brushed the remaining dust from his clothes.

"Now let's go home."

I nodded.

"After we climb back to the roof," I said, pointing upward. "I need another shooting star."

"Oh. Right," he said.

Together we climbed the observatory's spiral staircase once more. I guess all the training paid off because I wasn't even winded when we emerged beneath the endless stars.

I leaned against the stone railing and waited. It only took another minute before a shooting star streaked across the heavens, and Star mana flowed into my soul. I used that mana to bank a return wish before I walked over and wrapped one arm around Calr's shoulder.

He stiffened slightly. Then he looked at me for the first time since I had returned.

"You fixed your hair."

"I did."

I grabbed one of the ends and held it up.

"What do you think?"

"It's shorter than before."

"Unfortunately." I sighed. "I didn't have much choice."

He looked at it for another second.

"It looks nice."

I smiled.

"Thank you."

He immediately looked away.

"You're welcome."

Another shooting star crossed overhead. I felt my Star mana reach full capacity.

"So," I said. "Where do you want me to drop you off?"

"Near Sara's Soulbook shop is fine."

"Straight to your sister."

"Yeah."

I tightened my grip on his shoulder.

"Ready?"

He nodded.

"I wish we were standing outside Sara's Soulbook shop."

The rooftop disappeared, and a heartbeat later, we stood outside the familiar storefront.

Calr took one step away from me before realizing I was watching him.

His ears had turned slightly red.

"Say hi to Sara for me." I grinned.

"I will."

"And tell her I'll stop by soon."

He nodded without meeting my eyes.

"I will."

I gave him one last wave before my return wish activated.

The freelancer guild courtyard vanished, and once again I found myself standing beneath the endless stars atop my future home.

I leaned against the observatory's stone railing and considered what to do next while waiting for my Star mana to be fully restored.

I could return to Hano and start the paperwork for claiming the tower.

I could spend a few more hours exploring here.

Or...

A smile slowly spread across my face.

"Nina."

If anyone would appreciate an ancient ruin built by the Telepaths, it would be her.

I raised my spear as the next shooting star crossed the heavens.

"I wish I was next to Nina."

The world folded around me.

I had expected to arrive inside Nina's familiar workshop.

Instead, I found myself standing inside an industrial building unlike anything I had seen before.

The left half was a forge. Several furnaces glowed orange as apprentices pumped bellows while two smiths hammered glowing metal around cylindrical molds. The rhythmic ringing of steel echoed through the building, accompanied by the hiss of quenching water and the smell of coal smoke.

The right half belonged to the glassworkers.

Long tables were covered with polished lenses, glass blanks, brass measuring tools, and racks filled with cooling crystals. Two men carefully ground lenses against rotating polishing wheels while a woman I recognized as one of Nina's apprentices inspected each finished piece beneath a bright enchanted lamp.

It was busy, with more than half a dozen people working together, each focused on a different step of the same project.

The moment Nina spotted me, her entire face lit up.

"Alice!"

Before I could even wave, one of her wings wrapped around my shoulders and pulled me into an enthusiastic side hug.

"You're back!"

"I am." I laughed. "Good to see you too."

Only then did I notice another familiar face.

Standing near one of the workbenches was Tan Je'e, Ja'a's sister.

The merchant greeted me with her usual confident grin while speaking with a young black-haired craftsman whom I also recognized as Nina's other apprentice.

"It's finished," Nina announced proudly before I could ask.

She spread both wings dramatically.

"I'm finally handing over the spyglass project."

"Really?"

She nodded excitedly.

"I can finally focus on something new." Her eyes sparkled. "I'm thinking of a musical instrument, perhaps."

"I thought you said your apprentices weren't ready yet."

"That's exactly it!" she laughed.

Her wings tightened around me again.

"After everything you showed me in your dream sanctuary, I realized we had been approaching the entire project the wrong way."

"Oh?"

"I kept trying to teach them exactly how I did it, and in doing so, I had glassmakers act like smiths for half the time."

She pointed toward the forge, then toward the glass workshop.

"So instead, we hired journeyman smiths to build the tubes, the mounts, and the stands. The glassmakers only make lenses."

Finally, she pointed toward the young craftsman beside Tan Je'e.

"And Jonna coordinates everything."

The young man bowed politely.

"It's an honor."

"That was fast." I frowned. "It has been less than eighty-four hours since we dreamed together. How did you have time to build all this?"

"No, this workshop has been in the works from the start, as my own place was never suited for mass production," Nina explained. "The new addition was hiring the smiths and a couple more glassworkers."

I nodded in understanding. So the new development was that, instead of making every craftsman work on his own telescope just like Nina had done, she had divided the work between specialists. It was practically an assembly line. Not quite modern manufacturing yet, but definitely a huge step in that direction.

I turned toward Tan Je'e.

"So everything is working well?"

"It is."

The merchant's smile somehow became even wider.

"We brought the first products, the ones made by Nina, Jonna, and Mira, to the market yesterday."

"And?"

"They sold out."

"Already?"

She nodded.

"We've also received advance orders."

"How many?"

"Just over five hundred."

I blinked.

"Five hundred?"

"The demand is much higher than we expected," Je'e smiled. "Luckily, the current configuration is better suited to handle it."

"How much are you selling them for?"

"Four silver each."

I turned toward Nina.

"How much do they cost to make?"

"Around ten bronze in materials."

"Closer to fifteen if you include labor," Jonna corrected her.

I quickly did the math.

"That's..." I gasped. "Around sixteen times the production cost."

"For now." Tan Je'e nodded. "I expect competition to appear sooner or later."

"The price will gradually fall over the next year." She folded her hands behind her back. "I imagine it will stabilize around one silver."

"That still sounds incredibly profitable," I said.

"It is." Je'e didn't even try to hide her satisfaction.

"So..." I looked back toward Nina. "Does this mean you are free to help me with something?"

"Technically..." she admitted. "I'm supposed to supervise Jonna's transition for another month."

"But honestly..." She looked toward the young foreman. "He's already doing an excellent job."

Jonna scratched the back of his neck, clearly embarrassed by the compliment.

"So..." I tried to sound as casual as possible. "Hypothetically..."

Nina narrowed her eyes at me.

"What if I happened to discover an ancient telescope built by the Telepaths?"

The room fell silent.

"And what if I wanted you to restore it?"

Nina froze.

Jonna froze too.

Both of them stared at me.

"You found an ancient Telepath telescope?"

"Maybe."

Nina grabbed both my shoulders.

"Give me," she pleaded. "Please."

Jonna nearly stumbled forward.

"Can I see it too?"

"It's too large to move," I shrugged. "So I have to take you there."

Nina didn't even hesitate.

"Do it."

I smiled.

"I thought you'd say that."

I summoned my spear and wrapped my hand around Nina's waist just below her wings.

"I wish we were standing on top of the ancient observatory."

The workshop vanished, and a heartbeat later we appeared beneath the endless night sky of the Sunless Reach.

Nina gasped, watching the stars stretch endlessly overhead while another shooting star crossed the heavens. For a long moment, she simply stood there, overwhelmed by the view.

Then she saw the telescope.

"No..."

Her mouth fell open.

Without another word, she leaped into the air. It was the first time I had ever seen Nina truly fly outside the dream sanctuary. She wasn't nearly as graceful as Obora, nor as fast as the Valkyren arena fighter I had once watched.

But she flew well enough to circle the enormous instrument from every angle, landing on the brass framework before taking off again to inspect another section.

She ran both hands across the engraved metal, examined the gears, and peered through the cracked eyepiece.

"This is impossible," she whispered, touching one of the ancient runes with almost reverent care.

I smiled to myself.

She was going to be occupied for a while. Rather than interrupt her, I teleported back to the workshop.

Jonna looked at me expectantly.

"Ready?"

He nodded.

Tan Je'e folded her arms.

"If examining that relic gives you ideas for improving the spyglass..." She looked directly at Jonna. "Wait at least six months before implementing them."

Jonna blinked.

"Why?"

"Because if you suddenly release dramatically improved spyglasses right after announcing the current design," she smiled knowingly, "everyone will be reluctant to buy it, thinking an even better third design is still in the works."

He slowly nodded.

"I understand."

"Would you like me to take you as well?"

"Nah." She waved dismissively. "I'm far too busy. I'll be holding the fort here today. I'll leave the engineering to you three."

With that, she returned to discussing trade contracts with one of her aides.

I wrapped one arm around Jonna.

"Ready?"

His eyes lit up.

"I've been born ready."

A moment later, we vanished.

When we reappeared atop the observatory, Nina was already halfway inside the telescope's tubing where the glass lenses were supposed to be.

She looked like someone who had just discovered buried treasure.

"This is enchanted!" Nina shouted excitedly.

"I know!"

She pointed deeper into the mechanism.

"No!" she shouted back. "You don't understand!"

She practically disappeared into the framework before poking her head back out.

"This thing isn't like anything modern."

She flew down from the telescope, landing beside me with her wings spread wide in excitement.

"You think you can fix it?" I asked hopefully.

"Fix it?" she repeated, sounding almost offended by the question. "Alice, this thing is a masterpiece. It's almost a living thing."

She reached into her bag and carefully pulled out a thin sheet that looked almost like parchment, except the paper shimmered with a faint cyan color that reminded me of clear skies.

"Look."

She held it toward me.

"What is it?"

"It's mana-sensitive paper."

"Of course, that's a thing," I said. "You people are obsessed with paper."

"Well, duh." Nina laughed. "Paper is awesome."

She lightly tapped the sheet with one finger.

"This changes depending on the ambient mana, and cyan means Sky mana."

"I could have told you that. This is the Sunless Reach. Everything here is saturated with Sky mana."

She placed the paper on the telescope, and the glow grew even brighter.

"This telescope is soaked in the stuff," she said, tapping it with the paper. "Which means every repair we make has to match that level of affinity."

"Oh."

"If we replace damaged components with ordinary materials, the enchantments won't circulate mana properly."

"So we'd end up weakening the whole device."

"Or ruin the enchantment as a whole."

"I did recover the Sky Elemental core." I reached into my Bag of Holding and pulled out the glowing sphere. "Would this help?"

Nina's eyes widened.

"Oh." She carefully accepted the core with both hands. "This is perfect."

"It contains an enormous amount of refined Sky mana."

She turned it over several times before nodding enthusiastically.

"We can probably use this to boost the mana capacity of our materials."

That reminded me of something else.

"What about these?"

I pulled out several dark translucent fragments from the Elemental's shattered outer shell.

The pieces looked like broken glass shards, with faint light occasionally dancing through them like stars in the night.

Nina carefully examined one, holding it toward the stars, watching it twinkle, and tapping it with a fingernail before handing it to Jonna.

"What do you think?" I asked.

"I honestly don't know." She smiled. "But that's the exciting part."

She rubbed one fragment between her fingers.

"If this melts, we might be able to mix it into fresh glass."

"You think it would inherit the Sky affinity?"

"Maybe." She shrugged. "Most likely, but it needs testing."

"We've never worked with anything like this before," said Jonna, his eyes bright with excitement.

Another idea suddenly entered my mind.

"Wait," I said. "Glass is mostly silica, right?"

"Yes." Nina nodded.

"I have an idea."

Without waiting any longer, I summoned my spear.

"I wish I was standing on top of silica-rich rock here in the Sunless Reach."

The world blinked, and a heartbeat later I found myself standing atop a rocky plateau less than a kilometer from the observatory, the tower still dominating the horizon behind me.

Scattered across the ground were enormous black volcanic boulders surrounded by shattered stone.

I crouched beside one and pulled out my phone.

Using the flashlight, I shone the beam across the broken surface.

Tiny points of light glittered back at me.

"There you are."

Silica.

It wasn't pure, obviously, but it was definitely present in large amounts.

I pulled out my trusty shovel.

Over the next fifteen minutes, I chipped away at the rock until I had filled an entire hemp sack with fragments.

It was heavy enough that I had to drag it closer before lifting it into my Bag of Holding.

Satisfied, I teleported back to the observatory.

Once there, I took out a few of the rocks and handed them to Nina.

"What's that?"

I dumped several more stones onto the rooftop.

"Silica-rich rocks."

I held one beneath my phone's light, and the tiny reflective crystals sparkled.

"Glass doesn't have to come from sand, right?" I said. "It just needs silica."

She examined the rock I had given her.

"You're right," she said. "This isn't pure, but it could work."

"More importantly," I said, "it's from here, so it must have a lot of Sky mana."

Jonna's eyes widened.

"I never even considered using mountain stone."

"It'll require more processing," Nina said while examining another sample. "We'll have to separate the impurities first."

"But..." She grinned. "It should absolutely work."

For the next hour, the three of us examined the telescope some more, discussing every piece of machinery in detail.

I explained everything I remembered about gears, bearings, leverage, and mechanical tolerances from Earth.

Nothing especially advanced. It was hardly my field of expertise, after all. Just the sort of engineering principles everyone took for granted back home.

Nina, meanwhile, explained how the enchantment didn't use Soulscribing or even divine blessings, but instead relied on natural mana flow like a living thing.

Jonna barely spoke. Instead, he followed us everywhere, carrying a notebook, writing as quickly as his hand could move. Occasionally, he would stop us to ask a question, then immediately scribble down the answer.

"I should probably take Jonna back," I said. "It is probably evening outside the Reach."

He reluctantly looked away from the telescope.

"Already?"

"You still have a workshop to run," I said.

"I suppose." He sighed.

I wrapped an arm around his shoulder.

A moment later, we were back inside the workshop.

Tan Je'e glanced up from a stack of ledgers.

"So?"

"Nina isn't coming back tonight," I said.

"I figured as much." The merchant didn't even seem surprised. She simply returned to her paperwork, giving me a dismissive wave.

With that, I teleported back to the observatory one more time.

Nina hadn't moved very far.

She was still sitting beside the telescope, sketching diagrams beneath the light of the stars.

"So..." I walked over beside her. "Want to spend the night here?"

She looked around at the endless sky.

"It is a little cold."

"It will never rain."

"Sure." She smiled. "As long as I get to cuddle you for warmth."

"Sure," I said. "Your wings are lovely."

"Oh." I paused. "I almost forgot."

I reached into my Bag of Holding and pulled out the star maps Calr and I had made during our previous observations, followed by the telescope she had made herself.

"We've already identified quite a few planets using your first telescope."

"Really? Is it working well for you?" she asked excitedly. "You've been charting the sky?"

"We tried."

We spent the rest of the evening stargazing, and when hunger finally interrupted us, I simply teleported to Hano, bought some kebab sticks from a food vendor, then picked up a large futon from a furniture shop before returning in less than ten minutes.

If I were going to own an ancient observatory, spending the first night beneath its endless stars felt appropriate.
