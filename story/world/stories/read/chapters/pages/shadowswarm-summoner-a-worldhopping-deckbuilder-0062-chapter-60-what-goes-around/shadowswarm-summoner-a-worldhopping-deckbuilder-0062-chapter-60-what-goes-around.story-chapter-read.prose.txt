Chapter 60: What Goes Around

Luthor was mostly okay with waiting. A lot of life was spent waiting around on others, or for the right opportunity or moment. Patience was a virtue, and a huge part of discipline and restraint.

That said, he didn’t enjoy waiting when they were in enemy territory, in a high end place where they obviously didn’t belong, with a prisoner and a friendly molekin who belonged even less than he did. As the minutes ticked by, he started to sweat, and not just from the city’s position over an ocean of magma.

“Why is the city built over all this magma again?” Luther finally asked, if only to distract himself from waiting. They were seated on a bench about a hundred yards away from Javon’s Emporium. Up here in the Hermanic District, there were far more guards, and one seemed to be watching them from afar.

“Does it matter?” Jack was in a worse mood than before. No one would’ve liked being a prisoner, but sitting and waiting in the heat, unable to go and get a drink or do something stimulating…Well, the kid needed better discipline. He’d get there, some day.

“It does to me,” Luthor said evenly.

Mulch cleared his throat, nose wiggling with stress. “That expedition we all teamed up for? This was the spot where we went both high and low. When we went low, we hit the magma. And straight up above us…” He looked up, beady eyes squinting towards the top of the cavern that was shrouded in darkness. “The surface is up there, and whatever monsters our people woke up.

“When the Hermanic Empire came, they dropped in from the surface and said they were fightin’ the monsters. The ones we unleashed. This was the biggest battleground, and it left a hell of a crater when everythin’ was said and done. So they rebuilt here, to be ready just in case the surface gets dangerous again.”

“And you actually believe it?” Jack sneered.

“Of course not,” Mulch scoffed. “Just saying what the official story is. What crawled up your ass, son?”

“I’m not your son,” he said immediately.

Luthor held up a hand, stalling the impending fight. “There’s no point in fighting. Jack, by this time tomorrow, you won’t be a prisoner anymore. I’m not asking you to like us, but I am telling you to behave, or -- “

“Or what?” he demanded, standing. His fists were balled at his sides, and he leaned in with all the reckless abandon of a teen who hasn’t been punched in the face enough to know not to do that. “Your friend is going to throw me off the side of the city? Or will you?”

Ahh, that made sense. Jack had been mostly silent since that threat. Luthor nodded grimly. “I’m sorry about that, Jack. Ambrose is -- “

“A piece of shit?”

Mulch let out a surprised guffaw. “Doesn’t seem inaccurate, all things considered.”

A thin sliver of anger worked its way under Luthor’s normally sturdy armor. “I have no intention of making excuses for him. He’s abrasive, rude, borderline sadistic, and also someone who tends to make the right choices when it matters. And for what it’s worth, I would never have let him hurt you.”

“Even if I snitched?” Jack crossed his arms over his chest.

This question seemed to matter more than the words indicated. He deserved honesty, and sometimes it was ugly.

“If we wanted you dead, we would’ve left you for the ghouls or spiders. If you snitched, I wouldn’t want you dead. Nor would most of my friends. But…if you were to turn us in, we would be executed. If you did that, I don’t think I would be able to stop Ambrose from killing you. At that point, Raeva would probably help him, come to think of it.

“So, for just another day, one more day, please sit tight and do not test anyone’s patience. I want to get you to safety, Jack. Don’t make my job any harder than it needs to be.” Luthor even tried smiling. Just a small one. He wasn’t an unhappy person, but smiling always seemed like such an effort. Just a performance for others unless he allowed himself to actually relax.

At first, he thought it might’ve worked. The teenager’s glare softened a little, and he looked down at his feet. His fists unfurled, and the boy took a deep breath. He even smiled.

Luthor’s gut twisted, warning him only a second before Jack took off running towards the guard watching them from afar.

Alarm flared, but not panic. There had always been a chance of this. The circumstances were about as bad as they could get, but Luthor motioned for Mulch to follow him. He walked, not ran, towards Javon’s Emporium, where a line stretched a ways. He ignored them and headed straight for the door.

“Back of the line, pal,” said the guard, putting his hand on the sword at his belt but not drawing it.

“I don’t want to enter, I need to pass a message onto my friend, who’s inside.”

The other guard laughed in his face. “Not going to happen. Get out of here, and take the mole with you.”

Honestly, it was a wonder they’d only been stopped twice, and the excuse of Mulch being a guide had barely worked. There was no winning here.

Luthor nodded and backed up. He looked off in the distance. Jack was about to reach the guard, who now walked to him. It was too far to see details, but he thought he could imagine the suspicion turning to concern, and then the rush that came before a fight or hunt.

“What do we do?” Mulch asked, wring his shovelhead-like claws together. “We’re worms after a long rain!”

His pulse picked up, a tapdance in his chest. If it did come down to a fight, he had no clue how Mulch would be able to handle himself without cards. Luthor himself worked best as part of a team, and outlasted his opponents and took care of his allies. Impossible to do if he was the only one fighting.

The door to the store opened, and Ambrose and Raeva strode out, beaming. They cleared the entrance before stopping, smiles dropping at his expression.

“We have a problem,” said Luthor, control slipping just a bit.

Ambrose cursed and looked over, to where Jack was pointing right at them. “I’m going to kill him. And none of you are stopping me this time.”

Raeva of all people seized the moment. “C’mon, this way,” she hissed, and strode past all of them. Luthor instinctively followed, Mulch at his side. Ambrose took up the rear, and they moved away from the Emporium, and back where they came.

“Are we seriously just going to walk away like nothing happened?” Ambrose complained as they wound their way through the Hermanic Quarter, past restaurants and into a crowd standing outside a store, where a man gave out little slips of paper.

“Yeah,” said Raeva, pushing her way through for all of them. “First they have to believe the little shit. That might buy us a few minutes.”

People shot them dirty looks, or at least to Mulch, but no one moved to stop them on their way to the stairs down. Luthor focused on his breathing, cursing his momentary weakness. The important thing now was finding Jessica and Raeleq, and then finding a place to hide for a bit, until they could safely board the trains and get the hell out of there.

That hope lasted until they made it to the next level. A crackle in the hair pricked Luthor’s ears, and then a voice spoke, magically enhanced and broadcasted across the city.

“Attention Bedrock Spire. Remain calm, but there are intruders here: fugitives wanted by the Emperor. Guards will be conducting sweeps, so please remain calm and remain indoors until their job is done. And remember, if you see something, say something. Be safe, and praise Emperor Turhan!”

“Aw, shit,” Mulch groaned.

“Run?” Raeva asked.

“No!” Luthor said before she could. “We need to act normal. All they said is intruders, and sweeps. Do you know anything about the sweeps, Mulch?”

The molekin winced and rubbed the back of his head. “Yeah. They start from the bottom and the top and work their ways to the middle. Twice sometimes. Look, I might be able to get us a way out of here. Shit, I didn’t want to have to call in this favor, but…Do you trust me?”

“Yes,” said Luthor.

“I guess,” said Raeva.

“Not like we have a choice,” Ambrose grumbled, eyes darting around wildly.

So far, no guards accosted them. The people they saw looked more bored and annoyed by the alarm than anything else. Chances were it was a routine thing. And if they were going to start the sweeps on opposite ends, they had time.

“Whatever you need to do, do it,” said Luthor, grimacing. “We’ll meet where we agreed, near the trains.”

“Ah, right, the plan we all agreed to. The plan that Jack knows about.” Ambrose covered his face with his hands. “Should’ve let him die when I had the chance.”

“We need to find the others,” Raeva insisted. “Whatever happens, we need to be together to face this. Whether that’s escape, or…”

Out of all of them, Luthor knew only he and Raeva had dreams of a heroic death. A warrior’s death. Being butchered on a foreign world while on the run wasn’t the most honorable way to go, but fighting until one dropped was…well, better than just being executed, at least.

“Awright, you guys can doom and gloom all you want, I’m getting help,” said Mulch with a nervous chuckle. He and Luthor grasped each other by the forearm, and shared a nod of understanding. This wasn’t over yet.

“When this is all over, you two really should get a room,” Raeva mimicked, making her and Ambrose laugh. Even Luthor had to chuckle a little.

Mulch parted, sticking to that level and disappearing into a crowd of other molekin.

Luthor took the initiative and kept on down the stairs. The train station level wouldn’t be great for hiding, given that it was a temporary place by nature, but it was close to the center, and it would give them time to find the others and give Mulch a chance to do whatever it was he was going to do.

“Oh, hold on,” said Raeva. She reached until one of her pockets and pulled out two cards and handed them to Luthor. “Quick, make room for these, in case things get ugly.”

[Prison term: Uncommon Enchantment. Punish the guilty with a short stint in the Aether.

Spell Effect: Target opponent is banished to a prison realm for up to 30 seconds. Stronger foes may break out faster. 4 mana]

[C&C: Rare. Compassion for the innocent, and Contempt for the guilty.

Spell Effect: Choose one between the following: heal an ally or harm a foe. The effect is increased for both when the target is close to death. 3 mana]

They were good cards, and would serve him well. They were also expensive, and using one would likely mean not casting other spells that round. However, both were powerful enough that they’d change the tide of battle if used right. He removed a weaker, self-only heal and slotted them in. His deck now had its full six cards and two personal spells, and would stay that way until he reached Affinity Level 5.

“What did you two get for you?” Luthor asked, impressed with their choices for him. His trust hadn’t been misplaced.

Raeva grinned. “Not what you would expect, but they’ll be helpful. We even got Ambrose something so he won’t be so squishy.”

“I can’t equip it yet,” he complained, “but when I do, I’ll go from being the most dangerous person in the group to…Oh, there’s nowhere higher for me to go.”

Luthor smiled half-heartedly. Another performance, given the fear. But they were as ready as they were ever going to be.

“Alright,” he said. “Let’s get moving. Pray to whatever gods you believe in that we’re overreacting, and that this won’t turn into a fight.”

Judging by the looks on everyone’s faces, no one was fooled.
