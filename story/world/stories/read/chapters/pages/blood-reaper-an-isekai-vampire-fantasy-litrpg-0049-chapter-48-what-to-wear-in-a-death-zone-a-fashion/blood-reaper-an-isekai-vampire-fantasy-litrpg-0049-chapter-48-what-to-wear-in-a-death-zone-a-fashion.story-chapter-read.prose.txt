Ace glared down at Victor’s corpse as the man’s veins turned black.

“Uh, Ace?”

Marcus’s voice cut through his thoughts, and Ace instantly shifted his intense glare toward him. Marcus flinched, momentarily frozen in shock, but he ultimately pointed at the Thornhide Behemoth’s carcass.

The monster’s body had begun to deteriorate.

Ome of its bark plates crumbled into fine powder that drifted upward rather than falling to the grass. Green light flickered once beneath the disintegrating remains—the monster's final system reboot before permanent shutdown.

He had apparently done his job a little too well.

“It’s just…” Marcus cleared his throat. “If we want the loot, we should probably get it now.”

The man’s voice shook, and he glanced anxiously back at Rachel’s remains. He tilted his head, as though he were trying to pull his gaze away, but he kept looking back at the horrible scene.

“Right.” Ace’s jaw was so tense he could barely form words, and he shot one last furious glare at Victor’s corpse.

With his hands full of his newfound loot from his duel with Victor, Ace knelt beside the monster’s corpse. He set his other gains on the ground, ready to see what else they’d won.

The Thornhide Behemoth lay shattered at the center of a perfect circle of devastation. What had once been fifteen feet of living nightmare—part ancient tree, part primal woodland horror—now collapsed in on itself like a dying star. Its once-magnificent antlers, previously gleaming with living gold, had dulled to tarnished brass. The moss and foliage that had clung to its skin now withered and crumbled, drifting away as purple motes of light that defied gravity.

Ace studied the dead thing, his blood-red eyes tracking the unnatural deterioration. Purple sparks cascaded across the beast's remains, each tiny explosion of violet energy signaling the release of loot. The System's way of rewarding killers with the spoils of their violence.

Tara trailed behind Olivia, walking with slow and deliberate steps as she struggled to keep her balance. Though her face was paler than usual, at least she was up and walking.

That was a good sign.

One by one, the surviving team converged on the fallen giant. Tara knelt beside Ace, her blonde hair catching purple highlights from the sparks dancing across the Behemoth's skin. Her fingers brushed against a section of bark that once formed the creature's chest plate.

Ace pressed his finger against the Behemoth’s corpse. The moment his fingers touched the deteriorating bark, another loot screen materialized, pulsing with the same purple energy that sparked from the corpse. It flashed once, and glowing text appeared to outline what he’d won.

He exhaled slowly, focusing on the translucent display only he could see. After everything they'd sacrificed to bring down this monster, the rewards had better be worth it.

With a glance over one shoulder at Rachel’s remains up the hill, his shoulders slumped. This fight had cost them far more than it should have—no amount of loot could compensate for that.

The screen shimmered as the Behemoth continued to collapse beneath their touch, its bark-covered plates crumbling to ash and floating skyward like strange, inverted snow.

"Anyone else seeing rare-quality drops?" Marcus asked, but there was a heaviness to his voice that didn’t match his words. "I think the System actually rewarded us for once."

Ace didn’t answer.

Instead, he focused on the loot list. He needed better weapons, better armor—anything to give them an edge in this hellscape. Every loot drop could mean the difference between survival and permanent death.

No pressure.

The forest and Highlands alike remained eerily silent. No birds. No rustling in the grass. Just an oppressive silence, weighing on them like a blanket someone was trying to use to smother them. Nothing dared approach the unnatural circle of destruction their battle had carved into the landscape. Only the continuous patter of disintegrating monster flesh, like morbid rainfall, broke the stillness.

The System reappeared, skipping around the clearing like a demented child playing hopscotch. "Time for presents!" she sang, clapping her hands with mocking enthusiasm. "Who wants to go first?"

He groaned in disgust.

He couldn’t deal with her right now.

He scanned the loot screen. Iron chits, a blood-filled vial that had no data on it, and a piece of gear.

Ace tapped the last item. The System's voice, cheerful and mocking as always, filled his mind as the details expanded before him.

—---

Name: Phantom Step Resonance Boots

BASE STATS:

Weight: 2.4 kg

Special Property: Shadow Tread: Each step absorbs ambient sound within a 3-meter radius, creating zones of perfect silence that last for 2 seconds. Consecutive steps create overlapping silence zones, allowing for truly silent movement. (Passive)

Unique Property: Resonance Perception: While moving, detect vibrations through surfaces you touch, granting awareness of moving entities within 20 meters even through walls. Sensitivity increases with movement speed. (Passive)

Requirements:

Description: Oh, aren't these just the most delicious little accessories? Not just boots, darling - they're practically an extension of your shadow-touched senses!

The leather comes from some unfortunate creature whose very skin absorbed sound - a useful adaptation in a world where being heard meant being hunted. Those curious patterns along the soles? Ancient sound-dampening runes that activate with each step. And those metallic inlays along the ankles actually vibrate at frequencies just beyond perception, turning the wearer's feet into exquisitely sensitive detection instruments.

What I find particularly amusing is how they teach your vampire senses to "listen" through the ground itself. The more you move, the more you'll know!

—---

“Oooooh, shiny!” the System cooed. She floated in front of him, laying on her stomach despite hovering three feet off the ground, and she rested her chin in both hands.

It was a pleasant surprise, for sure.

He wasn’t about to tell her that, though.

“Well,” she prompted. “Put them on!”

Ace bent to untie his combat boots, but he paused as his fingers touched the laces. These boots had been with him through war, through desert sand and highland grass alike, and to take them off felt like a sort of treason.

It gutted him in ways he didn’t fully understand.

“Feeling nostalgic?” the floating sadist beside him asked. “You can always get rid of the shiny magic boots. I’m sure one of your teammates would kill to have them.”

He glared at her briefly.

“Interesting choice of words,” he pointed out.

She just smiled.

He unlaced his boots and yanked them off. He hit accept on the glowing loot screen before him, and the new boots materialized on the ground at his feet. Blue runes glowed along the soles of the black leather, and he couldn’t deny that they looked damn impressive.

The System was crazy, but at least she made cool gear.

The only downside was that, yet again, his new gear demanded he use HP to bond with them.

"Always with the blood sacrifices," he muttered. "Heaven forbid magical gear just works when you put it on."

The System giggled. "Where would be the fun in that?"

Ace set the boots down and drew his knife. The offering was simple—a shallow cut across his palm, letting his blood drip over the intricate runes etched into the leather. The droplets didn't pool or stain, though—they sank into the material, disappearing completely as the boots seemed to drink his offering.

As the wound on his palm began to close, Ace eyed the boots with suspicion. He didn’t like the idea of sliding his feet into something that literally drank his blood, but this was the System’s world—that shit was normal here.

Fine.

Here goes nothing.

He slid them on. The leather conformed instantly to him, growing in size to accommodate his larger feet. To his surprise, they were the most comfortable thing he’d ever worn.

For a moment, nothing happened—until, in a violent rush, the world around him transformed.

It started with his sense of sound. It heightened in waves that expanded with each second that passed. The whisper of his clothing against skin transformed into detailed clues about fabric tension and material composition.

The boots weren't just footwear.

They elevated his senses to something beyond supernatural, and yet the intensity of the sounds didn’t bother him in the slightest.

"What the—" he began, his voice suddenly rich with undertones he'd never noticed before. He could hear the vibrations of his own vocal cords, the way sound waves bounced off the nearby trees and returned with information about texture and density.

He stood and took an experimental step forward. The moment his foot connected with the Highland ground, a perfect radius of enhanced acoustic awareness expanded outward. The ambient sounds of the Highlands—the wail of wind, the rustle of the grass as it shivered in the gust, the soft voices of his team muttering about their prizes—all sharpened into distinct signatures, each one carrying tactical information.

The second step brought something entirely new. The moment his foot touched the ground, his senses expanded with even more information. Vibrations—no, something more profound, but which he couldn’t quite name—surged up through his legs, translating into a three-dimensional map of his surroundings that existed purely in his mind.

Twenty meters ahead, something large crested the top of the hill. The movements were heavy, methodical. The creature—something like a bison with fangs—paused, its head rising above the swaying grass, its nostrils flaring as it sniffed the air. Through the boots, Ace felt its weight shifting—uneven distribution, favoring its right side. The detailed perception faded slightly when it stopped moving, becoming less defined, but he could still sense its presence like a shadow in his mind.

To his left, smaller vibrations rippled through the soil. A group of burrowing creatures hurried beneath the surface, approximately fifteen meters away. The sensation was distinct—not quite rodents, but something with more legs. Ace could almost count them through the subtle disturbances they created. Six? Seven? They moved in unison, like soldiers on patrol rather than wild animals.

Each step brought new information flooding through his senses. The faster he moved, the more detailed and extensive the sensory map became. When he broke into a light jog across the highland clearing, the range extended dramatically. Suddenly he could perceive the edge of the forest with remarkable clarity—sensing trees with hollow centers, detecting the difference between solid rock and loose shale beneath patches of grass, even feeling the subtle movements of wind through the canopy as vibrations transmitted through root systems.

"Holy shit," he whispered, hearing how his own words disturbed the air around him, creating distinct patterns he could now interpret.

The System clapped her hands in childish delight. "I knew you'd appreciate them! The previous owner used them to hunt dragons as they slept. He'd feel their heartbeats vibrating through the mountainside, walk right up to them, and slit their throats before they even knew he existed."

Ace came to a sudden stop near a large boulder, and the sensory map faded to a basic awareness of his immediate surroundings. The boots hummed faintly against his skin, eager to continue their work.

"This changes everything," he said under his breath.

In the Marines, they had night vision, thermal imaging, advanced comms—but nothing like this. With these boots, he could navigate completely blind, sense ambushes before they happened, track enemies by the sound of their breathing alone.

He took another step, feeling the awareness bloom again. Deep in the forest, something massive was dragging itself along, leaving a trail of vibrations that felt... wrong. Slithering, but with too many points of contact with the ground. Multiple limbs? A segmented body? Whatever it was, it moved with purpose toward the meadow's edge.

The boots didn't just offer information—they translated it through his vampiric senses into something his brain could interpret. Movement became intent. Weight became a threat assessment. Vibration patterns became species recognition. The large creature in the grass ahead was hunting. The burrowing things were patrolling. The massive entity in the forest was... hungry.

His mouth curved into a predatory smile. In the Marines, it had been about overwhelming force and superior tactics. But now, with these boots...

…now he could become the perfect hunter.
