Eric charged around the tree, hurtling toward the Ice Elf. The creature jerked at the sight of him rushing toward them and fumbled another arrow onto their bow, but there were only a few trees between them. Eric cleared the distance before they notched their next arrow and dispatched them with a bat to the face. As much as he loved being a mage and using magic, there was nothing like a good old-fashioned Slugger kill, and it didn’t hurt that it cost him no mana either.

There were a lot of Ice Elves to slaughter. Even with his expanded mana pool, it really might not be enough. Not only that, but when his only serious counter to the archers was Explosion, the more expensive of his two base spells, he really needed to conserve mana. If he ran into another wide empty field with archers on the other side, he’d need as many shots of Explosion in the tank as he could afford.

An arrow shrieked through the forest at him from his right, deeper into the trees. Eric dashed behind a tree, then peeked out from behind it. An Ice Elf crouched on a branch not far behind him, bow aimed at his tree. The second their eyes met, the elf loosed their arrow, and Eric ducked back behind the tree. The arrow whistled past a second later. Eric whirled back out and thrust his hand forward, shooting a Firebolt at the elf, and the blue-skinned monster went down. He paused for a moment just to be sure, but it was down; its body laid there, blackened, then dissolved into black smoke.

Okay. They can’t all take a Firebolt and stay standing. Was it a difference in their stats? Some of the elves had higher MDF, maybe? Or had he just hit the first elf less squarely?

An arrow buzzed by his face, cutting a notch in his ear, and Eric whirled, eyes narrowing. There were plenty more elves to figure it out on, and he had plenty of mana left in the tank.

He caught a flash of blue skin and black armor vanishing behind an evergreen. Eric knelt, scooping up a snowball. He threw it at the tree, and snow rained down from its branches. An Ice Elf dashed out from under the tree, swatting snow out of their hair. Before they could regain their composure, Eric finished them with a Firebolt.

“Sorry. I need the EXP,” he muttered, mostly to himself. A moment later, he frowned. Who am I apologizing to? They’re trying to kill me. Actively plotting my death, right now!

Something struck him in the back. Eric stumbled forward, expecting piercing pain, but instead, cold spread over his back, sinking through his protective jackets. He whirled, surprised. Had one of the Ice Elves countered his snowball play with a snowball play of their own? How’d they get behind me?

A white crystal floated behind him. Smaller crystals of various sizes orbited the large crystal, swirling around it at random trajectories; it looked almost like bits of ice on a thawing river floating around the large central crystal. The central crystal wasn’t regularly-shaped either, but instead a natural hunk of quartz or something similar, with some neat lines and some rough-hewn edges.

Eric blinked. What the…

The central crystal glowed with white light. The external crystals churned, flowing faster, then all gathered up into one big clump and shot at him at speed. Eric dodged to the side, and a clump of ice smashed into the tree behind him and exploded into a rapidly-expanding patch of slick ice, freezing the surface of the tree with a crackle.

Oh, shit. It was an ice elemental or something. Its initial hit had kind of bounced off him, but he suspected that he had the Warm Fluffy Coat to thank for that. If whatever he got from clearing this dungeon first was enough to trivialize the Trinotaur, then the Warm Fluffy Coat on its own seemed vastly underpowered, so from the beginning, he’d suspected there was more to the story, and now that he was face-to-face with an ice elemental, he suspected he’d discovered just what the “more” was. The ice bolt had frozen the tree behind him solid. If that had struck him without any protection, he’d probably be frozen solid, too. Instead, it had kind of bounced off.

He thrust his hand out, shooting a Firebolt its way. The Ice Elemental dodged. He shot another Firebolt, but the slippery crystal dodged again. An arrow hurtled past, slicing his arm, and Eric cursed and jumped back behind his tree. Elves ahead and elementals behind. What a nasty place to be.

“Just hit it already,” Cy complained from his shoulder.

“I’m trying,” Eric replied. What, did the rabbit think he was shooting these Firebolts for fun?

“Why aren’t you steering the bolts, then?”

“Why aren’t I… huh?” Another blast of ice hurtled toward his head. Eric quickly ducked, and it once more splashed on the tree behind him. The crunch of snow from past the tree warned him that the Ice Elves were closing in, walking around to the sides to try to flank him. He had to take this elemental down, and fast, but the damn thing wouldn’t stand still!

Cy waved his paw. “Steer the bolts. You don’t lose all control of your mana when it leaves your body, you know. Just… steer them. It should be easy.”

“Just steer them?” Eric muttered under his breath. Ah, whatever. Let’s give it a try. He lifted his hand and narrowed his eyes, then shot another Firebolt at the elemental. As expected, it dodged, but this time, Eric leaned with the shot, willing it to turn left to chase the elemental the same way he’d lean with the car when he was trying to turn around a tight corner in a racing game. To his shock and surprise, the bolt moved the way he always willed the car to, darting around to smash the elemental. The two collided in a blast of water vapor and ice chunks, and the central crystal fell out of the sky, defeated.

Just in time, too. Black flashed in the trees to his left, and he caught a glimpse of blue from his right. At least one elf on either side of him; that meant the elves were in flanking position. He had to move, now. Eric glanced between them, judging the distance in a split second. The one closer to the edge of the woods was also closer to him, but he gritted his teeth, then ran deeper into the woods. More than anything, he didn’t want to put himself in a position where the elves could force him back out into the clearing and rain arrows on him again. The one deeper into the forest was further from him, but not far enough to matter. Going after that one made it more difficult for them to maneuver him back into the clearing.

An arrow screamed toward his back. Eric threw himself behind a tree, but not fast enough. The arrowhead clipped his arm, slicing through the (invisible) fluffy jacket and the snakeskin jacket alike. Sharp pain bit into his flesh and blood ran down his arm. Eric cursed under his breath, instinctively slapping a hand to the wound. Keep moving. He shot a Firebolt blindly behind him, waited a beat to hear the elf dive out of the way, then took off again.

Ahead, the elf pulled their bowstring back, aiming at Eric from nearly point blank. Eric didn’t wait for them to fire, but leaped sideways. The arrow screamed through the gap between his arm and body, bouncing off his coat. Before they could notch another arrow, Eric grabbed the tree, swung himself around it, and slammed into the elf from behind, knocking them over with his body weight. They started to rise, but he didn’t let them. He stepped on their shoulder and smashed a Firebolt into the back of their head. This wasn’t the place to worry about propriety or fair play. It was him versus a platoon of elves. He fought dirty, or he died.

The elf under his foot kicked once and died. Another arrow screamed his way. He stepped around the tree to dodge it, only for it to hurtle past from straight ahead, narrowly missing him. Eric looked up, startled. When had the ones over there gotten so close?

It didn’t matter. He wasn’t flanked from three sides, but two was still bad enough. He glanced toward the clearing. The elf on that side darted from one tree to another, a flash of blue amongst the white. Eric crouched over to a dense bush and hunkered there, a tree between him and the elves ahead, the bush between him and the elf toward the meadow. From behind the bush, peering through the snow-laden branches, he watched the elf toward the meadow dart from tree to tree, counting the time between leaps. Seven, eight, nine… it darted to the next tree. He lifted his hand, charging up a Firebolt. It darted again, vanishing behind a tree. Seven. Eight. Eric stood and aimed just as the elf stepped out. It backstepped, but too late. The Firebolt caught it in the chest, lifting it off the ground and slamming it back to earth in a spray of powder snow.

While he was dealing with the flanking elves, the other elves had been steadily moving closer. He couldn’t see them yet, but he heard their cries and the crunch of their feet in the snow as they zeroed in on his position.

The (perhaps obvious) downside to magic was that it wasn’t quiet. It was both loud and visually flashy. No, that’s not fair. All magic wasn’t flashy, probably. The familiar spell, for example, left almost no traces, and he was sure there were stealthy spells like shadow magic, poison magic, and the like, he just didn’t have them yet. The point was, his current spells definitely qualified as flashy. Whenever he used them, all the elves were alerted to his position. That didn’t make it unusable; it just meant he had to work around that reality.

That, and… He did have one stealth trick up his sleeve. He glanced at the bunny on his shoulder. “Cy. I can cast spells through you, right?”

“Correct. Why?”

Eric lifted Cy off his shoulder and set him down on the snow. The white rabbit blended into the snow except for his bright red horns, and even then, his body was so low to the ground that they vanished below every lump of snow and small clump of undergrowth. “I need you to help me out here.”

“I’m not sure I like this.”

“It’s okay. I’ll do the heavy lifting. I just need you to…” Eric explained his plan.

Cy scratched behind his ear with his back leg, then sighed aloud. “Fine. I suppose I can help, just this time… if you refer to me by my proper title.”

“Your proper title?”

“Lord Cy, please.”

“Lord Cy, will you help me with this plan?”

Cy sat up tall, shuffling his paws forward and perking his ears up tall. “I believe I can.”

“Excellent.” Cy hopped off, quickly vanishing into the snow.

Eric watched him go, then turned and slunk through the trees, winding his way around the front of the elves and into the forest. They’d had the jump on him for all this time. It was his turn to give them a shock.
