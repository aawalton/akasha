Lathani, on your right! I call urgently, sending a pulse of magic through the earth but knowing I’ll be too late.

Fortunately Lathani takes the warning seriously and disappears into the shadows, to pop up a body-length away. The crocodilian’s breath rolls over where she was a moment ago. Directing air-mana into my left hand while continuing to channel earth-magic through my left, I direct the fog of corrosive mist against the Tier three’s allies instead.

A quick glance at Lathani shows her busy with the parasite-controlled beasts surrounding her, but not in trouble yet.

Refocusing on the crocodilian, I instruct the earth-mana I’ve fed into the earth, doing my best to snare its feet. It isn’t making it easy for me – every time I think I’ve got a grip, it moves and its prodigious strength shatters the bindings I’m making.

I’d ask Artio for help, but the Tier three’s breath and the slime it oozes over its skin are almost as noxious to plants as they are to flesh. Even its own allies are giving it a wide berth. Instead, Artio is helping Maxen and the human fighters – he’s doing an excellent job breaking the horde of beasts down to a manageable size for the humans, and the Classers are doing the rest.

The crocodilian roars and surges forward, surprisingly fast even in its awkward-looking gait. I leap into the air, snapping out my wings and soaring high above its head. The crocodilian tenses and I pulse alarm though the Bond.

Ninja runs away the moment she feels my warning; Fenrir crouches down to the earth and braces for impact.

The crocodilian sweeps in a circle, its tail acting like a battering ram that sends Fenrir flying even as its jaws gape open reaching for the fleeing raptorcat. My heart rises into my throat as I see those teeth snap shut only inches away from Ninja’s tail.

Instinctively, I throw a fireball at the crocodilian, clenching my jaw as, once again, it peters out almost immediately upon touching the slime on the crocodilian’s back. An inferno would probably be able to cook the fire retardant away and render the Tier three vulnerable, but that would put everyone else at risk too. And waste all the experience from the deaths of the rest of the beasts that my soldiers could benefit from.

But I’m not willing to risk my companions being eaten, so I need to find some way of capturing the crocodilian so I can Bind it – its eyes are in an awkward place and I haven’t managed to catch its gaze.

Roaring in frustration, the crocodilian exhales another yellowish-green mist and my stomach clenches as I see it heading straight for the biggest clump of Maxen’s fighters.

I reach out with air-magic again, diverting the mist into the mess of fighting Tier twos ahead of my troops.

Thanks, Maxen sends shortly to me.

No problem, I tell him quickly even as I consider the possibilities.

Fire-magic won’t be useful unless I use it in quantities that will cause other problems. I don’t have enough control over water or air to be proactive with them. Bastet does, but she’s busy with the male larnatis that came out of nowhere. Plant-bindings are too quickly corroded. Earth-bindings are broken before they’re strong enough. The hide of the crocodilian is thick and resistant.

Perhaps not underneath the beast, I consider, diving to the earth behind the crocodilian that’s now tearing into its own allies in its bloodlust. It will realise the fallacy soon – or the parasite within it will – but for now I can take advantage.

Pressing more and more magic into the ground as quickly as I can, I prepare a trap for the beast.

The crocodilian moves forward, aiming for Maxen’s soldiers – the parasite has taken control again.

Ivor, Noir, Ninja, Fenrir, distract it, keep it in place – but don’t risk yourselves! I order and feel their acknowledgement in return.

The forward movement of the beast halts and I concentrate once more on pressing magic into my trap.

I’m jostled as a creature attacks me from behind, attempting to gnaw through my air-wings. Its teeth have little effect; its body weight is far more impactful. I have to pause my work in the earth to strike at it with a spear, the crab-like beast putting up as much resistance as it can, but falling within seconds as my spearhead smashes through its shell and deep into its brain.

Unfortunately, the death of that one has attracted the attention of several more beasts around me. I grit my teeth – killing them wouldn’t be too difficult if not for the grip on compressed earth-magic that I’m only just succeeding in holding onto. If I could be confident in using another magic alongside building up the trap, I would make short work of my attackers, but, as it is, I fear I’m more likely to release the trap too quickly. And I need to keep contact with the earth or I would fly up into the air.

Little help here? I call out in general even as I start defending myself with my spear, taking whatever shots I can to reduce the numbers of beasts attacking me. Unfortunately, for every one that falls, another one or even two pop up in its place. I wish I hadn’t had to use my armour and tattoo shields already, but I can’t regret putting myself between the Tier Three and Ninja in a previous clash – that was before we realised how quick it was.

A fire blade decapitates the closest beast to me and a shriek heralds Sirocco’s arrival.

A moment later, another basket-ball-sized sphere of sentient flames bashes into another of the beasts and sets it on fire. Parasite-controlled beasts don’t seem to feel pain, so it continues attacking even as flames run all over its body, but the flames suddenly turn white-hot and burn their way into the core of the creature. Its eyes go wide a moment before it tumbles to the ground, cooked from the inside out.

We’re here, you concentrate, Sirocco tells me firmly, diving to bite and claw at the eyes of another beast. Aingeal dances, seconding her assertion.

Thank you, I send to the two gratefully, then turn to concentrate on my own task, confident in their protection.

In the meantime, I notice that the others have managed to keep the crocodilian in place, even keeping its head away from me, amazingly – I wouldn’t have done so well if it had struck at me directly.

Pouring more magic into the ground, I’m almost ready to trigger the trap when Elarion reaches out over the Alliance Bond.

We need you. We have the Tier three digger pinned down and two others temporarily bound in place, he tells me briskly.

Can it wait a minute or two? I ask, clenching my teeth as I pour more magic into the trap, even knocking back a mana potion so I can add more.

No, he replies, sounding strained. We cannot keep this up for much longer.

I curse aloud. Three Tier threes pinned down – we can’t risk them escaping and we risk losing more if I give the order to kill them.

Fine. I’m coming, I tell him shortly. Concentrating on the crocodilian, I trigger my trap – I might not have built up as much mana as I’d have liked, but it’s probably enough.

A spear of earth punches up out of the ground. As predicted, the underside of the crocodile-like beast is far less armoured than its top, and the earth spear sinks deeply into it. Unfortunately, since I wasn’t able to put in as much mana as I wanted, the spear doesn’t have quite enough power to punch right through the beast, but I see the bulge its tip creates in the back of the beast.

My aiming was also off by a little bit – instead of hitting it in the belly, I hit it just forward of its back legs. As a result, its back legs are lifted off the ground and scrabble fruitlessly in the air even as it does its best to wrench itself free.

Still, hopefully that will keep it occupied for now – Ninja, Fenrir, and the two alcaorises will continue harassing it, keeping its attention on them as much as possible. The only issue is that it’s still pointed towards Maxen and his unit – without me to catch the corrosive breaths with air-magic, they might easily be caught in the crossfire.

As I soar into the air on my wings, I send a warning to Maxen even as I also send my thanks to Sirocco and Aingeal for their help.

Gaining more altitude, I have a much better view on the battle as a whole. Bastet and her counterpart are battling on the far right hand side of the battlefield, at times in the air, at other times on the ground, tearing at each other with air, lightning, fang, and claw. Catch and Pride are helping keep other beasts on the ground from interfering, and now I don’t need any more help, Aingeal and Sirocco go back to doing the same in the air.

Maxen and his unit are making good progress – moving in from the right, they’re acting as the hammer and Valence’s forces as the anvil.

Meanwhile, the right hand side of the battle seems to have been dragged away from the hill, following the three Tier threes to attack what looks to be the combined forces of Elarion, Josiah, and Layton. Thanks to them taking away a good portion of the army, once Maxen reaches Valence, they should be able to make a final push and make it through to the cave system entrance. From there, we can fight a defensive battle while our elites search for the Tier four.

But that all depends on us removing the Tier threes from the battle, particularly the one capable of swimming through the earth as if it’s water.

Pressing as much magic as I’ve managed to regenerate into my wings, I jet over to Elarion’s part of the battle, avoiding the parasite-controlled beasts in the air rather than wasting time or mana in killing them.

Which is the digger? I ask Elarion urgently as I hover over the two beasts – one looks rather like a large bull and is bellowing angrily, whipping the beasts around it into a frenzy; the other looks rather like an elk and currently is doing its best to pull its antlers out of the side of the bull, hampered by the vines that have snaked around its rear and are doing their best to bind it to the ‘ally’ it apparently attacked. Neither of them look like they can swim through earth.

I have it pinned down with my Forestheart, Elarion answers shortly.

I blink, briefly confused as to what he means. But when I realise that the massive tree-like combination of ice and wood is the Forestheart, and see where it’s concentrating volleys of freezing ice with its branches, I put the pieces together.

Right, I send back, deciding that questions about where the hell this weird tree came from and how it can move and throw ice can wait until after the battle. Dropping through the air I land just outside the blast-zone of ice. I’m on a fairly clear bit of earth but I activate Stealth and Fade just so the beasts a bit further away won’t notice me immediately. Then, tossing back another mana potion, I once more sink mana into the ground, searching out the Tier three.

Its presence is pretty obvious – its movement through the earth leaves unmistakable traces. I don’t try to debate its control over the earth, instead choosing to create a layer of earth between it and its escape route – one completely under my control. It might be able to seize control of the mana, but that will take time.

It’s moving, Elarion warns, sounding even more strained. The Forestheart groans – perhaps what he’s doing is straining it too.

Can you eliminate all the ice away from around it? I ask, lifting up into the air again and preparing to surge forwards. I need an unimpeded view of its eyes.

I can, he confirms.

Then do it in three…two…one…now!

The ice-tree stops volleying clouds of freezing ice shards, stilling with another groan that I’m probably imagining sounds relieved. Left behind is what looks like an iceberg, yet Elarion was right that the beast, impossibly, is moving – shadows shift within the ice. Within a couple of seconds of the end of the volley, the iceberg disappears, melting away as if under immense heat.

The beast, looking distinctly like a massive mole, blinks for a moment, taken by surprise at the sudden change.

I take advantage, swooping in and triggering my Skill.

Though not currently Bound, I feel the effects of the mole’s disorientation as the pressure against me isn’t quite as strong as it might have been.

This time around, I have no time to try to convince the beast to accept the Bond. Instead, as I press forward as fast as I can, I quickly explain exactly what’s happening – even if it doesn’t help reduce the pressure, it makes me feel better about forcibly Binding the Tier three.

It still seems to take an age before I get to the point when the mole gives up fighting.

As soon as I’ve felt the Bond snap into place and reality bleeds back into the soulspace, I bark out an order to the mole to stay still, and then immediately fly to the other two beasts.

Which one is more urgent? I ask Elarion.

You’re done already? he asks with clear surprise in his voice. Clearly my perception of time in the Battle of Wills was a bit off.

Yes. Now, which one is more urgent? I repeat with impatience.

The bull, he answers quickly. Its bellows have an effect on the other beasts and the udan is currently neutralised.

I send him a wave of acknowledgement, diving for the bull’s head – though not so closely I’m at risk of being swiped with its massive horns – and dive into my second Battle of Wills in less than five minutes.

This one takes a little longer – the resistance might not be much greater than against the mole, but I feel sluggish, like I have weights attached to me.

Pulling out of the Battle of Wills, I find myself reeling in the air, the weight of so many Tier threes – and many of them unwilling at that – dragging on my soul. Nessie, the okapi-like beast, the lion-like beast, the mole, and now this bull.

The last two are struggling hard against me too, and the lion-like beast’s Bond starts slyly trying to take advantage. To make matters worse, I feel Nessie’s stirring – the last thing I need is for her to pull free too.

I hesitate. Unless I choose to just kill this elk, and then the crocodilian and larnatis after it, I need to make space for more Bonds.

The lion is currently fighting near Maxen – not part of his command, but willing to attack the same enemies. There have been no problems with it. The okapi, meanwhile, is at the edge of the battle, occasionally leaping in to kick a Tier one here or there. It doesn’t really want to fight, only doing so for the promise of reward and being able to stay in the territory in peace.

The elk bellows and seems to shimmer as it backs up from the bull. Under the pressure of whatever it’s doing, the vines holding it in place begin snapping. I have no time. I don’t want to release Nessie, and I can’t release the mole or bull. My choice is clear.

I’m going to release you both, I send to the lion or okapi while making others in our network aware of the oncoming change. Continue fighting by our side or run away as you choose – but if you attack us, we’ll kill you. However, if you return to us and want to take up another Bond in the future, I’ll give you the Cores I promised.

I close my eyes briefly, hoping desperately that this is the right choice.

Then I release the lion’s and okapi’s Bonds at the same time.

Immediately after, I dive into another Battle of Wills with the elk.

The last thing I hear before the world disappears around me is Maxen’s alarmed, Markus, it’s coming for us!
