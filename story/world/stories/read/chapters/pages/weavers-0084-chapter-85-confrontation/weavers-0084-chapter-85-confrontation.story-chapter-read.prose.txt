The chamber smelled faintly of salt and cold stone.

Kana stood near the centre of it, boots planted wide, shoulders squared toward the man who had emerged from the shadows behind her. The dark cloak swallowed most of his shape, its heavy folds hanging low enough to conceal his hands completely. Only the faint glint of his eyes beneath the hood he wore told her where to aim for.

Her pulse quickened. Not just from fear, but urgency too.

Aric was probably already with the others. Kaelin, Aria, they’re in danger.

I don’t have time for this.

She exhaled slowly, lowering her stance.

“Well,” she said lightly, circling left, “I was hoping for a dramatic confrontation with the mastermind himself.” Her eyes flicked over him, measuring up his build, what kind of magic he might use, his skill levels. “Guess I’ve been stuck with the warm-up act instead.”

The man didn’t grace her with an answer.

He moved first.

A blur of motion, flashing forwards aiming straight at Kana.

She pivoted just in time, the strike missing her shoulder by inches as she ducked under his arm and slid past him. She felt the rush of displaced air against her cheek and rolled across the stone floor, coming up on one knee.

He seems well trained, probably better than me.

She grinned despite herself. “Good. At least you’re not boring.”

But there’s a reason I’ve made it so far, and you’re no mage.

He advanced again, this time more cautiously.

Kana shifted her weight from side to side, watching his feet instead of his hands. Every motion told a story. The slight turn of his hips before a strike, the way his shoulders tightened before he lunged.

He came in low.

She sidestepped, grabbing the edge of his cloak and using his own momentum to pull him forward. He twisted mid-fall, recovering faster than she expected, and a gloved fist slammed toward her ribs.

She barely blocked it.

Pain shot up her arm as the impact rattled through bone.

Kana hissed, retreating a step. Fuck, he’s strong, too.

“Look,” she muttered, circling again, “I really don’t have the luxury of losing here.” she said, her voice raised, “My friends are probably fighting for their lives right now.”

The man remained silent, pressing forward relentlessly.

Kana ducked another strike, sweeping her leg low. He jumped over it, landing in a crouch and swinging toward her head. She leaned back just far enough for the blow to skim past her nose, then drove her elbow forward into his torso.

The impact forced a grunt from him — the first sound he’d made this whole fight.

She seised the opening.

Kana surged forward, her movements sharper now, more aggressive. She didn’t try to do any flashy techniques; she targeted his joints, trying to get him off balance, weak angles. A quick strike to the knee forced him to stumble. A shove to the shoulder unbalanced him further.

Kana stood leaning over, panting.

I need to end this, and fast.

He recovered, attempting a sweeping counterattack meant to force distance between them.

But Kana had anticipated it.

She stepped inside the arc instead of away from it, grabbing his wrist and twisting hard. His arm locked, and she pivoted using her full weight to throw him over her hip.

He hit the ground hard enough that the sound echoed through the chamber.

Before he could rise, she dropped to one knee and got him in a hold, her leg wrapped around his torso, and his arm threaded through on the verge of breaking and choking himself in the process.

“Now stay down,” she said while panting, clearly without much energy either.

He struggled once — twice — then went limp.

Kana held the position for another few seconds, just to be sure. When she finally released him, she pushed herself back to her feet, chest heaving.

“Sorry,” she muttered, glancing down at him. “Wrong time to pick a fight with me.”

Her heart pounded as she turned toward the chamber entrance.

Now for them.

She took a few steps forwards, and froze.

Movement flickered at the far end of the room.

Another corridor.

Figures.

More than one.

Dark silhouettes spilling into the chamber.

Kana’s stomach dropped.

“Great,” she whispered. “Of course he had backup.”

She didn’t wait to count how many.

If they were here, then clearly Aric thought he could take on Kaelin and Aria alone. So whatever was happening elsewhere was already escalating. Every second she stayed meant another second the two of them were alone with him.

Kana turned and sprinted for the opposite exit, boots pounding against the stone floor. The sound of footsteps echoed behind her, growing louder as the men stepped into the large chamber — but she didn’t look back.

Her lungs burned as she ran through the corridor, weaving through the maze of tunnels she’d memorised on instinct alone.

“Hold on,” she whispered under her breath, pushing herself faster. “Just a little longer.”

The air grew colder as she raced deeper into the underground passages, every turn bringing her closer — she hoped — to the chamber where her friends faced Aric.

The wall Lena raised trembled beneath the first impact.

Stone cracked outward in jagged veins as Aric’s magic struck it from below, waves rippling through the structure like a pulse. Dust cascaded down the face of the barrier, drifting past Kaelin’s boots as she crouched at the top beside Lena and Aria.

“Brace!” Lena shouted, palms glowing as she reinforced the structure.

Kaelin didn’t need telling twice. She slid into a ready stance, breath slow, eyes locked on the man below.

Aric stood at the base of the wall, cloak fluttering around him like a living shadow. He looked almost calm — more disappointed than angry — as if this were a tedious situation, rather than the start of an epic battle that they’d seen it as.

“I offered you a chance,” he called up, voice carrying easily through the chamber. “We could’ve ended this without bloodshed.”

Aria’s answer came in the form of a lance of stone that split the air beside Kaelin’s shoulder. It struck the ground near Aric’s feet, shattering into thousands of pieces, forcing him to step back.

“Save your speeches,” Aria said coldly. “You’ve done enough talking.”

Kaelin’s pulse thundered. This is it.

“Aria, stay back and don’t try help too much. You know you’re not great at combat and it’s just too dangerous if you can’t properly protect yourself.”

Aria stared down at Aric, her eyes blazing with anger. Closing her eyes, she nodded, “Yea… sure, you’re probably right.”

Aric sighed, lifting one hand as though he were disappointed in a stubborn student which, in a way, he was. “You misunderstand me,” he said. His gaze rose to meet Kaelin’s, and they locked eyes. “I’m not your enemy, I’m trying to give you purpose, make your life worth something!”

Lena glanced sideways at Kaelin. “Don’t listen to him. You know he’s just trying to use you.”

“Don’t worry, I’m not,” Kaelin muttered, but she didn’t look away.

Aric’s eyes burned with strange intensity. “You feel it, don’t you?” he continued. “The power you have is special, not like anything I’ve seen before at least.”

The wall shuddered again as he moved — not with brute force this time, but with terrifying precision. Stone beneath his feet warped and shifted, creating a ramp that spiralled upward toward them.

Lena cursed. “He’s coming up!”

Kaelin moved first.

She leapt from the top of the wall, Time threads igniting around her limbs as she accelerated toward him. The world blurred — sound stretching, motion dragging — as she landed halfway down the ramp and struck.

Aric met her with a flick of his wrist.

Their magic collided in a sharp crack that echoed through the chamber.

Kaelin slid back several feet, boots carving grooves into the stone. Aric barely moved.

“You’re powerful,” he said calmly, stepping forward again. “But you’re still holding yourself back.”

Aria descended behind Kaelin, striking from the flank. Lena reinforced the crumbling ramp, turning loose debris into jagged spikes that erupted toward Aric’s legs.

He moved through it like water — deflecting Aria’s attack, shattering Lena’s constructs with precise bursts of magic.

Kaelin gritted her teeth.

He’s not even trying to kill us.

That thought alone made her stomach twist.

Aric turned his attention fully to her, ignoring the others for a heartbeat too long — and Kaelin seised it.

Her Fate threads unfurled.

They shimmered faintly around her fingers, invisible to most, but to her they felt like currents beneath her skin — subtle, dangerous, alive. She reached forward, not striking directly but pushing — nudging the probability of Aric’s next movement off balance.

The world tilted.

Aric stepped where he meant to stand — and the stone beneath his foot cracked unexpectedly, throwing off his stance.

Kaelin surged forward, accelerating her movements with Time magic and driving her palm into his chest. The impact forced him back several steps, cloak whipping violently around him as he slid across the stone floor.

For the first time, surprise flickered across his face.

The chamber fell briefly still.

Dust hung in the air between them.

Kaelin’s chest heaved as she steadied herself, Fate threads trembling faintly around her wrists. Lena and Aria regrouped at her sides, forming a tight line across the broken ramp.

Aric straightened slowly.

A faint smile curved his lips — not angry, not frustrated.

Proud.

“There it is,” he said softly. “You see? You don’t just fight with strength. You bend reality itself.” His voice lowered, almost reverent. “With you at my side, we could reshape this broken world. No more blind suffering. No more meaningless death.”

Kaelin’s stomach twisted.

“Stop talking like you’re the hero,” she snapped, though a small part of her hated how calm he sounded — how certain.

“I am offering you truth,” he replied. “You already know this academy fears what you are. They will never let you reach your full potential. But I will.”

The words lingered longer than she wanted them to.

She tightened her fists. “I’m not joining you.”

Aric’s gaze softened — almost regretful. “Then you’ll force my hand.”

The air thickened.

Magic crackled across the chamber walls as the tension between them stretched thin, ready to snap.

Kaelin shifted her stance, preparing to launch forward again —

—and a sudden rush of footsteps echoed from one of the side entrances.

All three of them turned.

A figure burst into the chamber, breath ragged, eyes wide with urgency.

Zephyr.

Kaelin’s heart lurched.

For a brief, fragile moment, relief flooded through her — another ally, another familiar face—

—and then she saw the tension in his posture, the fear in his expression as his gaze was trained on her.

“Lena!” he shouted immediately, voice sharp with urgency. “Get over here, now!”

Kaelin blinked, relief evaporating before it could settle. She had expected confusion — maybe even anger — but not this. Zephyr’s eyes were wild, fixed on Lena with a desperation that made Kaelin’s chest tighten.

“Please, she’s dangerous,” he added, pointing straight at Kaelin.

The words struck harder than any spell.

Lena stiffened beside her. “What?” she said slowly, disbelief lacing her voice. “Zephyr… what are you talking about?”

Kaelin didn’t move. Didn’t breathe. She just watched him — searching his face for a glimpse of the friend she knew.

It wasn’t entirely gone. But something was wrong.

“You don’t understand,” Zephyr insisted, stepping forward. His hands trembled slightly at his sides. “Kaelin’s not who you think she is. Aric told me — he showed me what she can do. She’s going to hurt you.”

“That’s not true,” Kaelin said quietly, but he didn’t even look at her.

Lena hesitated, her gaze flicking between them. Confusion clouded her expression, though she didn’t step away from Kaelin.

“There’s evidence against him,” Lena said after a moment, voice uncertain but firm. “We’ve seen things, Zeph. You weren’t there. Aric’s been—” She faltered, clearly unsure how to finish the sentence.

“Manipulated?” Zephyr snapped. “Framed? Lied about?” His voice rose with each word. “You’re listening to them over someone who’s been guiding us, training us, trying to help us improve!?”

Aria shifted slightly beside Kaelin, tension radiating off her like heat.

“Lena,” Zephyr continued, softer now, but more frantic, “please. Just come over here. We can fix this. You don’t have to get dragged into whatever she’s planning.”

“I’m not being dragged,” Lena shot back, though doubt flickered briefly in her eyes. “I chose to help. Kana’s missing, the academy’s in chaos, and Aric’s right in the middle of it. How are you not seeing that something’s wrong?”

Zephyr’s breathing grew uneven. His gaze darted between them — lingering on Kaelin with something like fear.

“Can you not see what they’re trying to do to Aric!?” he shouted suddenly, voice cracking. The sound echoed harshly through the chamber.

Kaelin flinched.

Lena’s shoulders sagged slightly, uncertainty creeping in. “Zephyr…” she said more quietly. “We don’t want to hurt him. But he’s hurting people. He’s—”

“He’s trying to save us,” Zephyr interrupted fiercely.

Aric stepped forward then, calm as ever, placing a hand lightly on Zephyr’s shoulder. “Enough,” he murmured. “You’re letting emotion cloud your judgment.”

Zephyr swallowed hard, leaning closer to him. His voice dropped to a whisper Kaelin almost didn’t catch.

“You said… you said Lena’s death had already been prevented,” he muttered. “You said the precautions were taken. That nothing bad would happen to her.”

Aric gave a faint, reassuring nod, eyes never leaving Kaelin. “It has been handled,” he said quietly. “Everything is in place. As long as you stand with me, she will be safe.”

The tension in Zephyr’s shoulders eased, just slightly, although he did seem less sure about his decision.

He stepped back from Aric, turning to face them again. But something in his posture had changed. He moved to Aric’s side — not aggressively, not proudly — but with the resigned stiffness of someone clinging to the only version of reality that still made sense.

“I’m sorry,” he said to Lena, voice low. “But I can’t let you get hurt.”

His stance shifted as he got ready to fight.

Zephyr struck first, attacking each of them in turn, his skill not enough to multitask.

He was fighting, but not with all his strength. Anyone could see that he was holding back.

He didn’t go in close, despite that being his speciality. He stayed at range and tried to wear them down with his attacks over going straight for a lethal hit.

Kaelin stepped back slightly, watching the clash unfold in a blur.

An orange glow sat around her fingers, she wanted to fight, but there was a bigger picture they’d been missing. aric wasn’t fighting. Maybe he didn’t need to now that Zephyr was here, but he couldn’t win against all three of them. Aria and Lena, maybe. Especially since he was holding back, but with Kaelin it would be too much.

This is getting worse.

Her gaze drifted briefly to Lena — still standing strong, but still conflicted nonetheless — and then to Zephyr, whose expression twisted with anxiety every time his magic came too close to her.

Her stomach tightened.

The forbidden spell flickered into her thoughts like a shadow.

She could feel its presence — heavy, waiting — the last resort she’d promised herself she would only use if there was absolutely no other choice.

Her fingers twitched.

If I use it now… this ends faster.

But she hesitated.

The danger wasn’t absolute yet. No one was dying. No irreversible line had been crossed.

Not yet.

Kaelin exhaled slowly, forcing the thought away. Not now. She wasn’t willing to risk everything — not when there was still a chance to stop this without unleashing something she might never be able to take back.

She stepped forward again, magic gathering at her fingertips as the fight intensified around her.

She had to force Aric’s hand. Force him to make a move.
