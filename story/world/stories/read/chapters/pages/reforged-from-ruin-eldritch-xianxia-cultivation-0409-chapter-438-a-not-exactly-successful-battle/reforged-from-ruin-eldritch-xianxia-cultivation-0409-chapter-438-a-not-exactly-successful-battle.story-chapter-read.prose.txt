Someday, inevitably, you will find someone who will win against you.

Not necessarily someone stronger. Not necessarily someone faster or smarter. Hell, they might not be better than you in any way whatsoever, but they’ll win. Eventually, you will meet someone that’s found a blind spot, or who is designed asymmetrically to you in such a way that you can’t defeat them, or who you don’t have the resources to properly counter.

This is a good thing. It might also kill you, which most people view as a bad thing, admittedly for good (if personally motivated) reasons. But it is a good thing.

Nothing helps you more than failure. Nothing gives you clarity more than experiencing the complete and total collapse of who you are in the face of someone else’s efforts. So long as you survive your experience, it can be one of the most impactful teachers you ever get, and it will find you someday. No one is so complete or so perfect that they won’t inevitably experience this, and if you think you are, then you’ll be one of the ones that probably won’t get to learn any lessons from it.

Because you’ll be dead. In case that wasn’t clear.

You can’t win every time, and that’s a gift. So the trick is to figure out how to survive, and then to figure out how to find the reasons you lost and make sure they become strengths. Or at least stop being weaknesses. Otherwise it will happen again, and that’s not the sort of thing you can guarantee survival from.

Loss is inevitable. Accept it, let it be your friend, guide how you let it shape you, and you will be the thing inevitable to someone else someday.
