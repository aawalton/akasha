With night now taking over, the fires in the yards stopped being cute places to hang out. People had pulled benches closer, and new braziers had appeared. Small pools of light and fire dotted the field.

The innkeepers were still serving food, although the orderly boards were a thing of the past. Most tables now had piles, and people helped themselves. Many of the roast pigs had been reduced, and new ones were turning.

The largest fire in the middle still held most of the people who were still dancing. This was where Alric was currently.

Dancing in this world sure seems to involve a lot of arguing.

The dance he had learned: link your arms with people nearby and then three steps left, push a leg out, then three steps right and give a cheer. Despite the simplicity, the circle acted more like a xylophone of compression and decompression. Still, people laughed and kicked at the wrong time; occasionally, a section of the circle fell over.

In the middle stood a large, bare-chested man wearing twigs in his hair. Alric recognized him from the games, but not which game.

Then whoever was playing the lute sped up, the accompanying drum picked up too, and remembering the steps became difficult. Alric laughed as he saw Seren across from him slip, only to be caught by Brannick and nearly taken out by the circle moving faster.

“Other foot!” Someone shouted over the music, which was a mistake, because Alric picked the wrong time to do the kick. His section of the line collapsed amid laughter.

Alric was still chuckling when he got up and moved away from the circle. Alric breathed hard, his face aching from smiling, and he felt hot despite the cold. He picked up his stein that he had dropped. He had eight lines on it. Nine he could consider respectable. He nodded to himself and set off for the beer dragon.

He passed Pavise heading for the dancing circle; he was still wearing the wreath, but one side had collapsed, and leaves stuck out everywhere.

“You still wearing that?” Alric asked, laughing. Pavise stopped and started laughing too, the comment being extraordinarily funny.

“Still won it,” Pavise said, straightening up and showing his spoon still tucked in his belt. “You getting beer?” He pointed to Alric’s upside-down stein. Alric nodded, then found Pavise’s huge arm around his shoulders.

Pavise took a deep breath and launched into deep baritone

“There once was a sailor named Old Tom Jory,Who sailed the coast chasing ale, skirt and glory,A wife in Drawner, another in CrayAnd three more who’d kill him if ever they met one day.”

Alric’s eyes widened at Pavise, but nearby other adventurers picked it up and bellowed out for all they were worth.

“So, haul up the skirts and haul down the sail,He’s got a wife in each harbor, and each gets a tale,One warms the bed while the other waits ashore,And if the winds hold steady, he’ll marry one more!”

Thankfully, Pavise at least stopped singing when they reached the beer wagon, although the adventurers were still singing for all they were worth behind them. Pavise grinned widely, glancing back at them.

“I do love that song!” Calvern rang out from nearby; Alric and Pavise turned toward him, wide-eyed. Calvern cleared his throat and launched into a new, even dirtier song about a man who lost his trousers in the siege. Alric couldn’t even pretend not to be amazed; Calvern really could sing, but the song itself was horrific.

Nearby soldiers cheered and began singing about how he was defending the walls.

Alric and Pavise were bent double, laughing at Calvern. Nearby women listened too and laughed. “That’s terrible,” Alric said between breaths.

Calvern had somehow heard that and grinned, “Being cooped up at a border fort will do that to you.” He said, holding up a stein to show his six lines.

Alric considered that offensive; Calvern hadn’t been here nearly as long and was catching up. Alric headed to the beer wagon properly, handing over his stein. When he turned around, Pavise and Calvern were comparing who knew the dirtiest ditties. Alric shook his head, grinning.

Alric glanced across at their earlier table; someone had lit a brazier by it, but it was still empty. Alric decided some air and a sit-down was just what he needed and headed over. Behind him, his two guards followed, still grinning ear to ear.

Alric had just sat down when he noticed them. “You guys don’t seem to be having much fun.” He pointed out, but they were both grinning. “Go grab a beer; I’ll be here.” He said. One nodded and headed to the wagon; the other sat down near the fire.

“Enjoying yourself?” Alric turned to find Rosa nearby. She was holding a tankard of her own. Hers had many lines as well; Alric couldn’t help but be impressed.

“Far more than I expected,” Alric said with a grin. He lifted his own stein, showing the lines. She grinned, approached closer, and knocked tankards with him. “I also learned more things than I wanted to know,” Alric said after sipping. He gestured toward Calvern, who was now leading a new dirty song about sailors.

Rosa scoffed, looking off at it. “He does have a good voice.”

“He does! And that makes it so much worse!” Alric replied, and they all laughed.

Rosa shifted closer to the fire; Alric moved over slightly to give her room.

“Did you plan this whole thing?” Rosa asked, her arm sweeping to the whole muster yard.

“That was Pavise; I supplied beer,” Alric said, holding up his stein, “The adventurers did the rest.”

“That does sound like you.” She said with a smirk.

Alric gave her a mock scowl. “Is that an insult?”

She shook her head, grinning. “Seems smart.”

Alric nodded; he glanced at the fire for a moment. Just taking the event in. The cool air felt good against his skin. Nearby, two soldiers were arguing over the words to a ditty they should never sing in polite company but had been doing so with gusto.

Rosa watched them for a while with a small smile. “I wanted to ask. Did you ever find anything out about the herbs?” she asked. She had glanced to where Alric’s guard was.

Alric’s stein stopped halfway to his mouth. He glanced for his guard, noticing he was relieving himself a short distance away. He lowered the stein and looked at Rosa. He felt cold water wash over himself; he shook his head slightly, partly to say no and partly to try to sober himself up. “No, nothing useful.”

Rosa didn’t say anything for a while; she just regarded him. Alric tried not to wince. “I see.” She said, glancing at the stein he had lowered, “Well, glad you are enjoying the party, Mister Alric. A lot of us sure needed it.”

Alric just nodded. “You too.”

She smiled as she left; Alric frowned, watching her go between the fires. Alric heard his guard return. He considered the words he had said carefully. He hadn’t said anything. He let out a deep breath.

“Alric!”

Alric jumped slightly and turned. An adventurer was approaching, holding up his tankard; it had more lines than Alric could rely on himself to count. “What ya on?”

Alric grinned, “Nine!”

“Shameful!” The adventurer said, shaking his head in mock disapproval; nearby fires laughed. “The dwarf says he has won this thing!” he said, pointing to a ring near the beer wagons.

Alric shrugged; he glanced at his two guards. Both nodded; he didn’t notice the other return. They all stood and headed over.

“Aye! I’m on nineteen!” He said, holding his tankard above his head. A loud cheer rang out; the others standing nearby had their shoulders drooped.

“I think the priest has more.” One of the beer pourers called out; the party went quiet.

Stromni turned toward her slowly. “WHAT? Tarrow!” Stromni said, glancing around the yard. “Where is ‘e?” Stromni shouted.

Several laughing people pointed to one of the smaller fires. Alric followed and saw a small group following Stromni.

“Tarrow!”

Father Tarrow looked up; he had been telling a story. “Stromni?”

“Tankard!”

Tarrow looked at the tankard by his elbow, then back at Stromni. “Why?”

“Give it!”

Tarrow shrugged and handed it over. Stromni immediately began to count the lines. The nearby crowd had gone quiet. Stromni started again. “How ya on twenty-three?”

The nearby crowd let out a deep laugh. Stromni’s shoulders fell, then came back. “Ya been cheating! Using ya priestly magics!”

Tarrow let out a loud laugh. “What for?”

“The competition! That’s what?”

Tarrow, the crowd, and Alric all started laughing. Stromni was staring at him.

“Stromni, you think the gods would let me use magic to cheat in a drinking competition?” Tarrow asked, he slapped the table while he laughed.

Stromni glanced at the fire and let out a small pout. “Fine! Night ain't done yet!” Stromni said, raising his tankard. The crowd cheered again.

Alric took a while to recover from laughing. The priest who sampled every beer cask would pretty much have built an immunity to the stuff by now in Alric’s mind. He was wearing a goofy grin when Stromni spotted him.

“What ya grinin’ at?” Stromni asked but was grinning himself.

“Nothing, just think you funny is all,” Alric said, raising the stein.

“Aye well, I need a refill, en ya need ta stop disappointin’,” Stromni said, pointing to the beer wagon, which was how Alric got dragged off to the beer wagon again.

Sometime later, Alric sat down at the table again, his ears burnt, his cheeks warm and a bit sore from smiling. His two guards had settled at the table too, grateful to be off their feet.

Alric just watched, enjoying a sense of contentment. People he knew were everywhere. He could see Mara and Lessa nearby, sipping tea; Seren and Brannick dancing; Pavise and Calvern trying to outdo one another with dirty ditties, but Calvern clearly in the lead.

His thoughts turned to the things he had done, the people he met and cared about, the brewery, the parts that made it work, the carters who brought bedrolls to parties; he chuckled at that.

The still he had built with his mate, even Calvern, who was an annoying noble bastard, but he was a funny annoying noble bastard.

His thoughts turned to the stupid things he missed from Earth: hot showers, toilets, cars, food he couldn’t make or hadn’t tried to. And yet, he wouldn't trade it for the people he had now.

“If my people are here, then this is my world too. Doesn’t have to be complishated.” He mumbled, raising his stein and taking a sip.

Enjoying the moment, he noticed someone approaching, though "approach" was a strong word. Stromni was heading in Alric’s general direction. He would have to stop, adjust his trajectory, and try again. He held his stein carefully.

“Lad!” Stromni said when he got close. Alric shifted over to make room. Stromni didn’t sit; he thought. He carefully bent over slightly, making himself eye level with Alric with one hand on his shoulder. “Ya know what ya are?”

“I bet you’re gonna tell me,” Alric said, gesturing to the bench next to him, but Stromni didn’t sit.

“Ya a good lad,” Stromni said, nodding slowly. Alric had to look away not to laugh.

“You my best mate, you a good friend,” Alric said. He knocked his stein against Stromni’s tankard. Stromni looked down at it in confusion before looking at it in delight.

“Aye, ya a wonderful bastard,” Stromni said, finally sitting down. Alric took another sip. The two sat in companionable silence after that. “Best friend,” he mumbled looking out over the party as well.

Alric’s memories of the rest of the evening, however, turned fuzzy.
