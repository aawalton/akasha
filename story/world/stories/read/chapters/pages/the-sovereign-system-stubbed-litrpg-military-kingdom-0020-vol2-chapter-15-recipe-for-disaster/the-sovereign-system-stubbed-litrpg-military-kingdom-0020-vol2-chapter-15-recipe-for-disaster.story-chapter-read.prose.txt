Luke’s body shuddered, and his eyes widened for a brief moment.

He frowned, staring at the dying man he had just stabbed in the throat. As the light faded from the man's eyes, a wave of discomfort washed over Luke. The mental clarity he’d felt only moments earlier receded like the tide, leaving behind a heavy sense of exhaustion.

‘That didn’t feel right,’ he thought, his gaze drifting to the bloodied sword in his hand.

He had killed without hesitation—merciless and brutal. Though it had been self-defense, a part of him refused to believe he was capable of such violence. At least, not without help.

Had he truly lost himself in that moment of panic? Or was this his true nature?

Luke shook his head, forcing the thought away. There was no time to dwell on it. He needed to reach the palace before anything else went wrong.

He wiped the blood from the borrowed sword and slid it back into its sheath. After a quick glance around, Luke began retracing his steps. He wasn’t familiar with the capital, so it was the only option he had.

His thoughts shifted to the Black Tiger gang—the ones behind the ambush. But Luke had no doubt they were just pawns in a larger scheme.

‘Whoever’s pulling the strings is the one working with Lhair,’ he thought, his fist clenching at his side.

That person had orchestrated Lhair’s infiltration of the Marxx Kingdom, the fall of Valand City, and the death of this body’s father. Their actions had led to the deaths of countless innocents.

Still, after just two nights in the capital, he had finally uncovered a lead.

Navigating back through the winding alleys, Luke emerged onto the main street. Guards had finally arrived, questioning witnesses about the skirmish.

He cursed under his breath and immediately changed direction to avoid them. He didn’t know whether these guards were friend or foe—and he wasn’t about to take that risk.

If the traitor held a high-ranking position, it was entirely possible these guards were under their command. Deploying soldiers wouldn’t have been possible otherwise.

Luke kept his head down, taking the long way around. After several tense minutes, he exhaled in relief as the palace gates came into view.

“You made it. Thank the gods,” said Blake Thompson, the Royal Alchemist. His shoulders sagged with relief as he gestured toward the entrance. “Come on, let’s head inside.”

Luke didn’t need to be told twice. He shot a wary glance at the two palace guards—neither paid him much attention—then followed the short alchemist through the gates.

“What happened?” Blake asked, his voice taut with concern. “Did you manage to lose them in the alleys?”

“Yes. I got out without being injured,” Luke replied, keeping his answer deliberately vague.

He still didn’t know who he could trust.

Better to be cautious—for now.

“You’re very fortunate… Do you think they were after you for the gunpowder recipe?” the man asked, brows furrowed.

“Either that, or it was the Marquess’s men looking to avenge his son,” Luke said quickly, hoping to throw him off the scent.

“You’re talking about Marquess Ring?” Blake stopped in his tracks, turning to Luke with an incredulous look.

Luke narrowed his eyes. “Yes, why? Does that sound unreasonable to you?”

“Viscount Drakon… the Marquess was executed for treason—alongside three generations of his family,” Blake said grimly.

Luke froze, surprise flickering across his face. Even he hadn’t expected the King to act so swiftly—or so decisively. It only confirmed how valuable the gunpowder recipe truly was.

“I see. Then I suppose that’s… good news,” he replied curtly, signaling the end of the discussion.

‘The King isn’t a fool… He understands exactly what this technology can do,’ Luke thought, his mind racing. The weaponized potential of gunpowder wouldn’t be wasted. The King would use it for war—immediately.

Luke had already considered giving Blake a modified version of the recipe. Now, he was certain. There was no way he could hand over the real formula—not with a traitor still lurking in the Kingdom.

If the worst came to pass, and Lhair got their hands on it... the consequences would be catastrophic.

Still, providing a fake recipe came with its own risks. It needed to be close enough to yield partial results. If it failed outright, suspicion would fall squarely on him.

Fortunately, Luke had a few ideas.

As they passed through the palace’s main entrance, Luke spotted the servant who had caused him trouble on his first day here. The man turned pale at the sight of him and quickly disappeared around the corner.

Luke scoffed, ignoring him, and followed Blake deeper into the grand hall.

“The Royal Alchemy Workshop is in the west wing,” Blake explained. “We’ve got a large herb garden nearby for tincture ingredients, and any supplies we receive from across the Kingdom are stored in our main warehouse.”

“We should already have everything you need to create the gunpowder. As long as you give me the recipe and show me the process, it shouldn’t take long for me to learn it.”

Luke tapped the side of his head. “It’s all up here. The recipe’s too valuable to write down—I’m sure you understand.”

“O-of course,” Blake stammered.

“Good,” Luke said, patting him on the shoulder. “We wouldn’t want it falling into the wrong hands.”

Blake flinched slightly. It was as if the weight of the situation had only just dawned on him.

“R-right this way, Viscount,” he said quickly, eager to move on.

They made their way to the west wing and stepped into the Royal Alchemist’s workshop. Compared to the modest warehouse Luke had used in Clayton City, this place was enormous.

The sharp scent of fresh herbs filled his nostrils as he entered. His gaze swept across the vast room, immediately noting the far superior equipment and resources. Floor-to-ceiling shelves were stacked with glass bottles and ceramic jars, each labeled and filled with dried herbs, colored powders, or mysterious compounds.

At the center stood a massive alchemy furnace, towering at six feet tall. Scorch marks on the floor surrounding it hinted at the intense heat it could generate.

Several figures moved through the room with practiced ease, tending to various tasks. Luke assumed they were apprentices—assistants to the Royal Alchemist, each focused and deliberate in their movements.

Blake clapped his hands, drawing the attention of the apprentices scattered throughout the workshop.

“All of you, stop what you’re doing. Today, Viscount Drakon will be imparting the gunpowder recipe,” he announced, gesturing toward Luke. “Follow his instructions as you would my own. I won’t tolerate any mistakes.”

The three apprentices bowed deeply in unison, their expressions turning serious.

“Viscount, please come in,” Blake said respectfully. “Let me know what ingredients you need.”

Luke stepped forward without a word, pacing slowly through the room. He took his time, casually inspecting the rows of jars and glass bottles along the shelves while his mind worked in the background.

“I need pine and bamboo, saltpeter, and sulfur,” he said at last, his tone commanding.

“Yes, Viscount!” the apprentices responded immediately before scattering to fulfill the order.

Luke already knew exactly how he was going to manipulate the recipe. It had taken him days of trial and error to discover the perfect ratios—now he intended to sabotage them.

Even a 20% shift in the mixture would dramatically reduce the effectiveness of the final product. The trick was to ensure the result looked and felt promising enough to satisfy the King, while being functionally inferior.

No one except his allies in Clayton City had witnessed the true power of the thunder bombs. That gave him a margin for deception.

‘If I reduce the saltpeter, the powder will burn slower and generate less pressure,’ Luke mused, pacing the room in slow, deliberate steps.

That would require him to increase the amount of both charcoal and sulfur. More charcoal meant thicker smoke—harmless. But more sulfur would make the mixture far more volatile.

‘I’ll just have to make sure I’m not the one grinding it,’ he thought, casting a pitying glance at the apprentices.

The original formula was precise: 75% saltpeter, 15% charcoal, and 10% sulfur. His doctored version would be: 50% saltpeter, 30% charcoal, and 20% sulfur.

In theory, it would still ignite—but the explosive force would be drastically weakened. In some cases, it might not detonate at all, merely fizzling out in the thunder bombs.

‘I can always blame the quality of the ingredients—or the incompetence of the apprentices,’ he thought with a smirk.

It didn’t take long for the apprentices to return with the requested materials. Luke eyed them with subtle pity before barking out the next orders.

“Burn the pine and bamboo until they’re reduced to charcoal. As for the saltpeter—boil it in water, filter it through cloth, then boil the water again until crystals form. I’ll give you more instructions after that.”

His sharp gaze locked onto the trio.

“What are you waiting for? Go!”

Startled by the sudden shout, the apprentices scrambled, nearly tripping over one another as they rushed to carry out his commands.

Luke turned to Blake with a raised brow. “Are they always like this?”

Blake gave an apologetic sigh. “Usually, they’re a bit more competent than this…”

“Let’s just hope they don’t mess anything up,” Luke muttered.

Luke felt a pang of guilt for the apprentices, but he pushed it aside. He had no choice. If the gunpowder failed, someone would need to take the blame—and it couldn’t be him.

Unfortunately for these men, they were the scapegoats.

Considering the alternative—Lhair getting their hands on the real recipe—a few injured alchemists was a small price to pay.

“You’ll help me prepare the sulfur and the clay shells,” Luke said, turning to Blake.

With that, the workshop fell into a steady rhythm. To Luke’s surprise, everyone worked with impressive efficiency. He had once thought Adam Searle, the alchemist in Clayton City, was meticulous—but these apprentices were even more capable.

Perhaps it was the palace’s superior facilities, but the difference in pace was staggering. In no time, the apprentices had processed the saltpeter and burned the pine and bamboo into charcoal.

One of them even had the clever idea to use the residual heat from the nearby kiln to dry the saltpeter crystals, rotating them periodically to ensure they were dried evenly. Luke felt mildly embarrassed—he’d wasted an entire day drying his in the sun back in Clayton City.

He cleared his throat and stepped forward to continue the process.

“Grind the charcoal and the saltpeter separately,” he instructed. “They need to be reduced to a fine powder.”

So far, the steps mirrored the original process he’d used to create gunpowder. But from here, the changes would begin.

He had already altered the ratio to weaken the explosive force. On top of that, he’d chosen pine charcoal—something he knew would burn faster and produce more smoke. That alone would create inconsistencies in the burn, leading to partial ignition or, in some cases, no explosion at all.

Best case? It would explode, but deal minimal damage.

Once the individual components were ready, Luke provided the apprentices with the altered ratios and instructed them to grind the powders together using a pestle and mortar.

As they followed his orders, he subtly stepped back and motioned for Blake.

“Blake, let’s go check on the clay shells,” he said, heading toward the kiln.

“But Viscount, the mixing process will take at least another eight hours. We should stay focused on—”

A sudden flash of light erupted behind them.

Crack!

The sharp sound of shattering stone echoed through the workshop. One of the apprentices let out a piercing scream as the mortar split in his hands and the gunpowder ignited in a burst of flame, searing his face and hands.

The other two recoiled in horror, frozen for a moment before rushing to his side.

Luke turned to observe the scene, his expression unreadable. A flicker of pity stirred in his chest.

‘I’m sorry, friend… but this was a necessary sacrifice,’ he thought grimly.
