Neris. The Great Spirit of the Sea. One of the five current Great Spirits, the sovereigns of the Concord, leaders of the spirits who dwelt within. She who was the mistress of all the oceans of Vereden.

In absolute terms, very likely the strongest of the Great Spirits, simply because of the size of her domain. One of the two Great Spirits who had betrayed the very world they were meant to shepherd. Her and Orus.

They had allied with Anguis, and helped him loosen the Mad God’s Mantle of Divinity so the Dwarf could purify it, and then steal it for himself. It was by their hand that all of the death and suffering and destruction wrought by the Skyfall had become possible at all.

And she was here, somehow, in the physical realm, in the form of some kind of monstrous woman of black water.

“Well, well, well,” Neris drawled, laying a single hand on a slick black hip, and cocking it. “What do we have here? It almost appears to be interlopers. I was informed, by someone who was meant to be a reliable partner-”

She glared across the cavern at the still immobile form of Ul-Korveth before me.

“-that such a thing should be impossible. What was it you said, little boy? ‘Karag Korvok is an impenetrable fortress, defended by forces without number?’ Clearly, this was nothing more than a boast.”

Silence descended across the entire God-forsaken chamber, as she stared impatiently at the object of her ire.

A long moment passed…and then Neris rolled her eyes, and gestured with one limp hand at the form of the Korvokhan.

Abruptly, Ul-Korveth staggered in front of me, as his faculties were apparently restored to him. He dropped to his hands and knees and gasped for a moment, shaking from the self-same experience that everyone else in the chamber was still suffering through. It took the Korvok several minutes to regain his composure, and when he did, he was still visibly shaky from the experience.

“Well?” Neris said irritably. “What do you have to say for yourself, boy? I’ve been more than generous in allowing you disgusting insects so close to such a sacred material locale.”

Ul-Korveth straightened his now stained white robes as best as I could, and though I couldn’t see his face, frozen as I was, I could still hear the same slimy smile he’d been trying to use on me in his voice. “You have indeed, Mother of Water. A thousand apologies for any perceived slight you might feel at the presence of our…” He paused for a split second. “Guests. But I will remind you that you were informed of the possibility of the Precursor bumbling into matters-”

Neris flicked an annoyed finger at the Korvok, and Ul-Korveth doubled over in pain as a jet of his own apparently pressurized blood burst from the right side of his chest.

My eyes widened at the sight. Not only did Neris apparently have enough control over blood to freeze thousands of people all at once and maintain that immobility, but she could weaponize it against us? That was…indescribably bad.

What the Hell do you do against an opponent like that?

As Ul-Korveth desperately tried to reason with the inherently unreasonable, unstable Spirit, I heard the barest hint of a whisper in my mind. “I finally realized why I was freaking out…” Fade ghosted to me, speaking as faintly across the bond as I had ever heard from him. “This place, we’ve both seen something similar before. I spent more time there than you did, but don’t you remember? Taran’s garden, it was a nexus just like this one. The Silent Eye…”

I did my best to hide my reaction, but I couldn’t stop a slight widening of my own eyes. He was right. That’s why I had had a strange sense of déjà vu earlier, before Neris’s appearance. The black water in the pool in the center of the cavern was nearly identical to the same fluid I had seen at the nexus point. The Great Spirits had private meeting places in the material realm, each for certain combinations of the sovereigns. I had encountered two of them, in my time. The volcano home of Shurenga, the nexus point between Earth and Sun, Orus and Tarus.

And the nexus point between Moon and Sea. Elys…and Neris. There, the nexus had manifested itself as a pond of blackened water, reflecting the exaggerated form of Elys in that dreamlike, bounded realm. The result had looked just like an enormous eye.

If Neris was manifesting like this in the Material…this had to be another Nexus point. And I had an unfortunate guess as to just who it was between, judging by the titanic, upside-down pyramid hanging from the center of the cavern.

But…where was the second Great Spirit, if one had chosen to show themselves?

While I was considering the depths to which we might be screwed, the small confrontation between Neris and Ul-Korveth seemed to be coming to a close.

“Silence, fool. Know your place,” Neris sneered. “You do not have the right to lecture me on possibilities. Now cease your prattling…”

I grit my teeth as the crimson not-eyes of Neris’s avatar turned back to me. I didn’t care for the considering look in them.

“If you won’t be of help, I’ll simply confront another source.” I was incredibly wary when that same dripping black finger rose to point at me, considering the wound I had seen her inflict on the Korvokhan only moments before.

I was…fairly relieved when instead of maiming me as well, she abruptly released her control over my blood. Unlike Ul-Korveth, I only staggered a little as I regained sensation in my limbs. I certainly didn’t fall to my knees. Instead, I took a deep breath, straightened my coat…and met Neris’s eyes across the distance.

I almost thought I saw an approving glint in the Great Spirit’s false orbs. “Come closer, Precursor. I believe it’s time for you and I to have a little chat.”

I considered my options for a split second, and even craned my head behind me to see how my forces were doing. As expected, it was incredibly eerie to see everyone locked into the last position they had been in before Neris had forcefully seized control over their bodies. Many of them were locked into expressions of fierce determination and outrage at what they had witnessed in the chamber just before us. But their eyes belied their internal panic. Just like myself, they retained control over their eyes and breathing alone, alternatively darting every which way or begging me to save them.

However, I noticed one thing in particular that caused a surge of fierce satisfaction. A particular absence among my retinue. I very carefully didn’t react to it, nor did I even dare to think upon it too deeply. I frankly had no idea about Neris’s capabilities in this form.

I would not betray their trust.

I turned back around to stare at an increasingly impatient-looking Neris, and slowly stepped forward into the chamber proper. My footsteps echoed oddly across the stone of the chamber, as I approached her approximated form of a human woman. I paid absolutely no mind to Ul-Korveth not far from our position. As far as I was concerned, the mutilated Dwarf didn’t matter at all right now.

It was time to treat with the power behind the flayed throne.

When I came to a stop before her, the two of us simply stared at each other for a moment. This close, it was far easier to tell that this body of hers was entirely composed of the black liquid that seemed to be her calling card. There was no structure beneath the flowing surface of it.

Just a seemingly endless abyss that I swore I could see moving shapes within, if I focused.

I tried not to.

Eventually, I inclined my head to the Great Spirit. If nothing else, an existence like hers was owed at least a little respect.

Otherwise, I feel like she would simply smear me across the floor.

“Neris. It’s been some time since last we saw each other.”

Her response was an oddly dry cackle, for such a wet existence. “So it has, Precursor. When last I saw you, you were naught but an ant that required the help of all five Great Spirits to swat a single gnat. You’ve grown, in what feels like a blink of an eye. A benefit of your abominable little ‘System’. I wonder. Would you even need our assistance once more, should you find yourself face to face with the same foe?”

I’m not afraid to say that startled me. She thought I might have been able to slay Rhazal all on my own? I’m…not sure about that. Yes, it was a fact that I had grown quite a bit since my confrontation with the Calamity in Elderwyck. But to say I could have taken him without their help?

I had to assume she was just trying to butter me up.

Honestly, I took it as a good sign. An…odd one, to be sure, considering she was party to a plan to indirectly eradicate most life on the face of Vereden, but still a good one.

I allowed a small smile to grace my lips in return. “I thank you for the compliment, o’ Lady of Deep Waters. And you are…looking far more corporeal than I believed most Spirits, much less Greater ones, could comfortably stand. Is it because we find ourselves before the physical Nexus point between your realm and that of Lord Orus’s?”

“Noticed that, did you?” Neris gave me an approving smile. “Yes, that’s part of the reason. This space, as tawdry as I find it, is seeped in my essence. It is…tolerable enough, for the moment. Still, I would prefer to finish out business, so I can return to the Concord and continue my...work.”

I nodded slowly, even though I had no idea what she was talking about. “Before we continue, my lady…might I ask a boon?” When the avatar ‘graciously’ gestured for me to continue, I turned in a side profile and looked pointedly at one person in particular among my retinue. “Would you consent to loosening your hold on my Familiar? I believe it would be appropriate for one closer to Spiritkind to be present for these talks. Surely, one of the Sons of Lady Elys can be party to our talks?”

Neris tapped her bottom lip in thought with one wet finger. “Hmm. I suppose so. If you had asked for anyone else to slip beyond my grasp, I would have had to punish you for the impudence. I’m familiar enough with this one, after all the time they’ve spent in proximity to another one of my Nexuses.”

I carefully didn’t react to the implied threat. It was just a fact of life, for an existence as volatile as Neris. They were going to casually menace you from a position of superiority.

Abruptly, Fade regained control over his body, and a full-blown lupine shiver traveled up and down the length of it. Hesistantly, he trotted forward to join me before Neris, and dipped into a brief bow using his front legs.

“Lady Neris,” Fade said carefully, keeping his gaze lowered. “It’s…good to meet you. Taran wasn’t sure if your attention ever fell on the Silent Eye, in modern times. I’m sure he’ll be…overjoyed to know you still consider us worthy of it.”

I saw Fade fight off another full-body shudder as Neris reached down and patted him on the head almost fondly. “Oh, I bear no antipathy for the sons and daughters of my siblings,” She chuckled. “It’s only certain…other players that I would see erased from the material.”

“I suspect that has to do with your presence here, Lady Neris,” I said evenly, reaching down to lay a comforting hand on Fade’s furry head. The horned wolf gratefully leaned into it. “I…will admit to having some contact with Lady Anima, and she informed me of your…confinement of her in the core. Further, she believes you to have some manner of alliance with the mortal Dwarf currently attempting to wrest Fynneas’s mantle away from him, by casting it into the skies above Vereden.”

I was careful not to directly accuse her of anything, but that didn’t seem to be enough caution. My cheek twitched as I felt the very blood in my veins surge oddly.

“Do not…” Neris said warningly. “Speak that name in my presence. But I am unsurprised that somehow, dear Anima found a way to circumvent her little time-out, while Orus and I handle matters. She’ll be let out in good time, Precursor; don’t you worry about that. My sister simply doesn’t have the stomach, as you mortals say, for what must be done.”

I considered her quietly for a moment. “And what must be done?”

The resulting smile she sent me was so hard, so full of wrath, and hatred, and spite that I felt a chill down to my very bones.

“Vengeance,” Neris hissed. “After oh so many years, finally the Great Spirits are on the verge of our ultimate triumph over those who robbed us.”

“And there is nothing that will stop our revenge from being bloody.”
