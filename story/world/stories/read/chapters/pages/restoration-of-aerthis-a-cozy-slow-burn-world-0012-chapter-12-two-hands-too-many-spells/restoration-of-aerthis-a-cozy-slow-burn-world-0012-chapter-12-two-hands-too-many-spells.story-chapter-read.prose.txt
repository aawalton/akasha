The next couple of days passed quietly, one cloudy and the other she spent almost entirely indoors due to the rain. It was a good thing too, she figured. Hydration was important! For people and plants alike.

Elynara kept herself to light chores, small guild quests, helping with laundry, and reading in small stretches tucked away in the library’s warmth. She kept up with her mana practice however, just not overmuch. Enough to keep progressing, but never pushing to the edge of exhaustion like before.

The change was subtle at first, but she noticed how the mornings came easier. Her head felt clearer, her body less weighed down by the constant ache that came from pushing herself too far, too often. She even caught herself humming once while sweeping, stopping only when she realized what she was doing.

As an added benefit, when she drew in mana now, it was cleaner, smoother, the threads settling into her core without the resistance they once held.

Recovery, she realized, was its own kind of training.

And on the morning of the third day, she woke early, the soft gray dawn light painting quiet lines across the ceiling. She lay there a moment, listening to the faint stirrings of the church coming awake around her.

Her legs stretched easily, toes pointing until a quiet pop eased through her ankles, and her arms lifted overhead without the same heaviness that had settled there days prior.

A slow exhale left her lips as she sat up, a small, satisfied smirk flickering across her face.

Better.

She reached for her notebook, flipping to the mana notes she had marked carefully the days before. Her pencil hovered, then moved in her clean, careful hand.

Reached 52 capacity.

Confirmed the requirement increase, now needing 7 cycles in a row.

Whether it changes at higher levels is uncertain, but for now it holds.

The numbers matched, the pattern held, and her focus had grown sharper for seeing it confirmed by her own testing rather than vague guesswork.

It was always nice to confirm a new discovery!

She closed the notebook with a quiet snap, setting it aside.

A flicker of green light brushed the edge of her vision. Starpuff drifted near, its glow pulsing gently as it looped in a small, smug arc before it came to hover in front of her face.

Elynara raised an eyebrow, flicking a finger at it lightly. It dodged with an exaggerated tilt, looping around her finger before darting up to hover near her hair. She giggled at its antics.

“You’re in a good mood today.”

It puffed up, glow brightening as it spun lazily, clearly pleased with itself.

She shook her head, unable to stop the soft snort that escaped her, before turning to get ready for the day. A few hops and stretches later and she was ready for the day ahead.

But before taking off, she decided to test an idea she had just the other day.

Settling back onto her bed, she let her hands rest on her knees, her staff leaning against the wall within reach.

The memory of the children’s laughter during her courtyard demonstration bubbled up, and with it, a thought.

“What’s stopping me from having two spells active?”

She pulled in a slow breath, reaching for mana carefully, shaping a small flame in her right hand. The warmth flickered gently, a soft, steady glow.

With her left, she reached for mana again, forming it into a small sphere, the shape wavering before settling into a pale, pulsing orb.

Not wanting to cause an accident, she made sure the ones used were weak and easy to dispel, just in case.

And for a moment, both held.

Then came the strain.

It was like trying to read two lines of text at once, eyes sliding between them, unable to focus fully on either. The flame wobbled, pulling too much, and she adjusted her grip, only for the sphere to pulse unevenly, demanding her attention.

She let out a quiet breath, narrowing her eyes, forcing herself to steady both shapes.

Seconds passed, stretched thin by the concentration required.

Finally, she let them both disperse, the flame snuffing out, the sphere dissolving into fading sparks.

She huffed and flexed her fingers at the failure, rolling her shoulders once as the tension drained from them, before trying again.

This time, she formed two mana spheres, one in each hand, letting them float just above her palms. It was easier, but not by much. The moment she tried to spin them in place, to test how much control she could keep, they wobbled, one nearly slipping free before she reined it back in.

Next she tried to push it even more, less focused on shaping and more on the quantity. But even just holding three unshaped blobs of mana separately was quite an ordeal.

Elynara let them dissipate, breath steady, pulse quick.

“Again.”

She tried two small flames next. Harder. The warmth of each fought for her focus, flickering differently, demanding her attention like two children calling her name at once. Holding them still took everything, the thin line of sweat at her hairline proof enough of the strain.

When she finally let them go, she slumped back, arms heavy, but the corners of her mouth tugged upward.

“Hard to hold many at once, but two are barely manageable. Will need consistent practice.”

She didn’t bother writing it down this time, but the note settled firmly in her mind.

For now, it was enough.

A small, satisfied smile tugged at her lips as she glanced at Starpuff, who hovered smugly, as if to say Of course you can.

She let out a small, quiet laugh, letting her shoulders relax.

Taking a few calming breaths, she went on to finish organizing her things for the day, tucking her notebook into its usual spot, adjusting the strap of her satchel with practiced motions.

Her staff tapped softly against the floor as she made her way down the hall toward breakfast, the quiet hum of the church wrapping around her like a comforting shawl.

She caught sight of Father Alaric near the doorway, speaking in low tones with one of the sisters. He glanced up as she approached, and a warm, easy smile settled on his face. It was the kind that made the edges of the day feel less sharp.

“Ah, good morning, Elynara. Hope you have slept well.” he said, inclining his head in that gentle way of his.

The warmth of it settled in her chest, and she returned the greeting, dipping her own head in kind. She let a small, genuine smile tug at the corner of her mouth, a little more cheer entering her legs.

It was weird how so little could brighten up one’s day.

Not that she was complaining.

The dining hall was alive with the muted clink of bowls and the soft, bright voices of the children clustered around the long tables. She hadn’t even taken three steps in before a handful of small hands shot up in greeting.

“Morning, Miss Elynara!”

She raised her hand in return, giving a small wave, the flutter in her chest blooming as their bright faces turned back to their meals with laughter.

Starpuff drifted near her shoulder unseen, but she could feel the smug air it carried, hovering with the unspoken confidence of a spirit companion proclaiming:

Yes, my hooman is importantest. Bow, little minions!

A small, helpless laugh slipped out, hidden behind the spoon she raised as she took a mouthful of warm porridge.

Breakfast passed in gentle warmth, the smell of spiced oats and freshly cut bread lingering as she cleaned up, setting her bowl aside before stepping out front.

The air outside was cool after the rain, but carrying the promise of warmth to come.

Deciding to take the scenic route, she stepped out into the morning light, letting the breeze brush past her hair as she turned down one of the quieter side streets.

These paths weren’t as well-kept as the central road, the wood and stone worn, some spots patched, others left to crack.

She noted the thin lines of smoke rising from chimneys, the distant clatter of a blacksmith’s hammer ringing in a steady rhythm. The air smelled faintly of bread baking, layered with the morning’s cool scent that would soon give way to warmth.

The early market was beginning to stir, a few stalls creaking open, merchants rolling up awnings, crates shuffled across the ground with dull thuds.

Her thoughts drifted as she walked, her staff tapping lightly against the packed dirt.

Fifty-two mana capacity.

She was proud, yes, but the reality settled in with a dry edge. Pushing further, toward a hundred and beyond, wouldn’t just take effort.

It would take time.

Far more than what it took her to get here.

Although also less than one would expect. Her cycling was getting just a little faster, just a little more effortless, a little more efficient. Practice makes perfect was a saying she heard a variation of even in this new world. But even then it would be an uphill battle.

Nothing she could see in the horizon would make it easy to speed that up..

“Progress will be over twice as demanding as when I started, even with it becoming easier.”

The words were a mutter under her breath, her lips pressing into a thin line.

Annoyance sparked, and she let it sit, simmering as she tried to pull something - anything - from the swirling fragments of memory that still lurked in the corners of her mind.

Passive processes? Automation?

The words came, vague and useless, without explanation, slipping through her mental grip before she could hold onto them.

“Completely useless now,” she muttered, rolling her eyes at herself.

She thought back to every practice session, every cycle she pushed to the edge of discomfort. She had never managed to cycle mana without her full focus, and despite the whispers of a memory claiming doing things passively was largely possible, reality had yet to match.

Perhaps I’ll find a solution later. But for now, I keep pushing.

Starpuff zipped by in a spiral around her, plopping directly into her cheek at the end of its arc. The sudden ‘attack’ nearly made her stumble.

“Wh-Hey! You little rascal!”

If its glowy trill and puffed up bobbing in place was anything to go by, it was innocent of this crime!

Snorting, she let out a slow breath, the irritation fading. Her audacious spirit companion was many things, but accountable certainly wasn’t one of them.

Her mock-disapproval only made it even more inclined to do weird things.

‘Asking for forgiveness’, my butt!

You don’t fisticuff people into forgiveness! If it peppering her with little ‘boops’ could even be considered as such, that is.

“Argh, alright, alright! Just don’t do that again.”

She was absolutely certain it would do it again.

Seemingly satisfied, it zoomed over to settle down in her hair as if nothing happened.

…That little troublemaker.

Letting out a breath, her thoughts swirled back to the problem at hand, now weirdly lighter after the distraction.

After all, keeping her practice a steady routine was really the best she could do. That had carried her thus far, a reliable companion on the path of progress.

And she saw no reason to stop.

The guild came into view as she rounded the last bend, the sign creaking softly in the morning breeze.

Elynara lifted her chin, focus settling in her eyes as she approached the door, ready to see what requests the day would bring.

She spotted Marcus near the entrance, leaning against the doorframe with his arms crossed, half-watching the street with a lazy sort of alertness.

She gave him a small nod in greeting.

He responded with a grunt that somehow managed to sound jovial, paired with a crooked smile that made the corners of his beard twitch.

She blinked once, realizing she hadn’t known a grunt could sound jovial, before stepping past him, the door creaking softly as she pushed it open.

The guild hall was comfortably busy, the hum of low conversation mixing with the occasional scrape of a chair or clink of coin. The scent of old wood, oiled leather, and the faint tang of ink settled around her as she stepped over to the request board.

Alicia was at the counter, speaking quietly with a young adventurer who looked like he hadn’t quite grown into the weight of the gear he wore, her fingers tapping the counter as she explained something with crisp efficiency.

Elynara let herself take her time, scanning the pinned requests with careful attention. Most were routine: deliveries, minor gathering runs, the occasional request for pest clearing outside the walls. She paused over one that asked for temporary help within town for simple errands, light lifting, and helping to organize stores.

Perfect.

She pulled the slip from the board and turned, waiting until Alicia finished with the young adventurer before stepping forward.

Alicia glanced up, her eyes flicking to the slip in Elynara’s hand before she took it, scanning it quickly.

“Good pick,” she said, giving a small nod of approval as she reached for the stamp, pressing it down with a clean thump on the edge.

She slid the processed slip back to Elynara before adding,

“By the way, up for filling another mana battery? Same as before.”

Elynara didn’t even hesitate.

“Of course.”

Alicia’s mouth curved, the barest hint of warmth beneath her professional exterior, before she moved to set the slip aside.

Seeing that there was no one behind her, Elynara shifted her weight slightly, adjusting her grip on her staff, before letting her curiosity run her mouth free.

“Are these mana crystals used for the Tablet you mentioned before?”

Alicia paused, one eyebrow lifting slightly as she glanced back at Elynara, a hint of impressed approval in her eyes.

“Partly, yes,” she said, setting a small stack of papers aside. “But not only for that.”

She gestured lightly as she explained.

“The stored mana we collect is used for a few things. Emergency communications through the Guild Communication Tablet, when needed. Operating special devices and artifacts, though with Oltenar being a frontier town, there aren’t many of those out here. It’s a small but necessary system that keeps things running when the usual methods fail.”

Elynara nodded slowly, the image of the rune-etched tablet flickering in her mind.

She hesitated, remembering her talk with Josh and the stories of spirits out by the farms, her confidence in her own skills having grown with every practice session.

“Are there any jobs around the farms? I would like to check them out.”

Alicia shook her head lightly, tucking a strand of hair back behind her ear.

“Nothing directly at the farms right now, but…”

She flipped through a ledger, eyes scanning lines before continuing:

“There are some gathering requests near the area. They would take you close enough.”

She closed the ledger, her gaze lifting to meet Elynara’s.

“Check back tomorrow. I’ll see what can be lined up.”

Elynara felt a quiet excitement spark in her chest at the thought of safely exploring beyond the walls, of seeing more than the narrow roads and stone corners of Oltenar.

“Thank you.”

Alicia gave a short nod, already turning back to the next task at hand.

Elynara stepped back, slip in hand, the hum of the guild folding around her as she tucked it carefully into her satchel. Her staff tapped softly against the floor as she made her way toward the door, her mind already moving ahead to what the day would bring.

Stepping out of the guild, the town was already alive with the sounds of clattering carts, snippets of conversation, and the steady rhythm of boots and sandals on packed earth.

She moved through the morning with quiet purpose as she worked through the guild’s local help request. The work wasn't difficult as it was mostly carrying small bundles, lending a hand with shifting crates, and waiting while a shopkeeper sorted what needed to be handed off to another vendor.

It let her watch how the town moved when people weren’t paying attention to who was watching.

Children zipped between carts with sticks in hand, shouting things only they knew the meaning of. A baker flicked flour from her hands as she called greetings to a passing guard, the guard tipping his head with a small grin. Someone’s laundry snapped in the breeze, shirts dancing like lopsided ghosts above the street.

Now and then, Elynara caught a wave from a child she recognized, or a nod from a face she’d seen before. She returned each with a small lift of her hand, feeling a soft warmth settle into her chest as the town’s rhythm moved around her.

By the time the tasks were done, the sun had crept higher, the smell of midday meals curling between stalls and drifting down the streets. Her stomach gave a quiet, pointed reminder that breakfast had been a while ago.

She stopped by a smaller food stall she hadn’t tried before, drawn in by the scent of warm spices and sweet glaze hanging in the air. The vendor, a middle-aged man with sleeves rolled high and a patchy beard, called out to her as she eyed the skewers, flipping one with practiced ease.

“Hungry, miss?”

“Just a bit,” she admitted, eyeing the unfamiliar glossy pieces lined alongside more standard fare. “What’s that one?”

“Sweetroot. Good chew, sweet and tangy. Locals like it.”

She considered, then pointed.

“One sweetroot, one meat skewer.”

“Coming right up.”

He handed them over wrapped in a thin leaf, and she passed over a few coins, tucking the change away before stepping aside.

She carefully bit into the sweetroot skewer, and hissed as soon as the steam hit her tongue.

“Hot!”

She fanned her mouth, cheeks pink, before she blew on the next piece, managing a second bite with less drama.

It was worth it, though. Sweet, with a tart edge, a warmth like cinnamon sitting comfortably on her tongue. The texture was soft, almost like potato, but had a pleasant chew she found herself appreciating.

“Not bad. Might get this again.”

A flicker of something near her cheek made her pause, and she felt, more than saw, Starpuff’s invisible smugness as it hovered close. But before she could pull the skewer back, a faint tug on the piece at the end of the stick told her it had nibbled its share.

“Hey–”

It bumped her nose lightly, a wordless, playful “thanks” before darting back, leaving only the faint tingle of mana in its wake.

She rolled her eyes, a small smirk pulling at her lips as she took another bite, letting herself enjoy the warm moment.

Once the skewers were finished, she cleaned her fingers on a cloth from her pocket and made a final round of errands, picking up a few perishable supplies she would need in the coming days.

She paused at a small stall selling simple dismantling tools, checking them over before selecting a few she would need for training and gathering once she was ready to venture further.

Satisfied with her purchases and with little else she could think of needing from the market, her steps took her in the direction of the church.

The walk back felt lighter somehow. Like the day’s activities and leisure walks were all that was needed to dispel her frustrations.

Or was that just the power of tasty food? Only Goddess would know.

Her steps were steady, staff tapping softly against the road as she moved past the slow swirl of midday activity, her mind quietly turning toward the battery filling task waiting for her.

Ready, and without rush, she made her way home, getting there just as afternoon struck.

She passed through the main hall, nodding to a sister carrying a basket of folded cloth, before slipping into a quiet corner near a small window where the light pooled warm against the floor.

She lowered herself to sit cross-legged, setting the crystal in front of her with a small clink. Her staff settled across her knees, grounding her as she rolled her shoulders back, letting out a slow breath.

The soft sounds of the church moved around her. The distant voices, the creak of wood, the faint breeze slipping through the window.

“Alright.”

Focusing, mana easily heeded her call, her larger pool letting her push it out in calm, firm pulses. The battery accepted it, the glow inside barely visible at first, a faint spark that grew as she maintained the rhythm. She let her breathing match the pulses, slow and even.

Above her, something flickered, and she felt a soft tug at her hair.

“Really?” she muttered, eyes flicking up.

Starpuff hovered, invisible to anyone else, but she could sense the smug amusement as it tugged a strand loose, letting it fall across her eyes before swirling lazily above her head.

When she pulled the hair back with an exasperated huff, it bumped her forehead lightly in what she could only interpret as encouragement.

Or perhaps it was just hungry.

Rolling her eyes, she shifted her focus back to the crystal, testing herself by pulling in the thin trickle of ambient mana while still pushing energy out. It was like balancing two buckets on a narrow beam; it held for a few moments before she felt the tension snap, forcing her to pause and reset.

She tried again, determined, feeling the strain in her shoulders as she settled back into the cycle.

A shuffle of steps and a faint, high-pitched chuff made her glance over. Mina stood in the doorway, Pufflet prancing at her feet, the small creature’s ears flicking with curiosity. Mina opened her mouth to say something, then hesitated.

Without even looking away from the crystal Elynara greeted her lovely little ‘intruder’.

“Hi Mina. You can stay, I don’t mind.”

Mina’s face brightened, and she stepped in, dropping to sit a few steps away.

Pufflet immediately bounded off, chasing something only it could see, before returning to drop a small scrap of rolled cloth at her feet.

Mina threw it across the room with a flick, and Pufflet dashed after it, skidding across the wooden floor with a soft thump.

The small chaos of the game filled the air with low, happy energy, a grounding counterpoint to the steady flow of mana around Elynara.

Smiling at the distraction, Elynara pulled in another breath, before restarting her work on the battery with renewed confidence.

She let the playful noises settle in the background, and soon enough she was back in her trance, slowly getting a better grasp on her mana multitasking.

It wasn’t long before Starpuff took its chances, brushing her cheek with a tickle of energy before darting forward, trying to nip at the stream of mana as it left her hand.

“Don’t you dare.”

Starpuff paused, hovering with exaggerated innocence, before drifting back, leaving a faint prickle across her wrist.

She almost smiled, shaking her head as she pushed another pulse of mana into the crystal, its glow growing brighter, warm and steady.

Pufflet crashed into her side as it missed a turn, making the mana flicker in her hand.

Catching the little troublemaker before it could scurry away, she ruffled the fluff out of the little furball. Her mock-indignation clear in her voice:

“Not. Helping.”

“Sorry!”

Mina called, trying not to giggle as she scooped Pufflet back up, scratching its chin as it grumbled, tail wagging.

The hours moved quietly, punctuated by the soft sounds of Pufflet’s paws, Mina’s gentle laughter, and the whisper of mana shifting from Elynara’s hands to the crystal. Each cycle grew smoother and less jarring, until she could hold both streams flowing comfortably enough.

When the crystal finally pulsed with a soft, complete glow, she let her hands drop, the last threads of mana settling back into her core. She stretched, rolling her shoulders with a small, satisfied groan as she leaned back on her hands.

Mina was sitting cross-legged now, Pufflet sprawled across her lap, its tiny paws twitching in sleep. She glanced up, giving Elynara a bright, content smile.

“Done?”

Elynara nodded, picking up the crystal, now warm under her fingers.

“Done.”

She slipped it back into her satchel, pausing as Starpuff drifted down to nudge her cheek once before darting away, leaving a faint glow in its wake.

“Thanks for staying,” Elynara said, standing and adjusting her staff.

Mina smiled, brushing Pufflet’s fur back from its face.

“It was fun.”

Elynara gave her a small wave, before setting off to the guild once more.

Leaving the church with the mana crystal tucked safely in her bag, her staff tapped lightly against the stone as she made her way down the street. Her arms still felt the steady pull of the hours spent charging it, but it was nothing a little bit of stretching and rest wouldn’t help with.

No sooner had the thought formed did she decide to act on it, tiptoeing in place while extending her arms overhead and to each side. A few passersby were clearly amused by the unusual sight.

Satisfied and already feeling lighter on her feet, Elynara’s attention turned towards the town around her. The late afternoon light washed it in soft golden colors, with shadows stretching between the buildings.

A few shopkeepers were pulling in signs, and she caught the faint scent of roasting meat drifting from an open window as she passed.

The guild hall was quieter at this hour, with only a few adventurers moping around. Marcus leaned near the quest board, arms crossed, and gave her a nod that she returned. She caught the hint of a small grin before he turned back to whatever notice he was reading.

She stepped up to the counter, where Alicia was sorting papers into neat piles, her eyes flicking up as Elynara approached.

Without a word, Elynara set the battery down, the faint glow within it catching the light.

“It’s done.”

Alicia raised an eyebrow, picking up the crystal and turning it in her hand before setting it back on the counter.

“That was quick. Managed it in one go?”

“Took a while. But it’s getting easier.”

Alicia’s expression softened into a small, quiet smile.

“Good work. Two requests done in one day. Keep it up.”

She marked something in her ledger before sliding a small pouch across the counter. The coins clinked softly, the sound crisp in the calm of the guild hall.

Elynara tucked it into her pouch, the weight grounding her.

“Thanks,” Elynara said, stepping back.

Alicia gave a small nod, already shifting her focus back to her paperwork.

Elynara turned, taking in the surroundings of the guild hall for a moment: adventurers at a side table checking gear, the murmur of a conversation about tomorrow’s weather, floorboards faintly creaking as someone moved past.

For better or worse, the place felt completely normal to her now, the familiarity growing on her. A brief flash of memory from a place long gone… Felt like a hazy reminder of something she couldn’t quite make out.

Shaking her head from those weird thoughts, she stepped back into the street, the light outside warm against her face as she adjusted her grip on her staff.

She welcomed the distraction, enjoying the fading warmth of the day on her way back to the church. Soon after her arrival, she was quickly whisked away into lending a hand with preparations for the evening meal.

It was a good while of helping with all sorts of random chores before she was let go to enjoy her own meal in the dubious quiet of the children-filled hall. The warm scents of herbs and bread was enough distraction for even the little ones to keep some semblance of order, it seemed.

Her thoughts of the quiet were quickly squashed when Starpuff decided messing with her food was the newest best thing to do.

Seriously, that little bugger!

Fending off its attacks with her bread went about as well as expected, to the joy of one of the kids. Embarrassed, she scarfed down her food, shooting the little troublemaker a look it pointedly ignored.

After helping a little with the post-meal clean-up, Elynara made her way back to her small room. The quiet creak of the door and the soft scent of old wood greeting her like a familiar blanket, and the steady tiredness in her limbs was all the reason she needed to flop onto her bed with a groan.

Recharged after a few minutes of laying down, she got back up to light a small candle, letting its warm glow chase away the corners of dusk, before sitting cross-legged on her bed.

Her staff leaned nearby within easy reach, her notebook settling into her lap as she flipped it open, pencil ready between her fingers.

She took a moment, her eyes drifting over to the small flame, as she let the day’s activities settle into place.

Filling the mana crystal while pulling ambient mana. Testing dual-shape handling. Watching how mana moved under sustained output. And how her progress would likely slow down soon.

Bits of moments flickered through her mind, and she began to write.

Daily Notes:

My mana control is improving. Dual-shape handling is possible, but it drains focus quickly. Worth training. Ambient intake during output is certainly possible. Needs refinement. …Maybe I can learn how to do it constantly? That way I wouldn’t need to worry about mana, if I pace myself.My capacity growth is going to slow, but for now I can only practice.

Her pencil paused, tapping lightly against the page. She thought back to her frustration from earlier while walking to the guild, the realization of how much longer it would take to push her capacity further.

At a hundred mana or beyond, every further improvement would take more than double the cycles it had when she started. The quiet math of it settled like a stone in her mind, firm and undeniable.

And those memories of passive systems still sat just out of reach, like an itch she couldn’t quite scratch. She frowned, writing down what fragments she could recall, though they were vague and infuriating in their half-clarity.

They weren’t even about mana either.

She set the pencil down, letting her eyes skim over the clean lines she’d written, the organized words pulling a sense of calm and direction into her chest. There was still so much to work on, so much that needed practice, but the progress was undeniably there.

And she was happy for it.

“Bit by bit,” she murmured to herself, a small, tired smirk tugging at her lips.

Across the room, she felt Starpuff’s presence, drifting in quiet loops, radiating its usual puffed-up approval. She glanced at it, letting her smirk soften into something close to a smile.

“Yeah, yeah. I know.”

She closed the notebook and set it on her small side table before sliding under the blanket, shifting to get comfortable as the candle’s flame flickered gently. Starpuff followed, drifting down to settle near her shoulder, a small, warm pulse in the dim light.

She reached up, giving it a light, sleepy pat.

“Goodnight.”

It hummed softly in response, and she let her hand fall, eyes closing as the warmth of the blanket settled around her.

A small, quiet excitement flickered in her chest, simmering under the calm as she thought of tomorrow’s plans, of the exploration near the farms she had set her sights on.

Another small, steady step forward.

The thought carried her gently into sleep, a soft smile still on her lips.
