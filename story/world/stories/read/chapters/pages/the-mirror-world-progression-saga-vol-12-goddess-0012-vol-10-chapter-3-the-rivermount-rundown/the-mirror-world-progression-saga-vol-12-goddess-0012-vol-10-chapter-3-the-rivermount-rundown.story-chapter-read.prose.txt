“We’re still finding traps, believe it or not,” the mystagogue in yellow — Yellow served well enough for his name — declared at the great table in the mindscape. “Not as many as before, and not to the same degree of power, but the Dominion remains committed toward ongoing petty terrorisms. On the plus side, hatred for the Dominion has rather skyrocketed. I suspect the number of conversions to the church is also up, due to this.”

All at that great table were masked and wore hats, naturally. Samantha gave the Rivermount folk she’d brought into the fold all desperado-style white hats and bestowed upon them a special honor: the only color allowed at the table, as a narrow ribbon around the hats. It was only fair, considering their long-time cultic traditions, and they did indeed seem very honored by the gesture.

As long as they don’t expect the sex stuff, too.

“The concerted sweep operation of our Negators is complete,” reported Eupraxia — the highest-ranked full Samanthian Antimage (as opposed to Glae’s loyalists) after Vitarion. “They’re exhausted and about three-fourths of them need a day’s rest, by the way. But they uncovered three more stubborn spell auras that did not dispel correctly and defy identification, but are almost certainly like the others — scrying vectors for clairvoyance. The goddess will have to use her tricks again to dispel them.”

Samantha sighed. It was likely pointless, as there were almost certainly auras that had been missed entirely—anchors she had to first detect in order to counter. Boosting passive contests was perhaps one of her greatest weaknesses, as she simply couldn’t utilize Alter Fate for it. Fortunately, these were usually subtle things of highly mitigated danger…

“No promises,” Samantha declared with a shrug. “At least we probably got most of those. We just need to stick to warding and absolutely securing the most important locations. Fanatically. This is being done, right?”

“Yes, Madam,” Eupraxia replied. “I trust Pythia can confirm that.”

“Naturally,” the Cat Sith, Pythia, said, in bored tones. “I agree with the goddess. We don’t quite have the manpower to ward the whole city. Fortunately, in addition to Samantha’s direct expertise, the talents of the Seven Mystagogues at being clandestine are unmatched, particularly the specialties of Red. Now that they don’t actually need to hide that they’re hiding, so to speak, well, they’re that much more potent.”

“Oh, stop it,” Red said in her rich, feminine tones, flicking a hand and chortling, every bit the spitting image of a noblewoman sophisticate. “Flattery will get you everywhere! You’re just too adorable over there, Pythia. My favorite feline, by far, truly.”

Pythia chortled and pawed right back at her. “No, you stop it! Eh hehe…”

“Never, dear! But I should say, those talents have only bloomed under the power of the Goddess of Illusion. Why, it’s like I’ve cast aside chains, fled from a dungeon, and frolic in Elysian Fields! Meanwhile, deeper forests call to me, promising those juicy, juicy higher levels of the [Priestess] class… tehehe!”

“As if you’d ever flee a dungeon,” Purple muttered under his breath.

“Oh, quiet you! I resemble that remark!”

“As we all do,” Brown replied. “What else did we ever do but muck about in the dark?”

“Oh, I did far more than muck about,” Green said in boast. “Though certain muck going in certain crannies was in fact my very favorite thing in the dark. Speaking of which, I recall that time you were very drunk, and your own little nook-”

Brown rounded on him, pelting him with a thrown chocolate from the table. “Quiet that despicable mouth of yours, Green! I swear, you are the worst of us in every way…”

The greater majority of the mystagogues of Rivermount laughed and talked over one another, carrying on for a spell, with seemingly ancient interactions.

Samantha smiled, watching them continue their banter and letting it go on for a while. It was nice to see them in high spirits. They’d been more somber in the time immediately after the battle, particularly as they’d lost one of their own, Orange, who, per tradition, was replaced from a small pool of candidates in the ‘outer cult.’

The old Orange had been in the central castle in the instant it had killed everyone inside when it turned into a golem. Unwittingly, their deaths were part of the spell itself, and there had not been anything Samantha could do. Countering it wouldn’t have stopped the sacrifices. Orange had understood the dangers of being in that keep, but stayed to the last, just to keep feeding intel as if he were an ignorant loyalist. He’d been the first to raise the alarm about the spell to Jeeves. Essentially, without him, Samantha would not have succeeded in countering and stealing the golem at all. He was a hero, and Samantha made sure everyone knew and honored that.

On the plus side, the gentleman was not entirely gone, either — he had his place of honor in Heaven, as he’d been a Follower to the last. It did take a bit of the sting out. Technically, he could’ve even continued his role as Orange, but the mystagogues were very stubborn and set in their ancient ways. None of them wanted that, and the tradition of replacement continued as normal. Samantha had to respect it, of course. The old Orange was a valuable asset regardless, almost immediately plugging in and becoming a leader, conferring with the Brain Trust from the outside.

Of all the Rivermount folk, the new Orange was unsurprisingly the quietest, being new. Much younger, too, by a couple of decades. At least he’d come in at a point where everything would be new to some degree.

“Alright, alright,” Samantha called, raising her hand and bringing them to silence, still smiling. “Enough with the banter. Let’s continue with the pertinent. I’ll be putting the finishing touches on the new castle soon.”

“Oh, that will be so wonderful, Goddess, dear,” Red cooed. “I can scarcely wait for the grand opening, it looks so beautiful already. Please, darling, tell me we’ll be the only city with such a crystalline palace like that! We’d be a-a tourist attraction one day!”

“Considering it has a giant golem as part of it,” Purple said, “I’d truly think there couldn’t ever be another like it.”

“Yes, yes, but I’m saying that glittering crystal, wouldn’t it be just fabulous as a Rivermount exclusive?”

Samantha laughed and shook her head. “Since you asked so nicely, Red, darling?” She said the word teasingly, looking over at her and leaving a long, pregnant pause, baiting out that anticipatory look she got in return, with the woman’s breath clearly being held in her chest. “Yes. That specific look to a castle I will leave for this project only.”

“Ooh!” Red made a very showy expression of happiness, as she flopped back, put the back of her hand to her forehead, and gasped with great, relieved melodrama. “For me?! I’m so touched, Your Majesty! I cannot handle the glory!”

A few chuckles went around the table at the display.

Lucky openly sniggered. “She’ll get along well with Constance, this one…”

Samantha turned to Aramis. “How is that crazy girl doing in Burnstire? All is proceeding as planned?”

Aramis nodded. “It’s ironclad, Madam. Burnstire is already moving. On the surface, they appear to be simply marshaling their forces and remaining neutral, in the logical deduction that your armies have little interest in wasting perhaps months trying to secure their flank. We’ve received intel that the Capital has already arranged for an equally neutral understanding, sweeping the incident in Port Al Kenstrom under the rug. Our own leaks show us to have impartiality agreements with purely domestic trade agreements in their favor. That’s all business as usual for Burnstire, who’d benefit from two giants killing each other for a while, with trade effortlessly still coming by sea. It appears our enemies are buying it.”

“And Burnstire is truly with us?” Brown asked, voice still mystified by this. All of the Rivermount folk had never ceased to be. They’d been preaching caution about trusting the duke, giving up their paranoia slowly and grudgingly. “They’re literally marching on Matrazere?”

Aramis nodded again, this time to Brown, soberly, insistently, and wearily. He’d been arguing with them doggedly about it for quite a while, particularly with Brown. “Half of their forces, anyway. It includes a great lot of very renowned and formidable knights we desperately need to square off against the best of the Heaven Knights, as our equivalent best are mostly the heavily limited numbers of the minotaurs. These Burnstirian knights are grizzled troll fighters — so to speak. For the first time, they’ll be side-by-side with their former enemies, no less.”

Brown snorted. “I’ll certainly believe that one when I see it.”

“Yes. As you’ve said before. Multiple times.”

“You should ask her to dinner, Aramis,” Green said, leaning over the table to meet his eyes. “I think she likes you, which is why she gives you so much trouble. Her tastes have become rather… picky, in recent years. Let’s just say, some tension isn’t relieved with any amount of debate.”

Brown glared over at him from the first words. “When we’re back in the real world, Green, I am going to bleed you for your improper audacity at this table.”

“Would you say no, then?” His voice was very teasing.

“Green. Shut. Up.”

Aramis was suddenly squinting in thought and smiling. “Now I’m curious as to that answer…”

Brown’s head turned to no one in particular, stiff-necked, as if she expected a subject change to save her without having to give a public answer. Samantha was sure she was red-faced behind that mask.

Zephyr saved the day, clearing his throat as he looked over at Samantha. “I’ve done as you asked, Madam, and verified all the information from the very busy Orswyth, while putting together everything I could on these mysterious lands we know of from his vision, that hypothetically still contains the artifact known as Last Light, invested with intense divine power…”

While still listening, Samantha got Aramis’s attention, pulled her mask up, and cupped her hand to clandestinely mouth the words, “Ask her in private!” She shrugged emphatically. “If you want!”

Aramis, expression amused, nodded in understanding. She gleaned it likely that he would, as he appeared a man (well, a dragon, actually) who had been pleasantly surprised by the revelation of a puzzle he had, until recently, been utterly confounded with. Now, it was definitely intriguing him.

She’s in luck. He’s picky, too, I think, but intellect and maturity are most of that pickiness.

Samantha dropped her mask back down and turned her attention back to Zephyr…
