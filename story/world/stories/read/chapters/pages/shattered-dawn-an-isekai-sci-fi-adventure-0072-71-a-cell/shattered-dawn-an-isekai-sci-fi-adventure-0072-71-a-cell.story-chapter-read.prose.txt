Elion looked at the Red Judge, and decided he wasn't going to make Prator order him to go into the room. Better to go in of his own free will. He pushed the door open and stepped into the dark dingy room.

A low wooden bench rested on the far side of the room. A woman lay on the bench, scrawny, tall, lanky. She did not react or look or move, other than to turn her head and to glare darkly at Prator through the gap in the door.

Bortim shut the door, darkening the room. The lock clicked.

Elion stood on the far side of the room, feeling frightened and alone. Who was this woman? Why had she been screaming?

He stared at her, his eyes adjusting to the gloom. She seemed familiar. Chains bound her hands and legs, the same type of manacles Kendry had used on Elion and Keyla.

The room, lit only by a grubby pane of glass high in the outer wall, held nothing else of interest. Dark shadows filled every corner.

“Another spy,” the woman groaned, chains clinking as she stretched. “Tell me,” she hissed. “Tell me, tell me, tell me!”

Elion took a half step back.

“Tell you what?”

“Tell me, who it is that whispers in my head?” The woman asked. She placed her hands on the bench, pivoting her hips and sitting up with her feet on the floor.

Staring across the room at Elion, she hissed. Drool dripped from her chin. Black eyes reflected the ray of light shining across the room. A yellow and blue bandana tied her hair back from her face.

“They whisper, whisper, whisper. Tell me… Lies.”

Elion’s fingers strayed to his side, to the place where this woman had shot him; on the dock in Aterfel.

Keyla’s mother.

“Selna?” Elion asked, cautiously. The woman twitched, snarling at the ground. The infection had not cleared from her.

“Selna Aerden?”

She snapped her head up, meeting Elion’s eyes. “That’s me!” she said. She chuckled, a mild, gentle laughter at first, swelling up to a full throated, belly shaking guffaw. Standing, she strained at her chains, attached to a loop on the ground.

Laughter gave way to screaming. “I’ll kill you!” she screamed. “I’m going to kill you!” Unintelligible words spilled out of her mouth, and her body shook. Manacles bit into red, raw skin.

“What happened to you?” Elion whispered. He couldn’t believe that they had been so close to Keyla’s mother before, without even realizing it.

“It’s okay,” Elion said, holding up his hands. “Hey, we’re okay!”

But Selna continued to rave, her voice hoarse and cracking. “He says we need to kill them. Kill them. Kill them… but I already knew that. I tried to kill him. He lied to me!”

“We need to get you a healer,” Elion said, mouth hanging open. It was a good thing Keyla hadn’t seen her mother like this. He wondered where she was now, what Boff had her slaving over.

Selna showed no signs of relenting. Elion moved, edging away from the door. Selna did not seem to notice him, raging on.

A flap at the bottom of the door, designed to allow food trays to be slid through, shifted. A ray of light shone into the room, breaking across the dark stony floor. The light was blocked as something furry pushed through beneath the door, straining at the narrow slot.

A moment later, Snickers shook himself, and batted with his paw at the slot until it closed.

“You’re still here?” Elion asked. “Good to see you!” he reached for the cat. Snickers nuzzled Elion’s hand, then prowled across the room and began rubbing his face against Selna’s legs. The woman calmed instantly. Her ranting and raving stopped as she sat down on her bench.

Snickers climbed into the woman’s lap. She stroked him gently, cooing at the cat like he was a baby. Snickers purred.

Elion grinned. “I guess Bortim’s not the one doing the work to calm you down, is he,” he said.

“Bortim is an idiot,” Selna spat. “He’s a fool.” A tremor ran through her body, and her jaw snapped, gnashing her teeth.

Elion started, surprised to hear coherent language from the woman. He needed to take advantage of this situation, learn something from her.

“You are Selna?”

“Yes,” she said. “Who in Zelian’s Halls are you?”

“My name is Elion,” he said. “We’ve met before.”

Selna writhed, her head twisting from side to side as she spoke, out of her control. “I don’t recall. Forgive me. I’m not really in a state—” A cough cut off her sentence, and then she began whispering to herself.

“Little legs, little legs,” she said. Or that’s what Elion thought she said. It might have been ‘middle eggs.’

“That’s okay,” Elion said, hoping to draw her out of the madness, back into lucidity. “You have a daughter. Keyla. Do you remember her?”

Selna’s eyes snapped back up to Elion’s face, her entire body going ridged. "Where is she?” she hissed. “What did you do with her?”

“Nothing,” Elion said, raising his hands defensively. “She’s here, in New Kairn Tol. She came to find you.”

“If you so much as plucked a hair from her head, I’ll—” Selna’s jaw snapped shut again, and she yanked at her chains, wincing as the manacles bit into her skin.

“We came to find you,” Elion said. “But she’s in trouble, and I need your help to…” but the rest of his sentence was drowned out by several aggressive shouts.

Snickers purred more loudly, and she began stroking his fur. Her movements were jerky and aggressive at first; marionette like, but with each pass of her hand, she calmed.

“You’re in danger here,” Selna said. “Prator can’t be trusted. He’s using the Red Judge to bind people against their will.”

“I know,” Elion said. “I’ve been bound.”

Selna groaned, pressing her head into her hands. “He lied to us. He fooled so many of us, building up his own empire here. I should have killed him when I had the chance.”

“Tell me,” Elion encouraged. “What happened?”

Selna peered across the room at him, her dark eyes reflecting the faint light shining in through the small window.

“You’re another of his spies.”

“I’m not,” Elion said. *Am I? I agreed to work with him. If she tells me secrets he could simply order me to spill them.* “But he has my Bondhold, so don’t tell me anything that he could force me to share.”

Selna considered this.

“I want to know what happened here. How did New Kairn Tol end up like this?”

“It was beautiful once,” Selna said. “My husband and I lived here. I ran the Thrandicite refinery, and the town was growing. We pushed roving bands of bandits out of the ruins, and created a good place for people to live.”

Selna reached down and gripped the bench with both hands, knuckles white.

“But it all fell apart after The Breaking of The Sky,” she whispered, voice thick with emotion. “The wall was broken down. We had to flee.”

A tear fell from her eye, dropping down into Snickers’ fur. “Davin wanted to come back. He thought we could rebuild. He wanted our daughter to know the life we had once lived.”

Elion watched intently, not daring to interrupt.

“He tried to go back, to reclaim our home. But amid the chaos of Dorian’s reign, it had become too dangerous.”

Selna took a deep, ragged breath. “They killed him,” she said, crying freely now. “He could have made them wealthy, but they killed him for scraps.”

She coughed, a wet, phlegmy cough. “Twist and pull,” she whispered, then began muttering under her breath.

“What was that?” Elion asked.

“Pull it tight! Pull it tight! Feel the warm blood on your hands, and let it drip from your elbows…”

She shouted loudly, her cries turning into barks. Her arms flailed, yanking against the manacles which bit deeply into her skin. Snickers slipped discretely away from the raving woman, hiding under the bench.

Elion tried reengaging Selna, but the moment of clarity seemed to have passed. He didn’t dare approach her, for fear of being attacked.

She continued howling angrily for at least an hour, before finally settling down. Snickers reemerged and returned to her lap. Selna stilled, calmly stroking Snicker’s head.

Sinking to the ground on the far side of the cell, weariness overcame Elion. He watched Keyla’s mother stroking his cat, and wondered if he could discover anything about the plot to kill Prator from her. Maybe he could leverage that information to help Keyla.
