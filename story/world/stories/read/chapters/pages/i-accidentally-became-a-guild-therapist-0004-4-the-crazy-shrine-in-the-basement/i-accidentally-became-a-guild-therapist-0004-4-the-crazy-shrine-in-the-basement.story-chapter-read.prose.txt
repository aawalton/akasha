ARC I: THE ACADEMIC EXILE

SYNOPSIS: Livia Quinn, now the Mental Supporter Lv. 2, starts to feel relatively adjusted to the madness of Bloodbath & Beyond. However, Guild Master Miles Vexley drags her to a secret “strategy room” beneath their base. There, Livia discovers a reality far more terrifying than dragons or immortal existential crises: A Mass Obsession Cult dedicated to her—The Emotional Buffer.

Livia Marcelline Quinn, the freshly leveled-up Emotional Buffer 2.0, was starting to think her new life as a Mental Supporter Lv. 2 wasn’t the worst fate.

This was a low bar. The bar was on the floor. The bar had, in fact, been buried underground and was currently being used as a foundation for a worse bar. But she was clearing it, and that counted for something.

Sure, the Bloodbath & Beyond guild was a walking disaster—Bron’s existential meltdowns, Phina’s slime poetry, Nyx’s Hello Kitty flashlight that was always, always on—but she’d survived their chaos.

I was racking up EXP by soothing their absurd traumas.

Her Therapist’s Log was a mess of scribbles: Patient: Phina. Issue: Slime crush. Approach: Don’t laugh. Her HUD glowed with new skills—Soothing Aura Lv. 1, Verbal Hug Lv. 1, Group Therapy Lv. 2. Her robe was slightly less itchy.

That counts as progress, right?

Still, the camp’s relentless chaos grated on her introverted soul. Players shouted about “raid metas,” NPCs looped glitchy lines like “Error: Quest Data Corrupted,” and a stray fireball nearly singed her hair. She sat on a salvaged bench, doodling a stick-figure slime, muttering, “If I get one more ‘Sentiment Processor’ comment, I’m throwing this log at someone.”

[Achievement Unlocked: Grumpy Therapist. +10 Sarcasm Points (Still Cosmetic)]

Livia swatted the air. “Yeah, yeah, system. Keep laughing.” Her progress bar read Mental Supporter Lv. 2: 200/2000 EXP. She was one crying paladin away from another level.

I desperately need a break.

As if on cue, Miles Vexley, the Psychoblade Commander, sauntered over, his smirk sharp enough to cut through her defenses.

“Yo, Buffer,” he drawled, leaning against a tent pole with the practiced ease of someone who had never in his life leaned against anything without making it look intentional. “You look like you’re plotting an escape. Don’t. We need you.”

Livia shot him a deadpan glare. “Stop calling me Buffer. And I’m not escaping, I’m… strategizing.” She waved her notepad vaguely.

Miles chuckled, his armor glinting with menace. “Strategizing, huh? Good luck. This guild’s a dumpster fire, and you’re our only extinguisher.” He jerked his thumb toward the base. “C’mon, we’re having a strategy meeting. You’re invited. Don’t ask why.”

Livia’s stomach sank. A meeting? With those lunatics? Her curiosity—damn it—dragged her after him.

The guild’s base was a labyrinth of chaos: enchanted weapons littered the ground, a slime juggled mana orbs, and Alaric, the gambling paladin, was trying to bet his boots on a “sure-thing” goblin race. Livia dodged a stray spell and nearly tripped over Nyx, who was hiding inside a crate with his flashlight aimed at the gap between the lid and the rim, illuminating absolutely nothing useful but refusing to be switched off.

“This place needs a health inspector,” Livia muttered, scribbling: Guild Base: Hazard Level—Therapist’s Nightmare.

Miles led her to a stone staircase descending into the base’s underbelly. “Watch your step, rookie,” he said, his tone teasing but edged with something unreadable. “This is where we keep the real treasures.”

Livia raised an eyebrow. “Treasures? Like… loot?” Her gamer instincts—dormant since she discovered her combat stats were literally zero—perked up involuntarily.

Miles smirked. “Something like that.”

I should bolt now.

The staircase spiraled down in tight loops, the walls pressing closer with each step. The runes carved into the stone glowed an unstable blue—the kind of blue that suggested they had been installed by someone with enthusiasm but limited technical understanding.

Halfway down, the staircase widened into a landing, and Livia’s gaze snagged on something to the left that had nothing to do with whatever waited below.

A shelf. Old wood, slightly warped, holding a row of ledgers and record-plates that looked like they predated everything else in this building by a significant margin. She slowed without meaning to.

“What’s this?” she asked, already reaching for the nearest ledger.

Miles didn’t stop walking, but he didn’t hurry her either. “Guild archive. Old registry stuff.”

She opened it anyway. Names. Dozens of them, in a font the current system didn’t use anymore. Some were marked ACTIVE. Most were marked RETIRED. A few had no status field at all—just a name, and beneath it, blank space where a class and level should have been.

[DATA UNAVAILABLE]

She turned the plate over, like the back might explain the front. It didn’t.

“How long has this guild existed?” she asked.

Miles was quiet for exactly one beat too long.

“Long enough,” he said, and this time he did stop—just long enough to take the ledger gently out of her hands and set it back on the shelf, exactly where it had been. “Old server garbage. Come on. Almost there.”

He put it back facing the same direction it was already facing. That’s a strange thing to be precise about, for garbage.

She filed the observation somewhere she wouldn’t lose it, and followed him down the rest of the stairs.

Her HUD pinged: Warning: Restricted Area. Access Level: Guild VIP Only.

She froze. “Uh, Miles? I’m an NPC. I don’t have VIP anything.”

He waved a hand. “You’re with me. System’s fine with it.” His smirk widened, and Livia’s gut screamed trap.

I should have bolted.

But before she could run, the chamber’s contents came into view, and her jaw hit the floor.

The room was a shrine.

A deranged, obsessive, deeply committed shrine.

Dedicated entirely to her.

Livia stood in the doorway and did not move, because moving would mean accepting that this was real, and she was not ready to do that.

The first thing she registered was the body pillows.

There were a lot of them. They lined the walls in two rows, floor to ceiling, each one bearing her face—her actual face, rendered with alarming accuracy, her expression serene and slightly confused in the way she’d looked during Bron’s breakdown. But the outfits were not hers. The outfits had been chosen by someone with strong opinions and absolutely no restraint. There was a maid dress version. A battle armor version that somehow still showed midriff. A “cozy librarian” version that had taken considerable liberties with the definition of “cozy.” A bikini version—two, actually, different colors, clearly a debate had occurred. And one in a black lace lingerie set that Livia was fairly certain cost more in the real world than her monthly rent had.

Why the lingerie, Livia thought, with the detached calm of someone whose brain had temporarily suspended processing. What narrative are they constructing where I own lingerie. I died in a gray robe. I am currently wearing a gray robe. Where does the lingerie enter the timeline.

[Emotional Resilience: Pending Review]

Not now, system.

The second thing she registered was the statue.

It stood in the center of the room on an actual plinth—carved stone, polished, the kind of plinth you commissioned when you wanted something to last. The statue depicted her at approximately life-size, mid-hug, arms wrapped around a slime that was smiling with an expression of pure contentment. The plaque at the base read, in formal engraved lettering:

LIVIA, EMPATH ETERNAL  —  She Listened When No One Else Would

Livia read the plaque.

She read it again.

She Listened When No One Else Would.

They had written her a memorial inscription. She was still alive. She was standing in front of it.

[Achievement Unlocked: Achieved Posthumous Fame While Still Breathing. +1 Existential Crisis Point]

The third thing she registered was the fanart.

It was everywhere. Stacked in crates, pinned to boards, rolled in tubes, loose on the floor. Most of it was the heroic kind—Livia standing in shafts of golden light, battling dragons with a notepad, the word BUFFER written in the sky like a divine title. Technically accomplished. People had put in hours. This somehow made it worse.

And then there was the other pile. An entire crate, overflowing. The top piece depicted her in the black lace lingerie from the body pillow, except now she was also holding the notepad, and the caption read “Therapy Session: After Hours.” The one beneath it was a beach scene. The one beneath that was… Livia closed the crate. She closed it firmly. She put her hand on top of it for a moment, as if it might try to open itself.

Livia closed her eyes.

She opened them again.

The crate was still there.

[You discovered NSFW fanart of yourself. +999 Embarrassment Points. Achievement Unlocked: I’ve Seen Too Much]

[System Note: Therapy for this may be required. Unfortunately, the only available therapist is you.]

The fourth thing she registered—and this was the one that broke her—was the terminal.

It stood in the corner, glowing, running what appeared to be a live feed. The interface was unmistakably modern—a real-world website somehow piped into this fantasy basement through some architectural miracle of cross-dimensional internet infrastructure. The header read:

r/LiviaWaifuHub — Members: 50,247 — Online Now: 3,891

Pinned Post: [MEGATHREAD] Best Moments of Our Therapist Queen 🧠❤️ — 14,302 upvotes

Top Comment: “I cried when she told Bron to breathe. I wasn’t even playing the game.” — 👍 8,847

Fifty thousand members.

Fifty thousand people had found each other specifically to discuss her.

She had been in this world for three days.

I failed practicum, Livia thought, with the hollow clarity of a person standing in the rubble of their own expectations. I got a C-minus on my thesis. I died eating a stale croissant. And I have fifty thousand parasocial fans.

Livia’s face burned. She turned to Miles, who was doing a very bad job of not laughing. “You people built a creep shrine. To me.”

Miles shrugged, his composure cracking at the edges. “Not my idea. Blame the fans. You went viral after Bron’s breakdown. Players in the real world are obsessed.” He gestured at the terminal. “There’s even an ASMR compilation. Someone uploaded forty minutes of you saying ‘breathe in for four.’ It has two million plays.”

“Two million—”

“You have a fan-made lore wiki,” Miles continued, clearly enjoying himself. “Your class, your stats, your psychological methodology. Someone wrote a twelve-thousand-word analysis of your therapeutic approach titled…” he squinted at the terminal, “‘Livia Quinn and the Epistemology of Emotional Labor in Late-Stage MMO Capitalism.’”

Livia’s eye twitched.

This is worse than failing my thesis. At least that didn’t involve slime fanart and epistemological analysis.

“This is a nightmare.” Livia clutched her notepad, her introversion imploding in real time. “I’m a background NPC! I have zero combat stats! Why—” she pointed at the body pillow wall, specifically at the black lace version, —“why does this exist? I wear a gray robe. A GRAY ROBE. Where did anyone even get the reference material?”

Miles glanced at the lingerie pillow with the expression of a man who had clearly seen it before and formed no opinion. “Fan theories,” he said simply. “They think there’s a ‘true form’ under the robe. There’s a seventeen-page thread about it.”

There is a seventeen-page thread dedicated to speculating about what I look like under a gray robe.

I have been here three days.

[Achievement Unlocked: Lore Character Before You Even Did Anything. +5 Notoriety Points]

From the shadows, Nyx emerged, his flashlight sweeping the floor ahead of him in its perpetual pink beam. He looked at the shrine, then at Livia, then at the floor. “It’s… not that bad,” he mumbled, his hood doing most of the work of hiding his expression but not quite finishing the job. “The art’s… kinda nice.” A pause. “Some of it.”

His blush was visible even under the hood.

Livia added a new entry to her Therapist’s Log: Nyx. Issue: Has a shrine to his therapist in his guild’s basement and thinks I don’t notice that he’s been in this room before. Approach: Unknown. Pray.

Phina glided in, the slime—Glimmer, apparently—trailing at her heels with its usual ambient jiggle. She surveyed the room with the expression of an art critic attending a retrospective. “A shrine to your empathic radiance!” she declared. “The fans capture your essence, though they lack the poetic nuance of your soul.” She tilted her head at the NSFW crate. “This section, however, shows considerable technical skill.”

I am seriously considering screaming.

Bron stomped in, took one look at the statue, and lit up like a man who had found something genuinely wonderful. “YO! Buffer! That’s you! That’s literally you but in stone!” He turned to Livia with the enthusiasm of someone about to ask an extremely reasonable favor. “You gonna sign my body pillow?” He held up the bikini variant. The red one.

Livia stared at the bikini pillow.

The bikini pillow stared back.

She opened her notepad and wrote: Shrine: Existential horror. Issue: I am a meme, a lore character, a waifu, and apparently the subject of seventeen pages of fan speculation about my body. Approach: Cry forever. She underlined cry forever twice.

Miles clapped a hand on her shoulder—a brief, solid thing, nothing performative about it. “Relax, rookie. Fame’s a weapon. Use it.” His voice dropped, losing its usual edge. “Besides—this guild protects its own. Nobody touches our therapist. Not players, not fans, not the system, not anyone.”

Livia looked at him.

He was not smirking.

That, somehow, was the most alarming thing in this entire room.

Her chest did something complicated—a tangle of dread and something warmer underneath it that she immediately tried to catalog away into a separate folder labeled Do Not Process Right Now. These people were chaos incarnate. They had built a shrine. Bron was asking her to sign merchandise featuring her own face. Nyx was blushing in a corner. Phina was complimenting NSFW crate organization.

And they… were protecting her?

Her sarcasm kicked in because it always did. “Great. My personal security detail is a crying immortal tanker, a slime-obsessed archmage, a rogue who requires a cartoon flashlight to function, and a gambling paladin who currently does not own shoes.”

Miles’ smirk returned, but quieter than usual. “And me.”

Livia opened her mouth.

Closed it.

And me.

She wrote it in her notepad, small, in the margin, where the actual session notes went: And him.

[System Note: You are not furniture. You are not a meme. Classification: Pending.]

As the guild erupted around her—Bron demanding his own shrine, Phina composing an ode to the statue, Nyx retreating further into the corner with his flashlight and his feelings—Livia stood in the center of it all and looked at the plaque.

She Listened When No One Else Would.

She was just an NPC. A glitch. A budget support character with zero combat stats and an itchy robe and a notepad she’d gotten from a Comic Sans scroll.

But they had carved her in stone.

And that, she thought, was either the most horrifying thing that had ever happened to her—or the first time, in this world or the last, that anyone had.

She was way, way over her head.

She was also, possibly, exactly where she was supposed to be.

She was not going to examine that thought right now.

[END OF CHAPTER 4]
