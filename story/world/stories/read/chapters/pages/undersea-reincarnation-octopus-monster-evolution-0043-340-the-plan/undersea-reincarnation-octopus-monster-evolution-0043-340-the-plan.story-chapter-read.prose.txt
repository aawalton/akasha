Rummaging in side pouch for a few seconds, Velistraine pulled out a small, golden contraption that looked somehow like a pocket watch. She held up, glanced up at the sun and hovering planet, then narrowed her eyes. She’d already taken the newly summoned ring and had splayed a map of the world on Henry’s table and now, she was trying to approximate the directions of the three remaining orbs.

Eying the device in her hand she rotated the map a little, then tapped it while Henry peered over her shoulder, trying to see what the compass was displaying. Unfortunately, it didn’t seem to be mechanical and most likely, it was working on some divination enchantment and projecting its content straight in her mind.

“There,” she said. “The middle one is likely pointing at the Emerald Isles. So Atall’s corner. The right one could be anything between us or the Reach. Or past it. But considering the history of the place, I’d bet my hat it’s in there. Which… depending where that is, might be challenge. I can’t say if it’d be worse if it’s in a human settlement or in monster territory. But we’ll deal with that when we get to it.”

Henry peered curiously at the map. While he didn’t think everything on the parchment was to scale, it was interesting to see how large the Reach was compared to everything else. And how… empty the whole map was. The map was centered around a series of islands and archipelagos that bordered a single bifurcated continent. Not a lot of details were on that side of the map, aside from a few dotted names near the edges and golden circle near the coast. As for the rest, it was all water, with four golden circles spread out somewhat evenly.

There are five pillars in total, Henry hummed. Velistraine continued, looking up at the sky for a moment.

“So. We got one in the Isles, one possibly in the Reach. And the last one is that way,” she said, tracing with her finger over an empty stretch of ocean that was only interrupted by one of five golden circles. “There’s no land in this direction, unless we go all the way around. But there’s another pillar. Either way, it’s likely underwater.”

“What are we starting with?” Maurice asked excitedly as he floated himself above the table. Zerathstra shrugged, scratching his head while Arisia observed intently as Velistraine tapped the map, an excited glimmer in her eyes.

“Atall first. We know exactly where that one is so better to get it over with first. We’ll keep an eye out if the other two happens to be closer than we thought. We’ll be able to tell if we watch the angles for the ring’s paths. Then we’ll go from there. Do we bring them all back here?”

“I’d appreciate it,” said Arisia, while Zerathstra nodded. “I’d love to see what happens when they’re all together.”

“Probably safer as well,” mumbled Zerathstra. “Considering the forces at play, it’d be better if we’re together. In case it summons something. Or if it teleports all those present somewhere we can’t follow.”

Henry nodded. “That’s probably better. I can maybe shorten the trip by then with a few hops through my clones. By then, I should be able to handle it a little better. Especially if we can meet some A-ranks on the journey.”

Velistraine clapped, a wide smile on her face. “We’ve got a plan, then.”

***

After a quick session of catching up, Velistraine, Zerathstra and Arisia all trailed away to let Henry get some rest, which left him alone with a slowly levitating crab.

“Ready for a new journey, Maurice?” asked Henry, laying back on the lounge chair.

“Yes!” Maurice said, raising both claws as two of Henry’s Octominds raised their arms in concert. The crab had been hanging by the edge of the pool, the water lapping at his dangling legs. The two Octomind projections had latched on to Maurice’s shell and were just goofing off with the crab. Henry eyed them for a moment, lids heavy, and yawned. He hadn’t had that reflex in a minute, but it felt good to do it in his human form.

“I’m gonna miss it here,” Maurice began as he landed at the edge of the pool. “But I want to see the other places too. How was your fight by the way?”

Henry would be lying if he said he wasn’t curious as well.

“It was fun. Painful, but fun. I didn’t really get much out of it when it comes to new skills, but I’m pretty sure both Lightning Discharge and Lightning Dash got a big boost from Magic Mimic when I got a taste from it. It’s pretty specialized in lightning. It was good all in all. Got some more skills ready to upgrade thanks to whole thing. I just need to take a few hours to go through the process. I’ll do it after my nap.”

“Right… we don’t have access to the System to do it for us, anymore,” Maurice mused as he produced a kraken roe and turned it around in his hand while Henry nodded.

“Exactly. It’s not too difficult, though. How about you? What have you been up to? I was surprised you didn’t want to join to fight the lobster.”

“I wanted to! But I needed help from Arisia for something. Also… lightning isn’t fun,” he said, drooping a little. “I have the resistance and everything but…”

Maurice trailed off, shuddering, and Henry chuckled. “Fair enough. It’s not fun most times.” Henry sunk resting his head against the weaved fibers, closing his eyes for second. “So? Did you get what you want from Arisia?”

“Yes! But it still needs work. I’ll show you when it’s ready.”

Henry cracked an eye open and eyed the crab a little. “Hmmm… alright then,” he grumbled. Then closing his eye, he continued. “I’m gonna take a nap. I’ll see you later.”

“Okay! Bye!”

Maurice zipped away, with Henry’s two Octominds raising their arms as if they were riding on a roller coaster, and Henry’s consciousness slowly drifted away a small smile on his lips.

***

Henry wasn’t exactly sure how much time had passed when he’d woken up, but it was in the early hours of night. The soft purples over the horizon told him it had been barely an hour since the sun had set, and feeling rested, he began pulling on his stash of cores, a big grin on his face.

“Let’s do some upgrading, then. But before that…”

Taking a second, Henry made sure everything was fine through his Octominds’ vision and memory so he could focus on his task, and he quickly confirmed that nothing problematic had happened. With that done, he got started.

Guiding his consciousness inward, Henry assigned two Octominds to isolate the area in his soul that held Lightning Discharge and Lightning Dash, then with the others he slowly scanned the rest of his abilities for what else was ready to take the next step.

Voracious Grip is ready to move on to B-rank. Maw and Fortitude of the Trickster as well. Spiteful Detonation is D-rank… and it’s ready to take the next step. Toxic Ray isn’t ready to upgrade from D-rank, and I still need to mess with it a little. Echoing Arm and Summon Illusions aren’t ready to get past D-rank… but Siphoning Touch is. Mana Nexus is ready to go up to B-rank. Telekinesis is ready as well. Identify?… feels ready. Hopefully once it’s at C-rank, I’d be able to sense high levels a little better. Domain and Pathfinding are both going to stay in C-rank. They need a little more time.

With his list established, Henry drew on the potential trapped in the cores and started dousing the abilities he felt had matured enough. He still had to work on them one at a time, just in case he ran out of tokens. But concentrating on holding the energies felt a lot easier than it had been the first time.

One by one, the abilities stepped into the next rank, their spiritual form pulsing and growing larger in his spirit and in the end, after a handful of hours, all the abilities he’d singled out had upgraded. All but Spiteful Detonation, who Henry guessed he had misinterpreted what he felt from it. But it was close, he was sure. He’d bet he just needed one or two more fights with the explosive skill to see it upgrade.

Getting up from his chair, Henry stretched his arms above his head as his remaining Octominds orbited around excitedly, one of them landing on top of his head. Henry looked up at the dangling arms and smiled. “How about you start looking into changing Toxic Ray a little? I want to be able to use my own venoms through it. Try… working with a bit of conceptual essence of Predation and Thaumaturgy. Create a one-time skill with it, and we’ll see if we can patch that in Toxic Ray.”

The Octomind in question acquiesced and faded away, disappearing back within Henry as it went to work.

“Let’s grab a bite to eat, then,” Henry mumbled. He glanced up at the city, but after considering it for a moment, he just popped a couple of skewers he’d kept from the last celebration, and ate those before he washed them down with fresh, refreshing water.

Then, finally, Henry got some much needed rest.
