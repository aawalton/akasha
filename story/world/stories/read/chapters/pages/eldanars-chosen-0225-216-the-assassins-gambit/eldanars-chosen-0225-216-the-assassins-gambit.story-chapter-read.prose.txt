The heavy iron portcullis of Ashenfall was raised just a few feet, leaving a narrow, jagged gap above the freezing cobblestones. Perberos slipped beneath the rusted iron spikes without making a single sound. He disappeared out into the pitch-black void of the northern plains, leaving the fragile, candle-lit safety of the city behind him.

The elven ranger was completely in his element. The chaotic, deafening noise of the Guildhall and the suffocating emotional weight of his farewell with Carcan were entirely locked away in a small, compartmentalised corner of his mind. Out here in the dark, he was not a brother, and he was not a friend. He was a weapon, honed to a razor-sharp edge by years of grief and survival.

He paused a few yards past the city gates, allowing his luminous silver eyes to adjust to the oppressive darkness.

Ronald melted out of the shadows to stand beside him. The feline beastkin wore no heavy metal, his muted green cloak blending perfectly with the dark horizon. Behind Ronald stood the hand-picked squad of elite stealth operatives the beastkin had assembled for the mission.

There were only four others. Garth was a heavily scarred, grizzled human tracker who carried a massive, oiled-wood heavy crossbow on his back. Lyra, a lithe half-elven rogue with cynical eyes, was currently spinning a throwing dagger across her knuckles with practised ease. The final two were a pair of human rangers, one a seasoned veteran named Silas, and the other a remarkably young, eager scout named Finn, whose hands trembled slightly as he gripped his shortbow.

Ronald did not speak a word. He simply raised a clawed hand, gave a sharp, forward motion with his fingers, and began to jog silently up the old, frost-covered dirt road leading north.

The squad fell into a loose, staggered formation behind him. Perberos took the rear-guard position, his senses fully extended to ensure they were not being tracked by any rotting hounds.

As they moved away from the city walls, Perberos experimented with the physical reality of his recent body evolution. He focused his mind on the ambient darkness pressing against his leather armour. He felt a cold, comforting rush of magic flood his veins. His physical form seemed to lose its sharp definition. His boots did not crunch against the frozen earth. His movements did not disturb the freezing winter air. He was actively integrating the aspect of shadow, becoming a living ghost walking across the plains.

They travelled in complete, unbroken silence for nearly an hour. The pace was punishingly fast, a relentless, ground-eating jog designed to cover maximum distance before the true cold of the night set in.

However, as they crested a low, rolling hill, Ronald suddenly threw his fist up, signalling a dead halt.

Perberos dropped into a low crouch, instantly reaching over his shoulder to draw a heavy, steel-tipped broadhead from his magical quiver. He peered into the gloom ahead, expecting to see the glowing green eyes of a Crypt Weaver or a dense pack of skeletal warriors.

Instead, he saw the landscape itself shifting.

They were still miles away from the abandoned forward operating base, yet the pristine, frost-covered grass of the plains simply stopped. The ground before them was consumed by a creeping, foul-smelling tide of black sludge. A thick, ankle-high layer of necrotic fog rolled lazily over the corrupted earth, actively killing the frozen roots of the roadside weeds.

Ronald crept backward, crouching low beside Perberos.

"The corruption is spreading significantly faster than our original estimates," Ronald whispered, his voice barely a breath against the freezing wind. "It is acting like a magical floodwater, preparing the ground for their advance."

"But where is the advance?" Lyra murmured, stepping quietly up beside them. She kept her twin daggers drawn, her eyes darting nervously across the empty, blackened plains. "If the corruption has reached this far south, we should be knee-deep in ghouls by now. The outer edges of their horde should be swarming right up against this fog line."

She was entirely correct. The dark, corrupted plains were eerily empty. There were no wandering packs of rotting stags, no massive, scuttling spiders, and no clanking, rusted infantry. It was a dead, silent void.

"I assume they are maintaining some form of formation," Perberos noted coldly, his strategic mind analysing the empty battlefield. "Mindless undead do not march in tight, disciplined columns. They spread out and consume everything in their path like a swarm of locusts. If the plains are empty, it means the boss monster is keeping them tightly leashed."

"A unified army," Garth grunted quietly, spitting a small wad of chewing tobacco onto the frost. "That is the worst-case scenario. If they hit the walls of Ashenfall as a single, concentrated battering ram instead of a disorganised wave, the main gates will shatter in minutes."

Ronald’s tail lashed once beneath his cloak. "We push further north. Stick to the dead tree line bordering the road. We need to get eyes on their actual numbers."

They stepped off the frost-covered dirt path and moved into the creeping black sludge. The smell was immediately overpowering, a suffocating mixture of stagnant water and old blood, but they ignored it. They slipped into the sparse, dying trees lining the eastern side of the road, using the thick trunks and deep shadows to completely mask their approach.

They moved deeper into the corrupted zone for another thirty minutes. The necrotic fog grew thicker, rising past their knees, obscuring the ground and making every step a calculated risk.

Then, Perberos heard it.

He stopped instantly, closing his silver eyes to isolate the sound from the howling winter wind. It was a deep, rhythmic, terrifying vibration travelling through the corrupted bedrock. It was the sound of tens of thousands of skeletal feet hitting the earth in perfect, unnatural unison. The heavy, metallic clanking of rusted iron armour and the groaning of massive wooden wheels accompanied the dreadful march.

The undead army was moving, but the sound was distant. They were significantly further north than Ronald had originally anticipated.

The squad gathered tightly together behind the massive, rotting trunk of a fallen oak tree.

"They are moving slowly," Silas, the veteran human ranger, observed in a low whisper. "If they maintain that marching speed, they will not reach the city walls until mid-morning at the absolute earliest. We have found the leading edge. Do we wait here in ambush to count their numbers and identify their siege weapons, or do we turn back now and relay the timeline to Caistina?"

Ronald frowned, his slit pupils dilated entirely in the gloom. He was weighing the immense risk of staying against the vital necessity of gathering accurate intelligence.

"We do not wait, and we do not fall back," Perberos interrupted softly.

The elven scout stepped forward, his luminous silver eyes fixed entirely on the distant, dark horizon where the rhythmic marching echoed. His voice was completely devoid of fear, carrying only a cold, uncompromising, and lethal certainty.

"We are going to cut the head off the snake," Perberos stated flatly.

Ronald looked at the elf, his ears flattening slightly. "Explain yourself, Perberos."

Perberos reached behind his back, tapping the intricately carved leather of his magical quiver. Carcan had spent a small fortune in the Artisan District earlier that day, entirely draining the Guild's promissory note to fill his spatial storage with the highest grade ammunition available in the city.

"I currently possess three hundred heavy, steel-tipped broadheads, and fifty solid-steel, armour-piercing bodkins," Perberos detailed quietly. "I am carrying enough concentrated stamina draughts to run for two days straight without sleeping. I did not come out here to simply count walking corpses."

He pointed a pale finger toward the north.

"We know this horde is being commanded by a highly intelligent entity, likely the Bone Goliath general or a Crypt Wraith," Perberos explained, his tactical logic flawless. "If that creature reaches the walls of Ashenfall, it will coordinate a focused breach, and the city will fall. However, if we push further north, bypass their marching lines, and locate the commanding unit in the dark..."

"You want to assassinate a general in the middle of an undead army," Lyra breathed, her eyes widening in a mixture of profound shock and genuine respect.

"If the commanding intelligence dies," Perberos nodded, "the dark magical tether binding the horde together will instantly fracture. The army will revert to its basic, primal instincts. They will scatter. They will become a disorganised, chaotic swarm that the heavy fighters on the walls can easily manage and destroy over time."

Perberos looked directly at Ronald, his expression carved from pale stone. He thought of his sister, currently waiting in the city behind them, and the heavy oath he had forced Josh to swear. He refused to let the walls fall. He refused to let Carcan be put in that kind of danger.

"I am going to put a steel bodkin directly through that creature's skull before it ever sees the lights of our city," Perberos promised. "I will end this siege."

Ronald stared at the elven ranger for a long, calculating minute. The beastkin was a pragmatist. He knew the odds of successfully infiltrating an undead army of that magnitude and surviving an assassination attempt were microscopically low. It was, by all rational definitions, a suicide mission.

But Ronald also knew that if the undead army reached the city gates fully coordinated, everyone inside the walls was going to die anyway. It was a horrific gamble, but it was the only offensive play they had left.

Ronald gave a single, slow nod of agreement. He did not waste time with dramatic speeches or warnings.

The beastkin turned his jade-green eyes to Finn, the young, trembling human scout.

"Finn," Ronald ordered quietly, pulling a small, blank scroll from his pouch and pressing it into the boy's hand. "You are returning to Ashenfall immediately. You will inform Rochelle and Caistina that the corruption line has advanced three miles further south than anticipated. Tell them the horde is marching slowly, and estimate their arrival at the main gates for three hours past dawn."

Finn swallowed hard, gripping the scroll tightly. "What about the rest of you? What do I tell them you are doing?"

"Tell them we have identified a high-value target, and we are moving to engage," Ronald replied smoothly, offering the terrified boy a fierce, confident grin. "Run fast, Finn. Stick to the shadows. Do not stop until you are behind the heavy portcullis."

The young scout gave a shaky salute. He turned immediately, melting back into the sparse treeline and sprinting south toward the distant, unseen city, carrying the vital timeline back to the defenders.

Ronald turned back to the remaining four veterans. They were the absolute best stealth operatives in the province. They all understood the lethal reality of the choice they had just made. There would be no rescue squad coming for them if they were caught.

"We need to be absolutely silent," Ronald commanded, his voice dropping to a harsh whisper. "No magical auras unless absolutely necessary. We push past their front lines, locate the command centre, and we strike fast. Perberos takes the primary shot. If he misses, we all go in."

Garth grunted his approval, loading a heavy steel bolt into his massive crossbow with a quiet, well-oiled click. Lyra slipped her throwing daggers back into their sheaths, readying her short recurve bow.

Perberos pulled his elven longbow from his shoulder, checking the tension of the string. He allowed the shadow aspect of his body evolution to wash completely over him, his physical form blurring at the edges as he fully integrated with the suffocating darkness of the corrupted forest.

With a final, silent nod from Ronald, the five assassins slipped deeper into the creeping necrotic fog. They turned their backs on the safety of the city and began their terrifying, silent hunt, marching directly into the teeth of the approaching apocalypse.
