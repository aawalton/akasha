I ignored the chaos that broke out below me and focused on pursuing my prey. While I hoped that no elves would get hurt, they also outnumbered that kitsune hundreds to one. There was only so much I could do beyond presenting them on a silver platter, and I really didn't want the other one to get away. I couldn't fix the firewall, but I could prevent another infection.

Visually, I hadn't been able to spot anything that separated the illusions from the real one. There had been too much ambient smoke to tell which one had a personal smoke column of corruption. But the other one had not only the visual smoke billowing from her, she also carried the corresponding scent with her.

I dove to the spot where the first copy I'd seen entered into a building, and there wasn't any trace of that smoke scent. At the next entrance, I had more luck. Definitely a trace of smoke here, very recently. That had to be the other kitsune.

Off in the depths of the building, I heard their rapid footsteps. I contemplated simply flying off to the other side and waiting for them to exit into the jungle, but those buildings were labyrinths, capable of housing thousands. If they had time to hide their tracks and assume a new glamour, it could take hours to hunt them down.

Ten paces inside, I realized that no matter how much I had surprised them here, they came prepared for someone doing what I was doing—tracking them by scent. I caught a tiny trace of it before the main body hit me, but there was no dodging scent.

No idea if it was magic, alchemy, or chemistry, but what they had deployed was what could only be called a stink bomb. The smell that hit me was a mixture of the sweet, rotting scent of decay, the sharp, headache-inducing scent of ammonia, and other foul notes I didn't even have names for.

I staggered, came to a halt, and had to realize with no small horror that this shape didn't come with a gag reflex. Part of me wanted to violently react to this, while the body lacked the capability. Which I should probably have been grateful for, as it likely wouldn't have helped.

Still, I wasn't left with any other choice but to retreat. Outside, I found myself blinking rapidly. This shape also lacked the capacity for tears or a runny nose, so apparently there was no mechanism whatsoever for flushing the smell out of whatever passed for my olfactory system. I needed another shape, but I wasn't sure which yet.

Jamie and Zi'rah came running up to me, Jamie's wings already on display. Before either of them could ask what happened, I waved them off.

"Fly. Don't enter. Cover exits."

There was a very short negotiation, entirely carried by exchanged looks between the two, before Jamie unceremoniously picked Zi'rah up, princess-style, and took off into the air. They'd had that discussion before, when they came to join me toward the end of the fight with Aleria.

As I tried to shake the lingering daze off, I focused on other senses. With a hand and ear against the brick wall, I could both sense the faint impact of running and hear the corresponding steps. And as they grew fainter, I became certain that they didn't plan to hide but to escape into the jungle.

While I was still gathering myself and my thoughts, there was a muttering, and the steps became nearly inaudible—the impact I couldn't sense at all anymore.

Fuck.

If I had to guess, they had something akin to the scout's Cloaking spell. Even dizzy as I was, I took off into the air, through the canopy and across the building. The owl shape might be required to beat this camouflage, but I didn't want to risk it while those scents lingered. There was a non-zero chance they'd knock me right out.

On the other side, Jamie was not flying a perimeter as I had expected. The minimap put him inside the building facing the jungle, and as I got closer, I could hear him arguing with an elf three floors up. Zi'rah was outside the bounds of the city, and with her spear in hand, she was taking cover behind a tree that already had two arrows sticking from its bark.

Those elves were very lucky they missed. Tearing their heads off for hurting one of my own would seriously threaten our mission, but it was the perfect alignment of the jungle's and the shape's desires.

I dove into cover next to her. Jamie's argument had at least stopped any of the guards on this side from blindly shooting at everything that showed up here.

"Did you spot something leaving?"

We both peeked around the tree, looking for movement.

"Sorry, boss. Bit of a hostile work environment. No idea if something snuck past. What are we hunting?"

Right. From where the elves had kept those two at sword point, they didn't have a chance to even catch a glimpse of the infiltrator's glamour giving out.

"Kitsune. Fox people. Did you get hurt?"

Zi'rah huffed. "You saw me fight your knight, didn't you? Takes a bit more to get me." My knight. Something to clarify later. "What happened to you, though?"

"Stink bomb."

Zi'rah turned her head to sniff in my direction before she began howling with laughter.

I elbowed her head. "Be quiet. They could be sneaking right past us."

Zi'rah quickly regained her composure. "Sorry, boss."

With every passing second, I became more and more certain that they had already slipped past us. The elves firing at Jamie and Zi'rah had been the perfect cover. Which meant I needed a better tracker. Sadly, the gryphon's sense of smell matched its eagle head, not its lion body.

["Jamie. I'm going to take your wings away. I've got to track on the ground."]

The discussion paused briefly before I got a response in chat.

["Roger that. Will catch up."]

I turned to Zi'rah and gave her a grin. "We are going on a fox hunt."

The tree was barely sufficient cover for the dire hyena. I quickly followed up the shape with casting Spectral Tackle, and Zi'rah didn't hesitate before jumping on my back.

Even though the monstrous scavenger's nose was a lot more powerful than that of the Archon, it was also a lot less bothered by the repulsive smells that still lingered with me. Before the first elven guards could loose arrows in my direction, I found a trail that confirmed my suspicion. Now their stink bomb worked against them, as it very faintly lingered, even in their magically concealed tracks.

I bounded off into the jungle after them.

While I couldn't chase them at the full speed this shape was capable of, I was still gaining on them. Their trace stood out more distinctly with every minute that passed.

There were, of course, also chimeras. None crossed our path directly, but we saw multiple signs of their presence around us. The omnipresent bloodlust that had been there when I first entered the jungle had given way to some kind of non-violent coexistence between the survivors. They'd likely rapidly fought their way to a very violent food chain but stopped at utter mutual extinction.

Any carcasses I came across were at least a day old at this point.

The kitsune hadn't left any more nasty surprises. In fact, when I finally caught up to her in a small clearing, she was kneeling on the ground, bowed so deeply that her nose nearly touched the ground. Not facing me, but facing east. I quickly checked if there was anything there, but the moment I jumped into the clearing, her head snapped around, and her eyes widened—fox eyes.

She was, without a doubt, a she, despite the male elven persona she'd assumed. Her face was mostly human, besides the fox eyes and ears. And from under her outfit, which looked like a black-and-silver mix between a kimono and a cliché ninja outfit, two very fluffy white tails poked out.

And she was utterly terrified. She scrambled to face me, though she never stood.

"Mercy. Please. I cannot fight you," she said.

I circled her, and Zi'rah jumped off my back.

"Permission to stab, boss?"

"Wait," I said, using Queen's Voice. All of my senses told me that none of the kitsune's fear was faked. She was afraid to die—justifiably so.

I was fully aware that part of the reason for my hesitation was that this plea came with a very pretty face. Objectively speaking, she was very likely involved in sending an assassin after me, working for an enemy who wanted the extinction of everyone I knew, and would probably stab me in the back the moment she got the opportunity.

But she was also unarmed, no danger to me at this moment, and begging for mercy. She also hadn't heard my reply to Zi'rah and went on.

"I will talk. You have my word. Take me with you and take me prisoner. Break my arms and hands if you must. But please don't kill me. Please take me with you." Punctuating that last sentence, she glanced back in the direction she had previously faced.

That's when I sensed it. Something very heavy, running up to us, trying to do so silently. And it was silent; I only felt the impacts of its paws through the forest floor.

She was scared for her life. But what she'd been terrified of wasn't me.

"Zi'rah! My back! Now!"

Once more, Zi'rah showed zero hesitation, and that might have saved both our lives. My legs were already tensed for a leap, and the second she gripped onto the saddle, I leaped while turning into the gryphon.

Behind us, something burst out of the jungle. I only caught a glimpse of it—of her—out of the corner of my eye. Riding on the back of the freakishly giant tiger was a tall figure covered in a cloak of liquid night, her head hidden beneath its hood, with only traces of white hair spilling out from underneath. She carried a spear at least eight feet long that gleamed with silver.

The last thing I saw was her arm pulling back for a throw before the canopy blocked my vision. I still did a sharp turn to the right in case she decided to take a blind throw. The spirit had only been a warlord's minion, and those arrows had eventually leveled a building. I had no intention to land on the receiving end of an actual attack.

But that never came. I stuck low above the trees as I flew as fast as I could, my heart pounding in my chest.

Fuck, that had been far too close.

At some point, Jamie intercepted us in the air. At first I barely even noticed his presence. My mind was so focused on threats that anything outside that pattern failed to register.

"Did you catch them?" Jamie asked.

"Yes. No. We did, but then a woman on a tiger showed up, and we fucking booked it," Zi'rah replied.

"You okay, Eve?"

"I'm fine. Only a bit spooked. She promised she'd kill me when we next met. That was last time we met. She promised she'd kill me this time. I think I got away, but that kitsune didn't. And she really didn't want to be with her either. I'll be okay. I think."
