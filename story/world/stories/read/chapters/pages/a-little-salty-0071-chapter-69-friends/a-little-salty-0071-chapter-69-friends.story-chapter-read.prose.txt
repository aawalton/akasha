It’s been a while since I’ve had one of the lucid dreams. Getting stabbed in the last one was not a pleasant experience, even if it hadn’t been real. Can’t wait to see what I’ll get to endure tonight.

The smell of firewood fills the air. It’s deceptively pleasant, reminiscent of campfires from my childhood. Unfortunately, it’s not firepits that are burning.

If there are people screaming for help, their cries are swallowed by the roar of the flames. The wood and paper houses all went up like kindling—even the wards set up to prevent fires couldn’t do anything to stop such devastation. A cold fury rises up within me, and I clench my teeth.

Rinviu would pay for this. Reckless, singleminded, arrogant—

A whimper catches my attention. I turn toward a burning building and cock my head. The intense, wavering heat makes it hard for me to see anything inside. But the sound comes again: the whimper of a child. I step forward—

And wake up with a gasp. The heat still feels tight against my skin, but now I’m wreathed in unfamiliar shadows. Where am I?

Álvaro snorts nearby, then rolls over and goes back to sleep. My disorientation fades. Right; we’re still at the healing clinic. They let us stay in an empty room while we wait for Cyros to recover. I let out a weary sigh and begin to settle back onto my sleeping mat.

“Sorry to wake you,” a voice says, and I just about leap out of my skin. “But it seemed like you were having a bad dream.”

I sit bolt upright, but by now I already recognize the voice. “Lisari,” I greet with a suppressed groan. “It’s the middle of the night.”

“An astute observation,” the god agrees. She’s crouching in a shadowy corner of the room, back leaning against the wall, arms draped casually over her knees. “Don’t worry, I’ll direct our words so they won’t wake your friend.” A gentle breeze brushes against my skin.

Words appear in my vision.

[L: Unless you’d rather use the Contact Interface.]

I squint and wave a hand before my face before realizing this is a System message, and I get Echo to dismiss it. I don’t like that she can put words into my head. I glare her way, saying nothing.

“Piria told me what happened.”

I sigh through my nose, and my annoyance with the god fades as I think of Cyros. A pit of regret settles in my gut instead. “It was a mistake.”

Lisari tilts her head. “What was?”

“Teaming up.” I tuck my knees up to my chest and wrap my arms around my legs. “I shouldn’t have let Cyros come. He almost died.” Why can I never learn? Every time I let someone get close, they get hurt. Every time.

Lisari snorts. “You shouldn’t have let him? It was his mission.”

I shrug, resting my chin on my knees. “But I was the one who took the job. I could have done it by myself.”

The god chuckles. “If he’d operated on that logic and done it alone, he’d be dead. But he’s not, because he did let his friends come and help him. Allies aren’t a weakness, you know.”

I glare at the shadows where Lisari is seated, but there’s no heat behind my look. I just feel tired. “Where are all your allies, then?”

My night vision spell has long since worn off, but I can at least make out the white of her smile as her face splits into a grin.

“I’d like to think you’re one,” she says.

“Yeah,” I say flatly. “Forcing me to be your champion is a great way to make allies.”

“I did ask first,” she says, amusement in her tone.

“And then you didn’t take no for an answer!”

“Unfortunate,” Lisari agrees. “I’d hoped our time spent becoming friends first would have counted for something, but Widengra rather poisoned the well where gods are concerned. Still, I couldn’t afford to pass up the opportunity.” She gestures toward Álvaro. “At the time, you were the only Traveler I had encountered.”

There’s a whole lot of things to react to in that little admission, and I’m not even sure what to address first. “We weren’t friends. You lied about who you were.”

“I didn’t lie!” Lisari objects. “I just didn’t disclose the whole truth.”

I think about the conversation I had with Piria, where she’d insisted Lisari was just as much who the god was as Shirasil. I don’t know about all that. I still feel a deep sense of betrayal at being manipulated. But there is some truth that the god I’m coming to know is a lot less different from the human I’d been friends with than I’d like to admit.

“You’ve met more Travelers since me?” I ask, deciding to pivot the conversation away from our supposed friendship.

“Indeed I have: six others at this point. It’s been an exciting few months!” She flashes a grin. “Though you’re still my favorite.”

I roll my eyes. “Why did you need a Traveler to be one of your champions, anyway?”

Lisari hums thoughtfully. “I wouldn’t say need is the right term. But I did want one. You all are so anomalous—I had to see how you would interact with the System! There’s undiscovered exploits you’ll be able to access, I’d hazard.”

Great. “So I was just an experiment for you.”

“Not at all,” Lisari objects. “Well, maybe a little. But more than that, I see you as an investment. A seed that could use a bit of water and sunshine. And I’m very eager to see what you bloom into.”

Shockingly, this explanation does not foster a lot of gratitude in me.

“I want to help you,” Lisari insists. “I want to help you grow stronger. I want to help you achieve justice. And I want you to help me achieve the same.”

“Me?” I ask skeptically. “How am I supposed to help you?”

“Oh, thank you for asking,” Lisari says. “You’re such a dear!”

I just scowl at her.

“Do you recall what I told you before?” she asks. “When I first made you my champion.”

This feels like some kind of trap, but I pause to think back—it’s not hard to remember. That day has been seared into my mind.

“You said the Heavens had grown stale,” I recall. “You wanted to usher in change.”

“Pricely so,” Lisari agrees. “You see, there is a rot growing in the pantheon—and I don’t mean that metaphorically. The way we’ve been doing things is not sustainable, and the cracks are starting to show. Lorata is in denial, of course; she believes the path we are on is the only tenable option. But I’m certain things will soon fail catastrophically if we don’t find a solution.”

“And I’m that solution?” I ask skeptically.

“You’re a possibility,” Lisari says thoughtfully. “Though more and more, yes, I believe you’re the answer. You and people like you.” She twirls a finger through the air. “You know, I don’t believe that refiner came into your hands by chance.”

My gaze flickers to the marble on my wrist. Álvaro helped me braid it into a bracelet earlier this evening when we were still waiting for Cyros and couldn’t get to sleep.

“Someone sent this to me?” I ask, immediately suspicious.

Lisari shakes her head. “No, no, that’s not what I mean. I don’t think it ended up in your hands intentionally—but you noticed it, didn’t you? When no one else did. Many magical artifacts have passed through your hands since you started working in this city. But you felt there was something different about this one.

“There’s a connection between Travelers and remnants, like magnets drawn toward one another. It’s a relic of how you ended up in this world, I believe.” She waves her hand dismissively, even as my mind is spinning with a dozen new questions. “At any rate, I believe the connection between Travelers and remnants is the key to solving our problem. But given remnants are one of the few things that actually pose a threat to the Heavens, you might imagine why Lorata would like to prevent you all from obtaining one. Which means the thing that might save us also makes you a primary target for Lorata. So that’s fun!”

We have different definitions of fun, I’m pretty sure. “But what do you want?” I press. “None of this explains how I’m helping you.”

“I want variety,” Lisari admits. “I want you all to do whatever it is you like to do with these remnants. Use them, master them, befriend them—it doesn’t make a difference to me; each individual who comes in contact with one is a different data point to analyze. This is an opportunity to experiment with remnants in ways Lorata has never permitted us to. Maybe by the end, I’ll have figured out which path is the most promising.”

So we’re back to me being nothing more than a guinea pig. Great. “I don’t think you actually know how to make friends.”

Lisari barks out a laugh. “You know? You’re absolutely right. People have always been a bit of an enigma for me—more so these days than ever. Well. Good chat.” She stands, dusting off her pants with theatrical pats.

I watch her get ready to go, a bit perplexed by this whole conversation. Somehow, knowing Lisari just sees me as an interesting experiment makes me feel better about our relationship. I mean, not great, that’s for sure. But the transparency makes me feel less like I’m being used. It’s good to know that she’s not steering me in any specific direction, but rather just curious to watch which direction I’ll pick. And knowing there are other Travelers out there she’s also watching makes me feel less like I’m in her spotlight.

I know it seems counterintuitive, but weirdly, I think I dislike her less now.

Just a little less, at any rate.

“Lisari,” I say, and she pauses. “Just… thanks for sending Piria. She saved my friend’s life.”

Lisari smiles, and this time it’s not in some insidious or sarcastic way. Just a faint, affectionate smile. “Be nice to her. She deserves better than the likes of us.”

And with that, the god melts into the shadows and is gone.

The next day, Cyros steps out of the healer’s room, smiling wearily.

“Hey!” Álvaro sits up straight from the table we’re eating at. “Dude! You’re okay!”

“I am,” he agrees, heading over to our table. He gestures behind himself as Piria trails out of the room behind him. “Thanks to Lord Piria. And you, Sal.” He sits down heavily at my side. “I owe you my life.”

“Oh, no.” I wrinkle my nose. “None of this life debt stuff. You’ve saved me plenty of times before. We’re at least even.”

He blinks at me. “I meant it as a figure of speech.”

“Oh. Well.” I awkwardly shovel a spoonful of porridge into my mouth.

He cracks a grin. “Good to see nothing’s changed.”

And what’s that supposed to mean?

Piria sits down with us as the healer fusses over Cyros and orders him to eat well for the next week to make up for the loss of calories the healing consumed.

“So you’re a champion, too, huh?” Álvaro asks Piria, talking across me. “That’s cool. My sib met one. Or saw her from a distance, at least. I think she abducted someone.”

“Oh,” Piria says, fidgeting with a utensil. “Yes, that… that has been happening lately. Which champion was it?”

Álvaro taps his chin thoughtfully. “They said it was a tan arachnoid woman. Uses a chain made of light?”

“Ah.” Piria grimaces. “That sounds like Zeyaelid. One of Lorata’s champions.”

I tune into the conversation at that. “Lorata, as in, head of the pantheon Lorata?”

“The same.” Piria pauses. “Unless you know of any other Loratas?”

Álvaro whistles. “Pretty high up the ranks, sounds like. Why is she abducting people?”

“Travelers, specifically,” I say, and Piria nods.

“Lorata has ordered all gods to apprehend any Travelers they encounter,” Piria admits. “I believe it’s for their own safety, given the Roles they’re forced to endure.”

“All Travelers?” Álvaro repeats. He glances at me, clearly amused. “That must put you in an awkward position.”

I scowl at him. “Oh, shut up.”

“Lord Shirasil has always had a more… loose interpretation of the pantheon’s orders,” Piria says. “Sal has clearly been able to manage her Role. As have you, from what I can tell. Therefore, it isn’t necessary to take either of you in.”

I’d say it’s a bit of a stretch to say I’m “managing my Role,” but I’m not about to admit that to her.

“Would Lorata agree with that assessment?” Álvaro asks, raising a teasing eyebrow.

Piria continues to nervously fidget with whatever utensil she can get her hands on. “I trust Lord Shirasil in this matter.”

I frown to myself, thinking over her explanation. Is it true that it’s the Roles that have Lorata coming after us? If she has some way to get rid of them, then I wouldn’t be opposed to that. But I wonder if it’s something else, after what Lisari told me last night. I glance at the refiner sitting quietly on my wrist. If these remnant things are a danger to the gods, and we seem to be connected to them somehow, then I’m a bit skeptical that the pantheon has our best interests at heart.

And that’s not just my anti-Widengra bias talking! Well. Not entirely.

Whatever. As long as I continue to stay out of the pantheon’s crosshairs—and it seems Shirasil is happy to let me continue to do so—it doesn’t affect me. At least, not until I’m strong enough to take them on. And I’ll be doing that on my terms.

“Sal.” Cyros finally joins the conversation once the healer has let him be. “Did you get it? The lindwyrm.”

Álvaro laughs. “Can you believe this guy?” he says to me. “So singleminded.”

“Sounds familiar,” Cyros says dryly. “So…”

“Yes, we got it,” I say. “It’s in Álvaro’s Inventory. I can store my Apothecary at my house and then use my Inventory to take the wyrm to the Guild. Should have your adderstone by the end of the day.”

Cyros slumps. “Thank you. Really.”

“So you ever gunna tell us who it’s for?” Álvaro asks, resting his head on his fist.

Cyros hesitates, glancing at me. He told me a bit of the truth when he was in the cave, preparing to die. But I’m curious about the whole story myself.

“It’s for my parent,” he finally says. “They’ve been ill for a while. I’ve tried so many things. Healers, potions, water from the Lifespring Oasis... The Guild has provided me with access to resources you can’t get by conventional means, but even that hasn’t helped. I was beginning to wonder if I’d exhausted all options.” A fire lights within his eyes. “Until I saw the adderstone on the job board.”

My stomach twists. I know first hand that a parent would do anything to save their sick child. My parents would have killed for me, if it could have saved my life. I have no doubt Cyros feels the same about his family.

“Is that why you joined the Guild in the first place?” I ask.

Cyros drops his gaze. “Yeah.”

I wonder how many people he’s killed in the pursuit of saving his family.

How many people have I killed in the pursuit of revenge? I can claim it’s because the gods are unjust—that stopping them will save more lives in the long run. But I don’t know if I even buy my own excuse.

“Well then,” Álvaro says, breaking the silence. “Where’s this family of yours?”

Cyros shifts uncomfortably. “Don’t worry about it. You all don’t have to get involved.”

I snort. “Obviously we’re getting involved."

“I think, technically, we’re already involved,” Álvaro agrees. “No use trying to get rid of me now!”

Cyros looks up sharply. “Is it because of your Role? Is it making you help me again?”

“No, dummy,” Álvaro laughs. “It’s because you guys are friends.”

Me included? I couldn’t stand the guy when we first met. And he’s still obnoxiously loud. But it is nice having someone else from Earth around—someone who gets what I’ve been through.

Yeah, alright. He’s grown on me.

“It would be good to get out of the city again,” I admit. “Widengra’s champions might think we’ve moved to a different city if our trail goes cold for long enough.”

Cyros looks between both of us with an unreadable expression. Then he passes a hand over his face, and I think he covertly swiped a tear from his eye. When he looks back up, he’s smiling. “Thanks. I’d love to have you guys come with me.”

“Great,” I say. “And when we get back, if those champions are still around, you two can help me take them down.” I grin wickedly. “Think killing champions are enough to move me up the job board?”

Piria makes a distressed sound. “I know those champions are trouble, but killing them will only draw Widengra’s attention.”

“Hm.” That’s a fair point, actually. And I’m not strong enough to face Widengra yet. “Then what am I supposed to do? Let them run around trying to kill me?”

“Ideally, no one has to kill anyone,” Piria says.

How is this woman Shirasil’s champion?

She smiles slyly. “Setting a trap would be far more effective.”

Oh. Nevermind, she’s totally Shirasil’s champion.

“You have an idea?” I ask.

Piria tips her head, looking innocent once more. “Perhaps. We can discuss options once you’ve returned from your errand.”

“You’re not coming?” Álvaro asks.

“I feel I would be intruding,” Piria admits. “I’d rather not impose on Cyros or Sal.”

“Me?” I ask. “Intruding on what?”

Cyros at least seems to understand some sort of implication. He smiles through a grimace. “We’ll be headed back to Fairwood for this trip.”

The name of the city immediately summons painfully strong memories. Of the Gods Tournament—of Talia. Of death.

Of Iski, Gugora, and the Starlight Inn.

My heart throbs. “Oh,” is all I can think to say.
