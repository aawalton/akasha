"We are gathered here today to bid our farewells. Luis was a man who possessed a remarkable talent to touch the hearts of everyone he met, no matter who they were. While the world may remember him as a hero who helped prevent a great catastrophe, we here will remember the man himself. His kindness, his generosity, and the blessing of his company. The life he lived was…"

Jade stepped away from the congregation, retreating beneath the cover of a couple of small trees that stood in the corner of the memorial grounds. Grey, overcast skies shrouded the midday sun, delivering a gentle rain that pattered against the grass like rattling beads. She wanted to pay her respects, of course, and she would. Later, when the pain in her chest felt less like a twisting knife.

"Sorry, amigo." Jade murmured, resting her forehead against the trunk of one of the trees. "Hope I'm not letting you down."

"Jade?"

She straightened at the sound of her name and looked back over her shoulder. Naomi was standing behind her, her head cocked curiously. If she'd been paying attention, Jade would have sensed her approaching. Strong emotions were always easier to notice, and today’s were especially potent.

"How'd you know?" Jade asked, putting her back against the tree and pulling the rim of her ballcap lower. She hadn't told anyone she was coming, and had even gone so far as changing her hair color and height for the ceremony.

"We spent almost every waking moment for half a year together," Naomi said with a wry smile, "I know how you carry yourself."

Jade's demonic nature had come out during their time away, and they'd returned to find mass speculation that she was yet another of the demons who had been in hiding amongst the people of Earth. L-Ref leadership knew the truth, but after discussing the situation with the new (certifiably human) deputy director, Jade had agreed to remain incognito. The last thing anyone wanted after such a public series of global catastrophes was the emergence of another potential threat, whether it was real or not. The public had a poor track record of accepting reasonable explanations for exceptional events, and any return on her part would no doubt birth a dozen dangerous conspiracy theories.

Jade didn't mind all that much. Everyone who was important to her knew the truth, and the situation merely solidified the idea that this wasn't the right place for her anymore. Besides, given her powers, adopting a disguise was trivial.

"I just… needed to step away." Jade shrugged, looking back over the small crowd. "I thought I was prepared after Siora's ceremony in Lysara, but…"

Her words trailed off as her gaze fell upon a certain group. Luis's family was standing together, maintaining their composure with obvious difficulty. They were accompanied by Alan, who was holding the hand of one of his sisters. The two stood close together, and Jade could feel a bourgeoning intimacy growing between them. A few other familiar faces dotted the gathering: A few wayfarers they'd aided in the Labyrinth, the newly recovered director of L-Ref, Irene, a soldier they'd worked with on their first return to Earth, and a couple of others.

"I'm glad your brother is up and about again." Naomi said, steering the subject towards safer territory. "The healer the goddess sent made it, then?"

"Yes..." Jade sighed. She felt conflicted about how she'd ensured Elaria would remain asleep for a while longer. It didn't feel fair to the goddess, after all that she'd done to help them. But neither did the alternative, considering all the suffering and death that had resulted from their actions. It was only later that she realized she hadn't completely been herself in that moment. Her own spirit had been saturated by that of Ira's. Even in death, the celestial possessed a powerful will. Only once the vestiges of her spirit had fully faded from Jade's own did she come to realize that the angel's burning desires had been bleeding through to her, mingling with her own.

In the end, the result was the same. The amount of essence that she, a mortal, had been able to drain from the deity was miniscule. Elaria would awaken again, one day. Jade just hoped that, when she did, the goddess would act with greater care.

At least the celestials are free, Jade thought, whatever else Ira was, she was right about that. They didn't deserve to be trapped.

Jade shook her head to clear her mind of troubled thoughts. Naomi was looking at her curiously, waiting for her to continue.

"It was a selfish thing for me to ask for," Jade said, picking up where she'd left off "but… I don't regret it."

"You shouldn't." Naomi punched her lightly in the shoulder. "You spend too much time worrying about the past. You're allowed to prioritize yourself, Jade."

"He didn't."

It came out as a whisper, nearly drowned out by the gentle patter of the rain.

"You say that like you didn't do the same thing when we were fighting Seraphine." Naomi crossed her arms. "I'm sad he's gone, Jade. Devastated. But we all knew what we were getting into. We all chose it. And, given how things turned out, I guarantee you that he'd make that same choice every single time."

"You're right. He would."

"Exactly. So, grieve, Jade. Let it all out. But don't twist his sacrifice into another burden for you to carry." Naomi stepped forward and pulled Jade into a tight embrace. She continued more softly, choking back tears. "He wouldn't want that."

"I… know."

They held each other for a long time. Naomi's grief filled the air around Jade like a thick fog, heavy and cloying. It worked its way into her mind, building up against all the walls she'd erected around her deepest feelings and knocking them down. Before she knew it, Jade was crying too. The two women supported each other, sharing their loss with the only other person who could truly understand.

Eventually, the tears stopped. Jade took a deep breath, filling her lungs with the crisp late summer air. A gentle breeze rustled the branches above them, carrying the scent of petrichor. It reminded Jade of childhood, and the carefree days of youth.

"He really is doing better." Jade said, nodding towards her brother. "I heard the healer caused something of a stir when he showed up unannounced. But it all worked out."

"Will he make a full recovery?"

Jade wobbled her hand in an uncertain gesture.

"According to Alan, he probably won't ever be fully healed. The disease is gone, but some of the damage it did to his body was too old to fix. Even magic has its limits, I guess."

"It's not perfect, but that's still a lot better than it was. How is he?"

"Happy." Jade smiled. "More than a little suspicious about what I did to make that happen, but he's happier."

"What did you tell him?"

"That I'll explain it to him later, once things have calmed down."

"Not sure how to explain the goddess?"

"Hopefully, I won't have to."

While both Jade and Naomi had provided L-Ref with detailed accounts of their mission to stop the demon invasion, they'd deliberately avoided namedropping the goddess. That was a whole can of worms Jade didn't want opened, and Naomi had gone along with her suggestion. Their story had been met with a large dose of skepticism at first, but once delegations began arriving from other city states in the Labyrinth, that quickly melted away. Apparently, Seris, Tara, and Fenrin were sharing their own versions of the adventure, and whatever they were saying was painting Earth in a good light.

The exact details of their mission aside, L-Ref was grateful to the two of them for helping start foreign relations on a positive note. That appreciation came with material benefits, and Jade could now rest easy knowing that her family, and those of her friends, would be taken care of as the global community adapted to a new normal. That was a welcome relief, as most countries were still dealing with political and economic turmoil resulting from the Labyrinth's activities.

Their conversation lapsed, and the two of them turned their attention back to the ceremony. Naomi took Jade's hand and gave it a gentle squeeze. She squeezed back. The speeches were over, and now the group was lining up to place flowers at the base of the memorial. Luis's parents stood on either side of the monument, welcoming each attendee and thanking them for paying their respects to their lost son. Jade was struck by the depth of their kindness. Even while they must be the ones who hurt the most, they busied themselves consoling and thanking their son's final visitors. Perhaps that was their way of coping. Either way, Luis truly had been their son.

A short while later the crowd began to disperse. Some two hundred people had gathered to pay their respects in-person. Most were friends and family members, but there were a handful of government and military personnel in attendance as well. There would be a public ceremony the following week, but Jade wasn't planning on sticking around for it. She had mixed feelings about the whole thing. Part of her was glad that her friend's sacrifice would be remembered, while another side of her disliked the idea of his death being treated like a spectacle. Speeches from multiple politicians were already on the agenda, and she had no doubt that most of them would try to use the situation to forward their own goals.

"Are you still planning on leaving today?"

Naomi's question brought Jade back to the present. That's right, she'd almost forgotten.

"Yeah, I am."

"Is your brother coming with you?"

"No. Not yet." Alan hadn't left with the crowd, instead remaining behind with Maria. "He says he wants to finish school, first. But I don't think that's the only thing on his mind."

Jade finished with a sad smile, watching the pair. Naomi squeezed her hand again.

"Maybe you'll get both of them, when they're ready."

"If that's what they want." Now it was Jade's turn to give her friend's hand a squeeze. "You're welcome to come, too, Naomi."

"Thanks, but I think I'll stay on Earth for a while." Naomi paused and shook her head. "That would've been a ridiculous thing to say just last year. But, anyway, I want to stay with my family for now. Things back home aren't as stable as they are here. They could use the help. Sorry, Jade."

"Don't apologize," Jade said, pulling her friend into another hug. "I'd do the same, but things are a little complicated for me here."

"Don't get me wrong, if I can convince them to move to Lysara, I will. We may end up there with you before long."

"Even if you don't, I hope you'll visit."

"Oh, don't worry about that," Naomi laughed. "I'll see you there, one way or another. But still, I never would have expected the director to ask you to be our ambassador to the elves. Or for you to accept."

"It's nothing official," Jade shrugged. "I just told her that's where I'd be, and she asked if I would help. The elves are still grateful for our help with the Stronghold, so it makes sense."

That, and Jade wasn't about to let anything else happen to Lysara. She owed that much to Siora, at least.

"Elyx is still there, right? Helping improve their monster defenses?"

"That's what she told me."

The portal from the goddess's realm had taken Jade to a Sanctuary near Lysara. She'd found her companions there, delivering news both of their mission's success and of Siora's tragic fate. Elyx, the demon ritualist who had helped them defeat Zertrolk, was still with the group when Jade finally rejoined them, if only because she didn't have anywhere else to go. The kind people of the elven village took her in, offering the demon somewhere to stay while she tried to find a place for herself in this strange new world. Jade wasn't sure if their acceptance of Elyx was partly due to her own nature as a demon, or if they simply wanted to repay someone who had lent aid to one of their own. Either way, Jade planned on keeping a close eye on her. Nothing she'd sensed in Elyx's emotions led her to believe that the demon had ulterior motives, but one couldn't be too careful.

"Well," Jade said, after another short pause, "this is it, then."

"Don't be so dramatic," Naomi laughed, "we'll see each other soon."

"I know. It's just… things are different, now. It's going to take some getting used to."

"We'll get there. But before you go, I wanted to thank you."

Jade raised her eyebrows.

"Thank me?"

"That's right," Naomi nodded. "You and Luis are the only reason I made it out of the Labyrinth. Made it past my first week, really. So, thank you, Jade. Really."

Jade shifted awkwardly. She'd never been good with this kind of straightforward sincerity, which was even more true now that she could feel it directly. Besides…

"I didn't do much, you just needed a little push to get started. None of us would have made it far without your support, either. Or without his."

"Maybe," Naomi shrugged, turning to face the memorial with Jade, "but the two of you got me started, and I wanted you to know how I feel."

A shaft of sunlight broke through the overcast sky, bathing the memorial grounds in bright, sunny warmth. The rain had finally stopped, leaving a blanket of glimmering droplets carpeting the grassy grounds like sparkling diamonds. Naomi clasped her hands behind her back and smiled, unshed tears glistening in her eyes.

"From now on, we have to keep doing our best."

Jade nodded in agreement, returning her sad smile. Naomi didn't have to explain what she meant. Luis had passed them the torch, and it was up to them to keep it burning.

"We will."
