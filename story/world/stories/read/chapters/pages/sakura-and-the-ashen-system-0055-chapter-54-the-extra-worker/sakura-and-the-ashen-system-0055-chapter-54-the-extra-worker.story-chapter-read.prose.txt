A loader named Tarven did not like morning deliveries from the Castle. Prison laundry always smelled as if it had been dragged through basements, then soaked in blood, then dragged through basements again, and only after that had someone decided to send it to the workshop.

The wagon with the creaking wheels rolled into the yard and stopped by the unloading platform. One of the drivers immediately jumped down and slapped his palm against the rear door.

"Watch it with this thing," he muttered. "It came off on the road."

Tarven raised his eyebrows.

"The door?"

"No, the whole wagon. Of course the door."

The second driver jumped down after him and adjusted his belt, but said nothing.

Tarven slid the bolt aside and swung the door open. Inside, as always when they received this kind of delivery, there were a lot of sacks. They were coarse, damp, and tied with ropes. The usual morning mix: bandages, robes, sheets, tarps, protective canvases, and other trash that first had to be carried from place to place, then sorted, then boiled, dried, carried again, and loaded back up.

"Well," his partner said, looking inside. "Fun."

"Take the closest one."

Together, they grabbed the first sack. It was heavy, but not too heavy. The second was lighter, probably stuffed with bandages and dry sheets. They dealt with that one fastest. The third pulled their arms down as if there were a wet horse inside.

Tarven cursed.

"They’re loading wet stuff like it’s stone again."

His partner grunted, shifting his grip on the rope.

"Why don’t they wring it out at all?"

"Because they have us for that."

They dragged the sack off the wagon and carried it to the common pile by the entrance to the laundry hall. The sack was awkward and disgustingly warm in one spot, but that surprised no one. Wet bundles were often strange anyway, since the cloth inside clumped together, robes twisted into lumps, and blankets held water worse than a bucket.

Tarven only clenched his teeth harder.

"Throw it over there."

"On the ones going into the vat?"

"Where else? Not the drying room."

They swung the sack and dropped it onto the other bundles. It hit the floor with a dull thud, sagged a little, and went still.

"Next," Tarven said.

And he and his partner did not look toward that sack again.

Sakura did not move. Even when she was thrown onto the floor hard enough that white spots flashed before her eyes, she only clenched her teeth and forced herself not to make a sound. Dirty cloth pressed down on her whole body and crawled into her face. Inside the sack, it was hot, damp, and disgusting to the point of nausea. Somewhere right by her nose lay a wet sleeve that smelled of old blood and something sour. Sakura remembered how she and Gilbert had jumped into filthy sewer water, but even that memory seemed better than what was happening to her now.

But the important thing was that she had not been discovered. Her plan to climb into a sack of dirty laundry had been risky, but it had worked. They had not paid attention to the weight. While sorting through the sacks, Sakura had found several that were too heavy, and that was how she had realized it could work.

Still, it felt like a miracle. A stupid, stinking, painful miracle, but Sakura was not going to complain about the form of her rescue right now. Right before the wagon entered the workshop, realizing there was simply no other way out, she had managed to climb into the largest sack, pull wet robes over herself, and almost tighten the rope from the inside. That had been enough for two tired loaders to suspect nothing.

Sometimes the world was saved not by cleverness, but by the fact that people did not want to dig through disgusting things without a reason.

For a while longer, footsteps rumbled outside. Men cursed, dragged sacks around, slammed the wagon door, and then the voices began to move away.

Sakura slowly exhaled, knowing it was still too early. She counted to thirty first, then reached fifty and decided she should probably get to one hundred, but then realized that if she kept counting, she would simply suffocate from the smell. Besides, she definitely needed to get out of the sack before the workshop staff came for it to send it into some vat the loaders had mentioned.

Sakura carefully moved her hand. The rope around the sack had been tied badly, just as she had left it, so she managed to slip her fingers outside and loosen the knot. She climbed out of the bundle and immediately crouched behind the pile of sacks.

The workshop yard turned out larger than she had expected. On one side stood an open unloading shelter, and on the other stretched a long stone building with wide doors. It was noisy around her, which meant there were a lot of people. Steam rose above the roof.

According to the original plan, Sakura was supposed to sneak into this workshop at night, when there would be the fewest people. She was supposed to find a weak point, slip inside, and steal whatever would be needed for the mists Gilbert had warned her about. According to the original plan, she was definitely not supposed to arrive here in a sack of prison laundry.

But the plan had already died several times that morning.

Sakura ran her palm over the sleeve where the scrap of paper with the code was hidden. Gilbert had done his part, which meant now she had to do hers.

First, she needed to stop looking like a girl who had crawled out of a dirty sack.

She waited until two workers passed by, then slipped between the bundles to a side door. It was slightly open. Behind it was a narrow utility room with buckets, rough brushes, and work clothes hanging from hooks.

Sakura almost could not believe her luck. A simple gray dress hung on one hook over a rough shirt, along with a thick apron, a headscarf, and a mask for the nose and mouth. Beside them lay leather gloves darkened by water and solutions.

She changed quickly. The clothes were too large for her, scratched her skin with their roughness, and did not smell especially pleasant. On the other hand, right now everything smelled clearly better than the sack of dirty laundry she had been forced to sit in. Best of all, the mask hid part of Sakura’s face and made her less noticeable. There were many people like that in the workshop: women, men, teenagers, all in gray work clothes, with their hair covered, in masks or scarves.

But Sakura was also aware of the other risks. If someone decided to check her through the System, everything would end at once. No mask would hide her name, which the whole of Veyr likely knew by now.

However, there was simply no other choice. She had to risk it.

She went out into the corridor with her head lowered, holding an empty basket she had taken by the door against her chest. People rarely bothered someone who looked busy with unpleasant work.

The workshop looked like a huge craft anthill. It consisted of stone halls, wooden drying frames, long tables for repairing cloth, crude lifts, and devices Sakura could not even have named if she tried. The whole place boiled with work and stank of chemicals, and every worker knew exactly what they needed to do right now.

Sakura followed their example and walked confidently, as if she knew where she was going, though in truth she only knew approximately. Or did not know at all. She and Gilbert had managed to get a plan of the workshop, but it was old and simplified. And right now, Sakura did not even understand which part of it she was in.

The smell led her to the laundry hall. Large, crude, soot-darkened vats with thick walls and iron hoops stood there. Under some of them, coals still smoldered, and steam rose over the surface, stinging her eyes even through the mask. Wet fabrics hung on long poles. Nearby lay dark tarps and cloth that had already gone through the solution.

Sakura immediately remembered the laboratory where the slime had been kept in the sewer. There, the slime collectors had walked around with covered faces too, just like in this workshop, because of the poisonous fumes.

There was almost no one in the hall with the vats, and that made her wary. Then Sakura came closer to the nearest vat and understood why. Even through the mask, the acrid smell hit so hard that her vision darkened for a second, her nose and eyes stung, and her throat tightened.

She stepped back and clenched her fingers around the edge of the basket. The vats had probably just been opened, and normal workers, people who actually worked here and were not stealing under cover of work, were waiting for the worst of the vapor to leave. Sakura did not know the local rules, but it explained the emptiness. No one wanted to stand near this filth unless necessary.

She was about to leave too when a voice sounded behind her.

"Hey. What are you standing around for?"

Sakura slowly turned. A man of about thirty had entered the hall, broad-shouldered, in a work apron. His mask hung around his neck, but he quickly pulled it up over his red face. He looked at the basket in her hands, then at her.

Sakura lowered her head.

"They sent me for cloth."

"Who sent you?"

That was a bad question. But she could not hesitate before answering.

"The supervisor."

"Which supervisor?"

Now everything became even worse. Sakura coughed into the mask.

"From the laundry."

The man narrowed his eyes.

"We have three supervisors in the laundry."

Sakura said nothing. He took a step closer, and his gaze became not just irritated, but suspicious. He looked at her as if trying to assemble a puzzle in his head. Then his eyes widened slightly. Sakura saw that moment. It was not just bad. It was very bad. Maybe he had checked her through the System and seen her name. Then matched it to the facts.

But the man did not shout. Instead, he reached out and grabbed her by the wrist.

"Come on," he said in a different voice. "We need to take you somewhere right now."

Fear came at once. Hot, sharp, almost blinding. But there was no time to panic. If the man led her out now or shouted, if she was exposed, Gilbert was finished. No one would get him out of the prison. Sakura herself would be finished too. They would recognize the Obsidian Hierophant in her. And what would happen next was not something she wanted to think about.

So with her free hand, she grabbed a damp cloth from the edge of the nearest table. Pain burned her fingers almost at once, even through the glove. The cloth had been soaked in the solution from the vat.

The man had only time to jerk before Sakura pressed the cloth to his face. He wheezed and tried to push her away, but inhaled too sharply. The fumes struck his nose and mouth. His eyes instantly reddened, his body arched, and his grip on her wrist weakened. He took one uneven step back, then another, struck his hip against the edge of the vat, and began to fall.

Sakura barely managed to drag him away from the hot edge. He hit the floor heavily. Sakura checked his pulse almost immediately. He was alive, and that was good. But he could wake at any moment, or someone could start looking for him, and that was already bad. It meant there was less and less time.

With difficulty, Sakura dragged him behind a stack of tarps and covered him with one of the rough canvases.

Her palm burned. She looked at her fingers. The skin under the glove already ached and stung. Great. Now she had a burn too.

Sakura still took another small piece of damp cloth, but this time she carefully wrapped it in thick leather she found on the table, then in a piece of dry tarp, and only after that sent it into [Inventory].

A dangerous thing, but useful if she needed to knock someone out very quickly.

Sakura left the laundry hall faster than she had entered it. Now she could not keep pretending to be calm for long. Sakura passed through the corridor, turned behind a row of drying frames, then turned again, toward where there were fewer people. Here, it no longer smelled of vats.

She found herself by the storeroom. The door was open. Inside stood shelves reaching to the ceiling, and on them lay rolls of protective fabric, dark tarps, folded suits, filters, belts, and bundles with colored tags. Sakura saw green tags, blue ones, red, yellow. For a second, her eyes darted everywhere, because this was exactly what she had been looking for. Sakura had found treasure.

At the far end of the storeroom, two workers were arguing by a table, paying no attention to the aisles between the shelves. There was enough space between the rows to hide. And Sakura also saw a door in the back wall, very much like a back exit.

Sakura slowly exhaled. The night plan was dead. But the day plan, it seemed, was in full swing.
