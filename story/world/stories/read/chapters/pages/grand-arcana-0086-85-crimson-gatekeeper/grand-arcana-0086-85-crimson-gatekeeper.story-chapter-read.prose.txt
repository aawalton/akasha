Restrict! I struck the side of the statue’s arm.

It was a lot smaller than the ones I had come across in the underground prison, though just as durable. None of my attacks managed to cause a dent. The moment I got enough gold, I was going to awaken my divine blades a third time. With luck, they might acquire the ability to slice through stone.

Behind me, Sparta was dealing with a stone archer. There was a good chance that he had been among those responsible for the death of the snapdragon. In many ways, it was similar to the entity I was fighting, only instead of a sword, it was equipped with a stone bow.

Restrict! I combined a butterfly strike with a multi-attack.

My opponent blocked the attack with its left arm, using it as a shield, then kicked me in the chest.

His foot felt like a wrecking ball, throwing me several feet back. Neither my current strength nor my strengthened skeleton would have helped me survive. Thankfully, I had also managed to cast a spell circle, creating a shield of rock that cushioned the blow.

Just smash his head, Serp said, as if it were the easiest thing in the world.

Nonetheless, his comment gave me an idea. A new spell circle appeared on the floor, instantly thrusting the statue up.

A loud bang echoed throughout the corridor as the marble head slammed into the ceiling. Anywhere else and the statue would easily have created a hole. This was an aelf castle, however. A participant in my previous run had shared that the aelves incorporated spells in every inch of their structures, making them largely immune to damage and virtually indestructible. Although I didn’t see it with my magic sight, they must have also included some kind of divine safeguards, for the statue’s head shattered on impact.

That makes four, I thought as I turned to assist Sparta.

Lacking an arm, her opponent kept on swinging wildly with the other. It was impressive to watch the woman defend herself using the divine threads of her weapon. The sight was undeniably as impressive as I remembered it, yet compared to the siren, it was like watching someone trying to cut through leather with a spoon. It was definitely possible, yet the amount of brute force it required made it seem outright crude.

I cast another spell circle on the floor.

Instantly, the statue leaped off the circle’s edge. The entity was learning. More specifically, the person controlling it was.

Two thread-daggers ripped the air, flying round both sides of the statue’s neck. Both twisted around, letting out an almost whistling sound. Then Sparta pulled.

The woman’s body leaned back as she put her entire weight into the action. The thread dug into the marble, moving further and further until the head spontaneously fell off.

Just watching it happen caused my neck to itch in sympathy.

“You all right?” I asked, using the Listener card to check for other enemies in the vicinity.

The only meaningful sounds were of Cathy and Marcus. They seemed to be relatively close. From what I could make out, Cathy had become significantly more destructive, using arrows to shatter statues as if they were made of porcelain.

“They’re a bit more durable than I thought,” Sparta replied in her usual sassy style. “Nice use of magic.”

If only that were true. “They’re learning. I might not be able to use that trick again.”

Closing my eyes, I tried to think back to when I had been in the castle. Most of the time the trial had ended at the foot of the mountain. A few times we had come close to the gate, though no one was crazy enough to actually breach it.

“The main tower,” I said, following the direction the blades were directing me at. “He should be there.”

I looked at the door further down the corridor. Chances were that there would be several more statues there as well. So, it was time to think outside the box.

Bracing myself, I cast a mega spell circle on the floor, only this time, instead of the earth spires going up, I made them go down. Fine dust filled the space as the spell struck the floor. Whatever enchantments the aelves were using, they were a lot stronger than I expected. Yet, there was one thing that they hadn’t taken into account.

Holding my breath, I combined the spell with a multi-attack. One after another, spires were thrust into the floor with merciless determination. The first five times there seemed to be no change. Then, I heard it—an almost undetectable crack indicating that the floor was reaching its limit. The sound grew more distinctive with each spell until finally the entire thing collapsed.

Far greater than I expected, the destruction spread along a wider area than my original spell circle, causing massive cracks to spread along the rest of the corridor.

Perfect, I thought, letting myself fall to the floor below.

From what I could tell, thanks to my Observer card, there were four new statues expecting me.

The moment I came into contact with the floor, four new spell circles formed beneath each, instantly propelling them forward until their heads were smashed into a solid surface.

Nine, I thought.

“Show off.” Sparta said, behind me. “Next time, give me a heads-up.”

“And ruin the surprise?” I cast a wind circle to clear the air.

The corridor continued to a staircase leading down. There didn’t seem to be any statues in sight, but I kept my guard up.

In a slow, steady pace, we continued forward. As we did, the sound of destruction came from further ahead. Immediately, I recognized the rhythm. There was only one person who fought in that fashion, though I was curious how she had managed to get ahead of me.

“Ele’s ahead,” I said.

“I know.” Sparta nodded. “Only she could be so chaotic.”

“Marcus and Cathy are also nearby.”

Based on the little I knew about aelf architecture, there was supposed to be a large open area surrounding the main tower. Being creatures of pomp and ceremony, they put a lot of effort in appearance and performance. The high nobles needed to be seen leaving, emerging from the main chamber of the castle under the glances of their guards and inferiors. A few moments later, I proved correct.

The staircase overlooked a wide-open area surrounding the tower. Before I could take a good look, an arrow struck me in the shoulder.

Quickly, I cast a spell circle, creating a wall of ice in front of me. Sadly, it was already too late.

Another arrow pierced through my makeshift barrier. This time, I managed to pull to the side just in time to avoid a second hit.

Too close, I thought. I had already used my one wound for the trial. If I hadn’t evaded the second, it would have been all over.

“Down,” I told Sparta, following my own advice.

As I glanced at where she had been, the woman was already gone.

I really need a Thief. I pushed the arrow through my shoulder. A jolt of pain ran down my arm. Likely I had nicked a nerve. The good news was that I was still able to use it.

That was piss, old man! Serp said, sharp disapproval in his voice. You could have avoided it.

Gritting my teeth, I broke the shaft of the arrow. The pain increased, making me briefly see flashes of light. Once it was over, I pulled both parts of the arrow out, then used the Two of Chalice to perform a quick heal.

“Careful!” I shouted. “There’s one at the base of the staircase.”

The arrow wasn’t made of stone, so there was a chance for it to be an actual aelf.

Cautiously, I waved my sword above the stone railing. An arrow struck the blade head-on, jerking it out of my hand.

Nasty shit, aren’t you?

My attacker had no intention of letting me see who he was.

“Sleep, sleep, is a word,” I sang.

Aether threads came out of my mouth, flowing in several directions. As I continued, they went over the railing, heading for the area below. I knew that as long as I kept this up, I’d affect my target.

Just in case, I reached out, attempting to cast another earth barrier. No spell circle formed.

I’ve been restricted? I tried the spell again, but the result was no different.

“He’s got a Gatekeeper card!” I shouted.

That was a bit on the nose. I was tempted to say that only Harlequin would place someone with a Gatekeeper card to guard a door. In this case, I didn’t believe he was the reason. Raising the other sword above the railing, I waved it around. When no attack followed, I dared a peek.

As I suspected, an aelf was standing several floors below, right in front of a large pair of double doors.

Crimson Gatekeeper

Level: 20

Cards: Gatekeeper, Archer, Thief

Specials: Eagle Eye, Natural Resistance, Absolute Loyalty, Athletic

Weapon: Bonebow, Aelf Rapier

Shit! It was one of those!

Crimson armor covered a delicate white shirt of spider silk. I could instantly tell by the spacing in the chest area that its owner was female. Strictly speaking, there was no need for such modifications, but aelves were above all else show-offs. Leather trousers ended in something that looked suspiciously like my Boots of Swiftness, only a lot flashier.

Standing a full head taller than any elf I had seen before, the creature looked in my direction. Silver hair flowed down from the bright red half-helmet that covered the upper part of her head.

You sneaky bitch, I thought.

I knew that my singing hadn’t affected her in the slightest. There was no way it could. Unless I was very much mistaken, the item she was wearing was a Helm of Protection. Conveniently covering her eyes and ears, it ensured that no songs, orders, or illusion would affect her in combat. My singing was useless.

The elf was provoking me into using the Emperor card. No doubt she was aware that I wasn’t alone. If I tried to crush her will, I would end up being just that. The power of the card would affect everyone within earshot, while only the aelf would end up being protected thanks to her gear.

“Obey!” I shouted, yet instead of using the Emperor card, I summoned and threw Serp right at her.

The aelf didn’t flinch.

In less than a second, she raised her bow, shooting an arrow that deflected the saber midair. A second shot was aimed at me, missing by a whisker thanks to my quick reaction.

“She can restrict from a distance,” I shouted, unsummoning and summoning Serp.

I don’t think I can fly at her again, Serp said.

She had got him as well? That was unexpected, though it made sense. He was a living weapon, after all.

“Cathy, Ele, she’s all yours!” I yelled. “Drill her to—”

Before the final words could leave my mouth, I heard the aelf act. The faint sound of footsteps dashed in my direction. I felt them move along the floor, then up the ceiling as she used the ability of her boots to climb up the wall as if it were a ladder.

Without a shadow of a doubt, she had set her sights specifically on me.

Fourteen of Swords!

I slashed the air in the direction I estimated she’d appear. My prediction proved correct, my attack—not as much.

My saber met hers as both weapons clashed together, releasing red sparks.

“Restrict,” she said.

“Restrict!” I shouted in near unison.

A Gatekeeper game had started. Normally, this was something that occurred between participants at the arena. It was always amusing to watch two people try to outstrategize each other. Since both restricted the other’s movements, the fight transformed into a very long battle of chess in which every move could be used only once. Being aggressive no longer provided an advantage, for this way only more options would be negated, leaving one side with significantly fewer methods of engagement.

Twelve of Rings. Eleven of Chalice.

Green flames surrounded my blades.

Restrict! I thought, performing a multitude of power thrusts.

Each of my attacks was deflected, though that didn’t grant her the advantage she thought it had. While it was true that all my future thrust strikes were severely limited, the same could be said about her ability to defend. The Gatekeeper skills affected a wide range of actions. All I needed to do was exhaust her defensive capabilities before finishing her off with a single strike.

“Chains,” the aelf said.

Silver chains shot out of her blade, aimed right at me.

Instinctively, I struck the nearest chain with Serp, but that only caused them to entangle it completely.

I tried to pull it out, but neither my arm nor the blade obeyed.

Unsummon. Summon.

The saber vanished and reappeared once again. The difference was that for the moment, I had regained the ability to move my hand.

She’s tough, Serp noted. Think you can take her down, old man?

“Bottom line, I have to,” I replied, then launched another attack.
