The townsfolk emerged from their hiding places, cheering and celebrating Sigurd’s “victory.” A makeshift festival sprang up in the town square, complete with music, hastily prepared food, and a jovial atmosphere. Children ran around waving wooden swords, mimicking Sigurd’s battle stance, while merchants quickly set up stalls selling anything from roasted meat skewers to dragon-themed trinkets.

The mini-dragon waddled behind Sigurd, its immense weight causing it to leave small craters in the ground with every step. It sniffed curiously at a food stall, accidentally knocking over crates of apples. The vendor began to shout before noticing Sigurd, who offered an apologetic shrug. Meanwhile, Sigurd’s catkin daughters, Nia and Mia, squealed with delight, practically tumbling over each other in their excitement.

“Papa! Papa! Can we keep it?!” Mia begged, her cat ears twitching with excitement. She was already climbing onto the dragon’s broad back, her small hands gripping its stubby spine with confidence.

“Of course, we can keep it!” Nia chimed in, scrambling up beside her older sister. The girls bright sparkled as they settled near the dragon’s neck, giggling uncontrollably. “It’s so funny! Look at how it waddles!”

“Hold on, little ones,” Sigurd said with a mix of exasperation and affection, his hands steadying them as the dragon began to move in awkward circles. Nia, always the bold one, threw up her arms as if commanding the beast. “Faster, faster!” she called out.

Mia, meanwhile, wrapped her arms around the dragon’s neck, her small body shaking with laughter as the creature let out a squeaky roar. “It’s like a giant stuffed toy!” she declared, her tail swishing wildly behind her. The dragon, for its part, seemed to tolerate the attention, occasionally sniffing at the giggling girls as they squealed with delight.

Sigurd couldn’t help but smile faintly despite himself. “Alright, alright, don’t get too carried away. It’s not a toy—it’s still a dragon,” he said, though his tone lacked its usual sternness as he watched his daughters’ joy.

“Oh, great dragon tamer,” Ada teased, leaning on her staff as she watched the scene unfold. “Now you’ve got a walking paperweight that can destroy your furniture. Maybe you should train it to fetch ore for me. Two birds, one stone.”

Sigurd shot her a withering look. “You’re not helping.”

Ada smirked, unfazed. “Hey, I kept you alive, didn’t I? And you still owe me for dragging me into this mess.” She leaned closer, grinning slyly. “Or was this your way of recruiting me as your new court magician?”

“Mess?” Sigurd repeated, raising an eyebrow. “If I recall, you insisted on coming.”

“Details,” Ada said breezily, waving a hand. She then adopted a mock-serious tone. “But seriously, let’s talk about my business plan. I’m thinking of a warranty system. Revolutionary, right? Buy my enchanted tools, and if they explode, I’ll replace them for free. What do you think?”

Sigurd sighed, shaking his head as he adjusted the straps of his sword. “Let's hope you don't bankrupt yourself before you even start.”

As the group walked toward the town square, the mini-dragon followed, occasionally sniffing at townsfolk and eliciting both awe and alarm. Its waddling steps caused barrels to topple and tables to shake, but it seemed completely oblivious to the minor chaos it created. Nia and Mia jumped off the dragon and ran ahead, chasing each other in circles, their laughter echoing through the square.

A group of townsfolk approached Sigurd, offering mugs of ale and hearty pats on the back. “A true hero!” one declared. “Grimroost will never forget this day!”

Sigurd forced a smile, muttering under his breath, “At least until the next disaster.”

Ada, catching his muttering, nudged him with her staff. “Oh, cheer up, big guy."
