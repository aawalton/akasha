There are certain moments where a person realizes, very clearly and very suddenly, that they probably should have listened to the part of themselves that was telling them to leave before things got to this point.

This was without any question one of those moments.

I put what I hoped looked like a respectful smile on my face and gave a courteous bow to the ancient spirit that was floating in the air in front of all of us, managing to say "Honored ancestor" out loud while every bit of recovered red sun energy I had was quietly being redirected into preparation for the fastest Blink Step I had ever attempted in my life.

We had done the scouting, we had confirmed that something strange was happening in these mountains, and as far as I was concerned, it was time to retreat and let the sect send someone who was actually qualified to deal with this, preferably an entire group of Life Realm cultivators or ideally someone from the Civilization Realm themselves.

I had no interest in attending this particular family reunion.

But before I could actually activate the rune, something unexpected happened.

The being’s qi signature began fluctuating and it didn’t make any sense, one moment it was radiating pressure I would expect from a Civilization Realm cultivator and the next, it was dropping down to barely Qi Condensation levels. His form was wavering at the edges reminding me of a reflection in water that someone had disturbed, the outline of his spectral body going indistinct before pulling itself back together again.

Then the floating figure dropped toward the ground without warning.

For one brief, hopeful moment I thought that was it, that he was simply going to fall apart.

That wasn’t what happened. He caught himself at the very last instant and landed, somehow managing to look intentional despite the obvious loss of control that had just occurred.

"Tch," he said, clicking his tongue with annoyance and looking down at his own semi-transparent hands with a critical expression. "Even with the Divine Spirit Reclamation technique, true resurrection is... problematic."

His qi signature settled and stabilized at what felt like peak Qi Condensation, which was still dangerous but was a very different situation from the realm-ending threat he had once been.

"No matter," he said, lowering his hands to his sides. "Even this level of power will be enough."

"Enough for what exactly?" Liu Chang asked.

Ke Jun's smile was cold. "Enough to take what is mine." His red eyes moved back to me and stayed there with an intensity that was deeply uncomfortable. "Blood calls to blood, after all."

I looked around at the people standing with me hoping to see someone signaling that we should pull back, but what I saw instead was Liu Chang shifting his stance into something clearly combat oriented, Bing Lan's hand already resting on the hilt of her sword, and the other disciples quietly moving into defensive positions.

Which made sense when I thought about it from their perspective.

Our group had five Stage 9 cultivators plus another five at Stage 8 and two at Stage 7.

Liu Chang and Yan Li had already shown more than once that their actual combat ability went well beyond what their cultivation stage would suggest on paper, and against a single opponent whose power was clearly unstable this probably looked like a very manageable situation to everyone standing around me.

But something about this still felt wrong to me.

I had read enough about cultivation to know that someone who had once reached the Civilization Realm didn’t simply lose access to everything they had learned just because their current power level was reduced.

So much for retreating.

"You are the one responsible for what happened to Chu Feng?" Liu Chang asked, cutting through my thoughts.

Ke Jun didn’t appear troubled at all by the fact that our group had gradually spread out to positions that surrounded him on multiple sides, if anything he seemed to find the whole thing entertaining.

I couldn’t tell whether his calm came from confidence in his own abilities or from a personality that simply couldn’t resist explaining everything before doing anything, but either way, nobody was going to interrupt him because this was our best opportunity to understand what exactly we were actually dealing with here.

"That useless waste?" Ke Jun made a dismissive gesture with one hand. "He failed to fulfil the terms of our arrangement. His fate became inevitable the moment he demonstrated that he was inadequate for the task."

Liu Chang's fists tightened at his sides and the stone-like quality of his skin from the Immutable Titan Scripture was already beginning to spread across his knuckles. I could feel his qi gathering and making the air around him feel heavier and more dense than it had been a moment before.

Even though Chu Feng and I hadn’t gotten along. I understood the anger on Liu Chang’s face, because Chu Feng had been under his command and his responsibility and possibly his friend, so finding out the young man had been used and then discarded when he was no longer useful had to be a painful thing to learn.

"I had such hopes when I first awakened," Ke Jun continued, either not noticing or simply not caring about Liu Chang's reaction. "I founded this lineage and elevated an entire family of ordinary mortals to heights they never could have reached without my intervention. We were meant to become a dynasty, each generation surpassing the one before it."

His expression became darker. "Instead, I wake to find my descendants have fallen so far that they do not even remember what they were supposed to become. Simple villagers who can barely sense qi at all. It is deeply disappointing."

"Why does it have to be me specifically?" I asked, partly to keep him talking and partly because I wanted the answer to that question. "Why do you need my body and not someone else’s?"

His red eyes fixed on me again and the smile that came onto his face had something that was almost like fondness in it, which wasn’t reassuring.

"The Divine Spirit Reclamation technique allows a spirit to persist beyond death," he said. "But it can’t restore true life on its own. This form I currently have is temporary and unstable. Without a proper vessel that shares my bloodline, my spirit will eventually come apart."

He began to move in a slow circle as he spoke, and I noticed that his path just happened to force several of our cultivators to shift their positions to keep him in front of them.

"I planned for this situation long before my death, I established the shrine and built the formation network through these entire mountains specifically for this purpose. The original plan was for my descendants to bring their most talented male children to the shrine as vessels, so that there would always be a suitable body ready for me when I was prepared to use one."

Something ugly moved across his expression. "But they forgot. Generation after generation I waited and watched while they brought food and wine and treated me like a local spirit to be kept happy with small offerings rather than remembering what they actually owed me and why. Not once did a single generation do what they were supposed to do."

His eyes came back to me. "And then just when I decided to take direct action myself, you walked into my shrine completely on your own. The irony of that is something I find pleasing."

"The heavens will not permit this," Yan Li said, and his voice carried authority that came from somewhere deeper than just personal confidence. "The Mandates Guardians will not allow a perversion of the natural order like this to continue."

I noticed that Ke Jun's form flickered slightly when Yan Li said that, which was interesting because whatever these Mandates Guardians were, they were clearly something that could make even a former Civilization Realm cultivator hesitate.

"The heavens are very far away," Ke Jun whispered. "And their guardians cannot see everything. It is better to exist in defiance of their rules than to simply fade into nothing." He rolled his shoulders in a motion that was slightly too fluid to look natural. "Now that we have gone through all of the necessary explanation, shall we move forward? I have missed having a body very much."

I felt the change in spiritual pressure a fraction of a second before he actually moved, the air itself getting heavier like it does right before something very violent is about to happen.

"Ke Yin, get back!" Liu Chang said sharply.

"Do not listen to him, my child," Ke Jun said, and he pointed directly at me. "Come here."

Before I could actually move backward, I felt something pulling at me that was different from any physical force I had experienced before. It was pulling at something underneath the physical level entirely, resonating with something in the blood and essence of the body I was living in. The bloodline connection he had established earlier was being used as a channel to try to make my body obey him without my consent.

But he wasn’t the only one with options available to him.

I activated the World Tree Sutra and drew as hard as I could on its stabilizing qualities, and I felt roots of pure qi spreading through my meridians and anchoring my consciousness down firmly where it belonged.

"Interesting," Ke Jun said, and his eyes narrowed just slightly as he looked at me. "You actually cultivate the World Tree Sutra. How exactly did someone like you manage to successfully practice a technique like that, and why does your qi signature feel so unusual?"

I didn’t answer him because I was too focused on maintaining my resistance against what he was doing.

Around me I could feel everyone else preparing to move, Liu Chang’s stone skin had spread to cover his entire body, Bing Lans sword was already drawing patterns of azure light through the air around her, the Heavenly Jade disciples had their weapons raised, but Yan Li was the one who acted first.

"Heavenly Titans Judgement!"

Golden light exploded outward from him as he thrust his hand forward. The air in front of him crystallized into the shape of an enormous fist, each finger larger than a full-grown man, and the whole technique was wrapped in divine authority that pressed down on everything around it.

Ke Jun raised his hand and his fingers moved in what should have been a counter technique of some kind, but nothing came out of it, and the look on his face in that fraction of a second before the golden fist connected was the first surprised expression I had seen from him.

When the fist hit him, his body didn’t break so much as it simply scattered outward into red mist that settled across the ground in a wide circle around where he had been standing.

Everyone stood very still for a moment and looked at the empty space.

"That seemed..." Liu Chang said slowly, frowning. "Easy."

Bing Lan hadn’t put her sword away just yet, her eyes were still moving across the area around us. "Too easy."

I wanted to be able to tell them that it was over, but I had read far too many stories where the ancient and powerful enemy pretended to be destroyed and came back stronger than before, and more importantly, I could still feel that resonance in my blood, that connection he had made was still there.

It hadn’t gone away. Which meant he hadn’t either.
