“What?” Álvaro hisses.

“Quiet,” Cyros signs sharply, gesturing to the lindwyrms.

Cyros winces at Álvaro’s whispers. “I didn’t come all this way for nothing. But you can leave if you want.” He looks at me. “You still in?”

I have no idea how he intends to nab just one of these monsters out without enraging the entire nest, but like he said: I didn’t come all this way for nothing. I give him a sharp nod.

Álvaro winces as he glances between us. “Oh, hell with it. I’m in. Not like I could leave before my Role Requirement is satisfied, anyway.”

“Thank you,” Cyros signs, giving him a sympathetic smile. “Now please, stop talking. Don’t you know any signs?”

Álvaro flips him off, and I have to stifle a laugh. Cyros gives him a confused look, but Álvaro seems to appreciate that at least I got it. I don’t think rude gestures from Earth actually count as sign language, but I doubt Álvaro cares about the semantics.

Cyros gestures for us to back away from the cavern entrance, which I’m more than happy to do, given the overwhelming musk of the den. We stop one tunnel down, where Cyros apparently deems it safe enough to talk.

“I have a plan,” he whispers. “Álvaro, I’ll need you to be the bait.”

“What?” he whisper-yells. “Why me?”

“Because Sal has all the potions we’ll need,” Cyros says. “We can’t risk losing her whole arsenal if one of the wyrms catches up.”

“Oh, but you can risk losing me?” Álvaro narrows his eyes. “Don’t answer that.”

“Which potions?” I ask Cyros.

“Silence, slow, and sleep,” he says. Then he lays out his plan.

Luring just a single lindwyrm out of the den is easier said than done. The fact that most of them are going to sleep is both to our advantage, because it means we aren’t being swarmed by dragons, and disadvantage, because it means we aren’t being swarmed by any dragons.

The three of us all dose-up on Silence potions—everything I have left. Our footsteps fall silently, like a flock of ghosts. The effect barely extends beyond our bodies, however; our clothes and the ground we step on are all muted, but if we start a fight, the weapons will make noise.

Ideally, we won’t be starting a fight.

Cyros and I crouch on opposite sides of the tunnel, both of us clutching a potion bottle. He’s at a junction in the tunnel, tucked away around the corner, while I’m in a natural alcove of the wall. I wait, straining my ears, squinting down into the dark, for any sign of Álvaro. Something’s wrong. He’s taking too long.

Once all the lindwyrms settled down, Álvaro was supposed to creep up to the opening and pick out a target that was separate from the others. He’d then throw rocks at it until it woke up and took notice. With his smell and sound being disguised by my potions, he should be able to get pretty close without anything noticing; and while lindwyrms can see in the dark, it’s their weakest sense, so it should be able to give chase without being much of a threat.

Well. Slightly less of a threat.

I catch Cyros’s gaze, and lift my eyebrows in question.

“Wait,” he signs. “A little longer.”

Right then, I catch a flicker of movement at the end of the tunnel.

Álvaro is sprinting down the hall at full speed. He’s flailing his arms at us and his mouth is open in a silent cry, and I can’t tell if he’s laughing or screaming. It’s a little comical looking, actually. Any instinct to laugh is quickly wiped out as enormous coils of a beast come spiraling down the hall after him.

My heartbeat quickens; it looks even bigger in person, and Álvaro sure seemed to take Cyros’s reminder to “make sure it’s large” to heart. I tense as he runs toward us, ready to launch my potion the moment it’s within range. Slow first, then Sleep. Since the sleeping potion has to be drunk to take effect, the slow potion is to provide us with an opportunity to administer the sleeping potion. From there, we should be able to kill it quickly and painlessly. It’s a good plan. Humane.

As Álvaro reaches us, it abruptly strikes me that him flailing his arms around wasn’t just to draw the attention of the dragon; he was trying to tell us something. And now that he’s close, I can see what it is.

Among the ever shifting coils of the wyrm are two sets of claws that tear up the stone beneath them like wet sod.

Two sets. Lindwyrms are only supposed to have one set.

The realization hits me right as Álvaro blurs by, and only seconds later the lindwyrms are here—both of them.

No time to change plans. I throw the potion into the face of a lindwyrm as it blurs past. The bottle shatters on impact.

[Slow status effect inflicted.]

Good! It worked.

Unfortunately, it only got half the dose.

The beast hisses, untangling itself from the other wyrm as it digs its claws into the earth and skids to a stop. The other wyrm follows Álvaro down a side tunnel—the tunnel Cyros is hiding in. I don’t have time to see if they’re okay; the lindwyrm I hit locks eyes with me.

I dart from my alcove before it can corner me there, already reaching for another potion. A flicker of green glows between the wyrm’s teeth, and it leans its head back, as if winding up for a throw.

I flatten myself to the ground just in time; a glob of green flies over my head and strikes the wall behind me, hissing and spitting as it eats into the stone. Acid magic. Great.

Before I can scramble to my feet, the wyrm snaps forward like a snake. No time to dodge—

[Noxious Gauntlet activated.]

Green light blooms into existence, scattering sickly shadows through the tunnel. The abrupt light stabs into my vision, but it must be worse for the wyrm, because it lets out a startled whine, and its strike doesn’t find its mark.

Which isn’t to say I dodged—it just got a mouthful of my claws instead of my face.

The wyrm bites down, its teeth sinking into the ethereal gauntlet. I try to jerk my hand back, but I’m pinned on the floor and lack any form of leverage. It raises one of its claws in an attempt to eviscerate me, and I jerk to the side, barely deflecting it with the felis claws on my other hand. But it’s strong—far stronger than me—and even with the Slow potion laboring its movements, I won’t stand a chance if I can’t weaken it further.

Grabbing for another potion with my right hand, I channel my feeble reserves of mana into my left. The Noxious Gauntlet wicks up my poison arcana, dispersing into the claws still pressed between the wyrm’s teeth.

On instinct, I push the mana from the gauntlet, and I can feel my poisoned magic eke into the wyrm’s teeth and gums. At the same time, I crash a bottle of Frost Seeds into the wyrm’s claw, which explodes in shards of ice and begins to freeze the beast’s limb to the floor.

[Minor Poisoned status effect inflicted.]

[Frozen status effect inflicted.]

It worked—the gauntlet let me poison it. Barely. But I barely have any more mana to work with, and if I want to do more damage, I’m going to need more ammo.

This time, instead of pushing on the Gauntlet, I pull.

[Mana Drain activated.]

Magic pours into my hand like I’m ringing a sponge dry. The wyrm hisses, clearly feeling what’s happening to some degree, and lets the Gauntlet go, allowing me to scramble back. The wyrm turns its attention to his frozen arm, its coils lashing haphazardly as it contorts around the ice.

Magic is swirling around my hand like cool eddies of water. There’s enough to feel tangible, and as I extend myself into it, the currents seem to smooth, falling in line with my poison affinity. I try to draw it into myself, but the sensation stops at my wrist, where the gauntlet’s bracelet is fixed. I frown. It lets me steal mana, but won’t let me use it for other kinds of magic? That feels intentional on Shirasil’s part; he’ll give me more power, but only if I channel it through his weapon. Annoying.

But that’s a limitation I’ll find a way to work around when I’m not fighting for my life.

By the time the wyrm breaks free of the ice and turns to me, I’m on my feet and already have another potion in hand. This time, invisibility. My sound and smell is muted—time to disappear completely. I break the bottle against the wall behind me, and a white mist hisses out to envelop me. The wyrm lunges, and I dodge to the side as I fade from sight. Some of the invisibility potion settles over the wyrm as well, before the mist can disperse, but only half of its muzzle and neck vanish from sight, so luckily I can still keep track of it.

As I slip to the side, the wyrm jerks its head around, searching for me. It hisses and snuffs at the air. It seems confused, yet still turns in my general direction. It might not be able to pin me down, but enough of my smell or sounds are getting through that it might get a lucky strike in—so I need to finish this before it has a chance to do the same.

I focus on the mana in the Noxious Gauntlet, pushing instead of pulling, getting ready to feed more poison arcana into the beast.

But as I do so, I feel my mana push into something else. Like there’s a dry sponge on my wrist drinking it all in. I spare it a glance, and the little black refiner on the back of my wrist seems to wink back. Then it tugs on my magic, and all the mana that I stole from the wyrm spirals down into the remnant, just as quickly stolen from me.

“What the hell?” I hiss, the words muted by my Silencing spell.

What a little thief! I was going to give it some mana after the fight anyway, but I can’t do that if I’m dead!

The noxious gauntlet flickers, and the wyrm’s head whips in its direction. I swear under my breath. The Invisibility potion may still be working on me, but whatever the remnant is doing to the magic in the Gauntlet is clearly shaking off the effects of my potion.

Whatever. I still have the felis claws I can fight with. I try to deactivate the gauntlet—and find I can’t.

The wyrm snaps my way and I throw myself to the side, catching and rolling before I can hurt myself. I’ve barely come out of the roll when the wyrm is striking at me again. I swipe the Gauntlet toward its face and it jerks back with a hiss. With my free hand I lob another potion at it, a smoke bomb exploding from the bottle as it shatters across its face. Again I try to deactivate the Noxious Gauntlet, its faint green light growing brighter by the second as it chews through the invisibility spell. This time I can feel what’s happening; when I try to pull the Noxious Gauntlet back into the bracelet on my wrist, the remnant is pushing back.

Still carefully circling around the wyrm and keeping away from its random slashes and snaps, I squint down at the Gauntlet. I can barely make it out through the smokescreen, but I’m more feeling what’s going on than seeing it, anyway.

The remnant took the mana I stole from the wyrm; that much I’m sure of. But that’s not the whole picture, exactly. The mana in that Gauntlet hasn’t gone anywhere; it’s more like the remnant leaked out into it, and now we’re in a mental tug-o-war over who gets to use it.

Even more strange, I think I can hear it. Or at least understand it, a little. It’s like when I was feeding mana into the refiner’s crack before, when I was able to detect hints of a consciousness. A growing awareness. Now, it’s much louder and fully awake.

It’s warry—confused. Disoriented, but also cautious. I don’t think it knows where it is or what’s going on. Which, fair, given we’re underground in the middle of fighting a wingless dragon. I don’t imagine many people would wake up to that and take it in stride.

Though I’m not sure it’s accurate to say it’s a person. I’m only getting feelings from it, not words. And its consciousness just feels… alien. Like it doesn’t work the same way my mind does.

I’m given a good ten seconds to contemplate all of this before the wyrm comes barrelling out of the smoke. It strikes toward the Noxious Gauntlet. No point in continuing to run away, what with the Invisibility potion wearing off, so I strike back.

The corrupted magic presses into the dragon’s gums, sinking through its skin and infecting the beast.

It’s as fascinating as it is horrifying to witness. The remnant is using the Gauntlet’s ability to turn the wyrm’s own magic against it. Infecting it from inside. I back away, lines of magic still connecting the Gauntlet to the wyrm, and seem to come up against the end of the magical tether, unable to tug the Gauntlet any farther away. On the bright side, I’m outside of the Wyrm’s range as it starts to thrash and convulse.

I can sense the remnant’s influence sink back into the claws as well, now feeling sluggish and sated—a cat that’s just eaten a full meal. Its consciousness brushes over mine as it sinks into the refiner. It’s aware I’m here. It doesn’t see me as a threat—though I don’t think it quite sees me as an ally, either. Instead, I get the vague impression of acknowledgement, and then the entity quiets, as if settling in for a nap.
