I could hear music.

It was lovely. Beautiful beyond words. So achingly perfect that I wanted to cry. Haunting harps and somber violins and melodic woodwinds. All winding together very slowly. Intimately. It was the kind of music you’d sway to in a lover’s arms.

The marble floor was cold against my split, swollen cheek. It felt nice. Everything else ached, or was numb, so I just let my eyes stay closed and focused on the pleasant coolness.

There. This isn't so bad. Maybe the hag will come back and eat me while I’m here. And maybe I won’t feel it at all; I’ll just lie here and listen to music and it’ll be like falling asleep.

I could hear voices, too. Coming from a short distance away. Louder than the music, but so many that any individual words drowned each other out. The voices themselves were lovely. Rich and mellifluous, musical and sing-song, dulcet and sultry. Beneath the chatter I could make out rustling fabrics; swirling lace and silk and gossamer.

Is he having a party? That’s what it sounds like. I can’t imagine him at a party. He probably sits and drinks wine and looks bored with it all.

Cool air brushed against my unhurt cheek. I heard the coo of an owl from somewhere nearby, followed by the whisper of rustling leaves and grass.

Am I outside? Feels almost like it. That’s nice. I’d rather be outside than trapped in his home.

I cracked my good eye open. I was on an ivory balcony. Endless stars glittered like diamonds high above.

Mm. Yeah. That’s a much better last look than some fancy chandeliers.

I heard a sharp intake of breath from off to one side.

Turning my head seemed like it would take much more strength than I had in me at the moment, so I just swiveled my good eye towards the sound. My little balcony jutted off from an enormous banquet hall lit by flickering silver and blue torches. A cracked glass door separated me from the room.

It was full of people. No, Fae. There was no mortal among the crowd, at least not that I could see. The women were ethereal and willowy, gliding across the marble with grace I could only gawk at. The men were muscled and tall, with proud jawlines and gleaming eyes and full, arrogant lips.

Their clothes were a marvel. Gowns spun from gossamer and spider silk and dripping rubies, vests of silk and velvet and moss and cascading jewels. Shades so vibrant they made my vision swim, all of them shimmering like water beneath sunlight.

One of the dancing pairs had frozen. They stared at me through the glass. The woman was beautiful, with short golden hair, an ageless face, and skin the color of honeysuckle petals. She was wrapped in gossamer hued like the skin of a dragonfly. The man had one hand tangled in her hair, and the other cupping the small of her back. His eyes and tousled hair were the color of bronze. He had a gray vest on that seemed somehow hazy, like the corners of the fabric were drifting away as smoke.

I could just make out the woman’s voice. It was low and tinged with amusement, as if she was holding in laughter. “And here I thought the Sellians had brought all the flesh. Ugh. Look at the state of her, Embriel.”

The man drifted closer. His nostrils were flaring. “I told you. Vel’s been holding out.” His teeth flashed in a triumphant, cruel smile. They looked quite sharp. “Same blood I smelled the other night.”

The woman turned her pointed nose up, sniffing, then gagged. “And you thought that was enticing? Have you lost your senses? She smells like a bog.”

He didn’t answer.

Probably can’t expect either of them to rush out and untie me. Not from the way they’re talking.

I don’t see… him.

I couldn’t quite prop myself up enough to get a good view, but from what I could see of the swirling sea of fabric and color he was nowhere within. Just strange Fae. I’d have doubtlessly been terrified, had my circumstances not already been so dire.

Maybe I can… I don’t know, roll myself off the balcony and find a very sharp stick lying on the ground. Or crawl in and hope one of them unties me… and then leaves me alive long enough for me to snap back to the real world. I wish I could call out. Or get a better vantage point; he must be in there somewhere, but all I can see through the door is...

The door. The door!

I fixed my eye on it. Marble frame. Curved golden handle. Thin glass, clear as fresh water and cracked into a beautiful, splintering design. And it looked like the hinge opened outwards, towards me.

That was good.

My nostrils flared. I rolled myself over once, then twice, groaning with the effort and ending up on my back before the door. My hands were long-numb, but the marble bit painfully into my wristbones.

Doesn’t matter. Got to bear it. Worth it.

I lifted both feet, sucked in a breath, and kicked as hard as I could.

The glass did not shatter. That was fine. I hadn’t thought I’d get it in one try.

The woman yelped a little laugh. “Oh! How funny! What’s it doing, Em?”

The man drew closer, head tilted to one side. He watched me, tongue sliding along his upper lip, and offered no response.

I closed my eyes against the cruelty brimming in that gaze. Lashed out again. My feet impacted with a dull thud. No splinters. Yet.

Just need one. A shard or two big enough to… salt, I don’t know, to somehow press between my ankles or something. Maybe I can try to catch one on my tunic. And then I’ll have a way for us to cut ourselves free.

Renner would doubtlessly have questions when I woke up cradling a piece of broken glass. That was fine. I’d cut us free, and we’d find a way to break out and save Teela and I’d burn the awful hag and I’d think up some reason that sounded logical enough to satisfy him and we’d all escape and-

“Is that a human?”

“What’s it doing?”

“Who brought it?”

“I’d heard the rumors, but…”

“Is it for dessert?”

“Is it trying to get in?”

“Don’t let it, it smells foul.”

I cracked my good eye back open. My antics were drawing a small crowd. Men and women more beautiful than I’d ever imagined all stared down with various expressions of bemusement, curiosity, and cruel apathy. I sniffled up at the gathering, wondering if any of them would be moved to help me.

But, no. They just stared and laughed. Some of them licked their lips, baring too-sharp teeth.

They don’t care that I’m hurt. They don’t even see me as a person. I’m just a thing.

I closed my eyes, kicking the glass again. Something cracked. My heart soared with triumph.

My audience abruptly fell silent. And then I heard him; his voice was barely a murmur from beyond the door. I couldn’t make out the words.

“Care to explain, my lord?” It was that deep, vaguely amused tone. Embriel, the golden-haired woman had called him. I’d heard the name somewhere before, I was fairly certain.

“Leave. Now.”

I kept my eyes closed. Sucked in sips of cool, clean air.

“So that’s a ‘no…’?”

“Leave.”

Probably not a desired feature of his fancy party. ‘Tonight we have beautiful music, delicious food, gorgeous outfits, and one battered mortal trying to break down a door. Only the best.’

I think I’m delirious.

The dulcet voices and swishing fabrics drifted away. The music stayed, slow and soothing alongside the sound of a gentle night breeze.

There was a quiet click as the door handle unlatched, then a strange, metallic crack that made me flinch. A moment later, cool glass nudged my toes. I curled my feet up as he opened the door.

Surely he’ll untie me. He won’t just leave me like this. Even if he’s angry about the intrusion, about me causing a scene… he won’t just leave me.

My good eye slid open. The Fae loomed over me, a blurred, dark visage that blocked out the silver stars. He stood rigid, staring down and making no move to help me.

Saints, oh, saints, he won’t leave me like this. Will he?

He drew a deep breath. One pale fist was knotting around-

Thud. I whimpered as warped gold- the inner doorhandle, now a mangled lump- hit the marble beside my hip.

Please, please, don’t leave me like this, please, please, please…

Cold fingers gripped the gag, then ripped it away with such force that I saw stars. One hand slid into my blood-matted hair, pulling me upright. His other hand flickered with blinding silver as it dropped to my swollen eye and split cheek. I shied away with a cry, and the grip on my hair grew painfully tight. Held me still as he tapped my nose, traced my bloody lips.

“Who did this?”

The angry, throbbing pain faded, replaced by an evening breeze and a wash of silver as moonlight spilled across my skin. My chest heaved as cool night air flooded my starved lungs.

I sucked in blessedly clean breaths. Couldn’t seem to find my voice as the stars spun above.

He dragged me further upright. I felt the sharp, sudden relief of pressure as the ropes around my wrists tore away and my bloodless hands flattened against the ivory.

Answer. Gotta answer.

“People,” I choked out, blood dribbling from my mouth. Dark crimson spattered between us, staining the immaculate white stones. “P-pacted. Bound to the… h-hag.”

The world rippled. The stones shivered.

“Thank you.” Silver swirled above us. “This m-might be… I’m in a c-cage, and she’s… she’s going to… I might not… s-see you again. But… thank you.” My vision blurred, breath hitching as numb fingers clumsily clutched black silk.

As though, if I held on tight enough, I could take him with me.

The cloth was soft, but the form beneath was rigid. That fathomless, unreadable gaze fell to my hands.

And then he was gone.
