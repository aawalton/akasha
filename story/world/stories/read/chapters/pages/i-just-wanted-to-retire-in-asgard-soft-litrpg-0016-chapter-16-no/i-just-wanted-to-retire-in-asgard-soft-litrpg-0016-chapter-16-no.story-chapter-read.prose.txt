Barry's hand was already rising, his fingers curling around the trigger, when Freya stepped into the empty corridor.

She just planted herself in the exact spot where his portal was about to open.

"My queen, you may not realize it, but you were seconds from dying just now."

She looked at him, from head to toe. "...I beg to differ." Her poise didn't slip. It was the same face he'd seen her wear at court, the one she kept for Odin on his bad days. Only her red-rimmed eyes gave her away.

"I heard every word," she said, her voice flat. It was exactly how Odin delivered bad news: one blunt fact at a time. "The collapse at the heart level. His power eating him alive. Yggdrasil eating him alive."

Barry lowered his hand. The footsteps he'd heard in the distance had faded, and nothing had come to replace them. Had she cleared the corridor before he got here, or had the guards simply been told to stay away from her?

"So you knew he was dying. What is this, you need someone to talk to? Something to get off your chest?"

"I knew he was changing. I just didn't know how fast." She glanced past him, down the corridor, then made herself meet his eyes. "I'll be direct. He would sooner die than give you what you asked for. The seven books in the root-layer paths. He's kept them sealed from everyone. Even from me. Even from Thor."

"Then the ones he let me read..."

"Fakes," she said.

Barry waited. "And what do you want in return?"

"You try to heal him," Freya said. "If it's possible. And in exchange, you get the real books. All seven."

Her husband was turning into part of Yggdrasil, and she was handing over the books he wouldn't let anyone touch, herself included.

Odin's decay rate had jumped again in the past twelve hours. Thor and Loki were still stranded on the high-energy branch. And the books were the one variable he hadn't been able to measure.

He nodded once.

For half a second, her shoulders dropped with relief. Then she remembered herself and straightened.

"I know what I just did," she said quietly, not quite to him. "I'll be carrying it long after he's gone. But he can never know about this."

She touched two fingers to her breastbone, then held her open hand out toward him, as if sealing the bargain. Maybe it was a ritual Barry didn't know. He felt nothing. No surge of binding, no pressure at all.

"Fine. I'll head back."

He turned and opened a portal to the workshop and started through. Halfway across the threshold, he froze.

"What the..."

Something was wrong, and it wasn't the room. A faint prickling ran along his nerves that hadn't been there in the corridor.

"System, scan me."

[Foreign hex detected]

[Type: tracking and compulsion]

[Location: nervous system]

[Time of entry: when Freya's offer was accepted]

Elegant work, honestly. She'd buried the compulsion layer under the tracking layer, so a quick scan would have stopped at the tracking and called it done. Barry was almost impressed, right up until he read what the lower layer did. It could steer his decisions, and he'd never notice it happening.

"The apple doesn't fall far from the tree, huh," he muttered, thinking of Loki's antics.

He stepped into a glass cylinder, and the seal closed behind him. The System could have burned the hex out on its own, but he wanted data first, so he told it to strip the hex off one layer at a time and log each one. Five spells fired around him, two from emitters in the floor plate and three from a ring above his head, and golden script crawled across the glass. None of it came from his usual scanners. It all ran on the crystals Loki had conjured. The tracking layer came away first, then the compulsion layer under it, and within a minute a second scan found nothing left.

"So the books were lies too," he muttered, weakly.

Seven thick books appeared on the central table with a thud. As the last one settled, every bulb along the wall went out, though nothing in the room had moved. One of the covers ticked, just once.

"System, analyze the room. Look for curses like the one that was in me."

[Scanning...]

[Unidentified magic detected]

[Source: the new books]

Barry backed away from the table until his shoulders hit the wall.

"Activate containment protocol."

A large glass jar dropped over the books. Then a shell of divine alloy closed around the jar, its surface etched with fields meant to smother any spell cast inside.

"What the hell did that witch give me?" he muttered, edging closer again. He'd asked for information, and the first thing the books had done was kill his lights. "System, scan the books."

[Scanning...]

[Estimated time: 24 hours]

A full-day loading bar sat at 0.1 percent. Barry watched it long enough to see it tick over to 0.2.

Seven books should have taken seven minutes, tops. Twenty-four hours was ridiculous. Whatever was in those books, there was a lot of it.

He was still watching the number creep up when an alarm cut in.

[Alarm!]

"Oh, right. They're still out there."

He tore open a retrieval portal to the branch.

They stumbled through, gray-faced. A thin crackle of lightning ran down Thor's forearm and died before it reached his fingers. Loki didn't get as far as a spark. They made it a few steps, each holding the other up, until neither of them could. Thor hit the floor first, and his precious Mjolnir tumbled out of his hand and clanged against the table leg. Loki went down half on top of him, one arm pinned under his shoulder.

Thor's hair had gone white at both temples.

Loki's left hand, the one still visible past Thor's shoulder, wouldn't close all the way. She tried twice, probably reaching for a spell, and both times her fingers stopped short. Barry couldn't tell if it was exhaustion or something worse.

"Y-you. How dare you." Loki couldn't even get her eyes fully open.

"Hmm." Barry stood over them and didn't lift a finger, and they kept at it until their voices gave out.

Then he switched on the recorder.

"Experiment: Yggdrasil visit, trial four. Failed."

He left them on the floor and turned back to his monitor. He pulled out a crystal, the same kind he'd given the clones on Jotunheim, and linked it to the screen. It was all there now: population, progress rate, governance. All green, for the moment.

He wondered what Bela had chosen. The button that would tell him sat right under his finger, and he left his finger hovering a hair above it. If he looked, he'd have to do something about whatever he found.

He pulled his hand back and shut the monitor off. "Stop caring, you piece of shit," he told himself. "You know what happens when you start caring."

Barry checked the scan, hoping at least one book had finished. It was still under one percent, and the estimate had stretched to a week.

"What the..."

He looked over at the central table. It was warping, the wood bowing inward under the shell that held the seven books, the grain pulling out of line.

Then the stone walls flexed, rolling in the same slow wave as the table. The containment fields inverted, every gauge on the wall swinging into the negative, and the air pressure fell so hard his ears popped. Every scanner in the room swiveled toward him.

The shell split along its seam, and a book bound in dark leather began turning its own pages.

You chose the shorter path again. Efficiency over loyalty. That's the third time. You were warned.

Barry didn't answer. He kept both hands well away from the book.

The second book, thinner and the color of old bone, tore a rift open above the workbench. Golden light came through it and stripped the traces of Yggdrasil off Thor and Loki a strand at a time, pulling them back into the rift.

"Shit. Shit, shit!"

Thor and Loki still lay where they'd fallen after the last experiment. Barry left them there, slammed a palm against the nearest console, and dumped every capacitor bank in the workshop into the containment field.

The field stuttered, and the surge he'd pushed into it rebounded through the nearest emitter and hit him square in the chest, hard enough to knock him back a step.

"Come on, what the hell are these books?" The field was gone. The capacitors that fed it were drained, and they wouldn't recharge tonight, maybe not all week.

He rolled under the table just as a third book, bound in something like scaled hide, threw a web of glowing lines across him. The lines settled over his jacket and traced its power distribution conduit by conduit, the same way he'd trace a fault on a diagnostic board.

"Stop analyzing me. I'm the only one who gets to analyze."

He fired a localized EMP from his left gauntlet, the only one still loaded. It was his last pulse until the gauntlet recharged, hours from now. The other two books slammed shut. The leather one only paused, a page hanging mid-turn.

The world and all its realms will be ours.

Golden light touched the toe of Thor's boot, and Barry took a step toward him before remembering he had nothing left to throw at it.

[Gravity failure detected]

The gravity field, steady all night, started rolling in uneven waves. A wrench lifted off the bench and fell toward the ceiling.

Barry locked his boots to the plating, and the next wave tried to lift the rest of him off the floor anyway. He went hand over hand along the console rail to the emergency override. He ripped off the cover and fed a hard-coded shutdown into the System's local node.

The node accepted it and went dark. Whatever he'd already pulled was safe, but everything the System had recorded in the last hour went down with it, along with any proof of how this had happened.

Warnings filled every screen that still worked.

[Hostile archive integration in progress]

[Containment failure imminent]

[Recommendation: abandon workshop]

He ignored it. If he left, the archive would follow the power lines out of the workshop and into the rest of the facility, and he had nothing left to stop it with.

Thor slowly sat up.

He didn't reach for Mjolnir, still lying where it had rolled. He looked at the split shell, then the dead consoles, then the gauntlet still venting heat on Barry's arm. Then he looked at Barry, and kept looking until Barry shifted his weight.

"I will stop this for you, but no more experiments," Thor said, his voice level, his hands resting open on his knees. "Not with me. Not with her. Not until my conditions are met."

Barry kept one eye on the books. "Name them."

"Nothing much. Just stop being an asshole."

Barry opened his mouth to say okay and ended up looking at the floor. Not being an asshole meant caring about people, and caring was exactly what he'd shut that monitor off to avoid.

The light reached the monitor desk, where the crystal that fed him Jotunheim's numbers still sat linked to the dead screen. Its glow began to dim.

"No."
