Hayden turned back. “Is there something you need, President?”

Loricks pursed his lips and looked away. “If you win this tournament, it’ll be worse than last year. If you want to go to another B-rank, I can try to work something out. But Junolyx… Trust me. You don't want to go to Junolyx.”

Hayden was unsure about whether to answer him. Loricks had been good to Hayden; at least, that’s what Hayden had gathered. It was only fair to alleviate his worry, so he decided to speak.

“You know what prevents people like me from squashing people like Rosen, Loricks?”

“Power.”

“No. It's thinking that you need personal wealth or prestige to wield power. If I make an enemy I can't defeat, I'll befriend an ally who can.”

“And you think that'll be easy?”

“Who knows? The Iska girl seems to like me. Does she not?”

Loricks stared at Hayden dumbly, at a loss for words. Hayden had already captivated a powerful student at Junolyx—and he hadn't even gone there yet. That was hard to argue with.

“You should start a stronger institution, President,” Hayden said after the silence. “Your charity is a lesser person’s ambition.”

Loricks Raskin stared at the door long after Hayden left, trying to process what had happened in the last five minutes. He had called Hayden in to inform him that the powers that be had converged to stop him from entering Junolyx, and Hayden said Junolyx’s elites were unable to stop him with an undeniable argument. Then, Hayden told Loricks that Galma was a waste of his time. The whole ordeal was so absurd that Loricks forgot to get ready. As a result, he had to power walk to make it to the tournament in time.

Hayden had made it to the tournament five minutes before, and he was flanked by women whispering variations of “Do you think he knows?”

Before this gets out, huh? Hayden thought sarcastically. He motioned his palm, respectfully giving a “no need” signal to the concerned women.

That just spread more confusion.

One immediately said, “He totally knows. Did you see how unconcerned he looks?”

A group of men jeered. “Are you guys going to praise him for everything?”

One of the women said, “Yes,” and Hayden chuckled. Two girls squealed excitedly and said, “You just got him to laugh!”

Hayden summoned Gluttony. “See that?” He glanced at the animated women. “We’re celebrities. You know what that means?”

It shook its head.

“It means that we can’t just win—we need to put on a show. You think you can do that?”

It nodded once.

“Good man.” Hayden petted the mouse’s wings as he took his place in the crowd. There was already a crowd of four hundred, including staff and students, and it was rapidly growing. As he approached one of the stages, the crowd parted like the water in the Violet River.

It was clear who knew about the rule changes at a glance. Those who didn't looked excited to see him fight; those who did held mixed emotions. Some smirked, but most averted their gazes. He had been martyred, so even enemies sided with him. That said, most were glad that they could use their spirits. That way, they had a chance. The Redemption Festival, after all, was a life-changing event.

Hayden reflected on his position and what he should do about Rosen. His current self viewed him as little more than a pest, but his past self would have been humiliated and destroyed. He had to remember that the moment children involved adults, they became adults—and Hayden had no mercy for adults who tried to harm him.

Hayden decided: he would summarily destroy Rosen for the enjoyment of the others. He doubted it would make Rosen a better person, but it would teach him that money and connections weren't enough to save him from consequences. Ultimately, it didn’t matter if someone was right if they were dead. That was an important lesson all people had to learn—regardless of power or status.

Loricks scaled the stairs to the main stage, face solemn. His expression silenced the crowd. He waited for the crowd's attention and then spoke.

“As many of you already seem to know, a certain individual—”

“Rosen!” Renly yelled, making Rosen’s face redden in panic.

“A certain individual,” Loricks pressed, “An individual that will not be speculated on or named, has filed numerous lawsuits to investigate whether a certain student has been using performance enhancing drugs.”

The crowd rained boos as a nearby mob isolated Rosen. The women were loudest, as no man would fight too hard for Hayden to participate. But they did sneer. It was hard not to.

“And you have to admit!” Loricks yelled over the roaring crowd. “The gains are… pretty unbelievable.”

He cracked a slight smile, and the area erupted into a fit of involuntary laughter.

“Usually, we’d investigate and get it over with, but they slapped the lawsuits on us last night, making it impossible to respond. Therefore we were forced to settle on new rules. The new rules will allow men to use spirits during the martial tournament; the women's tournament will remain unaffected.”

A quick spell of silence overtook the area like the flash of lightning—then came the thunder.

“This is rigged!"

“First we can’t trade, now this?”

“They’re clearly trying to screw Hayden!”

Hayden turned to Rosen and saw the useless cretin cowering under the damning glares of his peers. Hayden could tell he didn't regret his actions. But that was okay—there was still time.

Loricks put up his hand to quiet the students. “I understand the concerns, and I’m firm in my stance that there will be an investigation into the spirit selection rule change…” He glanced at Rosen and Ms. Narin, both of whom looked away. “If someone has orchestrated an attack on our institution, no amount of money or legal aid will save them from severe consequences. Mayle Haya, the president of Junolyx University, has agreed to make the matter public on our behalf, should the investigation uncover corruption. She will also invalidate the scholarship of any student implicated in the scheme.”

Rosen’s face turned deathly pale.

“Until then, our tournament will continue on, and will do so without the slightest degree of randomness. The order of events will be male to female, with names in alphabetical order.”

The roster came onto the screen, with all the names alphabetically placed, giving a satisfying, categorical gradient. It would’ve been great if—

“Goren’s against Hayden first? Oh, yeah, that’s random.”

“Who would’ve thought?”

“Let me guess, Hayden can’t use his hands to fight him.”

“Nope, he has to use his teeth. No jump kicks, spins, or hops. Just teeth.”

Rosen was submerged in hatred and ridicule—but he accepted it. At the end of the day, people criticize the victors—but rarely learn about the losers.

The only problem was Mayle rejecting him and going public, but he doubted she would. Junolyx University was an intergalactic, multi-trillion-credit corruption scheme that catered to lawbreakers and invalids. Junolyx exposing corruption was like a store advertising spoiled food. It didn't make sense. No. This threat was all just for Galma to save face after a bribery scandal.

That said, even if Mayle did reject him, taking the shot was far better than doing nothing. Rosen wouldn't face real consequences. His father would dismantle Galma if Loricks tried. So in the worst-case scenario, Rosen would be disinherited, just as doing nothing would've guaranteed, and then he'd graduate from a C-rank university. Through private training and powerful spirits, he would complete the B-rank certification exam once he was good enough. It would be a harder road, but it wouldn't be terrible.

By contrast, Hayden would end up as nothing more than the subject of conversation at ten-year reunions. That was the bottom line, so Rosen just stood as stoically as he could, eyes fixed on Hayden's image beside Goren's on the roster. Rosen wasn’t certain that Goren could beat someone as supernatural as Hayden, even with a spirit, but he was certain that Goren could damage him enough that he’d go to the finals at half performance. That’s all Rosen needed—to get him down to half performance. Then, Rosen could definitely win.

He was certain of it.
