Oliver had his familiar core thingy sitting on a table in front of him. It was also surrounded by a bunch of blue hologram-illusions outlining what he thought it would look like, and Clark had to agree that it was pretty cool.

Alyssa was off to the side of their hearth-area, moonwalking on a line of green light, and that kept distracting him, because Oliver kept waiting just long enough between his 'questions' that Clark couldn't stay focused on just him.

"Well no, because if I don't do something to give it a tilt axis, it won't be able to move around at all," their Archmage muttered to himself. "It really does need a... hmm. Okay, Clark, do you see any disturbances in the mana around it if I do this? "

He turned his attention back to the artifact and its projected design, squinting at the space around the bronze sphere and its twin protruding gears. All kinds of mana was flowing through it, because it was a magical artifact and quite obviously glowed, but it looked no different than the time before. "No?" He tried.

"How about... now?" Oliver did something, and there was a change, a halo of Water forming around the center of the device. "Yes!" Clark happily agreed, pointing out where he saw it.

"Okay, that's good. So it does have an orbital hold position, and I can probably get rid of this," Oliver grabbed his new staff and swept it through a complicated two-handed wiggle, speaking a bunch of words that glowed to Clark's mana sight, and the image that they were looking at changed. Now, instead of a complicated set of interlocking rings, the hologram design just looked like a spherical drone-shape sitting inside an inward-facing gear. He kept fiddling with the design, doing things that Clark sometimes couldn't even tell had happened, and Clark's gaze wandered over to where Commander Inq's army of inklings were fighting a massive horde of ravenous dragons.

Any one of the ferocious beasts would surely tear Clark to shreds, with his lack of attack skills, but their Commander was handling a literally apocalyptic force with barely more effort than signing a document. Even now, bands of Hero mana swept around her while she drew in the air. Then, the lines pulled themselves into having volume, and something like two dozen new defenders joined the fray.

"I need a fix on the hologram," Oliver called Clark back to the present, pointing to a place in his display where the blue projection went all weird. It had too many quick turns and random changes and looked all glitchy, and Clark was happy to [Unblemish] the hologram into a nearly perfect-picture state. Oliver did a thing, adjusting his new staff a bit and then swinging it around so that the shiny copper and gold-ish bronze pieces sparkled in the not-day.

Oliver had sensibly taken Clark up on his offer to help him out with his style, and so his inkling clothes had been updated to a set of sleeveless purple casting-robes. He had even figured out how to make functional changes, like pockets! He needed to work with the Commander for that, but it made his newest subskill even that much cooler.

"Alright, prototyping time. Let's see if this works..." Oliver retrieved a bronze hoop from a nearby table, then swept it through the blue hologram-lines. Incredibly, the hologram caught on the hoop, blanketing it and very clearly highlighting the places it was different from what it would someday be. He then set it down in front of Clark, "Do your thing. See if you can feel the pattern."

Clark hesitated, "I do not believe my [Unblemish] has the needed capability to make such a drastic change, Oliver."

"What?" Oliver seemed confused, "Were you not..." He sighed. "Just... do the thing and see if it works?"

Given Oliver probably knew more about Clark's Skill than he did, Clark decided to give it a try anyway. He put everything he'd learned about reshaping, ⟨Retouch⟩ing objects into the attempt, using the hologram overlay as a guide while he pushed mana into the circle in very careful ways. It was not enough to simply desire change, he had learned! When looking to push a Skill beyond that which it did automatically, substantial skill and practice was required. That was likewise why, other than Oliver's confidence, Clark did not think this would function as described.

And yet, before his very eyes, the metal of the ring was retreating and expanding, substantially changing shape as it conformed to the hologram which Oliver had laid out. Encouraged and surprised, Clark redoubled the amount of mana he was pushing into the object, and currents of glowing gold and blue mana flooded down his forearms and through his hands, dancing around the gear and making it look like a crown.

"Yesss," Oliver quietly celebrated, "Keep at it, I think I still need to run it through some Attunement."

Clark did as requested, continuing the stream of magic going as well as he could. He was forced to slow down after a few more minutes, as his Capacity could not keep up with his Power, but Oliver said nothing and Clark did not wish to interrupt the spell he was casting to provide the information. It was incredibly important to never interrupt a mage while they cast, after all.

"You can stop now," Oliver eventually said. "I... think it worked perfectly? Honestly, I'm kind of surprised that went so well first try."

"What is it that you have accomplished? I have rarely seen [Unblemish] work in such a manner." Now that Oliver was no longer casting, it was less dangerous to ask questions.

"Planning. You know how you helped me get Illusion hooked into this thing?"

Clark nodded. It had merely been last week!

"Okay. So that appearance of form bears enough Association with Knowledge that I could tie them together, and that Knowledge tie was enough for me to [Cogniprint] an enchantment of intent to become, similar to the ⟨Planner⟩ subskill. That intent to become then, through the arrangement-of-tools of Technology and the mastery of magic for Arcane, sets a foundation which future effects may build off of, a kind of future-Attunement that acts as a resonant beacon towards the defined state. So when you [Unblemish] and ⟨Retouch⟩ the circlet, that intent-to-become empowers it beyond its normal limits to fulfill its own construct."

Oliver paused to breathe. "Does that make sense?"

Clark considered, then shook his head. "Not even a bit."

The Artificer sighed. "My staff lets me create holograms that enchant places or things with a... plan. That plan then interacts with other spells to fulfill itself. Does that make sense?"

"Sure!"

"Okay, good. Now, hang around for a few more minutes, I might still need you for this."

Oliver worked for a while longer, scratching at some mostly-finished runes along the gear Clark hadn't even noticed as he had been making it. Then, he held it in one hand, put his other hand above it, and a bunch of mana started pouring down as he used his [Order Mana] skill. Then, he spun the gear and the gear flew up. It immediately ran into Oliver's hand with a thud, and Oliver swore and began shaking out the just-injured limb. The column of mana stayed put, but as the gear stopped spinning it clattered to the ground as well.

Magic was so cool.

But he had a job to do! Clark gently coaxed Oliver's wounded arm out from where the Artificer was trying to put pressure on it, and ran [Unblemish] over it. Fortunately, there was already a bit of a bruise forming that Clark could use as a point to [Unblemish] from, because even with all his practice he still needed some blemish to... un. He was getting better at fixing internal stuff though!

"Thanks," Oliver gritted out after a moment. "Io, that hurt. Guess it's got enough push, though."

Oliver kept working doing a bunch of really shiny magic, and sometimes having Clark ⟨Retouch⟩ chunks of metal into gears. But then, he needed to go make dinner, and so he left Oliver to do his work.

By the time dinner was ready, and the Commander actually fetched Oliver for it, he'd created a spherical drone a bit smaller than his head. The familiar core was the center, but now it looked like a bronze orb with a blue lens-eye at the front, with a couple of little spurts of mana added to it for good measure, that looked like more gear-sprockets to accompany the physical ones which turned the flying gear Clark had helped to make. It wasn't floating right now though, instead resting on the table directly beneath the main body of the drone.

"I'm almost done," he spoke around a mouthful of food. "Actually, I'm so close... to the initial boot, anyway."

"Oh yeah, things always work the first time you test them," Alyssa remarked.

"Well. I've done some testing," he sheepishly admitted. "⟨Use Artifact⟩ can brute-force the individual systems, but once I do a proper full familiar boot, it should be more adept at... all of it? But like..."

Magic swirled around his hand and shot out to the drone, making it hum with potential and start to have bits of magic haphazardly leap out of its shell and back inside. He reached over, and pushed on the flying gear. It rose into the air a little bit, and Oliver kept turning it until it connected with the gear sticking out of the drone's back, which was already turning. That made a little net of mana connect the two pieces, and the whole drone rose into the air with a bit of a whirring-clicking noise.

"Looks like you won't need to use me as your familiar for much longer there, Smith." Henrietta remarked. "I'm impressed you got it working so quickly."

Oliver moved his hand back and forth, and the drone flew through the air to follow where he was pointing, propelling itself by changing the angle of its flight-ring. Then, he directed it to land, and it slowed down until it powered off completely, the flight-ring clattering to the table below with a noise that made Oliver flinch slightly.

"I'm kind of surprised as well, but the new staff is so helpful. It helps correct for a bunch of minor mistakes so long as I've got the original design right, which means I can actually go for some harder castings, and that... makes it easier. Tools. Got to love them."

"I want to make it clear I'm not doubting your decisions as well, Smith, but I thought you needed to use whatever you made with the Pillar of Industry in your subsequent creation, and..." Henrietta paused while she tried to figure out a good way to phrase 'this familiar doesn't seem useful.'

"What can it do?" Clark asked so she didn't have to.

"It's... a familiar?" Oliver was uncertain what the problem was, probably because he was the only Archmage on the team and was so smart he thought the rest of them were too.

As far as Clark knew, a familiar could be basically any kind of animal which a mage had as their companion. If Clark were to go back to the Age of Oppression, they would probably consider him a mage - and possibly kill him, of course, but if they did not do so they might think that Tully was his familiar, were she to come along with him. And she even had her magical shadow-hiding thing! It was magic, they were pretty sure. She was a magical creature, which made her extra a familiar, but while she was the best, it was quite clear that what Oliver meant was something different.

"Can it cast spells for you?" Clark asked. That seemed like a very useful thing for a familiar to do.

"Sort of? Well yes. Kind of. I can use it to cast some spells, because it's a familiar. That's... well, okay. I guess that isn't a thing all familiars can do. What counts as a familiar..." Oliver pulled up his System and cast spells to look up something, probably about familiars!

"Woah. That's a long page. Okay. So the drone is going to be... hang on, there's a lot of really similar categories. Um, okay. Might be wrong. But I think it counts as a constructed-focalbond Marlenson projectionoid familiar?" Oliver was way less certain about this than he had been a few minutes ago. "I had no idea there were so many differences. To me, this was all just a familiar. Never really had one, so it wasn't something I looked into much... it was all pretty simple until now. Wow. Okay. I really should read this later."

Clark looked at him, hopeful that he would say something in regards to his findings, but Oliver did not do so.

"Whatcha find out?" Alyssa was the first to actually ask.

"That there are a lot of types of familiar," Oliver replied, still distracted. "And I am used to a very small subset." He tore his eyes away from the invisible System display, "Pretty much every time someone used one in school, it was as a specialized focus. You need the right familiar for a certain spell or type of spell, so you create a familiar bond... nice and simple. And they're like any focus. Some people have a lot, some people have a few... I guess I assumed that was just how they worked in general? Anyway, this one's good at holograms."

Oliver cast a spell, and the blue 'eye' of the drone body lit up, projecting a blueprint-hologram of itself into the air in front of it.

"The thing I'm most looking forward to is that I installed a memory cache into it, so I can cast a blueprint through it, and then have it quickly reproject it elsewhere afterwards. Or when I'm making it, having it be the projection point means I can move more."

"Fancy," Alyssa whistled. "Can I have one?"

Oliver blinked, and looked at her in disbelief, "....No."

"But what kind of mage doesn't have a familiar?" She puffed out her chest. "Clearly, I need a familiar."

"There are so many things wrong with that logic. Suppose I shouldn't be surprised, though."

Clark was looking at the light projection hanging in the air, and tried to replicate it a bit with his own [Unblemish]. He was not successful, not even at making a ball of light that might have only been visible to himself and his mage sight. He had tried to learn the kind of magic which Oliver did, or even what Alyssa could do, but neither quite worked for him. But that was alright! Clark was very used to not being able to do more advanced magic. It had never come easily to him, no matter how much his father attempted to teach him, and his cousins demonstrated. They always used really big words and pushed him to do... things. But usually, Clark could just do those things after a couple of levels, and then they moved on to something else.

But this team was so awesome, they had even been able to teach him pieces of magic that he never had before! Using Skills without needing to say them was fun, and the time and need to explore his one Skill had worked really well for him! It was just... such cool magic. Even if he wanted some way to defend himself... oh! He should ask the Commander about that. She had wanted him to come to her with any asks beyond his 'domain,' and offensive prowess was definitely outside of the upkeep and cooking he was trying to do.

He asked as much of Henrietta once he had a moment, and she stopped to consider. "That isn't... quite what I meant by that, but that's mostly an oversight on my part. It was something I was going to prioritize for a while, but when Smith managed to set up good defenses, it fell by the wayside. I... No. I will consult with Veeran on this matter."

Her gaze drifted over to the front gate of First Tower, where Jacob had needed to run off. A pack of inklings were at his heels as he dove into a newly-refreshed wave of dragons, each the size of a car, as they bore down on him like a tidal wave of teeth and claws.

"It might be a little bit before that works out well, though."

"That is entirely alright."

"At least he's having fun," Henrietta muttered, probably more loudly than she intended.

"It is always better to than not," Clark wholeheartedly agreed. "I wonder if he will teach me to use a sword?"
