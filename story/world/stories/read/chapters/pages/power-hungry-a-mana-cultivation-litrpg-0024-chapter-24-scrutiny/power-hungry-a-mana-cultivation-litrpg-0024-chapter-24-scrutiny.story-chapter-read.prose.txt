Cale woke up on his back right where he had blacked out; at the feet of the dais. He tried to move, but there was a lot of pain. He closed his eyes and groaned.

“Still now,” Aura said gently. “I am healing the damage, but my sigils will be spent. I will need a top-up.”

“You got it…” Cale muttered.

“Who’s he talking to?” someone said.

Damn it. I need to be more careful.

“Probably got brain damage pushing so far,” Fiara said somewhere above.

“Crazy bastard,” Ragnak’s loud voice said. “I want to fight him.”

“He can barely get up, you idiot,” Fiara snapped.

“I didn’t say now!” Ragnak said. “When he gets up of course.”

“Just a question,” someone said calmly. Probably the medallion guy. “Can any of you sense his mana?”

“I was wondering about that myself,” Cassidy said.

“I couldn’t sense anything,” the rich guy said. “Probably has some tech.”

“He is Middle Body Tempering,” Risto drawled. “I tested.”

“How the hell did he get so far ahead of us then?” Madia snapped. “He must have cheated!”

“Only one who cheated was you when you grabbed him,” the medallion guy said calmly.

“It was not forbidden,” Madia said quickly. “Besides, he tried to kick my nose in! I lost because of that!”

“No less than you deserve,” the medallion guy said.

“Artan, you are a real pain in the ass, you know that?” Madia said.

“The same mindset that leads you to cheat to defeat someone of your own faction in a friendly competition is very similar to what brought these Sullied their fate,” Artan, the medallion guy said. “You should contemplate on that.”

“It wasn’t a friendly competition!” Madia said. “There was a damn Sulflower pill on the line! A divine Sulflower pill!”

“And why do you think Darius offered up one,” Cassidy said gently. “Now he knows exactly the kind of person you are.”

“I did what I did, and it wasn’t wrong. I won’t apologize for trying to win!”

“You don’t have to,” Cale croaked, silencing the group. “But I got your number now.”

That elicited scoffs and chuckles. Fiara knelt on the floor. “You okay?”

“Been better,” Cale said. “Who won?”

“You did, you idiot,” Fiara said.

“Oh,” Cale said. He opened his eyes. It was a painful endeavor. He saw eight faces looking down at him and a high wooden roof.

He had won. A rush of satisfaction ran through Cale’s body. A sensation so sweet, even pain couldn’t dull it. Cale grinned to himself and reveled in that feeling. He wasn’t even sure what a divine Moonflower pill was, but that wasn’t the point. The point was that he had measured his will against these other elite cultivators, and they had come out wanting. Cale had won. He had the strongest will. Well, maybe. Aura had been awesome. But Cale would never tell her that. She definitely did not need more encouragement in the self-esteem department.

“The interesting question is how,” Cassidy said. “Madia here calls you a cheater.”

“That’s rich,” Cale said and chuckled weakly. It turned into a painful cough. “If I had cheated, I would have done it when my bones started creaking.”

“How did you get so far in the pressure?” Artan asked. “You are apparently Middle Body Tempering?”

“I am,” Cale said. “Fifth stage.”

“That’s bullshit!” Madia said. “No way you got further than me without cheating. I’m seventh stage. That’s Upper Body Tempering!”

“Just shut up for a moment and maybe we will learn something,” Cassidy hissed and knelt down beside Cale. She gently placed a hand on Cale’s forehead. “Do you need anything, darling?”

Cale suppressed a response. He didn’t trust Cassidy as far as he could throw her, no matter how friendly she suddenly seemed. This wasn’t genuine concern. This was a move. They wanted to know what his advantage was, and he was not going to spill.

“I need to see Darius,” Cale said, turning to Fiara. “Where is he?”

“Coming back soon,” Fiara said. “He said he needed to make a call.”

“A call?” Cale asked. He hadn’t thought there were phones in this world.

“He’s an executive operative,” Fiara said. “He has an RCI.”

“RCI?” Cale asked.

“What kind of boonies did Darius find this guy from?” The rich guy asked in a haughty voice.

“It’s complicated,” Fiara said. “And RCI means remote communication Integra.”

Slowly Cale moved his elbows back and pushed himself up. It was like trying to rip himself up from a giant flypaper. But he managed. “Give me some room.”

To his surprise, the other recruits did take a few paces back. They must have really wanted to know what Cale was hiding.

Ragnak crouched to level with him. “Tell you what. Fight me now. If I win, you have to tell me how you won.”

“Uhhuh,” Cale said, positioning himself to lean against the dais. “And if I win?”

“I uhh… didn’t think about that.”

“Yeah…?” Cale said, still focused on making himself comfortable. It might have been a study in futility. Everything just hurt. “Let me get back to you on that then.”

Fiara snorted.

“Here, Cale,” the red headed pig-tail girl said, offering a translucent pill. “Glassweed pill.”

Cale took it and inspected it. It seemed fine. He threw a quick glance at Fiara, who gave a discreet little nod. The pigtail girl laughed nervously.

“I’m not trying to poison you just for beating me,” she said.

Cale popped the pill in his mouth and rolled it under his tongue. “That’s what someone who was trying to poison me for beating them would say. I’m Cale, but you know that.”

“I’m Sintra,” she said and offered a hand daintily. It was small and pearly white. Cale grabbed it gently. She smiled.

No games? No moves? What’s going on?

“Nice to meet you,” Cale said and took the hand cautiously. “Why are you making friends?”

“Why shouldn’t I?” Sintra said. “I saw how you treated Ragnak even though he is… Ragnak. We went to the same preparatory academy, so I would know.”

“I’m right here you know,” Ragnak said grumpily.

“You’re alright, dude,” Cale said to him. “Just… turn it down a notch sometimes.”

“No can do, man,” Ragnak said. “I am who I am, and denying my nature is an oppression of my power, and I will not have it!”

Sintra and Fiara shared a look. The rich guy shook his head. Cale only watched all four of them in bemusement. Sintra then turned back to Cale. She had a round and freckled face with gentle and large lips and large green eyes.

“How did you temper your will so?”

“I would also like to know,” Artan said and took a step closer.

Even Madia uncrossed her arms and stopped glaring at Cale, clearly waiting for insight.

Cale looked down and searched for an answer. He didn’t want to give these people nothing. Especially Sintra seemed to be earnestly wanting to learn. Half of them were assholes for sure, but he would be a fool to turn down friends or alliances.

But he wasn’t sure what to say. He remembered fragments of his old world. He remembered watching television. Enjoying an ice cream in the summer sun. Being late from a class. Sitting on a log, sharing his first kiss with a girl next to a dwindling fire under a clear starry sky. The generic nature of his memories struck him odd.

Who was I? Are these memories even mine?

He needed to know. Every time he thought of who he was and why he had been in the cryopod, he felt a pang. He needed to be patient, he knew. To gather strength to acquire agency. And he had a mountain to climb to achieve that. There was no room for rest or laziness.

“I…” he said, stretching the letter. “Have to keep going full speed to know who I am. I need to discover myself and I’ll go through any pain to do that. And to do that I need power.”

“Unbelievable…” Fiara muttered. Cale wasn’t sure what she meant. When Cale looked at her, she turned away.

“This is an answer aligned with your Drive. To have been able to develop it at only Body Composition is remarkable,” Artan said and nodded to himself.

“Thanks man.”

“It was not a compliment.” Artan said flatly.

Darius’s boots echoed against the stone as he waltzed into the hall, all sharp strides and absolute confidence. His voice filled the space like he owned it. “Good, you’re awake. Time for you guys to hear the results.”

“We already know he won,” Madia said sullenly.

“He did,” Darius said and tossed a pouch at Cale as he got closer. “But if that’s your only takeaway from this exercise, you are not going far.”

Cale plucked the pill out of the pouch and looked at it. It was small, no bigger than a brass tack. But it shone with a luminescent golden light that radiated warmth and intensity. It felt heavy in his hand. Much heavier than an object the size of a small pea had any right being.

“You eat that pill and digest it,” Darius said. “Not like you need this lesson.”

“What does it do?” Cale asked, still looking at the pill.

“I can’t believe the boonies guy doesn’t even understand what he won,” the rich guy said.

“Hey,” Cale said to him. “What’s your name?

“Galvon Gauss,” the guy said, narrowing his eyes.

“Shut up, Galvon, I’m trying to learn here.”

That drew a snort from Cassidy. Galvon took a side glance at Darius who was watching the situation. In the end Galvon settled on a low growl as he stared Cale down with a murderous glare. “Do you know who I am, peasant?”

Cale just gave him an unimpressed look. He couldn’t believe the amount of tools that the cultivator population contained. Galvon was breathing heavily, as he squeezed a fist, waiting for an answer.

“Are you done?” Cale asked.

Galvon snarled, but didn’t seem to want to say anything else. That suited Cale just fine. He was in pain and tired and seriously had no time for little shits like Galvon. He had seen him be one of the first to give up in the previous challenge. This guy might have been ahead of Cale in advancement, but he was all muscle, no strength.

“He is at Body Tempering seven,” Aura said. “What an absolute waste.”

Cale smirked at that. Galvon thought it was meant for him. He wasn’t exactly wrong. He wasn’t good at suppressing his rage either.

“Enjoy your win, peasant. We’ll see how long that luck lasts,” Galvon spat out.

Darius did not say a word. He only had a slight smile on his face and watched the situation unfold like his favorite show. Once Cale turned back to look at Darius, who raised an amused eyebrow.

“As I was saying,” Darius said. “The divine Solflower pill improves your body’s natural adaptability with mana. It is a growth elixir. The sooner in your cultivation that you ingest one, the more the benefits compound. It’s quite valuable, you know.”

“Huh,” Cale said and looked around. Madia and Galvon were bristling. The rest of the recruits weren’t exactly happy for Cale either. Only Fiara and Artan maintained a neutral expression. Cassidy watched him with a smile, but there was something hidden in her eyes.

“Think of it this way,” Darius said and stepped on the dais. He started pacing back and forth, hands behind his back. “As you know mana of any kind requires attunement. They need to be speaking the same language. That is what cultivation is. Understanding the True Language and then speaking it, using the mana. That pill is like a dictionary for the body.”

The whole nature of True Language still eluded Cale, but he understood the metaphor well enough, but this was all very abstract. “That sounds amazing, but what does the pill actually do?”

Darius stopped in his tracks and made a face of approval, nodding to himself, as if appreciating the insight of the question. Nothing he would admit in front of a crowd, of course.

“The mana needs to be on the same wavelength as your body. Otherwise you can’t attune it. Think of this pill expanding the wavelength. So you don’t have to expend as much focus and energy to get what you want. In short, it makes everything a little smoother. Cultivating Mana, using it, breaking through to higher advancement. And it’s an advantage that keeps accelerating if you manage to get more of those pills. Each compounds on the next.”

“How do I get more?” Cale asked.

All of the recruits and even Darius burst into laughter. Ragnak clapped Cale on the shoulder. “Good one, man!”

“You make yourself valuable to the syndicate,” Darius said and nodded for emphasis. “That’s how.”

Cale looked at the large and heavy golden pearl in his hand. He could see how everyone around him coveted it, so Cale took his distance and went to lean against a wooden pillar further away from everyone, as Darius started a lecture on the importance of willpower to the other recruits.

Cale rolled the golden pill in his fingers. This was his prize. He noticed his hand shook. This was pure excitement. That dark, greedy part of Cale was not happy, it was ecstatic. Like a wolf licking its chops after felling a deer. He had earned this. He had managed to obtain a rare resource and others envied him.

Goddamn, winning is a thrill.

With a smile, Cale popped the pill into his mouth.

The pill was sweet and fragrant, like honey and flowers. The taste spread into his mouth, overwhelmed it.

The sweetness spread through his mouth like honey and flowers. And then more honey. And more flowers. He gagged slightly, blinking back tears. This was too much. It was like drinking an entire bottle of syrup. Scratch that; a bucket of syrup. The sickly sweetness was overflowing. Cale almost felt like retching.

That was no option. Cale embraced the sweetness, holding back tears and coughs. He kept swallowing frantically. The pill was seeping some strange subtle mana into him. With each swallow a warmth not unlike liquor went down his gullet into his stomach and settled around his solar plexus. The mana was very thin and very subtle, as if made of smoke and silk. It flowed freely around his solar plexus and Cale tried to manipulate it, make it circle around his abdomen like regular mana.

“Stop it you damn nincompoop,” Aura said immediately. “Let the pill do its job you fivefold fool. Don’t make extra work for yourself, you’ll end up altering the mana pattern and diluting the effect!”

That was all Cale needed to know. He tried to endure the excessive sweetness of the pill and let it do its job. He kept swallowing the warmth down and breathing steadily. The smoky, silky golden mana started building a strange and complicated geometrical latticework into his abdomen, where the nexus of his mana resided.

It took a long time to digest the pill. Darius finished his lesson on how all of them relied too much on their advancement and not enough on their other assets. Cale remembered snippets of the lesson. Playing the game. Using all the tools. Boldness. Taking risks if you wanted to achieve things. Darius had remarked that Cale had the lowest cultivation out of all of them, but he had been the most willing to go furthest. Madia had said that Cale was too simple to understand the risks of pushing too far into a stronger spiritual pressure. Darius had said that winners don’t make excuses, they just play the game. Typical Darius stuff. His mentor had been right, the lesson wouldn’t have been useful to him. Not because Cale didn’t need honing in those aspects, but because Darius yammered about them constantly.

Cale was sweating and his mouth was numb by the time the pill was spent. There was now a strange warm structure of mana made of golden smoke inside of him. Cale did not know what it was, but it felt good. Aura said that the mana signature was benign and she could learn to enhance this effect. Cale was grateful for that, but was too tired to comment. The compound effects of surviving the pain, the sweetness and the focus required to digest the pill had all drained him dry. Cale found himself slumping against the pillar that he had been leaning against.

But a satisfied, victorious smile spread on his lips before he decided he was too tired to find another place to sleep.

Too tired to move, too tired to think, he drifted off. But the fire inside him had never burned brighter.
