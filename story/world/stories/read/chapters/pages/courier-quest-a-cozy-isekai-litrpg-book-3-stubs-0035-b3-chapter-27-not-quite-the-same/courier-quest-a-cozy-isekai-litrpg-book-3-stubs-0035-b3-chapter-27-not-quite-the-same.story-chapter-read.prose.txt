The line to board was empty by the time Trevor and Adabelle had reached it, and Lora had performed a sneaky teleport after giving them their tickets. There were only a few members of the crew waiting, and each of them were attentive from the moment they set foot on the dock.

All three of the crew waiting for them were demons. Trevor had met the handful that lived in Tosa and knew that they were a varied bunch even within the same families. Their skin tones ranged from colors similar to humans both light and dark all the way to red, purple, and even green.

Aside from their humanoid shape, they almost always came with horns and a tail. The appearances of those, too, were incredibly varied. Two of the waiting crew had curling horns while the one in finer clothes had a spiraling one coming out of his hairline in the center of his head. One had a thin, almost rat-like tail while the others were thick and scaly.

“Salutations, my friends!” the one in finer clothing greeted, raising his hand to wave as he approached. His black eyes scanned for any bags and, upon seeing there weren’t any, he smiled. Trevor arched an eyebrow. It sounded like he was a New Yorker. “Am I right to guess that you are the newlyweds I have been told to expect?”

“We are,” Adabelle answered.

“Ah, what a pleasure, and congrats!” he said loudly before bowing almost ninety degrees. His black horn seemed to have been recently waxed because it glinted in the sunlight. “I am Captain Braidley, Summoned of Aletander with the Power of the Gale. It is an honor and privilege to meet you, Lady Adabelle, and Trevor, and Clay. Welcome to my not-so-humble airship, the Red Dream.”

“It’s a magnificent craft, captain,” the noblewoman praised as she looked up at it.

“It’s really cool,” Trevor agreed, marveling at the Red Dream now that he was closer. “We’ve been pretty excited ever since we heard about it, and it lives up to the hype.”

“I’m very glad to hear you say that. You three are special guests of Klarkartar, so anything less than total amazement means I’m not doing my job properly,” he said with a laugh as he straightened out.

“Do you work with him often?” Adabelle asked inquisitively.

“He and I went halfsies on this bad girl about three years ago,” Braidley said with a grin, turning to look at his vessel. “I’ve since paid off his side of it, but old Klar still gets special treatment. And he asked me to take special care of you on your honeymoon. What could I say other than of course?”

“Klar’s a really good dude,” Trevor agreed. “He’s facilitating this whole thing for us as I help him out making long-distance deliveries.”

“I hear you, brother. He found me scrambling for quests, didn’t even ask me what my Power was, and offered me a job on the spot,” the demon said, putting his hands on his hips. “I’m a much hotter commodity now than I was back then, but he got me back on my feet. We bought the Red Dream together and made the renovations to make her the best airship on both sides of the Darel Ocean. Now I can go where I want, do what I want, and generally enjoy life. Still got a soft spot for him, though.”

“Right?” the courier laughed. “I met him within my first, like, ten minutes of arriving here. He knocked me over and, just when I thought I was about to get beat up for staring, spent his time explaining things about this world. Didn’t ask for my Power, either. At first he tried to tell me that being a merchant prince wasn't a big deal, but I’ve come to learn that he obviously just thinks differently.”

“Yeah, that sounds like Klar,” Braidley said before moving and smacking Trevor on the back. His thick, lizard-like tail waved as he walked. “Come on, let’s get you two situated. Don’t let the tail hit you. It will hurt.”

He started walking, and the courier shifted his shoulders to ease the dull pain he now felt. It didn’t seem like the captain had done it on purpose; he was just strong. The other two crew members bowed but didn’t follow.

“Since you were staring, I assume you came from a place that didn’t have minotaurs, huh?” Braidley asked, making conversation. “I find all these worlds fascinating. If you don’t mind me asking, where’d you come from?”

“A human-only Earth, unfortunately,” Trevor answered. “I would have loved a world like this one, though.”

“For sure, for sure,” the captain agreed with a bob of his head before turning to look at the noblewoman over his shoulder. His eyes flashed to her horns for a split second. “And how about you, Lady Adabelle? Again, if you don’t mind me asking.”

“Another world full of humans, though not Earth,” she replied in a tone that made it clear she thought this topic was brought up far too casually. “I would rather not speak of it any further.”

“Fair enough,” Braidley replied, holding up his hands in surrender. “I come from an Earth, too, but all of my people look like me. Made things pretty different from most of the other Earthlings I’ve talked to, but there’s enough similarities that it’s made relating to them pretty easy.”

“It does seem like a lot of them are fairly close together in terms of, well, everything,” Trevor said. “We actually just got done fighting a five hundred year old ghost pirate who came from Earth and had a bunch of Princess Bride references, like calling himself the Dread Pirate, though his name was Tanazi instead of Roberts.”

“The Dread Pirate Tanazi?” the captain asked. “Unimaginable!”

It took a lot more effort than Trevor thought it would not to correct Braidley. He smiled, nodded, and continued anyway. “You keep using that word. I do not think it means what you think it means.”

Their guide laughed. “Not quite the same, huh?”

“You could tell?”

“Yeah, that’s not the right quote in my world, either.”

“Stellar movie, though.”

“Oh, a hundred and ten percent,” Braidley agreed.

They had passed one of the doors into the Red Dream and continued down the dock to the second that was nearer to the front. He led them into a gilded hallway. The airship was floating in place, rocking side to side. It was distinctly different from what Trevor expected. There was some steady motion, but not in the same way waves would produce.

Braidley brought them to an elevator but, to the courier’s surprise, didn’t go higher in the ship. Instead, they descended.

“We generally like to keep guests away from the envelope. The balloon,” he explained when he saw Trevor’s confusion. “The crew is higher up because of it. You’ll still have access to the deck, of course, there’s just some behind the scenes things you’ve got to be qualified to see. It’s better down here, anyway.”

“I was wondering about that,” Trevor said. “While I didn’t really go on cruises, my parents did from time to time and they liked being higher up when they could. I guess it doesn’t really translate well when you don’t have to worry about looking out of a porthole and seeing nothing but water.”

“True, true,” Braidley agreed with a smile. The elevator stopped and the doors opened automatically. “There’s no need to worry about the view. You’re going to get a splendid one.”

“It did seem like there was a lot of glass around your hull,” Adabelle mused. “However, it seemed like it was covered from the inside.”

“Yup. Good eye, there,” the captain said with a nod. “To which I say, you’ll see once we take off. It’s one of my favorite things to hear about after we begin. Which will be soon, actually. We had a last minute cancellation so you were the last ones we were waiting on.”

“Hopefully nothing bad happened to them,” Trevor replied.

Braidley shrugged, but had an impish smile on his face. “Not sure, they didn’t tell me. The only thing I know is that their tickets are non-refundable.”

“And how many passengers are on the ship?” Adabelle asked.

“Thirty-seven, with twenty-two crew and then myself,” the captain answered. “Not the biggest crowd, so it might seem a little empty, but we’re racing across the Darel anyway so I figured it’s okay not to wait for critical mass to shove off.”

“Racing?” she followed up.

“Racing to the races,” Braidley confirmed as he turned a corner and stopped in front of a door labeled 02. “The Red Dream is a Cyclone-class airship, and I intend to race her in the 15th Aletander Skybound Grand Prix. Goes all the way around the country, and this year’s our year. I can feel it.”

“Well, I certainly wish you luck,” Adabelle said before looking at the door. “Is this our room, then?”

“Of course, Lady Adabelle,” he confirmed before retrieving a thick bronze key from his pocket and offering it to her. “This is the suite Klar likes to reserve. Not the biggest, but it does have one of the best views. He has told me in no uncertain terms to take care of you, and that is exactly what I shall do. So, if you need anything, Horatial has been assigned to you specifically, and he is only a ring of a bell away. Is there anything else I can do for you?”

“I believe that is all, captain. Thank you,” the noblewoman answered.

“Then keep your ears open, I’ll be announcing take-off soon,” Braidley said with a grin before turning around and heading back to the elevator. “Enjoy the room!”

“He seems fun,” Trevor said when he thought the captain was out of earshot.

“He seems like he enjoys the sound of his own voice,” she returned before her expression softened. “But he doesn’t seem like a bad guy, no. How’s your back?”

“Better. He smacked me good,” he remarked, and she rubbed it with her tail while unlocking the door to their suite.

Adabelle opened it, revealing a room nearly as large as the bedroom of the last place Klar had allowed them to stay in. The red and gold theme continued throughout with plush carpeting beneath their feet and wood panels on the wall. There were two tables near the door hosting a pair of chairs each, and one of them had a string in the wall. It led to a bell above, then continued upwards through a hole until it was out of sight.

On the opposite side of the room, the wood panels were more like slats. It appeared as though they would be moved later on. There was a long couch in front of it, a couple of recliners, and a strange circular seat where the entire middle was the back. Trevor had seen these on his world before, but only at airports.

There were two other doors. One of them led to the bathroom, complete with all the amenities including a shower big enough for a minotaur. There was everything both a human and a demon would need set up neatly on a storage rack, clearly set up for his and hers.

The other door led to the bedroom. It was small compared to the main suite, but that didn’t mean much when they were talking about sizes meant for someone both taller and wider than either of the newlyweds. There was a wall that had the same slats here, too.

Trevor immediately started putting things away in the dresser and closet. It had become a habit at this point and didn’t take him more than a minute.

“Good afternoon, ladies and gentlemen!” Captain Braidley’s voice called from the living room. They looked for where it was coming from, seeing that there was a speaker built into the wall by the bell. “It’s another wonderful overcast day here in Port Farro, Garend. We are expecting gloomy skies all the way to Aletander, but there have been no reports of big storms and thus we’ll have smooth sailing along the way.

“While it is a delightful seventy-two degrees outside, just the perfect weather all around, I ask that you stay in your rooms until we are done taking off. There will be a bit of a jolt as we pick up speed, so I recommend staying seated. Any bumps or bruises are not covered by your tickets and, while we have a doctor on board, he’s also a wizard and prone to insult you as part of their incantation. Alright, that's enough from me. Take a seat, check the green lights, and we’ll be off in a jiff!”

A bar of green light appeared over the slats in the wall, and the newlyweds looked at each other before shrugging. They moved to the couch, which seemed like it was the comfiest option with the best view, and sat down. Adabelle leaned against Trevor, her tail wrapping around his arm, while Clay jumped down to sit on the other end.

The little earth elemental pretty much had the whole half to himself.

“Are you excited?” Trevor asked.

“Very,” Adabelle answered. “I wonder what makes this suite so desirable to Klar. Aside from the fact that it’s big enough for him, I mean.”

“We’re probably about to find out,” he said as he smiled at her. Reaching up, he tucked an errant strand of white hair behind her ear. “I love you.”

The noblewoman smiled back. “I love you, too,” she returned before kissing him.

Above them, the green light shut off and the sound of mechanical gears whirred to life. The slats turned, revealing a window behind them, before lifting into the ceiling. Trevor noticed a pedal on the ground that was clearly labeled with direction that would allow them to move the wooden blinds themselves, and was about to point it out to Adabelle when he saw the view.

They were underneath the dock, and most of the cove could be seen from their position. The glass even made up the floor somewhat, allowing them a better view of the things below them.

“Oh, this is the front of the ship, isn’t it?” Trevor asked excitedly. “I mean, the front front.”

“Yes, it is,” Adabelle answered with a smile, patting his hand. “I would have to agree with Klar on this one; this is definitely a desirable seat on an airship.”

Before Trevor could respond, the Red Dream lurched forward. The initial jolt had him tense up, though it wouldn’t have been enough to knock him off his feet normally. They gained speed quickly as the airship tilted slightly. The sound of the wind could be heard through the walls before it suddenly dulled to a quiet roar more akin to a ceiling fan.

“Right, the Power of Gales,” the courier said with a nod. “He’s probably got a pocket of air up around us, pushing us faster. That’s why he didn’t mention any turbulence.”

“That’s probably correct,” Adabelle replied as she put an arm around her husband’s shoulder.

Her eyes were fixated on the waves below, just like Trevor and Clay’s. There were several glowing spots under the water, marking the jellyfish, and they passed plenty of uninhabited islands. There were many more ahead. All three of them sat in silence, watching the world as they flew it by.
