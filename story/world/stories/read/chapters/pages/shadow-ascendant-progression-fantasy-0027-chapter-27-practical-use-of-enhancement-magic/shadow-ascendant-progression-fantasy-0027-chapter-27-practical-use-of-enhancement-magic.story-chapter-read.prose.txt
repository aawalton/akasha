“Marcus is surprisingly observa… observant!” Mabel corrected her words, he tends to pick up random stuff.

“I wouldn’t really call affinity ‘random stuff,’ we are in magic school, aren’t we?” Marcus said flatly as he looked at Mabel. “Anyway,” his arm stretched in my direction, “I’ll keep an eye on you, Arnos, to see how much of what Mabel told me was ‘exaggeration.’” he added, with a weird smile.

Marcus smiled strangely. It didn’t show one apparent emotion. He wasn’t happy. He wasn’t amused or threatening. It was something in between.

Exaggeration, huh? He’s not going to execute me if I turn out better than mediocre… right? I wondered, meeting Marcus’s brown eyes for a second too long. Realizing I was starting to stare, I glanced away and reached out my hand for a handshake.

Was I allowed to touch him? I wondered, knowing how pesky nobility could get about physical contact, though refusing a handshake would definitely get me executed.

“I’ll try my best not to disappoint you, Lord Marcus,” I murmured, letting my hand free and allowing our lord to shake it whichever way he wanted.

“Are you not familiar with handshakes, Arnos?” Marcus asked, his brows raised.

“Ahhh… I am,” I began, but paused at the sound of footsteps. I looked near the stairs. Instructor Serena and Chloe were walking toward us.

Releasing his grip, Marcus glanced at Mabel. “We should go back to our place,” he said.

Mabel glanced at me, then back at him. “You go on ahead,” she added, moving closer to me.

Marcus nodded before walking away.

“Why did you bring him here!” I yelled, in a whisper. “And did you really have to tell him about it?”

“About what? Be specific, Arnos, I don’t really wanna think much right now, and just drift with the sky…” Mabel said in a low voice as she looked at the ceiling.

“You can’t even see it from here!”

“The real sky is in our heart!”

“Mabel, could you be serious for a second? Class is about to start—” I began, but was interrupted midway by her.

“It was a slip-up.”

“Slip-up?”

“Yeah,” she nodded. “I mean, why would I brag about you even if I had to do it? I am amazing enough, aren’t I?”

“You shouldn’t praise yourself.”

“That’s what incompetent losers say.”

So half of the historical figures are losers, got it.

“So, I was talking about dungeons, then we talked about monsters, carrots, red-eyed bunnies and then I mistakenly bragged about my genius plan.”

Mistakenly, haah, pretty sure she brought it up.

“And Marcus ended up asking who the other person was. So yeah, I totally didn’t intend to tell him about you… I just didn’t lie when he asked. You know, it’s the way I was brought up. I was always told a lady should never lie unless it benefits her directly, and so… Hey! Are you even listening to me?”

“I am,” I nodded, while I had turned my face away. I paid attention to what she said. It was just that with instructor Serena and Chloe now standing in front, it would be rude not to see in their direction… and talk in between too, though everyone was talking, so it was fine for now.

“So, that was all. I’ll be going back now. It seems I am the only one out of place. Bye.” Mabel murmured, walking away from me before I could even respond.

Then again, all I would have said was ‘sure.’

Overall, the conversation I had with Marcus wasn’t as bad as I expected, he was well spoken, over friendly with Mabel, maybe he has some ulterior motives… I thought as I glanced at them, though with the way they talked it didn’t seem that way.

Mabel was also quite sociable and had a nice personality… She was also rather aware of the way nobles act and talk, and by it, I don’t mean she was emulating the way they talked, no, NO! That wasn’t the case, a noble lady would certainly not threaten her friend with a fountain pen, nor would she hide Ero books in holy scriptures.

“That’s totally not something they’d do,” I chuckled quietly.

Mabel was just… her. She didn’t replicate others’ behaviour, though she was aware about how she should behave with a person based on their said characteristic. And when she knows a person well enough, she’d drop her guard?

But then again, I could be completely off base. I’m not Mabel, and I can’t read minds. All I can do is observe what someone chooses to reveal, never delving too deep, never probing the emotions they conceal. There has to be a reason, doesn’t there? If someone’s hiding their true selves, do I really have to know?

“So…” instructor Serena spoke up, breaking my line of thought, “today we are going to move on to the practical aspect of enhancement magic.” Her eyes drifted through the line as she continued. “As I have already briefed you all twice about the basics, I won’t be repeating… Ahem,” she cleared her throat. “Talking about the training I am going to make you do, you must remember my feat from yesterday,” Instructor Serena smiled with pride.

“About your breaking the rock, ma’am?” Someone asked.

“Yes,” instructor Serena nodded.

“About how you used Shatter Fist instead of ‘normal enhancement magic’, ma’am!” Noelle chirped in. “Just to show off,” she added her voice low.

Though I am almost certain everyone heard it.

Instructor Serena’s lips twitched at Noelle’s comment. Her prideful smile now seemed to be replaced with a forced one. “It was for motivation… to let you all know the full potential of enhancement magic,” she sighed.

“Well, anyway, coming to the main topic, what you all are going to do today is basically replicate the said feat,” Instructor Serena said flatly.

“Replicate?” someone repeated.

After which, in unison, several students groaned, “What?” with a terrified expressions on their faces.

“There’s nothing to worry about,” instructor Serena waved her hand dismissively. “We even have a personal healer today!” she added, excitement lacing her tone as she motioned toward the girl beside her—“she can handle any minor injury you lot manage to pick up.”

Despite her words, the fear in the crowd didn’t disperse; even Seraphina Victoria seemed a bit troubled. I wasn’t too sure why everyone was so fearful of the concept of breaking a huge rock? It can’t be that hard, right… I mean, she’s a reputed teacher in Cellera, of course she’d know getting us injured would be a bad thing.

Wait, no, my eyes narrowed as I looked at Instructor Chloe… she wouldn’t let injury worsen. I swallowed.

Instructor Serena stepped back, raised her arms, and closed her eyes, her palms facing the students at the end of the line.

Immediately enough, a brown aura of condensed mana seemed to have enveloped her body, as if she was wearing a really thin brown cloth fluttering in the air. There was no air though, and what was in front of me wasn’t a cloth for sure. It was a manifestation of condensed mana of the earth affinity and nothing else.

Instructor Serena’s lips parted as she breathed in. “Wall of stone,” she murmured softly, and immediately in front of all fifteen of us, an enormous ball seemed to take form.

The stone didn’t magically appear in front of us. No, it wasn’t like that. First, Instructor Serena’s mana seemed to have created a sort of rough, transparent, brownish outline of the stone. Though my hands couldn’t pass through it... looking back, I shouldn’t have touched it; getting stuck in there would have been awful.

Then, the mana’s exterior turned to stone, and a cracking sound echoed from within the stony sphere, as though the inner rocks were colliding. To become more resilient.

“We can’t break this…” Maya cried out.

“You’d never know if you don’t try~,” Instructor Serena sang.

“Are you sure you aren’t pushing them too far, Serena?” Instructor Chloe spoke up. Her voice was soft… it suited her, being a healer and all.

“If I don’t push them now, they won’t be ready for the tournament, besides,” she smiled, looking at Instructor Valmont. “I have you to heal them, don’t I?”

“You know I am teaching Class C, right? Or have you forgotten about it already? You are showing me the condition of your students, putting them in a vulnerable situation.”

“Oh, but you’d never…”

“Do not expect me to withhold any information from my class; they take priority over you—”

Instructor Serena didn’t let her finish. In the blink of an eye, she vanished from her place and appeared in the corner of my eye.

She was now leaning on the rock in front of Seraphina, with her legs pressing the rock beside for support. “You see that terrible teacher over there,” she pointed at instructor Chloe. “She’s threatening us. If you all mess up and she tells Class C… ‘oh Seraphina doesn’t know enhancement magic, just close in and punch her,’ you’d lose.”

Seraphina didn’t react to Instructor Serena’s teasing, though a small smile spread on her lips.

Maybe she was wondering how anyone could even close in against her… or maybe she just found her funny; it was something I’d never know.
