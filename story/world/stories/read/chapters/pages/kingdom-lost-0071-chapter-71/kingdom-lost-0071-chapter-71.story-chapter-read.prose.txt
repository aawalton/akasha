Thorne stood with his group of soldiers in the middle of the Hoshin village. The villagers gathered around them in a loose circle and stared with wide eyes and cautious expressions. Dust from the dry ground clung to the edges of their simple clothing as a light breeze moved through the open space between the huts.

Tama stepped forward and spoke clearly to the villagers. She explained the nature of Riley's community and the reasons the soldiers had come to help with the gathering. Her words carried steady confidence as she described the protection offered and the shared need to meet the quotas without drawing notice. The villagers listened closely and nodded in understanding as the truth settled over the group.

The soldiers had removed their armor before they marched through Riley's gates that morning. Their tunics underneath appeared different from those worn by the Hoshin yet after a full day of gathering they would gather enough dirt and wear to blend in more naturally. Tama and Riley had reasoned that to a Clawborn most humans probably looked similar enough and the overlords would care little as long as the resource quota arrived on time.

Riley's soldiers still carried their swords at their sides. They had received strict instructions to hand them over to Tama once they reached the village. The Hoshin possessed only spears for hunting and nothing like these blades so the weapons risked drawing unwanted attention from any passing eyes. The soldiers watched carefully as Tama and Thorne along with a few other Hoshin villagers carried the swords toward the equipment hut where the gathering tools were stored. One by one the blades were placed inside the simple wooden structure and the door was secured.

The group remained silent during the process. Thorne kept his gaze steady on the hut while the soldiers shifted their weight and scanned the surrounding villagers. The air held the faint scent of wood smoke from nearby fires and the distant call of birds echoed from the forest edge.

Riley sat at the sturdy wooden table inside the central tower and kept her gaze fixed on the glowing HUD map that hovered in her vision. The blue blob representing the soldiers had moved steadily across the terrain until it reached the Hoshin village. She studied the layout for a moment longer and then issued her orders by dividing the group into three roughly equal sections. She tagged the different colored resource clusters scattered around the village with clear markers so each section knew its target area. Once the assignments locked into place she designated the Hoshin village itself as the rallying point where the soldiers would return with whatever they gathered.

Tama directed the villagers with calm efficiency. They brought baskets woven from sturdy reeds and other gathering tools from the equipment hut and arranged them in a neat pile in the middle of the village square. The soldiers approached one by one and selected the equipment suited to their assigned tasks. With their choices made the groups formed up quietly and set out toward their designated resource areas. Dust rose gently from their footsteps as they moved away from the square and disappeared into the surrounding fields and forest edges. The villagers watched them go before turning their attention back to their own preparations for the day's work.

Tama turned to the villagers and spoke with a steady voice that carried across the square. "The group that have seen Riley's community is leaving tomorrow. If you wish to leave too, then you must decide by tomorrow for the preparations to be made. I will be leaving with the last group. If there are any that wish to stay here, you will truly be on your own. Riley's soldiers will not be staying. They will return in the near future."

The villagers dispersed quietly after her words. The first group that planned to leave hurried off toward their huts to begin preparations for the journey at first light. A buzz of excitement filled the air as the undecided villagers moved among them to help with packing and supplies. Their conversations created a low hum that drifted through the open spaces between the simple dwellings.

Once the last of the villagers had retreated into their huts the square grew still. Thorne and Tama remained standing alone in the center of the open ground.

Thorne looked at her and asked, "What do you think the undecided group will do?"

Tama met his gaze and replied, "Most will come. Only a few might refuse to leave. It will be suicide for them to remain once the soldiers leave."

Thorne suggested, "We could just take them."

Tama looked at Thorne for a long moment then lowered her eyes before turning her gaze outward toward the villagers who moved in and out of their huts in the distance. "Yes we could, but if they are truly free then they have the right to choose their end too."

Riley sat alone on the second level of the tower. The meeting with Nikola had ended some time ago, and she had just finished deploying the soldiers to the Hoshin village through the glowing interface of the HUD map. The quiet of the room wrapped around her like a familiar blanket as the last traces of the map faded from her vision, leaving only the soft natural light filtering through the narrow windows. She leaned back in her chair and exhaled slowly, letting the tension of the day ease from her shoulders.

Her mind drifted in the stillness. Thoughts of her father surfaced unbidden, along with the countless afternoons they had spent together watching war documentaries on the old television in their living room. A small smile touched her lips at the memory. Those quiet moments had always felt special, a shared world between father and daughter where history came alive through grainy footage and solemn narration.

With all the constant activity of building and managing the growing community, she had not allowed herself much time to think about home. The demands of leadership, the endless decisions about resources and defense, and the integration of new arrivals had filled every waking hour. Yet in this rare pocket of solitude, the memories returned with surprising clarity. She had loved spending time with her dad more than anything else in those early years. He had been her guide in so many ways. On bright sunny days they would venture outside into the woods behind their house, where he taught her to identify trees and track small animal signs through the underbrush. On rainy afternoons they would settle onto the worn couch with bowls of popcorn, the room dark except for the flickering screen as old war movies played out in black and white.

She had always been an inquisitive kid, full of questions that poured out one after another. Her father never grew tired of answering them, no matter how simple or complex they seemed. One particular memory stood out now as she sat there in the tower. They had been watching a documentary on weapons of war, the kind with detailed diagrams and expert interviews that explained the evolution of military technology through the ages. Riley had leaned forward on the couch, eyes wide with fascination as the narrator described ancient siege engines and modern artillery. Something in the footage had sparked a dozen new questions in her young mind.

Riley had been scanning the room absentmindedly while the documentary continued when her eyes suddenly stopped on the two mine samples resting on a nearby shelf. Something about the two samples seemed almost familiar from the documentary she had been watching in her memeory. Without thinking she stood up abruptly from her chair. She crossed the room in quick steps, picked up the two samples, and clutched them carefully in her hands. Then she raced down the stairs, her footsteps echoing against the steps, and burst out into the open air outside the tower. The fresh breeze greeted her as she emerged, carrying with it the distant sounds of the settlement at work. She held the samples tightly, her mind already racing with new possibilities and connections she wanted to explore.

Riley stopped at the forge where a fire had been burning steadily outside for several hours. The flames danced with a steady rhythm, fed by logs that had turned to glowing embers beneath a layer of ash. She reached into a nearby container and took a generous handful of the rough brimstone. With a quick motion she tossed the pieces directly onto the fire. At first nothing visible happened. The stones sat among the burning wood without any immediate change. Then as the heat built higher the brimstone began to melt slowly. During the bright daylight the transformation remained subtle at first. Soon however a distinct blueish tint started to spread through the flames giving them an unusual hue that flickered against the surrounding daylight.

Riley took a step back as a sharp pungent smell suddenly emanated from the fire and filled the air around her. The odor stung her nostrils and caused her to cough sharply. She covered her mouth with one hand while her eyes watered slightly. This is sulfur she thought as the realization settled in her mind. The scent matched exactly what she remembered from old chemistry lessons mixed with the war documentaries she had watched with her father years ago. Those programs had often explained the materials used in early explosives and gunpowder and the memory now connected clearly with the samples she held.

She held her breath to avoid inhaling more of the acrid fumes and reached for the second sample. With careful aim she tossed a handful of the fine white powder directly onto the fire. She jumped back immediately as the reaction took hold. The fire flared up with sudden intensity sending a bright burst of flame higher into the air. Sparks flew outward in a dramatic display and the heat surged for several long seconds before settling back down. The spectacle was large enough to draw attention from across the settlement. A number of soldiers who had been training nearby stopped their drills and turned to stare at Riley and the fire with open curiosity. Some exchanged quiet words while others simply watched with raised eyebrows wondering what had caused such a sudden and vivid reaction.

Riley smiled to herself as satisfaction warmed her chest. The experiment had confirmed what her memories and the mine samples had suggested. She turned away from the forge without another word and walked back toward the tower at a hurried pace. Her steps moved quickly across the packed dirt path as ideas already began to form in her mind about how this discovery might strengthen the kingdom in the days ahead. The soldiers gradually returned to their tasks but a few glances followed her until she disappeared inside the tower entrance. The blueish flames continued to flicker behind her gradually returning to their normal color as the added materials burned away. "Gunpowder," she thought. "The kingdom just gained teeth."
