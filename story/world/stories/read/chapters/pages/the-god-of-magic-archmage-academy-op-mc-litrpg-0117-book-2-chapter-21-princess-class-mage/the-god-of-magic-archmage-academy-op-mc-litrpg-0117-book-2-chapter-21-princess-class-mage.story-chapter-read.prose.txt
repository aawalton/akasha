Sen’s opponent was quick, powerful, and extremely adept at magic. She struck at him once again with a mana-infused punch. He returned a strike of his own under the power of Aggression. In its boosted form, it was more effective at tearing through an opponent’s wards or barriers, and that seemed to apply to the mana-infusion as well.

But it wasn’t enough to rob her force of its strike entirely. Her blow landed hard.

The wind rushed around him, tearing grass from the ground and creating a vortex that pulled birds from the sky. Sen braced himself, skidding backwards in the dirt for a moment before his own mana reinforced his positioning.

Her magician rank wasn’t just for show then; she was a powerhouse through and through.

“Regretting getting on my bad side yet?” she asked.

Sen laughed. “I’m wishing I’d done it sooner. It’s been a while since I’ve had a decent sparring partner.”

[Master, need I remind you that your last fight threatened to wipe a major town off the map?]

Sen gathered his mana for a powerful spell. He’d only gotten to test it against one other human, and fighting a princess-class mage was a rare opportunity to see how it matched up to someone that technically outranked him in modern times. He held his hands out toward the woman and smiled. “Tri-Infernal Hellscape Maxima.”

The world around him trembled at the force of his mana. Rocks floated into the air around him, and the air pressure intensified tremendously. Cracks formed in the ground and spread like a spiderweb across their makeshift battlefield.

His opponent raised a brow as the magma lines threatened to erupt. She didn’t budge or even raise any additional defenses beyond her basic wards. “It looks nice, but can it do anything?” she teased.

Sen responded by flooding the spell with even more mana. “I suppose we’ll find out together.”

The ground exploded, covering her in magma and searing fire.

Sen squinted. The fire obscured his vision of her, but her silhouette persisted, visible throughout the blazing furnace of his spell.

“That's all?” Amusement laced her voice. “My turn then.”

Sen smirked. “I’m not going anywhere.”

[Master, I’m not sure that’s wise. We should at least expand our defenses.]

The woman held a hand up toward him, and the flames of his own spell were sucked into a ball of fire forming in her palm. The power of a princess-class mage, the ability to control and recycle the mana of others. He hadn’t hurt her at all. She seemed to have an unusual affinity for fire magic. In other words, she wasn’t the ideal foe to use one of his fire techniques against. Even if it was one of his most powerful spells.

A tsunami of mana rushed forward from her in an amount so impressive that even Sen’s eyes widened. He hadn’t experienced a wave of power like this since encountering the gods after his graduation duel against Snake.

Interesting. But what kind of spell would she put all that power towards?

He waited for it eagerly. The best place to study powerful magic was on the receiving end of it. As long as you were strong enough to endure it, that was.

But the words that came from her mouth were a shock even to him.

She grinned. “Fireball Full Cannon!”

The fire condensed into a cannonball-sized, molten red sphere. It pulsed several times, as waves of thick mana formed rings around it and intensified the pressure behind it.

[Master!]

“But how?” Sen asked.

She took his response for fear, shock, even despair. And to be fair, it was an impressive display of power.

But the real reason for his surprise was that she was using a Maximizer he was very familiar with. It was the same Maximizer he’d created for Diablo after their duel outside the Kingdom of Magic. The man had challenged him shortly after his hundred-year nap. After trying to teach him Maxim and finding the man incompatible with it, he’d created a fire-based Maximizer. One he’d quite literally forgotten about until now.

He’d set conditions about passing it down. Diablo could only choose a single successor, and the power could not be used for evil. So long as that condition wasn’t broken, each successor of the Maximizer would be allowed to pass it down in turn. Could it be? Could this woman have inherited a power he’d granted to another over a thousand years ago?

[There’s no time for stray thoughts, Master! Her attack is incoming!]

She released the attack and sent a technique toward him that was so powerful it would’ve split Solomon’s Ceres into millions of pieces with ease. That was the difference between a prince or princess-class mage and a Lord.

But Sen was the creator of Full Cannon, and that gave him an option here.

One that would completely wipe the sheer confidence off his opponent’s face.

“Primordial Full Cannon,” he said with a glint in his eyes.

The miniature blazing sun dissolved into stray embers in the blink of an eye. One moment it was an attack capable of utter destruction, the next it presented no threat at all.

Sen stared at the woman. For the first time since their encounter, she was at a loss for words, her face completely racked with shock.

“Wait! What?” She looked around as if she expected her attack to reappear and continue its trajectory at any moment, but that didn’t happen. Instead, her eyes only grew wider, and her questions only grew more pressing as she realized he’d just dispelled what was likely one of her most powerful techniques.

Primordial. A spell representing absolute authority over another.

When a spell’s creator passed knowledge of it to another, they still retained authority over that spell to varying extents. When that spell was used in their presence, they could usurp, cancel, or otherwise exert their authority over it. This didn’t come into play often.

For one, the creators of the most common spells of today, like fireball, enhancements, and other more basic ones, were long dead. The kinds of spells taught at magic academies, or passed from teacher to student, were often created long ago. More proprietary techniques were often only passed down to trusted apprentices who swore oaths of loyalty or agreed to uphold certain conditions to inherit that power. Sen had enforced such conditions on Diablo to ensure that the power didn’t go to anyone evil in the future. Despite the fact that this woman had attacked him, by his own standards, or at least the ones he’d embedded within the spell, she wasn’t a bad person.

But she was using a spell of his creation, and because of that, he could cancel it.

Primordial wasn’t a perfect solution however. There were cases where he couldn’t simply use it to override techniques, even if he had contributed significantly to their creation and development. For one, Primordial couldn’t cancel or alter a spell that was still connected to the caster. In other words, enhancements, or spells that didn’t fully leave a caster’s control, would operate as normal. Also, it wouldn’t work against creations, spirits, or summons.

If that hadn’t been the case, he could’ve simply canceled Marrow’s Flesh Golem spells since he was the original creator of the spell. But a Maximizer like True Cannon powerfully enhanced a fire or projectile-based spell and then flung it as hard and fast as possible at an opponent. It was almost ideal for Primordial cancellation.

There were other conditions that might come into play in the future when he encountered similar situations. For instance, he couldn’t use Primordial if the spell had been heavily modified or altered by the caster. And in cases where someone had a tome or spell Sen hadn’t fully developed, but had merely started in his time, Primordial simply wouldn’t work.

Milim, for instance, her journal of dreams was formed from his mana and work, but she’d contributed to it far more than he had at this point. So he couldn’t possibly exert control over it. But the technique was still extremely powerful, and the fact he’d used it to completely erase a princess-class mage’s spell from existence like it was nothing was proof of that.

“Who are you?” she asked when she finally found the words.

“I was wondering the same thing,” Sen said. “How did you come to have that power?”

Normally, answering a question with a question might lead to some hostility. But in this case, she seemed almost proud to answer.

She grinned and thumbed toward herself. “That’s easy. I’m a descendant of the legendary King of Fire, Diablo! My name is Diabla! Crown Princess of Iscella!”

[You’ve got to be joking.]

Diablo and his descendants had in fact protected the power all this time. And now it stood in the hands of Diabla. A powerful princess-class mage, who for some reason wanted to meet with the Cult of the God of Magic. That was interesting, and he suddenly was a lot more interested in what this meeting with Dirke could be about.

[Master, others are approaching.]

Sen sensed it as well. He looked toward the group approaching on horseback. “Friends of yours?” he asked.

Diabla turned and sneered at the group. “Not friends exactly. I’d use the term…training dummies.” Her eyes suddenly lit up with an idea. “Want to play a game? Let’s see who kills the most?”

“Uh…first of all, who exactly are they?” he asked.

“They work for The Snake Charmer.” She sneered as she said it.

“And I should know who that is?” Sen asked.

Diabla sighed. “They’re assassins. They’re members of the Cult of Serpia.”

Sen’s eyes widened. What was going on today? Diablo’s descendant, and now Serpian Assassins?

Still, that was enough justification for him. It seemed he wouldn’t just be facing his past with Garlon. But he’d already known that from the moment he’d learned the truth about Serpia and Snake. No, he had a lot of work to do, and these men were a start. He’d been hoping to find a lead on Serpia’s current whereabouts. This might provide the perfect opportunity.

“You’re on, Diabla. By the way, my name’s Sen.”

Her eyes lit up in recognition. “That’s…you’re a descendant of the God of Magic, aren’t you?” she asked. So she recognized his name. It seemed Diablo had passed down more than just the spell. His successor recognized Sen’s name, but not the fact that he wasn’t merely a descendant, but the God of Magic himself.

“Sure, let’s go with that,” he said.

The two of them readied their magic.

Sen grinned. “Game on.”
