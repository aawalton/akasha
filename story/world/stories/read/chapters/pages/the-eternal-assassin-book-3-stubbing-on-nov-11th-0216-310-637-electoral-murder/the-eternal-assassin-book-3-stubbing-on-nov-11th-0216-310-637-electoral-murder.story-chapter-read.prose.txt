“The Lambit Republic,” Asher repeated, scratching his head as he thought back to what he knew about them. Admittedly, the answer was ‘not much.’ He’d Incorporated a few basic books about their continent that he could skim through with Comprehend to read up on the usual things like population density, geography, and overall culture, but that was about the extent of his knowledge. “So… I guess the big question is why do they want to kill me?”

“Fantastic question,” Donvath snorted. “What do you know about them?”

“Their country has a somewhat similar political structure to where I came from where everyone gets to vote on their leaders, unlike the monarchy of the Noala Kingdom. It’s broken into sixteen districts, each one of which has an elected representative, and then all of them vote to pick a president from among themselves,” Asher said, basically just reading from the book in his head. “They’re much larger than the Noala Kingdom, and they despise the Eight Crests for some reason. Enough to fight against them in the Eternal War for the last millennia or two.”

“To be fair, most countries are larger than our little slice of paradise," Donvath grinned. “They don’t like us very much either, on account of our entire kingdom sort of being founded by a few of their armies who were tired of fighting the Eternal War.”

“Why haven’t they ever tried to invade and take the land back?” Asher asked. “Honestly, that question’s been rolling around in my mind for ages now. You said it yourself, the Noala Kingdom is puny compared to them. Couldn’t they just roll right over it?”

“While the Lambit Republic is larger compared to us, they’re not really all that important on the grand scale,” Donvath explained. “Remember our discussion about greater powers? Despite the Lambit Republic's larger size, they don’t have a single greater power to their name, whereas my ancestors have always made very sure that the Noala Kingdom had anywhere between two or three at all times. Before you showed up, that was myself and Winged Death. Not to mention Gran happens to like us more than them, so she’d throw in with us if things really got that far. While the Lambit Republic has at least a few people in the Stage of Power, in a direct confrontation, it's highly likely we'd manage to take them down. Assuming we’re fighting defensively of course.”

“Wow. I mean, I’d understood the importance of greater powers like you said, but hearing that just a few people are all that stands between the entire Noala Kingdom being invaded or not…” Asher shook his head. “...Just seems a bit wild.”

“That’s life,” Donvath shrugged. “Anyway, that’s most likely why they tried to take you out with a Shadow Squad. They already know trying to assassinate me is pointless, because I’d either see them coming or just unwind time with my death. And they can certainly try to assassinate the Winged Death, though their most recent memory of her is of her using her Dragon element to wipe out an entire army all on her own that tried to cross through our territory to attack the Eight Crests, so they’re probably a little intimidated of what failing to kill her would drive her to do in retaliation. What do you think their first thought was when it was suddenly revealed that there was not only a new greater power that popped up out of nowhere, but one with a Spatial element of all things? They’re already intimidated by the fact that they can’t attack us. To suddenly hear that we’ve got an up-and-comer joining our ranks? The fabled Reaper whose name and deeds are already slowly sweeping across the continent? I’m not surprised they tried to take you out.”

“When you put it that way…” Asher couldn’t help but nod along with everything Donvath was saying. “Still, if greater powers are all so dangerous, and I’m apparently one of them now, why only send three people after me? Wouldn’t more have been smarter?”

“I’m willing to bet going after your head wasn’t exactly a sanctioned order,” Donvath snickered. “The Lambit Republic tries to put on a show of unity and agreement between their districts, but that’s about as far from the truth as it gets. In order to do anything ‘official’, they need ten of the sixteen districts to agree, and the president to sign off on it. You could set the entire kingdom on fire and I guarantee you that you still couldn’t get all of them to agree that putting it out was the right thing to do. My guess is some of the districts wanted to take you out while others disagreed for one reason or another, and in the end, a small handful of them pooled resources that wouldn’t be missed in order to send the attack under the table. Or they just thought that if they sent too many powerful people I’d see them coming, there’s no real way to know. Congratulations. The wonders of politics may have very well saved your life.”

“Alright… While I appreciate knowing who was responsible for trying to kill me, does this actually matter in any way? I mean, I’m not about to run over there and start killing all their leaders in the hopes I get to the one who signed off on the order. Not to mention we’re sort of in the middle of taking down the Council of Death right now. I don’t have time for any side project of that magnitude.”

“I’m in agreement that you probably shouldn’t do anything just yet,” Donvath nodded. “The main reason I wanted to tell you all this was as a warning. Another lich or two and we’ll be ready to reveal to the rest of the continent what we’ve been up to, ideally forming a unified coalition to take down the remainder. When that happens, we’ll be working closely with some pretty powerful figures from a number of different countries. Just because they don’t have any greater powers, that doesn’t mean the Lambit Republic is devoid of strong warriors. You might end up working closely with them on a lich hunt, and the last thing we need is for them to try to stab you in the back.”

“Fair enough.” Asher sighed as he rubbed at his eyes, feeling incredibly tired all of a sudden. Having people out for his head wasn’t a new feeling by any means, seeing as he’d had both the Council of Death and the Assassin’s Guild after him since practically day one in this world. But politicians? People he doubted had seen a single day of combat in their entire lives who had suddenly decided he needed to go on a whim, rather than any sort of real personal interest? Something about it just weighed more heavily on him than the others, not to mention it rubbed him the wrong way.

“...Maybe I can devote a day or two to this after all. I mean, Moxy needs some time to heal up, and Samantha could probably use a little rest and relaxation after what she’s gone through. I don’t suppose there’s any chance you can tell me which district the Shadow Squad came from?”

“Unfortunately, I can’t,” Donvath admitted, giving him a cautious look. “Figuring out they came from the Lambit Republic at all was tricky enough. I didn’t think I was going to have to make this request, but please don’t run over there and start killing people at random. It’s one thing if you can figure out which representatives are responsible, but you can’t rely on Judgement for something like this. I’d bet just about all of them are as dirty as they come. And before you go getting all high and mighty on me about ‘anyone with a bad aura deserves to die’, take a moment and think about the optics such an action would entail. If The Reaper starts wiping out leaders of countries, how do you think the rest of the continent is going to react? You won’t just be dealing with an underequipped Shadow Squad, you’ll be taking on greater powers sent with the explicit purpose to kill you. You think you can take out Groln in a fight? Or multiple people like him? He’s not the Hojon Empire’s only greater power, for the record. Unlike us, they have nearly a dozen.”

“I see your point,” Asher said, shivering at the thought of taking on the huge, royal slayer who had come to give him an offer of citizenship from his queen. Groln had a Demon element of all things, and Asher didn’t have a doubt in his mind the man knew how to use it. “Okay, I’ll hold off for the moment. In exchange, I want one of those training artifacts your truthseekers use. Bordan lent me a ring a while back that let us utilize the Honest Ears skill. If I’m going to be dealing with people who might very well be after my life, I want to be able to tell if they’re lying. I think that’s a fair compromise.”

“I think I can do that,” Donvath muttered, his eyes going distant as he stared up at the ceiling for a moment. “It’s tangentially related, but it wouldn’t directly be because of the Council of Death… And it’s not like knowing if a lich was lying to you would matter… Alright, futures look safe enough. You guys have your next visit with Brian coming up, I’ll have one of my truthseekers give him the ring to give you.”

“Thank you,” Asher said, yawning as he turned to leave before pausing with his hand on the doorknob. “Oh, I almost forgot. It slipped my mind what with all the utter insanity my life has been these past few months, but I spoke with Rosh just the other day. Why did you warn me to tell him to keep such a weirdly specific number of shards on him in that letter you had Bordan give me all those months back? He wanted to know if it was safe to deposit them in the bank or not.”

“What do you mean?” Donvath asked, sitting up in his chair as he gave Asher a weird look he couldn’t quite place. “I remember the letter you’re talking about. I went on a whole rant about how I was going to charge you for makeup because of the bags you were giving me under my eyes. I don’t recall mentioning your friend in there.”

“Yeah, you did. You scribbled a little ‘p.s.’ at the bottom with the warning that Rosh should keep eighteen thousand, three hundred and forty-two shards on his person at all times,” Asher said, frowning as Donvath’s entire body seemed to go rigid. “Are you okay?”

“Yeah… Yeah, I’m fine,” Donvath said, taking a long, deep breath before letting it out slowly and giving him a tired smile. All of a sudden the annoying, teasing man before him turned back into the High Prince. A man who was forced to shoulder the lives and security of all his people without rest. “Sorry, I guess my last few months have been pretty hectic as well. I don’t actually remember why I made that warning in the first place. He’s probably fine to deposit them.”

“...Fair enough. I’ll let him know,” Asher said, hesitating for a moment. “...Are you alright, Donvath? You look… tired. More so than usual.”

“You’ve got more important things to worry about than me,” Donvath chuckled, going back to his paperwork as he waved Asher away with his quill. “Take a few days to rest up and recover before you go after your next lich. You’ve earned that much, and Samantha could really use the break.”

Asher couldn’t shake the feeling that Donvath was trying to make light of a more serious situation, but he didn’t know how to go about trying to wrangle details out of him. As annoying as he could be, Donvath was the High Prince of the entire kingdom and could summon a small army of royal guards to his side in an instant. Hell, if he pushed too hard, there was every chance Donvath could use Timeline to simply undo the last minute or two and prevent Asher from even realizing anything was wrong in the first place. Deciding he’d rather keep his memories and figure out how to broach the subject later, Asher nodded, bidding Donvath good night as he left his office. Something was going on with the High Prince…

And one way or another, Asher was going to get to the bottom of it.
