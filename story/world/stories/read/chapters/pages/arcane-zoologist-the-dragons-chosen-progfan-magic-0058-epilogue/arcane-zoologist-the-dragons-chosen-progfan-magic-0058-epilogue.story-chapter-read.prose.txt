Despite feeling incredibly tired, Callie forced herself to her feet. If their family was leaving, then there were things she had to do before then. Lots of things actually, but she knew she couldn't manage them all, so she started with the most important one.

Callie visited Granny Ethel. Coming inside the healer's hut, she saw Callen laying in one of the beds, sleeping, while Granny Ethel was settling a blanket over him. The elderly woman lifted a finger to her lips when Callie, indicating her to be quiet.

They headed to the medicine room to talk there without disturbing Callen. He needed all the rest he could get before the journey. Callie's stomach churned just thinking about it. Leaving their village. In the middle of winter nonetheless. It was madness, even though she understood it.

But they didn't have a choice.

The moment the door was closed, Callie couldn't help but start sniffling as tears filled her eyes. She wouldn't be able to see Granny Ethel anymore.

"There, there, child, there's no need to cry," Granny Ethel said, gently patting her head.

Callie wrapped herself around the elderly healer. "I'm sorry, Granny Ethel. I won't be able to be your apprentice anymore. Because I wasn't careful enough with my magic, my whole family will be forced to flee. I don't know if we can even survive on the road in this weather. It's all my fault!"

Granny Ethel rubbed her back consolingly. "It's not your fault. You didn't make the world this way. Your gift is a wonderful thing. Nobody could have predicted that the merchant would turn out to be such a snake."

"M-Maybe it would have been better if I let him take me. Callen wouldn't be hurt, my family—"

The woman grasped Callie firmly by the shoulders and looked her in the eyes. "No, Callie! Don't you ever say that. Your family would never be happy if you were taken away. Do you really think they would continue living here peacefully, knowing that you've been kidnapped?"

Callie shook her head. She knew Mom, Dad and Callen would never give up on her.

"Exactly. Yes, the situation you're in is horrible. But even so you have to stick together with your family."

Callie nodded. "I'm going to miss you, Granny Ethel."

"Oh… I'll miss you too. I'll pray for your safety every day!" The elderly woman said, and they hugged, remaining together for a good while.

It was hard to separate from Granny Ethel, and even harder to eventually wave her goodbye. But Callie still had a few things to do. First, she had to visit the neighbors. Specifically Tristan.

Arriving at his house, it was auntie Mara that opened the door, peering down at Callie.

"Is Tristan at home?" She asked.

"Sure is. Did that boy do something again?" Auntie Mara narrowed her gaze.

"No, it's nothing like that. I just needed to talk to him."

The woman looked skeptical, but nodded, calling for the boy. He appeared quickly, looking annoyed.

"What is it, carrothead? Hey, have you been crying?" Tristan panicked.

"No! Maybe a little… I need to talk to you," she said, and leaned in to whisper. "Alone."

The boy flushed, awkwardly rubbing the back of his neck. "We can talk in the barn, I suppose."

They headed into the barn, where Tristan crossed his arms over his chest, muttering grumpilly. "What do you want, carrothead?"

"My whole family and I are leaving the village tomorrow," Callie said.

Tristan blinked, his arms falling to his sides. "Wh-What? That's not a funny joke!"

"It's not a joke. We really are leaving."

"Wh-Why?! It's the middle of the winter. You will freeze to death on the road!" Tristan shouted.

"Shh! Not so loud!" Callie chastised, then sighed. "Silas found out that I have magic. He tried to kidnap me, but Callen got me back. However, Silas escaped, and undoubtedly will return soon with dragonbloods. We have no choice but to flee."

Tristan gaped at her in disbelief. "Y-You can't just leave…"

"Our family is leaving tomorrow morning, but I wanted you to do something for me," Callie continued, despite Tristan shaking his head.

"No, no…"

"When the spring comes, can you—"

"NO!" He suddenly screamed. Only then did Callie noticed that his eyes were filled with tears. "You can't just leave, Callie!"

She was surprised. This was the first time Tristan had actually called her by her nickname, rather than some insult. Combined with tears streaming down his cheeks, Callie wasn't sure what to do or how to react.

"I… I don't want to leave. But dragonbloods will want to take me away because of my magic. We have to leave…" Callie muttered.

"Th-That's not fair! Things were finally starting to get good!" Tristan muttered, and suddenly pulled Callie into a hug, crying.

She cried too. They both cried for a good while, frustrated with this situation. By the unfairness of it all. But eventually they managed to calm down, pulling away from each other.

"Your eyes are red…" Callie said, laughing at Tristan.

"I don't want to hear that from you. You look horrible!" Tristan retorted.

Callie sighed, then looked at him. "When the spring comes, can you please find Wiggles and tell him what happened to me? He'll be looking for me. Maybe you could play him sometimes. He gets lonely."

Tristan nodded. "I'll tell him, and I'll play him too. That mireling is cool. I loved that time when he melted everyone's shoes with his acid."

Callie laughed. "Don't let him do that! He'll get in trouble."

"It's fine. I'll just tell the chief it was me. Nothing will happen to Wiggles."

Callie nodded, and for a moment there was a heavy silence. Neither of them knew what to say. How to properly say goodbye.

"Let's make a promise!" Tristan announced.

"A promise?"

"Yeah! We'll meet again one day. When we're adults. I'll study with Granny Ethel and learn more about the arcane beasts that live around here. I'll tell you everything I have learned about them when we meet again."

Callie smiled at the boy. "I'm going to learn everything I can about arcane beasts too. I'll grow my magic, and learn how to control it. Once I'm strong enough, we'll meet again."

"We must definitely meet again!" Tristan said firmly, extending his pinky fingers.

"We will meet again!" Callie nodded, locking her pinky with his. This promise was something that would be buried deep in her heart. Something she wouldn't forget no matter where she went.

Because this was a promise that she made in a place where she grew up with a boy, who while initially a bully, had become her closest friend.

With that conviction, Callie headed to the last place she needed to visit. While meeting Wiggles would be impossible. There was somebody who she had to say goodbye to.

Snowglow and her colony. She headed to the dragon's cave. It was some distance away and it was difficult for Callie to get there with the thick layer of snow. But she felt that if she didn't do this, she would regret it for the rest of her life.

Going into the cave, everything seemed calm, without a single glimmerhare in sight. But she knew they were there.

"Snowglow, Snowglow!" Callie called.

It took a while for the glimmerhare to appear, but eventually she did.

Callie friend. Something wrong?

It appeared that the arcane beast could instantly sense that Callie wasn't feeling well. She crouched down and patted Snowglow's soft fur.

"I'm afraid I'm going to have to leave. I came to say goodbye," Callie said.

Leave? Why?

"A bad person found out about my magic. The same one who had placed that trap in the forest. My family and I have to run away to remain safe," Callie explained.

There was a moment of silence as Snowglow processed this information. Then she hopped closer to Callie and cuddled up to her.

Miss Callie.

Callie crouched down and gently embraced the glimmerhare. "I'm going to miss you too, Snowglow. Will you and your family be alright?"

We good. Callie give carrot. We eat.

That was a relief. Callie had been worried if the bushel they to the glimmerhares would be enough, but it appeared they would be fine. That's all that matters.

"Be careful, Snowglow. Bad people will come to the village soon. It's best if you and your family stay away from the village. Warn other arcane beasts too if you can."

I tell the guardian. He will protect others.

Callie nodded. The drownhart was intelligent. He would know what to do. Not to mention that the forest still had another guardian observing everything quietly.

As Callie said goodbye to Snowglow, she couldn't help but wonder about the dragon. She never did see it again. Rolling up her sleeve, Callie saw the five petals of her magical mark. In the end, she didn't manage to complete it.

Perhaps it's for the best. Her magic was already difficult to control, and they would undoubtedly have to hide a lot if they wanted to avoid detection from dragonbloods.

But it also made Callie feel melancholic. She had this magic gifted to her by the dragon, but in the end, she never really had a chance to use it properly. Not for the good of arcane beasts at least. She had saved herself, her family and even the village with it, but Callie couldn't help but feel that it wasn't its true purpose.

"I'm sorry, dragon. I don't think you chose the right person for this magic…" Callie muttered regretfully.

I did not make a mistake.

The voice came so suddenly that Callie yelped. It was the same voice she had heard in the swamp. The one that had warned her to leave. In retrospect, the voice had likely warned her to stop searching for the lantern mushrooms. Maybe it had known that it would be used to lure the drownhart into a trap.

"Who is there?" Callie asked nervously, looking around the forest. It didn't seem like there was anyone there. Surely if there was a huge dragon nearby, she would have noticed it.

Suddenly, right in front of her, a green, scaled head appeared, followed by the neck, body and limbs. It was as if the dragon had appeared out of thin air. Callie fell back into the snow.

"Y-You're the dragon!" Callie managed to say, gazing at the creature in awe.

This was the first time she could see its whole body in proper light. It was larger than Callie thought, its head reaching almost up to treetops once it stretched out its neck. But the dragon wasn't bulky. It was merely very long, with slender body and limbs, with two long wings pressed firmly to its body.

You are leaving the village are you not?

"H-How do you know?" Callie stammered.

The dragon didn't answer. Instead it lowered its head to be on the same level as Callie.

Protect your family, my young one, and keep growing your power. I cannot follow you where you go, but should you show your mark, others such as myself will help you.

"Others? There are more dragons like you?"

The dragon nodded.

Find them. They will help you.

With that the dragon, lifted its head, and its body began to disappear again. No, not disappear. Rather its scales where blending in to match the surroundings.

"Wait, don't leave! I have so many questions! Why did you give me magic? What am I supposed to do with it? I don't understand anything."

Seek out the blessed. Avoid the bloodcursed.

"The blessed? Who are the blessed?" Callie asked.

The dragon had completely disappeared from sight. Callie called out to it multiple times, but it didn't answer anymore.

"Ugh… Now I feel even more confused than before!" Callie muttered, looking down at her mark.

But there was something crucial she learned. There were other dragons out there. It wasn't like Callie's dragon was the only one left. There were more.

While Callie didn't understand why the dragon wanted her to seek out other dragons, and these "blessed", she knew that she would be foolish to ignore its words. After a while, she finally said, "Thank you, dragon! I hope we meet again one day!"

A deep rumble resounded in reply, echoing through the forest.

While Callie and her family wouldn't be able to remain in their home, they would remain together. So long as that was true, they could face anything. As heavy as the burden of magic was on Callie's shoulders, she didn't regret it.

The dragon hadn't regretted it, so she would believe in it and in herself. She would continue forward and do everything in her power to save as many arcane beasts as she could.
