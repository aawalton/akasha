The rain had started an hour before we reached the inn, and it had not stopped since.

We had barely made it. The inn sat at the edge of a cluster of buildings that was too large to be called a village and too small to be called a town. The Inker had said this place was half a day's ride from Koralis, our destination. It was close enough that the road widened and other travelers appeared far more frequently.

Most of the silver soldiers were outside in the rain, or crammed into a second, lesser establishment down the road. I had glared at the Inker, and he had secured rooms at this inn for himself and for us. One room for him. One room for us. It had not been long since we'd arrived.

I sat cross-legged on the floor of our room, drawing mana through the Requiem. It was a strange thing, to draw mana from a specific part that wasn't one's core. The difference was rather obvious. When I drew mana through my core, it came in thin, the way it always had in this world. When I drew it through the mark instead, the mana arrived a tad slower, but it was heavier.

And there was a flavor to it. A…heaviness that had nothing to do with weight at all. Perhaps this was what 'endings' tasted like. Drawing mana through the Cradle made power that was just as heavy, but felt light. I preferred that. So this was Essence then. Requiem Essence and Cradle Essence, I would call them.

The Inker had been rather confident that this part would take weeks at best. It had taken perhaps forty minutes. I could see why it might take someone else weeks. Naturally, after that, I tried to form an Essence Core.

The attempt had failed. The Inker had not explained, and the best theory I had was to force Essence into my Mana Core and see what happened. All that did was make the Essence leak out uselessly, just as the man had said. Still, one could technically use this Essence without storing it, simply by using it as it came. There was even a way to store it, by circulating it through the body, everywhere but the Mana Core. I would admit there was little point to this, save for being petty.

I held out my right hand and called Ruin. Dark flame bloomed in my palm. The flame formed a ball of flickering darkness in my palm, larger than my head. The cost was a tiny fraction of what it had been. I held it there, turning it, watching the black fire curl and dance. It had taken around thirty seconds to gather this much Essence, so it was not practical at all without an Essence Core. Still, I was proud. Now this was the kind of power a Demon Queen should command.

The door opened. Ash stepped in. She stopped and stared at the ball of dark fire hovering above my hand. "What are you doing?"

"Harnessing the power of Essence," I said, letting the smirk show. "Or the beginning of it, at least. You may watch." I let the flame swell, then compressed it. The darkness deepened. "It barely costs a thing compared to before. Would you like to practice as well?"

Ash sighed. She stepped forward, took hold of my left arm, and pulled me to my feet. I was so surprised my concentration broke, and the dark flame broke with it. "There is an entire inn downstairs," she said, already steering me toward the door. "Come on, you didn't even look when we came in. There are some fun things. You are not sitting alone here and playing with black fire."

"I was not playing. I was cultivating," I said sharply, though I did not pull my arm away. I could have.

"You were playing."

I sighed and grabbed the satchel on the way out. The egg was warm through the cloth. I checked it, adjusted the strap over my shoulder, and followed the Hero downstairs. It would not be wrong to humor my retainer, would it?

The common room was almost uncomfortably loud. Rain drummed against the windows. A large fire crackled in a stone hearth at the far end, casting orange light across a room packed with bodies. There were many smells, and quite a few of them were unpleasant. I had been in many places, and had seen more still. None of them had been quite like this.

It was the noise I found strangest. Battle was loud but it had direction and purpose. This was just…sound screaming over other sounds. There was laughter overlapped with arguing, which overlapped with the clatter of dishes. All of those overlapped with five more things my ears did not pick out. All of these sounds were competing to see which could be the loudest.

Three silver soldiers from our escort occupied a corner of the room, looking rather miserable. Across the room, the Inker sat at a table surrounded by what appeared to be local farmers. His hands moved through the air as he regaled them with some tale. I caught fragments of it, as he also competed to be the loudest in this place, and was succeeding more than most, "…surrounded on all sides, you understand, and I alone stood between the beast and-"

I stopped listening. Ash led me to a table near the wall. I set the satchel on the small chair beside me. My hand rested on it for a moment before I turned to look at the room.

The room was so full of bodies it was a wonder there had been an empty table at all. At one sat seven men throwing cards between each other, loudly laughing and pointing at something only they could understand. Most of the tables had a small group of men and women, all idly conversing.

Three men sat at a table to my right. One of them was staring at me. He was young, with dark hair and a face unremarkable even for a human. His face reddened and he looked away when I met his gaze.

Ash ordered wine when a serving girl passed by. I wanted to return to my room and practice more. I was sure I could get better with more- "Lys, the Inker is paying for everything. Are you really going to make sure he doesn't have to?" Ash asked, staring at me. That did make the prospect of whatever this was more appealing.

Soon, a large cup was set before me. The liquid inside was a dark red, almost black in the firelight. I lifted it and sniffed. It smelled rather strange. I took a large sip and it burned my throat. I coughed, sputtered, and set the cup down hard enough that it sloshed. Some of the liquid had spilled down down my dress. I glared at Ash. She was grinning, and now she laughed. She laughed, and my irritation died somewhere between the sound and the warmth it left behind.

Reluctantly, I took another sip. It was less terrible. A third and the warmth spread through my chest and settled there. Only some of it was the drink. Near the hearth, a broad woman was singing a ballad. The man in Hamel had not been good. I did not know what good sounded like for humans, but I thought it might sound like this. I listened for a few moments before I actually focused on the lyrics themselves.

She was singing of the Great Demon War, of the heroic deeds of the Band of the Golden Dawn. I snorted.

"What?" Ash glanced at me.

"That band was anything but heroic," I said. "They were usually the third to run. Not the first or the second, mind you. Was that what made them heroic?"

Ash sighed. "They were skilled."

"At running? They certainly were. Even I found it hard to catch them."

That got a smirk from Ash. She looked around, then leaned slightly closer over the table. "They tried to recruit me once, actually." Ash took a sip of her own wine. She seemed far more practiced at it than I was. "Wanted me to be their leader."

I turned to her, genuinely curious. "And? What happened?"

Ash set her cup down. "I may have shoved their vice leader into a well when they were being too pushy. Well, to anyone watching, it probably looked like quite the accident."

I stared at her. "You are joking."

Ash sighed. "No. It was a deep well, too."

I laughed before I meant to, and it was a small thing that wouldn't stop. Ash grinned at me, and I supposed I could allow myself the lapse, just this once. For a moment, I did not mind wasting my time. For a moment, I didn't consider it wasting time at all.

We began to trade stories. I had not meant to share, but she pried the words out of me. I told her about Zarvok's attempt to tame a Hellstag, a rather formidable beast. It had ended with my General lodged headfirst in a crevasse while his sister laughed so hard she collapsed. Ash told me about a quest her party had taken to clear a cave of slimes, only to discover that the quest giver had used that to make them clear a den of Cave Trolls for cheap instead.

My gaze drifted during the rare lull in our conversation. A different one of the three men was staring now. I frowned slightly, then Ash said something else, and I turned back.

I reached for the satchel and adjusted it. Once.

Bren was on his fifth drink. That was three too many. Three more than he could afford, not that the innkeeper knew that.

The world was spinning around him. He looked across the common room and saw everyone and everything smearing. Even the noises were blurring into one another. That almost made them pleasant.

All the drink was almost enough. Almost enough to forget the twenty silver he owed for the boarding house. Almost enough to forget the way his boots were coming apart with every step. Almost enough to forget Mira's face the last time he'd come home poorer than when he'd left.

He was a D-rank adventurer with two marks. One at Spark, and one at Line. Two marks were a blessing among blessings, if only either one had ever been worth a damn. One gave him enhanced speed that barely let him do more than the occasional pickpocket, and the other mark was an appraisal mark that could let him see and tell him exactly how valuable the thing killing him was. Everyone said you never know what your marks can really do, but Bren was pretty sure the Divine had used him as the butt of a joke.

Every party he'd applied to wanted more out of their members. They wanted people who could take a hit or deal one. Nobody wanted the man who could tell you what a rock was worth or maybe point out where a crystal was buried.

His friends were no help to his souring mood. Garrett had been staring at the half-demon since they'd walked in, and he'd gotten louder with every drink. Pol wasn't far behind. "I'm going over there," Garrett announced, swaying slightly in his seat.

"Which one?" Pol asked. "The blonde?"

Garrett shook his head, grinning. "The half-demon."

Pol choked on his drink. "Are you mad? Did you not see those jutting horns?"

Garrett laughed. "I've seen her jutting everything else, and that's fine enough by me. How the hell do they even stay upright like that, you think?"

Pol raised his cup to that. "I've heard those half-demon women can crack a man's ribs just with their legs. Bet they know they need a good man to-"

"The horns make for good handles, I'd wager," Garrett said, and the two of them laughed. They must have laughed too loud, because the people in the nearest tables all leaned away.

Bren laughed along, as any friend was supposed to. But he was damned sure Garrett hadn't so much as held a woman's hand without sweating like a pig. He was even surer he'd never done nothing beyond that neither. The one time a girl at the Koralis market had smiled in his general direction, the man had walked into a cart. As for Pol…well, Bren liked to think the only woman who had ever called him handsome was his own mother. And his mother was a liar and a thief, so that made sense.

Then his appraisal mark tingled. It happened sometimes. His mark always tingled when there was something worth a damn nearby. It was a damned annoying tingle, one he was used to ignoring, especially in cities. There, just about everyone and their ma had better stuff than he did. He didn't expect to feel it here, and he didn't expect the tingle to be so…strong. His entire arm had almost jolted from it. He followed where his mark pointed, and his mark was pointing at…right at the half-demon. Or rather, at the satchel next to her.

Bren frowned and channeled a whisper of Essence towards the real power of his mark, as meager as it was. The bag became transparent to his sight -cloth and stitching peeling away until only the contents remained. Inside, nestled in white cloth, was a beast egg. A strange-looking one at that. He'd never seen one with stripes like that before.

Even a common beast egg fetched more than he'd make in three months. Something like this? Bren didn't know what it was worth, only that it had to be worth a hell of a lot. Maybe more than he'd made in his entire bloody career. Bren's hand tightened on his cup. With money like that, he could pay off the house. He could take Mira somewhere decent. He could drink something that wasn't cheap swill. Hell, maybe he could buy gear and claw his way over to C-rank. Start a new life.

Garrett was already standing, announcing his intentions with a drunken slur. Pol was egging him on. Bren watched his friend lurch toward the half-demon's table and began thinking.

I was telling Ash about the time one of Zera's pets had escaped into the upper halls of my fortress. The beast, the [Dragdar], had terrorised three floors of my fortress. I was about to tell her of the men I had sent to recover it, when a man arrived at our table.

The man stopped two paces short of us and said nothing for a few seconds, and his mouth hung open for the entirety of those few seconds. "Your-" he started. He stared at my face. The color that rose in his cheeks was rather alarming. Some kind of mark ability? "You have very- your eyes are-" He stopped, swallowed, and tried again. "I just wanted to say…that your eyes…are."

The man's sentence did not finish, no matter how long I waited. And I waited long. He just stood there with one hand raised. If the gesture was supposed to mean something, it did not convey it well. "My eyes are," I confirmed with the barest of nods.

"Red- They're really red," he finally managed. And even the few words he did manage all tripped over each other.

Was the man a fool? "They are. That is their color. It is a common trait among those with demonic blood. It's good that your sight does not fail you," I said.

The man's face reddened even more, until he looked like one of the tomatoes Martha had shown me. It was one of the things she had used in her stews, one that had made the whole thing taste sharp.

The man glanced back at the table he'd come from. His round friend was making frantic gestures in the air, and the motion of them made his jutting belly wobble. If there was some deeper meaning to those gestures, it was entirely lost on me. The man turned back to me and swallowed. When he spoke next, his words still stumbled. "I also- I uh-" He pointed vaguely at my horns. His entire arm trembled. "Those are very- They're really- You have them."

"I do have them," I said. "Your powers of sight are truly a marvel."

The man let out some kind of strange choking sound. If that had been an attempt at a word, then it was no word I had ever heard. Ash had gone very still beside me, and I could feel a tremor running through her that surely came not from fear.

The man straightened, and seemed to gather himself for something. He took a deep breath. "Would you- Could I maybe- do you want to- I mean-" His mouth hung open. He had just said a sentence without saying anything at all. Even my most foolish of Generals could not have managed that.

"You seem unwell," I said. "Perhaps you should sit down."

"Right!" He sat. He sat immediately, pulling out the chair across from me with such force it nearly toppled. That was not what I had meant. I glanced at Ash. Her face was hidden by her hand, and her shoulders were shaking. "Sitting…yes! That's- I'm doing that."

We stared at each other. I took a slow sip of the wine and the man watched me all the while. His eyes only widened, and he stared as if I had just done the most extraordinary thing he had ever witnessed. All of my acts were extraordinary, so perhaps he was not entirely a fool.

The man's round friend appeared at our table then. He had been round from a distance, but he was practically rotund up close. He clapped a heavy hand on his companion's shoulder. When he spoke, his voice was only slightly steadier than his companion's had been. "Sorry about him. He's just- we both- we just couldn't help but notice-" He looked at me, then at Ash. He stared at Ash for a very long time. "Y-you're both ve-very- b-beautiful." It seemed he had lost the powers of speech just as his friend had.

"We are. It seems your sight works as well as your companion's," I said.

Ash made a strangled sound beside me. When I glanced at her, her lips were pressed together so tightly they had gone white. Her shoulders were shaking and her eyes shone.

The round man was still talking, or trying to at any rate. "Are you two- I mean- together? Like, together?"

"We are sitting together, yes," I said. "That is apparent."

"No, I mean-" He made a gesture with his hands that conveyed nothing to me whatsoever. His friend put his face in his hands.

Ash took a very slow, measured breath. When she spoke, her voice sounded strained. Her whole face was straining under the effort of something. Of what, I could not guess. "You should go back to your table," she said, looking between the two men. "Both of you." Her voice was gentle, almost kind. It sounded more strained now than when it had begun.

The first man lurched up. His chair scraped against the floor and he almost tipped over before he righted himself. "Right. Yes. Sorry. You're- sorry. Sorry." He backed away quickly, almost hitting another table in his retreat. His round friend followed, nearly tripping over the first man's feet.

They retreated to their table, almost tripping over each other. I watched them go. It would have been amusing if they had tripped over each other. Someone bumped our table in the retreat. I felt a flicker of...something. There and gone.

I looked back at Ash. She had both hands pressed over her face and was laughing through them, though she seemed to be trying very hard to disguise it as a cough. It seemed her strange composure had been a truly fragile thing after all.

"What was that about, Hero? What did they want?" I demanded.

Ash removed her hands. Her cheeks were red, and in the light I saw that her eyes were wet. "They-" She pressed her lips together again. They trembled. "They found you attractive…and they were trying to express it. Poorly, but by the Gods did they try." Ash's lips pressed together again, and she shook her head.

"They were…expressing it?"

"Gods, yes. And in the only way men know how when there's no blood going to their brains. I'm pretty sure their blood was somewhere else entirely."

I considered this for a moment. There was only one thing the Hero could have meant, though it was a strange thing to believe. "You are saying they wished to court me?" If she was right, then these humans of this time really were an audacious lot. No human had ever dared try to court me before. Perhaps I should have shown them Ruin.

"That- that is a very generous word for what they wished to do," Ash said, and she sounded like she was in physical pain.

"Then what-"

"Lys. Please…I am begging you to stop asking."

Ash's face was red. Almost as red as that strange man's had been. It was so stricken that I almost pressed further simply to see just how deep that red could go. I chose to be generous and not press, at least for now. These humans and their incomprehensible rituals were a subject I would need to research more thoroughly. I would ask her again, soon.

Minutes passed. The rain hadn't let up. The fire burned lower now, and the crowd had thinned. We sat a while longer in the kind of quiet that had become comfortable between us. I drank the rest of my wine, which I decided was merely adequate. At least the infernal drink no longer made me cough.

Eventually, I rose to leave. I lifted the satchel and reached inside to check the egg, as I always did. The cloth was there. Folded neatly, exactly as I had arranged it. The egg was not.
