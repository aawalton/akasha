“I am Ji Ning,” Ning said calmly. “I’m here to exterminate the Red-Eyed Spirit Rabbits that your family posted as a mission to the sect.”

As he spoke, Ning glanced once more at the wooden nameplate hanging above the gate.

Mi Family.

There was no mistake. This was definitely the right place.

So what was the confusion?

The middle-aged man’s expression turned awkward.

“I see,” he said after a brief hesitation. “The problem is... there’s already an outer-sect disciple staying with our family.”

“...What?”

Ning froze.

After painstakingly choosing this mission, after all the work Senior Sister Si Sihua had put into filtering and registering it for him, someone had beaten him to it?

Seeing Ning’s expression slowly darken, the man hurriedly added, “There must be some misunderstanding.”

Ning took a breath and forced himself to remain calm.

“When did this other disciple arrive?”

“Two days ago.”

“Two days ago?” Ning frowned. “The mission was only posted four days ago. I registered it at the sect and spent most of the remaining time traveling here.”

Something clearly didn’t add up.

If this other disciple had accepted the mission normally, he would have needed to register it at the Mission Hall first. There was no way he could have arrived here two days before Ning.

The middle-aged man seemed to realize the same thing.

“Yes... yes, you’re right.” His expression became increasingly ugly before he clasped his hands toward Ning. “I am Mi Zheng, head of the Mi Family. There seems to have been a serious misunderstanding.”

He turned toward one of the nearby guards.

“Go get Shuxin. Now.”

The guard immediately hurried away.

Mi Zheng turned back and gave Ning a small bow. “Please forgive us. I’ll get to the bottom of this.”

Ning smiled faintly and waved it off. “It’s fine. I don’t mind.”

Internally, however, his thoughts were already moving in an entirely different direction.

If the Mi Family ended up owing him a favor because of this little incident, obtaining those Pure Qi Sutra annotations might become considerably easier.

Ning decided to call this purposeful efficiency.

It sounded much better that way.

A short while later, hurried footsteps came from deeper within the residence. A young man in his early twenties soon entered the courtyard. His cultivation was only at the Second Stage of Qi Condensation, and the moment he noticed Ning standing beside his father, his eyes began wandering everywhere except toward them.

One look was enough.

Ning already knew this was the culprit.

“Shuxin.” Mi Zheng’s voice had gone cold. “Explain what happened.”

Mi Shuxin shifted uncomfortably under his father’s stare before finally giving in.

“I... I met an outer-sect disciple at the tavern,” he began. “He caused some trouble there, and the place ended up closing for two days. When I learned he was also willing to deal with the rabbits, I thought it would be easier to bring him here.”

Mi Zheng’s expression darkened further.

Mi Shuxin hurried on. “He didn’t even want spirit stones. He only asked for the Pure Qi Sutra annotations. If he dealt with the rabbits, we could withdraw the sect mission afterward and save the reward.”

The courtyard fell silent.

Ning stared at him.

That was certainly... creative.

Mi Zheng, meanwhile, looked like he was having difficulty deciding whether to shout or simply kill his son on the spot.

Mi Shuxin apparently mistook the silence for permission to continue.

“And even if there was some trouble afterward, Uncle Mi Shing is still backing our family, so I thought—”

“You thought?”

Mi Zheng’s voice rose sharply.

Mi Shuxin flinched.

“You posted a mission through the sect, secretly brought in another disciple to complete it, and planned to withdraw the mission afterward so you wouldn’t have to pay the reward?”

“I only wanted to save—”

“And you dared drag your uncle’s name into this?”

Mi Shuxin’s face flushed. “They’re only outer-sect disciples! With Uncle Mi Shing behind us, what could they even do?”

That was the last straw.

For some reason, Ning heard a distinct Fah! in his head as Mi Zheng stepped forward and punched his son straight to the ground.

The dull impact echoed through the courtyard.

Mi Shuxin folded around the blow and landed heavily, clutching his stomach.

“I’ll deal with you later,” Mi Zheng said coldly.

Then he turned back toward Ning and bowed much more deeply than before.

“I sincerely apologize for the trouble caused by my rebellious son.”

Mi Zheng had risen from poverty himself. Unlike his son, he knew exactly how foolish it was to casually offend cultivators.

An inner-sect disciple backing the family certainly gave them status, but that didn’t make them untouchable. Every outer-sect disciple had at least some chance of rising higher in the future, and turning two sect disciples into enemies just to save a few spirit stones was stupidity of the highest order.

Mi Zheng glanced down at his son and suppressed a sigh.

Unfortunately, Shuxin really did take after his uncle in all the worst ways.

Mi Shing was talented, but arrogance had always been one of his greatest problems. Even after entering the inner sect, he had made few genuine friends there. Now that he had left on a long-term mission, the Mi Family had been forced to rely on the sect’s Mission Hall when the rabbit problem grew beyond what they could handle themselves.

And his idiot nephew had somehow managed to make even that complicated.

Before Mi Zheng could say anything else, footsteps came from inside the residence.

A young man stepped into the courtyard wearing the robes of an outer-sect disciple.

The moment Ning saw his face, his expression changed.

Mi Zheng noticed immediately as his heart sank.

“Is... something wrong?”

Ning stared at the newcomer for several seconds.

“...There might be.”

After all, he couldn’t very well point at the man and say, You’re housing a jinx.

But the moment Mi Shuxin had mentioned an outer-sect disciple getting involved in trouble serious enough to shut down a tavern for two whole days, Ning should have known.

Now that he had actually seen him, there was no longer any doubt.

Xiao Fan.

Of course it was Xiao Fan.

With the protagonist involved, this mission was almost guaranteed to spiral out of control, and that usually meant one thing:

Danger escalation.

As if responding directly to his thoughts, a shrill scream suddenly echoed from outside the compound.

“They’re here!”

“The rabbits are attacking!”

The courtyard erupted into chaos.

The Red-Eyed Spirit Rabbits had arrived.

...

The group quickly made its way toward the spiritual farm.

The farm wasn’t located within Redhorn Village itself, but in a low-lying valley among the mountains behind it. Hills surrounded the area on all sides, their trees providing ample shade, while a layer of fog perpetually hung over the valley.

“Please wait a moment. I’ll open the formation.”

Patriarch Mi took out a jade talisman and infused it with spiritual energy. A faint ripple spread through the fog before it gradually thinned, revealing the path ahead.

The formation protecting the farm was one of the cheapest kinds available. It had no offensive capability and could only confuse sight and obscure the valley from outsiders, but for a spiritual farm of this size, that was usually enough.

While they waited for the path to fully open, Ning fell into step beside Xiao Fan.

“So the bandit mission brought you here?”

“More or less.” Xiao Fan seemed slightly surprised that Ning was speaking to him so casually after everything that had happened. “I finished the mission nearby and came to Redhorn Village to rest for a few days. Then I heard the Mi Family had some rare annotations of the Pure Qi Sutra, so I decided to stay.”

Ning nodded slowly.

So Xiao Fan had completed a mission, stopped in a random village, discovered something useful, and somehow picked up another side quest along the way.

Classic protagonist behavior.

“I’m still officially responsible for this mission,” Ning said. “But since you’re already involved, you might as well help me finish it.”

Xiao Fan looked at him strangely. “You really don’t mind?”

“Why would I? More people means less work for me.”

“I don’t need the spirit stones,” Xiao Fan said after a moment. “I only came for the annotations.”

“Even better.”

Technically, the two of them were only casual acquaintances, but Ning had dealt with Xiao Fan several times before and even sold him a few things at reduced prices. More importantly, with Xiao Fan standing here, Ning had a terrible feeling the mission was about to become considerably harder than advertised.

Having the protagonist fight alongside him seemed much safer than trying to compete with him.

Xiao Fan was quiet for a moment before his expression softened.

“You’re a good guy, Ning.”

Ning stared at him.

Did I just get a good-guy card from a protagonist? Damn.

Before he could properly process this devastating development, the formation finished opening.

A shrill screech tore through the valley.

Then another answered it.

And another.

Dozens of cries overlapped until they became a single piercing noise, and movement erupted from the tall grass beyond the Mi Family’s fences.

Rabbits surged into view.

Far too many rabbits.

Most were only mortal beasts or early-stage Red-Eyed Spirit Rabbits, though calling them ordinary rabbits felt somewhat unfair when some were already the size of large dogs. Their red eyes glowed through the lingering fog as they charged, long incisors exposed and powerful hind legs tearing chunks of soil from the ground.

Then Ning noticed the larger figures among them.

One.

Two.

Three...

His expression slowly changed.

Several mid-stage spirit beasts moved within the horde, each nearly the size of a calf and surrounded by traces of crimson demonic qi. One in particular remained farther back, noticeably larger than the others, its scarred ears standing upright as it watched the battlefield.

Ning stared at them.

The mission notice had mentioned one mid-stage rabbit.

His gaze slowly shifted toward Xiao Fan.

Xiao Fan noticed. “Why are you looking at me?”

“Nothing.”

Ning looked back toward the rabbits.

Two days.

Apparently, two days in the presence of a protagonist were enough for the mission difficulty to adjust itself.

“Forty at least,” Ning muttered, narrowing his eyes. “And several mid-stage.”

That finally made Patriarch Mi’s expression change.

“This many?”

The guards around him grew visibly nervous. The original report had been troublesome but manageable. What now stood beyond the fence was something else entirely.

Xiao Fan, however, had already begun tightening the red gloves around his hands.

“Everyone, get ready. They’re coming.”

His words snapped Patriarch Mi out of his shock. The man immediately ordered the Mi Family guards into position while Ning glanced between the approaching horde and Xiao Fan.

The protagonist looked entirely too eager to run directly into them.

“Wait,” Ning said. “We should at least decide what we’re doing first.”

Xiao Fan looked over.

“You fight up close, and I’d rather stay as far away from those teeth as possible. You hold the front. I’ll cover you from behind.”

Xiao Fan glanced at the bow in Ning’s hands. “Just make sure you know where you’re shooting.”

His lack of confidence was somewhat insulting.

Ning chose not to dignify it with a response.

“Go on, then.” He raised his bow. “Xiao Fan, I choose you.”

Xiao Fan’s eye twitched.

Then Ning unleashed him upon the rabbit horde.

The first wave came without hesitation. Mortal rabbits and early-stage spirit beasts bounded across the field while the stronger ones remained behind, moving with enough coordination to make it obvious that this wasn’t a mindless charge.

Ning had already moved toward higher ground by the time Xiao Fan met them.

Red-Eyed Spirit Rabbits were speed-type demon beasts. Their hind legs gave them explosive acceleration over short distances, while their teeth and claws were sharp enough to tear through an ordinary cultivator if they managed to get close.

Ning stared at the charging mass.

In this world, even rabbits had chosen violence.

Xiao Fan reached them first.

He stamped forward hard enough to crack the soil beneath his feet, driving his fist directly into the head of the leading rabbit. Bone gave way under the impact, and the beast was thrown backward into the ones behind it.

Two more came at him almost immediately. Xiao Fan twisted aside from the first and struck it across the skull with his elbow. Before the second could land, his knee slammed upward into its chest and sent it tumbling through the grass.

His movements were direct and brutal, with hardly any wasted motion.

Ning’s bowstring sang from behind him.

Thwip. Thwip.

Two arrows crossed the field in quick succession. The first disappeared into a rabbit’s eye, while the second tore through the hind leg of another and sent it rolling across the ground.

Ning immediately nocked a third. This one buried itself in another rabbit’s flank, and the beast managed only two more steps before its limbs stiffened and it collapsed sideways.

Ning smiled.

“Paralysis poison. Nature’s pause button.”

Xiao Fan either didn’t hear him or had wisely decided to ignore him.

Another rabbit sprang from Xiao Fan’s right, hidden behind the body of one he had just killed. Ning’s spatial perception caught the movement almost as soon as it left the ground.

“Right!”

Xiao Fan shifted his footing without turning around. The claws passed inches from his shoulder, and his returning fist smashed directly into the rabbit’s ribs.

The beast dropped.

Xiao Fan attracted almost everything in front of them, while Ning watched the angles he couldn’t easily see. Whenever a rabbit tried to circle around or leap at him from outside his vision, Ning either warned him or sent an arrow its way.

Then one of the mid-stage rabbits moved.

It launched itself over the smaller beasts in a single leap, crimson qi gathering around its claws as it descended toward Xiao Fan’s head.

Ning’s expression sharpened. He immediately pulled a black boar tusk arrow from a separate section of his quiver, one of several he had prepared specifically for tougher spirit beasts, and circulated his qi around the arrowhead.

[Spiral Arrow]

He drew the bowstring to its limit and released.

Ning wasn’t aiming for where the rabbit was, but where it had to be.

The arrow arrived first.

The rabbit twisted when it finally noticed the threat, but its momentum had already committed it to the leap. The spiraling arrow punched through its eye and drove deep into its skull, sending its body crashing into the ground several paces from Xiao Fan.

Xiao Fan glanced toward it briefly before continuing forward.

“A little thanks would be nice,” Ning called.

“Tsk.”

Xiao Fan redirected whatever irritation he felt into the next rabbit unfortunate enough to approach him.

The battle quickly settled into a rhythm.

Xiao Fan kept forcing his way through the densest part of the horde, using sheer physical strength to prevent the rabbits from surrounding him. Ning constantly shifted position behind him with Shadow Steps, taking whatever opening appeared rather than wasting arrows on difficult shots.

Whenever Xiao Fan forced a rabbit aside, Ning used the opening to target another. Whenever Ning crippled one, Xiao Fan finished it on his way past.

It wasn’t graceful teamwork, but it worked.

Another group tried to close around Xiao Fan from both sides. He drove straight through the weaker side before they could complete the encirclement, breaking one rabbit’s jaw with a short punch and sending another tumbling with a kick.

Ning picked off the one trying to follow behind him.

“You know, situational awareness is very important,” he called.

“Then keep watching!”

Fair enough.

Ning released another arrow.

At one point, five rabbits attacked Xiao Fan almost simultaneously. Two were struck out of the air before reaching him. Xiao Fan caught the third with his knee, seized the fourth as it came within reach, and smashed it into the ground.

The fifth slipped past his guard, only for an arrow to punch through its spine before its claws could reach him.

For a brief moment, Ning and Xiao Fan ended up back-to-back as Ning repositioned past him. Xiao Fan’s breathing was noticeably heavier now, though his movements remained strong, and Ning could see that he was burning through his qi faster than necessary.

During the next brief lull, Ning tossed a pill toward him.

“You’re burning through qi too quickly.”

Xiao Fan caught it without looking. “I know.”

“Clearly.”

Xiao Fan gave him an irritated look before swallowing the qi-replenishing pill.

Ning took one as well.

They had already killed a significant portion of the horde, and the Mi Family wasn’t standing idle either. Patriarch Mi and the other cultivators held one side of the field while the mortal guards remained behind them, dealing with whatever weaker rabbits slipped through.

Thankfully, most of the beasts had focused on Ning and Xiao Fan.

That was probably for the best.

The Mi Family only had three early-stage cultivators besides Patriarch Mi, who himself was at the Fourth Stage of Qi Condensation. The rest were mortal martial artists. They could hold their own against scattered weaker beasts, but throwing them into the center of the horde would only create more problems.

For a moment, it seemed like the battle was finally turning in their favor.

Then the largest mid-stage rabbit at the rear raised its head.

A sharp screech cut across the valley, and every remaining rabbit reacted at once.

The hesitation vanished from the beasts. Wounded rabbits forced themselves back onto their feet, while those that had been circling the edges abandoned all caution and charged directly toward them.

A desperate final rush.

Xiao Fan flexed his fingers and stepped forward again.

Ning drew another arrow. “Same plan?”

Xiao Fan stepped forward.

"Yes."

Ning released.

Fists moved like iron.

Arrows fell like judgment.

And the field ran red.

...

It was a first for Xiao Fan.

“Up!”

The largest mid-stage rabbit leapt high, its mouth stretching unnaturally wide, jagged teeth bared as it descended toward him. Used to Ning’s commands by now, Xiao Fan reacted immediately.

[Heaven-Shattering Fist: Rock Shatter]

His fist met the rabbit head-on and caved in its skull, sending the corpse tumbling through the grass.

Glancing to his right, Xiao Fan caught sight of Ning. The unassuming guy he had traded with several times before seemed like an entirely different person on the battlefield.

There was none of his usual casualness. Ning’s attention constantly moved across the field, his bow following soon after. An arrow would suddenly strike a rabbit approaching from Xiao Fan’s blind spot, cripple one that was about to leap, or force another directly into the path of his fist.

Three rabbits rushed Xiao Fan from the left. Two arrows immediately crossed the field, killing one and crippling another, leaving Xiao Fan free to deal with the third. When another rabbit tried to circle around him, Ning’s warning came almost immediately.

“Right.”

Xiao Fan turned and crushed it with a punch.

He had teamed up with others several times before, but they always turned out to be somewhat unreliable. Either they were too arrogant and never listened to suggestions, or they were fair maidens who somehow always seemed to attract even more trouble.

Ning was different.

Whenever Xiao Fan created an opening, Ning noticed it. Whenever something entered his blind spot, a warning or an arrow arrived before it became a problem. After several exchanges, Xiao Fan had already stopped questioning the directions coming from behind him.

With Ning covering the gaps, he could afford to become more aggressive. Qi coiled around his fists as he smashed through another rabbit, then immediately turned toward the next. A third tried to bite into his leg, only for an arrow to punch through its neck before its teeth reached him.

Xiao Fan didn't even look back. He simply continued forward.

Eventually, the incoming tide began to slow. Corpses littered the spiritual field, while the remaining rabbits finally started hesitating instead of mindlessly charging.

“It seems it’s over, just as I thought,” Xiao Fan said with a smile.

With half his face smeared in blood, his expression was considerably more terrifying than reassuring.

Ning stared at him.

“You just had to jinx it.”

A shriek exploded through the valley.

The sound hadn’t come from the remaining horde. It came from deeper within the spiritual farm, powerful enough to send visible ripples through the surrounding fog. The surviving rabbits immediately lowered themselves toward the ground.

Patriarch Mi froze mid-motion.

“This is...”

Something emerged from between the trees.

A rabbit nearly the size of a grown man stepped into view, thick muscle bulging beneath its dark red fur as demonic qi rolled from its body in waves. Its crimson eyes swept across the corpses scattered throughout the field before settling on the cultivators responsible.

Patriarch Mi’s face went pale.

“Late stage...”

One of the guards stumbled backward. “How can there be a late-stage spirit beast here?”

Whatever relief the others had been feeling disappeared immediately.

Ning swallowed and slowly turned toward Xiao Fan.

“See what you did.”

Xiao Fan looked wronged. “I didn’t do anything.”

“You did. You just don’t know it.”

What should have been an ordinary mission to hunt a mid-stage Red-Eyed Spirit Rabbit accompanied by a group of early-stage beasts had somehow escalated into several mid-stage rabbits and now a late-stage spiritual beast.

Ning didn't even need to guess why.

Protagonist halo.

Before he could complain any further, something tugged sharply at his spatial perception.

Ning neither heard nor saw the attack coming. He simply felt something cross the space in front of them, and his body reacted before his mind had time to catch up.

He kicked Xiao Fan sideways and threw himself in the opposite direction. A pair of crimson claws ripped through the space they had occupied a heartbeat earlier, one catching Ning’s sleeve as the enormous rabbit flashed past.

Ning hit the ground and rolled. A sharp sting spread through his forearm, and blood was already running toward his wrist when he pushed himself back up.

He quickly checked the wound.

Just a flesh wound.

His body cultivation had paid for itself right there. Without the additional toughness he had gained from refining his skin, that swipe could have done far worse than leave a few bloody lines.

“You alright?” Xiao Fan shouted, already turning toward the enormous rabbit.

“I’ll live.”

The rabbit moved again, but Ning’s warning came first.

“Right!”

Xiao Fan twisted his body without questioning him. A red blur passed through the place where his spine had been and slammed into the ground behind him, scattering dirt and broken stone before the rabbit landed several meters away.

Ning already had an arrow nocked. The bowstring snapped, sending it straight toward the beast’s head, but the rabbit casually struck it out of the air with one claw.

A moment later, Ning felt another disturbance.

“Left! Now!”

Xiao Fan ducked as a crescent-shaped blade of compressed air sliced over his head, cleanly severing the trunk of a nearby tree before disappearing into the forest.

Ning’s expression grew serious.

Fast enough that his eyes could barely follow it, strong enough to threaten Xiao Fan directly, capable of ranged attacks, and intelligent enough to know when to use each advantage.

This was going to be troublesome.

The rabbit disappeared again.

Ning activated Shadow Steps the instant his spatial perception warned him. Crimson claws passed close enough to brush his robe before he slipped aside, and Xiao Fan immediately stepped between them.

The rabbit lunged, and Xiao Fan met it head-on.

Fist collided with claw in a deafening crack that echoed through the valley. Xiao Fan’s boots carved deep trenches into the earth as he was forced backward, blood immediately dripping from his split knuckles. The rabbit stumbled several steps as well, but recovered almost immediately.

“Don’t chase it,” Ning warned as he repositioned. “It’s faster than you. Let it come to us.”

Xiao Fan gave a short nod.

The rabbit lowered itself before disappearing once more.

This time, Ning stopped trying to follow it with his eyes. Its speed made that nearly pointless anyway, so he concentrated on the strange tug in his spatial perception whenever the beast crossed the space around them.

There.

“Three meters ahead! Low!”

Xiao Fan stomped down without hesitation.

Qi surged through his leg, cracking the ground beneath him and throwing dirt and stone into the air. The shock disrupted the rabbit’s leap just enough for its body to become visible.

Ning released immediately.

The rabbit twisted, but the arrow still grazed its flank and carved a bloody line through its fur.

The wound itself was shallow, but that wasn't the important part.

Poison.

A late-stage spirit beast wouldn’t be paralyzed as easily as the weaker rabbits, especially from such a small wound, but Ning only needed to slow it down.

The rabbit landed and looked toward its injured flank before its crimson gaze snapped toward Ning.

Ning's expression changed. “Oh shit.”

It charged.

“Switch!”

Xiao Fan crashed into its path before it could reach him. Razor-sharp claws tore through his gloves and carved bloody lines across his hands, but he held his ground long enough to drive an elbow into the rabbit’s chest.

The beast stumbled backward and immediately lashed out with both hind legs. Xiao Fan crossed his arms in time, but the impact still threw him several meters through the dirt.

The rabbit tried to pursue him.

Ning fired an arrow directly into its path, forcing it to shift aside and giving Xiao Fan enough time to regain his footing.

“Again!”

Xiao Fan surged forward.

His fist swept toward the rabbit’s head, but the beast ducked beneath it and raked its claws across his side. Xiao Fan twisted enough to prevent the attack from digging deeply, then answered with a punch across its jaw.

The rabbit reeled just as Ning’s next arrow arrived.

The arrow struck its shoulder, though the hardened hide prevented it from penetrating deeply. The rabbit immediately snapped its head toward Ning and released another crescent of compressed air.

Ning used Shadow Steps and threw himself aside. The attack passed close enough to tear another strip from his already-damaged sleeve.

“Seriously?”

The rabbit gave him no time to complain and moved again.

Ning prepared himself for another desperate dodge, only to notice something.

The disturbance was slower. The difference was so small that he probably wouldn’t have noticed it without his heightened perception, but it was there.

The poison was beginning to work.

“Xiao Fan! Keep it busy!”

Xiao Fan didn't answer. He was already moving.

For the first time since the late-stage beast appeared, they began forcing it backward. Xiao Fan kept it occupied at close range while Ning shifted around the battlefield, firing whenever an opening appeared.

The rabbit avoided Xiao Fan’s first punch and tried to circle toward his side, but Ning sent an arrow into its path and forced it back. Xiao Fan’s next strike clipped its shoulder, and when the beast retaliated, another arrow buried itself in its foreleg.

It shrieked and lunged toward Ning.

Xiao Fan intercepted it again.

The rabbit was still faster than either of them and strong enough to injure Xiao Fan whenever they clashed directly, but the poison was slowly eating away at that advantage. Every time it focused on Xiao Fan, Ning attacked from a distance. Whenever it tried to break away and target Ning, Xiao Fan forced himself back into its path.

The beast soon abandoned the careful hit-and-run tactics it had used at the beginning and began attacking more violently.

A claw tore across Xiao Fan’s shoulder, but his fist slammed into its ribs in return. An arrow pierced the rabbit’s flank as it pulled away, causing it to turn toward Ning, only for Xiao Fan to catch up again.

One of its hind legs struck Xiao Fan in the stomach and sent him rolling across the ground.

The rabbit immediately tried to follow.

Ning fired twice in quick succession, forcing it to pull back.

“Xiao Fan!”

Xiao Fan coughed, spat a mouthful of blood onto the ground, and pushed himself upright.

“I’m fine.”

Good enough.

Ning reached toward his quiver and closed his fingers around his final specially prepared arrow.

This one had soaked in paralysis poison the longest.

He nocked it and drew the bowstring, though his fingers had begun trembling slightly from exhaustion. Between Shadow Steps, Spiral Arrow, and the constant strain of tracking the battlefield, his qi was running dangerously low.

They needed to finish this soon.

The rabbit disappeared again.

Ning didn’t bother trying to follow it visually. He waited for the familiar disturbance in his spatial perception and felt it a moment later.

“Two steps behind you.”

Xiao Fan reacted without hesitation.

Instead of immediately turning, he deliberately shifted left and exposed his right side.

The rabbit took the opening.

Its crimson body appeared behind him, claws already extending toward his exposed flank, but Xiao Fan suddenly pivoted. His fist shot upward and smashed into the beast’s jaw at almost the exact moment Ning released the bowstring.

The poisoned arrow buried itself deep into the rabbit’s shoulder.

The beast shrieked and staggered.

This time, the poison entered deep.

Its legs faltered for the first time.

Xiao Fan immediately surged forward, refusing to give it time to recover. His fist struck the rabbit across the face, followed by another blow to its chest, forcing the beast backward.

Then its crimson eyes flared.

Demonic qi erupted around its body.

Ning’s expression changed.

“Back!”

Xiao Fan tried to retreat, but the rabbit had already accelerated. Burning through whatever strength remained in its body, it lunged forward with its claws aimed directly at his throat.

Ning raised his hand and extended two fingers like an imaginary pistol.

[Spell: Golden Finger]

A beam of golden light struck the rabbit’s forelegs.

The spell lacked the power to pierce the hide of a late-stage spirit beast, but Ning had never expected it to. The impact only needed to disrupt its footing for an instant.

And the spell did its work.

“Now! Jump that beast.”

Xiao Fan didn't need to be told twice. He stepped inside the rabbit’s reach and drove a straight punch into its chest, stopping what remained of its charge. He planted his foot firmly into the earth as qi surged through his body and gathered around his fist.

[Heaven-Shattering Fist: Rock Shatter]

His fist slammed into the rabbit’s sternum.

Crack.

The rabbit’s body convulsed violently. It remained standing for another moment before the light slowly faded from its crimson eyes and its legs finally gave way. With a heavy thud, it collapsed motionless onto the ground.

Silence settled over the field.

Then, just as suddenly, a pile of items appeared beside the corpse.

Ning stared blankly at the collection of spirit stones, beast materials, and medicinal herbs scattered across the ground.

It looked exactly like a boss dropping loot after being defeated in an RPG.

His gaze slowly shifted toward Xiao Fan, who had landed the finishing blow.

“Ref! This guy’s using hacks. Do something.”
