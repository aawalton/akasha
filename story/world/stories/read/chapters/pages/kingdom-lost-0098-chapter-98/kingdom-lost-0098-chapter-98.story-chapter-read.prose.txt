Chapter 98

The upper room was quiet when Riley closed the door behind her. The afternoon light came in long across the floor, the colour of old straw. On the table was the scroll with the broken wax seal lay where Riley had left it. The chest sat next to the scroll, waiting for its mysteries to be revealed.

She coiled the whip and laid it on the table beside the scroll. The leather settled into its coil and lay still. She looked at it for a moment, then turned and grabbed four wooden cups from a nearby shelf. She set the cups along the edge of the table at intervals of a hand's width and stepped back. They looked very small from across the room.

She picked the whip back up. It came into her palm the way it had come the first time, smooth and willing, and the familiar hum started under her fingers.

She had been waiting for a quiet moment like this and she started to grin. She faced the first cup and lifted her arm the way she had lifted it the first time, the overhand throw, and snapped it forward. The whip went where it was sent. The crack rang off the stone walls; the target cup was still on the table. She had missed by half a foot.

She lowered her arm and looked at the line of her throw and reset her shoulders and tried again.

The second crack was cleaner. She felt it travel down her arm and out through the leather and into the air with the particular satisfaction of a motion that fits. The cup tipped. It did not fall. It rocked once on its base and settled back upright.

She smiled again without meaning to.

She tried the third. The whip came forward and the cup went off the table, end over end, and landed on the stone floor with a hollow knock that bounced once and rolled to a stop against the leg of the bed.

The fourth she took without pausing. The fifth she took faster. The sixth she did not aim, she simply moved her arm and let the whip find what she was looking at, and the cup left the table cleanly and was gone before her arm had finished its motion. The cracking sound changed her grin to a toothy smile.

The HUD pulsed at the edge of her vision.

She did not look at it for a moment. She let the whip settle back into her hand and let her breathing come down and looked at the empty table where the four cups had been, and only then did she let her eyes shift to the line at the corner of her sight.

❖ New Skill Acquired

❖ Skill: Whip handling Level 1

This was new and it had not been on her HUD an hour ago. She read it twice.

“My first proper weapon skill!” Riley said.

She walked the whip back to her hip and threaded the handle into her belt and let the coil fall into place. She walked to the table and looked at the chest.

She looked at the chest properly for the first time in days. Iron-banded oak. The lock was a simple ward, the kind a house clerk would put on a strongbox. She tried to pick the lock, with a dinner knife to no avail, the lock did not yield.

She felt for it again, more carefully. The lock turned a fraction and stopped; she did not force it. She took a step back, looked at the lock and decided it would wait one more day.

There was a knock at the door.

Nikola entered with the three sheets of parchment he had carried into the council and a fourth folded behind them. He did not look surprised to see her in the upper room rather than the meeting room below. He had been in the settlement long enough to know that Riley took conversations in the room that suited her, it was the leader’s prerogative.

"Warden."

"Sit."

He sat. She took the chair across from him and pulled the table's edge a little closer with her boot.

"The speed-up reserve," she said. "What is in it?"

Nikola did not have to consult the sheets. "Forty-seven hours total. Eighteen hours in single increments, the rest in the larger blocks. The largest single block is twelve hours."

"Spend it."

He paused for half a beat.

"All of it?"

"All of it. Push the forge through to Level 9 today. Whatever is left, put on the tower upgrade the moment it opens. If there is anything still in the reserve after that, the academy queue. Empty it."

Nikola made a mark on the top sheet without looking down at it.

"Yes, Warden."

She watched him while he wrote. She had carried the speed-up reserve in the back of her HUD for weeks as a kind of insurance, a thing she might one day need, and had never quite been able to decide when that day would arrive, today was the day. It had come with a Corvessa scroll in her pocket and a King in the south who had been watching her for months and an army of Clawborn in a valley without a clear reason. She would lean into the build and have nothing in reserve. That was the right decision, level up hard and focus on battle preparations.

"When can we train level two soldiers?" she said.

Nikola lifted his eyes from the parchment.

"My guess is that it is a Level Ten tower unlock. The academy has had no research line that would produce a Level Two soldier from the existing barracks at any level below ten, and the barracks itself has stopped offering meaningful upgrades since Level Eight. The obvious answer, is levelling the tower."

"So, three weeks?"

"Three weeks at the soonest. Possibly less now that you have spent the reserve."

"What can we do in the meantime that prepares us for them?"

"Three things, in my view. The first is equipment. Let’s forge top-tier blades, shields and armour. We don’t know what equipment they will come with but if we have the best the forge can yield a level two soldier would possibly perform like a level 2.5. They would deal more damage, they could take more punishment.”

"Do it."

"The second is training, Thorne and Valrik were training the level one soldiers and they seemed to pay off in battle. If we apply the same idea to the level 2 soldiers, have them work both in larger groups and smaller one too. This would give you more choices in battle deployment. This would be an additional edge in battle.”

"Do that too."

"The third is the harder one. The Level Two soldier, whatever he is, will not arrive in a vacuum. He will arrive into a unit. If we are going to have mixed-tier units, which I expect, we need a doctrine for how the tiers work together. Thorne has been thinking about it. He would like a half hour with you when you can give it."

"Tell him tomorrow morning, first thing."

Nikola wrote that down as well.

She let him finish before she spoke again.

"The silo."

He did not look up at once, but continued making notes.

"Underground."

She did not answer immediately.

"Reasons?" she said.

“Resources can be offered into the system from above and those resources will accumulate in the silos wherever they are. If a silo is destroyed during battle, we will lose the resources it holds. If we build silos underground, they would be sheltered from the battle. I do not believe this has been attempted, ever. Since buildings have to be built within range of the tower, it stands to reason that the range works as a radius around the tower and also below it as well.”

“Yes, I have been thinking about this since we entered the new sections of the mine were found,” Riley replied. "What about Nami's pots? They will also have to be stored safely too."

"Nami's pots and what goes in them. We will be producing the firebomb mixture in volume from this week forward, and storing it. Inside the walls in a wooden building, even a well-built one, is a risk we should not be carrying.”

Riley looked up and she squinted slightly.

She could see it the moment he had said it. This was something she didn’t have to deal with in the game at home. There was never a risk of one’s own artillery blowing up during battle, but it was a real concern in all those war documentaries and war movies she watched with her dad.

"We need a second access," she said. "Hidden, maybe within one of the non-system buildings, into the mine where the pots are stored."

Nikola did not speak for a moment.

"That is not a storage feature," he said.

"No, if we need to block the mine opening, if the walls are breached."

He thought for a moment longer.

"Yes, Warden. We need to keep invaders out of the mine."

“Exactly. I don’t expect the walls to be breached…”

“It’s just a precaution,” Nikola finished.

“Exactly. Once the forge is upgraded, we will go together to test your theory and build a silo in the mine.”

"Yes warden. Now, the bunkhouses."

He drew the third sheet with their plans, forward without ceremony.

"Two more, of the same footprint as the existing ones. The lumber from the southern cutting crews will cover both with margin. I have men available from the wall detail to begin framing the first one tomorrow. The second goes up behind it inside the week."

"Do it."

"Yes, Warden."

She watched him gather his sheets back into a stack. He did it the way he did everything, neatly and without performance, and she let him finish before she spoke.

"Nikola."

He looked up.

"You and Landryn have been working ahead of me on this," she said. "I noticed. I should have said so sooner."

He held her eyes for a moment, then he gave a small nod.

"We were doing our jobs, Warden."

"You were doing more than that."

He did not answer. He stood, gathered the sheets under his arm, and crossed to the door, and at the door he paused and turned back.

"The forge will clear Level Nine tonight."

"Good."

He closed the door behind him.

Riley stood at the table for a long moment after he was gone, then she turned to the window and looked down into the yard.

The forge column was darker than it had been an hour ago. She did not know whether that was the speed-up already working through her HUD or whether she was simply looking for it. The yard moved through its late-afternoon rotation, soldiers crossing between the wall and the barracks, a wagon coming in through the inner gate with a load of timber for the new bunkhouse framing. The watchtower's silhouette had gone darker against the sky behind it.

The chest sat on the table behind her, waiting.

The whip sat at her hip, quiet.

She walked to the stairs and went down to find Thorne.

The work had begun.
