Isha and I lay beside each other, both tired and a bit sweaty.

“Adia says there's nothing wrong,” she said, sounding almost lost.

“If she does then she's probably right love.”“And yet... she's still our only one. I've not even conceived another. Not all of them make it for others, but we've been trying for years and years now, and nothing, not a single one. Nor you I assume, no problems on your end.”“No love, I may be seeing different healers than you, but you know I do periodically, and they claim nothing is wrong either. That doesn't seem uncommon once we get a bit older though. I'm told that for elders one child a century isn't overly odd,” I said with a sigh. “Though there are few who know for sure, and to be honest it sounds like many have theirs before they get that old and just, don't talk about it.”

We'd gone back and forth on this hundreds of times, and never come to a good conclusion. Her own beliefs, mine, her pain, my hatred of that pain. It was like a sad merry-go-round. Our culture, elven culture, placed a high importance on children, massive, and for her, being unable to have another was devastating.

“I never should have made that crystal,” she continued, voice hurting.

“If it would have spared you your suffering I agree,” I said, wrapping my arms around her.

“You mean it too, don't you?”“Of course love. I like my inventions, but I love you and Adia. Your happiness is more important to me than them.”“If I asked, would you stop making them?” she questioned.

“If you ask,” I affirmed.

“Really?”

“Yes.”

“One year then. For one year I want you and all the others to stop making any of the crystals. We'll call it a test. I don't want hundreds of children... well right now, but one more...”

“I'll tell them in the morning, I hope you don't mind if we take a few days to start? Adia won't care, but the others might. Chien will be a pain, but he'll do it if I ask him to.” I requested.

“No, that's fine,” she answered cuddling up close, mollified. “Expected more of a fight.”

“Isha dear, when have I ever been unwilling to give you what you wanted?”

“Once or twice, but you did always try,” she said, not even opening her eyes.

“Well, this time I'll aim for more than simply trying.”

For the first time in a long time she actually seemed in a good mood. I'd missed that, missed it dearly, and if the price was just halting some of my projects for a year, that was a price I'd gladly pay. I'd agreed to avoid adventures for years for her peace of mind, this was less of an imposition. I also wouldn't fail at this one if I had any say in it, and I really did.

I was in fact correct, Chien was unhappy.

“You're telling me we have to stop all work for a year because Isha asked us to?” he said as the next morning's meeting of the various local heads.

“Yes.”

“And you're happy with this?”“Not really, but it will make her happy, so we will,” I continued.

“You're serious?”“Yes.”“Really?”“Yes, and don't think of it as a setback, think of it as a learning experience, we can take other routes to do things. We've got more than enough to work on, can spend the year making our way through plans, updating some of our facilities, and doing organization. All of that needs to be done anyway.”

“Right, well... there was some research into tattoos that needed doing anyway...”“Oh, I think Adia mentioned wanting to do more research into those. You should consult with her while she's in town. She's been looking at the various techniques she can as she goes, though sadly there aren't many who can do them anymore.”That too was a fact. Mostly they'd been done by either elders or very specialized makers, and with the shakeup in leadership many of those had been lost. We'd lost much during the fight with the dinos and the following destruction that resulted from our lack of higher end protectors. Even the elders who hadn't gone had taken absolutely insane losses over the years, dropping like flies.

“Right, I'll add it to the list.”“You realize that this will delay your research facility?” one of my advisors asked.

“Sure, but it still needs to be actually built anyway, and the systems for the controls will probably need updating for the next several centuries before I'm happy with them, so who cares?”“Me,” Chien grumbled, but he didn't give any indication that he would actually oppose me on this.

The others accepted it too, with a bit of goading. Honestly some of the people below us looked... relieved. Maybe I'd been running them a bit hard, pushing a bit more than I should, well what was done was done. This would be a good chance for us to step back and look at other solutions to problems too, get some more work out there that anyone could do, some chemistry, some physics, just good old fashioned crafts and building. We had more than enough of that to be getting on with anyway.

So they dove into what they had to do. I did too, trying to dig more designs out of my memories, things that would help us, small things, things anyone could make but would change paradigms if used correctly. Honestly there weren't really many things left for me to think of there, I'd had long enough, but I could work to push things in this direction or that, help people make a few small discoveries.

Pen in hand I began sending out notices and writing plans.
