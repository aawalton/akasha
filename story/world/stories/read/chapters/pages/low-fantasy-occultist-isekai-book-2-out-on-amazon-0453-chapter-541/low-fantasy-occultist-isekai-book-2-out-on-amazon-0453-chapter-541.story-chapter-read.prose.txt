His soul shone, and Nick let go of his restraint.

Reality screamed in visceral response as the Empyrean buckled under the unbounded expansion of a Prestige existence.

Sand fused beneath his feet into a sheet of red-hot glass. The air distorted, bending light and sound as his mana flooded the arena.

For the first time, [Blasphemy] answered his call as an active power rather than a passive defense.

The World around them darkened. Where Xander’s presence had once commanded the space—carving fractures with his mere existence—the spiritual winds now fought back.

Nick weaponized the environment itself, stripping away the conceptual truths of the Grandmaster's domain. The air resisted the black steel. Mana refused to part for the blade. The World itself turned hostile to Xander Wolfram.

Even that would not be enough, so Nick layered the rejection with the [Shroud of the Thunderbird].

Blinding, white-hot plasma erupted from his skin, weaving with the immaterial eddies. He drew on water and wind, synthesizing the elements with the raptor's divine spark. Searing electricity crackled into a torrential cyclone of pressurized water and razor-sharp gales.

He became a storm incarnate, a walking disaster across the ether and the material, and met the Grandmaster’s challenge with his own roar.

In the physical world, the arena vanished into a tempest of divine lightning and churning rain. On the esoteric plane, their auras collided like grinding tectonic plates.

Still, Xander advanced, swinging his sword to carve a clear path through the howling wind.

Nick felt the cut in his soul before he saw it in the physical world. Xander had severed the cyclone's conceptual basis, collapsing the wind into harmless breezes. The black blade continued on its path, aimed at Nick’s throat. Even though the distance between them should have kept him safe, his instincts screamed danger.

Following them, Nick slipped into the ether, letting his physical form bleed into raw energy. The blade passed through his afterimage, steel singing a hairsbreadth from his skin. He reappeared ten paces to the right, cracking the fused glass, then thrust the Shard forward.

As Lasazar said, he needed more than raw power. Xander’s cuts bypassed any resistance he offered, slicing through intent and magic alike. Nick reached into his mind, drawing on the ancient myths and histories he had devoured in a past life and in his current one, synthesizing the elements to forge a new spell on the fly and anchor his magic in the narrative weight of a saga.

He couldn’t say why, but he leaned on the First Flame, the mythological fire that birthed the world, and a torrent of crimson fire erupted from his palms, braided with the golden plasma of the Thunderbird.

The heat was apocalyptic. Had they stood in the center of Alluria, the resulting firestorm would have incinerated the city blocks in mere moments. The arena’s containment wards groaned, flaring red to hold the explosion at bay.

Unconcerned about the danger, Xander stepped into the inferno and slashed.

His blade met the spell, and for the first time, the steel caught.

The mythological structure of the First Flame resisted its path, anchored not only to Nick’s existence but to more. Sparks of abstract energy showered the arena floor as Xander’s blade ground against the spell’s narrative.

The Grandmaster grunted and slid back an inch.

A feral grin stretched across Nick’s face. What he was doing was dangerous. He’d seen that in Oakenhollow; calling upon such powers could have unexpected results, but that only made him more interested, eager to plunder the depths of this new power.

And really, I’ve been doing something similar for quite some time. Great Works and minor rituals alike need something to anchor to. So I’m just applying that knowledge to a new field.

"Better," Xander rasped, then twisted his wrists, angled the blade, and pushed. The First Flame shattered, its fires splitting down the middle and washing harmlessly over the black armor, giving him space to lunge.

The speed was almost too fast for him to react, yet Nick barely managed to gather all the lightning back into its original shape, infusing it with concepts of endurance until the golden shield was almost solid.

Xander’s sword blurred. He struck once, then struck again at the exact same point. The great bird cracked and exploded into a shower of bolts that did little to stop the sword’s edge from grazing Nick’s ribcage.

White-hot agony flared in his chest. The wound was a shallow line across his ribs, but the damage tore through his soul, sending a violent stutter that threw off his control.

He gasped, stumbling backward.

Xander didn't pause, pressing his advantage and battering his hasty defenses with a relentless flurry of strikes, just a bit too fast for him to recover.

Nick fell into a desperate rhythm of evasion and counterattack. He hurled spears of pure lightning, walls of compressed air, gouts of steam, and lances of divine plasma. He wove the elements together, layering them with [Blasphemy] to weaken the incoming blade.

Yet still, his opponent punished every mistake.

When Nick overcharged a plasma thrust, hoping to buy a moment to regain his bearings, Xander flowed around it and severed the spell, causing the energy to detonate in his face. When Nick tried to gain altitude, Xander cut the air pressure beneath him, sending him crashing back to the floor.

A cut opened on Nick’s cheek. Another across his left thigh. A grazing strike along his forearm.

None of it should have slowed him, yet it all compounded, weakening his magic and forcing him to clamp down on his power as it buckled.

Yet amid the relentless beating, Nick’s mind worked with crystal clarity, and the pressure forced him to do more than he’d thought possible. He merged water and fire into a scalding mist, masking his movements, and shaped it with the Thunderbird’s plasma, turning the space around him into a conductive minefield. He then detonated it.

The resulting explosion shook the arena. The spectators above vanished in a blinding flash, and the shockwave pulverized the remaining glass, reducing it to fine dust.

Through the haze, Xander stepped forward.

His black armor was scorched, the joints glowing cherry-red. A thin line of blood trickled from his nose, a testament to the concussive force he had just tanked.

Xander wiped it away with his gauntlet, revealing it was only a drop. Still, when he looked up, Nick’s hair rose, and he knew he’d done enough to be taken seriously as killing intent bloomed.

It slammed into Nick with the weight of a collapsing mountain, driving the breath from his lungs. The ether froze, paralyzed by the sheer breadth of the Grandmaster's malice.

Nick stared into merciless eyes, feeling the primal terror of a lamb before a tiger. This was no longer a test. It was no longer a demonstration for the crowd.

Xander intended to kill him. If he didn’t rise up to the challenge, he’d die.

The fear was great, but Nick embraced it. He let the terror fuel the furnace of his soul, burning to produce more mana. He drew the broken remnants of his spells from the air, drawing the scattered plasma, the dying fires, and the howling winds back into himself.

He then channeled everything into [Blasphemy], focusing the rejection of reality into a single point, feeding it all the mana he could spare until he could no longer do so.

"Come on," Nick snarled, his voice crackling with static.

Xander smiled and met the challenge, raising his sword.

The Abyssal Arena dissolved as Nick became a nexus of howling energy. The storm ripped pulverized dust from the floor, spinning it into a cyclone of glass and plasma. The containment wards shrieked as runes cracked under apocalyptic pressure. Spells capable of obliterating armies cascaded from Nick’s form, a relentless barrage that on Earth would have been called divine judgment, all reinforced by the impossible power of [Blasphemy] to become more.

Xander stood in the center of the howling apocalypse bearing down on him, then closed his eyes.

For a moment, Nick let himself hope he was done, that he’d seen enough and that he was letting him have it, but when he opened his eyes, the man was gone.

There was no old soldier in battered armor. There was no flesh, no bone, no history. Only the Sword remained.

He was the tool that separated one thing from another, crafted solely to kill. What he willed to be cut would be cut. There were no exceptions.

He charged the storm head-on, his blade meeting the magic, and his cut echoed across the dimensions.

Nick felt reality tear. The blade severed the storm, cleaving his spells in two. The two halves of the cyclone rushed past Xander, vaporizing the obsidian walls behind him and leaving the Grandmaster untouched in the eye of the destruction.

The cut continued.

Nick tried to fade. He threw himself into the ether, abandoning his physical anchor and becoming a being of pure thought and energy to escape the path of the steel.

It was useless. The blade was merely the conduit through which Reality was enforcing itself. The cut had already happened; it just had to catch up.

The pain eclipsed anything Nick had ever known. As the blade carved through the ether, striking both his soul and his physical form, his remaining defenses were shattered, severing his connection to the [Shroud] and breaking the [Blasphemy]'s hold on reality.

The only reason he survived at all was that his Trait refused to let another power taint him, so only the damage remained, and little more.

Nick fell out of the ether and crashed hard onto the floor. He rolled uncontrollably, his momentum carrying him across the field until he slammed into the base of the containment wall.

The storm died. Plasma faded into nothingness. The roaring winds fell silent, leaving only the ringing echo of the sword strike.

Nick lay on his back, staring up at the magical spotlights. A diagonal gash ran across his chest, cutting through his coat, his shirt, and his skin. Blood pooled beneath him, warm and slick. His mana was empty, and his soul ached in ways he couldn’t describe. He couldn't even move his fingers, much less lift his head.

He had lost. Footsteps crunched as Xander stopped beside him. When he looked down, the suffocating killing intent had vanished. His black armor still hissed as the lingering heat dissipated, but he didn’t seem bothered.

"You did well," he said in a surprisingly soft tone. "Come see me before you leave the city.”

Nick tried to speak, but only a wet cough came out. He blinked, the sweat and blood stinging his eyes. When his vision cleared, Xander was gone, leaving no trace except the trench he had carved through the center of the arena.

High above, the containment wards powered down as runic arrays shifted from angry red to a clear white.

The silence held for a breathless second. Then, the crowd roared.

It was an avalanche of sound. Three thousand voices screamed in awe. They hammered against the glass, stamping their feet on the stone tiers, their cheers cascading down the walls and washing over him.

They had come to see a boy put in his place. They had watched a true mage challenge a god of war, bending reality to his whim and unleashing destruction that would be etched into the Tower's history for centuries to come.

Nick closed his eyes. The pain in his chest was too much, and the cold stone sapped the last of his strength. The crowd's roar faded into a distant hum.

He had found his limits, pushed beyond them, and discovered even more. A bloody smile spread across his face.
