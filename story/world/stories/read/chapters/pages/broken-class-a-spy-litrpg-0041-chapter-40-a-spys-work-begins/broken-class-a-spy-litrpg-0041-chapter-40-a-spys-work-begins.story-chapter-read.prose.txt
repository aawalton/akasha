The steam of the bathhouse had long since faded, but the day's successes had finally given way to an overwhelming exhaustion. He found a room for the night in a dingy boarding house on the edge of the merchant district, paying with the coin he’d earned as Zane. The room was little more than a closet with a straw-stuffed mattress, but it had a sturdy lock and no windows facing the main street. It was perfect.

He barred the door, his gaze falling on the sagging mess of straw that passed for a bed. It was a miserable bed, but it wasn't the cold earth of a cavern or the damp hollow of a tree. For the first time since his rebirth, he had a roof over his head that he'd paid for. For one night, reflection and planning could wait; the predator's hyper-vigilance could recede. He collapsed onto the mattress, and the simple rustle of straw was a welcome sound as darkness took him.

Despite the unfamiliar comfort, old habits held fast. He woke before dawn, his internal clock honed by a lifetime of sleeping with one eye open. The boarding house was already stirring with the quiet shuffles and coughs of its other residents. He splashed cold water on his face from a basin in the corner, dressed, and headed downstairs.

The common room was a cramped, low-ceilinged space that smelled of stale ale and woodsmoke. A few other grim-faced adventurers were hunched over bowls of watery porridge. Arthur took a seat in a shadowed corner, paid the proprietor a few coppers for a bowl of the same, and began to eat. As the bland, hot meal settled in his stomach, he finally allowed himself a moment of pure, unfiltered assessment.

He had set three goals for himself when he first stepped out of that secluded cavern.

One: Win the Great Hunt. Done. The Mask of Infinite Faces was a reward far more valuable than any sword. Two: Infiltrate civilization. Done. He was here, in the heart of Copperpine, with a stolen identity and coin in his pocket.

The third goal, revenge, still burned like a cold fire in his gut. Rajak’s sneer, the chimera’s soulless eyes... those debts would be paid. But revenge was a dish best served from a position of absolute power. He was in no hurry to claim it, but the debt would be paid. That final objective could wait, but it would not be forgotten.

With two of his three initial goals met, it was a success by any metric. It was time to stop drifting from moment to moment. He needed direction.

His thoughts turned to the princess. Ysara. The rumors painted her as a political anomaly, a royal who cared for slaves, an idealist isolated from her own class. In a world built on brutal hierarchies, such a person was either a fool on a path to martyrdom, or she was a cunning political player, using the mask of compassion so effectively that her rivals dangerously underestimated her. Arthur needed to know which. She was his most valuable, and most volatile, unknown.

A new strategy began to form, built not on a single mission, but on a foundation of carefully constructed power.

His first new goal was to fully understand Princess Ysara. He had to dissect her motives, beliefs, and ambitions. Was her compassion a genuine conviction he could exploit, or was it a carefully constructed political tool? If it was the latter, it meant she was a far more cunning player than anyone imagined, one whose motives and network he could potentially leverage for his own ends. Determining whether she could be an asset in his quest for political power was paramount.

But political power was a long game, and he was currently operating from a position of weakness, living in a hovel under a stolen name. He remembered the adventurers' ecstatic celebration over a single monster core. His ring held a king's ransom in cores and parts, yet he was living like a pauper. This was a tactical vulnerability.

A memory from his old life surfaced: a history lesson on the rise of oil states after 1950. They were nations built on sand, not ancient dynasties, yet their control over the world’s energy supply gave them wealth so immense it made them central players on the global stage. They raised gleaming cities from the desert sands, funding massive infrastructure and technological advancements, proving that true power didn't always come from an army, but often from controlling a vital resource. His second goal, therefore, had to be establishing economic power. He needed to convert his hidden hoard into real, usable wealth, acquire a secure base of operations, and build a financial foundation that couldn't be swept away overnight.

And wealth, like power, attracted predators. He was operating blind, relying on tavern gossip and chance encounters. It was amateurish and unsustainable. A professional did not rely on luck. His third goal was the most crucial for a man who lived in the shadows: intelligence. He needed to build a network, to place his own eyes and ears in the places he could not go. He had to see the threats before they materialized, to understand the city’s secret currents so he could navigate them, or redirect them to his own ends. In this world, knowledge was not just power; it was survival.
