The blue-white light of the rift transitioned suddenly to red hues, and poisonous air filled Ethan’s lungs. He landed in rust-colored mud, the ground so barren that he’d have believed the weeds slaughtered one another until none remained. The sound of the wind whistling past his ears was interrupted only by distant roars, and he felt an eerie stillness in every direction. Yet it didn’t give the impression of peace, but rather the temporary absence of violence.

Potentia.

Ethan and Selina looked around rapidly, every instinct already screaming of inevitable ambush. They were surrounded by rolling hills, and dense forest that writhed, rather than shifting with the wind. Dense clouds muted the sun above in some kind of perpetual storm, and in the distance, some kind of flying demons moved through the air like bats, before dropping down on unsuspecting prey. Ethan’s knuckles were white on his swords as old fears tried to take over.

“This…this is where my people really come from?” Selina asked.

Tomo appeared at the size of a nator–nearly eight feet–and looked around. “No, kinswoman, this is not where we come from. This world is but the burnt out husk of the beautiful, dangerous land that birthed our people.”

Selina actually managed a smile for the demon. They’d been speaking more often lately as the trip to Potentia loomed, and Ethan was glad they had each other. His own connection to this place was purely antagonistic, and he imagined it must be a comfort to experience this with someone who could understand.

“Where are we?” Selina asked. “Did it work?”

Ethan looked around, hoping for some kind of landmark, though he couldn’t imagine what it would be. “We’ll have to trust Noctem,” he said. “This should be the equivalent of central Mexico on Earth, or the southern kingdoms on Nexum. From here, it’s a straight shot north to The Throne.”

“The Throne,” Tomo whispered, as if needing to taste the word once again now that he was home.

“How can you be sure it’s there?” Selina asked. “I thought the whole point was that no one could find it.”

“Yes and no,” Tomo answered. “For those of us trapped in the cycle, we know of The Throne as if it were water, and we, dying of thirst. We never see it, only lust after it. The light guides us to ever more power, as that is the real key. We cannot find The Throne until we are worthy of it, and in Potentia, worth is tied to strength.”

“But you know where it is?” She asked Ethan.

“I do. I’d suspected for some time, and Noctem confirmed it. When I met Dawn beneath Sanctustella, there was an enormous rift in place, waiting for her. Taunting her really, just out of reach of her broken body. Noctem left it for her, that’s part of what he does as the fourth god.”

“So…he makes a rift for each one?” Selina surmised.

“Exactly. The one on Earth was once right beneath our feet, in the tunnels where we found the Followers. Noctem had already closed it so they wouldn’t be able to escape through it. As for here, well, I saw it the first day I arrived on Potentia. It’s where Viridus is in Nexum…in fact, Corvale once patrolled directly around the equivalent location.”

“That’s quite a coincidence,” Selina said, her disbelief plain.

“I agree. I’ve wondered for some time whether there’s a connection we aren’t seeing.”

Selina clapped lightly, and bounced on her toes. “Well, no use just standing around…utterly exposed. How do we begin?”

Ethan smiled. “We have a long journey ahead of us,” he said. “So we start the same way as our last long journey.” He withdrew the Fable Cube from his inventory, and tossed it to the ground some distance away.

It began to unfold, chrome, silver, gold, and brass appearing rapidly as it assembled into the form Ethan had been working on for months. As it slowly took the shape of a train, he had to admit that he missed the old fashioned model that had been based on something from the early 1900s, but right now he needed function over form.

This model looked more like a diesel engine, though smoothed out and streamlined like a bullet train from Japan. The size was more comparable to a jumbo jet, however, as Ethan had need of the space. At Twilight, the cube was capable of constructing something more akin to a skyscraper on its side, but that wouldn’t serve much purpose at the moment.

There were only three cars behind the engine for now, though he expected to add to that as they progressed. The first was where he planned to spend most of his time. It held Fable’s nerve-center, where she kept the complex series of Monster Cores that granted her consciousness, and expanded her capabilities beyond what she would otherwise be capable of. Additionally, it held the lounge area, which doubled as a command center, as well as an armory.

The second car was meant for living in. Bedrooms, washrooms, games and distractions. The small library Selna had amassed. It was where they’d rest and decompress whenever possible, and Ethan had put considerable effort into making sure it felt like a safe-haven. They had a lot of work ahead of them, and burnout would be a serious concern.

The final car held everything that he and Ulric had worked on together to prepare for this journey, and it looked quite different from the others. It was a large, open platform, surrounded by a perimeter of weapons. Approaching it, the dozens of elaborate Rune Circles came into view, all carefully protected behind rows of defenses. The roof would even seal over if they were attacked from above.

In the center was a large open space, Ethan’s destination. “Tomo, how’s the corruption?”

“We are strong, Ethan Bishop, and resist it well, but I feel it seeping in, nonetheless.” Selina shuddered at that. She was right to. It was that corruption that had transformed her people over the centuries. Some of the demons lurking in the shadows right now could even be distant cousins of hers, in a way.

“Well then, let’s not delay,” Ethan said.

He summoned his spirit, a gargantuan version of himself, seated on a throne–one built in the imagined likeness of the very one they were searching for. His Astral self had become far more manageable since it had first been split from him while healing Dawn. It still looked utterly imperious, glaring directly forward from its seat of power, and it still wore a crown, despite the actual item it had once possessed being devoured by Ethan’s Techwork Armor, Prime.

But it would need that confidence, that arrogance, even, to achieve what Ethan set out to do. The spirit dutifully moved to the center of the flat platform, where its throne lowered until it was flush against the steel. Chains shot up from the Fable Train, securing the spirit, binding the two together. Ethan could tell that the spirit would now be visible even without looking into the Astral, as it was tethered to reality. Just one of the elements needed for their plan.

As soon as it was secure, Ethan released its aura, something he normally kept contained on Earth–it was far from pleasant to be around, especially for the Unbound. But that aura was more than simply intimidating, it was an extension of the construct Ethan had created in that strange throne. It drew in the corruption of Potentia, turning it into raw, magical energy.

On Earth, it was a tool with occasional value. He’d cleansed several people of Potentia’s touch with it. But here, where the world was the corruption…it was a source of limitless power.

Selina gasped as she felt the taint she’d already begun to accumulate pulled from her, and Tomo straightened, relief obvious. The Rune Circles surrounding the spirit glowed as power flooded through them.

A figure composed entirely of metal appeared then, in a whirlwind of tiny shards, echoing the construction of the train itself. Fable, the entity which the cube had become, looked around with steel eyes at the world she’d landed in, then turned to Ethan. She still wore a semblance of a doctor’s coat to match his, which made him smile.

“Welcome to Potentia, Fable,” he said. “Is everything working?”

“Indeed, Doctor Bishop. My power reserves are filling rapidly, and at a rate that should allow liberal use of my defensive systems without meaningful loss.”

“Excellent,” he said. “The aura spreads out in a radius of about a hundred feet, but that will increase as my spirit gathers power.”

“It’s a shame that it won’t be enough for The Throne,” Selina said. “I think I’d prefer to simply take a train trip across this world, your spirit slowly devouring the corruption.”

“Agreed,” Ethan said. “But that’s the exact opposite of what it’s made for. I designed that construct to cleanse the corruption, not accumulate it. For that, we have to do things the hard way. Come on out, Apollo.”

In a flash of blue-white energy, a golden-haired version of Ethan appeared, hovering above the ground in overly long white robes. He looked around, then frowned. “This place is disgusting,” he said.

“Quite right,” Ethan agreed. “Think you can clean it up?”

“Angelos and I stand ready,” he said with a smirk. “I guess I’ll go to my assigned spot. I can’t wait to spend god knows how long with your ridiculous spirit staring at me.”

“Make friends,” Ethan said with a shrug, and Apollo chuckled as he rose into the air, moving to hover directly above the final train car.

More Rune Circles flickered to life, and this time golden chains rose up, wrapping themselves around Apollo’s outstretched arms. He waited, eyes glowing as Fable tilted her head in consideration.

“Connection established, Doctor Bishop,” she said.

“Perfect,” Ethan said. “Then it’s time to begin.”

“I still don’t fully grasp these runes,” Selina said. As a life-long Runemistress, that clearly both distressed and fascinated her. She pointed at the first set. “Those attach your spirit to the train, like an enormous battery. And these new ones connect the train to Apollo, but there are more here that are entirely beyond me.”

Ethan nodded. He would have liked to include Selina more in the planning phase with Ulric, but she had been busy fighting the war…and Ulric refused to allow anyone else into his tower. Especially not a Dusk.

“Two purposes. The first is to turn the train into an anchor for Apollo, so he’s not relying directly on me to sustain him. It should provide him full access to his powers, and allow me to cycle through my Familiars without him winking out of existence.”

“Thanks for that!” Apollo called down.

Ethan chuckled. “Secondly, it gives him access to my Anima, the power of a Chosen. Before we were joined, Apollo still had his own, through Angelos, the Holy Guardian Beast. But…well, Angelos isn’t exactly real, in certain ways.”

“Incredibly uncool to phrase it that way!” Apollo called down.

Ethan cleared his throat. “Anyway, the point is that Apollo lost his access to Anima when we joined together. For most things, that’s unimportant–the power is ultimately meant to be a tool for communicating with the gods during the Union. However, Angelos and Apollo found a more creative use for it. You saw the results when we were chasing after him originally.”

Selina paused to consider, her scholarly mind hard at work. “So that was how he was able to take control of people–even Followers.”

“Exactly. Anima has a domineering tendency, it’s kind of like a failsafe the gods created so they’d actually listen to their own creations. Revan used it to scare the hell out of some demons, but Apollo went a different way. Regardless, these last Rune Circles let him use mine in place of his own.”

“Will it work?” she asked.

“We’re about to find out.” He looked up at his golden reflection. “Ready?” he asked.

“I am,” Apollo said, and his voice was the deep monotone of Angelos.

“Then let’s do this,” Ethan said, closing his eyes for a moment.

On the train, his spirit made a fist, then slammed it into the armrest of its throne. It did it over and over, like a terrible heartbeat, and Ethan could feel the power rippling out through the Astral. It was answered by the sound of scrambling feet, and roars of challenge.

The demons were coming.

Ethan tossed Selina onto a raised platform behind his spirit, where she’d be safe to use her abilities, then he drew his blades, waiting. There was a tense moment where he worried that a demon Champion would rise up from some unseen place, but Ulric had been confident it wouldn’t happen. “Avoid the sea,” was all he’d been able to say, and it seemed to prove true as the denizens of Potentia appeared.

They slithered from cracks in the stone, rose up from the mud, and poured out of caves. Dozens of them, but no Champions. Most were actually Dawn rank, with a slightly smaller number of Dusks. No real threat, and a good place to start.

Ethan leapt to meet their charge on one side, while the Fable train held the other flank. As he slashed and danced among demonic wolves, felines, snakes and insects, the train unleashed fiery devastation. Yet they were being careful.

Ethan could have annihilated them all with Twilight power. He could have let Revan simply burn them to ash, but that wasn’t the plan. Instead he injured them, weakened them, made them vulnerable. Apollo did the rest.

Golden light shot down like arrows, each one striking a demon, and leaving a thread of light connecting back to the Holy Chosen. Glowing runes appeared on their savage, demonic faces, and their wounds healed rapidly. Then they turned on their own kind.

Five, then ten, twenty, then fifty. Apollo’s power snapped out again and again. Ethan’s spirit enraged the creatures, preventing their retreat, while Apollo captured them in mind and body. Each one became his, and therefore, Ethan’s. Each one was another sliver of Dusk’s errant power, gathered together in one spot. An offering for The Throne.

Eventually, it was obvious Ethan wasn’t needed, and leapt up to join Selina as the Fable Train began its slow trek north, more and more demons drawn toward it every moment.

“You’re building a most-disturbing army, Ethan,” Selina said in an awed voice.

Ethan just shook his head. “No. I’m starting a crusade.”
