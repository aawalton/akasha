Pain radiated from Luke's chest, concentrated in the area around his heart, making it difficult to breathe. That same pain and discomfort then radiated outward into his arms and legs, seizing his muscles with cramps. While the episode of immense pain was debilitating, Elijah still wasn't far from death. That was the more important task. Not more so than Luke's own life, of course, but a quick check showed him it wasn't something that would kill him right away. It wasn't even his heart, not really.

Pushing through the pain and immobilization, Luke reached out with another thread and finished healing Elijah. Spending more mana was like pushing a nest of angry wasps through his mana channels while using a cheese grater to carve thin slices off his Meta-Heart.

"Luke!" someone shouted, but he ignored them.

If he wanted to get through this with both Elijah and himself still breathing on the other side, he needed to focus on the problem at hand. Sepsis, the insidious bastard killing Elijah, wasn't a simple thing to deal with even in a proper medical setting. Sure, there were antibiotics to deal with the infection, but you needed to catch it before it was too late. In Elijah's case, it wouldn't have mattered if he were in a hospital bed with IVs stuck in everywhere. The whole bleeding out thing didn't help either. In Luke's case, it was a matter of wielding enough mana to get the job done. In most cases, it would have taken a lot, but it was nothing he hadn't dealt with before. Problem was... Luke doubled over and went down to the floor of the school bus, clutching his chest as he let out a low whimper. Even so, he wasn't about to let a little pain take another guild member of The Band. Managing the pain as best he could, unable to numb it, Luke flooded Elijah's system with healing mana, chasing away the infection before treating the affected areas.

Following that, he braced himself and activated Weaver's Renewal to restore the torn-off limbs. That almost made him pass out. Darkness crept into his vision, and the roof of the bus spun around him, dimming from the tears streaming from his eyes. In other cases, he might've used the patient's own mana to heal them, but a hunch told him that would be a bad idea now. Even as he worked on Elijah, he had gone through a quick diagnostic check on himself and found nothing wrong, nothing at all. Nothing physical, that was.

Healing complete, Luke looked inward, not bothering to even attempt to get off the floor. His Meta-Heart was strained, and almost every inch of mana channels running through him looked battered and bruised. The pain had been like a much worse version of experiencing infernal mana in his system. That had been the first hint. This wasn't infernal mana, though. No, this was something much worse. Infernal mana, he could deal with, could even produce it on his own. Not that he'd tried it yet, but still. This was void mana. The mere thought was enough for the pain to return.

A sliver of that which made up the inner workings of the void being had made it across Luke's thread of mana and ended up in his system. In that moment, with the desperate struggle to get away, wanting to rescue Hannah, and Smudge's reappearance, Luke hadn't considered what that might mean. He'd noticed it then, that small speck of nothingness, but since it hadn't immediately killed him, it hadn’t been a priority.

Now, aware and hurting, Luke found them, both the memories of what had happened and the void mana itself. Void mana was not a great name for it, since it wasn't mana in the traditional sense, he thought. Jaw clenched against the pain, he examined it as best he could. Piece of the void, yes, part of an incomprehensible being of unfathomable strength and mysterious wants, yes, but the part Luke concentrated on while he spasmed on the floor of an old, yellow school bus was the immense power held within it. Withstanding and enduring pain for the benefit of power was nothing new to him. Could he perhaps harness the small piece of the void itself, now stored in his Meta-Heart? Maybe, just maybe, he could. It would take work, and a lot of it, but if his hunch was right, and he possessed the fortitude to seize this new opportunity, new heights would be reachable before too long. But to get there, there would be much suffering in the way, Luke imagined.

For the moment, he needed to calm himself and find a way to shield what he still decided to refer to as void mana from the rest of him, or healing the others would be impossible.

"Luke, what's wrong?" Milla asked, her face close enough to his that he felt the warmth of her breath.

Opening his eyes, he tried and failed to force a couple of words out of his mouth. Instead, he had to settle on giving a slow and pained thumbs-up that did little to wipe the worry off her face. In that moment, Luke needed to focus inward, not take care of the feelings of someone else, not even his sister. He closed his eyes again and calmed himself, staying far away from touching his mana. The void mana was there, like a discoloration on the inside of his Meta-Heart, almost as if it was hiding from his attention. Impossible, of course, since it didn't have sentience, but having a spot of nothingness stuck inside you was some pretty serious nightmare fuel. Even so, Luke kept his calm. He'd been in the void itself and lived to tell the tale. Now, the void was in him, in a manner of speaking, and he'd live through that too. Eager as he was to see what he could do with it, Luke instead used the method he'd brought out to keep Smudge from destroying the world and everything in it. Guardian Weave kept the void mana in place and prevented it from affecting any other part of Luke. That was the idea, anyway.

Once it was done, he moved mana through his mana channels with more hesitation than he wanted to admit. No pain. Relief washed over him, and Luke opened his eyes and gave Milla a big smile. "I'm back."

"What happened?"

"The void," Luke said, getting to his feet and shuffling over to the other injured Integrated before stopping and frowning. Looking back over at Priyah, past Milla, he asked, "Didn't they have that old woman healer with them? What happened?"

"She didn't make it," Milla said with a quick look out the window to where Nash stood, before shaking her head. "Lana tried to create a dungeon area spot, but couldn't do it, not yet. It's all too new for her. Anyway, that meant we couldn't get another healer to help."

"Oh," Luke said as his breathing turned a little more relaxed as he reached out with threads of mana to heal the remaining patients. "These guys will be fine, all of them."

"Thanks," Milla said. "But don't think you're off the hook explaining that whole twitching episode just now. Just casually dropping 'the void' isn't sufficient."

"Sure," Luke said. "I'll explain later. What about ISG? Did Ray break them up over here?"

Milla sighed and plopped down onto a seat. "As far as I can tell, they're all dead or running. The new guildmistress of ISG messaged me yesterday. Luke, they want a truce. An alliance, even. The new leadership is claiming that they didn't know anything about the Singers."

Luke chuckled and sat down next to his sister. "What?"

Nash entered the bus and sank to his knees next to Priyah, but was satisfied to just sit there, stroking her hair.

"It's what they're saying," Milla said, giving Luke side-eye.

He frowned. "You're not actually considering it?"

"Most of the US Army is gone, Luke. They attacked the Singers in Washington, Dallas, and San Diego in some sort of coordinated strike. Their casualty rate was damn near a hundred percent. Whatever is left of them is working in splinter groups. Most of Europe is under their control. Other than here in New York, we haven't made meaningful progress anywhere except in New Belgium and Japan. The Singers won't let up, and we're losing. I can even see it in the faces of those of us in The Band. People are scared."

"We'll go from Singer to Singer if we have to. An alliance with ISG is out of the question. It's their fault they're even here."

Being saved by the army had been a long shot from the start, but the relative calmness of the surrounding city turned eerie when Luke heard just how bad Earth was doing, like the silence was in anticipation of challenges to come, more than one such challenge won.

"You're starting to sound a lot like Curtis. Also, it's not a direct alliance with the remaining parts of ISG that's being proposed."

"What do you mean?" Luke asked.

"It's called The Earth Defense Pact. Guilds from all over the world have joined. Some have tight connections to their countries' militaries, but most are free agents like us. Phoenix Phalanx has already joined, along with several other US guilds. If we're going to beat them back, we have to work together. Now more than ever."

"Huh."

"And Luke?"

He leaned his head back, facing the ceiling of the bus as he drew in a deep breath. "Yeah?"

"The EDP, that's the shorthand for Earth Defense Pact... Well, they're asking for you. Your little stunt with their portal here made an... let's call it an impact."

"Well, damn."

"We're joining the EDP, Luke."

Luke sighed and shook his head, repeating himself. "Well, damn."
