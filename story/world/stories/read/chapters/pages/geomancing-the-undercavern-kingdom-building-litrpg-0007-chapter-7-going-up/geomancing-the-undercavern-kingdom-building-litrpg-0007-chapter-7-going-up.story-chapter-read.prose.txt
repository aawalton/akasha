Carmack led the group, forming a spiraling ledge as he walked up and around the spire. Behind him, the young lieutenant, twelve guardsmen, and two warlocks trailed close behind.

Despite being of a higher level than himself, Carmack doubted these men could take him in a fight. Not down here, surrounded by earth. Perhaps the lieutenant—Gorn could, if they were above the surface, he mused. The lieutenant was at least a rank higher than he was.

Still, I lack mana. A prolonged fight could turn deadly. I should tread carefully until I increase my reserves further.

Carmack had sensed that Captain Cella was more powerful than any of the lieutenants based on level and rank alone. But this man exuded potential. He needed to be tested, but his instincts told him he had made the right decision.

Before they even reached the cavern’s ceiling, it was obvious that things from above could get through. The spire climbed up and connected with the center of the cavern’s stone ceiling. And as they got closer, they spotted countless holes pierced through the slate-like rock.

Using his mana, Carmack sensed things moving up above, beyond his purview. There were dozens of them. He would have preferred waiting to take on such a threat, allowing his mana reserves to grow and gaining additional levels. However, he couldn’t leave something like this; it posed too much danger to the settlement.

With any luck, it was nothing more than beasts. But he feared running into another subterranean civilization like the rats. A threat of that magnitude would likely force them to flee their new home.

Reaching the top, Carmack placed a palm against the stone and pulsed mana through it. He was surprised to find that the rock was quite thin, and with little more than a thought, his Earthshaper spell carved out a hole.

“Come,” he whispered and stepped through the hole.

Once everyone was through, he closed the passage behind them. Protecting the settlement was more important than retreating. Even if there were other holes, they weren’t connected directly to the spire, and Carmack didn’t want to risk creating a direct passage to the people below.

“There's something up here with us,” one of the guardsmen said as he scanned their surroundings and drew his sword.

“Remain in formation,” Lieutenant Gorn commanded.

“Warlock?”

“On it, hero,” one of the warlocks said, magically producing a red orb in the center of his palm. The orb then flew up to hover above their heads, providing blood-red light.

Carmack had already seen the bones and figures in the distance, but he needed his men alert, even if it risked bringing attention to them.

“Careful of your step,” Carmack said, kicking bones out of his path.

“This is… not right,” a guardsman muttered with a grimace on his face.

“Keep it down, men,” Gorn commanded.

Carmack narrowed his gaze on the hulking forms ahead. Light provided clearer details and color, but he could see great distances and all the important outlines in the dark. These winged beasts had both fur and scales, with bald, beaked heads.

We definitely don’t have these on Avalock.

Turning to the party, the beast let out a gargling croak, and the rest of the men drew their weapons. But it didn’t attack.

“What now, hero?” Gorn murmured, holding a hand up to steady his men.

Carmack focused his senses throughout the cavern, picking up at least two dozen large beasts dotted throughout the chamber.

That sounds more like a warning than a call for attack. Still, we should tread carefully.

“Keep by me and stay quiet.”

“You heard the hero, men. Keep your mouths shut and stay close.”

Despite being hunched on all fours, the beasts were big and taller than a man, but Carmack didn’t detect monster cores in any of them.

They can’t be too strong, otherwise they would have formed cores.

Leading the group forward, he stepped through scattered bones, and the smell of festering corpses hung thick in the stale air.

Muffled gasps escaped the lips of a couple of guardsmen. Still, they pressed on, assurance hanging on their lieutenant and their hero.

He didn’t say anything, fearing he might scare the men. But Carmack hoped that once the red light of the warlock’s orb reached the beasts, his party might identify them for him.

The red light crept forward as they walked, and the hunched, feathered beasts were revealed. Crawling on all fours, they used the claws protruding from the pointed tops of their reptilian wings as front legs. Their heads were fleshy and bald with sharp beaks, but the rest of their faces were uncannily human.

“Carrion wyrms,” the warlock gasped at Carmack’s back.

“Why haven’t they attacked? Carmack said with a curiously raised brow as the wyrm croaked angrily again.

“Fear. These are only juveniles. It’s their brood mother you have to be careful of,” he said, looking around with fear.

“Don’t worry, warlock. I don’t sense anything around stronger than these guys.”

“That is good,” the warlock exhaled. “She must be out collecting food from the surface. From my understanding, they can spend weeks, and even months, filling their gut with tons of meat to bring back for their young. But make no mistake, that thing would kill us all if it found us here.”

“Tell me, can they dig?”

“Beg your pardon?”

“Can they dig?” Carmack repeated. “The carrion wyrms.”

“I’m no bird or dragon expert, but I don’t believe so.”

“Dragon?”

“Yeah, exactly. Dragons. Carrion wyrms are one of the weakest dragons, but technically, they’re still dragons. And while these little ones might be too scared to attack without their mother, she can destroy entire cities alone.”

“Gotcha.”

Dragons were not common, but they existed on Avalock, and Carmack knew enough about them to be wary.

“And so, are they intelligent?”

“Smarter than a beast but dumb for a dragon.”

“Tell me, warlock. Why do these powerful dragons lower themselves to eating carrion?”

“Like I said, I’m no expert. But I know dragons eat a lot. As powerful as they are, my understanding is that their unquenchable hunger makes them lots of enemies. It is not unheard of for powerful adventurers to slay dragons at the behest of kings and emperors.”

“So, you believe they eat carrion to avoid making themselves targets? Interesting.”

“Yeah, well, that’s my best bet. A hungry dragon can eat dozens of cattle in a single feed. So you can imagine how much chaos an entire family can cause if they build their nest on your land.”

“Undoubtedly.”

Dragons causing problems was nothing new to Carmack. The beasts were fierce competitors for valuable gemstones on Avalock, and he knew well not to cross them. But he couldn't just allow this nest to remain. Even if they mostly fed on carrion, he wasn’t about to take the risk that they wouldn’t grow hungry for his people.

There was another reason he needed them gone. The cavern they found was too good to be true. The warm air ventilating through the chamber and resources in near reach alone were too much to be left unexploited. And it seemed he had found the reason why.

Carmack knew he could seal up the holes in his geomancy as he had done elsewhere, but feared it wouldn't be enough to hold back a dragon, even a weak one.

He could have collapsed the ceiling either as they the chamber was directly above their cavern. The chances that the ceiling would collapse the floor in turn and rain down on their settlement was far too high.

And lastly, he didn’t like the idea of just killing the dragon’s spawn. Dragons were intelligent, and even if these were dumb by dragon standards, he didn’t want to risk pissing off their mother.

I’d rather not turn that thing’s fury against us.

There was one other option he toyed with, framing another beast for their deaths.

“Let us make our way around the beasts and see where this cavern leads.”

“Carefully,” the warlock warned. “They might be too afraid to attack now, but step too close, and you might change their mind.”

“Understood, warlock.”

“Follow the hero and keep your distance from the birds,” said Lieutenant Gorn.

Keeping a wide berth of the croaking and snarling beasts.

Carmack found footfall by a narrow path that wound through the cavern’s rock formations, the guardsmen close behind. Beady red eyes followed them in the dark, but none dared attack.

Soon, they reached a rocky ledge at the chamber’s far end and climbed up. From its vantage, Carmack spied rays of warm light piercing down in the distance.

That must be how the brood mother makes her way to the surface.

As they climbed over the rock's edge, he realized it wasn't the only one. Looking out, he realized they had gained vantage over an underground valley, streams of light peppered across it. Some were narrow cuts through the earth, others huge chasms exposing the cavern to acres of light.

He had thought the other caverns and chambers were big, but this dwarfed the rest. The other end must have been miles away. Dozens of pillars of rock held up the ceiling, though none were as large or impressive as the spire they had occupied. In the distance, he spotted wandering beasts and flying creatures. There were rivers, tunnels, and a vast body of water that extended into the darkness.

And in the far distance, he spotted tightly packed dots of light and thin streams of smoke.

A city? Not surprising considering the size of this place.

“This is…” the warlock trailed off.

“It's huge,” Gorn muttered. “I never imagined so much space below ground.”

Making contact with a friendly civilization could prove profitable, but we cannot risk that until we can defend ourselves.

“It's an opportunity, but one for another day. Come, let's move,” Carmack waved them on as he continued down the steep path of loose rock into the underground valley.

A manta ray-like beast flew overhead, barely a dozen meters away, and the party instinctively ducked.

“Do you know what that beast was, warlock?”

“No, haven’t a clue,” he said with deep, steadying breaths.

Interestingly, the surface people of this world are not well-versed in what lives beneath them.

“I don't mean to question your expertise, but what are we doing, hero?” The warlock said as Carmack began to walk again.

“Looking for a bigger fish.”

“A bigger fish? What in the seven moons are you on about?”

“We need plausible deniability, or rather, a scapegoat. Something we can blame for the deaths of this carrion wyrm’s spawn and, hopefully, lure it away from our home.”

“And what if it returns?”

“That depends entirely on the scapegoat. The perfect outcome would be that they kill one another. I doubt we can kill the dragon ourselves, but that all changes if the scapegoat can cause serious wounds to the wyrm.”

“And if it can't? You really think finding a beast that can fight a dragon is easy, hero?”

“I suppose we'll have to evacuate and find a new home.”

“Don’t be so nonchalant!”

Carmack didn’t reply, taking in the beauty of the enormous cavern. Beneath where light pierced through, shrubbery and even trees grew. Outside that, forests of giant mushrooms and herds of huge beasts marched the subterranean plains.

Carmack took note of it all. This cavern held many riches, but they were nowhere near as defensible. If they were to secure the wealth of this place, they would likely need the power to defeat armies.

“Look there,” Carmack pointed, attention returning to the task at hand. “Animal tracks, and they're big.”

“We should have brought trackers and scouts,” Gorn said.

“We should have, yes,” Carmack agreed. “But this wasn't my initial plan. If we fail to find anything, we'll go back and collect them.”

It wasn't out of stubbornness that Carmack didn't want to head back down. Developing their new home was a lot of work, and he couldn't waste time coming up here twice if it could be avoided.

“So, we just follow these tracks and hope for the best?”

“Something like that. Come on, let’s hurry. Who knows when that brood mother will return?”

The trail of large, clawed prints the size of wagon wheels continued around a rock bend to their left. Promisingly, the trail kept to areas of the cavern with high ceilings, convincing Carmack they were indeed following something big.

Soon, they reached a huge tunnel. The tunnel must have been at least eight or nine meters high and equally wide. But what was unusual about it was the rough, shattered edges of the stone dirt that made its walls.

This isn’t a natural formation. I have a feeling that whatever we’re following dug this into the cavern.

Burrowing beasts were hardly uncommon on Avalock. Living underground had a habit of encouraging those kinds of evolutions.

“Find cover and keep watch,” Carmack turned to his party. “I’ll be going in alone.”

“Are you sure that’s wise, hero?” Lieutenant Gorn said. “Me and my men are ready to accompany you anywhere you order.”

“It’s fine, lieutenant. It’s best that I go alone for this part.”

“The guardsmen have a point,” the warlock interjected. “If you get yourself killed, how are we going to get back down to the settlement?”

“I’m less likely to die alone. Whatever is in here is too big for us to handle. But perhaps I can get a better look without antagonizing it.”

“Right, as you say, hero,” Gorn nodded. “Come on, men, let’s find somewhere good to hide and keep an eye on the area.”

“Fine,” the warlock hissed. “Just don’t be stupid. I don’t want to die up here.”

Once alone, Carmack continued into the tunnel. He was no rogue, but through his geomantic magic, he could reduce the sounds of his steps against the cold, hard earth to almost nothing.

He also enveloped his body with his Stonekin spell. It wasn’t just a protective layer of armor, but camouflaging, since the spell covered his clothing and himself in rock identical to that around him.

Giant carcasses littered the ground ahead, easily bigger than the juvenile carrion wyrms.

It preys on big things, that’s good.

Even a dumb dragon should be smart enough to know whether or not the accused was big and powerful enough to eat its young. Now, he just had to assess its aggressiveness. If this beast didn’t attack and kill, it wouldn’t be a believable culprit, and the brood mother would go hunting for the true criminal.

Lying amongst huge bones, he spotted the Goliathan figure, its furred body gently rising and falling with breaths of sleep.

The beast was some kind of giant scorpion hybrid, at least four or five times bigger than the juvenile wyrms, with several sets of horns jutting out from its wolf-like face. Cloven hoofs marked its feet, and fur covered its giant legs, face, and underbody. However, its back and tail were covered in an insectoid carapace, with a scorpion pincer curling at the end of its tail. But most importantly, Carmack could sense the powerful monster core inside it.

Yeah, I think this beast might just do.
