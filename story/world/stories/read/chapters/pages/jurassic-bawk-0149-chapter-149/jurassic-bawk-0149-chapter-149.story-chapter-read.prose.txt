Charlemagne woke up with both the mother and the father of all headaches. He pulled himself to his feet, not noticing that his sense of balance had somehow been restored while he was unconscious. What he did notice, however, was the soft light reflecting from the stacks of gold piled high all throughout the chamber, which looked more like a storehouse than a bedroom. The yellow glint of the precious metal reminded the rooster of one of his favorite foods: corn.

It took Charlemagne a few moments to get his bearings. When he was more or less fully functional, he realized that he had several messages from the Squiggles.

Skills Increased:

Mana Surgery 2 -> 5

Congratulations, you have earned the Achievement “Tribial Brain Dablage”! You have destroyed more than twenty five percent of the neurons within your cerebellum. Attributes are ten percent more effective.

Congratulations, you have successfully completed Floor Three of the Delve Challenge with a grade of ‘B’! Please pass through the portal to receive your pending experience.

While the rooster was reviewing his notifications, a glowing doorway appeared. Charlemagne quickly hurried through it, worried that Labrissa would show up again to put him under her sway. He appeared back in his chambers within the Sun Queen’s castle to find Josephine and Phatagin in a heated argument. Ignoring them, he focused on his rewards instead.

Congratulations, you are now level 76.

Congratulations, you are now level 77.

Congratulations, you are now level 78.

Congratulations, you are now level 79.

Congratulations, you are now level 80.

Congratulations, you are now level 81.

Congratulations, you are now level 82.

Congratulations, you are now level 83.

Strength 1187 -> 1487Dexterity 1155 -> 1455Durability 1064 -> 1314Special 1047 -> 1297

The rooster’s body swelled with power as his Attributes rose almost thirty percent across the board. The incredible feeling that accompanied the burst of Attributes made Charlemagne wonder if, by the end of the Systemic Games, he might be able to eat the Sun Queen. After all, he was now more than three times stronger than when he entered the Delve, going just by Attributes alone. But Attributes alone were not the only rewards he had gleaned: he had also consumed a copy of the deity-forged sword Phosphor, upgraded his Core, evolved his Class, leveled some of his Skills, and acquired several Achievements. Taken together, his combat proficiency was closer to an order of magnitude greater than when he entered the Delve. How strong would he grow during the next events?

Charlemagne’s pleasant daydreams were rudely interrupted by the spat between his Squire and his Attendant.

“I still say that we try to make it to the throne room. The Sun Queen will be able to protect us,” Josephine insisted. “There are enemies in the hallways, yes, but you should be able to handle those.”

“You must be mad! The System message instructed all non-combatants to shelter in place! If we leave the safety of these chambers, we’ll both be butchered,” the pangolin retorted.

“Yes, but you’re a combatant. You are literally a Squire. Do you understand what Squires do? They fight alongside their Knights,” the Attendant argued.

“Bawk?” the rooster demanded, wondering what had gotten the two least useful members of his group all wound up.

“Did you not read the message from the System?” Josephine asked, sounding exasperated.

“Bawk,” Charlemagne stated as he pulled up the newest message from the Squiggles and read it.

Alert: the Elite Systemic Games have been compromised by anti-System Forces. A partial System lockout has been achieved. Certain System-granted abilities may be unavailable. All non-combatants should shelter in place. All combatants should report to the throne room to reinforce the Sun Queen and her guard and assist in destroying the anti-System Forces. Knights are expected to aid each other to the best of their abilities.

“Well if you’ve already seen it, then you must realize that we need to get to the throne room,” Josephine said.

Charlemagne said nothing for a few moments as he continued to look over the instructions.

“You’re reading it now for the first time, aren’t you?” the Attendant said with a heavy sigh. “Whatever. Just tell your cowardly Squire that we need to get a move on, would you?”

“Bawk!” Charlemagne agreed, confidently walking to the door and throwing it open with a quick bit of mana manipulation.

As the chamber opened, the rooster suddenly felt weaker again. The ambient mana, which had been thick inside his rooms, seemed to vanish almost entirely. It was almost like being back in space, where there was no air and the cold stole the heat from his bones. His Ignition Core sped up to compensate, meeting the needs of his augmented body, but that put the rooster on the block. He needed to find a new source of mana before his internal stores ran out.

“Bawk!” he complained as he wrapped a thin layer of mana around his body and moved out into the corridor. Despite the Squiggles stating that he might lose access to some of his Abilities, Charlemagne still felt in complete control over his mana, allowing him to slow the loss to a minimal amount.

Extending his senses outward, he detected two creatures up ahead. They seemed to be locked in combat, so Charlemagne made haste, planning to ambush them while they occupied each other’s attention. But, when he made a sharp turn to the right and spotted the pair, he realized that they were a Knight and his Squire. For a moment, the rooster’s heart froze: the pair looked an awful lot like the armored human that had killed his mates and offspring, since they were also humanoid bipeds that had grafted weapons and armor directly into their bodies. But upon closer examination, there were enough differences that Charlemagne did not feel the need to murder them on principle.

Although murder on principle was out, Charlemagne considered murdering the Knight and their Squire for other reasons, but ultimately decided against it. After all, the Squiggles had told him that he needed to help them, and eating other beings was generally considered unhelpful for some reason. Instead, the rooster prepared to assist the Knight and Squire, who were fighting back to back since they were outnumbered. Conscious of his inability to regenerate mana, Charlemagne decided to rely on his Advanced Fowl Arts to cut down the assailants.

There were four opponents, representing at least three different races. It was difficult to tell, since they all wore black suits that completely masked not only their features but also their mana signatures, assuming they had any. They were armed with a variety of bladed weapons: short blades, longer blades, blades on sticks, tiny blades made for throwing. One of the creatures even had blades in place of hands on two of its four arms. One of them screamed something in a harsh, guttural language as Charlemagne launched himself into the fray, vaulting off the wall and then using a mana shield to redirect his momentum at the last moment.

The anti-System fighter that Charlemagne targeted whirled at the last possible moment, raising a blade to meet the rooster’s razor-sharp beak tip. But Charlemagne’s beak was no longer there. A flap over his wings carried him to the far side of the combat, where one of his temporary allies had just blocked a particularly vicious overhead blow that knocked it off balance, preventing it from counterattacking and leaving it open to a follow-up attack. The rooster’s beak thrust itself deep into the side of the assailant, drawing blood. But it was not deep enough.

The rooster quickly recovered his stance and leapt into the air again as his opponent stumbled away in shock. This time, he landed on the creature’s shoulders, locking down its arms while severing the muscles, tendons, and arteries near the armpit. The already wounded creature hissed in agony, but could not mount an effective counterattack with its arms pinned.

Its ally, however, could. Charlemagne’s world exploded into white hot pain as a blade slipped between his ribs and punctured one of his lungs. Incensed, he squawked as he gripped his victim even tighter before unleashing a flurry of pecks into his opponent’s skull, ignoring another heavy stab as he rode the ruined carcass down to the ground before rolling away. He whirled around to face the creature that had wounded him twice, only to find that one of his allies had taken advantage of the distraction to disembowel the black-clad fighter.

“Bawk!” he complained, feeling robbed of his vengeance.

“Do not worry,” the strange cyborg-like creature answered calmly in a deep voice. “I am sure that your thirst for violence will be slaked before the end. Let us assist my Squire in the meantime.” With that, the Knight turned and began to aid its comrade against the remaining foes, producing a spike from his forearm that he used to parry a hasty horizontal slash from the black-clad assailant on his left.

After taking a moment to seal up the puncture in his lung with a small mana patch, Charlemagne once again leapt into the air. This time, however, he didn’t bother with a feint, choosing instead to invert himself midair. The maneuver allowed him to claw the enemy’s face with his talons while simultaneously taking out its groin with a well-placed peck. There was no way he was going to let the other Knight or his Squire get another killing blow.

Righting himself, Charlemagne flapped his wings and caught the final opponent off guard. Having seen Charlemagne go down with two deep stab wounds, the assailant no doubt expected a two-on-two battle. Now, instead of an ally guarding its flank, there was nothing but a corpse…and an angry rooster. Before it could even lift a blade in defense, Charlemagne’s talons had penetrated its strange, cloth-like armor and shredded both jugular veins.

With a final flex, the rooster sheared through the creature’s neck entirely, sending its head flying through the air. It bounced off the Knight.

“Bawk,” the rooster announced with satisfaction.

“It was not a competition, but I thank you, good Ser Knight, for your assistance,” the other Knight intoned. “We are heading to the throne room, but our Attendant did not survive the ambush. Do you know the way?”

“Bawk,” Charlemagne answered, looking around and wondering where exactly he was. Thankfully, Josephine chose that moment to poke her head around the corner. She grimaced at the sight of four fresh corpses, but quickly recovered.

“Yes, I know the way to the Sun Queen’s throne room, Ser Knight. Could you share your name? I am Josephine, one of the Attendants.”

“I am Teem. My Squire is Teem,” the Knight responded with a nod. “Well met. And you, Ser Knight, might I know the name of the creature who assisted us?”

“Bawk,” the rooster answered.

“Well, enough pleasantries. Let us be off,” Teem the Knight suggested.

Charlemagne took a moment to cast out his mana senses, but detected nothing in the vicinity. Satisfied, he allowed more mana to flow to his wounds, healing them rapidly at the expense of his available mana.

“Bawk?” he inquired.

“I do not possess a means of locating these anti-System fighters from a distance. In fact, they appear to be incredibly difficult to detect in general. In addition, my capabilities have been somewhat….degraded…by this ‘partial System lockout’.”

“I hate to say this, Charlemagne, but you know who could really, really, help us here,” Josephine suggested.

“Bawk?” the rooster guessed.

“No, not Phatagin, he’s literally cowering around the corner.”

“Bawk?” Charlemagne asked.

“No, not Phasima either, although I’m hoping that she made it back here with the person I’m thinking of…”

Charlemagne froze as he recalled who he had last seen the Vessarian with.

“Bawwwwwk…” he groaned, his voice so low it was barely audible.

At that moment, a familiar voice echoed through the corridor.

“Well, fancy meeting you here. Although your mana signature is pretty unique,” Phasima laughed as she floated up to the quartet.

“Phasima!” Josephine exclaimed. “How did you get here? I figured that Labrissa would have…you know…kept you around?”

“Oh, yeah,” Phasima chuckled. “She would have, if I hadn’t killed her first.”
