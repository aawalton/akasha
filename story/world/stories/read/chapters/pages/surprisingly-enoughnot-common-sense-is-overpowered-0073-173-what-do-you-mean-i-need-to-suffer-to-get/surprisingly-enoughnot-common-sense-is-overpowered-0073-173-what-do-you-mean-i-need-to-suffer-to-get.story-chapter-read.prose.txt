[Name: Ji Ning

Spiritual Root: Mid-grade Spiritual Root

Attributes: Five Elemental Attributes

Talent: Extreme Space-Time Sensitivity

Realm: 6th Stage of Qi Condensation (113/3200), Marrow Refining (78/600)

Cultivation Methods: Pure Qi Sutra (3rd Floor), Jade Skin and Ice Meridians (3rd Level)

Martial Arts: Bone Forging Fist (Great Accomplishment: 222/400), Shadow Steps (Mastery), Turtle Breathing (Mastery), Pure Eyes (Starting: 34/300), Falling Leaf Spear Technique (Penetration), Spiral Arrow

Spells: Small Cloud Rain Technique (Mastery), Soil Refining Technique (Great Accomplishment: 312/400), Scorching Purge (Great Accomplishment: 40/400), Golden Finger (Minor Accomplishment: 166/200), Hidden Ice Mist (Great Accomplishment: 9/400), Withering and Flourishing Technique (Great Accomplishment: 219/400), Fireball (Minor Accomplishment: 51/200), Crimson Flame Control (1st Level: Great Accomplishment: 13/400), Soul Ferrying Technique (Starting)]

Ning glanced at the panel, checking his current progress.

Most of his techniques remained at the same level. His cultivation progress was moving steadily, though he had encountered a minor bottleneck in body refining due to the lack of higher-grade ice-attributed materials.

The most notable improvement was undoubtedly his Crimson Flame Control. He had already reached Great Accomplishment in the first stage.

Aside from his consistent practice, the rapid progress likely had something to do with Fenghuang.

Most alchemists possessed their own special flames for refining. At lower levels, these could be flames produced through martial techniques, while at higher levels, they might be rare spiritual flames born from heaven and earth. Another common method was the use of beast flames, unique fires produced by spiritual beasts.

A White Flame Raven’s flame was yang-attributed, carrying properties such as purification. Among beast flames, it was considered high quality.

Crimson Flame Control was fundamentally a technique focused on manipulating different kinds of spiritual flames. Practicing it with a high-quality beast flame significantly accelerated Ning’s progress.

As expected, entrusting the protagonist with a task rarely went wrong. Fenghuang was exceptionally well suited for alchemy.

With that thought, Ning reached out and petted the raven perched on the bed.

Fenghuang simply raised his head and remained silent. After spending the past few days with his human, he was… somewhat exhausted.

As a proud spiritual beast, Fenghuang adhered strictly to the principle of constant progress. Despite his young age, he had already reached the mid-stage of Qi Condensation. Naturally, he had assumed that training alongside his new human would be manageable.

He had been mistaken.

For the past week, Fenghuang had tried to follow Ning's routine without complaint. At first, anyway. After all, how difficult could a human’s training be?

The answer, as it turned out, was: very.

Fenghuang had pushed himself harder and harder, unwilling to fall behind his own beast master. Losing was not an option.

Unfortunately, that only made things worse.

Even for a diligent spiritual beast, Fenghuang had to admit that his master was something of a lunatic, maintaining an unrelenting and frankly unreasonable training schedule.

Ning, completely unaware that his contracted beast had been silently judging him, took out a Beast Raising Pill and handed it over.

Fenghuang accepted it immediately.

The Beast Raising Pill functioned similarly to Qi Gathering Pills for cultivators but was specifically tailored for spiritual beasts.

Normally, such pills would be a considerable expense. However, as an inner sect disciple, Ning received two high-quality cultivation pills each month from the sect.

This was a significant benefit. Even poorer disciples could accelerate their cultivation with pills, allowing them to progress far faster than through absorption alone.

At the very least, Ning had already seen quite a few advantages to being an inner sect disciple.

After feeding Fenghuang, Ning tidied himself up and prepared to head out.

Today, he would perform his first alchemy refinement.

...

“Master, you're awake?” Ning asked, unable to hide his surprise. His master waking up on her own was… unexpected.

Hearing the surprise in her disciple's voice, Ling Xin snorted. “Why do you make it sound like I'm some sleepy old woman who can't even wake up on time?”

“Well…”

Ning wanted to argue that, in terms of behavior, Ling Xin certainly acted like one sometimes, but he knew better than to take that risk.

Ling Xin folded her arms, looking pleased with herself. “Besides, today is your first attempt at alchemy. How could I, as your master, possibly miss my disciple's first pill refinement?”

“Then I'm fortunate to have such a dedicated master,” Ning replied with a faint smile.

At least in this regard, Ling Xin was a very qualified master.

“In our alchemy lineage, a disciple's first refinement is traditionally witnessed by their master,” Ling Xin explained. After a brief pause, she added, “There's also a practical reason. Beginners make mistakes without even realizing it. Sometimes they ruin the ingredients, sometimes the cauldron, and occasionally, the entire room.”

Ning considered that for a moment before nodding. “That makes sense.”

“Don't worry, I'll be here if anything goes wrong.” Ling Xin gestured toward the cauldron. “Just remember to stay calm. Trying to force every little change usually makes things worse.”

“Understood. Then I'll be troubling you, Master.”

With that, Ning turned his attention toward the Origin Spirit Cauldron resting on its stand. It looked fairly ordinary at a glance, but considering how much he'd spent on it, he was glad to finally put it to use.

For his first attempt, Ning had chosen to refine a Fasting Pill.

It was among the easiest pills to make, with relatively stable ingredients and a forgiving margin for error. More importantly, it was genuinely useful, allowing cultivators to go several days without food, which meant there was always a market for it.

Besides, Ning knew better than to aim too high on his first attempt. Trying to refine something like a Spirit Replenishing Pill right away would only be asking for trouble.

He began by preparing the ingredients.

The basic spiritual grain required its outer husk to be removed without disturbing the qi within. Ning coated it with a thin layer of spiritual energy and carefully peeled away the husk, his movements steady and precise from years of handling spiritual plants.

Ling Xin watched from the side, giving an approving nod.

“Your control is quite good. Most beginners damage the qi channels while removing the husk, which lowers the quality of the pill before refinement even begins.”

“Farming has its merits,” Ning replied.

This was another reason he'd chosen the Fasting Pill. Basic spiritual grain was something he'd handled countless times, and most Fasting Pill recipes used it as a foundational ingredient.

Next came the Cloudwheat.

Ning separated the fibrous body from the essence nodes, carefully isolating the dense clusters where spiritual qi had accumulated. The process required some attention, but it was familiar enough that he completed it without much difficulty.

Ling Xin remained quiet until he finished.

“Not bad. Let's see how you handle the actual refinement.”

Ning nodded and turned toward the cauldron.

He inserted the spirit stones and activated its formation. A steady warmth spread through the chamber as the Heat Stabilization Array began regulating the internal temperature.

Once everything was ready, Ning ignited his Crimson Flame.

The flame appeared small and stable, though its edges were slightly stiff, with the flow of qi not yet entirely smooth.

Ling Xin noticed immediately. “You're holding it too tightly. Relax your control a little.”

Ning adjusted his qi, causing the flame to loosen and spread.

“Not that much. You still need to guide it.”

He made another adjustment, and this time the edges gradually smoothed out.

“There, that's better.” Ling Xin nodded. “Remember that feeling. Let the flame flow naturally, then guide it where you need it.”

Ning committed the sensation to memory. Having an expert point out his mistakes immediately was certainly convenient.

Once the flame was stable, he guided it into the cauldron.

The temperature rose steadily, with the Heat Stabilization Array compensating for minor inconsistencies in his control. Ning could immediately feel the difference compared to maintaining the temperature entirely by himself.

He added the prepared spiritual grain.

Under the heat, the grains gradually softened and began to dissolve, releasing the spiritual qi stored within. Thin strands of medicinal essence rose through the chamber and gathered toward the center.

Once the grain essence had been extracted, Ning added the Cloudwheat.

As the two essences came into contact, they resisted each other, creating faint turbulence within the cauldron.

Ning reduced the heat near the bottom while maintaining the temperature at the center. The convection pattern shifted, allowing the lighter Cloudwheat essence to rise while the spiritual grain essence remained below.

After a few moments, the turbulence began to settle.

“Good,” Ling Xin remarked. “Adjusting the conditions instead of forcing the essences together was the right choice.”

Ning nodded slightly, keeping his attention on the cauldron.

It seemed all that time he'd spent assisting Su Fan was finally paying off.

With the two essences stabilized, he moved on to the Morning Dew Essence, introducing it carefully through a thin thread of spiritual qi.

The previously separated essences began moving toward each other, with the Morning Dew Essence acting as a bridge between their different qi signatures. Gradually, the two layers started to merge.

“Now you're entering the Gather Liquid stage,” Ling Xin explained. “Since you haven't developed spiritual sense yet, you'll have to rely on what you can sense through your qi. Make sure the essences have completely merged before moving on.”

Ning followed her instructions, adjusting the flame whenever he sensed an imbalance. The essences continued merging until they eventually formed a single mass of medicinal liquid.

Only after confirming that it had stabilized did he proceed to the next step.

Spinning Condensation.

The Crimson Flame began rotating around the medicinal liquid, applying pressure from different directions as the mass slowly compressed.

“Be careful here,” Ling Xin warned. “The outside will solidify before the center. If you compress too quickly, you'll trap unstable medicinal liquid inside.”

Ning nodded and gradually increased the rotation.

At first, everything seemed to be going smoothly. The medicinal liquid compressed steadily, and its outer layer began taking shape.

Then the exterior started hardening faster than he'd expected.

Ning immediately tried to adjust the flame, but the imbalance had already formed. The outside continued to solidify while the liquid within remained unstable, and before he could correct it, the entire structure collapsed.

The half-formed pill broke apart inside the cauldron.

Ling Xin let out a quiet hum. “And there it goes.”

Ning stared at the failed mass for a moment.

“The outside solidified before the center.”

“Exactly. You rushed once you thought you were close.” Ling Xin pointed toward the remains. “Next time, give the moisture and unstable qi inside enough time to escape before allowing the outer layer to harden.”

Ning nodded thoughtfully. That was certainly more useful than simply knowing he'd failed.

Under Ling Xin's watchful gaze, he recorded his observations in his notebook before cleaning the cauldron and preparing another set of ingredients.

The second refinement began much like the first, with Ning carefully extracting the essences from the spiritual grain and Cloudwheat before introducing the Morning Dew Essence.

This time, the earlier stages went more smoothly. He already knew how the ingredients would react, and the medicinal liquid formed with far less turbulence.

Soon, he reached Spinning Condensation again.

Ning guided the Crimson Flame around the medicinal liquid, keeping the rotation and pressure lower than before.

“Good. Hold it there,” Ling Xin instructed.

The liquid slowly compressed, with faint vapor rising from its surface as excess moisture escaped through the cauldron's formation.

Ning resisted the temptation to increase the flame, even as the outer layer began to solidify.

“Watch the center,” Ling Xin reminded him.

He maintained the flame and waited, carefully observing the changes through his spiritual qi. After several breaths, the movement within the medicinal liquid gradually settled.

“Now,” Ling Xin said.

Ning increased the flame, allowing the medicinal mass to solidify. This time, the outer and inner portions stabilized together, forming a more uniform structure.

Ling Xin watched closely before giving an approving nod.

“That's it. Keep the heat even.”

Ning maintained the flame until the structure had fully stabilized.

“Acceptable,” Ling Xin said. “Now, Lock the Spirit.”

Ning immediately executed the technique, guiding spiritual energy toward the forming pill to seal the medicinal essence within before it could disperse.

“Good. Remember the timing. Lock too early, and the pill won't have finished stabilizing. Wait too long, and you'll already have lost part of the medicinal essence.”

Ning continued maintaining the technique until the pill finally settled, its internal qi becoming stable.

After examining it for another moment, Ling Xin nodded.

“Alright, you can collect it now.”

Ning activated the Collecting the Elixir technique, sending a thread of spiritual power into the cauldron. A low, resonant hum sounded from within before the lid opened and a single pill rose into the air.

He caught it and immediately examined his handiwork.

The pill had a faint golden hue, with a smooth surface and no visible cracks or discoloration. More importantly, the spiritual qi within felt stable.

A complete Fasting Pill.

Ling Xin stepped closer and took the pill from his hand, turning it between her fingers as she examined it.

“Hmm. Around forty percent purity,” she said. “Your refinement is still rough, especially during condensation, and you lost some medicinal essence while Locking the Spirit.”

She handed the pill back to him with a smile.

“But for your second attempt? Very good.”

Ning looked down at the pill in his hand, unable to keep a smile from forming.

Finally, he had taken his first step toward becoming a drug lord.

“I couldn't have done it without your guidance, Master.”

Ling Xin seemed quite pleased with that answer, while Ning found a place to sit and took several deep breaths.

Alchemy was more exhausting than he'd realized.

“You may be tired, but we're not quite finished yet,” Ling Xin said. “There's something else I want to teach you.”

“Huh?” Ning raised an eyebrow. He'd thought completing his first pill refinement would be the end of today's lesson.

“Now is the perfect time to teach you the technique I mentioned before.” Ling Xin smiled. “The Medicine King Sutra.”

Medicine King Sutra?

The name sounded a little underwhelming, but that wasn't the point.

“Master, I'm not exactly in the best condition right now. Are you sure this is the right time to learn a new technique?”

Comprehending a technique usually required a refreshed mind, or at least one that wasn't already exhausted from two rounds of pill refinement.

“Normally, you'd be right, but this technique is a little different,” Ling Xin answered without looking up, her hands already busy with something.

Ning noticed that she was holding six formation plates, three in each hand. Their surfaces were covered in inscription lines so densely packed that he could barely make sense of them.

He looked at the plates, then at Ling Xin, before looking back at the plates.

“…Master, may I ask what those are?”

“Sit back down. I'll explain.”

Ning decided that further persuasion was probably pointless and obediently returned to his seat.

Ling Xin arranged the six plates around him, placing two at his sides, two behind him, and the remaining pair at an angle in front. Ning tried to follow the arrangement, but couldn't immediately recognize the formation.

She crouched to adjust one of the plates, examining its position with the same care she normally reserved for her cauldron.

“I've been waiting for you to successfully refine your first pill,” she said. “This is the best opportunity to establish the foundation of the technique.”

“Why now, specifically?”

“Because of what's left over from your refinement.” Ling Xin gestured toward the cauldron. “When you refine a pill, some of the spiritual qi involved undergoes a transformation as the medicinal essences are extracted, combined, and stabilized. The resulting qi is called danyuan qi, though most people simply call it alchemical qi.”

Ning's interest immediately rose.

“Alchemical qi?”

He had a rough idea of what she meant. Beyond the five attributes and yin-yang qi that formed the basis of the world, countless other varieties of qi existed, either occurring naturally or produced through cultivation and refinement.

Alchemical qi was presumably one of the latter.

“Unlike ordinary spiritual qi, danyuan qi carries traces of the refinement that produced it,” Ling Xin explained. “The Medicine King Sutra allows an alchemist to gather this qi and use it for their own cultivation.”

She sat down across from him before continuing.

“These days, most cultivators call it the Alchemy King Technique. Medicine King Sutra is the original name passed down through our lineage, but the newer name became more common over time.”

“I see.” Ning nodded.

“In ancient times, the greatest alchemists were known as Medicine Kings. They spent much of their lives refining pills, which naturally left them with less time to cultivate. This technique allowed them to benefit from the refinement process itself.”

Ning thought about that for a moment.

“So every pill they refined also helped improve their cultivation?”

“Exactly. Even a failed refinement produces some danyuan qi, though the amount depends on the grade and complexity of the pill.”

Ning was beginning to see why this technique was so valuable.

That sounds suspiciously like a cheat.

“The Medicine King Sutra is one of the four great techniques passed down by our founder, Li Changsou, and the core technique of our alchemy lineage,” Ling Xin continued proudly. “It's also why I waited until your first successful refinement before teaching it to you.”

She tapped one of the formation plates.

“The danyuan qi produced during your first successful refinement is especially compatible with you. We call it primordial danyuan qi, and using it to establish the technique makes future circulation much easier.”

Now Ning understood why she had insisted on waiting.

“The formation will gather that qi before it disperses,” Ling Xin explained. “But first, we need somewhere to store it.”

“The dantian?”

“No. Until you've learned to control danyuan qi properly, mixing it directly into your primary circulation could interfere with your Pure Qi.” Ling Xin pointed toward his chest. “Instead, we're going to open an acupoint and use it as a separate reservoir.”

Ning's expression grew serious.

Opening an acupoint was much more difficult than simply circulating qi through another meridian. Acupoints could serve as independent nodes for gathering and storing qi, but forcing one open carelessly could damage the surrounding pathways.

“Which acupoint?”

“Zhongting, located near the center of your chest, just below the sternum. Its position makes it suitable for the Medicine King Sutra's circulation route while keeping the danyuan qi separate from your main cultivation.”

Ning nodded thoughtfully. Having a separate reservoir would allow him to accumulate alchemical qi without disturbing his normal cultivation.

Just as Ling Xin finished explaining, the door opened and Ling Yinran stepped inside, carrying a small jade bottle.

“Aunt, I brought the pill you asked for.”

“Perfect timing.” Ling Xin accepted the bottle and tipped a single pill into her palm before holding it out to Ning. “Here, swallow this.”

Ning examined the pill curiously.

“What is it?”

“An Opening Acupoint Pill. My master refined it specifically for this, so don't waste it.”

Ning's opinion of the pill immediately changed.

Anything personally refined by Grandmaster Fu Xuan probably had enough zeros attached to its price to make him uncomfortable.

He swallowed it without further hesitation.

The effect was almost immediate. Heat surged through his chest as the medicinal energy spread through his body before gathering around a single point near its center.

“Normally, someone at your cultivation level wouldn't attempt to forcibly open an acupoint,” Ling Xin explained as she moved behind him and placed a hand against his back. “Fortunately, you have me.”

The six formation plates activated, their inscriptions lighting up as the residual danyuan qi from his refinement began gathering around him instead of dispersing into the surroundings.

At the same time, Ling Xin's spiritual qi entered his body through her palm.

Ning immediately noticed the difference between her control and his own. Each strand of qi followed a precise route through his body, passing through his meridians without disturbing the surrounding pathways.

“I'm going to guide you through the Medicine King Sutra's circulation route,” Ling Xin said. “Don't resist my qi. Try to follow it with your own.”

Ning concentrated on the unfamiliar flow, but before he could properly adjust, a sharp pain erupted in his chest.

The Zhongting acupoint reacted violently as the medicinal power of the Opening Acupoint Pill gathered around it. His breathing faltered, and he had to force himself to remain still.

“Focus, Ning. Follow my qi and guide yours along the same route,” Ling Xin instructed calmly.

He gritted his teeth and concentrated through the pain.

Ling Xin's qi advanced toward the acupoint in controlled waves, applying pressure before withdrawing slightly and pressing again. Ning tried to match her rhythm, adjusting his own qi whenever she changed direction.

“Don't rush. Your body needs time to adapt to the new pathway,” Ling Xin reminded him. “If we force it open too quickly, the surrounding meridians could be damaged.”

Another surge of medicinal energy erupted from the Opening Acupoint Pill, sending a fresh wave of pain through his chest.

This definitely wasn't part of my plans for my first alchemy session.

Ling Yinran, who had been watching from the side, seemed to notice his struggle.

“Junior Brother, you know, even the first stage of the Medicine King Sutra can improve how efficiently you absorb medicinal pills,” she remarked. “You'll also be able to accumulate danyuan qi for your cultivation and future breakthroughs.”

Ning heard her, but he was too busy concentrating to respond.

Then she added, “Think of how many spirit stones you'll save.”

His focus sharpened immediately.

Ling Yinran nodded to herself in satisfaction. She knew her junior brother well enough by now.

Saving spirit stones.

That was all Ning needed to hear.

He steadied his breathing and concentrated on matching the rhythm of Ling Xin's qi. Once he stopped instinctively resisting the pressure, he found it easier to follow her guidance, gradually learning to support the circulation with his own qi.

The resistance around Zhongting began to weaken.

“Good, just like that,” Ling Xin encouraged him. “Keep following my lead.”

Ning guided his qi through another cycle, and this time, something shifted deep within his chest.

The resistance that had held firm until now finally gave way. A surge of spiritual qi rushed through the newly opened pathway, causing his entire chest to jolt.

Then he felt it.

A small reservoir had formed within his body, connected to the circulation route Ling Xin had established but separate from his primary cultivation.

The danyuan qi gathered by the formation immediately flowed toward it, settling into the newly opened Zhongting acupoint.

Ning carefully observed the unfamiliar qi as it accumulated, forming a small but stable reserve.

One by one, the formation plates dimmed, and Ling Xin finally withdrew her hand from his back.

“There. It's done.”

Ning managed to remain sitting upright for approximately one more second before the exhaustion finally caught up with him.

He collapsed backward.
