Chapter 45

Milena and Sari – Part two

Mai leaned against the railing of the deck, staring toward the horizon where the moon reflected on the water.

She needed to breathe. Her mind was still filled with the screams from the interrogation, the words she didn’t fully understand, the secrets, and also the emptiness left behind after each mission once the chaos subsided a little.

She looked back and, in the distance, detected the silhouettes of the other two ships that had also been part of the mission. The coasts of North Korea had disappeared hours ago, and thanks to the magical camouflage system, they had not been detected. Now, in international waters, they would be re-entering Japanese territory within minutes.

Behind her, she heard footsteps. Her instincts tensed for a second before relaxing: she recognized the voices.

“I thought you’d stay below,” said Milena, her voice tired but firm.

Mai turned. The two sirens were without their gear, obviously having changed and cleaned up. Mai remembered Sari almost covered in blood during the operation. Now Sari sat atop a supply crate, calm, and Milena rubbed her arms to warm up.

Over the past months, Mai had become quite close to the two, and in fact, she got along best with them. Both had also been recent additions to Wishper, and Mai, being the last fey to join the Asian team, had helped them form a small, tight-knit unit where it was normal to share hotel rooms, bunks, or tents during missions. That friendship had even led to a certain intimacy at times, though Mai had tried not to interfere too much, fearing a misunderstanding between the two girls. She cared for them, and so she preferred not to disrupt their relationship.

“I couldn’t stay there,” Mai replied. “I needed some air…”

Sari let out a soft laugh, without joy. “Good luck with that. Not even the sea is quiet tonight. Probably we will have nightmares tonight.”

Mai nodded, and Milena leaned beside her, looking toward the horizon.

“We received a message a few minutes ago,” she said. “From the Nekyia. And also from the Baltimore.”

Mai glanced at her sideways. “What happened?”

“Nothing bad,” Sari lifted her gaze, her expression tired. “All operations… succeeded. The objectives were neutralized.”

“That means…” Mai didn’t finish the sentence.

“That it’s over,” Milena said in a low voice. “At least this part.”

Silence settled between the three, broken only by the sound of waves hitting the hull. Sari ran a hand over her face, exhaling. Milena, however, kept staring at the horizon. Her jaw trembled slightly, though it didn’t seem to be from the cold.

“Are you okay?” Mai finally asked.

Milena took her time to respond. When she did, her voice was barely a whisper: “We made it. After everything…” She broke off, lowering her head. And then she cracked. Tears more salty than the waves ran down her face.

Mai stayed still. She didn’t know whether to speak or leave her be, but finally she brushed Milena’s back, she was shaking. Mai had always seen Milena as the stronger of the two, the one who stayed composed even when everything fell apart. Milena’s physique gave her the air of an action girl, contrary to Sari, who seemed much sweeter, playful, and cheerful.

Sari got up slowly and hugged Milena from behind. She didn’t say anything, simply held her, stroking her wet hair. Then she turned her toward herself and, without hesitation, kissed her. It was a brief, sincere gesture, full of both relief and sadness at the same time.

Mai looked away, but a smile escaped her. This was neither spectacle nor drama, it was simple, something she had grown used to seeing with them. Amid so much horror, there was still room for affection. After all, the two had joined Wishper when they had lost their two lovers, seeking answers to what had happened. And in the past few weeks, they had found some answers and, in a way, a measure of retribution for their loss.

But the truth was that neither of them had ever been violent unless it was in self-defense. These last few months had been a new experience for both of them.

They had been together for decades... Mai could hardly imagine how much they had shared. But the truth was that she felt close to them, just as she had on the day she met them for some reason.

Milena and Sari had also felt a little strange around her, but as the weeks went by, they got used to it.

Then the missions and the shared adrenaline rush almost made them feel like they had been together longer than they actually had.

When Sari pulled back, she wiped Milena’s tears with her thumbs. “It’s okay, love,” she whispered. “It’s okay.”

Milena nodded, though her shoulders still trembled. “Sorry,” she murmured. “It’s just… I thought we wouldn’t make it. I thought one of us…” She broke off again, taking a deep breath. “I’m not cut out for this shit.”

“And yet we’re still alive,” Sari replied, trying to smile. “That’s already rare.”

Mai turned to them. “You’re right. Rarely does anyone come out of something like this with their soul intact.”

"Do you think we’re still whole?" Sari asked, raising an eyebrow.

Mai didn’t answer. She just shrugged. "Maybe not. But if you can still cry and hold each other, you’ve got more than most."

The two sirens looked at each other, and for a moment, all the fatigue seemed to dissolve. Sari took Milena’s hand and squeezed it, saying nothing.

The sea continued to roar, but it no longer seemed hostile.

Mai leaned again on the railing and sighed. She thought about everything they had been told, the years they had spent together, hiding between worlds, surviving a century already. She admired them.

In a world where nothing lasted, they had refused to separate.

No one spoke for a long while. Only the sound of the wind and the rhythmic slap of the waves filled the space.

For the first time in a long while, Mai allowed herself to think that maybe, just for one night, they were safe.

The wind had softened. The waves struck the hull with an almost hypnotic rhythm, and the distant lights of the other two ships twinkled like fireflies lost in the surface fog. The three remained silent for a while, each caught in her own thoughts.

It was Sari who broke the calm.

"Even so," she said, crossing her arms, "this doesn’t end here. Yes, we dealt a heavy blow… but the real culprits weren’t at any of the bases. None of the high priests, nor the mid-level leaders of the Star Worshipers, were captured. At least that was what we hear in the command deck."

Mai looked up, unsurprised.

"None? How about in the other operation overseas?"

"None," Sari repeated, frowning. "They disappeared before they arrived. As if they knew we were coming."

"But many from the Octogonus Circle fell," Milena intervened, in a calmer tone, though fatigue was evident in her voice, "too many, actually. Some of the most important. And that’s no small matter."

Mai nodded slowly.

"The Octogonus Circle…" she repeated. "I always thought they were involved in something bigger than simple terrorism."

Sari sighed, adjusting the hair the wind pushed into her face.

"They were. And now, with what happened on land, their network will be broken for years. But that doesn’t stop that kind of people…not for a while at least."

"It never does," Mai murmured, lowering her gaze, thoughtful. "And the rescued? The ones in the facilities?"

Milena leaned again on the railing, looking toward the lights moving in the distance.

"They’ve already started medical evaluations. Many are in shock, but they’re alive. The Inoue Section and Fu’s faction offered to take care of them."

"Really?" Mai raised an eyebrow. "Inoue doesn’t usually get involved in joint operations."

"Probably right now there’s some meeting between foreign ministers and the ones in charge to sort this out," Sari said.

"Precisely because of that," Milena said. "This time, it’s not just any mission. And Fu’s faction has the resources to deal with those who… changed too much during captivity."

Mai nodded. She had heard of those cases. Feys or espers subjected to cult or clandestine laboratory experiments, returning with altered bodies, fragmented minds. Some didn’t even remember who they were.

Silence returned for a few seconds. The waves broke against the bow, and the sound of the engine vibrated under their feet.

"Mile," Mai said after a while. "Down there… I heard something. The interrogators mentioned a name. Project AFT."

Both sirens looked at each other. Milena squinted. Sari lowered her head, as if the breeze had suddenly grown heavier.

"What do you know about that?" Mai insisted.

Milena took a moment to respond.

"AFT…" she began. "There are no official records. Just rumors. But the rumors go way back, to the nineties, even before."

"They say," Sari interrupted, "it was a group operating outside any known jurisdiction. Neither TF, nor Wishper, nor the old fey councils controlled it. They kidnapped feys, espers, mages… creatures of all kinds. No one knew why. And those who were taken…" —she paused— "never showed up again."

Mai shivered.

"Never?"

"Never," Milena repeated, looking at her directly. "And every time someone tried to track them, the case ended in silence or tragedy. Apparently, these people were contacted by AFT at some point, looking for feys who were still alive."

Sari bent down to pick up a spider walking on deck and let it crawl between her fingers, distracted.

"Once, by accident, I overheard two high talking about it," she said, in a lower tone. "They didn’t realize I was nearby. One of them said AFT wasn’t an organization, but something else. Something that had appeared over decades, under different names, different faces. But the same pattern. Some says is like the Shadow People? Those guys in suits that also appears sniffing around but no one knows that they want. "

"What pattern?" Mai asked.

"The disappearing pattern, which is why it was believed that uncovering the cult would reveal something more," Sari replied.

The wind carried her words away for a moment. The surface of the sea reflected the ship’s lights, distorted by the waves.

Milena spoke then, almost in a whisper:

"Some also say that some of the feys captured by AFT… ended up manifesting Muryan syndrome."

Mai looked at her with a hint of disbelief. "That can’t be. Muryan syndrome should takes decades to develop, or centuries, right?"

"Maybe," Milena replied, "but old stories exist for a reason."

Mai remained silent for a moment, staring into the darkness of the sea. She knew exactly what that name implied.

Muryan syndrome… The complete disappearance of a fey who exhausted their life energy or used their powers beyond their limits. Some called it "dissolution." Others, "the return to the original dream."

"I’ve heard it can manifest earlier," Sari added, "in the form of magical instability, memory loss, or bodily alterations."

"That’s why hibernation is mandatory," Mai said. "If a fey wants to stay long-lived, they need to enter latency from time to time. It’s… a way to reset the cycle."

The two nodded. The breeze was colder now.

Mai allowed herself a sad smile. "Miho was afraid of that," she said. "When my hair started changing color sometimes, she thought it was the beginning of the syndrome. She spent weeks worrying."

"And was it?" Milena asked.

Mai shook her head.

"No. The exams showed nothing abnormal. It turned out the change was related to training to use my echolocation ability… and with the accumulated activity over the past years. But nothing to do with Muryan." —She paused, lowering her voice— "Still, she didn’t stop fearing it. But I have no signs that I’ll need a latency period soon either."

"Fear leaves marks too, always be mindful of that. Resting is important to us." Sari said softly, leaning on the railing next to her.

Mai nodded. "Yeah. I know."

Sari hugged Milena once, and standing on her toes, kissed her nose, then her lips. Both of them made their eyes shine in that moment.

Mai, watching them, smiled unconsciously. Get a room, you two. She had learned not to interrupt those moments. She knew that for the two sirens, showing affection in front of others was normal.

"I wonder how Liz is doing now.?" Sari asked quietly, barely pulling away from Milena’s lips.

"Sleeping, surely. Our lemon siren." Milena replied after a silence.

"Yeah… but, I wonder if she’s dreaming about us," Sari said with a mischievous smile. "Or maybe about Shin. Or the other girl who was with them."

Milena rolled her eyes and smiled, though a faint blush appeared on her cheeks. "Who knows…" she said in a tone that tried to be neutral but failed.

Mai raised an eyebrow. "Liz?" she asked curiously. "Who’s Liz?"

The two sirens looked at each other, and a shared smile crossed their faces, as if a warm memory had sparked between them. Sari sighed, her expression tinged with melancholy. "Someone we met a long time ago," she began. "Liz and Shin—they were our lovers for a while."

Milena nodded softly. "We lived with them. A beautiful time, though brief. But we ran into them sometimes in other years. Let's say we're friends... with benefits."

“Shin...” said Mai. “Is he Japanese?”

“Oh, he originally came from Russia. But yeah, he looked Asian.”

“Looked? Why past?”

Sari laughed, lowering her voice. "Yeah… Shin and Liz… Mimi too… well, let’s just say they are hard to forget."

"Sari…" Milena interrupted, half embarrassed, half amused.

"Oh, come on!" Sari winked at Mai. "He had the energy of a horse, literally. Remember when we meet them… that poor bed."

"That was you putting on a cowgirl show, you pervert," Milena said in a scolding tone.

"Was it? Cowgirl siren. Sounds hot, doesn’t it?" Sari asked, scratching her head and smiling mischievously. "And you don’t get to call me a pervert."

Milena sighed.

Mai let out a brief laugh. Milena covered her face with her hands, murmuring something in a language Mai didn’t recognize, but it sounded like a mix of plea and embarrassment.

"It was another time," she finally said, laughing through her fingers. "And we… other beings."

The humor, however, faded like mist drifting away from the sea. Milena took a deep breath before continuing.

"Over the years we crossed paths with them again. Always on the margins… but when things got difficult. If we needed help, they were there."

"You two never told me that."

The two looked at each other and brought a finger to their lips. Then Sari said, "The thing is, both of them were part of TF. The team that investigated dark events and other things."

TF. Tempus Fugit. Mai had heard the name several times, as it was a kind of legend in the occult world. Rumor had it that all its members had perished. Apparently, that wasn’t true.

Those who remained had withdrawn from the world and tried to lead a different life. It was said that the last things they did slightly changed the politics of the kingdom and the Tripartite Empire. Even that ZAIEN was involved.

"And now?" Mai asked. "Where are they?"

Sari looked down, and Milena remained silent for a few seconds.

"If they were here… they could have helped us." —Milena’s voice trembled slightly— "But… at the beginning of 2006, Liz contacted us to say bye. She said she would go into hibernation."

"Hibernation?" Mai repeated.

"Yes. She said she is in a secret facility, in Argentina." —Milena looked at the sea— "A few weeks later… the attack happened."

Mai nodded slowly, recalling the reports, the names, the rumors that circulated in the underground circles.

"Then… she never knew that…?"

"No," Sari interrupted. "The incident in Shinjuku changed everything. That meeting had to remain hidden. No one was supposed to know. So she couldn’t have known anything. Her girls neither, since they’re almost always in South America.”

Milena nodded “It’s the problem with secrecy in the occult world. There are so many compartmentalized things that even among fey circles, some things aren’t known.”

“Right now we’re against a cult and Octogonus. And somewhere else, another conflict is happening at this very hour. After all, the Tripartite bloc is currently allied with Russia in the Middle East war against another Octogonus faction.”

The wind picked up, stronger now. The ship’s lights swayed, casting long shadows across the deck.

"And the one you mentioned… Shin? He couldn’t help?" Mai asked cautiously.

The two looked at each other. It was a brief moment, but enough to understand the answer before it came.

"He disappeared," Milena finally said. "In 1999, in Japan. No one ever heard from him again."

Sari stepped aside slightly and gazed at the sky, where the moon peeked through thin clouds. "Maybe he’s still out there… or maybe he’s grown tired of this world." —She smiled without joy— "I wouldn’t blame him. That curse on his body may have made him withdraw from the world."

"Curse?"

Milena nodded. "Shin has a curse around him. Basically, he can’t stay with the ones he loves for long. Strange things start happening."

“I see…” Mai remained silent. There was an immense sadness in the air, perhaps it was better not to ask further. After all, she had just learned this part of their lives, and it had never been revealed before. "And you?" she finally asked. "What will you two do now?"

Milena let out a long sigh, almost a lament. "Leave," she answered. "We want this strike to be the last. We’ve fought too much, lost too much."

“From what you said before, I thought you were going to continue with the team.”

“We’ll keep in touch. But that was always the idea.”

It was true that Sari had mentioned something weeks ago, but Mai didn’t expect the attack to end so soon. Although, knowing them, she wouldn’t be surprised if they jumped off the ship at that moment to get lost at sea.

"We want to find a calm sea," Sari added. "A place where no one is looking for us, where we can simply… live in peace. We’re both close to a century old, hiding, running, sometimes fighting for causes we didn’t even understand, but this was the worst time. We’ve done our part."

Mai lowered her gaze, understanding. "Maybe you’re right," she murmured.

Sari looked at her with tenderness. "And you? What will you do now?"

She didn’t answer immediately. She looked toward the horizon, at the waves crashing against the hull, the distant lights of the other ships.

"I don’t know," she admitted with a weak smile. "Maybe I should go back to Tokyo."

"You should," Milena said, with unexpected warmth. "We’ve seen you these months, always moving like a ghost. Maybe it’s time to return to the life you had. We were the ones who got you out of there."

Mai didn’t know what to say. She felt a strange pressure in her chest, a mix of gratitude, fatigue, and melancholy. Maybe they were right. Maybe it was time to stop running and go back home.

At that moment, a muffled roar echoed in the distance. The three lifted their heads.

Through the clouds, a blue glow with streaks traced a luminous spiral. A Canglong—one of the guardian dragons—descended upon the ship, its serpentine movements like a veil of liquid blue fire. Behind it, its two siblings circled majestically before soaring into the sky.

The three watched in silence. The largest dragon roared, a sound that seemed to hold both a farewell and a blessing. Then the creatures vanished among the clouds, leaving a luminous trail that gradually dissolved into the night sky.

Sari rested her head on Milena’s shoulder.

Mai closed her eyes and took a deep breath.

The sea had finally calmed a little. And with it, her heart as well.

She would surely have work to do when she returned to Tokyo after all those months.
