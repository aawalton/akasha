The carriage stretched twenty metres long, six metres wide, and stood three metres high. Right now, a wooden log with a pointed tip—shaped like an arrow—was jammed diagonally between two sides.

Exactly two seconds later, a second arrow dropped inside and started to expand too.

‘Wood?’ Pronto sparked a small flame in his palm, lighting up the inside of the carriage, ‘That’s impossible! IMPOSSIBLE!’

He panicked for a moment, then steadied himself as a third arrow clattered in. ‘There’s no Level 2 Elf here. And the Elves of the past—they’ve all been wiped out. Even in the whole of T’Hara Forest, let alone inside the Elven Prison.’

The Humans had taken the World Tree hostage. So every Elf still alive back then had tried to storm the Elven Prison to reclaim the region—only to be executed by the Warden.

After the Era shift, Humans from Earth were reincarnated into Gangnea, stuck inside the Elven Prison. No one had ever broken out. Which meant T’Hara Forest shouldn’t have a single surviving Elf left in it.

No matter how Pronto ran it through his head, he couldn’t understand how not one, not two, but probably hundreds of Elves had made it into the lava moat.

‘I need to survive! I can’t die here!’ Faced with the chaos, Pronto’s eyes darted to the sealed compartment in the carriage—the one holding the Relic of the Ogre Race. He hesitated... then backed off.

‘Shit!’

His team consisted of himself, the old man with weak Level 2 strength, and five Level 1 soldiers. One of them had already been skewered through the head. Assessing the situation, he glanced at the old man. “Make fire, as much as you can. We’re burning our way out!”

He grabbed the soldier’s corpse and used it like a shield, braced his legs, then launched forward with a powerful thrust, shooting out of the carriage in a blur. “Urgh!”

“Follow His Highness!” the old man shouted, rushing out right after. He threw up a shield of fire over the carriage, cranking the heat to the max in a desperate attempt to burn away the incoming arrows.

But then, a wooden log slammed into his leg and tore it clean off.

“AARGH!”

“Cough!”

“Argh!”

The remaining four soldiers were trapped inside. Before they could climb out, another wooden log crashed into the entrance, expanded, and sealed them in. The old man’s fire ignited it, but instead of clearing a path, it filled the carriage with choking, toxic smoke.

“Keep shooting!”

A calm voice echoed out—then came the relentless shriek of arrows in the air.

“W-What is this?” Pronto had made it ten metres into the sky, his face contorted in pain as several half-burnt arrows were lodged in his legs. He fumbled for the Healing Potion—one Rachad had given him—but just before he could twist it open, his eyes locked onto the carriage behind him, filling with an endless storm of arrows until the fire burning inside was snuffed out for a few seconds.

The old man lay nearby, riddled with arrows like a porcupine—already dead. The shafts sticking out of him glowed red-hot, reigniting moments later. From within the metal carriage came the rasping coughs and dying screams of the four soldiers, trapped as the smoke choked the air.

Pronto’s face drained of colour as he scanned the area. On the ground, in the trees, hidden in the foliage—they were everywhere.

Elves.

Arms ripped, legs lean, bodies lithe. Each one held a heavy bow and a quiver full of deadly arrows. Their armour looked like living bark—tough, flexible, and able to endure a few blasts of intense fire.

In a two-hundred metre radius around him, nearly two thousand Elves stood, waiting.

He barely had time to process the scale of them before they raised their bows and, in perfect unison, fired.

[Spell: Solar Hero]

Within a four-metre radius around him, fire erupted, swirling like a storm in every direction. The flames formed a blazing sphere that looked like the sun itself. Flares burst out from below, holding him in the air with raw, burning thrust.

Solar Hero was the most balanced spell for a Level 2 Human, a perfect blend of offence and defence. But its real strength came from the sheer heat it could unleash—devastatingly lethal.

Arrows rained down, vanishing into the sphere of fire with a hiss, vaporising within a metre of depth. But every arrow that disappeared drained more of the spell’s flames, and the Solar Hero shrunk slightly in response.

As the heat inside the sphere dropped, the arrows started piercing deeper. With a grunt, Pronto flared the flames again, pushing back against the unrelenting barrage.

The thrust beneath him steadied, slowly lifting him higher. Once he gained enough altitude, he'd be able to shift his focus to offense. But, of course, Pinaka had no plans to give him that chance.

[Spell: Pinaka Arrow]

Pinaka raised his bow toward the sky and fired a shot.

[Spell: Pinaka]

The arrow soared to a height of about eighty metres before spawning a clone of Pinaka beside it, transferring Control Authority to the duplicate.

As it descended, the clone conjured a massive wooden sphere and then kicked it straight toward Pronto.

“What?” Pronto gasped, eyes wide as the burning sphere of Solar Hero failed to eat through the wood fast enough. He threw all his focus into evasion, channeling more power to his legs and thrusting himself sideways.

He managed to dodge the wooden sphere—barely—but the opening cost him. The arrows caught up.

They stabbed into him from every angle.

[Spell: Pinaka Arrow]

The clone returned Control Authority as Pinaka fired another arrow, which grew larger as it streaked toward Pronto.

“Argh!” Pronto twisted away in a burst of panic, blasting streams of fire behind him as he blazed through the trees. He aimed to ignite the forest and turn it into a burning shield. But every time he tried, an arrow would pierce the base of a tree, and its wood would ripple like liquid, curling around the flames to suffocate them.

The fire dimmed with each attempt, the heat no longer enough to scorch through the damp, writhing trees.

“Who are you?!” Pronto shrieked, his voice cracking as a cluster of half-burnt arrows drove into his body. He yanked out the Healing Potion in one shaky motion, gulped it down in a single swing, and rocketed higher, desperate to escape.

But just like before, Pinaka fired another arrow into the sky. It created a clone mid-air, transferring Control Authority to it. The clone summoned another massive wooden sphere and hurled it down at Pronto.

Even if Pronto managed to dodge, Pinaka would only repeat the combo—over and over again.

After each shot, Pinaka melted into the ranks of his clones, shifting positions. Surrounded by duplicates, he became impossible to spot.

“There!” Pronto snarled, lashing out with a Flame Whip. It cracked through the air, coiling around the neck of an Elf who had just launched a growing arrow at him. The whip snapped tight—and the Elf's head was severed clean off.

But it was only a clone.

“If you surrender, I’ll at least spare your life,” the Elves said in unison. Their tone, their voices—everything about the way they spoke in perfect sync was deeply unsettling.

“I surrender!” Pronto shouted as he landed on the ground, raising both hands. “Please, spare me. You can treat me as a prisoner.”

“Whatever your objective is, I’ll cooperate.” He couldn’t keep fighting anymore. Stat-wise, he had the strength to shatter Elf limbs, escape, and even come back to hunt down Pinaka. On paper, he still had the upper hand.

Physically, he was wrecked—but the Healing Potion was kicking in, mending his injuries fast as he begged for mercy. The problem was mental. He had completely lost the will to keep going.

The whole situation was eerie, confusing, and completely outside anything he understood. ‘What is happening? How are there so many of the same Elf? How can an Elf even be here? What the heck is this power? Nothing makes sense anymore!’

He had never faced someone so far outside his realm of experience. It was crushing—enough to scramble his mind and strip away any desire to fight.

“You made a good choice,” Pinaka said, stepping forward until he stood just an arm’s length away. “You will be granted rights as a prisoner.”

“W-Who are you, actually?” Pronto asked, eyes darting around. “And why did you attack me?”

“Who am I, you ask?” Pinaka tilted his head slightly, like he was thinking about it—when suddenly, Pronto erupted.

A sea of flames burst from his body, flooding everything within two hundred metres. It swallowed the entire area in fire, engulfing every Elf in sight.

He’d been waiting for the perfect moment—to unleash one attack that would wipe out every single enemy around him. That was the plan. Reality, though, had other ideas.

The massive blaze he had summoned, meant to scorch the entire forest and vaporize all the Elves, was casually sucked into the mouth of a nearby Elf.

“What…?” Pronto froze. It was like watching his worst nightmare play out in slow motion. His eyes locked on the Elf who had just swallowed every last flicker of his flames.

Once the fire disappeared, the Elf calmly closed its mouth and gave its stomach a satisfied pat. A second later, it burped.

What Pronto didn’t know was that inside that Elf’s stomach sat a Sun Stone. And connected to the Sun Stone was the nervous system of a Level 1 Human.

The Elf had hijacked the Human’s nervous system by using its Control Authority over Lifeforms, allowing it to tap into the Human’s Authority.

The process was temporary—the Human’s nervous system wasn’t built for it and had already begun to reject the interference. In resisting, it was slowly breaking down, destroying itself and dragging the Elf’s nervous system with it.

But for now, it worked. And that was enough. The only goal was to activate the Sun Stone.

Unlike Humans, who could seamlessly draw out and store flames from a Sun Stone as if painting with fire, Pinaka’s current control was crude. All he could do was treat it like a container—a source and sink for fire.

After absorbing Pronto’s inferno, the Sun Stone was now half full. The Level 1 Human’s nervous system, on the other hand, was nearly burnt out. ‘I can harness at most one proper Sun Stone activation per nervous system.’

There was a Sun Stone in every prison. In the prison Pinaka came from, the soldier who brought food carried it. Pinaka had simply borrowed both the Sun Stone and the soldier’s spine.

In his place, he left behind a clone—one that would probably die in some random accident before anyone noticed.

If Pinaka’s third clone managed to bring back a Sun Stone, he’d simply return it to the owner’s corpse. He had no intention of leaving a trace. Everything would be written off as a string of unfortunate "accidents."

“Are you done?” Pinaka asked, standing before Pronto with the same flat expression as always.

“I… I…” Pronto’s face twisted between nervous laughter and raw panic. His grip on reality was slipping, his emotions spiraling out of control.
