I sat there, silent, for a long moment. I don’t know how long. It could have been a minute. Or ten. Time had no meaning as the gravity of everything Jericho said pressed down on me—where I was, what I was supposed to do.

The old man, for his part, simply stared at me, unblinking, the entire time. Like the fucking Mona Lisa.

The salamander thing had since nuzzled a little nest into my hair. Its tiny chest rose and fell, warm breath fanning against my scalp in soft puffs.

Between what Jericho told me and what the angel-being had insinuated, my circumstances were starting to take shape. I was here, along with other Reapers, to kill monsters that embodied human sin. And in doing so, supposedly help purify and save humanity.

It sounded noble enough, if not for the fact that I DIDN’T WANT TO FUCKING BE HERE. I had a life back home. In… Vitra-whatever-it’s-called. I had Avery. A wedding in three months.

How is she feeling right now? Does she know what happened to me yet? I don’t even know how long it’s been. The thought sat heavy in my chest, more suffocating than the burn of Zeus' lightning.

“You’re actually taking this rather well,” Jericho noted at last, shattering the silence. His voice carried a strange lightness, as if the weight crushing me simply didn’t exist for him. “Most new Ambers immediately start swearing up a storm.”

I almost smirked. “If only you could see my inner monologue.”

“Ah, you’re one of those thinking types. Never could get into that myself. Tongue won’t let it happen, unfortunately.”

“So we’re supposed to keep killing these monsters? Sins. Why? And what’s forcing us to comply?” I had the general idea, but I needed confirmation.

“Ah. Going straight for the big questions, are you?” Jericho winked. “I’m not exactly the professor of Penumbra 101, you know. I just found you lying here as I passed by, that’s all. You’ll figure all this shite out eventually.”

“You sure you’re not secretly my mentor? You do have a bit of a Gandalf-Dumbledore aspect to you…” If the famous wizards had maybe lost a few marbles, survived the seventies, and… yeah, nevermind.

“I appreciate that. Kindest words anyone’s ever said to me. Truly. But no. There are no official mentors or anything of the sort. This isn’t some silly school or some grand quest. I can give you the lay of the land and whatnot if you desire. Then you never have to see me again. Though if you frequent the taverns, I’m sure we’ll run into one another here and there.”

“There are taverns in the spirit realm?”

“The Umbral Plane,” he corrected smoothly. “And yes, there are taverns, shops, etcetera, etcetera. You can even get drunk, believe it or not. Makes things easier for some.” He used air quotes as he said the word ‘things.’ “Any other burning questions?”

I had approximately a million, but one in particular pressed at the front of my mind.

“Do I have a dick on my forehead?”

Jericho only grinned.

A short time later, I sat in the covered wagon behind Jericho as we rode through the field toward one of the taverns he’d spoken of. The wheels creaked and rattled over uneven ground. The scythe—my scythe—rested, still bloodied, on the floor near a large locked chest and a coil of fraying rope that looked about as sturdy as a roll of toilet paper.

The orange salamander had snuck its way onto the wagon as well. It now perched happily on my knee, tongue flapping in the wind. I patted it on the head, hating the fact that the stupid creature was starting to win me over.

We passed several of the human-animal hybrids on the way, though always at a distance. They moved like shadows along the fields, their postures drooping, their eyes too hollow. Each time one came into view, Nuzzle—that’s what I’d started calling the salamander, since it clearly wasn’t planning on leaving anytime soon—let out a little chirp, tail twitching like a cat spotting prey.

Jericho had said they wouldn't attack. Only the ones that were 'marked' were a threat, indicated by the glowing skull I'd seen above my crocodilian friend earlier. If it was unmarked we weren't supposed to kill it or there'd be 'consequences.'

Besides the monsters—Sins—whatever—more animalistic shapes prowled the land. Wolves with bones jutting from their flanks, deer whose hooves bled mist, even squirrels that left trails of shadow like ink dribbling through the grass. Birds with translucent wings glided overhead, their screeches echoing like distant metal scraping glass.

Jericho eventually handed me a small mirror, which revealed I looked nothing like my real self. My hair was teal, stylized with lighter streaks, like I’d walked straight out of a surfer magazine. As I feared, my right eye was black like Zeus' and Jericho's with an orange iris glowing faintly in the reflection. Overall, this Umbral Plane version of me was far more attractive than I had ever hoped to be, despite the… oddities.

The mirror also revealed that I did not actually have a sharpie penis on my face anymore, contrary to Jericho’s earlier reaction. The bastard. He reminded me of Ben aged about forty or fifty years and dipped in LSD. I hoped he and the others were alright and weren’t trapped somewhere here like me. None of them would've been able to handle this, except maybe Derek. Hell, I wasn’t even sure I could handle this.

And actually, thinking about it, Derek would love this. If only he'd been taken instead of me.

What the fuck, Miles? Literally wishing your own brother dead. What's wrong with you?

But maybe I had the better end of the deal. I was still alive… sort of. Just trapped here instead of there. How would my family be handling this? Avery? I prayed it wouldn’t break her. She didn’t deserve that. No one did. But especially her. She was such a light in the world. For her to lose that… And because of me.

Nuzzle licked my hand, almost as if he could sense my spiraling thoughts.

“Over there!” Jericho shouted, his voice carrying over the pounding hooves of the undead stallions.

He pointed to a small cluster of cabin-like buildings nestled before a swath of the red-trunked trees. His sapphire cloak fluttered behind him. I hadn’t noticed before, but the fabric of the cape had patches depicting several emojis sewn into it. A monkey covering its eyes. A smiley with its head exploding. Heart on fire. Sushi. A cat face with heart eyes. Rainbow. Man meditating. Even a carrot, for some reason. There had to be some story behind that one.

The wagon slowed to a stop, and Jericho hopped down with surprising agility.

I climbed to the front, grabbing my scythe. Nuzzle squirreled his way up to my shoulder as I peered out at the mini village.

The four buildings ahead were well-kept, constructed from the dark red wood of the nearby forest and topped with thatch roofs. Each was barely more than a hovel, one or two rooms at most. The farthest sat beside a stable where several zombified horses grazed within a fence.

A man in a straw hat leaned against the fence with a long blade of bright green grass dangling from his mouth. His posture was lazy, but his gaze watchful. Two normal eyes. He didn’t carry a scythe or wear a cloak.

Jericho glanced over his shoulder. “This isn’t my usual haunt, but it’s probably the best choice for a newbie such as yourself. Why don’t you go grab a drink and make yourself comfortable in the tavern. I’ll meet you inside once I’ve had a chat with the stable master.”

So the tavern is here? None of these buildings seemed large enough for one, maybe a bar and a handful of tables.

“Which one is—” I started to ask, but Jericho was already halfway to the stable, waving at straw-hat man.

I sighed and climbed the rest of the way out of the wagon. It turned out to be fairly obvious which building was the tavern, what with the giant sign carved into the shape of a frothy beer mug. The words across the wood read: 'The Meridian.'

A large post outside held an oil lantern, flickering despite the daylight. The tavern door was painted yellow but made from the same red timber as the rest and had a sign pinned to it that read: 'Shepherd's Not Welcome.'

I didn't know what that meant, and just prayed it didn't apply to me as I pushed the door open and stepped inside.

The scene within stopped me in my tracks.

The interior was enormous, far bigger than the hovel it had looked from outside. A massive dining hall stretched out before me, clean and well-maintained. The door I’d entered had been at the center of the building’s facade, yet I now found myself in the far right corner. The rest of the inside somehow stretched far beyond the external walls. It wasn't just a weird perspective trick. The inside was truly, impossibly, larger than the outside.

Thick glass high-top tables filled the center, lined in neat rows, each with a matching set of stools. Long immaculate bars ran along every wall, cushioned stools set evenly apart. Specific themes decorated the back wall of each bar.

To my left, enormous blue trees sprouted straight from the wall, trunks splitting into branches that climbed to the cathedral ceiling. Vines hung down in curved strands over the bar like living banners. Red blossoms the size of my head blossomed along the branches, filling the air with a faint floral sweetness. A miniature majestic river flowed along the wall, light twinkling in its current.

The right wall hosted a canyon, the counter behind the bar, resting atop a sheer rusty cliff. Upon the wall, an infinite selection of liquor rested on uneven layers of striated rock like rock formations I’d seen at several of the national parks out west.

Meanwhile, the back wall straight across was constructed from pure ice. I could even see the cold air fogging off the surfaces. The bartender poured colorful mixed drinks into little slides carved into the back wall that led down to serving glasses.

The last bar was the most interesting though. Dark igneous rock made up the entire wall, while glowing veins of lava weaved throughout the surface around the various bottles residing there. Nuzzle cooed softly as he looked at the volcanic facade.

“Not sure I’m ready for that one quite yet, buddy,” I whispered, patting his head.

Someone cleared their throat behind me.

“Jericho, this is—”

I turned and found myself face-to-face, not with Jericho, but with a brute of a man.

He had to stoop to fit through the doorway, his shoulders twice as broad as mine. The full suit of gleaming silver armor encasing his body clanked with each step. His eyebrows were furrowed, eyes hard.

“In? Or out?” he grunted, voice deep and rasping, carrying the weight of boulders sliding down a mountain.

“Uh… I…”

He squinted at me, accentuating the many scars scattered across his forehead and cheeks. He reached out a gargantuan hand covered in snow white carapace and slid me to the side with zero effort, then shouldered past.

“Right… Sorry. I’m new here.”

He grunted again and lumbered away, a silver-white cloak flowing behind him that sparkled like a million tiny stars. Was that strength from his intense carapace or just his natural strength enhancement? The exoskeleton—aside from being white—was far more ornate and extensive than my own, with excessive spikes and thorns extending off.

He headed straight to the volcanic bar and sat alone off to the side. Probably to avoid poking anyone. The barkeep immediately slid a shot glass of black smoking liquid over to him. The brute downed it in one gulp, the smoke curling from his lips.

Yeah, definitely not ready for that one.

The other patrons weren’t nearly as massive, though their presence was still intimidating. Cloaks of every color shifted in the flickering light. Amber, like me, blue, red, green, purple. Only the armored brute wore silver, though.

I shook my head in disbelief.

“What the fuck have I gotten myself into?”

Nuzzle just purred in response.
