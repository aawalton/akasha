Lir was the first to notice that something was wrong. He stood a little ahead of Sakura, still staring at the open gates of the obsidian castle, when a young man in a long gray cloak hurried toward them across the bridge.

Lir automatically stepped back, but not fast enough, and the man walked straight through him. Lir spun around and stared after him.

"What the..."

Sakura froze in surprise too. Instead of the collision she expected, the man simply passed through Lir’s body as if he did not exist at all and kept walking toward the castle. Gilbert reached out and tried to touch the shoulder of the next passerby, but just like with Lir, his fingers went straight through. So this is probably what ghosts feel like in the world of the living.

"An illusion," the mage said.

"Or something like a magical memory?" Sakura suggested.

Lir stared at his hands as if seriously considering the possibility that he might not exist.

"So technically, we aren’t here?"

"Not to them," Gilbert replied.

Sakura looked again at the man who had come through the gates. People around him moved aside. They bowed their heads and greeted him with short, polite gestures. He responded calmly, without any particular ceremony. He stopped near two men much younger than himself and pulled back his deep hood.

Now Sakura could finally get a good look at his face. His dark hair was brushed back, with gray already showing at the temples. He had high cheekbones and a straight nose. What stood out most was the slightly tapered shape of his dark eyes. He looked about forty-five, though something in his expression made his age hard to determine. Still, nothing about his appearance screamed stern ruler or someone truly dangerous. He looked more like a tired scholar. In his left hand, the man held a thick book in a dark cover, and Sakura recognized it almost immediately.

"Wait."

She took several quick steps forward, examining the letters on the cover. The same ones she had seen when she still knew almost nothing about this world or her own class. It was Crowlis’s book.

"That’s what we’re looking for," Sakura said.

Gilbert came closer.

"I already figured that out."

"No. I mean the book. That’s the one I found on my first day here."

The mage looked more closely at the cover and frowned.

"Then there’s no doubt left. That’s Crowlis himself."

Meanwhile, the man began speaking to the two young men who had been waiting for him near the road.

"You changed the outer circuit again?"

One of them nodded.

"Only the third bundle. The result is more stable than the previous one."

"By how much?"

"Almost four minutes."

Crowlis stopped.

"Almost?"

The young mage looked slightly embarrassed.

"Three minutes and forty-seven seconds."

"Then say that. I need numbers, not ‘almost.’"

Sakura glanced at Gilbert, and he raised one eyebrow slightly.

"He’s right," the mage said.

The second student smirked, while Crowlis had already opened the book and quickly flipped through several pages.

"And the reverse flow?"

"It didn’t collapse."

"Then that really is interesting."

He ran a finger along one of the lines and stood thinking for several seconds right in the middle of the road. Passersby calmly walked around the three of them, clearly used to this kind of behavior.

"If this pattern holds, we may finally stop working only with the external manifestations of a class," Crowlis said at last.

Both students immediately became more serious. Sakura saw their eyes light up.

"You think it worked?" one of them asked.

"I think one successful result is not enough for that conclusion."

Crowlis closed the book, ending the conversation, and Sakura noticed how his expression changed. Whatever this was, it had clearly stopped being ordinary research for him a long time ago. There was something deeply personal in the hope on his face.

"This kind of magic could let me bring back what I thought was lost forever," he said quietly.

The students exchanged glances.

"You’re still thinking about..."

Crowlis raised a hand, and the young mage immediately fell silent.

"Not here. We’ll discuss everything at the meeting. With the others."

He pulled his hood back up, though it did not fully hide his face, and the three of them headed away from the castle. Sakura immediately followed.

"He wants to bring something back," she said. "Or someone."

"Obviously," Gilbert replied.

"Any guesses?" she asked.

"If I had any, Ater wouldn’t have needed to waste his questionable brew on us."

Lir caught up with them.

"Maybe a person? Someone close to him?"

Sakura looked at him.

"Why do you think that?"

Lir fell silent, thoughtful and a little grim. Sakura understood why. He had only recently lost his own father, and now he was projecting his tragedy onto someone else’s life. Finally, Lir said:

"I don’t know. He didn’t talk about it like it was a thing..."

They followed Crowlis and the two students, and the road quickly led them into the forest. It was there that Sakura truly felt the difference between this time and her own for the first time. The place was not exactly safe. Beasts cried somewhere in the distance, small creatures flashed between the trees, and a bird with four long wings flew over the canopy. But on the other hand, Sakura was not searching for crimson veins between the roots or waiting for another obsidian creature to appear. The other people walked calmly too. A little farther ahead, a woman and a child were picking berries right beside the road.

"There aren’t any obsidian creatures here yet," Sakura said.

Gilbert watched the forest for a while.

"It’s too early for them."

Ahead, Crowlis suddenly stopped. A small creature about the size of a large cat emerged from the bushes beside the road. It had a round body, short legs, and enormous ears that almost dragged along the ground. Its fur shifted from blue to silver in the light. One of the students reached into his bag.

"He’s here again."

"Because you feed him," the second one said.

"He comes for the teacher, not me."

Crowlis crouched and held out a piece of dried fruit. The creature approached cautiously, sniffed his fingers, grabbed the treat, and sat down right in the middle of the road. Crowlis calmly petted it between the ears.

"You’ve gotten fat again."

The creature whistled indignantly.

Sakura stopped a few steps away and suddenly remembered everything she had heard about Crowlis in her own time. A man with his name was remembered because of catastrophe, death, and the appearance of obsidian creatures, yet right now he was crouching in the road, feeding dried fruit to a fat, long-eared animal.

"He doesn’t look like a monster at all," Sakura said quietly.

"Because right now, he isn’t one," Gilbert replied.

Lir looked around at the peaceful forest.

"It’s hard to even imagine that all of this will become what it is now..."

Crowlis stood and continued on. The forest gradually grew thicker, and the trail led them toward a low stone ridge. A narrow cave entrance was hidden between two cliffs. Before entering, one of the students looked back and carefully checked the road.

"They keep looking around like they’re afraid of being followed. This must be a secret place," Lir said.

"Looks like it," Gilbert replied.

The cave was dark and cool. Crowlis and the students lit small magical lights that floated ahead beneath the ceiling, illuminating the way. The passage gradually sloped downward. At first, the walls remained natural, but soon carved steps and reinforced arches appeared, and shallow water channels ran along the floor.

Sakura heard the water almost immediately. For now, the voices were quiet and did not address her directly. They only whispered somewhere at the edge of her hearing.

The tunnel ended in a large underground hall where more than ten people were already waiting. Most were noticeably younger than Crowlis. Some sat around a long stone table, others talked near the walls, and the space around them was clearly being used for research. Books and scrolls lay everywhere, along with vessels and several strange metal devices. There were only two women among the group, and Sakura noticed one of them almost immediately. She had straight dark hair, pale skin, and a familiar shape to her eyes. Even her clothes had a slightly different cut from the local style.

"She looks like you," Lir noted.

Sakura looked at the woman again.

"A little, I guess."

Meanwhile, Crowlis stepped up to the table, and the conversations gradually died down. He placed the book in front of him.

"While the stone remembers fire."

The people around him answered almost at once:

"Fire remembers the way home."

Sakura exchanged a glance with Gilbert.

"A password?"

"Looks like it. Secret societies rarely use passwords like ‘good evening.’"

A solidly built man around thirty immediately approached Crowlis. Compared to the other researchers, he looked more like a soldier, with close-cropped hair, broad shoulders, and a grim expression.

"We’ve waited long enough."

Crowlis looked at him calmly.

"Then it’s good that I’m here, Nobus."

"I’m not talking about today’s meeting."

Nobus placed a hand on the edge of the table.

"We’ve been waiting for months. Testing the same thing, changing runes by a hair’s breadth, repeating experiments. The structure is obviously stable."

"No."

"Teacher..."

"It holds long enough for you to want to call it stable. That’s different."

Nobus pressed his lips together.

"We can’t keep testing the same thing forever."

"And we won’t."

"Then why haven’t we started integration yet?"

Sakura frowned. Beside her, Gilbert also became more attentive.

"Integration of what?" Lir asked quietly.

"A new class," the mage replied.

One of the students at the other end of the table joined in:

"Nobus is right. We’ve already done more than anyone thought possible."

"That is exactly why we cannot rush," Crowlis said. "We are going to integrate a new class into the System. If we make a mistake here, we won’t be able to erase the result and start over."

"And if we don’t make a mistake?" Nobus asked, and now real excitement finally entered his voice. "We’ll do what no one before us could. Create our own class and make the System accept it."

Several people immediately began talking excitedly. Their eyes held the same fire Sakura had seen in the young men Crowlis had spoken to when she first saw him.

"The magic schools will have no choice but to recognize the result."

"This will change class theory."

Nobus grinned.

"We’ll become legends."

Crowlis looked at him with obvious exhaustion.

"And that is the part I like least."

"That we want results?"

"That you are already thinking about what you’ll get afterward. I want to finish this work too, Nobus. I have more reasons for it than most of you. But if seeing your own name in a book becomes more important than what exactly we’re about to release into the world, then it’s time to stop and check everything again."

"Ambition isn’t a crime."

"Of course not. Not until it makes you start ignoring mistakes."

Nobus turned away, but Crowlis continued more calmly:

"If this works, people really will remember us. If we make a mistake, the magic schools and governments will come here. They’ll burn the research, shut everything down, and maybe kill us too. And you know what, Nobus? I would understand them."

From the expressions around the table, it was obvious he had not convinced everyone by a long shot. Some looked thoughtful, but many were still unhappy. Nobus was simply the one who spoke the most.

"I don’t like any of this," Sakura said quietly.

"Neither do I," Gilbert replied.

Nobus started speaking again, but Sakura suddenly stopped listening. The whisper of water in the channels changed and grew louder. She turned toward a small natural spring at the far end of the cave and noticed almost at the same time that Crowlis had done the same. He had heard it too. Fine ripples moved across the spring even though no one had gone near the water. The voices whispered several words over one another, but Sakura understood one perfectly clearly.

"Heiress."

Her fingers went cold.

"Heiress."

Crowlis took a step toward the spring, ignoring Nobus.

"She hears."

Sakura looked at him, no longer sure whether she was hearing Crowlis himself or the voices of the water.

"The Heiress hears."

Crowlis flinched almost imperceptibly.

"She hears us."

After those words, he turned sharply and looked directly at the place where Sakura stood.

Lir quietly said her name, but Sakura did not react at all. Gilbert stood beside her in heavy silence, while Crowlis kept looking straight at her, at Sakura.
