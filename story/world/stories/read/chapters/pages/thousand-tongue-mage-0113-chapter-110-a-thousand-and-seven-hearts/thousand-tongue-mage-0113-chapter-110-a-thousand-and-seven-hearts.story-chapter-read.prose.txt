Kita’s arms still trembled with the aftershock of clashing with the Grafting Bug Afflicted, but at this point, pain was secondary.

She lifted her head and looked up at the southern wall.

For decades, it’d held back floods and plagues and armies and the hungry, colossal bugs beyond the continent, but now Regalia’s zombie ants were turning it into rot. The black webbing cracks spread from where Regalia’s hands were plunged into the stone from the top of the wall, and they were branching, burrowing, fanning outwards and eating the wall from the inside out. In no time at all, the cracks would become gaping veins. The harmless seams would become unrepairable chasms—and if the wall were to break, the Crawling Seas would flood in with no end.

The wall could not be broken.

So Kita swallowed and turned her head to the side, expecting a command from Zora and Enki. Maybe they had some brilliant solution to their quandary.

Instead, the two of them were speaking in hushed voices. Zora’s head was tilted slightly towards Enki as he waved his staff between whispers, casting spells every now and then to lift earth in slabs and ribs and compacted pillars and arcs. Something was forming piece by piece, and Enki was teaching him how to make it.

Kita blinked again when the two of them suddenly turned to look at her squarely.

“We need time to figure this out,” Enki said plainly. “Get us time.”

Her throat tightened. For half a heartbeat, the child in her—the girl who’d once watched war from the safety of stone balconies and thought bravery was a banner—wanted to ask ‘how’. For a moment, she wanted to demand clearer instruction. A schedule would be nice. A promise would be even nicer.

But then the heiress in her rose, and she nodded sternly before turning back to the southern wall.

Regalia stood a hundred meters up with her cape of black zombie ants flowing around her like a river. Kita’s own cape of muddy-red army ants felt incredibly small in comparison, but… that was probably the point. She hated Regalia—she despised the bug who wore the shape of a human and dared to touch the empire’s walls with her rot—but even hatred was a candle next to this mountain-sized shadow. Even hatred would not make Kita Salaqa foolish.

She couldn’t defeat Regalia here.

‘The flood shall come again, but flee it not.

Stand as the earth stands, unyielding and unbroken…’

“… And thus alone is the tide undone,” she whispered.

She sprinted forward.

She flew over shattered stone and scorched timber—over the collapsed remnants of Zora’s artificial forest—and as the gates at the base of the wall grew larger and the veins of black rot grew even larger, what she needed to do became clear.

Before reaching the gates, she sheathed her twin sawtooth blades, but not before cutting her palms on the edges. Chitin steel ripped through her skin, and blood welled fast. The pain was bright, clean, and immediate—so she gave herself new pain to focus on as she slammed both palms into the gates, as if she were trying to push the entire wall over.

Then she poured herself into the wall.

The blood spilling from her palms twisted and bubbled and turned into tiny army ants, crawling between her fingers and surging onto the gates. They split into a hundred thin rivers of red, streaming upwards, spiderwebbing into cracks and gaps to hold the decaying wall in place.

Twenty meters up, the first army ants hit the zombie ants. Where the zombie ants devoured stone, they also devoured life. The black veins swarmed into Kita’s red rivers and ate them easily, but her army ants did fight back. The clashing ants locked mandibles. They tore into each other. Her army ants wedged themselves into cracks and refused to be pulled free. They healed what they could by binding and reinforcing stone, holding the wall together with their own bodies by becoming living mortar.

It was just a stopgap measure to delay the decay of the wall, but Kita gritted her teeth and forced more blood out anyways.

The cuts widened. Her own blood vessels pulsed. Her vision blurred at the edges as she began losing too much blood—colors became dull and sounds lost a few layers of bite—but she told herself, more. The zombie ants were far more numerous. They were a plague. Her army ants may be just one person’s worth of blood, but hadn’t the Earthen Princess stood as such against the unending flood?

‘And from that day forth she was called no more the Child of Ill Omens, but the Earthwall Princess.

Nor did she tarry in her home, but went from valley to valley, raising walls against rivers and storms, teaching all who would hear to set their own hands to the earth.’

… The Earthen Princess wouldn’t give up here.

Isn’t that right?

The Attini Empire agreed.

Across the street, across the block, across the district, across the wall—hundreds and thousands of palms slammed into the stone, like rain striking roofs, and she grinned tiredly as she glanced sideways. Not all of them managed to make it all the way down here, but those few thousand soldiers who did had cut palms, just like her, and they were summoning whatever their Art allowed: common carpenter ants, silver ants, mortar ants, fire ants, turtlehead ants… ants that couldn’t hold the wall together like her army ants specialized in healing and holding things together, yes, but whether one was a commoner or a Noble-Blood, all ant-based Swarmblood Arts had one thing in common:

Blood was blood.

Hundreds of thousands of tiny ants surged up the wall from a thousand hands, and they collided into the spreading black rot like a tide slamming into a reef. The zombie ants devoured hundreds of ant streams at once, but there were thousands more of them, trampling over the zombie ants, clogging cracks and chasms with physical bloodmass, and pushing the rot back up to the top of the wall.

She couldn’t help a short laugh now.

“The blood of the empire still stands strong!” she roared. “Don’t give in! Keep pushing ants up the wall—”

“You delay your fate,” Regalia rasped, her voice echoing down the wall. “You tire yourselves for nothing.”

Kita dragged her head up and grinned, because despite Regalia’s attempts at trying to sound relaxed, it was obvious to anyone with high perception that the Greater Ant God of the South was irritated.

“And I was told courage isn’t pretty,” she said quietly. “But we fight against overwhelming odds nevertheless.”

… But her strength was thinning, that much was true. Even a thousand soldiers summoning ants to feed Regalia’s black rot wouldn’t last forever, so she whipped her head back and prayed the warlords of the empires were doing something constructive with the time she was buying them.

Her brows immediately furrowed as she saw the construct Zora had made out of pure earth.

That’s… a long-ranged artillery cannon?

And Enki was climbing into the barrel, going down feet first.

It took a while for Enki to properly explain how a long-ranged artillery cannon functioned to Zora—because according to the Thousand Tongue, he couldn’t create anything with his spells that he couldn’t imagine himself creating even without his spells—but once the cannon was complete, Enki climbed into the barrel and waited while the cannon angled up at Regalia.

It wasn’t really a cannon in the sense that it didn’t have any of the moving mechanical parts that made up one. The entire thing was made out of pure earth and soil, but it had the structure, composition, and stability of a cannon, which would allow it to at least handle the recoil and deliver a concentrated propelling force for whatever was placed inside the barrel. Gunpowder was unnecessary. Zora could provide the fire. All Enki had to do was be the shell.

As Zora began the chant to create swirls of fire inside the cannon’s tubes and pipes, churning and pressurizing it for one big boom, Enki focused on the sky above him. Holding his gaze, he ripped open twenty wormholes directly above the barrel, and these weren’t wormholes connected to anywhere useful. They were simply glowing blue rings that formed a straight line from the barrel to the top of the wall—a ladder of rings he could shoot through to lead to a single target:

Regalia.

He was ready.

“... Fire!”

The fire Zora had been churning inside the cannon immediately coalesced into a single, pressurized burst at the bottom of the barrel, and the force spat Enki into the sky

He shot out head-first, the heat snapping at his soles and singing the edges of his flower cape. The air immediately turned into a screaming, narrowing tunnel as the ground fell away. The wall grew in size. He was moving extremely fast—far faster than any human should ever move without tearing themselves apart—but the distance was still a cruel thing. A hundred meters of height was one problem; Regalia’s reflexes were another. At this speed, he would simply be swatted out of the air as he reached her.

So as he flew through each of his twenty wormholes, he collapsed it behind him without hesitation, the ring closing around his ankle.

As he’d explained to Zora before, one of the properties of his wormholes was that they couldn’t be used to ‘cut’. If something was caught between a wormhole when it closed, it’d be hurled back towards the side where more of its mass existed. If he put only his hand through a wormhole and closed it, the hand would be flung back towards his body, and that meant if he jumped through a wormhole and closed it while his ankle was still passing through, he’d be propelled forward with additional speed.

And if he kept doing it again and again and again…

He shot through the second wormhole and collapsed it behind him at ankle-height. Another shove forward. Another violent, invisible kick. He shot through the third, fourth, and fifth wormholes and closed them behind him again, each wormhole making him gain speed until he became a living bullet with impossible velocity.

This was the foundational principle he used for his ‘railgun’ attacks, just applied to himself.

In the one second it took him to fly a hundred meters up, he closed all twenty wormholes behind him, and his acceleration became so violent that when he reached Regalia, she didn’t even have the time to snap her head up. Only one of her eyes rolled up to look at him—and by then it was too late.

He reared his diamond bayonet back and drove it into her chest.

Stone chipped and burst under her as the impact ripped her off the top of the ramparts, tearing her free from the wall like she was nothing more than a banner pinned to a pole. For a heartbeat, she was suspended there—cape flaring, zombie ants scattering into the wind—before Enki’s momentum dragged her into the open sky.

They soared.

They cleared the southern wall in an instant, the empire dropping away beneath them. The World’s End Beach flashed by under their feet—salt-spray and blood, chitin and steel—where two hundred Spore Knights still fought in the sand against the endless wave of bugs trying to pour into the empire, Enki didn’t have time to watch them. The air was ripping past his ears too fast, and Regalia’s body was thrashing against his bayonet like a hooked beast refusing to die.

Then they soared beyond the wall, beyond the beach, and the world beneath them became nothing but the Crawling Seas.

He’d seen it in picture books many times before, but the illustrations didn’t do it justice. True to its name, the Crawling Seas was an endless carpet of giant black bugs crawling over each other, bodies stacked and shifting, mandibles and legs grinding to create living chitin waves. It was said many scholars fainted the moment they saw the Crawling Seas, because they realized it would be a statistical impossibility for humanity to defeat even a fraction of the sheer number of bugs that existed beyond the continent—but Enki had never been one for math, and right now, he had the strongest bug in the south impaled on the tip of his bayonet.

Regalia hissed in his face. She clawed at his arms, trying to pry him away and trying to tear the bayonet out of her chest by force. Her cape surged like a storm behind her, and hundreds—then thousands—of zombie ants spilled from seams in her chitin plates before crawling onto his body, washing up his shoulders, down his ribs, and into the folds of his cape. They tried to chew at him. They tried to rot him. They tried to turn him into meat and bones the way they could turn stone into dust.

But their mandibles scraped, skittered, and failed to bite through his half-inorganic body.

… They can’t decay me.

I’m not organic, but I’m not inorganic either.

Regalia’s dark eyes narrowed, and for the first time, her expression shifted into something almost… puzzled. She tilted her head as if she was trying to understand what kind of human could be immune to her zombie ants’ necrotic bite, but Enki didn’t answer that question.

He jammed his bayonet deeper into her chest, grinding it through resistance, and as they continued soaring over the black tide—he felt the blade sinking into something that beat against it.

For a fraction of a second, he, too, became confused.

I pierced…

Her heart?

She should be dead, but Regalia didn’t go slack. She didn’t fall limp like a Mutant-Class would with its heart crushed.

Instead, her hands remained closed around his bayonet like the pain was meaningless. She gripped the blade with four arms, holding it in place inside her own body, and her mouth twisted into something ugly.

“You overestimate yourself,” she growled, “if you think I only have one heart to destroy.”

Enki’s scowl deepened as he felt her body through the vibration of the blade embedded in her. He tracked her pulse, and… there wasn’t only one. There were seven pulses, staggered and scattered throughout her entire body. One in the left side of her chest. One in her stomach. Others were tucked behind dense plates and thick musculature, beating out of sync just enough to make it impossible to end her with a single thrust.

All seven hearts would need to be destroyed at the same time.

… It really is impossible right now, then.

So he ripped a diamond flower from his cape and chambered it into his rifle, meeting her gaze coldly.

“Make no mistake,” he murmured. “I will see you again.”

Regalia stared at him blankly for a heartbeat—as if the threat was a language she hadn’t expected a child to speak with such certainty—and then her lips curved into something queenly and proud, the expression of a ruler acknowledging a worthy obstacle.

“You call yourself the ‘Worm Mage’, do you not?” she laughed. “How ironic it is that, after all these decades, it is still another worm who stands in the way of the Swarm.”

In another time, Enki would like to ask her what that meant, but he didn’t indulge his curiosity now.

He pulled the trigger, and the diamond flower ripped into Regalia’s chest, tearing her away from his rifle and sending her straight down into the Crawling Seas.

Her cape of zombie ants flared behind her like a black comet tail as she fell, and she eventually hit the sea of bugs with a crackling pop, killing several dozen Giant-Class bugs in a spray of shattered limbs and cracked chitin. Then she vanished beneath the endless tide, swallowed by the bugs as if they’d claimed their queen back into the depths.

She didn’t surface.

He immediately expelled cryogenic mist from his feet in a sustained blast, the frigid force holding him up and allowing him to hover above the Crawling Seas. Far behind him, on the southern shoreline, the great earthen wall was still standing. It hadn’t decayed and crumbled into the beach. The cracks had been clogged, countered, and held together by the blood of the empire, and Zora was probably there, too, helping fix the wall with his voice and staff.

Even the Crawling Seas assaulting World’s End Beach began to falter. The tides shifted. The endless wave of bugs clashing against the Spore Knights began to retreat and back up a little, because the damage had already been done. Their queen’s attempt to destroy the empire may have failed, and now the Swarm would withdraw to strike again at full force another day.

But that day wasn’t today.

For the first time in a decade, the morning sun shone down upon the Attini Empire through clear skies.
