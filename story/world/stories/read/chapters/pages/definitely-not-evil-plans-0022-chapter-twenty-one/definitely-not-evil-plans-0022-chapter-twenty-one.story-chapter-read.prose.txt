“Have fun.” Kate urged as she handed me the mic before leaving the stage and heading back to our seats.

The stage lights were hot, and blinding. Which turned out to be a good thing since I couldn’t clearly see the crowd while they shone in my eyes. I grabbed the microphone and held onto it as if it were a lifeline and letting go would send me out to sea. I did my best not to scan the crowd, certainly not in the direction of the bar where a certain someone was now undoubtedly watching and wondering what kind of lunatic I was.

“So,” I said into the mic. My voice was shaky and squeaked a little. I cleared my throat and I felt the heat in my ears. I wanted so badly to disappear into the floor. “Apparently I’m singing now. Thanks Kate.”

Laughter from the crowd echoed across the room. Kate whooped and whistled from her spot at the table.

The DJ gave me a thumbs up, “Ready when you are!”

I had no idea what song she’d picked. I didn’t request anything and she hadn’t been kind enough to tell me.

The opening notes started and I recognized it immediately.

Of course Kate would pick Tearin’ Up My Heart, the most dramatic and cheesy boy crazy song from our youths. She knew that I knew this song as we’d performed it together at least a dozen drunken times over the years, usually in my apartment and only one other time in front of an audience.

The lyrics started scrolling on the screen and I took a deep stabilizing breath.

And I sang.

Look, I’m not a good singer. I know this about myself. I’m not pitch-deaf or whatever it is that they call it. My voice is okay, maybe even passable if I’m in the shower or alone in my car. But, it was not a ‘people cheering at karaoke’ voice.

Something strange happened when I stood under those lights. Maybe it was the insistence from Kate to have fun. Maybe there was a little bit of magic in the costume making me feel like someone else. Someone brave. Maybe by manipulating the fabric of reality for the past few weeks I finally realized that this, this was a very very small thing in comparison.

I stopped caring.

I threw myself into the song. Like my life depended on it I belted out every verse and when the chorus started I pointed at Kate before turning and pointing at the pirate at the bar. My voice cracked on the high notes and couldn’t reach the low notes, but I didn’t care. I was performing a role, and in this role, this Sam–she didn’t care. And so then, why should I?

The crowd cheered and began singing along. Cell phone flashlights went up into the air and the crowd was swaying back and forth. I think I even saw a group of women dressed as crayons at a table that were shouting, “Sam! Sam! Sam!”

I couldn’t be sure because the toxicity of the lights and the music had overtaken me and I was having so much fun. My heart felt full and my shoulders felt light, for the first time. Maybe ever.

At some point I looked toward the bar and my heart did a flip when I noticed the man watching me. He has an expression on his face I couldn’t quite place. Something between amused and impressed. Something else that I couldn’t quite identify, the closest I can guess was curiosity.

When our eyes met he smiled and raised his beer in a small salute.

I nearly forgot the next line. The song built to its crescendo, a final moment of emotion and all the boy band glory I could muster.

“And no matter what I do, I feel the pain. With or without youuuuuu.”

And on the final note I struck a dramatic (Or what I hoped was dramatic) pose and the crowd erupted.

I walked off stage feeling like I had conquered a small country.

“Samantha Marion!” Kate tackled me the moment I reached our table. She threw her arms around me. “That was INCREDIBLE! Where have you been hiding that?”

“I have no idea what happened, “ I said completely out of breath. “I think I blacked out somewhere during the chorus.”

“The chorus is what starts the song.”

“Oh yeah I know.”

“Well, you were a star.” She pulled back, eyes shining. “And, he watched you the entire time, Sam. He couldn’t take his eyes off of you.”

I glanced toward the bar, where my serenaded pirate still sat. He was still there, in conversation with someone. As though he felt my gaze he looked up. Our eyes met once more and I smiled.

He smiled back and I very deliberately turned away.

“You should go talk to him,” Kate urged.

“Kate, tonight… well tonight I’m here with you.”

“Sam…” Her eyes glistened.

“Kate, I just publicly humiliated myself in front of the entire bar. I would really like to keep spending the night with my best friend. If he wants to talk, he can come over here and talk to me. Plus, you can interrogate him that way.”

Kate placed one finger to her mouth as if she were deep in negotiations. Then dramatically sighed. “Fair enough. You win.”

“Thank you.”

“But the night is young and I’m not giving up.”

“I would expect nothing less.”

Kate was quiet for a moment, swirling her drink. Then she said, “You’re not actually scared of him. You know that right?”

“What?”

“The guy. You’re not scared of him. You’re scared of wanting something new. You’ve been so focused on whatever you’ve got going on and all of the life changes that you forgot that you’re allowed to want a different life than the one you had.”

She pressed a hand to her temples. “Sorry that was weirdly specific and cryptic of me. Must be the ‘brew’”

I didn’t comment on what she said. It was hard to really pinpoint my own feelings, and yet she spoke them aloud as if they were letters on a page. Instead I grabbed us each a couple of waters and settled back in next to her.

She, however, brought it back up when I returned. “Hey, sorry again. That was a weird overstep.”

“No,” I insisted, “You have nothing to feel sorry for. You’re spot on. I’ve been running in one direction with very little room for deviation for the better part of a decade and now I find myself not wanting to keep running.”

She looked surprised. “I think you’ve been burnt out on life for a long time. I’m glad to see you looking more, you.”

“More me huh? How very perceptive and insightful.”

“Must be the brew. Makes me wise.”

“No. The brew makes you bold and loud. Now wine… wine makes you wise.”

She grinned, “Hey wanna do a duet next?” And just like that the moment passed.
