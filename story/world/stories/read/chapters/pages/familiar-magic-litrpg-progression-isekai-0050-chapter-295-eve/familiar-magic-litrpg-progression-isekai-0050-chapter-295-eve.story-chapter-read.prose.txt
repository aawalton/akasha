Ethan took a deep, satisfying pull on his pipe. It was the very same one that had been left by Tomo’s master, reclaimed in Viridus, so long ago. The taste was somehow bitter and sweet at the same time, and did a good job distracting from the acrid smell of the demon, still burning up into the Astral around him.

Ethan ignored the small wounds covering his body, trusting his Twilight regeneration, and Prime’s impressive ability to isolate and seal injuries to take care of them. Instead, he looked down on the basketball-sized orb, which glowed softly with power before him. Ethan reached forward from the rock he sat on, giving the monster core a gentle spin, enjoying how the fading sunlight reflected from it.

“And so, the final tool is yours, and the final card is ready to be played,” Tomo said. The demon had shrunken down to the size of a particularly tall Nator, and spoke between puffs on his pipe–though his was made of glowing pink-hued energy.

“I keep trying to think of more,” Ethan said.

It struck him just how peaceful Potentia felt at that moment. Minutes earlier it had felt alive with his intense battle against the wolf-like Twilight demon Steelbinder had found for him, but now all was calm, seemingly for miles in every direction.

The sun was disappearing behind distant red mountains, and the writhing forests in the valley before him looked afire in the last rays of light. This wouldn’t be Ethan’s last day on Potentia, the journey north would take several more at least, but it had a sense of finality to it.

“You had prepared as best as you are able, Ethan Bishop,” Tomo said. The smoke billowing from his nose made him truly look like a dragon.

“It’s never enough,” Ethan said. “There’s just too many unknowns, too many scenarios we haven’t planned for. The Emperor–”

“Will have his surprises, as you have yours,” Tomo finished. “We have done all we can, there is no value in fearing the unknown. When the Emperor plays his own cards, what will you do?”

Ethan smiled softly. “I’ll react as best I can, because that’s all I can do.”

Tomo’s scaled face twisted into a toothy smile. “Wise words,” he said, the deep voice rumbling from his broad chest.

In a burst of flame, almost cool to Ethan, Revan appeared. The Guardian Beast of Flare was the size of a large dog, and he curled up at Ethan’s feet, burning tails dancing in the breeze.

Ethan’s smile grew, and he leaned down to pet the soft, black and red fur. As he did, there was a plaintive sound, and something nudged him from behind. Ethan raised an eyebrow as Deevees began settling down around him, more than one pushing forward to be pet as well. Others drifted lazily through the air, disappearing through rifts, then materializing a short distance away.

“You all sense it, don’t you?” Ethan asked.

They didn’t answer. They didn’t need to answer.

The reckoning was coming. The end was coming. It had nearly arrived already.

Ethan’s eyes were drawn back to the east, where in the distance he could almost see the towering form of the Leviathan, the greatest weapon he’d ever wielded. The colossal beast would open the way to The Throne.

And there, he would fulfill a promise to the man he might have been.

“You’d better be right, Strategus,” he whispered.

Tomo frowned. He knew of the deal that Ethan had made with his dimensional duplicate, and didn’t approve. Ethan could hardly blame the Familiar, he didn’t entirely approve, himself.

He’d come so far, and achieved so much, and while he’d relied on many to get here–friends beyond anything he could have asked for–it still ultimately felt like it must come down to him.

Ethan Bishop had healed Dawn, and started the Union. Ethan Bishop had found and restored Noctem. Ethan Bishop had won Atlantis, and brought Twilight’s errant children home. Ethan Bishop had to finish this.

Ethan Bishop had to take the Throne.

But he’d promised that he wouldn’t.

He scooped up a small stone, and gave it a lazy toss. It traveled the length of several football fields before crashing to the ground in a small burst of dust.

“It should be me,” he whispered, looking back to the sunset.

“It should be you,” Tomo agreed with a nod.

Ethan sighed, letting his head drop. “But when I tried it in Strategus’ reality, everything failed. I couldn’t control the power, couldn’t handle the corruption.”

He looked into Tomo’s dark eyes. “I’ve seen his memories. I’ve seen the world he created. It was a wasteland, and he was utterly alone. He let three worlds die for his arrogance. I can’t make the same mistake.”

Tomo puffed slowly on his pipe. He’d heard all this before. “You are not him,” he said calmly.

Ethan let out a long breath, looking back to the horizon, the sun having almost disappeared. “I just don’t know who should take The Throne,” he said. “I’m confident that I can use the corruption to hold The Throne in place. Strategus even figured out a way to let someone else take Dusk’s power. But who?”

He scratched Revan behind the horns. “Do you want to sit on The Throne, buddy? It could be cozy.”

The fiery panther opened one eye to look at him, then let out a growl and a spew of flames that might have been a laugh.

Ethan shook his head, then turned to the nearest hydra. “What about you? You’re the adventurous sort. Care to wield the power of a lost god?”

Deevee managed to look utterly indignant, and Ethan slapped a hand to his forehead. “Right. You sort of are a lost god. A small piece of one, anyway. Fine, family loyalty, I get that.”

“Selina,” Tomo said while tapping out his pipe. “I like her. She has a good head on her shoulders.”

“A horned head, you mean,” Ethan said with a smile. “But you’re right. She’s probably the best choice. She’s brilliant, and intimately familiar with magic, in ways I couldn’t come close to.”

“Dusk rank,” Tomo added with a shrug.

Ethan nodded. “That does feel like a problem. Strategus was nearly annihilated by the power when he took it. There’s a good chance Selina would turn into a vaguely Nator-shaped puff of smoke.”

Tomo nodded, conceding the point. “The other end of the spectrum, then?” he asked.

Ethan shrugged. “I’d considered Allyara. She’s fought for this for so long, and whatever else she may have been, she proved herself a hero.”

“She did at that,” Tomo said.

“Bu we lost her just getting this close to our chance,” Ethan said sadly. “That just leaves Steelbinder.”

Tomo looked at Ethan meaningfully.

Ethan sighed. “Unfortunately, I agree. She’s just too much of an unknown. It’s hard enough to even get a read on her personality through those damned drones, and willing to help or not, I don’t know if I’m ready to give her the power of a god.”

The four of them stayed silent for a time, Ethan thinking, the others seemingly at peace. “Maybe Cal,” Ethan said finally.

Tomo looked back at him. This, at least, was the first time Prince Calevaro had been brought up. “If we manage to free him from the Emperor’s control…”

“He’s always had a peaceful way about him. A meditative sort of calm. And a powerful sense of responsibility.”

“You do not know if those are the qualities needed,” Tomo reminded him. “Strategus admits that he doesn’t know why he failed, nor why another might succeed. No living being is meant for this power, nor this corruption.”

Ethan couldn’t argue that point. Strategus, despite his utter conviction that Ethan could fail, could provide infuriatingly little insight into how to choose a better candidate. There were a few more options, Ethan thought, looking again at the monster core, but they were longshots at best. And possibly no better than Ethan himself.

As he’d been forced to a thousand times before, Ethan pushed the question away. There was still the hope that he could somehow awaken Dusk to take his own power back, as it was meant to be, and Ethan would need to put his hopes on that slim possibility.

“We’ve come a long way,” Ethan said after a few quiet minutes.

“Each of us has had our own journey,” Tomo agreed.

Ethan reached down to pet Revan. “You’ve been traded across generations for this chance,” he said. “I don’t know if you were ever expected to even leave Nexum, but you’re here now.”

The Guardian Beast looked up at him, intense eyes staring into Ethan’s own, and the Bond sent thoughts of friendship and camaraderie that warmed Ethan’s heart.

“Thank you for making me your Chosen,” Ethan said with a smile.

He looked up at Deevee next, several of the hydras were circling expectantly. Ethan chuckled. “I’m not going to forget you, you collection of knuckleheads.”

The many hydra shot forward, forming into one body that wrapped him in an odd, many-headed hug. When the hydra pulled back, Tomo was chuckling softly.

Ethan gave the demon a sad smile. “I’m sorry you had to wait in that dark tower for as long as you did,” he said, “but I’m glad I found you, my friend.”

One of Tomo’s heavy arms reached out to rest a hand on Ethan’s shoulder. “I am glad as well,” he said. “Though you took your time,” he added with a wink.

Ethan laughed. “All leading to this,” he said, barely believing it was true. “All leading to that damned Throne.”

“I never thought I’d see it again,” he said. There was fear in his tone. And longing.

“Perhaps you were always meant to,” Ethan said. He picked up the monster core, spinning it again, and frowned. “I never figured out the targeting,” he said.

“Faith, Ethan Bishop,” Tomo said. “We will face something terrible. I can feel it in the winds of my world. We will need every advantage we can find.”

“We’ll face it together,” Ethan said, voice serious.

Revan let out a roar, while Deevee screeched in excitement. Tomo just smiled.

“As one, Ethan Bishop,” he said quietly.

“As one,” Ethan agreed. Then he looked to the north, utterly certain he was staring at his destination.

“To The Throne.”
