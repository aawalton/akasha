Deep beneath the surface cavern of Underlune, someone else was busily preparing plans of their own. Someone who was rather irked that the most recent shipment was late, and not a word had been spoken about it.

Karlin Fierkraag cracked open a crate with a prying motion of his claws and peered inside, frowning. The alchemical material he expected had been delivered as promised, but…these storage vessels looked dangerously fragile.

“Amateurs,” he muttered. He ruffled his feathered wings and glared at his assistant. “Missy? I specifically ordered these containers to be air sealed. Why are they packaged like a fire trap waiting to happen?"

The blonde woman sighed. “I put out the word and explicitly said they were to be airtight. The supplier regretfully changed their subcontractor mid-production. They gave us a ten percent discount.”

The furious dragon latched the crate shut and gave the woman a haggard look. “Missy? This is pyrogel. It is possibly the most dangerous alchemical product I’ve ever had the displeasure of working with. Exposure to moisture can cause it to smolder. And once that happens, you have minutes to either isolate it or run for your life. A ten percent discount doesn’t mean dragon crap if it blows up in my face.”

The woman wore an edged smile. “Our supplier–”

Karlin slammed a fist on the crate, startling her. He realized in hindsight how dangerous that was. It would be rather ironic for a cinder dragon to be subsequently turned into cinders.

It was not how he wanted to live up to his species name. His claw slowly unclenched. “That idiot oversized beaver couldn’t run a banana stand, let alone a smuggling network, and he screwed me once already. And his old man has gone legit. So, guess what option I have left for anyone willing to do business with me? I can’t believe he cut a corner like this. It’s so irresponsible!”

"Almost as if he wanted you dead," Missy said in an acid tone.

He glanced at the array of materials and alchemical products in the small laboratory. He swore he still smelled that noxious tavern grill upstairs, even with the air filtered by a complex runic converter assembly connected to a metal duct system feeding the air supply. “I don’t need to remind you that if this ignites, you won’t have an opportunity to say ‘oops’ and fix your mistake. No healer in the world would be able to piece us back together.”

“Respectfully, Karlin? I think there will be a giant hole in the world if this all goes off. There won’t be pieces to put back together. I’ll get the chemists to stabilize it and seal it properly.”

“Good. Make it fast. Anything that starts to smolder goes to the neutralizer tank.” He pointed to a metal container with a hinged opening angled upward. “How’s the minting going?”

“It would be helpful if you told me what, exactly, you’re doing with all those coins or that metal blend for the core. I presume it’s not a hoard, correct?”

“That’s speciesism,” he snorted. “I still remember the rather ignoble death of Sautharin, a resident of the Arkantine clan. He was crushed by his own hoard when he stacked it too high. Gold is not comfortable to sleep on!”

Missy rolled her eyes at that. “Okay, well, it’s just what I heard. Now, what are we doing with the pyrogel?"

“Need to know basis. No one knows all the moving parts. I had to flee with half my stuff on fire at the forge, remember? I’m busy outrunning Vale, Fiefdala, and Aegortin. And frankly, if I pull this off, it’ll be a miracle.”

“Karlin? May I be blunt?” she asked, as if she needed permission. She didn’t. “Give this up. This isn’t going to end well. You are going to end up in prison in one of a variety of kingdoms, or a decorative skull on someone’s mantle, if you keep treating people like commodities. All for what, gold? An obsession with stripping your brother of any joy in his life?”

“To bring her back.” He would look compulsively at his datapad for the image of the family–his brother, his father, and his mother, all gathered and smiling.

But the imprint of what happened meant that the photo would never bring him any comfort.

Missy looked like she was about to have a meltdown, based on the way she clenched her pen and her hand shook.  “If you don’t like the plan, you can leave, Missy. Nothing is holding you here. No contract, no collar around your neck, nothing. You could go wherever you want, and I doubt any authority would be able to pin anything on you.”

He didn’t like being manipulative to her. It was acceptable to rip people off who should know better, and acceptable to rip off people who acquired their wealth through ill-gotten gains. But Missy…her whole life had been ripped away from her for years.

And he couldn’t stand that.

She stood there silently, weighing her response. “You need to ask yourself what she’s going to think of you if you somehow pull this off. Or what Feo’thari will think of you, if there’s anything left of that goddess. Don’t lose sight of that, please?”

He let out an irritated grunt. “I haven’t.”

“Then don’t lose sight of the fact that your brother didn’t cause your mother’s death. That’s a fact you refuse to acknowledge. Let. It. Go.”

“He can’t be happy, ever. He let her die.”

“You know that’s not true. He fought to defend her. He got hurt. Badly.”

“And he should have died defending her." One look at her plain-stated revulsion and contorted face told him he had gone too far, and he sucked in a sharp breath. “Missy, I–”

“No, you’ve said enough, Karlin. You don’t care about him. In fact, all you want to do is hurt him.” Her words were flat, as chilly as a glacier. “We finish this job, I get paid, and I’m done. Now, what about Swiftheart? Those two are allegedly tied at the hip after Vale.”

“I’m ninety-five percent sure she’s a vessel. Could be Varith too. Both have unique marks, according to my reports.”

“You know they believe you’re crazy enough to try and gather enough aurelium to concentrate what’s left of Feo’thari. They won’t let you do it because of the harm you might cause. Swiftheart is untouchable. How are you planning on taking her captive?” Missy outstretched her hands, tapping her foot patiently

“Uh…” he trailed off as she tapped her foot impatiently, and gave him a look a schoolteacher would give a student who didn’t do his homework. That disapproving look. “I have a plan.”

“I hope this plan is better than your last one. The one that almost resulted in us being burned to death,” she snapped. "Or the plan where you went into a four-way brawl, and lost a hundred and sixty million gold!"

The door to the laboratory opened, and a few of the crew walked in, holding what appeared to be foil wraps. The burly man in front was talking excitedly as he unwrapped his meal, which Karlin got a sniff of. A tantalizing blend of spices, excellent use of mushrooms, and a steak that that looked and smelled of a perfect medium rare.

"Are we uh..." he trailed off as he read the contempt between Karlin and Missy, and suddenly looked uneasily for the door.

But Karlin wasn't letting him go that easily: he had something that should be impossible: a good meal.. The food in this pit of a town was so bad that if it was good, it was by accident.

“Frisk. What is that?” he demanded, pointing a claw at his steak and eggs. The omelette on the tray, too, had the aroma of fresh, melted cheese, and sauteed mushrooms and onions. “Where did you get it?”

“Uh, pop-up restaurant,” the man with the short black hair shrugged. His face drooped as Karlin took a piece of the steak and chewed it. "H-hey, it's really good, save me some. Please?"

Oh. Dear goddess. Who made this divine steak? The sauce on top had hints of mushroom, too, meaty and earthy. There was also a fine balance of salt and other seasonings! Only a professional could have made this.

One bite was not enough. “I want this. Where do I get more?” Karlin demanded.

A sharp-looking kitsune snapped his fingers. “Oh, that woman from another part of Underlune is visiting. What’s her name, Brightblade?”

“No, that’s a tragic hero from some fantasy book,” the third person, a woman with dark red hair, countered. “I think it was Brightstone.”

“Yeah, that’s the name!” the first guy said, looking excited.

“Wait. The Brightstone?” Karlin gasped. “He doesn’t leave his home city, ever!”

“Nah, he had a daughter. Apparently, she’s pretty good, too,” the fox said contentedly as he added the mushroom glaze to his steak–only to gasp as Karlin took that bite, too. “Hey, c’mon, this is mine!”

“I want to see this place. I haven’t had a good meal here in a month.” Karlin glanced at Missy, still fuming. “Okay, I’ve been a little irritable of late. How about I make it up to you with lunch, and maybe an apology?”

“That better be one big damn apology, mister big, scaly and broody,” she said through her teeth. She relented after she stole the fork right out of his claws and took a bite of the steak, making a high-pitched sound of delight. “Oh, you’re buying, Karlin. Someone cooked big on this one.”

“Why is my meal not sacred?” the first man complained.

The location under that rickety tavern, in hindsight, was not worth the effort it took to bribe, bully, or make people forget that Karlin appropriated the ground beneath it. And worse, the cargo elevator could barely fit him, so he had to scrunch his wings tight and bend low to avoid hitting the ceiling.

The tavern was empty. No surprise, since it was morning. Or what passed as morning around here, since the only indication of the sun was the clocks on the wall. Underlune moved to a beat different from the topside world, unseen and unknown.

Even with it empty, Karlin recoiled from the stench of spilled beer of questionable quality, body stink, and maybe, something that crawled and died under the bar. No, that was just the bartender, mumbling in his sleep.

Karlin pushed outside, feeling no need to hide–he might be wanted topside, but the surface dwellers rarely came down into Underlune, except for a few brave souls. And no one was going to bother dragging a dragon unwillingly all the way back, even with a teleport pad at the larger city locations.

He still needed to consult his engineer on the digging location. It had to be precise; if they were off by more than fifty meters on their estimate, this whole effort would be for nothing. And if he somehow managed to pull this off…

Well, this might go down as the biggest heist in the history of Fiefdala. Maybe even the whole of Cepalune! But that small ego-stroking accomplishment paled to what would come next.

Except…Missy’s scathing question still bothered him. Even if I could pull this off…would she be proud of what I did in her name?

He shoved the thought down and gritted his teeth before throwing the tavern door open and setting foot into the less rancorous smell of the caverns. He saw people attempting to clean the street of broken glass, drunks, and one smashed stall.

That sounded like an average day for this crapsack town. What wasn't average was the crowd of people growing, all trailing to the outskirts of the city, streaming toward a building that–

He stopped and stared. “Missy, remind me. That rather ornate-looking tent and long line…is that this travelling chef?”

He couldn't help but stare. The tent was massive, and likely magical in nature, composed of two large subsections. One was obscured by simple doorways where the presumptive cooking was taking place, andthe other space was a large gathering area with simple metal tables nearby. It had a pragmatic look about it, sleek and efficient, with a temporary white and black tile flooring pattern conjured up.

Someone had a really nice mark power.

“Great. Look at this line. Everyone is getting in line for a brunch to ease last night's overindulgence,” Missy muttered.

“How do you know what brunch is?” he asked, narrowing his gaze on her.

“There isn’t a slaver in Vale that hasn’t attempted to bring a summons illegally over the past hundred years for bodies to throw in their little spats, or stealing knowledge from everywhere else. You know this.” Her voice was grating enough to leave him bothered. “Ergo, we get a bunch of idiots from Earth, and their knowledge somehow infects Cepalunean culture. And brunch.” She paused for a moment and shrugged. “It’s alright. Not sure if it’s worth the silver, though."

“I…” he trailed off. My own mother hailed from that world, too. I wonder what is happening there today? “Let’s just get some food. I’m going to cut in line.”

She laughed at that. “The perks of being a dragon?”

“No, the perks of being the landlord of this slopheap,” he grumbled.

Someone had cleaned up the surroundings of the ornate black and white building that had popped up on the outskirts, and Karlin pushed himself to his self-declared VIP line. With himself at the front. He hadn't taken the title of mayor--that would attract too much actual work, and he was already tapped out on stealing from thieves and those too irresponsible with their coin.

He spotted a wolven with a thick mane of fur standing at attention, directing people to tables. A waitress brought orders into the tent on little scribbles of paper. A kitsune with bright red fur dressed in a pretty black and white uniform was busily enchanting dishes to float and from with clever telekinetics.

He'd dated a kitsune, once. He still couldn't understand that purring sound they made when they were contented. That contented feeling had also been preceded by claw marks on his wings and back.

Still worth it, in hindsight.

“Reservation for Fierkraag,” he announced casually as he leaned on the table, using his height advantage over the rather tall wolven. “Party of…five.”

“You ate my brunch, Karlin. I'd better be part of the reservation,” his associate complained.

The wolven glanced up, unintimidated. That was a first. People who weren’t swayed by his words were either gullible enough for him to take their money, too stupid to live, or subtly dangerous.

“Reservations were placed last night. Apologies, Mister Fierkraag,” the wolven said stiffly. “I can put you on the waiting list. It might be an hour. Miss Brightstone has a couple of volunteers and a large roster of people.”

“Wait. Miss Brightstone?” Karlin echoed. “Not Magnus Brightstone?”

“His daughter is visiting. She’s gained a reputation topside. Today’s brunch is a fusion of the cultures, and as you can tell…there’s a lot of interest.”

Karlin had been trying to ignore the tantalizing smell of searing meats, a hint of good cheese, and…mushrooms? Not only that, he spotted confectionery delights he had a soft spot for, baked golden brown with crumbles of cured boar bits. The whole presentation was finished with dollops of whipped cream and a lemon tart sauce. Okay, brunch, then resuming schemes.

He allowed himself a small smile. This might be worth it.

He only caught a glimpse of a few servers: the bright red kitsune from before, and a short man with bright red hair walked by, bussing tables. He looked like a gnome. Maybe. Inside, he saw a woman with blue skin, finely chiseled horns, and golden eyes in charge, directing people. She wore a white chef jacket, working the meals like magic.

She definitely looked like Brightstone’s daughter, based on his memory. He had heard that she had set up residence in Fiefdala. “I’d like to meet the chef. I can tell they have technique…and maybe a small, charitable donation to this cause?” Karlin added with a toothy smile, plunking down a small stack of gold coins.

The wolven frowned. “As generous as your offer is, it is first-come, first-served, to remain impartial. Miss Brightstone is a big believer in that–”

“Oh, Jakey! Let me handle this one.” A tall woman wearing a uniform similar to the kitsune’s appeared, energetically waving her hand. She was rather unremarkable in appearance–brown hair, relatively thin, nothing extraordinary in the looks department.

Except for the emerald green eyes and the strength of her voice. It sounded just a little familiar. She smiled politely and bowed to Karlin. “Welcome to Brightstone travelling cafe, Mister…”

“Fierkraag,” he answered. He got an uncanny feeling like he’d seen her before. “Have we met?”

“Us? Nah. I’d remember a dragon with red scales and scarlet feathers,” she laughed. “Oh, but don’t listen to me gab. Let’s get you and your party. seated In here, we’ve got dining, dazzle, and dragons,” she added with a wicked grin.

Karlin wore a proud smirk. This was the makings of a good day after a month of lying low. “Lead the way, Miss…”

“Call me Suzie.”
