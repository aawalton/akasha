Fiona stared at the results in her hand. This dragon was either too stupid to live or living dangerously on the edge. There was absolutely no way he could be this crazy!

No, wait, her adversaries were. Varith was a maniac with too much power, and Glados was certifiably crazy with her efforts to destroy one nation as part of paying off a self-perceived debt.

Karlin…Karlin was just stark raving mad, with a touch of sadness to him.

“Hey Bonnie? Our nefarious dragon is coated in volatiles that indicate someplace, somewhere down here, is about to become another gaping crater in the world.” She flashed the display to the kitsune, and her ears stiffened; her tail poofed out like a bottlebrush.

“Well. Guess we have our answer. We still don’t know why he needs so much, though.” Bonnie tapped a claw on the display. “I don’t think he’s gone mad and intends to destroy a city. Just something very big, or very hard to crack open.”

Fiona let out a titter of anxious laughter. “Sure, Bonnie. Act like this isn’t about to become the equivalent of an underground nuclear test. I don’t suppose pyrogel has any other uses, other than detonating?”

The kitsune listed a few ideas on her fingers. “You could add a stabilizer compound to it and make it an incendiary chemical. It’s far more stable and can only be triggered by a current of mana energy. Or, he could add it to some other compounds to make…magical lava. It gets so hot, it can melt rock, metal, glass, and pretty much anything else.”

Fiona blanched at that. “Lava is already bad. Magical lava sounds worse. Way, way worse. Think, Bonnie! If I were a dragon with an obsession to bring my dead mom back from the grave, and I needed an insane amount of Aurelium to do it, where would I find it?”

“Plenty of it in Vale,” she murmured. “That giant blob of Aurelium you used to shield us, maybe?”

“Nah, bigger, I think, and more accessible. He needs more. Way more. Where’s the best place to store that much gold?" she pondered aloud. "Fiefdala and Vale, along with Bar’dathi, likely have more untapped veins from when Feo’thari's myr rained down. But digging would take too long. He wants what is already out of the ground.”

“A bank?” Bonnie proposed.

Fiona let out a sigh. “Banks don’t actually store that much gold. They’ve been switching to a credit chit system. When I pulled my stunt with Barry and crushed the tax assessor’s desk with a deluge of copper coins, that was pretty much all the copper coins in the city. Except for the ones hiding in the couch cushions.”

“Well, where else then? A dragon hoard?” She rolled her eyes in response. “The last dragon’s lair you raided, he had no gold!”

“I heard that. Those artifacts had far more value than gold,” Doug muttered, having walked in mid-sentence. “So Karlin is–”

“Deluged in alchemical pyrogel. And it’s got about two days before it goes up in smoke,” Fiona explained. “Think. He wants to use it to blow up something or melt through a barrier, most likely. Where would I find that much aurelium in one–”

She blinked. “Son of a dragon. I think I know where he’s going. Or at least, I know the target.”

“Where?” Doug asked.

“Fiefdala Central Treasury. Where the vast majority of the currency reserve is sitting, and Fiefdala’s gold coinage is disproportionately higher alloy Aurelium.” She almost smacked her forehead. “It’s also one of the most heavily fortified locations in the entire kingdom. So, you know, basically a mission, impossible for him.”

Dun-dun dunn, dunn, Dun-dun dunn, dunn…Shut up, stupid infectious tune! Crisis moment! Fiona peeked around the corner, having grabbed coffee and juice for the table to keep the service going. “It’s a solid theory. We need to confirm it and follow him. And anyone he’s working with on this one. He must have gotten that pyrogel from someone.”

“Except for one problem: the treasury is built to withstand attacks of that magnitude,” Bonnie whispered, ears pressed tight to her skull when she glared at Karlin. “That smug dragon couldn’t blast a hole in it, even if he tried.”

“Then he either has a workaround for that problem, or he’s using the gel for something else. I need to go snoop,” Fiona whispered, gathered her cheery face back, and sang merrily as she went along.

“One for you, you, you…and a bowl with a handle for you, good sir!” She handed Karlin the soup bowl, which had a built-in handle, along with a small pot for cream and a sugar shaker. He raised a feathery brow at that. “I know, right? Couldn’t find any proper dragon-sized ones.”

He laughed at that, with the woman next to him shooting him a disgruntled look. “Oh, you know something, you’re a treat. I actually downsized my mug so I don’t rely on it every day. But thank you,” he added with a toothy smile. “So how did a woman like you end up down here, with a travelling cafe?”

“Eh, you know. I have a way of meeting people,” she shrugged. “Plus, when Miss Brightstone wanted to pay a visit back to Underlune, I figured I’d check out something few people topside have seen! You gotta love it here, with the giant mushrooms, the predators with large, pointy teeth, or just the weird lack of sun!”

“You get used to it,” he took a sip of the coffee before adding anything to it, and his snout creased into a surprised smile. “My goodness, that is good. She really is related to Brightstone. I had heard she was set up in Fiefdala somewhere, not sure where.”

“You can tell by just the coffee?” Fiona asked, narrowing in on an opportunity. She passed a basket of freshly baked sweet rolls to the others at the table. “How?”

“Mark based power. I can tell the true worth of something if I touch it. A little weird how drinks work, but, you know…not every power is easily explainable,” he added with a shrug of his wings. He then added the creamer carefully. But no sugar. “And you?”

“A scent for sweets. Dunno how I ended up here at this table, though,” she added with an impish smile. Inwardly, she was calculating how many of his teeth she was going to smash with her hammer for the grief he’d caused already, and that thought made her smile.

His assistant scowled at her. “Is flirting part of the service here?” she demanded, her brow twitching.

“Nah, that costs extra. This is just me,” she added as she sized up Missy. Aside from her plain clothes, she wore an ornate metal choker. One that seemed rather thick, made of a dark gray alloy with streaks of black; it was certainly sturdy, with a single red gem in the center. “So, what brings you guys to Underlune? Business, or pleasure?”

“Definitely not pleasure,” she retorted with a voice dripping with acid. “Business.”

Fiona glanced around the crumbling and hastily erected shantytown. “I can see this place is thriving. Must be good times!”

“I wouldn’t know,” the woman said, rubbing the choker gently. The move wasn’t lost on her–or Karlin. They exchanged a more somber look between each other, if but for a brief moment.

Then she glanced at Fiona’s basket in her hand. “Uh, I’ll have a sweet roll, please,” she asked.

The ones I made myself? Let’s see how my skills hold up. She passed it to the woman with a proud smile and promptly took a bite.

She let out a sound of delight, and a smile forced its way through the gloom on her face. “This…is really good. Brightstone cooks like a dream!”

Yes, except I find it odd,” Karlin murmured. “Her father, last I checked, was in Drakilgate, about seven hundred miles from here. Why is she here?”

“We’re right under Fiefdala,” Missy countered, scowling at him. “Don’t ruin this moment for me, Mister Broody.”

“Actually, I'd like to meet the chef,” Karlin asked suddenly, glancing at Fiona. “Suzie, I know that the chef is busy, but…”

“It’s not part of the regular service. But I can put in a word. Are you looking for a private venue?” she asked with a skillful insertion of an idea.

The dragon tilted his head. “Is it possible?”

“She gets many requests. But…I can try. Why the keen interest?” Fiona asked an honest question.

The dragon glanced at his associates. “Because so many things in life are so precious few and far between. And this would mean a lot to me and my associate, personally.”

They’re totally a thing. This is an odder couple than Doug and me. She let out a soft cooing sound in response, to throw them off a bit, and Missy rolled her eyes and groaned. Like this wasn’t supposed to be known.

“I’ll see what I can do. Your meals will be out shortly. See you in a bit!” she beamed, flashing a smile at Missy.

As soon as she turned her back, she heard one of the other guys laugh. “Hey, Karlin, she called you out! When’d you two become a thing?”

“We’re not a thing, and if you repeat it, I’ll pull your tongue straight out through some other orifice!” Missy snapped.

Fiona, in the meantime, had planted an idea. One that would hopefully take root. She had to play it cool and attended to a few tables eagerly awaiting food, but some of them were so hungover they barely noticed the slight delay. Elven grace was the only thing keeping her from dropping four plates on the ground while also balancing drinks on top.

This was one thing she didn’t miss. Retail wasn’t as intense, in hindsight. Waiting tables had been a one-and-done dead-end for her as soon as she realized it, back then. Even Bianca endured it better than her.

She danced into the kitchen tent, where Darla was cooking away. “I got us in.”

“Oh? Why do I feel like this comes with strings attached?” she added as she flipped an omelette pan with her tail and caught it without even looking over her shoulder. Doug slid by and grabbed the omelette with a seamless fluid motion, and Darla wore a proud smirk. “Or is it my turn to be the heroine of the hour?”

“Yes, annnnnd, yes,” Fiona said. “Private dinner, in the dragon’s lair. As long as his associate Missy doesn’t think I’m coming on too strong.”

“That’s gonna be hard,” Darla added with a smile. “Alright, don’t immediately cave. My dad told me that principles matter. Make him work for it; tell Karlin that I’m busy feeding orphans.”

“Are you?” Fiona asked, a brow raised.

“A whole shop full of them,” she added with a shark-toothed smile. “You know it’s true, dear.”

“I felt my fur raise on end when you said that,” Jake grumbled as he came back with markings for table placement. He smiled proudly when he saw Darla still working as they conversed. “How are you holding up?”

“This is more intense than I’m used to. But, you guys know how to hold a spoon the right way, so I’ve got this. Now let’s wrap this dragon up with a bow tie and get him back to Fiefdala, and not add another crater to the world, right?”

Fiona returned to Karlin’s table minutes later after bringing out numerous trays of food for a few other tables. Karlin’s associates had been making small talk, and Missy looked less bothered–even smiling slightly.

“Steak, omelette, hashed tubers…you guys want tomatillo sauce?” she asked the male kitsune, who nodded politely. Then she handed Missy and Karlin their meals. “Well then, there’s a snag. Miss Brightstone regrets to say that while she appreciates the offer, she has made commitments to other venues down cavern.”

But Karlin wouldn't be deterred. To him, she figured, exclusivity was something that would make him want it more. “Surely another venue can be compensated to change to another day?” he asked politely.

Missy frowned and reached for another sweet roll instead. “I want to know who made these. This is…really good. I don’t think Brightstone made these. Cinnamon, a touch of honey, the tiniest bit of western spice…this is subtle. Homely,” she added. “This doesn’t match the rest of the food. But it’s amazing in its own way: light, fluffy, not too much sweetness.”

Uh, what?

Missy jerked upright. “I want to meet the person who made this. This…this reminds me of home. Forget Brightstone, I want to know who baked this.”

The question was, should Fiona tip her hand? “I can check to see which individual made it, but Miss Brightstone certainly supervised it,” she added.

Finally, my baking skills pay off, and I can’t take credit! Why does it have to be from a possible nemesis?! Wingding, some goddess is writing a godawful holiday TV movie for this, and thrust me into the script! She wanted to groan in frustration, but that wasn’t going to happen in front of the present company.

“Can we get…both?” Karlin asked. “I could compensate for your time. And I believe my f-friend would appreciate it. We have an apartment nearby. Tonight, 6 PM?”

“But, Miss Brightstone is busy. Feeding orphans. Routinely,” she added. Admittedly, it was pretty true if she included Kali and Lex, now. And Lani.

Wait, Lani had a family that sold her into an abusive labor contract. They were dead to her, and that made her an orphan by proximity. It still rang true. "Miss Brightstone books in advance, and--"

She halted after Karlin plunked down a stack of gold, one brow raised. “How does a stack of gold for the underserved community of Underlune sound as a donation? I’m sure your chef would–”

Darla walked in after Fiona gave the silent cue of ‘orphans,’ with a frown. “I heard someone was asking for me. Did I hear about a generous donation to the poor?”

“To the Fiefdalaian orphanage above our heads, specifically,” he added, pointing a claw skyward. “I used to frequent the city for a time. And I know it was chronically underfunded, last I checked."

Hold up, is Karlin onto us? This guy is a kingdom thief, and didn't get this far by brazenness alone. He must know something. Fiona feigned a gasp. “Miss Brightstone, I apologize, but–”

“Oh, no need. But you must know, Mister…” Darla acted like she had to search for a name.

“Just Karlin,” he answered politely. “I know you have a busy schedule. I understand. It's important to make time for friends and family."

Darla glanced at her notepad. “I have a three-hour window tonight. I was planning on a date night with my boyfriend. Then we’re onto our next venue…”

She finally set the hook, with a frown turning into a faint smile. “I can arrange for tonight. I’d like to see my father next week without delay, yeah? Been a little while since I saw him.”

“Six tonight. Meet at the…” he thumbed toward the scrappy tavern they’d been at the night before. “I’ll have my associates bring you.”

Darla raised a brow. “This better not be another summoning gone horribly wrong, because let me tell you, there’s still a dent in this pan from when I gave a lecherous old man a thrashing,” she added as she spun her favorite pan on a fingernail.

Karlin laughed heartily. “Oh, no, nothing of the sort. You certainly sound like your father, tone-wise. You should be proud that your cooking is almost at his level. That takes a lot of talent.”

Darla blushed, Fiona tried not to scream in either delight or fury–or both, and faintly, she heard what might be an oddly specific sound of a kobold screaming into a paper bag. “W-why, thank you. What kind of meal are you thinking of? I have access to a magical pantry, so the ceiling’s the limit,” she added with a twirl of a pen in her fingers.

Hook. Line. And Dragon. Fiona knew they had their ticket in.

Now, all they had to do was put themselves in the middle of a dragon’s lair with a chemical time bomb ticking down dangerously fast, defuse it, not tip off the fire-breathing dragon before they did so, and bring him into custody.

She mentally tallied the favors Rikkard owed her for this one, and vowed he now owed an extra year of limitless sweet rolls.

And extra hazard pay.
