In the end, almost all of my Companions want to come with me, even the samuran Pathwalkers. The only exception is River, who – not unexpectedly – is determined to crack what she felt she had almost achieved last night. She was tempted, though, thinking she might find useful ingredients in the forest, but in the end she decided not to join this time. If she makes no progress with her research today and the forest turns out to be safe enough, she'll go out with another group. Perhaps one with other alchemists.

I did put my foot down and say I would only bring Tier two and above – bringing the Tier ones into a forest that might be far more dangerous than normal seemed like a bad idea. Trouble, Blaze, and Spot were not at all happy about that decision, and I have a feeling Trouble in particular is going to be giving me the cold shoulder for a while. Though as I pointed out, the juvenile samurans can't come either, so we needed someone to stay behind to watch them. I suspect that was the wrong thing to say – having to give up exploring a potentially dangerous forest in order to look after cubs is hardly what Trouble would consider a good time.

He was even more annoyed when I allowed Ivor and Noir to go out with Sirocco, despite one definitely being Tier one, and the other debatably Tier one as well, despite his Class. He didn’t want to hear about the skies above probably being safer than the forest below – there are certainly predators up there but there are fewer places to hide too. I stayed firm, though – I need my flyers’ eyes in the sky.

Eventually, Iandee volunteered to stay behind as well which calmed Trouble down a little. He wanted to come and explore, but he also genuinely enjoys spending time with the hatchlings, so I don't think it was too hard a decision. I'm glad to have someone there who can communicate with everyone in case of an emergency. Because the chances of something happening are pretty high. And I’m not even worried about Trouble this time.

The samuran hatchlings have been increasingly restless over the last few days of being cooped up in the cavern, and I've heard several reports of them getting into places they shouldn't and causing trouble. With fewer supervisors available, I shudder to think what they might get up to. But Iandee is well used to them. Hopefully between him and the three raptor cats, they'll manage to keep the samuran hatchlings under control. They’ve managed well enough so far.

It doesn't take long until we're all assembled at the cavern entrance, just waiting for the soldiers to unblock the gate – it had been barricaded as well as locked, just in case something tried to charge through it.

Bastet is by my side, and I reach out to place a hand on her shoulder just in front of her wing. I feel the tension beneath her feathery fur and see the anxious look in her eyes.

What are you worried about? I ask her quietly as we watch two of the guard force’s Classers lift the bar away from the gates, lean the veritable tree trunk against the wall, and move to open the door.

What if more powerful beasts have come and I can no longer protect my territory? Bastet asks anxiously. What if they attack my pack because I am too weak?

Then we'll deal with it, I reassure her. This is our land now. We'll defend it however we need to. You are the most powerful of my companions, those present, anyway – I’m still not sure how Bastet would do against Kalanthia, but you don’t have to do it all alone.

She doesn't seem entirely reassured, but at least some of the tension leaves her muscles.

My companions and I watch in expectant silence as the door creaks open. My eyebrows go up at the pitted, discoloured surface of the other side of the enchanted gate – side effects of the lightning, or the storm itself? Putting out a hand to stop the human scouts and my companions from immediately setting out, I send out a general Inspect, searching for any dangerous areas. For all I know, the ambient energy has left the equivalent of quicksand in our path.

As my Inspect runs, it highlights just how much ambient Energy has been left in the rock and soil. Everything is practically glowing. However, I don't see any signs of instability, or any areas that glow particularly deep – and nothing glows red.

I glance over at the highest-ranking of the five scouts going out to investigate.

"I don't see anything immediately concerning, but stay wary," I warn him.

"I will, my lord. Thank you," he replies with a quick half-bow. He signals the others, and they step forward. One of them has glowing eyes indicating the use of a Skill. Belatedly, I wonder whether I should have asked them what they could see rather than unconsciously assuming I knew best. But since they're already halfway out of the door, I decide not to call them back. My companions and I have survived this far. We’ll just have to be careful.

I shrug and turn to the rest of my group with a raised eyebrow.

"Well, shall we?"

What I receive back is a mixture of excitement and trepidation, sometimes both from the same beast. Fenrir is the first to step forward, his inner tranquillity unbothered by the potential for danger. He's with his pack, and therefore he feels safe. I can only try to ensure that his faith is rewarded.

Stepping carefully down the slope, I keep sending out pulses of Inspect, looking for dangerous Energy traces, beasts in hiding, and intentional or unintentional traps.

Every so often, I look further – at the forest. Already it’s obvious that things have changed there. One tree that I’m sure was almost dead when I last looked has now burst into full leaf and flower. Another seems to be leaking disturbingly blood-like sap. As for the farmland, that’s completely overgrown again – it’s like it was never cleared in the first place.

And the sounds are different, too. Perhaps I’ve got too used to how sound reverberates and amplifies in the cavern, but everything feels just a bit too silent.

Markus, I hear just as we’ve got halfway down the slope towards the forest. It’s Elarion, trying to sound unbothered but not quite succeeding. He's not as good at hiding his emotions over a mental connection as he is in person. I hear you've left the cavern.

I have, I confirm. The storm has finished.

A brief pause, then he continues, a faint hint of relief drifting over from his side of the Bond.

Well, that is certainly good news, and well past time. But why are you the one going out to scout?

I'm not the only one, I assure him. There's a group of scouts doing more of that than I am.

Then why are you going out? A hint of exasperation.

Because I want to see for myself what's changed.

That's not the only reason, he accuses. It's to do with those beasts of yours – the ones you left out in the forest.

I'm silent for a moment, surprised he made the connection. I haven't exactly been advertising where Artio, Artemis, and Orion are, but I'm not entirely surprised that Elarion keeps close tabs on me. I do something similar with him, after all. I decide not to deny it – it's true, and being caught in a lie would do real damage to our still-tentative relationship.

Yes, I'm going to check on them, I reply. Though I assure you that that isn't my only reason for going out. I wish to see for myself what the storm has done to the forest ahead of us convening a council meeting.

I see, Elarion replies. He pauses briefly before continuing. I want to come with you.

I blink.

What? Now?

Yes, he says decisively. I'm already on my way.

Why do you want to come, Elarion?

Markus, he replies with a flash of exasperation, surely you don't think you're the only one who would like to get out of this cavern, or who would like to see the sun again. Moreover, I'm anxious to know what has happened to the earth outside – it may severely impact our farming efforts. Like you, it would be useful for me to know this ahead of our meeting.

Good point, I grant. I hadn't considered that. Fine, we'll wait for you. We’re just down the hill.

Matching action to words, I signal for everyone to stop. They look at me askance, but once I quickly explain, the confusion clears to understanding, though some, Bastet among them, can't quite hide their impatience to get into the forest. Part of me wants to suggest she go on ahead, but another part would prefer to confirm it's safe enough before she goes alone.

Fortunately, Elarion is quick to join us, and we're able to set off again with little time lost.

As we walk, I notice Elarion holding his hand out parallel to the ground, eyes half-closed, at times closing entirely, yet he continues to walk unerringly, placing his feet in exactly the right spots.

When he opens his eyes again and focus returns to them, I decide it’s a good moment to satisfy my curiosity.

"Are you feeling the earth?"

He gives me a sideways glance, then returns his attention to the ground.

"I'm feeling the life within it. There's far more of it here than there was before the manastorm."

"What do you mean? Like, insects, worms, bacteria?" Unbidden, my eyes flash a glance towards the tree that seems to have come back to life since the storm.

"Certainly insects and worms," Elarion clarifies, which doesn't entirely answer my question, but perhaps it doesn't translate well – as my conversation with the cook indicated, bacteria aren't exactly common knowledge here.

"And is that a good thing or a bad thing?" I ask, deciding the details matter less than the results.

His lips press tightly together for a moment.

"I'm uncertain," he admits. "Healthy soil has a great deal of life in it, so in some ways this soil has become healthier. However, the more life, the more ambient Energy tends to be present in the soil, which, as we've experienced, actually makes farming harder."

“That doesn’t sound good,” I murmur, worry rippling through me. I knew that we wouldn’t be able to immediately start planting even with the storm gone, but if farming has been made even harder…. I can only hope that the forest and seas will still be good sources of food or we’re going to be just as badly off as we were trapped in the cavern by the storm.

We reach the edge of the forest, not far from where the farms have begun being established.

"I'll leave you here," Elarion tells me firmly.

I eye him dubiously.

"Will you be safe on your own?"

He sends me a flat look.

"I am perfectly capable of defending myself. You are headed deeper into the forest – perhaps I should be questioning whether you are safe, O great leader of our expedition." His tone is sardonic, but only faintly offended – I decide to push a little further.

"I have a whole group of companions to help fight anything that comes our way," I point out. "If you're alone, you won't even have anyone to give warning while you're focused on the soil. Let me leave a couple of companions with you, at least."

Elarion looks over those around us, then sighs with a touch of exaggeration.

"Fine – how about those two?" He points at Catch and Pride. "At least I know one of them can communicate with me."

I don't appreciate his tone, but he is still an ally, and losing him to a mana-crazed creature of the forest would not be helpful in the slightest.

Would you be willing to guard him? I ask Catch and Pride.

Pride sends a flash of disgruntlement, but quickly follows it with a firm determination to stay with Catch, whatever he does.

Catch hesitates. I would prefer to stay with you and the other Pathwalkers, he says. But if you need me here, I will stay.

I'm sorry, I tell him. You're the only one I'd trust with this.

It’s not entirely true – Happy would be an option too. She’s perfectly competent with her axe and is becoming more and more confident in battle with her Metal-Shaping. But I don’t suggest her because I know Catch wouldn’t agree to leave her alone in potential danger. Similarly, I wouldn’t consider Hunter, though not only for that. She’s better in group fights than individual ones – her runes make useful bombs and shields, but her individual combat abilities are limited. Fenrir and Pride can’t communicate with Elarion, and Bastet won’t want to stay – shouldn’t, anyway. As for Lathani, she’s grown up a great deal, but she’s still quite a young nunda. She might easily get distracted and leave Elarion unprotected at the wrong moment.

Then I will stay, Catch agrees.

Pride immediately declares he'll stay with Catch. I turn back to Elarion.

"It sounds like you have your guard."

The Ice Druid gives a curt nod, though his attention is already half on his surroundings. Catch and Pride move to stand beside him.

"I'll come by once I've checked on my companions and we've explored a little," I tell him. "I hope to hear some good news from your findings."

"Don't count on it," Elarion warns pessimistically – though he's probably right and it probably counts more as realism.

With that, we head off in the direction of where I left Artemis, Orion, and Artio, hoping for good news on that front.
