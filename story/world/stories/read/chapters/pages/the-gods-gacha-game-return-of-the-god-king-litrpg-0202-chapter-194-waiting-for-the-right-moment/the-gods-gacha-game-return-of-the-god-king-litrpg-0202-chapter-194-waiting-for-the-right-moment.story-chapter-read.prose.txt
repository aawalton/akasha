A sudden change swept through the fortress. The patrolling warriors paused almost imperceptibly before adjusting their positions, forming a precise corridor that led toward the cathedral’s entrance. Then a lone figure stepped out.

He wore a long, dark coat that fell past his knees, while a half mask concealed the upper half of his face—smooth and unadorned, offering nothing that could be used to identify him. His posture appeared relaxed, yet there was a weight to his presence that pressed steadily against my senses. More notably, his physique stood in stark contrast to his attire; the broad shoulders and muscular frame beneath the coat gave him the impression of someone more suited to the front lines than giving command.

The moment I laid eyes on him, I activated Inspect Eye.

Inspection Failed.

The target possesses a skill that prevents external analysis.

“Again?”

Just like with Hyunwoo, the man turned his gaze at me. Even from over a kilometer away, he was able to lock onto me, sending a chilling sensation beneath my skin and causing cold sweat to run down my back.

The resistance concealing his status wasn’t merely the result of a high-level skill. It felt denser… as if something far beyond the commander himself was interfering. The information didn’t just blur, but it also refused to exist.

Undeterred, I activated Grant Plausibility.

You have used Grant Plausibility.

You have greatly enhanced the skill Inspect Eye for a single use.

A total of 80 Plausibility has been consumed.

“Inspect Eye.”

The target possesses a skill that prevents external analysis.

This time, I managed to pierce through his concealment.

The target’s status concealment skill has been partially broken.

Unfortunately, the moment a fragment of his status screen surfaced, the connection snapped almost instantly. A faint but overwhelming pressure pushed back toward me, erasing everything before I could grasp it fully. It felt as though a higher authority had intervened, forcibly reasserting control over what I was allowed to perceive.

“Tch.”

Having anticipated this outcome, I reacted without hesitation. I activated Predator’s Veil and immediately cast {Gravity Launch}, using the surge of force to propel myself away from my position. I had no desire to be tracked down the same way I had been when scouting Hyunwoo.

Even so, the attempt hadn’t been entirely fruitless. Though the information I obtained was practically nonexistent, I still managed to glimpse something.

“Kov…” I murmured the syllable under my breath.

For some reason, it sounded familiar. It felt like I had heard or seen it before, lingering right at the edge of my mind, just beyond my reach.

“Could it be…?” No, it could just be a coincidence. There were countless divine warriors, and similar names weren’t unusual.

In any case, it wasn’t worth dwelling on. With that thought set aside, I decided to return to our fortress after absorbing the faith contained within the statues scattered across the thrall camps.

When I returned to the supermarket, the streets surrounding the area were littered with the corpses of monsters. Broken bodies lay piled against the barricades, blackened scorch marks spread across the asphalt, and the faint scent of burnt flesh lingered in the air. The first, second, and third waves had clearly been dealt with.

“Looks like they managed to hold just fine without me,” I muttered.

I slowed my pace as I approached, concealing the remaining undead—now reduced to no more than two hundred. Even though I wanted to replenish their numbers, my current level of Basic Necromancy only allowed me to summon and maintain around two hundred undead at most unless I possessed Voldrath again.

As I stepped closer, several guards atop the barricade immediately turned toward me, weapons raised before gradually lowering them. I had already taken off the Mask of a Thousand Races beforehand, so I was now in my original appearance.

“Maxim!” Michelle’s voice rang out first. She stood on the elevated platform with her bow still in hand, her expression shifting from tension to visible relief the moment she recognized me.

“You’re back…” she exhaled softly, a hand resting over her chest.

“Yup.” I nodded as I walked through the reinforced entrance.

Inside, the atmosphere was far from relaxed. Divine warriors moved quickly through the interior, some tending to the injured while others reinforced damaged sections of the barricade. The third wave had just concluded, and preparations were already underway for the next one that would arrive first thing tomorrow.

Boris stood near the central area with his arms crossed, his massive frame radiating absolute confidence and pressure even at rest. The moment he saw me, a wide grin spread across his face.

“Gahaha! There you are!” he laughed. “You always show up after causing chaos somewhere, don’t you?”

“Well, you know me,” I replied.

“Hah! Come, get some rest for now.”

“There’s a more important issue at hand.” Nearby, Lucian descended and fixed his gaze on me. “You took longer than expected. I assume you found something?”

“I did,” I said simply before explaining everything I had discovered during my scouting, leaving out certain sensitive details regarding my abilities. I had shared fragments of information with them before, but never in full, so I used this opportunity to provide a clearer picture of the situation.

After that, they filled me in on everything that had transpired over the past couple of days. Just as Michelle had mentioned, they had been attacked by a group of enemy divine warriors, with the seemingly invincible old woman serving as a distraction. Thankfully, they failed to destroy Istellia’s statue. However, as all of them were strong Daleth-rank divine warriors, they ultimately managed to escape, suffering only minor to moderate injuries.

Boris also mentioned that Kaela, along with her kin, had already been dispatched to carry out hunting operations and independent offensive missions. Being a bunch that refused to listen to most divine warriors, this was indeed the best course of action, as they could also become a distraction for the enemy.

I gave a slight nod at that decision since it aligned well enough with the current situation. For now, forcing control over individuals like Kaela would only create unnecessary friction. Letting them act freely—so long as it benefited the overall battlefield—was far more efficient.

Still, I felt that there wasn’t much else to do over the next few days. After all, I had no intention of taking the offensive and risking an enemy strike at our rear. The remaining waves were the primary threat, and as long as the statue remained standing until the end of the stage, we would win. There was no need to take unnecessary risks, and I made that clear to them. The first stage of the Battle of Divine Will was almost always simple and straightforward.

“So you’re saying we shouldn’t counterattack?” Michelle crossed her arms lightly with a thoughtful expression.

“For now, no.”

Boris let out a low chuckle. “Gahaha… playing it safe, huh? You’re right, lad. Anyone who takes the offensive right now will be the one who loses the most.”

“Mhm.” I nodded. Boris was always quick to grasp these kinds of situations, so he must have realized this from the very beginning.

This was a three-way battle, and we didn’t need to win early. We just had to make sure that we didn’t lose first.

A few days passed in relative tension, each one marked by the relentless advance of the monster waves. By the time the sixth wave struck, the difference was undeniable. The number of monsters, which had started at only a couple thousand during the first wave, had now swelled to well over ten thousand. Moreover, the quality of the monsters had risen drastically.

Now, it wasn’t uncommon to encounter Gimmel-rank and higher-tier monsters, giving them both a qualitative and quantitative advantage. With their increased resilience and far more aggressive behavior compared to earlier waves, casualties on our side began to mount. If not for the defensive advantage we held—and my role in assisting the defense—the losses would have been significantly worse.

Naturally, the divine warriors were also growing stronger with each wave, gaining experience points and leveling up through constant combat. Many had already reached their level caps as a result of the relentless fighting. Morale, though strained, was kept relatively stable thanks to a single person who stood at the forefront of the battlefield no matter the situation—Boris.

“Brace for impact!” Boris’s voice roared across the fortress.

Explosions lit up the battlefield as ranged attacks rained down from the rooftop, but unlike before, the monsters didn’t fall as easily. Some pushed through the barrage, their bodies enduring hits that would have obliterated earlier variants. Others moved with unnatural speed, slipping through gaps in the defensive lines and forcing the melee fighters into direct engagement.

I stood atop one of the elevated platforms, watching the battlefield unfold with squinted eyes. “They’re getting stronger faster than expected…”

Even with the fortress fully reinforced and Boris’s near-perfect command structure, the strain was beginning to show. Several sections of the barricade had already been damaged, and divine warriors were being rotated out more frequently to avoid exhaustion. Casualties, though still contained, were steadily increasing. By the end of this wave, there might be fewer than a thousand combat-ready divine warriors remaining.

Still, what worried me most wasn’t these enemies—it was the other two factions. If they were going to make a move, it would be soon.

“Tonight… or tomorrow during the seventh wave,” I murmured.

Michelle glanced at me from the side. “What do you mean?”

“It’s when the pressure peaks. If they’re going to act, it’ll be then,” I explained.

“I see…”

The seventh wave would be the hardest—the point where even a well-prepared defense would begin to show cracks. If I were the enemy commander, that would be the exact moment to strike—when our attention was divided, our forces stretched thin, and our resources already depleted. Even if they were weakened as well, they would still have the advantage of defending their own fortress. And most importantly… neither side would be willing to settle for a tie.

At that moment, a faint light appeared on the marks on my forearms. Right on time. I immediately moved toward a more secluded area where there was no one else, and a streak of crimson-black emberlight and a gentle golden radiance appeared beside me as Ember and Solana returned.

“Heh… things are getting interesting, nya,” Ember said with a grin.

Solana folded her wings neatly, her expression calm. “Master, we have gathered information, coo.”

“Tell me.”

I had sent the two of them out to conduct reconnaissance in my stead while I focused on assisting the defense and leveling up whenever possible. Because of that, I was able to gather useful information without ever leaving the supermarket.

Solana spoke first. “The two enemy factions have already clashed multiple times over the past few days. However, none of those engagements escalated into a full confrontation, coo.”

Ember snorted. “They’re just testing each other, nya. Small fights, quick retreats. No one’s going all in.”

“As expected,” I said.

In a battlefield where a third party could benefit from an all-out clash between the other two factions, everyone would naturally act with caution. After all, they weren’t fools. The potential rewards for destroying an enemy’s statue and eliminating their commander simply weren’t worth the risk of leaving themselves exposed. Both sides were clearly conserving their strength and waiting.
