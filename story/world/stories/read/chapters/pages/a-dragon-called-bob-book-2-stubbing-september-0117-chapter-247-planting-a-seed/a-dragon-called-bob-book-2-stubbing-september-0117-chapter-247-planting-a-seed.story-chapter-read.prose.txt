The hangover was worth it. Dwarven ale, while it tasted like regular beer, was incredibly potent. Allowing for their body size, I was confident that their body mass was eighty percent liver by volume. It was the only way they weren’t all dead before their thirtieth birthday.

Kat had been instructed, very politely, as she was still mad at me for ruining her planned premiere, to begin acquiring more Golden Jack. I was going to make a killing. If only this world had opium… No. Bad Bob.

Emerging into an orlic camp with a hangover and a sense of smug self-satisfaction at an excellent trade deal was a heady brew. The first orlic who attempted to greet me with a slap got punched so hard he flew through three tents. That was all it took to get them to leave me alone.

“Geeku?” I said softly, regretting the noise as it only added to the raucous background of the greenskin encampment.

The place was popping off. Half-sized orlic were everywhere. Orlic children were as adorable as the adult version, which is to say not at all. They were also as violent and unruly as their older…siblings? I still didn’t know how orlics reproduced. Did they do the mum and dad thing, or did the little ones bud off the warriors who had fun in the battle?

Violence as an aphrodisiac was an interesting idea that I couldn’t see working long-term for any species. If you had to fight in order to want to fuck, I mean, how would that even evolve?

“Geeku!” I raised my voice a little too much and clamped my hands on either side of my head. Never get drunk with dwarves.

“Bob!”

“Keep your damn voice down.”

The tent flap swung wide, but it wasn’t a green arm that moved it. Gleaming steel, in the form of a giant pneumatic claw, swept the material aside.

“How’s ya doing?”

I was grabbed and vigorously hugged, one arm cold, the other sweaty. I pushed him away and fought down the nausea. “Have you showered since you lost your arm?”

I blinked slowly. “You only had twelve thousand before the battle of Gallow’s Garnish.”

I reconsidered my idea to dump Viktarlpth’s heart way out in the northern wastes as a punishment. There’d be millions of orlics, unaffiliated ones, in a matter of months, assuming Vik didn’t just overrun them and become a plague himself. But maybe there was something I could work with here?

“How would you like to have an infinite enemy to constantly fight?”

“Wass infnite?”

“Never ending.”

“Sounds good. We love a fight.”

Shifting back into my dragon form, I knocked Geeku back into his tent. The local children suddenly found other places to be, and the adults grunted in approval.

“Hop on.” I bent a shoulder so he could clamber up and take a seat at the base of my neck. His metal claw clamped down on one of my horns and tightened to the point it affected my neck movement.

“Ease up on the grip their, dude.” He slackened off slightly. “Do you need a towel?”

“Whatsa towel?”

“Of course. Well, hold on but not too tightly.”

The midnight feathers of my wings shook the air and threw up a cloud of dust as I headed for the sky. The trickle of undoubtedly foul-smelling warmth that ran down my back informed me that Geeku had indeed needed a towel.

We circled into the sky, hopping from thermal to thermal as I gained altitude. “Where’s a good place to put down your new frenemy?”

“What is he?”

“A shapeshifting demon. He’s the ruler of a circle of hell. The fourteenth, I think?”

“Ooooh, put it anywhere! Dat’s a good fight!”

“I don’t want him to overrun your camp.”

“He won’t.” Geeku laughed evilly. “You should see what ‘appens when the girls get stuck in.”

The idea of pissed-off female orlics, setting aside my surprise that they weren’t just part of the regular fighting force, defending their younglings and tents, also offered some interesting strategic possibilities.

“How would you feel about relocating your main camp south of Mount Bob?” I asked. I banked around and beat my wings to circle the aforementioned and eponymous mountain.

“Why?”

“You understand I’m now in a war with the Emperor?”

“Madson gonna get his shit pushed back in,” Geeku chuckled. He still sounded terrified by the long drop all around him.

“It’s not the fall that’s the problem, Geeku, it’s the ground. I’ll open a portal if you fall off. I’m expecting to have some armies marching north against me. Right now, I’m not too worried about other orlic tribes coming south, I’m rather concerned about human armies marching north.”

“When you put it that way, it makes it sound bad—”

“Fookin’ do it, Bob! More fights? More hoomies to piss on? More WAAAAARRRRR!”

In his exuberance at my morally grey idea, he had let go and used all three hands to gesticulate wildly, which caused him to fall. As his scream quickly dopplered away into the distance, I tucked my wings and curled into a sharp turn and a dive.

I’d need to time this fairly carefully, and in light of the brown now being added to the gold in the liquid trail he was leaving, I had no wish to have him sit back down on my shoulders.

The portal snapped open and instead of falling, Geeku was now moving towards me at terminal velocity. If i’d timed this right…

I hadn’t. I snatched the soggy and filthy orlic in a claw, and all the air left his lungs because he hadn’t quite reached the zenith of his ascent. Gravity had cancelled most of the momentum it had given him on the way down, but not all of it, and he slammed into my palm with a painful wheeze. Which was quickly followed by a pained grunt as my wings snapped out and caught an updraft.

“When I said don’t hang on too tight, I meant a bit more tight than that. You ok?” I craned my neck down to check his condition. He was not looking happy.

Perhaps eyeballing this wasn’t the wisest move, but I had debts to repay. Disgusting word, debt. Worst came to worst, I could always send Jace out on a bombing run.

Unsurprising. The question had been a long shot. I’d been told to plant the damn thing so…
