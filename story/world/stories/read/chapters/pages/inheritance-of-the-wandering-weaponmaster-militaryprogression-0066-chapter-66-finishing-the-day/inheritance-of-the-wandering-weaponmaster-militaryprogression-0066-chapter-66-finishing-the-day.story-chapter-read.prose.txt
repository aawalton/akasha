The five commoners came together and drank plenty of water as they took just a few short minutes to recover. The nobles, or whatever they technically were, stayed only for a moment before they went and walked out to wherever they were going. Once there was some separation between the commoners and them, Telemachus was quick to give an explanation to Ezra’s question about Mikhail.

“There’s tons of rules here in the Garrison, and I don’t know how many of them you’ve been told. In general, though, I’d say it’s pretty simple: don’t start fights, don’t steal, don’t try to kill each other, you know?”

“You mean starting fights like trying to trip somebody in the mess hall?” Ezra asked, disbelieving. “Or attacking somebody in the mess hall or trying to break their skull with your sword?”

“Yes, basically,” Telemachus grimaced. “It goes both ways, but if something is too difficult to prove, it’s not worth trying to bring it before the officers. They outnumber us, so we don’t start things, and they can’t do much other than just generally be arseholes. Even when they try to torment us, sometimes they get their ankles broken, like today. So, in training, like just now, we stick to working with each other or with some of the non-political other trainees. They aren’t cruel, just practicing like us, and we get along fine with them.”

“And am I right to guess that Anders and my new friend Tobias aren’t some of those people we can trust to have any interaction with?”

Nobody answered him verbally. The wry chuckles that filled the air were more than enough to let him know that he already knew the answer.

“So there’s petty things to look out for,” Ezra clarified. “Little things like making sure you don’t get tripped or paired up with some jackass in practice. We don’t do anything back because there’s more of them, and, if we are being honest with ourselves, we’re not about to go up against the children of nobles.”

“That’s about right,” Dirk shrugged. “Really, if you stay out of their way, nothing notable happens. We aren’t worth their attention most days, so if we don’t make a nuisance of ourselves, they’re fine leaving it that way.”

“That’s some fucking bullshit,” Ezra granted.

A chorus of sounds of agreement answered that.

“Now that Chloe’s back,” Telemachus said, pulling Ezra’s attention back to him, “it should be quite a bit better. She’s the only one of the Pseudo-Knights that’s willing to stand up to all of the nobles and Mikhail instead of just arbiting fairly. She’s firmly on our side.”

“So what’s Mikhail’s problem?” Ezra asked, getting to his original question as the other commoners prepared to walk to their next destination.

“He’s on the side of the nobles who want to be able to buy even more power than they already have,” Rose answered. “He comes from a really wealthy merchant family, and his position has helped them to continue to spread their influence. He has a very obvious reason to want to be able to buy additional power.”

“But he doesn’t give more than a slight preference to the arsehole nobles,” Telemachus shrugged. “He’s playing both sides, and since he’s a good Pseudo-Knight, the Duke and Dominick have no reason to throw him out. At least, that’s what we think.”

“All this politics stuff is stupid,” Ezra said as he followed everybody else’s path into a tunnel.

“Yes,” Dirk answered. “That’s why we try to stay out of it.”

A part of Ezra recognized that Chloe had told him the exact opposite, that he would have to learn politics, how to interface with people socially instead of just as a soldier. He wanted to ask why people were getting different instructions, or at least hadn’t gotten that particular suggestion, but he was brand new here. He wasn’t going to make any more waves than he already had, if he could help it.

“Most of us just hope we can learn how to become Knights before we leave,” Telemachus said as the group walked quietly. “The Duke is still hiding that from us for now, so we’re just waiting to become at least Pseudo-Knights before we get going.”

“You don’t want to keep working here? Get hired on by the Duke?” Ezra asked.

“You want to keep working for nobles?” Telemachus demanded, his disbelief obvious. “I get that you only got here because a noble took notice of you, but you want to keep bowing the knee to them?”

“No,” Ezra scowled. “Haven’t met a noble yet that I would say deserves to have me serve them blindly. But if you want to make money, to be able to take care of people, it seems to me like signing on with a noble is one of the best options, if only in the beginning.”

“It’s true that they’ve got most of the money,” Telemachus answered reluctantly. “But if there’s another way, you best believe that I’m going for that before I swear some oath to a fat-headed—”

“Shut the fuck up,” Dirk said, elbowing the other man in the ribs. “We get it, don’t go making enemies for yourself and the rest of us.”

Telemachus raised both of his hands in apology and refocused on Ezra’s question.

“So, the deal with Mikhail is while that he sucks and will never be somebody you can trust, you can rely on him being reasonably fair, even if he slightly favors the nobles. The other Pseudo-Knights, when they have time for training us, make no secret of how they favor the Duke. That’s the best way for us to get the best training, so, as far as they know, we’re on their team.”

Ezra nodded slowly, understanding, and wondering just how much of this Chloe knew about. After all, she’d said that she thought he would be well and truly on the Duke’s, and by extension her, side.

“So other than training,” Ezra asked as the conversation died out, “what do we do? Are we sent on patrols? I’d imagine that we don’t spend all day every day training like this.”

“Depends on the day,” Rose said, flouncing forward, “but you’re right. We go on patrols. Since we’re pretty close to the Dragon Wilds, it’s not too uncommon for various kinds of Dragonkin or goblinoids to be pretty close by, so we do little exterminations as well. They keep us busy and want us to get as much real combat experience as possible.”

At that, Ezra remembered his own fight against the army of beasts and nodded. As they walked all the way through the tunnel and into another room, a question occurred to him.

“How many of the prospective Knights are Adepts?”

“Five, other than you,” Telemachus answered immediately. “In the anti-Duke faction, Willem and Louis are the two to look out for. Then Patrice, Tula, and Quinn are the others who aren’t openly associated with the other faction.”

“So is Anders the leader of the anti-Duke faction, even though he’s not even an Adept yet?” Ezra asked.

“Yes,” Dirk nodded. “Since his father is a Count and he is supposedly the one who’s going to inherit that position, that political power is much more important than being able to win a couple fights here.”

Ezra nodded, starting to understand the situation he was in. Then, with a deep breath, he followed the other commoners into the room where they were going to have their... what did Mikhail call it? Individual weapon training?

The room they stepped into was different from the last, where instead of just a series of different sand pits, there were five different sections for practicing. At each of the sections, six potential instructors stood. Having come ahead of the commoners, the noble trainees had already divided themselves up to stand in front of a different section.

“It doesn’t really matter where you go,” Telemachus said, his tone of voice much more casual than it had been. “Since none of us are even close to Knighthood, they constantly have us rotating between different instructors. Right now, you’ll be working with somebody who’s an established proficient user of a particular weapon.”

Ezra nodded in understanding, and, just as he realized there was somebody that he hadn’t seen, he locked eyes with her across the way. He wasn’t sure what kind of reaction he was going to get, but was pleased when Artemis smiled and gestured for him to come and join her.

“You said you weren’t a womanizer,” Telemachus laughed. “Already, though, you’re having women inviting you to come over?”

“I was there when her older sister died,” Ezra said, feeling a sense of melancholy come over him as he remembered Helen.

The others went silent at that, and, to Ezra’s surprise, Miriam stepped forward and placed a hand on his shoulder.

“I’m glad you knew Helen. We miss her. Now go, don’t leave Artemis waiting.”

In that moment, Ezra remembered that, if Artemis was here, of course Helen would have been here before she died just a month and a half before. With that in mind, Ezra hustled over to Artemis, who spread her arms to give him a hug.

“I’m glad you’re here. After I saw what you did, and especially that basilisk, and Chloe saw, I knew it was only a matter of time, but this is faster than I expected.”

“Here I am,” he shrugged as he accepted the quick embrace. “How’s your family?”

“We miss her,” Artemis said simply, shrugging. “It’s hard, but we’ll make it. We always have until now anyways.”

Before Ezra could ask a follow-up question, Artemis gestured for him to pick up a sword and shield. He fought to hide the frustration he felt at that, but he did do as she commanded as she led him over into a section of the practice field.

“I’m one of the sword-and-board instructors,” Artemis said by way of introduction. “Considering your lack of training, and how frequently people seem to think this is the right way to fight anyways, I figure getting you a crash course on this will be better sooner rather than later.”

“Fine,” Ezra replied, rolling his shoulders. “I was hoping for something other than the weapon that just beat me black and blue, but I guess you’re in charge, huh?”

“Beaten black and— you were paired up with one of the nobles?” Artemis immediately understood what he was getting at. When he nodded, she shook her head and sighed. “I wish I could help you more during those times, but unfortunately, I can’t.”

“Why aren’t you one of the people in the prospective Knights squad?” Ezra asked, just as much out of curiosity as out of a hope to delay the inevitable.

“Too old, took too long to become an Adept,” she replied, not hesitating. “I was in there for a while, but if you’re not showing progress for too long, you just get moved into different parts of the army. But that’s enough about me, let’s get you trained up.”

That made sense, and Ezra squared up, holding his shield in his left hand and his sword in his right.

“How bad of a beating are you going to be giving me?”

“A beating?” she scoffed. “Not at all. I am going to make sure you keep getting better at using the weapon you’ve got, though. Now, first things first, edge alignment.”

To Ezra’s surprise, Artemis was a quite good instructor, probably better than Chloe. Chloe, though obviously more powerful than Artemis, was an instinctive fighter, and, as a result, wasn’t very good at explaining her thought process or the reasons why to do one thing instead of the other. Artemis, however, was thorough, and her attention to detail was greater than Chloe’s. The small things that the Pseudo-Knight struggled with didn’t slip Artemis’s notice, and over the course of the next hour and a half, he enjoyed making strides in his ability to wield the unfamiliar weapon.

“I’m glad you’re here,” Artemis said, a sad light touching her eyes as they finished their training.

Ezra nodded as the training came to an end, understanding a small portion of the emotion that troubled her. He followed along with the other commoner trainees and had a quick dinner that resulted in no further issues. Grateful for no arguments or fights, Ezra followed the schedule the rest of the day, though the final section of the day was dedicated to meditation. He thrived there, and didn’t mind taking some time to help Dirk specifically with better sensing the difference between earth-affinity and stone-affinity aura.

After several hours of meditation, finally, the day was through, and Ezra enjoyed the opportunity to wash himself and retire to his room. There, though he wanted to ask Jormun questions, he prioritized sleeping for now. He slept dreamlessly.
