My pulse raced in my ears, drowning out the sound of the indicator as Mom turned into Bracken Berry Farm animal rescue. I could feel my hands trembling in my lap, and I took a deep breath, trying to soothe the anxiety that I could feel swirling within me, but it didn’t help.

It was almost time to see Rex again, to hopefully be able to bring him home. Well, kind of. It was twenty to twelve. We were early for our appointment, but I hoped that wouldn’t really be a problem. I assumed they’d probably have a waiting area or somewhere for us to sit until it was time.

Shopping for all of Rex’s things hadn’t taken anywhere near as long as I thought it would. I expected we’d spend hours in the pet shop, or maybe even have to go to multiple shops to get everything, but it wasn’t too bad. The first store we went to seemed to have everything we could possibly need.

I was worried that we’d forgotten about something, though. That anxiety pulled at the back of my mind the entire drive, but it was only growing stronger as Mom slowly drove down the long, bumpy path towards the car park. I’d checked the list I’d written multiple times, though. I’d even called up the animal rescue to make sure there was nothing else that we needed, and they’d reassured me that we had everything, but it wasn’t enough to set my mind at ease.

Chewing on my lower lip, I unlocked my phone again and found the list in my notes. My eyes flicked back and forth as I read it, but every item had been ticked off. There was nothing that we’d missed.

It wasn’t the end of the world if we did forget something, I tried to tell myself. When I called earlier, Surina said there was a shop in the rescue centre, and Mom had said everything there was probably horrifically overpriced, but I was pretty sure she wouldn’t mind too much if we did have to buy something from it.

We had his collar, though. That was the most important thing. They’d said we wouldn’t be able to take Rex home if he didn’t have an ID tag, and it had been the first thing we’d bought. We didn’t put Rex’s name on it, but it had both Mom’s and my phone numbers etched into it, so I hoped that was okay.

It probably was. The employee at the pet shop had said we shouldn’t put Rex’s name on it. Apparently, that was dangerous. If he got lost again or if someone stole him, someone could use that to bond with him or something. I didn’t quite understand that, but Mom immediately agreed with them.

Another shaky exhale escaped my lips as Mom pulled into a parking space, and my eyes found the building in front of us. It was huge. Much bigger than I’d been expecting. I thought it would be about the size of the vets, but it was bigger, and there was a huge field attached to it. I could see dogs playing behind the fence, and I immediately tried to search for Rex, but they were too far away for me to work out if he was in there. I could see a few black dogs, but I wasn’t sure if they were him.

The knot of anxiety in my stomach grew tighter, making me nauseous. I was so excited to see him again, but part of me was also nervous. I was scared, and I knew that was stupid. I’d been looking forward to that moment the entire week. I’d been thinking about him almost constantly, but suddenly, I was terrified.

What if he didn’t remember me? Or what if he now hated me? The last time I’d seen him, he was in pain. His back had been sliced open by the fence in my garden, and we’d taken him to the vets. He must have been so scared, and I’d just abandoned him without saying goodbye. What if seeing me again reminded him of that pain and fear, and he attacked me?

An image flashed behind my eyes, sending a burst of adrenaline through me. I saw a dog, or some kind of beast, diving towards me. Their fangs sank into my arm as I fought to get away, but they were too strong. With a single tug, pain exploded in my shoulder, and my arm was torn off.

A shudder slipped down my spine, and I pushed the image away. That wouldn’t happen, I told myself. Rex wouldn’t attack me, and even if he tried, there were people there. Surina had said that our visit would be overseen by animal handlers, and surely they’d step in and stop him if he tried to hurt me, wouldn’t they? I wasn’t sure. I hoped that they would, but it didn’t entirely stop my anxiety.

It would be good to see him again, though. Despite my fear, I was looking forward to that because it meant I’d finally be able to see his back. Then, I’d know that he really was healing and getting better. Whenever I’d asked throughout the week, I’d been told that the cuts were doing well, but seeing him and the injuries with my own eyes would help convince me that he was truly okay.

The car engine stopped suddenly as Mom removed the keys, and my eyes snapped towards her. I was far too aware of how fast I was breathing. Anxiety was making my breath shallow, and I could feel my chest rising and falling too quickly. If Mom were too look at me, I knew she’d notice, but she didn’t even glance at me as she grabbed her handbag and got out of the car.

Scrambling for my backpack, I quickly followed her and threw my door open. She’d already started to make her way across the grassy car park, and I rushed to catch up with her, but her eyes remained fixed on the building before us. It wasn’t until we neared the fence and a symphony of barks sounded that she even slowed.

My eyes darted towards the dogs on the other side of the fence. Most of them were focused on the animal handlers who stood in the field, playing with the dogs, but a small crowd was starting to form near us. They seemed to be rushing to greet us, and immediately, I realised that Rex wasn’t there.

“Hello,” my mom muttered as she leaned toward the dogs, her voice taking on a tone that was normally only reserved for very young children.

I glanced at Mom in surprise. I’d never heard her sound like that, and Mom looked just as shocked as I was. She glanced at me before straightening and continuing to walk without looking at the dogs again.

The urge to follow her pulled at me, but I couldn’t help lingering there for a couple of seconds longer. The dogs were just too cute, and there were so many different breeds in there. Giant huskies and German Shepherds towered over tiny little pugs, and I wanted to pet every single one, but I forced myself to start moving again.

My gaze remained on the dogs as I walked, unable to look away, and I found myself feeling glad that I already knew which dog I was going to adopt. Otherwise, it would have been almost impossible to choose between them all. I had no clue how anyone did it. I was pretty sure I would have just adopted multiple dogs.

Mom had already entered the building, I realised when I finally managed to rip my eyes away from the animals. The entrance wasn’t too far from where I was, and I glanced back at the dogs once more before hurrying inside, catching up with Mom as she came to a stop near the front desk.

Another customer stood in front of her, speaking to the receptionist, but my attention was immediately grabbed by the cage at their feet. I glanced at the man again before peering into the cage and spotting a tabby cat napping inside.

Perhaps I would have gone for a cat too, if I had free rein over which pet I wanted to adopt. I knew I wanted a dog, despite being a little scared of them, and I’d never really considered getting a cat, but it was kind of cute. Maybe I would have gotten both.

Anxiety continued to bubble in the pit of my stomach as we waited for the man to be done. I shuffled back and forth between my feet as restless energy simmered within me, but it wasn’t enough. I needed something more to distract myself, and my gaze darted around the room.

The entrance of the rescue centre looked fairly similar to the vets. It was clean and very open, with the same shiny vinyl flooring, but unlike the vets, there was so much to look at. Next to the front desk, there was a machine that engraved ID tags and rows of shelves that held a selection of dog and cat beds, food, and leads. My eyes scanned the items quickly, checking whether there was anything there that we hadn’t gotten for Rex already, but I was pretty sure we had everything.

My gaze left the dog food, moving on to the wall of photos next to it, and I felt myself take a step towards it without really noticing. I couldn’t help but stare, my jaw turning slack. All of the animals in the photos had been adopted from that animal rescue; the sign in the middle explained that. Their new owners had sent in the pictures, but the thing that stood out to me was how happy the pets all looked. Some were playing in a garden, others napping on a bed or being held by a smiling person, but the animals all seemed so… loved.

“Hello, thanks for waiting. How can I help you?” a familiar voice said from behind me.

I quickly turned around and joined Mom at the reception desk again, my eyes fixed on the woman behind the counter. I’d only ever heard Surina’s voice a handful of times, but I recognised it immediately.

“We have an appointment at twelve,” Mom replied. “We’re a little early.”

Surina smiled before glancing at the computer in front of her.

“Okay, great. Can I get a name?”

“Amanda Barker.”

At that, Surina looked up again, her eyes finding me.

“And you must be Grace!” she exclaimed.

I had to fight to hold back a grimace as embarrassment washed over me. Surina must have hated me. She, and everyone else who worked there, must have found my constant calls so annoying, and I felt so foolish as I stood in front of her. I could barely meet her eye.

“Yeah…” I mumbled. “Nice to meet you.”

An apology had almost slipped out, but I managed to catch it before it could. Somehow, apologising felt even more embarrassing than not saying anything. I just wanted to ignore it, to pretend that I hadn’t been that pathetic and childish, and hope that no one brought it up.

“It’s nice to meet you too!” Surina replied, her tone enthusiastic. “It’s great to finally put a face to the voice, and I’m so glad this has worked out for you and Rex so far. He’s such a sweet dog!”

“Yes, we think so too,” Mom said once it became clear that I wasn’t going to speak.

I wanted to; I just couldn’t bring myself to do it. It felt like my voice had been snatched away from me, or there was something in my throat, blocking the words from escaping. I was too ashamed, too nervous to say anything.

“Fantastic. I have no doubt that you’ll get on wonderfully,” Surina told us. “Did you manage to find the shampoo Lily recommended in the end?”

“Ah, yes. Thank you for that.”

“No problem at all! Well, if you want to take a seat or have a look at the items we have for sale, I’ll mark you as arrived, and one of the handlers will come out to grab you soon. They shouldn’t be too long,” Surina said as she clicked something on the computer.

“Great,” Mom replied before turning away from the desk.

I followed her awkwardly as she moved towards the shelves, unsure what else to do. Part of me wanted to go sit down, but I was pretty sure Mom would have found it rude, and I was so filled with anxious energy that I didn’t think I would have been able to sit. I wanted to pace or break into a run, but I didn’t want anyone to know how nervous I was. For some reason, it always just felt safer to hide that from people.

A soft snort came from Mom, and I glanced at her. Her eyes were fixed on a bag of treats, and her lip was curled into a smirk.

“See. What did I tell you about the price? Ten pounds for this?” she scoffed, tapping the price sticker.

My eyes flicked towards Surina, hoping that she hadn’t overheard Mom. I wasn’t sure why I was so worried about that. I doubted it was Surina who decided the price of the items they sold, but it still felt rude to comment on that.

“Can we sit down, please?” I asked, before quickly adding, “We already got everything we need, right?”

“And more,” Mom said snootily. “Fine. Go on then.”

She gestured towards the seats, and I glanced at Surina again as I hurried towards them. Her gaze was fixed on her computer screen, and her expression didn’t seem offended. It looked fairly neutral, so I hoped that meant she hadn’t heard Mom, but I was still worried. There wasn’t anything I could do about it, though. I couldn’t turn back time or take back Mom’s words.

Choosing a chair as far away from the front desk as possible in case Mom said something rude again, I slipped my bag off my back and sat down. Mom took a seat next to me and immediately pulled out her phone, tapping on the icon for the mobile game she always played.

After a moment of hesitation, I unzipped my bag and reached inside. My fingers brushed against the collar we’d bought for Rex, causing my heart to start racing again, before closing around my phone. I sat back, subtly tilting the screen away from my mom as I unlocked it to hide the messages that had come through from Duncan. I’d read them later, once I wasn’t with Mom anymore.

Swiping through the apps on my phone, I tried to find something that would distract me. There were a few games that I’d downloaded, but I hadn’t played any in a while, and nothing appealed to me. Instinctively, I opened Instagram, staring blankly at the posts as I scrolled down my homepage. I was barely paying any attention to what I was looking at, though. My eyes kept flicking towards the door.

I was so scared that someone would walk through it and announce that they were there to pick up Rex, that they’d been searching for him all week, and they were so excited that they’d finally found him that they hadn’t even thought to call. They’d just gotten in their car and driven straight to the rescue centre.

What would happen then? Surely, he’d be given back to his old owner. Maybe they’d had him since he was a puppy, and they already loved him much more than I did. That felt impossible, but it would have been cruel to let us adopt him if his owner wanted him back. We would have just had to let him go. Even just the thought of that broke my heart, and I stared at the door, half-expecting someone to appear, but no one did.

“Stop bouncing your leg. You look like a drug addict,” my mom snapped under her breath. “Just sit still.”

My leg immediately fell still. I hadn’t even noticed it was bobbing until Mom pointed it out, and I knew she hated that. It wasn’t the first time she’d told me off for it or compared me to someone who was addicted to drugs, and I’d never really understood it. Did people who were addicted to stuff bounce their legs a lot?

I had no idea. I’d never even used drugs, and I didn’t know anyone who did. Well, not really. I was pretty sure some people smoked weed at parties sometimes, but I didn’t think they used much. Maybe they did, though. I had no way of knowing, and it smelled so gross, so I tended to stay away from them.

Restlessness grew within me. I could feel energy coursing through me, searching for a way out, but it was trapped. I couldn’t bounce my leg again, and I knew I needed to remain seated. Mom would judge me if I didn’t. She’d told me to sit still, and getting up to pace or move around would be going against that. It might annoy her so much that she’d change her mind about adopting Rex. It seemed unlikely, but I didn’t want to risk it.

Footsteps broke the silence that had settled over us, and my head snapped up. My eyes found the archway on the far side of the room, and I held my breath as I waited for someone to appear.

Within seconds, a blonde woman in a matching khaki polo shirt and trousers appeared. She smiled as her gaze landed on us, and I felt my heart lurch with a mix of excitement and anxiety.

“Amanda and Grace?” she checked.

“Yes, that’s us,” Mom replied, tucking her phone away and standing.

“Great to meet you,” the woman said as she crossed the room and held her hand out to her. “I’m Lily, one of the animal handlers here. We spoke on the phone earlier.”

“Ah, thank you for your help with that.”

Lily held her hand out to me, and a flare of fear exploded in my stomach as I stared at it. I had to shake her hand. It would have been so rude of me to refuse, but my palms were so sweaty, and that felt gross. I didn’t want her to have to feel that, but there was no other way. I wiped my hand on my leg as subtly as I could before reaching out.

“Not at all. I’m always happy to help,” she told us as we shook hands. “If you need anything, even after you adopt Rex, feel free to give us a call.”

“We will.”

“Great! Well, shall we go back there?” Lily asked.

“Sounds great,” Mom said as I nodded eagerly.

Lily’s smile widened, and she gestured towards the archway she’d appeared through before starting to walk. I swung my back on my back, almost tripping in my haste to follow her. I could feel my heart pounding in my ears as we made our way along the corridor, and my eyes darted from side to side, glancing through the window on every door we passed. I didn’t see much, though. There were what looked like a couple of store rooms, but the rest of the rooms were empty.

Soon, Lily stopped outside a door and pushed it open. I immediately looked for Rex, expecting him to be in there, but he was not. Like most of the others we’d passed, the room was pretty much empty. A few chairs had been placed off to one side, and there was a box of balls and dog toys in the back corner.

“If you want to wait here,” Lily started, “I’ll go and get Rex.”

“Great,” Mom said.

Lily smiled before turning and leaving the room, and Mom moved towards the seats. I started to follow her, but I only managed a few steps before falling still once more. My feet felt too heavy. I couldn’t lift them from the floor, and all I could do was shuffle nervously as I waited for Lily to return with Rex.

Time seemed to stand still as I stood there in the middle of the room. I wasn’t sure if seconds had passed or hours, but eventually, I heard the click of nails on the floor in the distance, and the air caught in my chest.

“Whoa, boy,” I heard Lily mutter. “Stop pulling.”

Tears stung behind my eyes as I stared at the door, fighting the urge to run through it and find Rex. It took every ounce of my self-control not to do it, but somehow, I managed to remain in the room, listening as the clack of his nails drew closer.

After an eternity, Rex appeared in the doorway. His eyes seemed to find me immediately, and he let out an excited bark as he pulled at the lead, trying to run to me, but Lily held him back.

Fear fluttered in my heart, but it was drowned out by the sheer happiness and excitement that flooded me. He was okay. He was still there. His back was healing, and we were actually going to adopt him.
