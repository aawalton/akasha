Scalefort Bastion sprawled across the horizon, larger than anything Ferris had ever seen.

The portal had opened to a stone gate at the edge of the Wild. It was one of a long line of circular gates built to receive portals. Ferris’ group had to move quickly to vacate the space for other teams.

Many groups were exiting from portals all around. Bluereef Bastion only had two gates, one used to enter rifts and one to exit. If a gate were in use, the portal from the rift would not open. The sheer number of gates Scalefort required really showed how big it was.

A steady line of delvers walked between the city and the rift gates, and Ferris’ team joined them. The city had tall walls, while the area surrounding was stripped bare. Many groups of people were running or training in the open space outside the walls, although everyone kept a healthy distance away from the barrier to the Wild.

“Now, before we enter the city, we should have a talk.” Winton walked beside Ferris, once again using his water magic to clean the trail’s dirt off them. Magic must make laundry a breeze.

“I can’t believe how big it is.” Ferris said, smoothly keeping pace with the aid of his crutch. They had a good view of the city from here.

“Big, and different, my boy. Bluereef Bastion only has one clan. We’re all related to each other, through blood or marriage. The clan supports everyone.” Winton waved his hand at Valda and the brothers, who were walking in front of them.

Ferris looked at the three delvers. They had risked their lives traveling the rift understrength for him. The large clawling could have seriously hurt or killed Valda, which would not have happened with a full team. She never complained, she had done it for the clan. For family.

“Scalefort Bastion is home to countless clans, societies, guilds, and even a couple cults.” Winton continued. “People here draw a line between their faction and everyone else. The larger groups have internal feuds that can be as fierce as enemies.”

“I’m not entirely naive, uncle.” Ferris protested with a smile. He had heard all of this before, although never so bluntly. The only people he had ever met not part of his clan were honored guests, but internal politics were present everywhere, even in Bluereef.

“Pay special attention to the rule of law,” his uncle continued, ignoring Ferris’ response. “Back home we mostly get to govern ourselves, but with so many different groups here it is more complicated. There is a governor and city guard, backed by the entire might of the Empire,” Winton said. “They come down hard on any infractions, especially if it risks destabilizing the bastion.”

“I’m no criminal!” Ferris exclaimed, no longer smiling.

“I’m not saying you are,” Uncle Winton replied, “but it’s more nuanced than that here. Most of the guard will be fair… until they decide the greater good is at stake. The people in charge are often forced to decide between justice and the safety of the bastion, and they choose the bastion every time. Most people have a local group to protect them. Keep your wits about you. They will take advantage of you if you let them, right or wrong be damned. You have a good head, just remember to use it. Don’t forget that some are willing to bend the rules further than you.” The man shrugged. “At the same time, they need each other to keep the Wild back, so there is some cooperation. I also don’t plan to leave you completely alone, so you will have your own support.”

Ferris wasn’t sure what to say to that. They’d had long conversations about how to act in the city, but this seemed beyond manners, contracts, and trade. Before he knew it, they had reached a line of people waiting at the edge of the city.

Entering the city required either paying a fee or proof of residency. There was a perfunctory check done by the guards, but the team had no problem paying the fee and passing through. Ferris’ crutch raised a few eyebrows, although no one commented on it.

Valda, Dan, and Dean left together once they reached the inner city. They would go to the trading district to sell off much of the resources gathered by the entire Bluereef clan over the last two weeks. Soul storage made carrying trade goods convenient—at least if their storage was large enough. Then, they would purchase anything the clan couldn’t produce on its own.

“Now, we are headed to the Hollowfield clan.” Uncle Winton led him down a different path. “I’ve traded with them for a number of years, and Bluereef has a good relationship with them.”

“Why are we visiting a clan?” Ferris asked. “Wouldn’t joining a guild be more usual?”

“Most guilds have recruitment tests. They offer generous terms for new recruits, but only want the strongest.” Winton shook his head. “Clan Hollowfield is large enough that they hire retainers. They will never treat you like one of their own, but they’ll give you the opportunity you need.”

“I’m not afraid of work. How big are they?” Ferris asked. He’d heard Scalefort was large, but he didn’t truly understand until seeing it for himself. Bluereef Bastion had several thousand people all told, but this place must have at least a hundred times that.

“Hmm, they are one of the stronger groups in Scalefort. Probably a couple thousand active delvers, and tens of thousands of clan members behind them,” Winton replied. “They have a large walled off compound on the west side of the city. Internally they are divided into seven branches, each managing one of seven districts dividing their compound.”

“Which one are we going to?” Ferris asked.

“The fourth.” Winton said. “I was able to meet with an Elder with the authority to recruit you. We are lucky, many places rejected me outright.”

Ferris could hear the emphasis his uncle placed on the title ‘Elder.’ It was an important role in any clan, and luck alone sounded too good to be true. What does this Elder expect to get, when everyone else wouldn’t even talk to us? He wondered. Few of his ideas were good.

“Here we are.” Uncle Winton broke him out of his reverie, stopping in front of a large pair of double doors. The massive doors were open, with many people entering and exiting the compound. ‘Hollowfield’ was written in large letters on the wall.

They approached the gate and Winton stated their business to one of the guards, requesting to meet an Elder Harper. The guard nodded and sent a messenger. It took a while, but there was a pleasant bench to sit on while they waited. Eventually, a man appeared to guide them.

“Greetings, I am Hemon of Hollowfield.” The guide—unlike the guards—was mundane: he had not unlocked his soul to become a cultivator. It took meditation, perseverance, and talent to take that first step. Ferris may only be on the first tier, but he had crossed the important first barrier. Some took decades, while most never achieved it. Even in a large clan like Hollowfield, only about one in ten were cultivators.

Ferris was considered talented to have unlocked his soul at seventeen, although not exceptional. Valda had done the same at fifteen, and she was nothing compared to the young geniuses of the stronger clans.

Ironically, Ferris had only become cultivator after losing his leg. The standard advice was to combine intense physical training, breathing exercises, and meditation to achieve ‘mental harmony.’ Ferris… well he hadn’t been standard.

“I will show you the way. Please follow me.” Hemon led them down a well-manicured path. Beyond the heavy doors was a city within the city. The entire compound was surrounded by its own walls, with more guards patrolling them.

“What can you tell us about the clan?” Ferris was curious what he was getting into.

“Hollowfield was one of the founding clans of Scalefort.” Hemon said, walking a little straighter. “Adeline Hollowfield herself helped conquer the bastion and settled it after. She had seven children, each of which established one of the seven branches.”

“What about the fourth branch?” Ferris asked. Winton had only mentioned that Elder Harper was a member of the fourth branch, but no specifics about it.

“Our branch was founded by Godun Hollowfield. While some of his siblings were more martial, he valued knowledge above the others. He dedicated himself to his research. The clan’s library is maintained by us, and we lead the research department.” The man nodded to himself. “Everyone knows how critical the fourth branch is to the clan. Without us, Hollowfield would never have risen so far.”

Ferris was careful not to roll his eyes. Yeah, it sounds important, he thought, but not as much as going out and fighting monsters. Someone must protect and feed them, right? Which branch does that? Hemon sounded more than a little biased. Of course, he admonished himself, knowledge is the entire reason I’m here, asking for help.

Deciding to say nothing, Ferris smiled and nodded to the man’s words. Hemon appeared pleased with the response—or maybe just having enjoyed bragging—and took them to an administrative building nearby.

They were led to a small waiting room. It was tastefully decorated with a table and several chairs. Hemon prepared tea off to the side, serving it to the two of them but abstaining himself.

The tea was still too hot to enjoy before an older woman arrived, entering with brisk confidence. No hesitation.

“Elder Harper, it is good to see you again!” Winton stood. Hemon had been standing from before, while Ferris slowly followed, propping himself up with his crutch.

“Adept Winton Bluereef, I see you have returned.” Her gaze fell on Ferris, taking in his face, missing leg, and body in a sweeping glance. “And this must be the one you told me about.” She sat at the table. Hemon poured her a cup of tea while Winton and Ferris took their seats.

“Elder, may I introduce my nephew, Adept Ferris Bluereef.” Winton gestured to Ferris. He used the title of ‘Adept’ to signify a person had unlocked their soul and become a cultivator. While Elder Harper was also technically an ‘Adept’, her more formal title of ‘Elder’ took precedence. It was reserved for those that held a position of power within their clan. Titles like ‘Adept’ were typically only used for introductions or to be formal, but skipping a title like ‘Elder’ would be disrespectful for any except close friends and family.

Ferris had heard rumors that every Elder in the large clans of Scalefort city were all at least tier three. There was no way he could tell by looking at her, but the prospect was a bit scary. Tier two delvers like his uncle were like mountains towering over his small hill. To think he was in the presence of someone that eclipsed his uncle by the same amount…

“Tell me, young man.” Elder Harper said, as the woman turned her piercing gaze to Ferris. “How did you lose your leg?”

Ferris straightened his back, taking a deep breath. Focus on the words, not her, he told himself, this is why you practiced. This conversation would determine much of his future, after all.

“It was a monster surge,” he said. “We were training near the edge of the Wild when a pack of grifwolves broke through the bastion’s barrier.” He looked her in the eyes, as his uncle had taught him. “They tore through our training group. I was lucky to survive long enough for help to arrive, but lost my leg.”

“I see,” she said, eyes sharp. “And what do you expect from Hollowfield?”

“Opportunity. The knowledge to regrow my leg, and the opportunity to achieve it,” Ferris replied.

“Knowledge we have,” she said, “but know that the resources required will not just be handed to you. You will have to work for them, and then work to put them to use.”

Ferris nodded. “All I ask for is a chance.”

She watched him for a moment before speaking. “Our offer is as follows. We will take you in as a retainer of Hollowfield. In addition to the privileges of that position, we will provide you with access to basic information on bonds with a known tier one ability to regrow limbs, or other means to solve your problem. Once you choose a bond, we will provide any information the clan has on how to acquire the ability.”

Ferris couldn’t help edging forward in his chair as he listened. At a high level, Uncle Winton had already negotiated terms Ferris was willing to accept—or else he never would have risked the journey here. Yet, the specific details had necessitated waiting for him to be here in person. Nothing was final until they signed a contract, so it was still possible they wouldn’t be able to make a deal. Ferris would have to try other groups at Scalefort, or eventually risk another trip back home, but that was his choice.

He was willing to give a lot, but he had a bottom line. Uncle Winton had made sure Ferris knew his own worth, after all.

“Will I learn what resources the ability requires before choosing a bond?” Ferris asked. “I wouldn’t want to pick a path that I could never afford.”

“Yes,” Elder Harper nodded. “Typically that information is restricted, but I can authorize it in your case. We will provide a brief summary of known requirements for each potential ability.”

Ferris was excited, although he tried—and likely failed—not to show it. It was generous; much could be derived from the required resources alone.

“We will grant access to our smiths to forge the bond item,” she continued, “but please be aware that you will have to pay for the item itself.”

“Clan Bluereef will pay for it, as long as we view the price as fair and within our budget.” Winton interjected.

Elder Harper nodded to him, then continued. “As a retainer, you will also gain access to our clan merit store. Beneficial actions are rewarded with merits, which may be exchanged for weapons, pills, manuals, and more. Finally, food and shelter are provided while you reside at the clan headquarters.”

The food and shelter were mostly an afterthought. The weakest cultivator would still be valuable enough that basic accommodation wouldn’t be a problem.

Ferris was hopeful about the clan store. A large clan like this would have many exotic abilities and items. The best techniques would be restricted to bloodline members of the clan, but it was still a great opportunity. This was likely the reason others took a similar retainer contract. Beyond healing his leg, there was the potential to acquire the strength to delve the underwater rifts back home. Yet, knowledge always came with a cost…

“What are the restrictions after I leave?” Ferris asked. Eventually, he would return home. He didn’t want to have anything binding him for the rest of his life.

“That depends,” she said, “if you decide to apply to join one of the clan’s martial traditions, and they accept, there will be strict limitations on what you can share. Often, this includes further long-term commitments to the clan.”

Ferris frowned; he didn’t like the sound of that. He wanted to trade his service for his leg, not abandon his home forever.

“There is another option,” Elder Harper said, obviously seeing his expression, “forge your own path. Everything that is offered to retainers is understood to be publicly available elsewhere—unless explicitly stated. While you are under contract, it is forbidden to sell or teach outside the clan without permission. Afterwards, you are free to do as you will, although intentionally selling secrets to our enemies is… discouraged.”

As Ferris considered her words, they felt more in line with what Uncle Winton had told him. They would never give him the true clan secrets, and they didn’t want him to simply take everything and resell it, but they would open their less sensitive library to him. A large clan like Hollowfield had likely forgotten more knowledge than Clan Bluereef even had access to. He could take as much as possible for himself, then refine it into his own martial tradition that helped his family. Also, their definition of ‘publicly available’ likely meant that their competitors already had it, not that Ferris could walk up and buy it on the street. If it were that easy, he wouldn’t be here.

She claimed he was free after the contract, but the warning not to sell secrets carried weight. He would probably be fine teaching a few things to students back home, off in another bastion. Yet, if he went around offering wholesale copies of their library to every group in Scalefort, they would come for him. Claiming he hadn’t broken the letter of any rule wouldn’t save him from their wrath.

Still, Ferris wasn’t concerned about that part. He knew they were powerful and had no intention of working against them. He wanted to use what they had to help himself and eventually his clan, which shouldn’t conflict with Hollowfield’s interests. That only left confirming what they offered could actually help him, rather than leaving him stuck with no way forward.

“How would that work, if there is no martial tradition to follow?” Ferris asked. Back home all new cultivators followed the clan’s tradition. It provided the path to advancement.

“Hollowfield has many techniques that can be used by anyone,” Elder Harper explained. “I am confident that we can help you advance, no matter the bonded type or form. Of course, this doesn’t replace the need for a mentor for specific advice, but the library has a series of manuals and techniques that can substitute for a martial tradition. You will have to discover many things on your own, although that is not a bad thing.”

Notably, Elder Harper wasn’t offering Ferris a direct mentor; just stating that he needed one. That didn’t mean he couldn’t seek one out… but at this point Ferris didn’t want that. The relationship between a formal mentor and a student was a lifelong commitment. Ferris already had one: his uncle. While the man wouldn’t know Hollowfield’s techniques and methods, he was still a tier two cultivator with decades of delving experience. Most important of all, Ferris trusted him.

“In return,” Elder Harper said, “we require a decade of service. You will be assigned a job based on the nature of your bond and your abilities. This could involve menial work, guard duty, delving, and any other service the clan requires. It is not guaranteed to be safe. It is guaranteed to be boring, at least at times. Jobs are compensated with merit points.”

“Will I have time to cultivate? May I delve separate from the job?” Ferris asked. Even if he had a clear path to regrow his leg, it wouldn’t be easy or quick. He would need long hours to cultivate the method. Plus, he thought, once I get my leg back, I don’t want to sit around on guard duty all the time. I want to get out there and delve!

“The situation changes based on the task assigned to you,” she answered. “Standard jobs occupy no more than three out of every seven days. Any requiring more time provide extra compensation with a minimum standard rate.”

Ferris could see a lot of ways that could be abused. The clan would likely take everything it could from the deal.

“Delving on your own time is encouraged—but restricted to groups within the clan. Anything brought back by clan groups can only be sold to the clan store, at least if it isn’t claimed for personal use. If you choose to pursue a crafting profession, then you are also required to sell within the clan, or through a clan vendor to outsiders.”

Ferris frowned at this requirement. Only delving with their groups wasn’t a big deal, but the clan store would definitely give lower prices for everything. It would likely only pay merit points as well, forcing him to purchase from the clan. Even though he would get four days to himself, they would still profit off his time.

“Additionally, you will be required to defend the clan,” she said. “If the clan goes to war, you will be required to fight on our behalf.”

He doubted this would happen, but there was always a risk. A retainer wasn’t considered family, but they were taken in as part of the clan. He would be expected to contribute and belong, but in return the clan would aid and support his growth. It also made him think back to Uncle Winton’s comments on the ‘fairness’ of the local guard. If Ferris wanted to avoid becoming a sacrifice for the greater good, he needed backing. Hollowfield likely wouldn’t help him as much as a family member, but they also couldn’t be seen to neglect their retainers.

“Furthermore, your unique circumstances present an opportunity. You will be required to work with me on a research project. This will involve investigating, testing, and reporting on your cultivation. It is in addition to the standard retainer work requirements.”

Ferris’ eyes narrowed. This was more concerning. A cultivator’s path was very personal, and this could mean revealing way more than he was comfortable with. Sharing personal cultivation details was dangerous; at least he had been told.

“What would be the nature of the research?” he asked.

“That will largely depend on your choice of bonds, although I expect it to be related to first tier healing abilities,” she replied. “We will begin by reviewing the clan records together, then I will make a list of potential topics for you to choose from.”

Ferris tried not to grimace. He’d thought things sounded too good to be true, but at least now he saw the real price he would be paying.

He had come to Clan Hollowfield for information on a healing ability. If he was a research project, then did the clan have the knowledge he needed, or would they be using him to acquire it themselves?

He studied Elder Harper. This was most likely the entire reason she was offering him the deal. Research branch indeed. No wonder she has been accommodating so far. If I refuse this, I bet the entire offer disappears.

“What if we cannot come to an agreement on the project?” Ferris asked. He wasn’t going to give her free reign to present a list of unreasonable ‘options.’ At the same time, he doubted they would let him learn what he needed while rejecting all the choices.

“In such a case, you would instead join one of my active projects as an assistant.”

Ferris felt conflicted. He didn’t know the first thing about research, so the only way he could help would be menial. Of course, everyone had to start somewhere, but the work would be yet another distraction from his goals.

On the other hand, he didn’t love the idea of picking one of Elder Harper’s projects. If the goal was to regrow his leg, he wouldn’t mind the extra work…

Ferris just didn’t like the idea that he would be the focus.

Beyond the problem of additional scrutiny, there was a clear conflict of interest. What was best for him might not align with the Elder’s goals.

“The final choices will be mine?” Ferris asked. “In either case, after I’ve agreed to one of your proposed projects or instead while working as an assistant, my personal cultivation decisions will be respected? You will only document and provide advice, while I only need to share information relevant to the project?” This was the line he was not willing to cross.

“Yes,” she said, unfazed, “the choices will be yours, but you will be expected to actively support the research. You may pick your own path, but ‘choosing’ to not put in effort is unacceptable.”

Ferris nodded, at least they didn’t expect him to bare himself completely.

“I would like my participation and obligations to any project restricted to the first tier,” he said. Ferris had noticed she left the scope open-ended, while her word choice implied a tier limit.

“This is acceptable.” Elder Harper gave a small smile, but didn’t appear surprised or offended by his negotiation.

Ferris sighed, but nodded. It wasn’t ideal, but at least the project might help someone in his situation in the future. Without Hollowfield’s knowledge, Ferris would be guessing in the dark. Even if they used him to learn, they would at least have a starting point for him to follow. In a way, this was lucky. If Hollowfield already had all the answers, they wouldn’t need him.

“Lastly, we require a binding oath agreeing to the contract and pledging loyalty to the clan,” she said. “Do you know how bound oaths work?”

“I understand the theory, but have never made one,” he said, not surprised. Uncle Winton had warned him of this.

“The oath will be written out, describing the agreement and a pledge to not betray the Hollowfield clan for the next ten years or until released. It will be bound to your cultivation.”

Bound oaths were oddly personal things. Uncle Winton had expected and discussed it with Ferris before they left Bluereef. The oath would have real consequences, but only if Ferris believed himself to have broken it. If Ferris thought he had honored the oath, nothing would happen. It was a powerful restriction in that he could not knowingly break his word, but it was also powerless against self-delusion. All it took was for Ferris to truly believe to have been deceived or mistreated, and the oath would lose power.

Strangely, if Ferris unknowingly broke the letter of the contract, nothing would happen. Only when it was proven to him would he suffer the consequences, and they wouldn’t be as bad as intentional violations. Of course, the Elder may dole out her own retribution.

The intent was a double-edged blade. He was protected from breaking the contract accidentally or binding himself in ways he did not intend, but it gave no wiggle room for technical loopholes. If he believed he had violated the spirit of the agreement he would still suffer magical backlash. At the end of the day, the oath followed his own conscience.

The time limit on the oath was an important point. Swearing an oath to anything forever would dramatically shape one’s cultivation, eventually twisting and corrupting over time if the cultivator resented it. It would be improper to ask for an eternal oath for something like this, or for anything really.

“These terms are agreeable to me.” Ferris committed himself.

Elder Harper nodded as if there was never any question. “Then, we will commence with the oath immediately.”

Hemon, who had quietly watched the entire exchange, presented the Elder with three sheets of paper. She briefly looked at them, her eyes flashed, and then some strange skill produced writing on the sheets. She placed all three sheets in front of Ferris.

Ferris and his uncle carefully read them. They were identical copies that outlined the agreement they had discussed, with Elder Harper’s signature already at the bottom. The wording was very specific, and certain points were described with more detail, but Ferris knew the intent mattered more than the phrasing. If he believed he had been tricked, then the oath would not hold. Elder Harper knew this as well and had kept the wording plain. It wasn’t easy to manipulate yourself into truly believing something obviously false.

Still, ‘not easy’ was not ‘impossible.’

Thinking of his uncle’s earlier warnings, Ferris wondered if the clan had other ways to enforce the contract: through spying, coercion, and threats. They showed a lot of faith in a method with known limitations; would they really not have a backup plan? Clan Hollowfield had survived a long time… it was best to assume they weren’t easily cheated.

Earlier, Elder Harper had said he was ‘forbidden’ from selling outside the clan while under contract, but given a threat to enforce his compliance afterwards. The contract wouldn’t hold him after it ended, but that wouldn’t stop the clan from sending people to make sure they got their way.

“If everything is in order, then please sign them, place your hand on the center sheet, and then repeat after me,” she instructed.

Ferris looked to his uncle, who nodded. Hemon presented a quill and inkwell, which Ferris used to sign all three copies.

When he placed his hand on the middle sheet, Elder Harper placed her hand over his. The physical contact was unexpected, and Ferris almost pulled back.

“I swear to be faithful to this agreement, to hold it true in my soul for the next ten years, or until I am released from this oath by an Elder of Clan Hollowfield,” she stated.

Ferris echoed the words, aloud and in his heart.

Once he had finished the statement, her hand flashed, and the paper burned up beneath. It felt eerily like he had sold his soul.

Ferris could immediately feel something had changed. The contract truly did exist on a deeper level than words. Hemon took one copy of the contract and left the other. Ferris handed it to Winton, who would store it with the clan. The only copy that truly mattered was the one that had burned with the oath, but bureaucracy demands its paperwork.

Elder Harper’s smile was cordial, but he couldn’t help feeling like prey before a predator.

“Adept Ferris Bluereef,” the woman said, “allow me to welcome you to Hollowfield.”
