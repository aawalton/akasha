Nasha stepped out of the hidden staircase and back into Viper’s Nail. The clerk from before was already waiting for her.

The door shut behind her as she took a few steps forward. The cold underground air vanished in an instant, replaced by the smell of perfume, nail polish, and the floral scents common to a nail parlor. Ahead, soft conversations drifted through the building as women chatted while having their nails done.

The clerk nodded and gestured for her to follow. Together, they made their way back toward the main lobby.

As Nasha stepped out of the back hallway, she noticed that the clientele had completely changed. The women who had been there when she arrived were already gone, replaced by an entirely new group. New faces. None that would recognize her.

“I hope you enjoyed our premium services,” the clerk said politely. His demeanor had shifted completely, the silent hand of Vermillion replaced by the courteous professionalism of a business attendant. “We'd appreciate it if you recommended us to your friends and family.”

Nasha returned a practiced smile. “Of course. The service was exceptional. I’ll be sure to let everyone know.”

The clerk gave a small bow.

Nasha returned it before turning toward the exit.

None of the women in the lobby spared her a second glance. Some sat with beauty masks covering their faces. Others were absorbed in conversation with friends while attendants worked on their hands.

Nasha left like a ghost.

The air outside was warmer. Roughly half an hour had passed, and the sun was already beginning its descent.

For a moment, Nasha stood beneath Viper’s Nail's hanging sign, listening to the city around her. Wagons rumbled along the road. Families wandered past, children weaving through the crowds as they laughed and played. A group of adventurers stumbled by in battered armor, drinks in hand and faces flushed from alcohol.

How... nice.

Nasha stepped onto the stone sidewalk and let the flow of pedestrians carry her forward.

Charlotte's voice returned to her.

“This mission came from above,” Charlotte had said, sitting across from her in that sealed room with a coffee cup in hand. “Apparently, Varce is quite pissed at the fuck-up that happened in Ronswick. Because of it, he had to change his plans.”

“Varce?”

The smile on Charlotte’s face disappeared, not like it had been real in the first place.

“Whoever this Veronica person is that you’re traveling with, she’s essentially made an enemy out of Vermillion by sticking her nose where it doesn’t belong.”

“So?” Nasha said plainly. “What is it that you want?”

“Not what I want,” Charlotte said, pointing at herself. “It’s what he wants,” she continued, pointing at Nasha now.

A few seconds of silence passed. Charlotte leaned back and shrugged. “Sorry. Don’t know why I pointed at you. Just felt like I needed to point at something to get my point across.”

Nasha pursed her lips. “Stop with the jokes, Charlotte. I’ve already told you what you need to know. She’s just a normal mage traveling around. She happened to be fond of one of High Crate’s merchants.”

“I don’t care,” Charlotte said, her eyes drooping dangerously low. “And Varce doesn’t either. The fact that she disrupted operations for his latest business plan makes her an obstacle that needs to be removed.”

“You should blame the Scavengers Transport Group for all of this. They were sloppy enough that even a Tier-3 mage managed to tear down their entire business.”

“Maybe. And I think Varce knows that too. However, the problem still stems from this mage you’re traveling with,” Charlotte said, sitting back and crossing one leg over the other. “The mission has already been given. Since you were the closest member with the most information, this task is being assigned to you.”

“I already told you, I’m not working for Vermillion anymore.”

“Vania.” Charlotte looked at her with sharp eyes. “Once Vermillion, always Vermillion. You can’t escape your past. I’ve told you this countless times. You can enjoy your singing. But you can’t escape your roots.”

Nasha glared back at Charlotte, her own stare just as fierce. Yet there was a slight hesitation in her eyes. Almost like she was afraid Charlotte was right.

Afraid she truly couldn’t escape.

“I’m not jealous that you found yourself a new woman to sleep with,” Charlotte said lazily.

The cushion beside Nasha suddenly tore, a puff of stuffing spilling out and falling near her leg. Nasha glanced to the side for just a moment and saw that a dagger had stabbed deep into the seat beside her.

Her gaze flicked back to Charlotte.

Charlotte stared back. “I’m just here to state your assignment. So don’t glare those beautiful eyes at me, Vania. Your mission is simple: get rid of that woman,” Charlotte said, leaning forward, eyes unblinking.

The killing intent she released made Nasha’s mouth go dry, but she refused to let it show.

“Kill Veronica Everwells,” Charlotte continued. “And maybe there’s a way to bring your grandmother back.”

A carriage passed in front of Nasha, its wheels rattling against the stone road.

She stopped at the edge of the street and waited.

Her reflection flickered for a moment in the polished black side of the carriage. A red-haired woman standing beside a crowd of innocent citizens, all talking without a care in the world.

I’ll be watching you.

The carriage passed by, and Nasha continued moving. Soon, she detached from the group of others, taking a lone alleyway shortcut back to their inn.

I’ll undo the seal on you for now. So enjoy the power while you can.

Charlotte’s last message echoed in her mind.

Consider it a gesture of trust. Complete the mission, and we’ll permanently remove the seal.

Nasha’s eyes didn’t blink as she kept going. The darkness of the alley shadowed her, yet her red hair shone like scarlet as she approached the light at the other end.

…and I’ll tell you more about your grandmother’s condition.

The next morning.

“Tell me more about this library,” Veronica said.

The three of them, Veronica, Gwen, and Nasha, were walking toward the library Gwen had visited the day before. It was still early, a few hours before noon. After finding a public training facility on a city map, Veronica had completed her daily jog, returned to the inn, and bathed. By then, both Nasha and Gwen were awake, so the three of them had eaten breakfast together.

The food at the Maristar Gold Inn wasn’t bad. But considering it was an inn reserved for those of status, Veronica couldn’t help feeling that it lacked something.

Now, at her request, the three of them were making their way toward the library.

“It’s called the Starfault Archives,” Gwen answered. She wore a white tunic and long brown shorts, with a satchel slung across one shoulder. She looked like any ordinary commoner.

“It’s actually a really large place. Most of the first floor is open to the public, but the upper floors aren’t.”

“What do you mean?” Veronica asked.

She had also changed into more casual clothing: a long sweater cut open at the shoulders, worn over a fitted black underpiece that flowed into a narrow skirt and long black stockings. Naturally, Nasha had chosen the outfit for her.

“Apparently, the archives operate on some kind of honor system. Unless you contribute to them somehow, you can’t access the deeper materials on the second, third, or fourth floors. But if you’re a noble, or someone with high status like the Twistwell Commerce Group or Lilybed, you can ignore all of that and go up to the higher floors.”

“Veronica has the viscount’s signet,” Nasha said. “Would that be enough?”

Gwen slowly shook her head, lips pursed. “I think I saw someone backed by a baron try to enter yesterday, but he was denied. A viscount outranks a baron, so we might still be able to get in. But I have a feeling you either need to be the noble yourself, or have them there in person before they’ll let you access the upper floors.”

Veronica hummed. “Then we’ll have to see how much weight Viscount Leopold’s name carries here. Otherwise, we’ll make do with the first floor.”

“What are you looking for?” Nasha asked.

“Yeah,” Gwen added. “The spellbooks are for me, right? Doesn’t Sage already have, like, everything recorded inside her?”

Veronica glanced back at Gwen with one brow raised. “Her?”

Gwen tilted her head. “Is… Sage not a she?”

“Sage is not exactly a person,” Veronica said. “Sage doesn’t have a—”

[I am female.]

Veronica stared ahead, the rest of her sentence dying in the air.

It was Gwen’s turn to raise a brow. “What is it? Did Sage say something?”

Veronica sighed. “Yes, but it isn’t important.”

“Sounds strangely important,” Nasha said flatly.

“Sage has a feminine voice, yes. But that doesn’t mean anything. It’s still a piece of magitech. More machine than flesh.”

[I am also female.]

Veronica’s eye twitched.

Gwen pressed her lips together, clearly trying not to laugh. She couldn’t hear whatever Sage was saying, but she had a few guesses.

“Excuse me,” a man called from behind them. “Could you ladies move along a little quicker?”

The three of them turned.

A large carriage waited several paces away. Its polished darkwood frame gleamed beneath the morning light. Thin brass lines ran along the wheels, glowing faintly with stored mana, while a uniformed servant sat at the front and managed the controls.

Two guards walked beside the carriage on opposite sides. One was a broad man with a sheathed sword at his hip and a sharp, disciplined gaze. The other was a woman dressed in a fitted black uniform, though she carried no visible weapon. Perhaps she relied more on her fists or magic.

Inside the carriage, peering through an open window, sat a young man. Or perhaps nobleman would have been the more fitting description. He wore a dark green coat embroidered with silver thread. His black hair appeared unkempt at first glance, though it was clearly styled that way rather than neglected. His eyes remained fixed on them, but his expression held more impatience than anger.

There was also a bead of sweat running down his forehead, which Veronica did not fail to notice.

The three of them weren’t blocking the road, but they had slowed while talking as they crossed the street.

Still, Gwen frowned. “We’re moving.”

“Come on. Let’s hurry,” Veronica said.

The three of them quickened their pace and crossed the rest of the street. The sound of the carriage wheels passed behind them before soon fading into the distance.

Once they were clear, Gwen crossed her arms. “What’s wrong with rotten nobles? He could have waited three seconds for us to pass. Calling us out like that only made it take longer.”

“It’s just how nobles are,” Veronica said, glancing back in the direction of the carriage. “Sometimes they need to show off their authority to feel important.”

“He did seem to be in a hurry, though,” Nasha added. “He looked nervous, too.”

“Perhaps,” Veronica said. “Could have been in a rush and wasn’t thinking straight.”

Gwen kept her frown. “Still rude.”

“Alright, let’s forget about him and keep moving. We’re almost there.”

“This… is the Starfault Archives?” Nasha asked, her eyes traveling up the enormous building before them.

The Starfault Archives was larger than any single building in Ronswick. Its entrance stood beneath two grand, carved spires rising from a stark white foundation. Several trimmed hedges lined the outer walls, each shaped like a different mythical beast or creature. There was a small gryphon, a two-headed serpent, and even one sculpted in the likeness of Yggdrasil.

Even if they ran, it would take several minutes to reach the other end of the building.

“Yeah, I asked the same thing when I first saw it,” Gwen said, looking up at the building. “I thought it might have been the duke’s mansion or something until I saw people walking in and out of the entrance.”

“Commoners, you mean,” Nasha added.

Gwen nodded. “Commoners, yeah. But there are also a lot of nobles and wealthy people who come here. Most of them looked like alchemists or members of the nobility.”

“I’ve seen bigger,” Veronica said, unimpressed.

Both Nasha and Gwen looked at her.

“What?”

Gwen leaned toward Nasha and whispered in her ear. “Imagine if you two weren’t dating.”

Nasha whispered back, “Yeah. Poor guy.”

A light scowl formed on Veronica’s face. “I can hear you two, you know.” She pointed at her ears.

“Ah, right. Elf ears,” Gwen said.

Veronica’s eyes flattened. “Nooo,” she said, stretching out the word. “Sage. Elf ears aren’t any better than anyone else’s.”

She stepped forward. “Now stop with the crude jokes and let’s go inside.”

Gwen and Nasha exchanged a brief glance before following after her.
