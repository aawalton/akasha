Kavard and Estophon left the palace first, in the closed carriage with a couple of guards, while Aric’s expedition followed an hour later. Aric did not come to see Lira before his departure, which she told herself was a good thing, though Deren did show up at the library while the mules were still being loaded to say goodbye.

“Sorry for not visiting much these past few days,” he said.

“I understand,” Lira said with a nod. “Aric was giving me space to think through his proposal.”

“Honestly, I think he is being ridiculous,” Deren sighed. “If you were a spy, you wouldn’t have been this helpful.”

Lira only smiled at that. “Are you excited about the expedition?” she asked, changing the subject.

“Of course, though I doubt we will find Almara. I am hoping to slay a few trolls, since they are known to roam the Holtarry Mountains.”

“Be safe,” Lira said, and she meant it. She realised she did not want either Aric or Deren to get hurt.

“Don’t worry about me,” the knight said with a smile. “I have slain trolls before, and with Salvek’s enchanted sword and the techniques from the manual you gave me, it should be child’s play.”

“Just be careful, please.”

Deren’s expression turned serious at the pleading look on her face, and he nodded. “Keep an eye on Luxia while I am gone.”

“I will make sure to check on her.”

“Goodbye.”

“Travel safe.”

As she watched him go, her eyes flickered to his scabbard, where a thin sheet of paper was glued to the inside, impossible to see. She hoped it would survive the next time he oiled his sword.

She spent most of the day spying on both parties, though she never kept a link open for more than a minute at a time. She would cast the Remote Eye spell on one of the sheets to check the surroundings, then activate the funnel rune only if she saw people speaking, collapsing the link before the thread lingered long enough to be spotted. Aric’s expedition moved at a brisk pace in a tight military formation and never said a word, while Kavard sat in the carriage looking furious and refused to answer anything Estophon said to him.

“Don’t pout. A king never pouts.”

Or, a while later…

“Do you know why Aric decided to go on his silly expedition only after you left? Nothing to say? Hm?”

Between those checks, she kept to her regular duties, helping Lyth with his Magari verbs and suggesting novels and books on history and moral philosophy to the noble youths who visited with him.

Henry was watching her more closely than ever, so every time she wanted to spy on the expeditions, she slipped behind a shelf with one of her mana-obfuscation bookmarks to hide her casting. She also kept an eye on Estophon and Aric through the magical view, in case either of them noticed the thread of disturbance. Neither of them seemed to do, which was a relief.

She stayed in the library that night, spying on both princes once every thirty minutes. Aric’s expedition had made camp beside a farmer’s field, with the mules and horses tied to a tree and the knights taking turns on watch duty, while Estophon and Kavard had stopped at a roadside inn; the two of them were sharing a room on the upper floor while guards were sleeping in the common room. By midnight, Lira thought it was time for her to sleep as well, since nothing had happened for a couple of hours and she had assumed the day was over.

Then she noticed Kavard waking up through the Remote Eye.

He glanced at Estophon’s sleeping form, then reached for a pendant hidden inside his clothes and opened it. Lira activated the funnel and horn runes, but she heard absolutely nothing, not even the sound of his feet as he stood up from the bed. She watched him cross the room and open and close the door without a whisper of noise.

Was her funnel damaged somehow? No, more likely the pendant had a sound-dampening enchantment, which would explain why a man who had never been quiet a day in his life was suddenly moving like a ghost.

He went down the stairs and through the common room, where one of his guards gave him a nod before closing his eyes to pretend to sleep.

Lira frowned at that. She guessed that was what happened when they kept the same men with a prince. Sooner or later, they started obeying him, whether through bribery or coercion, she didn’t know.

Kavard left the inn and walked to the edge of the village, with Lira following him through her magical brushstroke view. It did not rely on light, so the darkness did not trouble her at all. The prince met up with a man, and to Lira’s surprise she recognised him, after shifting her magical view to the man’s face.

Lord James Evergreen, standing next to a large black stallion that was already saddled, and a smaller mare beside him.

Kavard closed the pendant, and sound returned to the funnel.

“Evergreen,” the prince said. “You have my gratitude. When the matter of the territory dispute with the Musgroves comes before the throne, I will speak on your behalf, I promise you.”

“Your Highness is too kind,” Evergreen said with a smile. "I am only doing my duty to my prince."

“So, is this the horse?”

“He is one of the best I have ever bred.” Evergreen patted the stallion’s neck with the pride of a man talking about his own child. “There is nothing in a village this size that could keep up with him, and the captain is a big man in heavy armour, so whatever poor horse he manages to get will be half as fast.”

“Good.” Kavard swung into the saddle. “You never saw me today, Evergreen.”

“I never left my estate, my prince,” the lord replied.

They nodded to each other and parted in opposite directions, the prince heading north back toward Halthar and the lord heading south on his own steed.

Lira sat back in her chair, thinking that this had been Kavard’s plan from the start. At the very least, it would give him a few hours’ lead on Estophon, and that was all he needed.

She kept watching as the prince rode back toward the capital. She was slightly impressed by how well prepared he was, with the pendant to keep quiet and avoid waking Estophon, the guard on watch cooperating with him, and a lord with an expensive stallion in his side. Kavard really could plan things when he wanted to, and sadly, what he wanted was to kill her. Her provocation or not, he had clearly planned his escape long before their encounter, which meant this had always been the plan: to slip away from Estophon and kill Aric’s supposed mistress.

The horse was fast, and Kavard looked like a decent rider, so Lira estimated it would take at least six hours for him to reach the capital, perhaps less, since he cut straight through some of the farm fields without caring about the damage he did to the crops. Normally, she would have been wary of keeping a link open for so long, but Kavard was untrained in magic, so she did not need to worry about magical detection. His face was dark and angry, and when the funnel caught his voice, she heard exactly what she expected.

“She dares call me an idiot. I will show that stupid bitch.”

Maybe her provocation had worked far better than she had expected. Kavard was definitely coming for her, and now all she had to do was keep watching and wait for the perfect opportunity to enact her plan. The blot of ink she had added to his sheet sat ready in his pouch, waiting to bloom into a rune.
