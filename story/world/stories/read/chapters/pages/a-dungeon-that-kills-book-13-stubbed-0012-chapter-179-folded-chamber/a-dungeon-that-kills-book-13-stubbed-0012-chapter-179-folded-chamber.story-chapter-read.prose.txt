“Celeste told me you’ve located those masked men’s secret hideout,” Viktor said. “Is that correct?”

Galatea nodded. “Yes, Master.”

“Where is it?”

“It lies north of town, approximately two miles away. The lair exists inside a Folded Chamber.”

Viktor frowned. “A what?”

“It is a spatial construct,” Galatea replied. “A distortion of reality. An isolated space separated from the outside world by a boundary created by magic. Once such a chamber has been formed, its interior no longer needs to obey the physical limitations of the exterior. For example, a Folded Chamber within a small wooden shed could hold a castle, a palace, or even an entire village. Also, since the chamber is disconnected from the outside world, entering it is impossible without a designated point of access created by its maker. You cannot force your way in by trying to break through the barrier. Still, a high-level practitioner of space magic might be able to breach the boundary to gain entry.”

“The Riftwalkers of this world could do something similar,” Viktor said, leaning back in his chair. “They have a spell that allows them to create a magical vessel of which internal capacity is far greater than its appearance suggests. A traveler can store an absurd number of items within the container, while its size and weight remain unchanged. Frankly, that sort of thing is very useful for carrying stuff around.”

“That’s right. The principle involved is basically the same. But the storage container you mention is relatively small and portable, because the folded space is tethered directly to the object itself. A Folded Chamber, on the other hand, is much bigger and more structurally rigid. Once established, its boundary becomes fixed in place and cannot easily be changed without dismantling the entire spatial framework that supported it.”

“Come to think of it,” Viktor said, fingers tapping lightly against the armrest. “Isn’t the dungeon itself also a Folded Chamber?”

“Yes, Master. Every floor of a dungeon exists within its own Folded Chamber. That’s why the size of a dungeon can vastly exceed what should physically fit under the ground above it. And those floors are connected to each other or the outside world only through specific pathways created by the Dungeon Core.”

And the Abyss is an ancient dungeon, Viktor thought. So it was hardly a surprise if its underlings could be granted the ability to manipulate folded spaces.

“Tell me more about the Folded Chamber they’re using as their base of operation,” he said.

“It takes the form of a large tree standing among countless others in the forest, indistinguishable from its surroundings in every way. When one of the masked men approaches the tree and speaks a specific phrase, the hidden entrance will reveal itself. The phrase is ‘Glory to our Father,’ by the way. I heard him say it while watching. Once it is spoken, a rift manifests on the trunk of the tree, big enough for one person to pass through. After he crossed the threshold, the opening sealed itself, leaving no trace behind.”

“Have you tried to enter yourself?”

“No, Master. I was afraid opening the rift could alert them to my presence. Therefore, I immediately returned to report my findings so that you could decide how we should proceed.”

“In other words, we have absolutely no idea what is waiting inside that place, or even how big it really is.”

“Yes,” Galatea said with a nod.

Now that was problematic. Launching an attack against the enemy stronghold without any intelligence was recklessly stupid. At the same time, there was no way to gather information without first sending someone into the damn place.

Of course, he could just order Galatea to go in, but as she had already pointed out, the moment she entered the Folded Chamber, the men inside might notice the intrusion and realize their base had been discovered. Which meant the attack would need to follow immediately, before the enemy had time to fortify their defenses or simply abandon the hideout entirely and disappear without a trace. However, mounting a hasty assault right after the scouting would undermine much of the value provided by that scouting in the first place. What a dilemma.

In other words, the instant he had that rift opened, he would have committed himself to the operation. There would be no going back from that decision; the countdown toward confrontation already began.

So all planning had to happen in advance. Every possible scenario needed to be accounted for before anyone crossed that threshold. And that circled back to the very first issue: he knew absolutely nothing about what was inside the chamber, which made planning extremely difficult.

Ugh.

And then there was the attack itself.

Even though he had a steadily growing army at his disposal, those minions were bound to the dungeon. His ability to project force beyond its boundaries was very limited. In fact, at the moment, that amounted to only himself and Galatea, who could provide little more than reconnaissance. Hence, he was going to be the one who had to do most, if not all, of the fighting. Alone.

Sure, the last skirmish against the masked men had ended in his victory, but that battle had unfolded under conditions favorable to him. This time, he had to invade their territory, and that was a different beast altogether.

“Celeste,” Viktor said, glancing at the Dungeon Core who was hovering beside him. “Can you teleport me back to the dungeon when I’m inside a Folded Chamber?”

[Probably not, Master. Just as the Riftwalkers cannot teleport directly into our dungeon from the outside and vice versa, I likely cannot retrieve you from someone else’s Folded Chamber either. At least not with my current level.]

Yes, that made sense.

One could not simply teleport in or out of a Folded Chamber. That counted as breaching the boundary between the sealed space and the outside world, while such a boundary existed precisely to prevent this sort of intrusion.

Which meant, there wouldn’t be a convenient retreat option for him if things went south, and the prospect of him being trapped inside was a very real possibility.

Viktor sighed. This operation had turned out to be considerably more troublesome than he had expected.

Still, it was not like raiding this Folded Chamber was the only path available to him. After all, the masked men themselves weren’t the true objective. His ultimate goal was to kill Xander; he was merely trying to lure the bastard back to Daelin by attacking his allies here. So if an assault on the hideout proved too risky, he could target the Iskora adventurers who were occupying the west side instead.

This, however, introduced a different set of complications.

A battle at the slums would inevitably bring unforeseen collateral damage to the area, and... the Sanctuary was also there. On the other hand, fighting the masked men inside a folded space at the very least wouldn’t affect anyone uninvolved.

Well, he didn’t need to make a decision immediately. It was better to let things settle, then come back to them later with a clear mind.

“Thank you,” he told Galatea. “You can leave.”

She inclined her head, and her form unraveled into scattered light.

[What are you going to do, Master?]

“We’re going to attack that lair, that much is certain. But not right away. I need to come up with a proper plan first.”

[And... what about Daelin?]

Viktor glanced at his Dungeon Core. “What about it?”

[I mean... are you going to spend more time there and less time in the dungeon, or...?]

“Nothing changes. I’ll keep coming here every day, just like I always have.”

[But... you once told me that you would have to visit Claire daily, and you would stay by her bedside for an extended time. And that would reduce your time in the dungeon.]

“Oh, that. Yes, I did say that. But I’ve thought about it, and in the end, I’ve concluded it isn’t really necessary. After all, people have different ways of dealing with grief. Some cling to what they’ve lost, unwilling to move on, while others run from it altogether, continuing their normal routines as though nothing happened. Which means I don’t have to act in one specific way. There are so many options for me to choose from, and I simply picked the one that is most convenient.”

A faint sound escaped Celeste’s crystalline form. Something like a sigh.

“What’s the problem?”

[Nothing, Master.]

“Hey,” Viktor said, “it’s not like I’m completely neglecting her or anything. I’m still making meals and delivering them to the Sanctuary three times a day. I hand them over to Jeanne, or Alycia, or Rhea, whoever happens to be there. And I do inquire about her condition before leaving.”

[And none of them ask you why you refuse to stay, why you don’t remain by your sister’s side?]

“Well, they look like they want to. That much is obvious from how awkward and uncomfortable they all are. And yet, not a single one can bring themselves to actually say it. See? My acting is flawless.”

[...]

“What’s the matter, Celeste? You seem a bit strange today.”

[I am just thinking, Master... The dungeon is now in a normal operational state. There are no noteworthy adventurers arriving. There are no construction projects that require immediate attention. So perhaps... you don’t have to be here as often as you do.]

Viktor frowned. “Are you saying I am no longer needed?”

[That is not what I meant. What I was trying to say is... maybe you should get some rest.]

“I need no rest,” Viktor said. “I’m perfectly fine. If there’s nothing here to manage, then I’ll focus on training instead.”

[Training, Master?]

“Yes. After all, I’ll need to take on the masked men’s hideout, and then Xander himself eventually. So I need to be in my best shape. My Thaumaturgy cannot be improved any further at the moment, so I’m going to focus on refining my physical capability instead. So yes, structured combat training would be appropriate.”

[You intend to spar with Haku every day from now on?]

“Well,” Viktor said, rubbing his chin. “Fighting the same opponent over and over might not be ideal. If refinement is the goal, I need to expose myself to a wider range of combat styles. Time to face someone else, I guess.”

[Who are you going to train with, Master?]

“Let’s see...” Viktor chuckled. “How about... Sebekton?”
