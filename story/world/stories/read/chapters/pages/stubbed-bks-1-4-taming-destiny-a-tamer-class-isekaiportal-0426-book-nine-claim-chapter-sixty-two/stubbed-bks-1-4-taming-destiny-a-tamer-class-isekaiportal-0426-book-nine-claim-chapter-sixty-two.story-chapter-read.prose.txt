In the end, I ask my allies to update me through the Alliance Bond, allowing me to stay beside Artio for hours. I try different things in an attempt to reach him – none that work. I even attempt to contact the root network, but the Alliance Bond I offer goes ignored, as does the Tame Bond I tried afterwards. It’s not that they were rejected or didn’t work in the first place – it felt more like they failed from lack of response.

In between attempts to reach Artio and updates from the other lords, I find myself soothing my companions, many of whom are unsettled by Artio’s absence from the Bond. He might not be one of the lynchpins of the group, but he’s been with us for months by this point – even a solitude-loving and grumpy owlbear makes friends during that time.

I find myself sympathising with Bastet and the others who were present when I first learned to access and heal my soul. Those three days when I lay unconscious and unresponsive with blood occasionally leaking from my nose must have been just as agonising for them as this is for us. Probably more: at least Artio’s body is in good condition. His mind may be absent, but there are no visible signs of harm.

I know I have duties elsewhere, not all of which can be carried out through a Bond, but it’s hard to motivate myself to return when one of my companions is in such need. Still, when darkness finally begins to fall, I force myself to my feet. As worried as I am about Artio, there has been no response from him and I’m needed back at the settlement.

But I still hesitate, my fingers lingering in his fur. There’s one thing I haven’t tried – throwing my own consciousness after his. Yet that’s highly risky. I might easily end up like Artio here – my mind and soul trapped or lost elsewhere while my body lingers in a coma.

I’m still highly tempted to do it. Artio is one of mine after all, and there’s little I wouldn’t do for those who are mine, as Torrent’s kidnappers found to their detriment.

However, I force myself to pull my fingers away. I’m the expedition leader, and that’s in far too precarious a state right now for me to risk being absent for who even knows how long.

Anyway, Artio might pull himself out of this state soon enough.

I’ll give him some time, I tell myself firmly. But if he still hasn’t found his way back to us by the time our food supply is sorted and the settlement is more stable, I’ll go after him.

Having made the promise to myself and, silently, to Artio too in case he can somehow hear me though I can’t hear him, I feel very slightly better. Turning to the two deris, I look at them in concern.

“Will you be alright here?” I ask Artemis and Orion.

We will be fine, Artemis assures me firmly. We have eaten, we have drunk. We will guard through the night – or for as long as is necessary.

You do not need to do it alone, Bastet adds just as firmly. We will take turns. Artio is one of us, and we will keep him safe until Markus wakes him.

A flicker of embarrassment runs through me at her certainty – and at how none of them express any sort of doubt that I’ll succeed. Pushing that to one side, I send them wordless gratitude for stepping in to help. I might not be as sure as them that I’ll succeed, but what I do know is that their support and belief is all that keeps me standing when things get stressful.

“I’m glad that’s settled,” I say at last, then fix a stern gaze on Artemis and Orion. “Keep yourselves safe. If something threatens you and you’re not certain you can handle it, don’t hesitate to call for me.”

Of course, Artemis replies, as if the thought of doing otherwise would never occur to her. Which, natural pack-based creature that she is, probably is the case.

Before I leave, I send my flesh-magic again through Artio’s body, ensuring he is hydrated and nourished. We’ve been giving him small amounts of water, trickling them into his mouth, but the necessary nutrients from food have had to be replaced by my magic. It’s not a long-term solution, but for now, it will suffice.

If this continues for weeks, I’ll have to start thinking about muscle atrophy as well. At least my magic gives me more tools than any doctor on Earth ever had to support my patient.

As we turn to walk back to the settlement, I find myself surrounded by the raptorcats.

“Any issues in the territory today?” I ask Bastet, trying to get my mind off Artio and back onto other concerns that, while technically no less important, have found themselves replaced in my list of priorities.

Only one small challenge, she replies dismissively. He ran as soon as I extended my Domain.

“The best kind of fight,” I say, attempting a smile.

The least risky, she agrees.

“What about the rest of you?” I ask, glancing around at the others. “Were you all with the samuran hatchlings?” They had been herded off hours ago by the Warriors. I had been glad of it – their constant low-level chaos was hardly conducive to a good working environment.

Not all of us, Ninja replies. Only my sister and I went with them.

“Your sister?” I ask, surprised.

Ninja had clung so tightly to Storm’s memory that I wasn’t expecting her to use the term for anyone else, even if it’s obvious who she means.

She flicks her tail in a shrug, uncertainty bleeding through the Bond. It’s not directed at me, but at Lathani, who pads beside us, half-hidden in shadow.

Of course you’re my sister, Lathani replies, clearly confused. What else would you be?

Then, as if nothing has happened, she launches into an enthusiastic recounting of the day. I send Ninja a warm wave of reassurance and pride, and then focus in on Lathani’s report.

Apparently, Orion’s concerns weren’t entirely accurate – the samuran hatchlings did actually manage to take down some prey. They also ate it immediately, so there’s nothing to bring back, but it’s progress.

And frankly, I’m not inclined to enforce the old rule that they can’t eat unless they bring sufficient resources back to earn the right. I never agreed with it anyway. It wouldn’t change the food issue with our settlement anyway – if they didn’t eat the meat soon after killing the creature, they would have eaten it later. And if they need more tonight? So be it. They’re growing; they need the energy.

Still… the thought brings me back to the larger issue of food and our increasingly desperate need to find a solution to the Energy density of the plants before we over-use the food resources we have. At least the samurans mostly feed themselves, but if we over-hunt the forest, then they won’t have anything to eat either.

When I tune back in, Lathani and Ninja are swapping increasingly outrageous stories about which hatchlings were the best – and worst – hunters. Given exactly what they’re saying, I’m not sure I want half of them to be true.

It takes me a moment to realise that Trouble is being unusually quiet. He would usually be right in the middle of this kind of discussion, his sent communications not quite focused enough to be verbal, but close to it.

Today, though, he seems to be withdrawn. Is he hurt? I wonder, then reach out to him privately.

Is something wrong?

He hesitates, reluctantly sending a vague sense of not wanting to bother me.

I’m asking, I point out. That means you’re not bothering me if you respond.

There’s a long pause, long enough that I wonder if he’s just going to clam up. Then slowly, tentatively, he sends me a packet of emotions and images. With the experience I’ve had in understanding Tier ones, it only takes me a moment to interpret.

You’re worried about why you haven’t Evolved yet, I conclude.

He agrees, his ears and tail drooping.

Would you like me to take a look at your internal matrix?

His ears perking up slightly with hope, he agrees tentatively. Not because he fears giving me access, but because he fears that I might find something wrong that even I can’t fix.

Not looking won’t change anything, I point out. I still won’t be able to help you. At least if I look, there’s a chance I can help.

He side-eyes me then gives a huff, conceding my point.

“Lathani,” I say aloud, tilting my head toward her, “would you mind carrying me for a bit?”

She pauses mid-conversation. Why?

“I want to check something in my Core space,” I reply. It’s technically true – the nature of the Dominate Bond and its derivative Companion Bond is that my Companion’s Core spaces largely do count as mine. Magically-speaking, anyway.

She studies me for a moment, perhaps sensing I’m not being entirely honest. Then she shrugs and crouches.

I climb onto her back, scratching behind her ears in gratitude for the lack of questioning as I settle.

“Thanks.”

She flicks an ear in acknowledgement and stands up, continuing to walk and chat with Ninja as if nothing had happened. Several of those around glance at me, but, following Lathani’s lead, they don’t voice any of the questions I can sense they have.

Closing my eyes, I descend into my Core space and find the Bond that connects Trouble to me and the others in the network. It would be easier if I were touching Trouble, but our Bond is strong enough that I don’t have to. Touching our Bond, I send my consciousness along it and into his Core space.

When I first enter his space, the overwhelming sense of his presence just reminds me of how wrong Atio’s Core space had felt with his absence. Then I push the yelna out of my mind and focus on Trouble.

I study the mess of bronze threads that cradle an area that will clearly be his Core when he finally manages to Evolve, looking for anything that appears wrong or like it’s holding him back.

By the time I open my eyes again, we’re almost at the settlement gates.

There’s nothing wrong with your Core space, I tell Trouble contemplatively, considering what I saw. Relief flares, only to falter a moment later with a questioning feel. I know what he’s asking. There’s nothing obviously wrong, I repeat, but something is missing.

‘What?’ he demands, his communication almost sharp enough to translate as words in my head.

I’m not entirely sure, I admit. Do you mind if I ask Bastet?

He hesitates, torn between pride and fear. Then, reluctantly, he agrees, feeling more like the sulky cub he used to be than he has for a while.

I reach out to Bastet, sharing my impressions.

This is for Trouble, she says immediately.

I blink.

Yes it is, I confirm, but how did you know?

Because it is as I had already thought. What is missing is identity, she replies.

That…isn’t what I expected.

I didn’t think he struggled with identity of all things.

It is not only him, Bastet explains. Most males do. They play, they explore, but few truly ask who they are or why they exist. Without that, growth is limited.

But I thought that Evolution just requires intent, I argue. A desire to become something more and a vision of how to become that.

For the first Evolution, it is usually enough to know what we wish to be. To become Enlightened, we must know why.

I consider that, the pieces slotting into place along with what I already knew.

But Trouble isn’t trying to Evolve a second time – he hasn’t even Evolved the first time. Shouldn’t just knowing what he wants to become be enough?

Yes, Bastet agrees. However, to know what we wish to be, we must first know what we already are. It is that which is missing.

I frown, not quite sure what she’s getting at. How could Trouble not know what he already is? He’s a raptorcat – isn’t that enough?

Bastet clearly senses my confusion as she sends me a sense of indulgent exasperation.

I will speak to him. This is not something that will benefit from being translated through someone who does not fully understand.

Well that has put me right in my place. But since I’m not insecure in the slightest about Bastet and her affection and respect for me, I just send her a sense of warm ruefulness – and gratefulness that I have someone competent to delegate to.

You’re probably right, I admit. When Bastet flickers affection back at me and then turns her full attention onto Trouble, I feel a little sorry for him and wonder if he’ll regret telling me his struggles. Certainly, he sends me a faintly betrayed look when Bastet first reaches out.

But his attention is soon caught by the conversation she starts through a private link. As the master of the Bond network, I can feel it, though I choose to tune it out – they’ve both returned to messages that are emotions and images and little else and it would take me too much effort to translate.

I feel confident that he’s in good hands – or, rather, talons. With no issues in his Core space and Bastet guiding him, I’m confident he’ll Evolve soon. Which, considering Ninja managed it five months ago, is long overdue.

It will be good to have another Tier two raptorcat around. Maybe I should ask Spot and Blaze if they’re having any issues too – the only thing better than two Tier two raptorcats is four of them. And then they’ll be able to go out on their own into the forest which should please them.

The settlement gates creak open and we walk through. Glancing around at the well-built buildings and the increasingly-thin inhabitants, I feel a knot of tension inside me. It grows even bigger when I notice two people coughing and looking a little unwell.

I hope Thessalanor has checked out that well as we discussed, I think to myself, that meeting with Thessalanor this morning feeling like a long time ago. Maybe I should investigate it anyway since I’m here. We can compare results.

I hop off Lathani’s back, patting her shoulder affectionately.

Where are you going? she asks curiously.

“Not far,” I promise her. “I just need to check something.”

Before I can get more than two steps, though, a voice reaches through our Alliance Bond to me.

Markus? It’s Josiah’s mental communication. Can I speak with you? I am just outside the settlement – on the side closest to the sea.

I hesitate, casting a glance back at the gates, and then another in the direction I was intending to go.

Is it urgent? I ask him.

It’s his turn to hesitate for a moment.

I believe so, yes, he replies reluctantly.

I sigh heavily. Don’t we have enough problems? What is it now?

I’m coming, I tell him.
