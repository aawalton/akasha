While dread and chaos colored the skies and fissures scarred the ground, Varéc phased through the rupturing earth at lightning speed, barely able to keep his form intact as the earth shuddered and erupted all around him, massive chunks of frozen earth caving as destabilizations rocked the bedrock.

The hiss of static interference snapped at his form as he phased deeper into the earth, twisting and tearing like a pack of snarling wolves. But Varéc pushed through it without hesitation, feeling no pain even as he slowly lost mass.

He burst into Serythion's prison a few minutes later, or perhaps, what was left of it. Only this time, it held nothing prisoner.

Runes flickered across the chamber like dying embers, and debris rained down like a storm of metal and jagged stones. The entire chamber was wrecked beyond recognition—a shadow of its former self, and at its center, Serythion floated, humongous wings flapping in anticipation as he opened his maw to unleash what Varéc believed to be the fourth beam of condensed void essence at the cratering bedrock.

The beam slammed into the earth with the force of a terrible explosion, and several layers of bedrock simply vanished into nothingness as the beam smashed into it, a burning hole the size of Serythion himself drilling past several layers of reinforced stone and soil in an instant.

Debris rained down like colossal hail, smashing against the ground beneath them with sounds like thunderclaps. Space itself burned and hissed—shrieking as several rifts of unstable void ripped through its fabric.

The fabric of reality within the entire chamber was dying and screaming, but all that noise was drowned out by another guttural roar as Serythion unleashed another beam.

Varéc watched the destruction in awe, wondering when he would be capable of such tremendous feats. The screech of an erupting rift was what snapped his mind back to the present, and with a guttural roar, Varéc flew toward Serythion, seamlessly dodging a host of falling debris and erupting rifts.

Had he been a creature of flesh and blood, his heart would have been pounding erratically within his chest. But Varéc wasn't a creature of flesh and blood. Neither did he have a heart, so his excitement was manifested as a dense crackle of lightning around him.

The closer he drew to Serythion, the denser the coat of lightning became until it completely cloaked him—all six colors shimmering so powerfully they burned the very air around him.

The brightness caught Serythion's attention, and as the dragon turned to gaze at him—pupils of inky darkness gleaming—Varéc felt an instinctual urge to lower his head. To submit.

He resisted the urge with a flare of willpower, and for a moment, he feared Serythion would attack him, maddened by the idea of freedom. Instead, the dragon simply bared its fangs in amusement, a resonant growl emanating from its throat.

"I see you've fulfilled your part of the deal, Little Fury."

Varéc growled confidently. "We did. And now it's time for you to fulfill yours. But it seems you're in a hurry for freedom."

Serythion swished his tail in excitement. "If you had been bound for as long as I have been, Fury, then you would be just as enthusiastic."

Varéc flapped his wings once, golden pupils narrowing. "I have no problem with your enthusiasm. However, it seems you've forgotten to create the spawns you promised."

Serythion bared his fangs once more; only this time it was less amused. "I have no patience for spawns. I will fight for you. I am enough. Now join me to rip this earth apart. I want to taste the skies again."

Without waiting for his cooperation, Serythion turned his gaze to the blazing hole and unleashed another beam of absolute destruction.

That beam was the last straw.

The earth caved in an instant later as the beam finally broke through the surface, massive platforms of rocky earth descending like boulders of doom. But Serythion didn't care, because for the first time since his captivity, he felt the warmth of sunlight as a single ray streamed in, inky scales gleaming like liquid midnight.

Despite the constant crash of debris and shrieks of ripping space, the whole world seemed to hold its breath in that moment as Serythion took in the single ray of sunlight with an expression of awe.

Memories of gleaming scales and fluffy clouds flashed across his mind in that moment, and with a broken roar, Serythion flew toward the light, another beam of condensed void gathering within his throat to finish the work he had begun.

Fangs bared in genuine happiness, Varéc followed him, sending a slew of images and emotions to Aodhán through their psychic link.

And when Serythion finally unleashed the gathering beam, Varéc unleashed a condensed beam of red electricity alongside it. His beam barely did half the damage of Serythion's void beams. But it did do some damage, and that was all Serythion cared about.

With each explosion, more rays of sunlight spilled in until they were completely bathed in it.

And then Serythion roared, so loud it shook the entire earth to its core as he tasted true freedom.

He was no longer Serythion the Bound.

He was free now—unshackled—the last of the Voidborn Brood.

He was Serythion. The Unbound dragon of void-ridden skies. And he had a debt to pay.

I haven't tested the limit of the miracle rain since I entered the advanced class. It could enhance you far more than I could imagine. But until we test it out, I cannot be certain."

They had put a significant distance between them and the blood phoenix. If there were ever a time and place to use Tempest of Transformation, it is here and now.

The platform came to a sudden halt as soon as he had the thought, and he glanced at Baxter. "Gather as many people as you can. From the twin-peaked mountains. From everywhere. If we are to survive, then we must band together."

Baxter didn't even wait for Aodhán to finish speaking before vanishing, the world a blur of cosmic essence as he teleported toward his closest target.

There was no time to speak or try and convince them.

Baxter simply kidnapped them.

The world blurred in a chaos of screaming colors and fractured space as Baxter tore through space like a blade through silk, Spatial Rend reducing distance to a suggestion rather than a barrier.

His heart pounded in his chest, beating with fear and anticipation as he felt the earth shudder violently even within the space of his cosmic portal. The realm was destabilizing, and from its depths, creatures of insane strength were emerging.

Tier ninety! Baxter couldn't believe it.

The cultists were no longer playing around. They wanted to eradicate them swiftly and instantly. But all hope wasn't lost yet.

Aodhán's miracle rain was a truly miraculous skill, and though it couldn't turn the whole situation around, it would go a long way toward their survival.

It was a lifeline, and Baxter was holding tightly to it.

He didn't even care about the twisted effect anymore. All he cared about was the prospect of power. Of speed—that overwhelming feeling of clarity and godlike perception he had always heard about.

If nothing else, the skill could help them match the creatures for a few minutes. They could do immense damage during that time. All of them together, moving at immense speeds.

The mental image caused his heart to pound even harder, and he smiled. Perhaps they could win this. Perhaps they had a chance.

Hope rose within him like a dawn, burning through the cloud of despair in his heart to fill him with determination. And with that determination, Baxter pushed himself to go faster.

He couldn't sense the cores of people like Aodhán could, but that didn't stop him from finding all those scattered all around them—sensing their positions through the gravitational distortions their bodies created in space.

Tearing through chaotic space, Baxter appeared above a trio of fleeing Calodans, barely dodging an eruption of frozen earth as it shot into the sky like a geyser. One of them looked up, somehow sensing his arrival despite the chaos all around her.

Eyes widening, she opened her mouth to speak, perhaps about to beg him for a portal ride to safety. Little did she know there was no safety anywhere.

Baxter didn't even give her the chance to speak before obliging her—warping the space around her and her comrades to teleport them in an instant. Her comrades tried to resist, willpower flaring in fear, but Baxter squashed their feeble resistance with a flex of his willpower.

At the 47th tier, he was one of the most advanced persons in the whole realm, meaning that those who could contend with him in terms of control and willpower could be counted on one hand.

That didn't include Aodhán and Daruk, of course.

Channeling a ton of willpower into astral projection, Baxter teleported again, plucking a man out of the air before he even realized what had happened.

And then he teleported again, appearing before a duo of Unorians—one a plant awakened, and the other floating on a platform of gleaming metal. Instinctively, he stretched a hand out to warp the space around both men, but the earth heaved before he could, a piercing hiss slicing straight into his skull like a blade.

Baxter felt his heart stutter in absolute terror as he sensed the chilling aura of the basilisk rise below them. Directly below them!

His hands moved before he could even think, and space warped violently, teleporting them all away in an instant.

Fortunately for the Unorians, neither of them resisted as space pulled them from inevitable death and dumped them at Aodhán's location. He could still feel the aura of the basilisk on him, like slime and rock at the same time. Even from the aura alone, Baxter knew he would be lucky to last even a minute against that creature alone.

Shaken, he took in a deep breath before teleporting once again, dreading his next exit.

Breath hitching, he appeared over the next set of people, body tense in preparation for an attack. However, the moment his gaze landed on the two Calodan women beneath him, Baxter let out a relieved sigh.

It was Magda and Borevin.

Teleporting to stand before them, he created a portal and shouted. "Get in. We don't have time to waste."

Magda didn't even bother asking questions. She raced into the portal immediately, Borevin hot on her heels. The portal snapped shut the moment they vanished, and Baxter teleported again, astral senses expanding to find the nearest person.

The next few seconds were a series of repetitions. Teleport. Save. Sometimes speak. Teleport again.

This continued until Baxter found another familiar face, and for the first time since his kidnapping spree, Baxter encountered his first real resistance.

This time it wasn't a contest of willpower but of control over the concept of space.

One Baxter distinctly got the feeling he would lose.

For the first time since he began teleporting around, Baxter bothered to explain what was going on, expecting a snarl of fury the moment he mentioned Aodhán's name. He had been present during their last conversation, after all.

But Cyrus seemed to have been fundamentally changed since the last time he saw him.

Only they weren't waiting for salvation as Cyrus had said.

They were under attack from a pack of death wolves—hundreds of them pouring out of a jagged fissure, darkness spilling out of their bodies like ink. Each wolf was nearly as tall as he was, and floating above their heads was a line of text that sent a lance of utter terror through him.

[Death Wolf Spawn—Tier 50]

Every skill he had active in that moment deactivated, and he instantly plummeted to the ground between both parties. He barely managed to regain control of {Cosmic Flight} before the pack of wolves was upon him, fangs gleaming with death itself.

The calamity had begun, and Baxter found himself vastly outmatched.

Sebastian Rune stood at the top of the twin-peaked mountain, an expression of horror on his face as he watched the army of death wolves spill out of a jagged fissure like liquid darkness—their auras so dense with death it was a hassle just to breathe.

But he’d been wrong. So very wrong.

Perhaps even right from the beginning of this whole mess.

Mirith Vaelys.

He, a Rune, helpless before a mere telepath.
