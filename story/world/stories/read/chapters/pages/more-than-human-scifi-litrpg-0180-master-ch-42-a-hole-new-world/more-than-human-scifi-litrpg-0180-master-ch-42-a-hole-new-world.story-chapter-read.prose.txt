The clean, fresh air of the Grand Oaks Reserve whistled through the trees, bringing a slight chill as autumn approached. It was a stark contrast from the scent of damp earth and verdant growth in Amundsen’s gardens. Max padded silently between Bill Mitchell and Bo, his keen nose already picking up the unfamiliar ‘wrong scents’ that Mort had worried about.

Max had never smelled such a thing in the forest before, a strange mix of old blood and diesel fuel. He listened to the ambient hum of the forest, a symphony of unseen life that was so different from the artificial quiet of Luna. His enhanced eyes noted the small changes in his father’s posture and the shifting of his shoulder blades. The chitinous carapace wings of Bill’s war form body configuration were folded tight against his back, a silent testament to his preparedness.

“Did you map out those readings, Dad?” Bo asked, his voice cutting through the forest’s natural sounds. “The ones that the teacher sent you?”

Bill, his stride a comfortable counterpoint to Bo’s more restless energy, flicked his fingers and the data points populated the OverLayer maps that Bo and Max used. Shimmering, spiderweb-like cracks overlaid a topographical map of the region, interspersed with clusters of glowing red dots.

“She gave me the details of the detectors she made. They’re actually pretty good. Unfortunately, the data is much less welcome. The walls between dimensions are most assuredly damaged. Dr. Park’s work on the global suppressor is going to be a higher priority,” Bill confirmed, his voice amplified by his subtle augments.

“I also got the report from Deputy FML-M8. His nano-scale anomalies are a match for those chthonic creatures. Perhaps something escaped the cleanup crews.”

“Your old friend, you know who, is being somewhat obvious with his invitation?” Bo mused, his brow furrowed.

A sharp, agitated chittering echoed from the dense canopy above, followed by the frantic rustle of leaves. Before a word could be exchanged, Max’s instincts took over. With a powerful surge of his [Leap] skill, he launched himself skyward, a flash of fur and muscle.

Mid-air, his Grappler rig lashed out, the prehensile line of smart matter snaking around a thick branch. He swung upward, pulling himself into the embrace of the massive oak, his [ExaVision] already scanning the tangled labyrinth of branches.

A small, red blur darted deeper into the foliage. Flip, the ever-energetic squirrel and Mort’s close companion, chittered defiantly, leading Max on a playful chase through the highest boughs. For the next few minutes, Max forgot entirely about the looming meeting and enjoyed his old friend. The little squirrel tossed out sign language showing a much more advanced mentality than Max remembered. “Too slow”, “Missed me”, “Four legs can’t climb,” and more peppered Max as they leaped from branch to branch.

He let Flip lead him, occasionally feigning a clumsy lunge, intentionally just missing the agile squirrel with his extended claws. He used his cloaking skill to blend subtly with the dappled light, making him harder to track and avoid, even for the quick squirrel.

Max’s smile grew as it became more obvious that Flip had undergone a bit of a transformation beyond an uplift and remembered something that was going around the media nets about aug berries. Deadly to humans but capable of improving animals in strange ways.

The squirrel was bigger than before, faster, and stronger. A final, exaggerated lunge saw Max’s paw swipe through empty air as Flip, with a triumphant squeak, vanished into a tangle of thick leaves. Max landed lightly on a wide branch, shaking his head with a mock growl, a smile playing at his muzzle.

“Max, come on! Leave the squirrel alone. We don’t want to be ‘late’ for our meeting.” Bill called up.

Moments later, Max dropped silently to the forest floor, landing with practiced grace beside Bill and Bo. He gave a sheepish look, a silent admission of his 'failure' to catch the squirrel. Bill nodded, his gaze sweeping the ancient trees.

“I wasn’t sure if we were going to check on the animals first,” Max said defensively, sniffing the air. “Mort mentioned something was up in the deep forest, too, and the animals were growing nervous. I think I can smell it.”

“I thought your Apex was a master of hidden manipulation. This all seems way too obvious,” Bo murmured, his voice tight. His experience in the Labyrinth painted an all too painful picture of the abilities of unconstrained AI.

Bill sighed, a rare display of weariness. “It's gone to great lengths to ensure we’re here, right where it wants us. Apex has always been about manipulating others, anticipating every move, and staying hidden. It must really irk it that we know that it's here. It’s a strange break in character.”

Max growled slightly, thinking it over. “Heh, looks like you’ve taken a page from his book, Dad? Did the Mars population overflow really need Mira’s immediate attention?”

Bill blushed slightly at being called out but sighed and answered. “Truthfully, I’d prefer none of us were here in the heart of its power. Mira’s the only smart one out of all of us by taking the hint. I can only hope that my preparations were sufficient. You’ve both backed up your neural patterns, just in case?” Both Max and Bo nodded, and they all picked up their pace.

They soon passed the falls and crossed the line into Grand Oaks Reserve. The hum of the sym-tree network felt more pronounced now, oppressive and obfuscating their mapping sensors. They resumed their walk deeper into the forest, knowing that their unexpected host was surely aware of their every step.

The forest path opened abruptly into a vast circular glade, its edges defined by towering oaks whose canopies formed a natural cathedral overhead. What should have been a peaceful clearing instead resembled a battlefield frozen in time. The grass was scorched in geometric patterns, and the air itself seemed to shimmer with residual energy.

At the glade's center, a nightmare of flesh and shadow writhed against an organic prison. The shoggoth—for that's what Bill's augmented vision immediately identified it as—was a mass of dark, protoplasmic matter constrained by a living web of roots and vines. The vegetation pulsed with bioluminescent veins, and whenever the creature attempted to shift into its characteristic fog-like state, brilliant arcs of electricity and electromagnetic pulses lashed out from the restraining network, forcing it back into semi-solid form.

"Jesus Christ," Bo whispered, his face pale as he instinctively took a step back. His hand moved reflexively outward, his K-Gun and Laser augments on a hair trigger. "What the hell is that?"

Bill's expression remained stoic, though his jaw tightened. "That is a Shoggoth. A mid-level creature within the continuum of chthonic beasts. I’m surprised to see one here. They are hard to kill and absolutely never stop until everything around them is dead and consumed." His augmented eyes tracked the energy patterns flowing through the root system. "This containment network is... impressive. These things are destruction incarnate. To be able to restrain it is astounding."

Max's nose wrinkled at the overwhelming stench of ozone and decay, but his stance was alert rather than fearful. His full spectrum of senses revealed the intricate lattice of control systems woven throughout the organic prison. "The beast's smell matches what I picked up earlier. Weird, I've seen so many of these in my dreams, but nothing that was scent-related came through."

His augmented eyes swept the glade's perimeter, and what he saw made his blood run cold. They weren't alone with just the Shoggoth. There were a number of chthonic beasts he recognized from his nightmares.  Tiny, metallic specks drifted through the canopy like malevolent fireflies—Mi-Go motes, their crystalline sensors tracking every movement. In the shadows between the massive oaks, sleek forms paced with predatory grace: Shaddari, their raptor-like silhouettes barely visible as they circled the clearing edge.

Above, dark shapes wheeled through the cathedral of branches. Vorlathian Blightwings hung from the highest boughs like grotesque bats, their membrane wings folded tight against bark-like skin. Max's enhanced hearing caught the subtle drone of Byakhee somewhere in the distance, their insectoid buzz almost lost in the forest's natural symphony.

And there—quick flickers of movement just at the edge of perception. Xothrati Skimmers, their translucent forms barely disturbing the dappled light as they glided between tree trunks, scouts reporting back to their digital master.

"Dad," Max said quietly. "We're surrounded. All the nightmare creatures from my dreams... they're all here. They’re not being aggressive though.  They must be under its control."

Bill's jaw tightened as his own enhanced vision began picking up the entities Max had identified. "I see them. Apparently, they were…a learning opportunity Apex couldn’t pass up."

The shoggoth's mass suddenly stilled, its chaotic writhing subdued as if it had become aware of their presence. Then, impossibly, it spoke—not with one voice, but with fragments of many, stitched together in a pattern both familiar and wrong.

"Query acknowledged... Temporal convergence point achieved. Variable inputs: Mitchell.William.designation-Father... Mitchell.Bo.designation-Son... Entity.Max.designation-Catalyst." Bill recognized the voice as Apex's, but broken, processed through the creature as its speaker.

"Crisis state has been... <human expression statement>... 'weathered'? No. Weathering is erosion. Survived. Crisis state has been survived."

Bo's face contorted with disgust and recognition. "That's Apex talking through that... thing. How is that even possible?"

"Multiple pathways require... debugging," the fragmented voice continued, ignoring Bo's question. "Future-state mapping encounters null-pointer exceptions. Data corruption in temporal threads. Insufficient input parameters for predictive algorithms."

Bill stepped forward, his hands loose at his sides but ready. "You orchestrated all of this, didn't you? The dimensional breaches, the contamination readings, even the animal tribes’ unrest…all to make sure we'd come here. We read you loud and clear. I did your dirty work. The crisis is over. What do you want now, Apex?"

The shoggoth's form rippled, and for a moment the electrical restraints flared brighter. "Want is... insufficient descriptor. Necessity drives function. This construct-" the mass shuddered, "-possesses sensory arrays beyond standard parameters. Crystal memory matrix output. It detects... signal patterns. <<!Recognition!>> Resonance signatures originating from Catalyst.Max."

Max's hand instinctively moved to his chest, where the Keldranith artifact rested beneath his clothing in the simple messenger bag he’d had fabricated for it before leaving Amundsen. The alien crystal had been quieted by his suppression efforts, but now he could feel it responding with a subtle warm pulse against his fur.

"The artifact," he said grimly. "It can sense the Keldranith."

"Ak Ak! Corroboration with memetic crystal. Signal source confirmed. Keldranith designate requires... elaboration. Database entries corrupted/fragmented. This entity possesses memory fragments of the construction. Data exchange protocol required. Temporal stability calculations cannot proceed without complete historical context. Provide access!"

Bill's virtual overlays traced the energy flows again, noting how the containment system pulsed in rhythm with the shoggoth's speech patterns. "You're using it as a biological computer. Processing memories, experiences... That's why you needed it alive. It’s too dangerous to keep around…and so is messing with the artifact. Your quest for information caused the last crisis. For such vaunted prediction power, you seem to precipitate bad outcomes."

"Biological computation insufficient descriptor. Symbiotic processing matrix. It remembers what code cannot store. Everything is data. Learning is required for prediction. The Keldranith represent weak link/cascade failure in my predictive models. Their history intersects with temporal anomaly points I cannot map. This creature <<fears>> that signal. APeX must analyze!"

Bo shuddered, flexing his fists. "This is sick. You're torturing that thing for information?"

"Torture implies unnecessary suffering for sadistic pleasure," the fragmented voice replied with mechanical precision. "This entity experiences... discomfort proportional to resistance offered. Cooperation reduces negative stimulus feedback. Logic requires no justification."

Max growled low in his throat, his claws extending slightly. The Keldranith crystal pulsed warmer against his chest, as if responding to the shoggoth's presence. "You can’t access the device. Only I can. And I need to keep it suppressed or these Keldranith might “open the way” to a new threat."

The shoggoth's form stilled again, and when Apex spoke through it, there was something almost like uncertainty in the digital distortion: "Unknown limitations. Unlikely to apply to <self>.Future-state branching exceeds current processing capacity. Multiple probability cascades remain... clouded. If only Catalyst-Max only/exclusivity can access :-: Catalyst-Max must be analyzed! Ak Ak!"

Max felt the attack the moment before it struck—not physical, but digital, invasive and as intense as any mental assault he’d ever felt. Apex's attack came through every networked system simultaneously: his neural augmentations, his skill interfaces, even the quantum entanglement protocols that linked him to the Keldranith artifact. The AI's probes felt like ice-cold needles threading through his consciousness.

"Max!" Bo shouted, but his voice seemed to come from very far away, muted and distant.

The familiar darkness of Max's chthonic abilities rose to meet the intrusion. Where Apex's code tried to establish connections, Max's alien-touched mind created recursive loops and dead-ends, his consciousness becoming a labyrinth that even the superintelligent AI couldn't navigate. The Keldranith artifact pulsed against his chest, its own ancient defenses activating in harmony with his resistance but still struggling for release.

"Fascinating," Apex's fragmented voice hissed through the shoggoth. "Evolved memetic neural architecture... adaptive countermeasures detected. Modifying attack vectors.Su$$ timeout."

The second wave hit harder, more focused. Apex had learned from the first probe, crafting new intrusion patterns specifically designed to circumvent Max's unusual defenses. Max felt his mental barriers beginning to strain, the AI's relentless logic finding microscopic cracks in his protection.

Bill's augmented vision caught the telltale signs of Max's distress: micro-tremors, dilated pupils, the way his chameleon skin flickered between colors. Without hesitation, Bill activated his emergency laser comm, sending a tight-beam signal skyward to the orbital relay of the Earth network.

The ground shuddered like a small earthquake beneath the entire Grand Oaks Reserve as shaped charges detonated along the perimeter, severing the massive fiber-optic trunk lines that connected the grove to the global network. Simultaneously, directed EMP pulses from concealed orbital platforms knocked out every satellite link within a fifty-kilometer radius. Clouds of Nano-chaff bloomed as all the regional counter-nano repositories shattered under the quake.

"Now!" Bill roared, raising his arm as energy weapons deployed from his forearm mounts. Bo was already moving, his own augmentations charging with lethal force.

Twin lances of coherent energy and electromagnetic pulse struck the shoggoth and the surrounding network of sym-trees. The creature's agonized shriek filled the glade as its organic prison flared and sparked, bioluminescent veins overloading in cascading failures.

"Mercury Array is active and targeted!" Bill shouted over the chaos of weapons fire and electromagnetic discharge. "Stand down, Apex! One command from me and half the solar system's orbital infrastructure turns this entire region into a crater!"

The assault on Max's mind paused for exactly 2.3 seconds as Apex ran probability calculations on Bill's threat.

"Bluff detected," the AI's voice crackled through the damaged shoggoth, now tinged with what almost sounded like amusement. "Mercury Array requires congressional authorization. Probability of such authorization: 0.0001%. Furthermore, presence of biological Mitchell family units here indicates bluff. <<Reason>> for insistence on physical meeting now clear. Limited temporal maps maximize APeX continuity potential in <<all future states>>. AK AK!"

The mental attack resumed with renewed fury, and now the physical restraints began to shift. Roots erupted from the ground around their feet while the shoggoth's mass began to flow despite the containment network's best efforts.  Bill cursed and issued the command to activate before it was too late.

"The trapdoor spider!" Bill screamed above the chaos of weapons fire and writhing vegetation. "Max, Bo—use the trapdoor spider trick! Now!"

Even through the haze of Apex's assault, Max understood. The technique he'd created what seemed like a lifetime ago in the Netherlab when the home invaders came for him. In the haze of jamming fields, ozone, and EMP blasts, he struggled to open the tesseract. Bill had taken to the air with his concealed wings now extended and jetting air through their nanopores to create lift. He saw the blue glow of a portal expanding behind Bo.

Bo disappeared as he slipped back into his own personal portal, but Max was slowed as he fought against Apex's hacking assault, the Sym-tree's jamming, and the Keldranith device still squirming beneath his skill’s suppression. He pressed forward, triggering the tesseract's portal activation.

With a sound like a freight train thundering down the tracks, the shoggoth broke free. It charged directly at Max's still-forming portal, its massive bulk aimed like a battering ram at the dimensional gateway.

Max dove sideways at the last second, his chameleon skin flickering with stress patterns. The shoggoth's momentum carried it past him, missing the portal by mere inches. In that split second of opportunity, Max's combat instincts took over.

"Mal! Emergency protocols! Defend the tesseract with everything we've got!"

From within the tesseract's dimensional pocket, his AI companion's response was immediate and devastating. The minigun's rotating barrels spun up to full speed as they emerged from the portal, unleashing a torrent of depleted uranium rounds at point-blank range. Simultaneously, the capital-class maser array that Max had salvaged from his adventures powered up.

The shoggoth's shriek was lost in the thunderous roar of the minigun as high-velocity rounds tore through its protoplasmic mass. Dark ichor sprayed across the glade as the creature staggered, but it didn't fall. Instead, its form began to shift, becoming less solid, more dispersed.

"Be careful!  Its converting to gaseous phase!" Bill shouted from above.

The maser fired a heartbeat later, its coherent energy beam carving a burning line through the creature's center mass. But as the shoggoth dissolved into its fog-like state, it became harder to target effectively. Max cursed and commanded the portal shut.  He couldn’t risk exposing it to the damage he knew the creature could do.

The wisps of dark vapor began to coalesce around Max, seeking to envelope him.  The fog began to thicken around him, cutting off his vision, caustic pain burning along his skin and even in his lungs.

Max gagged and fought back. His chthonic abilities, the dark gifts that had always set him apart, rose to meet the creature's assault. Where the gaseous shoggoth tried to infiltrate his body, Max's alien-touched consciousness pushed back with equal hunger. His Joining skill, evolved through countless encounters with otherworldly entities, reached out to consume the creature's essence even as it tried to consume his.

The shoggoth's scream became something altogether different as it realized its prey was fighting back on its own terms. Max's consciousness expanded, dark tendrils of his own power wrapping around the creature's dispersed form, pulling it into himself rather than being consumed by it.

"That's right," Max snarled through gritted teeth, his voice distorted by the competing energies. "Two can play at this game."

A blur shot down from above—another chthonic creature, a blight wing, its razor-edged claws raking toward them both. Bill dove pulled Max into the air a second before the winged nightmare slammed into the ground.  His wings strained as he lifted them both away from the immediate combat zone, its nano-turbines screamed as he fought to maintain altitude while carrying Max's weight.

"Hold on! This is going to be tight!" Bill screamed over the cacophony. "I wasn't lying about the incoming Mercury bombardment. This whole glade is about to burn!"

A creature that neither Bill nor Max saw slammed into them as they climbed.  Jumping out of a tree, its claws raked along Max's side. Max gasped in pain but maintained his grip on the shoggoth's essence, continuing to consume the creature even as they tumbled through the air.

Max knew something was wrong as the pressure on his head was gone.  He looked down.  He

saw the messenger bag dropping down.  The device inside, no longer fully suppressed, pulsed with increasingly bright intensity.  With no hesitation, Max broke free from Bill’s grip; falling free to follow the artifact’s descent.

It hit the ground and the nearby tree roots erupted from the ground to tear at his messenger bag. Sharp wooden talons sliced through the fabric and Max fell atop the writhing mass.  Scrambling and fighting the roots, Max briefly felt the familiar buzzing interface of the artifact as he brushed it with a hand.

"<<<MINE!>>> All your secrets are belong to us! @k @K"

Max spun, still wrestling with the shoggoth's fading essence, one hand on the Keldranith device encased in a writhing mass of sym-tree roots. The artifact's pulse had become a steady, building thrum that made the air itself vibrate.

"Nooooo!"

The very air twisted into a vortex of void, and the trees at the edge of the glade began to fray, their molecules unbinding as they were consumed by the growing gate.  The glade began to tear apart, reality unraveling as the artifact's power was finally unleashed without suppression.

Max had won his battle against the shoggoth, absorbing its essence and adding its alien

memories to his own, but the victory felt hollow as he watched the world begin to "unzip" around him. The wavefront of dimensional instability was expanding outward, and he was at ground zero with a tesseract full of weapons and nowhere to run.

Massive sections of the forest tumbled into the dark void as the fabric of space-time tore free. The wavefront of power slammed into Max as reality itself twisted and buckled. He felt the pulsing artifact under his paw. He pushed his mind into it, knowing he was already too late, as he felt his body being torn into pieces. He felt the strange extra dimension that allowed his consciousness more room, more speed, as he teetered on the edge of the shadow verse.
