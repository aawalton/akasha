Trudging through the depths of the night in a deadly dungeon full of undead was a lot more serene than Samuel would have expected. The air was cool, a pleasant breeze caressing his skin. The stars and moon gave him enough light to not even need his flashlight, though he made sure to keep it in his left hand regardless.

Better safe than sorry.

Of course, Samuel was not under any illusions as to whether the dungeon was a pleasant place. Being pursued by poisonous snakes had thoroughly dissuaded him of that notion. The serenity was only a mirage, one he suspected was a result of being on the outskirts of the Hollow Fortress.

He had been walking for some time now, though he could not tell how long. He had a watch in his bag, but he was too wary of a potential ambush to try taking his attention off his surroundings for even the smallest time. Still, he did not think he had been walking for more than fifteen minutes. It was likely even less, as the lack of activity made it seem longer.

Much to his surprise, he found himself notably less skittish than he was in the Venomous Forest. Perhaps it was the lack of trees and other hiding places in the flat bedrock that stretched on for as far as he could see. The new terrain was definitely something he could get behind.

He also suspected that his reduced anxiety had something to do with the fact that he was dealing with something other than venomous snakes. A blindingly fast enemy that could one shot him in a fight was a terrifying opponent, and he knew he would have been dead many times over without his new [Telekinesis] skill. Even though he hadn’t seen the undead, he doubted they would be like that.

The moment he had the thought, Samuel mentally kicked himself. He had resolved to make sure he did not let his presumed knowledge of certain fantasy concepts cloud his decision making. He knew what undead looked like in movies and games, but there was no guarantee the one’s he would meet would be even remotely similar.

Besides, even in fantasy, there were undead races that could one shot their victims with blinding speed.

As the thought entered his mind, Samuel desperately hoped he would not meet any vampires in this dungeon. Especially not in the darkness.

Despite his meandering thoughts and the deceptive silence, Samuel made sure to pay keen attention to his surroundings as he walked. Regardless of terrain advantage, it was better to err on the side of caution.

It was because of his sharp attention that he saw the first undead enter his vision even though it was nearly two hundred metres away in the dark.

The moment he sensed it, he tensed instinctively, preparing for it to run at him or attack instantly in some way. It was only after a few moments of staring awkwardly that he realised the undead had not spotted him yet.

‘Poor senses,’ he noted mentally. It was either that or the undead just did not pay attention to their surroundings much.

Not intending to let the opportunity go to waste, he tried to study the opponent as much as he could without getting spotted. He couldn’t do anything too thorough, as he didn’t want to expose himself early, which meant he had to point his light away and keep his distance.

That did not stop him from noting every detail his eyes could make out in the darkness. The outline of the creature, which was likely undead, was humanoid. He could see clothes covering a fairly large frame, though it did not seem armoured,thankfully.

It also seemed to move normally, as it walked around leisurely with seemingly no sense of direction. It seemed fairly normal, all things considered, and might have even looked human if not for the fact that its skin was pitch black and wrinkly.

At least that was what it looked like from the distance.

The light of the moon was enough to pick up a few other details, like the fact that the undead did not seem to be armed. That filled him with both confidence and doubt simultaneously.

On one hand, he was glad that he would not have to face an agile undead with a sword. On the other hand, it didn’t seem likely that a dungeon would just leave even a lower level mob unarmed. Unless, of course, it had other weapons that were not visible.

It might have been his paranoia talking, but in a world that had been trying to kill him for the past few days, Samuel was happy to assume the worst if it might save his life.

After he was fairly certain he had accessed all he could with the distance and lighting, Samuel steeled his heart, gripped his staff tighter and started walking forward to the waiting undead.

When he was less than twenty metres from the undead, the creature suddenly raised his head sharply and turned to his direction.

Samuel had put his flashlight inside his backpack to avoid losing it in the coming fight, but he was finally close enough to properly make out the thing’s appearance.

It was dark skinned, like he thought, with skin even more wrinkled than it had previously appeared. It was so wrinkled, in fact, that he wondered how it had not simply fallen to the ground. Even the truly old people he had seen above ninety years old did not have skin so gnarled.

The fact that it could move like a normal human when its skin was practically dropping was just one more reminder of his new world where sense and reason were abandoned and the laws of physics did not work as it should have.

Immediately the humanoid monster spotted him. It screamed, a guttural thing, closer to the cry of an enraged tyrannosaurus rex than anything a human lung should be able to produce. Then it charged at him, its faced twisted in anger.

Samuel was as mentally prepared as he could be, his hands already poised to use his spirit weapon the moment the creature took off. By the time it finally reached him, his hands had already swung the stick with all the strength he could muster, adding a burst of telekinesis at the tip of the stick as he did so.

Magical weapon or not, a walking stick would normally not be able to actually crack the undead’s skull, something he suspected was at least as durable as a human’s skull.

Unfortunately for it, Grandma’s Walking Stick was merely the vessel. The real killing blow was the burst of telekinesis that waited at the end of the stick.

From what limited practice he was able to achieve, he had figured out how telekinesis worked to some extent. A burst of telekinesis with a fairly small part of his mental energy was a lot more powerful than he had previously thought. How effective that power ended up being, however, was up to the way it was used.

He could simply send out a burst at something, which was a fairly inefficient way of doing it. It could be used to stun things or fling them away like he had done with the snakes, but that was all it would do. Not particularly ideal if he wanted to end a fight and actually land a killing blow.

There were, however, other things he could do to use the same amount of energy far more efficiently. His experiments had shown him quite a few things that might be possible, including giving his mental energy a bladed edge, something he knew was sure to make his dungeon run a lot easier.

Unfortunately, most of those methods, including the bladed edge, required far more finesse than Samuel could currently manage. He could only mould his mental energy into something that could barely be called a blade and would struggle to cut even paper, as it was, so he was forced to find other alternatives, for now.

One of those alternatives, he found out, was using his telekinesis in conjunction with his bound weapon. The stick was a fairly ineffective weapon on its own, but it responded far more easily to it than to his knives. That, along with the superior reach of the weapon, made it the easiest way to channel his telekinesis with the best efficiency, at the moment.

So when the stick hit the creature, it did so with enough mental energy to send a python flying backwards, concentrated into a single point at the tip.

The skull of the low level undead mob did not stand a chance.

You have slain an [Undead Revenant - level 2].

The skull caved in under the force, sending brain matter flying and forcing him to jump out of the way to avoid it. For a moment, he simply stared at the broken skull and bits of gooey brain, the reality of what had just happened.

Only the increasingly pungent stink of the corpse managed to snap him out of his trance and bring him back to reality. He took a breath and gagged, unable to bear the stench before running away as far as he could.

He didn’t stop until he was far enough that the smell was reduced to a faint lingering odour. The corpse was likely nearly a hundred metres away from him, though he had moved to its right instead of going backwards to his former position.

He first checked out his backpack and his clothes, thankful that they both managed to escape getting the disgusting pieces of flesh on them.

After that was confirmed, he finally took a breath and panicked.

A dozen golems, powerful beasts of lava and stone, chased after two unfortunate souls. Running ahead of them with all their mights, were a pair of siblings who cursed every decision that led to their current predicament.

The chase continued for a while, neither side tired enough to give up. Fortunately for the fleeing siblings, rock golems were not known for speed.

Not that it meant much when they also did not get tired. With the way things were going, the golems would tire them out.

They were already feeling rather hopeless when they finally found salvation. For a few short metres ahead, the landscape transformed from volcanic rock to a lush grassland.
