"Get back!" Mom called, pushing both Callie and Tristan away from the raging arcane beast.

Callie watched in horror as the majestic moose tried to reach them, swinging his head left and right. But with each movement, the snare wires dug deeper into its flesh, causing more blood to spill onto the snow.

Mom withdrew her sword and took a step back from the arcane beast. It didn't look like she wanted to fight it at all. In fact, she had a pained expression on her face as she saw the blood spray on the snow.

"Please, calm down! We came because Snowglow asked us to help you!" Callie called.

The glimmerhare then appeared right beneath Callie, looking nervously at the deer.

This my friend. Friend help!

The deer paused and looked at the glimmerhare between Callie's legs.

Friend help.

"We'll free you from the trap and heal your injuries," Callie said, hoping that the arcane beast would listen.

It looked at Mom's sword and snorted.

A hunter never heals. Only kills.

"Mom, put your sword away!" Callie insisted.

"No, Callie! It is dangerous!" Mom shook her head.

"What was the point of coming here even if you're going to point a sword at an injured creature?" Callie snapped.

Mom looked taken aback. She looked at the moose, then down at her sword. Finally, she tossed it some distance away.

"Thank you, Mom!" Callie said, taking a careful step closer to the moose.

"B-Be careful…" Tristan muttered, looking nervously at the large creature.

The moose studied Callie carefully. Despite being ensnared and injured, it still held a sort of regal majesty. Its head was held high, and Callie held no delusions. Should it choose to, it could fling her like a ragdoll with its mighty antlers.

"We only wish to help," Callie said calmly, while reaching out to her magic. She was trying to channel the happy, safe feeling usually felt when growing vegetables.

You… You possess magic!

The moose's disposition shifted. It lowered its head and sniffed at the air curiously.

"Yes, I do! I try to use it to help arcane beasts," Callie said, while slowly stepping closer to the moose. She was hoping that the conversation would distract it from what she was about to do.

Is that so? Never thought I'd meet a human with magic. And one so small. What is your name?

Callie was right next to the moose. She felt absolutely minuscule next to it. She was barely as tall as one of its legs. One move and she would be squashed. Mom looked absolutely terrified, being so close to such a large arcane beast.

But Callie wasn't going to abandon it just because of fear. This wasn't a predator. If she treated this creature with the respect it deserved, she would remain unharmed.

"I'm Callie," she replied, and reached out to the closest snare wire.

The moose shifted nervously, so she stopped.

"I'm going to cut the snare first, okay? Then we'll try to do something about the large trap," Callie explained.

The moose lowered its head, looking at the snare wire tangling it up.

These human traps are horrendous. Any weaker creature would have died.

It sounded angry. Callie swallowed hard as her heart thundered in her chest. "I… I'm sorry you had to go through this. Nobody should be allowed in this part of the forest. Everyone in the village has dubbed it the Deep Forest."

"That's right. We normally do not come here. We know this is where you live, the swamp king," Mom said cautiously.

The moose regarded Mom suspiciously.

I am no king. I am a drownhart, the guardian of these swamps.

"A drownhart?" Callie repeated the unusual name.

Mom grew pale at the mention of the name, taking a few steps closer to the sword she had previously tossed aside.

"Drownhart? I heard from Ma that they drown people who go too deep in the swamp. Do you mean that this…" Tristan looked at the arcane beast with newfound fear. "You mean that the drownhart and the swamp king are the same being?"

Callie wasn't familiar with the term drownhart, but it appeared that some people knew of them. Still, this didn't change anything for Callie. She was here to save this arcane beast, and the name as such didn't mean much to her. Beyond writing it down in her notes, of course.

"I'm going to cut the snare wire now, so please hold still. I don't want to accidentally hurt you," Callie said, retrieving a pocket knife from her satchel.

The drownhart gazed at her suspiciously, but remained still. Despite the cold, Callie felt herself sweating from nervousness. When Snowglow had come to her for help, Callie had not expected that the creature would be so large and fearsome.

She began her work slowly, cutting on the loose bits of snare wire and pulling them away gently. Unfortunately, many of the wires were so tangled up that she couldn't pull them free even after cutting them. There was no choice but to approach the more tightly wound wires.

How did a child like you obtain magic?

"I… I don't know. It happened after I helped Snowglow. She was also trapped, unable to get free from a horrible metallic trap," Callie said.

The glimmerhare hopped up to the drownhart.

It's true. Callie helped. Callie save.

The tightly wound muscles beneath the drownhart’s dark green fur relaxed at the sight of the small glimmerhare.

Is that so? So you try to use your magic for good, unlike those other humans?

"What other humans?" Callie asked.

The ones with blood magic. The foul monsters have tried to capture me many times. But I can sense them. Smell the blood on their hands.

Callie's hands trembled as she gently nudged her finger in between the wire and the drownhart’s hide. He felt warm and also slightly moist to the touch. As if his fur was made of wet swamp moss. It was surprising that it hadn't frozen over in the cold winter air.

She carefully used the tip of the knife and cut the wire, which had tangled itself around the drownhart's front legs.

"Blood magic…" Callie muttered. "What is that?"

It sounded ominous, and it also made her think of dragonbloods.

Magic taken forcibly from arcane beasts. It has been festering in this world for a while now. This forest has been mostly safe from it, but I've recently felt its foul presence even here.

"It couldn't be anyone from the village. My Mom and Dad make body-based magic while I use the one from…" Callie trailed off, remembering that Tristan didn't exactly know the source of her magical power. She cut another snare wire, wrapped around the drownhart's chest.

The arcane beast straightened, most of his body now freed from the wires.

Yes, I do not sense any blood magic on you or your companions. But they are somewhere close. You must be wary!

"Is the one with blood magic the one who trapped you?" Callie asked.

I do not know. Admittedly, I was a fool. I ate those mushrooms, thinking it was an offering from the villagers. I have occasionally received similar gifts in the past. But hidden among the snow was a trap!

"Mushrooms? What mushrooms?" Callie asked as she cut the last of the wire.

The drownhart suddenly reared up, and she fell back. Mom hurriedly rushed forward and pulled Callie to herself, covering her with her body.

Much better. But I cannot free myself from the trap.

The drownhart pulled on the large bear trap that held its hind leg in place.

"Mom… the drownhart can't get free of the trap. Can you get it off?" Callie asked.

Mom observed the drownhart cautiously for a moment. Seeing that it was standing calmly, she released Callie and looked at the trap carefully.

"I'm not sure… Your father could have gotten this off without a problem. Maybe we should go fetch him," Mom said.

"We can't leave him! What if the person who set up the trap comes back and…" Callie didn't want to finish the sentence.

Everyone understood her meaning nonetheless. Whoever laid this trap did so with the intention to capture the drownhart. It wasn't a coincidence. Especially not if it was those mushrooms that were used as a lure. Callie felt her stomach lurch at the unpleasant thought.

Mom stared at Callie, then at the trap, then finally nodded. "Yes, you're right. This was definitely deliberate. Beast, I'm going to approach you now to open the trap."

I am a drownhart. Not a beast.

He let out a dissatisfied snort.

"Mom, you're being rude! You should call him the drownhart, not beast," Callie said.

Mom appeared taken aback, but then inclined her head politely at the drownhart. "My apologies, drownhart."

The drownhart nodded in agreement and allowed her to approach.

While Mom dealt with the large trap, Callie went up to Tristan, who was still dumbstruck by the sight of the arcane beast. "Come on! We're going to apply healing ointment and patch up his wounds."

"I… I…" Tristan gaped. "It's not going to eat me, is it?"

Callie laughed. Even the drownhart looked amused by the question.

I do not consume human children.

"Of course it won't eat you. It's a herbivore. They only eat plants and mushrooms. Now, come on! We need to help as much as we can!"

After much nudging and prodding, Callie finally got the boy to move. Initially, he only handed Callie the medical supplies, while she applied crushed millefollia leaves and ointment, as well as wrapped bandages around a few places where the snare wire had dug really deep in the drownhart's skin.

But eventually Tristan gathered the courage and joined her, also applying ointment while staring at the drownhart with eyes as wide as saucers.

"I can't believe I'm doing this…" Tristan muttered. "Pinch me!"

"Sure," Callie said, pinching the boy without holding back.

"OW! I didn't mean it!" He glared at Callie.

While they worked on the drownhart's injuries, Mom battled with the large bear trap. Instead of trying to pull the jaws apart, as Callie had done with the small trap, Mom was doing something with a mechanism at the base of the trap.

But it appeared to be rusted, so getting it to do anything was a challenge. She pushed the spring with one foot while trying to press down on the hinges. It took several tries before she finally managed to open the jaws.

Drownhart carefully removed his hind leg, but it was in a terrible state. Even worse than Snowglow had been in.

"That's pretty bad… doesn't it look like it needs to be sewn?" Tristan asked.

Callie looked at the drownhart as it looked at its injured limb. It might be an intelligent creature, but being stitched up without any pain mitigation… No, it would be too dangerous for everyone involved.

"Do you even know how to do that?" Callie asked the boy.

"Uh… no. But Granny Ethel does."

"No, stitching is out of the question," Mom said firmly. "It would be painful without any painkillers, and we don't know if any human painkillers would work on the… drownhart."

I do not understand what you are saying, but I thank you for releasing me. I shall be going now.

"No, you can't go like this! You're bleeding horribly!" Callie said.

The drownhart nodded solemnly.

I am, but it is not a deadly wound. I shall recover.

"Let us bandage it at the very least!" Callie insisted.

The drownhart conceded, allowing Callie and Tristan access to his injured leg.

They worked quickly but carefully, washing up the wound as best as they could and then rubbing the millefollia leaves on it. It should stop most of the bleeding. They also wrapped several layers of bandages around the wound. They quickly stained red, which worried Callie, but it was better than nothing.

"Ideally, we would have to change the bandages the next day," Callie said to the drownhart.

No, child. I shall retreat to the depths of the forest and remain there, away from any humans.

Callie nodded somberly. It was an understandable reaction.

The drownhart turned to leave, but there was still an urgent question she had to know the answer to.

"Wait, what were the mushrooms used to bait you here?" Callie asked.

You can find them in the snow.

That was the last thing the drownhart said before disappearing into the forest.

"Help me find those mushrooms!" Callie said, crouching down into the bloodied snow and searching.

"Does it matter?" Tristan asked nonchalantly. "It was lured into a trap by a mushroom. Who cares what kind it is?"

"I care!" Callie snapped as she continued searching. She had a very bad feeling in her stomach.

"Here, I found one!" Mom called as she dug one out of the snow.

They both stared at the mushroom in horror, recognizing it despite half of its cap being covered in blood. It was the swamp lantern mushroom. The very same Callie had gotten from the glimmerhares.

The very same that Callen sold to Silas.
