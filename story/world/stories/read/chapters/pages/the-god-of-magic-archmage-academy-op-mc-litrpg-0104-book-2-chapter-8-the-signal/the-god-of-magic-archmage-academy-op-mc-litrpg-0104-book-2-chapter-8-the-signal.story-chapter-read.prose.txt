Before The Uprising

Sen

Garlon’s breath came more slowly now as the embrace of death approached. The dreadgiant stared up at the sky, taking in his final sights. “I’ve never told anyone that before,” he said as he finished his story.

Sen sighed and looked up at the sky as well. It was a gloomy today. Sad weather for a dying king, but perhaps appropriate, considering the mood. “If we had met sooner, perhaps we could’ve resolved things differently,” Sen said. He couldn’t help but feel it was true.

“Perhaps. It’s too late for that now, though.” Garlon paused a moment before speaking again. “Would you…would you promise me something, God of Magic?” He addressed Sen by the title the System had bestowed him following their fight.

“It depends on the promise.”

“Would you—”

“Honored one, is everything okay? You look troubled,” Drussan said.

The dreadbeast in human form got his attention, and Sen’s mind returned to the present. He let out a breath. “Sorry, I was lost in the past, I suppose. Just reminiscing. Are we almost there?” he asked.

Drussan nodded. “Soon.”

The two of them now walked through the streets of Vitree together. Thanks to the bracelet Sen had given him, Drussan was currently in the form of Malachi, the human slave owner. Sen had also disguised himself with a spell. He wore black hair in a ponytail, and his eyes had changed from red to green. The golden mana channels on his cloak had faded away, and the cloak now resembled the black clothes of a swordsman more than they did a mage. He’d even torn his necklace, Malleore, from his neck and had it change form. It now appeared as a katana and its sheath. Sen had affixed it to his waist.

He was now Gosen, a katana-wielding mercenary recently employed by Malachi.

Tutor hadn’t stopped joking about it since Sen settled on the temporary name.

[Very creative. You just removed the m from Gom and put it before Sen.]

Sen hadn’t had the luxury of exploring much of Vitree since arriving, but admittedly it was a pleasant town. Even the side streets were often filled with businesses and customers during the day. At night, most of the deals being made occurred on the main roads and in the bustling city square. That was when the dreadbeasts would make their move.

That was when they would escape.

His thoughts lingered on his companions. The others had been assigned towns and encampments they should have no problem liberating. The dreadbeasts had strategically placed Khans amongst the slaves of towns with higher-ranked mages among the guards and slavers. Between that, and the element of surprise, their plans should work. He only hoped his companions were safe. They’d been thrown into this because of him. When all of this was behind them, he’d give them a better explanation of it all. The truth behind the war that had ultimately led to Sen and Garlon’s climactic fight to the death. The reason this was so personal for him.

[You’re putting a lot of pressure on yourself, master. None of your allies needed convincing to free slaves.]

Still, he owed them the truth.

They made good progress and eventually reached the temporary residence of Malachi during his stay in Vitree. A sizable mansion in the heart of the city. The entire estate was fenced in by decorative but very functional fences. Guards patrolled the outer perimeter, while gardeners tended to the massive assortment of plants and greenery that colored the estate a vibrant, fertile green. There were plants from all over within. Sen recognized lava lilies and small, carefully maintained frost pines from the Siersen Plains. The mammoths of those plains had once planted one of them in his honor. Two broad-shouldered guards stood watch over the front gate.

“I thought Malachi was only visiting for his son’s wedding?” Sen asked. This was…extensive for temporary lodging.

Drussan nodded. “He is. That’s the home of the bride’s father. He’s known as Lord Thessus, a corrupt nobleman with enough wealth to make Malachi look destitute by comparison.”

Sen tilted his head at that. “Then why join houses with Malachi?”

Drussan’s face hardened. “Because Malachi has access to a market Thessus wishes to tap into. Dreadbeast slavery is still slavery, and most of the noble families cling to power by reminding the world of their families’ former allegiances to King Ragmár. They rebuke the practice openly, or turn a blind eye to it, but the winds are shifting. Thessus already has a questionable reputation. He’s not afraid of soiling his good name to increase the coin in his purse. He fully intends to be the first of the former noble families to embrace the practice openly, and with Malachi’s connections, it wouldn’t take long for him to cement a foothold in the trade.”

Sen gripped the handle of his katana. “Perhaps we should add another to the list.”

Drussan shook his head. “No. Thessus must live. We kill only the slave traders. If we strike down a nobleman, even one like him, it could bring war to our people. We must avoid that at all costs, honored one.”

“Gosen,” Sen corrected. “But I’ll respect your wishes.”

[That doesn’t mean I have to. Let me loose, I’ll handle him!]

What can you do? Analyze him to death?

[You joke, but I can do it. Watch me.]

The guards opened the gate upon seeing ‘Malachi’ and allowed them inside without trouble. The mansion really was a fine building. A stone path led them to the building’s large brown doors. They had intricate black embellishments that depicted the symbol of a fox. The doors grated open on their own, responding to a necklace around Drussan’s neck. Sen eyed it. “A key of some kind?”

Drussan nodded. “You’ve never seen one before? They’re popular among the wealthy. Try to enter their property without a mana-key and alarms go off to alert their guards. It could also trigger spells or runes, depending on security.”

In this case, it would be Lord Thessus’ private guard. Drussan’s intel said they wouldn’t be a problem. Knight-commander and low-noble class mages at best. After all, Thessus lived in the center of a major town. A wealthy one at that. Captain Fulcrae was apparently a force to be reckoned with. These private guards were more for peace of mind than anything else.

When they entered, Sen was almost reminded of Harpis Castle. The mansion was an opulent residence. A ridiculously expensive-looking mana-chandelier hung from the ceiling. Hundreds of smaller mana-light gems dangled from it, creating the impression of a moon surrounded by stars. The walls were adorned with paintings of historical battles, of which depictions of Gom were in more than a few. There were also busts that he could only assume were Lord Thessus and his family. A central staircase led to the upper floors of the mansion, and a dozen odd doors led in every direction. Navigating this place on his own would be a nightmare. At least, if he didn’t have Tutor.

[I’ve compiled a map of the building based on Drussan’s descriptions. The stairway to the basement is through the door on your right.]

“Ah, Malachi! You’ve returned!” A grand voice called out. Sen and Drussan both looked up at once to see a face that was a perfect match for one of the busts. He had a long, robust nose and shoulder-length white hair that curled in on itself towards the bottom. He was of average height and had the eyes of a cold, calculating man.

“Lord Thessus! It’s good to see you,” Drussan held his hands out. The two men rushed forward to embrace.

[Drussan plays the part well.]

He’s spent a lot of time by Malachi’s side.

“I was wondering where you snuck off to. We should be drinking and celebrating. Our families will be joined soon. This is a time for fellowship, no?” Lord Thessus grinned. “By the way, where is Micah? Orianne said he went out last night and never returned.”

[He’s been tied up and left next to his father in an abandoned store. Oh, and we turned him into a dreadbeast just like his father!]

Sen rolled his eyes at Tutor.

Drussan smiled. “That boy never could hold his liquor. Found the brat stumbling around last night and got him a room at the inn. Didn’t want his bride to be to see him in such a state. She might change her mind about the wedding!” Drussan cracked a smile.

The smile on Lord Thessus’ face faded. “She wouldn’t dare.” He looked up at Sen and tilted his head. “Who’s this?” His false cheeriness returned. “I don’t think I’ve ever seen you travel without Drussan around. Don’t tell me you hired new security? A nine foot tall dreadbeast will scare off more troublemakers than a human with a sword, no offense.” Lord Thessus said.

Sen chuckled. “You say that because you haven’t seen how I use my blade.”

Lord Thessus nodded in approval.

“Yes, I thought hiring some extra security would be good for the wedding. Besides, we don’t want dreadbeast guards on our children’s big day, now do we?” Drussan asked. He truly was a natural at this. Sen wasn’t sure he’d be able to do the same. If it were up to him, Thessus would be a smear on the carpet by now.

Lord Thessus didn’t laugh at that. He simply nodded. “You’re right. Though I am excited to have beasts of my own. Don’t forget you promised me fine stock. But you’re right. I’ve hired some additional security as well. I can’t wait for you to meet them. They’re good, Malachi. They’re really good. With them watching over everything, we won’t have any problems. I can assure you of that.”

“That’s…great news.” Drussan eyed the fading rays of the sun through a window. “Well, there are a few things I must get in order. Please excuse us.”

Lord Thessus nodded. As Sen followed Malachi through the door to the basement, he felt the corrupt nobleman’s eyes on him.

Drussan had told Sen that the dreadbeasts had all been stored underground in the basement. But Sen hadn’t been fully prepared for what the basement actually was. An entire underground complex existed beneath the house. They passed dozens of locked doors and rooms, but some were open as well. Sen saw a room full of men and women hastily scratching fake runes on weapons and armor, people preparing ingredients from beast parts, and he even spotted what appeared to be some kind of drug production facility. This Thessus really was involved with some shady stuff. Of course, it wasn’t enough. Of course he wanted to add slavery to the mix. It didn’t take long for Drussan to lead him to the other dreadbeasts. They were in an underground warehouse. Some of them slept, or talked in whispered tones. Some paced nervously, and a few of them stood near the warehouse door, as if they were on guard duty. There were hundreds of them crammed into the underground warehouse. Hundreds of them. One man had gathered this many; how many others were out there, deprived of freedom?

The dreadbeasts froze when they saw Malachi and his swordsmen guard. But they glanced at the bracelet on Malachi’s arm and realized it was Drussan. The dreadbeast Khan activated the bracelet and returned to his natural form as soon as the doors behind them were shut.

“Khan! There’s news—” one of the dreadbeast approached. He was a wiry dreadbeast with a gazelle’s face and horns.

Drussan nodded. “I know. What have you learned about the new guards Thessus hired?”

The dreadbeast frowned. “They’re adventurers. Strong ones. They’re best known for their work hunting cults. Three are high Noble class, and two are high Lord class. It’s a problem. We’re outnumbered. With just four Khans among us to deal with them, plus the other guards already in Thessus and Malachi’s forces…”

Drussan put a hand on his shoulder. “Don’t forget, we have new allies now. My friend here is the equivalent of a Khan and more. We’ll handle them if necessary, but with any luck, we won’t need to. If we stick to the plan, everything should be fine. Just have faith.” Drussan’s voice had grown louder now, and Sen realized why.

This was no longer a small conversation. As they spoke, the rest of the room perked up to listen. Hundreds of dreadbeasts, all looking to the one they saw as a leader. Drussan looked around them and nodded. “Tonight, we taste freedom!”

Drussan introduced Sen to the other Khans, and they went over the plan one more time. As they finished, Sen felt a subtle pulse of mana. He raised a brow. “What is—”

The heads of the dreadbeasts perked up as one. Sen looked around and recognized what this was. A spell attuned to a particular group of people, a spell known as Mana Frequency. In his time, it had been used to signal the start of battles, ambushes, and wars.

But tonight, it was a signal to the dreadbeasts.

A sign to take back their freedom.

It was time.

Micah

The karmic spell had turned him into a dreadbeast. But he wasn’t the slobbering, convulsing mess his father was. His father needed help, but neither of them would get it in the backroom of some old, dusty store. They’d been tied up and left by Drussan, who somehow had stolen the form of his father and led him here into an ambush. That white-haired freak had taken one look at him and then cursed him to this horrid fate. If he didn’t get his human form back soon, the stench of dreadbeast might linger on him forever. He wrestled with the rope restraining him, and with strength he’d never known as a human, he freed himself.

“Yes!” He grinned. It was a jagged reminder that he wasn’t human. Fangs gritted together in his mouth. He leaned over his dad and ran huge hands over the old man. “It’s okay, I’ll be back soon! I’m going to get help!”

Micah kicked open the store’s front door and then hesitated for an instant. He had no idea how many others were in on Drussan’s plan. Sure, the other dreadbeasts were probably in on it, but what about his father’s guards? Who could he trust?

He ran a hand along the top of his head while he thought. He frowned when that hand ran into one of his horns. “Ugh! I just wanted to enjoy time with Orianne tonight!”

Wait…that was it. Orianne! Her father had guards of his own. Lord Thessus was the answer. If he could warn Lord Thessus what Drussan was up to, then—well, to be honest, Lord Thessus kind of scared him. He’d go to Orianne. He’d tell her, and then they’d go to her father. Her father had the resources to help them. His mages could turn him and his father back into humans, and then they could hunt down the traitors together. They’d make Drussan and his human friend pay.

He peeked through the door to the outside and looked around for any sign of people. It was getting late, so luckily he might make it to Orianne without being spotted. If he were careful.

“Just hold on, Orianne. I’m on the way!”

Slowly on the way.

He crept down the street on the tips of hooves. A ridiculous sight for a six foot tall dreadbeast.
