The goddess Celestia floated in the empty void above the world. Her presence cast a soft, golden glow across the fading currents of mana, once so common they would weave through every inch of Aerthis.

What had once been a world of boundless energy and magic was now struggling against decline. Its lush landscapes dulled, its great cities crumbling under the slow decay of time. The air, brimming with power in ages past, now carried only faint traces of what used to be.

She had watched it happen – centuries of slow withering, the mana growing thinner with each passing age. Cataclysms and the endless passage of time grinding even the greatest monuments down. The very balance that used to sustain Aerthis had begun to unravel, and no force in the world had yet risen to mend it. Even she, divine as she was, could not simply restore what had already been lost.

Something more was needed.

Her gaze turned to the great beyond, past the veil that separated life from oblivion.

In the endless Void where lost souls drifted, some lingered in quiet slumber, their essence too scattered to return. Others had long since faded into nothing.

And yet, in that vast emptiness between the stars, a drifting flicker caught her eye – a faint, fragile shimmer, barely holding form.

It was damaged, worn from its journey, yet not entirely lost. A remnant of something that once was.

Celestia reached out, her light wrapping gently around the fractured soul, to pull it from the Void’s unfeeling grasp.

It was fragmented, its essence dim and frayed at the edges, yet it clung to a spark of something – determination, perhaps, or the simple will to exist. She extended her hand, her warm voice resonating in the emptiness:

“You have wandered far and endured much on your journey, little one. Broken but not beyond repair. Would you allow me to mend you?”

The soul quivered, its light trembling faintly. Words did not form, but the essence of its answer was clear: Yes.

Celestia thus began weaving the strands of the soul back together. Her power flowed gently, filling the cracks and smoothing the jagged edges.

Even as she worked, she could see the limitations. Not all could be restored; pieces of memory and identity had scattered irretrievably into the Void.

When the work was done, the soul glowed brighter, steadier, though its light was still delicate. Celestia drew it closer, cradling it as she spoke.

“You are whole enough to begin anew, though the scars of what you endured still remain. I, Celestia, shall offer you a second chance, a life in a world that is in desperate need of your help.”

The soul pulsed faintly, hesitant.

“Know this: the world I speak of is fading. Its mana wanes, and with it, the vitality of all living things. I merely ask a single boon: whatever aid you can provide to nurture its magic, to help it thrive once more."

A pause. Then, slowly, the soul’s light brightened, its acceptance clear.

Celestia smiled, her expression tinged with both kindness and gravity.

“Very well. I shall grant you this new life. You may ask of me one thing, and if it is within my power, I will see it fulfilled.”

The soul pulsed again, a flicker of thought coalescing into a simple request:

Magic.

“Yes, you may have it. I will weave it into your very being, so it becomes a part of who you are. And...”

The goddess paused, her gaze once again staring into the resilient little soul before her.

“...A name, Elynara, freely given.”

The soul glimmered in gratitude, its light steady now.

“Go, then, and awaken to your new journey, may it bring you joy.”

As the soul vanished into the distance, Celestia lingered in the void, her thoughts heavy.

The seed had been sown, yet the task remained monumental. Will anything come out of it, in the hands of this renewed soul?

Not even she could tell.

Elynara stirred, the damp scent of earth and moss filling her senses.

The air was cool on her skin, carrying the faint rustle of leaves and the distant chirps of unseen creatures. Dappled light filtered through the trees, casting soft patterns across the forest floor, a few stray rays warming her face.

Pleasantly warm as it was, she still squinted at the invasive brightness.

Laying still for a moment longer to enjoy the warmth the sunshine offered, she perked her ears up, listening.

It was quiet here, and everything felt a little muted. She had a nagging feeling the faint chirping echoing around the place should have been louder. Slowly, she sat up, her fingers brushing over the soft, slightly damp ground.

The motion felt strange and a little stiff, like waking from a dream she couldn’t quite recall. She stretched, shuddering slightly at the stiffness leaving her limbs.

It also helped dispel the lingering cold, her mind finally catching up.

Memories of a long, long lonely journey drifted at the edge of her mind. Of how it ended with warmth, a new body, and a new name.

Elynara couldn’t help but laugh joyfully at that.

“I’m alive!”

Yet beyond that, everything felt fragmented, like she lost a little bit of everything, her old name, her old life, there were flashes, fleeting feelings, so much lost…

But so what? She was alive and well, even if her old memory was full of holes. It would be silly to worry over what was already gone.

She could just make more!

Still a little giddy – certainly too giddy to care about a little blinking dot in her vision – her curious eyes swept through the forest clearing.

All around her were trees and towering time-worn stones. Her little moss patch to the side, a vague outline of what looked to be a dirt road leading out, and an altar, at the very center.

It looked old, carved from pale stone, yet its surface remained smooth, clearly having been used and polished many many times over the centuries.

Something about it called to her, a quiet hum beneath the air…

Was it the mana Celestia spoke of? How exciting!

Elynara pushed herself to her feet, wobbling slightly before regaining her balance. The first steps were a little unsteady, but quickly gaining in confidence.

Now sure of footing, her hands patted over her body instinctively, taking in her form as though confirming she was whole.

Her scattered memories unhelpfully noted her figure was on the verge of growing into adulthood – perhaps 2-3 years away?

Slender arms, delicate fingers. Long strands of hair, catching faint glimmers of green as they shifted in the light. A simple dress adorned her adolescent body, earth-toned and soft against her skin. Secure and comfy, it reached just beyond her knees.

Elynara felt… small. Light. Yet something inside her was steady, like a thread connecting her to the world itself, such a weird feeling, but not unwelcome.

Her gaze returned to the altar, a quiet pull urging her forward.

She stepped closer, placing a hand against the cool stone. A gentle warmth pulsed beneath her fingertips, faint but present. The words came without thought.

“Thank you, Celestia.”

The stillness of the clearing absorbed her reverent voice, as if the world itself had acknowledged the prayer. A slight shudder ran down her spine, and the weight of her feelings seemed to lift off her lithe shoulders.

Focused again, she withdrew her hand from the sun-warmed stone, her eyes caught on something resting beside the altar.

A bundle of items lay neatly arranged – nothing elaborate, just simple adventuring gear. A sturdy brown cloak, a water flask, a small etched satchel. A belt with a sheathed knife, boots that looked well-worn but solid.

Celestia was quite a considerate goddess, wasn’t she?

Kneeling down, she started to look them over.

Practical, nothing fancy. The kind of supplies one would expect for a long journey, yet all just the right size for her! She couldn’t help but marvel at that.

Quickly equipping everything, she twirled in place, ensuring everything was well-attached.

Totally not because she was happy, goddess no!

She could feel how the fabric shifted against her skin, how the weight from the knife and the flask settled around her waist, the belt securing them well.

Satisfied, she picked up the satchel last, turning it over in her hands before pulling it open–

Space. More than should have fit inside, more than the size of the satchel would indicate.

She blinked, then looked inside again, reaching a hand in cautiously. The interior stretched deeper than the bag’s exterior, holding more than it should.

A bag of holding.

She must have looked quite silly with her entire arm put inside!

“Right. Basic adventuring gear,” she murmured.

“From the point of view of a goddess.”

A soft huff of amusement escaped Elynara as she secured the bag at her side. It made sense, in a way. Celestia hadn’t given her much, but what she had given was meant to last.

The weight of the gear was reassuring, it was tangible proof that she wasn’t just a drifting soul anymore. She was here. She had a body. And perhaps even a purpose.

That thought did weird things to her body, her emotions almost threatening to spill…

But thinking about that could come later, she still had a whole world to explore!

And a quiet hum she has been ignoring all this time was simply the first of the many things on her quickly growing list.

Quickly finding her new favorite mossy spot to sit on, she stilled, putting all of her growing excitement into focus.

Slow minutes ticked by, as Elynara tried her best to sense that mysterious force, to find where it hid, to extract its secrets. Secrets she hoped to learn sooner rather than later.

Until finally, it happened.

The air around her carried a faint presence, something unseen but undeniably there. The same force she recognized from the altar. It was subtle, like the whisper of a breeze against her skin.

Mana.

The realization sent a thrill through her. And as her smile grew at the wonder she was feeling… her focus was lost.

“Ah! And I was so close too!”

Pouting at the interruption, she took a few calming breaths, letting the cool earthy air fill her lungs, before trying again.

The feeling was much easier to grasp this time, only taking a few minutes of focus.

And then she sensed it.

It was thin and far weaker than she had expected, but still present.

Carefully, she reached for that sensation again, stretching her awareness outward.

It was like catching water with open fingers – it kept on escaping her grip. Slipping away faster the more she chased.

…Perhaps she was being too forceful in her attempts?

A little patience was a small price to pay for access to the wondrous realms of magic!

Calming down, Elynara tried again, slower this time.

Working mana required constant focus, and each failure meant another few minutes wasted. Long minutes turned into an hour, but with practice…

She could feel it, and she was making progress. Slowly getting familiar with its ebbs and flows.

Until she was ready for the push forward.

Furrowing her brows and concentrating harder, until finally she could feel a single strand of this elusive force.

…But what should she do with it now?

The answer was quite simple, as it turned out, and one her newly developing magical senses helpfully provided.

She had mana inside herself too!

Focusing on her own body, she quickly found what she was looking for. At the center of her body…

Or was it slightly to the side? Was it her heart? It was too fuzzy to tell.

But she found what she was looking for, a core of mana, much denser than what little strands she could feel out there.

“Ah.”

The joy of that revelation made her lose grasp on the mana strand.

Pouting at the failure, but undeterred, she began to draw it in again. It came even easier this time, but still far from smooth.

And after a few more minutes of effort, and a little bit of experimenting, she managed to draw it to her core.

She felt a little fuller now, but not in the hungry sense.

Determined, she continued her little exercise for a while longer, but as the fifth one entered her core, it suddenly became uncomfortable.

Unpleasant, bloated, like she had eaten too much, except not?

It probably wouldn’t be a good idea to try forcing more in? How about the opposite then? What could she do with her mana core?

Turned out, not a whole lot, and it took a good while longer to figure out how.

But once she got it, it was easier than moving the strands outside her body!

At least until she got it out.

Still easier, but nowhere near the ease she could move it around her body.

And taking it out made the bloated feeling go away too, so interesting!

Opening her eyes, she looked at the little fuzzy ball of her mana floating before her face. It wasn’t much, yet looked to be denser, more refined… and she clearly took out more than she intended.

Suddenly a flicker of movement caught her eye.

A faint green blur zipped past, darting toward the little ball she had gathered. Before she could react, it swooped in, snatching the mana away.

She jerked back, startled.

The blur vanished before she could make out what it was.

The sudden movement reminded her she had been sitting in one position for a little too long… but first she had to try and catch that little thief!

Frowning, she tried again, pulling out her mana carefully, a little less this time.

And just as it coalesced… –another swoop, another stolen ball of energy!

Her irritation flared.

“Hey! That’s mine!”

The culprit zipped around her, a flash of luminous green. It was small, fast, and completely unbothered by her protests.

Every time she focused long enough to make progress, it returned, darting through her magic.

What a playful thief!

She clenched her jaw, narrowing her focus. Fine. If it wanted to play this game, she would play.

Each time it swooped in, she adjusted, watching more closely, predicting its movements. The flickers of light, the way it moved. The way the air shifted just before it struck.

Something clicked.

The next time it lunged for her mana, she moved first.

Her hands snapped out, catching something warm and buzzing. The tiny creature wriggled in her grasp, a pulse of soft green light radiating from its form.

Then, without hesitation, it booped her nose!

She yelped, nearly dropping it.

The sprite trilled, then wriggled free just enough to nuzzle against her cheek, as if declaring victory.

“So that’s how it is, huh?”

The little spirit hummed in delight, settling against her shoulder. It didn’t seem inclined to leave now, clearly pleased with itself.

Sighing, she shook her head at the little one’s shenanigans.

“Fine. You can stay.”

It chirped happily, curling against her like a satisfied pet.

It was only then she noticed that something in her vision had shifted slightly. There were some very faint glimmers at the edge of her awareness that weren’t there before.

The world looked just a little different… but how exactly?

She couldn’t quite tell.

Where before she had sensed nothing, now there was a vague presence, a fuzzy feeling of tiny silhouettes hiding just beyond her vision.

It only became apparent when she focused, remaining unclear even then, but similar enough to the little green fuzzy ball now nuzzling her cheek.

More spirits, lingering at the edges of the clearing. Watching, but keeping their distance.

A new sense had awakened.

Elynara exhaled, allowing herself a moment to take it all in.

This world was much more mysterious than she initially assumed it would be.

But that’s what makes it so exciting!

“Do you have a name, little one?”

She prodded at the creature, but it only buzzed happily, making no indication it understood her.

“Right. You seem satisfied now, and your friends don’t look like they want to approach either.”

Stretching out her limbs, she noted how empty her core now felt… And she was actually hungry now too, and perhaps a little parched.

Celestia was considerate enough to prepare gear for her, but what about food?

Quickly taking a big sip from her flask, and rummaging through her new satchel, her trust in Celestia was rewarded!

What looked like dried bricks of some kind of mixed grains and stuff ended up in her hands; were those adventurer rations?

There was enough in there to last her a week!

Taking one out, she munched on it happily, taking a few small sips of water to gulp it all down. They weren’t great, but it was food!

Saving a little piece she tried feeding it to the little green spirit, but it didn’t seem interested in the slightest.

Shrugging, she ate the last piece, stretched, and decided to refill her mana core next.

The process was becoming slightly easier now, all her practice coming to fruition. She also had to refill less than she thought – had some of the mana regenerated during her break?

It wasn’t long before she felt full again.

Looking over the clearing again, she mouthed one more ‘thank you’ at the altar, before finally turning towards the path leading out.

It was a simple dirt trail, old but well-trodden. Yet to her it was an entrance into the magical world of unknowns and mysteries.

Just what was it that she would find beyond? Elynara could hardly wait to find out!

She cast one last glance at the altar before stepping forward, the green little sprite nuzzled into her cheek.

This would be her first step into this new world full of wonders.

And she couldn’t help but look forward to it.
