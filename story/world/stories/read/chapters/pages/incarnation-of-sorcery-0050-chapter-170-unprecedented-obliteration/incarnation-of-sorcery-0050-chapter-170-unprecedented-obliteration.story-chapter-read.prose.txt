Ryland wasted no time.

“[Dragongod’s Dimensional Claw]!” he shouted.

Of course, with Acrypha having both severed the entrapping link between Viren and Mavar while also clearing a path straight into the heart of the Living Mana apocalypse, Ryland didn’t need to waste him. Just before the gargantuan rift tore open in the star-studded blackness of the cosmos to unleash a draconic claw that could wipe out mountains, Ryland had already [Translocated] Viren away.

Then his spell—the manifestation of his fury—struck the would-be Great God, Mavar Val Arona.

The skeletal draconic claws impacted the golden, shining Demiurge and all the super-condensed Living Mana around him, crashing into him with frightful force and power. A blast of energy and light detonated outwards from the impact. Ryland had to make sure his barrier was intact so that he remained unaffected.

Sadly, Mavar was more or less unaffected too.

The Living Mana. It had protected him. While the opalescent strands of the mixture of matter and energy had been torn to shreds at Ryland’s violence, none of that force had struck his actual target.

Magic. Either a property of his Soul Glyph of Unity that allowed damage and momentum transfer between Glyph-carriers. Or, a direct spell that Ryland hadn’t noted.

Which was worrying.

Mavar straightened. He had a faint scowl, though it looked more amused than annoyed. “Really? Must you interrupt the process right when it reaches its zenith? I am about to claim godhood, Ryland. Give me a moment, I beseech you, and then I will give you all the attention you’ve been craving for centuries.”

A game. Mavar was treating this as some sort of game. One where his victory was so assured, he couldn’t be bothered to understand why anyone else was playing.

“Enough,” Ryland said. “Enough madness has occurred. Enough patience has been tested. Enough time has passed. It ends now.”

His Incarnation form blazed. His world turned into a series of Conceptual Glyphs, the language of magical reality superimposing over everything in existence. Including Mavar. Including…

Including the Incarnation he had become. He was in a transitional state of his morphosis.

“Sorry, what was that?” Mavar said. That density of Conceptual Glyphs around him… Ryland had only seen that density stronger in one source. A Great God. “I couldn’t hear you over the sound of my own glorious ascension.”

Mavar started ascending. Literally, this time, although the figurative one was still ongoing as far as Ryland could tell. But the point was that the Living Mana was reforming around him. Twisting into something else, spreading out farther somehow, forming links that stretched off into the far distance.

Ryland’s face set in a frown of determination. Right. He was drawing on all the Living Mana across practically all of the Accorded Realms. He briefly wondered if they all knew about this.

Then he channelled more magic than he recalled doing in centuries.

If Mavar was outshining the nearby sun, then Ryland had formed a mirror. Twin lights had blotted out the stars across the rest of the universe, leaving only them. The two most powerful possessors of inconceivable magic in all of the Accorded Realms. Two beings whose clash would resonate through both space and time.

“Ah,” Mavar said. He was finally serious. Much of the Living Mana around him was turning towards Ryland with deadly intent. “You intend to take this fight to the bitter end, I see.”

“I intend to stop you. Once and for all.”

“You couldn’t do it earlier. What in the world makes you think you’re capable of it now?”

In answer, Ryland simply let his magic speak for him. Mavar was right to be contemptuous. Ryland hadn’t dealt with him as eradicating a manner as he should have, which had led to this.

His ruthlessness had dulled. Going by the nature of how Acrypha had been taken away, she would have been kidnapped by the Flourinus Order regardless of their circumstance. And Ryland was beyond feeling useless selfish emotions like regret or anger.

But a surprising scene floated into his mind just then. Sint asking him if he had ever met his match. If he had ever needed to pull out all the stops.

Well, this situation certainly counted.

So, Ryland attacked by simply manipulating the Conceptual Glyphs around Mavar.

The Demiurge’s eyes widened for a brief moment, delightfully visible even this far out. Space itself closed in on him, losing the integral property that kept them separated into individual pockets of existence. Now, after Ryland had simply deleted the Glyph of Compartmentalization, every pocket of space around Mavar simply blurred together into one location.

Essentially, multiple realities tried to enforce their truth into the same physical location. This led to situations where parts of Mavar were simply crushed, while others were just torn away entirely.

Or would have been, had he not been capable of controlling reality just as deftly as Ryland was.

“How?” Ryland asked. In a blink, reality had reverted and the different pockets of space were more or less back in their original locations. “What vector did you just manipulate?”

“Must I reveal my secrets to an enemy who wishes for my demise?” Mavar asked with an almost teasing lilt.

Ryland didn’t rise to the bait. There was no point in doing so. Overpowered mage though Mavar had become, he was still trying underhanded methods of making his opponent lose his composure. Childish notions Ryland had no reason to even consider.

“It’s alright,” Ryland said. “I will simply beat it out of you, then.”

[Extinction Event] turned him into another irrepressible meteor as Mavar gathered his Living Mana around him like a protective shield. Conceptual Glyphs shifted between the two of them in a storm as they both attempted to rewrite reality at the same time, causing the world itself to warp and twist. Space cracked. Time shortened until they were both right up against each other.

Incarnation collided with Incarnation, and the outpouring of cataclysmic magic fell all the way to Rhel itself.

Ophelia hadn’t felt this antsy in so long, she couldn’t even recognize the feeling at first. Her legs were begging her to move, her whole body was twitching, her every instinct was screaming at her to stop whatever she was doing and immediately start getting the Calamity off Rhel.

Because this world was on the verge of collapsing, and if she didn’t depart soon, she’d end up getting caught in its apocalyptic end too.

The finality of Rhel was both literal and figurative. With how its civilization was collapsing thanks to the departure of its populace, the figurative end of Rhel had more or less been ensured the moment their vaunted Demiurge had admitted to manipulating all his followers via his Soul Glyph.

Ophelia’s feelings were far too complicated to articulate just what she thought about that. There was too much going on, too much she really shouldn’t ignore as a Mage Knight.

But her survival was the bigger priority. The literal collapse of Rhel had started the moment she had been contacted by Ryland of all people, who had actually placed a small request before her.

Small but significant.

She was to wait on Rhel, despite the departure of the rest of the Arcoryx contingent, just in case there was someone she needed to take with her. Someone who’d specifically need her assistance.

Someone like Viren.

The boy hadn’t even taken a second to recalibrate himself. He had appeared with a bunch of strange goo that had almost felt alive somehow, for a moment, before it had all fallen dead and inert. Viren had coughed and shaken, with a very confused Ophelia unsure what to do but still trying to calm him down.

Which he hadn’t needed because he had calmed down himself far faster than she herself would have done in the same situation.

“I need to get back!” Viren said. It was almost a shout. “I need to go help them!”

She didn’t understand quite where he intended to return nor what exactly he possibly expected to do there, but she had no problem understanding that it was something to do with Ryland.

After all, that terrifying mage had picked a fight with the Calamity-cursed Demiurge of all people, and now the two of them were on the verge of destroying Rhel itself utterly.

Ophelia placed a firm and unshakeable hand on Viren’s shoulder. “Look around you, Viren. Look up. What in all the Realms do you expect to do?”

“I was there! I was literally there, Lady Ophelia! If I could help free Acrypha before, then I’m sure I could help them again!”

Ophelia was even more confused and intrigued at the same time now. What exactly had happened to embroil Ryland in whatever the Demiurge had been intending to do? How had Acrypha herself gotten involved?

She recognized she wasn’t going to get answers easily. Not even with Viren in her grip. The boy was far too distracted by his own silly hubris to explain what was going on.

“Viren.” Ophelia squeezed his shoulder. He winced even though she had modified her strength a great deal to ensure she didn’t hurt a first-year academy student. Then again, that had almost seemed like a traumatic wince rather than a painful one. What in the blasted Realms had happened to him? “Do you trust Ryland?”

“I—” Whatever he had been about to impulsively say quieted down, probably out of his own internal conflict. “I understand why he sent me here, but—”

“Come with me. I’ll show you something.”

It was eerie walking through the institute with almost no one left within. Not only had everyone in all the foreign contingents departed, but so had the vast majority of the Rhellians too. High Academ Zevo was among the only ones left, and he was still at Ophelia’s destination.

Though, there was a new Rhellian with him. New, but not unknown. That white armour belonged to the Guardians, one Ophelia had seen at the institute during the little tournament.

Except, the armour was dented and broken and smudged with blood and dust. The golden-haired man in the armour sported signs of having massive injuries recently healed, like large patches of pale skin unblemished with any marks or even hair, the weird gait of one who was adjusting to a missing limb, weird twitches that reminded Ophelia of phantom pain.

In other words, he had just been in a fight where he had been grievously injured, if not almost killed.

And the only person capable of that sort of destructive violence was Ryland.

“You!” Viren shouted, drawing both Rhellians’ attention to him. Ophelia tensed up. Would Ryland mind if she knocked the boy out completely? It was for his own safety, after all. That way, he wouldn’t be able to antagonize two Rhellians who eclipsed Ophelia in magical power. “You Calamity-cursed bastard! What—?”

Ophelia clamped a hand over a struggling Viren’s mouth before he got both of them killed. While she believed neither Rhellian should bear them any true ill will, in truth, it hurt to act almost subservient towards people she could call enemies from certain perspectives.

But Ryland had charged her with Viren’s survival. He was the last person she wanted to let down.

“Excuse us,” she said. “He’s a bit too high-strung at the moment.”

Despite her words, the tension didn’t go down. If anything, it grew even more intense, the Guardian watching them both like a hawk that had spotted prey. She didn’t know what his prerogatives were. Maybe he was looking for vengeance after his altercation with Ryland.

A familiar clearing of a throat prevented anything untoward from occurring. “Ah, Mage Knight Ophelia!” Solverfel was swaggering in like he owned the entire institute, with an easy smile that belied their current circumstance. “How fortuitous I find a kindred soul in amongst this madness.”

How fortuitous for Ophelia that he had chosen to appear right now. She’d have to thank the elvhenan at some point.

“Greetings, Mage Knight Solverfel,” Ophelia said. Thanks to her mother’s training and years of experience, her voice never wavered despite the situation. “We meet again.”

With a tut, the Guardian spared Ophelia and Viren one last scathing look before turning back to the Glyphtech screen, which was what she had come to observe. “Keep the peace, Mage Knights. There is enough madness to be going on with.”

High Academ Zevo raised a hand to stop them all from talking. He hadn’t turned away from the screen for the entire duration of Ophelia’s entrance. For good reason.

The battle between Ryland and the Demiurge was taking a turn for the cataclysmic.

Even Viren’s struggles died down as he watched the screen. Like the rest of the audience, he was fascinated by the chaos that was transpiring. Magic of that level was… unfathomable.

Madness, the Guardian of all people had called it. Madness indeed.

Like some time before, the combatants turned into meteors. They were already glowing so brightly that the world of Rhel was awash with burning light that turned the sky a strange shade of blue and green, the sun turning into a weak source against the two mages.

Now, as they collided with inconceivable fury, Ophelia and the others didn’t even need a screen to see the explosion. They could all feel it where they stood.

The entire institute shook. They were all forced to shade their eyes as the High Academ cast a light-suppressing spell, for the meteoric collision between Marionys Ryland and Mavar Val Arona had made it seem as though the sun itself had descended to the atmosphere of Rhel.

Other conflagrations and collisions appeared and detonated at such a fast pace, Ophelia was lost in awe of the sorcery involved. How could two people cast spells so rapidly and so powerfully at that too?

Mountains went flying like rockets besieging ancient cities, borne aloft by great gouts of molten magma venting out of the earth as though Rhel was a pressure cooker. Storms writhed through the sky with nation-destroying potency, always ending with lightning strikes that seemed to leave a scar on reality itself. Winds shredded stone, rain flooded the world, and light turned into lasering pillars.

That was all mostly Mavar. It felt like Rhel itself was fighting on his behalf, tearing itself apart in order to aid its Demiurge.

Ryland’s counters were his rifts. Ophelia thought she had seen the extent of his insanity when he had fought against that newborn Calamity near Gildharbour. But oh, how wrong she had been.

A deluge of meteors rained out of the cosmos at Ryland’s bidding. That surely would have wiped out Rhel itself, but he had cast a spell that ensured they all targeted his adversary directly and specifically, tearing through many of the light pillars, storms, and other devastation he had pulled up.

And that was hardly the worst of it all.

One rift revealed demonic eyes that turned the whole atmosphere black and red. Another summoned up an undead dragon that took up the entire dome of the sky. A whirling cyclone that could have swallowed Rhel’s whole moon scoured across the planet’s surface at his bidding.

Ophelia swallowed. She had seen vestiges of some of those spells before, but it was only now that she was seeing the true extent of what Ryland could pull out.

Just how much had he been holding himself back, even against something like that Calamity?

“I’ve never even seen that,” Viren breathed out in gasping awe. He was so taken in by the battle between Ryland and Mavar that he had forgotten all about his anger. “What else has he been hiding all this while?”

His words had come about as another rift had called forth a giant red pearl, embedded in a moon-sized mountain of twisting steely rocks. An aurora of destruction blanketed entire quadrants of Rhel, vaporizing even the earth and the magma far beneath into smithereens.

Mavar was appearing and disappearing through much of that devastation. Now, he was attacking with lightning twirling around him as though wielded by a dancer. Claws of metallic ice and pure sun fire tore at Ryland, each the size of mountains, yet the blaze of incandescent mana around him ensured he remained protected.

The destruction was spreading. Ophelia didn’t know how much they were actively trying to control their own powers from destroying the whole planet, but the institute wouldn’t be safe for much longer.

Pillars of burning, molten light were striking down just a few dozen leagues away from Rhel. An ocean of glowing aquamarine energy from one of Ryland’s rifts had flooded the next city over. A smoking titan of molten earth and metal wrestled against a summoned glacial colossus wreathed with storms, and the earthquakes from their clash were slowly fracturing the institute structures.

“At this rate, we’ll need to move,” the Guardian said grimly. He glanced back contemptuously again. “If only for the weaklings.”

Ophelia scowled at the man. She was about to ignore her earlier impulses and fight back, but a strange urgency from Viren pulled her back into the thick of the battle between Ryland and Demiurge Mavar.

On the screen, something strange had come up. A twist in a reality, a different sort of scar, that had both somehow escaped both combatant’s attention.

Something—someone—emerged.

“Is that…?” Viren’s body turned cold where Ophelia had a hand on him. “Is that Acrypha?”
