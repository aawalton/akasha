Chapter 291

There was a single restaurant in the middle of Site 00, the first of hopefully many as the place slowly developed into a bustling city in the middle of nowhere. This restaurant was the only place where you could buy Fae food nowadays, despite the portal linking the surface directly with the Valley, because the increase in people working at Site 00 made serving Fae food to everyone unsustainable.

The food could be bought with cash, magic coins, or Origin System XP. The rates were not the same every day, changing not only because of supply and demand, but also because coins and XP were a new thing and their value when used to purchase things and to exchange them with one another was still prone to wild swings.

It was late night, but despite that the place was full to bursting. The goblin chef did not seem to mind, nor did the strange forest Fae that worked at the restaurant. The only one slightly grumpy was the single human server, who would rather join the party instead of having to work, but the pay was too good to pass on.

“Into fucking space!” a man cried out, holding his mug of Fae ale. He shook his head and clumsily clinked the thick glass of the mug against a few others held by other men, spilling some of the contents on the thick wooden table. “I can’t believe you guessed right, Jonas.”

Jonas laughed, downing his ale. He got to his feet and then climbed onto the bench, yelling to get everyone’s attention. “Next round’s on me, boys!”

The restaurant erupted into cheers and choirs as all the men lifted their drinks and food into the air. Jonas sat back down, and the few operators sitting close to him nodded at him and laughed.

“Thenks, chief,” one of them said. “I can’t believe you are wasting your hard earned XP on us.”

“It’s not a waste,” Jonas replied. “It’s my responsibility to make sure morale is high around here.”

“Jeez, man,” another said. “You even invited the nerds!”

Indeed, a corner of the restaurant had been colonized by the researchers, who did not mingle with the others and only partook in the festivities in small, controlled amounts. They were talking among each other animatedly, discussing nerdy things. The words that sometimes managed to reach Jonas’s ears through all the noise flew right above his head, their meaning utterly lost to him.

He shrugged. “They are part of the company too,” he said. “Besides, this stunt is only costing me a couple k, the rest I’m going to spend to power the fuck up.”

“Don’t leave us behind, chief!”

***

Later that night, the festivities wound down and only a few people remained behind. They were the men closest to Jonas, his old squad members and some others that had gathered around him as he rose in power within the company.

The music was lower, and the place felt cozy and warm, although the darkness of the rest of the large room seemed to crawl toward them when they did not look. They huddled together with what remained of the copious amounts of beer that had been brought out to celebrate, nodding as the last of the goblin staff left to return to the Valley. The human server also joined them, and they made room for him to sit.

“We can stay as long as we want, but only if you’re helping me close for the night.” he said.

“Eh,” Jonas said. “Say what, Mack, how much XP to have the System take care of this? I don’t know if you’ve heard, but I’m rich!”

Mack’s face suddenly lost some of its color. He leaned in, whispering as the light of the few lamps still on cast long orange shadows on his features. “Don’t say these things out loud, you might give them strange ideas.”

Jonas laughed, quite inebriated. “What are you talking about?”

“Yes,” Mack continued as his face became more and more ominous. “The Quests and the Origin Shop. Haven’t you seen the strange things that have been popping up recently?”

One of the men shook his head and burst into laughter. “Mackieeee,” he said, slurring. “C’mon, this is not a horror story.” He pushed a mug of beer toward the man’s face. “Here, Mr Nerves.”

“No, he’s right,” Jonas said. “I have been getting some weird Quests too.”

A few people hummed. Now that the big boss had said it, everyone was taking it more seriously. The cheers died down, many faces becoming pensive, the shadows dancing on their features. It felt like a night out in the woods, sharing horror stories with only the light of a fire and darkness all around, for indeed the restaurant was mostly dark save for the few warm lights they kept on, near them.

“The other day in the Shop,” one of the men began, adjusting his glasses. “I saw an item called Complete Optical Restoration. I could pay XP or coins, not cash, to access the Saint Hernest clinic and get my eyes fixed. It’s like they knew how much I hate these glasses.”

There was a round of hums and nods, then another person perked up. “The other day we were gathering some herbs or whatever in the woods behind Biolab 4.”

“A quest?” Jonas asked, interrupting him.

The man nodded. “The quest popped up in the morning, and since it was our free day, me and two buddies went out to scout. The guards at the perimeter let us out without flinching, I’m thinking the System just told them to.”

“What was it about?”

“It said: gather 25 Sunset Dew for the research division. There was more flavor text and explanations, about the anomalous properties of this plant and whatnot, but we didn’t care much. It was free magic coins and XP, why not?”

“So?” Jonas asked. “What’s weird about it?”

“Save for the fact that it turned my fucking life into an RPG game?” the man said with a dry laugh. “And that I liked every moment of it? Well, as you know, the Inventory is among the first things they made us unlock. The herbs went straight there, ‘cause I can’t be bothered to carry them around and using the inventory is cheap when there’s so much mana in the air. Then, when the quota was met, the quest was not yet marked as completed.” He leaned in, his face darkening as his voice became a whisper. “There was a button to turn in the materials automatically at a small price. Again, like the eye surgery, I could choose what to pay, be it my mana or coins or even XP. I did. Do you know what happened?”

“What?” Jonas asked, enchanted by the story.

“The items vanished from the inventory, that’s what happened.”

Jonas scoffed. “That’s it? You fucko, you wasted all this time to tell us nothing!”

Laughing, he offered the man a beer. “Drink this and shut up asshole.”

“No, you don’t get it,” the man said, voice lower and lower. “When it happened, I realized something I should have known all along. If they can take the quest items from my inventory, then they can take anything we put in there, at any time.”

Hums all around.

“We did not think about it because it was so convenient,” the man continued.

“Did any of you have anything weird happen with your inventory?” Jonas asked. As people shook their heads, he turned to the man. “See? Nothing to worry about.”

“Perhaps,” the man said. “I can’t deny how convenient it is to have the Inventory but… I can’t shake this feeling I have. That if you want something to be truly yours, you should not put it in there.”

Some people fell silent, while one or two laughed and declared that with how useful the inventory was, they didn’t give a rat’s ass if they caught Michael himself rummaging through their things.

“I don’t know… let’s not be fools,” Mack said. “Let’s not pretend he can’t just, I don't know, one day he decides he wants to take a stroll into Fort Knox or whatever. People will not be able to stop him. You were in a room with him, right Jonas? How was he?”

As they turned to him, they noticed that Jonas was no longer drinking nor participating in the conversation. He simply stared into the distance, lost in thought.

“Jonas?”

He snapped out of it, looking at his men with a complex expression on his face. It was dark enough to make them all worry. “Perhaps you don’t get it, guys,” he said. “Did you not read the fine print when you got the Origin System? Do you now know about Domains? If you have the System, then you are in one of their Domains.” He said the word like a whisper, although he knew there was no point. “Anywhere in the world, let alone here or in one of the Sites, where even the air is under their control. They can see, they can listen, and,” he paused, shuddering. “You said it yourself, the System sometimes acts weird. Of course it fucking does, it can read your thoughts.”

“Shit, man. Are you sure about it?”

“No, of course I’m not!” he snapped. “But what does it matter? We are here, talking shit about them, and we are still alive. This means that either I’m wrong, or they don’t give a shit.”

“Or they are letting us,” Mack suggested.

Jonas shrugged. “Would you rather give up the System?”

Every single one of the men present shook their heads.

“Are you sure?” Jonas pressured them. “Travis told me that it can be done. The only thing that would remain behind is a Geas that would prevent you from ever talking about any of this.”

“Do you believe him?”

“You weren’t there,” Jonas said. “You did not see the look in his eyes. If it were for him, he would rather kill anyone who defects. He can’t. Meaning that this order came from above. From either Michael or…”

He shivered as he recalled the faces of the only two beings in the world that could even dare to give Travis Tyrell an order.

“Who?” one of the men asked. “Michael and the other guy who always clings to him? Who is he anyway? His boyfriend? He shouldn’t be bringing random people to Site 00 like that. Just because he’s the owner–what!”

If looks could kill, then Jonas would be a murderer right now. He looked at the man for a long time, expecting something to happen, anything, a consequence of the words spoken. None came.

“You should thank whatever god you believe in that you are still alive, asshole,” he said.

The man recoiled at the sheer venom in Jonas’s voice, then the sudden realization that Michal could probably hear him made him freeze and drained his face of all blood. “Oh.”

“Yeah,” Jonas said. “In fact, you should do one better. Apologize and switch gods, because this god here is one who can smite you for real, and they chose not to.”

“They? You are… not talking about Michael anymore, are you?”

“The other person,” Jonas said, mouth feeling so dry that his tongue stuck to his teeth. He spoke carefully, as if fearing a presence behind his back even though there was none that he could feel. “They are not a he. They are Icarus.”

“Fuck,” the man whispered, and it was more of a whimper than anything else. “I just talked shit about them and their relationship with Michael, didn’t I?”

“And you are still alive, aren’t you?” Jonas said, fake cheerful, a bit of moisture returning to his dry mouth. “Had it been me there, with that sort of power, you probably wouldn’t be. Not after talking shit about me and the people close to me. I suggest you keep your head down, do your job, and watch your tongue from now on.”

“Of course, chief.”

It was at that moment that a message appeared in everybody’s vision.

“Is it me or–”

“Not another word,” Jonas growled. “Not. Another. Word.”
