Chapter V.XX (5.20) - Visiting Sojan

Kizu jerked awake and nearly tumbled out of his hammock. Only his monster leg gripping the hammock’s netting kept him from falling on his face.

Mort scurried across the floor to check up on him, his big, golden eyes scanning Kizu for any sign of harm.

“I’m fine,” Kizu said as he untangled himself. “Just had a dream. A weird one. I remember it perfectly too. In fact… it was identical to those soul dreams that Zayne puts me through whenever I drink a soul unifying potion.”

He hoped to prompt the sliver of soul that rested inside him into talking. Alas, he heard nothing from his stowaway.

That left the question open as to whether or not this had been a flash from Zayne’s past or just a strange dream prompted by the weird pickled broccoli Ione had convinced him to eat yesterday. It was a toss-up, but Kizu was leaning towards the former option.

Standing up, Kizu stretched. A glance at his scrying orb told him that he was awake an hour before dawn. But he had enough packed in his schedule today that he figured he might as well stay up.

He’d fallen asleep last night while in the middle of reading about one of the many invasions of Tross by the Hon Empire. This particular campaign was started because of an assassination of a particularly horrible sounding prince. The man had a long record of all sorts of heinous crimes. A list that contained, but was not limited to, torture, cannibalism, murder, human experimentation, and animal abuse. Regardless of the man’s horrible ethical decisions, Hon had taken the assassination of one of their royals as a personal slight and retaliated in force.

Kizu debated picking up the book and resuming where he’d left off, but his heart wasn’t in it. He knew the ending of that story. Maybe Hon would seize and hold some land for a few years, but his knowledge of the modern borders spoiled the end of that story. Another senseless war with no real gain.

Instead, Kizu got dressed and stepped through the portal that led to Taroe’s home. Mort, knowing his intentions and not wanting to be left behind, leaped onto Kizu’s back. Careful not to wake the grumpy ex-Elite, Kizu snuck through the house and out the back door.

The sun not yet risen and it was quiet as he strolled through the town’s dark streets. He only saw one person also out this early, an elderly fisherman whose body was hunched over as he hobbled down in the direction of the docks. Kizu nodded a greeting as they passed one another. The old man ignored him. A bit rude, but maybe he hadn’t seen Kizu.

A few minutes later, Kizu stood in front of an old, decrypt shed. It was in even worse condition than when first encountered the sad looking building, back when Roba had asked him to help with a task to recover a mythical hebijikan for Professor Knoff. Now the entire left side of the shed had collapsed inward and a purple mold could be seen growing from within the splintered wood. Unlike the major buildings that were actively under construction after the attack, this shed had gone forgotten by the vast majority of the town population. The weird glowing mold also likely wasn’t an incentive that workers were keen to work with.

He pushed open the door and examined the ancient stone arch within. A mere attack of Dragons wasn’t likely to destroy this ancient monolith. It remained the same as ever, though it did now have a pile of collapsed wood pressing down on its left side. Kizu pressed a hand against the cold stone and activated the ancient wards carved into it. A portal distorted the space within the arch, swirling and twisting. A single step through it sent him a matching arch on the other side of the island.

Funnily enough, despite the drastic difference in their size and population, the tiny hamlet over here had more people out and about than the town. Fishermen called out greetings and wished him a good morning as he passed though. A stark contrast to the one old man he met back in town.

Mort hopped off his shoulder and darted around the hamlet. Kizu mentally sent a warning to his familiar, reminding him to watch out for sharp things like sea urchins and discarded enchanted fishing hooks.

“Kaga Kizu,” Jiwan called out to him. The old man slowly approached, cane bearing the brunt of his weight to keep it off his brittle bones.

“How’d you know it was me?” Kizu asked with a smile. “You finally let a healer take a look at your eyes?”

Jiwan scoffed and shook his head. “Never let one of those healers touch me before, not going to let one now. Made it this long, haven’t I? It’s a matter of pride.”

“That doesn’t seem like a productive thought process.”

“Bah. Enough on me. Off to the forest again?”

“Yeah. The headmaster, um, hid something for me to find. Sort of like a…scavenger hunt.”

Kizu had been through here only one time since Roba granted him permission but Jiwan, despite his elderly age, was sharp and had likely deduced something more than a challenge was going on nearby. Kizu just hoped that nothing and no one had made it too obvious.

“A hidden treasure? A fun little distraction for youngsters like yourself. You know when you were leaving last time that headmaster of yours asked me to report to him whenever I saw you? Well be careful out there. Dangerous monsters roam about. Might be hard to believe, but those goblins you found last semester had pals. Well, perhaps they were more like acquaintances. Nobody knows what goes on in those evil creatures’ heads. Bet peaking in would drive a mind mage mad. Madder than any mage studying minds already is.”

Quickly saying his farewells before the old man trapped him with rambling words, Kizu departed the hamlet and entered the forest.

This wasn’t like the gradual, peaceful forest on Shinzou Academy’s side of the island. This forest was dense and the wilderness struck immediately.

He felt Mort dart across the hamlet with the intention to reconvene with him, though the monkey got distracted almost immediately and veered off to the side.

Kizu had to keep alert and constantly flash his spellsense to avoid the dangers that lived over here. Even still, a carnivorous plant got close enough to take off his arm. Only swift reflexes saved his flesh as he lit the threatened arm on fire with a spell. After that, Kizu slowed down his trailblazing.

It took an hour or so of trekking through the wilderness before Kizu finally reached their destination. Pushing aside thick foliage, Kizu took in the sight, illuminated by the rising sun.

He stood on the rim of a massive crater in the ground. The natural indent could probably fit Shinzou Academy five times over. At the very bottom of the dip, there was a large green pond.

Even from afar, Kizu saw it ripple and the trees trembled. Something detected his presence.

“WHO DARES DISTURB ME!” a booming voice demanded. The noise echoed and bounced across the basin. The words vaguely reminded Kizu of the Genie from his recent dream, though they lacked the sheer power carried in that creature’s words.

Kizu cupped his hands and shouted back.

“Sojan! Get over here!”

There was a pause, then a creature of darkness burst from below. The velocity and strength of its wing beats knocked Kizu back a step. Birds squawked and took flight. When the creature slammed into the forest ground next to him, trees toppled over, uprooted by its mass pressing against them.

A black, reptilian monster the size of a small house stood beside him, its red eyes locked on Kizu. It opened its mouth, revealing a row of wicked teeth and a glint of something metallic sealed behind them.

“Kizu!” a familiar voice called out. “Great to see you stop by. It’s been dreadfully dull alone out here. Not as dull as being asleep for centuries, mind you, but at least when I’m asleep the time passes faster. Though that’s not here nor there, it’s nice to have someone to talk to. That headmaster of yours stops by every couple of days, but he always seems to be in such a hurry. And the Awakened sparrow I encountered fled before I could get a word in.”

“You met an Awakened sparrow?” Kizu asked. “Are there many Awakened beasts in the forest? I met a snake half a year ago.”

“‘Met’ is a strong word for our interaction.” A cloud of black smoke left his snout as he huffed. “I haven’t come across any other Awakened critters though. What do you think their blood tastes like? I imagine it a bit tangy. Magical creatures often make me vibrate a bit when I drink their blood. You think it might taste similar to a harpy?”

“Questions like that are probably exactly why that sparrow ran away.” Kizu decided to make himself comfortable. He jumped over to one of the trees Sojan had knocked over and took a seat. “How’s the training been? You flew up to me this time.  That’s progress.”

“I’ve controlled creatures with wings in the past, so flight isn’t some unique concept to me. Though I admit Dragon’s flight depends a great deal more on innate magic. Their bones are more solid than most flying creatures. Have you ever seen the okatori over on the tall islands? They’re quite not as sentient as you and definitely not as much as me. But they’re smart and taste decent. Flick them with a finger and their bones break though. Like hollow twigs.”

“You know, you and an old man I just spoke to are remarkably similar in your ramblings. You just talk faster.”

“That makes sense, I’m far older than probably the oldest human you’ve ever met. Empires crumble long before I do.”

“How much of your existence actually had you conscious though?” Kizu asked. “You mentioned before a few decades where you were kept awake. That was the longest, right? Even if you accumulated everything, I bet I’ve met people older than you.”

“Simantics!” Sojan roared. Birds, who’d only just dared to resettle in the nearby trees, fled once again.

Kizu held back a chuckle. “Fine. You’re ancient. How about that new body of yours though? Is the Dragon also centuries old?”

“Half a century at most. Very juvenile. Which is excellent because it offers me a great deal more dominion over it. I can still feel the echoes of a soul rattling around, which helps with controlling the innate magic, however if it was an older creature with more experience, even those echoes might fight me for control.”

“But as you are right now, there’s no risk of that?” Kizu asked. If there was any chance of a Dragon’s soul reemerging from within that body, he doubted the headmaster would risk allowing Sojan to puppet it. But still, better to check just to be certain.

“Nope! Not a hazard at all. And the quantity of the blood in here is massive. Not to mention the quality, mmm.” Sojan shuddered in pleasure, shaking the nearby forest and raining loose leaves down on them. “It’s very nice.”

“Do you have control over anything beyond flight yet? The headmaster mentioned giving you a list of exercises.”

“Flight, fire breathing, and motor control,” Sojan listed off the exercises with a bit of annoyance in his tone. “And I’ve mastered none of them. I can clear kilometer stretches for flight so far, but nothing  more than that.”

Sojan raised the Dragon’s head skyward and let out a pathetic ‘burlulurp!’ noise, followed by a large cloud of black smoke. Then he shook his snout back and forth before continuing. “That’s what I have for fire breathing. Not quite the same as the majestic flames you fought.”

Kizu frowned. “But when you controlled other bodies you instantly knew how to use magic.”

“Not quite. I knew how to perform human magic. Even when I took control of the Bloodlord briefly, I used the human magic he had stored in his blood. Not his own innate powers like his glamour. And human magic still probably took me a while to get used to. It was just so long ago that I don’t remember learning. Like you learning how to walk or chew mangos. I drink blood and cast human magic.”

“I guess that makes sense. And motor controls?”

“Fine. No spectacular gymnastics just yet. But I can avoid crushing things I don’t want crushed. Most of the time.”

To demonstrate, Sojan rolled over, smushing several more smaller trees until he came to rest against a massive tree trunk the size of a castle tower. He then wrapped his body around the wide trunk and dug his claws into its bark. From there, Kizu watched as the Dragon spiraled his way up the tree as he climbed.

“Impressive,” Kizu commented.

“Not especially,” Sojan said modestly. He peaked down from behind the tree. “My speed is slow and my strength lackluster. I haven’t learned how to use this body to its full potential. If I was in your human body, I could make it climb here in a fraction of the time it just took me in this strange, new body. Of course, when I control a human body I have far more access to forcing it to respond to my commands than you do. Without my assistance, you could never naturally match my speed.”

Kizu’s monster leg flexed. He had the sudden urge to meet that challenge and race up the tree. The tree called to him, begging him to scamper up it. He only barely managed to beat back the instinct. Then he glared over in Mort’s direction, knowing exactly where that instinct originated.

The monkey, who’d arrived in the middle his conversation with Sojan, smiled and dashed up a different tree.

Sojan fell back to the forest floor, flattening the nearby area. And then they resumed chatting. The dagger went into detail about how he’d put up preventative measures to keep his blade embedded in the roof of the Dragon’s mouth, sealing it in place with a build up of more and more flesh until only the last third of the hilt remained poking out. The dagger also spoke of less pressing, mundane activities he busied himself with everyday. Halfway through a description of one of the exercises assigned to him, Kizu realized the sun had crested the horizon and they were now well into the morning.

“Before I go,” Kizu said, “I wanted to ask about your past. You mentioned earlier that you’re ancient. Do you remember anything about Shades or Genies?”

“Shades?” The Dragon cocked his head. “As in the creatures? Someone’s mentioned them to me in the past. Can’t recall who. But I know they’re supposed to be very hard to stab. Like stabbing a shadow. I’d like to try sometime though! As for Genies… the word tickles my memory, but I’ve got nothing.”

Kizu shrugged. It had been a long shot but he would have been foolish not to at least try asking about them. “One last thing. I don’t suppose… you offer rides?” He felt a bit embarrassed forming the request into actual words. “I mean, like, Dragon riders are mythical. Flying through the sky at breakneck speeds and together delivering destruction on their foes.” His face burned red as he put into words scenes from story books. Still, another subject he had to ask about. The old myths and legendary stories had been bouncing around in his head ever since Sojan embedded himself in the Dragon’s mouth.

Sojan twisted around to look at his back. “You might fall off. Do you have a saddle? Or something to make you sticky?”

“Ah, not on me.”

“Well if you find something like that then maybe. Though I don’t see much of a point. You can just jump anywhere you want to go. You could even just jump up to the clouds and create a barrier platform to overlook a landscape. I feel like my wings would just block your view. And don’t you have that enchanted broomstick from your mentor?”

“I… do,” Kizu admitted sheepishly. “Well… if I find a saddle, I’ll let you know. Bye!”

He turned abruptly on his heel and jumped to the academy beacon.
