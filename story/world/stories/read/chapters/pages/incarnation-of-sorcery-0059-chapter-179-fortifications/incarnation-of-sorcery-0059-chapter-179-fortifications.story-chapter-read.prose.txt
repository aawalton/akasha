Ryland didn’t feel particularly elated when the Grand Frame decided his efforts were worthy of a reward. He had already achieved what he was seeking. Still, he wouldn’t look a gift horse in the mouth.

[ Achievement Recognized: Divine Wrangler

You’ve assaulted and escaped from a Great God, despite being only Empyrean-ranked.

Rank up from Empyrean [4] to Empyrean [29].

Reward: 5,000 Glyph Prints. ]

That was… a lot of Glyph Prints. Hmm. He’d need to find a use for those.

For now, he was feeling something he hadn’t in a long while. Exhaustion and weariness. Taking on a Great God had taken more out of him than he had been fully prepared for. He had thought that he would exhaust himself to a powerful degree, but he hadn’t been prepared for the altercation to need that much effort from him.

Was he underestimating the Great Gods themselves? After he had already encountered other Great Gods?

Hmm. Maybe he’d need to deliberate more on how exactly he proceeded to deal with these situations. Even after he had planned the entire meeting with Jahaevo—well, not necessarily him, whichever Great God decided to intercept him after he started the incident—it had taken too much effort and energy. Even for him. More deliberation was necessary.

After he dealt with an Archdragon of course.

Vorkorax’s maw had taken the place of the artificial moon that Mavar had used before as a conduit for conducting all the Living Mana of the cosmos. Right now, the Planetary Archdragon’s eyes were brimming with tears, unleashing a rain fall that clouded over Rhel before falling with a cataclysmic downpour.

Thankfully, Ryland had reconstructed the barriers around Viren before coming up to meet with Vorkorax.

“You tricked me!” the enormous dragon said. Its voice overcame the thunder of the clouds far below Ryland.

“Did I?” Ryland asked. He recalled well how the dragon had a lot of fun at his expense. “You did say you’d lend your assistance.”

“Which I am doing right this instant!”

That was true enough. Ryland needed a particular, powerful ingredient as one of his main components for the ritual Inscription he had created on Rhel. This ingredient was the tears of the Planetary Archdragon, liquid that was imbued with lifegiving and time-barricading properties. It would form a powerful base for the spell’s main effect.

“Apologies,” Ryland said. “I admit I needed a little bit of assistance against a Great God. Besides, you didn’t appear to mind the altercation in the moment.”

“Gah!” the Archdragon said. “Do you know how much stress you put my poor dragonkin through? They have been praying for days now at the sky turning into pure gold and the shakes that have wrought my whole body.”

Ryland was relieved to hear that the civilization upon the Archdragon’s back hadn’t been harmed. “I appreciate the assistance, Vorkorax. Especially after you took the time to blurt out my past and had such a wonderful time laughing about it. Although, I should hope you would return to your quadrant of the galaxy before your sun’s trajectory once again leaves its ideal direction.”

The enormous dragon considered Ryland for a long while. It was always difficult to tell if Vorkorax was considering trying to eat Ryland and destroy Rhel, or if that was just the Archdragon’s general appearance.

Then Vorkorax stopped crying and huffed out a smoky breath that Ryland would annoyingly need to rift away since he didn’t want it to cloud the entirety of Rhel’s atmosphere.

“I admit,” Vorkorax said. “That I haven’t faced that sort of excitement in centuries. And that you were always there to take away that annoying golden prick’s attention.”

Indeed, Ryland had. He hadn’t really wished any harm upon Vorkorax, and certainly none upon the civilization on the Archdragon’s back. The fact that he was so enormous meant that the Great God simply hadn’t found any time to retaliate properly. Not with Ryland occupying most of his attention.

“May Fate favour your ritual, Ryland,” the Archdragon said as it turned to depart, its form starting to glow as little rents joined together all across its body. “You won’t die, but make sure no one else has to die for you.”

That was as much a parting dig at Ryland as it was sobering advice. Nevertheless, Ryland maintained his polite equanimity.

“Fate hasn’t favoured me for a while,” he said. “But I’ll keep it in mind. Thank you again, Vorkorax.”

The rents closed upon his body and the Planetary Archdragon finally disappeared.

“Fortification, sir?” Viren asked.

Ryland nodded as he led his student to one of the main corners of the Inscription. Here, there were no Glyphs encrusted into the ground. Just a wide, open space where much of the rainwater had gathered. “Yes, we must fortify the Inscription. A Great God trying to trap us in the Overrealm isn’t the only problem we’ll have to deal with.”

Viren’s expression turned a little grim. Ryland didn’t want to scare him, but the reality was that their troubles were only just starting. There would be more interruptions. More attempts to stop what he was trying to accomplish.

To safeguard against that, Ryland would need to layer in powerful Inscriptions on top of the main one. There was no end to his efforts.

“So you’ve got two of the main ingredients, sir,” Viren said. “And getting one of them was why we didn’t just run away from the Great God.”

That was quite perceptive of him. Honestly, Ryland was surprised Viren had been able to make out anything within that chaotic battle against Jahaevo. His eyes and senses were sharper than Ryland had expected. An enhancement thanks to Vorkorax’s blessing? Maybe…

Ryland partially pulled out a glimmering strand of light from within a rift that led to his personal inventory pocket Realm. It writhed and twisted in his grip like a worm, fraying into multiple smaller strands that tried to break off the main one and escape. A grating noise, like a blade sharpening on a grindstone, tore from the strands. It was just Ryland’s magic keeping it intact.

“I will need to bury this one in the other site,” Ryland explained. “And then fortify it as well. Then, there will only be two more left. However, fortifying is a rather time-consuming endeavour, so you’ll need to get used to a bit of boredom, Viren.”

The boy shook his head adamantly. “I could never be bored with you, sir. Actually… I kind of like the fact that we’re just dealing with simple magic for now, not moving suns and fighting gods.” He frowned. “Simple magic.”

Ryland chuckled. “There is a certain beautiful simplicity to it, so you’re not wrong.”

He began casting the fortifying magic. Solid mana started erupting out of his manasoul, filled with power and potential. He had intended to rest for a while longer, but after meeting Vorkorax and Jahaevo, he was prioritizing the timely completion of the ritual Inscription over anything else.

So long as he avoided magical burnout himself, of course. That was where the enhancements from all the Relics and resources he had gathered over the centuries came in handy.

“[Eternal Bastion],” Ryland incanted. His Soul Glyph Aegis was the first to form a spell circle, before his Soul Glyphs of Genesis and Energy came into play. “[Living Fortress]. [Ambient Soul].”

Viren gasped in awe as Ryland’s spells took effect. The combined effect essentially created a complicated magical structure, a construct that was alive in a sense. Basically, Ryland intended to create a barrier that would protect the local Inscription area from a wide variety of threats.

But if he stood here and created multiple different barriers for all the various angles of attack and magical vectors his enemies might use, he’d be here forever. Instead, he was creating a spell that could learn and adapt to counter a wide assortment of circumstances.

Still, he did need to set the spell on its path. By creating a link to the ambient mana, Ryland had granted it a semblance of life with one main function—learning and growing.

So now, he needed to help it start learning and growing. By casting a wide variety of elements and spells, all of which were at a lesser level that the innate barriers of the spell could push back with ease, he ensured the spell got a taste of what it would face.

The process was somewhat similar to the inoculation process developed on Realm 38, where healers purposefully afflicted people with diseases and poisons to develop the body’s natural immunity.

“This is incredible, sir,” Viren said when Ryland finished explaining all that was going on with his fortification method. “Not that I understood even half of what’s going on—which isn’t your fault, sir.”

“Well, I wouldn’t expect you to understand all the intricacies right from the beginning,” Ryland said. “But I think you underestimate your own intuition, Viren. If given the right tools, I think you’ll be able to recreate it all. In fact, that will be your upcoming project when you’re not busy with anything else.”

Viren’s eyes widened briefly. “Me, sir? Cast this magic?”

“I hope you didn’t think you could escape your education just because you’re not at the academy.” Ryland smiled warmly. “Don’t worry, and don’t feel overwhelmed. You don’t have to do it in the same timeframe I did. But I have faith that you will learn in due time, and when I come to check it, I’m certain I’ll find a more than adequate Inscription.”

Despite Ryland’s encouragement, Viren looked a little bit daunted by the prospect of recreating the same fortifications that Ryland was creating.

But it was fine. Ryland had proof that Viren could rise above adversity and fear. He had already done so when Ryland had given him the task to create a Tier [7] Glyph despite not even being Silver-ranked yet. This time, Viren would do even better. With careful but firm guidance, Ryland would make sure of it.

The cage was cramped. Emotionally cramped.

It had taken a long while for Acrypha to come to grips with her new situation. She wasn’t shackled inside some sort of jail or cage. She wasn’t forced into a torture device or made to participate in any horrific experiment. As far as she was aware, she hadn’t been hurt at all since being swallowed up by what she had learned was a Wound Corridor.

That said, she was stuck. Whatever cavernous space she had been flung into had no windows or doors, no entryways or exits she could make out.

Unless one had access to portals or rifts or anything of the sort.

Sadly, Acrypha wasn’t Ryland, so she had to spend all her time inside the enormous white chamber with no way out, and no way to contact anyone or anything outside either.

In all honesty, it would have been fine. One of the things her Restorer parents had trained her on was what happened to those who were captured. The training was secret because most would have baulked at the things a child like her was knowingly being put through. Long hours without food or company, strict surveillance, deprivation of necessities many took for granted…

All sorts of torturous experiences that were on the verge of being outright abusive. Hmm, maybe it was abusive from certain perspectives, though she supposed there was a rather terrible logic to it.

Her parents had never physically harmed her, aside from their sparring sessions, but they certainly hadn’t been easy on her.

At the moment, she thanked them for it. Boredom was child’s play, and being stuck in one location almost felt like home. She almost wanted to meet whoever had captured her, because seeing the looks on their faces when they saw she was perfectly fine would have been tremendously satisfying.

The only thing was that she wasn’t really alone.

Somewhere farther off, one of the Pure Manifestations that was supposed to be her father was furiously chomping on Living Mana. His giant figure was hunched over a crack in the white floor, from which spewed Living Mana that the man continued endlessly devouring with ravenous delight.

The cannibalistic nature of it bothered Acrypha, but the long time she had spent in his presence had made her inured to it. She barely heard him physically ripping into the Living Mana anymore.

And why was there a supply of Living Mana in this place?

Far on the other side, a strange ghost was swimming upright against the walls. A ghost that was more like a wash of colours, one that was so wispy yet determined that she was reminded of her mother.

The two of them had appeared that day, after she had severed the connection between Demiurge Mavar Val Arona and all the Living Mana of the universe. And ever since that day, when she had discovered that the vestiges of her parents didn’t even really see her, she had been fighting off a terrible depression trying to suffocate her.

Acrypha hadn’t been able to communicate with them yet. They didn’t understand her words. She couldn’t control them with [Afterlife Guardians]. Acrypha had no idea what to do.

Until today, that was.

Today, in this moment when an idea had finally popped into her head, she figured she was ready. It was difficult to accept that it had taken this long, but then again, she had no idea how long “this long” was. Plus, what she was about to do was… wasn’t quite right. Her feelings about it were complicated.

But right this moment, there was no other choice.

Acrypha went over to the crack in the wall. She had tried investigating it before. There was nothing to glean, nothing to indicate where the Living Mana was coming from or what it was doing.

However, Acrypha knew one thing she could do. She went right up to it, then launched herself straight upwards with a powerful jump. By the time she reached the peak of her climb, she had already rotated her body mid-air so that her skull was pointing to the floor.

Then she began falling, her head aimed right for the crack. Right to that craggy incision in the floor that would split her skull in half since she had no magical barriers active, unless…

Unless the two strange manifestations finally moved, noticing her and the fact that she was in danger.

Both the giant figure and the wispy watercolour one appeared right on the point where she’d have smashed her head against the floor. Ah, so the gamble was paying off. She was right. The guardians, malformed by Living Mana and existing in a state she didn’t fully understand, would still recognize when she was in danger.

That was the only thing that would break them out of their stupor to act.

To recognize their daughter.

Acrypha fell into warm arms that kept her safe. They felt strange. Firm but not fully solid, buzzing with a strange energy.

“Do you recognize me?” she asked, not wasting this precious moment when their entire attention was focused on her and her alone. Her voice was scratchy and her throat terribly dry after not speaking for so long, but she forced the words out anyway. “Do you know who I am?”

They didn’t answer. They never talked. Acrypha wasn’t expecting to hear a tearful answer that yes, they knew they were holding their precious daughter.

She saw it in their eyes. In that moment, they did know her.

Or at least, they knew they had successfully executed the spell that had programmed them for a very specific function, which they had now completed.

Apparently, that was a signal of some sort because the chamber started shaking. A voice emerged from the walls and the ceiling. Acrypha looked around but couldn’t find the source, nor could she find any visible difference in her surroundings.

“Well done, Acrypha Lowen,” the female voice said. “We’ve been waiting for you to awaken our long-lost friends.”

“Who are you?” Acrypha asked. She pulled herself free from the guardians, her hands shaking as she realized her captors had finally come. “Show yourselves, cowards! What in the blasted Realms are you talking about?”

“My, my, what feistiness. What I’m saying is that just as you have recovered your parents, so too have we recovered our comrades.”

“What?”

“Don’t you see? These are our dear friends, the great mages of the Flourinus Order, Gracious and Chivalrous!”

Acrypha looked over at the guardians, at the twisted representations of her parents’ spirits, and her face paled.
