We spent the few hours before dawn huddled in a chilly bedroom, door barred and weapons bare. Royce and Teela whispered furtive questions and hisses of frantic disbelief while Renner snapped at them to be quiet.

I spent the time curled against a shadowed wall, shrouded in my black cloak and carefully cleaning my wrist. Slip’s claws had left five stinging punctures, but the bleeding stopped quickly. I pulled my gloves on and the pressure of warm leather stifled any remnants of pain.

Dawn crept far too slowly as I peered out the window. Dusky gray gave way to a horizon tinged pink, and as soon as the first glimmer of sunlight appeared Renner unbarred the door.

The common room lay barren and still, an expanse of jumbled furniture and shadowed wood. Our fire was nothing but a mound of ash. The pile of glass, blood, and water around the poor woman’s head glittered with mocking brightness in dawn’s light. Rank, sour smells drifted through the frosty air.

Royce was the first to speak. “Should we bury her?” His voice sounded very small in the dismal space.

“No.” Renner was already halfway to the door. “We need to go.”

“We could… burn her,” I offered meekly, nausea swelling at the thought. But it would be kinder than just leaving her, and quicker than taking the time to dig a grave.

“Leave her.”

“Renner-”

“No.” He stopped at the entrance, spinning to fix me with a hard stare. “Other people might stop here. They need to know there’s danger.”

“He has a point,” Teela murmured, wincing even as she said it. The Fae-touched woman trailed to the door, eyes downcast. “Ashes, imagine someone sleeping here without any idea there was a… a… dead woman walking around!”

“Breathless,” the man beside her muttered, tilting his scowl towards the head.

She squinted at him. “What?”

“That’s what they’re… I’ll tell you on the road. Just leave her. Let her be a warning.”

“You don’t think the broken window and the blood everywhere will clue people in?” Royce’s back was ramrod straight. “Salt, imagine if that was your mother or sister, and a bunch of strangers just left her on the floor like… like…” he went white, trailing off and turning away.

Saints, I wish he was back home. I wish he hadn’t seen all that. And he’s right. Whoever she was, she deserves better than just… just rotting away in a pile of glass.

I drew near, tangling one hand with his. Searching for strength in his warmth, and hoping to offer some in return. “We can at least say a few words, or find something to cover the... It’s not right to just leave her like this.”

Renner frowned at us. Then, in an ugly voice, “You’re right.” He stalked past me, straight to the bloated head. Glowered at it before stooping down, one boot pressed against her temples as a hand closed over the silver bolt.

I turned away as a wet rip filled the otherwise silent room. Teela hurried out the door, tail flicking behind her, while Royce watched the grisly scene in obvious horror.

Angry steps thumped to the window, crushing shards of glass and making the floorboards groan. Cloth rustled and shuffled, followed by another wet tear, then a dull thump. A dead woman hitting hard ground.

Renner swept past me, heading to the front door with two bloody bolts in hand. He adjusted his cloak and walked through without looking back.

My shoulders sagged as I watched him go. What did I expect? It’s not as though he’s… he promised to get me to Pinnacle safely. He never promised to be nice about it.

Royce shifted beside me. His face was mournful. Then he gave a little huff, ran a hand through his hair, and walked back to the bedroom.

He spent some time rummaging, then emerged with two quilts in hand. He lay one cloth gently over the swollen face, flinching back afterwards as though he half-expected her to start moving again. Then he crept to the window and repeated the process. “Who do you think she was?” His voice was raw.

I sniffled, moving to stand beside him. “I… I think she probably owned this place. Or worked here. She was wearing an apron.”

“We should let people in the city know. In case she has family there.”

We stood quietly for a moment, leaning into each other. Then, every word slow and careful, he murmured, “This really isn’t what I… expected.”

“Yeah.” A dry huff of laughter fought its way up my throat. “Me neither.”

“I’ll bet. Sounds like you’ve seen worse, too.”

I stared at the bundle of cloth and the grotesque lump beneath. Saw other, far more terrible things. Couldn’t seem to form an answer.

Royce’s hand tightened around mine. “You know, Brin, I’ve had this… I don’t know. This idea, I guess, for the past few days. Like a picture in my head.” He looked around, seeming almost lost, then gave a harsh little laugh. The sound held an empty ring, and the room fell cold and silent when it faded. “It’s dumb. I kept thinking I’d… I don’t even know. Ride up and find you by the side of the road, or something. Sweep you up and carry you back to Fellbrook, and we’d… and everything would be alright. Back like it was.”

A painful knot formed in my chest. I lowered my lashes, blinking back the sudden spring of tears. “That… sounds like it would’ve been a lovely picture. Not dumb at all.” Broad shoulders shook with another hollow chuckle. The knot tightened, and so did my grip. “It’s… it’s not too late, you know.”

“For?”

“If you want to… go back. T-take Barley and… Royce, I have to keep going, but you don’t.”

He went very still. Then, giving my hand a little squeeze, said, “Yeah, I do.”

A soft creak near the door made me turn. Teela stood watching us, eyes glittering dull red in dawn’s hazy light. Purple shadows framed her angular features and darkened the doorway. “You guys ready?”

Royce nodded, moving to leave. I stayed behind for a moment, hands knotting at my cloak and gaze downcast, before following.

Our bleak morning turned into a bleak afternoon. The frosty air seemed laced with weight. The road twisted and turned, a seemingly endless view of brittle grass and cold dirt framed by shivering firs and a pale blue sky. Rather than sit for breakfast, we each nibbled quietly on rations while Renner set an unforgiving pace. Everyone looked and sounded miserable, save for Barley, who was in his normal good spirits.

After hours of this, Teela’s steps began to drag. Her eyes grew glassy and lidded. Royce glanced towards her every now and then, brow growing increasingly furrowed, until without comment he stopped Barley, shouldered some of the horse’s burden, and practically scooped the startled young woman onto the saddle.

Her cheeks were pink for some time afterwards.

No one spoke until we stopped for a very late lunch amidst dark, hissing pine boughs and a soft carpet of moss. Barley was tethered loosely to a nearby tree, so he could graze, and the four of us slumped onto the ground to slowly parcel out satchels of food.

Teela stifled a yawn. “So. Breathless. What’s that?”

Renner made an ugly face. “What it sounds like. Some monster kills a person, and a while later their body gets back up. No breath, no brain, slow as salt, and hungry. Easy to take apart, even easier to kill.” He snorted. “Probably the least dangerous monster I know of, actually.”

Least dangerous. I shuddered, picturing the lapping, swollen tongue pressed against moonlit glass. “But… not everyone killed by a Fae… salt, ‘gets back up,’ like you said. What causes it? Er… makes them?”

“Beats me. They’re not common, and it’s usually not much of a threat when one shows up. They were a problem up north a few years ago. Over near Lighthold.”

“Oh?” My ears pricked up at that. Lighthold was, according to Brother Clem, a city built around the study and research of runes. A place where the blessed could gather to show and share their powers, to hone and train their growing magic. “Did runekeepers help drive them away?”

“No. I’ve told you,” he cast an unreadable glance my way, “Your kind are in short supply. More so than usual.” His voice darkened. “More so after all that mess.”

I had just pulled a bundle of cured, linen-wrapped meat from my pack. It fell to the ground. “What?”

His face was grim. “It’s not a nice story. Word traveled fast, and the Order caught wind Lighthold was under attack. A bunch of us got sent out east, had a miserable little campaign. Weeks trekking through the cold to get there, and a damn bloodbath when we did. Two dozen pacted holed up north of the city, hiding out in caves, dragging people out and… well. I’ll spare you the details, but the phrase ‘ritual sacrifice’ ought to paint a good picture. Lighthold wasn’t exactly swimming in runekeepers at the time, and most of them got cut up just like everyone else. Lots of bodies came back breathless, kept wandering into the city at night.”

Teela shuddered. “Sounds awful.”

“Wasn’t pretty. Something big was roaming the woods, too, but I wasn’t a part of that fight. Probably a good thing. Whatever it was left a few paladins strung up in the trees and made off.”

“What happened to all the pacted?” I asked, though I felt sure I wouldn’t like the answer.

“They died.” His tone held a sharp edge that invited no further questions.

Red hair and horns glinted as Teela shifted forwards, passing out seed cakes and giving our taciturn companion a shrewd look. “So what was your part in all that? I remember what you said a few days ago, about how you’d answer questions once you were convinced Brin wasn’t a criminal.”

“I am a criminal.” I winced up at the canopy.

“Yeah, she’s real dangerous. An absolute menace.” She made a show of rolling her eyes skyward and fluttering both hands as soft laughter filled the frosty air. “But you know what I mean. Come on, spill.”

Renner hesitated. Copper eyes traced the ground thoughtfully, giving me the impression that rather than refuse an answer altogether he was… deliberating, perhaps, on how to answer.

He was certainly tight-lipped when I asked about his time in the Order before. Granted, he wanted to kill me when I asked, so perhaps that had something to do with it.

Royce leaned forwards, looking genuinely curious and, for perhaps the first time, quite enthused towards the other man. “Are you a Thalessian?” His eyes shone even brighter than the blue sky above.

One corner of my mouth curled in a private smile. Royce had dreamt of joining the Order when we were little. I’d lost track of the times he’d grabbed a fallen maple branch and a chunk of bark and declared himself a brave paladin off to protect me and his mother from some terrible evil.

Teela snorted. “No way! He’s one white cloak and a sack of chivalry too short for that!”

I was startled into giggling. Then, mortified, I clapped both hands over my mouth to smother the sound.

Fortunately, Renner laughed even louder. “Hah! White’s not really my color.”

My gaze flicked over, noting how the pale sunlight highlighted each shade of warm auburn and honeyed brown in his hair. “I doubt that.”

There were several long breaths before I realized I’d spoken the thought aloud.

Renner’s laughter died down and he gave me a narrow-eyed look, head cocked to one side, and my face grew warm. I looked down, studiously avoiding his gaze.

“Er…” Royce cleared his throat. “You made it sound like you were part of the Order.”

The other man shook his head. “Not anymore. I spent years with them, but I was never blessed.”

“Let me guess.” Teela’s tail twitched, and a catlike curve curled her lips. “They kicked you out?”

“Something like that.”

“Did they?” I regretted the question almost immediately. “I mean… sorry. You don’t have to-”

“I left. My choice.” His tone was curt, with an almost tangible weight to it. After a moment’s pause he added, more lightly and with a wry look aimed at me, “Told you; those white cloaks really don’t suit me.”

“But you… well, you trained with them. You went on missions, saw combat against monsters, that sort of thing? And that’s how you know so much about Fae?”

“Yeah.”

My chest swelled with a deep breath. “Do you… know anything about goblins?”

One brow quirked upwards. “Goblins?”

“Y-yes. Have you ever heard of them?”

Renner rubbed his jaw. “No. I’ve heard the word, but that’s it. Why?”

“Just… well… I met one. Last night. Her name was Slip, and she was… er, very interesting.” I forged on, describing the bizarre little Fae. “Truth be told, she wasn’t… I suppose under different circumstances she might eat me or… I don’t know, wear my skin or something. But… I actually liked her.”

Teela nibbled on a piece of dried fruit. “I guess there’s worse things than dreaming about some scatterbrained little monster, yeah?”

I let out a long, heavy breath, rolling my oatcake between my fingers. “You have no idea.”

Renner’s brows lowered. “And what else happened?”

I hesitated. Round, shining eyes blinked from a dark corner of my mind. Everyone’s finally getting along, smiling, enjoying themselves a bit. No one needs to hear that an evil owl thinks my heart’s going to be… eaten, or ripped out, or used in some kind of terrible sacrifice.

He was probably just trying to frighten me, anyway.

I drew my cloak tight. “Nothing worth mentioning.”

“You sure?”

My shoulders stiffened at that all-too-familiar tone. Too casual, too offhand, too certain that I was lying. “Yes.”

Uneasy silence crawled between the four of us. For several long breaths the only sounds were rustling pine branches, cloth shifting against moss, and crunching food.

Royce finally shattered the quiet. He held an oatcake out towards me and said solemnly, “Bacon, please.”

I jumped. Then, with a startled little smile, I imagined the flavor of crisp, savory meat and traced two fingers in my simplest rune. “Ulen.”

“Thanks.” He crunched a bite, sighing contentedly. “Salt, Brin, I missed that.” He held out his waterskin next. “Mm… fresh apple cider?”

I obliged, twisting the magic to settle on my own drink, as well.

Teela blinked, looking back and forth between us. “Huh. Did I fall asleep?” She scrubbed her face. “This is a weird dream.”

My friend turned to her, looking puzzled, then regarded me with obvious shock. “You haven’t shown them?”

She swallowed a mouthful of her own black, crunchy seedcake. “Shown us what?”

“Brin!” Royce looked aghast.

“It’s nothing,” I mumbled, feeling suddenly quite shy. Then I took a sip of my waterskin, tasted the tart tang of summer apples mixed with warm cinnamon and sweet, sticky honey, and let a grin blossom across my face.

“It’s everything!” my friend declared, beaming at the confused young woman beside him. “What’s your favorite food, Teela?”

“Favorite food?” Her nose scrunched in thought. “Salt, I don’t… mm. Y’know, there’s a bakery in Respite that makes these delicious quince tarts during Harvesttide. My mom and I used to stop by for breakfast and buy a little basket of them, and we’d go have a picnic in…” her cheeks grew pink as she paused, lips open and eyes fixed on the little cake. A shadow crossed over her features for one breath. Then she cleared her throat. “Sorry. Quince tarts, that’s my answer.”

“Brin?” Royce cocked an eyebrow.

“It… the texture won’t be right, but… here. Ulen.”

At Royce’s urging, our red-haired friend took a tentative bite of her meal. Ruby eyes rounded, jaw falling open, while a happy baritone rang out in delight.

“Hold on,” she parceled out after taking a larger bite, “Have you been able to do this the whole time? Whenever we sat down to eat bland oats and old nuts?”

I nodded, cheeks burning.

“Saints, Brin! You should… I don’t know, open a bakery or something! You’d be famous! It works on drinks, too?”

Royce, still chuckling, offered her his waterskin.

Teela took a tentative drink, then shot to her feet. “Brin!” She thrust her own waterskin out. “Hot chocolate, please!” As soon as the magic was cast, she took a sip and pirouetted, chirping a delighted giggle. “It’s even hot!”

Royce’s blue eyes sparkled. “It’s amazing, right?”

My gaze cut towards Renner, who’d been watching the display with an unreadable expression. “Any requests?”

One brow rose and, with a slow smirk and a syrupy drawl, “Oh, sure. I’ll take spoiled milk and… what in the realms was it? Lemons?”

I hadn’t thought it possible for my face to grow hotter, but apparently it was. “Ah… it was pickles.” I bit down on a grin. “Old ones.”

His eyes narrowed. Dark, but glinting a bit beneath the dappled canopy. “Uh-huh. Pickles and milk.”

“Do you… um,” I gave a little cough, laughter threading every word, “Do you not like those things?”

“In my ale? No, actually.”

“Oh. I hadn’t realized.”

“Real cute, ladyship.”

The heat spread down my neck. “I thought so.”

A pang of silence fell on our little clearing, and a quick glance up showed Royce watching me with the corners of his mouth pressed together. A moment later he cleared his throat and gave me a weak grin. “You, uh… yeah. You gotta watch out for that. One moment you think you’re safe, think you’re about to bite into a nice juicy steak, and next thing you know she’s got you eating grass.”

I spread both hands wide as my blush deepened. “Okay, but that time was an accident. I was trying for onions.”

“I didn’t want onions.” He made a face. “Who puts onions on steak?”

“Durst does, and it’s not bad.” I thought for a moment, then amended sheepishly, “Er, sometimes.”

He laughed. The sound echoed off the trees, quickly followed by more merry chatter. When we packed our bags and rose to continue on, the air didn’t seem quite so heavy.
