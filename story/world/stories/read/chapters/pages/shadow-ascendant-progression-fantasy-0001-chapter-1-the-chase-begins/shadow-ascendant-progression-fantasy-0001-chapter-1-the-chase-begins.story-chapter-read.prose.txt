To survive in a dungeon, one must follow its rules; a basic saying half the adventurers overlook.

And hey, let me make one thing clear. By rule I am not referring to the basics, ‘do not steal another party’s kill’ or ‘share everything with your adventuring buddy.’ Nope, I’m talking about the actual rules—like, don’t get cocky, don’t overestimate yourself, and for the love of the gods, don’t drag an angry Minotaur up to the third floor!

Anyway, back to the topic. I, Arnos, was exploring… or, more precisely, wandering to snipe the resident monster, Numbat, for a quarter credit each.

A quarter credit. Not even one. But numbats were in heat, which meant they were everywhere. And me? I was the genius who wasted four whole days reading about Anastasia the Traveller instead of working. Now I was out here, desperate, chasing pocket change just to scrape up a hundred and fifty credits before next weekend.

But I hadn’t lost yet. Being the ‘talented’ mage that I am, I killed twenty numbats crawling through the forest. Okay fine, they can’t detect movement in sunlight. Still counts.

I looked up. The sun, or the illusory ball of light, still blazed late in the day. The air hung still. Noon. Numbats were returning to their nests. Worse, their sight was returning, too. My boots crunched on dry leaves. Then, the forest gave way. A wide clearing opened, and in its heart, a thicket of Ereceus bushes stood, the smell of their leaves pungent and sharp.

“Haha,” I chuckled under my breath, for reasons I couldn’t explain.

My eyes darted around, searching for a bush thick enough to hide me, its needles not too unforgiving. I found one... though it didn’t have soft leaves!

Still, I shoved myself in. The Ereceus closed around me, its stiff green needles stabbing into places that had no business being stabbed.

Then the scent hit, sharp and herbal, like basil crushed under boots, only a hundred times stronger. I coughed into my sleeve, blinking back tears. Uncomfortable, yes. But Numbats hated it more than I did, and that made the pain worth it.

[Kruakk Kruakk]

Something yelled.

While it sounded like a dying pig’s last words, it was more than that. I turned my gaze to the sky. A single, lonely, and probably pregnant numbat flew over my head.

I quickly slipped my right hand outside the bush, it acting as a wand for my bush-body, a terrible wand, that is.

“Fireball,” I whispered, imagining a small version of the usual fireball spell. A spark ignited at my fingertip, immediately swelling into a ball of condensed flame.

I could feel its heat, its intensity equivalent to the normal one. “Perfect,” I muttered, adjusting my finger slightly lower to account for the Numbat’s trajectory.

My finger twitched, and the fireball burst forth. It tore through the air with a sharp [whoosh] before slamming into the Numbat.

It wasn’t a direct hit but a wing hit, the right one. A loud shriek left the monster's throat as it fell to the ground. The dungeon being rather damp, the sound of its body hitting was minimal.

“Whoa, lethal hit!” I shouted, springing up from my hiding spot while yanking an Ereceus needle out of a place it had absolutely no right to be.

I brushed leaves and dust from my clothes as I stepped into the open, crunching dry foliage under my boots.

My eyes drifted towards the fallen numbat. I couldn’t exactly see the corpse because of the thick grass, but judging by its flight path, I believed its nest might be on the other side of the entrance.

Flicking my left wrist, I glanced at the Celoris Band I wore—a white strap with a blue crystal at its center, an artifact that let adventurers track the monsters they’d slain.

A small blue rectangular hologram flickered into existence before my eyes.

[Name: Arnos]

[Class: Magician]

[Credits: 67.5]

Note: Displayed credits reflect this month’s accumulation. To view lifetime totals, shake the band again while focusing on it.

“It still hasn’t died, huh,” I murmured, lowering my hand back to the side, just as a message echoed inside my head.

[You have gained .5 credit for slaying two Numbats]

[Your total credits are now - 68/150]

“It was pregnant!” I yelled far too excitedly for someone who had just killed a poor, pregnant, venomous monster that could kill a human with a bite if they didn’t have anti-venom.

I had anti-venom, though. It was in the back pocket of my pants, with a prickly leaf still digging in my back. Unreachable, of course, I wasn’t stretchy enough to yank it.

Immediately after yelling about the monster’s pregnancy, I glanced around, hoping no one had heard me, not like I was in the wrong, though. It’s just that people have a weird way of thinking. If someone had heard me yelling about a monster’s pregnancy, well, let’s just say they would think I am a weirdo… which I am not! I mean, I have no interest in monsters unless it’s a succubus or something.

They don’t exist, though.

“Ahh,” I sighed, slightly saddened by my own thoughts. I began walking towards the fallen corpse, hoping to find its communal nest and cause a huge massacre to get those damned credits!

As a first-year at Cellera, I couldn’t sell the monsters I killed. The school gave us credits instead. These credits were what I required to continue staying in Cryz dorm and not fall off to lower ones.

Also, because of a technicality issue, I was the only student in Cryz dorm who got a room of his own. It was smaller and, well, previously a storage closet of sorts, but it had something I didn’t want to lose. Right, privacy! I mean, having your own room is rather nice. You don’t have to accommodate someone else, and besides, it was an unfamiliar experience for me, an experience I didn’t want to lose.

Besides that, we students could trade credits for things like weapons, potions, and books. And they were way cheaper compared to the market price.

We could also eat the monsters we killed, but people didn’t consider numbats a delicacy. In fact, they were the meal you’d serve a lover who had fallen out of love.

Yup, it was venomous as well as poisonous—a perfect creature, to be honest.

It wasn’t half-assed like most of the snakes were. No, no, it was better.

Reaching close to the corpse, I absentmindedly crouched beside it, next to a tree, not sure what its name was, since dungeon vegetation wasn’t really something teachers at Celera had taught us yet.

I stared at the corpse, spotting the hole in its right wing. It was about as large as my clenched fist, meaning the fireball I cast hadn’t dispersed. Which is really important, I mean, who in their right mind would want a fireball that disperses on contact?

I stood back up from the ground, pressing the soft grass with my boot, and walked again, this time straight to the entrance of the third floor, in the direction the monster was flying to.

After roughly twenty minutes of strenuous walking, I finally arrived at the entrance. Just as I had guessed, there were a lot more Numbats flying above my head, meaning? There was a possibility of a nest being situated nearby.

Which meant I could easily score a lot of credits if I played it safe.

“Buying the anti-venom was certainly the right choice,” I murmured, probably grinning like an idiot.

Brushing aside a low branch from my face. I stepped out into the open field, my gaze settling on the gate to the second floor.

It had the look of a cave, its mouth carved from rough, black stone. Within it lay the staircase leading up to the second floor, built from the same material, and lighted with torches.

Above the cave rose a pillar. Stretching from the top of the entrance to the illusory sky, tearing a clean hole through its centre.

“I walked onto the stone pathway leading to the entrance when a loud crashing sound caught my attention. I cocked my head ninety degrees to look for the reason, but I saw nothing.”

“Fuck! Ahhhh!” someone screamed. A man certainly said the “fuck” part, though the yelling confused me a bit. It sounded like a woman’s voice, or the voice a lot of men make when running away from something dangerous.

The sound of thumping massive feet and falling trees got louder until I finally saw the monster they were running away from, a MINOTAUR.

A red-eyed, brown-bodied, clearly bloodied, horn-bearing Minotaur. His eyes were glowing with crimson… a sign of something akin to an adrenaline rush seen in beasts.

My eyes widened. I spun around.

“Quick step,” I muttered, letting a burst of flame surge beneath my feet, propelling me toward the cave entrance. The ground cracked under the force. I’d probably overused my mana, but I didn’t care. One leap later, I was safely inside the gate.

I climbed a stair and then noticed the worst possible thing… Yellow slimes, a type of decomposer of the dungeon that are also extremely slippery, filled the stairs.

“Fuck!” I yelled, turning around, running back onto the third floor. Then, using the Quick Step again, I moved away from the entrance.

As I moved out, I got a brief glance at the faces of the male and female adventurers. There was a smile, perhaps of relief.

That they would survive.

“No!” I yelled as they moved in. My eyes widened as the nine-foot Minotaur rammed its horn into the entrance in the next moment.

The whole pillar cracked, and the Minotaur moved back, pushing the mud with its hooved feet. It rammed into it once again, and the pillar splintered. The Minotaur moved back again, then rammed in. Again. Again. Again. And again until the whole construct came falling down.

The dust from the broken pillar covered everything around. I narrowed my eyes, leaving barely an opening to look at the scene as I continued to move back slowly.

I should’ve run… anyone in this situation should’ve—but I wanted to see what happened to them… the adventurers I saw moving in. Did they survive? Maybe they jumped over the slime. I mean, the people wearing knight armor are strong, right? Physically, way stronger than me.

“They would’ve survived. They surely would…” I murmured, unknowingly praying to the god I never believed in.

I stumbled backward as the dust that had clouded the air settled. My eyes stung as I squinted towards the entrance… an entrance that was now nothing but a pile of rubble.

The Minotaur still stood before it, massive and unmoving, its glowing red eyes glaring at the collapsed passage.

It lowered its head, sniffing the air as if it were trying to find its prey. Then…

“Grahhhhhhh!” it yelled.

The roar slammed into me like a shockwave. I could feel the vibration of the monster’s roar under my boots, and so could the numbats that now flew over my head as if they had seen a natural disaster.

A natural disaster? Right, a Minotaur was no less than that.

Then I saw it. A dark, crimson pool of blood emerged from beneath the feet of the beast.

They didn’t make it. The adventurers, who had been running away, had died. Horrified, I concluded. My eyes widened, skin crawling, as the scent of blood filled the air. The Minotaur stepped back from the rubble, its eyes still burning red… something was off.

Wait! How did I not see that before? Its arm, the left arm of the Minotaur, was severed.

I needed to escape. I turned around, my eyes now looking straight ahead at the forest side of the dungeon.

“Quick Step,” I said, letting the fire loose beneath my feet, propelling myself forward into the vines that I had previously emerged from.

The vines closed in fast. The thorns pierced my skin as I ran through, yet I could not stop.

I moved deeper into the forest, stepping on the wooden, overgrown roots. Using Quick Step here might cause a forest fire, but not using it would certainly increase the chances of being killed.

Quick Step, I thought, propelling myself with fire… once, twice, thrice. I kept going, kept running. Each spell brought more pain, pain I didn’t allow myself to feel, myself, not now.

My breath grew heavy. No sound of the Minotaur followed me. Maybe I was safe?

I broke free of the forest at last, stumbling into the open stretch of the Ereceus Field.

I stopped for a second, trying to catch my breath… panicked, I messed up my spells, wasting more mana than I should’ve used.

And that’s when it happened.

The sound of trees breaking caught my attention. I glanced back briefly. All the trees behind me shook. The leaves fluttered. Something big was coming, and I knew what it was.

“No, no, no! I should be far enough. Is there no one else who could become his prey?” I said, biting my lips.

“Quick step!” I murmured. Knowing full well that I was the monster’s next target.
