There were a lot more torches on the streets by the time Viktor returned to the slums.

It wasn’t just the Iskora patrols anymore. The scarred man’s scream had done exactly what screams always did: it dragged people out of their homes. Thin, weary figures clustered near alleyways and broken walls, whispering among themselves while craning their necks toward the source of the commotion. Some people peered down from cracked windows above the street, while others lingered in doorways even as the guards barked at them to get back inside.

Viktor paid them no heed.

He drifted high above in complete silence, passing over the scattered armed men and curious onlookers alike, his gaze fixed on the barely visible outline of the Sanctuary.

Once he neared, he descended toward a strip of shadow between two crumbling houses where the torchlight failed to reach. The veil of darkness wrapped around him for a moment longer before dissolving away like mist as he deactivated the Thauma. Adjusting his clothes, he stepped out from the shade and made his way to the building as though he had merely returned from an ordinary walk.

Rhea was standing at the entrance when he approached. The moment her eyes settled upon him, the tension on her face eased almost immediately.

“Where did you go? ” she asked as she hurried over. “I was about to go look for you...”

“I heard a scream,” Viktor replied.

Yes, he certainly did. Louder and clearer than anyone else. In fact, his ears were still ringing right now.

Rhea nodded, turning her head toward the distant noises. “I wonder what happened...” she murmured.

“I’m not sure either.” Viktor shrugged. “There were a lot of people there. I couldn’t get close enough to see anything, so I came back instead.”

“That’s probably for the best,” Rhea said. “I forgot to tell you, but they’ve imposed a curfew on the west side during the night. If the guards catch you wandering around outside, they will detain you.”

“I know.”

Yes, that was another matter he had become thoroughly acquainted with during the course of the evening.

“Anyway,” Rhea said with a tired smile. “It’s really late now. You should come inside and get some rest.”

“Sure. Let’s go then.”

Together they passed through the Sanctuary’s heavy wooden doors, leaving behind the cold night air and the distant sound echoing through the streets. As Viktor crossed the threshold, his eyes briefly lifted upward, taking in the small statue perched above the entrance.

A robed woman with calm and delicate features, her expression so serene it seemed untouched by fear or anger or any earthly burden known to the common man. Her head was slightly inclined forward, casting her merciful gaze upon any soul who sought refuge within these walls.

The Matriarch of the Emerald Order, of course.

Viktor had seen her likeness many times before throughout the continent, especially in the large and wealthy cities where the influence of the Order reached its greatest heights. There, the Sanctuaries rose like fortresses, all marble halls and soaring towers, built grand enough to shame even palaces of some lesser monarchs. And in those places, the depictions of their revered Mother were no less magnificent, enormous sculptures carved with impossible detail, elevated high above like a goddess descending from the heavens themselves.

Compared to such monumental works, this statue was painfully modest. Its surface was rough in places, the folds of the robe lacked refinement, and parts of the stone had already begun to weather from age. Yet, despite all that, it still carried that same quiet dignity that belonged to the holy woman who had founded the Order thousands of years ago.

Viktor regarded the figure for a second before continuing onward.

The Sanctuary’s interior opened before him in layers of dim light and stillness. High arches loomed overhead, supported by rows of worn stone pillars. At first glance, it resembled the main hall of a temple, possessing the same solemn spaciousness. But this was no place of worship. What dominated the air here was not the scent of incense offered to the divine, but the unmistakable smell of medicine.

Shelves lined the walls from end to end, crowded with folded bandages, ceramic jars, stitched linens, glass bottles, bundles of dried plants hanging upside down from the beams overhead. Bitter mixtures of crushed leaves, boiled roots, strong alcohol, medicinal oils, and countless other remedies lingered throughout the building, seeping so deeply into its very stone that the scent had become an inseparable part of it.

Several people lay asleep in the chamber. Hardly a surprise, considering that the Sanctuary did not turn away those in need, regardless of whether they came to seek healing or merely a roof for the night. The ones who had nowhere else to go inevitably found their way here after dark, and Tobias had never refused them shelter.

Against a pillar, a gaunt man sat upright with his eyes closed, while a little girl lay curled against his shoulder under a thin blanket. Near a far corner, an elderly woman shifted restlessly as someone’s persistent cough broke the silence at irregular intervals, each burst growing more ragged than the last.

Viktor let his gaze drift across the hall until he spotted Alycia lying near a shelf with her back turned toward him, her pigtails impossible to miss even in such poor lighting, spilling across the mattress like strands of gold that contrasted sharply against the building’s muted colors. It looked like she and Rhea were going to spend the night here, instead of going back to their house. Were they staying to watch over Claire, or were they unable to go out due to the curfew? Maybe both, he guessed.

“I’ll go inside,” he said. “You take a rest yourself.”

Rhea nodded in response, though she didn’t immediately leave his side. Her eyes lingered on him for a moment longer, before she finally turned away and walked to where her friend had settled. Viktor then headed for a door at the back of the chamber.

Beyond it was the inner section of the building, the quieter, more secluded part of the Sanctuary. Tobias’s private quarters were back there, along with the rooms for patients in serious condition or people with special needs.

Crossing the threshold, Viktor stepped into a narrow corridor where arrays of wooden doors lined one side of the passage. He turned left, walked a few paces, and came to a halt before one particular door.

Claire’s room.

She was inside.

For a moment, he simply stood there, fingers hovering just above the doorknob.

Then, he withdrew his hand, and continued down the corridor toward the room at the far end instead. He knocked it.

“Jeanne,” he called. “Are you there?”

There was no response.

He wanted to hear her full account of what happened today, to ask her how exactly she had managed to get away from that bastard. But if she was already asleep, or if she still didn’t want to talk to other people, then there was nothing he could do. So, he walked away.

However, he had only taken three steps before the door behind gave a soft, reluctant creak.

He turned around, only to find it slightly ajar, a narrow gap that allowed a sliver of the room beyond to be seen. And in that opening, Jeanne’s face appeared, her gaze meeting his for the briefest instant before dropping immediately afterward. She said nothing.

“Can we talk?” he asked.

She hesitated, then gave a slow nod. Without saying anything further, she withdrew back into the room, leaving the door exactly as it was.

Does that mean she is inviting me to go in?

He shrugged to himself, then reached for the handle, pushed the door a little wider, and stepped inside.

The room was no different from the one Claire was occupying. Bare walls, a narrow bed, a thin blanket that had seen better decades. Jeanne was sitting on the edge of the mattress, shoulders hunched, hands resting in her lap, her gaze fixed on the floorboards.

Silence settled again.

Now it’s awkward.

He had come to ask her questions, but with her in this state, it was kind of hard to press her to speak about the incident that had caused it in the first place.

“Well, maybe another time,” he said at last, preparing to go out.

That was when Jeanne lifted her head. And, in the very next instant, her expression collapsed.

Her freckled face contorted, like a thin shell that gave way after holding back mounting pressure for far too long, and before he could even fully register what was happening, tears had already welled up in her eyes and spilled over, streaming down her cheeks. Her breath hitched in her throat as she began to sob uncontrollably.

“I am so sorry...” She choked out between ragged gasps, her voice cracking under the strain. “It’s my fault... Everything is my fault... Because of me, Claire is like that...”

Her words fell apart, dissolving into incoherent fragments swallowed by sobs that she could no longer contain, her shoulders shaking as she bent forward.

Now it’s even more awkward.

Viktor glanced at the door. Nope, too late for that now.

He exhaled slowly. What am I supposed to do now?

Should he... comfort her?

But.

Wasn’t he supposed to be the one who needed comforting by other people, not the other way around? After all, he was the primary victim’s younger brother. Technically, anyway. Yes, strictly technically. Still, that was how everyone viewed the situation, and why Rhea and Alycia had come to check on him. Jeanne, on the other hand, had gone entirely off script.

Well...

A change of pace didn’t hurt.

So he stepped closer, wrapped his arms around Jeanne, and pulled her into an embrace. She stiffened for half a heartbeat, then collapsed into him. She buried her face in his chest, fingers clutching at his clothes as her sobs shook through her.

“It’s not your fault,” Viktor said, patting her back. “The only one to blame is that bastard.”

“We... we were just walking... and then...” Jeanne said in a muffled voice as she clung to him even harder. “Why... why did he do this?”

Viktor remained still.

Why, huh?

A long time ago, when he stood frozen before his burning house, he had asked something very much like it. And, eventually, he had gotten his answer.

This world was full of evil bastards, that was why.

There were great men with their great ambitions, who carelessly tossed out orders without concern for how those orders would ripple through lives they would never see. There were small men with their small selfishness, who happily abused the borrowed authority for their own gratification, indulging in cruelty wherever they could.

So, one might ask: what can be done about it? How could you defend themselves against those evil men?

Well, the answer was that you had asked the wrong questions. The moment you used the word “evil,” you had already lost.

Because you got screwed over not because the other side was evil; you were screwed over because you were weak.

In this world, there were stories where good overcame evil, and stories where evil prevailed over good. However, you would only hear about the strong oppressing the weak. The opposite never happened.

Which meant, that was the ultimate truth. The distinction between strong and weak was the only one that mattered; everything else was mere labels without substance.

And once you had accepted that, the conclusion became painfully clear.

You must get stronger.

Be more ambitious than anyone else.

Be more selfish than everyone else.

Do whatever it takes to become the one at the top.

Because only power—absolute power—could make sure that no one would ever fuck with you again.
