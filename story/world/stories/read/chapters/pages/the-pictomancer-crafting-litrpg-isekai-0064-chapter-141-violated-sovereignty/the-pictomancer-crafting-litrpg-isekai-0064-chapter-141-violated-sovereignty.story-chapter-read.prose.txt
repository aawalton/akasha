Ignis's breath erupted.

The world turned blue-white. Ignis ' breath was easily hotter than any tier four fire spell . The air itself ignited, expanding outward in a hemisphere of annihilation. Everything around them either vaporized or liquefied. The wave of superheated plasma rolled toward them.

Clive's brush moved. Black paint, the fastest barrier he could manage—

Too slow. His hand was still moving through the first stroke when the heat hit his face like a physical blow. The flame front was a second away. His barrier wouldn't manifest in time.

They were going to burn.

[Moonlight Barrier]

The notification flashed across his vision as a silver light erupted between them and the inferno. Liquid moonlight solidified into a translucent and shimmering dome.

The dragon fire hit the barrier with the sound of a thousand windows shattering at once.

Clive threw his arm up against the glare. Ignis's breath pressed against it, trying to crush them beneath tons of flame. Through his fingers, he saw cracks spiderwebbing across the moonlight shell. The barrier held, but barely.

Beside him, Jill staggered.

Blood spilled from her lips. Her hands shook as she held them out, feeding power into the barrier. The gashes across her torso had reopened. Fresh crimson soaked through the makeshift bandages, dyeing her clothes red.

"Jill—"

"Don't." Her voice came out strained. "Talk."

The barrier cracked further. A hairline fracture split the dome from apex to base. Heat leaked through the gap, scorching and immediate.

Jill coughed again. More blood. The moonlight shell flickered like a candle in the wind. The barrier wouldn’t hold any longer.

Then, a blur of motion filled his peripheral vision. In the blink of an eye, Azura flew across the space and slammed into Ignis mid-breath. Her full weight crashed into the Dragon King's flank. Both dragons tumbled sideways through the air, wings tangling, Ignis's head wrenching violently to the side.

The column of dragon fire carved across the sky before cutting off entirely as Ignis fought to recover from the collision.

The moonlight dome shattered into fragments that dissolved into mist.

Clive caught Jill as she collapsed.

Above them, Azura and Ignis separated. Azura's mental voice hit him like a scream.

Oh gods. Oh gods, I did it. I attacked him. I attacked the Dragon King.

"Azura—"

Do you understand what I just did?  That was Ignis. The Dragon King. I raised claws against—there's no forgiveness for this. No clemency. I can never return to the Dragon Lands. Never. They'll hunt me until one of us is dead.

"Relax." Clive kept his voice steady despite the adrenaline flooding his system. "We can do this."

How? How can we—

"We can. Trust me."

The crunch of boots on snow announced their approach. Sion descended first, then Guma's red dragon touched down to the left, Miranda's to the right. The three Dragon Knights dismounted in unison, weapons drawn, forming a loose triangle around Clive's position.

Clive was still holding the unconscious Jill. Blood had soaked through her robes completely now, dripping into the snow beneath them.

"We have you surrounded, Clive." Sion declared as though he had already won. "There's nowhere left to run."

Clive scanned the area. A depression in the snow fifteen feet away—where the dragon fire had carved into the mountainside, creating a pocket of relative shelter against an outcropping. Not perfect. But better than here.

He moved quickly, carrying Jill to the depression and lowering her as gently as he could manage.

"Stay here," he murmured, though she couldn't hear him.

He stood. Drew his sword with his right hand. Brush ready in his left.

The three Dragon Knights watched him. Waiting.

"You're outnumbered, Clive." Miranda said. "You can't fight us all. Just surrender. Please."

"He is not outnumbered."

The voice came from above. Clive looked up.

They were coming back. The cultists or what remained of them. Eight figures emerged from the tree line, from the cave mouths, from whatever cover they'd found. Maleus was among them, one arm hanging useless, his chest still blackened from dragon fire. The others were equally battered, limping and bleeding, but moving forward anyway.

"You came for us," Maleus called down. "We don't forget debts."

He raised his good arm. The others did the same—seven cultists, hands lifted toward the sky in unison. Their palms began to glow with that same silver light Jill had used.

The light rose from their hands in threads. Where they met, they began to weave, forming a crescent moon like a sickle ready to reap.

Within moments, the sky darkened further. The light dimmed as if someone had placed a filter over the moon. The pressure in the air changed as a heaviness settled over the battlefield.

Sion's expression changed. "No…."

The darkness coalesced.

Hoofbeats.

Thunder that had no sound, felt only in the chest like a second heartbeat. Each impact against air sent ripples through space. The dreadsteed materialized from shadow. And astride the nightmare mount was a figure in plate armor of the same darkness.

The Black Knight had arrived.

"What—" Sion's voice carried confusion breaking through his usual composure. "What is the Black Knight doing here? We avoided the border checkpoints. We flew over the patrols—"

"Did you, princeling?" The black knight’s voice resonated, as if the armor itself were speaking. "Did you truly believe you could slip through my domain without notice?"

The dreadsteed pawed at the snow. Where its hoof struck, the white turned gray, then black.

"The sky is not beyond my reach." The Black Knight pointed his lance at Sion. "Every shadow cast by your dragons. Every displacement of air from their wings. Every wingbeat that disturbed the clouds. Did you think I wouldn't notice you passing overhead?"

Guma moved to flank. "Prince Sion, we need to—"

"A prince of Euchronia." The Black Knight's helmet turned, tracking all the dragon knights. "Sneaking through my borders like a thief in the night. Thinking yourself clever."

The dreadsteed took a step forward. The frost beneath its hooves spread faster now, crawling across the snow toward Sion's position.

"How unsightly. Tell me, Your Highness—does your father know you're here? Does he know his golden heir has violated Vandelian sovereignty? Or did you hope to start your little war and present it to him as a fait accompli?"

"We're not starting a war," Sion said. "We're eliminating a threat to humanity."

"You're slaughtering refugees on foreign soil." The Black Knight's free hand moved to the sword at his hip. "Refugees who committed no crime save that of worshipping differently than your goddess prefers."

"They're corrupted—"

"They're mine." The sword raised. Black flame licked along its edge, the same substance as the dreadsteed's mane. "And you will not touch them."

The dreadsteed surged forward, charging at full gallop.

The Black Knight's sword swept down in an arc meant to cleave Sion in half.

Sion threw himself sideways. The blade missed him by inches.

Ignis roared and lunged, massive jaws snapping for the dreadsteed's neck. The Black Knight wheeled his mount, pivoting on its hindquarters, hooves leaving trails of decay in the snow. The lance swept up, catching Ignis beneath the jaw. The Dragon King's head snapped to the side.

Miranda and Guma moved in from opposite flanks, weapons raised.

The Black Knight didn't even look at them. The dreadsteed kicked backward—both hind legs striking out with force that sent Guma stumbling. Miranda's blade came down. The Black Knight's sword rose to meet it. The clash rang out across the mountainside, and Miranda's dragon flinched at the sound.

"Come." Maleus's voice cut through the chaos. He stood at the edge of the trees, one arm still hanging useless, but his eyes were alert. He gestured towards a cave. "We need to move. Now."

Clive rushed to the depression where Jill lay to scoop her up.

Clive?

"With me, Azura. Low and fast."

Clive ran. Jill's head lolling against his chest with each jarring step, but he powered through regardless.

Azura touched down behind him, wings folding tight as she squeezed through the entrance.

Maleus materialized from the shadows just inside, his good arm shooting out to grab Clive's shoulder. His fingers dug in hard, yanking Clive sideways and deeper into the darkness. "Keep moving. Tunnel goes down the mountain—we can lose them."

Behind them, a dragon roared. The cave swallowed the light within ten feet.

"How far?" Clive's breath came hard.

"Far enough." Maleus moved ahead of them, one hand trailing along the cave wall for guidance. "The tunnel splits three times before it exits. They won't know which path we took."

Azura's claws clicked against stone behind them. Her mental voice was tight, controlled. I can't see anything.

"Stay close," Clive said. "Follow my footsteps."

The sounds of battle faded. Soon there was only breathing, footfalls, and the steady drip of water in the dark.
