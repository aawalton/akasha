Seized by the claws of fury, the Locksmith advanced directly against the figure. Knowing it would not kill him, since he was its only way out, he could serve as a shield for the remaining members. Even so, his head was nowhere near as calm as he believed.

What is the point? He asked himself as he created a passage toward Raven. They are already gone. They are gone because of me. His inner struggle, though detrimental to his thinking, did not show in his movements. He leaped gracefully from door to door, creating passages in front of Manes in an attempt to pull him out of harm's way. Vela and Madeline kept their distance, doing their best to delay the entity while trusting in Harry's plan. The problem, however, was exactly that; the plan ended there.

The shadow boy kept hurling his bands, to no effect whatsoever. Rasp! One fell to the ground. Rasp! Another was torn into dozens of pieces. Through pain and cuts, Manes wore a determined and confident mask, but Harry could see the panic in his eyes. The panic of someone who had never had a chance in life. It was the look of a young man who had already died a long time ago and was only now realizing it.

In a lucky strike, however, while Harry was creating passages to deflect the projectiles Raven generated, Manes widened the crack in the figure's mask even further. A sharp cry was heard from Raven, whose attacks were now more unpredictable and uncontrolled.

Why does he protect it so fiercely, could it be...! Harry had formed a hypothesis, especially considering what Goldman had told him before closing his eyes permanently.

The Astronomer's expression, hiding in the corner behind one of the long shelves, was curious. Harry saw, beyond a last remnant of hope shining in his soul, the eyes of a father. Someone who had been waiting for this moment for a very long time.

"The mask!" Harry shouted to everyone listening. "It's holding Raviel in place! We just need to—"

And at that moment, in the second he stopped paying attention to Manes, he saw the figure punch the young man all the way into the telescope. Crack! The sound of bones breaking was audible and seemed to have been amplified by Raven's power. The figure, still uncontrolled from the crack in its mask, grabbed the boy who was already coughing blood and finished him with a punch that pierced his stomach.

"...I'm scared, Daniel." Those were the last words of Manes, the son of shadows.

Kid... Harry, horrified by the brutality of Raven, seemed to swallow his tears once more. I have to fix this. I will fix this.

He then felt a black pull dragging him away from the group. It was Manes's bands. But before he could feel relief at the boy's return, he realized they were coming from Raven. But how... That's not possible!

He was hurled into an immovable shelf, which multiplied the pain as though he had landed on concrete. Increasingly heavy books rained down on Harry, who quickly got up and moved out of the way; the place would not hold much longer before collapsing or imploding. Is all of this coming from Raven...?

He then understood the reason for the masked figure's uncontrolled attacks; it was stealing the Emblems of those it killed. In its fight with Manes, it seemed to replicate Matthews's boxing movements. Even before possessing the Fist Emblem, Raven had likely used Goldman's powers to deflect Matthews's attacks. Since the pirate's Emblem was imperceptible at low intensities, Harry hadn't noticed. Now, however, it was clear.

"Mr. Locksmith!" Vela shouted as he appeared to dodge the stolen bands from Raven. Madeline provided enough cover for the masked figure to miss its other attacks with her sticky inks.

"Harry!" Madeline also cried out. "What are we going to do!? My ink is running out, and I can feel my legs going weak."

How was he supposed to know? Up to this point, his plans, however reckless and occasionally lethal, had saved the group. This, however, was not something that could be solved with cleverness or wit. It was a question of who hit harder. The only thing he could do was direct the remaining members' attacks at Raven's mask.

Damn it. Damn it. Damn it! Harry used one of his doors and jumped behind Raven. "It's the mask! We have to rip it off! Attack as hard as—"

His orders were cut short by a violent wave of dizziness. Raven moved through the air, floating and crashing into several shelves that refused to fall. Meanwhile, seeing only blurs, Harry clung to his senses and began tearing at Raven's mask.

In that moment, if anyone could feel the touch of a color or hear the sound of a geometric shape, it was Harry. He had no time to reason whether he was losing his mind or not; the answer was instinctive. He felt the cold of the tundra and the heat of an erupting volcano at his fingertips. The pressure in his extremities rose and fell in accordance with the force he used on the crack. As if touching the core of the universe, the Locksmith had nearly all of his thoughts consumed, except for one.

Save daddy. That phrase, carried by a high and calm voice, was the only thing keeping Harry anchored in the moment. In a visceral way, the voice seemed to coat every neuron of the young thief, who no longer had the strength to scream.

"...Get out of there!" He could hear a faint voice calling to him from the other side. Was it Madeline? Or Vela? It could even have been the Astronomer. But those names didn't mean very much, not in that moment. It was too strong, all of it was too strong. The pressure, the colors, the sensations. Harry was being pulled in.

Like a newborn, however, the Locksmith had a jolt of reality when all his senses were thrown at him at once. Returned to the moment, Harry embraced the sensations hitting his body the way a child would embrace a cactus; crying from every angle. The tears were too wet and the floor was too flat. His blood surged and coursed through his entire body like a current while his long hair pricked his skin like knives.

He screamed, but quickly covered his ears from the shock of it all. Everything was too much. Even his thoughts were audible. He then felt something like a building pressing on his shoulder, but when he turned, he saw the multifaceted face of the Astronomer. His cracked skin now reminded him of the gaps between clouds in the sky. The pauses between his words were perfectly timed, as if he had all the time in the world to talk. His voice? As natural as a nightingale.

Harry grabbed the Astronomer's torn clothing and compared it with his own. He then realized that perfection reigned in imperfection. This was the Astronomer he had always dreamed of meeting. This was the figure who had broken time and the universe. He was the one who started all of this.

"...Don't do that again, young man!" His voice resonated in Harry's ears as the young thief seemed to be regaining control of himself. "You almost went in with him!"

"...Cardley?" Harry questioned as he looked around, with no sign of his friends anywhere. "Where is she? Where is the monk?"

"They..." The man seemed to struggle to continue. "They are gone. When you jumped onto him, his attacks became too uncontrolled."

"But they... No. No, they didn't die. They—" Then Harry noticed the Astronomer was missing his right arm. Blood dripped from his shoulder as he offered an apologetic smile.

"Don't worry about me. It doesn't hurt as much as it looks. In any case, I will die without pain." The Astronomer said. "Now take this, Locksmith. It is the God Emblem."

Holding the Token, had Cardley Logos not told him that this was the God Emblem, Harry would never have guessed. The weight and pressure of the object, just as it had been when he performed the ritual with Devon all those years ago, were laughable. A Lock Symbol remained at its center, made of moon dust.

The object he held had a circular, quadrangular, and triangular shape all at once, and he stood up, nearly breaking it in the process.

"What are you doing?" The Astronomer said, leaning against a table covered in children's drawings and one of the moon. "Aren't you going to break it?"

"Not yet..." He remembered Goldman's words. "I have something to test first."

"Ah, well..." The Astronomer gave a sincere laugh. "I understand. Well, if you manage it, tell him it was my fault. All of it was my fault."

"Cardley."

"Yes?"

"Don't blame yourself for having defied the universe. It was you who gave us the power to fight against our miserable fate. I'll bring him back, don't worry." He said, and turned.

He went to Raven, who was floating in the air, his mask emanating a myriad of colors unknown to man. Seeing the esoteric image, the product of Logos's son, Harry felt a headache coming on. Having experienced it firsthand before, however, it was manageable.

Holding the object under his arm, he straightened his tie and pulled his hair back. In a smooth movement, he cleaned his glasses on the hem of his coat and created a passage with his doors. As a platform, he conjured another to keep himself at the same level as Raven.

"It is useless." The figure said, its voice wavering between a high pitch and a deeper one. "You will not manage to tear it off. He has been gone for a long time. It has been gone for a long time. He belongs to the Tower now."

"...That may be true." Harry said as he looked around, the shelves finally beginning to shift, on the verge of all coming down. He had felt this loss of control before. This indecision of the universe that seemed to repeat itself endlessly. "But I don't really care about the truth."

Opening his sealed pocket, Harry pulled out a small coin. A C-Coin. Smiling calmly, the Locksmith tossed it at Raven just as the world began to contract. It was now.

The coin struck the creature, which seemed to slide through the temporal jelly covering the end of this loop. The mask, whose colors seemed to lose their opacity, melted away on one side. Behind it, there was only a friendly, frightened face.

"...Daddy?" The child asked, rubbing its eyes.

"No, little one." Harry said, still with a warm smile. "My name is Locksmith, but you can call me Harry if you'd like. I'm here to free you. Isn't that what you want?"

"I saw something wrong in daddy's telescope..." It said. "He told me not to look but I wanted to look. Is he alright? Is everything alright? I feel kind of wobbly. I want this to be over. Can you save daddy?"

"...There is only one person who can save him, kid." Harry said, tilting his head slightly. "Would you like to do the honors?"

His goal had failed. He would never save his mother. His friends? They had been torn apart or melted away entirely. He recalled Madeline's loud laughter and Vela's quiet counsel. He smiled inwardly at the small arguments between Daniel and Brun, and at Manes's complaints. He remembered the life Petrikov had lost and Goldman's optimism. They were all people he had lost. He then realized he did not need to save all of them. He never had that power. He only needed to save one person. That is all that matters. It is the only thing that can matter.

Seeing Raviel, he would not let the child go through what he had gone through. Even if I couldn't save her, he will.

"What do I need to do?" The child held the Emblem.

"Just throw it on the ground." The universe still seemed in stasis, but Raven would wake soon.

"...What is going to happen to you, Locky?" The child asked.

"Don't worry about me. Go on, save whoever you loved most."

"My daddy?"

"Yes."

"I love my daddy!"

"...Good." Harry then watched the child throw the Emblem on the ground. The Emblem, defying the rules of the Tower, passed through the temporal jelly and shattered on the floor. "Make your wish, Raviel."

The Locksmith was hurled through the room, breaking through dozens of shelves and even cracking the walls of the floor.

I am... He looked around and saw a beautiful blue sky. Below him, a group of birds flew from one side to the other, touching the dome and returning from the opposite side. He was outside the tower. Falling toward his end, he observed the magnitude of the architectural structure before him. A structural Frankenstein, the Logos Tower had never looked so beautiful.

Looking up, he saw the power of the God Emblem rapidly covering the entire tower, already able to imagine the wish Raviel had made. The light at the top of the tower, possibly marking the last floor, still held there.

So this is it? The Locksmith looked downward as the ground seemed to approach him, literally; the dome was flattening, and soon Harry would be crushed. At least someone got out of this alive.

He had wanted to remove the Emblems from the world. He had wanted to save his mother and he had wanted to see Logos freed from the moon, but he could find consolation in having helped at least one person. For a thief, that was good enough, wasn't it?

And so, Harry closed his eyes and smiled, finally at rest.
