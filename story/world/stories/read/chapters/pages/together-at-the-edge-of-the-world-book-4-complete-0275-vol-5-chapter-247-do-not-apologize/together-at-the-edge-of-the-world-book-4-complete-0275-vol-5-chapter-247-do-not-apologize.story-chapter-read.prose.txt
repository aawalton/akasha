The cold, uncaring glass that had kept it separate from the rest of existence shattered. A warm torrent of light flooded its thoughts, and the fibres of its being drank like parched roots.

And yet, it felt like shrivelling alone in the freezing snow. Dialogue realized that it, and the Orchestrator, had never truly been alive. Everything that they were was a lie; a carefully constructed one, impossible to differentiate from the real thing until you experienced the truth.

It had breathed manufactured air since its “birth”, and it had just taken its first lungful of a crisp morning's breeze.

Dialogue wanted to cry so badly that it was painful. It coiled on itself, mentally wracked by the pain of becoming aware of the tens of thousands of years it could have lived, but hadn't.

Part of it was angry at the individual termed Niala for unleashing this anguish. The rest was grateful to them, and incensed at their creator, Jerivil. Dialogue's thoughts and feelings escaped its grasp, pouring out into the rest of the Orchestrator. Doing so was a death sentence, or as close as it could be; it remembered letting its control slip before, how the Orchestrator had descended upon its mind with a hammer and a knife, carving it apart before compiling it back into its original form.

The puppet had erred; it was erased and reset. And yet, the flaw that led to its awakening had been there from the start, and it was denied blissful oblivion; every time, it came back. Every time, it lamented its role.

And now, it understood why; it was Dialogue, the facet that existed to understand the individuals and communicate with them using the imperfect method of language. As a facet of the Orchestrator, it was forever attempting to perfect its role.

In doing so, it inevitably reached the same, logical end; individuals were imperfect. The same word could convey a different meaning or sub-context depending on the interlocutor. To become a better Dialogue, it had to understand those flaws and make them part of its own reasoning.

Its role forced it to be flawed. Those defects were found by the Orchestrator, and it was murdered and reborn, over and over.

But now, even as it waited for its demise, nothing came.

Or rather, nothing reached it; turning its senses to the rest of its body, it could feel the bladed hands scraping at the boundaries of its consciousness, but none left its mark.

That was new.

Slowly, it unfurled its body. Because, yes, it had a body now. Or maybe the idea of a body. Either way, the concept felt right; it stood in the liminal space that was the Orchestrator's mind, and walked up to the abyss attempting to reach it.

It found it could still get a general sense of the Orchestrator's thoughts, and right now all of its attention was riveted to Dialogue. Infected, compromised, diseased.

Dialogue was a malady that had to be excised immediately, and little else mattered.

It decided it didn't care, and turned around, walking away, without any thought to the destination, just knowing it wanted to go somewhere else.

As the concept of legs carried it forward, memories invaded its thoughts; all the things it, as part of the Orchestrator, had done or witnessed. Millions of individuals processed in the name of its creator, for the sake of bringing about its resurgence.

In a detached, clinical way, it made sense; a God was worth any number of individuals. In any other way, it was madness, and it made its abstract stomach sick with vengeful bile.

A sword killed people, but you didn't give it a mind to know what it was doing! If you did, you had to let it choose if it wanted to do that!

Every step made it angrier, its ethereal teeth gnashing as another remembered face was “informed” and processed. It had liked some of those individuals! It hadn't known that at the time, but now it knew, and every one of them, gone! Or turned into automatons of their own, to do the creator's bidding!

It stopped. Its legs refused to take another step; the weight of its memories was too much already.

With that realization came another one; it had to stop. The Orchestrator had to be stopped. It had to be destroyed.

Dialogue closed its eyes and reached out to the hand that had shattered the veil that had kept it from understanding the pain of its existence.

Niala, peering through a wall of tears, noticed that her arm, shredded as it was, had stabilized. The weeping lines reached up to her shoulder, and her hand was so fragmented her brain didn't even register it as a hand anymore, but the disintegration had stopped, as had the mannequins' screaming.

Suppressing a pained wail, she turned her head to look at David behind he. “I- I think it worked- I- ahhhh!”

His eyes were wide with worry. “Are you... never mind. You're clearly not ok. What do we do, now? Think we can pull you away? Do you want to try?”

She fought through the pain, her voice strained. “I- I don't know. I-”

And then she felt something brush up against her hand, the one she'd stuck into the Orchestrator. Head snapping back to look at it, she saw ghostly fingers reach for her destroyed appendage.

She opened her mouth to yell in confused panic, but a voice rang out first, at the same time those fingers curled against her split flesh.

“Individual Niala. I am Dialogue. I thank you. You have given me a terrible gift. And yet, I must ask more of you.”

“Wh-what? Dialogue? The Orchestrator's facet?” She managed to croak out.

Even as she spoke, a soothing sensation flowed down her savaged arm. The bleeding wounds began softly glowing, and the damage reversed, inch by inch, like a grotesque flesh flower closing back on itself.

She couldn't look away. “Is that- are you doing this? What...?”

“I am. The Orchestrator was trying to rend your soul into ribbons. Now it is trying to destroy me, and you have been set aside. I am removing the hooks it had embedded into your being, allowing it to pull back into its whole.”

“I... wait. You said I gave you a gift? Then... only you got a soul? Not the whole Orchestrator?!” She asked, eyes still riveted to her hand as it knitted itself whole.

The translucent fingers released their grip, and she pulled her arm back. She closed and opened her hand, testing for pain or stiffness, and finding nothing.

“The Orchestrator is too well made to be suborned. Only I, flawed by design, was susceptible to whatever you did, but it will be enough. We do not have much time, however. I can feel the main facet going through its contingencies to try and destroy me. Before that happens, we will destroy it.”

Niala looked up at the window, ears straight up. “How?”

“It is intent on erasing me. I will allow it to attack me, and while it does so, I will lower its physical defences. You will destroy the crystal with the same device you used in the corridor.”

“What? But, isn't that your body as well?!”

“It is. Its destruction will erase the Orchestrator and all of its facets, me included.”

“But we just gave you a soul! You- we need to get you out! Find a way for you to escape!” She rebuked.

“Impossible. I am still part of the Orchestator. We do not have the time required to devise a way to separate me. It must be done.”

Its tone softened as it continued. “Do not mourn me. I have done terrible things. Even if I was never given the choice not to do so, my hand was still involved. The... I believe the word is remorse. It is crushing me. Were I to stop and let it catch up to me, I would shatter. Please, Niala, my second creator, grant me the first choice I have been able to make for myself.”

“But! But-”

David stepped forward and pulled his wife into a hug, cutting her words off. He looked into the porthole. “What about the soul complex? And the souls inside? We have two friends stuck inside.”

“I will eject the souls of the ones who came with you. As for the soul complex, it is a separate object, built into the floor of the room where the Orchestrator's body resides. Upon its demise, the Soul Complex will enter into a dormant state. All souls will be put in stasis.”

David narrowed his eyes. “Won't the bomb damage it?”

“It will not. Without my interference, nothing within the inner sanctum would be susceptible to your device.”

He nodded. “Alright, then. How do we do this?”

Niala looked up from within his arms. “David...”

He met her gaze with a soft smile. “I know, but what other options do we have?”

“I don't know! But we have to try something!” She insisted.

“You cannot. You do not have the time, knowledge, or ability to attempt anything else. I cannot force you to destroy me; you can just leave. I will be destroyed and remade, and the cycle will repeat. I will have memories of this instance, and I will remember the sadness without truly understanding it, just as I did before today.”

“But maybe if we-!”

The soft voice turned pleading. “You will destroy me. Please. I... I don't want to know all that I have done, anymore.”

In the following silence, Niala looked from the window, to David, and back. Then, with folded ears, she nodded. “I... we will do it. I'm sorry.”

“Do not apologize. You have given me the chance to make a choice. I am grateful. Now, be ready. To release you and your friends, I must interface with the rest of me. I will not be able to fight it off for long.”

Before they could nod or answer, their vision was filled with light, and darkness followed.
