Splinters of ashwood and bits of sheared iron rained into the dirt all around Eom Tae-seok.

He was standing in front of the wreckage of the siege engine, pleased to be able to remain upright. Stiff knees, burning lungs, and a thousand small aches all vied for his attention. Getting old was unpleasant.

Across the width of the road, the Meng-shan forces were splitting. The bulk of their troops rushed towards the rows of wooden abatis, while a separate detachment seemed to be coming to surround him.

Tae-seok glanced back towards the gatehouse, where he knew a thin girl in scholar’s robes stood, watching as the events she had put into motion unfolded. Then he sighed.

“That girl and her schemes. I can’t believe I’m about to do this.”

His mission was far from finished. Now that the siege ballista had been neutralized, it was time for the next step. He could almost see her finger pointing at the next target.

Tae-seok whirled, fixing his single eye on the team of oxen. The six beasts were already overcome with fear. The thunderclap from the Dan-hae-gyeok, the coppery stench of fresh blood, and the stinging shower of flying iron splinters had terrified the animals. They thrashed against their harnesses, white foam flying as they tried to break free from the thick wooden frame that was anchoring them to what remained of the chassis.

Tae-seok shifted his footing, drew his Cham-ma-do back, and prepared to strike.

With a sharp exhale, he brought the long blade down, sundering energy crackling along its flats. The steel whistled through the air, shearing straight through the wooden yoke that was holding the team together.

The ashwood framework exploded into kindling, freeing the terrified beasts from their restraints.

Before the disoriented animals could take advantage of their freedom, Tae-seok blasted himself back in front of them with a controlled release of pressure. He stopped directly in the face of one of them, his nose close enough that he could smell the ox’s foam-flecked snout.

The Tai-shan elder invoked the fourth mnemonic of the Chuk-ap-gyeol, unsealing his Tian Tu point once more. A deafening, primal roar tore from his throat, deep enough that people could feel the vibrations in their bones.

To the six oxen, the sight of a blood-drenched, one-eyed demon screaming directly into their faces was too much to bear. With panicked bellows, the half-ton beasts whirled around and stampeded away from the source of the terrifying noise.

The crazed animals plowed full-tilt into the front line of the Meng-shan spear column. The crunch of snapping shafts, broken bones, and thrashing hooves threw entire ranks into chaos.

But growing old came with a price, and the exertion was catching up to Tae-seok. His legs buckled slightly. The old man stuck the tip of his Cham-ma-do into the dirt, using it to keep himself upright as a wave of fatigue washed over him. Using so many different techniques in rapid succession had drained most of his stamina.

As he flagged, the remaining spearmen who were still trying to encircle him closed in. They formed a cautious, bristling line of steel, their bucklers overlapping as they slowly advanced towards the exhausted master.

Tae-seok squinted through the dust at the chaos sweeping the Meng-shan line, a grim smirk on his face.

“That brat better get down here soon,” he muttered into the rising dust, as he straightened up and stood tall once again.

“My back isn’t going to hold out forever.”

A loud thundering of hooves erupted outwards from the gatehouse, so fast and sudden that the wind of its passage snapped the hem of Chae-ha’s robes.

Mu-cheon exploded past her on a warhorse, crouched low over the animal’s neck like a hunter closing in for a kill. Behind him followed a tight wedge of Tai-shan cavalry, racing through the open sally port and desperately trying to match his pace.

Across the clearing, the Meng-shan vanguard was in total disarray. The dozens of soldiers who had managed to advance before the oxen collided with their line were now funneling into the maze of abatis Chae-ha had engineered. The sharpened tree-trunks were everywhere, and often at strange angles. Men stumbled into narrow bottlenecks, collided with dead ends, turned around in confusion, and wound up pushed right back where they started, all while being shot at by the Tai-shan archers.

The central channels were choked with bodies, but the outer flanks of the formation remained relatively open. And that asymmetry was the entire point. Chae-ha had been counting on it.

The young girl watched as Mu-cheon’s stallion raced forwards in a gently undulating line, passing by the abatis along the western edge of the clearing. From the enemy’s perspective, the timber stakes were a dangerous threat, breaking up their formation and forcing them into narrow lanes. But from where Mu-cheon was approaching, the spikes faced the opposite direction. The path through their center was slightly winding, but it was open and clear.

Which was no coincidence. Chae-ha had taken the layout they'd used to position the abatis from a very specific source.

No one else on the battlefield understood what she had done, but if any of the disciples could have looked down on it from the heavens, they would have immediately recognized the pattern. It was the One-Way Seal. The very first mnemonic of the Chuk-ap-gyeol, which Mu-cheon had taught her one moonlit night, all those years ago.

She almost wanted to laugh. The enemy infantry was stuck. And now, it was time to end things with their strategist.

Chae-ha felt a quiet, unexpected twinge of gratitude towards the Lesser Elder. She had learned a lot engaging with him. But the time for lessons was over.

There was nowhere for him to retreat. The path backwards was blocked by a wall of trampling oxen and broken infantry. Meanwhile, heading straight for him was the Tai-shan Young Master – a man whose very name was a rejection of divine fate.

The Sect Leader wanted a legend. Chae-ha intended to give him one.

A sharp shout from the ramparts momentarily interrupted her concentration.

Down one of the central abatis lanes, a Meng-shan spearman defied the odds. He parried two Tai-shan greatswordsmen in rapid succession, struck both of them down, and somehow navigated through a rain of falling arrows without taking a scratch.

Behind him, half a dozen spearmen followed, flooding through the momentary gap. They were charging straight towards the exposed, uncured section of the gatehouse wall. And the Tai-shan reserve line was slightly out of position, unable to shift across the field in time to block them.

Well. Mu-cheon would be able to handle the strategist. Chae-ha might as well handle things here.

“Sergeant Noh. It’s time.”

Noh Gu-shim and his three squad members took up their positions beside her as she stepped through the threshold of the gatehouse and onto the muddy battlefield, stopping two dozen paces ahead of the charging Meng-shan soldiers. Taking a slow, deep breath, she planted her lead foot into the soft earth and spun on her heel. The wide panels of her scholar’s robes flared outwards in a smooth, circular arc.

Mid-spin, her fingers caught two of the leather straps she had prepared along her inner seams, yanking the pair of pull cords in quick succession.

“Cheol-geuk-jin!” she declared firmly, announcing the technique for the benefit of her allies. “Formation Arts: Iron Thorn Array!”

Two dark arcs, each composed of a dozen individual caltrops, flew out from the hems of her robes. Twenty-four oil-darkened, four-pointed pieces of metal vanished into the dirt in front of them.

Chae-ha watched the men’s feet pound into the ground as they sprinted, knowing what would happen when one of those boots hit a steel spike. Breathing deeply, she walked up to the edge of her trap and began to step.

“Baek-pal Hwan-yeong-bo,” the young girl breathed. “108 Phantom Steps.”

Who stepped on a caltrop and when was essentially a matter of chance. But in order to strike at her, the spearmen would have to be operating in the middle of the spike field. The longer they stayed there, the more the odds shifted in her favor. This time was nothing like the last time she had taken on a group of enemy soldiers. She had full control of the terrain, sixteen different Phantom Steps to rely on, and allies that had trained to take advantage of her techniques.

All of which meant, statistically, her odds of survival were remarkably high.

Chae-ha grinned. Now, it was time to roll the dice.

Wind whipped through Mu-cheon’s hair as his warhorse thundered across the westmost lane of the clearing. In his left hand, he held the reins of a spare charger, leading the beast along at full gallop through the narrow corridor that Chae-ha had warned the archers away from.

Through the dust ahead, he spotted the ring of purple-clad spearmen closing in on Eom Tae-seok. The poor old man was facing down at least a dozen spearpoints.

Normally, charging a prepared line of spearmen with cavalry was a good way to lose a lot of horses. But these particular spearmen were out of formation, their backs turned as they tried to encircle the flagging elder. They would make excellent target practice.

The Tai-shan cavalry wedge slammed into their rear like an iron hammer hitting a terracotta tile.

The enemy line crumpled as horses trampled men into the mud. Mu-cheon drew the Dae-jik-do from his back in one clean motion, swinging the double-handed straight sword back and forth as he hacked his way through the few enemies that remained standing. The heavy blade sheared straight through wooden spear shafts and leather armor alike, scattering blood and severed limbs across the gravel.

Mu-cheon brought his stallion skidding to a halt beside Tae-seok. He leaned far out of his saddle, grabbing the old man by his collar, and hauled him up onto the saddle of the spare charger.

“Get back to the gate!” Mu-cheon yelled, tossing the reins into Tae-seok’s hands. “I’ll take it from here!”

The Elder’s face contorted in stubborn fury. “Retreat? The Sect Leader gave me a direct order to keep your arrogant hide intact, boy! I’m not about to head anywhere else. We’re not done here yet.”

The old monster spat a mouthful of red onto the grass and pointed the tip of his Cham-ma-do directly past the ruined siege ballista. He was aiming straight at Wi Mo, who was struggling to regain control of his own horse amidst the chaos caused by the rampaging oxen.

“Instead of talking nonsense,” Tae-seok growled, his single eye burning with deadly intent, “how about we finish this together?”

The resulting grin on Mu-cheon’s face ran from ear to ear. “Now you’re talking, Elder!”

The Young Master dug his heels into his stallion’s flanks. The horse reared back with a fierce whinny, its iron-shod hooves coming down on the chest of an approaching spearman, before surging forwards into a gallop.

Side by side, the Young Master and the scarred veteran carved a bloody trench through the remaining Meng-shan guard detail, driving their mounts straight towards the isolated strategist.

As the distance closed, Mu-cheon filled his lungs once more, raising his Dae-jik-do high into the afternoon sun and roaring at the top of his lungs.

“HEY, YOU FAN-FLAPPING FRAUD! REMEMBER WHEN I SAID I WAS COMING TO TAKE YOUR HEAD?!”

The young man grinned. Elder Eom may have been the superior warrior, but Mu-cheon still had the bigger mouth.
