"...And it's hard, you know? Choosing between my family and friends like I owe them something. I did a good deed, and I'm not apologizing for that." A guard confessed as he stared at a checkerboard with a distant gaze, his helmet carefully placed on his lap.

"Yeah, I too agree with your... situation." Opposite of the guard, a man with shoulder-length hair answered inconsiderately.

The place, at least during the wee hours of the night, was always dark. It was adjacent to the king's castle, who was probably already asleep. The thief wanted to finish this quickly, but couldn't wait when he saw a sad guard playing chess alone. Well, since we're here...

The guard narrowed his eyes as he searched for an answer on the gloomy corners of the room. Awaiting his opponent's turn, he felt conflicted by the man's answer. Was he being sarcastic or just a dick? In front of him, he could see the face of a man with glasses and a rickety chessboard. The silence was deafening, not that he cared, but for some reason he felt uneasy. Do I know this guy? He stared at the face of the long-haired man.

With the lamplight showing signs of weakening, the guard decided to be more direct.

"...So you do think I was in the right for brutally murdering a man who stole bread?"

"I..." In a lethargic manner of speaking, he picked a chess piece but soon stopped, widening his eyes. "Wait, what!? Y-you brutally murdered a man for stealing bread? God, I thought it was a metaphor! How are you able to work here!?"

"You weren't..." The guard retorted, more shocked by something else than the crime itself, "I just shared a big, introspective moment with you and you weren't even listening!?"

"Wow, alright." He backed his hands as he got up and spoke loudly to the roof, "Look, D-Man, I really tried, okay? But the only time I follow your advice and don't put them to sleep right away, I end up chatting with—"

"Put me to sleep? Who are you again? Are you that thief!?" He said with a quizzical look on his face.

"—a bloody guard who murders people for stealing bread. And then—Oh!" The thief stopped his ranting as he gently adjusted his glasses and stared at the checkerboard. A few agonizing moments of awkwardness passed for the guard's sake as the man grinned and concluded. "Checkmate!"

A mysterious door appeared behind the thief as he retrieved the helmet he stole. Above the guard, unbeknownst to him, another door seemed to have magically appeared. The door behind the thief opened as he fell backward. Due to the properties of his Emblem, however, he was now falling from the same door that had appeared above the guard. Before realizing what had happened, the guard only felt a sharp blow to his head. Both doors now evaporated.

After understanding how to use his Emblem, way back, the thief also understood that kinetic energy was on his side. Most of the time. He stood up from the guard and threw the helmet away.

"Now that's way better. Can't believe you're still on guard duty..." Unburdening himself from the weight of the armor, he let it go and picked up a few clothes inside a door he summoned to his right. Covering a Door Symbol on his back, he now wore a black coat and picked up a leather satchel behind one of the tiles in the room.

From arranging his tie to grooming his hair, he then felt small capsules inside the satchel and felt relieved. The metallic items, lock shaped, were destructive as they were cold. The man kissed one of the locks and put them on his waist as his coat covered them. Only two left... It's been a good run, though.

The thief though to hide the guard's body, but who was he kidding? He wouldn't return. Not if things went right. Let them find it. Let them know it was him.

"Okay, let's take off." He said as the guard drooled over the chess pieces. These games really rot your brain, don't they? Though chess is worth the rotting.

Following through dark corridors, the man carried only a lamp, cutting corners with his sturdy doors. He jumped across entire spaces while walking pompously as if he had complete knowledge of the place. Amidst muffled cries coming from the side cells, the man was more concerned with not soiling his shoes with the sticky dirt of the floor than being seen.

The corridor he was in was long, dark, and certainly hadn't been designed for easy movement around the prison judging by its confusing turns and claustrophobic feel. The stone floor seemed smooth at first glance, but upon closer inspection, the neglect was evident. Not only that, but also the rusty metal bars covering the solitary cells told the thief a lot about how this place was handled. On top of everything, it was cold. Freezing cold. How do they manage to survive in conditions like these? He thought to himself. And soon the thief felt a sudden anger towards the man in charge. Since most of these 'prisoners' were simply people who disagreed with the regime.

After a few moments, while still holding a weak lamp, the man soon found another source of light.

Not surprised by the lack of guards, he then spotted one, several keys around her neck as she almost slept, sitting on a box while her hands almost gave up holding a spear. There seemed to be a supply room behind the wooden door that was next to the woman guarding it. Well, almost guarding it. I'm so close, just don't fail now. Remember, I'm desperate to find the, um... Veris's scroll. Yes, that's right.

Stealthily, in the darkness, the man created a 2-meter door behind the guard. With various adornments and symbols engraved on it, the door opened and, from within, emerged a man with his neatly arranged tie.

"Boo!" The thief whispered as the guard jumped from her box and almost dropped her spear. "Do you come here often?"

"H-Hey! Stop right now, by the laws of..." When she saw the man, however, she lowered her spear and smiled as if recognizing him, "The Locksmith!? My god, it's you! I can't believe it... I've waited so long for you to rob the king! I mean, I didn't want you to rob him, but since you're here, could you—"

"Yes, yes, of course, but first, tell me something: Where is Veris's scroll?" The man spoke in a more serious tone but maintained an unassuming smile.

"Ah yes, it's..." She paused and made an embarrassed expression. "Actually, I don't know if I can tell you its location. B-because, well, this time I'm on the right side of law, you know? I can't just—"

"I'll give you one of my locks if you tell me—"

"Following the corridor, it's in the red door, then go up to the 2nd floor and go to the end." Like a devout follower, she confessed everything to the thief, who mentally noted the directions.

As soon as he heard that, the thief embraced the guard, who blushed slightly, and tossed one of his metal locks into the air as if it were a coin. Almost dropping it, she caught it and chuckled softly.

Fucking' hell, they really need to pick their guards better... He thought to himself as he headed towards the place. She knows me? Well, it doesn't matter; at least I know I had an impact. I'll have the chance to correct everything.

Using the doors again to get there faster, he followed the guard's instructions. Besides having enough information to confirm she was telling the truth, he could feel a powerful aura emanating from the door, something that far exceeded the power that a piece of scroll could provide.

The place was in the highest part of the castle. The windows, reflecting the moonlight, also gave texture to the city below, which from that height looked more like an anthill than the capital.

Although they knew I was coming, I didn't think they'd perform the ritual so quickly... He stopped using the doors, holding his leather satchel close, and sighed deeply. I just hope they don't try to kill me first...

The door, as he had imagined, was unlocked. He entered a place that looked more like a church than anything else. Rows of pews covered the space while a stained-glass window of a crooked tower stood in the background. In front of him, on a rocky pedestal, a scroll was perfectly placed. He strutted to the scroll, singing to himself.

"Wow!" With attentive eyes, he scanned the area, "I've been looking for this for such a long time...!"

Picking up the papyrus-made object and unscrolling it, he saw nothing written on it. Damn, I thought they'd at least use the real one...

"Stop right there!" A hoarse voice sounded from behind him, together with dozens of guards, "Under New Cavebrook's law, you thief, also known as the Locksmith, will finally be—"

"Please, call me Harry." The thief interrupted the chief commander, "We've been through a lot, so I think we can get past the pleasantries."

Confused, the sturdy man failed to give an immediate response. The commander wore dense fur-like clothes as his brown hair was being consumed by gray strands.

He ignored the thief and continued. "—brought down for your crimes against common people!"

"My crimes against common people? One of your guards literally murdered a guy for stealing bread!" The thief replied, indignant. "Also, where the hell did you guys come from? Oh no! D-Don't tell me I have fallen into a trap! What could I do?"

"He's part of a rehabilitation program—but yes! Your greedy pursuit of knowledge has brought you to this instant!" The commander spoke proudly. "And unfortunately, for you, rehabilitation is no longer an option. You, the Locksmith, is sentenced to try your luck on the Logos Tower!"

Yes, fucking yes! The Locksmith silently cheered as he awaited the commander to continue.

"A place from legends." He continued as if he had memorized the entire phrase. "Beyond the universe's reach, a Tower that stands taller than mountains and is denser than stars, stuck in a forever stasis of causality. A banned reality where the past and future meet! No heroes or demons have ever left the Logos Tower, so don't think you'll be the first, for the Astronomer who sees it all is a blessing to us, and a curse to your kind."

As if choreographed, the glass behind him exploded in a million pieces as a blue portal took its place. Three obsidian stones circled the portal that stood tall, a mirage to the thief's eyes. They each had symbols that, from afar, were hard to visualize. The complete ritual probably took dozens of scholars. They did all of this for me? He closed his eyes as he smiled and held one of his locks to his chest.

"Wait!" Feeling the tug of the portal, he exclaimed to the sturdy man. "Won't you guys confiscate my journal or locks?"

"Heh..." For the first time, the thief felt intimidated by the man's laugh. "Well, Harry, to your eyes we may be villains, but we're not monsters. Where you're going, you'll need all the help you can get."

Curious, he understood it as just another attempt from the commander to scare him. Or was it? He gave up the thought and stared at the bright blue ahead of him, an enormous energy sucking even the faith out of the church. Fiercely, the guards held on to each other to not get pulled in as well.

"You know what?" Said the Locksmith. "You can have them. Consider it a souvenir." Also, they're empty... And he threw all the locks into the crowd of guards. To their benefit, they didn't explode.

Once the portal closed and the thief, together with all the glass shards and nearby objects, vanished, the guards cheered joyfully. Even the commander showed hints of a smile as his comrades patted him on the back.

Why do I still feel like something's wrong? The commander thought to himself as he remembered the unassuming demeanor of the young thief. We should've hanged him when we had the chance. Although some say the Tower is worse than death...

"Hey, look alive, chief!" A fellow guard said. "You can always do the paperwork later. Come celebrate! Barry and the guys are going to the pub later, you should drop by"

"...You're right!" He complied and got up. "I suppose it wouldn't hurt anyone..."

Lost amidst a myriad of particles and illusory voices, Harry saw only a blue that consumed his tired and lost eyes. Spiraling, he was tossed about as if inside a cosmic bowl, his senses fading as he closed his eyes, fearing he would never open them again.

The afflictions, of course, lay only inside his head. Taking control of his own breath again, he opened his eyes and found what hid beyond the universe: a second chance.

Obscuring the skies, a monolith that spanned hundreds of meters hypnotized Harry. Lopsided, the asymmetry of the tower made contrast to the surrounding vegetation, immaculate in nature. Dozens of windows and doors were placed in weird spots all around the Tower, making Harry wonder their purpose.

Blinding, at the top, a light beamed and irradiated as if it were a second sun. Its brightness increased and decreased on a weird frequency, surely puzzling Harry whose hair danced to the wind. Moss and vines also trickled down the sides of the Tower like a druid's hair.

I'm here... He thought to himself. I'm actually here. Squeezing his hand, Harry felt the velvety texture of the grass around him and realized that a dome covered several hundred meters just around the Tower. Trees cut in half and clouds halved, a dome containing a 24-hour time loop, centered on the Logos Tower.

Standing on a ledge, Harry looked down at the faint path of tiles that led to the tower. The base was made entirely out of rocks and pieces of furniture, melted together as if they had always been there, which was partially true. Amidst the furniture Frankenstein, a simplistic corridor remained. Clean and unencumbered by the shit surrounding the entrance, a door appeared at the end, a few candles lighting the spot.

A prison made only for the mightiest. He felt proud to have made the list, but was even more curious to meet the heroes and demons the world had tried to forget.

With his journal in his pocket, he jumped up, adjusted his glasses and tie, and opened his arms as if praising the Tower.

"Time!" He exclaimed to himself. "We can't get enough of you, can we?"

And so he was ready to reach the top and steal the universe's clock. Or at least get halfway there. Or maybe die trying to get halfway there. Who knows?
