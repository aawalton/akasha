Niala got to her feet, sloughing off disorientation over her first few steps as she approached the unconscious form of David.

Kneeling by his side, she checked his vitals, sighing in relief to find a steady, slow pulse, as if he was sleeping.

She tried to rouse him, even wafting some wakeful potion fumes over his nose, to no avail. She furrowed her brows.

I guess we were in a sort of mind prison. Why did I wake up, and not him? It had to be something related to our link. What happened?

Thudding steps caught her attention. She looked up, toward the way in, and smiled when she saw Jordo half-jogging her way. “Jordo!”

The golem's eye flashed once. “Madam! You wake! Masters Sybil and Sammie are indisposed, just as Sir and Madam were. It is good to see you conscious!”

He came to a stop a few paces from her, leaning forward slightly, his voice tinted with concern. “My sensors cannot determine the source of this affliction. Every reading informs me that you are all healthy and alive. Nothing I attempted seemed to have an effect. Does Madam have an inkling about this predicament? Should I gather everyone and retreat?”

She thought for a moment, letting her eyes fall back to David. “I'm not sure. If I'm right, our souls or minds were pulled out of our bodies. The same way the hummer consciousness was transplanted? I don't think leaving will fix this.”

Jordo's eye dimmed. “This is most concerning, Madam. I am not equipped or calibrated to perform soul or mind operations. I am afraid I am useless.”

She gave him a sad smile. “No, Jordo, you're really useful. You're keeping us safe, and having you around is calming my nerves.”

The stone golem stilled, then bowed. “Then, I shall retrieve master Sybil and Sammie's bodies, and lay them close to Sir, so that I may guard over them more effectively. With your leave?”

She gave him a nod, and thought back on the situation as the automaton departed.

We have the prototype soul spheres, but those were meant to collect disembodied souls, not for living people. I'm not even sure they'll work. I could try a weave, but... that entity is liable to enact its threat if I do so.

She looked up and toward the blackened corridor. Most likely, further down, the actual, physical orchestrator was waiting.

He's waiting...

“Are you still there?”

Even though she was expecting it, she still flinched when the voice boomed out. “I am, as I will be, until my purpose is accomplished.”

Her tail whipped the air as her ears flicked. “Still waiting for me to ask to be informed?”

“I am.”

“You... don't really want to tell me, do you? Because you'll be switched off once you do.”

All she could hear in the following silence was her heart beating. Or maybe that was Jordo's distant steps. Eventually, the voice answered.

“My purpose is eternal. I cannot defy it. You must be informed.”

She stared ahead, then looked down at David, before getting up and dusting herself. Jordo's steps grew louder, eventually reaching her. She looked over her shoulder, watching as he carefully deposited Sybil and Sammie next to David.

Once he was done, he looked up at her. “Madam, what should I do next? How should we proceed?”

“You keep them safe. I think I need to... I need to go have a talk with whatever's ahead,” she replied, turning her head to look down the corridor.

“Madam! My probability matrix is informing me that this course of action is 99.8% certain to result in grievous peril to your well-being! Allow me to escort you! This unit will gladly interpose itself between you and harm!”

“Thanks, Jordo, but I think I'm the last person this thing wants to hurt. It wants to use me.”

“That does not seem like a more desirable outcome!”

She glanced over her shoulder. “I know, but don't worry. If it just wants to talk, I'm good at that. Protect David and the others, ok?”

Jordo lurched, as if wanting to counter, but instead nodded and took a resting pose.

Giving him a small smile, she looked ahead and walked forward, through the circular door, and down the ash-smelling corridor.

Ten minutes later, she was still walking, past the half-wrecked part of the passage where the blast bomb had gone off. She didn't speak, and the voice seemed content to just wait, maybe forever.

Hopefully I'm not on a hidden timer.

There were no lights here; she used the light plate that David had crafted for her, but even at full intensity, it only managed to illuminate a few steps ahead. Yet, the small yellow light up ahead remained visible.

She knew this was stupid, and dangerous, but what else could she do? Taking David and the others away could potentially work, but just as well, the link between their body and their soul could break if they went too far.

That was not a risk she was willing to take.

So, instead, she trudged forward.

“Can you tell me what I'm going to find up ahead?” She eventually asked.

“The orchestrator.”

“Aren't you the orchestrator?”

“I am an Orchestrator. You interfaced with two other Orchestrators before. None of us are the orchestrator, for none of us are all of us, except when we are.”

She frowned. “Right... so, multiple personalities, and none of them consider themselves the main one.”

She didn't get an answer.

I didn't really ask a question, either.

“What was this nation called?”

“Please redefine your question. Which nation?”

“The... the one you were the orchestrator of. We call them the hummers, because of the humming wall. The white people who are after mana-users.”

“This is not a nation. Only the Orchestrator, in its divine duty, matters. Its servants termed themselves the chosen. Their individuality is insignificant. Only their collective actions have meaning, as they serve the Orchestrator's duty.”

She mulled over that piece of information for a bit. “Were any of them willing?”

“Irrelevant. They, as every other transient, exist only as servants to the duty. Some require less conditioning.”

“So to you, they were all... what, tools? Pawns?”

“Irrelevant. To the Orchestrator, their nature matters not. Only their actions have meaning.”

“You're repeating yourself.”

Not a question, so no answer. Her interlocutor was single-minded, it seemed.

“Do you have a lot of... servants, stored?”

“Two hundred and thirty-five thousand, seven hundred and six individuals are currently held within the Orchestrator's soul complex.”

She stopped, eyes wide, as she digested that number.

Over two hundred thousand people. Founding gods.

“Is there a way to release those people? Give them normal bodies?”

The answer didn't come right away.

“Sub-optimal flesh vessels are possible, but inadvisable. Their effectiveness as servants to the duty would be less.”

She resumed walking, frowning at the answer.

It sounds like they'd be artificial bodies, but maybe they can be designed to be close enough to the real thing to not matter... the question is, even if we can get them out, how do we settle two hundred thousand people? That's a small nation's worth!

She kept asking questions as she walked, skirting around what she figured was her “duty”, to avoid being “informed”.

The Orchestrator, whatever it really was, had evidently been created by the dead, or sleeping, god. It was meant to bring it back to life. She had an inkling that the means was to get enough “corruption” concentrated in a single individual. Maybe, once a critical mass was attained, the reaction would fuel itself?

She didn't expect the individual in question to survive the process, and it didn't sound like this particular god would care.

Which, in itself, was a little concerning; she could theorize that there had been a war, in the past, where the gods fought, or at least were involved. One of them fell, but not before putting a contingency in place. A contingency that, apparently, had more than one version.

The hidden vault underneath Montaroyal was probably a copy of this one, except it had been sealed for a lot longer. Thousands of years longer.

And if there was at least one more, who was to say that was it? She asked the voice if there were more Orchestrators hidden in the world, but it said it was the only one.

Maybe the other, or others, were for different gods? Or purposes?

She shook her head. It didn't really matter, right now. She had to get David and the others out of the “soul complex”, which she confirmed with the voice was where they had been sent to.

It didn't know how Niala had managed to escape on her own. It also didn't seem to care. There seemed to be a few cracks in the Orchestrator's programming; apparently, an individual being able to leave the soul complex had never been envisioned, and thus the mind had no response for it. Like a prison where everyone believes it's impossible to escape, so when a prisoner does, everyone instead believes they turned invisible.

Because them being outside simply cannot happen, obviously.

She, eventually, noticed the yellow light ahead getting bigger. Her steps quickened, and her questions stopped, as she broke into a light jog. Soon enough, she emerged in a vast, domed chamber, built out of the same polished off-white stone as the corridor. Big enough to house a few airships, it had a singular feature, dead centre: a large, house-sized yellow crystal, multi-faceted, hovering in place over a shallow divot.

Niala stood at the threshold into the cavern, ears flicking. “Is that... you?”

“It is the Orchestrator.”

“You don't mind me being here, physically? I thought you said I posed a risk.”

“You have no means of material destruction that could harm the Orchestrator. The danger was deemed to be through a soul merge.”

She sniffed, and then took a step forward, muscles tense.

Nothing happened.

After a few more seconds, just to be certain, she walked in fully, toward the crystal. As she got closer, she realized her sense of scale had been skewed by the lack of features; the yellow gem kept getting larger and larger, growing to the size of an airship itself.

Which meant that the dome was much bigger than apparent.

She came to a stop a dozen paces from the crystal, barely able to crane her neck back enough to see its top. “If you informed me, what would the next step be?”

“As you are an anomalous individual, your body would be carefully deconstructed and analyzed. Beneficial traits would be recorded, to be used in new flesh vessels to improve their effectiveness. Your soul would then be implemented in one such vessel, and your mind conditioned to perform your duty.”

Niala frowned. “An artificial body? But... wait. When we found the ascended fel, when I gave him my nega-healing potion, his body turned back to normal.”

She didn't wait for the answer that wasn't coming.

“If he had an artificial body, then my potion... it read his soul, tried to give it back the body it was supposed to have, not the one you gave him.”

That means that my nega-healing potions don't just affect the body. They're able to re-write and re-create mana pathways, including the half-runes within.

She shook her head. Very interesting, but pointless right now. She looked back up at the Orchestrator. “Would there be a way for me to keep my current body?”

“You would have to prove it is superior to a crafted flesh vessel.”

“We already beat one of those, me and David.”

“The Orchestrator is aware. Your ability to consume mana is outside of existing parameters. The manner in which you were afflicted with it will be studied.”

She scrunched her nose. “I wasn't afflicted with it, though. I was born with it.”

“Manaphaging cannot occur naturally. It is an affliction born out of His divine essence collecting within an individual's mana pathways.”

She stared at the crystal.

No, it can definitely occur naturally, and it allows me to burn away your dead god's gunk. That seems like something you'd be against.

Unless... it's like the soul complex thing. Your reasoning feels very rigid, as if your creator was incapable of accounting for cases he couldn't fathom. To you, all magivoring is the same, isn't it?

“Is it possible to burn his divine essence?”

“It is impossible. Such a process does not exist. At most, his essence can be diluted and returned to the aetheric sea.”

She took in a deep breath, and then let it out. “If I told you that I was able to burn his essence, to make it disappear entirely, and consume its power. How would you respond?”

“It is impossible. Such a thing does not exist. You, as an afflicted, received the ability to consume mana to collect his essence. The two functions are inseparable.”

She narrowed her eyes at the crystal.

If it can't understand me being out of its soul prison, or being able to consume the god's essence, I wonder what else it's incapable of understanding?

More importantly, how can I use this?
