"I don't know," she said.

Clive felt his heart drop. It wasn’t no, it wasn’t the answer he’d hope for. But it wasn’t absolute either. I don’t know meant maybe. And maybe was something he could work with.

"Alright," he said.

Jill glanced at him, clearly expecting more. He didn't give her more. He picked up his jacket from the cave floor, shook the dust off it, and stood.

"We should keep moving," he said.

The remaining descent passed in silence.

The cultist trailed at a distance, fanned out around them in a loose protective formation. The path steepened and Jill's pace slowed. She was favoring her left side. She'd been hiding it since the cave, but on the uneven ground she couldn't quite keep the compensation out of her stride.

He fell back until he was level with her. "How bad is it?"

"I've had worse."

"That's not what I asked."

She was quiet for a moment. "It's fine. The healing will finish by morning."

He wanted to push, but he didn't. He knew Jill well. She tolerated concern in small doses and resented it the moment it started to feel like hovering. So he matched her pace and said nothing, and if his hand hovered near her elbow whenever the path got rocky, he didn't call attention to it and neither did she.

He spent most of the walk thinking about Miracles.

He'd met Miracles once. Briefly. She'd seemed strange to him then in a way he hadn't been able to name, and he could name it now.

Certainty was strange, but her strangeness had never frightened him. She was blunt, and childish. But there was something almost honest in that. Something human.

Miracles was different. Miracles had sat cross-legged in a cemetery picking dandelions next to a dead man's headstone, and she'd smiled, and she'd asked a grieving woman if she wanted a miracle in a form of a fourteen-story drop. She was inhuman. An absolute monster.

A few hours later, they reached the bottom of the mountain. The path flattened into a dirt road that wound between bare fields. Clive stopped at the edge of it, surveying his surroundings while his knees reminded him what two hours of mountain descent felt like.

"What now?" he said.

Jill came to stand beside him. She scanned the road in both directions.

"There's a village. Half an hour east." She paused. "We could rest there. But…"

"But?"

She didn't answer immediately. Her gaze had moved past him to where Azura was picking her way down the last switchback.

"Your friend is going to be a problem."

Azura reached the bottom of the path and shook the dust from her scales. She glared at Jill.

"What's wrong with her?" Clive asked.

"The San Dioral army uses dragons. Has for decades. Crown jewel of their military. Every border village between here and the capital has learned what it means when a shadow passes overhead." She glanced at him. "Seeing one standing next to a man is not going to read as casual."

Clive looked at Azura. Azura looked back at him with an expression that managed to communicate both she has a point and I don't particularly care.

"So we don't bring her into the village," he said.

"And leave her outside it? A dragon sitting in a field is not appreciably less alarming than a dragon in the street."

“She could also—” Clive had another idea, remembering Ignis’s human form. "Azura. How close are you? To the human form."

Jill turned to look at him. "She can turn human?"

"Some dragons can. I've seen it."

Not yet. Azura's voice settled into the back of his skull. But close. I can feel the edge of it.

"What does that mean, the edge of it?"

She tilted her head, considering. Our bond. When it deepens, the form follows. That's how it works.

Clive had roughly a hundred questions about what "deepens" meant in practical terms but this didn't feel like the moment. "And what does that need?"

A pause. Azura looked at him with her large amber eyes.

More meat.

He stared at her. "That's it?"

I am also low on meat.

"Those are the same thing."

Yes.

Jill was watching this exchange with a quizzical expression. "Is she—are you negotiating with your dragon right now."

"She wants meat."

"I gathered."

"Apparently that's the path to her human form."

Jill looked at Azura. Azura looked back with a haughty gaze, as if the matter was both urgent and entirely beneath her to explain further.

"There's a butcher in the village," Jill said. She sounded like she couldn't quite believe she was saying it. "I'll see what they have."

I prefer elk, Azura offered.

"She prefers —."

"I don't need to know," Jill said, already turning toward the road. "Tell her we'll see what they have and she'll be grateful for it."

Tell her elk, Azura said again.

Clive looked between Jill and Azura. He considered the village. Warm beds. A meal that wasn't travel rations. Then he considered Azura. Visible from a quarter mile in any direction.

Then he considered Azura. Visible from a quarter mile in any direction. Impossible to explain. A problem that didn't get smaller the closer they got to people.

"Jill."

Jill stopped walking. Turned around slowly. "No."

"You don't even know what I'm going to ask."

"You're going to ask me to go hunting for your dragon."

"She needs to eat before she can take a human form. And we need her in a human form to move anywhere without causing a panic." He spread his hands. "It's practical."

"It's absurd."

"It’ll be fun. Going hunting again. Just like the trip to Central Safari. You remember?"

Something shifted in her face. Just slightly. She did remember it.

"That was different," she said.

"You tracked a kudu for four hours in forty-degree heat and called it a vacation."

"It was a vacation."

"Then this is a vacation. Easier terrain, better weather, and the game's bigger." He glanced at the treeline. "Besides, I've gotten a few elk since I've been here. I know what I'm doing."

Jill looked at the forest. At him. At Azura, massive and patient behind him, pretending not to be part of the conversation.

"She follows at a distance," Jill said. "Far enough not to spook them."

I am very stealthy, Azura offered, to Clive only.

"She says she's stealthy."

Jill looked at the dragon.

"One elk," she said. "And I don't want to hear the word dragon again until morning."

She turned and walked north. Clive fell into step beside her before she could think better of it.

She's funny, Azura said.

He kept that one to himself.

Jill dispatched the cultists to head to the village without them. Maleus lingered until she added something in a lower register that Clive didn't catch, and then he too was gone.

They headed into the forest, tracking mud and broken stem. The light was thin and grey through the canopy. Cold enough that their breath showed.

She stopped at a creek bed. Crouched. Pressed two fingers into the soft bank and studied the depression they left beside the one already there.

"Recent," she murmured. "This morning, maybe."

"How many?"

"Hard to say. At least two." She stood, scanning upstream. "The ground's softer that way. There’s water there."

They moved without speaking.

Jill raised a fist.

He stopped.

Through a gap in the birch ahead, half-screened by brush: a bull elk, head down at the water's edge. Big. Wide rack, still carrying weight this late in the season. Steam rose from its back in the cold air.

Jill glanced back at him. He unshouldered the bow he'd manifested on the walk up, and one for Jill too. She'd already nocked an arrow.

They held position. The elk lifted its head. Dropped it again.

Jill exhaled slowly. Released.

The shot was clean. Clive shot followed a half-second behind. The elk went down in the creek bed without much noise, the water running dark around it.

"You're still shooting to the left," Jill said.

"It landed, didn't it?"

"It always lands. You still aim left."

He looked at her. She was already moving toward the elk, pulling out a knife to cut the elk.

Can I come now? Azura asked, somewhere in the trees behind them.

"Give us ten minutes," he said quietly.

A pause. Fine.

Jill looked up from where she'd knelt beside the elk. "Talking to your dragon?"

"She wants to know if she can come eat."

Jill looked at the elk. "Ten minutes, and it’ll be ready."

"That's what I told her."

She said nothing. Went back to work. But the almost-smirk had settled into something that stayed a little longer this time, and Clive filed that away the same way he'd filed the scar, and the I don't know, and all the other small things he was apparently collecting now.

The forest had gone properly dark by the time they had the meat prepared. Clive had manifested a plates from his sketchbook and laid the portions out while Jill washed her hands in the creek, watching the current take the red away.

Azura came over. She looked at the elk. Then at Clive with an expression of approval.

"Go ahead," he said.

She didn't need to be told twice either.

He sat on a fallen log at the creek's edge and watched her eat. There was something quietly satisfying about it. Jill came to sit nearby. She'd pulled her knees up again, the way she had in the cave. He was starting to think it was what she did when she was actually at rest. It looked like Jill had picked up some new habits over the years.

Yummy. This is so good.

They listened to the forest and the sound of Azura methodically working through an elk.

Then the air around Azura shifted. A green aura started to glow around her. Azura went still.

The shimmer spread across her scales, and then her scales were gone, replaced by something that took a moment to resolve into a person, the way your eyes adjusted to a dark room. A young woman standing in the clearing where the dragon had been, barefoot in the leaf litter, blinking at her own hands with an expression of deep concentration.

She was tall. Dark-haired, amber-eyed—the same amber, exactly. She wore an Azure dress that stopped just above her knees.

She looked up at him.
