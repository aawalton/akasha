Rather than join Ray in stealth, Dirk kept walking forward at a quick but unhurried pace. It was still quite far to the enemies when they noticed the Mirror Rogue. Even so, they did not move until he made it a lot closer. Only then did they lower their spears and charge forward in a soundless charge, both of them heading straight for Dirk. Ray appeared mid-jump out of thin air, behind the skeleton on the right, and slashed with two daggers. Even from a distance, Luke heard the dull clunk of metal on bone. The attempt to sever the monster's head in one decisive strike failed, and it forced Ray to duck under a swipe of the torch before driving a dagger straight up through the bottom of the skeleton's skull, killing it. That left one, and it charged on, as if it hadn't even noticed its friend's demise.

"Isn't he going to do something?" Hannah asked.

Nick looked on with disinterest. "He has."

The skeleton closed the distance to Dirk and took a leap forward as it thrust its spear straight for Dirk's chest with enough force to force it through the rogue's entire body. Rather than fall dead to the ground, Dirk, or the illusion of Dirk, wavered and disappeared into thin air. A moment later, Dirk reappeared by the skeleton's side, grabbing its outstretched limbs, then another Dirk appeared, grabbing its head. A third Dirk followed, this one wielding a short sword which he thrust forward with precision, slamming it into the skeleton's temple, killing it in an instant.

Ray appeared, slow-clapping with a whistle of approval, but stopped when all three Dirks disappeared, only for the man himself to appear right next to the Assassin.

"Not bad!" Ray said, impressed.

Dirk put a hand on his shoulder as the rest of the group caught up, nodding in satisfaction. "Not so bad yourself, but you'll have to work hard if you want to catch up."

"Kage Bunshin no-bastard," Ray muttered, shrugging the taller man's hand off.

"Any loot?" Nick asked.

Dirk shook his head, leaving the skeleton where it had fallen, while Ray bent down to loot the corpse itself.

"What are you doing, you foul little man?" Clementine burst out.

Ray looked up, confused. "Looting?"

"You are desecrating remains!"

He looked at Luke, who shrugged.

"Guess I'm desecrating remains, then," Ray said, glaring back in defiance. "Nun."

Nick sighed. "Calm down, Clementine. You know these are monsters."

She gave no reply, but it was clear to see what she thought of Ray's actions.

As they passed through the large gate, Nick turned to Luke. "Why loot the corpse?"

"Credits," Luke said.

"Really?" Nick asked, turning to give the remaining skeleton corpse a longing look. "A lot?"

"Every little bit counts," Ray said, his voice almost reaching Curtis's level of grumpiness.

The courtyard beyond the gate was different from what Luke remembered, to say the least. Before, there had been rubble everywhere, since the wall was on the brink of collapse, and it had been filled with people and monsters, both fighting and dying. He wondered what'd happened to Sir what’s-his-face and the mage circle that helped Hannah and himself to reach the dragons flying high up in the air. Now, it was empty, void of life. There weren't even any monsters around. Peculiar, that.

"I'm not that short, am I?" Ray asked Luke in a low, worried voice.

Luke gave a quick shake of his head. "Average height, I'm sure."

Ray narrowed his eyes but found no hint of a joke in Luke's expression and soon hurried his steps to take his place at the front of the group, muttering to himself. "At least I'm not fat anymore."

The empty courtyard led to the keep itself, which, to Luke's eyes, looked different. He turned to Hannah. "Doesn't it look a lot bigger?"

"Yeah," she agreed, pointing to a jutting tower. "That wasn't there before."

Ariel looked thoughtful as she scanned their surroundings. "This is this dungeon's future, in relation to your last visit, after all. It isn't strange that it looks different."

She had a point, but it still didn't sit right with him. There was still something about the large structure that told him something was wrong. When they stopped in front of the wide, closed doors of wood, inlaid with banded metal, he realized what had irked him about the place. The new additions looked just as old as everything else. Either they were far, far in the future, to the point that the new construction matched the old, or something else was going on.

"Want me to get the door?" Curtis asked.

Nick stepped up in front of the group, waving his brother back. "I'll handle this, Curtis."

"Hey Nick," Ariel said. "I won't be of much-"

"Poison sucks against undead, I remember," Nick said, not even looking back at her.

"Just making sure."

He reached over to his left hip to draw the sword out of its scabbard before pulling his arm back, like he was about to slash at the wood. "Hup!" he said, doing just that. His arm blurred with the speed of the attack, and the second slash that followed was even faster. Even though he stood nowhere near close enough to strike the gate, the metal screamed in protest as it was sheared through, and the wood of the massive door, at least four times as tall as Nick himself, broke from the upper left corner to the lower right, and then from the lower right to the upper right, a large X carved out of it, deep enough for the whole thing to come apart. It blasted inward with the force of the blow as Luke and the others watched in stunned silence, amazed at the result of such a quick and simple attack.

Once the wood and other debris had settled into a large pile on the ground in front of them and the racket had died down, Luke was first to speak. "That was something."

Curtis scoffed, walking past his brother. "Show off."

Sounds reached out from inside the large hall beyond the now gaping hole where the gate had been. Grunts and low moans, along with the unmistakable shuffling sounds of the walking undead.

"Here we go," Nick said, his grip tightening around his sword. "Remember the plan, and as always..."

"Don't die!" the rest of Phoenix Phalanx, the nun included, shouted.

Following that little ritual of theirs, Nick raced past the rubble without slowing, followed by Dirk, and the two caught up with Curtis as the Reaver brought out his two-handed sword of darkness.

"I'm going too," Ray said, heading into stealth.

Hannah hurried her steps and slipped past fallen timber and jutting bands of iron, calling back to Luke. "I'm trying to get used to close-combat fighting!"

"Ah, the vigor of youth," Clementine said, her voice even but with a hint of amusement.

George got up to walk beside Clementine, just ahead of Luke. "I've told you before, and I'll tell you again. You don't have to talk like you're ancient just because you're a nun."

"I will speak however I wish, George."

"Just leave her be," Ariel said. "She's never going to change."

When they crested the pile of rubble, Luke saw something he hadn't expected. They were all fighting hard, killing a bunch of zombies and skeletons, but it wasn't without serious effort, and both Ray and Nick had sustained injuries. Nothing more than scrapes, but it still showed how even these initial monsters weren't pushovers. More of them poured in from side chambers, like a wave that would break against Curtis, Hannah, and the others.

"I'd better head in there and heal," Luke said, setting off in a quick jog.

Before he made it there, Clementine spoke, each word louder than the last until they reverberated and echoed against the tall stone walls and the ceiling far above their heads. "MINIONS OF SATAN, BOW DOWN BEFORE THE GLORY OF THE ALMIGHTY!"

Most skeletons, and a large chunk of zombies, found themselves bathed in a bright white light without a visible origin. At first, it did nothing, but by the time Luke made it to Ray and stitched his arm together, bones were being bent apart, cracking and popping like a bag of popcorn in the microwave, splinters flying in all directions as the monsters fell to the tiled stone floor, dead or dying.

The wave hadn't quite broken yet, but the short breathing room offered by Clementine allowed the others to reset and ready themselves.

"She doesn't even have to speak, you know?" Dirk said with a smile. "I think she just likes feeling like she's smiting monsters."

"Isn't that what she's doing?" Hannah asked. "Smiting?"

"If the system is God, maybe," Ray said.

Curtis dissolved his blade, turning into several floating orbs of darkness, which he launched at a group of oncoming undead. "Stop yapping and start fighting."

"Incoming!" George shouted, pointing at the wide stairs at the back of the first chamber.

Robed figures were descending, at least thirty of them, their hands clasped before them to show skin. These new arrivals were not undead.

"Lilineese?" Luke asked Hannah, who shrugged.

Either way, from the large spellweave they seemed to be forming together above their heads, these newcomers weren't there to lend them aid.

"Spell incoming!" Luke shouted as the robed figures stopped and their spell completed.

Huge claws split the air in front of the robed figures on the stairs, tearing the air apart, cutting through several threads of the system in this place to reveal something entering from the other side. A creature unlike anything Luke had ever seen before.

Clementine's face had gone white, and she uttered a name. "Abaddon?"
