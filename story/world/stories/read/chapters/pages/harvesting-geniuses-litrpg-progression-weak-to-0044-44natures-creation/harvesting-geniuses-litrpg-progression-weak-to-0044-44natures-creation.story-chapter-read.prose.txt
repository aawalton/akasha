The Eltharok dropped into a sprinting stance and launched itself at Jinyo like a bolt of lightning. But no matter how fast it was, it charged in a straight line. Jinyo leaped off a tree trunk and used it as a springboard to reach the next one. The Eltharok had already learned that Jinyo could maneuver mid-air.

The Eltharok slowed by a few beats. It waited for the moment Jinyo approached another tree before lunging again—denying him any chance to redirect to a different trunk. Its powerful elbow smashed the tree off-balance and sent it toppling. Without stable footing, Jinyo was no different from a bird with a broken wing. All that met his feet was empty air and a hopeless plunge.

I win! The Eltharok's eyes said as much.

Its hoof-fist thrust upward, aimed to crush Jinyo's core. But as Jinyo fell, something yanked him to an abrupt halt in midair. What goes up must come down—yet this wretched human had defied the laws of nature. No—there was something tied to Jinyo's arm.

"Know what this is? It's called liana. The toughest vine in the world. Tougher than nylon!" Jinyo wrenched the vine and swung himself like a pendulum to another tree, then sliced the liana from his arm with his sword to free himself.

If stabbing the lungs and brain hadn't killed it, targeting the heart would be equally pointless. He wasn't a veterinarian, but he was certain no living creature could survive having its head separated from its body. A body that could regenerate to near-immortality—could it really sprout a new head without a brain to command it? Jinyo actually wanted to see that.

The problem was that this was only theory. The Eltharok's neck was as thick as a bull's, and the forms of Song of a Hundred Flowers were designed for piercing organs or severing blood vessels. Cutting clean through a neck required enormous brute strength—something Jinyo simply didn't have.

He'd need a different sword art with more devastating forms, or some other method to inflict greater damage. But sorry—Jinyo was a swordsman. If he didn't use a sword, what else was there? He couldn't even cast a single spell.

Jinyo used a vine to bind his broken arm as tightly as possible. The pain of fractured bones didn't stop him from betting his life on the next attack. Even though he could barely move, if he pushed himself to the absolute limit, he could manage one full-power strike. With his last reserves of strength, he drew the wooden practice sword from his pack and gripped it in his right hand.

Left hand: the Ether blade Sonneteer, a national-class heirloom. Right hand: a battered old practice sword meant for training—that a certain madman had been using to chase off thugs and shoo wild animals from behind his house. Dense Sword Intent enveloped both blades.

'If one slash won't cut through... then two will!'

Jinyo hurled himself at the Eltharok as it cocked its arm to punch. Flower petals swirled through the air, sowing confusion—illusions that blinded the enemy's vision. Jinyo kicked off one tree to the next, launching himself above the Eltharok's head. His eyes locked onto the exposed neck with perfect clarity.

He had to be insane to think he could sever an Eltharok's neck mid-leap using a wooden sword and an Ether blade—one in each hand. In principle, single-handed grip reduced striking power. That was why dual-wielding sword arts were unpopular.

But Jinyo didn't need brute force. He had something better. Sword Intent—razor-sharp, self-refined, growing more powerful with every clash. It compensated for raw strength with the number of strikes and an edge that surpassed any ordinary blade. He swung the first slash with Sonneteer, which channeled Planar energy more efficiently. The first cut severed the carotid artery and nerve tissue all the way to the spine. But it still didn't have enough power to cleave through to the voicebox.

Jinyo brought down the second blade. The wooden sword, loaded with an extraordinary concentration of Psychic Power, sheared through the bone and split the head cleanly from the body. He spun and landed safely—but could clearly see the muscle cells already attempting to fuse back together.

Had it failed? ...No.

Punishment Wave!

The dual-element Planar energy concealed within his prior attacks triggered a devastating delayed effect. The flesh that was trying to mend was severed completely, separating neck and skull for good.

The headless body stood tall. Black, putrid blood erupted in gouts. It stood there for several seconds—trembling, resisting, fighting to survive. The Boost status burned through every last year of its remaining lifespan. Its hulking frame slowly withered into a hollow, empty husk. The only thing still worth looking at was the severed deer skull lying on the ground.

[You have slain Eltharok — Level 30. You have received: plant cultivation ticket (15 hours)]

Done... he'd killed it!

Jinyo nearly hurled both swords away. He was so exhausted he felt like he might die. If decapitation hadn't worked, he couldn't imagine how he would've survived that blood-crazed deer monster. A 15-hour plant cultivation ticket didn't come close to being worth the effort! On top of that, he didn't even receive experience points—Jinyo could only gain EXP from harvesting crops.

Killing a monster outside the Garden of the Sword only yielded half the normal ticket reward. It was a cruelly painful trade. He'd lost the use of one arm and nearly his life, and the reward wouldn't even sprout a bean into a beanstalk!

"But at least now I know how to kill them. Decapitation really is the only way." From observation, the body hadn't died instantly—it tried to regenerate by burning through its future lifespan via Boost. But since it couldn't regrow a head, with no brain to issue commands, it was forced to keep regenerating endlessly until its life force ran dry.

Jinyo wondered if severing a limb would cause it to regrow like a lizard's tail. But he had no intention of testing something so reckless. Just the decapitation had nearly gotten him killed. One of his arms was in agony from forcing it past its limits.

He promised himself that when he got home, he'd focus on building real muscle—something worthy of a man.

Jinyo went to examine the bush the Eltharok had reached into before entering Boost and becoming practically immortal. What he found were purple fruits with inviting, vivid coloring. The plant had pale green leaves with tiny thorn-like splinters, and the leaves reeked with a pungent green smell. Jinyo had seen them countless times on forest treks. They were wild blueberries—common everywhere—except these were so deeply pigmented they looked purple.

[Cursed Fruit] Quality: F+ Status: Boost Dominant Gene: [Boost ★ — 1% chance to trigger Boost status] Properties: [Boost ★★ — +50% growth rate] [Stamina Recovery ★] [Quick Thinking ★] [Accelerated Cultivation ★] [Lifespan Reduction ★★★☆] [Mental Degradation ★☆] [Addiction ★☆]

A fruit cursed by the Witch of the Black Forest. Enchanted with vile dark sorcery. Anyone who consumes it will be claimed by the curse—losing their sanity and plunging into the darkest depths of emotion. Yet a curse is still a form of power. If you desire great power at the cost of your lifespan, then do not hesitate.

The Cursed Fruit reminded Jinyo of the Moonlight Carrot. The Moonlight Carrot was an extremely rare crop with a minuscule chance of appearing, but Jinyo had been lucky enough to eat one. His body had gained the Moonlight status, which triggered explosive physical development.

But these Cursed Fruits seemed to grow everywhere in the Cursed Forest. Jinyo reached out and plucked one. Slick to the touch, with colors that beckoned consumption. Like a poison-coated apple from Snow White—gorgeous, oversized, begging to be tasted—but far more terrifying than any poison.

'If I eat it, my body will develop... right? But if it shortens my lifespan, then it's...' Jinyo couldn't see the value. Yet if he wanted to grow dramatically stronger in a hurry, this was the ideal option. And now—trapped in a place like this—was exactly when he needed to squeeze every ounce of use from the power available to him.

Jinyo carefully surveyed each plant. Whether berries, nuts, or anything else—every single one bore the name "Cursed Fruit" and the Boost status. But the properties and dominant/recessive genes varied from fruit to fruit.

Not a single normal plant existed in this entire forest. Jinyo had no way to avoid Cursed Fruit unless he went back to gnawing tree bark. But the results of his survey sparked an idea.

"System—Bloodline Inference. Filter out recessive genes: Lifespan Reduction, Mental Degradation, and Addiction." Jinyo issued the command.

[Scanning... Current scan range: 300 square meters. No matching produce found.]

"Filter for: Lifespan Reduction 1-star, recessive genes excluding Mental Degradation and Addiction."

[Scanning... No matching produce found.]

"Filter for: Lifespan Reduction 1-star only."

[Scanning... Matching produce found.] The system projected a sensory trace. A line of light—visible only to Jinyo—guided him to the filtered fruit. He ran until he reached a berry bush hidden between two trees. It grew like a parasite, using both trunks as shelter from predators and disaster alike. Jinyo squeezed through a gap narrow enough for a child. There was only a single fruit, cradled in white light.

[Cursed Fruit] Quality: F+ Status: Boost Dominant Gene: [Boost ★ — 1% chance to trigger Boost status] Properties: [Boost ★ — +20% growth rate] [Stamina Recovery ★] [Quick Thinking ★] [Accelerated Cultivation ★] [Lifespan Reduction ★☆] [Mental Degradation ★☆] [Addiction ★☆]

"Excellent! If I filter specifically for low recessive-gene fruit, I can minimize the side effects. I wonder if there are any with zero recessive genes..." The system's scan range was 300 meters. As Jinyo traveled onward, he could keep using the system to filter for Cursed Fruit with even lower side effects. He pocketed the Cursed Fruit—not just the 1-star Lifespan Reduction one, but also other fruits lacking the Mental Degradation and Addiction side effects.

Even if he wouldn't eat them now, Jinyo wanted the fruit for experimental cultivation back in his sacred garden.

Jinyo scanned the area for anyone else from the Training Center but found no one. The gray fog blanketing this eerie forest gave an overwhelming sense of being lost. No matter how loudly he shouted, nobody could hear him. It was desolate beyond measure.

'Honestly, I'd like to find someone familiar. But maybe it's better that I don't.' Jinyo searched for a safe spot. He looked up at the tall trees, found handholds, and climbed branch by branch until he reached the very top. He spotted wild fruits he recognized by species—but when he checked them with Bloodline Inference, every one was poisonous. Species that should never have been toxic under normal circumstances.

Jinyo pulled a fresh Happy Carrot from his pack, which was crammed with agricultural produce, and ate it to sustain himself. He also had thousands of liters of water stored in the copper watering can he'd brought along—clean river water from behind the mountain, guaranteed safe. Not a single fruit or vegetable in the Cursed Forest was safe to eat without side effects. He couldn't trust anything that grew inside this Hollow Gate.

Once he'd eaten his fill, Jinyo immediately retrieved the Tome of Botanical Arts and began reading with urgency. If he hadn't gotten lucky landing Punishment Wave, he wasn't sure he'd still be alive. Punishment Wave was an excellent technique—but it relied too heavily on luck. He needed attacks more devastating than swordsmanship alone could deliver.

Those attacks existed within the Tome of Botanical Arts. Its contents were dense and required interpretation. Even after days of focused study, he still hadn't finished decoding it—but he was close. He was inside a Hollow Gate full of unkillable monsters that could only be stopped by decapitation. The pressure made it hard to breathe. Swordsmanship alone wasn't enough.

Jinyo sat and read in silence. Not a single animal sound reached his ears. The eerie, desolate atmosphere paradoxically granted him the focus to comprehend the book more deeply than ever before. Hours passed. At last, he decoded the entire text.

Jinyo closed his eyes. The thick-bound tome slowly disintegrated into dust, its purpose fulfilled. A hidden implication: no one other than Jinyo was permitted to learn its contents. Even if Jinyo wished to teach someone, he couldn't.

[You have used the Skill Book: Botanical Arts (S)]

[You have learned the spell: Otherworldly Domain (B)] Designate a territory. Cost: 100 Planar per 1 m². The area will replicate the conditions of the Garden of the Sword. Maintenance cost: 0.01 Planar per second per m².

[You have learned the spell: Nature's Creation (A)] A secret art that awakens the spirits of your harvested crops and generates skills from them. Spirits currently available to awaken: 3.

Otherworldly Domain designated a zone and transformed it into an extension of the Garden of the Sword. Beyond boosting Spirit Essence—which benefited Nature Synthesis Technique—it also buffed agricultural equipment and improved monster-hunting rewards. Enormously useful—but not a direct combat ability.

Jinyo turned to Nature's Creation. He focused his mind and dove deep into his spirit.

[Awaken Spirit of Immortal Rice — 20,000 spirit coins]

[Awaken Spirit of Happy Carrot — 5,000 spirit coins]

[Awaken Spirit of Exploding Potato — 5,000 spirit coins]

Jinyo had accumulated plenty of spirit coins from selling his agricultural produce. He had no other way to spend them right now anyway. Jinyo paid the balance in his account. Three spirits emerged from the void, orbited his body, and merged within him.

All three skills were gradeless—meaning they had no upper ceiling. Their power scaled primarily with Nature Synthesis Technique. Rice Grain Bullet was low-cost and had no cooldown. Carrot Weapon and Martyr Potato, however, still had time-based restrictions.

The drawback of these spells was that they required agricultural produce harvested from the Garden of the Sword. Now Jinyo understood why the system had been pushing him so hard to upgrade his land and plant Immortal Rice. It wasn't because the system wanted to buy his rice as quickly as possible—it was because these crops were essential fuel for powers he'd need in the future.

These crops weren't just for eating after all.
