On the morning of the invasion, the Heaven Reaching clan was in high spirits. Everyone was gathered on the rock plain before the mountain, discussing Gary’s fights, Feng Shen’s power, Sui Duang’s death, Elder Sui’s arrest. When mentioning the Muscle Sap ring, many sighed in disappointment for not having taken part.

A few Elders and almost all deacons were present. So were most Birth of Aura disciples and the many Core Formation senior disciples who rarely made an appearance but were still part of the clan. Many had been adventuring in higher floors but had returned for the quest. As for the deacons themselves, most had opted to participate in the quest, though some were bound to their clan duties. Those last few hadn’t come today—the rest were all smiles.

After all, this event was primarily meant for Core Formation cultivators. They would be the ones reaping the greatest rewards.

Feng Shen, Lin Xin, Alicia, Cog… Everyone was gathered at the high plain. With one exception.

Gary was deep in the forest, poring over the book that he couldn’t move from the freaking ground. Small mounds of dirt surrounded him as he flipped through the pages like mad, straining his mind to memorize as many of the early pages as possible. Even the little he’d seen so far marked this book as an incredible technique. Training it was hard but rewarding. If he could practice it for real, his strength would skyrocket, but alas, all he could do was hope he remembered the technique’s first stage.

“Starvein Immortal Body Manual… Damn weights, damn books, and damn the freaking tower,” he muttered, eyes scanning the leather pages.

A yawn came from beside his ear. “Good morning, Gary,” Goldie said.

“It’s only morning to you. I’ve been here for two hours memorizing this dumbass book.”

“But it just dawned.”

“I know.”

“Hmm. Why are you memorizing it?”

“Because I can’t fucking move it, Goldie. The book is stuck down there. I’ll just bury it and return to study it after the invasion quest. Maybe then, I’ll be able to move more than one page.”

“Right,” she said.

The little golden snake floated around Gary for a while, studying him as he studied the book. Wrinkles and veins were visible around his straining eyes. It made him look old.

“Isn’t everyone leaving soon?”

“One hour after dawn. That’s why I want to study this as quickly as possible.”

“Okay, but—”

“Quiet, Goldie. Let me focus.”

She shrugged with her tail. Time passed. Eventually, Gary sighed and closed the book. “Freaking stupid ugly book,” he said.

“Do you remember anything?” Goldie asked.

“A bit, but even the first stage is complex. I don’t know. I think it’s safer not to practice this technique until I can study the book properly. It sucks so hard, but the book mentioned backfiring at the slightest mistake.”

“Hmm.”

“Why do you keep humming at me?”

“I’m just wondering when I can speak.”

“What?”

“You told me to be quiet.”

“Oh. I didn’t mean to be rude. Of course you can speak.”

“Okay! If you want to study the book, why don’t you just put it in a space ring?”

Gary stopped burying the book and turned slowly to face her. “A what?”

“A space ring. You know, those things people use to store stuff. There’s no gravity inside them so they aren’t limited by weight, only by volume.”

“I’m sorry, did you say there are rings that can carry this book?”

“I think so?”

“THEN WHY DID I WAKE UP BEFORE DAWN TO MEMORIZE IT LIKE A DUMBASS?”

“I guess because you’re silly?”

Gary tried to speak, but there were too many words pushing through his mouth. In the end, he just dragged a palm over his face. “The group is about to depart. Where can I find this ring?”

“In artifact stores in the city, I guess.”

“What? There’s no time to reach the city!”

Goldie shrugged.

“Shit,” Gary said. Leaving the book half-buried, he drew Goldie into his spear and bolted for the direction of the event group. Trees blurred past him. Finally, he emerged in the rock plain to find the last of the group jumping over the cliff.

“WAIT!” he roared.

A couple people stopped to look at him. “Hey, it’s Gary Steel,” one said.

“Does anyone have a space ring?” Gary asked.

The cultivators, both at Core Formation, glanced at each other. “Why?” one of them asked.

“I want to buy it!”

“Sorry, I need it to carry my stuff,” one said. The other nodded in agreement and prepared to jump off the cliff.

“I’ll pay double!” Gary cried in desperation.

One of the disciples jumped, but the other paused. “Double? Really?” he said.

“Yes!!”

“Well, I’d be at a disadvantage if I gave you the ring, and I could even lose my life… I’ll give it to you for triple.”

Gary wanted to curse, but he knew he was helpless. “Fine! Triple! How much is that?”

“Really now?” the disciple replied with a frown and prepared to jump.

“Please wait! Yes, really! I have money, just tell me how much it costs! I’m a drafter, I don’t know!”

“How…” the disciple muttered, shaking his head. “They usually go for a thousand aura stones, so triple that is three thousand. If you don’t have the money, don’t delay me…”

“I have it,” Gary said. With pain, he reached inside his robes and tossed three pouches at the senior disciple, who caught them mid-air. After opening them, his eyes widened. He reached for a discreet ring around his index finger and tossed it to Gary.

“You can keep the contents,” he said, pocketing the pouches and dashing after the group.

Gary cursed for a third time, grabbed the ring, and bolted into the forest.

“I knew it was weird everyone wore rings!” he said.

“There are bracelets, too, and all sorts of jewelry. My father had a space backpack.”

“Please don’t tell me they’re cheaper.”

“Nope, rings are the cheapest.”

“Thank God.”

Gary reached the clearing and sighed in relief when the book was still there. “Okay. How do I use this thing?” he asked.

“Just slip it on and activate it with your aura,” Goldie said.

Gary did as instructed. The ring was a little small on his index finger, but a moment later, it magically reformed to fit him. It was gray, metallic, and thin enough to pass unseen.

As soon as his aura probed the ring, it was sucked into a spherical space surrounded by swimming colors. “The hell?” Gary said.

“That’s an astral space! It’s extra-dimensional,” Goldie said with pride.

“Why is there underwear inside it?”

Ten sets of underwear were neatly folded inside the astral space, which was only three feet in diameter. Besides that and various changes of clothes, the ring was empty.

“A danger to his life, my ass,” Gary muttered. “How do I put the book in?”

“Just touch it and use aura to link it to your ring,” Goldie replied.

Gary did as instructed. To his absolute shock, the multi-ton book was warped and easily sucked into the ring. In his mind, he saw it appear in the astral space and float next to the senior disciple’s underwear.

“Fucking hell,” he said. Then, without a second thought, he turned around and sprinted. He exited the forest, jumped over the cliff, then entered the other forest neighboring the Shores of Glory.

The distances involved weren’t trivial. Gary was at least fifteen minutes behind the group, and he had to catch up quickly or he’d be in trouble. Many clans would be headed the same way, and some would be glad to rid the Heaven Reaching of a promising disciple in the wild where no one would know.

Gary sprinted like his butt was on fire. Trees blurred past him. He jumped onto the road, surprising a couple merchants, then dove into a left turn and resumed sprinting. The Stairs of Ascension were two hours away, but everyone else was running too. Covering the gap wouldn’t be easy.

When he finally saw them, sweat was dripping from his brows. Gary made a final push to reach the back of the group, panting heavily. The disciples there gave him odd looks. Among them, Gary saw the two senior disciples he’d talked to at the cliff. The one who’d given him the space ring was also sweaty. His finger held another space ring.

“Did you run into town to get it?” Gary asked.

“So what?” the senior disciple replied.

Gary was beginning to dislike this man. The two thousand aura stones he’d overpaid were the equivalent of a normal person’s twenty monthly wages… At least, he had more stones.

The tournament rewards had netted him four thousand, and the Muscle Sap operation another eight. Minus the three he’d just given the senior disciple, Gary carried nine thousand on his person, which he’d split into pouches of a thousand each.

Thinking that far, Gary touched the pouches with his aura and sent them all into the space ring. They’d been rather inconvenient to carry.

At least, now I know about space rings… he thought. Goldie’s not to blame. I’ve been watching everyone pull stuff out of thin air since the Floor of Trials. I should have asked.

Once Gary recovered a bit, he accelerated to catch up with Alicia and Cog.

“How are you tired already?” Cog asked.

“You don’t want to know,” Gary replied.

“But I want to!”

“You know what? You’d actually love to know this, but I’m not telling.”

“Goldie, please tell me!”

“Sorry Cog, I’m with Gary.”

“Did you oversleep?” Alicia asked.

“The opposite.”

“Well, I’m just glad you made it in time. With all these hostile clans coming out, it wouldn’t be fun to run by yourself.”

Gary had to agree. The Heaven Reaching clan had allies among the major clans, but also enemies. Even those who were neutral had been divided by the invasion quest. Gary’s clan had sided with the Gymnasium, but most had gone with the puppeteer. In invasion quests, the attacking side usually won.

Which made it a question why any clan would side with the gymnasium. Gary didn’t know the answer, but he was glad it happened.

As they jogged down the road, more clans arrived from side-roads. Gary saw the wizards of the Deep Blue Academy arrive alongside skinny people in dark hoods and green auras. What looked like a Roman legion marched onto the street, waving yellow rustic banners, while a bunch of colorful individuals rode a collection of equally colorful mounts. Gary saw a clan of swordsmen and one of chanting priests.

Besides clans, there were also smaller groups, like a collection of broody cyborgs or a gaggle of merchants. The Witch Coven arrived at the Heaven Reaching’s side, the two factions almost blending together.

“Asim!” Gary cried out.

“Hey guys. This is Delilah,” the younger man said, accompanied by a beautiful woman. Gary spotted that many other beautiful women—who made up the majority of the clan’s disciples—threw her dirty glances.

“Pleased to meet you,” Gary said.

She gave a bewitching smile. “Likewise! I’ve heard so much about you!”

“Only good things, I hope.”

“Mostly, but I liked the bad ones better,” she said with a wink that threw Gary’s heart on a wild ride.

“Delilah,” Asim said strictly, and her overwhelming charm disappeared. She greeted the rest, too.

Each clan carried around two hundred people, making for thousands overall. Behind them and in the distance was the crowd of unaffiliated climbers coming for the quest, who were individually weaker than clan disciples but easily outnumbered them.

The clans walked together, not quite mixing, until they entered the vast black mountain that housed the Stairs of Ascendance. Once there, the Elders accompanying each clan conversed, and they walked up the stairs in order. Gary’s clan was one of the last.

He marveled as he watched thousands of talented cultivators vanish into the next level. When it was their turn, he noticed that Feng Shen walked with new aura—he’d broken through after their fight. Gary shook his head.

Figures…

After they finally made it up, they emerged in a swamp that brought back memories. It was empty, though. Only regular monsters populated it, not tentacle ones, and they were few and far between. Their still recovering population was butchered by the Elders who headed each clan, letting their many disciples proceed undisturbed. Climbers flooded the swamp.

Eventually, they reached another black mountain. This one made Gary pause. It would be the first time he visited the fourth floor. He would climb yet again. It made him proud.

Each clan had been teleported to a different location by the Stairs of Ascension, but over the next few hours, everyone arrived. They camped outside the black mountain. Only minutes remained until the event began, and most took the time to gather themselves before the battle that inevitably lay ahead.

Timer to Invasion Quest: 00:00:00:00. Happy warring!

Finally, the Elders gave the signal. The clans walked into the mountain and one-by-one disappeared up the stairs. Gary did, too.

“Let’s gooo!” Goldie chirped.

The fourth floor waited.
