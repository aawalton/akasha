Percy’s conflict with House Asclepius had already lasted far longer and undergone more twists and turns than he could have predicted, though perhaps he’d been a fool to think that bringing down a Great House led by a White-cored patriarch would have been easy.

Regardless of how they got here, Percy could tell that the proverbial walls were finally closing in on him.

His alliance’s growth had been crippled, while Machaon’s side was free to expand to the rest of the continent. Percy’s territory had shrunk to just the immediate surroundings of the Fungal Spire, as his forces could no longer defend any land beyond that. To make matters worse, the Ruby Empress was now gone, and the hive was barely under his familiar’s control.

Percy didn’t know when, but his enemies were bound to attack at some point. It might be this year – especially if they found out about the White wasp’s demise before Nephthys fixed the problem – or it might be when the enemy alliance’s growth hit its ceiling. No matter what, the Great Houses wouldn’t leave someone as dangerous as Percy alone indefinitely.

Next time they fought, there would be no ceasefire, nor anywhere to run to. It would be a battle to the death.

Percy had yet to figure out how to improve his familiar’s mutations or weaponize the swarm, so he simply did what he did best and dove back into training.

About a month after the death of the Ruby Empress, he finally mastered the Dance with his third core. The external flow took the form of an omnipresent halo that softly shrouded his body and allowed him to exchange mana with his surroundings. The orange glow couldn’t easily be concealed, so Percy would have to deactivate the external part of his boosting art if he went out in public.

Or he could just stay locked up inside his little cave, which was his preferred solution to such problems.

He began working on his artificial advancement immediately which, along with his relentless core-cleansing with Gloomy Dawn three times a day, led to a pair of interesting notifications about a month later.

[Congratulations! Your mana core has advanced: Red -> Orange!]

[Congratulations! Your mana core has advanced: Orange -> Yellow!]

“It’s done,” he exhaled in relief upon completing his second promotion.

His next natural advancement was over eight years away, but bringing his third core within a single grade of the others was enough to change a lot of things. It gave him two more ethereal cords and mental pockets to work with, while also increasing his hypothetical capacity for aspects to six. More importantly, he was able to pour nine times as much mind mana into Supercharged Cognition, which greatly amplified all the benefits it provided.

Micky couldn’t produce more than a hundred sentries without butchering their sensory domains, but the radius of each bubble of information expanded to eleven and a half kilometres so long as the Huehuan kept the size of his army within its previous limits.

Percy’s brewing yield rose to sixty-nine percent for regular elixirs – which was admittedly a smaller increase than he would have liked. Then again, this was precisely the halfway point between Phoebe’s old peak as a demigod and her current peak as a titaness – while Percy was merely at Green – so he shouldn’t be complaining.

His third core also started contributing to his physical strength and lifespan in a meaningful way. The effect was much smaller than that of his Green cores, but it was no longer negligible, which made him feel slightly better about the losses he had incurred upon activating the Void Decree.

The most important change by far, was to the rate by which he generated his dream mana.

When he had first discovered the presence of the strange resource inside his channels, he had been able to produce a single droplet every week. Repeated practice had accelerated the production to a droplet every five days, while his promotions had raised that number first to a droplet per day and, now, to five droplets per day.

Percy still had no idea what the new mana type could be used for, but he had finally accumulated enough to conduct more tests. The pair of soul and mind pathways that he had reserved for storage were almost saturated, so he had to start consuming his dream mana, lest he wasted valuable generation time or clogged up enough channels to interfere with his boosting art.

‘I should send some to Nephthys,’ he decided.

Percy had more than enough dream mana for his own experiments, and the reincarnated mind deity might be able to shed some light onto its purpose. At the end of the day, the pink mana was a variant of the soul affinity, as well as capable of circulating within the corresponding channels, so he should also be able to transfer it through the cord.

Except that when he tried to send the first droplet over, the cord itself reacted somewhat strangely.

The dream mana entered the magical tube without issue, but seeped into the ethereal walls and got devoured before it could travel very far. Shockingly, the cord’s diameter doubled as a result.

‘What the hell?!’ Percy raised an eyebrow.

He could believe that his new mana type was quite compatible with his cords, but wasn’t the effect rather exaggerated? The pink resource consisted of Green soul mana and Yellow mind mana, and a single droplet didn’t contain much of either.

Did his blessings also play a role?

Whatever the case, Percy eagerly sent a second droplet into the magical tube and observed the changes. His cord swallowed the dream mana as if it were a droplet of moisture seeping into dried dirt, though it only expanded by around seventy percent of its original width. Its size was clearly approaching some kind of upper limit.

Percy fed it a third droplet, which only caused the cord to widen by another thirty percent of its base, tripling its original diameter.

The fourth droplet wasn’t absorbed at all. It flowed through the cord unimpeded, reaching Nephthys and hovering uselessly inside her soul the very next moment.

Interestingly, the cord’s expansion didn’t just allow mana to flow through it more easily. It also strengthened Percy’s mental connection to his familiar and the clone possessing her. Their thoughts spilled into his brain like a chorus of disorganized whispers, reminding him of his experiments with his first mind clone.

‘Should I send it back through the second cord?’ Nephthys asked, her voice echoing inside Percy’s skull like a thunderstorm.

‘Go ahead,’ Percy replied with a wince as he sent her three more droplets through the first cord. Sharing their unfiltered thoughts like this was quite disorienting, but he couldn’t help his curiosity.

The connection he shared with Nephthys and the clone wasn’t a simple one, as it involved a pair of empowered cords twisted together into a stable, two-way cable. The first three droplets had only strengthened the first half, while the next three were absorbed by the other cord.

The seventh droplet completed the entire loop without being consumed, returning to Percy’s soul.

[Congratulations! You have mastered a new spell: Secret Art: Dream Harmony – Masterful!]

Dream Harmony…

That sounded like a direct upgrade of Percy’s old Soul Harmony spell, though Phoebe’s Decree had registered it anew since the original entry had already become part of his Ultimate Art.

The new spell was practically useless in its current state, since the chaotic connection would probably hurt his ability to coordinate with his familiar and clone more than it would help, though Percy was confident that he could fix it with another mindset.

Leaving its obvious flaws aside for now, he and Nephthys spent the next several hours merely observing the changes to their connection without doing anything. They didn’t think that the cord’s empowerment would be permanent, and they needed to understand how much dream mana it would cost to maintain it.

Percy was simultaneously surprised, annoyed, and elated that the cords didn’t shrink again until around thirty hours later. Spending over a day listening to the others’ inadvertent rambling without the ability to sever their connection had been a painful experience, but he was glad that his dream mana was so potent because of how difficult it was to produce.

‘Each cord only needs less than three droplets per day to remain perpetually strengthened…’ he concluded.

With his current output, he could permanently strengthen two of his cords. If he kept a mental pocket practicing affinity fusion for another decade and his next promotion accelerated his production as much as the last two, he might have enough dream mana to empower all his soul clones by the time his third core caught up.

For now, the best use of his time would be to design a new mindset to harness the strengthened cords.

Percy had a hunch that he was about to completely revolutionize the way he interacted with both his soul clones and his interstellar adventures.
