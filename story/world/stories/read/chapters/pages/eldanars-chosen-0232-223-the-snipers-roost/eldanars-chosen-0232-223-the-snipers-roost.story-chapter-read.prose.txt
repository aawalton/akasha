The freezing, corrupted mud of the ditch offered absolutely no comfort, but it was the only thing keeping the four assassins alive.

Perberos lay completely flat against the frozen earth, his face pressed mere inches from the foul-smelling, stagnant black water pooling at the bottom of the trench. Ronald was positioned directly to his left, the beastkin completely motionless beneath his muted green cloak. Garth and Silas were pressed tightly against the steep, rocky incline behind them. They had hastily covered themselves with dead branches and rotting moss, praying to whatever gods were listening that their scent would be masked by the overpowering stench of the surrounding decay.

They were less than ten yards away from the edge of the old northern trade road.

For the past two hours, they had not moved a single muscle. They had not spoken a word. They had simply endured the psychological torture of watching an extinction-level event march directly past their hiding spot.

The sheer, physical noise of the horde was deafening, a continuous, grinding roar that vibrated deep within their bones and rattled the loose stones of the ditch.

First came the chaotic, writhing ocean of the lesser infantry. Tens of thousands of reanimated zombies and disfigured ghouls dragged their feet through the freezing mud. They were a mindless, shrieking tide of bruised, purple flesh and rotting limbs. The smell was completely suffocating, a thick, oily cloud of stagnant grave water and old copper that burned the back of Perberos's throat and made his eyes water. The zombies did not march with any discipline. They stumbled and lurched, bumping into one another, driven forward solely by the relentless, invisible tether of dark magic pulling them towards Ashenfall.

Following the endless tide of rotting meat came the terrifying, mechanical precision of the skeletal ranks.

The tonal shift in the noise was jarring. The hollow shrieking of the ghouls was replaced by the deafening, rhythmic clanking of rusted iron and heavy boots. Thousands of skeletal warriors marched shoulder to shoulder in flawless, unnatural lockstep. They carried rusted halberds, broken spears, and heavy iron maces. Among their ranks lumbered the massive, corrupted beasts of the forest. Skeletal dire bears and rotting trolls draped in heavy iron chains walked alongside the infantry, their heavy footfalls shaking the frozen earth and threatening to collapse the fragile walls of the trench hiding the assassins.

Perberos kept his luminous silver eyes focused on the dark road, his breathing incredibly shallow. He was actively maintaining his shadow integration, allowing his physical form to blur and meld with the darkness of the ditch. The magical exertion was draining his stamina, but he did not dare drop the camouflage.

The endless sea of bone and rusted iron seemed to stretch on for an eternity. The sheer numbers defied all logical sense. It felt as though the entire northern province had been emptied of its dead and gathered into this single, crushing column.

Then, the ambient temperature in the ditch plummeted violently.

The freezing winter wind suddenly died away, replaced by a still, suffocating cold that pierced straight through their heavy leather armour and settled deep into their chests. The thick, necrotic fog rolling over the road turned an icy, translucent blue.

The deafening clanking of the skeletal ranks faded, violently replaced by a heavy, terrifying silence.

Perberos narrowed his eyes, peering through the tangled dead roots bordering the trench.

Marching at the absolute rear of the main horde was the final phalanx. This was not a mindless collection of zombies or fragile skeletons. This was the elite guard, and they radiated an aura of dark magic so dense it felt like physical gravity pressing down on the hiding assassins.

There were perhaps two hundred of them, marching in a tight, impenetrable square formation. They were Wights, long-dead knights of ancient, forgotten kingdoms, raised from their deep barrows. They were entirely clad in massive, heavy suits of blackened full plate armour. The metal was not rusted like the weapons of the lesser infantry. It was pristine, absorbing the ambient light and reflecting a chilling, spectral blue aura. Tattered, freezing tabards bearing the heraldry of ruined noble houses hung over their breastplates. They carried massive, perfectly forged greatswords and heavy tower shields.

The Wights did not make a single sound as they moved. Their heavy steel boots struck the freezing mud in absolute, terrifying silence, floating just a fraction of an inch above the ground. Bright, piercing pinpricks of icy blue soul-fire burned brightly from within the dark visors of their heavy helmets.

The predatory malice radiating from the elite guard caused Garth to let out a tiny, involuntary whimper of pure terror. The grizzled human tracker bit down hard on his own lip, drawing hot blood to stop himself from making another sound. Silas squeezed his eyes shut, clutching the wooden holy symbol beneath his armour with trembling fingers.

They all knew the horrific truth. If a single one of those Wight knights turned its helm towards the ditch, the four of them would be slaughtered in a matter of seconds. They would not survive for five minutes against a phalanx of that calibre.

But as terrifying as the elite guard was, they were entirely eclipsed by the entity walking at the absolute centre of their formation.

Perberos felt his heart skip a heavy, painful beat as he laid eyes on the boss monster.

The Bone General was an absolute monstrosity. The creature stood easily seven feet tall, towering completely over the massive Wights surrounding it. It was a skeletal figure, but its bones were thick, dense, and unnatural, fused together with dark, pulsing magic. It wore an incredibly heavy, ornate suit of ancient plate armour that looked as though it had been forged in the depths of a nightmare.

The dark metal of the General's armour was covered in jagged, deeply etched runic symbols. The runes were not dormant. They pulsed and flared with a sickly, toxic green light, casting long, eerie shadows across the frozen earth and illuminating the surrounding fog. The magical radiation pouring from the creature was staggering, actively warping the air around its massive frame.

Resting casually upon the General's broad, spiked shoulder guard was a heavy iron broadsword. The weapon was absurdly large, nearly as tall as the monster itself. The blade was thick and jagged, dripping with a constant, viscous stream of dark necrotic energy.

The entity did not walk. It rode atop a massive, heavily armoured skeletal warhorse, its glowing green eye sockets staring straight ahead towards the distant city of Ashenfall.

Perberos stared at the General, his tactical mind rapidly recording every single detail of the creature's armour, seeking weak points and gaps in the heavy plating. The dark magical tether anchoring the entire ten-thousand-strong army was clearly originating from the swirling vortex of energy burning within the General's chest cavity.

The elite guard and their terrifying commander slowly passed the hidden trench, their chilling silence leaving a heavy, suffocating wake of dread behind them.

Finally, the rearguard approached. A loose, chaotic pack of several hundred skeletal hounds and a few dozen Crypt Weavers scuttled along the road, ensuring there were no stragglers and guarding the vulnerable rear flank of the army.

A massive, rotting dire wolf paused near the edge of the road, directly above the trench. It lowered its fleshless snout to the frost, sniffing the freezing air intently. Its glowing green eyes darted towards the dead branches covering the assassins.

Ronald’s hand slowly, silently drifted towards the hilt of his hunting dagger. Perberos readied a throwing knife, his muscles coiled with explosive tension.

The hound took a single, slow step towards the ditch.

Then, a sharp, hollow shriek echoed from a Crypt Weaver further down the road. The skeletal hound snapped its head up, abandoning the scent, and trotted quickly away to rejoin the moving pack.

The four assassins remained perfectly still in the freezing mud for another fifteen agonising minutes, ensuring the absolute rear of the horde had completely faded into the distant fog.

When the silence of the plains finally returned, Ronald let out a long, ragged breath. The beastkin slowly pushed the dead branches aside and sat up, his muted green cloak completely soaked with foul-smelling sludge.

"Light preserve us," Silas gasped, pulling himself out of the muck. The veteran ranger was shaking so violently his teeth were audibly chattering. "I have never felt anything like that. The cold... it felt like it was freezing my heart solid. Those were Wights. And that General... it is a high-tier dungeon boss walking freely on the surface."

"It is a walking extinction event," Garth grunted, wiping a thick layer of mud from his heavy crossbow. The tracker looked entirely hollowed out by the experience. "Our arrows are going to bounce right off that runic armour. We cannot kill that thing, Ronald. We need to turn back."

"We are not turning back," Perberos stated coldly.

The elven scout rose smoothly from the trench, shaking the freezing water from his leather armour. His ethereal, aristocratic face was completely devoid of fear, set into a mask of pure, lethal determination.

"The armour is heavy, but it is not impenetrable," Perberos noted, his brilliant, analytical mind dissecting the target. "There are gaps at the neck joints, beneath the shoulder guards, and the eye sockets of the helmet are completely exposed. If I can put an armour-piercing bodkin directly through the glowing green core in its skull, the runic shielding will collapse."

Ronald nodded slowly, his jade-green eyes locking onto the elven ranger. The beastkin understood the horrific odds, but he also understood the tactical necessity of the strike.

"We trail them," Ronald commanded quietly, gesturing towards the churned, ruined earth of the trade road. "We keep entirely to the shadows. We maintain a safe distance from the rearguard, and we wait for the horde to engage the city walls. When the fighting starts, the General will be focused entirely on the siege. That is when we take the shot."

They climbed out of the freezing trench, leaving the sparse, dead treeline behind them.

Tracking the massive army was the easiest task Garth had ever performed in his long career. The horde had completely obliterated the old dirt road, churning the frost and stone into a wide, deep river of freezing black mud. The suffocating stench of decay lingered heavily in the air, creating a clear, foul-smelling path that cut straight across the plains towards Ashenfall.

They moved with terrified caution. They stayed far off the main path, using the rolling hills and the scattered, rocky outcroppings of the plains to mask their approach. They communicated entirely through hand signals, keeping their weapons ready for any stragglers or hidden traps.

The pale, bruised purple light of the approaching dawn slowly began to bleed into the eastern sky, illuminating the horrific scale of the destruction left in the army's wake. The creeping black corruption was spreading rapidly from the muddy footprints of the horde, actively rotting the frost-covered grass of the plains.

They trailed the army for hours, the slow pace somehow draining their stamina as it froze their limbs, until they finally reached the edge of the vast, open valley containing Ashenfall.

Ronald threw his fist up, signalling a dead halt.

They crept to the crest of a high, rocky hill covered in dense, dead pine trees, dropping onto their stomachs to peer down into the basin.

The city of Ashenfall looked like a fragile, wooden toy sitting at the bottom of the valley. The massive timber walls, usually so imposing, looked incredibly vulnerable against the vast, open plains.

And arrayed directly before those walls was the apocalypse.

The entire ten-thousand-strong undead horde had ground to a perfect halt just outside of extreme bow range. The sheer density of the formation was staggering to behold from a distance. It was a solid block of dark steel, bone, and rotting meat that completely swallowed the natural landscape.

The eerie, unnatural silence of the halted army was deeply unsettling, a psychological weapon designed to completely break the morale of the defenders lining the tiny wooden ramparts in the distance.

From their high vantage point, they had a perfect, unobstructed view of the entire battlefield.

"They are waiting," Silas whispered, peering through the branches of a dead pine tree. "They are trying to terrify the militia into breaking ranks before they even charge."

Perberos ignored the massive sea of infantry at the front. His luminous silver eyes scanned the rear of the formation, searching for the sickly green glow of the runic armour.

He found the Bone General stationed far back from the front lines, surrounded by the impenetrable square of the Wight elite guard and a thick ring of massive Crypt Weavers. The boss monster was positioned perfectly. It was far out of range of the archers and mages stationed on the city walls, keeping itself entirely safe from stray arrows or magical bombardments, yet it remained close enough to maintain the dark magical tether commanding the horde.

Crucially for Perberos, the General had halted near the eastern edge of the formation, bringing it dangerously close to the dense, corrupted treeline bordering the valley.

"There," Perberos pointed a pale finger. "He is staying in the rear. He is using the lesser infantry as a meat shield."

Down in the valley, the eerie silence was finally broken.

The Bone General raised its massive, rusted broadsword high into the air. The perfect, static formation of the army violently fractured. The heavily armoured skeletal knights and the towering Bone Goliaths held their ground, acting as a solid wall. The chaotic, writhing mass of lesser zombies and ghouls was thrust to the very front, letting out a unified, hollow shriek as they launched into a clumsy, heavy-footed charge towards the city gates.

"The battle has begun," Ronald noted grimly.

The sky above Ashenfall turned completely black for a fraction of a second as thousands of heavy arrows were released from the ramparts. The deadly volley fell like rain into the charging zombies. Immediately following the arrows came the magical bombardment. Balls of searing fire, jagged bolts of lightning, and massive shards of ice blew deep craters into the charging lines.

Through his enhanced vision, Perberos watched the magical explosions rocking the front lines. He saw a massive, continuous torrent of roaring, white-hot plasma erupt from the central gatehouse, completely engulfing the pitch-filled trenches and turning the approach into an absolute inferno. He knew instantly that it was Brett, unleashing his terrifying thermal energy.

But the sheer numbers of the horde were completely overwhelming the magical defence.

The zombies simply did not stop. They pushed through the roaring flames, ignoring the arrows protruding from their chests. They threw their burning, shattered bodies directly against the base of the high timber walls.

Perberos felt a cold, sharp spike of absolute horror pierce his heart as he realised exactly what the mindless infantry was doing.

They were not trying to scale the walls with ladders. They were piling up. Hundreds of zombies were throwing themselves into the roaring flames and against the solid timber, allowing the crushing ranks behind them to scramble wildly over their ruined bodies. They were actively building a horrific, writhing ramp of rotting flesh, intending to simply walk directly over the parapets.

Perberos dragged his gaze up the towering timber wall, his silver eyes searching frantically through the thick smoke and falling ash.

He found exactly what he was looking for, and it made the blood freeze entirely in his veins.

Standing on the reinforced wooden platform directly above the main iron portcullis, right at the absolute epicentre of the zombie assault, was a massive, towering figure clad in heavy steel plate armour, holding a dark heater shield. And standing directly behind the human Guardian was a flash of brilliant, unmistakable light.

Carcan’s silver-white hair was shining brightly, illuminated by the intense, pulsing golden light of her signature healing magic. She was weaving her spells right on the front line, exactly where she had promised to be.

Perberos watched as the writhing pile of burning zombies began to slowly, relentlessly rise towards the parapet where his sister stood.

The cold, tactical detachment of the scout vanished, violently replaced by a fierce, desperate, protective rage. He could not wait for the perfect opening. He could not wait for the battle to thin the ranks. If that ramp of flesh reached the top of the wall, the heavy fighters would be entirely overwhelmed by sheer bodily mass.

"I am moving into position," Perberos announced, his voice tight and ringing with absolute urgency.

He slid backward out of the brush, breaking away from Ronald and the others. He moved swiftly through the dead pine trees, sprinting along the ridge until he found exactly what he needed.

A massive, ancient oak tree stood at the very edge of the steep hill, its thick, dead branches extending far out over the sloping drop of the valley. The tree offered a commanding, elevated angle looking down directly at the rear flank of the undead army.

Perberos did not hesitate. He scaled the rough bark of the massive oak with impossible, liquid agility, pulling himself high up into the thickest cluster of dead branches. He found a solid, sturdy fork in the wood and planted his boots firmly, securing his footing.

He was incredibly high up, exposed to the howling winter wind, but the vantage point was absolutely perfect.

He looked down through the scope of the dead branches. The Bone General and the Wight elite guard were stationed roughly two hundred yards away, sitting directly below his elevated roost. It was an extreme, punishing distance for a bow, especially with the high winds whipping across the valley, but Perberos was not a normal archer.

He slowly pulled his beautiful, curved elven longbow from his shoulder.

He reached back to his magical quiver, his long, elegant fingers completely bypassing the standard ammunition. He grasped the smooth fletching of a heavy, solid-steel armour-piercing bodkin, pulling the deadly shaft free.

Perberos took a deep, slow breath, letting the freezing air fill his lungs.
