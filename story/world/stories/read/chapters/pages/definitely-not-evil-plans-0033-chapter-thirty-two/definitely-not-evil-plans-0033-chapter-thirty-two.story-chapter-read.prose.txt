Wednesday November, 14

Scott texted every day. It was a good distraction from my Greg destruction. But every time my phone chimed with a new text I found myself unsure of who I really wanted to be on the other side.  He sent a picture of Jay-C, the stuffed alien ‘reading’ Throne of Light. He sent a complaint about missing his house. Apparently, he was staying at a long term Air B&B while he handled this contract. His actual house was only about an hour's drive but he said the commute each day just wasn’t worth it.

He sent me a risque faerie meme that made coffee come out of my nose.

Me: You’re actually reading it?

Scott: Yeah. Aurora is just now finding out her best friend was a secret princess and is not even human.

Me: Oh wow, you’re still early.

Scott: Hey! I was proud of being 47 pages in.

Me: I mean I didn’t expect you to even get that far…

Scott: Is it my giant muscles? I know they give me kind of an illiterate vibe.

Me: your giant muscles… 👀

Scott: I’m offended…

Scott: [photo.jpg]

Me: Is that Jay-C under your shirt sleeve?

Scott: Whaaaaaaat. Never. These are all natural.

Me: Sure sure.

Scott: Would you like to grab dinner tonight?

Me: hmmm convince me.

Scott: Italian place. Garlic bread… good red sauce.

Me: You had me at garlic bread.

I had three hours before dinner. Plenty of time to do some light messing with an attorney. He was just about scared off of the case, one more incident would probably do it. I was watching his email and found my opening.

An email came in from Greg Harrison Jr.

Attorney Light,

I was under the assumption you are able to get the retraction posted. I’ve been heeding your advice and staying out of the public but every day that goes by is one more day my reputation is being damaged. What is our plan and timeline to make this go away? I am insisting that you get the news to stop reporting on this today.

As Attorney Light began typing a response I opened a tab on his computer, pulling a video up over top of his email. He closed the tab, but I reopened it. I played a song from a sitcom called Crazy Ex Girlfriend. The song I chose, Don't Be A Lawyer.

He tried to close out the tab, he tried to turn down his volume. I didn’t allow either to happen. When the video ended I closed out the tab and relinquished control back to the attorney. The mouse didn’t move for several minutes and then he began the email.

Mr. Harrison Jr,

After careful consideration, our firm has decided to withdraw from representing you in this matter, effective immediately.

Sincerely,

Atty Light

TTK & Associates

He pressed send and I actually jumped up and cheered. I disconnected from his machine. It was time I left him alone. Greg had the night to stew, and I had a date to get ready for.

I found myself staring at my closet and not having any idea of what to wear. I went back and forth between the new clothes I had gotten and a blue sweater that was my comfiest sweater, but also my tightest and lowest cut sweater as well. In case it wasn’t obvious, it was a gift from Kate.

I chose the blue sweater with a black camisole and a pair of black skinny jeans. I matched them to a pair of blue keds and did some light make up. My hair was pulled up and piled on my head, the way it usually was. Irish curls was the phrase I’d heard my whole life. After watching a least a dozen youtube tutorials I’d come to realize that Irish curls were kind of frizzy on top and curly underneath.

For me that meant, it was too curly to hold, being straightened and too straight on top to curl. It also meant I usually left it on the top of my head in a bun. I let it down now and after taking some water and leave-in conditioner to it, I realized that it was still futile, so I put it back in the bun. I looked in the mirror and decided that I looked okay. I always turned code vision off when I looked in the mirror. The thought of seeing if I too was made of code gave me the ‘no feeling.’

I shuddered and checked my phone. Almost time to go.

When I got to the restaurant I was led back to the table where Scott was already seated. This wasn't a super fancy restaurant by any means. But it was good with cute checkered tablecloths and unlimited garlic bread.

“Well,” Scott began after I’d settled in. “looks like the contract I’m on is going to keep me in town for a bit longer. The company hasn’t been able to identify which server is giving them issues.”

I stared at him blankly. “Is that a bad thing?”

“For them, probably. For me? It just means I get to stay in town a bit longer. They’re thinking through the holidays at least.”

“Oh awesome.” I said, maybe a little too eagerly and then added, “For you I mean, not for them.”

He chuckled. “You’re not a computer person are you?”

“No, not really. I admitted. I don’t even like hooking up a printer.”

“Who likes hooking up printers? Those things are the devil.”

Our garlic bread arrived and the waiter took our drink order. I got the house rose and he ordered a blue moon.

“I only had a little bit longer to read, she just met the handsome sun king. Her betrothed and destined husband or something.”

“And?”

“He seems too dreamy.”

“Too dreamy?”

“Yeah, like something is off. Only about a third of the way into the book one of–what did you say? Five? Clearly something is off.”

“Ohhh, you’re trying to outthink the author.”

“That’s kind of my thing. Solving puzzles.”

“Oh is it?” A smile crossed my face. “So have you figured me out yet?”

“The jury is still out on that one.” He smiled that dopey crooked smile at me.

“What about you?”

“What about me?”

“Tell me a piece of your puzzle. Something you haven’t told me yet.”

Scott thought for a moment, taking a bite of his garlic bread. “I am afraid of waterslides.”

“Waterslides? Really?” I teased

“Yeah.” He grimaced.

The waiter brought out our food and we sat quietly until the waiter left.

“Was there something that happened to make you dislike waterslides?”

Scott nodded solemnly.

“Oh,” I sat back suddenly feeling ashamed of teasing him. “What happened?”

“Well,” he sighed. “When I was ten or so I was kind of between sizes. Tall and lanky. Well, my mom bought me new swimming trunks for our trip to Geauga Lake that summer. So I went down the first slide and I must not have had them tightened enough because when I stood up my trunks were nowhere to be found.”

My eyes practically bulged. “Oh my god, that had to have been so embarrassing.”

He nodded before dramatically saying, “I’ve never been able to look at a waterslide the same way.”

“Wait. Are you messing with me?”

He laughed, “Yeah, kind of. I did get pantsed at Geauga Lake but I’m not afraid of waterslides.”

I threw my straw wrapper at him. “Aw come on you got me.”

“What about you? What is a secret piece to your puzzle?

I took a bite of my ravioli. “I don’t like dogs.”

“You don’t like dogs?”

“There’s like a smell, and fur everywhere. Not to mention the licking.” I shuddered.

“I can’t believe you don’t like dogs, only monsters don’t like dogs. They’re perfect.”

He was joking, but maybe that’s what I was, what I was becoming. A monster.
