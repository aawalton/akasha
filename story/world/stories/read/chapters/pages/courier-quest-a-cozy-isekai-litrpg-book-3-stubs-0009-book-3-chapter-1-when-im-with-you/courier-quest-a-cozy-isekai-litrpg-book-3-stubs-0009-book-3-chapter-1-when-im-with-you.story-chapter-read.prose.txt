In the comfort of his own home, Trevor didn’t dare move. He had been stuck on his couch for over an hour. One of his legs was cramping and he was too warm. His glass was empty, and the tray of snacks he had prepared for himself was barren of all but the smallest of crumbs. The book he had been reading was beside him, thoroughly finished. Something had to change.

Slowly, with all the care of a surgeon, Trevor shifted in an attempt to become more comfortable.

“Meow,” accused the cat in his lap. Mr. Tiggles scratched his ear before looking up at his captive with one amber eye. He stretched, barely moving, before burying his head against the Summoned’s knee. “Purr.”

“Yeah, yeah,” Trevor sighed as he ran his fingers through the cat’s thick, black fur. At least his leg was feeling better. “You’re lucky you’re cute, little buddy.”

Trevor smiled to himself as the other small creature resting against him asked for attention. Clay was a stoat made of marble and stone, though they were really an earth elemental in disguise. They rolled over onto their back and wriggled. This was the courier’s bonded familiar, who had been with him for almost a year now. He used his free hand to pet them, and felt a measure of satisfaction coming through their emotional link.

Both of them were lying between his legs, and together they were the source of his uncomfortable warmth. Mr. Tiggles was naturally a furnace, but he was aided by the fact that Clay could heat themselves up like a rock in the sun. After getting used to the earth elemental, the cat found he didn't mind having his own personal heating pad.

Trevor had long come to terms with the fact that, sometimes, you were just going to be stuck in one place if animals liked you enough. Clay would move away if he got too uncomfortable, anyway. Their emotional connection went both ways.

Plus, he had a lot on his mind and the companionship was nice. Trevor was nervous. It was, admittedly, something that happened rather often, but this was different.

Tomorrow was his wedding day.

There had been a surprisingly relaxed buildup to it thanks to Lailah. The Scottish elf was one of his fiancée’s oldest friends and considered the demonkin her sword sister ever since they used to adventure together. Lately, she had been going back and forth between Tosa and Eskretet on a weekly basis. Here, she was doing wedding planning. There, she was a consultant to the head of the adventurer’s guild.

It had sounded like a lot of work to Trevor, but she had thrown herself into it with gusto. If Lailah was idle for too long, then something was wrong, as she always said.

While she could be a bit much at times, he was thankful she was taking care of things. Adabelle was far too busy with her mayoral duties and trying to get the summoning ritual to work, and he could count the number of weddings he could remember on one hand. The elf was a godsend, if he were honest.

The sound of a key entering his door snapped him out of his thoughts. Only three people had a spare to his home. Adabelle, Rashie, and Wayne, though the shark only ever used it when she had a nightmare and her dad wasn’t around and the Texan dwarf preferred to knock. Based on the almost aggressive way it the doorknob turned, he would have guessed it was his self-proclaimed niece.

To his surprise, Adabelle appeared. The look on her face was fierce, and a pall fell over his living room. Her eyes locked with his. Some of the tension immediately left her shoulders at the sight of him before she closed the door, locked it, and approached the couch. She sat down beside him, her spade-tipped tail whipping against the furniture.

Without a word, Trevor wrapped an arm around her shoulders and brought her towards his chest. He pulled a pillow free and placed it in front of her face. Adabelle didn’t resist, instead gently planting herself into it in a way where her horns wouldn’t skewer her fiancé.

Then, she screamed.

“Meow?” Mr. Tiggles complained as his head shot up, sounding more like he was asking if he should be offended or not.

Trevor ran his fingers through her fluffy white hair, and the scream eventually petered out. He had picked out her frustration from the moment she had opened the door, and knew to take things slow. Eventually, her tail stopped swishing like that of a mad bull, instead finding his wrist and wrapping around it. A little tight at first, then it became comfortably light.

Pulling her face away from the pillow, Adabelle reached down and scooped up Mr. Tiggles with both hands. “Meow!”

She cared not for his protests as she cradled him close, and the cat shot Trevor a look that made it clear he considered the man at fault for this betrayal. Regardless of his feelings at the moment, it didn’t take long before he changed his tune as the demonkin stroked his fur.

“Purr,” he said, leaning into her. Clay, now with more room to lounge, allowed Trevor to shift before making themselves comfortable again.

“Sorry,” she mumbled, her voice softened by Mr. Tiggles’ neck.

“Apology jar,” Trevor said, summoning the glass container from his inventory. She peeked at him with one orange eye, and he shook it. The few bronze coins that were inside clinked together loudly. “Come on. It’s not just for me, you know.”

“Well, it should be,” she responded, childishly sticking her tongue out at him. That earned her another shake, and she grumbled. “Fine, after I’m done with Mr. Tiggles.”

“Purr.”

“That’s fair,” Trevor relented, putting the jar between their legs. He offered her a small smile. “You tried to summon again tonight, didn’t you?”

“Yes,” Adabelle grumpily confirmed. “I’ve been at it for weeks now, trying it out with six different summoning circle patterns, and I haven’t gotten it to work a single time. It’s infuriating, especially considering Jackson’s success.”

“Well, he was—”

“Seven hundred years old,” she finished. “I can’t compare myself to the world’s best summoner. I know.”

“And yet—”

“I can’t help it,” Adabelle continued. “I know it’s not a competition, but it feels like I’m losing. Six times, Trevor. I’ve been a part of almost a hundred summoning rituals, and the moment I try to do it without guidance or someone to pair with, I’m failing.”

“Which doesn’t make—”

“Me a failure, I know,” she sighed. “I tried to tell him I wasn’t ready before he left, and this just proves it. That’s why I took so long to actually start trying to summon. I did all the research, thought I was doing everything right, it’s just missing something.”

Trevor didn’t miss a beat. “You’ll—”

“Get it eventually. It’s only a matter of time and practice. It’s rare to start good at something, and improvement is inevitable. I’m smart, just impatient at times, so I just need to slow down and figure things out like a proper scholar,” the demonkin finished. She look at Trevor, who smiled softly at her, and snuggled in against him. “Thanks, son of Ander. You always know just what to say.”

“Let me tell you, it’s hard finding the right words sometimes,” he joked, kissing the top of her head. His hand went to Mr. Tiggles, who was nestled against Adabelle’s shoulder, and he scritched the cat’s ears. “I wasn’t expecting you tonight. Lailah was pretty adamant about us not seeing each other before the wedding.”

“She’s teasing us,” the woman said with a pout. “I know the Earth custom only deals with seeing the bride in her dress, so we’re fine. Though, if you were that concerned, you could have closed your eyes.”

“Nah,” Trevor immediately declined. “That would mean I’d miss out on seeing you, lady. I’m not about to deny myself of your gorgeous countenance.”

“Charmer,” Adabelle accused, gently slapping her tail against his wrist. She was clearly pleased, though; there was a slight glow to her orange eyes that only happened when she was happy. Her husband-to-be counted himself lucky that there was such an easy indicator to read.

“Feeling better?” he asked as he resumed stroking her hair, his fingers gently brushing against her horns.

“Yes and no,” she answered. “I’m still sore that the summoning failed, but it doesn’t seem to matter as much when I’m with you.”

“That’s a daunting task to attempt the night before our wedding. Don’t let it stress you out, okay?”

“It was my last chance before we get married and go on our honeymoon,” Adabelle defended, the tip of her tail swishing above his wrist. “I know it wasn’t the best time, but I wanted to do it. Prove that I could. Wayne’s got our adventuring guild up and running now, so he would have been able to take care of whoever came through and we would be off.”

“You’ll get there. It’s only a matter of time,” Trevor soothed, and the demonkin nodded. “But I’ve got big news that might distract you. I managed to get our first destination out of Klar during my bachelor party.”

Their honeymoon was being completely funded by Klarkartar, the minotaur merchant prince. He had met Trevor on the same day the courier came to Tosa and they became fast friends. With the help of a Summoned in his employ who had the Power of Teleportation, the itinerary could include just about anywhere.

The only stipulation was that Trevor would have to run some quick deliveries in between cities. Klar was the first to say that he didn’t want to overly impose on their honeymoon so there wouldn’t be too much to load and unload at each stop. He wouldn’t have minded if there was, though; the Power of Inventory made things easy, and a trip for two around the world that he didn’t have to pay for sounded amazing.

Adabelle carefully moved her head away from the courier’s chest before raising it so she wouldn’t scratch his face with her horns. “Really?” she asked incredulously. “I’ve been trying to get that information out of Griffin, but she won’t say anything. Not even Lailah knows, which is unfortunate because I could get her to talk at a drop of a hat.”

“That’s why she doesn’t know,” he said with a light chuckle. “It wasn’t a lot, though. Someplace secluded where we could spend our first nights alone without the hustle and bustle of a city to get distracted with.”

“Oh, I do like the idea of having you all to myself,” Adabelle said, her voice low as she looked up at him with a sultry gaze. “I might be looking forward to that more than the wedding.”

“Me, too,” was his instant reply, and he smiled. “Have you ever heard of a place called Todarro?”

The demonkin tilted her head. “Isn’t that a small country on the edge of the Wildlands?”

“Yes,” Trevor confirmed, nodding. “They’ve been pushing into it. One of the few countries that have, actually. It’s situated at the top of a cliff three miles high that spans for over a hundred in either direction. Apparently, the place we’re staying at is right at the edge and, according to Klar, it could put the view from the Grandmother Tree to shame.”

“That puts things in perspective,” Adabelle said thoughtfully. Mr. Tiggles wiggled to escape from her hold, and she allowed it. He didn’t make it very far, laying on her lap as he settled into a loaf. She reached for Trevor, but the sound of his fingernail tapping on the apology jar caught her attention. “You could have forgotten about that.”

“Would you have?” he asked, raising an eyebrow in challenge.

“For you? Yes,” she blatantly lied, looking away as she fished a bronze coin from her robe pockets. It fell into the jar with a clink.

“I’ll believe that when the day comes,” he said, clearly amused as he returned the container to his inventory.

“Mmhmm. Anyway, about Todarro,” Adabelle started, changing the subject. “That does sound pleasant. I’m sure we’re going to be very tired after the wedding, so it’ll be nice to recuperate away from people.”

“My thoughts exactly,” Trevor agreed. He was about to continue when there was a knock on the door.

“Adabelle,” came Lailah’s voice from outside. “I know you’re in there. What did we talk about?”

“Want me to lie?” he whispered.

“No, I suppose I should be going,” Adabelle sighed. Then, she looked around. “Are you okay all by yourself? I figured you’d be with Klar or Wayne. Or even Drelik.”

Trevor shrugged. “A little solitude felt right,” he admitted. “A good book, snacks, Clay and Mr. Tiggles, and my own thoughts.”

“You weren’t lonely?” she asked, making sure.

He smiled at her concern. “Why would I be? Tomorrow, I’m going to have you for the rest of my life.”

“Yes, yes, very sweet,” Lailah said through the door. “Please relinquish my sword sister, messenger boy, and you can have your solitude once more.”

“You know, I was going to keep her, but then you said please and I’m really a sucker for such politeness,” he teased loudly before looking back at his fiancée. “Are you ready?”

“I suppose,” Adabelle said as she moved Mr. Tiggles back between Trevor’s legs. The cat instantly made himself comfortable again. “Lailah’s probably going to lecture me about more Scottish traditions. She wants to wash my feet for some reason. Says it was for good luck.”

“And good fortune!” the elf clarified.

“I’ll see you tomorrow,” Trevor said, and Adabelle leaned down to kiss him.

“See you tomorrow,” she repeated, smiling before smoothing out her work robes and leaving his house.

“Took you long enough,” Lailah complained as Adabelle locked the door behind her. “Goodnight, Trevor.”

“G’night, Lai,” he returned.

“Oh hush, Lailah,” he heard the demonkin reply outside. “You didn’t have to come all the way out here.”

“We still have some things to talk about, so I do—”

Trevor chuckled as the women left earshot. He shifted again.

“Meow,” Mr. Tiggles complained, scandalized that the courier would try to move.

And he found himself in exactly the same predicament he had been in when Adabelle had arrived. Trevor hesitated for a few moments before allowing himself to sink back into the couch. He summoned a book from his inventory and opened it to the first page.

“At least I’m comfortable,” he said, accepting his fate as a cat and familiar pillow.
