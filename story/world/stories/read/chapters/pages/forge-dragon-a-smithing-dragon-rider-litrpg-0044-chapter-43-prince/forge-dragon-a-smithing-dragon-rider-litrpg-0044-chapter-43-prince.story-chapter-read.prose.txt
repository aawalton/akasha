Caleb took the hand and froze as he made contact with the metal. The mana inside was so tightly packed and intricate that it was far beyond his ability to decipher. Despite the inability, he froze, taking it all in, trying to glean any truths he could from it.

“He’s Caleb Kavilson,” Kelia said for her dumbfounded husband. “and I’m his wife Kelia—Kavilson I suppose, not that we’ve talked about it.”

Mettius released his grasp on Caleb, and it took a smack from Kelia for Caleb to do the same.

“Sorry, I—”

“Iron Affinity?” Mettius asked.

“And a blacksmith,” Canus added.

“Oh,” Mettius said, raising his eyebrows. “You better watch yourself. We’ll have to hide you away when my father’s inquisitors come sniffing for talent—if you have any talent worth protecting that is.”

Caleb said nothing.

Mettius turned to Canus,

“Is he mute?”

“No,” Caleb said. “Sorry, your gauntlets are… a lot to take in.”

“They better be for what I paid for them,” Mettius said matter of factly.

“How much did you pay for them?” Caleb asked.

“My entire inheritance,” Mettius said. “Deferred, obviously as my father is still alive—and may he reign forever.”

“Was that… a lot?” Caleb asked.

“Not as much as my siblings,” Mettius said. “But an outrageous sum all the same.”

Before they could discuss anything more, a commotion broke out in the camp just beyond the tent and shortly after a messenger was let in.

“The scouts found more corrupted animals,” the messenger said as he handed the missive he held over.

“Mettius,” Canus commanded, and the prince snapped to attention. “Take these two and Magnus and deal with them.”

Mettius eyed them both before nodding.

“Call for your dragon,” Mettius said “we’ll be going out on foot.”

Caleb sent a mental summons for bog, and a new commotion broke out distantly in the camp.

“Do we have time to get any equipment?” Caleb asked.

He had turned over all his new equipment to master Dimel save for his sword, hammer, and ingots. He planned to make own gear at the soonest opportunity but hadn’t expected to be in battle so soon after leaving.

“Five minutes in the quartermaster’s tent,” Canus said, “But no more.”

A messenger lead them to a tent that smelled strongly of oil, iron, and rust. The smell was all sort of nostalgic for Caleb. He reached out for his senses, disappointed to find nothing in the room had the telltale signs of magic. Kelia moved to trying on helmets until she found one that fit as Caleb quickly looked through the lorica—combination pauldron and chest armor that was legion standard. The armor was made up of segmented plates that overlapped as they wrapped the body.

Caleb immediately aw the benefits of the design. It fully covered the body while still allowing flexibility. Repairing the armor too would be a simple task as each plate could be removed to be repaired or even replaced.

The possibility of a set of armor made up of dozens of plates intrigued Caleb in a way he didn’t have time to consider.

“You won’t have time to put that…”Mettius began to say.

Caleb found a set he thought would fit him, and using his Imperium pulled the armor apart, loosening the laces. With the armor floating in mid air, he brought it above himself and then down over his head. Once it was on, he pulled on the metal aglet tips of the laces and drew the laces tight.

Caleb realized that might have been a mistake a moment later when it occurred to him that they had been made of brass and not steel.

Kelia was next, and he quickly found her a set of armor and she donned it in a similar fashion. Caleb took a helm from her. Finally he grabbed a spear just as their time was up. Kelia held only her bow as a weapon.

The ran out of the camp, armor plate rattling like crazy with each footfall.

Some downsides, Caleb realized.

The single breast plate he’d found in the cavern hadn’t made much noise as he moved around in it.

A fourth man joined them as they left the safety of the walls. He appeared to be a standard legionnaire though that was betrayed by the speed with which he moved.

“This is Magnus,” Mettius said, introducing him on the run. “He’s a spare blade centurion.”

Mettius gestured to the sword in Magnus’ hand as if that explained it.

Caleb was confused, as the sword in Magnus’ hand was nothing like the centurion blade he’d sensed on Canus during their journey to the camp. It was even less magical than the legionnaire blade he’d briefly held in the battle against the giant.

“What’s that?” Caleb asked,

“The Thousand Blade legion actually had a fair amount of extra blades of each rank,” Mettius explained. “They give them out to soldiers to have them experiment with new builds of the existing classes. Magnus here is a centurion with a speed build.”

Magnus saluted, but in no other way acknowledged that he was the topic of conversation.

Caleb chose not to ask why the blade wasn’t actually a centurion’s blade. He didn’t understand enough to know whether or not the question was a dangerous one, but he vowed to talk to Kelia about it later.

Then the rest of what Mettius sunk in and he was filled with relief.

“There are extra blades?” he asked

“Yes,” Mettius said. “You’re brother picked up a Decurion’s blade, right? He’ll probably not be executed if he proves himself useful. They’ve already replaced the Decurion in the ranks with a spare blade.”

“Why didn’t Canus tell us this?” Caleb asked.

“He likely didn’t outright lie, but I guess he wanted to hammer home the seriousness of the offense. It is an executable offense to take up a blade like he did.”

They stopped talking as they reached the forest surrounding the camp, following the path as instructed by the messenger.

The forest was dark, but with his boosted perception Caleb could see the forest with clarity that rivaled what he would have seen before in daylight. The light of the ring above never allowed it to truly become dark on Alius.

It wasn’t long into the forest that they heard the snarling. The scouts had reported they’d spotted monstrous animals fighting in the forest and fled before they could engage. Caleb saw now by ring light that a bear was fending off a veritable herd of deer. Both the bear and the deer were discolored with black splotches mottling their brown bodies.
