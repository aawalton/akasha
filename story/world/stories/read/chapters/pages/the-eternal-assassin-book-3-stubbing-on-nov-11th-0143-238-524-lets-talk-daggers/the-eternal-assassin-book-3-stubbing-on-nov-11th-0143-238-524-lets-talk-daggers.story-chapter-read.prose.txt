“Give me a second, then we can talk shop,” Helpha said, tossing her new hammers down on one of the already crowded tables before pulling off her coal-streaked apron and hanging it up on the wall. Clapping her hands on her thighs, which was rather loud, seeing as she was functionally just banging metal against metal, she nodded. “Alright. I assume you’re here about your old dagger? Gotta admit, I thought you’d show up long before now.”

“I tried! Every time I popped in you were out!” Asher frowned, knowing exactly what she was talking about.

The first and only time he’d ever spoken to Helpha, the ferrum had judged him worthy of one of her many weapons, before selling him Crescent at a criminally low price. Asher had done a bit more research since then, and he now knew that getting a masterwork mithral weapon of any kind, even just a dagger, was worth hundreds of thousands of shards at a bare minimum. But Helpha had charged him no more than just fifty thousand shards. Fifty thousand shards… and his old, rusty iron dagger that he’d taken from Zygrul’s tower back when he was first summoned.

Helpha had explained that she would do her best to restore the dagger to its former glory, or barring that, put the weapon to rest as all weapons would eventually deserve. The smith seemed to have some skill that allowed her to actually talk to weapons, or at least get some sort of vague sense as to what had been done with them. Asher didn’t know just how powerful it was, but seeing as she’d called him out for smacking his dagger against a wall weeks prior, he didn’t think she was lying.

“You did? Sorry about that,” Helpha said, raising a shining eyebrow as she looked him up and down. “You’re looking a lot stronger than the last time we spoke. Cusp of the Stage of Bleed?”

“Yeah. I have one last skill to evolve, currently sitting at level 19,” Asher nodded. Judgement was the final skill left in the first tier, having risen to level 19 over the past couple of days. He and Samantha had witnessed enough new people while flying back and forth across the kingdom to level the skill from the sheer number of different auras, and he was more than a little excited to hit the next stage of elemental advancement.

The thought of the three entirely brand-new skills he'd gradually unlock as he progressed through the Stage of Bleed was more than a little enticing.

“Anyway, let’s not waste time,” Helpha said, motioning for him to stay as she headed into the back of the shop. “Wait there a second. I think you’ll be pleased.”

Asher did as ordered, and it wasn’t long before Helpha returned with a small sheath in hand. Handing it to him, she grinned. “Take a look.”

Doing just that, Asher gasped at the gleaming iron blade before him as he slid it out of its sheath. There wasn’t so much as a single speck of dust on the shining blade, and he could practically see his reflection in the metal as he angled it this way and that. Despite knowing next to nothing about metallurgy, his Identify confirmed the transformation as well. The blade had once been labeled as ‘poor quality’ and ‘rusted,’ but that wasn’t the case anymore.

[Iron Dagger] - Masterwork Quality.

“You had a real champion of a blade hiding under all that rust and age,” Helpha said, clapping him on the back as he tried to compare this new dagger with the mental image of his old one. “It was all but begging for me to restore it to its former glory. You should be impressed. Most blades this old are tired and weary, ready to let their edges dull and be retired. But not this one. Did you know it has a name? I couldn’t actually hear it until we were nearly done with the reforging process, but by then it was practically screaming it at me, loud and clear. Its name is Nail.”

“How much do I owe you?” Asher asked, holding the gleaming dagger close to his chest as the memories of him frantically fighting for his life against terrifying imps mere hours after arriving in this world flashed through his mind. He owed this dagger his life five times over, and he was beyond pleased to see its true worth restored to it. It was almost like he wanted others to know just how amazing the dagger that he’d relied on so heavily his first day on this world truly was.

“No shards,” Helpha said, holding up a hand to stop him before he could start arguing. “No, I don’t want shards. Instead, you can pay me back by showing me the weapon you're hiding somewhere on your person. The one that sounds like it’s screaming into its own shadow.”

“Figures you’d be able to sense it,” Asher chuckled, slipping Nail into his bandolier before pulling the Void dagger out of his rift. He’d shoved together a quick fix to keep the dagger standing upright by the hilt to keep the blade away from the edges of his skill, but that had only kept it from eating its way out. Over the past few hours it had drained nearly half of his energy simply by existing within his rift. It was a shame, because Asher would have absolutely loved adding such a unique dagger to his collection, but he sort of needed the energy in order to… well, to live, for one.

“By the realms…” Helpha muttered, gingerly taking the blade by the hilt as she examined it. Turning it this way and that, her frown only deepened the longer she stared at it. Eventually, she placed it down on a nearby table, shaking out her hand as she shook her head.

“That is no weapon,” she said, still staring at the dagger as though it were impossible to look away. “There is no metal. The hilt is solid, but the blade is merely hunger given form. It will consume and take in anything in its path until the end of time. It screams, not because it has no name, but because it has devoured its own name long ago.” Finally, she managed to tear her eyes off the blade, directing her frown at him instead. “Where did you get this?”

“One of my teammates found it in a base belonging to a powerful wizard,” Asher said, hesitating before he said more. Donvath had instructed him after their initial conversation to keep the fact that there was someone with a Void element running around a secret. Apparently it would cause mass panic if word got out, so the various countries lending support to try and take down Zygrul were keeping things incredibly tight-lipped.

“Wizards. Bah,” Helpha spat, her brow furrowing as she stared at the dagger again. “This is what happens when one attempts to play with the powers of the world all on their own, rather than using what was so carefully laid out for us. We were meant to worship the Gods, not attempt to become them.”

“I figured out for myself that the dagger constantly drains tritessence,” Asher explained, glad that Helpha seemed content with the explanation that the dagger was some wizard’s mad magic experiment. “Is there any way you can think to stop that effect? Maybe some sort of fancy scabbard you could create?”

“Such a material doesn’t exist,” Helpha said, shaking her head. “Dragon scales are highly resistant to tritessence, but this dagger is crafty. Its hunger will slip around even the slightest crack. No, you would need something one-hundred-percent resistant, and such a material doesn’t exist on this realm.”

On this realm…

“Would these work?” he asked, pulling the scales he’d pried from the abyss monster guarding the cave in front of Zygrul’s base out of his rift.

Snatching one from his hand, Helpha held it up to the light, her aggravated expression quickly turning into one of curiosity. After turning the scale this way and that for a minute, she changed angles, placing the scale in front of the Void dagger and staring at it. A few seconds later, she turned back to him, looking utterly bewildered.

“Alright… Where did you get this?”

“Killed a monster that same wizard summoned from the abyss,” Asher shrugged, realizing how ridiculous such a claim sounded even as he said it. “I swear, I’m not making this stuff up.”

“If I wasn’t staring at the dagger and scale right in front of me, I would have assumed you were,” Helpha chuckled, fingering the scale as her mind raced. “I’m well aware that materials from the abyss play by their own rules… I’ve even seen one of the powerful artifacts that realm wanderers recovered from a dive into the abyss a few centuries back… But this would be my first time getting to work with abyssal material myself. You need a scabbard?”

“Yes! If you can come up with a way to keep the creepy dagger from sucking away my energy, that would be perfect!” Asher said, growing excited at the prospect of keeping the dagger for himself. Technically, Moxy had been the one to find it, but he knew she didn’t have the slightest interest in the weapon. She didn’t even like regular weapons, much less ones that felt like they were slowly sucking out your soul through a bendy straw. “How many scales do you need?”

“How many do you have?” Helpha countered, raising an eyebrow.

In the end, Asher gave her the small pile he’d managed to collect before the abyss monster had fully deteriorated. Helpha promised him she’d do what she could with the material, thanking him yet again for giving her the chance to work with something brand-new. Apparently, one of the downsides of functionally being immortal and having lived for centuries was that finding never-before-seen materials to work with grew harder and harder with each passing generation.

“As fun as sitting around in my empty, dark shop has to be for you, perhaps we should come up with a system in the event you need something from me,” Helpha offered after they’d ironed out their deal. “I can make a point to stop by my shop at least every few days at this time. That way if you show up and I’m not here, you can know to check back in the next day rather than waiting around for hours.”

“That works for me,” Asher said, scratching the back of his head sheepishly. “Sorry for coming into your shop while you were away in the first place. I figured you’d show up sooner or later, but I realize I probably could have waited outside in the alleyway.”

“Bah, it’s no big deal,” she laughed. “If you were a stranger, I would have tossed you out with a few pounds of flesh missing or bruised, but you are a customer! The few people in this world whose weapons will actually vouch for them are always welcome within my shop, even when I’m not there.”

Well, now I feel a bit better about using Helpha’s shop as my Recall point for Dormaul at the very least.

“Give me at least a week or two to finish up with your scabbard,” Helpha said, tapping a metal finger against the scale in her hand once more. Unlike the Void dagger, which she didn’t seem to like even looking at, the smith had yet to put down the abyssal scale he’d first handed her. In fact, Asher got the vibe she was trying to not-so-subtly get him to leave her shop so she could jump straight into working with the new material. “The work itself probably won’t take more than a few days, but I have a wide barrage of tests I need to run to make sure I can utilize the material properly. If this truly is one-hundred-percent resistant to tritessence, my skills won’t work on it, which is quite the interesting puzzle!”

“I hadn’t even thought of that,” Asher admitted, his eyes going wide at the very thought. “Do you still think you can do it?”

“You’re lucky I like you, or I’d punch you for such a remark,” Helpha laughed. “I’ve been smithing weapons and working with my hands for centuries now. Skills or no skills, I’ll find a way.”

“Sorry, I’m glad you’re confident,” Asher said, deciding to take the hint as he stepped out of the shop. “I’ll check back in a week then!”

Helpha had already dismissed him, leaning over a table as she poked and prodded at the abyssal scale with her fingers. Chuckling to himself, Asher decided to leave the expert to it.

He had plenty of other things to keep himself occupied over the next week.
