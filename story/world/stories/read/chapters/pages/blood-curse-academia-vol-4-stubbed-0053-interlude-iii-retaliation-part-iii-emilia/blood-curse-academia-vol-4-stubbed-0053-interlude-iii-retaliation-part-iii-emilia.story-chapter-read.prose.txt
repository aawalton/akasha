Interlude III - Retaliation

Part III - Emilia

Nobody else sat at the table with her. That fact irked Emilia. Normally, both Ichika and Cybelle joined her for dinner. For one to get busy and not show wouldn’t have been too abnormal, but both? Emilia couldn’t recall the last time she ate dinner at the academy alone. It was humiliating and soured her already foul mood.

She shushed the part of her that worried about her friends facing academic disciplinary actions. She’d spoken to the third best ranked diviner at the academy about making those golems impossible to track back to her. Everyone would suspect, certainly. But there shouldn't be any evidence.

Except for the facsimilater. The faculty could be questioning everyone who’d use the facsimilater this week. In that case, they’d track it to Cybelle. Emilia doubted her friend would rat her out, but she still had plans in place as a safety net. If Cybelle attempted to use her as a scapegoat, she’d soon learn that it was all twisted back at her. Emilia had recently learned a valuable lesson in trust. Whether it be trust in people or the status quo, everything could be upturned in an instant.

It wasn’t fair. Her family had spent decades building their wealth. Hundreds of thousands of hours dedicated to building an empire of trade, only for it to crumble from an unseen and unpredictable disaster. But fairness mattered very little in life. She needed to be careful if she wanted her family to ascend back to their rightful place. Scheme and charm.

She hated that she’d allowed Kizu to slip through her fingers last semester. The boy was an invaluable resource. She should have approached him after their disastrous date and been more forward. Instead, she had waited, convinced he would find the courage to return to her. Patience in a situation that required action. A misjudged opportunity. She was used to being the one pursued, not the other way around. And now, somehow, that lazy, pathetic waste of space had sunk her claws into Emilia’s prize. Never again. This time, she was staking her claim loudly for the world to see. Her actions couldn’t be tracked to her, but everyone now knew exactly what happened to those who crossed her.

From across the cafeteria, she caught the eye of her other problem. Ulric. The hulking boy’s dark eyes still flipped her stomach. Completely irrational.

Kizu appealed to her through the potential he represented. Ulric had none of that. In the last six months, Kizu had skyrocketed through the academy ranking system, earned the favor of the headmaster, and even befriended Emperor Sasaki of Hon. There were rumors that he’d faced down one of the Dragons that had attacked the academy, though Emilia remained skeptical of such claims. She knew first hand that Dragons were a caliber of monster that belonged in a tier alongside great warriors like the headmaster, not mere students. That was the thing though, when you achieved great things, normal people didn’t question rumors that would otherwise be utterly unbelievable.

In comparison, this semester Ulric had fallen in nearly every academic metric except for fighting. He’d given up on graduating as an official mage of Shinzou Academy. He’d already been offered three contracts by mercenary companies. His options were either the Tross military, their rebels, or a campaign to reclaim Edgeland. He’d probably be a corpse by the end of next year. At best, he’d be upgraded to the station of a dog under some more competent general.

Her mind flashed back to her last night with Ulric. He’d passed out, drunk, on a bench down on the coast. He’d been so intoxicated that he barely acknowledged her the entire night. It made her furious. And yet, she struggled to tear her eyes off of him.

She took a sip of her wine, watching him. It was sour. Improper storage had spoiled the drink. Serving it at room temperature didn’t help either. It also was nonalcoholic, a spell having removed the ‘poisonous’ aspect before serving it to the students.

A blue academy uniform stepped up, blocking her gaze on Ulric.

“Hello, Emilia.”

The voice surprised her. She glanced up to see a familiar head of green hair. Last semester, she’d been originally planning to ask Kizu to shave it, but now recognized it as an important defining feature. It made him stand out in a crowd. A status symbol. The same could be said of that monkey familiar of his. All tools which could be twisted to further his influence.

Emilia smiled at him and forced her body to relax. This was faster than expected, but Kizu had come to her.

“Hello, take a seat.” She gestured at the empty spot across from her, suddenly grateful for her friends’ flakiness. Time to seize this opportunity.

Kizu sat. His face remained neutral as he picked up a cafeteria menu. He pressed his hand against one of the listed options and a cup of soup and a small loaf of steaming bread appeared on a tray in front of him. Without a word addressing her, he tore a chunk off the loaf and dipped it in the soup. It came out of the cup stained red and with a bit of onion clinging to its edge. Minestrone then. Emilia had noticed it on today’s menu when she’d ordered her lackluster wine. She watched him take a bite, teeth ripping into the bread and silently chewing.

This was not the way Emilia expected to reunite with Kizu. Still, she supposed it wasn’t all that surprising. He’d always needed to be led to achieve things properly. At least he wasn’t on fire like when she’d last seen him.

“It’s nice to see you again,” she said pleasantly. “Is there something you’d like to discuss?” She gave him that little verbal nudge, attempting to reassure him with her tone that they could discuss anything openly without any judgement.

Kizu’s brow scrunched, clearly considering what to say as he took another bite of minestrone soaked bread.

She smiled. Now was the time for patience. She reminded herself that he had come to her this time. Nudge gently, don’t shove.

“Do you know what that treasure chest monster was?” he asked.

“It was a mimic,” Emilia replied, recalling the monster card’s title. She didn’t know where this was going, but him mentioning treasure was likely a good sign. Even with her family’s current state, she could offer him monetary gain through connections and thorough knowledge.

He nodded slowly and took another bite of a soup laden bread chunk. Emilia just barely managed to resist pressing him for more information as he chewed and swallowed. This conversation was moving far slower than she’d like.

“Their bodies are incredibly resistant,” he said. “Stabbing, burning, acid. None of that works against their exterior. You need to get them to make a move and expose themselves to be offered an opportunity to strike.”

“I see.” Emilia kept her face pleasant, despite the urge to narrow her eyes. Kizu was smart, nobody ranked up as swiftly as him without a half-decent brain, but this set up sounded borderline like a discussion of manipulation and control. Not something Kizu had ever been equipped to handle, let alone speak about.

“You’ve done a decent job of defending the obvious attack points. The golems can’t be traced back to you. You chose a day when the copying artifact had been used by multiple students for projects. In fact, other than your friends, I believe the only people who saw you in the dormitory at all were me and Basil. Why would you be there, after all, since you have a villa? There’s evidence, I could request a divination truth detection spell or potion. But it’s not easy to obtain.”

So far, he’d only complimented her approach. Maybe he meant to catch her off guard with an admission. That would line up with the exposed when attack analogy he’d led with. Instead, Emilia simply smiled. Best to let him continue to his point.

“However, those aren’t your true weak points. Those are problems created by your attack, not innate weakness. Your move only made your constant, existing pressure points more exposed.”

“Which are?” She finally took his bait.

Kizu leaned back and spread his arms wide.

Only then did Emilia truly feel exposed. She tried to cover her sudden wave of fear with a sip of the bad wine. The seconds of bought time did nothing to improve her situation.

“You attacked someone I love,” Kizu said bluntly. “And so, I will dismantle everything you’ve built. All your connections. Your associates. Your social standing. It will all crumble.”

In that moment, Emilia became acutely aware of a fact she’d been blind to. Kizu had long since passed her on the social ladder. While she’d fallen, he’d risen. Students talked about him in hushed whispers. His influence had spread not tenfold, but a thousandfold from when she’d met him last semester. He had all the power she’d been craving, and he never even tried to achieve it.

Kizu wiped his mouth with a napkin and tossed the newly stained cloth on the tray.

“You’re done.” He stood and departed.

Emilia stared at his abandoned torn loaf of bread and half drained cup of minestrone. A corner of the discarded napkin was dipped in the red soup. With a shaking hand, she took a gulp of her drink and welcomed the overwhelmingly sour flavor.
