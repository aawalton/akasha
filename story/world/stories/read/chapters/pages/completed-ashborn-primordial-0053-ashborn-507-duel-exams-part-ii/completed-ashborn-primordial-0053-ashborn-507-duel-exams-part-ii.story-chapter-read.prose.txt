“As this is an introductory match designed to get new students used to the concept, I will now summarize the rules of this bout,” the professor announced, standing between Vir and a very malicious-looking Vayren. “The suits you two wear contain in-built Elemental Protection orbs attuned to the affinity of your opponent. The orbs will absorb all the damage cast by that element. The first to deplete their opponent’s orb’s charge wins. Depleting an opponent’s charge is the only way to win.

“You will be graded on your control of prana, your tactics and strategies, and your general combat proficiency. Any attacks after the opponent’s orb has been depleted will be counted against you, and any severe injuries sustained by your opponent may result in expulsion or possibly even criminal charges. While physical strikes will not detract from your score, be careful you do not injure your opponent, as that will count against you. Furthermore, disarming your opponent or any similar actions will result in both contestants resetting positions. Do each of you agree to abide by these rules?”

Vir nodded, as did Vayren, though his smile seemed even larger now.

The rules were familiar enough, and although it didn’t exactly come as a surprise that the only way to win was via magic, Vir had been hoping for a loophole. Unfortunately, it seemed he’d have to rely on his new Lightning affinity after all.

“Then if there are no further questions, please step back ten paces between you,” the proctor said, standing between them.

They obliged, moving back and giving each other space.

“And begin!” the man shouted before rushing back to the edge of the room.

Vayren opened with a C-grade Ember spell which, Vir had to admit, he fired impressively fast.

Even so, it seemed the boy who talked such a big game was only capable of firing C-grade orbs. Vayren was not the sort to hold back. If only to flaunt his power, he would have come at Vir with everything he had. That he hadn’t spoke volumes. Additionally, Vir’s suit had only been fitted with Fire Elemental protection, meaning he lacked any other affinity.

Luckily, Fire was one of the easiest elements to avoid, and Vir hopped away, avoiding the small ball of fire as it impacted harmlessly on the ground, sending embers in all directions.

Vayren may have seized the initiative, but Vir’s vast ocean of combat experience wasn’t for naught.

He might not be able to win by physical means, but rushing Vayren with his katar drawn had the effect of scaring the boy half to death—which was almost as good.

Vir was already halfway through his incantation when the boy tripped over his own feet, falling on his ass.

Channeling a new affinity was a strange sensation, to say nothing of sending it out of his body.

He’d only ever done so to fire prana directly as an attack or cycle it within his body. Even tattoos were a part of him, and so his prana control was significantly more refined when channeling his Ultimate arts.

Vir grimaced as he struggled to maintain control of the Lightning prana long enough to charge the orb.

So clumsy… Vir cursed as the orb sputtered out, the prana dispersing into the atmosphere, wasted.

It wasn’t him. Compared to Ash prana, Lightning prana felt clunky and slow. Like oil instead of the pressurized jet of water he’d been used to.

Inferior form of prana indeed.

As if all that wasn’t enough, the fact that atmospheric prana could not be used to power orbs in this universe added an additional complication. Normally, mejai created a sort of prana vacuum in their palms, creating a convection current from the atmosphere into the orb to power it.

Here, he had to not only inject that prana directly into the orb from his body, he also had to properly guide it along the inscription pathway.

As he’d learned in the scant few minutes he’d had to practice, the task sometimes took mejai years to learn, and for good reason.

All of a sudden, Vir had a newfound respect for all mejai, being able to master the task to a degree that it became habitual and instinctual.

“Wha-what’s this?” Vayren taunted, scrambling back to his feet. “Can’t even activate an orb on your own? I should’ve guessed as much from a commoner. Give up now, before you embarrass yourself any further!”

Vir, of course, ignored the boy, focusing on channeling the spell again.

But Vayren wasn’t quite as stupid as he worked so hard to make the world believe, and did not allow him the luxury, firing two Ember spells in quick succession, forcing Vir to jump away.

Just when Vir thought he had dodged the attacks, the Sawai boy fired a third Ember just as Vir landed.

Trivially easy to avoid… if he had any of his abilities. As he was, he could only brace himself as the well-timed attack impacted as he landed, scorching his leg. The suit he wore absorbed it, diminishing some of the charge in his protection orb.

More worryingly, he couldn’t know how many more attacks he needed to empty Vayren’s. And so, for the briefest instant, Vir activated Prana Vision. To cheat. To gain a leg up on his foe.

And in that split second, he knew he was nearly there. Their elemental defense orbs were C-grade, after all. It would not hold much longer, and now he had confirmation. Two, perhaps three attacks at most.

But in that moment before Prana Vision ended, Vir was distracted by something.

He looked down at the vast ocean of Lightning prana that dwelled beneath his feet.

What in the world…

But that was all the time Vir had to be distracted, as Vayren rushed forth, trying to copy Vir’s tactic.

Unfortunately for him, he was terrible at it.

Rather than backing away, Vir jumped forward, meeting the boy head-on.

The sudden move caught the charging boy off guard. Panicking, Vayren fired off another Ember, which Vir easily dodged this time.

Learning from his previous mistake, Vir cycled the prana with more care this time. The manner and timing needed to be precisely dialed, but Vir was nothing if not a fast learner.

The inscription completed and lightning crackled as Arc speared out at Vayren, catching him squarely in the chest. While the Lightning protection orb absorbed the shock that would have otherwise paralyzed him, Vayren was still pushed back, losing his footing once again.

Now that he had the hang of it, Vir didn’t relent. He was already charging his second attack, which he fired immediately, scoring another hit.

That was one of the best aspects of Lightning. Impossible to avoid and quick to cast, especially at close ranges.

Vayren panicked, firing off two more Embers, which forced Vir to dodge. If nothing else, the boy had quite an impressive reserve of prana, considering he almost certainly was unaware of the soul-burning method.

Then again, Vayren likely had months to build up his prana reserves. Even a trickle could fill a pond given enough time.

By dumb luck, Vayren’s attack impacted the ground nearby, spraying hot soot on Vir’s suit leg, which further diminished his orb.

Clicking his tongue in irritation, Vir quickly charged his Arc orb once more, firing the spell faster than ever, scoring another direct hit against Vayren.

Vir had been hoping to end the fight with that because he only had enough prana for a single additional attack.

By this time, Vayren had managed to scramble to his feet and pointed his orb at Vir. But it was too little, too late.

Vir had been charging his orb for a follow-up attack the instant he’d fired his first one. Lightning affinity magic did charge faster than other affinities, and so Vir’s attack once again caught Vayren square in the chest.

Unfortunately, it wasn’t enough.

“God damn you! By the gods, I’ll have you! Who do you think you are?” Vayren cried. “A mere commoner—you can’t defeat me!”

And perhaps he was right, because Vir had just run out of prana.

He instantly switched to surging prana through Maiya’s tattered remains of a tattoo, trying to eke out every last drop before it burned away entirely.

And so, praying to all the gods, Vir channeled prana through the tattoo one last time, feeling it unravel from the surge of magical energy he was pouring into it.

Come on, he thought, rolling away from another Ember spell, just barely maintaining the concentration needed to continue his cycling.

Almost there.

He needed to buy more time. He could do that by running around, but of course, as Vayren had proven, he was quite adept at predicting his magic’s arc, thus overcoming one of the main deficiencies of his affinity.

So Vir once again exploited his enemy’s weakness, taking the fight to melee range, closing in with his katar and locking blades with Vayren.

“What are you doing?” Vayren said, almost indignant that Vir would dare think of fighting him in close combat. “This is a magic fight!”

“Uh-huh, and you have fire magic. You attack me now, you burn yourself.”

Vayren’s eyes bulged as he realized Vir’s gambit.

“The same goes for you. You can’t attack me with your lightning until you move away.”

“Is that right?” Vir asked, grinning. Vayren might not have been the worst fighter he had ever faced, but with Vir’s wealth of combat experience, it was like fighting a child.

And so Vir toyed with Vayren, watching the boy’s cheeks grow ever redder as he realized how outclassed he was.

Then, when he’d wasted enough time, he disarmed his opponent’s talwar. The moment he did, the proctor jumped in.

“Halt all combat! Reset to your original positions.”

And so they did, with Vayren giving Vir a smug look that showed just how thoroughly he misunderstood the situation.

“And fight!” the proctor announced.

He might as well have announced the end of the duel, for Vir fired his spell the moment the words were out of his lips, hitting Vayren and extinguishing his protection orb’s charge.

Unable to absorb all of the magic before it sputtered out, Vayren began convulsing in shock before crumpling to the ground.

“Who are you?” he wheezed, his smug superiority nowhere to be seen. “How can you be so good? For a commoner to have this level of skill—”

Vir silently backed away, not dignifying him with an answer.

“The winner is Darsh!” the proctor announced.

Vir dropped his shoulders and immediately used the last morsels of Lightning prana to power Prana Vision once again. Once again, the ground lit up.

But this time, he recognized its shape. Its distinctive, conical shape that could only be one thing.

The corners of his lips curled upward despite himself.

Found you.
