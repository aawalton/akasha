Two weeks had passed, and in that time, Lira had expanded her linked paper operation while also working on a countermeasure for the truth stone, while both Aric and Kavard got ready to leave.

Aric’s expedition had been packing in the military keep for days, with six knights in total, including Aric, Deren, and Sir Liam, and the other knights Lira knew only vaguely. Each of them would ride his own horse, while three pack mules, one for every two knights, were loaded with rope, camping equipment, spears, armor, torches, and tools for mining and maintenance. Kavard’s preparation was quieter, since Estophon would be taking him out to the country estate in a single closed carriage with a handful of guards.

She had gathered all of this because she had planted linked papers almost everywhere. One was slipped inside Deren’s scabbard, another inside Celene’s journal, Steward Berem had one hidden beneath the footbed of his boot, and Dalwin carried one in the folds of his purple cravat, while a few more were buried in busy places where gossip gathered, like near the well and the kitchen. Each of those sheets carried a funnel rune beside the Hano rune, both drawn in invisible ink that Lira made from garlic and lemon juice, and the horn runes stayed on the twin sheets she kept with her. She left every pair inert on her end and activated them one at a time while she walked the gardens, shutting each one down immediately if nothing interesting was happening on the other side, since a live thread ran between any pair that was active.

Focused Hearing was more convenient, if she was honest with herself, but it needed her to know where someone was, while this let her listen to Deren or Celene even when she had no idea where either of them had gone. The sheet in Deren’s scabbard was the one she had hesitated over, since Aric’s Magical Perception was one of the few things in the castle likely to notice a thread of disturbance, and he walked beside Deren every day. However, a thread that barely flared and sat inert for most of the time was a risk she could live with, and the scabbard was the only location she was sure would travel with the expedition all the way to the Holtary Mountains.

The problem she ran into was keeping track of all those little papers. Once a sheet was out of her hands, she had no way to know where it was except through her memory of where she had placed it. The ones buried were one thing, but the ones hidden on people moved wherever they went, so the first thing that came to mind was a divination spell that let her find them.

Ladar. Hano. Oshkan.Divination. Connection. Location.

The idea was simple, since she already had Find North and Find Place, and both worked like a compass needle pulling at the back of her mind. This spell would do the same, except that it would use a paired sheet to find its twin, so that holding one half would tell her the direction and distance of the other. She did not need a long meditation to imprint the spell, because the concept of connection was already settled in her mind from other spells, and she only had to picture two halves of a torn page that always knew where the other half lay, the way her own hand knew where her other hand was in the dark.

Click.

She blind tested it in the library first, with one sheet in her hand and the twin wedged behind a book in the history section, and the pull came as a short, steady tug toward the shelves, along with a rough sense of how far away it was, a dozen paces or so. Then she walked out to the garden and tested it on the sheet hidden inside Steward Berem’s boot, and it pointed toward the stable, finding his exact location. Unlike Find Place, which gave her only a vague sense of direction and forced her to narrow the field step by step, like she had done with the running girls back after Salvek’s death, the link let her find the paired sheet in exact detail, and it did not seem to weaken with distance, since the magical link helped fill the gap that normally made divination unreliable.

Knowing the distance and direction did not tell her what was around a sheet, only how to get to it. And since the link had removed a large part of the doubt that came with that school of magic, she decided to push it further and create a spell that gave her a visual of the paper in relation to the space around it.

Ladar. Hano. Oshkan. Syneth. Kurur.

Divination. Connection. Location. Interpretation. Space.

Five words made it a tier one spell. She had enough divination cantrips behind her to imprint it without trouble, and Syneth and Kurur were words she had used so often by now that they felt like old reliable tools, one from Eagle Eyes and its variants and the other from every pocket of space she had ever drawn.

“Pika. Selan. Tureth.”

She thought about how she had looked down at the gardens from the top floor of the tower, and about the view of the city from above the wall, and she combined that perspective with the previous divination spell to build a complete image instead of only a direction and a distance. Ladar would give her the location, using the link to pinpoint it with accuracy, and Syneth would turn that information into an interpretation of the space around the linked paper. It took her slightly longer than she had expected, as she meditated on the link between interpretation and divination, before the click finally came and the spell was imprinted.

She reestablished the link to one of the sheets she had left in the garden, hidden under a bench.

“Kisar. Asatan. Hano.”

Then she tested the new spell.

“Ladar. Hano. Oshkan. Syneth. Kurur.”

The alcove where she was sitting disappeared, and the garden took its place, seen from above, though it looked more like a painting of the place than a real view of it, with the hedges almost like brushstrokes, two maids crossing the path, each carrying a basket of fruit, and the bench itself at the center. The outline of the paper was somehow visible beneath it even though the sheet was buried. She could turn the view and widen it a little, though the further it stretched from the paper, the less detail it held, until the edges melted into a blur of lines and colors.

She tried again on the sheet hidden inside Celene’s journal, reestablishing the link before the divination, and a stretch of Market Street opened beneath her, with the headmaid walking outside the palace grounds, where she stopped to talk with a seamstress over a bolt of cotton. Lira could not see her face from that angle, but she could see the outline of the paper sitting inside the satchel at the woman’s side, and nobody else would be carrying Celene’s journal. There was no loss of clarity despite the distance, which confirmed that the link had made up for what was normally divination’s greatest weakness.

Then she cast Mage Sight to make sure the new spell did not disturb the thread in any way she could perceive. The link was the same faint thread as before, running between the sheets whether she was casting or not, and the divination barely made it flare, less than the horn and funnel spells did, which was a relief. She collapsed the link on her end once more anyway, since she had never kept one running longer than she needed to.

With this, she technically had eyes on the Aric expedition through the sheet in Deren’s scabbard. Now Kavard needed to be next, with two days left before his carriage left the castle and not a single sheet on him yet.
