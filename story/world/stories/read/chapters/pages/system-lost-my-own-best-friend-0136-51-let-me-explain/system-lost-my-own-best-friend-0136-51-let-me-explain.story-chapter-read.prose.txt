I wonder if the prevalence of open structures and courtyards in Fa’aun architecture is because of the warm climate—though from what I’ve heard, it gets very cold in the winter. The courtyard is pretty and green, and the smell of fresh grass and flower blossoms is carried on a warm breeze.

Nicest prison I’ve ever been in.

Hmm. Interesting that she usually refers to her mother as “Lady Gaa.” It’s oddly formal compared to how Talla and Rara are with their mom. Tamara is severe and intimidating, but she still treats her daughters with genuine warmth, and vice versa. Is the Gaa family more distant?

Lori leads me deeper into the estate, where her hoofsteps echo against the marble tiles. It’s eerily quiet. No staff, no other guests or family members, just the two of us walking in silence.

“Where is everybody?” I ask.

“Elsewhere,” she replies tersely. “Lady Gaa has ordered this wing of the estate to be cleared.”

“Why?”

“Ask her yourself,” she sighs, stopping next to an ornate wooden door and gesturing for me to enter. “The matriarch awaits within.”

I step inside, and Lori closes the door behind me rather than following me in. As the latch clicks shut, I feel an odd pressure in my ears, like they are just about to pop.

“There’s some kind of magic here,” Maggie informs me. “Hard to place, but it’s filling the whole room and the door is the origin.”

Well that’s not weird or concerning at all. The room itself isn’t anything out of the ordinary—more alchemical lamps for lighting, some religious frescos, and a low table with colorful cushions surrounding it for seating. The Goa estate has tons of rooms like this for private meetings, though this one feels a lot more private.

Across the table, Lady Arelii Gaa Baanu sits on her hooves with her head lowered and her eyes closed in an almost meditative pose. As I enter, she looks up and smiles warmly.

“Maev Allison,” she greets me, even using my full name despite how hard it is for Fa’aun to pronounce. She nails it, too. “Welcome to my home. Please, make yourself comfortable.”

She gestures across the table and I purse lips as I take the offered seat. I briefly debate whether to sit in the Fa’aun style or not, but decide to test her offer to make myself comfortable and sit cross-legged instead.

“Matriarch Arelii Gaa Baanu. To what do I owe the pleasure of your kidnapping?”

Rather than get offended, she just chuckles.

“You’re quite erudite for someone who’s only just recently learned our language.”

“I’ve been practicing.”

I blush and avert my eyes. Dang it, that must have been Evie clinging to him.

Oh for—really?!

Well, one of them was stabbed by Vi and another was shot—possibly by their own ally? That whole night was kind of a mess. But who’s counting?
