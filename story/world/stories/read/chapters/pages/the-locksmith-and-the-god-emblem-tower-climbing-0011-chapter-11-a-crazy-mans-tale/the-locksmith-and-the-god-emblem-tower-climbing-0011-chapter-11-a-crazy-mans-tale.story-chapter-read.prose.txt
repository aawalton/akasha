The church hall felt egregiously... unceremonious? If Harry knew one thing about religion, it was that people took it seriously. The lack of ornaments and overall bleakness of the supposed sacred space felt counter-intuitive to say the least. The pews were covered in grime and the candles felt naked without the dancing flames. Irregular pieces of the arched roof were brown, in comparison to its original white, probably because they fell apart. Even the illustrations faces were clipped, as if God himself didn't recognize this church.

Tossing aside the negative aspects, Harry focused on the beautiful red carpet, which was also chewed at the corners. Fuckin' hell... Is there something in this place that isn't destroyed or somewhat dirty? He thought to himself as he observed the fake sun through one of the windows.

His answer was no. There wasn't.

Alongside him was Madeline, Prism, and Brun. The latter had an empty piece of paper she took from one of her pockets. According to the old woman, the place was empty. Outside of it, there seemed to be a few hooded figures all around the church like ants surrounding a candy. Yeah, like that's not suspicious at all. But he trusted her still; he had to.

Goldman and Daniel were now, thankfully, dressed. The former wore several metal plates around his clothing, which was minimalist, to say the least. The Locksmith felt dumb to not have realized it sooner, but the man was James Goldman, a famous pirate who was hunted down by the marines for "disrupting order". The tales around Goldman told Harry he was as sentimental as a door and shared all the properties of a proper killer. But as much as he was worried about the gentle giant, something didn't add up; he was acting the opposite way. Well, the government had a way to change the narrative to fit their needs, but he also had to consider the fact that Goldman was a possible hazard.

Either way, the man seemed to be on his side. With his large boots with metal plates on the soles, Harry had to play into him. Huh... So these metallic parts help him in some way? Harry conjectured the purpose of these parts, as they clearly didn't seem like a regular armor. Glancing at Harry, the Goldman simply smiled with his bright white teeth. Wait, weren't pirates supposed to have—

"Where's the troll!?" Daniel bellowed, his voice echoing throughout the church. "Show yourself, you monster!"

The viking, as expected, wore a completely different outfit as Goldman. Everything around him was coated with fur, even his gloves. He was a wolf in... wolf's clothing. At least every part of him was sincere.

Brun simply looked away, ashamed for her father.

"We're here!" A voice, surprisingly, answered Daniel's request. "D-Don't hurt us!"

"...Are you serious?" Harry was the first to answer as everyone looked around, searching for the perpetrator. "You bonked us on the head just a few hours ago."

"We only did what he told us to!" It was a male's, shaken voice. "The Shepherd has all the answers! Just you wait until he tells you. He's seen the Astronomer. He will tell you."

What could it mean? Yes, the Astronomer was basically a mythical figure and one closely connected with the creation of the Tower. What begged the question was if he was real? Or maybe it was a she? He saw the Astronomer? The Locksmith thought to himself.

"Why should my crew believe you?" Goldman spoke. "Don't get me wrong, mate, we mean well; just wondering where's the exit for the next floor. That's all. But the Locksmith is right in fearing you."

Crew? Harry added inwardly.

"...The Locksmith?" The voice answered after a moment. "The Lock Emblem? Oh no... this won't end well."

"What is he talking about?" Madeline asked.

"I..." Sharing her confusion, Harry replied. "I don't know. Maybe he knows me from the outside?"

But who could've been? He knew the Tower messed with time in an unseen way, so it was possible that someone from the modern times knew of the nickname "Locksmith", but it was mostly in the capital. Maybe... Harry's thoughts became muddled as he thought about the possibility of a familiar skeleton inside the tower.

He couldn't also forget about the incident with the Lock Emblem many years ago.

"Stop!" A deep, hoarse voice came from the presbytery. Behind it, a passage seemed to have been opened.

With his hands behind his back, a Herculean figure could be seen. Certainly more than two meters tall, the hooded man made his presence known in the church as his footsteps put fear in the ground below them.

Harry noticed a curious movement of the mysterious man's head. It was as if he were waiting for their answer to choose where to look. Is that his Emblem?

"...Are you the Shepherd—" Goldman tried to start the conversation but was quickly cut off by the man.

"Yes. Yes, I am. I'm here, and I am the Shepherd." He replied frantically, "You, well, if you're here then it's because the Tower called you. They went through protocol, didn't they? They were tied up separately, and then they came together. Isn't that right? Separate and join, always like that, separate and join."

"Yes, sir." The cultist replied, still from outside the church. His voice vibrated in different frequencies, as if it was hard for him to say. "T-They were separated and then they, um... came together, as you wanted."

"Perfect..." The Locksmith could sense a smile on his unseen face, "I-I'm sorry, my colleagues. I didn't mean to scare you, not like that. I mean, I didn't want to introduce myself in a way that would frighten you... What I mean is that I need you, and you soon needed me. You want to climb the tower, don't you? The Logos Tower. Yes..."

Before answering, Goldman looked at his companions and shrugged, unsure of what to say. Slowly, he took another step forward while the colossus still looked helplessly to his sides.

"...I believe so... sir." He added the last part after seeing the way the cultists addressed their leader, "I see you're a little... worried about something. Is there anything we could help with?"

"There's always something." He said quickly before pausing for a moment, "I want—no—I need to climb the tower. I tried alone and couldn't. I went far, I know I went far. Beyond the mountains, beyond the floating island, even beyond the coliseum. I know there's a settlement forming somewhere now; His attendant told me. He knows everything. I reached the telescope. I need..."

Floating island? Telescope? Coliseum!? These were the thoughts of everyone involved. Even the shaken cultist had no idea what his leader was saying.

Confused, they exchanged frightened glances as the man seemed increasingly erratic; never underestimate the man who lost control of himself.

Perhaps his Emblem was strong enough to compensate for his shattered mind. Harry thought. What did it all mean? He had mentioned something about separating and reuniting, and that it had to be done this way...

After thinking for a moment, the Locksmith realized he would never reach the same conclusion as the leader. He was visibly disturbed by anything he had seen up there. Any argument that would be distorted by his already deteriorated mind to incorporate the flawed logic he already applied. Harry wouldn't find logic, at least not alone.

Even Goldman seemed uncomfortable, but still decided to try the diplomatic approach.

"Could you tell us what this means? At least tell us your name—"

"No!" The Shepherd cut him off again and shouted, "...No. You can't—I mean—you can't understand now. Not now. I can't talk, I have to show you, I have to show you what it is. The Emblems and the wall, all of that... They represent something, you know? Not just about control and obsession with some item. They have meaning beyond their purpose in our hands. They came from something, and we're just using them. He discovered this, he surely saw it a long time ago. I knew I had seen it. Even through my armor, I saw it. Did you know I was the first? The attendant told me I was. He's the oldest prisoner."

Oldest prisoner? Is he talking about the masked man?

"I am Conrad. But it isn't important. Yes, I went..." He continued looking down and raising his hands. "There was nothing here. Just books and books and... and monsters. They feed on the loop like a beggar feeds on kindness. They survive like that... They evolved with a purpose. That's why I want to climb, because now I understand. Now I can be a worthy vessel and help him. You who separated and reunited. You will help me reach the top, no matter what."

Conrad... the name's familiar. Brun thought to herself as she secretly tried to examine the colossus using the Manuscript Emblem. Oh. Oh no.

"Goldman! He is—" She touched his shoulder, but it was already too late.

"Wait a minute." Goldman now spoke less fearfully, "We aren't going to do anything, yet. Unless you tell us where Vela is, we won't help you with—

And without another word, the man, who looked moribund, nimbly used the ambo in front of him to jump, falling right in front of Goldman and breaking the tiles in his arrival. Like a golem, his landing shook the church and damaged the surrounding pews.

The Shepherd lifted his head and took off his hood; there weren't any eyes. As there weren't brows, mouth, nose or hair. His face was covered by a steel helmet, dense enough that it would be like a prison for anyone inside of it. Focused, the colossus stared coldly at the gentle giant. Although Goldman himself hadn't recoiled like the others, the sweat on his brow betrayed his concern.

After a few moments of silence, Goldman took another step forward, standing face to face with the titan. Harry hadn't thought he'd encountered someone as large as Goldman, but incredibly, this someone existed, and he was standing right in front of the pirate.

"If not for good, then for bad." The colossus whispered, then paused for a moment and said, "...Vela?" the colossus said, "The man with the candelabra? He is the sacrifice to the cycle and to the mother flame. That's... thematic, don't you think?"

And he punched Goldman with such force that he was thrown upwards, making a hole in the church's arched roof.
