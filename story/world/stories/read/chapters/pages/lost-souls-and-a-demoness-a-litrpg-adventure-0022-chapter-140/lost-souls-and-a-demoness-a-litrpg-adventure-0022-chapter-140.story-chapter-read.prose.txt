The fourth and final tower collapsed with the long, drawn-out rumble of an approaching storm. Jade descended back to the shaking ground, her vision obscured by the thick cloud of dust thrown up by the collapse. It had only been a few moments since she'd destroyed the last mechanism powering the barrier in the cathedral, but monsters were already manifesting all throughout this slice of the corespace. Just as before, they were radiant creatures of celestial light. Suits of armor that glowed with an inner radiance, accompanied by robed humanoid figures whose bodies were comprised of vibrant light that somehow formed physical substance. They approached at a run, each one turning towards their small group after emerging from the luminous pillars that spawned them.

"Tara?" Jade asked.

"Working on it…"

The tek'kalla had her wand out and was gathering an orange ball of fiery energy at its tip. The magic pulsed like a strobe light, flickering as tendrils of fire snaked around it.

"Not yet…" The tek'kalla muttered. "Almost…"

Jade held her blades at the ready, joining Luis and Fenrin at the front of their party. She could pick out individual monsters now. They were closing fast, converging on them like moths to a flame.

"They're almost on us." Luis said. His voice was steady, but Jade tasted his anxiety as the large cluster of monsters bore down on them. The last battle had been difficult, and now they were down Aylin and Parker as well. Jade wasn't sure they could take on this many of the celestial monsters, especially considering her weakness to them. If Tara didn't time her spell right-

WHOOSH.

A blazing sphere of fire blasted over their heads, arcing through the sky like a shooting star. It fell like a boulder from a catapult, smashing into the midst of the mob of approaching monsters. Flames exploded through their ranks, tossing many of the celestial creatures to the ground. The blast was bright enough to momentarily light up the entire battlefield, giving Jade a clear look at the charred remains of nearly a dozen of the center-most monsters. A wave of searing heat rolled over them, hot enough to make their enchanted wards kick in. The defensive barriers crackled and sparked as they absorbed the very edge of Tara's spell, throwing of scintillating sparks in a rainbow of color. Behind Jade, Tara grunted, dropping to one knee and clutching at her arm. Jade tasted the edge of the painful backlash from exerting her magical conduits again, but didn't have time to spare her more than a concerned glance. They had to move.

"Go!" She shouted as soon as the blast wave was past them. "Before they recover!"

She dashed into the smoldering remains of what had moments before been a beautiful garden, now blackened and charred. Fenrin and Luis were right behind her, accompanied by Naomi for support. Siora remained back with Tara, keeping an eye on her and their surroundings while the tek'kalla recovered.

It soon became apparent that either Tara had poured a little extra juice into her latest fireball, or the Celestial Spirits and Sanctified Spirits weren't as resilient to it as the other monsters had been. Their party cut through a good half of the assembled monsters before the rest even got to their feet, and another quarter before any were able to put up something resembling a decent fight. By the time they were fully recovered, there weren't enough of them to pose a real danger to their well-trained team. It only took a couple of minutes to finish the fight, the last of the radiant knights falling to a trio of well-placed electrified arrows from Fenrin's bow.

"Ouch…" Tara groaned as the team regrouped, opening and closing her fist repeatedly.

"How are you?" Jade asked, scanning the area for any monsters they might have missed.

"Worse than I hoped, better than I expected." The tek'kalla said with a wry grimace. "I'm not completely spent, but don't expect anything more than this for the rest of the day." She patted the pulsecaster pistol at her hip.

"Tara, you really shouldn't channel any more mana without resting." Siora protested, a worried look on her face. "I did what I could, but your mana channels are strained. That's not something I can fix with a potion or spell.""I'll be fine as long as I stick to this." Tara promised, drawing her weapon and stowing her wand. "Say what you will about them, and I do, but the one thing they're good at is mana efficiency."

"Only if you're sure." Jade said. "Luis?""All clear over here, chica."

"Over here, too." Naomi chimed in from a short distance away.

"Great." Jade took a deep breath, then touched her earpiece. "Everyone, to the boss room! It's showtime."

They took off at a run, dashing for the tall, imposing walls of the central cathedral. Naomi, running alongside Jade, raised an eyebrow.

"'It's showtime'? What's that? Our new catchphrase?"

"Oh, we should totally have one!" Luis said enthusiastically. "Something dramatic we say right before taking down boss monsters!"

"We aren't in an action movie, Luis." Jade said, rolling her eyes. Then, to her immense surprise, a system window appeared before her eyes.

New Quest: You Had Time to Say That?

What's better than slaying monsters and defeating evil? Doing it in style.

Objective: Deliver a snappy one-liner immediately before defeating an enemy. Rewards scale with the strength of the enemy and the quality of the one-liner.

Reward: Magic item

Reward: Crescent coins

"Really?" Jade read the message a second time, incredulous. She gave Luis a flat look. "Okay, now I know the system is messing with me."

"Oh, score!" Luis, having received the same quest along with Naomi, pumped a fist. "This is going to be awesome."

"I can't believe this…"

Beside her, Naomi and Fenrin stifled laughter, and even Tara looked amused. The tension in the group eased noticeably, and Jade wondered if that had been the point. Was the Labyrinth aware enough to offer up an amusing quest right before a dangerous battle? Had it been trying to get them to loosen up before they reached the boss? And stranger still, this was the first quest she'd seen that didn't offer experience as a reward. The longer she spent in this strange world, the more questions she had.

"Alright, let's focus, everyone." Jade said, unable to keep a grin off her face. "Time to do what we came here for."

Jade was the first to reach the large double doors of the cathedral. After doing a full circuit around the grounds while visiting the towers, they were back where they had started. They'd left the heavy doors slightly ajar, allowing a shaft of brilliant light to illuminate the gardens outside. Jade slipped through the opening and stopped, weapons ready and alert for danger. When it came to boss battles, you never knew what you were getting into. As much as Jade wanted to charge in and deal with whatever the Labyrinth threw at them this time, she knew better than to dive straight into the unknown. This time, at least.

The cavernous room remained clear of the debris they'd taken time to move away earlier, leaving a large, empty hall lined with rows of thick columns. At one end, an altar rested atop a raised dais, the only part of the chamber that looked spared from whatever had ravaged the rest of the structure. The floating Core crystal dominated the center of the space, as tall as a two-story building. It floated a dozen feet above the cracked stone floor, radiating a golden light that illuminated the entire room. As expected, the shimmering barrier was gone, dispelled when the last of the towers fell. Now, the figure who had been sealed within its confines stood before the Core, staring back at Jade with a cool, dispassionate gaze.

"So." She spoke as the rest of the group came to a stop with Jade, her melodious voice devoid of emotion. "You are to be my next challengers. The latest to be put to the test."

The woman had an angelic appearance. She was at least eight feet tall and was clad in gleaming silver armor adorned with etchings of a blazing sun. Her long, blonde hair spilled freely down her back, disappearing behind a pair of massive, white-feathered wings. A sheathed sword hung from her hip, its polished hilt glinting in the Core light as she took two steps closer to Jade and her companions, now arrayed in formation against her. They hesitated, unsure if they should attack. Boss monsters didn't usually speak. Taking advantage of the unexpected pause, Jade pulled up her info box.

Seraphine, Angel of Battle

Level: 33

Creature Type: Celestial (Core Boss)

Origin: Labyrinth-born

Jade grimaced, closing the window. She'd been expecting a powerful foe, but level 33 was a full seven higher than hers. And there was no doubt that a celestial boss would possess attacks that targeted her racial weaknesses. Again, Jade felt a familiar tingle in the back of her mind. She had felt this presence before, at the altar in Siora's home. She had been right. This place, perhaps even this foe, was imbued with the power of Growth. Jade didn't know what that meant, but something told her that it was important.

"And what are you testing us for?" Fenrin was the first to speak, stepping forward and addressing the angel. He showed none of the anxiety Jade could sense in him, instead projecting an aura of confidence that their weary group sorely needed.

"Your preparedness to fight in the battles to come." Seraphine replied coolly. "It is my… eternal duty."

For the first time, a hint of emotion slipped into her voice. Jade could sense nothing from the carefully guarded mind of their foe, but she still had the feeling that the angel before them carried a weariness beyond what she could hope to understand. Her words had a certain weight to them, laden with something Jade couldn't identify.

"We have plenty of battles ahead of us, and no shortage of fallen enemies in our wake." Jade said, masking her own trepidation with a defiant glare. "If you're volunteering to be the next one, be my guest."

"A demon wayfarer…" Seraphine focused on Jade for the first time. Her expression didn't change but this time, Jade did detect the faint scent of surprise in her mind. "I wonder… but no." she shook her head. "It is not for me to question."

The celestial being spread her wings and lifted into the air, her armor gleaming in the brilliant light shining from the Core. The turbulent energy contained in the large, crystalline matrix was fluctuating, and the gemstone itself was sparking with small bursts of magic. Seraphine didn't appear to notice. Jade briefly considered asking if they could take some time to stabilize the volatile artifact hovering in the center of the chamber they were about to do battle in, but one look at the angel's impassive expression told her that it would be a waste of time. She extended an arm to one side, ignoring the sword at her hip and conjuring a spear of radiant energy that she levelled right at Jade's chest.

"My duty is to serve. Again, and again…" Seraphine closed her eyes. When she opened them again, there was an emptiness held within the opalescent orbs. Something was missing from them, an absence Jade could feel but not name. And then she struck, and there was no more time for idle thoughts.
