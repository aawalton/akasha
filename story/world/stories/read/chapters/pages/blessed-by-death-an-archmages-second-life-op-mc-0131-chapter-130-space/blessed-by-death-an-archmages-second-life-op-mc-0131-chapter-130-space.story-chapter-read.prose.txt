They bid their farewells to Yuki. She seemed surprised that they were leaving so suddenly, but she took it in stride easily enough. She bowed respectfully to Anastasia, Selene, and Qorbin, then turned to Levi. She studied him for a few seconds before her expression set..

“May I?” she asked.

Levi frowned. “Sure…?”

He stiffened, then relaxed, as Yuki stepped forward and gave him a hug. She smelled like jasmine and white tea, and he instinctively breathed in deeply before realizing what he was doing. Fortunately, Yuki didn’t seem to notice.

“Thanks for saving my life,” she whispered.

Levi smiled. “Of course.”

Yuki pulled back a moment later. Behind her, Qorbin was grinning at Levi, Terminus looked vaguely consternated, and Anastasia’s expression was oddly blank. Levi tilted his head in puzzlement at their reactions, but before he could think too deeply on it, Yuki continued.

“Thank you all for helping Yukihasa,” Yuki said, glancing around at each of them. “We will not forget that in our greatest time of need, you answered the call.” She smiled. “Be well, and may we meet again.”

It had taken Levi, Anastasia, and Selene over two days to reach Yukihasa from Luminarche using a mixture of flight, teleportation, and shadow travel.

It took them, plus Qorbin, approximately two minutes to return.

“I don’t know about this,” Qorbin said, looking rather nervous as they shot straight up into the air, the buildings quickly becoming mere specks beneath them.

After saying their farewells, Levi had wasted no time in levitating the four of them vertically upward. They rapidly ascended, streaking through the air like a reverse meteorite. In just a few seconds, they broke through the clouds, and in a few more seconds, the clouds looked like dark wispy waves below them.

At this proximity, the full moon was utterly massive, shining down upon them in all its glory. Levi resisted the urge to shudder. The moon had always made his instincts prickle, and this close up, he could practically feel a frantic buzzing at the base of his neck. Though, none of the others seemed to be affected; Anastasia was taking in the view, Qorbin was preparing fall damage negation spells, and Selene…

Levi blinked. Selene was gazing up at the moon with a strange expression on her face, faint beams of silver reflected in her midnight black eyes. She seemed almost… wistful?

Huh. Levi supposed that the moon held an allure for many magical species, such as werewolves or vampires. Still, he didn’t recall archdemons being on that list…

Well, no matter. Maybe she was just an odd one out. At any rate, this should be high enough.

“Can’t you just teleport us back?” Qorbin asked, a bit more worriedly. “Is this really necessary?”

“International teleportation comes with a whole host of issues, you know this,” Levi said. “Besides, I’ve been wanting to test out this new skill for a while now.” He grinned. “Come on, it’ll be fun.”

Qorbin paled, and even Anastasia snapped her head over to look at Levi.

“Test out?” Qorbin repeated. “Wait, is this your first time using this skiiIIILLLLLLLLLLL–?”

[Windbreaker]!

A thin bubble of magic formed around them and without warning, they blasted forward, Qorbin’s final word tearing into a high-pitched shriek. The acceleration was instantaneous; one second they were at rest, the next they were hurtling through the air so fast that the bubble of magic instantly began glowing red from the sheer friction.

Levi himself let out a whoop of absolute joy. Holy fuck, they were fast!

The world blurred around them, stretching out from their unbelievable velocity. And they were completely silent, too! Well, aside from Qorbin’s screams of terror, of course. In addition to creating a sealed atmosphere, the bubble of magic also assumed an aerodynamic teardrop shape that let them carve through the air with hardly a whisper, even as they surpassed the speed of sound many times over–

Wait, why were they heading directly toward the moon?

“Holy fuck!” Levi swore as he scrambled to redirect their vector, releasing great gusts of wind. Several seconds later, he let out a sigh of relief. “Alright, we’re back on track.”

“How did we get off track?” Anastasia asked, her entire form tense. “We’re traveling in a straight line, aren’t we?”

Next to her, Selene let out a laugh. Instantly, they all went silent. They’d never heard Selene laugh before. She was already emotionless on the best of days; the most they ever got from her was a mechanical smile. Even Qorbin forgot to be terrified for a moment as they stared at her in disbelief.

Selene either didn’t notice or didn’t care. “We are traveling in a straight line,” she said, voice filled with a disbelieving mirth. “It’s just that we’re moving so fast, we got thrown off course by the curve of the planet itself.”

There was a long moment of silence as they processed her words.

“Oh,” Levi said. “I knew I was forgetting something.”

“You nincompoop!” Qorbin hissed. “You nearly sent us into space!”

“Okay hold on, you can’t blame me for that,” Levi protested. “How was I supposed to know the skill would be this fast?”

“By trying it out first in a controlled environment like a normal person!”

Within a minute, they crossed the border into Luminarche territory. Levi doubted a single border guard was able to detect them, considering their height and speed. Another minute later, and they reached Luxanne. Levi had to use [Supersensory Awareness] just to stop them in time; otherwise, they would’ve blown right past their destination. They came to an abrupt stop outside of Luxanne’s borders.

“We’re here,” Levi said, somewhat unnecessarily.

Then, gravity took hold and they dropped from the sky. Right before they hit the ground, Levi negated his momentum, Qorbin disappeared in a swirl of shadows, Selene became a swirl of shadows, and ethereal golden wings sprouted from Anastasia’s back.

“That was fascinating,” Selene said the second she rematerialized, and though she was expressionless once more, there was a certain delight in her eyes that Levi recognized plain as day. The pure, simple delight every mage felt at experiencing a new piece of magic.

Levi grinned. “Wasn’t it?”

He was already mentally breaking down the skill’s internal mechanics. The trick seemed to mainly lay in the magical bubble; the teardrop shape had maximized their speed while minimizing air resistance. Wait, but then why did the bubble begin glowing red? Oh, of course, it had heated up not from friction but rather air compression.

“We’re really back,” Anastasia said in wonder, taking in their surroundings as they began making their way toward Luxanne’s gates. “What tier even was that skill?”

“I think I used my level 800 skill slot for that.”

Qorbin and Anastasia both stilled.

“What,” Qorbin said.

“Level… 800?” Anastasia echoed.

Levi paused. Oh, right. It was probably a faux pas to reveal that part, huh? An individual gained a new skill every five levels; the higher the level, the stronger the skill. However, since levels were private information, people generally just classified skills by broad tiers. Low-tier for levels 1-200, mid-tier for levels 200-400, high-tier for levels 400-600, and ultra-high-tier for anything higher.

“Err, I meant ultra-high-tier,” Levi said. “Sorry.”

Qorbin stared at him. “I know you unlocked your System, but… what level are you?”

“Qorbin!” Anastasia snapped, sounding scandalized. “Where are your manners?”

“Somewhere in orbit,” Qorbin said. “Which, incidentally, is where Levi almost just sent us–” Anastasia glared at him, and Qorbin raised his hand in surrender. “Alright, alright.”

Levi eyed them, amused. “I don’t mind sharing… I do consider you all close friends, you know.”

“What?” Anastasia frowned. “No, it’s not about that. We might get interrogated by some third party, or even possessed by the Chorus. We still know nothing about its possession magic, remember?”

Qorbin also grew serious. “Ah, good point.”

Levi raised an eyebrow. “Does it really matter if others know what level I am?”

“Yes,” Qorbin and Anastasia responded in unison.

“People adjust their strategies all the time based on what tier their enemy is,” Anastasia said.

“It never hurts to be underestimated,” Qorbin said, rather meaningfully.

Levi’s eyes flickered at that. “I see…”

He turned to Selene in case she had anything to add, only to see her staring blankly at him. If he didn’t know any better, he’d even say that she was stunned.

“Friends?” she said uncertainly.

Levi regarded her for a moment. Then before she could react, he reached out and flicked her in the forehead.

She hissed, grabbing her forehead as her shadows rippled in agitation. “What was that for?”

“For asking silly questions,” Levi said. Ahead of them, the gates slowly came into view. “Speaking of, Qorbin, do you mind forging some papers for her?”

During their initial departure, they’d smuggled Selene out, but it was probably better to do things by the book.

“Already five steps ahead of you,” Qorbin said, reaching into his cloak and withdrawing a thick stack of parchment. “I prepared identification papers, adventuring permits, international travel documents, even tossed in an exotic hunting license.”

At the gates, the guards sleepily checked their papers. Qorbin had assumed the disguise of an old man – Levi had warned him that the Royal Sentinels were pinning the Montague murders on him. As usual, his disguise was flawless, and even if it weren’t, it was well after midnight and the guards clearly would rather be in bed.

“Welcome back, High Priestess Anastasia, Adventurer Ironwood, Adventurer Selene, Merchant Swansong,” one of them droned politely. He paused as if recalling something. “Ah right. High Priestess Anastasia, the Cardinal asked us to deliver a message to you. Please meet with him as soon as you return. He’s awaiting you in the main Church.”

Anastasia froze for a split second before her expression smoothed over. “Understood. Thank you for letting me know.”

And then they were through. At this time of night, the streets were nearly empty, a stark contrast to how Endo had been lit up in fireworks and celebrations.

“I should get going,” Anastasia said.

Levi glanced at her. “Will you be alright?”

Anastasia hesitated, then smiled. “Thank you for asking, but I’ll be fine. I’ll… I’ll see you later.”

With that, she turned and began making her way to the Church. Qorbin and Levi watched her leave with identical pensive looks on their faces.

“She mentioned she might get a Sealing Order placed on her due to her actions in Yukihasa,” Levi said. “As well as something about an Excision?”

“Punishments from the Holy Church for going rogue,” Qorbin explained quietly. “A Sealing Order would condemn her to lifetime imprisonment within the Grand Cathedral’s Inner Sanctum. An Excision would take her life altogether.”

Levi didn’t outwardly react, but there was a brief flash of killing intent so potent that several nearby insects curled up and died.

“Is that so?” he murmured.

“Barbaric,” Selene said. “This is why nobody likes the Church, in either world.”

“Yeah…” Qorbin hummed. “So. We going after her?”

For a second, Levi almost said yes. However…

“Not yet,” he said. “I need to take care of something urgent first. Besides, she might be able to talk her way out of things. If there’s anyone whose diplomatic skills I trust, it’s hers. If we just barge in, we might potentially make things worse. ”

Qorbin grunted. “True enough. Okay. They won’t make their decision immediately anyway. We should have time.” Then, he tilted his head. “Something urgent, huh? Are you ever going to tell me what that ‘something’ is?”

“Later,” Levi said. “I’ll fill you in later. I promise.”

Qorbin examined him. “Alright. I’ll be at the Rusty Dagger for the time being, then.” He glanced at Selene. “Care to join me?”

“Okay,” Selene said tonelessly, yet notably without any hesitation.

A bit belatedly, Levi felt like he had just made a very, very bad mistake. However, it was too late.

Qorbin grinned. “That’s what I like to hear. I raided the imperial breweries during the final battle and got my hands on some gorgeous bottles.”

“You what?” said Levi.

“Haven’t had me some good ol’ Yukihasan alcohol in years,” Qorbin said. “The Emperor’s damn trade embargoes… Thank the Goddess he’s finally gone.”

“I’ll meet up with you guys later,” Levi said. “Qorbin, please don’t corrupt her.”

“No promises,” Qorbin said.

The two disappeared in twin shadowy blurs. Levi watched them leave with some trepidation. Hopefully, the bar would still be standing by the time morning came.

Then, he turned toward the Institute of Ascension. Even from this distance, he could see its central tower piercing up into the night sky. His expression grew grim.

It was time to pay the Restricted Sanctum a visit. He needed to have a little talk with his former professor.
