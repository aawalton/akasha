The spirit of a cultivator was a complex thing, shaped and strengthened by a long litany of contributing factors.

Fortunately for Aodhán, he had amassed many of those factors—foremost of which was the world tattoo between his shoulder blades marking him as a transmigrant. Containing enough power to assist in the creation of a Calamity’s inner world, the tattoo elevated his spirit to a level only double inheritors could ever dream of reaching.

Yet with every step Aodhán took on the mountain, it became increasingly obvious to those watching that his spirit stood out—even among transmigrants of the same level. The World Tattoo wasn’t the only advantage he possessed. His bloodline, Icon, Ideal, and Seals all played their part in strengthening his spirit.

But the true contributors—the real source of that extra spiritual strength—came down to three major factors.

His Familiar. His Aspect. And his relationship with the Origin Plane.

His familiar, Varéc, was more than just a powerful asset. He was the draconic manifestation of his spirit, born from storm and chaos. Every time they merged, Aodhán's spirit practically doubled in strength, becoming whole.

But the contribution of his Aspect was even greater, having forced open a series of pathways within his spirit—pathways that should have remained inaccessible until the Mythic class. Those pathways had been further tempered by lightning tribulations so disastrous that he had been forced to crawl back from the brink of death, forging his spirit in a storm of chaos and fire.

And lastly, there was his relationship with the Origin Plane.

Unlike most advanced awakened, who had only reached the CHANNEL stage, Aodhán had reached the FORCE stage—chaos responding so readily to his call that he could pull it directly into reality and shape it.

That was a feat common only to Mythics. It was even considered the standard spiritual stage for early Mythics. Yet, Aodhán had reached that stage, his spirit steeped in the same volume of chaos that washed over the spirits of Mythics.

The point was simple.

Aodhán stood apart, even among transmigrants. Everything he had amassed made certain of that. And though he wasn’t as strong as a Mythic yet, he stood so close he could almost reach out and touch it.

And he proved it four hours later by taking a shuddering, dripping step onto the 1000th stair.

His entire body shook beneath the pressure of the mountain, now so overwhelming that even breathing was a struggle. Groaning in soul-deep exhaustion, he raised his gaze to the hundreds of steps still above him.

He was already halfway up the special sect, and though Aodhán didn’t think he could make it all the way to the 1200th step, he was determined to climb as high as possible.

And he wasn’t at his limit yet.

He had used up nearly all his buffers just to get here. But he still had stubbornness and willpower. And that was bound to take him a few more steps.

He glanced at the 1200th step again, sweat pouring down his shuddering frame as his expression hardened. Summoning all the stubbornness he could muster, Aodhán forced himself to take the next step.

***

News of Aodhán's unusual ascent up the Mountain of Tempest spread through the sect like wildfire—hushed whispers burning through each stratum until they reached the special sect, where the transmigrants and double inheritors resided.

There were only six of them in the sect, each a cultivator of immense strength and renown. Yet even among them—the peak talents of the sect—only one had ever made it to the 1000th step on Tempest Mountain.

Nymera Vaelthorne.

But even she had only achieved such a feat at the sixtieth tier.

Aodhán, however, was only at the thirty-sixth tier. And to make things worse, he was still climbing.

“How is this possible?” Sophran Thunderstride—third in rank of the special sect—asked the underling who had brought him the information, the world tattoo on his back glowing faintly against storm-gray skin.

He was at the seventieth tier of advancement and was widely known throughout the sect for being completely unflappable. A rock at sea, they called him.

This time, however, that rock cracked, his familiar smile absent—replaced by a frown of confusion.

“No, this cannot be right.” He shook his head in disbelief. “What sort of monster is this? Are you sure he isn’t higher than that, Merrik? Perhaps the judges made a mistake.”

“I doubt that is the case, Senior Brother.” Core disciple Merrik shook his head. “Reading a person’s tier is not so complicated that they would make such an error. They are Mythics.”

“Still,” Sophran muttered, unable to believe what he was hearing. The Mountain of Tempest was a trial he undertook every six months. But even at the seventieth tier, he had only managed to reach the 900th step—his highest so far.

Nymera had been considered a monster for reaching the 1000th step, ten tiers lower than his own.

But if the judges were right, then this boy was an even greater monster—his spirit strength so overwhelming it eclipsed them all.

Shaking his head, Sophran asked, “Aside from his bloodline and his obvious strength, did you notice anything else about this Aodhán?”

Merrik hesitated for a moment before nodding. “Wings, Senior Brother. Draconic-like wings. The judges suspect he has a familiar.”

“What?” Sophran exclaimed, his electric-blue eyes bulging. “You can’t be serious.”

Merrik shook his head. “I fear that I am, Senior Brother. The boy is a monster. He’s just like Dravion Ashscar.”

Sophran stuttered, too shocked to form words. A thirty-sixth-tier transmigrant—with a familiar and a spirit strength nearing that of elders?

He had to see it for himself. He had to see it with his own eyes.

And without hesitation, he grabbed his robe and rushed out of his mansion.

It was time to pay the outer sect another visit.

***

Sophran Thunderstride wasn't the only Special disciple with underlings in the core sect, and so it happened that as he prepared to head down to the outer sect, three other specials who had heard the news did the same.

Four of them arrived over the initiation center a few minutes later, their jade insignias gleaming brightly in the late-morning sun as they hovered high above the grounds.

Their arrival sent a ripple of awe and shock through the massive crowd of disciples, many of whom hadn’t seen them in months. Whispers flared and died just as quickly, heads tilting upward as their presence pressed down on the trial itself like distant thunder, felt long before it was heard.

For a brief moment, they stole the spotlight from Aodhán.

Everyone—even the judges—turned to glance at them.

Everyone except Crixis, whose gaze remained fixed on Aodhán as though glued in place.

After giving up at the 500th step, he couldn’t imagine how Aodhán was still climbing. The ascent had been arduous beyond words, the pressure so tremendous that at the 500th step, Crixis had been convinced that taking even one more step would have crushed his spirit to dust.

Too focused on his own struggle, he hadn’t realized how far Aodhán had gone until he finally gave up and looked back, expecting to see his new friend struggling close behind him—only to find nothing.

He had known Aodhán was stronger than he was, that he would climb farther. But he hadn’t realized just how far.

A sudden roar of excitement from the crowd behind him finally tore his attention away, and he glanced back, heart shuddering as he took in the sight of the gathering specials floating high above.

The implications of their presence struck him like a bolt of lightning. He glanced back at Aodhán, realization dawning all at once—if Aodhán had made it into the special sect, then he was either a double inheritor or a transmigrant.

His eyes widened slightly, and he swallowed in shock. He couldn’t believe it. He had exchanged casual conversation with a special and hadn’t even known it.

Aodhán took another step then, nearly fifteen minutes after the last, and another ripple of shock and admiration swept through the crowd. It was obvious he had reached his limit. At this pace, he would never make it out of the special sect—at least not today.

Aodhán, however, didn’t seem inclined to stop. His entire body blazed with storm light, sweat dripping profusely as he pushed forward with a tenacity Crixis could only dream of possessing.

An awed breath escaped Crixis’s lips. “You can do it, Elder Brother,” he whispered. “Take one more step.”

But Aodhán couldn’t go any farther.

In the last hour, he had managed to force himself up only eleven steps. His spirit ached terribly, as though it had been beaten and battered to a pulp. Even with a FORCE, channeling chaos through his spirit for so long had wrung him completely dry. He had already used two whole seals to reach this point, and the once-roaring cries of his Ideal had faded to a dull whisper. Willpower surged through every inch of his being, but it could not carry him beyond where he already stood.

And with his spirit so strained, even Varéc had begun to falter, their merge dissolving in patches to reveal his skin beneath. His horns dimmed, fading in and out of reality like a candle flickering in the wind.

Varéc had given him all his strength. He had nothing else to give he hadn't already given.

This was the end.

Aodhán was finally at his limit. He glanced up at the remaining steps—all four hundred and ninety of them—and knew that neither his willpower nor his determination could take him any farther.

Body shuddering, he looked down at how far he had come and smiled faintly. He had come very far. He had tried. It was time to go back.

Just as he turned, thunder boomed from the clouds above. Lightning arced, winds surged—and a single splatter of liquid struck him.

Shuddering violently, Aodhán looked up—not at the steps, but at the storm clouds roiling overhead. His vision swam from exhaustion, the edges of his consciousness blurring uncontrollably.

He blinked hard, forcing his focus to return, and gradually the clouds sharpened into view—a maelstrom of dark clouds revolving around the mountain. Thunder boomed again, lightning flashed, and the winds howled fiercely, completing everything needed for a storm…

Except for one thing.

Rain.

His aspect thrummed back to life at the thought, mewling within him like a starved animal. And in that moment, Aodhán realized what was missing. When he had first gained the Aspect, it had resonated deeply within him, as though it possessed a will of its own—guiding him while he merely followed.

He had gained a measure of control over it since then, but in his eagerness to command it, he had forgotten to learn. To listen.

The humming of his aspect grew louder in that moment—more resonant—and this time Aodhán listened. With trembling arms, he raised his hands to the storm above and reached deep within himself—past the exhaustion, past the strain and screaming fatigue of his battered spirit, to that place where his Aspect resided. To that fundamental connection with the Origin Plane of water itself.

And he pulled.

The clouds responded with a boom of thunder, and then it exploded in a torrent of rain that fell from the heavens in sheets, cascading down as the floodgates were forced open.

The cold water struck his skin like a benediction, running down his face, his arms, and his entire shuddering frame, washing away oil, grime, and sweat. Aodhán closed his eyes, unmoving, and simply let the rain drench him to the bone, beating against his muscles with a rhythm that made him groan in relief.

He remained that way for a moment before finally opening his eyes, a new awareness threading through his spirit in resonance with his Aspect. Droplets levitated around him as if seeking his attention before finally falling away.

Aodhán followed them with his gaze, watching as they struck the mountain’s surface, utterly unaffected by the crushing aura that threatened to flatten him to paste. Downward they flowed—never fighting, never forcing—seeking the path of least resistance, adapting to every curve and edge of the stone.

Relentlessly.

Drop by drop, the water wore away at the mountain itself—not through force or aggression, but through persistence. Through renewal. Each droplet that passed was replaced by another, and another, in an endless cycle that had carved rivers through stone and shaped continents over millennia.

Water did not exhaust itself. It renewed itself.

The understanding settled deep within him, not as a revelation, but as something he had always known and only now remembered.

The insight tingled in his mind like the fragment of an inspiration, and Aodhán was certain there was a seal there. He could feel it. But before he could decide whether to pursue it, a cascading wave of refreshment suddenly rippled through his spirit, as though rain were falling on parched land.

The essence of water rushed through his spirit, refreshing and renewing, and Aodhán felt life return to his battered spirit. Strength bloomed slowly within him, and the crushing pressure the mountain had exerted slowly began to recede.

Energy returned—not enough to conquer the entire trial, but enough to take the next step. And the next. And the next.

Varéc roared within his spirit, and Aodhán grinned as he fixed his gaze on the steps once more.

He might not be able to conquer the entire mountain—but he could damn well try.

***

High above the mountain, hidden within the roiling storm clouds, six pairs of runic irises watched Aodhán in shock, unable to believe what they were seeing. A thirty-sixth-tier transmigrant—with a Familiar, an Aspect, a FORCE, and such fine control over all of them.

Zarxan had informed them of the boy’s abilities before, but it was one thing to hear of them and another thing entirely to witness them with their own eyes.

“How is this possible?” the First Seat whispered.

Zarxan sighed, a suspicion settling heavily within him. He was almost certain he was right—but until that certainty was complete, he refused to voice it.

Instead, he replied, “I’m not sure, Torvas.” His gaze drifted over the other High Elders, and a faint smile touched his lips. “But I believe he has just shown us why he is so special—and why we must protect him with everything we have. If he is this powerful now, how much stronger will he be at the fiftieth tier? Or the hundredth?”

“Or the two-hundredth,” High Elder Velora breathed, her eyes wide as she tried to imagine how strong Aodhán would be at the Calamity class. At this rate, he would be a calamity so profound he was practically a god.

And all they had to do was protect him until then.

Silence reigned for a long moment as they watched the boy push himself beyond the 1020th step, his gaze the very image of determination.

He wasn’t accepting defeat. He wasn’t satisfied with being good enough. He wanted to break boundaries and transcend limits.

And all of that made him a perfect recipe for disaster.

Almost as one, the High Elders realized that there was no protecting Aodhán. There was no point in even trying. He hadn’t become this powerful by being coddled, and attempting to shield him now would only do him a disservice.

They would have to let him run free—within restraints. Which meant not shielding him from this war.

The First Seat glanced down at Aodhán and sighed. “The war will not be easy. But if we are to become the strongest sect in Gordon, then we will all have to grow powerful together.” A small smile curved his lips. “A storm of a sect, if you will.”

High Elder Tharn nodded. “Then we proceed as planned. The moment we proclaim him, every sect in Gordon will know—and we’ll have a target on our backs.” He glanced down at the thousands of disciples gathered below and sighed. “I hope we’re all ready for what’s coming.”

The High Elders nodded. But ready or not, a storm was coming.

And they would either ride it or drown in its depths.
