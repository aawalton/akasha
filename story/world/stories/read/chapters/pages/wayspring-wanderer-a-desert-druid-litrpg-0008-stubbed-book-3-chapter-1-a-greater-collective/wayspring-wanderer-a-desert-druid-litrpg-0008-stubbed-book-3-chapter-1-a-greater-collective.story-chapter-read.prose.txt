Chapter 1 – A Greater Collective

The Great Tree grew, reaching into the sky above with all the power of the Wayspring magic behind it.

Long, slender branches bent in the wind, coral pink and lavender leaves cascading behind them in what would have looked like the branches of a weeping willow if not for the odd, shifting coloring and the fact that, in less than a week, it easily became the largest tree Oskar had ever seen.

Those first few days, it had grown visibly fast. By the time it slowed, it was already reaching above the dunes toward the sparse clouds that dotted the sky. Oddly steady winds far overhead left the treetop looking eerily still. A silent snapshot in the red sky—a picture instead of the living thing that Oskar could reach out and touch. A living thing that radiated living magic and raw power.

It rose stark in both color and stature against the rolling dunes of this world, which of course had the added effect of standing out against the horizon like a beacon.

I guess I could just say tree, no need to switch the metaphor… it’s the only tree these people have ever seen.

Of course, the tree completely fascinated Fox.

It fascinated everyone, but the Kobold spent nearly as much time with it as Oskar. He could sense her now, atop the tallest dune on the east side, looking out… but stealing glances back at the wonder of life in the desert. The tree acted in some ways like one of his companions. It extended his senses, and he could get a general feeling how the tree was doing.

And it was doing incredibly well, pulling Wayspring magic in from further than he could guess, and still growing faster than seemed possible.

The remaining Kobold people were closest when the tree appeared and therefore the first to approach. Similar to the other groups that arrived in the past few days, though, they’d hovered a few miles out. Close enough to watch the tree, but afraid or unwilling to approach.

Fox eventually went out to collect them, and now, a week later, a dozen tents dotted the area just outside the underground cavern and Oasis where the tree had taken root.

There should’ve been ten times that, but the survivors of the heartbreakingly recent attack on their home base were doing everything they could to keep going. The missing were surely not all killed by Valla, the dark shaman, and her army. Fox and Touwon had led the old leader and well over fifty Kobolds out of the base alone before their headlong nonstop sprint to help Oskar fight and finally kill her.

Benedicuto, the disgraced former leader, was a name rarely mentioned after a single long, emotional group meeting where they all compared stories of that night and tried to figure out where he might have fled to. The only real conclusion they came to was that the old Kobold either worked for, or at the very least, worked with Valla. They were angry, betrayed, and though not dwelling on it, silently searching for more answers.

And so, every new Kobold that joined was brought in front of the other Kobolds and grilled as to their actions and whereabouts during the whole fiasco so that any truths or untruths would be revealed.

That had proved fruitless, however, and it appeared any Kobold who’d been in the old Kobold’s inner circle was either dead or had enough common sense to avoid the new Great Collective surrounding the tree. Which was likely best for everyone involved.

After their initial interview, though, they welcomed the individual, and old Bene wasn’t mentioned again until someone new showed up. It was a silence not of shame, but of cold, hard practicality.

He’d been a hero once, but now, he was poison.

It had been a hard lesson, and one learned the hard way. “Anyone can fall.”

When the next leader of the Kobolds appeared, they’d find themselves under more scrutiny than any leader before them. As for Bene, he was the first Kobold in memory to be openly labeled as kill-on-sight.

Wherever he slinked off to, he’d best stay there unless he wants Fox to skin him. Slowly. And with salt.

Not all trust was lost, though. Once a Kobold was approved, they were treated like everyone else. A few trustworthy Kobolds even received approval from Fox to collect water from the Oasis at the base of the tree. Others hunted and gathered food, mostly desert staples like lizard meat and cactus fruit.

Far overhead, though, there were small crab-apple like fruits that didn’t appear anywhere in his PUB’s limited records. Oskar might have flown up and retrieved one, but he figured they’d start falling when they ripened and finished growing.

And I’m in no rush to try random food, even though there’s no way the Great Tree is poisonous, right? Besides, knowing my luck, there’s a brand-new way to be poisoned I’ve never heard about, like Capacity Poison or Sora overload or something crazy. I’ve already poisoned myself once in this world.

Surprisingly, the PUB didn’t remark on the thought, but a flash of green swept across his vision through the Goggles to let him know the PUB was paying attention.

The whole situation was surreal to Oskar. Even though he’d been using it basically since he got here, magic still felt like something new and wonderful, and as he looked down into the crystal and glass cavern, he hoped he would never lose that feeling of wonder. Around the base of the tree in the glass cave below, the Oasis water filled the floor of the cavern, and lights flickered like living things. Blue moss grew along the glass, creeping outward to fill the room with its own soft, pulsing light. Being inside the cave always made Oskar feel like he was inside the lungs of a great creature, watching the lights slowly brighten and dim again like impossible breaths.

The tree gave the statue of a large cat—forever sleeping in stone at the center of the cave upon a dais—a wide berth as it grew outward. This created a natural cove inside the tree that quickly became the impromptu hangout for those who came to gather the precious Wayspring water.

Even in stone… even on this hell-forged planet… Bastet made everyone feel safer.

Kobolds sat on natural walls in the cool temperatures and gossiped.

Like a post-apocalyptic water cooler.

// You’re weird. //

You’re… weird.

// Wow. You’re getting better. You got me good that time. //

Don’t make me delete you.

Oskar’s PUB flashed red over his vision through his Goggles in its exaggerated offense, prompting a smile that held fast for a few seconds before fading.

We have so much to do. Like… figuring out how to use the Seed Forge. We can’t get ahold of Lavern, which maybe makes sense, but is still worrisome.

Also, I thought taking down Valla would make everything easy, but I never thought I’d be starting a Great Collective.

Kobolds weren’t the only ones who’d shown up to see the spectacle. They were just the closest. Now, Hyenamen and Crocs hovered nearby, a few hours’ walk away, and Oskar wasn’t yet sure what to do about it.

If he thought everyone could live in harmony, it would have been a simple decision, and food would have been the only thing they had to worry about. Living near the tree had its perks. No matter where the Kobolds put up a new tent, it would be in the tree’s shade until late afternoon.

When a new tent was placed, a branch would shift over the course of the next day. By the following morning, the tree’s shadows would shelter them until dusk arrived, and the suns sat low enough so that the tall tree could no longer shade them fully.

It wasn’t just normal shade, either. The temperature was lower, the air not as dry. Not… humid by any means, but the mornings felt more like a tent by a river than a tent sitting on hot sand under two suns.

Maybe most surprising of all, moss made its way up the tree from the Oasis and out through the hole in the glass ceiling of the cave, and short, soft grass grew where the moss wilted away under the sun. Wayspring magic was taking root in the world in a way that no one had seen in centuries and gave all who witnessed it one more reason to be near the Great Tree.

It felt safe here. The Tree called to everyone, not only Oskar.

Oskar hadn’t much cared where he placed his own tent, but Sara, his calico companion, was insistent on a specific spot beneath one of the larger branches. It didn’t take long for Oskar to realize that particular spot stayed in the shade for almost the entire day, only catching direct sunlight for a little over half an hour in the very late afternoon.

The area around Oskar’s tent was the first to grow grass, but it now spread in a tight circle a few yards in every direction around the opening and was growing thicker every day.

The grass was true magic… Oskar understood the basics of how soil worked, and he knew that nothing could have made this grass grow other than the Wayspring magic and the living magic around the Tree. He didn’t know if the living magic was being called or created here, but there was more than enough for him to experiment with his Seed Forge. At least if he could get a few hours to himself. Not to say his magic wasn’t growing or he wasn’t practicing, he was, but working on brand new magic took more time than he had.

As the magic within him grew stronger and his affinity with Cryon increased, the magic occasionally felt like a cold IV being infused. Having gotten used to the desert temperature, there were times in the early morning when he felt a little chilly in the tree’s shadow. It didn’t help that his last long sleeve shirt from earth was threadbare. He decided then and there to focus initially on increasing his mastery of Pyron to compensate.

It was an odd problem—one that had never occurred to him—that mastery over magic might change more than just his ability to fight and function in this world. It changed him in some fundamental way he was certain he hadn’t fully understood yet.

Oskar shook his head and looked back out over the tents nearby as he saw movement. Making his way up and out of the cave was easy using Sora. For those who came to get water, however, there was a bit of an awkward climb in and out of the cave via the sand that had poured back through the roof of the cavern in the back of Bastet’s—and now the tree’s—room.

After a thorough removal of glass shards, it served well enough for now, but planned on doing something a little more permanent. It might be possible to use Talau to create steps, but until Oskar had a long-term solution for keeping the sand out of the opening, it would do.

The fact remained though; this place was a proverbial gold mine of comfort and resources no one could have possibly imagined… they had it all here.

Which was why Oskar was so reluctant to pull in the Crocs and Hyenamen, who had more of a history of violent tendencies than the more peaceful Kobolds. Unfortunately, they also carried deep-rooted cultural ideas about territory and ownership that usually came down to who was left alive at the end of the day. Those traits would pose a real problem here, considering this place had to be one of the most resource-rich locations on the planet. All this made Oskar’s position as leader far less comfortable than it should have been.

As if the shade, grass, water, and eventual fruit weren’t enough… a slow wind wove between the tents, and the odd stillness he’d gotten used to was missing.

It was pure living Sora dancing below the dunes. An actual breeze.

How did Luth keep everyone from each other’s necks back in the day?

// You’ll figure it out. //

Oskar waited for a joke that never came, which was probably the joke itself.

The cool breeze was an unheard-of luxury. Even Gramm’s Collective atop the plateau didn’t have a cool breeze.

These people had been fighting for water their whole lives, but a cool breeze was a status symbol. That alone might be enough to start a full-on war, especially now that the little fruits were slowing their growth and changing color.

Oskar almost hoped they were inedible. That would simplify things some.

“How to I make it where everyone doesn’t feel like they have to fight for it?”

Beside him, Erik grunted, which was apparently less effort than a shrug. His brother’s barely verbal assessment was right, though.

Who knows?

He smiled as he looked up at the tree, squinting instinctively. The Goggles took care of the brightness, adjusting automatically to everything but pitch darkness, but old habits die hard.

“You gonna try walking on it or just sit there and pout?” Erik pointed a thin, white, bandaged hand at Oskar’s foot. The hand moved oddly, reluctantly, but Erik was getting better with it. Instead of following Erik’s finger though, he stared at the hand itself.

Oskar tried not to think about what was beneath the wrapped cloth of Erik’s recently regained arm, but it was impossible to ignore. It was all bone and odd changes in his brother’s power, and was something Erik spent hours focusing on learning how to use. Power glowed through the bandages to Oskar’s magic-sensitive gaze, and he had to stop himself from asking the dozens of questions Erik probably didn’t yet have the answers to.

The arm was also easily the most complex mix of Rakiyu he’d ever encountered. Every bit of the complexity of Sora he’d seen in Bluebottle.

His brother was doing it right, though. No shortcuts to power, and Oskar was keeping a close eye on him. Looking away from the skeletal hand, he finally stared down at his prosthetic.

“Dude, it just feels…” Oskar tried to find a better word than weird since he’d already used that one a few times.

“… weird,” he finished with a defeated sigh.

Oskar stood, his newly rebuilt foot situated beneath him awkwardly. His socket had been all but destroyed in his fight with Valla. Touwon, brilliant as he was, had done what he could, but Oskar woke the next morning to find the Tree… the freakin Tree… had reinforced it while he slept. The boot and metal remained, but tiny lines of wood as small as thread now wove through the entire prosthetic, reinforcing and strengthening in an odd mix of carbon fiber and wood.

“It’s either that or you’re hopping, man,” Erik replied, clearly amused by either the idea of Oskar hopping around or by Oskar’s obvious reluctance to put too much weight on the living wood he’d formed into a new prosthetic foot.

“It’s alive. I can feel it, so it feels like I’m gonna hurt it somehow.”

Erik grinned and began to say something insulting about his brother’s intelligence, but Oskar cut him off with a raised hand.

“I know, I know. It’s basically unbreakable. I just feel so… connected to it. It feels like I’m stepping on Penny or something.”

Close by, in response to her name, Penny’s copper-scaled head popped up out of the sand… and grass, causing her to shake dirt off her head in annoyance. Oskar smiled at her as his PUB piped in.

// You’re being ridiculous. You didn’t care when you were busting Croc heads, falling out of the sky at two hundred miles per hour with your living wood maul. This is exactly the same thing, except now you’re just putting your measly two hundred pounds on it. //

Staring upward at the massive tree looming overhead, Oskar sighed.

Instead of just growing taller, it was thickening now, spreading outward to further envelop the stone statue of the slumbering cat.

Bastet. I wish you were really here to see this. We used the seed you saved and planted a Great Tree. Well, the Great Tree. There’s only the one.

// So far. //

From what I understand, though, when a Tree seeds, it dies. As powerful as this thing is, I can’t imagine a single tree is going to be enough to save this world.

// Bastet had faith in you, and so do we. We’ll figure it out. Now, get to steppin. //

Oskar sighed, and then nodded.

Slowly shifting his weight, he tried to ignore how alive the wood felt. He leaned on his weapon a little, but that was the thing that finally made him realize how silly he was being. The weapon he was leaning on, now in spear form, was also living wood, and with a grunt, he shifted his weight fully onto the new foot and could have sworn he felt the foot respond to the change in his weight distribution. It also felt… for lack of a better word, satisfied?

Is it happy to be used for a purpose?

Erik grunted something about Oskar being a stubborn ass, interrupting his introspection.

“Well, it’s better than being a grumpy one.”

Behind him, Erik chuckled and scooped a handful of sand at him.

Laughing or not, they were all a little on edge, but in Oskar's case, it wasn't just because they were standing in the most resource rich place anyone had ever seen, slowly being surrounded by dangerous, desperate people.

There was also the Unique quality Insight Gem he’d received after his battle with Valla.
