Seth drank one of Marcus' Green-Iris Potions and walked out of the Adventurer's Guild, keenly aware of the weight of the new Endless Pouch from Toren on his belt alongside his own. Professor Reat fell into step beside him.

It was time to join the others from Trogan Academy and head toward the Warfare Rifts. Even if only ten squads from the hundreds participating would fight today, the entire force was marching—a parade to show their strength.

To Seth, it felt like a waste of time.

The unassigned Wielders could have spent these hours training. But war, Seth was learning, was a show. It was all about intimidation—breaking the enemy's will before the fighting even started. The empire would use this standoff as a stage to flaunt its superiority once again. He could already picture them: clad in gleaming armor, chuckling amongst themselves as they prepared for the bloodbath.

They would laugh even harder once they saw the squads Kastal was sending in first: Squads 42 through 48 from Trogan Academy. All commoners, half barely having reached Rank 30 and some still in the 20s. It was painfully obvious how the higher-ups were throwing away the cannon fodder to rob the rebels from joining their ranks in the future.

Seth refused to let his squad be slaughtered.

He hadn't risked his life in the Desert of Misery to save Selena just to watch her die in this sham of a ridiculous war. The same went for Henry and Eddin.

No, I won't let that happen, Seth thought, clenching his fists.

He navigated the crowded streets with Professor Reat, reaching the assembly point outside the west gate a few minutes later. The full contingent of Trogan Academy was already waiting—nearly a thousand students, wearing either crisp uniforms and personal armor. The air was filled with tension, faces were grim, and hands fidgeted with weapon hilts.

Instead of heading toward the waiting soldiers, Reat led Seth around its edge toward several canvas tents erected in the command area.

"Where are we going?" Seth asked.

"To greet that traitor," Professor Reat answered.

Seth looked at him, surprised. "Here? Now?"

"Yeah," Reat answered. "I already briefed Ryehill. He can't execute a Noble without irrefutable proof—the crown would have his head—but we can sure as hell throw her out of the academy."

That wasn't really an answer, but before Seth could press him, Reat stopped in front of one of the tents. Intendant Lancet stood inside alone, studying a map spread across a table. Her expression immediately soured when she noticed them stepping inside.

"Reat." Her gaze shifted to Seth. "And you brought your little pet."

"Good to see you too, Veronica," Professor Reat answered as the tent flap fell shut behind Seth.

Lancet's eyes narrowed as she looked at Reat more carefully. Surprise cracked through her expression. "Gold," she muttered.

Reat flashed a broad and unnatural smile. "Disappointed?"

"Why would I be?"

"Because it ruined your plans."

The woman's expression barely changed, but Seth caught the tightening of her jaw.

"I have no idea what you're talking about," Lancet retorted at last.

"Of course not." Reat casually stretched his shoulders. "I suppose the Wielders waiting to ambush us simply happened to know exactly where we'd be. Terrible coincidence, really."

Lancet stared at him, but remained silent.

"They won't be coming home, by the way," Reat continued. "They made the same mistake you did. They assumed the prey was weak."

The woman leaned against the table next to her as if trying to keep her face neutral. "That is a serious accusation."

"And yet you don't seem particularly surprised," Professor Reat answered with a shrug.

Lancet's eyes hardened. "Do you have proof?"

"You know we don't."

Something that looked like satisfaction flickered across the woman's face. "Then perhaps you should be more careful about the accusations you make against your betters."

Seth noticed a slight curl of Reat's lips.

"So you admit you knew we'd have no proof," the man added.

"I admit nothing."

"Come on, Veronica." Reat took another step toward her. "You wanted me dead. You wanted him dead. And you thought sending the Faertis would solve both problems. But you're just like them. There's nothing inside that skull of yours. You're only good at kissing the feet of whoever your House tells you to and parading yourself around as shamelessly as you always have."

Lancet's composure finally cracked. "You think you're untouchable because you reached Gold?" she hissed. "You think that branch of yours is going to save you? I warned you what would happen if you kept pushing. There will come a day when neither the Director nor Orwen Silvermane will be there to protect you. And when that day comes, your head will roll."

Lancet seemed to realize she'd said too much, but anger had taken hold of her. Her gaze snapped toward Seth. "And the same fate is waiting for you, commoner."

A voice came from behind Seth. "I suspected as much."

Lancet went rigid. The tent flap opened, and Director Ryehill stepped inside, his body slowly fading into view as the spell concealing him vanished.

For once, his usually impassive expression carried an obvious anger.

"I had hoped I was wrong, Veronica," Ryehill said quietly. "Despite everything between us, I had hoped you had not fallen so far as to arrange the murder of one of our own professors and a student."

Lancet's face drained of all its color. "You—" Her gaze snapped toward Reat. "You planned this."

"Obviously."

"You have no proof!" she retorted, turning toward Ryehill. "Nothing I said proves anything. They came here and deliberately provoked me into—"

"You're right." The simple answer silenced her as Ryehill stepped farther into the tent. "I don't have enough evidence to execute a Noble for attempted murder. Nor enough to drag your House before the crown and expect any justice." His expression hardened. "But I don't need that proof to decide who holds authority within my academy."

Veronica froze.

"You should return to your House," Ryehill continued. "Effective immediately, you are no longer First-Year Intendant of Trogan Academy."

"You think my House will let this slide?" she spat, a vein pulsing in her temple. "We will take this directly to the king, and you and I both know who he'll side with. Your time is over, Ryehill. This academy will finally be purged." She then whipped her head toward Seth. "And filth like you won't be allowed in."

"You should act fast, then," Seth answered, eyes fixed on her. "Because if you wait too long, someone might get so strong you won't be able to stop them anymore."

"Is that a threat?" she sneered. "From you? You just signed your own death warrant, commoner."

"A threat?" Professor Reat repeated as he placed a hand on his chest in mock surprise. He turned to the director. "Director Ryehill, did you hear a threat?"

Ryehill tapped his chin. "Hmm? No. I certainly did not."

"You!" Intendant Lancet's face contorted, a flush of purple climbing her neck. Her fists clenched, and the air around her began to crackle with erratic arcs of blue aether.

Almost instantly, a visible wave of heat shimmered around Professor Reat. His smile remained, but it no longer reached his eyes. "Please, Veronica," he goaded. "Try it. I'd hate to have to accidentally kill you in self-defense."

She took a half-step forward, coiled to strike, but her gaze flickered to Director Ryehill. He hadn't moved—except for his hand, which now rested on the hilt of the staff strapped to his back.

Seth knew she might have a chance against Reat, who had only just ascended; that wouldn't be the case against someone like the director.

With a choked sound, Veronica spun on her heel and stormed past Seth.

"You'll all pay for this," she muttered, the threat followed her out of the tent like smoke.

Seth watched the canvas flap settle behind her, the adrenaline settling into a cold resolve. It was the same driving need he had felt for the past year: to get stronger. Strong enough to crush people like her until they finally understood they weren't above anyone.

"That was the first and the last time I'll tolerate you threatening nobles in my presence," Director Ryehill said, breaking the silence. "And that counts for both of you," he added, his stern gaze shifting from Seth to Reat.

"Understood, sir," Seth said with a nod.

Professor Reat glanced at Seth, then gestured toward the tent's exit. "Go join your friends. We'll be moving soon."

Seth bowed to both men before turning to leave. He'd only taken a few steps out of the canvas when Nightmare's smug voice rippled through his mind.

'Making humans walk away with their tails between their legs seems quite enjoyable. I should give it a try.'

'One day,' Seth answered, fighting to keep the grin off his face. Among the sea of soldiers gathered outside, Seth spotted Elena, Devus, and Jenna and made his way toward them. All three wore deep frowns as they watched him approach, visibly wondering what had happened inside the command tent.

The moment he reached them, Devus, blunt as ever, spoke up."What the hell was that? Intendant Lancet looked like she was about to explode."

"She just got removed from the academy."

"What?!" The word came from Seth's roommate, but also Elena, Jenna, and Brandon nearly in unison.

"How?" Jenna asked, her eyes wide.

"Let's just say," Seth said, lowering his voice, "the moment Reat and I left the Rift, we had a 'welcome party' waiting for us. Courtesy of my favorite House."

"Wait, you got ambushed by House Faertis?" Elena exclaimed, scandalized. "How did you get away? And why didn't you tell me last night?"

"Well, Reat dismantled them. It wasn't pretty. And it was late when I got back. I was going to tell you today."

"What, did they underestimate him and send a few Irons and a Silver?" Devus asked with a frown.

"No," Seth said, the corner of his mouth ticking up. "There were eight Silvers."

"Eight! Then how the hell did he..." Devus' voice trailed off. His eyes went wide as the pieces clicked into place. "No, he broke through? Reat is Gold?"

Seth just smiled. "Exactly."

"And they didn't know," Devus breathed, a look of awestruck comprehension on his face. "That's incredible! He must be the youngest Gold Wielder in the whole nation!"

Jenna and Elena looked completely stunned. Who could have imagined that their perpetually tired professor was even more of a genius than they had thought?

"It's barely been a few years since he reached Silver," Jenna mumbled, shaking her head. "That speed, it's unheard of. Especially for a commoner. In which Rift were you in?"

"Can't say," Seth answered with a shrug. "But it was a nice place to train, for sure."

Devus' head snapped toward Seth, eyes narrowing with another realization. He pointed a finger at Seth's chest. "May I?"

Seth nodded.

Devus' jaw practically hit the ground. "Rank 45! Oh, come on, man!" he exclaimed, his voice cracking. "How the hell did you manage that?" He turned to Jenna, looking defeated. "And here we were, feeling proud of our progress. He just exists to remind us that we aren't geniuses like him."

Seth tensed slightly, bracing for Jenna's usual jealousy. But to his surprise, the woman just let out a short laugh before throwing her hands up in surrender.

"That's a fight we can't win, Dev," she said, looking at Seth with a strange sort of acceptance. "You should know that by now."

She's right, you can't, Seth thought, a pang of guilt tightening his chest. It wasn't a fair comparison. His core and his Legacy gave him a tremendous advantage.

"So, what Rank are you guys at now?" Seth asked.

"Thirty-seven," Jenna answered.

"Thirty-eight," Devus added, his chest swelling with pride.

Seth's eyes widened. "That's great," he said. And he meant it. "You both gained more than a dozen Ranks? Seems like you blew past the Rank-30 wall that held me up for weeks. How?"

"You can thank this knucklehead's sponsor," Jenna answered, jabbing a thumb at Devus. "He paid for a team from your branch—the Champions of Chaos—to take us on an expedition."

"The Champions of Chaos?" Seth repeated.

"Yeah," Jenna said. "And they brought us into a Rift and gave us these weird Iron potions. Those potions helped us understand aether, letting us progress faster than ever before."

"Don't worry, they weren't enhancers," Devus added quickly, seeming to guess Seth's thoughts. "No backlash. They just helped me feel its nature, you know? Like I was melting into it."

Seth froze. Clarity Potions.

It had to be a weakened version of what Marcus had given Orwen for protection. Seth still remembered how the Champions had reacted to the high-grade stuff; even a lower-Tier variant would be worth a fortune.

Add the cost of hiring Champions and the numbers made even less sense. Devus' sponsor had haggled over silver coins for spell-scrolls. The mysterious man wouldn't—likely couldn't—drop this kind of money.

Seth turned his head, his gaze finding Professor Reat in the distance. The man, as if feeling the weight of his stare, gave him an almost imperceptible nod.

Marcus, Seth realized.

The Alchemist had either asked the Champions to do this, or they'd done it as a favor to Seth. To make his friends stronger, so he wouldn't have to carry them and they wouldn't be liabilities.

Seth turned to Elena. "Did you go with them?"

"Only for two weeks," she replied with a smile.

"And you didn't tell me?" Seth said, arching an eyebrow. "See? I'm not the only one keeping secrets."

"We asked her not to!" Devus cut in as he puffed out his chest. "We wanted to surprise you—thought we could finally catch up or maybe even pass you. But no. As always, you make it impossible."

Jenna and Elena laughed as Devus feigned a sorrowful expression. Seth couldn't help but smile. Even if they hadn't caught him, this kind of progress was massive. It dramatically increased their chances of survival—especially since none of them were in the first wave of fodder squads the king was sending in, aside from him.

Seth was the only one of them stepping into the Warfare Rifts today.

Before he could say anything, Director Ryehill clapped his hands; the sound, amplified by aether, cut through the students' chatter.

"Alright, everyone! To your squads! We march for the Warfare Rifts!"

The crowd quieted as the man continued, his voice booming: "Remember who you are! From now on, you are soldiers of Kastal! Keep your chins up, show them your strength! Today, we go to war! Today, we show the empire that we are not the weak nation they think we are!"

A roar erupted from the students, Devus, Jenna, and Elena cheering with them.

Seth, however, remained silent.

He looked at the sea of faces around him. Even with his squad to protect, even with his new deal with Marshal Vancaws, he couldn't bring himself to feel an ounce of their patriotism. Not with the nation's current leader and the lie which had been fed to everyone.

The mass of students began to move, and Seth gave his friends a final nod before heading toward his squad.
