Congratulations! You have been blessed by the path of Accruing Storm.

You gain the temporary perk Accrued Momentum—every movement, technique, and attack generates momentum. Thus, each successive action becomes faster, heavier, and deadlier than the last.

Aodhán read through the benefit of High Elder Kaelthorn’s blessing as he and the others stepped into the teleportation circle, nervous tension radiating off them in waves.

The blessing was a great boon, guaranteeing that if they didn't die the instant they arrived on the battlefield, then each passing second increased their chances of survival. Perhaps it was possible to accrue so much stacked momentum that they could easily dodge Calamities and Mythics alike, but Aodhán doubted that would be possible—not because the blessing would be limited or capped, but because their bodies would probably rip themselves apart under such a force.

More likely, they'd have to continuously expel the accrued momentum every time it became overwhelming.

Still, the perk was an edge Aodhán wished he could make permanent. He already had {Surging Momentum}, which worked similarly in nature. If he survived this war, perhaps he could evolve it into something like this.

“Are you ready?” Elder Ghaltris asked again, and this time, it was Ashscar who responded.

“We are.” He nodded somberly. “We are nervous though.”

Elder Ghaltris nodded. “Nervous is good. Now remember, you work in pairs. Nymera, you’re with Mirelle. Ashscar, you’re with Aodhán. Your aim is simple. Draw attention and vanish. I’ve alerted several Calamities of your deployment. They will look out for you.”

Nymera nodded. “We understand.”

Elder Ghaltris smiled. "Good. The porters should be released shortly."

He left to speak to another elder, and they all turned to stare at the screen nervously as the war resumed with even more brutality. Devastation filled the screen. Half the time, they couldn't even see what was going on. But they could feel it.

Oh, they could feel it. Even across such a vast distance, they all could feel it—the hairs on their skin standing on end as the weight of each attack pressed against their spirit with an overwhelming sense of finality and inevitability.

And as a massive boulder slammed into their ranks with the force of a falling meteor—the eventual explosion ripping hundreds apart—Mirelle shuddered and looked away from the bloody screen.

Nymera gripped her hands in silent comfort, her own gaze still on the screen, and then she asked, "What will you do to draw attention, Mirelle?"

Mirelle looked back up, the question forcing her to think strategically. She glanced back at the screen and swallowed. "I'm a double inheritor. What I have is willpower. I may not be able to draw the attention of Calamities, but I'll certainly draw the attention of Mythics."

Nymera smiled. "That's a good idea. I think I might just use Levi myself. If a two-hundred-foot leviathan doesn't draw the attention of Calamities, then I don't know what would."

They all chuckled at that, then Ashscar spoke. "Well, Giselle isn't quite so large. But I have a few skills that should suffice. The amplification of a Calamity's attack with Herald's Toll is sure to catch their attention."

Nymera nodded with a small smile, then turned questioningly to Aodhán.

"What about you, Aodhán? What will you do?"

Aodhán swallowed, unable to tear his gaze away from the screen. “I don’t know, Nymera. I don’t know.”

The teleportation circle hummed to life in that moment, violet light erupting from its runic structure to engulf them.

It was time.

Inhaling deeply, Aodhán linked his arm to Ashscar's to make sure they reappeared together and closed his eyes. The light of the teleportation circle swallowed them whole a moment later, and the next instant, they arrived within the battlefield—the shift from calm to chaotic space so jarring it was like day and night.

The world snapped into focus with brutal clarity, and the first thing that hit him wasn't sight.

It was smell.

Blood. Ozone. Burning flesh. Sulfur. And something else he couldn't name—a metallic, acidic stench that came from reality itself bleeding oblivion into the atmosphere. The air tasted like copper and ash, so thick it was like breathing in steam.

Then came the weight—the pressure of thousands of powerful auras locked in intense combat. It was not a physical weight, but it was tangible nonetheless, crushing down on his spirit with savage intent—suffocating any will to act.

And then came the sound—an indescribable cacophony of screaming, groaning, dying, tearing, shattering…

It was too much.

Not only did the chaos make it impossible to track every attack on his person, it made it absolutely difficult to think.

Just react.

And thank the heavens for speed, because a Mythic corpse suddenly slammed into the ground he’d been standing on barely a second ago—the impact of its fall creating a crater in the earth.

The shockwave knocked him and Ashscar into the air and slammed them back down with a force that caused their bones to rattle. Aodhán barely managed to jump back to his feet before a massive spear of blood struck, having already skewered a Mythic to death.

His vision swam as he took in the destruction around him, but he knew staying in one place was begging for death.

He had to act now. That was his aim. Draw attention. Vanish.

The chaos around him, however, made it impossible to think, and so without thinking, Aodhán acted. He raised a hand to the sky above and activated one of his newest skills.

[Ruin's Obedience]

It was not a skill he'd tested before, but in a war like this—drowned in storm, lightning, and residual devastation—it felt like the most logical choice.

And it was.

The moment he activated the skill, willpower tore from his core like an avalanche, nearly a quarter of his reserves vanishing in an instant. Chaos rushed into his spirit overwhelmingly to fuel the skill, and the quintessence of an entire seal was drained dry.

But the result more than made up for its steep cost.

All around him, uncontrolled lightning froze—arrested mid-strike, veins of molten silver suspended in the air. Howling winds stilled as if seized by an unseen hand. The dissipating residuals of countless prior attacks reawakened and locked into place, coming under his authority as if drawn toward an unseen center.

Aodhán couldn't truly manipulate them. Not the way he did his own generated lightning. They were too powerful, far more than anything he could have created even with chaos layering. They were fading remnants of Calamity and Mythic attacks, after all.

But they no longer belonged to their creators. They were no longer wild or sovereign.

They were his.

And though Aodhán couldn't manipulate them like he could his own generated electricity, he could direct them.

With a swift motion, he stretched a hand to the deepest recesses of the enemy ranks and muttered a single word.

"Ruin."

And like soldiers obeying the commands of a general, the storm remnants moved without question—gathering from all around him, in the air, on the earth, and from the Ordinal Engine—to form a judgment of storm so vast and concentrated it rivaled what even early Mythics would struggle to create.

Light drowned the battlefield—so bright it washed out the world, and when it struck, Aodhán felt the world scream as another crater formed and plunged the earth into fresh annihilation as Mythics caught within the blast were torn apart and machines reduced to rubble.

For a single, terrible moment, the war hesitated.

Not because of the destruction—that was commonplace here—but because they could all sense that something was wrong. Different about the last attack.

Not only was its energy signature all over the place—like the culmination of thousands of Mythics and Calamities unleashing a single attack—but also each one of them could feel their attack and energies within it. That attack hadn't originated from them, and yet it had.

It was their lightning. Their energies. The generated static of their movements. Their sparks from every explosion they'd caused. The very charge of battle in the air.

They had all contributed to that last attack, both allies and enemies.

But even more shocking was the energy that had driven that skill. A signature so weak it shouldn't have been on the battlefield at all.

And as one, Calamities and Mythics spread out their perceptions to search for it.

It didn't take them long to find Aodhán, standing alongside Ashscar and Giselle—all three seemingly frozen in shock at the devastation Aodhán had wrought.

They recognized them immediately.

Transmigrants.

Storm Transmigrant.

The very reason for this war. The very reason for the Storm Origin Plane expansion.

For a heartbeat, disbelief reigned. Then the war exploded.

Every enemy Calamity reacted at once—not with strategy, not with coordination, but with naked, desperate urgency. Killing blows were abandoned mid-execution. Authority duels were severed without warning. Mythics tore themselves free from their formations, bodies rupturing under the strain as they redirected everything they had at the two boys.

Because this truth was absolute.

If these transmigrants died, the war would end. The expansion of the Storm Origin Plane would end. Balance would be restored.

And for sects like those of Illusion and Serenity, this opportunity wasn't one they were willing to lose.

Intent crashed down on Aodhán like a tidal wave.

It was not subtle. It was not distant. It was murder made manifest—countless killing wills locking onto him at once, so heavy they distorted space and twisted reality. But while the shocking effect of Ruin's Obedience had caused him and Ashscar to freeze in disbelief, they hadn't forgotten their aim in the war.

Draw attention. Vanish.

They had succeeded in the first, and now it was time for the latter. Unfortunately, however, escaping the attention of Calamities was easier said than done, and even with the stacking momentum, Aodhán quickly realized that running just wasn't going to cut it.

The air above him thundered as Calamities clashed—storm Calamities taking advantage of their enemies' single focus to attack them, thus keeping them away from Aodhán and Ashscar.

But even with the Calamities otherwise preoccupied, the Mythics were still very much a threat, their grasping hands reaching toward him with murderous intent.

The world blurred as Aodhán ran for his life, his speed seemingly growing with each step until his mind could barely keep up with his body. And yet he couldn't shake off the Mythics.

If anything, they seemed to be growing closer.

Grimacing, Aodhán muttered a curse, but the words were barely past his lips before a volley of jagged glass struck down on his position with intent to skewer him to pieces.

He teleported without thinking, feeling the skill activate faster than it ever had before. Everything was faster. His skill activation. His reaction time. Everything.

Yet shaking off the enemy Mythics only seemed to be getting harder by the second.

Mythics surrounded him quickly, predicting his trajectory and cutting him off. Somewhere along the line—between detonations and teleports—he had lost Ashscar, and he couldn't even spare a second to find him.

Heart pounding, he teleported again, the activation faster, but once more his enemies were there—closer than ever before.

They closed in like a pack of snarling wolves until he was utterly and completely surrounded, a dense wave of killing intent surging out of their cores. But just when Aodhán thought he was done for, a massive explosion of lightning erupted all around him, eviscerating hundreds of Mythics in an instant.

Eyes wide and heart thundering with fear and adrenaline, Aodhán glanced up to see a storm-cloaked figure floating above him, completely hidden from the sight of others.

A storm Calamity with a stealth path.

Aodhán couldn't understand it, but he didn't question it either. He simply teleported to the ranks of the storm army, a shuddering breath of relief escaping his lips. He had stirred up the battle. Now he simply had to wait a few minutes or hours before drawing attention again.

In the meantime, he summoned Varéc from his spirit space and smiled as the Fury burst out of his chest with a thunderous roar. Varéc wouldn't be able to wreak as much damage as he had, but he was still very eager to join the war, and Aodhán supposed he could be useful.

"Be careful," he shouted over the chaos of battle, and Varéc exposed bladed fangs in a smile as if to say, I always am.

And then he vanished, teleporting into enemy ranks to wreak destruction.

Aodhán watched him go, keeping a watchful eye on the sky lest an attack fall from above while he was distracted. A smile touched his lips a moment later, though, when he felt a massive draw of willpower from his core as Varéc simultaneously activated {Lightning Spear Rain} and {Ruinous Layering}.

That smile, however, vanished when a hand suddenly grabbed him, and he turned to find Elder Vasmos staring at him, half her face drenched in blood.

"Where is Ashscar?" she asked. "Elder Ghaltris said you would be together."

Aodhán opened his mouth and stuttered. "I don't—"

A massive explosion rocked the sky in that moment, so overwhelmingly powerful that it was unmistakable—energy roiling across the surface of the heavens like a sea of light—so bright it erased color, shadow, and depth alike.

Another Calamity was dead.

Only this time, it was a Storm Calamity.

A member of their own. The second Calamity to fall on only the first day.

Beside him, Elder Vasmos shook her head and whispered, "This is bad. This is—where's Ashscar?"

Aodhán swallowed back his dread, fear creeping into his bones as he whispered, "I don't know, Elder Vasmos. With all the chaos and everything, I… I lost him."

Elder Vasmos nodded, her grip tightening on his arm. "Find him, Aodhán. He's your partner. One does not leave a partner on the frontlines while hiding in safety. Find him immediately. My automatons will join you."

Aodhán nodded, and with a shuddering breath, he teleported back to the frontlines once more.

Dravion Ashscar—for all his pompousness and notoriety—wasn't a man of violence.

That didn't mean he wasn't dangerous.

Cultivating the path of Heralding Storm, he saw the storm not as destruction but as announcement. The pressure before impact. The fear before annihilation and the certainty before death.

Thunder was heard before lightning was seen. The sky darkened before the storm's arrival. Ashscar considered himself that darkening—the prelude to every stormy disaster.

But now, as he raced through the battlefield at a speed that most certainly broke the sound barrier, hunted by a litany of enemy Mythics, Ashscar didn't much feel like the prelude to any disaster.

Rather, he could feel that disaster closing in on him as he ran with Giselle in his wake.

Allied Mythics rushed to his aid, but more enemies kept pouring in—hundreds of them, then thousands—predicting his trajectory and uttering rules to try and trap him.

Ashscar uttered rules of his own, draining his willpower at a massive rate as he did his best to slip through their grasp.

Perhaps, if he'd had Core Sense like Aodhán, or even a chance to take a good look at the faces of his pursuers, he might have realized that they weren't quite as numerous as he'd thought.

They were illusions.

But Ashscar didn't have Core Sense, nor did he have the chance to look at his hunters.

So he ran. And ran. And ran until his body was practically ripping itself apart with all the momentum he had accrued.

He turned then, his entire body shuddering as if he were about to burst into bloody mist at any second, and with a cry like that of a cornered animal, he looked to the storm above and said, "I herald the storm."

Thunder boomed. Lightning flashed.

Ashscar smiled. "I herald your end."

He didn't shout. He didn't need to.

But the storm heard. And it answered—a single, titanic peal of thunder cracking across the battlefield like a verdict.

Roiling clouds folded in on themselves, pressure collapsing inward as if the sky itself had inhaled. And then destruction came—not in bolts, but in sentences, entire paragraphs of annihilation that slammed down on the hunters with the fury of righteous judgment.

Thousands of Mythics were erased in an instant, their forms unraveling and collapsing into nothing as the storm passed judgment.

Only it was the wrong judgment—or perhaps the right one for the wrong targets.

But real or illusion, the storm never discriminated.

Willpower spilled from Ashscar's core even as the realization struck. But it was too late.

A boulder slammed into him with crushing force an instant later, and he was thrown into the air with a cacophony of breaking bones. Giselle screeched, lightning erupting around her in retaliation.

But against Mythics, the skill was ineffective. Her attacks were futile. And Ashscar wondered where the Calamities who were supposed to look out for him were.

Racked with pain, he struggled to push himself back to his feet, but he'd broken too many bones. His vision swam with blood and distortion as the Mythics drew closer, and Ashscar realized with cold, sinking dread that if someone didn't save him soon, he was going to die—here, on the scarred ground of a battlefield like a common disciple.

He groaned, blood seeping from his wounds in rejection of such a fate, and raised his gaze to the storm above, determined not to die without a fight.

The Mythics reached him then, savage, victorious smiles on their faces as one raised a glass blade and whispered, "This is for balance."

He brought the blade down in judgment, and Ashscar did the only thing he could.

He whispered, "Herald's Toll."

The sound that followed was not loud.

It was felt—a pressure sweeping the battlefield like a breath held by the world itself. Every storm phenomenon within miles shuddered, lightning bolts trembling as if a ripple had passed through them.

So ominous and portentous the atmosphere became that, for a moment, the Mythics faltered. They looked up, expecting another judgment of storm—but aside from the chaos already raging, nothing else came.

They looked back down.

The glass cultivator sneered. "Trying to delay the inevitable, aren't you, Transmigrant?"

Ashscar smiled. "On the contrary. I've only made it much more painful."

And then, in the air above them, a single whisper echoed.

"Ruin."
