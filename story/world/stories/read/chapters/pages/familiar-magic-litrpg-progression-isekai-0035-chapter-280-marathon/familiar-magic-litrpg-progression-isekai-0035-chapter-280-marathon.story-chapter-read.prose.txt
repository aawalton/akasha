Ethan’s breathing came in short, desperate gasps, yet he couldn’t stop. Not until he found shelter and safety–however brief it might be. His left arm drifted down to his side as he forced his body forward through the dense forest. His fingers traced along the ribs that had already turned to stone, and his doctor’s mind couldn’t help but consider the medical mystery of what his internal organs must be going through.

The body slowly transformed as one increased in rank, and Ethan knew that a Twilight like himself could survive with injuries that should be more than crippling. But in the end, it still functioned largely the same way. He was more than usually aware of that, as his left lung had long-since turned to stone, and he felt the incomparable sensation of his oesophagus slowly joining it. He needed to stop, and concentrate on healing himself with his rune, but the Basilisk was just so damned fast.

Six days. That’s how long he’d been traveling west, desperately trying to stay ahead of the creature that had once been an artist and a madman. His mind had been reduced to constant warring thoughts. This is insanity! I can’t keep going! But always countered by, Just a little further, just a little further. Three worlds depend on me.

Neither of the thoughts were altogether cogent. He barely needed sleep anymore–his body, at least–but the effects were starting to show. Being on the sharp edge of certain death every moment for nearly a week was breaking him, slowly but surely. But he had to do this, he had to take the fight to the Followers, and he might have the only Master rank on the continent hot on his heels.

The chaos and damage that the Basilisk could cause…it had to be worth it. It had to.

Yet, as Ethan scrambled madly onward, flashing through a rift every time he felt the enormous serpent getting too close, his spirit burning as it scraped up the last of his mana, a more dangerous thought tried to worm its way into his mind. A dose of reality he refused to acknowledge.

It had taken weeks to reach the Followers last time.

Ethan knew he was traveling far faster this time, but likely he was only halfway there. Could he do this for another seven days? Maybe as much as ten?

Enough! He shouted the words into his mind, as if the echoes of it could banish the simple logic, and erase those many miles ahead of him.

“I will buy you the time you need to heal,” Tomo said.

Ethan stumbled, missing a branch as he leapt from tree to tree. He could the next one with his left hand, the strain on his injured side lancing through him, flesh tearing.

“Do it,” he whispered, cycling to mystic. Ethan couldn’t even pause to look back as his demonic Familiar rushed toward the relentless monster hot on his heels.

This wasn’t the first time Tomo had done this, and there wasn’t much left of him at this point. The Familiar had the advantage of retreating into the Astral, which thankfully allowed him to escape the petrification effect of Basilisk’s gaze. But the damage wasn’t gone, simply shifted. Tomo was barely a fraction of himself at this point, and Ethan knew the demon would be relying on illusions for distractions, rather than anything close to a real attack.

Revan was already sheltering in Ethan’s soul, taking the power of flare with him. Devee only had a few heads remaining, and had to keep them safe for rift anchors. They were all on borrowed time, and the intrusive thought was coming on stronger and stronger.

This was all doomed.

The moment that Ethan heard the sounds of frustrated roars behind him, he slid to a stop, ducking behind a particularly thick-trunked tree, and slapping his right hand against his side. He had to search for an old kind of calm, one learned in medical school, long before magic. He needed to find the concentration necessary to quest deep inside himself, and guide the healing power of his rune in restoring stone to flesh.

The first shaking breath he’d taken in hours without pain was such a relief he nearly lost hold of the magic. But he pushed through, knowing that Tomo could buy him minutes at best. Basilisk might only be in the early stages of Master rank, but his time on Potentia seemed to have consolidated all his powers into that terrible gaze. Even a glance was enough, and no Twilight had the speed to avoid it for long.

The same thing that made the serpent such a perfect weapon to unleash on the Followers, was more and more likely to be what killed Ethan.

“I am sorry, Ethan Bishop,” Tomo whispered from the Astral. Ethan’s eyes shot open. The demon was back–weakened, but not entirely defeated. He knew that a few extra seconds wasn’t worth depriving Ethan of a second Bond.

Ethan shot to his feet. “Close enough,” he muttered. Then he was running again, his side still paining him, but back under control. The petrification would creep up once again, but he could hold it back for another hour or so.

As the snapping of trees echoed close behind him, Ethan wondered if he could survive another hour. He flashed away through another rift, eating through his tiny reserves once again, but needing the head start.

He appeared just outside the boundaries of the forest that had sheltered him, and looked up a completely bare hill, nearly a mountain in height. A straight run to the top was the only way to progress west, and nothing more than waist-height to hide behind.

Ethan took one last glance behind him, just high enough to see the path of fall trees as Basilisk gained on. Then Ethan ran.

At Twilight rank, he was fast. Speed far beyond what he could have imagined as a regular human. He could lapped the greatest sprinters Earth had ever produced, even outpaced more cars. Dust erupted behind him as he shot forward, his worn-boots cutting into the ground as he charged forward with everything he had.

The roar from behind told him that he wasn’t fast enough. Basilisk was already beyond the trees, his long, slithering body devouring the distance between them. Ethan could feel the looming, heavy presence behind him, he would have sworn he could feel that heated breath of the enormous snake as if his stalker were right on his heels.

But Ethan refused to look back, he just ran, pushing his aching body as hard as he could, forcing magic through exhausted limbs. He absently tossed knives behind him, knowing they could barely be considered a distraction without the power of flare to power them. Prime, his unusual Techwork Familiar-turned-armor did its best to help him, condensing around his back to block the inevitable petrification.

The ground shifted beneath him, and he made the mistake of glancing back. The enormous snake’s white eyes were locked on him, barely a hundred paces behind, and closing quickly.

The top of the hill seemed like an impossible distance, although he knew it was only seconds away. Ethan tried to push his flagging body even harder, stumbling when the petrifying gaze flashed over him. He felt Prime scream inside his mind, and understood the brave Familiar had extended itself into a shield behind Ethan, much of his metallic Techwork mass crumbling away into stone.

Prime reformed into armor, but it was nearly paper-thin now, and would offer no real protection. Thankfully, the abandoned mass had erupted into a cloud of dust, acting like a smokescreen as Ethan continued to race away.

He was close now, so close. But the Basilisk was right on his heels. Even as Ethan crested that hill, that nagging pessimistic voice inside was already chanting that his death was moments away. Deevee had dutifully moved ahead, though Ethan lacked the mana for even a single rift. Yet in his mind, the hydra was…excited?

The moment Ethan moved over the red rocky pinnacle of the hill, a whole new landscape opened up before him, including the chance at unexpected salvation. A Twilight demon, looking almost like an elephant with bladed wings, was directly ahead. Ethan leapt for the distraction with desperate strength, landing on the beast’s broad back, then springing away.

It happened so quickly that he almost didn’t register the midnight-black color of demon. He looked back to see the Basilisk snapping forward, jaws closing around the creature’s heavy middle. Tendrils of liquid darkness erupted from the smaller beast, questing across the colossal serpent.

Ethan came to a rolling stop, looking out at the small valley between two hills, eyes widening as he recognized the Follower army, easily several times the size it had been when he’d last seen it.

They were here. Not hundreds of miles to the west, but right here. And from the direction the silent demons were heading, they were moving to intercept Apollo’s forces. With this many, they’d tear through them all, even Fable.

Ethan gave himself the barest fraction of a second to collect himself, then his expression went dark, and grim purpose rushed through him. He glanced back at the Basilisk, which had already torn the smaller demon apart.

It looked like he’d be able to use this weapon after all.
