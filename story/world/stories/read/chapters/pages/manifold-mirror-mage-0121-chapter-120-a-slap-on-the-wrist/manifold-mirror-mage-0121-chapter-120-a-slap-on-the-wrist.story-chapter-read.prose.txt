“Yes, Saint Baphelus. I apologize, Saint Baphelus.” Arnault militantly said.

The young man’s long hair now perfectly matched Gio’s, which was fitting, considering that the spirits had apparently just decided that Gio, who was in fact six years younger than him, was Arnault’s new father- complete with a name change and a matching system notification identifying Arnault deGloria as a new member of Gio’s household.

So much more could have happened… You are lucky that this… oddity… is the worst of your problems. Gio, one of my fears for removing Arnault’s royalty, and the reason that I didn’t attempt such a thing, is that you could have accidentally issued a challenge to the death to King Arawn for the rights to his child.”

Gio paled. “I guess I didn’t really… think it over.”

Baphelus tapped his chin.

“No, you didn’t. But, I suppose that I should leave a bit of room for a pat on the back, too, because had you not acted when you did, Arnault might have been dragged back to the palace,” the Saint relented.

“For which I am very thankful,” Arnault added.

“I mean… yeah, no problem.” Gio frowned. “It’s just that I’m too young to be a father!”

“Don’t worry. I’m not going to be asking for an allowance.” Arnault joked.

“Yeah… but I don’t really know what kind of issues are going to pop up because of this. Are the spirits going to like… penalize me if I neglect my new ‘kid’?” Gio nervously asked.

“Hah. If only the spirits took such a stance on deadbeat fathers. They do not.” Saint Baphelus said. “I wouldn’t worry too much about this. Your bond is akin to what one might refer to as a ‘foundling claim ’-usually something done by powerful animals… or dragons, as a way to bring an abandoned child into an established family.”

“I suppose that’s interesting…” Gio said. “But if the spirits aren’t going to enforce this ‘bond’ in any way, then I don’t get why it happened at all. Was it just a weird, chaotic quirk of magic?”

“Gio… you connected a concentrated reservoir of raw chaos to some kind of symbolic representation of authority that I cannot even detect, much less interact with, despite literal millennia of experience in dealing with strange magic and spiritual phenomena. A ‘chaotic quirk’ is putting it lightly. When you gamble with forces beyond your understanding, don’t be shocked when you win a prize.” Baphelus smirked.

“And besides,” Arnault said. “It’s not as if I plan on inserting myself into your family dynamic. I’ll likely still be spending much of my time under the enchantment of the Headmaster’s masks, so I doubt that my new last name will have the chance to raise any questions, either. I only have one concern: Saint Baphelus, I’m not going to start… changing, am I? How far does this ‘foundling claim’ go?”

He ran a hand through some of his newly silvered hair.

“No offense, Gio… but I’d like to avoid going through a second puberty, and I’ve grown comfortable in the body that I currently have,” Arnault said.

“None taken.” Gio agreed.

“The silver hair was surprising. I hadn’t realized that the Ataraxian silver was an inheritance… but no, your anatomy- nor genetics- should not change due to this change. Probably.” Saint Baphelus guessed.

“An inheritance, as with the gold hair and eyes of the royal family. Silver and gold… an interesting dichotomy. Almost poetic, really.” Baphelus ruminated.

“I am unfamiliar with that term in this context,” Gio noted.

“From my understanding, it’s usually the signifier of lineage, often in sorcerous bloodlines or mixed species heritage. You’ve definitely seen them before- you know how a lot of noble houses will have vivid haircolors, like bright red or forest green?” Arnault explained.

“I thought that was just genetics!” Gio said. “Or like… bold fashion choices.”

Baphelus laughed. “For some, it is. I’ve seen several entire families that force their children to dye their hair with cosmetic magic to present the illusion that they have significant magical lineage… but, no, many are legitimate traits that manifest in children in addition to genetics- or occasionally despite genetics.”

Gio thought back to some of the colorful features that he’d seen on fellow students.

“Interesting. Wait- so what lineage do I have, then? Silver hair is common among thousands of Ataraxians like my whole family. I know it isn’t as common in the Ringed City, but there are other immigrant families, especially in the Copper Ring.”

“Nothing significant, probably.” Saint Baphelus noted. “Much of the time, inheritances are just cosmetic. Heavens, I could tell you that just because a person looks magical doesn’t necessarily mean that they will have any innate talent beyond simple cantrips… and even when it does, it’s usually a minor affinity increase, or slight proclivity towards a certain type of spell. For example, because of my unique upbringing, I was born with a ‘Major’ affinity for Death-aspected mana.”

“Huh.” Gio hummed. “Interesting. So… if I lost my family ‘bond’, would my hair color change?”

“Maybe. Who knows? It could be that your silver hair is genetic in addition to being an inheritance. The Goldwatch family was very blonde, even before the contract with the spirits of the Gold Ring that turned the first king’s hair and eyes golden.” Baphelus said.

“I don’t mind the hair. Wait, what color are my eyes now?” Arnault asked.

“Hazel. Close to gold, but not quite.” Baphelus answered.

Gio produced a small mirror for Arnault to look at himself with.

“Thanks. That’s a handy spell.” Arnault said.

“I mean, I’m a mirror mage, so…” Gio waved at the conjured mirror.

“Oh. Right.” Arnault chuckled.

“Going back to the whole ‘inheritance’ thing - I’m not secretly the recipient of some crazy magic bloodline from the witches of Ataraxia, am I?” Gio asked, eyes wide in wonder.

“No.” Baphelus deadpanned. “In fact, I believe that the silver hair of the Ataraxian common-folk was in fact a result of a failed attempt at producing a caste of magically handicapped and neutralized servants who would never be able to rebel against their masters.”

Gio recoiled in horror.

“Wait, what? Someone tried to make my… ancestors into permanent slaves?!”

“Well, clearly, it didn’t work,” Baphelus noted. “Here you are, magical as could be.”

“But the fact that they even tried to do that is… awful,” Gio said, horrified.

“Oh, indeed. That’s why Augustein told them that if he ever caught wind of any more unethical human experimentation on their shores, he’d unleash a plague upon them that would harry all biological life on their continent into dust.”

“Augustein… as in Saint Augustein?” Arnault muttered.

“There’s a reason that his title is ‘the Ruin’.” Baphelus explained. “Auggie has a good heart, but he can be terrifying.”

“Can he… do that?” Gio wondered, thinking about the image of Saint Augustein’s mechanical Patient Saint body that his mentor had shown him a vision of. The horrifying machine had been some kind of antenna on moving legs that pulsed out a wave of malaise-inducing magic for miles.

“Well… the point is that the threat worked, and the witch responsible for the experiments was given a stern slap on the wrist by the Grand Coven… until she created a giant flesh-creature which prompted a massive international threat response team. Then we all sanctioned her and put her under supervision until her eventual disappearance.” Baphelus explained.

“Disappearance… so she could still be out there, making monstrosities? Doesn’t that seem kind of lax?” Gio asked.

“Oh, heavens, no. The monstrosity was an honest mistake, but we believe that she was genuinely trying to do good. She was also insane by that point, and hardly worth punishing further. We assigned her several trusted caretakers, and then she disappeared into one of her private dungeons. I am unconcerned.” Baphelus non-committally responded.

“Maybe this is one of those ‘Youthful ignorance’ things… but why wouldn’t attempted slavery and giant monster creation carry a harsher sentence than just… leaving her to her devices? Who is… or was… this witch?” Gio pressed.

Baphelus sighed. “Her name was Hilaria. To begin with, Ataraxia has never necessarily been monitored closely by the Saints. Their Goddess is difficult to deal with, and the only one of my cohort to have had successful dealings with the Grand Coven was Serelei, who herself is not the easiest to deal with. The effect is that Ataraxia has always been prone to rather draconian standards of living for those who do not find themselves among the ruling coven. We generally trust that the Ataraxians will police their own populace fairly… and so long as they don’t go too far, we largely leave them to their own devices. The Grand Coven has a… vested interest of sorts… in Hilaria and her legacy. There would be nobody more concerned with keeping Hilaria contained and alive than those witches.”

Gio suddenly remembered a small detail of the testing ceremony.

“Hilaria… I think I recall that name being mentioned in connection to that new professor, the one who is going to be the mentor of Princess Sybil.” Gio said.

“You remember correctly. Alecto Stradivari was the last protege of [Life-Weaver] Hilaria before her disappearance.” Arnault commented.

“So… Sybil’s mentor’s… mentor… was responsible for turning my ancestor’s hair silver hundreds of years ago through a failed, horrifying experiment. The world is a small place.” Gio deadpanned.

“Indeed.” Baphelus mused.

Even more reasons to be suspicious of the Goddess Cybele… also maybe Sybil and her mentor, to be honest. With every detail that I learn about Ataraxia, I am more and more glad that my grandparents sent my mom and her siblings away. I couldn’t imagine what my life would be like if I had grown up there. If I had the money, I’d suggest that we try and move Grandma and Grandpa deGloria here, to the Ringed City… I hope that they’re doing okay.

“Ah, Arnault… it appears that your services are desired elsewhere. My wife requests your presence in the entrance hall, which means that my next appointment shall be with us momentarily.” Saint Baphelus instructed.

“Well, I guess that I need to go stand beside my mentor. Gio… I’ll touch base with you soon about our new uh, bond.” Arnault said.

“Okay…” Gio replied.

Do I call him ‘sport’ or is it too early for dad jokes?

Before Gio could finish deliberating, Arnault had donned his golden mask and vanished. Only Gio and Saint Baphelus remained atop the dungeon seal, sitting in silence at a table in the direct center of the school. Gio sipped a cup of tea that had appeared before him, enjoying the simple flavor of the masterfully brewed beverage.

“You look like you still have something to say,” Baphelus noted.

Gio thought for a moment before responding.

“I was actually going to avoid voicing my thoughts... I think that they might be foolish.” Gio said.

“Foolish thoughts, in my experience, tend to lead to the most illuminating lessons. I’d love to hear them, if you wouldn’t mind sharing.” Saint Baphelus replied.

“I’d rather not, as the more I think about it, the more I think it’s quite a rude and presumptuous thing even to think, never mind say out loud to your face. Please ignore me.” Gio cringed.

Baphelus chuckled. “Dear boy, I would remind you that I am incredibly old, and have heard every insult that language can produce, many times said to me by beings that I find far less enjoyable to spend time with than yourself. Feel free to speak your mind, especially if doing so would benefit our understanding of one another.”

“Okay…” Gio began. “I was thinking that I don’t understand why you allow yourself to have the problems that you have. I’m nearly certain that there’s something that I don’t understand about you… But aren’t you powerful enough to just… do whatever you want?”

Gio fidgeted with his hands as his mentor wore a contemplative expression before responding.

“You’re wondering about the exchange that I just had with the King.” Baphelus guessed.

“Not only that… but also what you just told me about Ataraxia… and the fact that you admit that a single Saint could just wipe out the whole continent with magic. Not that I think that violence is always the correct choice, but here I am fighting for table scraps just to scrape by in my education, while you and the other saints have the power to just… change the world as you see fit. Why allow that witch… Hilaria… to live at all, after the bad things that she did? You said that you didn’t try to help Arnault because it might have forced you to challenge the King for the rights to his child, but why would that be an issue at all? Couldn’t you just annihilate him? You could even show mercy and force him to see things your way while also letting him live. I just… don’t fully understand why you don’t leverage the awesome power that you clearly have to solve these issues.”

Saint Baphelus set down his teacup. He folded his hands under his chin, leaning forward with an inquisitive glint in his eye.

“What a good question. Why, indeed?” Baphelus said. ”You are correct. If I so wished, I could set the sky on fire, rip the very souls from all that oppose me, and raise their corpses as mere playthings to dance for my entertainment.”

Gio looked at his mentor oddly.

“I didn’t say that I thought you would do those things,” Gio muttered.

“Yes, but I could, couldn’t I?” Baphelus mused. “But there’s a very important follow-up question to that hypothetical that needs to be asked, and one that I hope you will someday ask yourself, once you grow further into your own power.”

“Is it… ‘Why not just be nice?’” Gio guessed.

“Hmmm…” Baphelus pondered. “No, but I would agree that kindness is a privilege of the strong. The real question is this: ‘What next’?”

Gio blinked. “What next?”

“Yes. What happens after? When I have deposed all that oppose me and stand alone upon a glassy slab of hot slag that was once a thriving city, what happens then?” Baphelus asked.

After thinking for a moment, Gio had no good answer.

“I… guess you could start to rebuild?” Gio guessed.

“With what? After the kind of battles that require me to use my full power, the land is usually scarred and deformed for a long, long time. I do not have the talent for polite duels, you see- I am a walking disaster. And who would be there to help me? The kind of people who focus on magic that heals the land don’t usually last long in these kinds of affairs, and the ones who do won’t help a bloodthirsty murderer.”

“I think I’m starting to see your point.”

“Ah, but that’s only the first-order consequences of my actions.” Baphelus interrupted. “There are always unforeseen consequences. Besides the widespread destruction, if you dared leave any survivors, you’ll always be watching your back in case their descendants hold grudges, which, of course, they would. Hell, people hold grudges even when you try doing the right thing… never mind if you’re an actual villain! And then the spellblight - oh, the spirits love to play with the residual magic leftover from powerful workings. The life that eventually grows back upon that scarred land will be sickly and strange, usually with upsetting traits and abilities to forever remind you of your sins. And then the lost culture and history… the damage to the global economy… the resurgence of dungeons in the area unpoliced by local forces, the opportunists who come in afterward, the increased attention from the spirits, the politics and fear mongering, and perhaps worst of all… the regret.”

“Saint Baphelus, you speak as if you’ve…”

“Done it before? Oh, yes. Several times, in fact. For example, there was a small city-state named Dravonia that taught every citizen that the Saints were too dangerous to allow to exist. They successfully sent assassins after Ignatious’s wife and children… we wiped them off the map.”

“That's... I don’t know what to say,” Gio replied.

“It’s okay, Gio, you would not be alone in thinking ill of us… the world thought us monsters, and some still do.” Baphelus sardonically smiled. “It was a disproportionate response, driven by righteous anger and grief. Those assassins… they took my brother’s family from him… from us. A kindhearted woman and two little girls whom I had held in my arms. And in revenge, we massacred a civilization. It took centuries for the stigma of that crime to wear off, and Ignatious especially had to fight what felt like a never-ending stream of people looking to challenge ‘the strongest man alive’ for the destruction he wrought in his anguish. Afterward, several of us vanished from the zeitgeist until enough time had passed that only cautionary tales remained of Dravonia.”

“You killed… all of them? Every last person?” Gio dumbfoundedly asked.

“In a single afternoon. It was simple and unchallenging. At the time, and with certain voices in my ears…, it felt like the only correct decision. They had made us to be monsters in their minds, and so the Dravonians celebrated the deaths of Ignatious's children as they danced in their streets... They made mocking dolls, and hoisted them aloft as they burned. " Baphelus said, with a haunted gaze.

Gio sat in wordless shock.

"After that... the massacre felt justified.  The spirits even rewarded me - rewarded all of the saints for it. You wanted to know why we spared Hilaria? Because we have been the villains in plenty of stories. What she did, in comparison, was small. To Arnault, my wife and I are teachers who are helping him retain his autonomy… his parents, however, see us as monsters who have abducted their heir. Likewise, there are plenty of scampering little mice in my school that view me as a ruthless tyrant for prioritizing the youth, rather than investing my time and resources into their self-aggrandizing research projects or attending their simpering symposiums as they relentlessly chase fleeting improvements. Yet still, these sycophants demand that the school cheer and celebrate them for achieving a three percent yield in destructive potential for their exclusive family magic, all the while I have held more power in a single knuckle than they will ever achieve.”

Baphelus seemed to look off into the distance for a moment. Gio sat quietly, digesting his mentor’s words.

“I’ve once again gotten lost in my own ranting. I apologize.” The lich said.

“No! No. I appreciate your perspective… even if some of the things you say are a bit over my head, or frighten me endlessly.” Gio replied.

Baphelus pursed his lips.

“Perspective, indeed. If I could try to instill one thing into you from this conversation that I hope sticks with you on your life’s journey… it would be that I hope you will remember the power of that perspective. One day, you may find yourself in my position, or one like it, where you wield fantastic power and hold influence over the lives and well-being of others. Remember where you came from, and what it was like to lack those things. Be kind whenever you can afford to do so.”

Gio nodded, trying to cement his mentor’s words into his mind.

Baphelus raised his eyes to the edge of the dungeon seal, where a doorway had appeared.

“Ah. Good timing. It appears that our guests are nearly here.”
