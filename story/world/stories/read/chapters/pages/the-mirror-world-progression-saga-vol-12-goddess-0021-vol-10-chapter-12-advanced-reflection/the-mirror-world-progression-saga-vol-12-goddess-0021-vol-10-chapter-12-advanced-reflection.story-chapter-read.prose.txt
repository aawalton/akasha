Samantha turned her attention back to her realm, as observed in totality from the high throne on Heavenpeak. It had been a few days since Rivermount’s conquest and consolidation. She’d scooped up miscellaneous quests to trickle more gains in classes, though nothing had bumped levels yet. [Goddess] was getting close. Her next planned quest completion — which seemed nearly wide-open for her to go after soon — could possibly level her.

Her Follower count had climbed up to over 7200 strong. It would’ve been higher if not for a fairly big spike in deaths from the war. On that note, Heaven was churning with dead people. The city was buzzing with activity and fueling Calrenazzod with a continuous flow of magic item supplies. With the integrated realms, word about what happened to the Blessed Dead leaked completely, which was a mixed bag. Some people longed for the things they heard existed in the afterlife.

There were a few suicides, and not just elderly folk. It was up there among the worst feelings her machinations had spawned. One could rationalize that this or that person suffered too much, but she had to be the one questioning how much she was responsible for a person’s death.

In any case, it wasn’t prolific. If being accepted into her order did anything, it gave people a purpose to follow. The path was definitely one to walk down and actively contribute to. She just made sure to emphasize how much they needed everyone, from farmers, to common soldiers, to camp aides. Armies and armies of people. She also kept a desperate, strong lock on the details of Heaven’s organizational structure becoming fully public. She couldn’t keep it from embedded Followers anymore because of the integrated society, but she could keep people from attempting to join strictly out of a desire to ‘go to Heaven.’

For now, anyway. It was laughable to think she could keep that information clandestine permanently. Perhaps she wouldn’t bother with any of it after Matrazere. Let everyone know her name, unleash the chaos of full disclosure, and watch the numbers explode by an order of magnitude. Something told her if she didn’t do it intentionally, it would happen anyway. The pressure was clearly mounting as it was.

Her Devout numbers were up to 1285, her Fanatics 28. Gaining Fanatics was uncontrollable, but she still wanted Devout percentages way higher than that. The majority of them were still clerics, crusaders, and the handful of other special classes. The ‘basic’ Devout should at least be half of them, rather than a small percentage.

Despite the high costs of the war, Faith Energy ballooned, now nearly 80k. Fortunately, she’d bumped the maximum again through quests, now 120k. She was going to need to filter prayers to specialized FE even harder to sink the shit somewhere.

Mirrors FE was particularly useful considering the numbers she could move between Transition Points, or simply allowing for efficient [Mirror Walk] use, which the highest level Resemblants could arrange — more slowly — remotely, but requiring her FE directly, by ‘back-mind’ authorization. Curiously, by the result of their nature, they could send someone else through ‘in their stead’ and avoid the typical ‘triple cost’ penalty she needed to carry people with her.

It was fast becoming its own sink of energy, to say the least. It couldn’t be the standard form of transport due to the expense, but it was great for time-sensitive shit and VIPs.

She saw every reason to climb Mirrors FE reserves to 10k or even higher. As high as she could get it, basically. It would go away in a flash when she used it militarily, after all.

Truly, the number of mirrors she was having produced was staggering. Her cleromages could make them rather easily thanks to her heavy ‘creation’ type Domains. Mirrors, Illusion, Pneuma, Magic in combo? Yep, conjuring plain ass mirrors was not a task she had to see to personally, by any means!

Checking her numbers and presumed growth, her next day ought to climb over 700 Followers, maybe even 800. The Followers quest was for 8888, so she would definitely hit it before Matrazere. Good.

Finishing up the approvals and other organizational things that made Jeeves satisfied with her, Samantha shifted over to Itha Ranon and took a little time to finish off her next custom spell, one she’d been working on here and there over a few days, aiming to get a little ‘spell level 14’ power equivalency before she got there. In any case, it actually wasn’t a standard spell or spell effect, anyway, though there was something vaguely similar. She’d investigated this carefully.

Spell Reflector (50 MP, 2 Mirrors FE, 2 Magic FE)

(Level 13; Abjuration, Reflection, Negation) Invest a mirror at least 8x8 centimeters with the ability to automatically reflect any spells, arcane or divine, that target you individually, back upon the caster. It does not reflect any Area of Effect spells whatsoever. The mirror floats around you and appears only as a minor magic item unless targeted for identification. It is also possible to detect a (Reflection, Negation) status effect on your specific person.

The reflector affects spell levels up to your highest level of spell access, unaffected by caster levels (+20%, +1, fr. Abjurer). If a higher-level spell afflicts you, it is partially reflected, depending on the effect, while the reduced remainder penetrates through. ‘Level of effect’ is divided between you and the caster, as appropriate, and a new effect of the appropriate level is simulated.

Once expended entirely, the mirror shatters into dust. Casting time is 1 minute. The effect lasts for 8 hours. You may only have one (Reflection), (Absorption), or (Negation) spell active at one time; new will supersede old.

Oh my gawd, look at that Abjurer bonus! Fucking level 13, to 15.6, rounded up to 16, then +1 to 17! That’s an absurd bonus, to wipe out almost any spell! Even the highest level will be reduced to almost jack squat in remaining power.

Essentially, a spellcaster would need to sense the effect and remove it first before they could try an actual deleterious spell on her. She was aware that certain spells bypassed (Reflection) — namely and almost exclusively, ‘breaching’ specialty, anti-abjuration type spells and abilities that would strip these sorts of things. AoE dispel could also hit it and remove it, potentially. Level 15 had an ‘anti-breach’ layer that could stack on top of this to delay things even more, but of course, she wasn’t quite there. Not that it mattered that much when she had her own additional layers and options already.

This particular spell was simply noteworthy for its long duration and its passive operation. Her other defense options against spells, [Reflect Force], [Deific Counterspell], and Foolmaker’s Spell Absorption, were action-oriented to varying degrees.

As far as an ‘improvement’ over the normal effect of a Level 14 spell, the default was an (Absorption) type effect similar to what Foolmaker had. Reflection was not only more her style, but better on action economy and potentially quite devastating to enemies.

You have gained 2 exp in [Magineer] through spell acquisition thanks to [Comprehension: Evolver].

When it was over, and the spell was a nice, crisp entry on her status sheet, Samantha leaned back in her cushy chair in her research library and sighed contentedly, patting herself on the back for a job well done.

She smiled. “Very soon, it’ll be time to take on the Consolidator.”
