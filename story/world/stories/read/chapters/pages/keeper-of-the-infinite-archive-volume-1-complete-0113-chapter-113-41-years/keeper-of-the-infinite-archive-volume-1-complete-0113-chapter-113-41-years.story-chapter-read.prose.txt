Gregor took a slow, deliberate step backward.The door swung open and Morzath the Undying stepped out into the entrance hall.He was wearing his usual spiked blackened plate armour. A deep, heavy cloak trailed behind. In his massive hand, he casually held a pink pastry box and a small notebook. He looked perfectly at ease, a terrifying ancient warlord returning from whatever mundane baking errand then his violet eye flames settled on Bart, Albert, and the interface.

The legacy authority designation was still glowing in bright blue text.

Morzath stopped.His flames flickered once, drifting over the administrative profile that definitively exposed him as Keeper #4,091’s emergency continuity custodian.

“Ah,” Morzath said.

Bart looked at him. “You know why you're here.”

“Obviously,” Morzath replied, stepping fully into the hall.

Gregor pointed a finger at him. “You could have led with that months ago.”

Morzath calmly adjusted his grip on the pastry box. “I could not.”

“We found the restriction,” Bart confirmed, keeping his voice as calm as possible. “Your disclosure authority was withheld until primary service restoration was verified.”

Morzath looked at the glowing blue text, then back at Bart.

“Then the system still remains marginally competent,” Morzath noted.

Bart stepped out from behind the Patron Services desk.“Before we begin,” Bart said, establishing the rules of the conversation, “I am setting a parameter. For now, I only want what you personally know. Not what you inferred, not what you heard later, and not what you assumed Keeper #4,091 was doing. Just your own actions and knowledge.”

Morzath’s violet flames flared slightly in approval.

“Better,” Morzath said.

“Better than what?”

“Keepers generally preferred answers first and evidentiary standards afterward.”

Bart stayed on topic. He had three specific things he needed to confirm, and he refused to open another mystery before resolving this one.

“First,” Bart said, pointing at the slate. “The system identified you as the external continuity custodian.”

“I was.”

“But the record also states you were never a Keeper. You were never acting Keeper, temporary Keeper, or Keeper #4,091’s successor.”

“Correct.”

“You possessed no authority over the collections, the staff, acquisitions, patron access, or Keeper succession.”

“Correct again” Morzath said again.

“So your authority was deliberately narrow,” Bart concluded. “Continuity.”

“Continuity, yes precisely” Morzath agreed.

Gregor frowned, trying to parse the logic. “Which means?”

Morzath looked at the custodian. “That when your institution ceased answering its correspondence, someone still had to.”

It was the first truly coherent description of the service they had found.

Bart turned back to the slate. “What exactly was entrusted to you? What was inside the continuity package?”

Morzath did not hesitate. “Institutional identity. External credentials. The active obligation index. Patron and partner claim records. Emergency verification authority. Succession continuity material. Recovery procedures.”

As he spoke, Bart queried the System. A neat, corroborating list bloomed on the screen, matching the Lichs’s categories perfectly.

Gregor stared at the list. “No books?”

Morzath looked faintly offended by the question.

“I was not asked to operate a branch library,” Morzath replied.

Bart looked at the categories, and the shape of Keeper #4,091’s plan finally became clear. #4,091 hadn't tried to save the Archive's physical collection by sending it away. He had saved the institution's ability to remain itself. The Archive could lose rooms. It could lose entire sections of its network. It could lose millions of volumes. It could even lose its Keeper temporarily. But as long as someone external could still verify the records - Yes, this institution existed. Yes, this obligation remains valid. Yes, this claimant is recognized. Yes, these credentials remain authentic …then the Archive had not completely vanished from the wider network.

“What did maintaining it actually involve?” Bart asked.

“Administrative procedure,” Morzath said, listing them off. “Custodial status verification. Claim preservation. External status responses. Obligation maintenance. Credential authentication.”

Gregor stared at the armoured, undead conqueror of the Ashen Plains.

“You ended up with paperwork,” Gregor said.

“I maintained institutional continuity,” Morzath corrected.

“With paperwork.”

“Frequently.”

Gregor tried and failed to picture it. “Did you have a desk?”

“ of course.”

“A filing cabinet?”

Morzath’s eye flames narrowed defensively. “Seven of em.”

Gregor looked genuinely unsettled. The idea of the Undying Lich sitting at a desk, carefully filing institutional correspondence while kingdoms burned around him, was somehow more horrifying than his reputation as a warlord.Bart kept his focus on the institutional side. He had already inferred the candidate profile, but he needed the custodian to confirm it.

“Why you?” Bart asked.

Morzath placed the pink pastry box carefully on the counter.

“Because I was available,” Morzath said with a slight smirk.

Morzath held the silence for a moment, then eventually admitted the actual reasoning.

“Because I am independent of governments,” Morzath said, his voice dropping into a heavier register. “I was not reliant on Archive infrastructure. I am capable of maintaining identity-bound obligations. I am difficult to coerce and I was expected to remain alive / unalive for a very long time.”

Gregor distilled the phrasing into plain language.

“So he chose you because you were impossible to kill and impossible to order around.”

Bart replied considering the words. “Those are surprisingly useful continuity qualifications.”

“ The Keeper phrased it more diplomatically,” Morzath noted.

Bart looked at the Undying. “Did you actually fulfill the duties?”

Morzath drew himself up slightly, the spiked plates of his armour shifting with a, metallic scrape sounding almost insulted.

“I accepted custody,” Morzath said.

“That's not what I asked,” Bart said evenly.

Morzath’s eye flames flared, then settled. “Yes. I fulfilled them.”

“I verified old Archive credentials for institutions that no longer knew whether the Infinite Archive still existed,” Morzath said. “I preserved claims from patrons whose requests could no longer reach the primary facility. I answered partner institutions asking whether ancient agreements remained valid. I refused unauthorized claims against Archive assets. I maintained obligations that could not be discharged because the primary service was gone.”

Bart listened with quietly,It gave immense weight to the abstract records they had uncovered in the previous hour. Morzath had not merely stored a sealed box in a vault waiting for someone to return. He had kept the Archive legally and institutionally alive outside itself. He had honoured the promises.

Albert, who had remained unusually quiet beside the railing, finally spoke.

“You maintained all of that after the Ruin?” the Chief Custodian asked.

Morzath turned his horned helm toward the skeleton. “Affirmative.”

“I was completely unaware.”

“You were occupied in a way.”

Albert considered the blunt truth of it. He had spent six centuries trapped inside a ruined, collapsing library, desperately holding the physical structure together.

“Yes,” Albert agreed quietly.

Bart looked from Albert to Morzath and said nothing.

He turned back to the primary sinterface. He pulled up the legacy service record they had found earlier.

MINIMUM VERIFIED DURATION: 41 YEARS

“The Archive verifies continuous operation for 41 years,” Bart said.

Gregor looked at the Lich. “41 years of this?”

“Among other things,” Morzath said softly.

Bart continued. “You maintained the continuity service for 41 years.”

A long pause hung over the Patron Services desk.

Morzath looked at Bart.

“No.”

Bart didn't argue. He immediately applied his evidentiary logic, looking back at the exact wording on the glowing screen.

Minimum verified duration.

Not total duration.

“The Archive could verify you for 41 years,” Bart corrected himself.

“Correct,” Morzath said.

Bart met the Undying’s gaze. “How long did you actually maintain it?”

“Your archive records heard me for 41 years,” Morzath said.

The weight of the distinction settled heavily on Bart's shoulders. 41 years marked the end of the Archive's evidence, not necessarily the end of Morzath's service.

“Then why did they stop hearing you?” Bart asked.

Morzath’s violet flames went perfectly still. The conversational one vanished.

“At first, there were roads,” Morzath said.

“Roads?” Bart asked.

“Routes,” Morzath said. “Connections between destination anchors. Courier paths. Transit links. Your system has accumulated several names for them.”

Gregor frowned. “So not actual roads.”

Morzath considered and replied. “Some had paving.”

Morzath looked past them, toward the portal of the Archive that led out into the wider, disconnected network.

“Then they began closing,” Morzath said.

Bart stepped closer. “Who closed them?”

Morzath paused for a moment.

“Some failed,” Morzath said. “Some were sealed by the realms they connected. Others were closed from within the Archive itself.”

Bart's expression changed. “From within?”

Morzath’s violet flames narrowed into sharp, bright points.

“The Curator was one of those responsible.”

Bart stared at him. “Why?”

“That,” Morzath said quietly, “you should ask him.”
