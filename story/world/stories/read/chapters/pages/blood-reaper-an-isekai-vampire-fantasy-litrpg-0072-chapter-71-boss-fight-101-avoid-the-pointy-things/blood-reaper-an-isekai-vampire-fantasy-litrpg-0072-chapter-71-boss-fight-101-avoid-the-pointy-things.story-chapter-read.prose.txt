Marcus was going to die.

The distance between Ace and his fallen teammate stretched impossibly far. It didn’t matter how hard he ran—he would never make it in time.

Ace could only watch as Raven stalked toward Marcus. The corrupted guardian’s body rippled with tendrils of shadow that wound around his form like living smoke. The ground beneath Raven’s feet blackened further with each step, death spreading from his presence.

Marcus raised a trembling hand, a desperate attempt to unleash one final psychic attack. The air around his fingertips wavered, but the energy fizzled out before it could manifest.

Raven laughed, his voice layered with echoes of others, as if remnants of souls that had been consumed by the shadows thrummed through his own mouth. “If it’s any consolation, you survived longer than I thought you would.”

He raised a hand wreathed in darkness, ready to attack. The shadows around the sentinel’s form didn’t merely cling to him. They danced, responding to his will with eager hatred. As he raised his hand toward Marcus, tendrils of shadow peeled away from his body like smoke from a dying fire. These wisps twisted through the air, gathering above his upturned palm until they formed yet another deadly blade.

The hivemind connection pulsed once, strong and clear—a collective surge of refusal. Not just from Ace, but from the entire team’s shared will.

Ace’s disbelief.

Olivia’s cold fury.

Tara’s desperate resolve.

Marcus’s fading consciousness.

They all condensed into this single, shared intent—save him.

Ace pulled two Duskblades from the shadows, and the obsidian daggers materialized instantly in his palms. His muscles burned with exertion as he hurled them one after another toward Raven, each blade trailing shadows that writhed like living things. He summoned two more, and two more, each flying through the air one after another.

The first struck Raven’s shoulder, sinking deep into corrupted body. The second embedded itself in his thigh. The third missed as Raven twisted away with unnatural speed, but the distraction achieved its purpose—he stepped back from Marcus, shadows coiling around him in defensive patterns. Four through six landed squarely in the boulder, sliding to the hilt as they hit with terrifying force.

“Predictable,” Raven snarled, yanking the shadow blade from his shoulder. The wound closed almost instantly, but not before Ace spotted something crucial—a flicker of genuine pain in the guardian’s eyes.

He could be hurt.

Olivia reached Marcus first, little more than a blur of crimson and black as she darted in from behind Raven. Hydra’s Bane flashed in her hands, the dark blade pulsing with poison energy. Her face showed neither fear nor hesitation—only the cold calculation of an artist assessing her canvas.

When Raven pivoted to face her, his shadow blade was already aimed at her head. Olivia dropped low and struck with merciless efficiency as his weapon sailed over her. His blade sliced off a chunk of her hair as she changed direction, but if she noticed, she didn’t seem to care. Hydra’s Bane sliced upward, cutting through Raven’s defensive shadow tendrils and into his arm. The blade bit deep, parting flesh and bone with horrifying ease. Not a clean severing, but close—his arm dangled by threads of shadow-infused muscle as dark blood spilled from the wound.

Raven howled in pain, a sound that shattered the unnatural silence hanging over the lake. The water’s surface rippled in response, disturbed for the second time since their arrival.

“You’ll pay for that,” he spat, his shadows already rushing to repair the damage.

Tara closed in. Her Echo Staff gleamed with golden light as she rushed forward, the weapon humming with energy that directly opposed the shadows wreathing Raven.

“Don’t. Touch. My. Friends!” she snarled, swinging the staff in a wide arc.

The golden-tipped end connected with Raven’s temple with a sickening crack. His head snapped sideways, his eyes rolling back momentarily as the impact sent him staggering. The corrupted guardian shook his head, disoriented by the blow and the staff’s purifying energy. He screamed in pain, its magic conflicting with his own corrupted shadows, and he stumbled backward as he fought to regain his balance.

“Fuck,” Raven said, spitting out a tooth as he put some distance between himself and the girl with the shiny stick. “I get it. You care about each other. Doesn’t mean you’ll survive.”

Tara raised her staff again in a blatant threat, and Raven winced away.

Wow.

Whatever magic that staff had, it was way more powerful than any of them had realized.

Ace could use that to their advantage.

The ground beneath Raven liquefied into shadow. He sank into it, vanishing completely, leaving nothing but a rippling patch of darkness on the blackened grass.

A pulse of warning shot through him from Tara, and her eyes widened as she looked at something behind him. It hit him with such force that his body reacted before his conscious mind could process the threat.

He wasn’t fast enough.

As Ace pivoted, another shadow pool reformed on the ground behind him. In a flash of smoke and shadow, Raven erupted from the darkness like a creature from the abyss. In his hand he held a blade of pure shadow, its edge sharper than physical matter had any right to be.

Time compressed. Ace saw the blade’s trajectory, calculated the dodge, felt his body tense for movement—

—and there was nothing he could do.

It was going to hit.

The shadow blade plunged into Ace’s chest with devastating force, punching through the Blood-Bonded Thornplate Armor that had saved him in countless previous battles. The armor detected the critical strike immediately, and obsidian thorns erupted from its surface in a violent surge. The razor-sharp spikes shot outward in a defensive burst, designed to impale whatever attacker had landed the deadly blow.

It didn’t work. The thorns sliced through shadow rather than skin or bone.

The armor trembled as if alive in a last desperate attempt to protect him. Beneath the surface, its stored blood energy pulsed erratically, trying to activate its Blood Memory property—but with the shadow blade disrupting the magical connection, the final life-saving surge failed to deploy. Cold fire erupted from the wound as the blade drove deeper, cutting through his spine and piercing through his back.

Ace’s breath left him in a pained gasp. He looked down, oddly detached, watching his own blood spill down the front of his armor, spreading across the blackened grass in a widening pool.

Through dimming vision, he saw Raven’s triumphant smile, his teeth too sharp to be human and his eyes filled with sadistic satisfaction.

“Your loyalty is your weakness,” Raven whispered, his mouth so close that Ace felt the cold breath against his ear. “You could have phased into the Shadow Realm and put distance between us. Could have put enough distance between us that I couldn’t reach you. But no—you had to play the hero. You didn’t even see the trap for what it was.”

Raven twisted the blade, eliciting a fresh spray of blood and a strangled yell of pain from Ace.

“Marcus was never my target,” he continued, his voice almost gentle now. “It was you.”

He pulled the blade free with a wet splat that echoed across the silent lakeshore. Ace dropped to his knees and, after a moment of swaying back and forth, collapsed onto his side. Blood pooled quickly around him. His hivemind connection to the others flickered wildly as his consciousness wavered.

The System’s laughter rang out, delighted and cruel. “Ooh, plot twist! I did not see that coming!”

Raven stood over Ace’s fallen form, the sentinel’s shadow energy gathering around his hands as he prepared for a finishing blow. The corruption visibly spread further across the man’s body—what had once been a human form now looked increasingly like a creature woven from the void. Shadow tendrils extended from his back, reaching toward Tara, Marcus, and Olivia as they scrambled toward their fallen leader.

Ace’s vision blurred, the world around him swirling into indistinct shapes. More and more of his blood spread across the blackened grass, creating patterns that looked almost like runes—a thought that drifted through his fading mind with strange clarity.

Through the weakening hivemind connection, he felt his team’s desperation. Tara’s horror and determination flowed strongest—a healer watching life slip away, refusing to accept failure. Olivia’s cold fury and Marcus’s partially healed presence followed close behind, their emotions a storm of grief, rage, and resolve.

The last thing Ace saw was Tara’s desperate lunge toward him, her hands already glowing with light as she prepared to heal him. The light from her palms provided stark contrast to the gathering darkness of Raven’s shadow energy, a battle between life and death played out in light and shadow.

She wouldn’t make it.

She was too far away, and Raven would intercept her before she got the chance to help.

At the edges of Ace’s consciousness, something stirred. The Shadow Realm called to him, its endless void reaching for him as his awareness slipped away from the physical world. Familiar presences waited there—faceless entities that had watched him during his previous immersions, now approaching with greater intent.

Above him, Raven lifted his reformed shadow blade for a final strike, the weapon poised to sever Ace’s head from his body.

“The Sisters send their regards,” he said.

Darkness claimed Ace’s vision entirely, the Shadow Realm’s cold embrace calling to him like a siren.

Yet again, he had failed.

Yet again, he was leaving his team on their own when they needed him the most.

I’m sorry, he thought, and it was the last conscious moment before the physical world disappeared
