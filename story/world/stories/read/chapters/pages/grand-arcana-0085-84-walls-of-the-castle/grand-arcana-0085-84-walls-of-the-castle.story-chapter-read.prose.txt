FOURTEEN OF SWORDS (MINOR, SWORDS)

(Rarity – Common)

SKILLS (1/1):

1 BUTTERFLY STRIKE: (sharp weapon required) perform an uninterrupted curved slash attack that could be continuously repeated. The direction of the attack can be changed freely at any point without interrupting the attack.

A butterfly strike. That was always helpful, though it would have been a lot better if I had gotten it a few trials earlier.

Of the remaining cards, the only useful ones were the Paladin and the Rider. Naturally, I also took the minors to serve as food for my divine blades in the future. That left the question of the Major Arcanas. Up to now, I had deliberately not merged them to boost my existing ones out of fear the war might drag on. There was no need for that anymore.

“Merge,” I whispered.

THE RIDER has been improved

Skill 2: MOUNTED ACTIONS: perform actions while riding

THE PALADIN has been improved

Skill 2: CLEANSE EVIL: (weapon required) damage inflicted on undead and evil beings is increased tenfold

“Everyone set?” I looked around.

Despite our actions, the war was progressing pretty much as I expected: terrible for the humans and relatively well for everyone else. The shrieks of the snapdragons had terrified the majority of the soldiers to the extent that they still were unable to move. The few who had kept their composure were too concerned with follow-up waves to issue adequate orders.

“We only have four,” Reginald said.

His Beastmaster card was a lot more advanced than mine, allowing him to instantly become the creatures’ new favorite. I also got a feeling that the man had to be familiar with horses back on Earth, for he wasn’t at all bothered by one of the dragons licking his hair.

“Two of us will have to share,” I said.

“Better three,” Marcus corrected. “Three groups. Three archers. Better odds.”

Given that not everyone had an upgraded Rider card that made sense.

“Sure,” I agreed. “You take Cathy. I go with Sparta.”

As I mentioned that, the old man gave her a glance. Even after everything, team loyalty remained a thing. However, it was Eleonor who seemed most upset of all.

“Can you use the fourth one as a shield?” I ignored her, turning to Reginald.

“If needed,” he said curtly. “Take your pick.”

I chose what seemed to be the largest. It wasn’t like I’d be keeping it after the trial. In all honesty, I’d be impressed if it managed to survive the storming of the castle.

The dragon reacted to my weight with slight reluctance.

“It’s fine, little dragon,” I hummed a song.

The strands of aether came out of my mouth and attached to the beast’s ears, instantly calming it down. The Rider card did the rest, granting me full control over the creature.

“You handle dragons pretty well,” Sparta said as she took her place behind me. “Women, not so much.”

“What?” I looked over my shoulder.

“Your goth,” she whispered with a provocative smile. “She was hoping to ride with you?”

“Well,” I kicked the dragon with the back of my heels, “I don’t care.”

Up to this point, I had never voluntarily flown before. The only reason I had ever bought the Rider card during my previous run was that a goddess had told me that there would be riding in one of the upcoming trials.

Considering how rare such a requirement was, I ended up being the only one who had it and reached first place. It was also at that point that I had obtained my most valuable card: The Divine Page. To this day, I didn’t know whether that was planned by the goddess or not.

Yells came from the human army the moment we set to the skies. Suddenly, all of them were concerned that we weren’t following a plan, as if such a thing existed. The nobles, even when they weren’t busy ignoring us, had set off with the intent of marching up to the aelf castle and putting it under siege. Their misguided hope was that the two sets of monsters would resolve the issue between each other, leaving the humans intact. The only thing they were going to achieve was getting slaughtered on both ends like a lit candle.

“Can you make them go faster?” I shouted at Reginald. “Like they did?”

“Do you want to puke your guts out before the fight?” he asked in return.

The question made me smile. If the sudden speed boost had the power to achieve that, it was definitely worth it. Now, I really wanted to feel the sensation.

“Better before than after,” I shouted. “Do it!”

There was a moment’s hesitation. Several people had their doubts, though not enough to openly oppose me. Thanks to my future knowledge, I had successfully established my image as someone who knew how things worked. Ironically, being openly called Harlequin’s favorite probably had more sway than anything else.

“Get ready,” Reginald shouted, then bent forward and whispered something to his dragon.

A strange guttural sound came from the creature, soon to be shared by all the other snapdragons in the flock. Then, everything around us changed. The action was so fast that I didn’t feel it happen; instead, I felt the aftereffects. An invisible force slammed into my chest and stomach, making me feel as if I’d fallen flat in a pool of water.

My brain tried to come to terms with the change in landscape by ejecting everything out of my stomach. My will and combined resistances helped me prevent that. Unfortunately, I was the second to succeed. I heard Sparta hurl behind me.

Cathy and, to my surprise, Marcus were no different. It was nice to know that even psychopaths had physical limitations. The only other person to withstand the shock was Reginald, further proving that he was more than some ordinary old man. After the trial was over, I’d have to keep a better eye on him.

Of course, it was Eleonor who had the most difficult time of all. A long time ago, back before I joined the Grand Arcana for the first time, I would often hear the reference “vomit fountain” while playing games online. Even then, I never expected to actually see it. For several seconds the unfortunate goth ejected vomit like a water hose. The amount looked considerably more than her body could hold, or so I thought. Things looked so pitiful that Cathy instructed Marcus to fly closer so she could cast a healing circle on Eleonor.

“Not used to flying, I guess,” Sparta whispered behind me. I could feel the pleasure in her voice as she did.

“Let’s hope she’s fine,” I said. “She’s our second ranged fighter.”

“I know. I was just saying—”

My attention switched to a new series of sounds that reached my ears.

“Arrows!” I shouted, casting a wind spell circle beside me. If I had known more spells, I would have resorted to something more practical.

Cathy, thankfully, proved more resourceful. Dozens of spell circles covered her and both dragons she was close to.

I looked at the mountain. The castle appeared deceptively close, probably less than a thousand feet. The Observer card showed me that was an illusion. We were still half a mile away or more. It was the arrows that were enhanced to fly a lot faster than they were supposed to.

Barely had I caught sight of them when they punctured the solo dragon, turning it into a bloody pin cushion.

“Tell them to screech!” I shouted as the unfortunate creature fell toward the ground.

A loud shriek filled the sky, this time aimed at the occupants of the aelf castle. Unfortunately, that did nothing to stop the arrows. Barely a second later, a new wave followed, targeting specifically Marcus and Cathy. Thankfully, her spell held, bouncing arrows off like straw. They still proved annoying enough to force Marcus to break off from the group.

“They’re targeting us individually,” I noted. “Next one will be us.”

“I got it,” Sparta said behind me. “Just handle the dragon.”

“Withstand,” I said, making use of the Emperor card.

The third wave of arrows followed. Just as predicted, they were targeting me. Their approach was painfully familiar: take out the shields, then go for the mages. The first dragon had been considered a living shield, which it frankly was; then they had gone for the most magic-inclined participant there was. I also had a fully developed Magician—though no divine pact—so I was the logical third target.

Divine threads slashed around me. With lethal precision, they sliced every arrow that approached, simultaneously shoving them away. In the process, my spell circle was also destroyed, though it wasn’t like it would have done much given the situation.

A short distance away, Eleonor had also gone on the offensive, reverting to the best form of defense—attack. Force projectiles struck a section of the castle wall, like artillery. The area was ridiculously small, and yet somehow it had achieved its purpose. There was no immediate enemy attack.

You really are an oracle, I thought.

Having such an ability should have guaranteed her countless wins. No doubt it had something to do with her success early on, yet why was she using her skill so sparingly?

“Boost into the castle,” she shouted, her voice still weak from all the vomiting. “We don’t survive the next wave.”

“We won’t be able to steer them,” Reginald argued. “We’ll end up dead, anyway.”

For a split second, I saw the desperation on Eleonor’s face. Maybe it was due to the recent puking spree, but I wasn’t looking at the arrogant, smug goth anymore, but a scared girl. She was fully aware of what might happen, and still knew that it was the only choice.

“Do it!” I shouted, combining the Bard and Lover’s abilities.

There was no way of knowing what glimpse of the future she had seen, but I was willing to chance my life on it. Naturally, I also strengthened my bones just in case.

Another flash followed, only this time the change was accompanied by a wall slamming into us. The Snapdragon let out a violent scream as it was torn through two merlons of the castle wall.

My body took over, leaping off the moment I realized what was going on. Behind me, Sparta did the same.

“Back to back!” I shouted, drawing the divine blades out of my inventory. “Careful with…” I stopped.

There was no one there. Not only was the castle wall lacking guards, but there was no indication any had been there for weeks. As snobbish as they were, even aelves weren’t immune to dirtying up their posts. There was no sign of fires, spell circle impression, or even tobacco ash.

I turned around, expecting to see something on Sparta’s section of the wall. There was nothing there either.

“Think they’re waiting for us inside?” she asked.

Blanking my mind, I concentrated on the sounds. I could hear Eleonor attempting to vomit again. The lack of stomach liquids made her put in a lot of effort, but I didn’t hear anything coming out. Reginald was doing his best to try and get her to adjust. Unlike me, he had managed to land his snapdragon very much alive.

Elsewhere, Marcus told Cathy to look away before sinking a blade into his own mount. In typical fashion, he wasn’t one to leave anything to go to waste. Yet… those were all the sounds I could hear. There were no aelves talking, no soldiers running along the walls or corridors, no alarm sounding. For all intents and purposes, it was as if they didn’t remotely care that we had breached their outer layers of security. The only indication that there were strong enemies was the tugging of my blades.

“Someone’s definitely here.” I said, turning towards the direction the blades were indicating. “I can’t hear any guards.”

“Maybe they’re waiting to see if we can make it to them,” Sparta suggested. “You said the Grand Arcana holder could control statues. Maybe we need to go through those before we could face the aelves?”

“Maybe.” That was wrong.

Despite all their arrogance, aelves weren’t stupid. They had seen us ride in on the mounts that previously belonged to some of their nobles. The mere fact that we had managed to defeat them would have the rest of the monsters rush to get a piece of us.

“Can you cut through marble?” I asked, looking at her blades.

“You mean you can’t?” she responded with a laugh and a wink. “I think I’ll manage.”

“Good. I’ll take the lead, you’ll be my shield. Slice anything non-human on sight.”
