I was smiling, I realised as I joined the crowd of people making their way up the stairs. I hadn’t even noticed it before that moment, but I couldn’t help it. I was too happy, and part of me was almost glad I was finished with class for the day. Obviously, I’d enjoyed the lessons. They were always fun, but I was looking forward to being done.

Seth had said he’d meet me in the foyer so we could hang out for a little before dinner. He had acting last, which was held on the ground floor of the Academy, whereas I’d had history on one of the subterranean levels, so it made sense to meet there. It was kind of halfway. Technically, it was closer to him, but I knew my way around the Academy, and he didn’t, so it was easiest to just meet somewhere he could find easily.

Being in class without him had been weird, though. I wasn’t sure why, but something had just felt a little off all afternoon. I think I missed him, and that made me feel strange. He hadn’t been out of the induction wing for that long, and I’d only really had a couple of classes with him, but I’d enjoyed them too much. I liked being able to sit next to someone I already knew and having him there made it easier for me to actually answer questions and join in, but I wasn’t really sure why. He just made me more comfortable, and things felt less scary.

The smile started to slip from my face. That happened sometimes in my real life too. Like, in the lessons I had with Phoebe, I was always a bit less anxious. The thought of putting my hand up or being picked on by the teacher to answer something still made me want to crawl under my desk and hide, but it was a little bit less horrible.

I thought it would be different at the Academy, though. I’d hoped it would, at least. I was meant to be different there; I was trying to be more confident and better, but the same anxiety kept coming back to me, and I didn’t know what to do about it.

There wasn’t really anything I could do about it. Not really. Seth and I had different specialisms. He was training to be a field agent, and I was still undecided, so we were always going to be in different classes. Even if I were to make up my mind and decide I wanted to be a field agent too, we’d still probably be in different lessons sometimes. They were sorted by ability, and I knew there were some subjects that he was better at than me.

And I was better than him in a few. I knew that too. We’d been together in a lot of the classes in the induction wing, and I’d found some easier than him, so there was no way we’d have every single class together.

It was still kind of tempting, though. It shouldn’t have been, and I knew that, but I couldn’t stop myself from ignoring that and fantasising about how nice it would be to be able to walk to every single class with him. We’d be able to sit together too, and then, over time, I’d probably become more and more comfortable because I’d have a friend beside me.

But it wouldn’t just be Seth. If I changed my specialism and became a field agent, Katie and Abbie could be in our classes too. They were also training to be field operatives, so as soon as they got out of the induction wing, I’d always be surrounded by friends. It would be so fun.

An elbow caught my arm, jolting me from my thoughts, and I shook my head, blinking quickly as I tried to push the mental image of the four of us sitting around a table and grinning out of my mind.

“Sorry,” the girl who must have elbowed me said.

“That’s okay,” I replied, sending her a quick smile before looking around.

I hadn’t noticed just how busy it had gotten, but it seemed like half of the Academy had finished class at the same time and were trying to squeeze up the narrow staircase. It made it impossible to move more than an inch at a time, but I didn’t mind it too much. My mind was too busy swirling with indecision.

It wouldn’t happen, I tried to tell myself firmly. Even if I did decide on just the one specialism, there was no guarantee that we’d be in the same classes, so there was no point in dropping the others just yet. I’d just be limiting my options, and there might not be any benefits.

Plus, I enjoyed the other classes I was having too much. Having no specialism meant I got to take every single subject, and if I became just a field operative, I’d have to stop studying some of them. Immediately, I felt the temptation lessen. My mind seemed to recoil away from the idea, and it still appealed to me a little, but I couldn’t stand the idea of dropping any classes.

Also, I wouldn’t be able to spend as much time in the GSOD, I realised. Disappointment rose within me. As much as the GSOD did intimidate me, it was also fascinating. I loved being down there, and I wanted to learn more about it. I wanted to spend even more time in those rooms so that I could actually understand what the analysts were doing properly. There was no way I could abandon that.

And what about the labs, I found myself wondering. Did field ops have any research and development classes, or did they just never set foot in there? Surely, they had to. They needed to understand how to use all of the tech, and their insights could be really helpful. Sara had said that at some point.

I’d barely spent any time in the labs. My memory of those classes was a bit of a blur, but I could tell that I’d found them interesting. I could feel the intrigue pulling at me. I wanted to delve into them properly, and I knew I’d be so disappointed in myself if I dropped them just on the off chance that I’d be able to be in a class with my friends.

Plus, maybe that wouldn’t be so good after all. Perhaps it was just a fluke, and being in a class with Seth didn’t actually make me any more comfortable. I might be just as anxious and scared the entire time, and then I would have dropped classes I actually enjoyed for no reason.

A new fear began to pull at me, making my heart sink. What if I decided to become a field operative and was placed in all of the same classes as Seth, Katie and Abbie, and they got bored of me? It felt like it was only a matter of time before that happened, anyway. Katie and Abbie were probably going to move into my room, and if we had classes together too, they’d always be with me. There was no way they wouldn’t get sick of me.

I was annoying to be around. I tried not to be, but I knew I was. Mom had told me that on the way back from Scotland a few years ago before pulling over at the edge of the road and storming out of the car. I’d sat there, blinking back tears, whilst she chain-smoked for a little before coming back and pretending nothing had happened.

My eyebrows pulled together. What had I even done to cause that argument? I couldn’t remember. I didn’t think I had done anything, but I must have. There was always something. I wracked my brain, trying to figure it out, but I couldn’t. The memory stayed just beyond my reach, and there was nothing I could do to pull it any closer.

It could happen again, though. If I spent all my time with Seth, Katie, and Abbie, they might get sick of me, just like Mom did. Then, I’d lose all of my friends because I knew Scott wouldn’t want to hang out with me instead of the others.

That would be so awful. Even the thought made tears burn behind my eyes, and I blinked quickly to hold them back. My eyes flitted from side to side, checking to make sure that no one else had noticed, but I was lucky. Everyone else seemed too distracted to notice the fact that I almost started sobbing halfway up the stairs, and I was so glad that I didn’t need to come up with a lie to hide how ridiculous I was being.

It was fine, I told myself firmly. I didn’t need to change my specialism, and I’d probably start feeling more comfortable in classes soon. Once I’d gotten used to the tutors and other trainees, everything would be easier. Or maybe it wouldn’t, but that was fine too. I was used to being anxious and uncomfortable. That was just my default way of feeling, so it wasn’t exactly anything new to me.

I took a deep breath and squared my shoulders as I reached the top of the staircase and stepped out into the foyer. My gaze immediately began roaming the room in search of Seth, and my heart clenched as I found him.

He was on the far side, near the doors to the canteen, but that wasn’t what sent a wave of emotion over. It was who he was talking to. I didn’t know the girl; I must have seen her around the Academy before, but I’d never taken any notice of her until that moment. I couldn’t help but watch as she threw her head back, her perfect golden curls bouncing with the movement. Her musical laughter reached my ears even over the dim of people around me, and jealousy wrapped tighter around my heart.

Seth had made her laugh.

I wasn’t sure why that realisation hurt so much, but it did. My steps faltered, and I was almost tempted to turn and go back downstairs, to hide in the safety of the GSOD, but there were too many people behind me. They pushed me forward, and I couldn’t bring myself to fight against them. Instead, I just let them drag me closer to Seth and the beautiful girl he was still talking to.

“Yeah, I fully agree, but I still think Mercu—hey!” Seth called, breaking off as he spotted me.

The girl glanced up, seemingly surprised that their conversation was being interrupted. Her eyes scanned me, and I looked at Seth quickly, not wanting to see the smirk that I was almost certain would appear on her face.

“Hey,” I replied, trying to mimic his enthusiasm.

She reminded me of Ella, I realised as I fought the urge to look at her. There was something about the girl that made me think of Ella. She was always following Duncan around, and I was pretty sure she hated me. She never really tried to hide that. Unless Duncan was there, that was. Then, she was far too nice to me, but that was easy to see through.

“How was your afternoon?” Seth asked.

“Good, yours?”

“Yeah, really good!” he said enthusiastically.

I smiled at him awkwardly, far too aware of the girl whose eyes I could feel boring into my face.

“Good,” I replied, my eyes flicking towards her before I could stop myself.

“Oh, shit, sorry!” Seth apologised quickly, shooting her a rueful smile. “This is Sally. She’s in my acting class. Sally, this is Grace. We’re in the same cohort, but she was the first to get out of the wing.”

Finally, I looked at the girl again. I didn’t want to. She intimidated me, but I couldn’t avoid it for any longer without making it obvious, and that would have been even worse. I didn’t want Sally to know that her staring had gotten under my skin.

“Oh, I thought it was you!” Sally cried. “I’ve heard all about you.”

My anxiety spiked. I had no idea why she would have heard anything about me, and that worried me.

“You have?” I said uncertainly.

“Of course! Georgie’s one of my best friends, and she was so happy you wanted to know more about that horrifying experiment she’s working on,” Sally explained with a laugh. “She’s tried to explain it to me like fifty times, and I can’t wrap my head around it.”

A faint memory pulled at the back of my mind, but it refused to grow any clearer. I had no clue what Sally was talking about. I could vaguely remember being in a lab with Georgie, one of the ones that needed me to put on a full-body suit before I could enter, but I couldn’t recall what exactly the experiment was.

“Oh, yeah,” I said. “That was super interesting.”

I was pretty sure it was interesting, anyway. From what I could remember, most of the experiments they were running in the labs were, so it was a safe bet.

“Sure,” Sally laughed. “But I’ll be honest, even if Georgie hadn’t told me about you, I’m pretty sure I’d know who you are anyway.”

That didn’t feel like a good thing, and despite Sally’s cheerful tone, I felt my guard rise slightly. She was being perfectly polite to me. Nice, even, but I wasn’t sure whether I trusted it. Part of me was worried it was just an act, like Ella.

“Oh?” Seth said, glancing between us.

There was a smile on his lips, but he looked as unsure as I felt.

“Yeah! I mean, she was the first out of the induction wing, she has no specialism, and she managed to impress Ms Brice. Do you know how rare and impressive that is?” Sally demanded playfully.

I felt my cheeks begin to flush, and I had to look down as emotions slammed into me. Had I actually impressed Ms Brice? That didn’t sound right. She didn’t seem like a particularly easy person to impress, and I hadn’t exactly done anything particularly special since being in the induction wing or the Academy.

“Very?” Seth guessed.

“Yes!”

“Well, having known Grace for a couple of months now, I’m not surprised at all,” Seth said. “She is very impressive.”

I couldn’t help but glance up at him. There was something about his tone that made me feel like I was on a roller coaster. My stomach dropped, but it didn’t feel bad. I wasn’t scared or worried. I was just…

At a loss for words. I didn’t know how to react to what Seth had said, and the expression on his face didn’t help. Seth was looking at me with a slight smile on his lips, and I couldn’t hold his gaze. I had to look away again as I felt my cheeks turn even redder.

Sally glanced between Seth and me, a hint of a furrow appearing between her eyebrows.

“Aw, that’s cute,” she said after a couple of seconds before looking at me again. “Oh, wait. I never introduced myself properly, did I?”

“Uhh… no. You didn’t,” I replied, eager to change the subject.

“Sorry, I always forget,” she said with a laugh. “Well, I’m Sally. Second year, field op.”

“She’s our age, though,” Seth added.

I felt my eyebrows rise in surprise.

“Oh, really? I assumed you’d be older?”

“I know I do look it,” she said with a joking sigh. “I skipped a year in school, so I did all the testing and stuff early before they realised I was too young. They were going to wait and bring me in with your cohort, but then someone changed their mind about one of the other candidates, or some information came to light or something which meant they had a free space, so here I am.”

“Huh, that’s cool, though?” I said, unsure of what else to say.

“Yeah! I mean, I’m pretty glad Alistair took a chance on me because this place is so much better than back home, but your cohort seems pretty fun,” Sally said.

It was impossible to miss the way her eyes flicked towards Seth as she said that, and I felt a wave of jealousy crash over me again. She was clearly flirting with him. She was being so obvious about it, and I hated how that made me feel. It was worse than when Ella flirted with Duncan in front of me. At least I knew he wasn’t into her, but with Seth…

Not that I was into him, I told myself, trying to force the bitter feelings aside. He was just a friend, nothing more. And I wasn’t into Duncan either. Not properly. He was probably just a friend too.

“They’re pretty great,” Seth agreed before glancing into the canteen. “Hopefully, a few more of them will start getting out of the induction wing soon.”

“Yeah, I’m sure it won’t be too long,” Sally said. “Once the first few people get out, it’s normally a fairly steady stream until the last person gets sent away or let out.”

“Good.”

There was an awkward pause, and I chewed on the inside of my lip as I tried to think of something to say. I couldn’t think of anything, though. My mind was too consumed by the fact that Sally was clearly into Seth and how much I hated that, even though I was trying not to think about it too much.

“So, are you guys having dinner now? You’re welcome to sit with me and my friends,” Sally offered, gesturing towards the canteen. “I’m sure there will be enough space for a couple more people on our table.”

“Oh, thanks, but… are you hungry?” Seth asked me.

I was a little, but I couldn’t think of anything worse than sitting with Sally and her friends. They were probably all really nice people, like Sally and Georgie were, but I didn’t have the energy to be social. Not with people I didn’t really know.

“Mmmm, not really,” I lied. “But I’m happy to eat now if you want to?”

That was also a lie, but it was kind of Sally to offer, and I didn’t want to be rude. Plus, if Seth did like her, I didn’t want to stand in the way of them, regardless of how I felt about them.

To my relief, Seth smiled at me.

“I’m not really hungry either,” he said before looking at Sally again. “But thank you.”

“Yeah, thanks. Maybe another time,” I added, seeing the disappointment flicker across her face.

Even though that was what I wanted, I still felt bad. It was silly, and I knew it. I should have been happy about it. I didn’t have to spend the next however long pretending to be much more social than I was and watching as Sally flirted with Seth, but I felt strangely guilty about disappointing her.

“That would be great,” she said with a wide grin. “You’re welcome anytime!”

She seemed to be speaking to both Seth and me equally, and I couldn’t help but return her smile.

“Thanks.”

“See you in class,” Seth called as she turned towards the canteen. “So… what do you want to do now?”

I hadn’t thought that far ahead. My mind turned blank as I tried to think of something to suggest. There were options. I knew there were lots of things we could do at the Academy, but I couldn’t think of a single one.

“I don’t know. What do you want to do?”

Seth’s expression turned thoughtful, and he cocked his head to the side as he considered it.

“Mmmm, how about we go up to the roof again?” he suggested.

“Sure!” I agreed.

“Awesome. Do you want to swing by the lounge on the way to grab a pack of cards? I saw some in there this morning.”

“Uh, yeah, that sounds good,” I said as we started to make our way towards the stairs. “I don’t really know how to play many card games, though.”

That wasn’t true, I realised. That version of me had never set foot in a casino. It had played a few games of solitaire on the school computers, but that was it. I’d never played poker or blackjack or even cheat, but there was another version of me that had.

A sudden flash of a memory shot through me. I had to tighten my grip on the bannister to avoid tumbling backwards as a wave of dizziness threatened to overpower me. It stole the air from my lungs and made my head spin.

Blackjack was one of my favourite games. I’d learnt how to play it as a child. There was a scam that some of the others used to try on the unsuspecting tourists, and that was how I learnt the basis. Then, I started sneaking into the gambling halls not long after.

It was easy enough to get in without anyone questioning my presence, and I quickly discovered that each hall was different. That depended on who ran it, but some things were always the same: the flashing lights, the haze of blue-tinged smoke from the pipes, and the constant soundtrack of triumphant cheers and disappointed groans. It was addictive. It filled me with energy, and I loved it.

“Oh, don’t worry. I can teach you,” he told me, interrupting my thoughts.

I needed to be careful, I told myself as I tightened my grip on the world I was in. I didn’t know where those memories were from, and I couldn’t let myself get swept away into another world. It was too dangerous.

“Thanks,” I replied. “So, do you play often?”

“Uhh… not really. Not anymore, anyway. I used to have this maths teacher who would let us play cards if we finished our work early. We started off with just the basics like go fish and snap, but then, he taught us a few other games.”

“Oh really?”

“Yeah. I loved it,” Seth said. “I always used to think that there’s another me in some other world working in Vegas or something.”

He laughed, and I had to force myself to join in. The sound was too high-pitched, and I knew I sounded nervous, but I hadn’t expected Seth to mention another world. It had taken me by surprise because I was pretty sure that most other people didn’t think about that. It wasn’t normal, and I knew that.

“Yeah. It’s silly, I know,” he continued, rubbing the back of his neck with a sheepish expression.

“No, no,” I said quickly. “It’s not! I think it’s cool. I mean… I have no clue what I’d be doing in any other world.”
