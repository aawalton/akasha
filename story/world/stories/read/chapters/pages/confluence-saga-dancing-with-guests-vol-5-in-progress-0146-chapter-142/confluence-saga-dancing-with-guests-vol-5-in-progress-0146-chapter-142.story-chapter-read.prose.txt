People like to think of Constructs as physical objects. Things designed by Engineers or found in the Depths by the extremely lucky. People are right.

Their definition is also incomplete. Complex, long-lasting techniques, those that are set to perform tasks are a form of Construct. Arbiters use them to simplify verification of claims and contracts, Healers in larger institutions use them to monitor patients. Seers use them when searching for lost information, set them to find information and wait for their return.

These are Techniques, not Constructs, would be the obvious argument. But what of those searches that take days, weeks, longer still? No Seer maintains the technique, no Seer tracks the progress until it completes. Independent Techniques are Constructs.

What then happens when someone truly skilled, far down their Path and capable of such complex work, sets a Construct to monitor an entire Holding, an Organization? When the scope and expectation grows and the Construct adjusts to match? Does it develop a personality? Is it still safe?

Anya opened the door to their dorm and hopped to the side as Gwen stormed in without so much as a hello. Cara was only a few steps behind her, her hair somehow both nearly brown, typically a sign of distress for her, and spiked enough to be sparking, something Anya had only ever noticed mid combat when they went all out.

"He's being ridiculous," Gwen snarled. "We obviously can't go visit but he also has decided we don't need any visitors either. He didn't want me to even know he'd sent them notice that we didn't need, that we didn't Want them to see how we were doing."

Anya sighed as she shut the door, sending Seb a mental image of the state the pair were in. They'd been helping Fay set up the spare room for her stay when Gwen had knocked on the door. Any harder and Anya might have expected to see it crack, assuming someone could break down these doors that was.

"What..?"

"Our parents, cousins, anyone."

That didn't help and after a moment Gwen seemed to realize she'd forgotten some details.

"Bern. He sent a message home yesterday. I don't know how he convinced anyone to let him," Gwen said before letting out a snarling shout.

"We have no visitors," Cara said softly. "Their parents aren't coming, Morgan's family isn't coming."

"Yours?" Anya asked.

She saw Cara shake her head as her hair flattened to her head.

"Lifesworn become part of their partner's family once the oath is made."

"I didn't-"

"No," Cara cut her off then smiled. "You didn't know. Gwen is my family."

"Anya," Fay said as she stepped into the room. "Seb said I should-"

Anya didn't even consider her action, placing herself in front of Cara as she spun towards Fay. She didn't actually touch her friend, simply stood there ready to stop her if needed. It took her a moment to realize that Cara had her sword in her hand, that she was treating this as a threat.

"Cara!" she said sharply. "Look at me."

"Who. Is. That." Cara said as their eyes locked, her whole body tense.

"Fay. Our Holding Seer. Seb and my's sister. Our family." She stressed the last word ever so slightly.

She wasn't entirely used to Cara's reactions, to how Cara would listen to her instructions even when she wasn't trying to give any. This time she had tried to put what little she and Seb were sure about to good use.

"Not family," Fay said quickly. "Sister yes, but not-"

"Fay," she cut in. "If you're our sister you're family."

"I.."

"Fay," Seb said from behind her. "She's not going to budge on this, you know you're family and like it or not we're going to treat you that way."

"But-"

"Fay was it?" Gwen said. "I'm guessing you think you know what they're saying better than they do?"

"I," Fay paused as Anya saw her eyes dart between the two of them, taking in their similarities. "Milady Fae. This is my first encounter, but yes."

"Gwen. And you heard Anya call my sworn Cara."

"Gwen," Fay said with a nod. "I think of them as family, as kin, but I am not asking-"

"Cara," Gwen said sharply enough to pull everyone's attention at her tone.

Anya felt Seb's recognition of that strange Energy that affected fae commands pulsing as she continued.

"Treat her as family the same as Seb."

Anya felt herself calm as Cara's demeanor shifted instantly, the unspoken threat vanishing faster than her sword.

"I'm sorry. I…" Cara trailed off awkwardly.

"You didn't think we had company," Anya finished for her. "You saw a stranger and her brother is cranky because he lost again yesterday."

"Again?" Cara said before grinning. "Did he storm off?"

"Cara!" Gwen said despite the smile forming on her face. "Did you actually punch him this time Seb?"

"No," he said. "I managed to trip Keldren when he focused on Anya for long enough that she got free and tackled Bern."

"Tackled?" Cara choked.

"Pinned him," she said proudly.

"Morgan?"

"He's been sitting our fights out," Seb said. "He convinced Lord Percivus and Mistress Sarna that he wasn't making any significant contributions."

"He doesn't like being knocked out," Anya said.

That was one time, Seb sent.

"It was good practice," Gwen said. "I've had to deal with cuts and bruises, but I don't think Cara or Keldren ever let someone hit them in the back of the head hard enough to leave them unconscious."

"I'm not sure anyone our level could knock Keldren out," Cara mumbled.

"If he were half as tall…" Anya said.

"He'd still be too stubborn," Cara answered.

"Just like my twin," Gwen said. "Sorry. We should leave, let you spend time with your family."

Seb?

Up to you.

Do you think Fay?

She almost scowled at how funny Seb seemed to find the question. She wasn't going to ask Fay to put up with strangers. They hadn't seen each other for months and she was only here for a few days.

"Fay," Seb said. "Do you mind if Anya's cousin stays?"

"Only if I get to ask questions."

"You have to answer them too," Gwen countered.

"And not give boring answers," Cara said.

"Marten?" Fay guessed.

The answer was unanimous.

"Marten."

Fay lay back on the bed and stared at the ceiling. The afternoon and evening had been exhausting, yet at the same time thrilling. She'd spent the whole time exchanging answers with a fae, a Royal no less. Gwen was only level 15 and had spent most of her adult life Diving, but she still had grown up in a fae court and seen things first hand that Fay had only scant references to.

The amount of information she could add to those records was- No, she couldn't add it, it was family business not Seer business. It didn't matter though, she knew things now. She'd heard what growing up was like, actually shared a few stories of her own childhood, talked about her time in the tents and how her Clan had wandered across their Pond rather than build anything permanent other than the portals and transit hubs.

She knew Seb and Anya had even more questions, she'd opened up around these strangers and gone into details she'd never felt ready to share. She wasn't sure if she actually was ready or if she'd simply been honoring the agreement she'd made when the questions started. It didn't matter, she'd opened up, she'd shared and she didn't regret it.

Cara was a delight as well, rarely interrupting her Mistress, rarely speaking up directly. When she did it usually left Gwen speechless, or caught between glaring and blushing. She clearly knew what to say and how to say it, to someone who just as clearly appreciated it.

And they had named her extended family. She knew that Gwen hadn't needed to, that there had been plenty of ways she could have phrased things to keep her as someone to be trusted but distant. Gwen trusted Anya's opinion, or Seb's, maybe both?

It didn't matter. Tomorrow was a true family day. Gwen had promised she wouldn't barge in, that she wouldn't send Cara to convey anything, as long as Bern didn't do anything monumentally stupid. From the looks the others had shared Fay wondered if they'd actually have the day to themselves. It didn't matter though, they'd have what time they could, they'd be able to keep catching up, to talk, to spend time making up for absences.

Three days was a long time, but it was also no time at all. Three days hearing about everything, three days in the same building as a couple of fae nobles and their cousin…

Three fae nobles she realized. Anya was Anya. Her sister, Seb's wife, a dear friend. Anya was half fae, part of a royal line. This wasn't the first day she'd spent talking with a fae, this wasn't the hundredth day she'd spent doing that.

Fay rolled over and closed her eyes. She'd have to think about that. She, Fay, was sisters with a fae. She didn't laugh, she didn't chuckle, she was pretty sure she didn't start to giggle. Fay and fae. She'd have to see if she could fit that into what she said at breakfast, see their faces when she made such a horrible joke.

She just had to make sure they were sipping their coffee when she did.
