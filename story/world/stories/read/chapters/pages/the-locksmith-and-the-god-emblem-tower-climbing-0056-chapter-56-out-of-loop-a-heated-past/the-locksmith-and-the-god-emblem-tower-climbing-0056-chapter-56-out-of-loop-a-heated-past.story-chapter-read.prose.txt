Wearing simple yet meaningful clothing, a dark-skinned man smiled at a cluster of curiously shaped clouds. Alongside this, he observed the ocean above him as it stretched into infinity. Sitting on the grass, his thoughts were a mystery.

"Hey, boss!" A young woman's voice slightly broke his concentration. "What are you doing there?"

Without turning around, he knew the girl's name; Grace. Along with the other adventurers who had appeared the previous day, she was a rebellious and energetic young woman, the youngest of the group. They had arrived at the monastery a few days ago and already seemed to feel at home.

"...Nothing much," he said, maintaining his smile. "Why the question?"

"How can you stay like this without thinking about anything?" she asked, joining him on the grass. "I feel like I'd go crazy..."

"I don't stay without thinking about anything." The monk clarified. "I just redirect my thinking potential to deeper parts of my mind. That way, I let myself be carried away by my subconscious."

"...So you pretend to sleep?" She closed her eyes. "Like this? Man, that's boring."

"It takes time to get used to, I have to agree, but it's worth it in the end. It keeps your head clear, you know? But tell me; why are you here, Ms. Grace? I don't believe it's just to see me meditating."

"Oh yes, it's just that Liam was calling you. I think he likes you, although I'm not sure that's a good thing... He wants to chat with you before nightfall."

Liam, unlike Grace, was a more... solitary man. Introspective. Quiet and all the synonyms of a tired soul. Despite this, he seemed to take good care of his group, especially Grace. Perhaps even too much so.

"Ah, yes... Mr. Liam. He's quite the charming man, don't you agree?" The monk asked, now getting up without using his hands. He then handed his unused hands to Grace, who struggled to copy his movements.

"Ah, um... sure." She said, scratching her arm lightly. "He always knows what to say, I think."

But something told the man that Grace wasn't being entirely honest. Better not meddle in matters that don't concern me. He added inwardly as they continued walking through the monastery's green fields.

The complex of temples and small rooms was scattered across the uneven terrain. Although the place hadn't existed for very long, it had already become a kind of attraction for adventurers and hermits, who always stopped there with the kind monks. Which made sense, though; who wouldn't want a hot bath and home-cooked food before crossing a mountain or fighting an enemy guild? Besides, the view was spectacular. The place seemed to restore not only physical strength, but mental strength as well. They left there invigorated.

The recent group of adventurers who had arrived, led by Liam, was curious. Grace seemed to be a thief, while Melphor was some kind of squire. Clara and Richard were also part of the group. Despite having very different personalities from one another — which was unusual for a group that had been traveling for so long — the monks did not discriminate, and soon made the adventurers feel comfortable while helping, all within their capabilities.

"Brother Renji." Another man, similar in appearance to the one now heading to meet Liam, bowed. "Sister Grace. I hope everything is being catered to your needs."

"Yes, um..." Grace seemed to forget his name as she gestured with her hands.

"...Brother Loris." He completed for her with a smile, not a hint of feeling disrespected.

"Brother Loris!" She then grabbed his hands. "Of course. I apologize. But yes, everything is as perfect as it should be. And you don't need to fret over us staying another day. We'll be leaving first thing in the morning, okay?"

"That is no problem, Mrs. Grace." Loris replied. "Mr. Melphor and Mr. Richard are already sleeping since they had a long day. I recommend you do the same, ma'am."

"I surely will! But only after eating that delicious bowl of... what was it again?"

"Munchi."

"Yes! That was so good..." She then realized that Liam didn't like waiting. "Anyway, um, it was good to meet you. See you soon!"

Loris simply bowed and left.

"We will miss you, Mrs. Grace." Renji said after a moment of walking. "You bring an energy that was needed in this simple monastery. It almost reminds me of my younger days."

"You weren't born here?" She asked.

"I was, actually... a soldier." The man said, careful to chose his memories well. "I mean, perhaps soldier isn't the right word, but I had an... affinity with weapons, specially staffs. Violence was a part of my day to day. We still have some self defense classes led primarily by Brother Gaimon. But it isn't one tenth of the intensity I was used to... It teaches us balance and endurance."

"...Do you, um..." Grace found it difficult to continue without making him uncomfortable. "Do you miss it? Like, the old days?"

"If I miss having a broken limb every four months?" He asked rhetorically, chuckling. "No, I do not. One could say the heat of battle, and also pain, was good for the mind. I don't believe it, though. Not anymore."

The young adventurer simply nodded as they kept walking. In the distance, after a few minutes, they saw Liam muttering to himself.

"Well, we've arrived. You can go talk to him and I will, um..." Grace scratched her arm once again.

"Don't you want to participate in it, at least for a while?" Renji raised an eyebrow. "I'm sure he'd find it lovely."

"No!" She exclaimed, then lowered her voice. "I mean, no. I will go back, eat something and then go to sleep. Bye, Renji. Take care."

And although the monk found her reaction strange, to say the least, he didn't confront her about it, letting the energetic woman run through the green fields up to to the main temple.

In the distance, a bunch of the monastery members could be seen, catering to plants and animals as a few of them just lay on the grass, their orange robes offering a stark contrast to the monochromatic gray past that many shared.

"Mr. Liam." The man approached with a smile that could heal even the most broken of hearts. "You wanted to see me, yes?"

"Oh, hey Genji. Yeah, um, I—"

"Renji." The monk corrected.

"...What?"

"M-My name is Renji, Mr. Liam. Not Genji. A common mistake."

"Okay..." The man looked slightly annoyed as he went to a walk, monk promptly following him. "So, I wanted to talk to you about something, and I think you, out of everyone else in this fuc—functional, um, functional monastery would understand it the most. Since you were, you know..."

"Know what, sir?" The monk already knew what he was implying, but hoped it wasn't. "Perhaps about my... past?"

"Yes! Of course. You know what it's like to have power, right? You know what it takes to achieve victory, dignity and all that bullshit. To grab a woman in your arms and declare her yours..."

He then grinned at a figure who ran through the green fields.

The monk chuckled, visibly embarrassed. "I won't tell you what to do, Mr. Liam, but I can assure you that this path is a direct one to death and loneliness. Although having power and abdicating from it gave me a fresher perspective on what it means to live fully, you don't need to search for it for life to have meaning. If that's what you mean, of course."

"I..." Liam stopped for a second. Of course the monk wouldn't understand. Who would? Emblems were so new and there were so many possibilities waiting just beyond the horizon. He could be a king. Or perhaps a ruler. Or perhaps a God. It was all up to him.

"Have you ever heard of the "Emblem Theory"?" Liam asked. When Renji shook his head, he continued. "It's the new step for human evolution. Do you want to see it?"

"I don't think so, Liam." Renji said in a more serious tone. "And I don't think you want it either."

"Well, it doesn't matter." The phrase left the monk perplexed. Liam then patted his shoulder. "Nice talk. I'm going to bed. See ya!"

"...Help me! Please!" A desperate voice woke Renji up.

...What? He thought to himself, still groggy. The monk looked around, not noticing anything out of the ordinary. Was it all inside his head? Could this voice been from a dream? But soon this conclusion was discarded; the smell of smoke could be sniffed.

Renji got up in a jump, his eyes wide as he saw a bright glow coming from his door. The monk's room, as were all the others, was simple enough that it could drive a noble to madness. No paintings or posters, except manuscripts of religious importance plastered onto the walls as if they had always been there. The room's color was a monochromatic salmon red, but the low opacity made the room even duller. Artistically speaking, of course.

Swoosh! A gust of flames startled the monk, who proceeded to touch the door handle. "...I can't leave through here." He said to himself. But what about the voice? He now remembered as a woman's voice, a vaguely familiar. Could she have on the other side of the door? Although he made the right call, he still wondered if he should've just opened the door either way to see for himself. Being engulfed by the same flames that took that poor soul. Maybe then his life would've held meaning.

He shattered the window. Crack! Sounds of broken glass piercing the rough soles of his feet could be heard. It didn't affect Renji, however, who could endure much more. What he couldn't endure, even to the day he met Harry, was remembering the scenery he was now seeing.

"I beg you!" A bald man, wearing the same outfit as Renji's, cried out. "Why do you do this? Are you the devil's deeds or perhaps simply a man struck by madness. We can cure both! Just let us—"

Snap! The man's broken neck forcefully stopped his pleadings. Behind him, a man with a malicious smile appeared. His iris reflected a flame so pure but yet so evil. There was light in his eyes, but one that could've only come from darkness.

"Oh no..." Renji said to himself, not believing his eyes. "No. Please no... Grace. Brother Samsa! Sister Mary! Brother Loris! Can you hear me? Please, can you hear me?"

He walked to the monastery, now in shambles. The private temples being the only thing standing.

Someone must be here... He threw the pieces of wood aside. Renji only stopped when he saw a hand. Now viciously removing the rubble, he saw to which face that delicate hand belonged. Grace. Although he forced himself not to think about his past, Renji couldn't deny that he knew the look of a dead person. Lost. Only the dancing flame being reflected in those empty eyes.

"You!" Liam said. "Don't you see how beautiful this is? Call me Ogien now. This is only the beggining. The Fire Emblem still has a lot to teach me... how exciting!"

"...Liam?"

"It's Ogien, now!" He screamed as a flame seemed to appear out of nowhere. The scars in his hand seemed to just recently have been cauterized. "Actually, maybe I should just kill you right—"

And another monk ran in the green fields, capturing the attention of the maniac. "Hey! Come back!"

Should he attack him? Renji had been a fighter, and a good one at that. If it was just Liam, he definitely would've stopped his reign of terror right there and then, but this wasn't Liam. It was Ogien.

His mind now a fogged museum, Renji ran to the other side, making Ogien turn around. But it was too late, the monk was now nearly reaching the temple.

"Get out of there!" He pounded to the stone doors. "I'm going to fucking kill everyone and make you watch!" But the fire couldn't get in; the place was too fortified.

The only answer Ogien received was one filled with sobs and prayers as Renji knelt. His hands hugged the tears and sweat from his brows as a small statue oversaw it all. May no harm come to those who made it out. May death be swift and painless to those who succumbed to the flame. May the kingdom of heaven be fuller with their presence. May Liam burn in... He stopped himself, unwilling to let his raged mind take control of this supposed sacred time.

The monk opened his blurry eyes, only to realize the presence of dozens of candles. One of them was tall, just like Brother Loris. Another one was bright and the flame in its wick danced more than all of the others; this was Grace's candle.

They're all here. Renji gave a weak smile as he continued to cry nonstop. They're all here with me. The flame isn't the enemy, it's him, right? You're here with me, all of you. I'm not alone. I'll never be alone as long as I can feel the heat of your hearts.

Renji cried furiously as he held his stomach. His screams reverberated across the small room as if it was made for this moment.

This is where Renji became Vela.
