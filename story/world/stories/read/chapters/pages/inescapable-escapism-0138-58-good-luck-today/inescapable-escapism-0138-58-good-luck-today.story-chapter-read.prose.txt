The loud, thudding music was nothing more than a blur to me as I dozed lightly. I wasn’t particularly tired, but there was nothing else for me to do in either of my worlds. In reality, I was in the car with my mom, and at the Academy, I was in bed, trying to sleep.

It wasn’t going well. I was too awake, too filled with energy, and my mind refused to switch off. It rushed from topic to topic, making it impossible for me to start to drift off, and I knew why. It was because Seth was there. He was out of the induction wing, and I was no longer alone in the Academy.

I was so happy, so relieved. It was so nice to see him again and to have someone to talk to who I felt more comfortable around. Sure, I still got nervous and didn’t know what to say sometimes, but that was different. It was so much easier to be around him than it was to be around any of the other trainees that I’d met since I left the induction wing.

The urge to get up and go find him pulled at me, and I rolled over. I couldn’t do that. I knew I couldn’t. There wasn’t really anything I wanted to say to him in particular. I just wanted to talk about anything with someone I knew, but I could feel the words building up inside me, fighting to get out.

He was probably asleep. After dinner, we’d hung out for a little longer in the common room, but then he’d started yawning. He’d tried to say he wasn’t tired, but it was obvious he was. It seemed hard for him to keep his eyes open, so he probably fell into bed as soon as he got to his room and was fast asleep.

It would be selfish to wake him up. I knew that, and that was the main reason I hadn’t done it. He’d probably had a long day. It was overwhelming and intense to leave the induction wing, and he needed the rest, but that didn’t make it any less tempting.

I didn’t have to go find him, though. There were lots of other things I could do, I told myself. The gym was open all the time, and so was the library. I could have worked out or gone and gotten one of the many fascinating books that I wanted to read, but the idea of doing either of those things scared me.

There were cameras all through the Academy. That was the problem. If I left my room, the tutors would know that I hadn’t slept well, and I didn’t want them to. Especially when I had lessons in the morning. It wasn’t that I was scared they’d get annoyed at me. I knew they wouldn’t judge me or anything, but they might worry about me, and that felt so much worse.

Hannah had seemed worried before. I hadn’t even noticed that I’d been yawning a lot at breakfast the other day, but she did, and she asked me about it during our class. The expression on her face as she suggested I speak to the doctor if it was something I struggled with often made me so uncomfortable, and I was so glad I was the only trainee in the class because the thought of someone else overhearing the tutor’s worry made me feel even worse.

It was weird. I just didn’t know how to handle someone being worried about me. Judgement was different. I could handle that; I did all the time, but genuine concern was harder. It made me feel so guilty, and there was no need. I was fine. There was no reason for her or anyone else to worry about me.

But that meant I couldn’t leave my room, and I had nothing in there to distract myself with because I’d stupidly returned the book I’d borrowed to the library that morning. If only I’d waited a little longer, I could have at least read up on the common plants in the United Kingdom and how they could be utilised in survival situations.

I had a class on that. In botany, Surina was teaching us all about the different native plant species and how to use them, and it was so fascinating. I’d wanted to know more, but someone else had requested the book. I could have kept it for another day, but I’d decided not to, which was a mistake.

A sigh almost escaped my lips, but I managed to catch it just in time. That was good. I wasn’t sure which world I would have made the sound in. I was floating between the two, and if my mom had heard, she probably would have been annoyed. Although, it felt unlikely that she would have been able to hear me over her music. It was so loud.

I shuffled slightly in my seat, trying to get more comfortable, but it didn’t help. My butt was too numb. I’d been sitting on it for too long, and I longed to escape the car and move about, but there was nowhere for me to go in that world either.

The car was moving. I couldn’t exactly open the door and step outside. Mom was a slow driver, but I’d probably still die from hitting the road at that speed. Even if I somehow survived it, another car would probably finish me off.

Part of me was almost intrigued by the idea. My hand twitched around my phone, as if preparing to reach out and grab the door handle, but I forced myself to push the thought aside. I did not want to die, I told myself firmly. It was kind of tempting at times, and I didn’t exactly want to live, but that was different. I didn’t want to do anything to end my life.

But then, what was I meant to do? I didn’t want to go back to the Academy, where there was nothing for me to do. That would probably just make me more restless and frustrated than I already was, and that meant I either had to stay in reality and be bored or…

Temptation pulled at me, and I felt the dizzinesses at the back of my mind creeping closer. They still scared me. I didn’t know what awaited me in the other worlds, but I knew they could be filled with terrifying monsters. I didn’t want to face any of them, but the thought of staying in reality and doing nothing made me want to scream. It was unbearable, more painful than anything that had happened to me in any of the other worlds I’d visited.

Seemingly sensing that my resistance was waning, the worlds crowded even closer. My stomach grew tight, and my head began to spin, but I pulled back a little. I needed to be careful. I had to choose the place I was going to rather than just letting it pull me in because that would be a mistake.

Disappointment seemed to radiate towards me from the world that had almost ensnared me, but I ignored it as I examined the other options around me. I wanted somewhere I hadn’t been before. Somewhere that didn’t feel scary or dangerous but that could still be fun. I wasn’t sure what I’d count as fun, but I wanted… something.

There were so many options. Countless worlds pulled at me, trying to capture my attention, but none of them were right. They all felt too similar, too mundane, and that wasn’t what I was looking for. I wanted something completely different, a world unlike anywhere else I’d ever been.

I continued wading on, my head turning back and forth as I examined the choices before me, and finally, one caught my eye. There was something about it that felt strange, but not in a bad way. It didn’t make me recoil away or send a shiver down my spine. Instead, it just felt… different.

Before I could stop myself, I took a step towards that world. I could feel it before me. The world seemed to hover in front of me, encased in a bubble that I couldn’t quite penetrate. Emotions reached out to me, excitement and nervous anticipation, and I knew they were coming from that world, but I couldn’t work out what was happening there. It was concealed from me, held just out of reach, but I knew how to figure it out. If I gave in, if I allowed myself to fall through the barrier into the world, it would reveal itself to me.

Anxiety rose within my heart, making me hesitate for just a moment, but I couldn’t resist the curiosity that dragged me closer to the world that felt so new. It had happened before, I realised. There had been another place that had felt just as foreign to me.

The floating city. That had gripped me in a different way from the rest of the worlds, and I knew that should have made me more cautious. That world had ended badly. I’d been arrested, imprisoned on suspicion of wanting to bring ruin to the city, and my life had fallen apart. It had been ended. I was still alive, but I would spend the rest of my life in prison.

That should have been enough to make me pull back from the world I was standing in front of. I knew that it should have forced me to return to my reality and be content with it because, at least in that world, I was free.

I leant closer to the bubble before me, so close that voices began to float out towards me. They were muffled and indistinct. I couldn’t pick out what they were saying, but they sounded excited, and that was enough for me to push my fears aside and plunge forward into the dizziness.

My stomach clenched as nausea threatened to overpower me. My vision was blurred, and I couldn’t breathe. A rope had been tightened around my throat, but when I lifted my hand to touch it, I found nothing. It was just my collar. The hem of the shirt I was wearing came up too high. It pressed against the front of my neck and made it impossible for me to inhale properly.

“Don’t worry,” a voice said in a soothing tone. “I know it’s uncomfortable, but you’ll be able to take it off soon.”

“It’s just a formality,” another person told me. “A tradition.”

“Of course,” I muttered, my voice sounding strange to my ears.

There was something off about it. It was too high-pitched, and the accent was unlike anything I’d ever heard before. It matched the other two people who had spoken, but it was strangely lilting.

I lowered my hand, holding myself awkwardly as I tried to breathe properly. I wasn’t being choked, I told myself firmly. There was no need to panic. I’d worn clothing that was too tight around my neck before, like my school shirt. When the top button was done up, it always made me feel a little like I was being strangled, but I normally got used to the sensation. I just had to do the same in that world, and I’d be fine.

But where was I? My eyes hadn’t adjusted to the world properly yet, and I could barely see anything other than bright smears of colour as we walked onwards, but I ducked my head and blinked quickly. That usually helped. My vision was always blurry to start with when I first entered a world. I just had to wait a few more seconds, and it would go back to normal.

Impatience thrashed within me as my eyesight slowly cleared, and I couldn’t stop myself from looking up too early. My surroundings were still hazy, and dizziness reignited within me, causing me to stumble.

“Are you alright?” the man beside me asked as he caught my arm, stopping me from falling.

Embarrassment flared within me as I peered up into his warm brown eyes. He looked so concerned, and I couldn’t help the grimace that flitted across my face.

“Yes, sorry,” I replied, looking away again.

“It’s nothing to apologise for,” he said, his tone kind. “It’s normal to be nervous. It’s a big day.”

“But there’s nothing to worry about,” the other person said, seeming to sense the anxiety that started to rise within me. “You’ll be completely fine. I know it.”

I had no idea who the woman was, but her words did make me feel better. There was something so reassuring about both of them, and I was glad they were with me.

“You know, I cried on the way to my appointment,” the man admitted, taking me by surprise.

“What?” the woman replied, sounding just as taken aback as I felt. “You never told me that!”

The man laughed as he lifted his hand to rub the back of his neck.

“I know, I know. I was embarrassed. I mean, I know everyone cries sometimes. It’s a completely normal human reaction, but still.” He shrugged. “I was so worried about the results. I thought the doctor was going to tell me I was only fit for teaching.”

I laughed as he shuddered delicately, and the other woman joined in.

“Why would you think that?” she asked, still chuckling lightly.

“Ah, it sounds silly now, but my bios were teachers,” he explained. “And someone once told me I’d follow in their footsteps, but I couldn’t think of anything I wanted to do less.”

“Not that there’s anything against teaching if that’s what you feel drawn to,” the woman said, her tone ever so slightly pointed.

The man was quick to nod as we turned a corner.

“Of course not. Teaching is an extremely important and rewarding assignment,” he told me, his expression earnest. “It just wasn’t for me.”

I felt my lips pull into a smile as the woman laughed again.

“Then why did you think you’d be given teaching?” she asked. “You know they take your feelings into consideration. Everyone deserves to feel happy and fulfilled in as many areas as possible.”

“I know, I know. Our guardians told me the exact same thing,” he said. “I just couldn’t quite believe it until I received my assignment.”

I didn’t quite follow his words, but I smiled at him and looked away. That was the reaction it seemed like he was expecting, and I wanted to bombard him with more questions, but I bit my tongue. It would be foolish to ask anything when I still didn’t understand the world or know what he meant by his assignment.

He was probably talking about his job, I assumed. After all, teaching was a job, so it must have been linked to that somehow, but that didn’t make it any clearer for me. Why had he told me about that? Was it somehow linked to wherever I was going?

“Good luck, Daf!” someone called.

Somehow, I knew they were talking to me despite not even knowing my name in that world. My head snapped up, and my eyes widened as I finally caught sight of the world I was in.

It was incredible. We stood in the middle of a cavernous room, surrounded by greenery. If I didn’t know better, I would have assumed we were in a garden or a greenhouse; there were plants everywhere. Carefully planted trees lined the path, and flowers bloomed at their bases. They were brightly coloured, unnaturally so. The blossoms were almost neon, but the petals looked real.

They smelt real too. The air was heavily perfumed, and I couldn’t help but inhale, enjoying the scent as my gaze landed on the walls. Confusion washed over me as I stared at them, turning my head slightly to take it all in.

Most of the walls were silver in colour. They seemed to be made of metal, but there were a few panels that were different. Vibrant green and blue rectangles were dotted evenly along the walls, and something about them seemed familiar.

I’d seen them before, I realised. I was sure I had. One of the other worlds, the one I’d been imprisoned in, had them too. I could barely remember them, but I was almost certain I’d seen some on the outside of buildings. They provided the city with more oxygen or something.

Anxiety swept over me again. It was just a coincidence, I decided. Both worlds just happened to invent the same thing, and it didn’t mean anything. They weren’t connected in any way, and even if they were, it didn’t matter.

No one there knew who I was. They didn’t know I was the same girl who had been arrested on the floating island. We were completely different people, and we even had different names. I was called Clea there, and someone had just called me Daf.

I hadn’t answered them, I realised suddenly. I’d meant to, but then I’d gotten distracted, and that was so rude. They’d wished me luck, and I’d completely ignored them. My guardians were probably so disappointed in me.

My gaze roamed the room, searching for the person who’d spoken. It had only been a few seconds since then, I realised as my eyes found the smiling blonde woman who was kneeling on a cushion in front of one of the flowerbeds at the back of the room. It wasn’t too late for me to reply.

“Thank you,” I called back to her.

Her smile grew even wider, and she nodded before returning to her weeding. I couldn’t help but glance up at the woman beside me, concerned about how she’d react. Had she noticed my hesitation?

As soon as my eyes found her face, I knew I was wrong to worry. Rose didn’t get annoyed at me for things like that. It was so trivial, and they knew I was distracted. They’d probably just dismiss any perceived rudeness and assume it was because I was worried about my appointment.

I was worried about it. My mind spun as I tried to work out why or what the appointment even was, but the information was just out of my reach. The only thing I could recall was that I was nervous, but there was something else there too. I could feel an undercurrent of another emotion.

Excitement. I was excited about what was to come. I’d been looking forward to that day for… some amount of time; I wasn’t sure how long, but it was finally there. In a few minutes or a few hours, I couldn’t work out which, it would be over.

I wished I knew what I was so excited for, though. The curiosity burned within me, and I tried to access my memories again, but they remained stubbornly out of reach as we reached the thick metal doors at the end of the room.

They opened automatically as we approached, and I felt my eyebrows pull together. Where were we? What kind of place had doors like that? They looked almost like elevator doors, but they were thicker and more heavy-duty looking. I was almost certain they’d be impossible to force open once they closed, but why would wherever we were need anything like that?

I didn’t have an answer, but I felt my excitement fade slightly as we passed into the next corridor. It was less interesting than the room we’d been in before. There were no plants, but the lights were still too bright. It felt as though I’d just stepped outside from a dark room in the middle of summer, and it made my eyes begin to water.

Maybe I should have been used to it, though. The lights were always like that, I realised. They mimicked the day-night cycle of the sun to encourage a normal circadian rhythm. I’d been told that before. I could vaguely remember someone, maybe the woman walking next to me, telling me about that.

She’d brought me to one of the control rooms so I could learn more about them. The engineer or someone, whoever they were, had shown me how they worked and explained why it was so important, and I could remember how fascinating it had been at the time. Now, it just filled me with concern.

Why was it so important? The question buzzed through my mind as we reached the door at the end of the corridor. It also opened for us automatically, but I noticed something by that door. A number pad and a control panel. No one had used it, but it was there.

Did the doors lock? If I was by myself, if the people escorting me weren’t there, would the doors refuse to open? Would I be locked in the corridor, unable to escape, until someone came to find me?

Was that why the lights were so important? Were we locked in the building, whatever it was, and unable to go out into the sun? That would probably ruin our circadian rhythm. Someone in one of the worlds had told me the sunlight had an impact on that, so it made sense, but that didn’t quite feel right. I was missing something.

“Good luck today, Daf,” someone closer to my age told me as we rounded another corner.

“Thanks,” I replied distractedly.

They seemed too happy to be in prison. And there was no one with them. But maybe that was different. They could have been allowed to wander around unaccompanied. Maybe they were less dangerous than I was, so I had to have two people with me at all times.

But I could remember walking around alone. I’d done it before. It was how I normally got around the place. Either I was by myself or with friends. It was weird for me to be alone with the guardians.

“Not much further,” the man said as we came to a stop outside an elevator. “We just need to go up to the twenty-fourth level, and the doctor’s office is right there.”

“Great,” I muttered as my eyes caught sight of the map, and shock gripped me.
