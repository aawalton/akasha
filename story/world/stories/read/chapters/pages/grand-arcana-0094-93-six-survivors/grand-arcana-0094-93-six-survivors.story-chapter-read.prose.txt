I was the first to choose a reward card; I was also the only one. Harlequin didn’t want the knowledge of additional Arcana Houses to become public just yet. Everyone else got a healthy number of coins.

I examined the card. It was the third Grand Arcana in my possession. Even with a single skill active this could tremendously boost my combat abilities. Based on past observation alone, I could defeat most of the monsters I had encountered this run. The only reason that Maximillian had done so poorly in his fight was because he was freshly given the card. Just thinking how the siren might have used it terrified me to my core.

Go to the arena! Serp urged me. We must make up for lost treats.

“Yeah, yeah,” I muttered, sitting at the feast table.

An assortment of food was placed in six parts of the large table. When the group had been composed of twenty, there was hardly enough room for everyone to sit. Now, it looked deserted, just like the rest of the preparation hall. Six beds, six gambling tables—that was all that filled the space. It would have been appropriate for Harlequin to shrink the area at least until a new group was formed. Knowing him, he probably enjoyed making us feel smaller than we were.

I went up to the leaderboard hanging in the central chamber. My name was on top, written in bright gold letters. Eleonor was second. There were no scores, no explanation why the names were arranged the way they were, but everyone knew.

“You going to use the arena?” Marcus went up to me.

I spent three full seconds looking at him before responding.

“Maybe later.” There was something I needed to clear up before I got back to boosting my stats. “You go for it.”

“The distance has increased again.”

“What?” I asked, unsure of what he meant.

“The distance between you and everyone else.” His face remained expressionless as he spoke. “You pulled ahead with your third Grand Arcana. I’ll catch up to you, though.”

Everything for him continued to be a competition. The man had no emotions, no particular desires, no insane tendencies. His entire existence revolved around becoming the best and destroying all that stood in his way or annoyed him.

“I’m sure you’ll try.” I had no desire to let anyone surpass me, either. “Are you thinking of making a pact?”

“I don’t know enough, but likely no. Why trust any deity if I can become one?”

The comment forced me to smile. That sounded just like him. Just because there was no way of becoming a deity wasn’t a reason that he’d give up on it.

I was about to add something more when Cathy came up to me. The glow of divinity still emanated from her, yet her expression was dark.

Looking at her, Marcus gave both of us a nod, then walked off. I couldn’t help but wonder what his intuition had told him.

“Congratulations,” the woman said. “Again.”

“I was lucky on this one,” I said, and I actually meant it. “Could have gone either way. If he had killed me, any of you might have picked up the prize.”

“That’s probably true, but it wasn’t luck. It’s never luck with you.”

You’d be surprised.

“I hear you’ve become very close with Bea,” the woman said.

Hearing such comments from her was unusual, to say the least. In the past run there wasn’t a man alive that would dare get within ten feet of her. It was said that anyone who tried got a taste of pain they had never imagined. That’s how her most famous nickname came to be.

“Ele has no idea what she’s talking about.” I shook my head.

“She wasn’t the one who said anything. My goddess warned me about you.”

Diana had done that? That was peculiar and concerning.

“She told me to keep my distance.”

“Cathy, it’s all—”

“And I agree with her.” She looked around the hall.

Only Reginald was within view, calmly engaging in a gambling session with a yellow marionette. Based on the low stakes, he was doing it more for fun than anything else.

“Did Marcus tell you?” the woman asked.

“By the looks of it, no…”

“We’ll be joining a different group from the next trial.”

I just stood there. Part of me expected it might happen. The two of them had vanished during my previous run, so there was no surprise that they might leave the group this one as well. And still, I would have hoped that we’d stay together. Having me grow alongside them was a divergence from the past that I would have liked to experience. I knew better than anyone just how strong both of them would become. Also, they were among the few people I could trust. As nice as it was to be with Sparta again, the fear always lingered at the back of my mind.

“I didn’t know there was an option to choose,” I said, aiming to avoid a more serious topic of discussion. There had been way too many surprises in the last few hours.

“Let’s just say that promises were made.”

So, Diana had gotten personally involved. That could only mean that she held Cathy in very high regard. Curious how Marcus had gotten Harlequin to agree.

I would lie if I said I didn’t feel bittersweet about the change. In theory, we’d still see each other now and again. It was common for groups to merge for certain trials, though there was no telling when that might happen. More importantly, there was never a guarantee that we’d be on the same side. From this point on, fighting against participants would be almost as common as fighting against monsters. My weakness during the previous run had shielded me from that, though I had gotten a taste and I definitely didn’t like it.

“Thanks for telling me.” I looked into her eyes. “Don’t worry. You’ll do fine. I saw it in a dream.”

That made her chuckle. By now, she probably knew the whole dream excuse was a load of crap.

“That’s not the only reason.” She held her hand in front of her, palm up. Two bronze cards appeared. “The Drummer and the Knave,” she said. “I know they’re not much, but I was told that you might find them useful.”

Curious who had told her that? Although common, the cards remained part of the Major Arcana. I would have very much preferred if she had given me the Drunkard, though. With that, my Chalice set would have been complete.

“I can’t say that it covers everything you’ve done for me, but it’s a start.”

“It’s enough,” I took the cards, knowing that there was no way she’d accept that. Part of me hoped that she would. I knew too well what it was like to live with debt.

“I’m going to the arena.” She turned around as if we had completed a transaction. “See you around,” Cathy walked away, her back to me.

I looked at the cards. Three in one day. That was another reason for me to feel happy, but I couldn’t shake the hollow feeling inside.

The burden of the strong. Finally, I had gotten to experience it myself. Up until now I had a goal set in stone and a plan to achieve it. Thanks to my second youth and the knowledge gained through half a lifetime of completing trials, I had been convinced that I knew most, if not all, secrets of the Grand Arcana. The last two trials had shown me that I had barely scratched the surface. My plans were pretty much worthless, and my goal was only a small part of something far larger. Right this moment, I felt as if I had been pulled into the Grand Arcana for the very first time, only now I could actually see what was going on.

You alright, old man? Serp’s voice rang in my mind. That’s two you let get treats instead of us.

“You’ll get your fill,” I said.

There would be time for me to challenge the arena. I wouldn’t be giving Serp the boost spheres, though. Not immediately, at least. In order to stand up to the siren, Carl, and anyone else in their group, I had to boost my stats, and that meant getting as many Arcana cores as possible. Furthermore, I needed to boost my other Grand Arcana cards.

Aware that I still had a pain debt to pay off, I went to get some sleep. The agony woke me up exactly on schedule. All my gains during the special trial proved incapable of reducing the waves of torture even by one bit.

“It’s fine,” I heard Sparta in-between going in and out of consciousness. “I’m with you.”

I felt as if my insides were set on fire. The pain was so intense that I wanted to rip out my own ribs for the mere chance that part of it might subside. I probably had tried, for when I woke up, part of my shirt had been torn up. There was no sign of Sparta or anyone else. I didn’t blame them.

Two of Chalice. I used the card’s healing power on my clothes. All the signs of tears abruptly disappeared.

“What happened?” I asked as I stood up.

Just the usual, Serp said in a bored tone. You caused another mess. How long will you keep this up?

“Until the debt is paid.” Hopefully, there weren’t too many installments left. I had no idea how much the merchant valued our deal, but it was starting to seem that he had added a lot of interest to the experience.

Sure. Are we going to the arena now?

“Soon.” I lay back in what seemed to be my bed and closed my eyes.

Come on! You’re sleeping again?

“Close,” I uttered. Sleeper, I thought.

I lay there, waiting. For several seconds, nothing happened. Then, I felt something licking my hand.

I opened my eyes. A Dalmatian had climbed onto the bed.

“Hello, Pup,” I patted the dream creature. “Have you been keeping guard?”

The Dalmatian let out an affirming bark.

Gently pushing it away, I sat up. It appeared that I was in the preparation hall, although I knew that to be an illusion. To my surprise, there was no sign of Hypnos or Eleonor. Given their increased interest in me, I had assumed they wouldn’t miss an opportunity for another “chat.”

“Ready for a walk, Serp?” I asked.

You can be a piece of shit, the saber grumbled. He could tell this was a dream just as I did.

Ignoring him, I went to the gym.

Everything was just as it had been in reality. The walls were solid, the dumbbells had weight... There was a good chance that monsters would appear if I set foot in the arena. All that was fake, created out of dream mist.

I opened my status box and skimmed through the contents of my inventory. The black ankh was still there, or so I could assume. Both the name and description remained garbled beyond recognition. It wasn’t the reason I had come here, though. Reaching in, I took out a silver necklace.

The item had been given to me as a reward by the same deity that had given me the clothes. So far, she had been consistently helping me from the very beginning.

Observer. I looked closely at the item.

NECKLACE OF PASSAGE

(Rarity – Restricted)

Grants passage to unseen places.

I still had no idea what exactly the item would grant. There was a non-zero chance that it might end up being a curse. However, I had to find out.

Let’s see what you really do. I put it on.

A doorway of mist appeared in the nearby wall. Beyond it lay nothing but greyness.

“Don’t I get any explanation?” I asked.

There was no answer.

Clearly, there was only one way to find out.
