Chapter 81 - Caged

The forested arena had settled into a strange, deceptive sort of silence. The type that didn’t belong in a battlefield. It was the kind of quiet that came only when everything was already over.

Kaelin crouched high in the canopy, her fingers gripping coarse bark slick with moisture. The branches here were thick and gnarled, twisted by Professor Reed’s magic into an unnatural lattice overhead. Rainwater dripped slowly from leaf to leaf, tapping like a clock counting down seconds she didn’t have.

Her lungs burned. She forced herself to breathe evenly, silently. Below her, the Colosseum floor stretched out in a sprawling maze of ridges and roots, shuddering occasionally as distant duels shook the terrain. The occasional shouts, the boom of something striking hard from far away.

But all of that faded into the background.

Because she’d finally found him.

Zephyr stood alone in a clearing carved by combat. The ground around him was a tapestry of devastation: long trenches where spells had ripped through soil, splintered trees leaning in unnatural angles, scorch marks still smoking. Despite it all, he looked untouched, unbothered—like the chaos belonged to him.

Light filtered down through the canopy, catching in his hair, turning it a cool, burnished silver. His stance was relaxed but undeniably ready, the way a predator would seem lazy just before it strikes. One hand rested loosely at his side; the other hovered near his hip, fingers twitching every now and then with residual magic.

Kaelin pressed herself against the bark, watching over him.

He looks… calm. Does this not even faze him? We’re meant to be fighting with everything we got and hes just down there taking it slow!

She remembered the threads he wielded. All  three of his time, nature and mind. If she attacked him recklessly, she’d lose instantly. Zephyr wasn’t just good; he was designed for this. Every element, every obstacle, only seemed to make him stronger. A bead of sweat trickled down her temple.

I need a plan. I need… something.

Dropping down to the edge of a drop off which sat nearby to Zephyr, she canned the terrain looking for anything which may be of help.

A fallen log lay nearby, thick and covered in moss. Vines coiled around higher branches like twisted ropes. The air was still, humming with lingering magic. Below, the ridge she perched on sloped downward into a narrow ravine that could give her an escape route. That is if she moved quickly enough.

She shifted slightly, adjusting her balance, and—

A branch snapped.

The tiny crack echoed far too loudly in the silence.

Kaelin froze, her breath locking in her throat.

Below, Zephyr stilled completely.

He lifted his head, eyes sweeping the canopy, his expression slipping from neutral into something far more focused.

The aura around him condensed in an instant, like a storm tightening around its center. The air shimmered faintly, refracting the light like heat rising from stone.

And then, his gaze locked directly onto her.

Kaelin’s stomach dropped.

The look on his face wasn’t surprise. It wasn’t confusion. It was something far worse: Pleasure. He seemed glad to have finally found her, as if this whole time he’d only been sat still because he was waiting for Kaelin to come to him.

Slowly, he straightened, dusting soil from his hands as though this were all part of a routine.

“Kaelin,” he called out, his voice steady and infuriatingly calm. “Finally given up on hiding?”

His tone wasn’t mocking, not exactly. But there was something in it, something that made her want to punch him.

She pushed herself up on the ledge, jaw tight. “I wasn’t hiding. I was… thinking.”

“Oh?” His lips twitched into a slight smile. “Then I suppose you thought that you might have a chance here.”

A faint ripple moved through the clearing. He was activating something underneath her feet. The ground itself responded to his will. The leaves overhead fluttered without wind. Even the shadows seemed to sharpen around him, bending subtly toward their owner.

Kaelin swallowed, but it wasn’t from fear.

Zephyr took a step forward causing Kaelin to flinch, rocketing backward until she hit a tree.

Alright, maybe it was.

As Zephyr raised his arms, he took over the Professors area, and moulded the zone to his own whims. A smaller, sumo-sized, arena popped up from the earth. A ring surrounding the two of them, barricading her in.

Zephyr wasn’t just any student.

He was the student.

The youngest tri-affinity wielder in centuries. The prodigy who fought like the world bent slightly in his favour. The boy she wanted to be friends with, to fight, and to run from all at once.

And now she had to face him. So I guess that’s one ticked off the list.

Finally she spoke up in response to his challenging words, “I did. Now all that’s left is to prove myself right.”

Zephyr took another step forward. Slow and controlled, threading his weight through the earth like he belonged to it. His eyes never left hers. There was anger in them. at what? She didn’t know.

“Whenever you’re ready,” he said.

Kaelin steadied her breathing.

The branch beneath her groaned softly, leaves trembling. Her hands tingled, threads stirring restlessly in her veins, begging to be used.

She wasn’t ready.

She’d never be ready.

But this moment was unavoidable.

She stepped toward the edge of the branch, muscles coiled like wire, heart pounding so loudly she wondered if he could hear it.

Zephyr lifted his chin slightly, waiting.

Fuck. I can’t believe this is really happening.

Cold iron pressed against Kana’s cheek as she exhaled slowly, breath fogging against the steel bars.

The cage was cramped, barely large enough for her to sit upright, and the metal frame dug into her spine every time she shifted. It was nothing fancy, no magic anywhere near. Just thick, metal bars, fitted into a stone base.

Kana groaned and pushed herself upright, gripping the bars with both hands. The metal was slick with condensation. She was likely underground judging by the stuffy air carrying the damp bite of old stone and mildew. Somewhere in the distance, water dripped steadily, echoing down the corridor like a slow, mocking metronome, mimicking the sound of her life ticking slowly away.

Her skull throbbed.

She touched the back of her head and winced. A knot. No blood. He’d struck her cleanly—hard enough to knock her out, but soft enough not to kill.

“Perfect,” she muttered, fingers curling tightly. “Absolute psycho.”

Blinking through the dizziness, she scanned what little she could see. The corridor stretched into darkness in both directions, lit only by a handful of guttering torches. Their flames sputtered with each draft of air, casting the walls in uneven, restless light.

She inhaled sharply.

Where was Aria?

Kana gritted her teeth and forced her memory into focus. The last few seconds before she blacked out replayed in broken snapshots:

Aric’s voice.

The torches dying one by one.

That laugh, echoing down the hall.

A sharp pain in her skull.

Then nothing.

She swallowed, the dryness of her throat scraping like dust.

Aria had been there. Directly across from her. Aria was the one Aric should’ve gone for first. The one more dangerous, more experienced one. She’d have been more of a threat.

So why was Kana alone.

Her fingers tightened on the bars.

Unless… Aric never took Aria.

Kana forced her breathing to steady as she sank back onto her knees, palms flat against the cold stone.

Think. Don’t panic, just think.

She closed her eyes and reached inward, toward the thin psychic thread she’d planted on Aria days ago. A small, discreet mark resting atop the skin behind her neck, allowing for direct contact with her brain.

It wasn’t meant for communication. It wasn’t meant for control. It was a last-resort measure: a tether she could burn through for a few precious moments of borrowed senses.

It would cost her. It already was—her pulse fluttered unevenly, her temples throbbing under the strain—but she shoved more of herself into the spell.

The world lurched.

Her vision split, doubling, then reformed into a second perspective layered over her own: Aria’s. Blurred. Tilted sideways. Staring at cracked stone from floor level.

Kana’s breath hitched.

She’s on the ground. But where…

Aria groaned softly, she was disoriented. Kana felt it echo in her borrowed bones, a weak ripple of pain down a spine that wasn’t hers. No movement. No dragging sensation. No ropes binding limbs. No distant ceiling overhead.

Just the hallway. The same hallway. Aria right where she’d fallen.

Kana’s eyes snapped open. The second vision clung to her like an afterimage.

“He didn’t take her,” she whispered, the words scraping out. “But why did he take me instead of Aria…”

The realization dropped like a stone into her gut.

Aric could have carried them both, but he decided to take only one.

He wanted to leave one of us so that we would lead Kaelin straight to him. It makes so much sense. he feared that if he left me out there I might realise it was a trap, just like I have now.

A trap set with care.

Argh! Aria’s just gonna prance around in the palms of his hands with no idea whats going on.

“He wants me scared,” Kana muttered bitterly.

Her fingers curled around the steel bars, the cold biting into her skin. For a moment she simply let the fury fill her chest, hot and sharp.

“Aric, you incredible bastard.”

She forced herself to look again—really look—at her surroundings. The cage was crude but sturdy, welded together with an ugly confidence. The bars at least 4 centimetres thick. The hinges were old, but not loose enough to exploit. Rust speckled the metal, but it wasn’t weakened at all. The lock was simple, mechanical, and on the outside, just out of reach.

There was no magic to try and get around.  Just iron and stone.

Kana swallowed hard. The psychic tether trembled as it grew smaller, Aria’s breathing flickering in and out of her awareness. She could barely hold the link. Any more, and she risked collapsing.

But she’d seen enough.

Aria wasn’t dead and Kana had been taken. She was bait.

Kana’s jaw tightened. “Overplayed your hand this time, Aric.”

She braced both hands against the bars, pressing her weight forward—not expecting them to give, but needing the grounding. The metal stayed firm. Her muscles burned.

She let out a long, shaking breath.

“Okay,” she murmured. “No magic. No shortcuts. Just me.”

Her eyes found the lock again. Old and mechanical.

But it was made well, no way that was gonna break.

Kana drew in another breath, slower this time, letting it settle into her ribs like resolve crystallizing.

“You want me afraid,” she whispered into the stale, dripping air. “But you should’ve left me no options.”

Her fingers tightened on the bars.

“Because I swear to the Gods, Aric, once I’m out of this cage, I’m going to ruin your night.”

She dropped to her knees, inspected the hinges, and began working away at them.

It wasn’t much, but it was a start.
