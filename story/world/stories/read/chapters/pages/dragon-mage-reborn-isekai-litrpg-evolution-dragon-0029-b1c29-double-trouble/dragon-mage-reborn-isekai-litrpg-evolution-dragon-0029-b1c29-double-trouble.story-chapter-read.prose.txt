I was half awake as the sound of my mom’s voice turned into a rugged 40 year old man’s persistent yelling. Still dazed, I forced my body to cooperate with the sensation of crawling from unconsciousness. The feeling from my toes to my pudgy fingers were coming back to me, and before I knew it, I was looking up into a dungeon ceiling through half-lidded, hazy eyes.

‘Ughh…. Why does it feel like I got hit by a truck?’

“Hey, you all right?” Nolan asked as he continued to shake my back. “Can you get up?”

I groaned, my baby dragon scales scraping against the ground as I squirmed. My stiff limbs were still weak under me, but I managed to push myself up.

“Can you walk?”

‘I think so,’ I answered, wobbling forward. The last bit of the poison was fading from my bones and I was quickly returning to normal.

“You took one hell of a hit from those wasps,” he said with a relieved smile. “That stinger went right through your invisible white armor. A critical hit. Glad you’re back, short fry.”

I scoffed. ‘For your information, it was one wasp!’ I corrected, and then my eyes softened on him when I said, ‘Thanks for the rescue, Nolan.’

“Hey, what are partners for? I’m just glad that I gave you the antidote in time.”

‘I forgot you were carrying one around.’

“They are sometimes hard to come by, but you’ll learn there are a few must-haves you absolutely need to have in your tool shed. Dungeon crawling 101.”

I frowned. ‘I have one, but we were so caught up in saving our hides, that I had no chance to take it.’

He smiled. “Yeah, sometimes that happens. That’s why it’s best to travel in a party. Fortunately for you, you have a professional crawler watching your six.”

I smirked. ‘Well, aren’t I lucky?’

He got up from crouching and took a look around. “While we managed to escape the swarm, we wound up in a fucking nest,” he said, and that was when I actually took note of our surroundings. The clearing was filled from wall to wall with slimy egg sacs, each carrying a translucent larva in its encasement. I looked up to see how far it went, the narrowed ceiling disappearing into darkness with specks of glowing baby bugs inside of them.

Oh, this was just perfect! Baby creepy crawlies!

“Hey, by the way, what happened back there?” he asked. “You couldn’t summon your thrall?”

I sighed. ‘I have no idea. It’s not like the first time I tried summoning him, either.’

“Hm, interesting,” he said, and nothing more. Heck, I thought he would have had some idea as to why I came up short back there.

I slapped my tail on the dirt and grunted. Already irritated with this dungeon, I plotted to roast these eggs so we could find an exit, but it looked like Nolan had read my mind and immediately dismissed the idea. “We shouldn’t disturb the nest.”

‘And why not?!’ I groaned. ‘May I remind you, those giant evil wasps almost killed me!’

“Because as soon as we crack one open, I bet the whole damn colony is going to come after us again. And this time, you might not be so lucky.”

I pouted childishly. He was right, but I didn’t want to admit it. The last thing we wanted to do was cause a nasty chain reaction that would leave us severely outnumbered.

‘Remind me again what tier this dungeon is?’

He snickered. “Welcome to Etheryn.”

‘So what do you suggest we do? Comb through the valley of gloppy mucus sacs of larva for another way out?’

“I hope you’re not squeamish, too.”

I sighed.

We both made a point to be as cautious as possible, taking delicate steps around the eggs while I occasionally paused to shake off goop. I was impatient as I brushed my nose along the eggs, trying to see if I could sense something different in the air with my sharper sense of smell. Meanwhile Nolan was over there playing babysitter to those darn bugs, carefully plucking them one by one from the walls. At this rate, it was going to take eons for us to find a way out of this cluttered nest—mama hive was busy this breeding season.

Regardless, I wasn’t as locked in as I should have been on this dungeon run. That dream I had… it was still on my mind. It was a memory from when I was young, a memory I’d long forgotten. For someone like me to forget was unheard of. Seriously, my memory was like an elephant’s. So when I notice these empty pockets of information, I become frustrated. On that fair Sunday morning when Boson had paid us a visit, I couldn’t remember what had happened after I heard Dad walk to his office, until now….

What did I do with this information? From the chanting words to the magical levitation, I was at a serious loss. Was my Dad some type of real life magician?

Could he actually be THE Dr. Strange?

“You’re quiet, for a change,” Nolan said from across the nest, breaking my thought. “More than usual.”

‘I’m fine.’

“I hope those monsters didn’t shake you up too much. Thinking about abandoning the dungeon?”

I looked up at him curiously. ‘Could we?’

“No.” he said flatly and I deadpanned him.

‘So why offer?!’

“I didn’t. I was just asking a question.”

I sighed inwardly. ‘It’s nothing. I don’t want to leave. We made it this far, and there is no point in leaving now. Besides, I have all the reason in the world to stay with that prize at the finish line. So let’s focus on beating this web mistress and getting me to my next evo stage.’

He snickered. “Sounds like a plan.”

I sped up on searching for our escape route, wanting to leave this nasty cesspool of bugs behind for good. We were finally making progress. My keen sense of smell caught on something odd—moist air, mixed with the faint scent of something sweet. I followed it like a dog after a bone and called out to Nolan when I found what looked like a small opening in the ground.

It looks like we were going down another floor again.

‘Over here!’

He made his way over to me and crouched down to get a better look at the opening. It was narrow, maybe enough for me to squeeze in, but nothing more.

“There’s no way in hell I’m fitting down this hole,” he insisted, and then I proceeded to scratch along the ground where it was soft. The ground crumbled bit by bit. Little by little it gave way, creating a larger cavity.

When the opening was wide enough, I giggled playfully. ‘After you!’ I tweeted.

Nolan flashed me a sly grin as he peered down into the newly enlarged opening.

“It looks like a long drop.” He reached for one of the gravelly pebbles on the ground and dropped it into the dark cavity. The trip was short, according to my hearing. “Not bad, actually.”

‘So what are ya waitin’ for, princess? Dive in!’ I insisted impatiently. ‘The sooner we leave the nest, the sooner we can fight the spider que—’

-Squeelccch-

I froze.

“What the hell was that?” Nolan retorted nervously. I was afraid to move, feeling my hind foot suddenly covered in warm gooey sac juice.

“You didn’t….”

I gulped hard and slowly turned my head. My sharp claw had punctured a sac when I stepped back to give Nolan space to jump into the cavity. Like a cracked egg, the sac oozed all over the ground with a little baby spider upside down, its tiny legs flailing in the air helplessly.

‘Oh no….’ On cue, two pairs of huge glossy red eyes peered down on me from the ceiling,

“Move it!” Nolan shouted as he rushed to go down the hole. He leapt and landed with a grunt, his voice echoing below with a, “C’mon—hurry!”

I was already midair, diving after him just as I heard a war cry of a scream! It shook the entire nest, the vibration making me stumble a step when I landed. Suddenly, a pair of mandibles slammed through the opening we’d just escaped from, my internal banshee scream making Nolan wince as we escaped.

I turned my head over my shoulder as the monster suspended there with its chompers wedged in the hole, and then I [Inspect] it:

[Greater Insectoid, Level: 35]

Unfortunately for us, it wasn’t alone. I heard more angry chitters echoing from over our heads. Before I could process where it was coming from, a huge cannonball dropped in between us! Dust and debris flew everywhere, forcing Nolan and I on opposite sides of the tunnel. I shook my head and squinted, trying to cough up the heavy thick air. The impact was huge, and when I glanced up, I noticed the cannonball unraveling into an enormous bug.

I [Inspect] him, and just as I expected, this monster’s level wasn’t too far off from the first one:

[Greater Insectoid, Level: 38]

‘Crap baskets.’

The first one broke free from the ceiling of the cavern. That menacing look in his compact eyes was glued onto me. The both of them were easily seven foot tall, black, armored, beetle-looking humanoid monsters. Their shells were hard, like plate mail, with long pointed singular horns and terrifyingly huge mandibles. There were two of us down here, but for some reason, both of those spike heads were looking down at me!

I jerked my head back and forth, my body positioned to maneuver around them as soon as they sprung. I couldn’t see Nolan through the dense suspended debris coating the air, but I could sense him. And as soon as he called out to me, those two humanoid beetles sprang into action.

Simultaneously, they spread their wings and charged at me. One came in with his head spike on the offensive, and the other with his huge snapping jaws. While their flight put me at a disadvantage, their position on opposite sides of the tunnel helped me. It didn’t take much planning to route my escape as I rolled to the side as a horn came crashing down into the ground. They clashed like two idiots and I managed to buy myself enough time to counter their attack. I opened my mouth wide, a brilliant amber glow brightening up the entire passageway. Their eyes seemed unmoved as my fire breath engulfed both of them. A direct hit, but those two made no effort to escape as I poured more heat into the furnace. When I finally tapered off, I realized that they had no reason to flee the scene—those bastards were flame proof!

“Freya, move!” Nolan barked from behind me. Without a second thought, I did as I was told, expecting him to follow up my attack with one of his own. As soon as I stepped out of his line of fire, I heard a loud gunshot round, Scarlet feeding those bugs the business. Magically imbued pellets ripped through the air, slamming against each of the beetles with force. More sleeper shots? That was what it looked like from where I was hiding. The round looked similar to the one Nolan had fired to subdue Dusty, my adorable and humongous dire wolf.

The huge beetles staggered for a moment, but quickly bounced back. ‘They’re tanks!’ I shouted inwardly.

“Well, that’s interesting,” Nolan mused.

‘We can’t take them on. Not here. We are at a disadvantage with this limited space.’ We were too confined in this tunnel and restricted to two dimensions of movement. We’d have to not only fight smart but careful if we wanted to keep out of each other’s way and survive the wrath of these greater bugs.

“Right, but they trump us in speed,” Nolan said in a near whisper. “Escaping won’t be easy.”

He was right, but I’d be damned if I let them turn us into bug chow!

So I summoned [Cyclone Shield] to give us some much needed space to escape the fray. When it burst, the AOE was enough to knock those bastards off their feet for a short while.

‘I’ll buy us some time!’ I told Nolan.

He promptly replied with a snarky, “And what? Let you have all of the fun?”

‘Seriously, Nolan! Run while I have them distracted!’

“Poor choice in time to be a hero, kid. Combined, they are over three times your level. You might have been able to hold your own with those combustible killer wasps, but these two are a different circus show all together.”

I got that Nolan was way more experienced than I was, but I wasn’t trying to make a statement—or, maybe I was? Sure, being poisoned wasn’t fun, and almost dying wasn’t fun, either. Both experiences left me feeling helpless. But more than that, I hated being a burden.

It left a bad taste in my mouth. I understood that we were partners, and that it was our shared job to look after each other, but this was only our second dungeon, and already I was being rescued. Was I being too hard on myself? Maybe. But that was just my personality.

Every second we debated was a second wasted, so I decided to stop arguing. The beetle twins were already back on their feet and charging straight into my second [Cyclone Shield]. Right after, Nolan and I shared a glance, the both of us stubborn in our decisions. I understood then that he wasn’t going to leave me in this tunnel alone.

He scoffed. “You’re a real pain in my ass, you know that?”

I smiled. ‘I like you too, Nolan.’

“All right. If we can’t outrun them, then maybe we can soften them up a bit.”

‘I’m on it.’

My jowls opened nice and wide for a series of quick succession [Astral Blasts] straight out of the oven. My innate breath weapon was still low-leveled, so the impact was less fruitful than I wanted it to be. Even so, I managed to temporarily stun them between shots, and shave off a few HP while I was at it.

For every inch they tried to move, I served them my wrath. My stamina was taking a hit, but I told myself that I could handle it, until Nolan wanted to try a different approach.

“Hey, short fry, I know you want to keep this an all-hands in affair, but I need you to head south.”

‘What, why?’ I quickly asked. He hadn’t been trying to attack them with me, which left me wondering what plan he had in mind to deal with these relentless monsters.

“Just trust me. I have an idea.”

‘You want me to leave you behind?’

He looked down at me warmly now with a proud smirk on his face. “Do you trust me or not?”

Nolan had been calling the shots on this dungeon run, so I reluctantly placed my faith in him.

‘All right, fine!’

I didn’t hesitate to make a break for it. My stubby legs pumped hard and fast under me, and in no time, I could hear those insectoids gaining ground again.

I sprinted ahead and cut a quick glance over my shoulder just in time to see Nolan pivot and face the beetles again. Giant pincers chased the gunslinger down, Nolan weaving through those two monsters with some effort. He was lucky they had speed and not agility, but still, Nolan had used some type of item to facilitate between the beasts in order for him to keep up. It was subtle, but I could tell something had been boosted. Shots ripped through the air behind me, echoing off the walls of the tunnel. As I turned back to focus on sprinting down this never-ending path, my ears picked up on something peculiar. Nolan’s rounds—they were missing? They were striking all around the charging beetles, but not hitting them directly.

The hell was he up to? I turned my head over my shoulder again. He was switching between blade and shots, but those shots were strays. The shockwaves from the miss-fires created a fine mist of debris that quickly became thick and blinding, making it harder for me to see. I didn’t get it. Was his aim off? There was no way. Since I’d met him, he’s been a perfect shot!

Suddenly, I heard popping noises firing off one by one. I blinked when I realized what was happening–pellet bombs! Small explosions detonated at the point of impact where Nolan’s mana-infused rounds hit, causing more dust to kick up between us and the insects. Dust quickly turned into rubble, and soon, rubble turned into heaping piles of falling rock. He was causing a cave in!

‘Nolan!’ I panicked. This was a risky move. If he didn’t make it out of there on time, he’d be buried alive along with those two tanks.

“Keep going!” he shouted. His voice was still clear and strong, even between the roaring clatter of collapsing stone. The tunnel’s ceiling continued to fall faster, making the gap between us grow larger until I lost sight of him.

‘Nolan!’ I stopped and shouted, but there was no response.
