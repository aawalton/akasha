Colors muted by the darkness flew by in streaks as Oskar gained speed. He kept a careful eye on his brother’s icon in his HUB to make sure he was still within range and slowed a little to circle the area when he reached the altitude he wanted. Everyone was, more or less, where he expected them to be, but there was a nervous energy in the air. Not magical energy; none of his senses warned him of anything. It was just a feeling.

It reminded him of those nights out, or even on watch, with his Marines when everyone seemed just a little more prone to shenanigans. You’d look up, and sure enough, full moon.

No moons here, though, at least not that I know of.

The PUB’s silence was as likely to be a tease as it was confirmation, so instead of overthinking it, Oskar circled a little further out, reveling in the feeling of flying. At this point in his power, nearing peak C Rank physical stats, and solidly in B Rank Sora, he could now hover in the sky if he wanted. Just like the dream where he was in the storm. Absentmindedly, he reached up and felt the scruff of beard he’d grown since he’d arrived here. Not near as long as in his dream, but it made him wonder if he would be alive when, and if, the Great Storm came.

Great Storm, Great Tree… they’re as bad as me at naming stuff.

// No, man. You tried to call Touwon’s kurangs boomerukis. Boomerukis? //

Are you sure? I thought that one was your idea.

// Nope. Not falling for it. //

Oskar never felt more at peace than he did in the sky, especially now that the Great Tree had so thickly infused the air with living Sora. The growth on the ground was full of living Talau, and the most interesting thing about the whole situation was the people, the Kobolds, even Penny and Sara, were slowly filling with living Rakiyu. He had questions of course, but as his Seed Forge slowly filled to max capacity, he was more focused on what he could do with that power than to waste time asking questions he had no way to answer.

The Tree was changing the world and the surrounding people. His leg was the first, but broken things just became better, things like armor, bowls, tools. Never weapons, though.

Touwon was torn between fascination that it was happening and wanting to fix things himself, and finally, in the last few days and after repairing everyone’s weapons, settled on creating and inventing instead. He made toys for the kids; little helicopter things they could spin and chase around, and gliders they threw off the dunes that would fly almost too far to retrieve.

He made shovels and other tools, and then, departing from his usual mechanical tendencies, started working on a sappy liquid that could almost—but not quite yet—make something akin to cement out of the sand. That idea sprung from him spending a full day digging in the sand leading down to the Oasis after losing a tiny screw. Oskar learned a few new Kobold words that day, but he wasn’t going to try them out with Fox around. She had a really severe glare and would tell him he was a bad influence on the cubs.

But what’s the point of being an uncle figure if you can’t be a bad influence? No one remembers a well-behaved uncle.

Below, little moved that he could see, but as always, there was life. Two adult pangolors were resting under the sand, just covered up enough that they could shift to breathe as needed and hide again. A scorpion and an asp were both stalking a cricket the size of a clown shoe, and if the scorpion wasn’t careful, the much bigger asp would end up with a much bigger meal. Birds and bats were in the sky, a little more than usual, but that wasn’t what caught his attention initially.

Far beneath and a little behind him, the asp froze and then turned around, darting away with no attempt at stealth. A moment later, the pangolors came out of the sand and were tearing off across the desert as well, fleeing from something Oskar couldn’t yet see or feel. He shot off an “Orange Alert” to Erik, and his PUB sent Gambits to the others to let them know everyone should wake and prepare for a potential problem. That’s when it hit—that feeling… that energy in the air dialed up to eleven—and he worked his mind to figure out exactly what he was feeling. It was life, more than usual, but more than that. He slowed in the air and really focused on it and finally realized what it was. He’d not been strong enough to sense it before, but it was rot. The early stage of Dessication.

As best he could guess, when something was infected—however that happened—it always started with rot. If something was powerful enough, it could fight for a long time without fully losing itself. For example, the Gryphus Vulture was likely infected for a very long time. He didn’t make the connection immediately, because it seemed like it happened so quickly with Valla.

But Valla was different. She gave herself over. She didn’t fight it at all.

// Maybe that’s why she wasn’t totally mindless, just crazy. //

Either way, something is coming, and its most likely shadow creatures of some sort.

// I’ll update the others. Should we go back? We should go back, right? //

I promise I won’t attack anything, but I’m good in the air, and if we go back now, we won’t know what to warn them is coming.

// Annoyingly valid point, be careful. //

He went higher and scanned everything he could see in the distance. Far to the east, he saw massive dark spots dotting the sand in the distance, and he tried to scan to figure out what they might be. Whatever they were, they were moving fast. His eyes widened as he watched one of the dark spots bust through a dune, barely slowing.

Oskar didn’t dare try to get closer, but watched as they drew nearer, prodding the PUB to let him know when it finally got a scan off.

// I can tell you there’s over 40 of them, but they’re too far to make out exactly what they are. Oh wait… yeah, that’s a deinogator. Well, a shadow deinogator. And yeah, that’s bad. I can’t get a threat level on them but take my word for it. //

Oskar started to ask what a deinogator was when, with a surge of magic below he could feel even from the air, one of the shadow creatures he hadn’t seen burst through a dune, having branched from the main group chasing after the scorpion he’d seen earlier.

The sound and speed of the shadow creature was far too much for the scorpion, and the clack of the creature’s massive jaws slamming shut sounded like marbles being crushed in a machine, but… wet?

The shape was similar to an earth crocodile, but the rounded snout looked more like an alligator.

Okay, is this what happened to alligators in this world? Crocs went humanoid and grew smart, and alligators got huge? I wish I knew more about this kind of stuff, because evolutional divergence is crazy. Are the non-shadow versions this fast? What abilities do they have?

// Yes, they’re just as quick, but that’s almost all I got. No idea on abilities, just big, fast, and dangerous. Now pay attention, we can talk later. //

The one closest to him had finished with the big scorpion, and was already back up to its full, scary speed. The thing’s size, though, was really what made it so terrifying.

It was close to 40 feet long from snout to tail, and every bit of its attention was now on Oskar as it spun sideways, flicking its tail like a bullwhip.

There was a loud snap as it flung sand at him from the ground. Oskar wasn’t too worried, though. The creature was firing upward from a few hundred yards away, and he always kept his Resonant Aura up for protection in the air. The top-winds should send the sand attack wide, but he moved into the wind just in case to further ensure he was out of range of the slow attack.

Yep, the other creatures are not too far behind. It’s past time to get out of here and warn the others.

Suddenly, though, the outline around the sand went red, and the PUB sent a warning flash as the loose-flung sand picked up a burst of speed and followed his movement.

Desperately, he retreated as he pulled Sora in a swirl around him to intercept the attack. That partially worked, but he could hear the whip crack of something breaking the sound-barrier, which immediately sent his adrenaline into overdrive as some of the creature’s attack whipped past his face, leaving stinging lines. One hit his prosthetic, spinning him in the air, and he grunted as another hit his back with a heavy thump as he spun. Thankfully, his Ward aura activated, blunting some of the damage. A few more whistled by as he felt himself falling for a moment in pain and confusion.

He caught himself in the air and fled, thankfully holding onto his spear, for what good it did him at the moment.

That wasn’t sand the giant alligator flung at him. They had been shadow locusts. And they were heading straight for the Great Tree.

Oskar tried to cover himself in Sora, to hide in the sky, but another of the creatures flung locusts his way, and he poured on the speed to get distance, hoping that whatever they used to home-in on him had a range limit. He’d either been too slow or hadn’t gone far enough, and the dark blob of insect shifting in air to chase, gaining speed.

Since they’re locusts, I might be able to use my War aura and shred them before they get to me, but without Ward active, I don’t want to risk taking another hit.

Almost every living thing had cleared out of the area except him, and he cursed himself for thinking himself safe in the sky. The asp was being chased far below by the giant alligator thing that first shot at him, but the shadow creature had been stunned after flinging the locusts and was just now picking up speed again.

To the north, he saw another dark blob of locusts chasing after something else in the sky he couldn’t see at first until it flapped its wings. There was a glint of light that reflected from the creature’s wings that wouldn’t have happened if it had been a shadow creature. A quick scan revealed nothing. It was close enough, which meant it had some kind of frustrating ability to nullify the scan.

Abruptly, it tilted toward him with terrifying speed, but its own shadow locusts were still in pursuit and gaining speed. As it drew closer, Oskar got a better look as he tried to angle away, pulling his spear up between them, but his eyes widened when he saw Sora shimmering across the dark, metallic-looking feathers of a vulture.

Oskar’s eyes couldn’t have widened further as the creature—now between him and both blobs of locusts—tucked its head and spread its wings, coming to a nearly sudden stop in the air.

Whip cracks split the air as the locust swarms hit the vulture’s wings back-to-back with a series of splats. The bird flapped once, hard, like a swordsman flinging his foe’s blood from his blade.

After a short moment, the big vulture took off behind Oskar and headed west at an angle that would take him just outside the area of the Collective, leaving the druid even more confused than when he got bitten by the ribbon snakes.

Not too confused to flee, though.

He took off, full speed, back toward the Oasis, blaming himself for not making initial contact with the several, wandering Collectives of Crocs who were now in the direct line of danger.

We should have set up at least a warning system for each other or something. If we make it through this, there’s no more stalling, that’s for sure. I might learn my lessons slow, but I learn them well.

Pushing down his frustration, he flew, sending ahead a warning with everything he could relay:

Incoming from the east, 41 massive shadow alligators, and I’m not using that description lightly.

They shoot shadow locust swarms from their tails but seem to be stunned afterward.

Might be they can only do it once, but don’t count on it. There is, at least, a cooldown on the ability, though.

Shadow locusts are slow, but when they get closer, they get crazy fast and will track your attempts to dodge. Best to use shields.

They are heading straight for the tree. This looks like a direct attack from Desiccation, or someone infected by it.

Red alert, all hands awake and ready. Warn the Clinkers and the two groups of Crocs in their paths if you can.
