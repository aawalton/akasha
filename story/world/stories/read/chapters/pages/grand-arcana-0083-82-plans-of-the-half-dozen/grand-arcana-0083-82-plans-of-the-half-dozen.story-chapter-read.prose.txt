“You should have woken me up.” Sparta looked at me with a frown. “I would have obtained more answers.”

Probably she was right. If nothing else, her speed would have been quite suitable for such a situation. Then again, Eleonor also had a Thief card, and that didn’t prove particularly useful.

“Next time.” I stood behind her, holding her shoulders with both hands. “I promise.”

“If there’s a next time.” She pulled away. “Do you even trust the siren?”

“No,” I admitted. “Not particularly. She wants to be set free, I know that much.”

“Don’t we all? I still—”

Sparta was interrupted by the sound of barking. Pup was standing a few steps away, agitated about something. I was just about to ask what, when the sound of growling drowned his barks. A circle of mist had surrounded me. On it, hundreds of dream beast faces emerged and vanished, like a never-ending wave.

“Stay calm,” I whispered, drawing my divine blades.

There was no trace of Sparta or the rest of my room. All was replaced by the devouring circle. I could tell it wanted to tear me to shreds, but also that it couldn’t.

Who’s behind you? I thought.

The moment I did, the growling stopped. The dream beast heads kept glaring at me, eager to tear me limb from limb. I heard another sound, similar to a whistle. Hearing it, the creatures lost their form, vanishing into the mist.

I woke up.

I was at the inn again. The experience in the well had exhausted me to my limit, causing me to fall asleep the moment I had returned. I vaguely remembered collapsing on the bed with my clothes still on. Most of them were gone now. The only things remaining were the shirt and my necklace.

“You’re up,” Sparta noted, eating at the small table. “You must have been through a lot.”

Yeah. I stirred. Some habits didn’t change. I remembered she would do the same in the previous run. Of course, in that case she’d carry me out of the trial half-conscious as well. On the other hand, I had also done the same for her. That was why even the deities considered us a great pair, and also why I doubted she could betray me.

“Did I say anything?” I sat up.

“Just that you’ll explain everything in a moment.” She looked at me with a smile. “That was nine hours ago.”

Nine hours? I must have really been in a pathetic state. Magic had a way of sapping my strength faster than I assumed. I’d have to keep that in mind when fighting in the future.

I explained the situation, skipping a few bits in the process. Saying that I had made Eleonor strip might have been taken the wrong way. Also, the less people knew about the siren, the better. The important thing was that we had our target.

I was aware of five other participants in the city so far. Coincidentally, the only people I hadn’t found were from my own group. Ideally, it would have been better if all of us had been present for the planning. For that reason, it was decided that we gather everyone we could the following day. Sparta wanted us to take a while and think things over, but I couldn’t afford it. My divine blades were always hungry, and I didn’t want them devouring any of my cards.

Of all the participants in the city, I only managed to find four. Reginald had been the easiest to spot. As usual, he spent his days near the barracks, swapping stories with the city guards. He must have been a soldier back on Earth, for they seemed to take a liking to him even if he was a mercenary.

Eleonor had also shown up, of course. Acting as if nothing had happened, she remained quiet in the corner like a depressive shadow.

As for the remaining two participants, they had set off cache hunting, according to Sparta.

“None of us can take out an aelf noble on our own,” I stated the obvious, using the Lover card to boost my charm special. “Since it’s a special trial, everyone will get a reward at the end.” Although only one could claim the Grand Arcana.

“Are you sure he’s a Grand Arcana holder?” Reginald asked.

“Yes. Eleonor was with me when we came across his handiwork.” I glanced at her.

My comment made her slightly more uncomfortable than I had expected.

“It’s the Stoneweaver, so we’ll also be fighting lots of statues in addition to the aelves.”

“Stoneweaver and aelf nobles.” The old man nodded several times. “Your rule-master set us up for a fatal trial.”

“I’ll convince the duke to take the jungle elves’ side,” Eleonor said. “I just need one night.”

Everyone stared at her.

“I’ll enter his dreams,” she quickly added. “Him and the others who have a say.”

The thought sent chills down my spine. Even without Hypnos, she had the power to puppet the entire nobility of the town. That would definitely be useful. I pitied the locals. In a day, all of them would be eager for a war they hadn’t chosen.

“I’ll get the elves to join in,” Reginald said. “Might take a few days, but—”

“It has to be tomorrow,” I said firmly.

“Why?”

There was no good answer to the question. My reason was purely selfish. No one in their right mind would agree to put their life on the line just so that I could save a card. I couldn’t afford to lose it, though.

“He’s aware of us,” I lied. “The longer we take, the longer he’ll have to prepare. If we go tomorrow, we might catch him off guard.”

“That’s a lot of ifs, kid. Recklessness doesn’t always bring victory.”

“It’s helped me so far.”

“Even if we achieve all that, what chances do you think the four of us have? We should wait for the rest and do a proper attack. Catching a Grand Arcana holder unprepared isn’t worth starting with five participants less.”

Damn you, old man! He was annoyingly skilled.

I wouldn’t be surprised if he were a war veteran who had gone through these things. Given he was sponsored by a war deity, it made sense that he had shown exceptional combat and strategy skill during his life on Earth. In different circumstances, I might have relied on him to come up with the plan, only tweaking it here and there based on my personal knowledge.

“I know how they’ll act,” I said. In this case, it was even the truth. “After the initial clash, they’ll send a wing of dragon riders to cause some chaos and disrupt the front formations. They’ll be targeting the humans for greatest effect. That’s where we come in.”

“Dragon riding?” Reginald mused. This suggestion seemed to intrigue him. Leave it to a military man to see the benefit of air power. Also, I knew he couldn’t resist due to his Beastmaster card.

“They’ll be nobles, so they’ll have rider cards,” I said. “All we need to do is claim them.”

What I didn’t elaborate on was how difficult that might be in practice. In my previous run, I had been through trials, the goal of which had been to kill a single dragon rider. In the hierarchy of power, they were regarded as mid-level nobles and had the skills to match. Even the dragons had been less dangerous than the riders themselves. While given such a majestic name, they were only slightly larger than a paraglider. They remained a lethal tool, especially with their ability to breathe fire or spit acid.

“A wing is composed of nine units?” the old man asked.

“Twelve. They usually fly in groups of three,” I specified. “Greater impact that way.”

“How do you know that?” Sparta crossed her arms.

“The siren told me,” I lied. “She’s got a lot riding on this, so I trust her. If she lied… as Reg said, it won’t matter. The Stoneweaver will take us out one at a time.”

Silence filled the small room. Everyone knew I was right, just as I knew that charging the aelves with just four participants was near suicide. If it wasn’t for Harlequin’s restrictions, I would have expected everyone’s eyes to flash gold. This was by far the most challenging fight we had experienced this run so far, and comfortably one in the top twenty of my previous ones. Fighting demigods or grand Arcana holders was bad enough. In this case, both were one and the same.

“We won’t be four,” Eleonor broke the silence.

No sooner had she said it than I heard the inn door downstairs open. Two people entered and deliberately made their way to the second floor.

“Milady,” I heard the innkeeper say as the pair passed by him.

Instinctively, I drew my divine blades. There was a saying in the Grand Arcana: there are no good surprises. I had no idea what deal Eleonor had made and with whom, but I didn’t like it.

The steps steadily approached. Reginald and Sparta moved away from the door, ready to join in should the need arise. Then there was a knock.

“It’s open,” I said.

No immediate reaction followed. I couldn’t hear weapons being drawn, though that didn’t guarantee anything.

The handle squeaked as it moved down. Then the door opened, revealing a city guard.

“Marcus?” I asked.

The red mist had transformed him a lot more than anyone else. If I didn’t know better, I’d swear he was a knight and a member of the lower nobility. Despite the slight signs of travel and wear, the armor was in peak condition, complementing the bright green and blue uniform he was wearing.

Following him was a noble lady dressed in a red and purple combination of lace and silk, embroidered with obscene amounts of silver thread. Normally, such a person would feel at home in a palace. Yet, for some reason, the lady was Cathy.

“Starting without us?” Marcus asked in the most casual fashion possible.

As he entered the room, Reginald tensed up. It was obvious that the two didn’t like each other. Thankfully, they were pragmatic enough to leave their issues for later.

“Did you two start as nobles?” I asked, still dealing with the cultural shock.

“No,” he said in a firm voice. “It was the fastest way to get here.” He looked at everyone else in turn. “What’s the plan?”

“How do you know there is a plan?” Sparta challenged.

“I told them.” Eleonor flexed her skills. When it came to me or Sparta, the goth seemed to have a lot to prove. “The moment we started the trial.”

“How nice of you.” Despite her smile, Sparta’s voice could curdle milk. “Told anyone else, by chance?”

“There’s no one else. The rest are dead.”

Dead? That was unexpected. Carl had lasted a lot longer in the past. I could see him getting into trouble, though surely he wouldn’t just croak like that. Then again, he had consistently relied on others to save his skin.

“I guess there’s no reason to wait,” I took advantage of the situation. “All of us are here. Ele and Reg will set things in motion, so tomorrow we start. Then…” I paused.

What could I possibly say? Everyone’s skills were impressive enough to be noticed by deities and rule-makers alike, yet they remained newbies as far as the Grand Arcana was concerned. They had no idea what awaited us, and they couldn’t use the advice and assistance of their deities. Odds were that half of them would die.

“Then we see this through,” I added.

Not the best speech, but good enough. With that, the small meeting dissolved. Eleonor and Reginald left the inn, setting out on their tasks. Cathy and Marcus used a bit of money and their newly found influence to kick someone out of another room in the inn and went there for the night. I would have liked to spend some time with them, catching up, but I equally wanted to spend the night with Sparta. There was no guarantee she would survive this. Strictly speaking, there was no guarantee I would either.

At dawn the following morning, the sound of trumpets filled the air. After months of hesitation, the local duke had finally made his decision. In a matter of hours, we were officially going to war.
