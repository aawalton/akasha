Chapter II.XXIX (2.29)

“I don’t understand why we’re going through this whole ordeal,” Holly said. “The Selcouth Sable likely would have shipped us out to Porcelain Island with a lot less effort.”

“True. We probably wouldn’t need to hide in a smuggler closet to escape the island,” Mitsuko relented. Their accommodations were far worse in the crammed room with dubious imports than their room on the previous ship. “But in the past loops The Selcouth Sable always gets attacked by crusts.”

“And you think they have some sort of deal with The Terror? Or do you just think they’ll be better at fighting them off?”

“Both. I figured it would be worth experimenting with for a loop.”

Admittedly, Mitsuko had forgotten that she would need to hide below deck as the Sailor’s Guild meticulously checked The Terror as it was a local ship with different permits than The Selcouth Sable. It was annoying but manageable.

Sterling lounged on a crate full of lackluster enchanted gear. Holly read through an atlas she’d purchased on Mauve Island that Mitsuko had seen her read half a dozen times now. Mitsuko mindlessly practiced breaking and fixing magical trinkets.

Time passed. The sailors brought them food, a lumpy porridge, and they were given more freedom after half a day of travel. Mitsuko considered going to see the one armed cook, Sett, but decided against visiting with him. She’d had her fill of this ship’s politics in previous loops. All they needed to do was transport her to Porcelain Island.

“What are you reading?” the ship’s cabin boy, Wes, peered over her shoulder. Recalling his schedule from previous loops, Mitsuko assumed he must be on break for the next hour.

“A Brief History of Hon’s Prismatic Relations. I picked it up in town before we left.” Mitsuko shifted the book so the boy could better see. His shoulders slumped in disappointment when he saw the page of pure text.

“Any pictures?”

“Not many.” Mitsuko flipped through the pages until she came across a sketch of a Hon warship. It was one of three images in the book. “Can you not read the Universal Script?”

“I know the basics,” Wes mumbled. “Just not the hard characters.”

Mitsuko snapped the book closed and leaned in. “You know what? I don’t think this book will be all that useful anyway. Not nearly as useful as knowing how to tie a proper bowline knot or square lashing. Can you show me?”

His face lit up. “Oh sure! Do you know how to tie a sheet bend? That one is essential.”

Mitsuko did know but she lied.

The boy got out some twine from a pocket and demonstrated.

As Wes excitedly explained, Mitsuko noticed a one-armed cook lumber by. Her heart picked up as they met eyes across the deck. An ever so slight smirk twitched onto Sett’s lips. Then he passed by to speak to another sailor.

“Do you like your shipmates?” Mitsuko asked the cabinboy.

Wes paused, his fingers halfway through a loop. “Yes. They’re good.”

“Just good? No other thoughts?” Mitsuko pressed.

“I’ve only been here a little while now and I’m the newest. I guess… I wish there was someone else my age.”

“Have you ever seen them kill someone?”

Wes dropped the twine. “N—no! We’re not murderers!”

“Where are you going with this?” Sterling asked. “Are you attempting to distress the boy? What happened to wanting a safe, quiet journey?”

Sterling had a point. But Sett put her on edge. She’d seen him murder his captain in her first loop and had never shaken that sight. The image of Sett dead at Rodrick’s feet with his heart torn out also was fresh in her memory.

Mitsuko dropped the topic and continued to listen to Wes instruct her on sailor’s knots. His chipper tone had dropped away and she noted that his hands shook slightly as he weaved the rope through the loop.

“What do you know about the crusts?” she asked. If he was already unsettled, then she might as well mine him for information while he was off-balance. Probably not as effective a strategy as if she instead put him at ease, but she thought this might work too.

“The… crusts?” Wes accidentally wrapped the rope around the standing part. Two more steps and he blinked and cursed as he realized his mistake.

Mitsuko held back a chuckle. The boy swearing was incredibly cute. He said the words like a child fumbling around in his father’s boots.

“Yes. I’ve seen a few now since arriving on the Prismatic Archipelago,” Mitsuko replied. “They keep attacking me. That’s why I was asking about whether or not your crew can fight.”

A weight left Wes and he smiled, clearly relieved. “Oh, that’s all? Don’t worry about the crusts! Captain Alina struck a deal with them. All the ships here know the crusts.”

“Huh. Interesting.” Mitsuko had figured out this much already. “Where do they come from?”

Wes shrugged. “I dunno. I think some live in underwater caves beneath Mauve.”

“Underwater caves,” Mitsuko repeated. “Interesting.”

Wes proffered her a strand of rope for her to attempt the knot with. Mitsuko’s hands twisted, deftly weaving the rope together while she considered the fact a faction of crusts lived beneath Crocus City.

“How… I didn’t teach you that.”

Mitsuko looked down at her hands and silently told herself off. She’d gone a step beyond Wes’s instructions and had added a flourish intended to assist the knot’s ease to untie.

“Did I make a mistake?” she asked, lamely trying to play dumb.

“Not exactly.” Wes frowned. More than anything else she’d said or done, this slip up seemed to upset him. A minute later, he made an excuse about needing to scrub pots below deck and scurried away.

Mitsuko leaned back and sighed. She cast Mend on the rope and it untied itself. She needn't even bother with additional tricks to untie rope.

Getting into a comfortable position in the sun, she retreated to her readings for the next few hours. The book shop purchased this book from was woefully lacking in comparison to the vast library available to her on Amber Island, but this is what she currently had on hand and so she figured she might as well read what she could.

The book was a fisherman’s guide to the nearby seas. After catching sight of that coral reef inside Azure Island’s library vault, she decided trying to figure out what sorts of fishes made this water their home might prove useful.

A shadow crossed over her and Mitsuko looked up from a page on snails.

“You’ll burn sitting out like this,” Holly said. “Plus, what’s with the shorts? Your bare legs are getting stares from the sailors.”

“I’ll Mend any damage,” Mitsuko said, dismissing that concern. “And who’s to say I don’t want stares from the sailors?”

Holly rolled her eyes then slumped down next to her. She peered over Mitsuko’s shoulder at the open page.

“Sea life?”

“Hm,” Mitsuko muttered.

“This looks almost like a summoner’s bestiary. Any particular reason why you’ve taken an interest in marine life? Did you unlock more than temporal powers?”

“No. And I’m pretty sure this is designed for fishermen, not summoners. If you look in this corner you can see each creature is ranked by their edibility.”

“So… you’re thinking of picking up fishing as a hobby?”

“One of the islands I need to visit is underwater. I figured I might as well study up on what I’ll find down there.”

“Underwater?” Holly’s face scrunched up. “Mitsuko, you can barely even swim. How are you expecting to get to an underwater island?”

“Probably a potion?” That was the only solution she’d come up with so far. “There’s an apothecary back on Mauve. I browsed through their stock at the start of this loop.”

“They had waterbreathing potions for sale?”

“Well… not exactly.” Mitsuko tugged a lock of her hair and glanced up at the dome overhead. “But they had the right ingredients. I figured I could experiment and figure out a recipe over the next few loops.”

“Your brewing might be marginably better than your swimming,” Holly said. “Still, last I checked you were no witch.”

“No… witch,” Mitsuko repeated.

“If only you knew a witch to assist your efforts,” Sterling said from his perch on a nearby cargo crate, basking in the sun.

“Holly, what if I told you I knew of a witch on Mauve? Do you think it would be safe for me to approach her?”

“I suppose that largely depends on the witch. Plus, you know them better than I do. Why bother asking me?”

“Just looking for a second opinion,” Mitsuko muttered.

“A third opinion,” Sterling chimed in. “You know the proper spell to protect your mind from tampering. There is very little risk involved so long as you keep your meetings brief.”

Mitsuko grumbled while Holly said basically the same thing. As long as it was safe, there seemed to be very little risk. Still, Mitsuko didn’t like the idea of working alongside witches. Especially not one who both locked her in a dungeon years ago and now helped her father locate her on the other side of the world. Actions like that tended to make her a bit antagonistic.

“Fine. Next loop I’ll talk to Fuku.”

“Glad to hear you’re finally lis—aaahh!” Sterling’s comment devolved into a yowl as something slammed into The Terror’s hull. He tumbled over off his crate and narrowly dodged out from under a sailor’s heavy boot as he stumbled.

Mitsuko was immediately alert. Gripping the gunwale, she lifted herself up and created a sword. A quick scan of her surroundings revealed the sea around her looked much as it always did, the islands far off in the distance and the dome blocking out the horizon beyond. But she was willing to bet loops that this was the same location as the leviathan’s past appearances.

Still, no hulking sea monster emerged. The ship even steadied itself underfoot after the initial impact. She leaned over the gunwales and her eyes narrowed. She couldn’t see under the surface but she would swear that the hull sagged deeper in the sea than usual. As if something was weighing them down.

Mitsuko crossed the deck, her familiar blade of ice still gripped and offering her comfort. Captain Alina was bellowing down into the water.

Mitsuko’s heart skipped a beat as she noticed Sett easing his way forward. Her mind flashed back to the moment when he’d toppled her overboard in her very first loop. That wouldn’t be allowed this time. She needed less complications, not more.

“Are you blind?” Captain Alina shouted at the water. “Look at the paint, you dimwitted oafs!”

Mitsuko stepped between the captain and the cook, using her body as a barrier to protect the captain.

“The paint clearly shows we’ve paid the proper toll!” the captain continued to rant. She spared no attention to either Mitsuko or Sett.

The cook’s eyes narrowed and he stepped forward to stand centimeters away from Mitsuko’s sword. A head taller than her, her eyes were barely higher than his burly bare chest. The edge of her blade pressed against the skin.

“Leave,” she told him flatly.

He raised an eyebrow and smirked. “Brave girl. Fiery.”

Mitsuko hated how her knees went weak and her heart beat picked up at his words. Thankfully, he took a step back and retreated.

With a sigh of relief, Mitsuko lowered her sword and turned back to the captain.

She blinked in surprise. There was now a figure in the water. A crust’s head peered up at the bellowing captain. This one looked lobster shaped, with an orange carapace and big eyes. Mitsuko thought the crust looked vaguely familiar. She might have killed it in a past loop.

“Turnover in leadership,” the crust’s warbling voice explained. “New toll put in place as of yesterday.”

“A. New. Toll.” Now Captain Alina’s voice had dropped both in volume and octave. It was far more dangerous than her earlier shouts.

“Yes.” The crust splashed about and looked over its shoulder. “Not anything to worry about. We just need to assure your loyalty. Then this side of the archipelago will be open to you again.”

“You need to assure my loyalty,” Captain Alina repeated, the edge in her voice sharp enough to cut steel.

Again, the crust shifted around in the water. Mitsuko didn’t know the species’ common body language or tells, but even she could tell it looked nervous.

“Yes. All we want is a sacrifice. Our new leader has asked for the heart of a passenger. Preferably someone new to the ship. One life, and we’ll go on our way.”

Mitsuko tensed as she examined both the crust to the captain. To her credit, Captain Alina didn’t even glance in her direction.

That comment about surrendering the heart of a new passenger was undoubtedly intended to target the Prismatic Spiral’s Champion. Azure Island’s guardian had been pulling strings and adding complications into her life since her very first timeloop.

“What of the other side of the archipelago?” Captain Alina asked.

“The other… oh, well, that’s not under the ruler’s jurisdiction.” The crust paddled its body a bit closer. It smiled, its cartilage stretching under its carapace as it looked up at Captain Alina. “So we have a deal?”

Mitsuko tensed and prepared herself for a fight.

“Now.”

The crust blinked and cocked its head at Captain Alina’s response. “Now wha—”

A spear of ice rocketed from above pierced the crust’s open mouth.

“Ghaaaah!” it gurgled, bulging eyes flicking around.

Mitsuko whirled around to see up the ship’s elementalist up in the crow’s nest.

“Nobody changes the deal on me,” Captain Alina hissed down at her skewered foe. “If your new king wants to renegotiate, then he can tell me himself.”

The crust died. Unfortunately, the leviathan did not.
