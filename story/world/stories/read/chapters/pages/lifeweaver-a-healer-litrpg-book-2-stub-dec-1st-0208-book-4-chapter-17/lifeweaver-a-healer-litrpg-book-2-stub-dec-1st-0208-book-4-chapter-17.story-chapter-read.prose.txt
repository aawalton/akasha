Upon reaching Curtis in the middle of what appeared to be a fierce battle alone against all remaining Singers, Luke wasn't sure what he was even seeing. Undulating darkness spread across the road, like a pond of night. Small pieces broke away from it, and Curtis, hovering in mid-air, used them as weapons to attack Singers while using those same pieces to block other attacks. The Singers hurled spears, shot arrows, and cast spells across the darkness to strike at his limp, unnaturally pale body, but each of them was intercepted by darkness, eating every arrow and drowning all spells. When that darkness touched a Singer, Luke's stomach twisted. Rather than thump them into submission, like Curtis had done before, his attacks splattered against his opponents before growing.

"It's eating them," Luke whispered.

That's the best way he could describe it. The darkness enveloped Singers, and then they were just gone, and that same darkness moved back to the ever-reaching pool in the middle of the road. When an attack pushed through the darkness or avoided it, they just passed through Curtis as if he were a ghost. It was spooky as hell, and it was in the middle of the day still. Meeting Curtis on a dark street corner at night would be like something out of a horror story.

When Luke turned up, what little resistance the Singers still held on to bled away, and they turned and fled. Darkness chased them, eating away at them. One fell, screamed, but then managed to pull free. When he stood back up, the leg that had been enveloped by darkness was no longer there. The wound wasn't bleeding or anything. Actually, there was no wound, just a stump. Whatever a Hollow was, it was much scarier than a Reaver. That much was certain.

Once the last of the Singers had made it out of reach, the wide pool of darkness retracted, and Curtis dropped into it, submerging himself. Only then did he wake up, and he did so with a guttural scream that made Smudge whimper and return to Luke's Stable. Darkness poured into Curtis. With it, color returned. When it was all gone, Luke rushed up to his friend, who now lay unmoving in the middle of the freeway.

"Curtis?" he asked, trying to get a sense of the state of him.

It was weird, to say the least. As far as Luke could tell, Curtis didn't have vital energy, nor mana or stamina. His physical body was the same as ever, but the metaphysical side was anything but human. Dark mana? Or maybe just darkness? Everything had been replaced by darkness. While one might think that similar to the sliver of void mana in Luke, they were anything but. Putting it into words or even concepts would be difficult, but Curtis's darkness was still something, while the void was nothing. That was the best and only way Luke could describe it.

"Hey," Curtis said, his eyes open. "Did I get them?"

Luke looked down at his friend, still on the ground. "Almost all of them, but the wedge was too late. The others couldn't deal with that many Singers."

Curtis was silent for a moment, then asked, "Who?"

"Mas. Nash a little later."

"Fuck."

Luke nodded. "Fuck, indeed."

"What about the paladin Singer? The ones up the hill?"

"Killed them," Luke said.

Curtis sat up and turned to face him, arms resting over his knees. "You're stronger. Again."

Luke sat down to join him. "So are you."

"I am." The silence hung heavy between them for a moment before Curtis spoke the words Luke had been thinking. "We have to keep some distance from them, or more will get hurt."

"I know," Luke said, nodding. "There are a few who can keep up, like Martin, but it's too dangerous for most of them."

"We should quit the guild."

Luke caught Curtis's gaze. "No. Isolation isn't the answer. They still need us, and we'll need them. We just won't bring them to things like this. That was a mistake. Milla was right. Again."

"What now?" Curtis asked.

Luke had given the question some thought. "We've got about six months before the next step of integration. When that happens, the Singers can't be here anymore."

"What are you talking about?"

"Didn't you read the message?"

Curtis's eyes glazed over as he scanned his interface. Once he returned to himself, he nodded. "Huh."

"You were pretty out of it, huh?"

"Maybe I took it a little too far, but I got some levels out of it."

"Do you feel any different? You don't have mana or health. Not even stamina. Your meta-heart is... strange."

"I feel better than I have for a long time," Curtis began. "Like I'm part of something big. The darkness is... I don't know."

"Right... Anyway. We have six months, but I don't intend to let the Singers stick around that long. Perwen, that's the paladin, told me some stuff about the Singers. I might have triggered them to wipe out everyone on Earth."

"Figures."

"Ray is on their side right now, stalling them, but I think I'll have to go through their portals too."

"If you're going over there, then I'm coming too."

"Better if you stay here and kill as many of them as you can, if that's okay with you," Luke said, planning as he spoke. "If I can close the portals, that still means their armies will be on this side for the most part."

Curtis considered the suggestion, then nodded. "Fine."

"We'd better talk to Milla first and see about this pact-meeting thing."

"You go. I need a moment alone."

Luke got to his feet.

"Hey, Luke."

"Yeah?"

"Did you find Hannah? I sometimes feel like there's something bigger picture out there and that maybe she's mixed up in it somehow."

"I think you're talking about the weave. Or your version of it, maybe?"

"The what?" Curtis asked.

Luke gestured toward the empty sky, trying to encompass everything around them. "The weave is everywhere and everything, sort of. It's part of Earth, but also the system. Maybe that's what you're feeling? The void is past that. That's where Hannah is, somehow. She's around. Sort of."

"Maybe," Curtis said, frowning.

It looked like the conversation was over, so Luke turned away to face the desolation that was New York.

"I got stronger," Curtis said, but when Luke looked over, he had his eyes closed, and it hadn't sounded like he was talking to anyone but himself.

Wanting to give the Hollow some space, Luke set off walking toward the city. Riding Smudge to get there faster was an alternative, but he knew of a way that was even faster. From what Perwen told him, time was of the essence now. If the Singers did indeed decide that Earth would be better without humans on it, loss of potential experience or not, they'd start the bombardment right away. What happened to New York was just a taste of the destruction they would rain down on humanity.

With everything happening so fast, he hadn't found the time to distribute the attribute points gained when he leveled up once he was done messing with the void. Three levels’ worth of points. As he submerged himself in the state of mind needed to see the weave, Luke added them all to Willpower. Part of his newfound strength in wielding his will in and out of combat depended on that attribute. There was a part of that equation that was more akin to a test of Luke's character and force of will, but adding to the attribute helped, and it was becoming even more important to squeeze every drop of power from the system, wherever he could find it. With his Willpower attribute now at 68, far above all others, he perused the available skills. Two available skill points before, even before he leveled up, meant he now had five to spend.

Available skills:

Wounded Symmetry [Active]: Mirror a tear in the weave and create it anew.

Weaver’s Boon [Active]: Mana not only restores, but empowers.

Corrupt Thread [Active]: Force threads together in a harming weave.

Rank up skills available:

The Healer's Staff [Active]: Turn your weapon indestructible for a brief moment.

Weaver’s Rupture (Rank 2) [Active]: Create a controlled blast of mana to disrupt any who would threaten you.

Saving some points for level fifty was the smart play here, and the list wasn't all that exciting as things went, but both The Healer's staff and Weaver's Rupture had seen some use in the last couple of encounters, so he decided it was time to rank them up.

The Healer's Staff [Active]: Turn your weapon indestructible for a brief moment.

Turned into.

The Healer's Staff (Rank 2) [Active]: Turn your weapon indestructible for a brief moment. The staff is your center.

Right...

Weaver’s Rupture (Rank 2) [Active]: Create a controlled blast of mana to disrupt any who would threaten you.

Turned into.

Weaver’s Rupture (Rank 3) [Active]: Enchanced blasts of mana can take on many forms.

Damn it... Would it be so hard for a skill description to tell him what the hell it did? Just once? Wanting to add another layer of protection to himself, Luke also selected Weaveguard, thinking it would be better all around than Reject Intrusion.

That done, he snagged the echo thread he'd been searching for and used Weavestep, bringing him through the weave to his destination in less than a second. It was time to make preparations for a trip to the Singer system. There, he would bring the war to them, and he'd show them Earth was far from subjugated.
