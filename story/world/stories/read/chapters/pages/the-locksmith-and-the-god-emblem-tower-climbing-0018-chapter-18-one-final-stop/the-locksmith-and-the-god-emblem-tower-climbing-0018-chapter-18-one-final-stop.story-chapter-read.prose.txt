"Wow..." She puffed her cheeks and widened her eyes.

"Did I do something wrong?" Harry looked around.

"No, it's just... That came out of nowhere." She avoided the young thief's gaze. "It's a big question, you know?"

"Is it?" He replied. "Would you feel more comfortable if I shared you mine?"

"Oh, so we're exchanging stories? Because if that's what's happening, then there are a billion other ones I'd like to hear. Like the one time you flirted with the Duke's wife, or when the Duke almost beat you up—"

"I put myself here on purpose." The sudden honesty silenced Madeline. "Yeah, I know; not the brightest of my ideas, but I needed to. I fell into one of the King's traps, pretending to rob the Veris's scroll or something. They then caught me in the middle of it and threw me in here."

The young woman had a lot on her mind. Why would anyone volunteer to get here? She thought to herself. What madman would imprison his own destiny? And then it struck her; the prize at the end of the Logos Tower. But no, Harry wouldn't be so superstitious, would he?

"...Is this about the God Emblem?" She asked, wording it carefully so as not to put his heart in the same condition as his body.

"You don't believe in it?" He sensed her skepticism.

"I mean, is there anyone who does?" And she immediately closed her eyes in embarrassment. "I'm sorry, I just don't—"

"No, it's fine." Harry said with a weak smile. "I know that it may all not be true, but I have to believe, do you understand? I need this to be true. Besides, what would be the point of a bunch of thieves inside a Tower if said Tower doesn't even contain a treasure?"

"Maybe the Tower really is hell." She replied with a chuckle.

"Maybe it is." He paused for a moment. "Now you. Spill it out."

"Oh, you're serious?" She scratched her arm. "I mean, it's not even that fun..."

"Come on, Maddy. Tell me."

After a deep sigh, she began. "It was a trap, okay? I fell right into a trap. I know you said you pretended to fall into a trap, and I'm still skeptic about it, but I really did fall into one. Do you remember Trenton Scar? Big guy with an even bigger house? Yeah, so he had this replica of Glamour, and I really wanted it. The fat fuck was supposed to be sleeping when I got there, in the wee hours of the night. Turns out he had another meal after his third meal of the night. I mean, who eats this much?"

The Locksmith, who was now laughing, added. "Fuckin' hell... You got caught by the Big T?"

"Who would guess that? I mean, I should've, but yeah... I knocked him out but it was too late by then; the guards swarmed the place like ants near a piece of sugar. Apparently he had to pull some strings to throw me in here, but he did, and now I'm here..."

"It's a tad weird, don't you think?" Harry said, now with a pensive look. "How this Tower was supposed to store only the greatest of evils of the world, and yet, you and I are here."

"I guess we're just that evil, then, right?" She bumped him in the shoulder.

"No, that's not what I mean... I'm saying something isn't right. Someone told me, a long time ago, that maybe the Tower isn't what it's meant to be, you know? Like, why are we here. Why didn't the king and Big T or whatever didn't just kill us, why go through all the trouble of sending us here? I feel like we're missing something..."

"I..." She paused for a moment. "Never thought about it this way. Maybe you're right..."

"Oh, so the two lovebirds were lovemaking in the woods, huh?" Daniel's words brought a bright red color to Madeline and Harry's faces as they promptly turned around, only to see the viking standing proudly.

"I didn't know you were such a creep!" Madeline said as she got up with an accusatory tone. "You were spying on us! That's disgusting!"

"What? No, I—I did not mean it like that. That is—"

"Oh, yeah, that makes sense." Harry seconded. "You were always looking at us strangely... I guess that explains a lot of your behavior."

"No!" Daniel took a step back, unsure how to respond. "I..." Defeated, he turned around, blushing. "Brun told us she found the hatch to the third floor... Come on."

Holding their laughter, Harry and Madeline simply bumped fists and followed the viking.

"Are you sure this is the one?" Harry asked.

"What? Do you think there is another hatch that could lead to the third floor?" Brun asked sarcastically. "My Emblem says it's this one. We have at most two hours left until we can go in there."

"Why don't we go now?" Daniel said with a frown. "We are wasting time."

"If we get to the third floor and don't find a safe space to use the C-Coin, we might get dragged here once again." The old woman explained, then added. "Besides, you weren't so eager to leave when you were chugging down those beers."

"Wait, you were drinking them on purpose?" The Locksmith asked, dumbfounded. "I thought you were doing it as a challenge or something..."

"They weren't that bad!" Daniel raised his voice and puffed his chest, as if proud that everyone thought the drink was awful. "Apparently, it takes a real man to know their value!"

"Oh, dad, please..."

"You're not old enough to drink it yet, Brun. But one day..."

"...Are you serious?" She looked around with a smirk. "Dad, I'm twice your age and probably a bit more."

"I..." And for a moment, Harry felt sorry for the viking. Yes, he could be obnoxious and disproportionately loud at inconvenient times, but the young thief could see the lack of direction in his eyes. It was a weird dynamic, to have a daughter older than you, more experienced and mature. More closer to death. No father should have to see their own kid die... And Harry imagined that's how Daniel felt. Brun appeared annoyed, however, in how her father acted.

"Are we sure the cultists are all tied up, Mr. Goldman?" Vela asked from a corner, raising from a chair with his candelabrum.

"Oh, surely..." Goldman added with a smile as he looked around. "Although it would be best if I checked again... Do forgive me, lads, but I'll be right back." And the gentle pirate banished into the darkness.

As Brun and Vela began discussing something in regards to the next floor, Harry approached the viking.

"Hey..." The Locksmith's heart was now pounding faster. "How, um... How you doing, big guy?"

No answer. He added inwardly as he took a step forward. Daniel appeared to be carving a small piece of wood with a knife. No. With a closer look, Harry saw it was an arrow; a sharp one. With precision, he was tearing off small pieces from what seemed to be the front of the future sculpture.

"I just want to thank you for taking care of me—"

"Are you feeling better?" Daniel asked without looking at the thief.

"Oh, um, yeah, I'm feeling better." The Locksmith hurriedly added. "Everything's like suuuper fine... I just wanted to personally thank you for—"

"You already did." He replied bitterly. "You have already done your good deed of the day, Locksmith. You can now go back to spend time with your concubine."

"What the fuck?" Harry finally captured the attention of the viking. "No, man, she's not my... Look, I don't know what's going on with you, alright? But you healed me. And I wanted to make sure you understood that I... appreciate it."

Daniel's cold glare sent chills down Harry's spine. After a excruciatingly long second, he turned to his activity.

You know what? Fuck it.

"What are you carving?" He reached for a chair and sat on it, right in front of the man. "Is this your god or something?"

"...Njörðr." He answered simply.

"Oh, the God of Trade."

"...Do you know him?" With disbelief written in his face, Daniel carefully said.

"Of course. Devon used to have a lot of books about Nordic culture and insisted in me reading a book about him. He said it would help me elevate my salesman skills."

"He sounds like a wise man."

"He was..." Harry said with a smile. "He was."

Shaking his head, the young thief then added. "You speak my language very well. I never really thought about it now, though..."

"I had to learn at young age." Daniel said, continuing to carve with his sharp arrow. "I was a great trader. Traded with a lot of big names from the south. After a while, I realized that if I spoke more formally and natively, they would be more impressed, and therefore, susceptible to trades."

While he heard the viking's story, Harry realized he never heard Daniel speak so much.

"And why's your name Daniel?" The Locksmith asked with genuine curiosity. "Doesn't seem Nordic." But he promptly regretted his choice of words. Shit... Shouldn't have said his name wasn't Nordic enough.

"My father was an outsider, so that's why my name is... not common. It was a big deal at the time, especially during the ceremony. I was supposed to be named Norman, but he insisted. A lot of things happened because of it... Anyway, what matters is that I'm alive."

"Daniel..." Harry said with a smile. "It's a pretty name. Your father chose well."

"...Thank you, Locksmith." Daniel said after a moment.

Harry then saw Goldman arrive near the fire once again, having completed his check around the perimeter. The young thief bade farewell to Daniel as he went to talk to Goldman.

"Can I speak with you for a second?"

"Of course, Locksmith." Goldman replied. "What is it about?"

"...Can you show me how Dracno's doing?" He said as he tightly gripped his wooden crutch. "Maybe I could talk to him or something..."

"I, um..." Goldman scratched the back of his head as he looked around. "Look, Harry, he ain't the most talkative bloke at the moment, alright? You hurt him pretty badly. Even if he survives to the next loop, I'm not sure he'll be the same. Physical damage can recover, but a mind cannot. And his mind was already gone when we got here..."

"There's something I need to ask him." Harry said, resolute. "Please, Goldman."

"...You're walking the plank, lad. But sure, I'll take you there."
