Cassidy Shell was very pleased with herself.

Seven years. Seven long, patient years of playing the dutiful Gray Lotus initiate. Smiling at the right people. Laughing at the right jokes. Pretending to be just another ambitious young cultivator seeking advancement within the system.

All of it leading to this moment.

She couldn't stop the smile spreading across her face as she surveyed her handiwork. Everyone—even that ridiculous musclebound Ragnak—lay sprawled and helpless in various states of paralysis. Even the Sullied was down, curled on his side like a beaten dog.

Perfect execution.

The smile on her face was real, she was pretty sure. After years of calculated expressions and measured responses, it was hard to tell sometimes. Playing a role for so long had a way of blurring the lines between performance and reality.

The Arcane Veil would pay her well for this sabotage. Not just credits, but advancement. A direct path to upper Mana Circuitry without the years of grueling work. Those idiot Gray Lotus cultivators might be talented, but she was playing a different game. She would jump tracks, advance her cultivation in the fast lane.

Cassidy knelt beside Madia's paralyzed form, patting her cheek. The proud, sharp-tongued recruit couldn't even blink in response. She had spent years suffering the girl’s friendship in the preparatory academy.

"I always hated you the most," Cassidy whispered, just for the pleasure of saying it aloud. "So self-important. So perfect. And now look at you."

She stood, brushing off her knees. No time for indulgence. She had a job to finish.

The manapulse bomb needed to be installed on the payload. Once it detonated, it would fry every crystal and every piece of mana-tech within fifty yards. Darius Roas would find nothing but useless slag when he returned.

And the Arcane Veil would reward her beyond measure.

This was how the game was played at the highest levels. Not with direct confrontation—too messy, too traceable—but with subterfuge and betrayal. The Gray Lotus and Arcane Veil had been locked in their shadow war for decades, each strike carefully calculated, each response measured.

Cassidy was simply an extension of that. A pawn on the board.

But with this… she could become a player. The one moving her own pawns. Maybe not immediately, but this was a step in the right direction. Yes… It was right.

I've earned this.

Through years of hiding her true capabilities, of pretending to be just good enough but never exceptional. Through years of memorizing Gray Lotus protocols and security measures. Through enduring the patronizing lectures and the dismissive glances.

Cassidy Shell had finally arrived at her moment. She savored it. Oh yes, this smile was definitely genuine.

She moved to the payload, reaching into her storage ring to retrieve the bomb. It was beautiful in its way—a sleek silver cylinder etched with intricate sigils. Veil craftsmanship was always elegant, almost artistic, as opposed to the dry pragmatism of the Gray Lotus Syndicate.

As she worked, her thoughts drifted to the mysterious newcomer—Cale. He intrigued her. An unknown variable that had appeared out of nowhere, with abilities that defied normal progression. She had read the chatter. A new asset of the Gray Lotus, suspected to have some kind of mana draining ability. Most likely True Integra.

I wish I had time to know more… she thought.  But It is outside the scope of my mission.

She'd watched him during their encounter with the Praetorians, noted how he moved, how he fought. There was something... different about him.

And despite herself, despite all her training and focus, she'd found herself watching him more than was strictly necessary.

Just professional interest, she told herself. Nothing more.

But there had been a moment—fleeting, barely perceptible—when he'd looked at her across the battlefield. When their eyes had met over the chaos. She'd felt something then. Recognition, perhaps. A mirror.

Something about him reminded her of herself, before she'd become this calculating creature of ambition and deception.

Ridiculous, of course. She'd poisoned him just like the others. Would have killed him without hesitation if the mission required it.

Yet...

Cassidy shook her head, banishing the thoughts. She needed to focus.

She looked around at her handiwork once more. Everyone, even the big Sullied, had fallen to her poisons. They must have felt the ring prick, but no one said anything. Both the ring and the food had been laced with a massive dose of Dreadfang venom, enough to knock out a ridgeback whale.

She had to admit, these recruits were talented. Having fought with them against the Praetorians, she acknowledged they were skilled. The Gray Lotus only invested in the best, and she could see why they'd been chosen.

But they'd been so naive. Ravia Wren had made a critical oversight in not assigning a higher-tier operative to escort the convoy. Darius had trusted his secretary to handle things.

Complacency. That's why the Gray Lotus will eventually fall.

Cassidy felt a flash of anger when her thoughts turned to the secretary. Was that ditzy blonde the reason why Cale hadn't fallen to her charms? Cassidy knew her effect on men. Even that seemingly level-minded Artan had been stealing looks when he thought she wouldn't notice. Men were always men.

But Cale had never lowered his guard. Had never taken the bait. Were he and the secretary sleeping together? The thought bothered her more than it should.

Cassidy shook her head. Why did she care? She was here for a singular reason. She had won, and she needed to finish the job. Taking a moment to check on the poisoned syndicate recruits once more, she assured herself they were all immobile and pale as a fish belly.

Only...

Cassidy's head snapped back to Cale. There was no way, right?

No...

But Cale's face was flushing. He had a fever. Instead of a pallor, his skin was reddening. That was a telltale sign of recovery from Dreadfang poison-induced paralysis.

Her stomach dropped. Impossible. The dose she'd used would have killed a normal cultivator. It should have paralyzed even a Mana Circuitry practitioner for hours. Did this had something to do with his True Integra?

Cassidy didn’t know, but there he was, fingers twitching, eyes focusing.

Fear slithered in her belly, cold and sharp. Fear, and something else—a perverse thrill.

Well, well. You really are special, aren't you?

For the first time in years, Cassidy felt genuine uncertainty. And beneath it, a strange, reluctant admiration.

The mission might be compromised. But somehow, facing this unexpected challenge seemed almost... exciting.

It was an absolutely scintillating mix of emotions. Her ease and triumph were now mixed with fear, anxiety, uncertainty. Even anger.

She'd already made her choice. No turning back. If Cale really was recovering, she'd have to handle him—one way or another.

But as she watched him struggle against the poison, she found herself wanting to linger…

She had time for one more game. She wanted to experience the full breadth of this emotional cocktail.

Had she really found her equal in here? Someone so advanced? She wanted to know more about Cale. And besides. She couldn’t stop her. She had come with contingencies.

Cale struggled up with shaky shoulders, but he did struggle. The fever was dizzying. His whole body was flushed with an intense heat and he was sweating profusely, his dress shirt increasingly plastering against his skin.

Cale reached for his pocket for his thirdlast Nevani mana crystal. He sucked it dry. Despite his weakened constitution, the mana was extremely easy to manipulate after his training session with the murderbot mana cores. This didn’t fight. It was like training a domesticated dog after wrestling with a wolf pack.

He let the mana circle steadily around his solar plexus and extended to his full height. His legs wobbled, but he forced himself to stay moving. He grabbed his hoverboard.

Cassidy had seen him. But instead of preparing herself for a battle, she jumped up on the payload and threw four glowing sticks on the ground. Instantly a bright pink barrier erected around the payload. It was rectangular and hummed softly with energy. Cale shuffled forward, clutching his hoverboard.

“You are a special one, aren’t you?” Cassidy said as Cale approached. “Rabbits and hats. How did you manage to start recovering?”

“How about you come out of there and I’ll tell you,” Cale said in a raspy voice.

“Tempting,” Cassidy said in a sultry tone. “But I’ll have to decline. I have a jet to catch.”

Cale noticed an ever so much higher pitch in Cassidy’s voice. It was still silky smooth, but there was an undertone of nervousness.

“I got over your poison,” Cale said. “Want to bet I’ll get through this barrier too.”

Cassidy chuckled as she reached for a blue bracelet and pressed on it. A barrier appeared. She fished a device from it. Clearly a bomb. “You’re impressive. But you have no techniques. Just raw grit and power. Besides, you’re burning up.”

“So touching of you to care,” Cale said. “Why not just kill us?”

“The explosion might kill some of you,” Cassidy said as she installed the bomb on top of the payload. She fastened it with a cinch and started inputting a code on a managlass screen. “But I’d rather you survive. Killing someone in the Citadel causes such a mess.”

“You’re going to have to leave that barrier before the bomb goes off,” Cale said. “I might just cause a mess with you.”

“Oh, I’d love to see what kind of a mess we can make together,” Cassidy said and smiled like a cat with milk. “How about this: Join me and take the entrance exam with Arcane Veil. You look dashing in a suit, but I think I’d prefer you in a robe.”

“I’d prefer you in ropes,” Cale said.

Cassidy’s smile spread into a grin.

“Don’t,” Cale said, pointing a finger.

“Spoilsport,” Cassidy purred. Then her grin faded and she regarded Cale seriously. “Fine. You’re right, you’re causing a headache. How about this: I give you creds, and you play dead? That’s a guaranteed score for you with no downside.”

“Me failing my mission has no downside?”

“Sure they’ll ream your tushy for it. But they’ll get over it, and you’ll have an advantage. If you’re gunning for the Corussi academy, you’ll need advantages.”

“I don’t know if you’ve noticed, but I have some.”

“You do,” Cassidy said. “I hate for us to be enemies. I’d rather make sure our dorm rooms are close…”

Cale considered Cassidy for a moment. Tried to see things from her perspective. She was saying whatever cats said to dogs when cornered. She was trying honey first, but soon would come the vinegar. Damn, she was good at honey though. Cale could almost believe her, if she hadn’t just tried to poison him.

The fever was bad. Muddling his thoughts. They were slow and ponderous things like clouds on a still day, just rolling along idly.

The problem was clear. He wouldn’t be able to stop both Cassidy and her bomb. Hell, he might not be able to stop either. He could let Cassidy escape or fight her…

“How about a counter-offer,” Cale said. “You defuse that bomb, and I’ll let you walk away. I’ll be the hero, get rewarded, and later when our dorm rooms are close, I’ll pay you.”

Cassidy tilted her head and scoffed. “Look at you. You just keep surprising me. Not just grit, not just a talent for fighting, but a mind of politics too. You might go far.”

“Might do, might not,” Cale said and shrugged. “But if you rig that bomb, it’s the end of the line for you.”

“With that fever of yours, I doubt you can take me. You’re Body Tempering four.”

“Five,” Cale said.

Cassidy missed a beat. “What?”

“I leveled up when I was catching up to you.”

“You lie,” Cassidy said immediately. “There was no time. I never even saw you take out your mana attuner.”

Cale shrugged. His fever-addled mind was starting to formulate a strategy. “Last chance, Cassidy. Back away, and you’ve got an ally in Corussi and in the Gray Lotus Syndicate. Same what you offered me. No downside. Try to play this out? It’s your ass.”

“You think you could handle my ass?”

“Cute,” Cale said. “I don’t know, but I’ll give it a shot.”

After that he got on the hoverboard. Cassidy arched an eyebrow and went back to tapping on the managlass screen of the bomb.

“I’ll catch you with the hoverboard,” Cale said.

“You think I didn’t plan an escape?” Cassidy snapped, a little testily this time. “Why did you have to ruin everything? Just let it go. I’ll reward you.”

“Right back at you.”

She groaned in frustration. Cale took distance from the barrier array and the payload and angled the hoverboard in a direct line.

“You can’t pass the barrier,” Cassidy called out.

“Watch me,” Cale called back.

Cale pushed the pedal down and accelerated.

As he sped towards the pink barrier, he flexed every single muscle in his body. He constricted his mana, pressing it into a tight, curled up ball. Then just as he was about to hit the pink barrier, he relaxed, and the mana expanded. A green outline enveloped Cale. The hoverboard clonked on the barrier, sending pink sparks flying. Cale was thrown inside.

“You— You had a technique!?” Cassidy screeched, snapping up.

Cale got up and pushed the ache and dizziness of the fever aside. “Rabbits and hats.”

Cassidy growled and tossed a round and smooth metal object at him. A concussion grenade, just as Zavio had used.

Cale used Ghost step. The explosion passed through his ethereal body. Cale rematerialized completely unharmed, un-concussed. White smoke filled the barrier.

He lunged towards Cassidy. She kicked him away and growled in feral frustration. She jumped down and the barrier disappeared. She ran for a few yards and then short ceramic wings sprouted from her back, a pink glow emanating from them. She took off in the air.

“I’ll remember this!” she screeched from the sky. “You better hope we don’t meet in the exam!”

“Sure, sure,” Cale called and waved back. “See you there.”

Then he immediately turned his attention to the softly ticking bomb on top of the convoy. There were a lot of numbers and letters running on the managlass screen. The ticking was intensifying. Cale had the distinct feeling he didn’t have a lot of time.

“What do I do?”

“What do you take me for?” Aura said in flushed panic.” I’m not an engineer. DO SOMETHING!”

“Oh, screw it.”

Cale ripped the cinch off the payload and threw the bomb high and far. A high-pitched whine pierced the air just before the blast. Then—a blue shockwave rippled out, smashing into him like a sledgehammer. The impact stole his breath, sending him sprawling onto his back.

Cale slammed on the ground and air rushed out of his lungs. He groaned and coughed until he was satisfied. Then he shuffled up and got a bottle of water from the packs on the convoy and poured it over himself as he sat down.

“I better get a reward from all this extra shit…”
