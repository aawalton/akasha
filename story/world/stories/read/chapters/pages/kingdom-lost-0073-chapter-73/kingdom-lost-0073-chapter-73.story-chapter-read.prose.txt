Thorne walked at the head of the small column, his posture straight despite the long hours of leading the group back. Behind him stretched a line of twenty-five villagers from Hoshin, their faces a mixture of exhaustion and hope. They walked in clusters, some with bundles of clothing, tools, and precious household items wrapped in cloth. Others only had the clothes on their backs.

As the group neared the end of their return journey, the settlement came into view through the trees. The sturdy wooden gates stood tall and solid, reinforced through a recent upgrade and flanked by watchtowers that rose above the palisade. Sunlight glinted off the metal tips of spears held by the guards on the walls. Several of the villagers slowed their steps, eyes widening at the sight. An elderly man with a weathered face paused, his grip tightening on his walking stick as he took in the organized layout of buildings and the disciplined figures moving about inside. Murmurs rippled through the group and smiles started to spread through the group and the thought of starting a new free life was moments away and would start once they crossed the gates threshold.

Nikola had been monitoring their approach on the HUD map. The moment the group drew near, he sent the signal. The heavy gates swung open with a low groan of wood and iron, revealing the cleared ground and orderly structures beyond. He stood just inside the entrance, hands clasped behind his back, waiting with calm authority.

As the first villagers passed through the gates, Nikola raised his voice to carry clearly without shouting. “Welcome. Please keep moving forward in an orderly fashion. Bring your belongings to the large bunkhouse on the right side of the main path. Please settle where you wish inside, then gather near the central fire pit once you are ready. We will provide food and water shortly.”

The villagers nodded and continued forward, their steps quickening with renewed energy. The soft shuffle of many feet filled the air as the group made their way toward the bunkhouse, eyes still wide with the realization that this fortified place was now their new home.

Nikola waited until the last of the villagers had moved past the gates and started toward the bunkhouse. He then stepped forward and placed a firm hand on Thorne’s shoulder, drawing him a short distance away from the crowd. Thorne turned, his brow furrowing slightly at the serious look on Nikola’s face.

“Riley needs to see you immediately,” Nikola said in a low, urgent voice. “She is waiting for you in the tower.”

Thorne’s expression tightened. He gave a single sharp nod, his hand instinctively brushing the hilt of his sword. Without another word, he pivoted on his heel and strode away from the arriving villagers. His boots struck the packed earth with quick, purposeful steps as he crossed the open ground toward the central tower. Dust rose faintly behind him with each stride. The afternoon light cast long shadows across the settlement, but Thorne kept his gaze fixed ahead, his shoulders squared and his jaw set.

As he approached the tower, the wooden door at the base stood slightly ajar. Thorne pushed it open with one hand and stepped inside. The cool interior air carried the faint scent of fresh-cut timber.

Riley stood near the window, her back to the door and her hands clasped tightly behind her. She turned at the sound of his entrance, her face pale and drawn with clear tension and the table she stood at the head of was populated with a few familiar faces. Nikola’s earlier words still hung in the air between them as Thorne stepped fully into the room and closed the door behind him.

Riley sat down at the head of the meeting table. Landryn occupied the chair to her right, his thick fingers resting on the tabletop. Zelgra sat on the opposite side, her broad frame leaning slightly forward with both hands planted on the wood. A single soldier stood at the far end of the table, his back straight and his hands clasped behind him. His face remained mostly stoic, but a faint tightness around his eyes and the way his weight shifted from one foot to the other revealed a trace of nervousness. Thorne moved past the solider while giving him a nod and took his place at the table.

Riley leaned forward and spoke in a calm, measured voice. “So I understand you saw ten foot wooden walls with a large gate. And there were archer towers at each of the four corners of the walls?”

The soldier gave a small nod. “Yes, ma’am. I cannot be sure, but I think only every second tower were manned.”

Riley nodded slowly, her gaze steady on him. “I understand you only saw the base for a short time. If there is anything else you remember, please bring that to me.”

The soldier hesitated for a moment, then cleared his throat. “If I could make a request, I would like to be part of any plans to march on the raiders.”

A small smile touched Riley’s lips. She inclined her head slightly. “Thank you for your service. You will receive your orders shortly. You are dismissed.”

The soldier snapped to attention, clicked his heels together, and executed a crisp heel turn. He marched out of the room with measured steps as the door closed behind him.

With a steady breath Riley looked around at the gathered faces and spoke clearly. “Now that Thorne is here, I will call this meeting to order. I have asked Zelgra to be here for this. Nikola will be joining us shortly once he has facilitated moving the Hoshin villagers into the bunk house, but he is aware of what I am about to tell you.”

She paused for a moment, letting her words settle. Every eye in the room fixed on her. Riley straightened her shoulders and continued in a firm voice. “Valrik and another soldier have been taken hostage by the raiders while they were surveilling the raiders’ base.”

Landryn’s fingers tightened on the edge of the table. Zelgra’s heavy brows drew together and her mouth opened as if to speak. Thorne leaned forward sharply, his chair scraping against the floor. Questions rose at once. Landryn asked how many raiders had been spotted and whether the captured men still lived. Thorne’s and Zelgra started to speak at the same time.

Riley raised her hand, palm open, and the room fell quiet again. “We will respond. But we need to prepare.”

She turned her gaze first to Zelgra. “Zelgra, I need you to take a few soldiers with you back to the tar pit with buckets and gather eight to ten buckets of tar.”

Zelgra gave a single firm nod, her massive hands flexing once on the tabletop.

Riley shifted her attention to Thorne. “I need you to go hunting and kill two or three monsters as soon as possible. Take what you need to bring back the bodies. I need you then to process them and render the fat into buckets. Work with Nikola to have the forge produce metal buckets as needed.”

Thorne met her eyes and nodded once, his face pensive and harden with clear determination.

At that moment the door opened again. Nikola entered the room, still carrying the faint dust of the outer yard on his boots. He crossed to the table without a word and took the empty seat beside Landryn, his expression alert as he settled in to listen.

Riley turned her attention to Nikola, her posture straight and her hands resting on the table. “I need soldiers for a combat mission for tomorrow. I need twenty infantry and another thirty to forty ranged. I need all infantry to have their long pikes and large shields. I want all soldiers to have their swords as side arms. All must be properly armoured. How many hospital beds do we have currently?”

Nikola answered without hesitation, his voice clear and precise. “One hundred beds ready.”

Riley gave a small nod of acknowledgment before shifting her gaze to another. “Landryn, please start formal mining of those two new areas. Begin collection of both the white and yellow material. I want the access to the white material to be restricted to a small group and I want to meet with them before they start. Please work with Nikola to craft larger containers from the forge for these materials to be collected and keep separate. For now store them in the mine where they are collected.”

Landryn leaned forward slightly, his shoulders shifting as he absorbed the instructions. “I will start preparations right away.”

The room remained quiet for a moment as the weight of the orders settled over the group. Zelgra’s fingers drummed once against the tabletop while Thorne sat with his arms crossed, his expression focused. Nikola's eyes seem to defocus like he had pulled up the HUD and was preparing. Riley’s eyes moved across each face, gauging their readiness as the meeting continued.

Riley kept her hands flat on the table as she continued. “I also want those two raiders that have been working the mines made ready to travel when we are ready to leave. I also want them gagged and shackled.”

She paused briefly before adding her next instructions. “We will also need some axes and a few hatchets.”

She pushed back from the table and stood, her movements deliberate. The others watched her closely. Riley placed both palms on the tabletop and leaned forward, her voice steady and carrying through the room. “These are probably the same raiders that hit us before. At the very least they have our brothers. I know we have been waiting for a response to these injustices. We have an opportunity to teach this world what we are capable of by leaving our mark. We will march on the morrow and rain hell fire down on them like they have never dreamed possible.”

She leaned farther forward, her eyes moving across each face at the table. “We will destroy their base and leave none alive.”

The words hung in the air. Zelgra’s jaw tightened and she gave a slow, nod. Thorne’s hands clenched into fists on the table, his knuckles showing white. Landryn sat motionless, his heavy brows lowered in grim focus. Nikola remained still, his expression calm but his eyes sharp with understanding. A silence filled the room as the full weight of what the next 24 hours would bring.
