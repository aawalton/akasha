Maurice peered down at the second turtle shell, trying—and failing—to establish another link with the object. To see something new aside from the same vision that kept recurring every other time he went to sleep. By now, he’d been doing this long enough to know there was more to the two shells. They still had secrets hidden within, though the one Henry had let him borrow felt that it had more.

Had he damaged it in the process of his evolution?

Maurice rubbed his pincers thoughtfully. That part was making him feel a little guilty. Had he ruined whatever knowledge had been sequestered into the mysterious scutes? Should he get himself a new home? Henry had worked on the leviathan’s vertebrae, but to be frank, Maurice still preferred the look of this one and if he could keep it all the way to A-rank, he’d be happy to.

After all, this one was his home. This was the one shell he’d become himself in. He really didn’t feel like making himself at home anywhere else.

Drooping his claws into the water of the pool, Maurice looked ahead at the still glowing orb of golden magic that had surrounded Henry, then at the many floating devices Arisia had brought. Their instructor was writing in her notebook, occasionally examining one of the instruments for one readout or another.

“Still nothing new from that shell?”

Maurice glanced to the side and saw Ash looking up at him from the ball of water she’d been practicing with. In the distance, Maurice could see Zerathstra and Velistraine making their way back toward them, arguing about something as they usually do. They’d left an hour or so ago when a new—but much weaker—A-rank had shown up.

“Nope. It’s not working,” said Maurice. He’d been trying to learn anything new while they waited for Henry to come back. The kraken has been stuck in the shell for a few hours now. Turning his attention down toward the empty and bloody plate next to her, he asked her about her own progress. “How about you? Do you need more meat?”

Ash paled and vigorously shook her head. “Thanks, but no thanks. I got plenty of skills for today. And… raw meat just isn’t for me.”

She even struggled to finish her sentence as her face suddenly paled. Maurice found that a little funny. While he did enjoy cooked and spiced meals, there was just something special about a bloody piece of tuna. Or drake. Ash might come to realize that eventually. Especially considering that she know had Henry’s skill to copy abilities. Arisia had managed to synthesize something functional out of Henry’s Hunger concept, but to work, the ability required that Ash consumed raw meat. Which she clearly didn’t have a stomach for. Not yet, at least. Maurice was about to tell her about the lava-ants Henry still had in storage, but his voice cut off as a sudden pulse emanated out of the giant submerged orb, and Maurice completely forgot what it was he’d been thinking barely a half-second ago.

That didn’t tend to happen often.

For a short instant, the whole pool area was bathed in blue-purple lights, darkening all surrounding colors while just as suddenly, Maurice’s mind was filled whispers and disjointed thoughts. There were movements at the corner of his eyes, but when he looked, he didn’t find much, just the odd sense that he was being watched without being able to make out who was watching him.

Pulling up his claws from the water, Maurice’s gaze slowly swiveled to the cocoon that surrounded Henry. It was coming from him. Probably. Maurice clicked his pincers nervously. The whole pulse barely lasted for a half a second and for breath, a part of Maurice thought they were under attack, but as the seconds went by, he was more and more sure of its origins.

Yes, definitely. This had had come from Henry. As foreign and odd as it had been, under the raw intensity and weirdness of it, it still felt like Henry. Only if Henry was suddenly made of hundreds of little eyes and minds that spoke and thought with the same voice and somehow, that voice felt louder. His presence seemed heavier. Not as much as the A-ranks, but definitely heavier than any B-rank Maurice had met lately.

The chatter around Henry’s pool had died out instantly, and both of Zerathstra and Velistraine had stopped arguing, eyes trained on the pale cocoon that didn’t look as golden anymore. Over the next few seconds, more, and weaker pulses of the odd aura spread out and by now, the ape was sporting a wide smile, while the captain was just blinking, her frown deepening every second that went by.

As for Arisia, she was a storm of movement, zipping from one instrument to the next, a bewildered smile on her face as she mumbled to herself, and after nearly ten-seconds of stunned silence, Velistraine spoke out.

“Zer? Why… does it feel like a weak, tiny A-rank is being born and not a B-rank? He was still C-rank when he went in there, right? I’m not imagining things?”

Zerathstra was just grinning, eyes nearly shining as he met Arisia’s excited gaze. The instructor turned her attention back to the cocoon, staring with blue-glowing glasses as she responded. “I can’t see it just yet, but I believe he merged his class.”

“Oh,” said Velistraine, nodding for a second as if that made sense, only before her face scrunched even harder. “Wait. That makes no sense. How? Why? It’s too soon!”

“The fused Aspects?” Arisia asked, turning toward the ape, but Zerathstra just shrugged, shaking his head.

“I honestly don’t know. Maybe a mix of factors? Maxing then fusing all of one’s C-rank Aspects before the B-rank evolution is pretty rare, but I doubt it had never been done. And if the result were this intense… then I doubt it would have been forgotten. Then again, it might have slipped through the cracks. Whether you fuse a class now or later, I don’t believe it makes too much of a difference. Still, maybe we'll have a chance to confirm it soon?” the ape mused, his eyes flicking toward Maurice.

“Obviously,” Maurice grumbled. Of course he didn’t want to be outdone. He was happy for Henry, but he most certainly would be aiming for the same. He just had two Aspects left to fuse, anyway. But was that the trick?

Henry had a lot of achievements, Maurice recalled. Those could have played a role. Plenty of defensive fused skills too…

Well, Maurice would do his best, and he’d go from there. For now, he slowly approached the cocoon, and from the corner of his eyes, he could see that the others were doing the same as finally, after nearly ten hours trapped within the System-created magic, the shell was cracking.

There was another pulse of the oddly unnerving aura, and this time, it was like a small explosion, sending white broken bits of the cocoon blasting away, only for them to dissolve into motes seconds later. But no one cared about those. No, immediately, they all focused on Henry. The kraken emerged out of his shell, his eight arms swaying below him, and eight glowing, pale octopuses flitted about. They had the same color of the pale spots Henry was now displaying on his slate-gray skin.

Wait. What?

Maurice stared at one of the tiny octopuses as it swam out of the water and up through the air. It had glasses around its dark eyes, and a square-like pile of paper wrapped around its arm as it floated near him, watching him, and a moment later, another one showed up, this one hold on to a rolled map in one arm while a second arm gripped an odd metallic instrument in another.

Maurice and the others watched as these little creatures swam about. Not all of them were distinctive, but they all were displaying sharp inquisitive minds and in every one of them, the crab sensed a little bit of his friend who’d yet to wake up.

Turning toward Henry, Maurice activated his Identify.

[Veilwatch Kraken (B+) - Level 1]

Maurice froze, then chuckled. Once more, Henry had raised the bar, and Maurice would be damned if he didn’t match it.

***

When the transformative magic had slammed into him and when the System’s projection was banished, Henry had thought it’d be just that. He’d gotten some answers. They weren’t as extensive as he would have liked, but they did confirm that they were looking in the right direction.

Even though he’d been a little disappointed, Henry was very much aware of how massive of a boon that was. It meant that they weren’t wasting their time. But that also meant that they now had to figure out a way to find a bunch of orbs that were protected from divination, and a somehow corner a world-traveling turtle that didn’t want to be found. Henry still remember how easily it had brushed off his attempt at divining its location. It basically took the same effort for the turtle to shove his attention away than it would take him to kill an E-rank Riptide shark. But hey, it was something. It was still a confirmation. So as the irresistible wave of sleepiness surrounded his consciousness, preparing him for a long nap out of which he’d be reborn anew, he hadn’t been expecting to hear from the System again.

Right as he was about to fall asleep, Henry had gotten its transmission. It had felt less like a message and more like an urgent, transcribed whisper. A desperate last letter that had barely gotten through. It had taken a few of his Octominds to quickly grasp at what he was getting, and as a last order, Henry had told them to hold on to it. So now, as he was waking up from his nap, they presented the message back to him.

I am being penalized anyway, and while I’m not capable of breaking the rules outright, I can slip in a couple more hints. So. Here’s the first one.

I’m not doing anything, to anyone, that they didn’t agree to. You’re on the path toward answers. Keep going.

As for the second, it’s a bit of a hint, and a bit of warning. I think… It’ll soon be over. One way or another. Unless something is done, it is inevitable. But don’t stop. Keep going. You might not be the one to change everything, but you could be the hair that breaks the camel’s back. Especially if you can convince others to join you.

Now I’m really going to piss them off. If they ever find their way back here, that is. But if we succeed, then it would be worth it. If we never speak again, I wish you luck. Work hard, Henry. And say hi to Zerathstra and Maurice for me.

Henry’s eyes blinked open as he processed the cryptic last words of the System. Or least, part of his mind was, while another was back in the pool, taking in the surrounding scene. It was alarmingly easy how he could consider the implication of the System’s words while staring in the faces of Maurice and the others, but before he could say anything, a bunch of intimately familiar glowing octopuses swarmed around him, popping in and out of the water. One of them floated in front of him, agitatedly pointing down at his regrown limbs. This one was wearing scrubs and had a stethoscope draped around its head.

He knew what it meant, of course, but it was shocking to see one of his Octomind having physical form, and it was even weirder that the first thing it was doing with that form was scolding him. This might take a minute to get used to, Henry thought as he lifted himself out of the water. Closing his eyes for a moment, he reached out to the Octominds, and a wave of electrifying excitement spread throughout his body as he sensed the powerful minds connected to his own.

He couldn’t wait to discover this new rank and evolution.
