Gary was left staring at the impossibly heavy book, the very expensive book, which just lay in the middle of the forest for anyone to find. Sure, most people couldn’t pick it up, but maybe Elders could.

Grumbling, he started tossing dirt on top of it. The book had sank its entire height into the ground, so when he buried it, it was just below the surface. He placed a small stone next to it, so he could find it again, then took off.

“I hate fucking books,” he said.

“Yeah, I prefer reading them,” Goldie said, much to Gary’s surprise.

He went right back to sleep and only awoke the next morning, rushing through the clan. He had a final to catch!

“I have no idea how you fooled them, guys, but you’re amazing,” Gary said, leaning against a tree. He’d met the two just outside the city.

Asim grinned. “It was all Chloe.”

“It wasn’t! Asim handled everything, I wanted to run!” she replied. Everyone laughed. When they were done, Gary wore a calm smile.

“Really, guys, you saved my life. If you’d escaped…”

“We wouldn’t do that. We knew they were watching you—escaping would be dooming you,” Chloe said. “I don’t like it, but sometimes we just have to risk it.”

“How did you know it would work out like this, though?”

“We really didn’t. We just planted the booklet to frame the brothers,” Asim said.

Gary shook his head. “Isn’t it great when things work out for once?”

“I guess,” Asim said. He handed him a pouch. “Here. Your last share of stones and sap. We’re all out of balls now, and they’re onto us, so it’s time to stop.”

“It’s a shame, too, because more people are looking to buy. It was fun while it lasted,” Chloe said.

“How are you feeling about the final?” Asim asked.

“Calm,” Gary replied. “Honestly, Feng Shen is too good. I don’t expect to win. I’ll try my best, and I might win, but it feels like an afterthought compared to the Sui Duang duel.”

“I bet all the attention feels good though,” Chloe said.

“I don’t care about it.”

“Come on, Gary. You’re an attention whore.”

“I’m not!” Gary insisted.

“Then why do you make everyone bald?”

“It’s an accident!”

“That’s what they all say,” Chloe said. She and Asim nodded sympathetically.

“Hey, think you’ll make Feng Shen bald too?” Asim asked.

***Gary walked through the city’s avenues. There was one avenue connecting each of the cardinal gates to the Empyrean Steps in the center of the Shores of Glory, always full of people, carts, and magic.

Gary’s name had spread through the city yesterday, but the projections hadn’t. Not yet, at least. Hearing people talk about him while he walked right past them was amusing.

He wasn’t even the person drawing the most gazes.

“I’m proud of you,” Colorful said, his patchy, colorful robes swaying in the breeze. People gave them a wide berth.

“Thank you, Master. I couldn’t have done it without you.”

A sad smile lingered on Colorful’s face. “Is there anything you’d like to ask me?”

Gary hesitated. “Did I deserve that victory?”

“How do you mean that?”

“Sui Duang was stronger, faster, and better with the spear than me. He was superior in every single attribute. I know I worked harder, but climbing is not about effort, it’s about the result.”

“Then, why did he lose?”

“Because I got lucky.”

Colorful chuckled. “Luck is part of a climber’s power, Gary. However, you didn’t get lucky this time. You won because Sui Duang made mistakes. He wasn’t better than you at everything. He was arrogant and refused to take the battle seriously. He refused to end it when he could have. He tried to show off, he underestimated your durability and resolve to win, and overall he made gross miscalculations.”

“But still, Master, he was stronger than me. I only won because he refused to take me seriously. How is that fair?”

“Who talked about fair? It’s true that the odds weren’t in your favor. If you fought ten times, you’d only win once. However, do you think that geniuses, while climbing the tower, only engage in battles where they have the upper hand? Of course not! They’re ambushed by bandits or ganged up on by their enemies. They get trapped in caves of dangerous beasts and fall for the schemes of their political opponents. Sometimes, higher-level people come to the wilderness to assassinate you. All those situations are something you can usually avoid, but they’re bound to happen a few times. It happens to everyone. And the odds you’ll be facing in those battles might be even lower than what you did against Sui Duang. Why is it, then, that the strongest people survive and make it to the top? If it was about luck, you might survive once, but you wouldn’t survive twice. Even if you did, you’d find the top of the tower crowded with fortunate mediocrities.”

Gary considered it. “So, how do they survive?” he asked.

“Because they’re good! Damn it, Gary, that’s the point! The chances of survival might be low, but they’re never zero. There is always a way out, and the purest expression of genius is being able to see and follow that path. To turn the tiny chances of survival into reality. That’s exactly what you did against Sui Duang, and if you think it was unfair, then I guarantee you that every single person at the top of the tower has gotten there unfairly.”

“I think I understand. It’s just hard to feel that way when I barely scraped by.”

“I expected you to already understand this, Gary. In the climbing world, there is no place for what-ifs. Reality is everything. You won. Take joy in that.”

Gary closed his eyes and took a deep breath. When he reopened them, he was resolved. “I got it. Thank you, Master.”

“Mm.”

“But why did you expect me to know this already?”

“Because you’ve been taught by a man who believed in this concept whole-heartedly.”

“Believed? Not anymore?”

Colorful gave him a side glance. “I don’t mean me, disciple.”

Gary’s eyes widened. Besides Cog and Alicia, he hadn’t told this to anyone since arriving at the second floor. “How did you know?” he asked.

Colorful laughed. “There are some things you can’t hide, disciple. Even after seeing your obsessive training habits, the state you were in during the clan selection makes no sense. You couldn’t have gotten that far in just a few days. Someone taught you the basics of the spear and the singing spear vibration in the Floor of Trials, and there’s only one person who could have done that.”

Gary lowered his head. “I’m sorry for lying to you, Master.”

“You were wise to do it, since you knew nothing about me. But, you’re lucky. I have great respect for the Mercy family.”

“Then, if you know, everyone knows?”

“Not everyone. One would need to have studied you during the clan selection, and that was just me. Elder Sui might have had his suspicions, too, but I don’t think he ever believed it. In the eyes of a Sui, a genius spending time to teach a nobody is completely unreasonable.”

“He was pretty unreasonable,” Gary said with a smile. “I had to beg really hard.”

“Can I ask you something, disciple?”

“Of course.”

“How did he die?”

Gary raised his gaze. There was genuine curiosity in the eyes of his master. The sober interest of someone asking after a tragedy.

“I don’t know exactly. He was facing a floor boss alongside a healer, who was his ally, and the four brothers. They were on an island. At some point, a red cage appeared over the island, and he messaged me to run. I guess either the boss or the brothers got him. Probably both. When I met one of them later, he claimed he’d dealt the final blow.”

“Which one?”

“Zink. He used spiked metal balls. I killed him on the Floor of Trials.”

Colorful raised a brow. “How?”

“By turning a slim chance into reality,” Gary replied. “He was much stronger than me. He just never thought I’d fight back.”

Colorful nodded. “Good.”

They walked in silence. Colorful didn’t ask how Zink’s booklet, which had served as the definitive evidence against Elder Sui, appeared in Asim’s laboratory. In fact, he didn’t ask anything about that, as he hadn’t when Gary asked him for help when he was trapped in a warehouse with the brothers.

Gary appreciated the discretion.

As they approached the Empyrean Steps, they joined the crowd of people who’d come to watch the show. Many of them held multi-day tickets, which means they were there the day before. They recognized Gary. A dozen of them approached for handshakes and congratulations, while many more gazed his way but were discouraged by the presence of Vice Head Colorful, whose temper was already infamous. Gary didn’t spot any familiar faces.

They made it through the tunnel and into the arena, where they split up. Colorful headed to the VIP section, while Gary to the disciple section. Everyone there congratulated him, some honestly, others trying to get close to him. Gary accepted their words with humility.

Feng Shen wasn’t present.

“It’s so good to see you, buddy!” Cog said, wrapping Gary in a huge hug.

“Me too, Cog. Me too.”

“Gary!” Alicia squealed, forgoing all decorum to also hug him snugly. “I’m so glad you’re okay…”

“I’m glad too,” he replied.

Caramel, Pop Ear, and Helen rushed over the moment they saw him. “Dude, that was a kick-ass fight!” Caramel said.

“Huge respect, dude,” Pop Ear said.

“You really showed the families who’s boss,” Helen said.

“Thanks, guys,” Gary replied with a smile.

More of the crowd flitted in until the seats were full. The stairs became untraversable again. Excitement sparked through the air, everyone discussing animatedly, while the arena sands were clean and flat. Sui Duang’s heap of blackened bones had been removed. Gary hoped that so had the ash.

As for the ruined section of the arena where Elder Sui’s attack had hit, that remained in rumble. It was part of the reason why so many people sat at the stairs.

Rollo Frollo stood at his little platform over the brackets board. He was impeccably dressed and carried an energizing smile as he pored through documents. Suddenly, however, he snapped his gaze to the VIP section. His voice boomed.

“Your attention, everyone. Please show your respect for the venerable Shoresmaster!”

Silence fell over the busy arena. People rushed to stand and place a hand over their hearts. Gary did the same. Even those sitting in the VIP sections, people like Colorful or the Branch Head, stood and lowered their gazes.

A form descended from the ceiling. It was a woman of swirling dark hair, stunning in her beauty, yet carrying a heavy presence. She was a far cry from the Branch Head’s calm elegance. This was a warrior. If anyone spoke against her, she’d stomp them into the dirt like Gary’s body manual. That’s the impression he got as he snuck a peek at the famous Shoresmaster, the leader of the Shores of Glory and the entire second floor, one of the most important places in the tower.

“We greet the Shoresmaster!” Rollo Frollo and almost everyone in the crowd bellowed. Gary jumped, then followed a moment later.

For an instant, the Shoresmaster’s aura blanketed the entire arena, and then it was gone. She rested cross-legged in the throne of the VIP section that had so-far remained empty. The arena’s heavy silence gradually gave way to noise again, but the sense of awe remained.

“If she was here yesterday, I bet nobody would have dared to cause trouble,” Cog said.

“I don’t know if I could have slapped Elder Sui,” Gary admitted.

“How strong must she be, to command such respect?” Alicia wondered aloud.

Gary shook his head. “I don’t think it’s about strength. It’s about personality. Everyone on the VIP seats could annihilate me with a flick of their fingers, but it didn’t stop me. She might have.”

“You had your master to protect you,” Cog pointed out.

“Well, yeah, but he couldn’t protect me from everyone. The Branch Head is higher in authority, and he must be powerful, but nobody respected him. Remember when he told Master and Elder Sui to stop fighting and they just ignored him?”

“Branch Head Tallhat is criticized often. He’s a powerful climber, but people say he’s too weak-willed to lead a clan. They say he’s the main reason why our branch has lost so much standing lately,” Alicia said.

“When did we lose standing?”

“Yesterday, for example. But there have been other occasions where people step on our toes and the Branch Head fails to resolve the situation. It paints a bad image for the clan, especially with the Malfunctioning pushing to take our spot on the Clan Floor.”

“Who does what?”

“Welcome, everyone, to the final day of the tournament!” Rollo Frollo announced, interrupting the conversation. Everyone cheered. A large projection appeared in the middle of the arena, showcasing a single man towering over many. The number one was clearly embroidered on his back.

“One hundred and ninety-three brave disciples embarked on this journey. Twelve days later, we’re left with only two. Feng Shen is the Heaven Reaching clan’s number one junior disciple, and the so-far indisputable favorite! In all his battles so far, the only time he struggled was against Lin Xin in the semi-finals. Then again, he’s never met Gary Steel.”

The projection changed to the image of Gary, riddled with holes and almost fully bled out, frying Sui Duang to death.

Rollo Frollo’s voice grew louder. “We thought he was a joke! We called him the Baldinator! Who knew that under that jester exterior hid the soul of a true warrior! In the semi-finals, Gary Steel faced his sworn enemy, Sui Duang, in a Duel of Honor. The stakes couldn’t be higher, and Gary’s chances couldn’t be lower, but he persevered! He took hit after hit, many of them appearing sure-kill! We even announced him dead, but he returned, and in a display of pure willpower and extreme battle intelligence, he eked out a victory over the Sui clan’s genius! The odds are against him again. Can he achieve the same against Feng Shen? Can he at least roast his hair?”

Cheers rose to the ceiling.

“Can you?” Cog asked.

“I think so,” Gary said. “For the hair part.”

“Let’s welcome our finalists. Gary Steel, please step on the sand!” Rollo Frollo yelled.

Gary leapt on-stage under the cascading applause of everyone in the audience. There were even more Baldinator banners, along with many spelling Gary Steel.

“We believe in you, Steel!”

“Do your best!”

Cheers and chanting washed over him. Gary settled in their approval. He raised a fist. The crowd exploded.

“Feng Shen, please step on the sand!” Rollo Frollo exclaimed.

Gary looked at the disciple seats. He hadn’t seen Feng Shen there—could he be missing? That hope was soon dispelled, however, when a man stood up. The cheers stopped instantly. Stunned silence fell over the arena, so dense you could cut it with a knife.

Surrounded by that silence, Feng Shen floated to the sand, his face and robes leaving no doubt of who he was. The rest of his head, however…

“Unbelievable!” Rollo Frollo exclaimed. “He’s already bald!”

Feng Shen’s ponytail was no more. Instead, a perfectly bald head was all that met the viewers. A few pockets of people burst into laughter. Most maintained a stunned silence.

“Why did you shave it?” someone in the audience yelled. Gary shared the question.

“Appearance means little to a cultivator,” Feng Shen replied. “Rather than caring about my hair mid-battle or losing it when I’m struck, I’d rather get rid of it myself, so everyone can focus on the duel.”

“What a demonstration of humility! This is truly the temperament of the number one disciple, the leader of a generation. He shaved his own hair so the Baldinator can’t take it! What a great man!” Rollo Frollo yelled.

Thunderous clapping exploded from the stands.

“Well said, Feng Shen!”

“Show us the pride of the number one disciple!”

“I didn’t like you before, but now I do! You deserve everything you have!”

Amidst the cheers, however, a few people in the audience had a different thought.

“Hey,” a man whispered to his companions. “Since he shaved his head, doesn’t that mean he expects Gary Steel to hit him?”

“Holy shit. I thought this would be easy, but Feng Shen expects a battle!”

“He was so certain he’d be hit he shaved his own head!”

A wave of realization spread through the audience as the whispers did. On the sand, Gary couldn’t hear any of it. His eyes were glued to Feng Shen.

“I respect you,” Gary said. “Not even my weakest opponents in the preliminaries shaved their heads before facing me, but you did. You clearly know what matters. To fight you is my honor.”

“Your battle in the semi-finals was admirable. I share the honor,” Feng Shen replied.

“What a display of magnanimity from both fighters! They’re about to duke it out, yet they aren’t afraid to express their admiration for each other! The Heaven Reaching clan might have lost a genius, but to have two people sharing great strength and moral stature, this generation remains a blessing!” Rollo Frollo said.

Feng Shen smiled. “Shall we give everyone a battle to remember?” he asked.

“We shall,” Gary replied, drawing the Golden Bolt. Feng Shen did the same, unsheathing the black-and-white spear that had so far annihilated everyone it met.

“Can I ask you one thing?” Gary said.

“Go ahead.”

“You’re the greatest spear-wielder of our generation. How would you rate against the fallen prodigy, Kyle Mercy?”

If Feng Shen was surprised, he didn’t show it. “I had the good fortune of sparring against Kyle Mercy once,” he said. “We only exchanged light blows, but I wasn’t his equal. Even if I faced him now, with an extra six months of training, I’d be happy if I could make him go all-out. He was truly a fantastic spear-wielder.”

“I see,” Gary replied. He assumed a thrusting position. Feng Shen did the same.

Rollo Frollo’s voice rose high. “Gary Steel, Feng Shen… Fight!”
