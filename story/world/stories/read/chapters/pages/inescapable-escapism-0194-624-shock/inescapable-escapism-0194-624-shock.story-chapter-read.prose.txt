I stared up at Seth in shock. I hadn’t expected to find him standing outside my door, and I didn’t know how to react to it. I couldn’t react. Not really. My brain couldn’t handle it. It couldn’t handle forming words to any kind of thought because I was still fixated on the bed that should have still been in the room behind me, but it wasn’t.

Seth didn’t seem to have expected me to open the door either. I wasn’t sure how long he’d been standing there. He might have just reached my room, or he could have been standing there for an hour, however long it had been since I’d left him after dinner, but he looked taken aback.

And nervous. He looked nervous. He recovered quicker than I did to the shock of me opening my door, but his smile was still a little forced, and anxiety danced in his eyes.

“Sorry,” he said. “I was just coming to talk to you about… something.”

He fell quiet again as he lifted a hand to rub the back of his neck awkwardly, not quite meeting my eye. He didn’t say anything else, though. He didn’t clarify what he wanted to talk to me about or say anything more, and I just continued to stare up at him in silence.

I needed to reply, I realised belatedly. He must have been waiting for me to respond to him before he was going to continue, but I couldn’t do it. I felt as if the ability to speak had been stolen from me, and I was terrified that if I opened my mouth, I’d just sob.

Tears were already burning behind my eyes. I could feel them. They itched as I fought to hold them back, and there was a painful lump of emotion in my throat. Even if I somehow could work out what to say to Seth, I wasn’t sure that any words would be able to get out around the rock lodged there.

Seth’s eyes found me again, and his eyebrows began to furrow as he took in the sheen of cold sweat on my face and the too rapid movement of my chest, my breathing still barely controlled. The smile slowly dropped from his lips, and he stepped closer.

“What is it?” he asked, his tone concerned. “What’s wrong?”

“One of the beds is gone,” I said, finally finding my voice once more.

My words were barely above a whisper, and Seth leaned towards me instinctively. His eyes flitted back and forth between mine, his expression becoming confused.

“Gone?” he repeated. “What do you mean, gone?”

I swallowed, trying to force down the lump, but it just seemed to grow bigger. How did he not understand what I’d said? It felt obvious, straightforward, but he looked lost, and I didn’t know how to rephrase it.

“Gone. It’s not there,” I said, trying again. “It was before, but now, it’s not.”

The furrow between Seth’s eyebrows grew deeper. He continued to stare into my eyes for a few seconds longer before looking up. He was so tall that he could see into my room above my head, and I watched his confused expression slowly morph into one of horror.

His thoughts must have jumped to the worst-case scenario, just as mine did. That was the only way to explain the growing fear on his face. He must have realised exactly what had happened, and he stared into the room behind me before looking back at me.

“What—” he started, breaking off suddenly as his gaze flicked to the side, and his expression became even more fearful. “Weren’t there… weren’t there three nameplates on your door before?”

My head whipped around, and I stared at the door. Seth was right. There had always been three nameplates on the door, right at my eye level. One had my own name on it, and the other two were blank. They had been there ever since I left the induction wing, but suddenly, there was only one blank golden rectangle beneath my name.

How hadn’t I noticed it before? I must have seen it when I’d gotten back to my room, but I didn’t notice that one was gone. I was too tired, too exhausted by my day, and I had barely been paying attention, but now, all I could do was stare at the empty gap beneath the blank nameplate.

Another used to be there. I had no doubt. I could still see the outline of where the third one used to be. The varnish on the wooden door was slightly lighter in that spot. It must have been peeled away when they’d removed the other nameplate.

“Yeah,” I whispered, my eyes lingering on the door for a few seconds longer before I forced myself to look back at Seth. “What…”

That was as much as I could get out. My vision was starting to blur again. Seth’s terrified expression, mirroring the emotion burning within me, was starting to swim, but it was not another world threatening to steal me away that was causing the dizziness. I was hyperventilating.

“We…” Seth started, trailing off and taking a deep breath before he could continue. “We need to go find Rodgers. I’m sure it’s just… He’ll know what’s going on, right?”

It seemed like Seth was trying to sound confident, but his voice had trembled slightly, making it clear he was just as anxious as I was. He was right, though. We needed to go find Rodgers, just like I’d planned to before he’d shown up at my door. Rodgers had to know what was happening, and he’d be able to explain it to us.

I nodded, stepping out into the hallway. We rushed along it towards the stairs, darting around trainees who ambled at far too slow a pace, and I could feel them glancing at us curiously as we passed, but I didn’t stop to explain. Instead, I just sprinted as fast as I could.

It wasn’t too strange a sight. Trainees ran fairly often at the Academy. Normally, they were just running late for class or racing each other, and the tutors didn’t mind as long as they were careful, but we didn’t see a single tutor as we hurtled down the stairs.

By the time we reached the corridor containing the tutors’ offices, my heart was thudding in my ears. I could barely hear my own pounding footsteps, and I tasted blood. Pain burned in my side, and my breathing was ragged, but I refused to let it slow me as I bolted towards the door at the end.

My loud knock echoed down the corridor, and fear grew even tighter around my heart as I silently prayed to anyone who would listen to me to please, please let Rodgers still be there. I needed to know what had happened to Katie or Abbie, and I didn’t want to have to run all the way down to the labs or GSOD. I just… I just needed to know.

Movement came from behind the door, and my heart leapt into my throat. The burn of tears behind my eyes became so strong I had to blink hard in an attempt to smother the sensation, but it refused to be soothed.

The door opened, and the breath caught in my chest as I stared at Rodgers. Until that moment, I think I’d been clinging to the hope that I was wrong. Even though it felt unlikely, impossible, really, I really wanted to believe that it was all a misunderstanding. I hoped that I was just overreacting, but the moment I saw Rodgers, I knew I was not.

He looked exhausted. Dark bags had formed beneath his eyes, and his hair was messy. Some strands were standing up on end, as if he’d been running his hands through them all day, and his black t-shirt was wrinkled. I had no idea how long he’d been wearing it, but it looked like it had been far too long.

“I assumed you two would show up at some point tonight,” he said, with a tired, sad smile. “Come in.”

I couldn’t help but hesitate, glancing at Seth, who looked just as concerned by Rodgers’ unkempt appearance. I had to swallow and squeeze my hands into fists before I could force myself to follow Rodgers into his office, and with each step, I felt like my legs could give way.

Only one chair waited in front of his desk, but Rodgers pulled another from the edge of the room and gestured towards it.

“Take a seat.”

Immediately, I followed his instructions and dropped into it. The hard plastic was clammy beneath my legs, and I looked down in confusion. I was wearing pyjamas, I realised. I had changed into them earlier, before I realised something was wrong, but I had completely forgotten.

Seth sank into the chair behind me as Rodgers took his seat behind the desk. We stared at him as he began clearing the pile of papers, filled with dense writing, away into a light brown folder.

I assumed that he’d stop and talk to us once that folder was filled, but he didn’t. Instead, he just reached for the next stack of briefings that were strewn across his keyboard and began sorting them into a different folder, and I couldn’t work out whether it was necessary. Did he actually need to put the papers away, or could he just not bring himself to meet our gaze?

Anxiety and impatience warred within me as I continued to watch him fiddle with the paper, the emotions only growing stronger the longer the silence stretched out, and I wasn’t sure how much longer I could take it.

If we just stayed there, waiting for him to speak, he might never, I realised. But Seth was silent. He just continued to stare at Rodgers, but I couldn’t do it. I needed to say something.

“What happened?”

My voice was barely above a whisper, and I wasn’t sure whether Rodgers would have been able to hear me over the shuffle of paperwork, but his movement faltered. His hands froze for a second before carefully placing the papers he’d been holding on the table in front of him and taking a deep breath.

“There was an incident,” he said slowly.

Even though I’d been expecting those words, they still made my heart drop into the pit of my stomach.

“An incident?” I repeated.

“Yes.”

There was a pause as I stared at Rodgers, trying to bring myself to speak once more.

“What kind of incident?” Seth asked before I could.

A wince flitted across Rodgers’ face, and I felt sweat prickle as it broke out on my upper lip.

“Unfortunately, I cannot share that with you,” he replied hesitantly.

“What?” Seth said.

“Why not?” I asked at the same time.

A heavy sigh came from Rodgers.

“It’s Academy policy,” he told us. “Details of any incidents that happen within the induction wing aren’t to be shared with any trainees who have passed the induction period, and only details directly relevant to the physical and mental safety of those within the induction wing can be shared with those yet to pass the induction period.”

His tone and careful phrasing made it clear he was quoting something. I assumed it must have been the Academy policy, but he doesn’t sound happy about it.

“Why?” I couldn’t help but ask.

Rodgers hesitated before speaking.

“Because it’s meant to be easier this way. For everyone involved.”

I didn’t understand, and neither did Seth.

“What?” he muttered. “How can anyone think it’s easier for us not to know what’s going on?”

Rodgers pursed his lips, glancing down at the papers before him.

“Because some of the people who wrote these policies never trained at an Academy and don’t understand what it’s like,” he said, his unhappiness clear.

“But you did,” Seth pushed. “You trained here, right?”

“I did.”

“So you know what it’s like,” he continued. “You know that it’s worse not to know, right?”

“I do,” Rodgers said slowly. “But I still can’t break the policy. I’m sorry.”

“But I don’t get it,” Seth replied, sounding frustrated.

It didn’t matter, though, I realised as I stared blankly at the desk in front of me. I didn’t need to know the exact details of the incident.

“Who is it?” I interrupted before Rodgers could reply. “Who’s gone?”

Rodgers had been about to speak, but my question made him close his mouth. He looked at me for a couple of seconds, his expression growing sadder.

“Katie.”

Regardless of whether he’d said Katie or Abbie, it would have hurt, but the confirmation that one of my friends was gone made my heart shatter. I pressed a hand to my chest, trying desperately to hold it together as pain radiated through me.

“Oh,” Seth whispered.

“Is she…” I started before trailing off. It was too hard to say the word I’d been meaning to say. It had gotten stuck in my throat, refusing to go any further, and I had to swallow multiple times before I could speak again. “Is she dead?”

A sharp inhale came from Seth, and I saw him staring at Rodgers out of the corner of my eye. I wasn’t sure whether I truly wanted to hear the answer to my question, but I needed to know. I had to know whether one of my best friends had been killed.

“No,” Rodgers told us, his voice soft. “She’s still alive.”

Tears of relief welled in my eyes, slipping free before I could hold them back.

“But she was hurt, right?” Seth asked. “Something happened to her, didn’t it?”

“I can’t give any details,” Rodgers reminded us. “But… yes.”

Fear tightened around my throat, and I stared at him.

“Badly?”

I didn’t know why I’d asked that. I already knew the answer. If it hadn’t been bad, Katie would probably still be in the induction wing. They wouldn’t have taken her bed from our dorm room.

“Yes,” Rodgers replied, slowly.

It sounded as if the word had been hard for him to say, and it caused another tear to fall from my eyes.

“Is she okay?”

“She will be.”

A sob bubbled up in my throat, and I wasn’t sure if it was caused by relief or sadness. I was so glad that she would survive, but the fact that it had happened, that she’d been badly hurt, still broke my heart.

“Can she… will she be able to come back?” Seth asked hesitantly.

It sounded like he already knew the answer to that question, but just like with the question I’d asked before, he needed to say it. Part of me wished he hadn’t, though. Everyone in the induction wing, in the entire Academy, knew what happened if someone got badly injured during the induction period. They’d lose their chance at ever moving into the main building, and it would be over.

Rodgers’ face fell. The sad smile he’d managed to keep on his lips for the majority of our conversation finally dropped, and he shook his head.

“I wish she could, but… unfortunately, that won’t be possible,” he said softly. “She’s going to be in hospital for a while whilst she’s recovering, and she’ll have missed too much. It’ll be too hard for her to jump back in.”

Fear soared within me. I didn’t know how long Rodgers meant by that, but whatever had happened to her must have been bad if she was going to have to spend a while in the hospital. Perhaps she’d broken her leg or something. Maybe both legs. I wasn’t sure what could have caused that, but it didn’t sound too bad. It would have been painful, and I assumed it would take some time for them to heal. She’d need to learn to walk again, but that happened. People did that all the time.

Or maybe it was something worse. Perhaps she’d gotten shot. The bullet could have pierced something important, an organ or something crucial. Maybe—

My brain longed to continue to spiral, but I forced myself to stop. Rodgers had said it would be bad, but that she was okay. That was what I needed to focus on. She was still alive. She’d just been injured, but she’d recover, and that meant there was a chance I’d see her again.

Once she was better, once she’d healed from whatever happened to her, she’d be able to go back to school. Then, after she graduated, she could apply to the Academy again. We’d been told about that ages ago. The department in charge of the Academy kept an eye on the people who failed the induction period, especially those who failed for reasons that weren’t their fault. They monitored them, and as long as she was okay and wanted to come back, they’d give her the chance once she finished school.

A faint ray of hope began to flutter in the shattered remains of my heart, but it was quickly destroyed as a new fear gripped me.

“Wait,” I said slowly, looking up at Rodgers as my heart thumped in my ears. “What’s going to happen to her after? Like, after she gets out of hospital? She’s not going to go back home, is she? She can’t. It’s not safe for her. Her dad…”

I trailed off, unable to finish that sentence.

Her dad beat her. That’s what I was going to say, but the words refused to come out. I knew he did. I’d seen the bruises on Katie’s arms when we first got to the Academy, the finger-shaped marks that she’d tried to hide. She couldn’t be sent back to him. I didn’t know how bad it was, but I worried he’d hit her again. I was scared he might kill her.

Seth looked between Rodgers and me, his expression growing more and more troubled, but the sad smile just returned to Rodgers’ face.

“We’re aware,” he reassured me. “And don’t worry. She won’t be going back to him. As far as he’s aware, she’s remaining at the boarding school and will stay there.”

“Okay, good,” I said, sagging in my chair.

Rodgers hesitated. His eyes darted towards the papers in his hands, and it seemed like he was scanning the words written there for a few seconds before he spoke again.

“The department has already organised for her to be placed with a foster family once she recovers.”

Relief washed over me, but the emotion only just broke through the blanket of numbness that I could feel closing in on me.

“A foster family?” Seth repeated.

“Yes. They’re a very nice couple,” Rodgers told us. “I know them well.”

“You do?” I asked, sitting up slightly as hope pulled at me once more. “Do they live near here?”

Maybe I could go see her. If the family lived nearby, then surely Katie must have been in a hospital not too far away. I wasn’t sure how we’d do it. I knew contact with anyone who wasn’t a trainee, especially those who had failed the induction, wasn’t allowed, but there had to be a way we could get away from the Academy and find her.

“I’m afraid not,” Rodgers replied gently. “They live quite far away.”

“Oh,” was all I could say.

“It’s for Katie’s safety,” he continued. “So there’s no chance of her dad running into her.”

That made sense, and I was glad. Her safety was more important than my own selfish anxiety, but part of me was still disappointed. I still wished that there was a way I could see her again, but I knew the chances of that happening were small.

Silence settled over the room, and all I could do was stare at the table. A muted ringing sounded in my ears, and my thoughts felt weird. They were racing. I could feel them spinning frantically in my head, but I couldn’t actually hear them. They were out of my reach, distant and detached from me, and my brain was strangely quiet.

I felt nothing. The only sensation that I was aware of was the hollow ache in my chest, the sadness that would never truly be soothed. Katie was gone. One of my best friends had left my life, and she would not come back. I would never see her again.

“Do you have any other questions?” Rodgers asked softly after some time.

“None that you’ll answer,” Seth replied, his tone hollow. There was a pause before he added, “Sorry.”

Rodgers didn’t seem offended as he shook his head, though. He just seemed tired.

“Don’t be,” he said. “I know how infuriating this is. A similar thing happened when I was a trainee too.”

“But you still won’t tell us what happened, right?” Seth asked.

“I wish I could, but… I can’t. Whether or not I agree with it, I can’t go against the policy.”

“Okay,” Seth replied flatly.

There was a moment of silence before Rodgers spoke again.

“If you do think of anything, any other questions that you think I might be able to answer, come see me, okay?”

“Okay,” Seth repeated.

I could feel Rodgers’ eyes on me, but I did not look away from the spot on the desk that I was staring at. I wasn’t sure that I could. My gaze felt as though it had been glued to it, and all I could do was nod.

“Good,” Rodgers said. “Do you want to stay here a little bit longer? You can if you want.”

Neither Seth nor I spoke for a few seconds.

“No. It’s okay. I think…” Seth trailed off. “I think we’re ready to leave.”

That sounded like a question, and I could see Seth glancing at me out of the corner of my eye, but I didn’t look at him. Words were still evading me, but I nodded numbly.

Rodgers glanced between us again before sighing.

“Okay. Well… like I said, come back any time.”

“We will,” Seth promised.

He rose from his chair slowly, and it took me a moment to realise he was waiting for me to do the same. We were meant to be leaving Rodgers’ office, but I was still seated. My brain hadn’t quite realised that it meant I’d have to move until that moment, but I pushed myself to my feet.

My body felt weird. I could feel it moving. I was following Seth slowly towards the door, but I was numb. It felt as though my body was not my own, like it was being piloted by someone else, and I couldn’t force myself to return to it. I didn’t want to. If I did, it might cause all of the grief, frustration, and pain that I could feel floating above me to come crashing down, and I didn’t want that. I wasn’t sure I could cope with it.

Seth pulled the door open, stepping out into the corridor without another word, but I hesitated, looking back at Rodgers. He’d opened one of the folders again and was staring down at it, his expression pained. He didn’t even look up as I shut the door behind me.
