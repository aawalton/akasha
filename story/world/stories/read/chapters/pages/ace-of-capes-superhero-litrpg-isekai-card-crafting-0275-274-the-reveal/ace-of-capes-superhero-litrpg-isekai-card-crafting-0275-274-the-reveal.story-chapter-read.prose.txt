Lexie knew she was dreaming.

But somehow it didn’t matter. In her dream, she was no longer in pain. Her body wasn't screaming out in agony. Her mind wasn't in the middle of a bitter war.

On the contrary, she was happy.

It was different from the other dreams she'd had where she’d been a toddler.

Here, she was around six or seven years old, and she was holding hands with a woman as they walked along the sandy shore. The waves in the distance crashed, but the ones near the shore only gently lapped and extended up to Lexie’s bare feet, the coolness reducing the sting of the hot sand before they retreated.

It was a hot evening. The palm she was holding on to was sweating, but Lexie didn’t care. She didn't want to let go.

“Lex.” The woman holding her hand finally spoke up, and for the first time, her face came into view. Lexie’s breath caught. It was Lara. Her mother. “Are you okay?”

“Yeah,” she answered. “I think so.”

Her mother stopped to give her full attention. She gave a sad smile. “You know your father would be here if he could, don’t you?”

“You always say that,” Lexie grumbled.

“Because it’s always true.”

“No, it’s not. If he really wanted to be here, he would be. He just cares more about work than about me.” Lexie, at this point, had memories from being Lexie Evans, but had also regained her memory of Aiden and Lara being her parents. She knew how many times he'd already had to cancel on her growing up, and she had the added experience of being neglected in her past life as well.

So she wasn't buying the excuse.

Aiden wasn't working because he had to work. He was working because he found it preferable to do that than to spend time with her. And she knew why. “He doesn’t really care about me. Maybe because he knows I’m not his real daughter.”

“Don’t say that." Lara squatted so she could meet her eyes. “He does love you, and you are his daughter, no matter what you think.”

"No I'm not. I'm only half his daughter. The other half of me is a stranger. And with the way everything happened, who knows, maybe I even ate his daughter's soul up."

"I almost wish he hadn't told you about that part about how your souls combined. He thought it would make things better, but it only made it worse."

"He could have told me earlier."

"You know why he couldn't."

“Because every time he tells me something bad happens or I run away, and I lose my memory all over again?"

"Yes. We're trying it differently this time. But that's beside the point. The point is, your father loves you. He adores you, Lexie, you have no idea how much. He was on his way here, actually, but there was an incident in Alpeco–"

“It’s okay," Lexie interrupted. "I don’t need him here. It can just be us two.” She squeezed her mother’s hand, and the other woman grinned.

Their relationship had really transformed from contentious to familiar, really quickly, especially as Lexie regained more of her memories. In the beginning, she had also questioned Lara's affection for her and wondered if she only saw Lexie as a stranger who'd replaced her daughter.

But with time, those questions simply stopped entering her head.

Lara cupped her cheek. “While I’m delighted to win the favorite parent title, I don’t want to win it like this. Aiden works hard because he loves you, because he needs to afford expensive treatment for you."

‘But I’m fine now.”

“You’ve been hurt and lost your memory three times already. He’s trying to figure out why that’s happening, and that’s why he keeps working hard to pay all those researchers and healers. I would do the same thing, too, if I could.” She bit her lip in guilt before she shook her head. "He’ll meet us later. In the meantime, let’s enjoy the beach.”

“Okay." Lexie faced the waves, closing her eyes and inhaling the scent of salt and sea. "There were no beaches like this in New York."

Lara chuckled. "I grew up in a place with no beaches, too. No trees either."

Lexie faced her in shock. "Seriously?"

"It was more of an underground bunker than anything. There were about a hundred of us children all sharing one big room that was really just a hallway with mattresses. Every year, only a few of us would get to go above ground to join the Academy."

“That sounds terrible.”

"Yes, but it didn't seem as terrible back then. We were all orphans, and it was all we knew. Total isolation from the outside world was necessary to create good...agents. And we weren't abused. It was more boring than anything. So when I was finally called to test, I was just about ready to do anything so I would not have to go back there.” Her face rippled.

“What?" Lexie asked.

“I don’t know. Just thinking of all the mistakes I made.”

“Like what?”

“Nothing. Don't worry about it." She picked Lexie up. "Let's just enjoy the beach.”

And with that, fully clothed, she sprinted right into the waves, laughing at Lexie's desperate squealing to stop and paddling with her feet as Lexie clutched her shoulders.

Lara taught Lexie how to float that day. She also taught her what to do if she were ever drowning.

Lexie got ocean water in her nose a bunch of times, but in the end, she learned.

As the dream drifted farther away from her consciousness, Lexie’s eyes opened up to reality, a sense of loss lingering in her chest as she stared up at the dark wall of what seemed to be a cave.

Except it didn’t feel like she was lying on rocky ground. On the contrary, she was lying on a nice cushiony surface, with a blanket firmly tucked around her and a pillow underneath her head. She blinked as she sat up, trying to figure out what had just happened and how she ended up here. She had vague recollections of the battle at Indigo, falling out of the sky, and even attempting to teleport…did the teleport work?

Did it bring her here?

What was up with the blanket, the pillow, and the bed? Where was this place, even? Was she on Earth, or was she in her dungeon?

“Hello?” She called out, but there was no response. Her voice echoed in the hollow cave. She cracked her neck and analyzed herself internally, noting that she felt…strange. Mostly strained, but also her pathways stung.

That fight had done a number on her. What had the Emperor pumped into the atmosphere that had attacked her mana so viciously? That was awful.

Sure, it wouldn’t leave permanent damage for now, but eventually it might, and she probably shouldn’t push herself that much again.

She would find out what the Emperor used later, and she would pay him back, too.

But more importantly, she needed to figure out where she was and how she got here.

As her thoughts flitted from one topic to another without stopping, she finally sighed and got to her feet. She ventured outside and stared at the beach.

Somehow, she knew it was the same beach in her dream. It was the same pattern of waves, the same white sands, the same heat, even though it was technically winter time.

"Only my dad would know where this is,” Lexie murmured. Who else would bring her here? And she distinctly remembered scenting her father as she passed out.

Was it Aiden who brought her here? Was he back? Did he somehow teleport her here to save her life? How did he even know she was in trouble? The tag in her head?

Or maybe...

Maybe he'd left an envoy behind. Like a golem, just like the one he'd made for Tate.

But why wouldn't he tell her about it?

She should call him. Was he back online? How much time had passed exactly?

Lexie checked her cell phone, which had a number of calls, most recently from her Uncle Max. A lot of messages too. The calendar showed that she’d been out for over a day. Well, almost two days, actually.

From the deranged messages she'd received from her Uncle Max, he didn’t know where she was either. If Aiden had a golem, wouldn't he tell Uncle Max where to find her so he didn't panic? Or he would tell Tate at least.

Tate had sent one message to Lexie to the tune of, Everyone is losing their mind. Please call back if you’re available.

Lexie smirked. How official-sounding. That was probably him trying to control his own panic.

Doyle and Stanley had also texted, We had to give a statement on your behalf. Please call when you have time.

Give a statement? For what?

Lexie figured it had to do with her crime of killing Vulcan. Maybe the association had gone public and released a wanted poster for her or something because they thought she'd gone into hiding.

Lexie sighed. Well, that was probably the first thing on the agenda that she needed to fix. She didn't want to go into hiding at all, meaning that she would have to make peace with the heroes.

As annoying as diplomacy was–especially when she knew she was mostly right–Lexie couldn’t do what she wanted to do, emancipating Earth from the shackles of the Fae, without the help of the heroes and several Earth leaders who were responsible for creating the oath anyway. If they were the ones in charge of the oath, then they would be the ones needed to break the oath if need be. Most of the people who had signed the original oath were already dead, but maybe their representatives could break the oath in their place. Either way, she needed to work with them and convince them of her ideology, ultimately, if she wanted to be taken seriously.

Strength wasn’t everything. Monty was right. She needed to give them a chance to believe her, show them evidence of what she knew, and bring them around to their way of thinking.

Remnants of the dream still trickled in Lexie's mind even as she mentally itemized everything she needed to do when she got home.

Before she left, she glanced at the bed and blanket again. They didn’t look familiar, but there was no way they were just hanging out in this cave waiting for her to teleport in. The linen was far too clean.

“Thank you,” she said to the echoey cave.

There was no response, as she disappeared.

Lexie appeared in front of her house. It was a quiet late afternoon.

She assumed no one was around until she heard something whizzing in the air.

When she blinked, she saw it glinting against the sun. A PHORB.

It attempted to buzz closer, but it seemed ot be held off by an invisible barrier.

Just as Lexie was about to shoot it down, her door opened, and a hand pulled her in. Uncle Max took a second to shoot the PHORB out of the sky himself, yelling, “Leave us alone, you freaking lunatics!” before he slammed the door again.

Then he was in her face. “Where the heck have you been?”

She shrugged. “I don’t know. After I teleported out of Indigo, I passed out. I woke up in a cave like five minutes ago.”

“A cave? Why on Earth would you go there?”

“I'm not sure it was my choice.” Lexie barely got the words out before pounding footsteps preceded Tate's appearance at the end of the steps.

He exhaled in relief when he saw her. “You’re okay.”

"Yeah, I’m fine.”

“We’re the opposite of fine," Max said. "Tate was going crazy with worry."

Tate cocked an eyebrow. "I'm not the one who called every single person he knows and tried to ride his bike to Indigo to find you himself."

"They put out an APB for you," Max continued as though Tate hadn't spoken. "The media has been going crazy. They got footage of what you did in Indigo, and they’ve been replaying it on every single news channel bar none.”

“You’re the number one trending video on Video Alley," Tate said.

“Oh." That sounded major. "Is that why so many people are calling me? I thought the heroes had me on a wanted poster or something.”

“They wouldn’t dare. Not with the way the public loves you now.”

“They do?”

Tate gave her an incredulous look. “They literally watched you single-handedly stop the annihilation of everyone in Indigo. Emperor Rufus dumped damn near fifty bombs on the city, and you stopped almost all of them.

“Almost?”

"Well, you missed one toward the end, but that’s not important. The damage was far less than would have been caused if you weren't there."

It was also more damage than would have happened if Lexie were at her full capacity. Dungeon Lexie would have had no problem stopping everything that came her way. So would King Lexie. She missed that piece of her.

“In any case, everyone is talking about you.”

“I should call Doyle and Stanley.”

“Who?” Max called.

“My PR agency.”

He stared at her. “You have A PR agency.”

"Yup. They gave a statement on my behalf."

"Oh, that's who that was?" Max wondered aloud. "I thought that guy on the news was just a lunatic pretending to know you. I almost sent someone to teach him a lesson."

"You should probably call your friends first, though," Tate said. "They were really worried after you disappeared.

Lexie thought about it and shook her head. The conversation with Xena and Dewie was bound to be extremely difficult, emotional, and not something she could do while she had her mind on other things. She needed to be able to give them her full attention.

For now, the time-sensitive matters had to be handled first.

"Actually, before that, Max, can you call Stella and let her know I’m home. If you have her number, that is. Tell her to please come alone. I’d rather just talk to her than the entire hero association."

Max nodded and set about to do just that.

It didn't take long for Stella to arrive. Lexie had barely gotten Doyle on the phone to ask him about this supposed statement when an anxious knock was on the door.

"I'll get it," Lexie told Max as she hung up. She made sure to check the peephole before she opened.

Stella had come alone as promised.

Lexie opened the door. "Hey, Stella, I–"

Lexie's words cut off as Stela grabbed her and hugged her, squeezing the breath out of her.

"Stella?" Lexie's voice was muffled against Stella's shoulder as the older woman exhaled heavily. Her arms shook, even as they eased a little.

"Are you okay?" she murmured.

"Yeah," Lexie managed to say. "I teleported and passed out. But I'm fine now."

"Both of you get inside," Max called. "I don't need a stray PHORB getting wind of this tearful reunion."

Stella nodded and finally released Lexie to walk into the house, closing the door.

"I'm sorry," Lexie said. "About the Vulcan thing."

Stella shook her head. "Lexie, we don't need to talk about that right–"

"Yeah, we do. I should have...I should have trusted you guys to handle it. Or at least trusted you enough to tell you the truth about why I did what I did, and why I believed what I believe."

Confusion ruffled her eyebrows. The room grew still with both Max and Tate freezing.

"Lexie," Max spoke up in warning. "Are you sure?"

Lexie wasn't sure.

Trust didn't come as easily to her as it once did, and she knew this could backfire swifly.

But she also knew that she couldn't earn trust doing what she was doing. Max and Tate both trusted her because they knew the truth. They knew what drove her and what she was capable of. They knew what she had experienced.

Maybe if Stella could understand...she would trust Lexie too.

Lexie needed someone she could rely on in the Association, someone to be on her side. She'd trusted Vacek with this information, and now he was gone. So she needed someone else, and it could only be Stella.

"Yeah," Lexie said. "I'm sure."

"Sure about what?" Stella asked.

"There's something I need to tell you about myself. It's very personal, and it will probably change how you see me. It might even make you want to hurt me, but... I need to trust you.

"O...kay." Stella looked spooked. "What is it?"

"Let's go up to my room. We'll talk there."

That made Stella look even warier, but she nodded.

"Keep an open mind, Stella," Max called out as they walked up. "That's all I ask."

"That doesn't really bring much comfort, Max, but thanks."

Lexie didn't say anything until they were in her room. As she closed the door, Stella looked around, a half smile on her lips.

"I remember the last time I was here," Stella said. "I told your mother that I never imagined the day she would have floral-themed wallpaper in her home. She hated flowers."

"She did?"

"Yeah." The wistful expression on her face made Lexie remember her dream and the lingering emotions from earlier.

The dream.

Lexie and Lara had been much closer than she'd thought. The affection she'd had in the dream was real, her sadness at the loss still beating at her.

Whenever she'd had dreams of them together in the past, it was always with baby Lexie, and she assumed Lara accepted her because at that point, she was still mostly Lexie Sparrowfoot.

She didn't consider Lara forging a genuine relationship with Lexie Evans, too.

She didn't want to think about that right now.

"How come the heroes didn't put out a wanted poster for me after I disappeared?" Lexie asked Stella.

"Well, what you did in Indigo was kind of incredible, so we figured we should give you a head start," Stella joked. "Also, frankly, they kind of have better things to worry about.”

“Like the Emperor?"

"Exactly."

"What’s going on?”

Her humor vanished. “His act was pretty much a formal declaration of war. We tried to avoid it, and Jerry's still attempting negotiations, but the political faction…well, they can’t exactly let something like that slide, as much as they might want to. So we’re getting dragged into it anyway. Even if we don't directly fight in the wars, the heroes have to try to mitigate the damage on either side. That's a lot of work."

Lexie nodded. Her cards were even more important now than ever before.

"Okay, so here's the thing that I should tell you," she said. She gestured to her desk. "Can you sit? It's kind of a long story."

Stella obediently sat. "What's this about Lexie?"

Lexie tried to figure out how to ease into it. She thought of telling the story in a way that didn't implicate Aiden and Lara. Or in a way that didn't make it seem quite as major.

Then she decided to just jump into it.

"I'm half-Eldritch."
