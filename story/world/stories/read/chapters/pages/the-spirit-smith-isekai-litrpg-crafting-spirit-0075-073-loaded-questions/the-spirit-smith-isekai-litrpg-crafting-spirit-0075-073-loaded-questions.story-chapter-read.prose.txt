“First question, if you primordials are so powerful, why can’t you fight the malice?” I ask. “Surely it wouldn’t stand a chance if even a single one of you unleashed your full power.”

Valdir takes his time sipping his tea. “Starting off with the loaded stuff, I see. As for why? Very simply, why would we fight malice? It does nothing to make us any stronger, and really, malice’s existence only benefits us by strengthening others that might one day rise to challenge us. Oh and there is the small matter of collateral damage. Malice is generated by people, who tend to gather in cities. Sure, I could sneeze and wipe said city off the map, but that isn't very ideal, now is it?

“That isn’t to say all of us avoid fighting malice. I know of several who help to empower mortals, giving them strength to better fight malice. Or other ways of indirect contribution.”

So in essence, they are all so powerful and lazy that they can’t bother helping unless there is anything in it for them. Typical. Though I cannot really fault them either. I have come to realize that part of what drives this world is the inherent drive to do anything to get stronger. To become a primordial, I imagine one must be quite familiar with that pursuit. Fighting malice simply isn’t going to help them fulfill that pursuit.

“Alrighty then.” I take a sip of my tea, which is truly divine. Like seriously, it must be divine rarity. I have to be very careful and force myself to not just knock it back instantly. “How does magic work? Like, I know the basics. But for some reason, I seem unable to break the barrier to cast apprentice tier spells.”

Valdir laughs, “Geez, you are really just going for it. I did promise you knowledge, though, and I do not plan to step back now. Magic is fundamental, to put it simply. Quite literally everything in this world is magical in nature. The air we breathe, the ground our feet rest upon. Spells are just taking that magic and bending it. Elemental affinities, as I think they are called, simply mean someone has an innate understanding of some part of magic. Technically, they can be learned. But I do not recommend that unless you have a damn good reason to pursue it.”

I slowly sip my tea as Valdir casually blows my understanding of this new world wide open. What the fuck does he mean you can learn affinities? The other stuff makes sense, I guess. But I can tell this is only the start…

“As for your specific problem, well you lack understanding. Which makes sense, given you are not from this world. To those of this world, magic is life itself. Shaping it is as simple as saying a few words, a magical language as some might call it. I cannot tell you how this language works, for understanding it is unique to each person. There are many ways that the scholars of old came up with to teach their disciples, but in my opinion, only one is truly valid.

“Simply put, you must feel the magic. It seems you have pretty decent control of stamina and vital energies, at least for your age. Well, those same principles apply to mana, though it is far from intuitive. A lot of the natural sense is lost when you use a spell and let the world system do most of the heavy lifting. Convenient, but a roadblock that must be overcome to access all but the most basic magic.”

Hmm, so I need to learn how to cast spells without the system? That is quite a good point, now that I think about it. When I cast a spell, it feels like the system takes over my body for just a moment. Perhaps it isn't helping me say an incantation, but forcing me to in a way that prevents me from understanding it myself.

“So uh, how would I figure that stuff out?” I ask the ever wise being.

“You practice. For decades, centuries, millennia, however long it takes. First start with meditation, focus on extracting the magical language yourself. Understand how it applies to you, and so on. It is quite instinctive once you cross the first hurdle.”

Millennia? He says it so casually, but I don’t have that long. Sure, I have what could be centuries, perhaps even a millennia or at most two. But I also have a feeling that whatever plan the goddess has for us heroes isn't going to let me sit around and fumble through magic for that long.

To help soothe my mind, I grab one of the pastries. They are a nice golden brown color, and I think they are filled with some sort of jam. The moment I bite into it, a rich flavor fills my entire body. It reaches every corner, filling me with pure delight. When I get to the jam part, I fear that the sweetness will kill me. Though in a good way.

Still, I think Ari’s cookies are better. Not that I am likely to have anymore…

“Hmm okay, I think that's about as much magic stuff as you can tell me, right?” I say, “I bet you have a lot more to share, but I likely need those centuries of magical foundation first. The problem is, I don’t have that much time. So I will simply have to get creative.”

Valdir considers me for a moment. “Well, I wish you luck in that endeavor. To be frank, were you not a hero, I would doubt you. But you are a hero, and those bearing that title tend to defy standard logic.”

“That leads nicely into another question of mine.” I say, “What the fuck is a hero? Like, I know about the whole fighting malice stuff. Plus I get some special powers and whatnot. But am I just meant to do whatever? Follow the whims of the goddess? Why couldn’t she just empower some random person from this world. Why summon me of all people?”

Valdir smiles at me, “I think you have already answered your own question.”

“How?”

“Asking questions like you are. ‘What is magic’, ‘What is a hero’, and many more to come, I’m sure. These questions are not normal, so to say. None from this world would think to ask those questions. It would be like asking why the sky is blue, or why dragons don’t fart.”

“Wait, dragons don’t fart?” I ask, getting completely sidetracked.

“Well, technically we do. What exactly do you think the fire breath is? Magic amplifies it, of course. But the core of dragonfire is a fart, really. Took me bloody ages to figure that part out.”

At that, I burst out into a fit of laughter that makes my ribs hurt. After I am done wheezing, I return to the original train of thought. “So the goddess summons us because we are from different worlds?”

Valdir shrugs, “Keep in mind, that is merely my own theory. But it does make sense. By now I trust you have learned of the cycle of this world?” I nod. “The stagnation of this world between eras? Well the very reason that ever breaks is because of how different each hero is. For better or for worse, the cycle is what drives progress, and whatever other philosophical bullshit one wants to spin. I study magic and spirits, not the whims of the goddess.”

My poor mind is starting to hurt a bit from all this information. This is like world shattering stuff. But somehow Valdir says it all in a way that mostly makes sense. Maybe he has a skill for teaching? Or perhaps it is just a side effect of having so much experience.

Either way, I am not going to hesitate in asking more questions. “Hmm, okay. How about a lighter question. What are stars?”

Valdir laughs again, “That is your definition of a lighter question? Not that I mind, answering such things has given me things to consider as well. Stars… where do I start? They mark power in a more absolute sense than levels. While a level ten may be able to fight a level fifty, given buffs and whatnot. A zero star will find it near impossible to fight a one star. Those at the peak of a star may be able to beat the weakest of a star above, but to anyone else, the gap is practically insurmountable.

“Hence why the realms exist. They act as containers, to minimize the mixing of different star levels. Aside from the last one, that is. The Realm of Elementals is a mix of three four and the occasional five star. Though, that is not something for you to worry about yet.

“That is all pretty obvious though. As for something more interesting… Well, stars represent fundamental change. When undergoing a grade up, the corestone literally evolves. Classes improve, stats improve, skills may upgrade, and powerful abilities may be awakened as well. In a way that can simply not happen outside of a total restructuring of the corestone.”

“So, I want to get to higher stars as fast as possible? I have sort of just been trying to level as fast as possible.”

“That is one approach. You may find it difficult to reach the next star though. Rushing levels is great in the short term, but you often spend just as much time as you otherwise would’ve just trying to actually hit evolution point. The process is too complicated for me to explain, you will simply just have to wait and experience it yourself.

“To put it simply, leveling fast is fine. Just know that you end up having to study and do a bit of spirit searching regardless of how fast you level. Just make sure to be aware of your limits and not fight something you cannot beat. Levels are not absolute, just as a level eighty could beat a level sixty, a properly equipped and knowledgeable level sixty could in theory beat a level eighty.”

I sigh in relief. Seems my rapid leveling won’t be an issue. It seems I have quite a lot of thinking and reflecting to do, though. Which isn't the worst thing, I would much prefer smashing things and crafting new gear though.

That is a later problem though. I still have more questions to ask, my next one being one of the most important. “Next one, then: What is Luma?”
