Sally leaned over, hands on her knees, as sweat dripped from her face onto the ground. “Feels like my skeleton is radioactive.”

The wizard gave her a brief, sympathetic shrug. “If a lack of water makes you dehydrated, then draining your ‘mana’ to this degree has a similarly negative effect.”

“Never felt this exhausted from a fight before.” She groaned as she stood back up and wiped her forehead with her arm. “That’s saying something, given what I’ve been through. I guess it has something to do with the new System, huh?”

“In part, yes. A few near-death experiences, utilizing your skills past their normal limit repeatedly, and a new, unsettled System trying to power you.”

She pulled a face and looked around the small town they had teleported to. “Limit testing,” she murmured.

Archie raised his hands to look them over. “It’s going to be an interesting experience seeing Sanctuary from the eyeline of… someone not a cat.”

“How are you feeling about that?”

“Hmm. I cared little for shapeshifting in my old life. Whatever process shattered my soul and gave it life on Sanctuary might not have been totally in my control.” He shook his head. “Either way, what’s done is done. All we can do is move forward.”

“Mission accomplished?”

“Complete destruction of the scouting Radoch force. Retrieval of my soul shard. A powerful ally met. Sounds pretty good to me.”

Sally wrinkled up her nose and tried to see past the houses down the street toward the horizon. “You really think he can destroy a whole planet?”

Archie followed her gaze, but remained silent for a moment. “Unfortunately, yes. The Omen is already making its way back home. Once Chuck gives us the all-clear, we’ll leave.”

“Can’t wait.” The zombie shook her head. Long-haul flights were such a drag.

Theo bared his fangs as the grasping hand of the giant monster dragged him against the courtyard. He clicked his fingers and the dozen blood orbs hovering in the air exploded, bursting thick chunks of flesh along the outstretched arm pinning him to the ground.

The many maws of the Amalgamation Horror screeched out in pain, and the large hand released the vampire.

Max wiped the blood from the side of his mouth, his eyes glowing bright purple. “Quite the critic we have today,” he grunted. With a growl, he tensed his body. Three purple cards buzzed around him, doubling every second to become a whirlwind of razor-sharp arcane projectiles. The magician’s hat fell from his head as two horns grew from his head. With a flash of fanfare, two leathery wings burst from his back.

An explosion bloomed on the monster, bursting several of its many eyes. Ren racked her rifle, her face expressionless. A spiral of blue light ran down the barrel as she adjusted her aim slightly.

Theo pushed himself up to his feet. Blood soaked through the back of his torn suit. It was beginning to grate on his nerves that he didn’t have his usual bag of tricks. Being the living Aspect of Death was a little hollow without the overpowered abilities that came with it. Against most foes, that wasn’t an issue.

But the Amalgamation was something else. A building-sized god that looked like a mix of an octopus, a bull, and a four-armed titan. Hardier than the Jawgores, and currently undeterred by any of their attacks.

His fangs glimmered in the light as he put on a humorless grin. Bright white light illuminated his round glasses as he pushed them up. A breeze rustled through the bloodied courtyard, and the sky darkened.

Behind them, a bright moon rose into the sky.

Theo withdrew a sword from his Inventory and flicked it out to the side. Deep crimson painted every edge of the dungeon as the risen skylight became a blood moon.

//Chuck: Thought I’d give you a head’s up.//Chuck: Sally and the Omen are on their way home.//Theo: Noted.

His mood calmed and his smile grew less grim. One monster stood between him and reuiniting with his better half. Several limbs rose into the air, ready to strike down toward his position. When did lack of power ever stop him?

With a flourish, he sidestepped the slamming strike of a thick tentacle. A shadow version of himself mirrored the action, standing on the other side of the attacking limb. The vampire and his clone stabbed their swords down through the tough flesh of the tentacle, barely piercing through to muscle. The pair then placed a foot on the creature’s limb and levered their weapons against each other.

As the sharp edges split open a larger wound, Theo leaned forward and slammed a bottle of poisonous liquid into the cut. The reflexive convulsion of the tentacle whipped through the air, splitting his shadow in half and knocking the vampire a dozen feet away. The pointed end of the injured limb turned and darted toward him like a spear.

Flat-footed, he raised his sword up to deflect the strike. Before it could strike him, a flash of electricity flickered around the tentacle. It shriveled back, waylaying the attack against the vampire. Ren had fired on it. He took the opportunity to flash forward, severing the tip of the pained tentacle.

Max was a whirling dervish, flickering in and out of view, bright lights and colorful explosions masking his demonic form. With each appearance, he whipped a loose chain of floating cards at the monster, carving deep red lines into its flesh.

A white dove flew beside Theo, before it vanished and Ren replaced it. With a dull expression, she held out a pouch full of glass bottles. “Wolf is a much better tank than you.”

He smiled as he popped the cork on a restorative potion. “Monsters usually have the courtesy to die before I get hit too much.”

The elf grunted. “We have a few aces up our sleeves, but if those options don’t work…”

Retreat was always an option, even though Theo was reluctant to consider it. He shot the demonic magician a glance, as the man currently had the full attention of the massive creature. Even as blinding white cards sliced off thick fingers from an outstretched hand and several hounds harassed the other side of the monster, it felt like they weren’t making any progress.

His brow furrowed as something tickled at his sixth sense. “Find some safe ground,” he told Ren. “Phase two.”

With a brief nod, she vanished again, leaving behind the faintest cloud of sparkling glitter.

Theo had just enough time to retrieve his sledgehammer from the ground before his premonition came true. The entire area vibrated as the Amalgamation roared. A deep line ran across its wide body, splitting apart as if it had been disemboweled. Rather than spilling internal organs, the torn flesh revealed hundreds of sharp teeth.

Max vanished from the melee to appear near the vampire. His wings faded away as the horns on his head fell to the ground. “I have a plan,” he stated, turning tired eyes to Theo. “How do you feel about being stuffed inside a cannon and eaten?”

Before he had the chance to respond, another uncomfortable feeling ran through Theo. This time it was something very visceral and present. The trio looked at the creature as a dark shape began emerging from its newly formed mouth.

With a wet, retching noise, a boulder-sized chunk of meaty skin rolled out of its maw. After lumbering for a dozen steps, it stopped in place. As the Guardian brought up its broken limbs to eat at them, pointed bone shards burst through the outer skin of the meatball.

“Probably best you be in charge of the party teleport,” Theo murmured.

Max said nothing, but nodded briefly. He could activate things from his Inventory almost with a thought, so that made sense. His focus was currently on empowering a single card, his eyes focused on the large blob.

The bone tore away at the flesh, as whatever lay inside was born. Chunks of bloody flesh slopped onto the courtyard floor. More bone became visible until the shell of the foul egg reached a weak point and burst apart.

At least ten creatures clawed their way forth. Seven-foot tall slim beasts who had bird-like skulls and long, pointed arms. Not quite like wings, but not exactly like mantis blades either. The newly formed group cawed at the sight of the humans, some shaking the slime off their birthing material off first.

“Focus on the Guardian,” Theo said, and pushed his glasses up. “I’ll clear the distraction.”

“The stage is all yours.” Max was too tense to give him a brief bow. “Don’t be a hero, now.”

Theo grinned. Now that Chuck could project himself to other worlds, it was a little less pressure. Dying to this Guardian wouldn’t be great, but he was sure the Architect would find a way to communicate with the magicians and get their approval to absorb the world into Sanctuary. Worst-case scenario, they could send Sally or someone else over since the Omen was done with its mission.

Not that he wasn’t going to give this his all, of course.

Pink light illuminated the end of his hammer as he darted forward. Light and fanfare bloomed around him as the elf made him a more appetizing target for the new monsters. [Novice Strike] lashed through the air, striking the first monster before it could raise an attack of its own.

Bone crunched, and Theo was already twirling to swing at the second. Another hit, knocking back the second Bone Warden. As the third strike began, his action speed increasing, the creatures got their first attack off.

Sharp bone tore through his right arm. He rolled across the ground into a downward attack that shattered the knee of another monster. As the ever-increasing speed of [Novice Strike] took him into another attack, he moved into a wide one-handed swing behind him while his free hand went for another healing potion.

It did hardly anything.

He realized too late that the new monsters had a healing debuff aura. In the middle of ten of them, the potion had just been a waste of time. Not that he had the option of escaping now, as they were surrounding him. Two more speedy strikes with the sledgehammer rewarded him with more wounds of his own. Not ideal.

Ren reloaded her rifle. Creating the skeletons had clearly drained some strength from the Amalgamation, as her shots were doing more damage. As she readied her next attack, she glanced over at the vampire. He was clearly having a tough time of it, but he was earning them all this time to weaken the Guardian.

She hesitated and worked her jaw as Theo destroyed a monster, only for one of the Bone Wardens to put a spiked limb straight into the back of his neck and out through his throat. Rather than panic, the vampire simply retaliated, breaking apart another assailant before vanishing.

In a cloud of dark energy, he appeared behind Max. Ren fired, her eye half-trained on the pair. She watched as Theo whispered something to the magician before plunging his fangs down.

The real Max reappeared a dozen feet away from the scene and gave her a quick signal. Go all out. With a stressed sigh, she switched her intended next attack.

Theo lumbered back toward the fray, the fresh blood taken from the magician healing over some of his wounds. He was still bleeding profusely all over. Without his usual regeneration, fighting with such an open stance just got him torn up. He snapped ahead and burst the skull with a long jab, turning to bring the sledgehammer back in a blocking position.

All the explosions and flashing lights in the background became a muted noise as he focused. If only he had his full powers. Without using his signature skill, he was taking less damage, but opportunities to attack were becoming slimmer. Bone rang against metal as he deflected one attack, only for a slim bone to come in from the other side, piercing his thigh.

He dropped to one knee, turning to block another attack, quickly reposting to break ribs. Blood soaked through his trousers. Two points of pain radiated from his back as the monsters crowded around him. His shirt stuck to his body. The warmth of his life-force draining from a dozen wounds made him lethargic.

The next swing of his weapon missed. Wrist impaled and his hand became numb. Back slashed again. Hammer dropped to the ground with a clang. Before he could go into his Inventory for another weapon, a Warden impaled him through the chest with both pointed arms, lifting him from the ground.

Theo looked down at the hollow-eyed creature. Blood bubbled from his mouth as his lagging hand pushed up his glasses so that they caught the moonlight.

“I am become Death,” he whispered.

All the blood shed, whether lying sprayed across the flagstones or upon enemy weapons, erupted with a deafening explosion. As a thrumming shockwave blew through the dungeon, both Theo and the remaining skeletons were vaporized.
