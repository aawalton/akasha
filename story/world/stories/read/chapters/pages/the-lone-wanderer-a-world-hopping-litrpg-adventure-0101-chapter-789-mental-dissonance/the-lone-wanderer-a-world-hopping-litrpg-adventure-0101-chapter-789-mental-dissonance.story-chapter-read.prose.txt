Percy’s planning was interrupted by the formerly-plumb vendor waving at him from behind the counter.

Percy returned the greeting with a soft smile of his own, once again amazed by how much weight his old friend had lost. He still saw Freddy regularly – mostly whenever the guy brought him Honey Rolls and other supplies in his residence or inside the hive, yet Percy couldn’t help his surprise each time they met.

Freddy was one of the few people in the Guild who still hunted Starry Soldiers atop the giant mushrooms. There was no longer an outside need for that, because the royal wasps were more than happy to donate as much nectar as the alchemists needed, and stronger mages couldn’t get much practice out of the dim-witted creatures. Not to mention that it was more efficient to turn the oldest and most defective bugs into test subjects for Percy’s experiments or fertilizer for magical reagents.

However, there were still many wasps harvesting stray motes of ambient mana atop the caps of the giant fungi, and nobody at the top cared if a few people at the lower grades wanted to hone their spells against the creatures.

Freddy had admittedly given up hunting years before Percy first joined the Guild, so it was quite ironic that he’d chosen to come out of retirement at a time when nobody else bothered. When asked about it, he’d explained that he didn’t feel good lazing around after receiving a second core and so many resources from Percy. It wasn’t like he had any ambitions of attaining divinity or becoming a powerful mage, but he felt less guilty if he got some exercise and at least learned how to protect himself.

“It’s rare to see you outside,” the red-haired man said. “Couldn’t wait until my next delivery?”

Percy chuckled. “Something like that. There’s been a lot on my mind lately, and I figured that stretching my limbs might help me organize my thoughts.”

“You look different,” Freddy noticed while handing him his Honey Roll. “Paler… and slightly leaner, maybe? Don’t tell me that I’m not feeding you enough!”

“Don’t worry about it. It’s just a side-effect of some recent changes on my body,” Percy explained. “It’s a good thing. I’m a lot stronger. But I do burn food slightly faster now that you mention it, so it might not be a bad idea to go a little harder on the snacks.”

Percy deliberately avoided mentioning his new core, thanking his lucky stars that it wasn’t visible on the outside and that he could easily hide its mana signature using the seals sprinkled in his phantom mana. He didn’t mind hinting at his upgraded mutation, because the information wouldn’t really affect the war as much, yet his third core was much more of a strategic weapon.

Percy expected his new asset to greatly increase his strength – eventually, at least – so he’d rather not let the news leak to his enemies. Not that he didn’t trust Freddy, but there was no point in going around advertising the information to anyone but Nesha and the leaders of the alliance.

“I’ll see what I can do. Your girlfriend will rip me a new one if I end up making you fat, though,” Freddy replied with a smirk. That earned him another chuckle from Percy.

“By the way, the new robes look good on you,” Percy said before taking off, not sticking around for a response.

The six elders and the patriarchs of the allied Houses had been talking about introducing a normalized uniform for both the alchemists and soldiers, and it looked like they’d already started rolling it out. Much like the Guild’s original robes, the new ones were colour coded based on a person’s grade. However, the designers had apparently taken some inspiration from the tales of Percy’s interstellar adventures – namely his time on Thess’kala.

There still weren’t that many mages on Remior capable of fighting above their grade, but there were quite a few who had been given the Moirais’ Decree. Those people received customized robes with trimmed cuffs and collars, matching the colour of their second core. Freddy, in particular, was wearing yellow robes with an orange trim.

‘Good for him. At least I’ve made some people’s lives better,’ Percy thought as he flew back to his residence. ‘I just need to grow strong enough to help everyone else… and actually keep our planet safe.’

With the tension in his body having eased slightly and his resolve rekindled, he shut himself inside his room and began experimenting according to his familiar’s instructions.

He started by activating his bloodline.

The ghostly claw tried to appear inside his first core as it usually did. Percy had to actively resist that urge, and to redirect the ability to the new cavity inside his glabella. A similar construct manifested within the spherical organ. After feeding on his mind mana for a second or two, it shot out of his core and bounced around his skull.

Percy felt his brain being torn to pieces, though it was more uncomfortable than genuinely painful. It was almost like pulling apart a pair of pages that had stuck together, though the pages in this case were three-dimensional and made of brain matter.

As relieved as he was when the magical claw faded away without killing him, he still wasn’t sure what exactly had changed. Unlike his soul clones, nothing had shot out of his body this time – whatever the bloodline had done had been restricted to the contents of his skull.

‘Makes sense, I suppose.’

Generally speaking, minds couldn’t exist on their own. They had to inhabit either physical bodies or souls, unless they found themselves within a special environment that inverted those dynamics – such as Nephthys’ internal world. In other words, Percy would be unable to deploy his mind clones on the outside, assuming that this was what he had just created.

His right hand suddenly rose by itself, startling him.

For a second, he was scared that Baphomet was trying to possess him again, though it didn’t take long for his rationality to come up with a more plausible explanation. He tried to lower his arm, feeling some resistance initially. Whoever was fighting for control over his limb quickly relented, allowing him to do as he pleased.

How bizarre…

Percy had a pretty good idea what was happening. He was obviously dealing with the mind clone that he had just created, though he wasn’t sure whether it was even accurate to refer to his counterpart as such.

When creating soul clones, Percy had no trouble distinguishing them from his main body. Even though they started off with the same memories and personality, the main body was, by definition, whichever version of Percy remained in control of his flesh. In this case, both copies appeared to have full access to his body, making the distinction meaningless.

Then again, it didn’t really matter.

Apart from a couple of silly arguments here and there, Percy had never had any issues playing nice with his counterparts. If only he could figure out how to communicate with his other self, they could start discussing the best way to take advantage of this ability.

Percy was connected to his soul clones and familiars via ethereal cords, and even his relatives – who had been born with a different strain of the bloodline – had access to similar connections, allowing them to relay mana and instructions to their own clones.

This should be no different.

The problem was that Percy couldn’t even tell where exactly his counterpart was located. It wasn’t like their brain had neatly split down the middle into a pair of physical hemispheres. It felt more like his bloodline had duplicated his entire brain, allowing each copy to occupy a different layer of reality. Or rather, it might be more accurate to say that Percy and his clone still shared a single brain that worked twice as fast, their neurons firing two independent sets of signals that completely ignored each other.

That was technically a good thing, because it meant that Percy’s mind clones wouldn’t have to compete over limited resources. Each new copy would just make his brain more efficient. However, the same property that kept the clones separate was also making it harder for them to communicate.

‘If there’s no localized cord, maybe I can just create one.’

Expelling some mind mana from his third core, he scattered it across his brain, hoping that his Blessing of Connection would do its job. Funnily enough, a second trickle of mind mana rippled out from the organ at almost the same time, suggesting that Percy’s counterpart had come up with the same idea.

Obeying his intentions, the mind mana thickened and slowed down as it spread, soon forming a bridge between the two layers of signals, and causing a wave of chaotic whispers to flood his mind, seemingly spoken in his own voice.

‘It’s working.’ ‘As expected.’ ‘Did I need to use twice as much mana, though?’ ‘That was a waste of mana.’ ‘Shit, this is getting too confusing.’ ‘Having a pair of minds in the same head is already too much.’ ‘How bad will it be if I try to create more mind clones?’ ‘My copy didn’t try to raise his hand earlier, so there’s clearly some deviation right from the start.’ ‘Just stop talking for a moment!’ ‘Will you please shut up?!’ ‘You first!’

Already feeling a headache coming, Percy forcefully flushed the mind mana out of his brain, disrupting the mental bridge. He’d grown quite good at filtering his thoughts when interacting with his soul clones and familiars, but it was much harder to do that with two voices colliding within the same brain.

The closest thing he had experienced had been his fusion with Micky, but their minds had merged completely at first, so they’d never had to deal with anything like this. After creating their Shimmering Constellations mindset, the barrier they had erected and their distinct personalities, identities, and bodies had helped them isolate their thoughts. In this case, there was nowhere for each of Percy’s minds to retreat where the other wouldn’t have access to.

‘Wait… Micky! He might be able to act as mediator between us!’ Percy realized.
