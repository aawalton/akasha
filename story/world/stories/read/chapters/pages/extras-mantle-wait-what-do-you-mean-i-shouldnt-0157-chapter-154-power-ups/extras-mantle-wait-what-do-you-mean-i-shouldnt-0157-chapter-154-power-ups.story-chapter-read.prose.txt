The condensed essence in the pool clung to Jin's skin like cold ink as he went under the surface.

Trish's power came along with it, like tides breaking the dam—overwhelming power—creeping up past his ribs in fine branching lines of frost. The world of light and sound simply stopped. For him there was no up and no down. He was just weightless in the dark. Even the muffled thunder of his own heartbeat was already slowing.

Everything was slowing, yet the only thing that refused was the agonizing pain of the invading power. He could feel his skin being ripped apart.

Jin reached out for the grey and, like a faithful servant, Sovereign's Indifference washed over him. The agony became a number, and pain just a distant thought.

« Consolidating, » Angel said, his voice a thin thread stretched across a widening gulf. « Hold the breathing pattern, Jin. Whatever happens—do not let the harvest slip. »

I’ll try…

Although he said that to Angel, inside him… in his soul-space was a storm brewing—crimson and gold boiling under a lid, a sealed pot of water set over the intruding furnace of a higher power, the pressure climbing with nowhere to vent—and it was here, at the edge of his own existence, where the boundary between Jin and not-Jin was thinnest, that the fate decided it also wanted to walk in.

The dark, warped, and it pulled itself into clinical white walls, the sterile smell of a place he'd spent a lifetime dying in.

No! Not again! Jin screamed as he resisted whatever was pulling him, but despite all his efforts, his harvest was the one pulling him in.

The shapes resolved, and he was back in his hospital room. In the corner, a man with dark hair was reading aloud from a book. Mantle of Gods.

"Who are you?" the reader asked. "A thief wearing a dead man's name? A fake? A wannabe hero hiding behind a predator's mask because he's too much of a coward to face what he actually is?"

The phantom flickered, and the corpses came with it.

People—soldiers and innocent civilians—he'd watched die and hadn't reached in time. They crawled toward him through the cracks in his own mind, hollow-eyed, dragging themselves on ruined arms, blood-black fingers rising to point.

"You call it necessary!" they hissed, a discordant chorus with no single voice. "You call it the only way. But you're only selfish. You only ever cared about what was in reach. The rest of us? You let us burn. You'll let them burn too."

Pain drove through the centre of his forehead. Behind his eyes, the old warnings began to stack in flashing crimson texts—soul identity degradation—and with them came the weight, the impossible pressing weight of lives he had never lived and memories that were not his, leaning on his mind like a mountain settling onto a knife's edge.

His phantom hands shook. His teeth ground together hard enough to crack.

And for one long, terrible moment, the abyss looked restful. He could feel the shape of the surrender… how easy it would be to let go…

Under the grey, a spark refused.

“My name is Jin Winters.” He yelled to the abyss, and a cold realization settled in his gut. “You are not that thing that took over my body… You are me…”

“And a warrior takes the blow and gives it back. It doesn't matter that the stories were ink on a page.” He said, still yelling. “They're real now. They bled on me. I bled for them. I'm real.”

With a heave of will that felt like lifting the sky off his own chest, Jin seized the boundaries of his soul-space and forced the boiling lid down.

The phantoms screamed—and thinned—and were gone, dragged back into the cracks they'd crawled out of.

And in that exact instant, the tempest hit its peak.

The mantle heart at the centre of his soul and being cracked. Its dull, fleshly surface peeled away like old paint, and underneath was a blinding, brilliant silver.

A pathway unfolded in front of his eyes… a bridge across a chasm he could suddenly see the far side of.

He took a step and another. This was the threshold of Order IV. One step, and his essence would crystallise into a higher state of being.

It was close enough to touch. It wanted to be touched.

« Jin — no! » Angel's voice broke through, sharp with real fear. « Resist it! Cross now, and the flaws will be permanent—your foundation, your soul, all of it. DON'T take the step! »

Jin paused, forced himself to close his eyes and let go of the currents that were leading him there.

“Soon,” He mumbled as his spirituality was hauled back from the silver path. He reached and clamped down on his own advancement, holding his rank hostage at the very lip of the breakthrough. The pathway shuddered under him.

But the power didn't vanish just because he refused the door. That much raw power had to go somewhere, and if Jin denied its choosing, then it would choose for it.

So Jin made his soul a conduit, and he inverted the flow.

He caught the roaring torrent and shoved it outward—every scrap of it, away from his rank—into his attributes, his affinities, his skills and, when there was more than any of those could swallow, straight at the one part of him built to process the impossible.

Angel. Take it!

The backlash was fire in the shape of nails, hammered one by one into his skull. New channels burned themselves open through bone and nerve. His body burned, broken, and then was reforged again under that unforgiving and vast power.

After all, He knew one thing: pressure makes the diamond.

He felt his own being rewriting itself and had the grey to thank that he could observe it happening instead of simply screaming.

Runes of silver and gold erupted across his soul, wrapping around both his hearts.

The last and densest kernel of Trish's power he hurled into Angel.

The guide's presence buckled, the whole interface stuttering like a machine fed more current than its rating and then held. It then started steadily absorbing, converting and chewing the raw power down into something structured, until host and guide steadied together against the same trembling floor.

And the storm went out.

All at once the friction died. The screaming channels fell silent. The grey lifted a fraction of its own accord, because there was nothing left for it to hold back.

Jin opened his eyes.

He was not in the white room, and he was definitely not in the pool.

He stood on the surface of an endless black lake, flat and glassy as a mirror, under a starry sky.

Something rippled beneath his feet, and two enormous white lights appeared. They didn't illuminate the water. They just were, the way a wound is, and a voice drifted up through the dark with a lilt he'd have known anywhere.

"Congratulations, little thief."

Jin knew the voice and just shook his head at the fact that the voice had remained the same despite all that had happened. Amused and delighted.

"You survived me. And you kept all your little pieces together while you did it. I'm impressed. I'm rarely impressed."

The white light—which he was sure now was her eyes or at least represented her eyes—dilated in the deep.

"Now go and win your silly little war," she murmured. "Win it so I can come back and play with my bestie and you lot. For forever this time." A pause, warm, patient and awful. "And do be quick about it, little thief. You mustn't keep a lady waiting."

The lake shattered like a dropped pane of glass, and Jin fell through and woke in the real world with a gasping lurch, water sheeting off him, his lungs hauling in the cool, dry air of the bastion.

He caught the stone ring with both hands. His whole body shook with residual power, and where the water ran off his skin, a faint blue-silver light rolled off with it.

« You made a mess, Jin, » Angel said. He sounded remarkably clear and faintly, genuinely winded. « Good work, though. That integration was... substantial. I'll have the full status and racial-change report ready shortly. »

Jin nodded weakly, wiped grey water out of his eyes, and looked around.

Joe was still here. No… that, on its own, wasn't a surprise. What was a surprise was that Joe had, at some point during Jin's ordeal, decided to move in.

An oak desk stood a few metres from the pool, just beyond the magic circle's edge. It was covered in loose sheets and a couple of open books, a half-finished cup of something, and a small, tidy stack of snacks. Joe himself was reclined in a chair behind it with his boots crossed at the ankle and a pastry in one hand, for all the world like a man waiting out a rain shower.

He glanced up, saw Jin upright, and paused mid-bite.

Then he whistled, low and long.

Jin rolled his eyes at the man and stepped out over the stone ring. The pool behind him had gone pale grey, drained of whatever essence it had held. He pulsed the thermokinetic engine—just a flicker—and the water leapt off his skin in a fine mist, leaving him dry.

The action felt… different. Like the gap between deciding and doing had been quietly removed.

He flexed a fist and watched it close, and it was the most responsive thing he had ever felt his own body do.

"Hmm? What the—" He frowned, turned his hand over, and stopped.

Fine lines spread across the back of it. They ran up his wrist, up his forearm, branching and rejoining all the way to the side of his neck. At first glance, he took them for veins, but they were too perfect for that. A faint silver sheen caught the light for a single heartbeat and then sank back into the colour of his skin, as if it had never been there.

They had not been there this morning. He was one hundred percent sure of that.

"You planning to wear something?" Joe said, unfolding from the chair. "Or is the boxer look part of the new brand? It would do wonders for the younger generation of survivors. Our very own model!"

Jin rolled his eyes again and reached for his storage ring and remembered he'd stripped down to nothing but the boxers before getting in, ring included.

He looked up just in time to see the ring sailing at his face.

He caught it out of the air without thinking—the motion again, like with his skills, was embarrassingly quick—and shook his head as he slid it on and pulled a fresh set of clothes out with a pulse of essence.

"Hold still for sec," Joe crossed to him and rubbed his chin. He handed Jin a sandwich and a bottle of juice with the other hand. "Eat. You've earned the calories."

For the next minute, while Jin worked through the food, Joe walked slow circles around him, spells flickering off his fingertips and pale light and essence probes passing over Jin's chest, his spine, the back of his skull. Just as Jin swallowed the last bite, Joe stepped back and broke into a wide, honest grin.

"Good news. Your soul's on track to fully heal, give or take a few more hours, and from there it only strengthens." He said, then his expression sobered a degree. "Also—and I should've said this before you went under, except the whole thing happened about as fast as a house fire—good thing you didn't take that last step. If you'd pushed into Order IV in the state you were in, your internal landscape and your soul would've been… fucked—” Joe made a face as if saying the crude word pained him. “—And I say that as a professional. There's no cleaner word for it."

Jin nodded slowly.

"Though you might want to worry about a different thing." Joe tilted his head. "The aura coming off you right now would put a newly ascended Order IV on its arse."

Jin frowned. He closed his eyes, reached inward, and tried to get a feel for his aura.

And wow! His aura was like a standing inferno pouring off him in every direction, filling the chamber, pressing on the walls. He hadn't noticed. He genuinely hadn't noticed, because to him it felt like nothing, like breathing. He reeled it back in.

"I didn't even feel it," he admitted. "Everything's just clear, sharper and more responsive. It's like when I broke through to Order III, except a hundred times over."

"Yeah," Joe said, dry. "Tell me about it."

"And these?" Jin rolled his sleeve back and showed him the bronze-silver lines threading up his arm.

"Noticed those the second you sat up. They're all over you, by the way." Joe shrugged. "Best guess? New channels or related to racial stuff. Your body building infrastructure for whatever you just crammed into it." He looked Jin up and down and smirked. "Anyway. You're rocking the whole new-look thing."

Jin went still.

"...Is there more?"

He put both hands to his own face, and Joe's smirk climbed into a grin.

Jin felt his face—there was only one nose and two eyes, same as usual—and there was nothing weird. Then he moved to his ears and Oh…

There was a point to them now… it was small, subtle, but undeniably there, the faintest taper where before there'd been none. He pulled a mirror from his ring and, sure enough, they'd gone very slightly elven-looking, just enough to notice if you were looking closely.

His face had sharpened too, the lines a shade cleaner, though everything else was his same pale blue eyes with that silver halo bleeding softly into them.

He flexed his jaw, rolled his neck until the vertebrae popped with a satisfying pop, and set the mirror down.

"How long was I under?"

"A little over eight hours."

"Eight—" Jin balked. It had felt like ten minutes and a lifetime, in that order. He dragged a hand down his face and sighed. "Right. Of course it was."

He looked at the pool, at the now dull magic circle, then at the desk, then at Joe.

"Can you drop me at my room? I want to catch some sleep and some quiet before tonight's briefing.” He said. “Oh! And fill the others in on the broad strokes for me, will ya?"

"Sure," Joe snapped his fingers, and a door swung open into the air.

Jin walked to it, paused at the threshold, and turned back.

"Joe." He held the man's eyes. "Thank you. For real." He nodded once. "If there's ever anything you need that's within what I can do—anything—don't hesitate. You ask, it's done."

Joe blinked, and for half a second the man’s grin faltered into something quieter and more surprised.

Then it came back, tilted. "Careful, boss. Say things like that, and I'll start charging you for the snacks." He waved him off. "Go. Sleep. You look like a haunted painting."

Jin snorted and stepped through.

His new room was much like the old one, minus the psychotic murder-decor he'd covered the last one's walls in.

Small mercies. I guess.

He crossed to the bed and dropped into it face-first, then rolled onto his back and stared at the ceiling.

Angel?

« I’m ready. »

« I'll start with the headline, because everything else is downstream of it. Now you ready? »

Okay…

« For starters… your race changed. »

...Changed? To what!

« A high human. The Z.A.C records call the strain the Vaelthuri or Homo sapiens ascendens. »

“How does that even—” Jin shook his head. “Better than becoming a demon, I suppose.”

« True. Besides, you harvest the primes’ seed, you carry the Eternal One's blessing, you've harvested so many lifeforms, and you just assimilated a piece of a being who is, functionally, a demigod. No single one of those does this. All of them, stacked, do. » Angel almost sounded impressed against his will. « The system stopped trying to file you under '???' and provide sample genes to complete and push your muddled genome towards this, which allowed me to open a new drawer. »

And what does being a High Human actually get me?

« Growth. Your essence reserves have quadrupled; and so have reflexes… that's your new baseline. Your body's tolerance for essence, kinetic load, thermal load, all of it, has jumped exponentially; the Engine will stop trying to cook you alive quite so eagerly. And this isn't finished. Your body will keep changing and strengthening as the lineage settles over the coming days. Expect to feel like a stranger in your own skin for a while. »

Jin exhaled slowly and pulled up his status.

Jin read his—ever-expanding—status screen very slowly.

The stats change was an immediate hit. Every one of them dragged up out of the D’s and was parked in the C's. Which honestly should have translated to him being ORDER IV, which he wasn’t.

“So my stats are now that of ORDER IV?” he asked.

« Yes and no. They are still latent, but you would be comparable to a newly ascended ORDER IV. »

“What does latent here mean?”

« To put it bluntly, it means your body, though now enhanced, is still not strong enough to fully utilize the might of ORDER IV. So you are actually only feeling C9 worth of stats. »

“Even if they are higher?” Jin guessed.

« Yes. »

Then the titles. High Human. The Harvester. He wasn't sure how he felt about that last one; it read like something a cult would carve over a door. The One Who Made a Pact with the Night sat right beside it, and he thought of Trish… he didn't love the company his titles were keeping.

The affinities had grown massively; both Space and Void promoted to Major, sitting next to Darkness and Blood.

The cultivation path had jumped eleven percent off the back of a single night.

And the skills… a whole slew of new ones, Elite out of the gate, and Superior Regeneration was his favorite. A full suite of mind arts he'd never studied, courtesy of Kiyon no doubt.

Fate Variant Signature VII, he noted, and that number had been a VI the last time he'd looked. He was getting louder.

"Well," he said to the empty room. "That's a day."

He let himself fall back onto the mattress and stared at the ceiling for a couple of minutes.

Then he sat up, yelling to the ceiling. “Fuck it!”

He reached into his ring and drew out the two Ability Upgrade Tokens.

« You sure? »

"Hell yeah," he turned one over in his fingers. "Time to finally drag two to Master rank."

His eyes drifted back to the status still hanging in his vision, to the four marked skills sitting there in a neat, glowing column.

“Only problem is which two. Doom hits like a truck. The Engine's my whole engine. Spellshot and the Matrix are how I fight at all." He frowned. "Four skills, and every one of them earns its slot."

He tapped the token against his knee.

"Any ideas, Angel? Which one do we make a monster?"

For a moment, Angel didn't answer.

« I have an idea, » Angel said slowly. « But your body might not really like where it leads. »

“Ain't that the best!” Jin smiled.
