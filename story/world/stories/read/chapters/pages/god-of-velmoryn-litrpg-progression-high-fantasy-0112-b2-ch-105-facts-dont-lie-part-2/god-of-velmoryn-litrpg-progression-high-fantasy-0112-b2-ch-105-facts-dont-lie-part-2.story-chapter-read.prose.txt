Soon, more portals appeared. Some were enormous, far larger than even the tallest human would need to pass through.

For a moment, I considered using the divinity I had slowly seeped into the mortal world to shut them down. Even if I had never managed to figure out how to counter summoning magic properly, I was certain I could shatter the portals themselves.

But I held myself back.

At best, it would delay Noah for a short while, and in exchange I would practically announce that I was not in slumber.

It would be foolish.

“I will go warn them,” Amon said as his figure blurred.

A moment later, he appeared in Eriavel.

The velmoryns, already on high alert in preparation for war, recoiled at the sudden appearance of a stranger. Very few knew Amon by face, and his human appearance certainly did not help.

One of the faster velmoryns charged before anyone could stop him. Mana flared around his sweat-covered body as he dropped low, a dagger already whistling toward Amon’s side.

The blade never reached him.

Amon casually swept one hand aside, and a burst of mana caught the velmoryn mid-charge, hurling him backward.

“Now, now. That is no way to greet High Father’s son,” he said with a grin.

His eyes moved across the gathered velmoryns, searching for Dariel and Mirion. A faint smile appeared on his face when he saw just how tense and battle-ready everyone was.

“They’ve prepared well,” he thought, satisfied. Then he spotted Dariel and Mirion.

Both men were rushing toward him, Mirion carrying his axe while Dariel had already transformed into his beast form. But the moment they drew close enough to recognize him, they stopped.

“Amon?” Mirion asked, confusion briefly crossing his face.

Then his expression brightened.

“You kept your promise!”

“Of course I did.”

The cheeky smile on Amon’s face made it obvious he wanted to keep playing around, but the urgency of the situation quickly wiped it away.

“Our enemy is here. Prepare the army. I will join you after I speak with the Priestess.”

He did not wait for Mirion’s response. He turned toward the temple and started walking. But after only a few steps, he cursed under his breath and broke into a run.

“Verde, let me lead this battle. I will take full responsibility,” he whispered.

I briefly considered his request.

Mirion had already proven that he understood war. His victory in the last battle had been decisive. If not for the God of Beggars’ descent, the velmoryns would have suffered fewer than ten casualties.

That alone was an impressive achievement. Yet, it was also why I hesitated.

Mirion knew how to fight mortals. But this war would require someone who could anticipate divine interference, understood the limitations placed on gods, or even me, and was willing to achieve victory regardless of the means required.

Amon fit that role far better.

“Do not disappoint me,” I said before shifting most of my attention back toward the portals.

“My Liege,” Aurelion’s voice pulled me out of my thoughts. “Should I remain hidden like last time?”

I had already considered that possibility, and this time there was little reason to keep him completely out of sight.

“No,” I said. “Be ready to strike at my command.”

In the meantime, the portals’ violet glow continued to intensify while the chaotic energy rotating around them gradually slowed and stabilized, and I realized there might be a way to exploit Noah’s decision to gather his forces directly in front of my forest.

Was Amon right? Is the Night God really more careless than I thought?

Once all thirteen portals had stabilized, figures slowly began emerging from the smaller ones. The ten smaller ones were indeed meant for humans, with men and women carrying swords, shields, spears, bows, and magical staffs pouring through in increasingly larger numbers.

The first thing that caught my attention was their equipment.

Even with this army, the quality and purpose of their gear varied drastically. Some wore proper armor and carried well-maintained weapons, while others looked like mercenaries or hastily gathered fighters equipped with whatever they had managed to obtain. This only strengthened my suspicion that Noah did not possess a unified, properly trained army of his own.

Or, at the very least, he had no intention of using such a force at the beginning of this battle.

After confirming that the ten smaller portals were meant for humans, I shifted my attention toward the remaining three.

They were considerably larger and took much longer to stabilize, yet even after the violet energy around them finally settled, whatever waited on the other side seemed to be in no hurry to come through.

Finally, a shadow stretched beneath one of the portals, followed by a low creaking sound that steadily grew louder until a massive wooden structure emerged from the violet light.

If I had to describe it simply, it looked like an enormous wooden cart with a steel cage built on top of it.

Or rather, carts.

Once the first one passed through, many more followed behind it.

And inside the cages were wyverns.

At first, I mistook them for dragons, but then I remembered Athena’s believer and the beast she had used as a mount. Guidance had identified that creature as a wyvern, and these looked almost identical, except they were anything but obedient.

The beasts thrashed violently inside their cages, biting at the steel bars and slamming their bodies against the walls. Their long tails were thin enough to slip through the gaps between the bars, and they whipped them wildly at the men pushing the carts.

By the time figures began emerging from the remaining two portals, the human army had already fully crossed over and formed ranks. The wyvern carts were still being dragged through, though not because there were an overwhelming number of them. They were simply difficult to move, especially with the creatures inside constantly throwing themselves from one side of the cages to the other.

My attention remained fixed on the last two portals.

Then something stepped through.

At first, it looked more like a shadow than a living creature, darker than the night surrounding it. I could barely make out its shape because even the light seemed to bend around its body, reducing the figure to something resembling a shifting lump of black oil.

It was Noah’s creation - the creatures of darkness!

“Aurelion, now!”

The moment I gave the order, a crimson ray descended from above.

Before my angel could reach the ground, several humans dressed in the same dark robes as the other mages rushed forward and tore away the veils covering their bodies.

The crimson stitches running across their eyes made their identities impossible to mistake.

They immediately bit into their own flesh. Some tore off fingers, while others ripped chunks from their arms before spitting the bloody sacrifices onto the frozen ground.

The pieces of flesh shimmered.

At first, only thin strands of oily black vapor rose from them, but in the next instant the darkness erupted outward like an explosion, spreading across the battlefield and forming a vast black barrier over the army and the portals.

Through the Window, it looked as though a sphere of pure void had suddenly appeared in the mortal world, directly in the path of the descending crimson light.

But I was not worried. Perhaps I would have been had V not brought Aurelion his glaive.

My angel did not even slow his descent. His enormous crimson wings folded tightly around his body as he plunged toward the ground like a falcon diving after its prey.

He speared into the barrier with his glaive.

And pierced straight through it.

The black sphere contracted around him before a ripple spread across its surface, the hole widening rapidly until the entire barrier collapsed and dissolved into oily mist.

Aurelion emerged from the darkness with his glaive already moving.

One of the Inquisitors had either reacted too slowly or had been foolish enough to believe she could stand against an angel.

The blade passed through her body, splitting her in half before she could even move.

A blue Window flashed beside me.

[Warning: Aurelion has slain a soul belonging to another god.][Warning: Aurelion used the skill Reaper.][Warning: Reaper consumed a Gold Rank Inquisitor’s soul.][Warning: You’ve gained 100 Divinity Points.]

He turned sharply toward the two enormous portals and swung his glaive.

One of the creatures of darkness had already emerged from the nearest gateway, and the moment it saw Aurelion preparing to destroy it, the creature attacked, its massive, elusive body lunging toward my angel.

I knew exactly what made these things so dangerous.

They were immune to physical attacks.

Will it work?

For the first time since Aurelion descended, I became uncertain. His glaive might have been capable of cutting through anything, but in the end, it was still a physical weapon, and I had no idea how these kinds of immunities and unique traits interacted with one another. I could not be certain that the glaive’s properties would simply override the creature’s natural protection.

Aurelion glanced toward it and, without even turning his body, swept the glaive horizontally.

A line of pure light appeared across the mass of darkness. Then, in the next instant, the creature burst apart like a soap bubble.

Dark, oily divinity surged from its collapsing form, but even that dispersed almost immediately.

[Warning: Aurelion used the skill Reaper.][Warning: Reaper consumed an artificial (unfinished) soul created by another god.][Warning: You’ve gained 1,000 Divinity Points.]

One thousand…

From a single creature of darkness.

For a moment, I actually considered ordering Aurelion to let more of them through before slaughtering every last one. If each creature was worth a thousand Divinity Points, I would have gladly let my angel farm them for me.

The thought amused me for all of a second before I pushed it aside.

Taking a risk like that would be idiotic. Perhaps even more than that.

Instead, I watched with a surge of pride and satisfaction as Aurelion brought his glaive down on the portals.

The violet gateways flared brilliantly beneath the blade, their surfaces warping before collapsing into streams of dark vapor that twisted through the air and faded away.

If my theory was correct, then anyone who entered a portal did not simply appear on the other side instantly. There was some kind of connecting space between the entrance and exit, and crossing that distance required at least a little time.

That was why I had waited.

That was why I had allowed the creatures to get so close to emerging before ordering Aurelion to strike.

I wanted to make sure at least some of them were already trapped somewhere between the two gateways when the portals collapsed.

Perhaps they would even die there.

“My Liege, allow me to kill these creatures,” Aurelion’s thought reached me.

He was asking for permission, yet before I had even answered, he had already turned toward the human army and the remaining Inquisitors, all of whom stood frozen after witnessing what had just happened.

“Go for it!”

For the first time since I learned that I would have to face another god, genuine excitement surged through me.

Maybe I could end everything here, before Noah’s army even reached the velmoryns.

Was Amon right? How could Noah make such an obvious mistake? He knew I had an angel. There was no way he did not, so why had he prepared nothing capable of stopping Aurelion other than that barrier?

I was still enjoying the thought when the space between Aurelion and the human army distorted.

At first, it looked as though the air itself had been split open, a thin fracture appearing where nothing should have existed.

The crack shone brilliant white for only an instant before turning black.

Thick, oily divinity began seeping from within it, followed by a shrill, nerve-grating scream that reminded me of a rat trapped somewhere it could not escape.

Aurelion tensed.

The relaxed confidence he had carried only a moment earlier disappeared completely as he shifted into a fighting stance, sliding his right leg behind him before pushing off the ground and charging directly toward the fracture.

He closed the distance almost instantly and swung his glaive.

But before the blade could reach the crack, an immense force erupted from within.

Darkness itself surged outward.

It struck Aurelion and hurled him backward.

The burst of divinity lasted only an instant, disappearing almost as quickly as it had appeared.

And what remained in its place…

I had never seen anything like it.

Only one word came to mind.

An abomination.
