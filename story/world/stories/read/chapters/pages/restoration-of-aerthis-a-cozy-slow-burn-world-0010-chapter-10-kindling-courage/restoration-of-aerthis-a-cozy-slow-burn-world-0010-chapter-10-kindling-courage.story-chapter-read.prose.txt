The sun hung high in a clear sky, its warm light painting the fields in soft gold as Elynara walked through the fields surrounding the town.

She had to leave the path she followed as it led to the nearby village, and nowhere near the forest edge where the herbs could be found.

Her legs soon took her there, and she made sure to veer a safe way away from the deeper tree line. She didn’t plan to test her luck, and if she does, it will be on her own terms. A little caution, she had learned, went a long way.

Her basket hung over one arm, her eyes scanning the swaying grass and soft earth below. The herbs she was after weren’t too rare. She had even seen most of them before during her last trip. And this time, she wasn’t relying on blind luck.

The first patch appeared after only a few minutes of searching. She knelt, fingers brushing aside stalks to find the small cluster of dark green leaves beneath. A gentle twist, a careful pull, and it came free without damage. And into the basket it went.

She stood, stretched, then continued on.

A faint hum flickered just beside her ear, followed by a sudden flick to the left. She flinched slightly on instinct, turning just in time to catch Starpuff hovering barely a breath away from her nose.

She glared at it.

“Don’t act cute now, you little troublemaker…”

The sprite pulsed softly, almost sheepishly, before nudging itself against her collarbone like a guilty kitten. It nestled into the crook of her arm with a low, flickering buzz that could almost be mistaken for a purr.

Elynara sighed through a faint smile and gave it a light prod with her fingertip.

“Fine. You’re forgiven. But only if you help again.”

The glow brightened, then darted forward in a lazy arc.

They continued like that for a while. With her focus divided between the terrain and the occasional pulse of green that zipped off toward a promising patch of growth.

Sometimes Starpuff led her to useful herbs. Sometimes it just twirled above a flower it found amusing. But she could usually tell the difference by now.

Despite the task at hand, she made sure to have a clear sight of the forest at all times. Well, most of the time! She had to crouch down to gather the herbs after all!

And so they continued at this pace, unhurried. Hours passing that way.

By the time the sun had shifted further west, her basket held enough to fill the guild’s modest request. She double-checked the contents to be sure, then stepped off the trail and made her way toward a flat stone under partial shade of a lonely copse of trees.

Lowering herself onto it, she drew out her waterskin and took a long sip.

The breeze was soft, the sky unmarred by anything but clouds. Starpuff hovered beside her, drifting lazily like it, too, had earned a rest.

Elynara sat perched on a wide, sun-warmed rock, the herb-filled basket resting comfortably beside her. Her arms dangled loosely over her knees, staff lying across her lap. The breeze carried the scent of moss and crushed leaves, and somewhere off to her right, a faint bird call broke the stillness.

The job was done. She had gathered what she needed. She could have left. Should have, maybe.

But she didn’t move.

Instead, her eyes wandered to the edge of the nearby trees, where shadow pooled just thick enough to hide something. A hollow between trunks, hidden among the bushes. The same kind of place that the Bonefang had come from, once.

That memory still sat at the edge of her thoughts, though out here in the open, under the sunny skies, it didn’t feel so bad anymore.

And she had her staff and fire spell now. Her hand slid over to the trusty piece of wood she now always carried at her side.

She thought about that day. How frantic she had been. How she had to force the spell together on the spot and pray it hit. She had survived, but not because she was prepared.

Her gaze returned to the shaded patch near the rocks.

What if it happened again?

Not a second chance. Just… a second delay, a moment of hesitation. A stumble.

She could fix that. Perhaps not by being faster, but by being ready.

Her fingers tightened around the staff’s shaft.

Slowly, deliberately, she called the fire forth.

The mana responded. It came to her like a warmed breath, curling down her arm and into the staff. With a thought she shaped the spell out, but forced it to stay in place. A small orb of red-orange flame, resting at the tip of her staff like a waiting ember.

It pulsed gently in place.

Elynara focused on holding it steady. Letting it linger.

It was a test, to see if she could prepare ahead of time. So she could lose it the very instant danger approaches.

Almost immediately, she felt it draining her.

Not by much. But the longer she left it floating there, the more mana she needed to supply to keep it going. Like it was bleeding away at the edges, faint tendrils of heat leeching into the air with every passing second.

It looked like it wouldn’t be something she could keep up forever. At least not quite yet. The focus required wasn’t small either. Although it should be easy to manage with some training.

She adjusted her stance, feet set slightly apart now, staff pointed toward the treeline.

It wasn’t perfect. The orb flickered every so often, pulsing wider, then drawing back. But she could hold it.

This… could work.

She breathed in slowly through her nose, then out. Let the spell hover. Waited.

Nothing moved.

The shadows by the rocks stayed still, no leaves shifting, no underbrush rustling.

Still, she didn’t relax. If anything, her fingers pressed a little firmer against the smooth wood of her staff.

When the flickering grew sharper and the mana draw began to nibble at the edge of her focus, she let the orb go.

It streaked toward the rocky hollow in a sharp, clean arc. Its light flaring briefly against the tree trunks.

It struck stone with a sharp crack, a small explosion of flame bursting outward, followed by a brief cloud of smoke.

Silence.

Elynara immediately prepared the next spell, just in case. And waited.

Half a minute passed. A full minute.

She exhaled again, a little sharper this time, and let the spell she was holding fizzle out, capturing most of the mana back.

With no response coming, she turned and began walking. Further along the tree line, determined to try again somewhere farther away.

She moved with care, preparing another spell on the way.

Each step took her closer to a denser stretch of growth, where the trees leaned tighter together and the underbrush grew thick enough to hide more than just shadows.

Her staff remained level, the orb hovering just beyond its tip like a patient promise. She let her breathing guide her pace, careful not to let the tension in her shoulders rise.

Then she saw it: a rocky bend near the treeline, where the ground sloped down slightly and a thicket leaned heavy over the edge. If anything was going to be watching, that was the place.

Elynara shifted her weight, dug her boots in, and aimed.

She didn’t wait long.

The fire spell loosed from her staff in a straight, crackling line, slightly faster and cleaner than the ones before. It arced into the edge of the brush and hit just to the left of the rocks.

The undergrowth flared for an instant. Heat licked the air.

And this time… something moved.

A low, throaty snarl rolled out from the shadows. The brush shifted violently, and something burst out of it; a lean, half-matted shape bounding forward on crooked legs. Pale eyes. Ragged fur. Gaunt ribs.

Another Bonefang.

It locked onto her almost immediately. The briefest hesitation crossed its features, and then it dropped low and charged.

Elynara didn’t flinch, her next orb was already formed.

She didn’t let it build any further. Simply adjusted her stance and released.

The spell slammed into the beast mid-sprint.

A loud crack echoed as the fire burst against its chest. The Bonefang’s forward momentum was instantly halted, almost throwing it back.

Smoke rose faintly from the impact. It looked messy and scorched black, with dying flames flickering on pieces of fur.

She held her breath for another moment. Waited. Listened.

Nothing else stirred.

Then the sound came: soft, familiar, unmistakable.

Ding.

The blinking dot returned to the edge of her vision.

Confident that everything was safe now, she looked to see what the dot wanted to tell her. The status window revealed a new entry.

【Fire Magic – Lvl 1】

She stared at it.

Then at the scorched patch of ground. Then the motionless creature.

Huh.

“…I won,” she muttered aloud, almost disbelieving.

Her arms lowered slightly. The tension fled her limbs all at once, leaving a strange emptiness in its wake. It was a relief, yes, but also the sudden realization that she didn’t know what came next.

Her gaze returned to the corpse. Her steps were cautious, her hand gripped tighter around her staff.

Up close, the creature looked smaller than she expected. Its bones jutted from its flanks, and it smelled sharp and burnt. Her nose wrinkled.

It was gruesome. Unpleasant. But she knew it had been a monster, once. And it would have munched on her if she were to let it.

She had to get used to that.

Circling it once, she reached forward. And then stopped in place.

She had no bag, and no way to carry the corpse. She didn’t even know what to take. Claws? Teeth? A sample of the fur? What was valuable from something like this?

“…I should’ve asked Alicia.”

Her shoulders slumped a little.

She backed away slowly and gave the body a final glance before turning toward the path home.

At least her basket was full.

And she was, for the first time, walking away from a fight that hadn’t left her breathless or shaking.

Elynara adjusted her staff on her back and exhaled through her nose.

“Next time.”

Then she started back toward town, the trip completely uneventful. Aside from Starpuff being its cheeky self again, of course. Where did it even get all that energy from?

Spirits were really mysterious creatures.

She watched as it curiously tried sitting on a flower, slipped, and planted itself dumbly into the ground. She barely held the laughter in.

…Yes, very mysterious.

The guild hall’s door creaked open just as the afternoon sun began its descent. Elynara stepped inside, her cloak brushed with dust and her boots trailing faint signs of travel. She quickly made a beeline towards Alicia, her basket tucked neatly beneath one arm.

The main room was calm. With only a few low voices, the scent of ink and oil faint beneath the lingering aroma of earlier meals. Alicia stood at the counter, pen in hand, sorting through a set of worn request slips. Her eyes flicked up as the footsteps approached.

“You’re back in one piece. That’s good.”

Elynara nodded in greeting and stepped forward, laying the wrapped herbs onto the counter with care.

“I believe that’s all of them, and a few extra, just in case.”

Alicia pulled the herbs closer and began checking them thoroughly. Her fingers moved with practiced efficiency, though her eyes narrowed slightly with scrutiny at a few of the bundles.

Elynara hesitated, then leaned in a little.

“I… might have also killed a Bonefang.”

That brought Alicia’s hands to a stop. Her gaze lifted.

Elynara gave a nervous smile, before settling on a little white lie.

“I didn’t go looking for one. It just… showed up. After I released a spell near some rocks. I thought maybe something would be hiding there, and it turns out there was.”

She paused, a bit embarrassed.

“I didn’t know what to do with the body though. I left it there.”

A breath left Alicia in a controlled exhale. The corner of her mouth twitched, like she was fighting the urge to say something immediately.

Before she could, a familiar voice rose from nearby.

“You left it?”

Marcus’ laugh rang out, rough and unrestrained. He leaned one arm against the nearby quest board, his other hand half-raised in disbelief.

“Hells, girl. That’s like catching a fish and tossing it back because you forgot your basket.”

Elynara flushed.

“I didn’t exactly think to bring a dissection kit.”

Alicia was quick to admonish him, without even looking up from her work.

“Try not to be a dumbass, Marcus.”

His chuckle faded to a low hum of amusement.

Alicia refocused on Elynara. Her tone was smoother now.

“Still. First monster kill is not something to shrug off. You handled it well, congratulations.”

She pushed the herb slip aside, fingers tapping a new sheet forward.

“But now’s the perfect time to learn what to do with one.”

She nodded toward Marcus.

“Go on. He can show you. Better to learn now than after you let a second one rot.”

Marcus gave a lazy roll of his shoulders and a dramatic sigh.

“Guess I’m the teacher now. Let’s get this over with.”

Elynara glanced between the two of them…

Alicia had already turned back to her ledgers, while Marcus was grinning like he had just been handed a new toy.

…and tried not to groan.

“Fine. Let’s hope I don’t regret this.”

He motioned for her to follow, and soon they were in the part of the guild grounds she didn’t venture to before.

The back training yard they found themselves in was quiet, save for the low creak of wood and the occasional clang from a nearby workshop. Sunlight spilled in through high, narrow windows, catching dust in the air. A long table stood in the center of the room, surprisingly clean, with a few worn tools laid out in a neat line.

Elynara stood at one end, her notebook already open, a charcoal pencil balanced between her fingers.

Marcus loomed on the opposite side, sleeves rolled, hands resting on the edge of the table like he had done this routine a dozen times.

“Alright. First things first. Bonefangs. Nasty teeth, mangy fur, not much upstairs.”

She didn’t interrupt him, just nodded once and scribbled down:

Bonefang: Not smart.

“They’re not the weakest beasties out there, but they’re predictable. They lunge, and they bite. If you’ve seen one, you’ve seen all of ‘em.”

He reached down, tapped the largest of the tools without picking it up.

“Fangs are what you want. It’s in the name, really. Dense and sturdy stuff. People use ’em for weapon tips, small tools, or even jewelry if you’re tacky. Pulling them out is just a matter of leverage, push something under it and a good push should get them out.”

Elynara’s pencil scratched quickly.

Fangs: Sell. Valuable and sturdy.

“Sometimes, they’ve got mana cores. Rare and small, though. Usually depleted as well. Only ever seen it in the big ones. Not worth digging unless you’ve got time, tools, and nowhere else to be.”

She jutted down another little note: Cores – tiny and rare. Not priority unless full take.

“As for the meat?”

He gave a shrug.

“Technically edible. Won’t kill you. Guild buys it cheap. Ends up mostly in animal feed or dried and ground into dry rations. Smells worse than it tastes. Usually.”

Meat: Leave unless requested.

Marcus moved on to the last point, picking up a strip of prepared hide from a nearby hook and holding it up.

“Hide’s good coin. The fur not really, but leather is leather. If you don’t cut it up too much.”

Elynara winced, an unbidden memory of blackened hide and scorched flesh filling her mind.

Marcus smirked slightly at her expression but didn’t comment.

“If the hide’s intact, cutting it off carefully is the way to go. Try to scrap as much meat off as possible. Otherwise it’s very prone to spoiling. And smells quite bad. Some crafters will still take it, but only for scraps.”

She scribbled the final lines.

Hides: Good for money, but only if clean. Fire ruins it.

He stepped back, folding his arms.

“And that’s the short version.”

He gestured to the laid-out tools and walked her through the motions. Where to cut, how to peel back hide without tearing it, how to pop the jaw loose and retrieve the fangs without ruining them.

It wasn’t glamorous, and she shuddered a little imagining needing to do so in the future. But it was useful to know.

By the time he finished, Elynara’s page was filled with notes.

Marcus gave one final stretch, spine cracking audibly as he leaned back against the table, before leading her back to the guild entrance.

“That’s enough for today. You’ll remember it better the first time you’re elbow-deep in one.”

Elynara gave a polite nod and followed him back, tucking her pencil into her pouch.

“Thanks.”

She paused, flipping back a few pages in her notebook.

“Oh, right, uh, I was meaning to ask. I found something in the library. About slimes? Supposedly very common, but I have never seen even a single one yet?”

Marcus paused at the mention.

“Slimes?”

He scratched the side of his jaw, looking more puzzled than anything.

“Can’t say I’ve ever seen one. Heard of them, sure, but little else.”

Alicia looked up from her desk at that, her expression unreadable for a moment.

“As far as guild records go, they were quite common a very long time ago.”

That drew Elynara’s full attention. She shifted slightly, watching Alicia more closely.

“They used to spawn in places where mana was thick. High-density zones, like the old heartwoods or active springs. But as the mana thinned, they just… stopped appearing.”

“What happened to them, did they just vanish?”

Alicia seemed to consider the question before answering.

“Not overnight, I would think. They didn’t need much to survive. But they were pests, most of them. Vermin that melted everything soft and ruined gear. So they were hunted out over time. The stronger ones weren’t so easy, of course. And some likely just hid.”

“Monsters live long when no one kills them,” Marcus added. “You leave one alone for a few decades and it turns into a walking disaster.”

Alicia nodded.

“If any are still around, they would be in sealed ruins. Caves and dungeons. Pockets of trapped mana far from here. And if you do find one…”

She gave Elynara a pointed look.

“You run. Until you’re sure you’re out of danger. And then run some more.”

Elynara blinked, absorbing that.

“…Right.”

“It’s good you understand.”

Alicia said simply. Then returned to her paperwork without further comment.

Starpuff chose this moment to drift lazily right before her face, completely unbothered. She looked at the glowy green thing for a moment, before snapping back to reality.

There wasn’t much more to say, and both Marcus and Alicia were already busying themselves with something else.

Elynara left a moment later, quickly saying her thanks and goodbyes.

She slipped her notebook into her bag, turning the thought over. It was yet another reminder the world was shifting. And another entry to look into at a later time.

The sun was already beginning its descent when Elynara stepped through the church gate, the light slanting low across the yard. The golden rays turned the stone path soft and warm underfoot, and the air carried the scent of bread and cooked roots drifting from the kitchen windows.

She spotted Father Alaric near the garden fence, shepherding a trio of younger children back toward the main building.

His voice was calm and steady. And even when one of the girls giggled and tried to escape into the grass again, he simply caught her by the shoulder, turning her gently around with practiced ease.

“Inside now, all of you. Wash your hands properly this time. Yes, even the backs.”

Elynara raised a hand slightly as she neared.

“Evening.”

Alaric glanced over, smiling warmly.

“Ah, there you are.”

“Need an extra set of hands?”

“Always.”

They worked side by side for a time. Elynara passed bowls to the children, fetched extra ladles, cleaned up a small spill where one of the boys had gotten a bit too enthusiastic with his stew. Starpuff stayed close, brushing once or twice past her ear like a faint breeze. She suppressed a smile.

By the time the hall had quieted and the children had been ushered off to the dormitories, the last of the dishes were drying on the racks and the sky outside had deepened to dusk.

She found Father Alaric outside again, seated under the awning near the church’s side garden, a wooden bench supporting his back as he quietly watched the sky shift colors.

Elynara hesitated only for a moment before stepping closer.

“Father Alaric?”

He looked up at her, then patted the bench beside him.

She graciously took him up on the invitation. Though it took her longer than she would like to admit to find her words.

“I… leveled up today,” she said softly, eyes on her hands. “And… I killed my first monster.”

Alaric’s expression didn’t change, but something in his posture eased, like hearing that let him breathe a little deeper. As if he expected to hear something worse. Instead he responded with genuine care.

“Well done.”

“I wasn’t trying to,” she added quickly, then winced. “That came out wrong. I was careful, I swear. I just… wanted to see if anything would react if I cast near the trees. And it did.”

He hummed.

“That’s often how these things go. You’re learning to plan, not to react. That’s all there is to it.”

A small silence followed, filled only by the sounds of night life starting to stir and the faint clink of pots being moved somewhere inside.

She shifted again, weirdly relieved, like a small weight dropped from her shoulders. But it didn’t take long before her curiosity got the better of her.

“Can I ask you something else?”

“Of course.”

She hesitated, then spoke.

“What do you know about spirits? Really?”

Alaric was quiet for a long moment, watching the last rays of light slip below the rooftops.

“Very little. Only what the old records say. Which isn’t much.”

He looked over at her.

“You probably already know more than I do.”

She blinked at that.

“They used to be more common, or so the stories go. Some people even bonded with them. Helped them. Others offered prayers. Food, tokens, small acts of thanks. Here, we used to believe they blessed the harvest if you kept them content. Now?”

He shook his head slowly.

“Most have never seen one. I certainly haven’t. Though you will still find totems and little shrines to them in places.”

Elynara looked down at her hands, thumb brushing over the inside of her palm.

“And the System?”

Alaric leaned back against the bench, folding his arms.

“A blessing, we say. A gift from the divine, because we don’t know what else it is. It remembers things for us, but only those it recognizes. It wasn’t always so faint, if the old texts are right. It shows us who we are, yet has no hand in who we become.”

“But like the mana, it diminished?”

He nodded once.

“The world was richer once, in magic and meaning. And something… drained it. Slowly. Bit by bit. Maybe spirits left with it. Maybe not. Whatever happened is anybody’s guess, and beyond what this old man knows.”

He turned to her with a small, fond smile.

“But what matters is that you’re still growing.”

He reached out and gently patted her on the head, his hand warm and firm.

“I’m proud of you.”

Her face turned warm at the unexpected praise, a flush creeping up her neck.

“T–thank you.”

There was a soft puff of air behind them.

Starpuff, having perched atop Alaric’s head without notice, now hovered dramatically between them, then floated down to bop Elynara lightly on the forehead like it was giving its own proud approval.

She blinked, then laughed quietly.

“You too, huh?”

Like the breeze, the moment was fleeting, soft, and whole..

And soon she said her goodbyes, retiring for the night.

Later, in the quiet of her small room, Elynara changed into her sleepwear, then crossed to the window to pull it shut. The stars were only just beginning to show, a pale scattering across the darkening sky.

She sat cross-legged on her bed, the staff resting nearby, notebook set aside.

One last thing to do before sleep.

She closed her eyes, reaching inward, gathering mana slowly and gently, feeding it into her core until it pressed faintly at the edges. Pushing. Expanding. Growing.

It became her little routine now. The first on her path to doing magic.

When she finally lay back and pulled the covers over her shoulders, Starpuff settled nearby on the pillow, pulsing softly.

Elynara exhaled.

She was tired. Satisfied.

And tomorrow, she would rise again.
