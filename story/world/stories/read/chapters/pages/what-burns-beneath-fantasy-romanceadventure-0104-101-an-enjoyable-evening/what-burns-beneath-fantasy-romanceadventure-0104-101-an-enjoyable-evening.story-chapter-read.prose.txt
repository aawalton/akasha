Velidan trailed a short distance away, perusing stalls and watching nearby creatures while I stood lost in my thoughts. Weighing everything he’d said and trying to come up with any reason for him to have lied.

What could he possibly gain from making any of that up? Other than… perhaps he just thinks it’s funny to rattle me. But surely there’s better ways than by fabricating lies about history!

Although… what was it he said about Virtue? ‘All Fae know that tale.’ Ashes, and I’m surrounded by Fae, aren’t I? I don’t have to just take his word for all this!

I trotted back towards the beautiful, winged woman. She was kneeling in the grass again. A gaggle of those squat, hairy men stared dreamily at her from some distance away.

“Excuse me.” I blushed when she turned a serene smile in my direction. Once again, I simply couldn’t tear my eyes away. “I’m sorry to disturb you.”

“Disturb me? Never. Nymphs and the daughters of men were dear friends, once.” The statement only brought a new, dizzying tide of questions. The Fae- a nymph, apparently- wafted one hand above blue-green grass around her. “Will you sit?”

I nearly did, but then was struck by the unsettling feeling that if I sat beside her, I might never get back up. A chill followed the notion. I remained standing.

“No, thank you. I wanted to ask… do you know who Virtue was?”

Silvery brows drew together and her full lips pushed into a pout. “Oh. Mournful, mortal. A sorrow that echoes still. The seas wept when she died.”

I swallowed. “And… how did she die?”

The Fae bowed her head. Her shimmering hands pressed down, curling into the dirt, then splayed flat with a jolt. “Roots,” she gasped. “Binding, squeezing, feeding… oh, she was so frightened.” Her voice broke. Jewel-bright tears filled her eyes. “She cried for her mother, for her father’s arms. And still, the witch twisted-”

Then, as if a spell had been lifted, she raised both hands and the pleasantly distant expression returned. “Only a memory, mortal. The stars watched.”

My heart drummed a maddening rhythm. I stared, stunned into horrified silence, for what seemed a very long time.

Eventually my feet carried me towards three slender, green-scaled men who sat playing some kind of tiled game. The ground beneath them trailed from grass to ancient, cracked stone where the courtyard funneled into the castle’s massive interior.

“Excuse me.”

Six vibrantly shaded, slitted eyes lifted to mine. Their expressions were not friendly. One of the Fae snapped his teeth and gave a snake-like hiss.

Right. Perhaps not everyone’s going to be as friendly as the nymph or the satyr. But he did say violence was forbidden, so…

I stepped back but managed, “I’m sorry, I just… could you please tell me who Anduin was?”

One of the Fae bared thin, curving teeth at me and snarled, “Meat.” They returned to their game.

“Answer.” The silky menace just behind my back made me shiver, though it was surely not directed towards me.

The scaled Fae peered back up, looked past my shoulder, then dropped so his snout was touching the grass. The other two followed suit, and the hiss that rose up was muffled. “He who burnssss. The day prince.”

Another rasped, “Sssssun.” His voice was strained, as if he barely remembered how to use it. “Hurtssss.”

I hurried away. The patter of my boots on dewy earth became a harder tap-tap as I went inside, where floating globes and wisps illuminated vast, half-crumbling walls. One side of the space was a long segment of uneven stone with humpbacked figures stirring enormous pots, tearing meat into strips, mashing berries, cooking over smoldering green campfires, and ladling broth. Other creatures ate, spoke, and milled at tables and blankets scattered throughout the room. Ragged drapes and tarnished chandeliers hung high above. Smoke carried the heady scents of meat and spices through the air, casting everything into balmy warmth.

I stopped a few more times, at faces that seemed very puzzled to see me but not outright hostile, and asked similar questions. The answers were similar, too, and after a while of this I staggered to the far, more secluded wall. This one smelled of fragrant oils and sweat. A very tall, wide curtain strung from cascading flower petals and glinting beads hung further on. It presumably led to a space deeper within the castle.

Velidan watched bemusedly as I dragged a hand through my hair, shaking my head and feeling thoroughly lost. “You could go question Adara, if you’ve the stomach to re-enter her presence. I believe she was alive when the realms were sealed.”

I wasn’t sure I wanted to press for more. Not now, when this was already too much, not when my head was already spinning.

I spent a long moment gazing out into the sea of smoke, lights, and otherworldly creatures. When I finally found my voice, it was barely a whisper. “How could we have gotten it so wrong?”

He was silent for a while. Then, with a pensive frown, “How is human history recounted?”

I spread my hands helplessly. “Well… through books and stories, mostly. There are libraries and archives in a few of the greater cities, places where people record events. Lighthold’s full of scholars and… and…” I trailed off, gaze falling, because none of the answers seemed like enough. Saints, if any of this was true, they weren’t nearly enough.

“Ah. So events from ages ago were written down by confused mortals who likely had little understanding of what was actually happening.” The mocking edge made me wince. “And those stories were passed down through generations of humans who are terrified of Fae. Who would never think to venerate one.”

I hated his obvious disdain nearly as much as I hated how much the words made sense.

He finished with a derisive, “And you truly can’t fathom how the truth could be twisted into myth?”

“Well, when you put it like that,” I growled through clenched teeth. My anger just earned an infuriating chuckle, and then it was all followed by a thought so unsettling my heart skipped a beat. “What does this mean about Thalessians? Paladins? Salt, are they pacted?!” I felt dizzy again and leaned against the wall.

The Fae’s features twisted in thought. “Hm. An interesting question. That Order was formed long after the realms were sealed. It’s something I know little about. They’re skilled warriors, from my understanding.”

My mouth felt very dry. “They’re… y-yes, but they’re… I… I don’t know, exactly.” I looked at him, incredibly fearful of this line of reasoning. “They’re fast and strong. Almost like… like you.”

“Curious. From how Embriel tells it, they die easily enough.” He shrugged, dismissing the chilling statement. “Thalessa doesn’t speak, and if humans consider her a goddess… well, perhaps your paladins are bound in some sense, but I doubt it’s anything like a Night Court pact. They may not even realize it.”

“There’s some kind of ritual they do to receive her blessing.” I stared at the shadowed stones. Each word fell hollow and distant. “They drink wine and pray to her, and make some kind of offering. Then they’re… different, afterwards. And… Renner said there’s stories about how if they’re blessed too young, awful things start to… they have strange dreams and they… h-hurt themselves.”

Velidan paced a short distance away, scoffing. “This all sounds like further proof that you humans are little more than mindless sheep, Brin. I’m curious what the offering involved in this ritual is, though. And,” he added with a wicked laugh, “I’d wager they’re not drinking wine.”

My eyes stared unseeing. The world became a fuzzy hum once more as it all sank in, unbelievable and horrifying and overwhelming.

Because… what? If it’s all true, if Thalessa’s an ancient, monstrous Fae, what can I possibly do about it? Go tell everyone? They’d never believe me, and even if they did… witchwood kills Fae! We need it! And the Thalessians protect people! It’s not as though they could dissolve the Order or abandon their own strength. Saints, it would be terrible if they did! The realm needs them, probably now more than ever! Even if they are pacted, or something like it. Even if some of them are terrible, even if they’ll do all those awful things Horace said, even if…

A dreadful realization snaked behind all that. My gaze trailed to Velidan. He was watching several glowing wisps dance high above us.

“You don’t know.” The whisper slipped out unbidden. I stared at him, dumbfounded and dazed and fighting the sudden tide of panic.

He has no idea they execute pacted. He can’t, otherwise we wouldn’t be doing this! The whole point of taking me along tomorrow is to keep me safe from Fae, but then he wants me to run off and warn the paladins about what’s coming. He has no idea-

“I don’t know what?”

I dropped my gaze, teeth sinking into my lower lip and hair falling as a curtain. My mind raced alongside my pulse. Salt. Shouldn’t have said… he can’t know. He needs me alive. He wants the shard. He won’t send me back to warn everyone if he knows it’ll jeopardize those two things.

My hand closed around the red jewel. The silver felt heavy and cold around my throat. “Nothing. S-saints, nothing.”

I pushed off the wall- straight into him as, faster than I could blink, he’d crossed the space between us.

“You look frightened, Brin. What don’t I know?”

My mouth went dry. “It’s not important. Where should we-”

“Answer.” There it was again; that soft undertone of menace, and now it assuredly was directed at me.

I shivered, taking a quick step towards the field. “Let’s go look-”

An arm rose to brace between me and the wall. “We’ve discussed this, Brin.”

I swallowed and managed, somewhat breathlessly, “We’ve discussed where we’re going next?”

“We’ve discussed,” the murmur came alongside rasping silk and the warmth of a body shifting much too close, “how poor you are at hiding things.”

“I’m not-”

“What don’t I know?”

My pulse fluttered and the cape fell, landing between our boots. I sucked in a breath, inching back until my shoulders curled against cold stone, and fought desperately to stay calm. “Well, p-probably a lot of things. About vegetables, obviously, and… and how long humans live, probably other things about the mortal realm, mayb-”

Velidan grasped my jaw, tilting up until those endless eyes held mine. He waited for a dozen wild heartbeats. Then, low and measured, “I won’t ask again.”

Every muscle screamed to run, or to yank the dagger from my boot and try to force him back. But of course both those actions would end poorly.

So instead I closed my eyes, seized the first desperate thought I could find amidst the tide of fear, and whispered, “You… y-you promised I’d… h-have an… enjoyable evening.”

My voice cracked and the plea fell as pitiful and as weak as the smoke around us. He’d promised that earlier, before I agreed to be trapped. But now I was trapped; there was no more need for him to be cordial or reassuring, pleasant would shatter for cruelty and-

He let me go.

The motion was so slow that I half-imaged it was some kind of panicked dream. But then all the muscle and heat hovering over me shifted with a thoughtful noise. I opened my eyes to Velidan taking a deliberate step back. Then he stooped, retrieved the fallen cape, and said wryly, “Mm. I suppose I did.”

I waited for more malice, but he just looked bemused. Perhaps even a bit surprised. My heart drummed an unsteady rhythm as I stood rigid, unwilling to believe that I’d somehow… won.

He raised a brow. “Would you prefer we keep discussing the fallibility of mortal minds, or resume shopping? Or,” dark hair swayed as he nodded towards the tower, “are you ready for something better than stale bread?”

I stood for a very long time, wringing my hem and glancing about uncertainty. Inhaling deeply, steadying myself, and letting my racing pulse even out.

Then, finally accepting that the moment of threat had truly passed and was not returning any time soon, I offered, “W-well… I’ve… I’ve enjoyed looking around.”

“I’m aware.” The statement, and the way his mouth curved, positively oozed arrogance.

“Mostly, at least.” I managed my own weak smile. “I’ve wanted to run away screaming from a few things, but this place is… it’s amazing.”

He glanced towards the field, eyes catching a handful of starlight and glittering with silver. “And you haven’t even gone into a pavilion yet.”

I examined the vibrant silken structures in the field’s center. “What’s in those?”

He held up the emerald cape, nose wrinkling with clear disdain. “More enchanting things than cloth, raw meat, and questionably lucky stones.”

That thought brought a fresh thrill. I considered the tempting words, then nodded towards the nearby curtain. “What about in there? Are there more things for sale?”

“There are,” he said carefully. “But I doubt you’ll want them.”

“That just makes me more curious.” Dwindling tension finally gave way to nervous enthusiasm. I paced towards the colorful, dangling divide. “Can we go in?”

“We can.”

My hands parted the beads and petals. They were thicker than I’d thought and sang out a chimelike ting that echoed through my bones. I stepped through… into a space that simply could not exist.

The castle walls were crumbling and ivy-strewn, weathered by age and storms. But beyond the curtain lay a vast dome-like chamber of polished ebony walls, cascading silk curtains, and spiraling black-iron staircases. Fragrant lavender, mint, honey, vanilla, sandalwood, and a dozen other unfamiliar- but equally enticing- aromas drifted from every direction. Floating globes lit the space with cozy purples and blues. More smoke curled through the air. It seemed thicker in here; pearly and dense with alluring scents, casting every shape into a dreamlike blur.

I turned another dazed circle as Velidan stepped in behind me. I started to ask where exactly we were when a muffled cry echoed from beyond one of the silk curtains. It was ragged and distinctly feminine.

I froze for a breath. Then, ignoring Velidan’s inexplicably amused command for me to wait, I charged forwards.
