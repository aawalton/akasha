Scary.

Su Fan couldn't help but have that thought as he watched Ning move.

He had assumed Ning was already out of the running for the top three. His performance during the high-grade portion had been too poor, especially compared to them, and while choosing to attempt the Peak First-Tier refinement might earn him some additional points if he succeeded, Su Fan hadn't expected it to change much.

Yet somehow, in the final hour, when everyone should have been exhausted and the slightest mistake could waste everything he'd done so far, Ning had suddenly burst forward, moving with an almost inhuman fluidity that seemed to lay bare the difference between him and everyone else still refining.

Each motion flowed naturally into the next as he caught one change after another, his hands never stopping for more than an instant, the medicinal essences guided into place almost as quickly as they shifted, his momentum only building as the refinement continued.

More ridiculous than any of that was the smile on his face.

At a moment like this, with a Peak First-Tier pill hanging on the edge of success or failure and a single mistake capable of ruining the entire batch, the guy looked like he was having the time of his life.

Is he some kind of monster?

Su Fan's brow creased.

It hadn't been that long since Ning first started learning alchemy. Su Fan could still remember him standing beside his furnace, preparing ingredients while watching each refinement with an almost irritating amount of attention.

Back then, Su Fan had been ahead of him in practically every aspect of alchemy. Yet now, that same guy was attempting something outrageous in the middle of a competition, and Su Fan could only watch.

The sight brought back memories of the Heavenly Origin Tower, especially the illusion trial and what he'd experienced inside it. He had never told anyone about it, and even now, he didn't like remembering it.

In the illusion, Su Fan had continued down the path of alchemy just as he'd always intended. Years passed, then decades, and he became an alchemist of some reputation, refining pills that his younger self would have celebrated for days.

He simply never became exceptional.

Others passed him. People he'd once considered beneath him advanced farther and faster, while his own progress slowed until every improvement demanded years of effort.

Meanwhile, his sister Su Xue continued advancing along the path the Su Family had prepared for her, her Cold Jade Hands complementing their talisman inheritance so perfectly that her name gradually spread beyond the family.

Despite everything, Su Fan continued refining, even as the people around him gradually lost faith in his decision.

At first, his family still argued with him. The elders reminded him how much easier his path would have been if he'd simply learned the Su Family's talisman arts, while his sister continued showing up every so often to lecture him and generally behave like the irritating woman she'd always been.

Eventually, however, even the arguments stopped, and that was the part he hated most.

In the illusion, Su Xue had visited him after another failed refinement and quietly helped him clean the furnace. Su Fan had been irritated enough to ask why she wasn't going to lecture him this time, and after a long silence, she'd simply said that he'd made his choice a long time ago.

The Tower had then shown him the other path, the one his family had always wanted him to follow.

In that life, he studied the Su Family's talisman inheritance under his sister, progressing rapidly with resources and teachings his ancestors had spent generations refining.

He wasn't as gifted as Su Xue, yet he had everything going for him to achieve at least a reasonable degree of success. His family approved, his cultivation advanced smoothly, and even his relationship with his sister seemed better when they weren't constantly arguing over what he should do with his life.

It was everything people had told him he was throwing away.

Then, one day in that false life, he happened to pass an alchemy pavilion. The familiar scent of medicinal herbs drifted through the air, accompanied by the distant sound of a furnace opening, and before he realized it, his footsteps had come to a halt.

That was where Su Fan had failed the illusion, and even now, he hated remembering why.

He hadn't wanted to abandon alchemy, yet when the Tower placed the two lives before him, he couldn't immediately choose it either.

For all his arrogance, for all those years spent insisting that he would carve out his own path and prove everyone wrong, some part of him had still wondered whether loving something was enough when another road might carry him so much farther.

After leaving the Tower, Su Fan had tried to answer that question the only way he knew how: by throwing himself into alchemy with even greater determination.

Whenever his progress fell short of his expectations, he practiced more. Failed refinements were repeated until they succeeded, and if someone else could refine three batches before exhaustion set in, he would force himself through four.

Tang Feiyu had told him more than once that he was being an idiot, advice Su Fan had ignored.

Yet now, looking at Ning's ridiculous smile, another memory surfaced.

It was what Ning had said before beginning this final refinement.

Failure is the mother of success, Elder.

And then, more seriously—

I would rather fail while moving forward than stop simply because success isn't guaranteed.

Su Fan watched him for several breaths before his expression gradually soured.

Damn it.

He'd been so busy worrying about whether he'd chosen the wrong path that he'd somehow begun treating every failure as evidence that perhaps he had.

How arrogant of him.

He hadn't even walked far enough to see where his path led.

What right did he have to decide its end already?

Su Fan let out a long breath. Strangely enough, he felt better than he had in months.

He still wanted to surpass his sister.

He still intended to become an alchemist so great that nobody in the Su Family would ever again suggest he'd wasted his talent, and, if possible, he would prefer to do it while looking sufficiently impressive that Su Xue became deeply annoyed.

But that could wait.

Right now, he was standing in an alchemy competition.

So he should stop worrying about some imaginary version of himself decades in the future and show what everything he'd learned until now was actually worth.

Su Fan raised his hand.

A flame appeared above his palm.

It had no wick and no visible source from which it burned, only a pale core surrounded by almost transparent fire that remained perfectly steady despite the movement of spiritual energy throughout the competition grounds.

Sourceless Flame.

Cultivators had discovered countless strange fires throughout the world.

Some were born from spiritual beasts and inherited through their bloodlines, while others emerged naturally where unusual conditions gathered over centuries, from the depths of the earth, ancient spiritual veins, lightning-struck lands, or places saturated with different forms of heavenly and earthly qi.

Alchemists prized such flames because each possessed different characteristics, and the right one could change how medicinal materials were purified, extracted, or integrated.

Beast flames were the most common, like the White Flame Ning borrowed from Fenghuang.

Sourceless Flame belonged to a rarer category.

It was prized for its stability. Once given qi and a desired intensity, the flame could maintain an unusually even output without constantly fluctuating with the cultivator's control, which made it excellent for long refinements and delicate stages where even minor changes in temperature could affect the medicine.

More importantly for Su Fan, it demanded far less attention than an ordinary spiritual flame, leaving him free to concentrate on the medicinal reactions inside his furnace.

He still remembered the day he'd received it.

It had been shortly after the Heavenly Origin Tower.

Su Fan had shut himself away after returning, and Su Xue had eventually barged into his room without bothering to knock, as usual. She'd tossed a sealed vessel onto his table and looked at him with that irritatingly indifferent expression of hers.

"I found this during my travels. Take it."

Su Fan had looked at the vessel, then at her. Even through the seal, he'd recognized what was inside.

"Sourceless Flame? You're giving this to me?"

"You've been moping around long enough to become annoying." Su Xue had turned toward the door before he could say anything else. "I don't need it, so use it if you want."

Then she'd left.

Su Fan had known she was lying.

A Sourceless Flame might have been more suited to alchemy, but a stable spiritual flame of that quality had plenty of uses even for a Foundation Building cultivator, especially one who practiced talisman arts. Su Xue could have kept it, traded it for something she needed, or sold it for a large number of spirit stones.

Instead, she'd brought it to the foolish younger brother who'd locked himself in his room after failing an illusion.

At the time, Su Fan had been too proud to say much.

Even now, he probably wouldn't.

The flame above his palm flickered as he looked across the competition grounds once more.

Ning's refinement was reaching its end.

Spiritual light had begun leaking from his furnace, and Su Fan could see that crazy smile still lingering on his face despite how exhausted he looked.

Su Fan gave a snort. "Don't get too pleased with yourself, Junior Brother."

He fed the Sourceless Flame into his furnace for the final stage.

For months, Su Fan had refined as though every pill needed to prove something about the rest of his life.

Enough of that.

Today he would simply refine.

He would use everything his sister had beaten into his skull, everything his master had taught him, every failure he'd suffered and every improvement he'd clawed out of those failures, and when this competition ended, he could look at the result and decide what still needed work.

The Sourceless Flame spread evenly through the furnace.

Su Fan's eyes followed it, the old arrogance returning to them as his lips slowly curved upward.

Ji Ning had certainly become troublesome, but Su Fan had no intention of being left behind.

...

By the time the last furnace had gone cold, the remaining contestants had gathered near the front of the competition grounds, where the pills produced during the final portion had been arranged separately for assessment.

Ning's Ninefold Essence Restoration Pill rested inside a small jade dish with the other successful Peak First-Tier pills. After everything that had happened during the refinement, seeing it sitting there so peacefully felt strangely anticlimactic, especially when he remembered how close the entire thing had been for him.

He truly had given everything he had and more.

Several elders stood behind the table, making their final assessments while the contestants and spectators waited. Most of the results had already been recorded during the competition itself, so only the final products needed to be confirmed before the overall rankings could be calculated.

Ning wasn't that nervous anymore. There wasn't much point.

One of the elders finally finished examining his Ninefold Essence Restoration Pill and passed it toward Fu Xuan, who studied it briefly before announcing, "Sixty-nine percent."

A small stir passed through the surrounding crowd.

Ning's lips twitched at the universal constant number he seemed to stumble upon quite often.

But in the end, this was much better than he'd expected.

It wasn't exceptional by Peak First-Tier standards, but none of his previous attempts at the formula had ever crossed forty percent, so he looked at the little pill with real affection.

Of course, Ning knew that his state during the final refinement had been exceptional. He had been in the zone, and it would probably be difficult to reach the same state of concentration and mind-body unification again.

And sixty-nine percent was respectable even among the Peak pills produced today.

Unfortunately, Ning wasn't the only person who had done well.

"Seventy-two percent."

Yue Chan's result drew another wave of conversation from the stands. Her performance had been stable throughout the entire competition, and even after so many consecutive refinements, she had still managed to produce a Peak First-Tier pill of that quality.

Then came Su Fan.

Fu Xuan examined the pill for several breaths before raising her eyes slightly.

"Seventy-five percent."

This time, even Ning turned fully toward him.

Su Fan stood with his arms folded, looking as though seventy-five percent had naturally been the expected outcome all along, but Ning could see the faint tightening around his eyes.

This guy had really pulled it off.

A few of the other Peak pills were assessed afterward, with He Ming also producing an excellent result while two more barely crossed the acceptable range, and once the final scores had been recorded, the elders gathered briefly around Fu Xuan.

It didn't take long for the result to be announced despite the number of things that needed to be judged, from material preparation to even failures in refinement.

Fu Xuan eventually stepped forward, and the conversations throughout the competition grounds gradually disappeared.

"The assessments are complete. Now, I will announce the results."

Ning straightened along with everyone else.

Fu Xuan began with the lower placements, announcing those who had reached the upper ranks one after another, and each name brought its own burst of applause from the stands.

Luo Xiulan appeared among them, followed by Shen Lu, whose excellent first-stage performance had carried considerable weight despite his later results being less dominant.

Then Fu Xuan reached the four names Ning actually cared about. "Fourth place, Ji Ning."

Cheers rose from somewhere behind him.

Ning released a slow breath.

Fourth, huh?

He'd known it was possible. Honestly, after the mess he'd made of the high-grade portion, placing fourth overall was already better than he'd expected, especially considering how little time he'd spent learning alchemy.

Still, the result stung.

Almost instinctively, Ning glanced toward Ling Xin.

His master was clapping enthusiastically and shouting his name at the top of her lungs, displaying none of the dignity one might expect from a Foundation Building cultivator.

Well, at least she didn't look disappointed.

"Third place, Su Fan."

The cheering grew louder, especially from the section occupied by the Alchemy Hall disciples, and Ning saw Tang Feiyu's expression soften as she looked toward her disciple.

“Second place, He Ming.”

That announcement drew another round of enthusiastic applause.

He Ming was practically the definition of keeping a low profile while quietly getting things done. He rarely attracted attention, but his steady performance and years of experience had clearly paid off.

Finally, Fu Xuan announced, "First place, Yue Chan."

The competition grounds erupted.

Yue Chan bowed deeply toward Fu Xuan before turning toward Cai Yun, whose composed expression lasted for approximately three breaths before Ling Xin leaned toward her and said something Ning couldn't hear.

Whatever it was, Cai Yun's expression immediately changed.

Ning wisely looked away.

Some battles were better left between masters.

As the cheering continued, Ning glanced at the rankings once more before turning his attention toward the other contestants.

"Junior Brother Ning."

Ning turned and found Su Fan approaching him. He wasn't openly grinning or gloating, but the slight upward curve at the corner of his mouth made his satisfaction obvious enough.

“It seems I came out ahead of you after all.”

Ning looked at him for a moment before smiling. "You did. It seems all that hard work actually paid off in the end. Congratulations, Senior Brother."

Su Fan's expression turned complicated. “…That's it?”

Ning blinked. “Hm?”

“I thought you'd be more upset.”

Ning considered that. “Would you prefer it if I cried?”

Su Fan's face flattened. “Forget I asked.”

Ning laughed lightly, but before he could turn away, he noticed that Su Fan was still standing there, his earlier smugness gradually fading. For several seconds, he seemed to be considering something, which was unusual enough that Ning waited instead of interrupting him.

Eventually, Su Fan spoke. “Thank you.”

“What brought that on?” Ning asked, surprised.

Su Fan glanced toward the competition grounds, where several attendants were already beginning to clear the furnaces.

“I was starting to doubt whether I should continue pursuing alchemy.”

“I see.” Ning nodded. He didn't immediately say something like impossible or anything of the sort.

Perhaps it was the illusion in the Tower?

Su Fan hadn't elaborated on what had happened inside the Heavenly Origin Tower, and Ning hadn't asked. Whatever Su Fan had seen during the illusion trial had clearly stayed with him, judging by how hard he'd been pushing himself ever since.

“For a while, I wasn't certain whether I'd chosen the right path,” Su Fan continued. “I thought that if I simply worked hard enough, I'd eventually find the answer, but the more I refined, the more every failure began to feel like proof that perhaps I shouldn't have chosen alchemy in the first place.”

He paused, looking faintly annoyed at himself for admitting even that much.

“Then I watched you.”

Ning pointed at himself. “Me? That's flattering.”

“Your high-grade refinements were terrible,” Su Fan said, ignoring him. “Yet you still chose to continue, and when you started that final refinement, you looked happier than you had at any other point today.”

His gaze shifted toward the jade dish containing Ning's pill.

“Then I remembered what you told the elder.”

Ning remembered. At the time, he'd just been farming social points as usual.

Su Fan continued, “I don't know whether I'll become the alchemist I intend to be, but deciding I've already failed would be ridiculous.”

Ning's expression became increasingly strange.

Social Fu... too strong.

Had he seriously inspired Su Fan by accident? He hadn't realized his charisma stat was this high.

Su Fan gave him a suspicious look. “Why are you making that face?”

“No reason.” Ning coughed into his fist. “Well, if that's the case, I'll accept your thanks. I was pretty cool back there, wasn't I?”

“Hmph. Don't get too full of yourself. I suppose your performance was barely passable.”

Su Fan's mouth twitched, though Ning could see some of the tension leaving his expression.

“Besides, if someone had to push me out of the top three, I'm glad it was you,” Ning continued. “You helped me a lot back when I was still in the outer sect, so I'm happy I could return the favor.”

That caught Su Fan off guard.

Ning meant it, too. Back when he'd barely started learning alchemy, Su Fan had gifted him flawless Star-Marked Red Bud Pills and even allowed him to observe his refinements up close.

“So congratulations, Senior Brother,” Ning said. “You earned it.”

Su Fan was silent for a moment before giving a small nod. “If you say so.”

“That's a strangely reluctant way to accept praise.” Ning couldn't help but smile.

“I don't need your praise.” Su Fan glanced toward the rankings again. “In any case, don't become discouraged. Your performance wasn't terrible.”

Ning raised an eyebrow. This senior brother might be the second most awkward person he'd ever had a conversation with. First place, of course, still belonged to the protagonist.

It was funny when he thought about it.

Su Fan had helped him when he'd barely established himself in the sect, and today, without realizing it, Ning had returned some of that help when Su Fan needed it.

One person helped another, who eventually returned that help in a completely different form. It was all just a huge cycle.

The thought lingered.

Is this... karma?

Something stirred in his mind, half-understood, and the mark on his forehead briefly reappeared.

...Hmm.

It had reacted so weakly that he might have missed it if he hadn't become so familiar with his own body, but there was no mistaking the sensation. The mark connected to the Soul Ferrying Scripture had given off the faintest pulse before settling again.

Ning's thoughts raced at the implication.

"Anyway." Su Fan's voice shattered the thought. "You shouldn't feel too bad about losing. As a young master of the Su Family, it's only natural that I'm better."

Saying that, the young man walked away with light steps, seemingly satisfied with whatever that exchange had been.

I guess at least he didn't say smell ya later.

Ning shook his head and watched him leave.

Well, whatever. He had more important things to worry about. He had come here hoping to place in the top three, and despite everything he'd managed during the competition, he'd still fallen short.

Before Ning could dwell on that disappointment for long, however, two familiar figures had already made their way through the dispersing contestants and crowded around him.

"You did great, my dear disciple!"

Ling Xin got there first, naturally, looking happier about fourth place than Ning himself felt. "Especially that last Peak First-Tier pill. Everyone thought you couldn't do it after seeing your high-grade refinements, but you still pulled it off. As expected of my Ling Xin's disciple, Ahahaha!"

Ning watched his master as she laughed proudly enough for several nearby alchemists to glance over.

Ling Yinran arrived a moment later and smiled at him. "Congratulations, Junior Brother. Fourth among everyone who participated is already an excellent result, especially considering how long you've been learning alchemy, and that final refinement was..." She paused, searching for the appropriate word. "Rather frightening to watch."

"Thank you, Senior Sister. I think." Ning didn't know what was frightening about it. He'd gotten the same feeling from Su Fan when he talked about his refinement.

He'd just been doing everything normally, though?

Ling Yinran laughed softly.

Ning looked between them and smiled. "You know, the two of you are making it difficult for me to feel miserable about fourth place."

"What defeat?" Ling Xin waved the matter away as though fourth place had been the plan from the beginning. "Everyone fails sometimes, and you still accomplished your main goal. You wanted the Alchemist Jade, didn't you? You got it, refined a Peak First-Tier pill, and even came fourth after studying alchemy for barely a year. As for what you told Master about reaching the top three..." She shrugged. "I'll talk to her about it. You don't need to worry."

Ning fell silent.

Despite not being the most sociable person, she'd noticed what was troubling him and had already offered to take care of it. Ning looked at his master for several seconds, finding her smiling as casually as ever, as though she'd done nothing worth mentioning.

"I'm happy to be your disciple, Master." Ning smiled. "I couldn't have asked for a better master."

"W-Where did that come from?" Ling Xin blinked, caught off guard by the sudden praise, before breaking into a delighted grin. "Hehe, then you can make me more snacks!"

"...Master, never change."

"Aunt!" Ling Yinran immediately protested. "Don't take advantage of Junior Brother just because he's being sincere."

"How is that taking advantage of him? Your junior brother likes cooking, I like eating, everyone benefits."

"I don't think that's how everyone benefits."

Ning sighed, although the smile refused to leave his face. "I'll make something later."

"See?" Ling Xin looked at her niece with a triumphant expression. "Everyone benefits."

Ling Yinran glanced at her aunt, then at Ning. “Junior Brother, you spoil her too much.”

“Senior Sister, I've realized that resistance only wastes energy.”

“Good disciple.” Ling Xin nodded approvingly. Yes, it was only natural for disciples to spoil their master.

“That wasn't praise, Master.”

Ling Xin seemed perfectly willing to accept it as such anyway.

It wasn't long before the others found him as well. Wei Zhusang arrived with Zhang Feng, Lang Rulang, Fatty Li, and several familiar faces from the Mutual Aid Association, all eager to congratulate him.

“Brother Ning! Fourth place!” Fatty Li exclaimed, clapping him enthusiastically on the shoulder. “And that final pill! You should've seen everyone's faces!”

“I'm glad I could provide some entertainment for you all.” Ning could only laugh.

To be fair, none of them knew he'd been aiming for the top three. As far as they were concerned, placing fourth despite having only recently started learning alchemy was an incredible achievement, and their excitement showed it.

Fatty Li was practically acting as his personal hype man, and he was damn good at it, too. Then again, after hyping up Xiao Fan for so long, he'd probably attained Mastery in the art by now.

Ning was a little surprised by Zhang Feng's fascination with the pill he'd improvised earlier. Normally, the guy was pretty chill about such things.

Lang Rulang, meanwhile, simply congratulated him before adding, “Win next time.”

Well, that was probably the most encouragement anyone could expect from Lang Rulang.

The competition didn't end immediately after that. Once the rankings were settled, the elders moved on to the rewards, calling the highest-ranked contestants forward one after another while attendants brought out jade boxes.

The rewards were, frankly, outrageous.

Yue Chan, as the first-place winner, received an entire year's refinement allowance amounting to one hundred and twenty First-Tier ingredient portions, three rare First-Tier formulas of her choice, several precious Second-Tier medicinal materials, access to one of the Alchemy Hall's superior refinement rooms, and first priority for the upcoming spiritual alchemist certification assessment.

He Ming received a smaller but still ridiculous version of the same package in second place, while Su Fan's third-place reward included sixty refinement portions, a rare formula, a Second-Tier medicinal material, access to the superior refinement rooms, and the final priority position for the certification assessment.

Even the rewards below the top three were substantial. Ning received thirty portions of High and Peak First-Tier medicinal materials, the right to select a rare First-Tier formula, and, most importantly, the Alchemist Jade he'd originally entered the competition hoping to obtain.

He turned the small jade token over in his hand after receiving it with a smile.

At least, I fulfilled one of my objectives for the competition.

Still, when Ning glanced toward the sixty free refinement portions allocated to Su Fan, the difference between third and fourth suddenly became more painful.

Thirty extra batches.

Ning's money-loving brain mourned every spirit stone he could have made from them, but it wasn't meant to be.

Had this been almost any other hall, Ning might have suspected that someone had accidentally added an extra zero to the rewards, but this was the Alchemy Hall, one of the wealthiest branches of the Pure Qi Sect.

Even then, the reactions around him made it obvious that today's generosity was unusual.

Ning supposed Fu Xuan hadn't been exaggerating when she'd said the sect intended to invest more seriously in the younger generation.

By the time the rewards had all been distributed, and the elders finally declared the competition concluded, much of the afternoon had passed. Contestants gradually separated to join their masters or fellow disciples, and Ning found himself dragged through yet more congratulations before he could leave.

Ling Xin and Ling Yinran stayed with him through most of it.

"Seriously, Master, Senior Sister, you don't need to keep looking at me like that." Ning tapped the jade hanging at his waist before smiling. "Fourth place isn't exactly something I'm going to cry myself to sleep over."

Ling Xin studied him suspiciously for another moment before deciding he was telling the truth. "Fine. But we're celebrating later."

"It's still about food, isn't it?"

Ling Yinran laughed, while Ling Xin simply ignored the complaint and began suggesting dishes anyway.

Eventually, there was nothing left keeping them there. Ning said goodbye to his master and senior sister, exchanged a few final words with Wei Zhusang and the others, then started down the path leading away from the Alchemy Hall with Fenghuang sleeping comfortably in his arms.

The raven had thoroughly exhausted himself during the final refinement and, after receiving what he considered an appropriate amount of praise for his heroic contribution, had decided consciousness was no longer necessary.

His head rested against Ning's arm while his feathers rose and fell faintly with each breath, and even when Ning adjusted his grip, Fenghuang merely shifted closer and continued sleeping.

Ning smiled down at him. "You worked hard too."

Fenghuang gave no response.

Probably for the best. If he were awake, he'd demand food.

Now that he thought about it, he really was surrounded by quite a lot of foodies.

Eh, must just be a coincidence.

Ning continued toward the outer courtyard when the sound of an argument caught his attention.

"How many times do I have to explain this? It was all part of my plan!"

Ning slowed his steps.

Oh? That voice sounds familiar.

Sure enough, a short distance ahead, several disciples had gathered around a familiar figure in lavender robes. Feng Yue stood among them with her arms crossed, looking thoroughly offended by whatever accusations they were throwing her way.

"Your plan?" one of the disciples demanded. "You blew up half the ingredients during the second stage! Do you have any idea how much trouble that caused us?"

"Half? What an exaggeration!" Feng Yue scoffed. "Besides, there were plenty of ingredients left afterward. If you couldn't adapt to a little unexpected difficulty, you should reconsider becoming an alchemist."

"A little difficulty? I spent months preparing for that formula, and because of your explosion, I couldn't even complete the refinement!"

"Then you should've prepared another formula. Honestly, do I have to teach you everything?"

The disciple looked ready to explode himself.

Ning watched with growing amusement as Feng Yue insisted that the entire incident had been a valuable lesson in adaptability. When someone pointed out that she'd crashed into the table after losing her balance, she blamed the disciple who'd released the Silvermind Root, only to claim a moment later that she'd anticipated the explosion all along.

The other disciples looked increasingly exasperated, though Feng Yue seemed quite satisfied with her explanation.

"Forget it. There's no point arguing with her," another disciple muttered. "She couldn't even prepare her own ingredients properly, and after all that boasting, she didn't even make the top three."

Feng Yue's smug expression stiffened. "Hmph! As if someone like me cares about a mere ranking. This competition is only one small step on my path to becoming the greatest alchemist in the world!"

"Then you should learn how to prepare a Violet Dusk Flower without murdering it first."

Several disciples laughed, and Feng Yue's face turned red. She opened her mouth to retort, but for once, no suitably magnificent explanation seemed to come to mind.

Ning noticed her eyes growing suspiciously red. So, he stepped forward and cupped his hands. "Senior Brothers, the competition is already over. I understand everyone's frustration, but arguing here won't recover the ingredients or change the results. Besides, we're still inside the Alchemy Hall. It would be unfortunate if the elders had to come over and settle the matter themselves."

One of the disciples frowned. "Junior Brother Ning, you were caught in that explosion too. Surely you understand how troublesome it was?"

"Of course. I had to change my formula at the last moment because of it." Ning sighed. "But I managed to refine something in the end, and I learned quite a bit from the experience. Since nothing can be changed now, it's better to leave the matter here."

The disciples exchanged dissatisfied glances, but none seemed eager to cause trouble in the Alchemy Hall. After a few more complaints, they finally dispersed, though one of them still warned Feng Yue to stay away from them.

Feng Yue immediately shouted after him that his mediocre ingredients weren't worth her attention in the first place.

Only when the disciples disappeared around the corner did she finally stop glaring after them.

"Hmph! What a bunch of sore losers. Blaming someone else for their own failures. Honestly, the younger generation these days has no sense of responsibility."

Ning looked at her.

Senior Sister, are you sure you want to discuss responsibility?

"Anyway, why are you here?" Feng Yue demanded, deciding that the previous conversation was beneath further discussion.

Ning glanced toward the path leading to his residence. "I'm walking home."

"...Oh."

Feng Yue seemed to realize that this was, in fact, a perfectly reasonable thing to do on a path leading toward the residential courtyards.

Ning smiled, though his gaze briefly drifted toward her reddened eyes. "Are you all right, Senior Sister?"

"Of course I'm all right! What could possibly be wrong with me?" Feng Yue answered immediately, rubbing at one eye before he could say anything else. "Something flew into my eye, that's all. Very irritating. Both eyes, actually."

Ning looked around the remarkably clean stone courtyard before nodding solemnly. "That makes sense."

"Exactly!"

Feng Yue seemed relieved that the matter had been settled through such flawless reasoning and quickly changed the subject.

"Anyway, you received your Alchemist Jade, right?"

Ning nodded. "I did."

"Good." Feng Yue folded her arms, recovering a little of her usual confidence. "If I'd performed slightly better during the first stage, you probably would have gotten priority for the certification assessment as well."

"Maybe," Ning said. "Or maybe not. There were three stages, and quite a lot happened afterward. There's no way to know whether those points would have changed the final ranking, so Senior Sister doesn't need to worry about it."

To be honest, Ning didn't know if what Feng Yue did boosted his score or tanked it.

"Worry?" Feng Yue's back straightened. "Why would someone as magnificent as me worry about something like that? I was merely pitying you."

Ning smiled.

After spending an entire competition around Feng Yue, Ning was beginning to understand her temperament. Her ability to explain away even the most disastrous failures was genuinely impressive, and he couldn't help wondering how long she could keep it up.

"I see. Thank you for your pity, Senior Sister."

"You're welcome."

"Then I suppose not reaching the top three was also part of Senior Sister's plan?"

Feng Yue froze for a moment before quickly raising her chin. "O-Of course! Someone had to give the others a chance. It wouldn't be much of a competition if I took all the glory for myself."

"How generous of Senior Sister," Ning said, suppressing a smile. "So Senior Sister wasn't crying because of the rankings."

"Obviously not! Something flew into my eyes, just as I said." Feng Yue stuttered for a moment before continuing, "If I were in your position, though, perhaps I would cry a little. You were only one place away from the top three. That's much worse."

Ning's eyebrow twitched.

Thank you for reminding me, Senior Sister.

"It's okay, Senior Sister. I knew failure was possible when I entered the competition, and now I know where I'm lacking. I'll just improve before the next one."

Feng Yue nodded approvingly. "Good! That's the proper attitude. Of course, someone like me has no need to worry about such things, but ordinary alchemists need to learn from their failures."

"...Thank you for your guidance, Senior Sister."

"You're welcome."

Ning decided that he'd probably received enough profound wisdom for one evening.

He reached into his storage pouch, where he still carried a few pieces of candy, and after briefly considering his options, pulled one out and held it toward Feng Yue.

"Here."

She stared at his hand. "...What's this?"

"Candy. It's for cheering you up."

Feng Yue's face reddened. "I already told you I'm not upset!"

"Then it's a celebration candy."

Faced with such flawless logic, Feng Yue could only snatch the candy from his hand, turning her face away as she examined the wrapper.

"Since you're insisting, I'll accept it. You should've said it was a celebration gift from the beginning."

"Of course. My mistake, Senior Sister."

"Hmph. At least you know."

Ning started walking again before pausing after a few steps. "Senior Sister."

"What now?"

"We should work together sometime."

Feng Yue blinked, looking genuinely surprised.

"On alchemy experiments," Ning clarified. "Some of the things you tried during the competition were interesting."

"So you've finally recognized my brilliance." Feng Yue, thy name was confidence.

"Something like that."

"Very well. I suppose I can spare some time for a promising junior brother who knows how to appreciate true talent."

"Then I'll find you later. Good night, Senior Sister."

Ning continued toward his residence, leaving Feng Yue standing beneath the stone pillar with the candy still in her hand.

She watched him disappear around the corner before looking down at it. For a while, she simply stood there, her fingers slowly turning the little wrapper over.

Then she sniffed and hurriedly rubbed her eyes with her sleeve.

"...Stupid Junior Brother."

Feng Yue unwrapped the candy and popped it into her mouth, chewing thoughtfully for a moment before giving a small, satisfied nod.

"Hmph. Not bad. It seems Junior Brother has excellent taste in candy, too."

Unfortunately, there was nobody around to appreciate this profound conclusion, which was probably for the best.

By then, Ning was already on his way home, occasionally thinking back to the peculiar senior sister he'd just left behind.

For a while, he'd genuinely wondered whether Feng Yue had been planted among the contestants by the Alchemy Hall to deliberately make the competition more difficult. After everything that had happened during the first two stages, he wouldn't have been surprised if Fu Xuan had personally arranged it.

Still, some of the things Feng Yue had attempted were genuinely interesting, even if the results had been somewhat disastrous. Ning had noticed a few unusual medicinal reactions among her experiments, and he was curious whether any of them could be reproduced under more controlled conditions.

By the time he reached home, darkness had begun settling across the valley.

Ning carried Fenghuang inside and placed him in the little bed he'd prepared for him. The raven shifted when his body touched the bedding and tucked his head beneath one wing without opening his eyes.

"Sleep well."

Ning stretched until several joints cracked, then dragged himself toward the bed. The moment he sat down, the exhaustion he'd been keeping at bay seemed to remember that it existed.

He took a pill to refresh himself slightly before falling backward onto the bed, where he remained for a while with his face buried in the soft pillow.

Unfortunately, his mind had no intention of letting him rest just yet.

The final rankings came back to him.

Fourth.

"Darn it. After I said all that."

Not only that, he'd even soliloquized, done the whole dramatic finish and everything. With that kind of setup and all that drama, the ending had genuinely made him feel as though he'd reached the top three.

Then, at the end, Su Fan had come out of nowhere and defeated him.

Ning knew that defeat was natural, but it still irked him. Especially when he remembered how he'd acted.

"Ugh." He rolled back and forth against the pillow. "...This really sucks."

From the other side of the room came the faint sound of Fenghuang breathing in his sleep.

Ning remained facedown on the bed for several more seconds before letting out a long, muffled groan into the pillow.

"...Senior Sister Feng Yue was right. Why is there so much dirt in this place?"
