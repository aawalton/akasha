Eola’s eyes were glued to the yellow envelope with the school’s wax seal. She knew exactly what it was; it sat on her desk, where she’d tossed it fifteen minutes ago.

She’d locked her door, and, every so often, her bleary eyes shifted to the portrait on her wall. Her parents stared back at her, and so did the younger version of her, from before she’d gone to live with her uncle, but after she’d learned about her attunement.

Eola didn’t get letters.

With her parents trapped in the dungeon below Lord Card’s castle, she couldn’t exactly write them, and they definitely couldn’t respond. And she and her uncle had agreed when she went to Varin’s that the fewer eyes in Greenarbor on her, the better. If she could disappear from the town without so much as a sign, that would be best. She’d sent one to him to confirm she’d made it, and she’d send and receive one during the three-week break between terms, but other than that, nothing. Her suitemates had probably noticed, but she didn’t care.

It was better this way. Safer. Lord Card might be watching.

Her spellbook sat in her lap, both here in the room and in the portrait. All three sets of painted eyes watched her as she reached for the letter for the fourth time. Her breath stopped.

This time, she slid a finger under the wax and popped it open, then started to read.

Miss Eola Lemiene,

Your marks for your first-term, first-semester courses are as follows:

Ideograms: 4Mana Studies: 1Introduction to Monsters: 2Magical Dueling: 3

The breath released. Eola’s shoulders slumped, and she reached up to wipe a tear of relief. She’d passed most of her courses—and the one she hadn’t…well, that was Madam Reyanna’s, and she hadn’t even tried half of the test. Still, she’d thought that her effort on the second half might’ve mattered.

Varin’s evaluates its students on a five-point scale. Please be aware that, while these marks are not end-of-term scores, they should be treated as indicators of weaknesses and strengths. If you have scored a 1 in any subjects, please contact your professor for remedial coursework as soon as possible.

Eola’s eyes flicked to the portrait again. It looked like her mom was proud of her. She hoped so; after all, it wasn’t as if she hadn’t tried, and her scores were acceptable—especially for someone whose main goal wasn’t Mana Studies or, until recently, keeping up with Introduction to Monsters’ lessons.

All first-year students are expected to report to the Grand Lecture Hall one hour after noon to be selected for their apprenticeships. Selections usually take between three and five hours.

“How’d you do, Eola?” Colin called through the door.

“One minute.” Eola looked around hurriedly; she was still in the borrowed nightgown Patrice had gotten her into. She pulled it off, shivering, and quickly dressed in wool leggings, a thick cotton shirt, and her blue school robes. Then, without any more waiting, she pulled the door open.

Colin, of course, was still dressed in yesterday’s clothes. He smelled like stale wine, and Eola winced a little as his nose wrinkled. She probably did, too. “I failed Mana Studies. The others were passes. Magical Dueling was lower than I hoped,” she said.

“Oh,” Colin said. “I’m sorry. I failed Dueling, but got passing scores in the others.”

They stood there, Colin’s weasel draped around his neck and Atta staring down from the loft, for a moment. Then Colin broke the silence. “Should we ask Patrice how she did?”

“Absolutely not,” Eola said. “She’s asleep, and I don’t want to hear her gloating about her scores.”

“Gloat?”

Eola grinned. “She’s proud to be the worst mage at Varin’s. When her scores show it, we’ll never hear the end of it. Now, get cleaned up and change into something clean. You look like death, and you smell worse.”

“Rude, and right back at you,” Colin said, eyeing her up and down theatrically. He disappeared, heading for the washroom, and Eola closed her door behind him.

The Grand Lecture Hall sat empty most of the time. Eola had only been in the cavernous, echoing room twice.

The first had been on a brief tour of the school given by a shy second-year boy on her first day at Varin’s, and her second had been later that same day, when the headmaster had given the first-years the welcoming speech. Needless to say, she hadn’t paid much attention at the time.

It was a third of a circle of granite, tucked against the wall surrounding the town and academy. Tall, narrow windows let light pour across every row of desks, and a massive demonstration circle large enough to duel in filled most of the front of the arch-ceilinged auditorium. Banners in blue and silver hung from the ceiling overhead, waving slightly in the cold breeze that seeped in through the windows.

When Eola whispered something to Colin, there was no echo, and the sound seemed to die within feet.

A few of the other first-years shot Eola funny looks as she sat behind one of the curving oak desks near the back, shielding her eyes from the Bright Balls floating mid-air. Whatever she’d done in Varin’s Town last night, it had—apparently—gotten around. Her cheeks heated up, and she found a desk with two empty seats near the back.

The room was cold; a storm was blowing in, and the wind had sucked every bit of warmth out of it. She hunched over the desk and watched through slitted eyes as the seats slowly filled. All twenty-four chairs in the front, behind the lectern and demonstration circle, were empty. So was the lectern itself.

By the time Patrice showed up, there were only a handful of seats left; she sat on the hall’s far side, waved, and flashed a series of numbers on her fingers. “One, two, one, two,” Eola muttered. “Those are…pretty bad scores.”

Then Patrice shot a thumbs-up at Eola and promptly put her head on her arms.

“Is she…sleeping?” Colin asked.

“I think so.”

Before she could say more—like, for example, being impressed that Patrice really had performed so poorly—the professors filed in, followed by an ancient-looking man with a bent back and absolutely no hair on his head. Eola stared at the headmaster as he shuffled to the lectern and gripped it tightly.

The Great Mage Tomlish was long past his prime, but even so, he was still capable of Worldshaping magic. His soul was rumored to be one of the strongest in northern Alemia, and he was one of the most skilled glass mages who’d ever lived. He, rumor had it, had even invented a Third Order spell—an impossibility in Ordered Magic, and one he’d supposedly stumbled on by accident. That wasn’t confirmed or in the textbooks, but that fact didn’t stop the rumors.

But, unlike the other professors, Tomlish wouldn’t be taking apprentices today. He usually taught only one course—a third-year class for the most advanced students. Worldshapers. The rest of his time was spent running Varin’s Academy—or, more frequently, applying his magic in dungeons across Alemia. The illusionist’s skills were in high demand—glass attunements commonly lent themselves to illusions, and Tomlish was no exception.

He didn’t look like much of a fighter, though, and Eola was pretty sure the sword at his hip was more ceremonial than anything else.

“Welcome, first-years, to apprentice selection,” he said, age dripping from his every word. The room was pin-drop silent, and his voice carried all the way to the back. “Our school, and our very understanding of magic, is based on commonly replicable principles. Every practitioner of Ordered Magic, for instance, has an attunement. Every spell is cast the same way—with an ideogram that is filled with Mana from the caster’s soul. Every soul functions in a predictable manner for its size, the mage’s skill, and their attunement, and so does every mage’s magic.

“Over the last forty hours, the rest of the professors and I have analyzed your magical aptitude, taken guesses at your attunements, and in some cases, debated fiercely about your futures. They are now ready to begin selecting their apprentices for the next three years, based mostly on those predictable factors.”

He paused to let that statement linger. When he continued, his voice was much quieter, and Eola leaned forward in the too-bright room to hear better. Next to her, Colin did the same. “As you grow in skill and soul, your relationships with your new masters will likely change. You will become partners in your education, and they will facilitate your personal learning rather than instruct you on a specific path. That time will come soon. As first-year apprentices, however, your master’s role is that of teacher, not partner.”

Eola’s eyes flicked from Instructor Tarik to Maestro Yarrowbloom, then back. With only one hundred students, surely her marks in Ideograms or Magical Dueling would be enough to earn an apprenticeship with one of them. She leaned back in her chair and waited as the ancient Headmaster Tomlish spoke for another minute or two about the relationship between master and apprentice.

It was going to be fine. She’d worked hard in both courses.

She glanced over her shoulder as two students started whispering. To her surprise, they weren’t looking at Headmaster Tomlish, but at her. “What did I do last night, Colin?” she asked after a moment.

“After the third bottle, you—“

But before her friend could finish, the headmaster continued. “Each professor will select a single student in rotation, then will continue until each has four new apprentices. As the letter said, this process typically takes a few hours, so I hope you’re all ready to sit for a while.”

Patrice’s eyes met Eola’s, a mix of smugness and genuine worry in them. She’d been right; the math of twenty-four professors and one hundred students suggested that four wouldn’t get apprenticeships. Eola’s confidence wavered just a bit.

“Now, let us begin with Lieutenant-Librarian Fringe.” Headmaster Tomlish stepped away from the lectern, and an armored gnome Eola had never seen before took his place.

There were a lot of professors at Varin’s Academy that Eola had never met. Then again, the school taught a lot of different subjects. While Ideograms and Mana Studies were mandatory, and Intro to Monsters and Magical Dueling were almost as essential to anyone trying for the combat track, Eola expected that their next two years would be more customized to each student’s needs.

Still, when the fourth professor she’d never so much as seen took the podium and said Bannoque’s name after a moment, she couldn’t help but wonder just who these instructors were—and what subjects they taught. There was a round of applause as her first roommate took the stairs three at a time, bounding down to the lecture stage and taking a small sheet of parchment from a woman who looked like she was permanently sucking on a lemon. He disappeared down a side door a few seconds later, and the sour-looking instructor sat back down.

It took Madame Reyanna less than three seconds to take the podium, clear her throat, and say the name Eola had been dreading. “Lady Catrine of House Andrese.”

She’d given the blonde noble girl’s full title, too—making it abundantly clear that Catrine’s status mattered as much as her scores. Eola started to roll her eyes, but her tormentor’s gaze crossed hers, and the sheer…smugness…radiating off of Catrine was all but overwhelming. She mouthed something in Eola’s direction.

Eola mouthed something back: Latrine Catrine.

Her fingers sketched incomplete runes on her wrist as she stared. It didn’t matter if Catrine got picked before her. It really didn’t. But at the same time…it did matter. It mattered a lot. Eola knew she was behind the other students in Ordered Magic. Her Ideogram scores aside, the reality was that the professors had almost certainly discussed their students—and while Instructor Tarik had her in an independent study, that didn’t mean he thought she’d be a Great Mage or anything.

And speaking of Instructor Tarik…

The young-ish professor strode to the podium, cleared his throat, and stared in Eola’s direction. It was about to happen. She was about to get picked—and only two spots behind Catrine!

“Roth Gerr,” he said.

“Who’s Roth Gerr?” Colin asked.

“I beat him at dueling,” Eola said. Her mouth felt suddenly dry, and she closed her eyes as Instructor Tarik handed a piece of parchment to the angry-looking, dark-haired boy, who crumpled it in his grip after reading it and stormed to the door. It slammed shut, and the professor shrugged and took his seat with a bemused look on his face.

Colin wasn’t the only one asking about the angry-looking boy, either. A storm of whispers rippled around the room in an uncomfortable mirror of Catrine’s applause. None of them sounded polite, and a few were downright hostile. Eola thought she heard someone say ‘incompetent,’ but she couldn’t be sure. It wasn’t like she was paying attention, anyway.

She was more interested in Instructor Clearance’s selection, and in the ones after hers.

A few more names were called before Evelyn—Eola’s second roommate and Bannoque’s couch make-out partner—was selected by Maestro Yarrowbloom. That only raised Eola’s anxiety, especially as the first round of choosing ended and the gnome clomped his way back to the lectern to make his second choice.

If anything, the second round felt slower than the first as each professor shuffled their notes. Eola couldn’t help but notice that there was a lot of whispering among them, as well. It made sense; every professor had to start working with their second choices now, or adjusting their lists based on others’ selections. But that didn’t make Eola feel any better about it.

Madame Reyanna selected Garreth Skaget second. Eola was just as happy it wasn’t her. But the small, crimson professor’s eyes locked onto her in the moment before she sat down.

Eola had a horrifying thought: would a professor really choose a student they didn’t like just to torment them?

No. Madame Reyanna wouldn’t do that. She didn’t like Eola, and neither did Catrine. Whatever she was planning, the professor would almost certainly wait for Eola to go unselected, then for her to drop out at the end of the year when her attunement became common knowledge.

Somehow, that wasn’t as comforting as Eola had hoped it’d be.

Instructor Tarik strode back to the podium, carrying a single sheet of parchment. The rest of his copious notes were on his chair, and he didn’t look down at the one he held. Eola tensed. She had to be selected by him soon. She had to be. He was so interested in her breakthroughs on unattuned spell-chains, and he’d been her independent study instructor. There was no reason for him not to take her.

Eola’s stomach flipped uncomfortably. She closed her eyes and breathed, pushing on it with a few fingers to try to settle it.

Her eyes flew open as the professor said a name.
