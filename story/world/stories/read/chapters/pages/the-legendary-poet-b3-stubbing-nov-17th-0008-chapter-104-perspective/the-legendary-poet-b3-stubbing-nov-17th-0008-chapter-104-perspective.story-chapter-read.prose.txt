Kage lifted his hand and pointed through the forest of frames.

“That one.”

Every head turned toward the same canvas.

From three steps away it looked almost insultingly plain. A doorway painted from a dim interior, opening out toward a patch of green under warm light. No monsters. No runes blazing over the frame. No obvious distortion. Just a door and grass.

Jax frowned. “That?”

Finn leaned forward, shoulders tight. “The grass moved.”

“It did,” Lily said softly.

Zara narrowed her eyes at the painting, then at the ones around it. “I don’t see anything.”

“Look harder,” Kage said.

Vami had already moved up beside him. He studied the painting for less than a second.

Then his mouth curved.

“The shadow composition is wrong.”

Silence.

Zherdev blinked. “My king, you lost me at composition.”

Vami lifted a hand and pointed with two fingers, impatient already. “The painted sun sits low and right. The grass blades cast leftward and shallow. The threshold throws a shadow from somewhere above and behind the viewer. Two different light sources occupy one image.”

Julie smiled at the group. “Translation, that’s the real one.”

Vami clicked his tongue. “Yes.”

Finn squinted harder. “So the painting’s... leaking perspective?”

Zara’s expression shifted by one degree. For her, that was basically a standing ovation. “He’s right.”

Kage kept his eyes on the canvas.

The wrongness sat inside it like a splinter. Every other painting in the lane obeyed its own frame. Even the moving ones did it. Their motion belonged to the image. This one had a world behind it. The grass bent for a wind coming from somewhere outside the painting.

There you are.

Around them, the lane answered his discovery with immediate hostility.

The banquet canvas three easels down bulged outward. One of the empty chairs scraped forward across painted tile with a sound like wood dragged over stone. A servant’s hand pushed through the varnish up to the wrist, pale and flat as wet paper. The seascape opposite them heaved, and a sheet of cold saltwater slapped onto the mosaic path. The faceless knight lowered its scraped helm and planted one painted gauntlet over the frame.

Zherdev made a strangled noise. “BRO. BRO. The art is hatching.”

“Move,” Kage said.

Valdrias stepped forward at once, hauling up the new shield Zherdev had gifted him. “A SHIELD TO RULE ALL MANKIND.”

Julie momentarily froze. “What—what did you just say?”

“It helps with aggro,” Valdrias said with complete sincerity.

“It absolutely does NOT,” Zara snapped.

Vami reached the painting first. He pressed two fingers against the threshold. The surface rippled under his hand like varnish over deep water.

“Live portal,” he said. “Immediate transition.”

Ren had already drawn his blade. He shifted to the flank with that same precise, irritatingly perfect spacing he brought to everything. “Order?”

Kage answered without looking away from the moving lane.

“Valdrias first. Lily behind him. Finn. Zara. Zherdev in the middle. Julie, Jax. Ren. Vami. I’ll close.”

“On it,” Lily said.

Finn’s hawk launched from his shoulder with a nervous beat of wings and circled once above the lane. Then even the bird seemed to think better of the place and dipped after Finn as he ran.

The servant’s hand tore free from the banquet painting up to the elbow.

The faceless knight forced half its torso through the frame.

A curl of smoke spilled out from the abstract red-and-black canvas, carrying the smell of singed cloth and lamp soot.

Zherdev shuffled toward the portal with portrayed dignity. His cape snagged on an easel. Jax swore, yanked it free, and shoved him toward the painting.

“Inside, moneybags.”

“Jax, my brutal champion, I always believed in you.”

“You’ve known me thirty minutes.”

Vami grabbed the back of Zherdev’s armor and simply threw him the last step.

Zherdev vanished through the painted doorway with a yelp.

One by one the party followed. Valdrias hit the threshold shield-first and disappeared. Lily took one breath and stepped through. Finn hesitated only long enough to catch his hawk on his shoulder again. Zara went with obvious reluctance and obvious speed. Julie moved cleanly. Jax rolled his shoulders and went. Ren backed through with his eyes on the lane.

Vami stayed a heartbeat longer, watching the paintings push outward.

The seascape wave spilled over its frame. The faceless knight planted one foot on the cobbles.

Vami flashed a sharp grin. “Now it looks like a raid.”

He stepped through.

Kage held the rear.

The banquet servant clawed its way free to the waist. The abstract battlefield canvas split down the middle, and the red inside it moved like a wound opening. The knight came fully out with a shriek of stressed wood.

Kage drew Mumyo.

He cut the servant’s wrist on the way past. Painted flesh separated under the blade and burst into a spray of pigment that smelled like vinegar and old flowers. The hand slapped onto the stones. The knight lunged.

Kage stepped into the doorway.

Cool varnish dragged over his coat.

The lane vanished.

Grass brushed his boots.

The transition hit so cleanly his body almost rejected it. One step on Korros mosaic. One step on springy earth.

He came out under a broad sky washed in late gold.

The whole party stood scattered in a field of waist-high grass and white seedheads. Wind moved through it in long, smooth passes. The air smelled of crushed clover, warm stems, and a faint sweetness like fresh paper left in the sun. Behind them stood the same doorway frame they had crossed, freestanding in the field with no wall around it, a rectangle of painted dimness hanging upright above the grass.

Then the surface inside the frame clouded over and turned blank.

Jax looked around, sword half raised. “That’s it?”

Finn’s first inhale came deep enough to lift his shoulders. For a second the tension drained out of him completely. “Wow.”

His voice softened. “This place smells... good.”

Julie turned a slow circle, hands loose at her sides. “Pretty.”

“Suspiciously pretty,” Zara said.

Kage scanned the field.

Rolling green. A low rise to the east. A cluster of flowers the color of cream near a shallow dip in the land. Sky overhead in vast blue layers that looked natural until you stared too long and caught the faint sweep of brush texture buried in the light. No ruin. No gate. No enemies. No obvious path.

No system banner.

Lily looked up too, waiting for the familiar flash of text.

Nothing came.

"Um," Finn said slowly, lowering his hand from the hawk on his shoulder. "Shouldn't there be, like, a big dramatic message? 'You have entered THE SHIFTING GALLERIES, blah blah, good luck, don't die'?"

“Usually,” Lily said.

Jax frowned at the horizon. “Yeah, this doesn't look like a gallery.”

Ren swept the field with a duelist’s calm. “This terrain offers no cover, no approach constraints, no evident encounter markers.”

Zara opened her map and stared at it for half a second before lowering her hand. “Map’s blank.”

Valdrias planted his shield in the grass and looked deeply offended on behalf of all normal architecture. “Nothing's shifting there.”

Zherdev, who had landed on both knees in the grass and was still recovering his soul, pushed himself upright. “Guys.”

Nobody looked at him.

“Guys.”

Julie glanced over. “You okay?”

“Guys.”

This time everyone looked.

Zherdev held up the Curator’s Invitation.

It was changing in his hand.

The brass key had already lost its shape. The old teeth softened and drew inward. The ornate head flattened. Gold filigree spread through the material like veins in cooling sugar, dividing it into irregular translucent sections. Within a few breaths the whole thing had become a palm-sized pane of thick pressed crystal, its surface cloudy at first, then clearing from the center outward.

Jagged runes lit up inside it. Not on the surface. Inside.

They burned in layered lines of pale blue and amber, like someone had trapped stained glass windows inside ice.

The pulse that had led them through Korros was gone. In its place Kage felt a steady frequency, a clean high note pressing against the back of his teeth.

Great. The key turned into a very expensive window fragment.

Zherdev held it with both hands now, afraid to squeeze too hard. “I do not want to alarm the squad, but my key has become church.”

Vami stepped closer. “Give it here.”

Zherdev recoiled. “Counterpoint, absolutely not, luminous psycho. I paid for this.”

Julie sighed. “He has a point.”

Kage studied the crystal. The runes inside it looked wrong in the same way the painting had looked wrong. Almost readable. Almost aligned. He felt his own interface filigree stir at the edges of his sight, silver roots crawling around the item outline that refused to fully resolve.

No system identification popped.

That told him enough on its own.

“We’re not in the raid yet,” he said.

Jax turned to him. “Then where are we?”

“Lobby,” Kage said.

“Vestibule,” Vami corrected.

“Front step,” Kage said.

Zara exhaled through her nose. “Meaning we still need to unlock the actual entrance.”

Valdrias looked around at the field again. “Where is the door?”

“That,” Jax said, “is a fantastic question.”

They spread out just enough to search while keeping one another in sight. Kage kept them tight with a raised hand.

“Sightline only,” he said. “Nobody wanders.”

Nobody argued.

The field encouraged wandering. That made it worse.

Finn sent his hawk up again. The bird climbed fast, circled once, twice, then let out a sharp cry that sounded smaller than it should have in all that open air.

“Anything?” Lily asked.

Finn squinted upward, one hand shading his eyes. “It keeps banking toward the same point, but there’s nothing there.”

Zara crouched and touched the grass. “No seams. No hidden hatch. No collision edge.”

Ren took three measured steps, then stopped to examine his own shadow on the ground. His gaze sharpened.

“Kage.”

Kage was already looking.

His shadow stretched away through the grass at an angle the visible sun did not support.

He shifted one step to the right. The shadow obeyed the same false geometry. Hard edge. Clean fall. Wrong direction.

So did Valdrias’s. So did Lily’s. So did the freestanding frame behind them. The sun hanging in the painted sky sat low and warm over the western field. Their shadows all pointed as if the light stood somewhere else entirely.

Zara followed his line of sight and swore under her breath. “There it is.”

Finn looked from the sun to the shadow and then back. “Oh. That’s bad.”

“Why is that bad?” Zherdev asked.

“Because,” Zara said, “the world is lying.”

Vami folded his arms. “The visible sun is part of the composition. The real light source sits off-frame.”

Valdrias looked up into the empty air. “So, there are two suns.”

“There is one painted sun,” Ren said. “One operative light source.”

Jax glanced between them. “You guys say weird things when art gets involved.”

Julie tapped her own shadow with the toe of her boot. “So the lock uses light. In some way.”

Kage extended Mumyo and used the tip to draw a line in the grass along the edge of his shadow. Ren caught on instantly and did the same from another position. Zara stepped left, crouched, and marked hers.

The three lines converged.

In the air.

Somewhere above the eastern rise where nothing visible existed.

Finn stared at the empty patch of sky. “There.”

His hawk banked again and passed under the invisible point. Its shadow sharpened so hard for a split second that the grass beneath it looked inked.

Kage turned back to Zherdev’s transformed key. The crystal hummed harder when he angled it toward the hidden source.

There. That was the click.

The runes inside the pane were backward. Reversed.

A lens.

Of course it was a lens.

Regular dungeons used locks. Korros gave him a practical exam in composition.

Kage held out his hand. “Zherdev.”

Zherdev hugged the crystal to his chest. “I respect your leadership deeply. I am also holding the only proof that my purchase wasn’t a scam.”

“You are still holding it,” Kage said. “Lift it toward that point.”

Zherdev looked at the empty patch of sky, then back at the crystal, then at Kage. “Toward what point? Brother, you are asking me to present glass to the concept of lighting.”

Vami pinched the bridge of his nose. “He means the actual light source. The one your eyes failed at.”

Julie patted Zherdev’s shoulder. “You can do it.”

“I hate team-building,” Zherdev muttered.

He raised the crystal with both hands toward the empty air above the rise.

Nothing happened.

Then a faint projection spilled across the grass. Thin amber lines, broken and stretched, scattered over the field in a crooked smear.

Finn jumped. “Something’s there.”

Zara leaned in. “Too low.”

“Rotate clockwise,” Vami said immediately.

“Use words for non-deranged people,” Jax said.

“Turn it right.”

Zherdev turned it right.

The runes on the grass crawled sideways and lengthened, still warped, still unreadable.

Kage stepped closer and watched the projection. The lines moved like calligraphy trying to remember its own shape. The constant note inside the crystal rose in pitch each time Zherdev found a better angle.

“Higher,” Ren said.

Zherdev lifted his arms until his giant shoulder plates creaked.

Julie moved behind him and caught his elbow before he wobbled. “Hold steady.”

The projection sharpened for half a heartbeat, then blurred again.

Kage looked from the ground to the false sun in the sky, to the shadows, to the invisible point above the rise.

The field itself was part of the painting. The light source sat outside it.

Perspective. Viewer position.

The key did not only need the light. It needed the right audience angle.

“Stop,” Kage said.

Everyone froze.

He walked ten steps left, then three forward, and looked back through Zherdev, the crystal, and the empty patch of sky.

The projection shifted immediately, even though Zherdev had barely moved.

There it is. The beam wants a line.

He planted Mumyo into the grass at his feet as a marker.

“Zherdev, face me.”

Zherdev slowly pivoted, holding the crystal up with grave concentration, treating the stained glass as if it were a bomb he was about to diffuse.

“Lift it a little more,” Kage said.

Zherdev obeyed.

The runes on the ground snapped into tighter focus. Reversed still. Nearly legible.

“Half step right.”

He shuffled.

“Too far. Back.”

He shuffled back.

“Hold.”

The note in Kage’s teeth turned pure and bright. The grass at the edge of the projection stood straighter, each blade lit from within.

Vami’s eyes sharpened. He saw it now, the final adjustment. “Tilt the top edge toward the source. Three degrees.”

Zherdev stared at him. Julie physically pushed the crystal a fraction.

The projected runes flipped, line by line.

Amber and blue strokes rolled across the grass, inverted calligraphy righting itself under invisible light. They spelled a word Kage's Synesthesia tasted as parchment and old promises. The jagged symbols elongated, connected, and began to arrange into a shape too large to read from any single glance.

A frame. No. A doorway. Massive.

Its outline spread across the field in front of them, taller than a city gate if it had stood upright, its edges burning with clean geometric fire. The grass inside the lines flattened. The earth beneath it took on the sheen of polished stone. More runes flooded in to fill the threshold, locking together in panes like a cathedral window laid flat over the ground.

Nobody spoke.

Even Zherdev shut up.

The constant note inside the crystal swelled until the whole field seemed to hum with it. The painted sky deepened. Light gathered along the door’s outline in hard luminous bands.

In the middle of the empty field, light drew a door where the earth had been empty a heartbeat before.

Then the system caught up.
