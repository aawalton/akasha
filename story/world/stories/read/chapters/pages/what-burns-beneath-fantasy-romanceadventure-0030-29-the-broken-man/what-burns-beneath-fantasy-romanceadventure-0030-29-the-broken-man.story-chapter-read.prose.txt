My knees hit the floorboards, both hands grasping his shoulders. The cloth beneath my fingers was cold, sweat-soaked, and moved with the rhythm of heavy breaths. “He’s alive!”

Gloved hands appeared beside mine, turning the man onto his side. Renner inspected Gil, nose wrinkling, then straightened. “Looks like he drank himself unconscious.”

As if to confirm, the stablemaster stirred. Cracked lips parted, revealing yellowed teeth, and he peered up with red-rimmed eyes. They were dull and bloodshot, with obvious tear-tracks smeared through swathes of dirt beneath them. His beard was crusted with half-frozen drool.

“Wha… who’s here?”

I looked at my companion, unsure of how to proceed.

I’d thought we’d find him… I don’t know. I don’t know what I expected, but it certainly wasn’t this.

“Rough night?” Renner paced through the small kitchen. He seemed tense, coiled; as though every muscle was ready to spring into action. His eyes flicked all around, taking in every fragment of the man’s home.

Which was… dismal. Every kitchen surface was long-browned with use and propped up by molded, damp wood. Sparse, wrinkled fruit sat in a woven basket near a dark washbasin piled high with smeared pewter plates and bowls.

And there were bottles. Everywhere. Some half-full, some fully drained. Some tipped over, some neatly stacked, some lying shattered in heaps against the bases of molding walls. As if they’d been thrown.

The smell of it all made my eyes water. Sour, dank air stung my nostrils. I covered my mouth and stood.

The disheveled man squinted up at me. “You… I saw you. Saw you. Left you.”

A shiver ran down my spine. “Left me? What do you mean, you left me?”

He coughed, sending a spray of yellow spittle onto my trousers, then grunted and turned away. “Don’t mean nothing. Get, hey, get away from all that.”

Renner was prodding at bottles and cupboards. He didn’t respond.

The stablemaster heaved himself upright, face turning red. “Why’re you in my home? Didn’t ask you here.”

“You were pretty worked up about something last night,” Renner flashed a chilling half-smile, nodding towards a pile of half-frozen glass and sour-smelling liquor near my boots. “Want to talk about it?”

Gil lifted both hands, rubbing at the wash of dirt and spittle smeared across his face. The twisted stumps of his two fingers were bright red from cold. “No.” He kicked at the glass. “Got nothin’ to say to… to… what, robbers? Thieves?” He barked a harsh laugh and stumbled forwards, collapsing into the room’s sole, warped-wood chair. “As if I got anything to take!”

I bit my lower lip. “Sir, please, we’re not here to take anything. Our friend is missing.”

He went very still.

Renner, watching with the attentiveness of a hawk, followed suit.

Bloodshot eyes met mine. Wide. Alarmed.

That’s exactly how he looked at me last night. It’s like he’s scared of me.

He looked quickly away. “Didn’t do nothin’ to your friend.”

Renner snorted. “Now, why don’t I believe you?”

I stepped forwards. “Please, just-”

Gil lunged upright, knocking the chair backwards with a clatter.

I jumped backwards with a cry as Renner stepped between us. Metal hissed, and a wicked-looking dagger gleamed in one gloved hand.

Gil, wild-eyed, seemed to not even notice the weapon. He roared, sending spittle flying and slapping his good hand against the stained countertop. “Damn it all, I didn’t do a thing! Not a blasted thing!”

Renner’s voice dropped to a dangerous pitch, “Maybe not, but you sure as salt thought about doing something.”

Gil winced. Cut his eyes back towards me.

Triumph flashed across my companion’s face in the form of a vicious leer. “Yeah, that’s what I thought. Tell us about your night.”

“My night? I drank.”

I wrung my cloak. “And… and what did you mean, you ‘left me?’”

He flinched again. “Didn’t mean salt.”

“Liar.” Wicked steel prodded forwards, tapping at Gil’s soiled tunic.

I took a step forward, heart hammering as the stench of sweat and sour ale filled my mouth. “Sir, please, we’re just trying to find Teela. It… it seemed like you were watching us last night.”

The stablemaster looked me up and down, rubbing the back of his neck. “So? New faces in town, I’m not allowed to be interested? Doesn’t mean anything.” He cut his gaze back towards Renner and, grimacing, shuffled backwards to lean against a moldering countertop.

And with the movement… there was very little light in Gil’s home; just what pale gold and pink crept in through dingy windows. But it was just enough to make out the warped shape behind him.

It was twisted. Arms wrapped around itself, legs thin and bent at an odd angle. A black somehow too black, swallowing up all nearby glimmers of dawn.

I cried out, in equal parts vindication and fear, whirling towards Renner.

“Look! Look, do you see it?!”

“See what?”

“On the wall- his shadow! Look!”

Gil turned, too, shoulders hunched and hands out as though to ward away danger. Both men spent a moment observing the wall.

“It’s a shadow, ladyship.”

“It’s all twisted and… can’t you… don’t you see it?” My triumph died alongside my voice.

He doesn’t see anything. So it’s me, then. There’s something wrong with me.

The stablemaster curled his lip. “Ain’t nothin’ wrong with my… get out of my home, both of you, or I’ll call the Watch! Now!”

Renner sidestepped, putting himself between Gil and the front door. “We’re not going anywhere. Why were you watching her?”

I couldn’t tear my eyes from the horrible black shape. It shivered. Spindly fingers curled, the drooping head swayed, and deep inside of me something snapped.

“Vir!” Warm light blossomed from my palm.

Renner bit out an oath and lunged for the back door, slamming it shut. “Are you insane?!”

I approached the wall, hand outstretched. Wordlessly daring the awful shape to respond, to react, perhaps even to retaliate against my holy light.

And in the blink of an eye, it was just a normal shadow.

“It’s gone.” I let out a breath. My brows knotted together, teeth clenching as I tried to puzzle through what it could all mean.

Gil, hollow eyes lit with gold, sank to his knees. He stared at my hand in awe.

Awe. Not fear. He knows it’s divine magic. The thought came like a bolt of inspiration. I bit my lip and stepped forwards, holding the light out. “Please, sir, I’m asking you, as a runekeeper, as someone blessed by the gods; please, anything you can tell us…”

Gil’s lips moved soundlessly, heading shaking back and forth. Abruptly he cried out, gaze locking onto mine, eyes bright with moisture. “Leave, my lady!” The words came out raw and hoarse. He doubled over, hands clutching his throat.

I sank to my knees. My own voice quavered. “Please, sir. H-her name is Teela, and she’s very kind and she has a mom who loves her and…” I dared to reach out, laying a hand on top of Gil’s mangled one.

He flinched, as though I’d burned him.

I pressed on. “Please, whatever’s happened, whatever you know… I won’t tell anyone. I won’t say a word, just please tell us where she is.”

Narrow, shaking shoulders hunched. He moaned, a low, anguished sound, and breathed, “Gone.”

My nostrils stung with the smell of sweat, soured ale, and something vaguely rotten as I leaned in. “What did you say?”

“F-forgive me, stars above, I didn’t… I never wanted to…” Tears and snot slid into the dark, fetid curls of his beard. “Lady, forgive me.”

“Where is she?” Renner snarled from behind me.

Gil clutched my cloak with one grimy hand. “W-we were… supp…” his mouth twisted into an awful shape and he cringed, as though he’d been struck. “I didn’t. I only drank and… and… I left. I swear on my life, I left and I came here and I didn’t… t-take…” The sentence ended abruptly as he choked.

“But you know what happened?” I struggled to find the right words. “What… took her?”

Bloodshot eyes held mine. Fearful. Shamed. He opened his mouth and doubled over, gasping for air.

I looked desperately at my companion. “What’s wrong with him?”

“He can’t tell us.” Renner crouched beside me, lips pressed into a bloodless line. His eyes simmered with loathing, and the glinting blade was bare in one hand. “It won’t let him.”

I wrapped my hand around Gil’s shaking knuckles. “It? The Fae, or…” I swallowed and, dreading the answer, whispered, “Or, sir… your… pact?”

He flinched. Heaved a sob. And then his face lifted to mine.

And for just a moment, the world slipped away. Melted like shadows before a rising sun.

He looked like Durst.

It wasn’t just the black hair, the weathered skin, or the curling beard. It was the look in his eyes. One I’d glimpsed a thousand times.

Haunted. Shadowed by grief. As if a great, heavy stone was weighing him down.

And for a moment I was twelve years old, sitting in a tiny kitchen with my arms wrapped around my knees. Staring into a face that was haggard and weary and broken… but still looked at me like I was something precious.

Like, somehow, I made the world just a little bit brighter.

Beside me, Renner tensed.

That subtle movement brought me hurtling back into reality. Kneeling in a different kitchen with a different man. One who was very likely inches from death.

“Don’t answer,” I pleaded, fresh tears blurring my vision. I held my breath, praying that Gil would listen. Too frightened to look at the man beside me.

Surely Renner won’t… he doesn’t know for sure. He only suspects. Saints, it’s obvious he’s involved somehow, but without proof he can’t… surely he won’t…

And surely he wouldn’t do anything before we figured out where Teela was. That meant I had time to alert the authorities, or find the right words to dissuade Renner from doing anything… ‘ugly.’

“Don’t answer.” The light dimmed in my hand.

“No, do.” Renner’s voice held a frosty calm. He leaned forwards. “Answer.”

“No, don’t… Renner, stop! Just… just think for a moment, would you?” I stood, rounding on him. Wildly, I wondered if perhaps the menacing behavior was just an act; a play at intimidation, an attempt to frighten Gil into telling us where Teela was.

But the knife was bare. Ready. And… I wasn’t sure it was possible to fake the naked hostility on his face.

“Lady… could you… will you…?” Gil lifted his mangled, shaking hand.

My breath hitched. “I… I’m sorry, sir.” Vaguely, some part of my mind screamed that I should be on the floor shouting until my throat was ragged, demanding answers and raging at him. Not apologizing.

I’d wanted to shout at Durst, too.

“My magic isn’t… I can’t heal you. I’m sorry.”

A little spark in his eyes faded to black.

“Tell us about your deal,” Renner demanded, attention never leaving the man’s face.

Gil’s chest swelled with a steadying breath, gaze fixed on mine as I shook my head earnestly. “No.”

One gloved hand snapped out, grabbed Gil’s matted tangle of black hair, and with a bone-rattling crack Renner slammed his head into the counter. Then, as calmly as if nothing had happened, “Tell us about your deal.”

My jaw dropped in horror. Shaking hands grabbed one firm shoulder, trying in vain to haul him back. “Get away from him!”

“Get… get…” Gil’s words slurred as one hand pushed out, slow and dazed. “Out of my...”

“No. Tell us about your deal.”

“Renner, please, just give him a chance to… look, sir, you said… you told us to leave. Just a moment ago, when you realized what I am. Why?” My mind raced. I had a guess as to the answer, but I wanted to hear it for myself.

“Not… safe.”

“You didn’t think to warn us of that yesterday?” I tried not to sound bitter. Fought back the sudden, wild urge to laugh.

The stablemaster staggered to his feet, eyes glazed.

Renner rose, too.

Gil shuddered away. “I can’t…” he choked again, hollow cheeks turning mottled red.

I glanced around, growling in frustration. “You… okay, okay, you can’t say; could you write things down?”

“Write?” He winced.

“Okay, no writing. Could you perhaps… if I ask a yes or no question, could you nod your head?” I tried, and failed, to keep my voice from turning shrill with desperation.

He hesitated, dark brows drawing together.

It seems like he wants to help us. If there’s something actually, physically stopping him, is there any way around it? I wish I had some clue about how all this worked.

Somewhere in the back of my mind, the thought that I could perhaps find out- quite easily, as easily as simply asking- rang like a bell. I shivered at the idea.

He probably wouldn’t tell me. Or he’d lie. And… and even if he didn’t, it doesn’t matter right now.

“Will you try? Please?”

Silence snaked in between the three of us. I waited, counting my heartbeats. Inhaling the stench of soured beer and stale sweat.

Finally, tentatively, the stablemaster nodded his head.

My heart swelled at the small victory. I turned towards Renner, hoping this would be enough for him to ease back… and my little spark of joy died.

He still wasn’t looking at me, he just had his attention fixed on Gil. Waiting.

I swallowed. “Okay. Okay, that’s helpful. Was it… did someone take her?”

A pause, then another nod. His skin was turning a pallid hue. Tiny, shining beads of sweat dotted his forehead.

“A person?”

He met my eyes, all weary grief and despair, and shook his head.

“A Fae took her?” I whispered, wringing at my cloak.

“She’s gone.” Once again, the words sounded as though they were tearing their way out of his throat. “Gone. Leave. Please.”

I pressed on, fighting back the icy crawl of dread. “A Fae… A Fae took her. A Fae took Teela. Do you know why?”

He hesitated. Then, with a slowness that ached, raised his mangled hand.

Renner swore, jerking backwards.

I shook my head, unable to look away from the twisted red fingers. And I was suddenly quite sure that, whatever had happened to Gil’s hand, it had not been an accident. The fingers hadn’t been cut cleanly off; one was a bit longer than the other, and the ends of each were scored by small dents and divots. Not as though they’d been cut, or even torn; as though they’d been bitten…

Vomit bubbled up my throat. I stumbled away, fighting for breath. Saints, the air was so thick and sour and everything in this awful place was rancid, I couldn’t breathe-!

I was out the back door before I even realized it. My forehead pressed against cold wood and my breath came in choppy gasps. I seemed to be on my knees, a world of yellowing grass and gray stones spinning around me. I sucked in frosty air, fighting to at least stay upright.

You’ve got the trembles.

My fingers scraped into the earth. I could almost hear her saying the words; a declaration made with such matter-of-fact kindness, as though my numbing panic and inability to even stand up right now was just… alright. Like it didn’t make me any less.

She’d stayed. Even after I’d nearly gotten all of us killed, even after I told her that Fae might be chasing me. She hadn’t left. Salt, I didn’t even think she’d considered it!

Frosty wind bit at my cheeks. My chest swelled as I clawed for clarity within the stark cold.

There’s time. There’s still time. There has to be. The monster took her; it didn’t just devour her right there in the room. And it didn’t do anything beneath the window; we would have seen it. It took her somewhere else, so that means she might still be alright.

I climbed to my feet, cursing myself for leaving Gil alone with Renner. But the house behind me remained silent, almost deathly so, as I lurched back inside.

Nothing had changed. Gil was slumped against a moldering counter, head buried in his hands. Renner stood barely a step away, glowering at the older man.

I marched forwards. “Do you know where it took her?” Before he could say anything I growled, nails digging into my palms. “And don’t… don’t just say she’s gone! She’s not! It’s only been a few hours- maybe not even that long.”

Something flickered across Renner’s face. He looked down, giving his head a little shake.

Gil seemed unable to meet my eyes. “There’s no saving your friend. I’m sorry. You don’t… truly, you can’t understand how much I-”

“Your apology’s as worthless as you are,” Renner snapped. “Do you know where it took her, or not?”

The stablemaster expression twisted into a one of pain and fear. Sweat trickled down his hollow cheeks. Then, with a voice as quiet and cold as a grave, he whispered, “No.”
