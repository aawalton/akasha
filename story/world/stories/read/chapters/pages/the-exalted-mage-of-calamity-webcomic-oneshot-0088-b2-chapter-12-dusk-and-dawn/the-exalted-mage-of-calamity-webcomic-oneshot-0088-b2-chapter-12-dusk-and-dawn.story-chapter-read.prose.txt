Where is the demon right now?

[It appears the demon is further below, near the base of the sanctuary. I believe it is inspecting the guardian and attempting to absorb its power.]

Absorbing it?

Veronica moved between the unconscious bodies, checking each of their pulses. Their expressions were tense and uneven. Presumably, they were all trapped within their own nightmares.

[This demon appears to possess an ability related to siphoning energy. It has been draining ambient mana from the atmosphere, along with excess mana and life force from your bodies. I have temporarily taken control of your mana core to maintain a barrier around everyone and reduce the rate of absorption.]

Veronica narrowed her eyes.

That explains why the ambient mana is gone… and why it isn’t recovering. It’s all being stolen. Is it just keeping us alive so it can keep feeding on our life force?

[That is the most probable explanation.]

Veronica’s gaze swept over the others.

Is there any way you can help me wake everyone else up like you did for me?

[If you connect your mana core to theirs and establish a link, I can temporarily connect your consciousness to theirs. You would then need to wake them from within the nightmare while I maintain the connection. This would, however, leave your body unprotected in a state of pseudo-unconsciousness.]

Her eyes shifted toward Gwen. How long will the process take?

[That will depend on the complexity of the nightmare and the strength of their willpower.]

Veronica hummed softly. Then… let’s wake Gwen first. If she wakes up and uses her dispel magic on us, we can skip the others entirely.

Kneeling beside her, Veronica placed a hand on Gwen’s forehead. Alright, go ahead and do your thing.

[That will not be necessary.]

“Huh? What do you mean not neces—”

Gwen suddenly jolted upright with a loud gasp. She clutched at her chest, breathing heavily just like Veronica had moments earlier. Her eyes darted around wildly through the black mist.

“Wha-what happened?” she asked between breaths.

Veronica stared at her, dumbfounded. “How did you wake up so quickly?”

Gwen blinked several times, still trying to gather herself. “Huh? O-oh. Uh… I cast Lesser Cleanse on myself beforehand, like you told me to,” she replied. “I also managed to cast it on you and Nasha, but I didn’t have enough time to fully cast it on Dusk.”

So her magic helped me wake up, too?

[That is correct. Gwen’s magic was slowly weakening the curse that was plaguing your mind. With it, I was able to find a gap and retrieve your consciousness.]

“That’s good, then.” Veronica glanced toward Nasha, who still hadn’t moved. “If that’s the case, she should wake up soon, since you did.” Her gaze shifted toward Dusk. “The problem is him.”

“Do you want me to cast cleanse on him, then?” Gwen asked, looking Dusk over.

Veronica nodded.

Gwen immediately knelt beside Dusk and began channeling mana through her staff. Luckily, the demon hadn’t bothered disarming them. It had simply trapped them in nightmares and left their bodies where they fell.

“What happened to the demon?” Gwen asked while casting. “I’m assuming it’s still alive if all this mist is still here?”

“It’s beneath the sanctuary,” Veronica answered. “Near the guardian’s core after it fell. Sage thinks it’s absorbing its energy. Apparently, it’s been doing the same thing to the ambient mana in the valley.”

Her eyes darkened slightly. “And to us. It’s slowly absorbing our vitality.”

Gwen’s expression tightened. “Wait… so it’s slowly killing us?”

Veronica nodded.

Gwen looked down at herself, inspecting her body while channeling her spell. She couldn’t find anything wrong with her.

“Life force is a tricky thing. Having it absorb life force doesn’t mean its stealing years off our lifespan. First, it has to drain all of our stamina and energy. As long as we get out of this situation quickly enough, we’ll probably be fine with a few days’ rest.”

“You think you can defeat it?” Gwen asked, looking slightly less worried. “That demon overwhelmed all of us almost instantly.”

Veronica’s lips pressed flat. “I’m not sure. I might be able to create a barrier strong enough to block its demonic energy while I fight it.” Her gaze shifted toward the others. “The problem is you guys. I won’t be able to extend the barrier far enough to protect everyone at the same time.”

Several minutes passed as Gwen continuously applied her magic to Dusk. Veronica remained beside Nasha, holding her hand. It hurt seeing Nasha’s face twist with sadness and pain even while unconscious.

Veronica could roughly guess what nightmare Gwen had experienced. Most likely her captivity under Lorence.

But Nasha? Veronica had no idea.

It didn’t take long before Nasha suddenly jolted upright, breathing hard. Veronica had immediately moved to calm her down, only for Nasha to suddenly pull her into a tight hug.

Veronica hugged her back, still unsure what exactly Nasha had seen within her nightmare. But perhaps it wasn’t wise to ask.

After all, Veronica herself had no desire to explain her own nightmare either. It was enough to know the world ended and everyone died.

Slowly, Veronica caught Nasha up on the situation. Meanwhile, Gwen continued trying to dispel the nightmare affecting Dusk. However, before long, she ran into a problem.

“Hey… I don’t think it’s working on him,” Gwen said.

Veronica turned toward her. “What do you mean?”

“I… don’t know,” Gwen admitted. “My magic just isn’t working. It’s hovering above him, but it won’t actually touch him. I thought maybe I just needed to push more mana into it, but…”

Veronica and Nasha both came over and knelt beside Dusk.

Nasha pressed two fingers against his wrist, then moved her hand toward his chest.

“I don’t… feel a heartbeat,” she said slowly. “But he’s obviously breathing.”

That explained why Veronica hadn’t been able to hear his heartbeat back inside the wagon.

He never had one to begin with.

[Veronica. Make physical contact with Dusk]

What?

[I would like to confirm something.]

Her brow raised slightly, but she didn’t question Sage further.

Veronica reached down and grabbed Dusk’s wrist.

It was cold.

So? Find anything?

[The strange energy Dusk was emitting has weakened considerably. Based on my observations, the demon appears to be absorbing it, even from here.]

Veronica stared more closely at Dusk. So it’s feeding on him too? Is he in danger?

[I am unable to confirm. However, the energy surrounding Dusk is particularly potent. It is not inferior to mana in quality. It is currently decreasing at a rapid rate. The mist around you is feeding on it.]

[It is also this energy preventing the mist from approaching further. His biological functions appear directly tied to it. I hypothesize that Dusk may die if all of this energy is depleted.]

“Sage says the demon is absorbing some kind of energy from Dusk through the mist,” Veronica explained aloud. “It’s getting stronger the longer he stays unconscious. At this rate, it might kill him.” Her eyes narrowed. “And apparently, the only reason the mist hasn’t fully consumed us yet is because of him.”

“What?” Gwen asked, turning toward her in alarm. “Is there any way to stop it? My magic isn’t working on him.”

Veronica fell silent for a few moments.

Normally, curses and mental afflictions were the domain of Blooming mages. They specialized in cleansing, healing, and restoring the body and mind.

But Revelation mages could also interfere with them in a different way. As long as it affected and clouded the mind.

Sage. Earlier, you mentioned you could link my consciousness with someone else’s, right? Could you do that with Dusk? If I can go into the nightmare and try to wake him up myself?

[It is possible. However, if his consciousness fully collapses while you are connected to him, you may suffer significant psychological damage upon returning.]

How long does he have?

[Approximately one to two hours. The energy fluctuation is unstable.]

“So?” Nasha asked. “What’s the plan now?”

Veronica pursed her lips. “I can either forcibly enter his consciousness with Sage’s help and try to save him before he possibly dies within the next hour or two…”

“What? He’s going to die that quickly?” Gwen asked, looking between Veronica and Dusk.

Veronica nodded. “Either I do that, or I try to fight and defeat the demon within that same amount of time. It’s not guaranteed, but it might dispel the curse.”

Nasha stared at her. “You’re not willing to take that risk, are you?”

Veronica stared back at her. “…No. I’m not. There’s nothing saying the curse will be dispelled. Demonic magic is completely different from spells. Demonic energy can still linger even after a demon has left. Chances are, he’ll die during the fight or after it.”

Even though Dusk was mysterious at best, and suspicious at worst, he hadn’t done anything wrong. He was under attack by the demon just as much as any of them were.

He wasn’t on its side.

And after that nightmare…

Veronica clenched her fingers.

She couldn’t just let another child die in front of her.

Not again.

“I’ll dive into his consciousness.”

Both Nasha and Gwen looked at her.

“But while I’m doing that, I’ll probably be defenseless. I won’t be able to protect you if the demon comes back. The most I can do is have Sage use my mana to form a barrier around all of us in case it tries to attack.”

“It’s trying to absorb our life force, right?” Nasha asked. “Then it probably won’t try to kill us outright. Those settlers are still alive, after all.”

“Yeah,” Gwen said. “If that’s the case, we’ll be fine. We can just play dead again, like we’re still under its curse. I don’t want to see Dusk die either.”

“Then,” Veronica said, turning toward Dusk, “I’ll see if I can wake him up in time. And maybe I’ll find a clue about whatever energy is keeping the demon at bay.”

Nasha took Veronica’s hand and gave her a light kiss on the cheek. “Stay safe. And be careful in there. These nightmares are no joke.”

A faint smile touched Veronica’s face. “Thank you.”

Then she placed her hand over Dusk’s head. “Alright, Sage. Do it.”

[Initiating consciousness injection. Following the path of mana…]

Dusk was still a child. There couldn’t have been that much he had gone through already, right? Nothing worse than seeing the entire world die and burn before her.

Veronica closed her eyes and slowly lay down, keeping her palm against Dusk’s head.

[Consciousness successfully linked.]

Veronica opened her eyes.

A space of white. A space of blue. A space of… rainbow.

She looked down at herself. Her body was completely ethereal. Her hand was translucent, and she felt no weight behind her movements. She was even capable of flying, evident by the way her feet barely touched the ground.

“Where am I?” she asked aloud. Her voice echoed endlessly.

[You are now within Dusk’s consciousness. The connection is imperfect and unstable. You are currently viewing fragments of past memories. I will work on adjusting the link.]

“His memories?” she repeated.

She looked around. Everything seemed fragmented, like she was walking through a cave filled with cracked clusters of crystal. As she moved deeper in, she found a large, strange fragment. There was something inside, but it was too blurry to see clearly.

She stepped closer and pressed her palm against it. In a flash, the world around her changed.

Endless, lush green grass stretched in every direction. Flowers of different colors were scattered everywhere, many of them ones she had never seen before. Overhead stood large purple and blue trees. The colors were strange, but somehow, they fit the scenery. Everything brimmed with vitality, reminiscent of the elven forests.

Turning, she spotted others in the space.

She recognized one of them.

Dusk.

He looked no different from how he appeared outside. Same height. Same gray tunic.

Standing next to him was a girl of roughly the same height, and perhaps the same age. She had long blonde hair and green eyes, with a white lily flower ornament clipped near the top of her hair.

“Dusk?” Veronica called out.

[These are past memories that have already occurred within Dusk’s nightmare. He is currently not here.]

Veronica walked over to them. It seemed they couldn’t see her or feel her presence. She was simply a ghost looking into the past.

However… this couldn’t have been that long ago, judging by his appearance. But where could land this lush have existed?

“Selene hasn’t come back yet…” the girl said.

“She told us to wait, Dawn,” Dusk replied.

“But it’s been so long… she should have come back by now.”

He sighed. “Remember, she told us to stay here. If we leave, she’ll get really mad at us.”

Dawn pouted, looking down at the flowers near her feet. “Well… I don’t want her to get mad. But… I miss her…”

Dusk crossed his arms, frowning as if deep in thought. “Alright. How about I just go down and check on her? Once I find her, I’ll come back.”

She looked up at him. “Really? But she might get mad at you.”

“I think it’ll be okay. I’ll just say we were worried. I’ll take the blame.”

Dawn stepped forward and hugged him. “Okay. Thank you, Dusk!”

They broke apart, and Dusk walked a few steps to the side. He raised his arm. The space in front of him began to shudder, twisting like a mirage. A few moments later, light spilled out, and a portal opened.

He turned back toward Dawn. “Then I’ll be back soon. Wait for me here, okay? We don’t want Selene getting mad at both of us.”

Dawn nodded fervently. “Okay! I’ll wait!”

Dusk nodded and stepped through the portal.

Veronica’s brows lowered. What exactly was that? A child capable of creating portals? He was that young and already knew dimensional magic?

Not wanting to be left behind, Veronica ran forward and rushed through the portal.

The world around her flashed and warped. Everything turned white, then slowly began fading back into color. When she could blink again, she found herself back at the sanctuary. However… the scene wasn’t what she expected.

“What… is this?” she murmured.

In front of her was a large metal cage. Inside was Dusk, gripping the bars. All around him were people wrapped in black cloaks. None of them had their hoods raised, so Veronica had a clear look at their faces.

They were all roughly in their mid-thirties. Men and women both, with no other identifying features connecting them to any known group, despite how organized they seemed.

A man in his forties stepped around to the front of the cage. He had dirty brown, curly hair and a clean-shaven face, with exceedingly sharp features.

“So, it looks like what she said was true. There are two more of them,” he murmured.

He crouched in front of the cage, looking at Dusk.

“Say. You came here looking for Selene, didn’t you?”

Dusk furrowed his brows. “Yes. Where is she?”

The man’s lips curled. His smile reached past his eyes. “She took a journey to heal some people. She’s very far from here. Do you want me to lead you to her?”

“What? But… Selene said she’ll always be near the sanctuary.”

The man chuckled lightly. “Oh, kid. You might not know this, but the sanctuary is actually very, very large. She is still in the sanctuary, but it may take a few years before you can see her.”

“…How long is a year?”

“It’s not that long,” the man replied easily. “Do you wish to see her?”

Dusk nodded.

“Perfect,” the man said, pushing himself up from his crouch. “As you know, Selene has a very important job to do. So before you can meet her, we need to prepare a few things.”

The man turned to one of the cloaked figures. “Bring me the cuffs.”

Doing as he was told, the cloaked man came forward and handed him a set of metal cuffs.

“It is our tradition that guests wear these,” the man said, holding them up before Dusk. “We’ll have you put these on, and then we’ll take you to Selene.”

Dusk looked at them, then nodded. “Okay.”

“I’m glad you understand quickly. What a smart child you are,” the man said.

The gathered cloaked figures began to move. They released Dusk from the cage and bound him with the metal cuffs. After that, they started leading him down the sanctuary steps.

Veronica watched all of it in silence. “Dusk was… sold off as a slave?” she murmured. She turned around, looking toward the statue of Selene. “And from where you just came from…”

It all made sense now.

There was only one reasonable explanation she could think of.

Since Selene was a god, but not a Reverent Deity, then she was likely some kind of powerful mystical creature. Dusk must have been related to her. It would explain the odd energy and why it was clashing with the demonic energy outside in the real world. It would also explain Dusk’s age, his lack of heartbeat, and the odd interactions they had with them. Why he was the only one who had gone so far into the Valley of Silence without dying to the carrion beasts.

He wasn’t a mortal.

“A spirit,” Veronica said aloud.

Selene was a powerful spirit. And Dusk and that girl, Dawn, were both spirits as well.

The place Veronica had just come from was one only accessible to them.

The spirit realm.
