Day five marked the last of her survey.

The full route was etched into her notebook now, every shrine logged, every spirit cluster marked. Only one small site left to check before the map of the area could be called finished.

Mina stayed behind that morning, still focused on carving practice. She had taken to whittling uneven sticks into vaguely animal-shaped totems and calling them 'inspiration'. Though she also made quite a few ‘proper’ charms with Rina’s help.

Elynara had no strong opinions, only an agreement to let her be for the day.

Starpuff, of course, came with her.

Not that the little rascal would have stayed behind even if she had asked it to. It had taken one look at her pack that morning, seen her staff, and launched directly into it with all the elegance of a fuzzy green pebble.

Made of puff. And fluff.

She barely felt the bonk, but it still looked mighty smug, that silly sprite!

The last two farms sat close together, neither large enough to be much work on their own, and Elynara finished them both by midday.

Afterward, with nothing else pulling her forward, Elynara wandered up a gentle ridge just east of the last pasture. There, she found a flat patch of grass that seemed just perfect for sitting, and plopped down.

She didn’t pull out her notebook, or review her notes. Just closed her eyes and let the wind roll past.

It brushed her robe, light enough to lift the hem, then tangled her hair across her face before moving on. The strands tickled her cheek, but she didn’t lift a hand to fix them.

There was no shrine nearby, or spirits crowding her senses. Only the slope behind her, the faint rustle of the fields below, and the low hum of summer bugs keeping time.

She breathed in slowly, feeling her shoulders relax. The air tasted warm and green.

This wasn’t Meditation.

This was Relaxation. Capital R.

She almost laughed.

“Can you imagine?” she muttered aloud, tilting her chin toward the sky. “If the system decided to track this?”

A flicker of amusement tugged at her thoughts, and she imagined what that would look like.

【Relaxation – Lvl 1】 – Your ability to loaf in peace has increased. Absolutely thrilling.

She snorted, and exhaled. The slow in and out of air doing wonders for her mood.

“Level up from sitting down. What a concept.”

A few seconds later, Starpuff – hovering somewhere behind her shoulder – puffed itself up dramatically, and swelled to nearly twice its usual size, and let all the air out in one slow, exaggerated whoosh. Then, with theatrical timing, shrank back to ‘proper puff size’ and resumed floating like nothing had happened.

She watched the whole act from one barely-cracked eyelid, a grin tugging at the corner of her mouth.

“You’re mocking me.”

The sprite wobbled once, clearly pleased with itself.

She shook her head, resuming her little relaxation.

One long breath after another, the quiet settled deeper.

Elynara shifted her weight just enough to sink into the grass, letting the tension in her legs slip away. Her arms rested against her knees, and she let her thoughts drift. With no thoughts of channeling, work, or mana shaping. Just the slow melt of effort rolling off her shoulders.

It never felt like you carried much stress until you stopped moving long enough to notice how heavy it had been.

And now, there was nothing left to race toward. The job was done. A week of progress drawn into clean lines, clear notations, and confidence.

She blinked slowly and looked out over the hill. The fields stretched wide, sun-washed and pale green, broken only by a few grazing animals in the distance. Farther still, the road to town’s edge shimmered like a dream waiting just past the edge of purpose.

They would be returning soon.

Back to the guild. Back to Alicia, and more things to learn. She would have to dig deeper into learning some things, who knew what still awaited her? There was still much to learn and practice, and the world had even more to offer.

And the Plan.

Still very vague. Still missing half its pieces. But what she had was working.

Sort of.

Well enough to feel proud of.

She let the sun hit her face. Warmth soaked in through her skin, and she just enjoyed the feeling. Like the land was letting her rest for once.

Elynara saw no reason to argue.

“It truly was…” she said softly, eyes drifting back across the curve of the field, “…a good day.”

Starpuff bobbed once beside her, then twirled in a tight little loop before gently bonking her on the temple.

Mina woke up before sunrise.

That fact alone would have earned a raised eyebrow if Elynara weren’t already halfway through shaping a narrow double spiral midair, one hand tracing the loop while her other held a slowly compressing mana ring. Starpuff zipped through it like a pebble launched from a slingshot.

A second later it reversed course and threaded the next loop backward.

The challenge had escalated. Again.

Elynara didn’t turn, but she could feel the sleepy weight of Mina’s still-bleary gaze from behind. She had no questions nor commentary. Just the warm silence of someone too groggy to decide whether to be amazed or confused.

After a few seconds, Mina muttered,

“What race track did you build this time?”

“I’m trying to make a proper slalom,” Elynara replied, adjusting the angle of one hoop. “But Starpuff keeps on trying to cheat.”

Starpuff executed a perfect somersault and buzzed smugly in place.

“Uh huh,” Mina mumbled, then flopped sideways into a pillow with an exaggerated sigh.

Still, she watched until the next loop broke apart in a fizz of harmless light and Starpuff spun a victory lap through the rafters. Her expression twitched somewhere between judgment and acceptance as she tracked the changes in mana constructs.

Eventually, they both rolled off their blankets and made their way toward the kitchen, following the scent of warm bread and the sound of clinking bowls.

Rina greeted them with a nod and a ladle. Theren was already outside, checking fence posts with a determined look and an armful of tools. Finnie grinned through a mouthful of pastry and waved wildly with the hand that wasn’t holding a second one, while the other two were too busy stuffing themselves silly.

It didn’t take long before the conversation turned.

“Back to Oltenar today?” Rina asked, glancing between them.

Elynara gave a slow nod.

“Yeah. We finished the map. Everything’s covered.”

Mina shifted slightly but didn’t look away from the bread she was slicing.

“Feels fast.”

“It always does,” Rina said, then passed her a basket. “But that’s what makes it stick.”

The hugs began immediately after breakfast. Neither girl managing to escape.

Finnie launched first, arms wrapped around Mina’s middle with unexpected strength for someone so small. The girl yelped, stumbled back, and blinked like she had just been tackled by a sack of laundry.

“Hey! What–!?”

“Goodbye hug,” Finnie announced proudly. “You said you liked it here!”

“I did!” Mina squeaked, caught off guard. “I still do!”

That only invited a second ambush.

Rina stepped forward and folded her arms around both of them in one sweeping motion, her expression soft and impossible to argue with. Mina vanished somewhere between shoulder and apron, and Pufflet spun in circles, entirely baffled by the sudden shift in terrain.

Elynara tried to step back. Tried.

“Don’t you dare,” Rina warned without turning.

She was caught two seconds later, pulled into the group like a log tossed into a firewood pile. Warmth everywhere. Arms. Laughter. Starpuff popped between shoulders and settled like a scarf caught mid-float.

Her nose was pressed lightly against someone’s sleeve. It smelled like flour.

Rina finally let go after one last squeeze and stepped back, already packing more food into their satchels than should have fit.

“You don’t have to send anything,” she said, adjusting the cloth on the basket.

“I will,” Elynara replied. “Once I get back to the Guild, I’ll have a copy of the full map made. It’s good reference.”

“Then I’ll make sure the kitchen’s stocked before winter. Trade for info.”

“I wouldn’t say no to that, your meals are delicious” Mina added, patting her bag.

Finnie had already tucked an extra pastry into the front pocket of her cloak, looking extremely pleased with himself.

By the time they finally stepped past the edge of the orchard path, Elynara thought they had made it.

Then Mina shouted back,

“Thank you for having us here! I liked it very much!”

She probably didn’t expect both Rina and Finnie to come racing after them. Another hug hit her square in the chest before she could blink. Pufflet yipped in alarm and scrambled sideways into a boot.

“I was already hugged!” Mina cried, laughter spilling into her words.

“And now you’re hugged again,” Rina said firmly, pulling her close.

Elynara didn’t even try to avoid the next one. She just held her arms out and braced for impact.

The goodbye stretched, but only in a good way.

When they finally turned down the long dirt path leading toward town, the farm faded behind them in soft green and gold.

Elynara glanced over once more and waved, watching until the figures in the distance blurred into the morning light.

Then she let out a long breath and adjusted the strap on her shoulder.

“Alright,” she said. “Back to Oltenar.”

Mina hummed. “Think Alicia’s going to scold you for something?”

“She usually does.”

“I’ll make sure to mention the race track.”

“You wouldn’t.”

Mina grinned.

“I would.”

Starpuff spun once above them and zipped ahead, already tracing loops in the sky.

The road waited, warm and quiet.

So they walked.

Though they didn’t make it far before Elynara veered off the road.

Mina blinked, boots scuffing against the edge of a rut.

“Hey, town’s that way.”

“I know,” Elynara said, pulling her just slightly aside by the wrist. “One last stop.”

She didn’t explain further, only gave a small glance toward the slope on their right.

The path twisted off through a patch of windbent brush and old fence posts, half-sunken in overgrowth. Well-tread, but easy to miss if you weren’t looking for it.

They followed it between rows of low hedges until the land opened again. A small clearing spread out before them, cupped gently by shallow hills, with a broad shape at the center that rooted everything in place.

The tree stood tall, and then some.

It wasn’t towering like the deep forest kinds, the ones that grew sharp and narrow in search of light. This one was wide, ancient, weathered by time in soft and quiet ways. Its bark rippled like folded cloth, and its limbs twisted upward in arcs. The base alone could have hidden a small cottage in its shadow.

Mina stopped at the edge of the path and stared upward, mouth parted just a little.

Elynara smiled.

“I left this one for last on purpose.”

The air shifted when they stepped into the heart of the clearing.

The shrine rested at the base of the oak, carved into a ring of polished stones that curved around the roots.

Worn wooden tables filled the space beyond it, clearly meant for meetings.

Some were plain and empty, but a few closest to the shrine brimmed with planters and offerings. Jars of dried herbs, carved tokens, bundles of ribbon, smooth stones stacked in careful spirals. Signs of passing and care.

And above, between the branches, the spirits danced.

Elynara didn’t have to focus to see them. They shimmered on instinct.

There were at least a dozen, maybe more. Small ones mostly, shaped like motes of firelight or clusters of leaves given weight. They moved without pattern, yet the rhythm between them felt whole. Like music without sound.

They zipped through the canopy, tucked into branches, swirled around bark, and popped in and out of sight as if playing an elaborate game with rules they refused to explain.

Starpuff joined them immediately.

It launched ahead with a whoosh and flick and spun into their orbit without hesitation, letting off a little trill as it somersaulted past a flickering red one.

That sprite recoiled, offended, then retaliated with a bounce that Starpuff barely dodged. They collided once midair, spun out in opposite directions, and resumed the game as though nothing had happened.

Elynara let out a quiet breath and stepped closer to the base of the tree.

“It’s so alive here,” she said, voice soft.

Mina nodded slowly beside her. Her eyes stayed fixed on the upper branches, unfocused but shining. “It feels warm,” she said. “I can’t see them, but… it feels like something is there.”

“They like this place,” Elynara said.

The soil beneath their feet was rich and damp, covered in low moss and soft grass that didn’t crunch when stepped on. She crouched briefly to trace her fingers over a carved offering plate, just to feel the worn grooves along the surface. It had been cleaned recently.

Movement to her right drew her attention.

An older woman stood beside a line of planters near the far end, hair streaked with silver and pinned in a simple coil. She was tending to one of the pots, adjusting a trellis vine that had wandered off-course.

They greeted her with a wave and a quiet “Good morning.”

The woman looked over and returned a polite nod, her smile small and even. She didn’t speak further, nor seem to mind their presence. Just returned to her work, fingers brushing gently through leaves as if checking their temperature.

Elynara watched her for a moment, then turned back to the tree.

The shade reached wider than she first thought. From where they stood, the canopy formed a green ceiling above them, layered and woven tight. Light filtered through in patches, turning the air into something dappled and slow-moving.

The place didn’t feel closed in, but protected.

She stepped up to the shrine itself. A stone basin held water at its center, with flower petals floating on the surface. The faint scent of sap and summer leaves hung in the air, clean and grounding.

She bowed her head briefly and placed her palm on the flat stone nearest the trunk.

“I’m glad we came.”

Mina nodded again.

“It’s beautiful here.”

“It’s like the world just... breathes easier here.”

They stayed like that for a while. Neither spoke. Even Starpuff quieted, content now to orbit the outer limbs of the tree while its new companions danced loops around the drifting pollen.

Elynara didn’t need to close her eyes to feel the mana here. It wasn’t that much higher than anywhere around, but it was consistent; a heartbeat tucked into the roots.

She shifted her weight and let her fingers rest at her side, brushing lightly against the edge of the basin.

She would need to mark this place on her map.

Even if only to remember how it felt.

The wind turned again, lifting her hair against her cheek.

It was, in every way that counted, a lovely sight.

They lingered beneath the great oak, silent for a while, eyes fixed on the branches above.

The spirits never slowed. They spun, dipped, twisted through the canopy like threads caught in wind. Some vanished into the leaves, others danced figure-eights between the limbs, and one hovered upside down for a few seconds, apparently just because it could.

Mina leaned against one of the support posts by the shrine, her arms crossed loose and her gaze tilted skyward towards the canopy.

“I’m trying to memorize this,” she said. “It feels like one of those things you don’t get to see twice.”

“You’re not wrong,” Elynara murmured.

Beside them, Pufflet pawed lightly at the ground, tail twitching. The animal bounced once, then again, then let out a questioning yip that was definitely a demand.

Mina played with it for a while, letting it run around, careful not to mess up any nearby flower arrangements.

Elynara smiled, then shifted her attention.

She knelt again by the edge of the shrine, fingers brushing the small satchel pocket where she kept a few of Mina’s earlier practice charms. They weren’t polished, not really balanced either, but it didn’t matter. Spirits didn’t care about perfection, but about mana and intent.

She pulled one free, shaped like a half-melted radish, and pressed her mana into it. The charm held onto mana greedily. It only held a fraction of her energy, but it would last longer that way.

“Here,” she said, offering it to Mina.

The girl blinked, took it, then looked at the uneven charm in her hand, and understood what to do right away.

Mina stepped forward, lifted the charm, and waited. It didn’t take long. A low-blue spirit zipped close, paused midair, then nudged at the edge of the charm with a flicker like a sniff.

Elynara reached deeper.

Charging individual charms was useful, but too slow with this many spirits around. Her attention flicked to the water shaping she had barely practiced out.

But she had no use for spheres of water here.

What she needed instead was… mist.

She exhaled and pulled. Mana flowed smoothly, her experience showing. She shaped the spell low and flat, guiding it into a soft arc around the shrine stones. Droplets formed in the air, fine and bright, scattering light into soft halos that hung between the roots and planters. The air cooled slightly, just enough to feel crisp.

Then she layered in the mana.

Not a single blob, but as many as she could manage. Unformed, loose shapes of glowing energy, nestled into the mist. She didn’t care if a few broke. Spirits wouldn’t mind and would still gobble them up. They ate whatever drifted closest.

The reaction was instant.

A green blur shot past her face, followed by a pale gold flicker that dove straight into the mist. Then two more. Then five. Starpuff let out a delighted warble and spiraled into the center of the cloud, stealing a bite like a child swiping fruit from the market.

The mist shimmered.

So many spirits moved through the water vapor that their tiny shapes traced through the air like echoes. Ripples of mana trailed behind them, scattered across leaves and roots and petals.

Elynara blinked slowly, then tilted her head toward Mina.

The girl had gone still, completely wide-eyed, her charm forgotten in one hand while a flickering blue spirit tugged gently on the other. Her voice came out in a whisper.

“I still can’t see them, not really, but the mist... they’re lighting it up.”

“That’s what I was hoping for,” Elynara said.

Pufflet barked once in confusion, before spinning around, chasing after its tail in a slow circle, then promptly tripped over its own paws and rolled halfway into a planter.

Elynara didn’t break her focus. She watched the mist shift and part and re-form, her mana trailing in quiet waves with each movement. She could feel the drain already – slow at first, then faster, each new blob pulling from her reserves as the spell stabilized.

Two-thirds gone. Maybe more.

Worth it.

Even the older woman nearby had turned from her planter. Her hands rested on the rim of the pot, her posture still. A quiet spark lit her eyes as she watched the air flicker with spirit trails, a small, knowing expression resting at the corners of her mouth.

Starpuff dove again, grabbed one of the unclaimed mana puffs, and immediately got tackled by a green streak. Their spin carried them in a wide loop around the lowest branch before they vanished together into the tree’s heart.

Elynara let out a quiet laugh.

The bloom came after, as the now-fed spirits felt inspiration strike.

Leaves perked. Petals turned. The moss at the base of the shrine grew richer in color, almost damp with life. Even the vines along the outer tables seemed to stretch a little closer to the light.

Her spell dissolved a moment later, droplets falling in glittering trails before vanishing.

She stepped back and exhaled.

Mina still stood quietly nearby, head tilted as she followed the trail of mist with her eyes. Pufflet wobbled over and pressed itself against her boot, panting like it had run a mile in ten seconds.

The clearing pulsed with quiet joy.

Elynara let the moment settle. Her arms tingled from the drain, but she didn’t mind. The shine in the air, the bloom on the leaves, and the dance of spirits all spoke the same thing.

It had been worth the cost.

Elynara gave Mina’s sleeve a light tug, enough to break the trance.

“Come on,” she said, her voice still hushed from the quiet of the shrine. “We’ve got a town to reach today.”

Mina blinked once, then looked back toward the tree. Spirits still danced between the upper branches, a little more lively than before. Pufflet had finally stopped trying to climb their tree and was currently busy sniffing a planter.

Starpuff floated back to rest on Elynara’s head.

They waved to the older woman, who returned the gesture with a nod. Her hands were already back in the soil.

The clearing faded behind them as they retraced the path through the low hedgerow and out toward the road. Sunlight caught the tall grass along the sides, and the sound of insects filled in the gaps where words hadn’t yet returned.

They walked in silence, Mina quietly playing with Pufflet.

The breeze helped. It tugged lightly at their clothes, rolled between sleeves, and kept the warmth from settling too deep. The road sloped gently downward toward Oltenar, distant rooftops just beginning to peek between the low ridges ahead.

Elynara adjusted her pack and let her gaze wander, before her thoughts turned to her Plan.

Step one was the most grounded: help the land and people hold onto what remained. It would at least stop the slipping. Marking shrines, noting where the spirits were, sharing that knowledge – that was all a start. If people could see where to leave offerings, or understand what spirits liked, maybe they would start paying attention to what already worked.

And that attention could spread.

Focus on the parts that matter.

Mina kicked a pebble down the road and let out a long sigh. Pufflet chased after it like it had offended him personally.

As for step two, it would need time.

Somebody had to learn mana shaping. Sensing was a start, but supply took more than awareness. Someone had to know how to give, how to share it, and how to not accidentally blow a hole in a shrine doing it.

Preferably someone local.

Preferably someone short, sarcastic, and already prone to being dragged along.

She glanced sideways at Mina.

The girl was still watching Pufflet do battle with the pebble.

Elynara smiled.

If it worked, it would mean the district had a local mana source again. Perhaps not a big one, but consistent, and with her help it might be enough to keep the shrines humming. Enough to attract the spirits back. Enough to let her sleep a little easier knowing she didn’t have to stress too much over every little place where something could go wrong.

She might even be able to assign someone else to check on them all.

Now that was an appealing thought.

The third step stayed where it always did – slightly out of reach.

She needed to get stronger.

Not just with mana control or shaping techniques. She needed more knowledge. More efficiency. More tools. The problems were still big, still tangled, and she wasn’t going to wish them away with good intentions.

But progress was possible.

Every time she made a spell more efficient, every time she reshaped her mana instead of brute-forcing it, that was a small fix to a large problem.

And if she kept doing it, eventually the large problems would start to shrink.

At least, that was the theory.

A long silence passed before she said,

“Okay. It’s a good plan.”

Mina tilted her head.

“Which part?”

“All of it. Big plan. Grand vision. World-fixing blueprint. It’s good.”

“Does it have any steps?”

“Details.”

“Any goals?”

“Help the land. Teach some people. Get better.”

Mina gave her a look.

“That’s it?”

“Yup.”

“Wow,” she said, voice completely flat. “I feel deeply reassured.”

Starpuff, floating above them in a lazy arc, spun once and pulsed a faint yellow.

It looked suspiciously like disapproval.

Elynara narrowed her eyes.

“Et tu, Starpuff?”

The sprite didn’t answer. It just drifted down toward her shoulder and gave the side of her head a gentle, noncommittal bump.

“I’ll take that as encouragement.”

Pufflet returned from his victorious duel with the pebble and bounded back up the road, ears perked and tail held high like a banner.

Elynara shifted her pack again and resumed walking, this time with a little more spring in her step.

She didn’t have the full map yet. The world was still too big for that.

But she had a start.

And a Plan.
