‘Wuxing’s Embrace’ was back.

Jianyi did not have positive memories of the challenge, which was based on balancing on one of twenty wooden plinths that were spread across an area of the field.

While balancing on one leg, one had to hold up a stone sphere at shoulder height with each arm. That wouldn’t be too bad if the spheres weren’t covered in formations that not just made them heavier over time, but also alternated the force they exerted randomly.

On intake day, this challenge had contributed to his being convinced that he had failed the test.

Yet again, he told himself that this day would be very different.

The set-up around the plinth, with multiple desks and disciples overseeing the challenge, was unchanged.

“Disciples, you know the test; no need for me to explain it. Please note that the test won’t end after ten minutes—it will conclude once the last of you has stepped off a plinth,” Guanming explained.

There were enough plinths so everyone in their group could start at the same time.

Once more, Jianyi and Miyao nodded to each other in encouragement. This challenge was one where they couldn’t help each other.

Jianyi took a couple of steadying breaths and stepped on the plinth.

On a whim, he chose to take the Eternal Horizon position instead of Stillness. The differences were minor, primarily the angle of the knees, but it somehow felt right to tap his cultivation art for the challenge.

The moment the first toll of the bell rang across the field, he raised his arms with the spheres and closed his eyes.

Only moments passed, at least that’s what it felt like, until the spheres started to increase in weight. It felt heavier and the changes more abrupt than in the past, despite his improved physique.

But Jianyi was in a better condition than ever; the earlier run hadn’t negatively affected him at all. He knew he could do this.

His mind wandered. Eternal Horizon. All it needed to turn this into a cultivation session was for him to cycle his qi in the appropriate pattern through his pathways. No, that seemed inappropriate for the occasion.

However… He remembered how he had used qi cycling while in the wilderness to strengthen himself. Why not use it here?

It was worth a try in his mind.

He extracted two threads of qi and slowly cycled them through the spiritual representation of his body. Through his arms, the meridians in his chest, the legs. All the way back. Again. And again.

He lost himself in the rhythm. The adjustments in his arms to the ever-changing rhythm of the spheres happened without conscious thought.

Slowly, the pain in his arms rose, but it was nothing compared to what he had gone through countless times for the benefit of his cultivation.

Overall, he still felt good.

Suddenly, he felt hands patting him on his leg.

Just like during the first challenge.

Had his arms dropped too far?

He opened his eyes and saw Guanming standing before him, the other disciples of his group standing to the side, his arms still up where they should be.

“We have to move on,” the inner disciple said with an exasperated expression.

“What happened?” Jianyi asked, confused.

“We have waited for you for twenty minutes, that’s what happened,” the young man said, chuckling. “Let’s move on.”

Jianyi nodded. It seemed that the test had gone better than before.

Miyao smiled at him as he stepped towards the group of disciples. But it was clear that not all of them were pleased. Some looked at him with suspicion, others with surprise, having to adjust their impression of him from his public altercations with Elder Fang, Lu Shan, or even the initial test, to what they had observed this morning.

Maybe he had overdone it a little.

***

It was noon by the time all the groups had completed both the Everlasting Ascent as well as Wuxing’s Embrace.

Shuhua, Hong, Miyao, and Jianyi were walking up the mountain to the outer sect, slightly behind the rest of the disciples.

The second part of the assessment, the part that everybody had been preparing for, would be conducted on the training grounds.

“It was much easier than the first time, but still, I will never be a great runner,” Hong described the first challenge. “I completed the run in slightly more than forty minutes, which was somewhere in the middle of my group.”

Shuhua gripped his shoulder. “That is great progress, Hong. Combined with your strength, you are a force to be reckoned with!”

The broad-shouldered boy chuckled. “I am not sure about that, but at least I left some of the people behind who were well ahead of me when we started.

“How did it go for you?” Miyao asked Shuhua.

“I am satisfied. I was in the leading group on the Ascent, and I lasted for almost 20 minutes on the plinth.”

Miyao smiled at her friend. “It looks like we all did well and improved.”

“Indeed.” Looking at Jianyi, she added, “I heard someone in my group speculate that Elder Sun is feeding you on a diet of cultivation pills and that that is the explanation for your growth.”

Jianyi looked at her wide-eyed. He didn’t want to be looked down upon by his fellow disciples, but he also didn’t want to become the object of random speculation and gossip. Then he snorted. “You know what is funny about that: Elder Sun makes me sell my cultivation pills; he doesn’t want me to build up impurities from alchemical products and take the ‘easy way’.”

“Huh, I didn’t know that. Does he recommend against cultivation pills in general?” Hong asked curiously.

“No, he said that I will probably need them in later realms; however, now, given that I have the benefit of living in the inner sect, I shouldn’t rely on them.”

“What does the inner sect have to do with it?” Hong was clearly confused.

Jianyi realized that his friend had likely never visited the inner sect, so he couldn’t know. “There are qi gathering formations all over the sect, and the ones in the inner sect are a bit stronger than those lower down on the mountain.”

“My mother talked about that. She said that it feels like you are breathing qi when you enter the inner sect,” Shuhua added, the sparkle in her eyes indicating where she saw her future.

Jianyi laughed. “I wouldn’t say it is quite that dramatic, but I have to admit that the difference is meaningful.” Changing topics, he asked the question that had been with him since the morning. “Has one of you found somebody that could potentially join our group?”

As he said it, he looked at Hong, gauging whether the boy would take the question as dishonoring Jun; therefore, he was surprised when Shuhua pulled on his arm energetically. “Does that mean that you think we are a circle?!”

Miyao looked at him much less expressively, but curious still.

He cleared his throat. He now realized that they had never made this explicit beyond the training sessions and the merit missions they had conducted—but it had never crossed his mind that they wouldn’t be a circle. Based on his understanding, they had the perfect combination of elements if he served primarily as a Wood cultivator.

Momentarily at a loss for words, he shrugged, but she didn’t let go of his arm and continued pulling, willing him to say it. He sighed. “I… I had thought so, yes.”

A broad grin emerged on Shuhua’s face, and Miyao and Hong were not far behind.

“That is the best news of the day!” she exclaimed. “We will be a fantastic circle. The best! And the best of friends! And we will prove it on the initial mission! I will…, no, we all should look for the perfect fifth member! I have already thought about it, of course. Miyao, what are your thoughts on the members of group Jade?” she said as she pulled the other girl forward.

He exchanged a look with Hong, both shrugged, and followed. The next challenge was waiting, after all.

***

The tableau that presented itself as Jianyi and the other disciples returned to the outer sect’s training ground was… interesting.

Elder Gao’s wrinkled face looked upon them from her dais impassively.

Slightly behind her, to her left stood Elder Fang. Less impassively. He looked as if continuously biting into a very sour lemon, and Jianyi would bet that his hands were balled into fists if he could see, yet they were clasped behind the elder’s back.

Surprisingly, Elder Zhang Zixia stood—or rather hovered—on Elder Gao’s right side. The beautiful elder had multiple jade sticks holding up her braided hair, and she was levitating in a lotus position above the dais, eyes closed, and seemingly cultivating.

Jianyi was baffled. Three elders seemed a lot for a mid-year assessment for disciples in their first year, and Elder Zhang’s presence and pose didn’t seem quite normal as well. Was she trying to indicate that she wasn’t really part of this? Protesting her absence from her usual cultivation chamber?

He shrugged. He had been too exposed to sect politics or elder shenanigans already; there was no point in thinking about it now.

“Whether you have done as well as you could or failed to live up to your own expectations, I recommend that you draw motivation from the first part of the assessment.” Elder Gao opened her speech, her gaze slowly scanning them all. “The second part of the day is dedicated to the spear. First, you will be going through three spars. The first one will be against somebody in your group. If the presiding Elder deems the performance worthy, the victor will face somebody from the next higher group at the end of the bout. Losers from fights in Jade and Lapis will be facing those from lower groups and will be at risk of losing their position. After three rounds, if you wish to move up a group permanently, you need to confirm your position by a final fight against another member of that group.”

For a moment, Jianyi was confused, but in the end, it didn’t matter. He would fight whoever he came across, and given that he had dropped out of Elder Fang’s training, he just needed to perform well in three spars. Given that the elder would determine worthiness, he doubted he had a chance to move up even if he wanted.

“Each spar is spear-only, no techniques, and ends with the third hit. Impress us with your progress.”

Done with her speech, Elder Gao produced a simple wooden chair from a storage device and settled down on the dais, her eyes closed. The image was very reminiscent of the first test, where Jianyi’s peculiarities had forced the elder to leave her position twice.

From the side, the inner disciples who managed the spear training for Elder Fang, including Fangyin and Ruolan, stepped forward to help organize the event.

Every disciple had to draw numbers to determine their first opponent. Once an order had been established, Elder Fang stepped down from the dais to get closer to the ring that would be used for the fights.

Given the number of fights to get through, it would be a long day.

Still, Jianyi looked forward to it—he had improved, and he was keen to test himself. He felt ready.
