Jora knew he had messed up when he saw Hayden grin. Hayden tapped Emmi on the shoulder twice, nudged his head toward Jora, and handed it to her. When he did that, he stopped hiding his wrist, which was glowing red. The situation worsened further when Emmi giggled, looked at Jora, and then handed the note to someone else.

A fourth of the class got it before Carian turned and glowered at the students.

“Is there something more important than class?” Carian asked.

Hayden reaccepted the piece of paper. “No.”

Carian grinned viciously, preparing to ask for the note. Yet when she saw Jora’s desperation, she turned back to the class. “Okay, I’ll pretend I didn’t see anything—but this is your one warning.” She looked at the digital clock. “Time’s up. I want you to submit your answers. But before you leave, I want your brazen fellow classmate to explain what spirits are—in detail. If I hear the word magic once—he’s being docked the day’s points.”

Students grinned as they fixed their eyes on Hayden.

Hayden’s expression didn’t fluctuate as he began. “Spirits are officially known as Cori here, mutualistic endosymbiotes that attach to those with cores. Once attached, they share their bodies and unique physics-altering powers with their host to help them hunt riftwalkers—the beasts in rifts.”

“And why do they want to kill riftwalkers?”

“Because riftwalkers are their food. The beasts release a refined form of cora known as tora—that’s what spirits eat. The more they eat, the stronger they and their host get. The stronger the duo becomes, the better they are at hunting. It’s a symbiotic relationship.”

Hayden nailed the answer, but Carian seemed unsatisfied. Everyone thought she would press him further, but the bell rang.

“That’s it for today. I don’t care how you learn about rifting but do it. You’re dismissed.”

Emmi turned to Hayden. “Tell us about what?”

Hayden grinned. “I have class, but I’ll tell you at lunch. You have some time?”

“I do! I can meet you at the quad.”

“That’s perfect. I’ll see you there.”

Jora’s heart fired like a combustion engine. No… no-no-no-no-no. Fuck! I need to stop him!

“I’m in a hurry, so I’m going off ahead,” Hayden said. “But I’ll see you there.”

You'd better be in a hurry! Jora thought, ignoring the man saying, “Hey, Jora, what was that about?” behind him. Jora ignored them and pushed past the crowd, chasing Hayden out the door. People yelled, “Oh shit! It’s going down!” and chased after them.

Hayden was already at the end of the hall before Jora even got out the door.

Jora blitzed after him as the students tried their best to catch up. They couldn’t. What ensued was a high-speed chase through the campus, weaving between people. Jora should’ve been able to catch him in an instant, but the runt was impossibly fast, constantly checking for cameras as they went deeper and deeper into campus.

Cameras won’t save you! Jora screamed in his mind as he rushed through campus.

Hayden kept pace, though, ducking between buildings. He might’ve escaped—but he hung himself by going into the servant district. There weren't cameras there, and there was a labyrinth of access roads leading to public housing where people wanted privacy.

Hayden suddenly turned down one of those alleys, and Jora followed closely behind.

Then it was game over.

Hayden slowed down and stopped.

Jora expected Hayden to be winded like he was—but his breathing was barely strained as he turned to face Jora. The look on his face was also malicious. It sparked primal fear in Jora, so he summoned Toro—his spirit. It wrapped his body with golden cora, making him nearly indestructible as his body rose off the ground. He was taller, larger, stronger—as if he were in a warframe. This was the B-rank spirit “Goliath,” and it was the ultimate weapon. In his current form, Jora could bend support beams and run through walls with ease. He was certain that if he played chicken with a maglev train and collided, he would be the one who survived the impact. After all, the spirit had also already evolved. Compared to other students, Jora was a god.

Despite that, Hayden was smiling at him.

“You think this is a game?” Jora asked. “You think I won’t kill you?”

Hayden didn’t stop smiling. “No… It’s just…” He lifted the note Jora wrote him. “If you’re going to write an anonymous note, you shouldn’t give it to people in public…” He turned the paper around. “With your family’s insignia on it.”

Jora’s face contorted into a look of terror when he saw his family crest embossed on the page. “Give me that!”

“It’s my property,” Hayden said.

“I said, give it to me!” Jora lifted his fist and charged it with cora, making his entire arm glow bright gold.

The next second, Hayden summoned his winged mouse, and Jora fell to the ground. He looked at his hands. His spirit was gone.

“What did you do…” he muttered as he stood up. “What did you do!”

“I taught you the importance of amplification techniques,” Hayden said. “Now I'll teach you why you shouldn't coerce people.”

“W-Wait. What?”

Jora tried to step back, but Hayden was too fast. He closed the distance, lifted his knee, and stomped Jora’s kneecap with the impact of a cannonball.

Jora screamed when he heard his leg snap backward at a terrible angle. Then he fell onto the ground—shaking and dry-heaving.

It all happened in an instant.

Jora blacked out from the pain, but a jolt brought him back to consciousness. He opened his eyes and saw Hayden’s demonic eyes staring deep into the depths of his soul. Hayden pulled out the note Jora wrote and waved it in front of his face.

“To be honest, I can’t remember why you made me throw the test. The fact that I don’t remember means that it wasn’t… that significant. I probably saw you hugging a man, or… getting rejected by a woman. I might have won a fight against you. Who knows what triggers you insecure cretins. But now…. with this silly piece of paper twenty people read, and that extreme chase scene you put me through, well… I guess I could say that I caught you having sex with your sister and people would believe me, now wouldn’t they?”

Jora’s mind flooded with primal emotions, fear, regret, and a desire to flee. Yet he couldn’t breathe from the shock, and his leg was bent at an unnatural angle. So he just had to listen.

“I’m going to leave this note and that lie with someone powerful,” Hayden warned, “someone who hates you enough to distribute it if anything happens to me. Do you understand me?”

“D-Don’t… P-Pl—”

“Save your breath. I own you, Jora. You will do what I say when I say it—and you won't ask questions. Otherwise, I will destroy you—you can count on it.”

That was the last thing that Jora heard before the pain took over, and he blacked out.
