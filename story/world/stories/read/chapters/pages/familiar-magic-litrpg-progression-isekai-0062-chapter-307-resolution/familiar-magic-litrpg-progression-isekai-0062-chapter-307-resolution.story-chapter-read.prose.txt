Something was wrong. Terribly, impossibly wrong.

Dusk knew this, knew it as much as he knew that the Union should have begun long ago. Already he senses flaws in his garden, the impact of him remaining for too long.

But why? What could have happened?

Dusk reaches out to his sister, bypassing the ancient directive as they’d done so few times in their long lives.

Almost as shocking, Dawn responds without hesitation. She is concerned. She too, knows the Union has been delayed. But the fault lies not with her.

Twilight.

Dusk reaches out again, ignoring the discomfort of his transgression. And once more he receives an immediate response.

Pain. Such pain as the three have never experienced. And with that pain, a message, but a short one.

The Fallen have found us.

Dusk felt pain that mirrored that of his brother. The Fallen were the greatest failures of the three, and it was fitting that those broken ones followed them across the stars.

But the shocks were not finished. The Guardian was reaching out to Dusk. Dusk didn’t realize that was possible. The Guardian was silent, and watched from afar. Sometimes the three wondered if it even existed.

Yet he heard from it now, a scattering of images, sensations, and…shame. Terrible shame.

The Guardian fought the Fallen even now, and the Guardian was losing. Worse, it was trying something that it knew it shouldn’t. A desperate, final gamble.

Then the communication ended, and Dusk felt very, very alone.

Then came the rifts. Nearly a dozen, opening all across Dusk’s world. Confused, he spread his attention out, and felt a sense of horror that the god didn’t realize it was capable of.

The Fallen were here. Champions of those failures, and they…they…they were devouring him. Ripping Dusk’s power from the very air around them.

The balance was being torn asunder. The Fallen didn’t belong here. They were ruining Dusk’s garden! They were upsetting the great project, they were destroying everything!

But what could he do? Dusk didn’t fight. Dusk didn’t take on physical form, like the others. He became one with his world, became the very soil from which his blossoms grew. The Fallen now fed on him, heedless of the damage they were doing.

They’d already taken so much, they were–

They were as flush with his power as any of his own creations. They weren’t so different from the other seeds in his garden. If he tried, Dusk might be able to…

They bloomed. The power erupted within the Fallen, reaching through them, changing them. Some were simply overwhelmed by it, their physical forms lost in explosions of energy. But others survived, shifting, growing, becoming something new.

Had he done it? Had he saved his garden? Was this what victory felt like?

Perhaps, but something was…out of balance.

Dusk’s consciousness quested out, inspecting the Fallen, or whatever they should be called in their new forms. They were twisted. Mockeries of his perfect blossoms.

They were massive things, like several creatures blended together, minds broken, and wild. But Dusk saw more than the simple danger they posed to his garden. Looking deeper within, he saw the lingering effects of what the Fallen had been.

These beings were old, if not so old as the three, and they’d tasted energy from hundreds of worlds. Then they’d feasted on the pure potential that was Dusk, and that energy had mixed, becoming volatile.

It was like an illness, an impurity…a corruption.

Dusk understood immediately that these creatures needed to be expelled from his garden, but lacked the power to do so. They were part of this world now, and he couldn’t banish them. But they were also his creations now, and that granted him some degree of control.

Dusk molded their fractured minds as best he could, sending them toward the deepest depths of the ocean, and giving them a powerful aversion to land. It was the best he could do.

Finally, Dusk would have peace once more. But what came next?

He reached out to Twilight once again, and even the Guardian when he felt nothing. But it was no use.

Dusk could only wait…and wait…and wait.

But as time passed, a terrible truth was revealed. The corruption within the former Fallen Champions wasn’t contained. It was spreading across the vast nexus of power that was Dusk, seeping into every living creature.

Everything was falling apart. Dusk’s garden couldn’t survive. Already he felt the corruption changing his perfect blossoms. So he did the only thing he could, and drew his power back into him, as much as he could.

The spread of the strange plague slowed, and for a time Dusk was grateful. But the corruption had not ceased, it had merely been moved. Now it corrupted him.

He tried everything he could, but the corruption possessed the very potential that was at Dusk’s core. It was limitless, breaking all bonds, and defying all resistance. He couldn’t stop it on his own.

He needed his siblings to save the project. He needed the Union. But at this rate, there would be nothing left of him when that moment came. So Dusk had to retreat to sleep.

But before he did so, he made one final change. He imprinted knowledge on the power of this world, carving it into the Astral, for anyone to find, provided they knew how to look.

Dusk could only hope his siblings would find that hidden blueprint, and use it to combine their power, and restore the balance. Restore him, and his garden.

As he drifted off his final thoughts were that vain hope.

Ethan took in thousands of years of memories in a matter of minutes, but it still wasn’t fast enough. He could feel the fragile minds and souls of the six men surrounding them, and knew they couldn’t protect him forever. But what he’d learned…

He understood that Dusk couldn’t have anticipated what came next. That the corruption, born from the Followers' own terrible hunger, would infect this world. Combined with Dusk’s own power of potential, every living thing would become trapped in a desperate need to feast, and grow stronger.

They became ever-hungry, and ever-hunting, and there was no meal greater than Dusk himself. Than The Throne.

But Ethan understood, now. He understood it all. Dusk had known that he’d likely be too far gone to participate in the Union, but that his own knowledge and power would be required for it to take place.

So he’d left instructions, hidden in the very energy of this world. And he’d left a guide-post, to draw the other gods to it.

Ethan didn’t think much of “the light” anymore. That strange guiding force that drew the corrupted toward greater sources of power. But he understood its origin now, and what it had been meant to show.

Ironically, Ethan didn’t need it. Now that he knew what to look for, he understood that he’d found it, long ago. With Tomo’s help, he’d been able to isolate the power of this place, and turn it into a tool.

That tool now ran up his right arm, the perfect symbiosis of three powers, the three worlds…[Hand of Three Gods], his healing rune.

Ethan had what he needed. He even remembered Dawn’s surprise when she saw that he’d managed to combine the three powers. He now understood that he’d only been able to do so by using the blueprint left in Dusk’s power, and the limitless potential it provided.

With his answers literally in-hand, Ethan didn’t wait another moment. He could feel the strain of the other men growing, and he had to act quickly.

The first thing he did was redirect the overwhelming power flowing into him from The Throne. Thankfully, his spirit was still cleansing the corruption–another gift of his rune, he realized–and the real challenge was to focus on guiding the Union.

He’d begun this process with Dawn, and Twilight had followed suit, but the last phase was now up to him. So he reached out, as Dusk had done in the memories they’d shared.

Ethan reached for the familiar energy of the other worlds, looking for…there.

Once he recognized it, it became impossible to miss. The power of two gods was extending through the Astral, which connected the three parallel worlds. He could feel it, waiting to be joined, waiting to be completed. Craving the balance of the three.

Ethan completed the connection with a thought, and even through the chaos of the power rushing through him, he heard the gasps of his brothers nearby. The Union was joined, yet they had little time left.

Once more, Ethan’s mind became electrified with knowledge and thunderous energy. He immediately recognized what Dawn had set in motion–with Ethan’s help–as well as the more malleable input from Twilight.

It seemed that the long-suffering god of Atlantis hadn’t added much to the mix, perhaps not trusting his own perspective.

That left even greater control to Ethan, which wasn’t necessarily a good thing. Through the chaos of what he was experiencing, Ethan forced his mind to consider what needed to be done.

“Diagnose the problem…” he mumbled.

The worlds needed to be joined. That was easy at this point, Ethan intuitively understood it would happen now whether he wanted it to or not. But there was more.

The corruption. That had to be dealt with, and he didn’t have the time to siphon all the power of Potentia through his spirit. It would have to be done as the new world formed.

Then there were the demons, and the Nator–can’t forget Selina’s people. For them, he reached into Tomo, and the evolution of a corruption-free Nator. He combined that with yet-more of Dusk’s memories, discovering the true path of these broken people.

A small voice said that this was enough. That he could join the worlds now, and the bleeds would end. He just needed to shift around populations, and create natural barriers–like the mountains in Nexum–to keep the monsters from overwhelming the newly displaced people.

The demons were no longer the problem that the Church had feared–they would be monsters when this was done, merely a different selection from those on Nexum. Of course, there were still those Champion rank monstrosities from the ocean, some of whom had actually been Followers, long ago.

Ethan couldn’t “fix” them, but he could isolate them, somewhere obscure. That would be enough. It should be enough, he could feel the strain on the others, and that his time was nearly spent.

But Ethan wanted one last thing, and so he began his treatment.

The blueprint of the new world came together. The continents would shift, slightly, as they needed more land. The oceans would need to be deeper as well, but Twilight could handle those calculations in an eyeblink. The demons would be cleansed, and seeded across the lands, along with Nexum’s monsters.

But it was the people that truly mattered to Ethan.

The Nator would be healed, and set back on their path toward Dusk’s planned perfection. Not accelerated on their path–that was a journey for the Nator themselves, but they would no longer be corrupt, and their future was their own.

The humans and elves of Nexum and Earth would find themselves in relative safety, though they’d need Bonds and Awakenings to survive. They’d find them, and could rely on the existing Bonded, and the powerful Nator, until they were ready.,

That left Ethan with his one final, simple change, one made all the easier with his rune.

No more sickness. No more degenerative diseases, or bodies failing for any reason but time. It was the least he could do to satisfy that part of him, the part he’d so often needed to suppress, but which he’d never abandoned.

Just as the strain was ready to become too great, he finally put his plan into action, and completed the Union.

Doctor Ethan Bishop healed three worlds.
