The next day, Hayden stood before the main stage, awaiting the lottery to obtain his spirit. Despite not meeting the requirements to get one—as he didn't open his main gate—no one denied him. In fact, one instructor said Loricks was working to obtain a better catalyst for him. Hayden would deny it, of course. He had a feeling his dream catalyst was in the Heline Rift, so he would wait. Until then, he would survive on clear spirits—”training spirits,” as this universe had deemed them.

It was good he didn't need one, though—because the lottery was rigged.

Hayden could see coran fluctuations, which allowed him to see through objects, within limitations, and he could clearly see a secret chamber in the lottery box. Judging by the way Rosen shifted his gaze from Hayden to the box, it was clear that Rosen had bribed someone to ruin him.

I wonder who that's for, Hayden thought sarcastically. How petty.

Hayden was starting to question how he relinquished his role as an intergalactic emperor, only to end up in a school for mentally deficient children.

A stern woman took the stage during his musings, sparking a manic grin on Rosen's face. She went by Ms. Narin, and she refused to look at Hayden.

“Welcome to the Selection Ceremony,” she said. “If you passed the burning ceremony yesterday, it will be the day that marks your journey as a rifter.”

Those who successfully opened their gates whistled and pumped their hands; those who didn’t folded their arms enviously.

“For the sake of fairness,” she said with a slight hint of hesitation, “we make the spirit selection process a lottery. To prevent digital tampering, we do it the ancient way—with paper. Once your name is called, I will pull a slip of paper out of the lottery box and read it aloud. You will then tell the title to the treasurer. They're the one who will give you your spirit. They will also give you a subgate catalyst required to use it. Are there any questions?”

No one raised their hand.

“Good. Let’s get started. Eastea Aeal…”

Hayden was impressed that Ms. Narin at least had the discipline to go alphabetically. It took her half an hour to reach his name. When she did, her expression nearly cracked.

“Hayden Reskco.” Electric murmurs spread among the teachers when she called his name. She clearly didn’t know what had transpired the day before because she shifted uncomfortably after the reaction. She hesitantly reached into the box.

Rosen couldn’t wait to see Hayden’s face when she announced his spirit. It would either be a look of absolute joy or existential dread—and both would be worth the money. But if he could choose, he’d choose joy; that way, Hayden would hit peak excitement, only to later realize just how screwed he was.

Ms. Narin unfolded the sheet. “It seems that you have… The Winged Horix spirit. It’s a rare clear spirit beast that’s widely sought after in the universe.”

A close-up on the widescreens caught Hayden’s eyes widening slightly, breaking his characteristically impassive expression. It made him look bewildered, as if he were expecting something bad and was surprised it wasn’t. Then, he nodded and said, “Thank you.” His face then returned to the bored expression that was getting on Rosen's nerves.

Female students went crazy around him.

“He got a spirit beast? How lucky!”

“Right? I wonder if he’ll trade.”

“Probably not. But I’m sure he’ll let us pet it.”

“Really? We should totally see him after he assimilates.”

In a disturbing twist, the pick had made Hayden even more popular. The only woman who didn’t seem jealous was Kayline, who had a lethal gaze fixed on Ms. Narin. She clearly understood the gravity of the pick.

“Hey, Hayden,” a student blurted. “Will you trade me? I have the Thunder Whip spirit. It's a C-rank. So it’s super good.”

The area erupted when the only person with a C-rank spirit offered it to Hayden for the horix.

Ms. Narin tried to speak, but Hayden said, “No,” so bluntly that the whole area fell silent.

“I won’t be trading the Horix for any spirit,” he clarified. “But I appreciate the offer. It was a fair trade.”

Rosen’s eyes drifted around aimlessly, wondering what he had missed.

Nothing.

He had missed nothing.

Hayden was clearly less informed than Rosen thought. Spirit beasts could become extremely powerful, but to get stronger, they needed to kill riftwalkers—cora-mutated beasts in the rifts—and clear spirits had no battle capacity. So the beast was unable to fight or get stronger. That's why Rosen chose it.

And yet…

Hayden’s reaction implied that he knew exactly what he got, and wouldn’t trade it—for anything. The confident reaction didn’t make sense. Luckily, he ensured there were no loopholes.

Ms. Narin took a sharp breath. “It’s good you didn't want to trade because I haven't clarified the rules on that yet.

“Galma Remedial is mostly a charity; the work you do pays for your food and board—but the spirits and catalysts are donations. For that reason, there are limitations on what you can do with them. You cannot sell your spirit. And to ensure strict compliance and prevent trade laundering, we have extended that to trading spirits—”

An outcry instantly broke out.

“—until a formal trade protocol is established!” Ms. Narin finished, yelling over the crowd. “It also eliminates shady spirit swap schemes at the tournament. Therefore, you can trade, but you'll need to wait a few weeks. It's a small price to pay for a free spirit. Now be quiet. The selection ceremony isn’t over!”

Kayline fumed after the ceremony. First the rigged lottery, now this? You'd think this place was Junolyx.

She turned to Hayden and saw him walking toward the treasury, apathetic as ever.

What's your deal? Gah. I’d turn her in if he didn't seem so… satisfied.

Kayline had seen the rigged lottery box with her coran vision and planned to turn Ms. Narin in, but then she noticed Hayden's surprised eyes and his aggressive rejection of Thunder Whip. After that, she worried that if she intervened, Hayden would be furious.

What's going on in that head of yours? Kayline wondered. She wanted to see him get his horix, but she had to get back to work. So she decided to have it investigated later.

Hayden was glad Kayline didn't intervene. Despite his calm demeanor, he was delighted when he reached the front of the treasury line. The treasurer looked as though he had been denied a week's worth of sleep, but he was friendly.

“Spirit name?”

“Winged Horix.”

The treasurer winced. “Give me a second.” He recovered a box and opened it, revealing a relic—an object that contained a natural spirit. This one was in the shape of a tiny bone.

“Here’s the horix. It’s a clear spirit, meaning that… well, it means you won’t have any problem using it. Just summon it by pouring in cora. It’ll come out and you can just… ask it to bind. It’ll do it. Thing’s probably lonely in there. As for your catalyst…” He winced again. “I can’t give you one. I know it’s not fair, but we get spirits and catalysts in pairs. If it doesn't need one we don't get one. I hope you understand.”

Elemental, physical, and entropic spirits operated from subgates. Rosen's Thunder Punch spirit, for example, required the lightning gate. Clear spirits didn't use a subgate, so Galma wouldn't give him a catalyst. That only made sense.

“That’s a shame,” Hayden deadpanned. “I was hoping that you’d have a time catalyst.”

The treasurer laughed. “At least you have a sense of humor. Take care, kid.”

Hayden left feeling as though he had won an empire in a lottery. Clear spirit beasts were among the rarest and most sought-after spirits in the Elysian Universe. They were incredibly useful—on and off the battlefield—and if someone bought one, they usually made their living on it. It was a suspiciously powerful boon.

I thought causality was trying to destroy me, Hayden thought seriously. So why give me something so precious?

He thought back to what the old man had said:

If you accept, the laws of causality will subtly interfere with the universe in an attempt to stop you. Of course, that won't eliminate your natural fortune or prevent you from accomplishing your goals. But it will make your journey substantially more difficult, especially as you reach major inflection points.

Hayden sat underneath a tree and thought about it. I suppose the key term is “natural fortune.” I wish I knew what that meant. He paused and pondered it. It probably means that it doesn't affect me directly. It just influences outside events. If that's true, things might not be so bad.

Hayden was worried that his luck would be terrible and that he would need to fight for every last victory, but that didn't seem to be the case. He could handle crises; fighting fortune was the unknown.

I guess I'll just have to wait. Until then…

Hayden summoned the horix from the bone, and grinned when the little beast spread its wings and flew around him.
