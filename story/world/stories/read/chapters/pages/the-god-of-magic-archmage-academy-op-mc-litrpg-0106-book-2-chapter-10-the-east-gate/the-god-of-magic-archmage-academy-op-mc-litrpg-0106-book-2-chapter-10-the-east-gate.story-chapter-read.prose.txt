Slari

Each drop of rain pressed against her skin with the force of an arrowhead. Dreadbeast had thick skin and were well-suited to rough terrain and environments. Even then, the rain was uncomfortable. But uncomfortable for a dreadbeast was unbearable for a human. This was a small price to pay to keep humans off the streets. It was a small price to pay for freedom.

With that in mind, she welcomed the water droplet arrows with a smile.

Not long after storm clouds blotted out the moonlight and heavy rain fell, the mist arrived. Between the thick rain and the mist that obscured the streets, her people were like ghosts moving in the night. Mana lights sputtered out as the horde approached. The windows of homes and businesses became so fogged that no one could see out of them. Doors clicked as magical seals settled into place to keep people from coming outside. The human Drussan had enlisted was powerful indeed. It was proof that the physical advantages dreadbeast enjoyed were not always enough against the magical ones that humans did.

She carried an axe in each hand, and tonight her head swiveled. Though she was not yet a Khan, Slari was on her way. Tonight, she’d been given the honor of serving as the Rear Guard. The dreadbeast warriors under her command ensured that no one would be left behind. Anyone left behind tonight would become the sole bearer of the fury of tomorrow. That was, if any of the slavers or their allies survived.

She hoped none did.

They were making good time, and though visibility was limited, her guards were positioned in such a way that no one could easily slip through their formation. It was almost over. They just needed to make it to the east gate of the city. Once they were beyond its walls, they were as good as home. They would rebuild under the leadership of Garvhadda. In the meantime, she just needed to guard their flank.

The muddy side street squelched beneath her feet, and each step churned up water from the ground. She swatted at the mist to take a look and noticed the footsteps that had formed under those that trekked before her were slowly fading. This human had even thought to cover their tracks.

She smiled in appreciation at the same moment her instincts gave her pause.

She stopped walking completely, and one of her guards noticed. “Rearguard, is everything alright?” he asked.

Slari nodded. “Yes, keep moving,” she ordered.

He nodded and turned his attention back to the others. She knew she should as well, but something was bothering her. Her senses were good. She trusted her instincts, and they had never misled her.

So why were they telling her that a dreadbeast was out there somewhere? That someone was being left behind.

Slari frowned. It was dangerous to break away from the others. But she knew the route, and her warriors were capable. She just needed to check. Her mind wouldn’t be at ease unless she checked.

If someone had slipped through their formation, then Slari needed to find them. She would rather die than abandon one of her own. The streets were dark, and though she refused to acknowledge the thought, turning back and facing these human streets alone frightened her. There was no shame in fear; even Garlon had surely felt fear. No, there was only shame in letting fear overwhelm duty. Her fear would not overwhelm her responsibilities.

She needed to move quickly.

The streets blended together as she ran at full speed, sheathing her axes to avoid injuring herself. She caught herself on the sides of buildings when she slipped in the mud. It was all she could do to stay upright and squint through the mist and rain as she ran in search of someone her instincts screamed was getting closer and closer. There wasn’t a single doubt in her mind now, someone was out here.

A dreadbeast was out here somewhere. Alone.

She reached another intersection of streets and turned to look in either direction. Her right appeared clear. When she turned left, she saw it. The distinct silhouette of a fellow dreadbeast kneeling in the road. Even over the pounding rain and the occasional flashes of lighting and booms of thunder she could hear the poor dreadbeast suck in air. Each breath sounded almost more labored than the last.

She needed to hurry. The others were getting farther and farther away. Neither of them could afford to be left behind. Even though the thought of being trapped in this city while the others escaped terrified her, the fact that her instincts had led her to this one gave her peace. The only thing more terrifying than being left behind on her own was leaving another behind.

So she ran. She ran and ignored the drenched hair that stuck to her skin, and the rain’s painful kiss. Slari ran until she reached the hunched-over dreadbeast and skidded into the mud to grab him by the shoulders. “Are you okay?” she asked.

The look on the man’s face was one of utter terror. He was short, only six feet or so, and his horns looked almost freshly grown and unmarred by time or combat. It was odd for a fully grown dreadbeast. But still, what stuck out in her mind the most was the unmistakable fear. Fear, it seemed, of her.

“Are you alright, brother?” She looked up and scanned their surroundings. “Why are you here? How did you fall so far behind?” she asked. They hadn’t passed through this way. Not only had this poor man fallen behind, but he’d gotten completely lost in the process. “What happened?” she asked.

He looked up at her and seemed to calm down a bit. But only a bit. His eyes slowly fell to the ground. “Orianne…she…she sent them after me. She betrayed me,” the dreadbeast said.

“Orianne?” Slari asked. That wasn’t a dreadbeast name. At least, no dreadbeast she’d ever heard of. But she knew sometimes the humans gave their slaves’ human names. She scowled at that. It was unfortunate she hadn’t been able to feed her axes slaver blood tonight. “Take a breath. What’s your name? And…who is Orianne? Is she one of us? Is someone else out there?” she asked. Surely she hadn’t missed two dreadbeast. Surely not…her honor as a rearguard was already beyond repair. She would submit her resignation as soon as they got out of Vitree.

“I’m Mi…” He paused, as if he’d said something wrong.

“Mi?” She asked.

“Just Mi. There’s no one else,” he said.

She frowned. He was acting weird, but was that so strange after being left here in the cold, dark of night? Surrounded by this horrid magic weather? No, that would affect anyone. “Come on, we have to get back,” she said. She moved beside him and took his arm over her shoulder. “The others are waiting.”

He looked up in surprise. “The others?” he asked.

She paused. “Did you hit your head? Are you feeling unwell?” she asked.

Before he could answer she heard shouts.

“This way! Hurry up! He ran this way!”

Humans. Humans were after him.

Slari’s eyes widened. How were the humans after them already? They’d followed Drussan’s plan exactly. No, she couldn’t worry about this now. They just had to run. “Let’s go! We can’t get caught here!”

Mi didn’t resist. Though he was still out of breath, the adrenaline of being caught kept him moving. Despite that, he was far too slow. It was obvious he wasn’t a warrior, nor had he trained his endurance much. Slari dropped his arm and grabbed him by the wrist. If he couldn’t run fast enough, she’d just have to pull him forward. They were escaping together. No matter what, she would get him to safety.

They turned down a new street, and she resisted the urge to look over her shoulder. It would only slow them down.

“Where…where are we going?” Mi called out.

“East gate. Don’t talk, just save your breath!”

The shouts of their pursuers were getting closer. Far too close for her liking. Though the rain was holding up, the mist wasn’t as thick as it had been earlier. They must have drifted too far away from the spell’s center. Still, that didn’t explain how the humans were gaining on them. It wasn’t like they were leaving behind any—

She turned and looked over her shoulder. There was a single set of tracks behind them. Whatever spell the human had cast hadn’t affected Mi for some reason. Maybe it was because he’d fallen too far behind, or maybe there was a different reason. Either way, this was bad. If they didn’t figure out something soon, they’d be caught. They weren’t supposed to kill town guards or humans not involved with the slave trade. She scanned the buildings ahead and her eyes fixed on some kind of restaurant. The curtains were pulled shut, and a sign on the door read ‘closed.’ She turned and lifted Mi up in her arms with a grunt.

“What are you doing?” he asked in surprise.

She grimaced. He might be short, but that didn’t mean he wasn’t heavy. She ran toward the restaurant and shouldered the door open. As soon as they were inside, she set him down and turned to pull the door shut. Their pursuers were close now.

She waited a moment, then peered out the window. She froze when she saw a human creeping past. A soldier, but he didn’t seem like one of the town guards. His armor carried the symbol of House Thessus on it, the familiar symbol of a fox. She peeked at the road and scanned the mud leading toward the door. There wasn’t any sign that she and Mi and passed this way. Fortunately for them, the spell erasing footsteps was still active on her. So as soon as she had picked him up the trail had gone cold. Still, she hadn’t carried him far, and the humans were bound to go door to door if they suspected dreadbeasts were hiding inside.

She turned to Mi and gestured toward the back of the store. “Come on,” she said.

Mi’s eyes threatened to bulge out of his head. “What? Why? We’re safe here!” he protested.

She frowned. “What? We’re in a human city. We’ll never be safe here. If we stay here any longer, we’ll be trapped. We need to catch up to Drussan and the others.”

Mi froze. “Drussan? Drussan will be there?” He swallowed.

“What’s going on with you? Why are you acting so strange?” Slari asked.

“I’m not. I just…” Mi seemed at a loss for words. “I—”

Someone knocked on the door to the restaurant. With the lock broken, it slid right open a few inches.

Slari pulled one of her axes from its sheath and pulled Mi behind her.

“Oh…it’s open! Are you in there you little beasties?” It was a man’s voice, and it gave Slari chills.

Khans Ebron and Vlay had both assured her they believed in a few years’ time, she would reach their rank. Though she hadn’t yet, she had already developed some senses and instincts typically associated with a dreadbeast Khan. Senses that allowed you to pick up on powerful people nearby. Senses that should have warned her someone incredibly powerful had been lingering just outside the door.

Instead, she hadn’t realized how close death was until it announced itself.

“Oh, come on, little beasties, it’s rude not to respond!” The voice’s owner pushed the door open all the way and smiled at them with black teeth. He was human, but nobody had told his body that. The man was broad-shouldered and tall enough to look down at even Slari. She was seven feet tall, and a human was looking down at her by a few inches. He had a wild mane of bright red hair and a thick beard that hung down to his chest. His eyes appeared devoid of sanity, and he carried a massive axe in his hands. It was a two-handed weapon, but in his possession it appeared weightless.

He stared at her, and then looked past her at Mi. “I heard you ate a human, little beasty!” He grinned. “I think it’s only fair now if a human takes a bite of you.” The wild man made a chomping motion with his mouth. “What do you think?”

Though they were inside, Slari heard something trickle behind her. She turned to see Mi had wet himself. “I…I…” Words failed to escape Mi’s lips. He turned to look at Slari, a sense of desperation in his eyes.

The red-haired man frowned. “Well, lost my appetite. I might still pluck out a beasty eyeball or two to snack on later.”

Slari took a step back.

The human frowned at that. “Huh? I thought little beasties were brave?”

Slari threw an axe at his face. She whipped around and grabbed Mi by the wrist and pulled him toward the back of the shop. She didn’t know what this human was talking about or if there was any truth to his accusations against Mi. But right now, all that mattered was the fact that they were both dead if they didn’t get away. This man was on the level of a Khan or higher. No, definitely higher.

This man was death.

There was no rear door at the back of the restaurant.

So Slari made one.

She barreled through the wooden wall at the back of the shop and burst through. “We need to reach the Khans!” she called out, almost as much for her benefit as Mi’s.

“What’s a Khan?”

A sob escaped her before she could collect herself. She was going to die. She was going to die in the middle of a human city, and no one would even know what happened to her. It wasn’t supposed to be like this. None of this was supposed to—her instincts cried out another warning.

She spun and pushed Mi out of the way as her own axe smashed into her chest. The force of the attack sent her tumbling back into the mud several dozen feet. The handle had been the part that hit her, but that hadn’t stopped it from forcing the air out of her lungs and breaking ribs. There was mana behind that attack. She ached in places she didn’t know existed, and one of her horns now had a crack running down its center. She tried to get up. Slari wanted nothing more than to get up and draw her other axe. A dreadbeast warrior should not die on her back.

“Oops. Sorry beastie. I meant to return your axe, but not until after I’d killed the snack.” The red-haired man chuckled. “Ah well. It’s all the same.” He walked slowly, as if catching up to them was inevitable.

Mi pried his eyes off the human just long enough to glance at Slari. “What do we do…” he whispered.

“Run,” she said. “Run as fast as you can. Get away from him,” she said. Her words came out far quieter than she meant to. She didn’t have the strength.

Mi swallowed. “I don’t think I can.” His legs were shaking. He turned to look back at the man and saw that the distance between them was shrinking by the second. Mi fell to his knees. The shaking had spread to the rest of his body. Perhaps part of it was from the cold, but Slari knew fear when she saw it. Mi had succumbed to it. She frowned. It seemed neither of them was escaping this fate.

The red-haired man was feet away when another voice spoke from the darkness.

“Easy. We haven’t questioned him yet.” This voice was deep. It carried power and authority within it.

The red-haired man’s mouth twisted to the side, and he sighed. “Ah, Solomon. I wondered when you would arrive and ruin my fun. Can I at least take a little nibble?”

Solomon chuckled. “That depends on whether he cooperates.” The man emerged from the darkness. Where he walked, the rain split to fall on either side of him. The mist swirled at his feet as if to announce his presence. Slari’s eyes widened. If the red-haired man was a hill, Solomon was a mountain.

Solomon’s head and face were both clearly shaved. He had a firm jaw and a powerful aura about him. His skin was dark, and he was around six and a half feet tall, making him taller than Mi.

He was at the peak of the Lord Class, perhaps beyond it. Even the crazed red-haired man stepped to the side at Solomon’s approach.

Slari fought closing eyes, but quickly realized it was a losing battle. “Mi…” she said, doing all she could to hold on.

Micah

It was unfortunate that he recognized the man standing before him. This was Solomon, leader of the adventurers that Lord Thessus had hired. A cult-hunting guild by the name of Meteor Haven. A small, tight-knit guild with quite the reputation. No one really knew where the name had come from. Some said it was because each of them carried the power of a meteor within, and their guild was a haven for that strength. Others said the name came from a technique or spell the guild practiced. But regardless of the origin of their name, they were dangerous.

And they believed he’d killed a man.

It was time to beg. It was time to really beg.

“Wait! Wait, please! I didn’t kill anyone! I’m Orianne’s fiancé!” He held his hands out in front of him. “I just want to leave the city. I promise that’s all I want.”

He noticed someone beside him, and he flinched. A woman leaned down to stare at him. She had a thin face and a long bluish-gray braid that hung all the way to the back of her knees. “Huh, if Orianne is willing to marry a dreadbeast, then even you might have a chance, Bart,” she said, looking up at the red-haired man.

“Baha I’ll pass! Too skinny for me!” Bart cackled.

Solomon sighed. “He’s a convincing liar. I suppose he has to be, though,” he said, observing Micah.

The girl with the ponytail nodded. “Worms have to find a way to survive when they can’t fight. What do we do with him, boss?” she asked.

Mi glanced at Slari again. She was fading in and out of consciousness. He turned his attention back to Solomon, hoping there was some way out of this.

“You’re coming with us until we sort this out. Now, where are the others?” Solomon asked. “I know there are other dreadbeasts in town. I can sense them.”

At least he wouldn’t be executed on the spot. Micah shook his head in confusion. “Others? What others? It’s just us.”

Solomon sighed. “She tried really hard to protect you. Now, it’s your chance to protect her.”

Mi followed Solomon’s gaze and looked back. A man with an eyepatch and wild brown hair stood over Slari with a spear pointed at her throat. He grinned at Micah. “Well?” the man asked.

“Wait!” Micah called out. His own worry surprised him. She was a dreadbeast. She was one of father’s slaves. Why should he care what happened to her? The thought bothered him. This woman had tried to save him. Sure, she’d thought he was one of her own people, but…she’d risked her life for him. Dreadbeast or not, she’d tried to save him. Had the other dreadbeasts escaped as well? If that was the case, then…it hit him. He knew where the others were going.

It was the same place Slari had been trying to take him.

“The east gate…” he whispered, realizing it aloud.

“What was that?” Solomon asked.

“Mi, no!” Slari called out. “Don’t tell them anything! They’ll kill the others! Please!” she cried out.

The man with the spear pressed it closer to Slari. “If you care what happens to her, you might want to tell the boss, mate.”

I’m sorry. Micah thought. This was for her own good. They didn’t have any power in this situation. All he could do was try to get the two of them out of this. She’d helped him, so now, he’d return the favor. She’d thank him later once she had time to think about it. If she had had time to think about it. There was no guarantee they would actually be spared here. But at least cooperation might earn them a chance.

He cleared his throat. “The other dreadbeasts, there heading toward the east gate. You can catch them if you leave now.”

“What have you done?” Slari sobbed.

Solomon nodded. He looked at the man wielding the spear. “Take her back to the estate and then catch up.”

“Got it.”

He turned to the girl with the ponytail. “Let’s use the city guard to our advantage.”

“Sure thing,” she said.

Bart stepped up beside him. “And what about the tasty-looking beasty?”

Solomon turned to look at Micah. “He comes with us. You all know what to do,” he said.

Mi swallowed as the leader of Meteor Haven finished his declaration.

“We’re taking the east gate.”
