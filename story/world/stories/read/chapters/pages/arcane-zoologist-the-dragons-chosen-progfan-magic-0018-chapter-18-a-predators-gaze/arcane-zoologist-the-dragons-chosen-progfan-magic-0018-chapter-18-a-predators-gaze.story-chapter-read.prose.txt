The glauxis was so far away from Callie that she couldn't even properly distinguish its features before it disappeared again into the forest.

"Carrothead!" Came a hiss from below.

Callie took another glance at the place where she saw the glauxis, her heart beating painfully against her chest. But once it was clear it wouldn't return, Callie finally climbed down.

"What took you so long?" Aurelia whispered angrily.

"I… Sorry…" Callie answered in a daze, unsure of what to say.

"You better be sorry. Let's get back and pass this thing already," Aurelia hissed.

Callie did her best not to think about what she had just witnessed. It finally made sense why this area was so eerily devoid of birdsong. Why she constantly had this feeling of being watched.

But she couldn't let others figure out what she had. Instead, she focused solely on the obstacle course, leading Aurelia through it as quietly as possible while striving not to fall into the muddy waters below. At one point, her leg slipped, and she nearly fell, but Aurelia managed to grab her at the last moment.

"Don't you dare to fall now!" The girl hissed.

Thankfully, Callie managed to keep her focus, and Aurelia was cooperative enough that they both passed the course.

"Good," Mister Blackthorn said. "It appears you have learned your lesson. What are your thoughts about this exercise, Aurelia?"

"Um… That it's important to recognize your strengths and weaknesses?" The girl guessed.

"That too, but this isn't the main point of the lesson. How do you feel about the Deep Forest now?" He prompted.

"I feel that we are more prepared to go into it safely," Aurelia said confidently. "If we continue our training, it'll pose no risk whatsoever."

Mister Blackthorn sighed. "No, no, no. That wasn't the point at all. Calluna, what do you think as someone who has actually gone into the Deep Forest? What was the point of this lesson?"

Callie hadn't actually gone into the Deep Forest, but even so, she felt like she knew the answer. "I-I believe it was to prove how dangerous it actually is… This course had paths laid out for us, and the water wasn't that deep. We were never in real danger but even so nobody passed on the first try. We all had to try multiple times, memorize the path, and get stronger to pass the course. In the Deep Forest, there would be no path, nor would there be anyone to help us in case we fell in."

"Exactly. You can't expect to navigate a swamp successfully on your first try without a trail to help you. The Deep Forest is especially dangerous because it is ever-changing due to the swamp king's presence. It is not forbidden because of some old superstition, but out of necessity to keep people safe. I hope you all will remember that," Mister Blackthorn said, giving Callie and Callen a significant look.

Callie nodded meekly. While she was slightly curious about what kind of creature the swamp king was, she wasn't crazy enough to venture into such a dangerous place. The obstacle course had more than proven to Callie that she would quickly end up at the bottom of a mire. Not to mention that at the moment, her mind was far more preoccupied with the glauxis.

"Okay, you are dismissed. Meet me again for the next lesson in two days. You deserve some rest," Mister Blackthorn said. "Callen, take the girls home."

"Of course," Callen nodded, and all the girls quickly gathered around him.

"Is everything alright, Callie?" Callen asked when she hadn't moved from her spot.

"O-Oh! I'm alright. Just a bit tired," she said, running to his side and grabbing his hand, much to the chagrin of the other girls.

Despite the noisy chatter of girls as they tried to talk with Callen, who tried to remain polite but also not indulge them too much, Callie was fully immersed in her own thoughts.

She had seen glauxis. Not closely, but it certainly couldn't be anything else. Under normal circumstances, Callie would be elated. She had seen a rare arcane beast that she had never encountered before. But in reality, she didn't feel a shred of happiness.

First of all, this was likely the same glauxis that had hurt her mom. While it likely did so out of self-defense, Callie couldn't help but feel a bit of resentment towards it. At the same time, she felt horrible for feeling that way. Mom was the hunter. She had tried to hunt the glauxis. But the fact that Mom almost lost her whole arm wasn't something Callie could brush aside so easily.

Second, if Callie had seen glauxis, then how long would it be before somebody else does? It was clearly wandering very close to the village, and for a while now. Far too close, even for Callie's comfort. It was only a matter of time before somebody saw or worse yet got into a fight with it.

No way could end well. Either glauxis itself would be killed, or somebody from the village might suffer the same fate as Mom. Or even worse. Then the village chief would likely call the dragonbloods.

Callie was terrified of that possibility. She had never met a dragonblood before, but judging from what Granny Ethel had said and the fact that hunted arcane beasts were sold to them, they sounded like incredibly scary people. Callie didn't want them to come to the village. She didn't want to be taken away from her family.

There was a little voice deep within her that whispered that perhaps the dragonbloods could teach her how to use her magic. But being separated from her family wasn't worth it. No, Callie was content to tackle the problem with magic slowly, as much as she was able to.

"Callie, what's wrong? You look very pale," Callen said, bringing Callie out of her thoughts.

Only then did she notice that they were already at home. She had been so deeply immersed in her thoughts that she hadn't even realized that.

"Oh… yes. I'm just relieved that I passed the test," Callie said.

Callen furrowed his eyebrows suspiciously.

"What are you two standing there for?" Dad called from the stove. He was already home, cooking dinner.

"Callie passed the obstacle course," Callen called back.

"Really? Well done!" Dad praised with a smile. "You must be tired. You can rest for the rest of the day."

"Thanks, Dad!" Callie responded and ran into her room, but Callen picked up on what was bothering her.

She felt like she shouldn't tell anyone about the glauxis. While a dragon definitely seemed like the scarier creature in theory, nobody besides Callie had actually seen it. Not to mention that her interaction had been a very positive one. Glauxis, on the other hand… Even Callie had to admit that it was dangerous to people.

I have to do something about it.

Callie shook her head. What could she do? She was just a child. This should be a matter best left to adults. But what could adults do? They didn't understand arcane beasts at all. They just called them monsters and decided to kill them.

No! This was something Callie had to deal with herself. There was no need to be afraid. She could talk to arcane beasts now. All she had to do was talk with the glauxis and warn it of the dangers. Surely, if it knew how dangerous hunters and dragonbloods are, it would leave.

While the thought of meeting a glauxis face-to-face filled Callie with both excitement and fear, the bigger problem was actually finding the time to search for glauxis. Callie was buried in chores from dawn till dusk. She couldn't just disappear for several hours. The best chance likely would come from Granny Ethel when she sent her out to search for herbs. Callie would have to wait until then.

To settle her nerves, Callie picked up a fresh page and wrote down what she knew about the glauxis. She didn't try to draw it yet, simply because she hadn't discerned any meaningful features from a distance.

Name: Glauxis

Appearance: Large lynx-like creature with white fur

Abilities: Poison claws

Notes: Supposed to live in the north, but appeared in our village during the summer. Injured Mom

She stared at the page for a long time, anxious knots curling in her stomach. Callie desperately hoped that she would be able to meet the glauxis before any of the villagers noticed its presence.

The next morning proved to be difficult for Callie. She didn't sleep very well, worried about the glauxis and dragonbloods. She also had to go and draw the water, as much as she could, and care for the chickens in the coop.

"Wiggles, what do I do?" Callie said, grabbing the mireling and hugging it close.

Play?

Wiggles enveloped his tendrils gently around Callie's hand.

"I wish I could, but I have to go to my apprenticeship now… Worst of all, I saw a glauxis! This is horrible!" Callie whimpered.

Gl-Glo–kys? Horrible?

"It's a very big arcane beast. One who hurt my mom. I have to warn them to stay away, but I'm afraid. Not to mention that I don't know when I could ever find the time to search for it."

Glokys bad. Hurt kin.

"Yes, that's not very nice… But my mom is a hunter, so I can't really blame it. I'm sure if I explained everything, it would surely be understood. Just like you!" Callie said, patting the mireling. Wiggles has been on his best behaviour, not bothering any of the villagers. At least as far as Callie knew.

Mireling wobbled with contentment.

Can help?

"No, I don't think so. I just want you to stay away from people and remain safe, okay? Remember not to hurt anyone with acid."

Okay.

After meeting with Wiggles briefly, Callie felt much better. Once she arrived at Granny Ethel's, the solution she had been hoping for appeared the same morning.

"Oh, dear. I'm running low on swamp moss. Tristan, be a dear, and go get me some," Granny Ethel asked.

"What? Not swamp moss. Mom will force me to wash later if I get covered in mud," Tristan complained.

"I'll go!" Callie shot her hand quickly. Granny Ethel and Tristan both looked at her suspiciously, so she hurriedly added. "I-I was in the swamp area yesterday, on the obstacle course. I need to wash up later today anyway."

"Um… thanks," Tristan said, scratching the back of his neck awkwardly. "I-I owe you one."

"I suppose if you're alright with that, go ahead, Callie. Don't stay there too long," Granny Ethel warned.

"I'll hurry back as soon as I can," Callie said. She grabbed the satchel where to put the moss in and hurried away before Granny Ethel realized that Callie didn't head to the swamp simply to help out Tristan.

This was her big chance to speak with the glauxis. Her heart was beating like crazy the whole way. A few adults stopped her on the way, asking where she was heading, after which she explained that she was getting more swamp moss for Granny Ethel.

Was it just her, or were they even more suspicious of Callie than normally? Perhaps the village chief had asked them to keep an eye on her. But in either case, nobody could doubt her when she had a task assigned to her by Granny Ethel.

Callie decided to head to the obstacle course since that was where she had seen the glauxis in the first place. Swamp moss was growing in heaps in that area; however, even after she had collected the satchel full of it, she continued heading deeper, going towards the area where she had seen glauxis.

Where are you? Where are you?

Callie thought hard. She didn't know much about glauxis since nobody really knew much about them, but she did know that they were similar to a lynx. She knew that lynxes liked to perch on tree branches and on fallen trees. With that realization, Callie looked up towards the treetops, searching for any glimpse of white.

Her efforts were rewarded as she saw something white perched high on a pine some distance away from where Callie had seen the glauxis yesterday. Her heartbeat sped up. It was right there. She had actually found it. It felt like her heart would leap out of her mouth.

Callie approached the tree slowly, keeping her eyes up, watching the white lump intently. When a pair of bright yellow eyes gazed back at her, Callie stopped, her breath hitching in her throat.

The glauxis… it wasn't like she had imagined it at all. Sure, there was some resemblance to a lynx. It had huge cat-like paws and a sleek, cat-like body with a long, white tail, which was rapidly moving from side to side. It also had cat-like ears with ear tufts typical of a lynx.

But that was where the resemblance ended. Its head was unmistakably that of an owl with large eyes and a powerful beak. It also had large white wings folded on its back.

For a while, they just stared at each other. The feeling of meeting Glauxis was completely different from when she met Wiggles and Snowglow, and she realized why. This was a predator. An incredibly intelligent predator was studying Callie with its gaze.

One wrong move and Callie could end up as its next meal. But Callie didn't want to fear it, no matter how much her hands trembled. She wanted to help this creature and also to keep her village safe.

So with as much strength as she could muster, she called out of it. "Hello! May we talk?"

The creature's gaze remained unflinching, not a single emotion visible in either its expression or body. When it finally moved, Callie inadvertently flinched.

Talk? With a human?

Its voice was deep and carried unmistakable pride and strength. Callie couldn't help but take a step back.

"Y-Yes… You see… I-I wanted to warn you," Callie stumbled over her words, despite her best efforts to appear calm.

Warn me?

Glauxis shifted his position, as if preparing to jump down. Callie took another step back.

"Yes… I-If you come closer to the village, then the hunters will come after you. Th-They could even call dragonbloods just to kill you," Callie managed to say. It didn't come out quite as she had rehearsed it, but hopefully the glauxis would understand the point.

All of a sudden, glauxis opened his wings, spreading them wide. They were huge. Magnificent. Callie couldn't help but admire their beauty.

You dare to threaten me, human?! You steal my eggs, attack me, and now threaten to kill me. Do not think that I shall let you get away with it!

The glauxis leapt straight in Callie's direction. It was then that she realized that she had messed up.

RUN!
