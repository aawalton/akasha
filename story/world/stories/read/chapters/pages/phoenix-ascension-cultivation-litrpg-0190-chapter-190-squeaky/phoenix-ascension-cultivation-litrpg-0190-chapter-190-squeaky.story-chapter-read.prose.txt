Hwei Badai was jumping through the snow, his heart racing and clothes torn. He was rushing toward the center, as the voice in his head had told him.

Behind him were at least three dozen illusory beasts, all feral and chasing after him with amazing agility.

Cursing under his breath, both the beasts and his laughing master, he channeled a technique into his fingers. Shadows gathered around, the light of the moon being a great boon in this situation.

He turned around and swiped his hand like a sword, sending forward a shadow blade that cut through the horde, but not enough for them to stop chasing.

Many red dots flew toward his forehead, but somehow it seemed that each time he took care of one, two more appeared.

Was it good for his ranking? Yes.

Would his stamina last him enough until he got to the center? Definitely not.

He was lucky enough to figure out a way to walk on the thick snow as it was, but killing these things in a short amount of time and catching a breath? Impossible.

There were only four hours left, and this cycle of running, killing, running, and killing had been going on for the better part of twelve hours.

At first, he didn’t know how he could deal with the beasts while stuck in the snow, but his master had taught him a trick, and he’d quickly gotten the hang of it.

But that also meant that he would attract the attention of even more beasts. While they were fake, the damage they did was one hundred percent real.

Left with no choice, he kept running, dodging and weaving, culling down the ever-growing horde and gathering points.

He didn't know how long it had been since he last ran like that, and to top it all off, his master refused to help.

‘It’s going to be a great training opportunity for you.’ My ass!

He was bitter, but also a little excited. If he could get a lot of points, then he might have a hope of breaking through into the top five. He didn’t know what the meaning of the points was, but he didn’t really care either. The more the merrier.

He had five large clusters of points on his forehead, only growing larger and larger the more beasts that he killed. Soon, they would all need to shrink to make room for more points.

‘Kid, look that way!’ His master yelled out in his mind and pointed in a direction, and Hwei Badai saw that it was a cave.

Won’t I just get cornered?

‘Trust this old man, fool. Go!’ Left with no other choice, Hwei Badai switched directions and rushed toward the cave. It was big enough for three grown men to stand side by side, and on their shoulders.

He reached it quickly, but the beasts were hot on his tail.

‘Erect a wall, quick!’

Hwei Badai did as he was told and raised a wall of shadows from the ground, covering up the entrance.

There was a loud banging almost immediately, with beasts trying and almost succeeding to rip it apart. He had to keep it together with his own Qi, which was rapidly depleting.

‘Use that thing I taught you recently.’

But my Qi reserves…

‘You either do it or you die kid, ke ke ke.’

Master… Fine!

Seeing as there was no room for negotiation, Hwei Badai threw all caution to the wind and performed a quick series of hand signs, shadows covering all around him. The darkness inside the cave had gotten sucked out, and all gathered toward him.

With one final sign, he shot it all toward the wall, blending together with it.

“Shadow Technique Third Rank, Devouring Shadow!” He yelled out as the wall turned into a giant, beastly mouth on the outside. The beasts didn’t care, not knowing fear, but what happened next made even these illusory beings afraid.

The shadow maw stretched in all directions, encompassing the entire horde, not leaving one outside its range.

Then, with a forceful movement, the maw snapped shut in less than a second, all of the beasts inside becoming nothing more than countless fragments of light that scattered in the air.

A horde of a hundred and more beasts, gone in the blink of an eye.

The shadowy maw, and by consequence, the wall disappeared.

Behind lay Hwei Badai, his illusory mask having vanished after running out of Qi.

The scarred face of a young man was revealed, deep wounds covering it.

A cloud of red dots rushed toward his forehead, gathering into multiple clusters that grew and shrank until finally, they all became a small dot on his forehead.

Of course, he wasn’t awake to see that, as the mental pressure and lack of Qi had made him collapse.

An illusory body flew out from his ring, the figure of an old man forming. His back was hunched, his beard was long, and his hair was almost gone. There were only a few strands remaining.

He looked at the young man collapsed on the cave floor and smiled softly to himself. “You did well, child. Now allow this old man to look over you and help you recover.”

The old man flicked his sleeve and pulled out a red pill, which he then gently placed in Hwei Badai’s mouth.

“This should help you recover in time.”

He looked toward the depths inside the cave and sighed, shaking his head. “If only you had enough strength… and no limit.”
