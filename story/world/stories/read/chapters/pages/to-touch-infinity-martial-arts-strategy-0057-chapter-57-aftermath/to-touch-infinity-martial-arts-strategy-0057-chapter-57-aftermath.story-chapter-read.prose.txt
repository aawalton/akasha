Mu-cheon bent down and scooped up a fallen Meng-shan spear, driving the iron point upwards through the neck of Wi Mo’s severed head. He hoisted the grisly trophy high into the afternoon sky, the sun highlighting the preserved expression of shock on the strategist’s face.

Taking a deep breath, the Young Master unsealed his Tian Tu point, letting his voice boom out across the clearing.

“YOUR COMMANDER IS DEAD!” he roared. “THE TAI-SHAN STAND VICTORIOUS!”

A few paces away, the last of the bleeding oxen finally shuddered, its legs buckling as it collapsed onto the road.

Behind the fallen beast, the remainder of the Meng-shan spear column looked on in horror. Through the haze of settling dust and drifting smoke, they gazed out on a nightmare: their vanguard was being butchered in the field, their prized siege engine had been reduced to kindling, and the Tai-shan Young Master was holding the dripping, grimacing head of their chief strategist, high against the sun.

The retreat started slowly. It began with three spearmen exchanging a quiet glance, backing away slowly towards the spear column. Then it was a half dozen, who were walking quickly. Then it was ten, who were running. A ripple of fear spread through the Meng-shan column like an infectious disease as the fleeing soldiers approached their already broken front ranks, dissolving into a disorganized, chaotic rout as hundreds of men threw down their shields and fled back towards the south.

Trapped inside the maze of wooden abatis, a few surviving Meng-shan soldiers looked around at the bodies surrounding them. One by one, they dropped their weapons and raised their empty hands in surrender.

Mu-cheon rode towards them, raising an open palm. “Hold your fire!” his voice boomed over the cheers, aimed at the friendly archers. “Round up the survivors! And bind their wrists.”

Tai-shan disciples quickly surrounded the remaining spearmen, efficiently securing the prisoners and herding them back towards the staging area as prisoners of war.

With the enemy soldiers secured, Mu-cheon resumed riding back towards the gatehouse, the rest of the cavalrymen trailing behind him like an adoring guard detail. Spotting Chae-ha in front of the threshold, he swung his leg over the side of his saddle and dismounted, jogging eagerly towards her.

“Watch your step, senior brother,” Chae-ha called out, gesturing towards the ground.

Mu-cheon paused mid-stride, his raised boot hovering inches above the dirt. He carefully pulled his foot back, deciding to walk in a large circle to reach her, instead of a straight line. He could have sworn he heard her chuckle, but her composed expression betrayed no hint of it.

“Congratulations on a decisive victory, Young Master.”

Mu-cheon offered her a broad grin. “It’s your victory more than mine, little sister. You drew up the plans for everything. You were the architect…I was more of a construction worker.”

Chae-ha’s left eyebrow arched slightly as she looked at the severed head mounted on the wooden pike, still in his hand.

Mu-cheon caught her glance and suddenly felt sheepish. He gave an awkward chuckle, hurriedly shifting the long spear behind his back in a clumsy attempt to hide the grisly trophy. It didn’t work very well.

A subtle glimmer of amusement crossed the young Inspector’s face as she gestured towards the ramparts, where dozens of soldiers were raising their fists in the air and shouting. Then she pointed to the cavalrymen standing nearby, who were staring at him with open reverence.

“I don’t think they see you as a construction worker, senior brother.”

Mu-cheon followed the angle of her finger and looked up at the cheering men, and then across at his comrades, feeling a deep swell of pride as he did so. He looked back at Chae-ha, another grin spreading across his face. The young girl met his gaze with an eerie calm.

“Now there’s only one thing left to do, Young Master.”

“Oh? What’s that, little sister?”

Chae-ha gestured around expansively, before pointing to the severed head floating above his shoulder, a small smile on her lips.

“I think you already know. It’s time to celebrate! Every good funeral ends with a feast.”

That night, although darkness had fallen over the valley, no one was sleeping. A dozen bonfires crackled in various places throughout the camp, casting a warm orange glow over tables loaded with roasted meats, flatbread, and jug after jug of celebratory rice wine. The grueling task of clearing the rubble, hauling away shattered timber, and burying the enemy dead could wait until morning.

Commander Shin sat at one of the tables, taking a long chug of wine from his earthen mug. He still couldn’t quite bring himself to believe it. They hadn’t just held the gatehouse – they had routed the Meng-shan. For months, this campaign had been a slow, constant loss of ground. Now, the heady sensation of victory felt far more intoxicating than the liquor in his cup.

His gaze drifted across the firelight to where Kang Mu-cheon sat surrounded by a dozen of their men. The Inner Disciple was gobbling down a large chunk of spit-roasted ox meat, after which he slammed his mug onto the timber table and bellowed with laughter. The Young Master ate with the men, drank with the men, cursed with the men, and in general fit right in amongst them. It was as if he had been born in a frontier garrison.

It was clear the disciples adored him, and why wouldn’t they? He had saved half of their lives directly over the past few weeks, and today he had galloped into a wall of spears and returned with the enemy commander’s head on a pike. Elder Eom Tae-seok was a legend, but the scarred veteran was cold and distant. Mu-cheon was the kind of hero these soldiers had never had: someone they could actually sit with, talk to, and who made them feel at ease.

His gaze continued drifting, and as it did, Shin’s mood darkened.

The contrast was jarring. Sitting mostly alone at a smaller table on the periphery, completely removed from the noise, was Chae-ha. She sat alone in her jade-green robes, accompanied only by Sergeant Noh Gu-shim and his three silent guards, sitting across from her. She looked to have barely touched the food on her small wooden plate.

Shin stared back down at his mug, a tangled knot of resentment and grudging respect warring for space in his gut. He didn’t know how he was supposed to feel about her. The girl had knocked him unconscious, made him complicit in her dangerous schemes, usurped his authority, and orchestrated the quiet death of a senior officer. She was manipulative, unnervingly sharp, and frankly terrifying. He didn’t think he could ever truly forgive her for the things she had done.

And yet, as he looked at the cheering soldiers around the fires, he couldn’t avoid acknowledging the truth. Without her, every man in this camp would probably be dead. Whatever methods she had used, it was clear now that everything she had done was for the benefit of the sect.

Sighing loudly, Commander Shin wiped his mouth with the back of his hand. He walked over to a nearby cask, filled a second earthen mug to the brim with amber wine, and carried both drinks across the yard.

He stopped at Chae-ha’s table, setting one mug down in front of her with a thunk.

She looked up at him in askance, her normally unflappable demeanor showing a measure of surprise.

“Wine. It’s for getting drunk,” Shin grunted, meeting her eyes for a brief moment.

He raised his own mug to his lips, drained every drop within it in three long gulps, and then tossed the empty vessel towards a camp servant.

“Hey!” Shin called out to the man, as he plopped down next to Chae-ha. “Bring me a refill! And, uh…keep them coming.”

Three weeks later, the Deer Valley mine was back under Tai-shan control.

The entrenched Meng-shan garrison hadn’t even bothered to stay and put up a fight. The moment they realized they had been completely abandoned by their main column, the guards had fled their posts rather than stand and face down the impossible odds that were sure to bear down on them. The Tai-shan army had arrived to find the place already deserted.

Now, dozens of li further down the road and past the junction, a new construction project was underway.

Chae-ha stood atop a low ridge beside Mu-cheon, Elder Eom, and Commander Shin, watching teams of disciples drive wooden stakes into the earth to mark the foundations of a second gatehouse. They were breaking new ground on a second set of fortifications, and using it to secure their territorial gains.

Eventually, an Imperial inspector would probably wander down this road and ask uncomfortable questions about why a martial sect was raising fortified gatehouses across public roads. It didn’t really matter, though. The iron and timber they were harvesting from the reclaimed valley would yield more than enough silver to pay for the bribe. And if the bureaucrat somehow defied the odds and proved to be an honest man...well, there would be other ways of dealing with him. For now, the structures were necessary. Once the Meng-shan were soundly defeated, they could always tear the walls down and issue a polite apology to the prefecture.

Chae-ha looked out over the buzzing construction camp, pleased with the progress that had been made.

One of her three blood debts had been settled: her father had gotten his funeral, and his honor was restored. But two still remained. Every stone laid here, every li of territory she reclaimed for Mt. Tai, was just a stepping stone on the path to paying off the second.

Her gaze shifted sideways to Mu-cheon, who was currently arguing good-naturedly with Tae-seok over where the kitchens should go. Her mind drifted to his upcoming arranged marriage to Cheok Yeong-ji, the heiress of the Cheok family. A small, unfamiliar twinge pulsed in her chest. It lasted for only a moment, and then…it was gone.

Chae-ha paused, confused by the sensation, and asked herself if anything had changed about her feelings towards him. She searched her heart, but no matter how closely she looked, she kept coming back to the same conclusion. She felt a profound, overwhelming gratitude towards Mu-cheon – a debt she might never be able to fully repay. He had supported her, trusted her, protected her, and given her the authority to act.

Looking at him now, she could clearly see the future that was waiting for him: a long, prosperous tenure as a revered Sect Leader, happily married, with a cadre of energetic children clinging to his muscular ankles. All while he laughed happily beneath the mountain sky.

But when she turned her mind to herself, no matter how hard she tried, she couldn’t see a future there. There was no quiet home waiting for her at the end of this road. No peaceful family life with cherished loved ones. No rewarding, joyful retirement. All she saw at the end of the path she was on was an enormous chasm, made of unfulfilled obligations, which would require an entire lifetime for her to try and fill.

Shrugging the uncomfortable thought away, Chae-ha looked back down at the paper plans in her hands. Her fingers traced the lines of the construction blueprints, outlining the positions for the new fortifications. As she did, a faint smile grew on her face.

Dark thoughts aside, for once – it seemed like everything was going according to plan.
