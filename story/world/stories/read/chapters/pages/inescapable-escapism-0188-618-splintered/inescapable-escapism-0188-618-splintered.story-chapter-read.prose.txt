And then she literally ripped her face off, and there was another one underneath!!! the text from Phoebe read.

My eyebrows pulled together as I read her message before typing a response.

What??? Like… the same face or another?

I wasn’t sure why someone would tear their face off and have an identical copy underneath, but it was hard to tell with the films Phoebe watched. Sometimes, the plot made no sense to me, but Phoebe always loved them, and I liked hearing her talk about them.

The mascara wand froze halfway to my face as my phone buzzed again, and I slipped it back into the tube before reading the message.

A different face! Do you remember that girl I mentioned who kept just randomly being in the background for like the first hour? Phoebe had written.

Yeah?

I didn’t even have time to pick up my mascara again before Phoebe began typing. A soft snort escaped me as I lowered my hand to stroke Rex’s head and waited for her response.

So it turns out it was her the whole time. I don’t know if she was stalking her the whole time, but at some point, she killed her and just took her place! She skinned her and was wearing it. There was this flashback where they actually showed it happening!

A shudder slipped down my spine. I knew Phoebe loved that kind of film, but I was so glad she had watched it without me. I didn’t mind horror movies too much. Normally, they were pretty fun when we watched them together, but I hated the idea of what she had just described. It made my skin crawl.

That’s horrible, I replied before putting my phone down and finally finishing my mascara whilst waiting for Phoebe to respond.

I know! It was awesome! I think the director has another film coming out in a couple of months. Wanna go watch it?

Maybe, I typed. Depends how bad the trailer is. I’m not sure I can watch something like that without hiding behind a blanket.

I bit my lip, reading the message again to make sure it was okay before hitting send and grabbing my lip balm. I applied it quickly, my eyes flicking towards my phone again as it buzzed multiple times.

Here! Phoebe said, sending me a link to the trailer. I think it looks great! I don’t thinkkkkk it looks as gory as this one was, but the trailer for this one didn’t look too bad either, so I don’t know.

I’ll have to watch it later, I told Phoebe. I have to go out in a minute!

She began typing straight away.

Oh my goddddd! Of course!!! Have fun at Duncans!!

My lips twitched into a smile, and my gaze darted towards my reflection, scrutinising it closely before typing a response to Phoebe.

Thankssss! Text you later!

Yes! I can’t wait to hear all about it, she wrote, following the message up with some emojis.

Blood rushed into my cheeks, and I bit my lip, but I didn’t reply to Phoebe. I wasn’t sure what to say to her, and I was too nervous about what was going to happen when I got to Duncan’s house.

His parents were home, and I hadn’t been there whilst they were around since we were much younger. I doubted they even remembered me, and I knew things would be different now. His parents were much more relaxed than mine were, but I still wasn’t sure whether they’d be okay with us being in his room with the door shut or if their presence in the house would make things weird. I hoped it wouldn’t, but I wasn’t sure, and that made me anxious.

But I said I’d get to his at about eleven, and it was already half past ten. It didn’t take that long to get there, only ten minutes or so, but I didn’t want to be late, so I had to leave. I was ready. My makeup was finished, and I’d already done my hair, so all I needed to do was take Rex out. Then, I could go.

My eyes landed on Rex, who was lying next to me, and I reached down to stroke him as guilt simmered in the pit of my stomach. I was planning on leaving him at home when I went out, and I felt awful about that. I wasn’t going to be gone for too long, only a few hours really. It was shorter than my normal school day, and that was why I wasn’t taking him. I needed to be certain that he’d be okay at home for that long before I went back. Then, if it went badly, I’d know that I needed to find a way to come home at lunchtime to let him out and play with him.

Mom had said it was fine, though. I’d asked her earlier when I went down to feed Rex, and she barely even looked up from her phone, but she’d said she’d look after him when I was out. She’d sighed, making it seem as though she wasn’t particularly happy about it, but I’d seen the way she smiled as she stroked him. I think she was kind of looking forward to it. She did say before the shelter called about Rex that she missed having a dog in the house, so maybe it would be fine.

“I won’t be too long,” I whispered to Rex, who stared up at me with wide eyes. “I’m just going to go out to see a… friend, and then I’ll be back. It’ll be a few hours. Three or four, and Mom will look after you until then, okay?”

Rex didn’t reply. Obviously. He couldn’t, but I hoped he understood and didn’t think I was leaving him forever.

The thought sent pain through my chest as my heart began to break, but I swallowed, pushing it aside. I took a deep breath, shooting my reflection one more look, before deleting my texts from Phoebe and standing.

“Okay, Rexie. Let’s go to the toilet, and then I’ll go out.”

He definitely understood that, though. He was on his feet immediately, and I smiled at him before slinging my backpack onto my back and grabbing his bed. He didn’t really use it, apart from on the first night. Since then, he’d mostly just slept at the bottom of my bed, and I didn’t mind. He was so warm, and it was nice having him there, but I wasn’t sure where he’d sleep whilst I was out. I’d leave my bedroom door open, in case he wanted to sleep there, but I wanted him to have more options.

Guilt continued to burn in my heart as I led Rex downstairs, dropping his bed in my lounge before opening the French windows. He ran out into the garden, his nose twitching as he sniffed the air before racing towards the back fence. Slowly, he patrolled the length of it, his head close to the ground, before seemingly giving up on whatever he could smell and turning away.

I fiddled with the strap of my bag as I watched him, anxiety and guilt still pulling at me. I was sure he’d be okay with Mom, but I was still worried. I was pretty sure that no matter what happened, I would be. He could be completely fine, Mom could tell me she had a wonderful time looking after him, and I’d still be on edge the next time I left Rex.

He followed the direction that I pointed in, moving towards his bed and sniffing it before turning to face me, and another shard of guilt speared my heart. Nothing had really caused it, but just looking at him made me feel bad. I never wanted to be apart from him.

“Okay, let’s go make sure you have enough water, and then I’ll go speak to Mom,” I told him.

I didn’t really need to be narrating what I was doing. I knew that, but I couldn’t stop myself. For some reason, having Rex around just made me want to, and I had to be careful. I was worried I’d slip up when I was at Duncan’s or at school or something, and everyone would think I was weird. They probably already did, but that would just make it worse.

Rex’s bowl still had some water left in it, but I refilled it anyway. It had been there since I’d got him his breakfast, and he’d barely touched it, but the moment I place the fresh water back on the floor, he began to drink deeply.

“Good boy,” I told him again, stroking his back as he continued to drink.

“Hey,” I called. “I’m going out now.”

“Where are you going?” she asked.

She already knew. When I asked her to look after Rex, I’d told her I was going to Phoebe’s house. That was a lie, obviously, but Mom didn’t know that, and I hoped she’d never find out.

“Phoebe’s,” I said. “I think we might go out to watch a movie or something, but I’m not sure yet.”

Mom pursed her lips as Rex trotted up behind me before sitting, his body leaning against my leg.

“Didn’t you see her yesterday?” Mom asked.

A hint of nerves rose within me, but I forced my expression to remain unchanged as I held her gaze. Part of me was worried that she had seen through my lie, but the other part was convinced she had not.

“Yes.”

Something flashed across Mom’s face too quickly for me to process, and her lip curled.

“And you’re seeing her again today?” she asked. “Do you not have any other friends?”

The judgment in her words was clear. It was obvious she wanted her question to sting, but I didn’t feel any shame, and I refused to let her get a rise out of me. I did have another friend. I was going to see him, but I couldn’t exactly say that to Mom. I wasn’t stupid enough to do that. Then, she’d never let me go.

“Phoebe’s my best friend.”

“Of course she is,” she said with a sneer. “And what an excellent choice you made there.”

She looked away as she shook her head, her expression disgusted, and I hesitated. Indecision pulled at me, forcing me to speak again.

“Are you sure it’s okay for you to look after Rex today?” I asked. “I’m sure Phoebe’s mom won’t mind if I bring him again. He was really well-behaved when we slept over.”

“I already said it was fine.”

She had, but that was before. She had seemed in a better mood then, and the way she was acting concerned me. She was on the verge of anger, and I knew she wouldn’t take it out on Rex. She’d never hurt an animal, but I was worried she might have changed her mind.

“I know—” I started, but Mom interrupted me.

“I grew up with dogs,” she said, her tone becoming dangerously icy. “I am more than capable of looking after this one, but if you don’t believe me, fine. Why don’t you take him with you?”

That was a trick question, and I knew it. It felt like a threat, and I was suddenly terrified that if I walked out the door with Rex, he would never be allowed back in. Mom already loved Rex. I was pretty sure she did, anyway, but I had no doubt she would take him back to the shelter to prove her point. Then, I’d never see him again.

Fear crackled within me as I forced myself to take a deep breath.

“Well, it’s far too late for that.”

She seemed annoyed at me but not particularly angry, and that was good. If she had been angry, I would have been more worried about leaving Rex with her, but annoyed was fine. She was pretty much always annoyed at something. Normally, it was either Dad or me.
