Stella watched the tiny shuttle shoot fire in a state of wonder. “It’s not just flight?” she whispered.

“No, you can recreate all physical technology with arrays,” Hayden said, “often better than your greatest advancements. Arrays can create and purify water—heat objects without emissions. There are endless use cases—and it could all be yours.” He motioned to her chair.

Stella sat, surprised she was speaking to him without her skin crawling. As much as she loathed it, his introduction worked.

“Why are you offering this to me? Because there’s an immediate concern only I can handle?”

“Oh, no.” Hayden poured wine without touching the bottle, using telekinesis with his Clear Line tendrils to fill two glasses. “With the power I wield here, you would yield to my request whether you wanted to or not. What I want is a patron who will leave me alone.”

Stella’s eyes moved from the wine back to Hayden, cooling as they moved. “Elaborate.”

“My ambitions are focused exclusively on rifting and starting an academy,” he said. “To that end, I will need military protection during my interstellar travel, and the backing to start my own celestial.”

“You want to start a government? An intergalactic government?”

“That’s what I said, yes.”

“That’s ridiculous.”

“How so?”

“Because without a military, you’ll need our aid for everything—which means abiding by our laws.”

“That’s incorrect. My school will host the Vast Four. They will seek to protect their royalty by their own means.”

“I see. So you want to bring our enemies' militaries into our expanse?”

“Unless you want to bring yours into Morine’s Domain.”

Stella fell silent.

“I am an emperor, Ms. Ingsley. I’ve ruled empires far larger than yours. I have more experience than you can fathom, and strength potential beyond your wildest dreams. I have enough power to transcend space and time, and have made preparations that will wreak catastrophic damage on anyone who kills me or bars my path. I suggest you take my ambitions seriously.”

Stella felt icy sweat run down her spine. The way he spoke wasn’t something that even Halkan Morine—the sovereign who ruled the largest and most militarily powerful galactic empire in the universe—could replicate. Halkan sounded like a childish ruler in comparison—someone with a need for attention and validation. Hayden was cold and refined—confidence absolute.

It felt ridiculous to reach that conclusion from a single statement, but she was confident in her appraisal. His words had a feeling, a vibe—something that ached in the marrow of her bones.

“If you are an emperor, wouldn’t it be natural for you to steal my territory?” she asked.

“If it were my ambition, yes. I’d marry into the Morine Domain—and then take over the Golden Belt. Thankfully for you, I’m returning to my universe some time within the next five years. My only ambition here is to spread my technology and legacy—hence the academy.”

Stella eyed his body curiously. “So you’re an alien?”

“Not exactly. I was born in this universe—in this body—but I left so long ago that I might as well be an alien.”

She searched for indications of him lying, but his face didn’t betray his thoughts or emotions. “You’ll need more than threats to obtain my patronage,” she prefaced. “That said, I’ll hear your request, as a sign of future negotiation. Is that permissible?”

“It is.”

“Then, let’s hear it.”

Hayden gave his request, and she resented Ritrix for not disclosing the plan in advance. There would be universal implications to what he was requesting.

“I’ll do it—but I can’t contain the fallout,” she warned. “It will become a political nightmare.”

“I’ll handle the aftermath,” he said.

“Remember you said that. Because I haven’t become your patron yet.”

“Yet,” Hayden repeated, drinking his wine. “I’d prepare for it.”

Stella raised her eyebrows mockingly. “I look forward to you making me.”

Hayden smiled for the first time. “To future partners.” He lifted his glass, and she had an uncomfortable premonition. The way he spoke made it seem as though his goals were personal rather than political. He was courting her—not the Golden Belt. She didn’t understand it—but she didn’t like it. It made her feel like a wolf scavenging in a bear den.

Ritrix was right—Hayden Reskco was a dangerous individual. He was unreadable, and his skill and ambitions were unknown. Even his request was insane. She didn’t know what was going to happen, but she knew one thing: if Hayden didn’t die in that rift, he was going to throw the entire universe into a state of chaos.

Things were changing, and she had no choice but to kill him or comply. Come tomorrow, the rift would open—and the true games would begin.

Hayden woke the next morning to the sound of rain slapping against his window. The day of the rift’s opening had finally come. He quickly showered and donned a cloak, entering the storm.

A guard stopped him on his way out. “Where are you going?”

“To train,” Hayden said.

“We’ll accompany you.” He offered Hayden his umbrella.

“There’s no need.” Hayden lifted his palm to show that a barrier of aura was preventing the rain from touching him. It wasn’t just that. Water separated underneath his boots, creating a surreal effect.

Today was the day he would stop hiding.

Ten minutes later, he separated from his guards and entered the gym where the Iska sisters trained in. Both were already inside, wringing out their hair with miserable expressions.

“How are you so dry?” Kayline whined as she twisted her hair. “There’s a hurricane outside.”

“I blocked the water,” Hayden said, glancing at Lina. “Did you get enough sleep?”

“Of course not,” Lina complained. “It’s four a.m. It doesn’t matter when you go to sleep, you’re five hours too few at this hour.”

Hayden smiled. “Since you have the energy to complain, it seems you’ve gotten enough.”

“I don’t think that’s how this works,” Kayline said. “Can we at least go home and change? We’re going to get sick if we stay wet like this.”

Hayden shot clear lines at the sisters. The lines touched the two before they could dodge—and water exploded from their hair.

“W-What the hell was that?” Lina cried.

“Hold still,” Hayden said. The lines touched their clothing, and the garments drained in waterfalls, splashing over the tiles.

“I forgot you could do this,” Kayline said, blushing as he dried Lina. “How does that even work?”

“Your hair and garments are made of fibers; I filled the fibers with aura, so they no longer had space for water.” He casually sat at their fold-out table. “Now sit. There’s not much time.”

“Until what?” Kayline asked as she sat.

“The rift opens. It could open any moment now. I don’t know when. I was asleep when it happened, so I’m not sure. That’s why we need to prepare.”

“And you didn’t tell us?” Lina demanded.

Hayden turned to her slowly. She swallowed and fell silent. He then began. “The rift is opening. When it does—”

A woman screamed, “Hayden!” outside the gym’s doors.

He closed his eyes. “Of course it’d happen right now.”

Kayline looked between him and the door. “Is that Mayle?”

“Open the door!” Mayle yelled, testing the door and finding it locked. Then, she mumbled, “Why am I even asking?”

The door beeped and opened, allowing her to stride inside.

“Can I help you, Mayle?” Hayden asked.

“Can you help me? A massive rift’s opening above Junolyx—during a conference with intergalactic leaders—and you’re going to ask me if you can help me?” Mayle turned and cupped her face when she saw Kayline and Lina. “Oh, great, you’re with the Iskas.”

“Is there a problem with that?” Kayline asked.

Mayle sighed. “Yes, there’s a serious problem.”

The guards outside heard Kayline’s voice and filed into the room.

“Kernant?” Kayline muttered when she saw her father’s guard walk in with a dozen rifters in Golden Belt guard attire. She noted their red-lit bracelets. The group was recording. “What’s going on?” she asked.

“We’re here to pick you up,” Kernant replied.

“Are you taking us to the rift?”

“No.”

Lina turned to Mayle. “What’s going on?”

Mayle sighed. “Your father’s withdrawing you from the ceremony.”
