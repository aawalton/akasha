A grin spread across Ruben's face at the chance of getting new skills. Of course, this was an opportunity they had all been waiting for, as getting new skills would considerably solve a lot of their problems at the moment, which meant that getting a merchant was a priority to improve their chances of survival.

It was apparent that they had all gotten the same system notification as he had, which meant they weren't too surprised, but he could still sense the eagerness radiating off them. Be that as it may, they all knew the decision of the merchant rested on Nathan's shoulder.

In fact, with the money he had received from the dying ogre, his personal savings, and the estimated value of his Tora mine, Nathan now felt he was financially stable at this point. At least enough to get the best of things for his stronghold and himself.

“Before we get a merchant, I think it is in our best interest to discuss what we actually need for Blood Rock,” Nathan said, “ So, the floor is open? What do you think we need?”

“A burger joint,” Ruben said.

“Yeah, great.” Tasha rolled her eyes.

Ruben perked up, his face sporting a look of mock hurt. “I’m serious. Look, we can take over the world and become the apocalypse's number one fast food joint, think of all the money we could rake in…” The berserker paused and put a finger to his chin. “We can even call it the Blood Burger.”

“That’s… actually not a bad idea, Ruben.” Ciara nodded. “But it’s an apocalypse, we don’t have any potatoes or rice…”

“Or oil,” Tasha interrupted.

“Guys, we’re trying to survive an apocalypse, not start a business to pay for college.” Nathan chuckled. “So, let's be serious for a minute.”

The trio nodded in agreement, and to no one’s surprise, Ruben was the first to speak. “We for sure need an infirmary.”

Soft nods accompanied with chuckles left the lips of the ladies at the words of the chatty berserker, and Nathan couldn’t help but agree with them.

The ogres had battered them, and without the luxury of fighting monsters to get to the next level, drinking health potions or receiving healing from a priest, the remaining members of his ‘blood army’ were going to have to deal with their injuries by themselves.

“You’ll probably need a lot more cabins if you are planning to bring more people to Blood Rock,” Ciara said, her emerald eyes fixed on Nathan’s blue ones. “Then, you’ll also need a hall, throne Room, or more likely a war room if you plan to address people based on the scenario. I know you didn’t ask, but if you want my opinion, you should get all three or at least two of those.”

“I’ll think on it.” Nathan nodded to the mage. “Anything else?”

“You could add a watch tower at all corners of Blood Rock. And if you wanted, you could consider improving the walls of the stronghold to those built by the system.”

“That… is a good idea,” Nathan said.

“Yeah, uh, you can also look into expanding your stronghold. I guess it costs a few coins per square meter, but yeah, you should also consider expanding your stronghold…uh, if you want to.” Ruben said.

Nathan leaned forward, intrigued. “How am I supposed to do that?”

“Well, it's simple. You just prompt the system, ‘expand stronghold’, and you'll be given a price per square meter to expand your stronghold.” Ruben said. “You should know that there is a bit of restriction on that front as the system will not encroach or go around any dungeon, tower, or stronghold in the way, but that can be handled by clearing any dungeon, tower, or stronghold in the way.”

“Noted. Thanks, those are all great suggestions.” Nathan said.

The ideas the squad had given him were the type that would undoubtedly vastly improve Blood–Rock if he implemented them. And to be frank, Ruben’s was probably the best one he had heard so far.

Expanding his stronghold meant much more space. And even apart from the expansion itself, Tasha’s words reverberated within his head.

The wall he had built himself, alongside the ones that the imps had built when he had first come across them, now seemed mundane compared to the ones he had seen at the Demon Prince Kabash's stronghold, which had been made of concrete with an actual gate to boot.

So by the look of things, Tasha was right; it was time to upgrade the walls, as well as the defenses of his stronghold, which included the watch towers, traps, and defensive techniques. “Anything else?”

“I wish we could get a pool, or a hot spring… for rehabilitation purposes,” Ruben said.

“I’ll think about it,” Nathan said.

He had to agree that something like that would definitely come in handy, even if it was just for a bit of relaxation. It’s not like I want my stronghold to be a prison or a war camp.

Blood-Rock was supposed to be a place for the helpless to feel safe and free. At least that had been his line of thought when he’d created it, but reality had a way of warping dreams, and so he found himself preparing his stronghold to be war-ready rather than a utopia in an apocalypse.

And it didn't look like he was anywhere close to achieving the utopia thing, but so far, so good.

In the end, it seemed like Nathan had his priorities set. Purchasing defensive techniques and upgrading the defenses of his stronghold were the number one priorities, followed by more cabins for the new people who were coming to Bloodrock to stay. Then he’d have to build an infirmary and a kitchen, and expand his stronghold to accommodate more people.

Yeah, that has to be it.

Nathan couldn't help but look around his cabin. Apart from the swords, apart from the trophies they had collected from killing the dwarf that had been in charge of the mine, his cabin was still as bare as the day that he had used the system to create it. There was nothing, no sofa, no couch, no bed.

He had to find out if an improved version of the cabins came with more utilities that he could benefit from.

“Oh, you should probably keep some space for a forge in case you manage to snag one of the blacksmiths in our world,” Ciara said, interrupting his thoughts.

“Sure,” Nathan said. He’d almost forgotten about the odd classes the system gave out.

“All right, I believe that is all about that,” Nathan said as he pushed off the cabin floor and went out to the porch to stare at the empty space in his stronghold.

Blood-Rock purchase buildings.

A blue screen appeared in his field of vision as the system responded to his prompt. Nathan let his eyes rake through his options, and he couldn’t help but notice the fact that a lot of buildings were now not grayed out, compared to the first time he’d used the system to purchase a building.

Shaking off the idle thought, he focused on one of the newer ungrayed options, an E-ranked Cabin. It seemed to have a bed-like structure and curtains, and the bathroom seemed to be equipped with an actual shower, a faucet, and all the other things his F-ranked cabin had.

Huh, I thought it’d have more rooms.

The E graded cabin seemed to have the same number of rooms as his F graded one, but he had to admit that the E graded cabin looked bigger if the mental projection he was seeing was to be believed.

Aside from that, the E graded cabin cost 25, 450 coins per unit, not much in the grand scheme of things as far as Nathan's pockets was concerned. Still, he hesitated to purchase the cabin.

The maths didn’t make too much sense to him. Especially since he could see the option of a barracks for 300,000 Ra’hal coins that could hold 2,000 people. It demanded a lot of space, and it seemed to be made entirely of evenly spaced bunk beds, with two other sections: the kitchens and bathrooms.

Nathan was two-minded on making a choice, because on one hand, the barracks didn’t fit with the aesthetic of the vision he had for Blood Rock, and on the other hand, the barracks would be able to accommodate significantly more people and, by extension, save the lives of a lot more people.

Dreams meet Reality.

In the end, the number of people that he could accommodate with 300,000 Ra’ahal coins worth of cabins compared to a single barrack was way too low for him to go for the former.

Maybe I don’t have to choose.

Nathan could afford to do both. 300,000 Ra’ahal coins worth of cabins came out to roughly about eleven cabins with some change left. If he added the cost of a barrack, he’d end up spending about 600,000 on accommodations.

With his mind made up, Nathan bought 11 E-graded cabins and placed them around the original one. He also purchased an E-graded Barrack for 300,000 Ra’ahal coins and placed it close to the southern walls of the stronghold.

Nathan still planned to expand his stronghold, so he supposed that it didn’t necessarily matter that the Barrack was close to the southern wall at the moment.

The berserker couldn’t help but smile at his newly purchased buildings. It seemed like this place would be evolving from a hermit's hole to sort of an island resort when he was done with it.

Well, as close as anyone could get to that in a fucking apocalypse.
