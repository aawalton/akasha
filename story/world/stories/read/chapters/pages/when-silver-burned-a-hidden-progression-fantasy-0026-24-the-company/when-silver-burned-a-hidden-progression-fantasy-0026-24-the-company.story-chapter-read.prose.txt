"Hold!" wind carried Brownweather's shout across the yard.

It was the second day of Kalsin’s training, which was, according to the plump woman who'd introduced herself as Fals, the twenty-fourth day of their group as a whole. Kalsin was standing shoulder to shoulder with Crawl, Fals, and Shutter—a quiet, dark-eyed fellow—holding a shield formation mirrored by several clusters of recruits across the training field. The objective was quite straightforward; four defenders, one pusher. The four had to defend against a single man fitted in plate armor, no weapons, just a weight slamming across their shields over and over again. The issue was, the man bearing his weight against Kalsin's group happened to be Littleguy.

Clanks and thuds of steel on wood assaulted Kalsin's ears from all around as he was yet again jolted by the hulking man. His kite shield was partly layered on top of Fals' on his left, with Crawl's doing the same on the right, giving them a shared weight distribution against whatever lurked on the other side. In this case, it was a steeled up freak of human nature.

Fals swore as another hit rattled their defense. "Just me, or is he getting denser by the second?" The one good thing about shield training was the fact that Brownweather could never hope to hear them share words in this racket. Littleguy gave his answer in the form of a well placed kick, eliciting another swear from Fals.

Crawl winced. "May I ask you two to settle this somewhere off the field?"

"Nothing to settle here, all's good in poking fun. Last I had someone trying to sweep me off my feet this hard was back in Seven Fallter's best gambling den. Got a mighty fine night outta it," Fals said. "Watch it, Ghost, your shield is slipping."

Kalsin hitched his shield back in position, sweating. There was a reason why he had a bodyguard doing this for him. A hard surface between you and the thing wanting you dead was all good and well, but Kalsin would much rather have his left unencumbered for a quick jab. Planting his feet didn't come naturally to him. "Better now?" he asked.

"Yeah. Still wouldn't want you next to me in line though, 'pologies. You understand, I'm sure. If you need private tutoring, Littleguy here would surely bash you in shape."

"Fucking hell," Kalsin said when an almost unnoticeable smile flickered across the giant's face, followed by a shoulder planted directly into Kalsin's shield.

"That it does," Fals agreed.

As far as Kalsin could tell, their training shields were bulked up to weigh nearly as much as the real thing, similar to how their wooden swords imitated steel ones. Brownweather had told them they would transition to steel soon enough, right after the recruits learned the process of sharpening, oiling and otherwise maintaining the expensive items. In this, Kalsin couldn't find fault, though it wasn't for the lack of trying. You could buy a house for the price of a half-decent sword. About the cost of shields, Kalsin had no clue. Jack would probably know.

Before his mind could drift off in the direction of his friend, his attention snapped back as Brownweather called for a break. The noise of grunts and abused equipment slowly retreated like a balm over the ears. Unfortunately, not everyone realized sounds would die down quite so quickly.

Ringtail's laugh cut through the air in the wake of something she'd told Dweller, a tall woman in her group. The lieutenant's eyes honed in on her like a wild dog marking its prey.

He walked up to her, now the yard cloaked in silence. "Got something amusing to share, recruit?"

Ringtail knew better than to answer. She straightened her back, staring ahead.

"I asked a question."

She gave a brief headshake.

"No, of course not. Soldiering, that's a grand joke compared to rotting in prison, hm? Funny business, funny until I say otherwise. After all, it takes guts to stand up for yourself, and you're nothing more than a spineless thief quivering in fear. Isn't that right, Ringtail?"

He moved in her face, spittle flying and voice low. "Admit it. Ran from the law, ran from probably everything in your worthless life. You're only here because you were too much of a coward to bear the judgment for your crimes."

"Shut your—" Ringtail burst out, but didn't get a chance to finish as the lieutenant backhanded her.

For a second, everything stood still save from the ringing clap of skin on skin, the cold eyes of their officer staring down the reddening recruit. With a shout of rage, Ringtail surged. Her knee flew out toward Brownweather's stomach in concert with a powerful fist like a blur, but the lieutenant sidestepped the knee and grabbed the fist in his own hand. Ringtail spun out of his grasp, raining down a barrage of hits, most of which the lieutenant blocked or evaded altogether. A few appeared to connect though, and Kalsin found himself rooting for his fellow recruit.

That was when Brownweather's surge rose. Not the surge of a dirt, but that of a hive.

Hopelessness crushed into Kalsin tenfold stronger than Littleguy's weight against his shield, carving his feelings void, emptiness calling to him in a hundred voices. His knees gave out, noting somewhere far away that he wasn't the only one toppling to the ground. Like an insidious worm, despair burrowed through his mind eradicating everything good and joyful. There was no point resisting. If a sword had been descending on him, Kalsin would have let it fall, his arm leaden and his shield only held by the straps across his arm.

And then it was over as quick as it came. The morale influencing surge receded, and the only one left standing was the man responsible for it.

Brownweather looked over the stricken recruits, finally resting his eyes on Ringtail in the dirt. "There it is. The glorious defenders of the Ember Republic. Fucking pathetic." There was a flicker of consideration in his gaze before he raised his foot and crunched down hard on Ringtail's side with his heel. She let out a scream, trying but unable to hold it back.

"Dismissed." His job considered done, Brownweather strode away.

"Shit," Littleguy said as they watched Dweller and another recruit help Ringtail stand, folk all around keeping their voices low, kept under a grim cloud.

"Is it the first time he disciplined someone like that, or... a repeat performance?" Kalsin asked.

Fals furrowed a set of thick eyebrows. "The beatings? No, those are normal. Never saw him choke the liveliness out of us before, though. I couldn't have guessed he was a hive. And a damn strong one at that."

"All lieutenants are at least hives," Shutter spoke for the first time today, so far Kalsin could tell.

"And since we're the Scarrd Division, they're not going to get higher than that. Good riddance, too. Imagine the prick with a manifestation, slapping you with a gust of wind from across the yard," Fals said. She shivered. "No, don't do it. Imagine it, that is."

"Ringtail let him get under her skin. Can't do that," Crawl said, in the process of unstrapping the shield from his arm. When he realized his one-handed pawing was futile, he glanced at Littleguy until the man took pity on him and moved to help. He shook a red lock out of his eyes, blinking at Fals. "Say, have you been spending time with our good lieutenant recently?"

"Ha! Nice one," Fals grinned. "So anyways, how's your mother, Crawl? I'm just asking since the last time I saw her she was down on her knees licking my—"

"Fals," Littleguy said with a warning tone, voice deep, not looking up from loosening the straps. "Control yourself. At least while I'm here."

Kalsin guessed there was at least one inside joke buried somewhere along that exchange, but he didn't feel like digging into that pile right now. After getting his shield off and helping the others—he had enough practice doing the same for Jack—they started back inside. With the wind having grown stronger in the last few minutes, Kalsin was glad to be with a roof over his head.

"Brownweather’s talk about dead men, giving us false names, this outburst," Kalsin said. "Personal issues aside, does the lieutenant actually have a reason for loathing us? We haven't got a chance to prove ourselves. He doesn't seem to want us to prove ourselves."

"Have you spoken to soldiers around the camp?" Littleguy said.

"Sure."

"Regarding your question."

"I haven't, no."

"Right." He glanced at Crawl, as if he was better suited for this topic.

Crawl took the hint. "If you did ask them, they would've said... Well, first they would've offered their condolences for being put under Brownweather's command. But more importantly, they would've said; survive your first battle on the border. Once you're bloodied, and once you've bloodied the Veiled back, that's when recruit becomes soldier. Thing is, Brownweather doesn't seem to have any problems with the soldiers stationed around here, it's just us he tears into. Meaning…" He scowled at Fals, who had been whistling with increasing volume over his speech.

"Meaning, he's going to be our best buddy when we survive our first encounter with the Veiled," Kalsin summarized.

"If," Shutter said.

Kalsin ignored that. "So you're saying I have to pretend I'm an incorporeal wraith from beyond the grave for possibly the length of several months?"

Ghost wasn’t the worst of names… but he did have something of a foreboding feeling about it. Long red hair flashed in his memory and made him shiver.

Fals mock pouted. "Cry me a fracture. I'll make sure to let our fellow recruits know that it was you, in fact, who got the shorter end of the stick. Hah, as if. One of the lads Brownweather named Batshit. Another one Uggo. Poor guy so hideous even our beloved leader couldn't get past it."

"What about you, Fals? Where'd his inspiration for your name come from?"

Crawl actually groaned out loud, while Littleguy shut his eyes in resignation. As for Fals, a lopsided smirk started forming on her lips. "That's a mighty fine story if I do say so myself. It was on a rainy summer day that I fled from the righteous claw of the law, and who would've guessed that when I walked through the gates and into the training yard for the first time—"

"Fuckin' annoying little shit," Shutter said. "The lieutenant named her fucking annoying little shit. She also might've been the reason we’re not allowed to talk in the first place."
