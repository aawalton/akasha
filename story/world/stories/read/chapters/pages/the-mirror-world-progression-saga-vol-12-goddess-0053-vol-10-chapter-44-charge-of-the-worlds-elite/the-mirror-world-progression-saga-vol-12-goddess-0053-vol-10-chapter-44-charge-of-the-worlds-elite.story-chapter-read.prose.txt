The giant figure at the front of the battlelines shook his blade emphatically and called in a booming voice, “I am King Keinauz!” The word was a thunderclap and an earthquake in one. “Victory for the Daenriar! Victory for the alliance! Victory for justice!” He then let out a literal roar that burst like a shockwave in every direction.

Some stirring buff soaked into everyone in the army, and across the way, his Daenriar fighters answered his cry and seemed to double in their ferociousness in cutting apart the endless hordes of the dead. Meanwhile, his own ‘elite of the elite’ were among those following the change behind him — along with high-level human knights, minotaurs, elves, fae, frogmen, and naugites in a deadly and varied stew of deadliness.

The giant king led the charge of champions as they spilled into the undead like a runaway train, saturated with the densest buff layers ever devised — a cocktail perfected and perfected day by day, adding new innovations, squeezing more and more total bonuses in the science conspired by thousands of wizards, enchanters, scholars, researchers, priests, shamans, and on and on, brought together from around the world. Their minds and varied powers made for this unique weapon, this Frankensteinian concoction of mystical evolution. And here, at Matrazere, was the greatest culmination, untold gold and wealth gone up in smoke to create it, and yet stronger than the most priceless of artifacts.

Among so many other things, they were double-hasted and speed amplified, to make them more overwhelming than charging horses, greater even than a line of elephants. They were like a rampaging pack of alpha werewolves. The enemy couldn’t move away even if they wanted to.

Zadkiel sent punches that carried the power and size of a titan, smashing dozens of zombies with each swing, and boy, did he swing a lot. He swung, and he stomped, and he cackled all the while. He slammed the ground and made shockwaves in massive cones, destabilizing the lines.

Redberry bloomed out with roots in a constant expansion, crushing apart bodies, and the enemy struggled to cut the growth down… it only got worse as giant flowers bloomed, and wooden monstrosities burst out in all directions, killing dozens before being finally hacked apart. Sadly, she had to stay out of the ground entirely here. From early on, she’d said the soil was corrupted to the bedrock in Matrazere. Poisonous, anti-nature, and uncooperative. Whatever of her that tried to root would simply fail, at best. Still, she was anything but helpless.

Gelmak Por threw his axe forward, and it grew, chopping through multiple rows of bodies before returning and chopping through more, as he smashed, stomped, and gored undead with ease with his raw body in the meantime.

Bast let loose great cascades of raw, destructive magic, too intense — too hyper-boosted by buffs on top of his strange class and its focus on lethality — for the long-distance counters of enemy mages to block him even slightly. He ate through supposed resistance like butter, wiping out hundreds in the blink of an eye with the wave of a hand.

Deikmorn Brakka was so fast under buffs that Samantha could barely track him herself, as he diced up the chaff of the enemy like a streaking lawnmower. Nyomel supported him from afar, throwing spells of blasting fire and energy, keeping the way for him cleared and confused to maximize his advantage.

Canmore led a pocket of knights and cleromages with Bammondy and Sir Remus, cutting through more enemies with simple, almost conventional martial technique, as supported by magic. Primarily, ‘light lasers’ were used by cleromages in support, so the quick, intense fire was difficult to counter from a distance.

The Samanthian Servitors were no easier to handle, and perhaps the hardest of all, as the distant enemy failed to pierce the veil of the Faerie Dragon glamour-shapeshifting. Emboldened to take advantage, great dragons burst up into the air and unleashed huge lines of breath attacks on great swaths of the enemy. The destruction was of many kinds — fire, acid, cold, force, lightning, and others, making for a rainbow panoply of damage and chaos. None would claim the effects were the same as real dragons, but even this shadow of them… truly took the breath away.

The enemy fliers briefly tried engaging, but interceptors — primarily Angels — vetoed them, as led by the likes of Michael, Gabriel, and Seraphiel. The sorry commitment of the enemy in these early stages was simply not enough, and they were reduced to ribbons. The cries of the strange, undead flying ‘witches’ did little to help them against these terrorizing elites, who all but ignored them.

Azure, for her part, took an almost relaxed role amidst these champions, hanging back and throwing exploding knives, decimating any straggler groups in between the others, or that slipped by the many branching lines of the expanding, semi-independent warpaths. With precision, she executed. Meanwhile, Deathsting was let loose, shooting like a lightning bolt this way and that under buffs, ripping undead to shreds with tooth and claw. He truly hated them with a passion, and ultimately seemed destined or cursed to face them again and again.

And then there was the fae king, Keinauz. Every swing of his greatsword cleaved entire lines, and he just kept going. Chop, step, chop, step, chop, step, each death fueling his ice-cold rage. Mostly cold, anyway — his ferocious bellows never ceased, a sound like a roaring animal. And any attack against his armor simply died, leaving not so much as a scratch on him.

Furthermore, every cut-apart enemy flared up the aura surrounding the entire army, giving them additional Effective Levels for ‘fighting’ and adding a similar ‘cleave’ effect to what he had. It didn’t help spellcasters at all, but every warrior got a little better, and the bonus slowly went up. A fifteen-minute duration, but he kept refreshing it with kills.

Radical!

One other, seemingly humble knight in the midst was nonetheless notable in his prowess, keeping up with the likes of the ‘mid-tier’ at mass slaughter, such as Deikmorn or Canmore. The knight was incredibly fast and efficient, his movements fluid and almost effortless. Somehow, those movements also communicated reluctance, like a man chopping up wildflower fields he’d really rather not.

Odysseus. May you see no face that you know, my haunted knight.

The ‘Counter Crew,’ primarily Antimages and Elven countermagic specialists, but with a smattering of others, ran interception, of which Samantha considered herself a part of, if in more of a ‘flex’ role.

A network of supports assisted them, including Resemblants and Cat Sith. The former could sling luck-based one-off buffs, and the highest of them even had a special countermagic-boosting buff along the same lines, which was unique in the world as far as anyone knew. The latter allies, the Cat Siths, could curse a spell in process, thus weakening it and making it easier to counter. The strongest of effects coming in from the tower, no doubt from their highest level specialists, needed everything working together to block them.

Flying over everything, and under as heavy an invisibility as she could manage, Samantha and the others of the crew blocked problem after problem, as sent from afar. The majority of things came from the tower itself, flung across and impossible to miss. They kept buff-strippers, dispels, magical sniper shots, and all such things away.

Here and there, something squeezed through, but not enough to overwhelm their ability to keep the offensive blitzkrieg going, so sudden and so removed from their true enemies’ proximity as it was. They were clearly struggling with this level of intensity this quickly in the battle.

There’s more where that came from, bitches.

Samatha snuck in some attacks when she could, thanks to the Antimages more or less having things under control most of the time. She got off an Alter Spell: Expand-enhanced Gravity Reversal, sending a small horde of zombies up into the air. It wasn’t high enough up to be lethal to the undead, but when the enemy finally conveniently dispelled it, and they fell, it caused plenty of disruption and wounding. Not the best against undead, but she had to do it at least once.

It also set up an excellent follow-up with a lined-up Wyld Inferno, as there was a higher concentration of enemies in an area, many already wounded. Most were incinerated within the propagating magical fire.

Naturally, she summoned both Ralli and Ozzy to play havoc without supervision. Mostly unsupervised — Ralli had a few limits to stick to efficient MP-use, as she could drain the hell out of it very quickly. Keeping her own casting of Wire Jammer going was one thing. Meanwhile, Ozzy could do her crazy Looney Tunes AoE contrivances of destruction mostly at-will, as she drained FE instead. She was smug about that, too, casting cocky gazes, asking Ralli ‘Did you see that?! Wow, that was incredible!’ and so on.

This kept Ralli pissed off and pushing the envelope of what was efficient. Fortunately, Samantha was changing out temp mana items to drain, a Servitor bringing her new ones as needed, while her natural regen replenished here-and-there usage of her natural MP.

Both phantasms got sniped often, but she just recast them, always under invisibility to ensure they did some damage before it happened again.

The elite group, splintered as it became, surpassed in mere seconds what had been accomplished in minutes before by their lesser brothers, and not by a small margin. All-in-all, over minutes, the undead were cut down like wheat in a storm of martial and magical prowess — scythes fit for the purpose. Few remains were left of the other side against these attacks; many turned to bits or to ash from magic and pulverizing force.

Well over ten thousand undead were wiped out, and a swath of empty space the enemy simply had no chance of filling in time.

You wanted expendable, Dominion? Here you go. We’ll expend them. And I haven’t even shown you everything!

Despite her cockiness, she was wary and worried internally. Waiting for the ‘other shoe’ to drop, whatever it was. In particular, waiting to be the target she knew she’d be. It occurred to her that the enemy was in no rush. They didn’t care about these resources she had to wade through. What smidge of attrition they applied, what little dulling of the blade they achieved, that was fine. There were so many more where that came from, and the walls remained untouched.

Whatever the case, the whole army advanced into the gap. One small victory taken, to start.

Closer and deeper into Matrazere they went, as the shadow of that horrid place cast longer and darker over their minds, bodies, and souls, seeping into them with every second spent within.
