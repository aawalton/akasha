The wall fell shortly after midnight, with Maiya watching it come apart from Vimana Kin’jal’s observation deck. Through it all, her heart was a roiling mess.

All that effort, strategic planning, and every trick of war the humanity knew, and none of it even remotely enough.

Still, Maiya had certainly not expected Tia to risk life and limb to defend the gate as she had. Certainly, the Tiyana of the past would never have jeopardized her well-being for the sake of others. For revenge, yes, but her actions didn’t strike Maiya as driven by rage.

She was stalling to buy the retreating human armies time, and she’d done a fine job of it, too.

Ten minutes may not have seemed like much, but it had allowed dozens of airships to land, taking on passengers well beyond their capacity before struggling back into the sky, fleeing for safety.

Maiya had every Asura vessel contribute to the effort, maximizing every second Tia had bought them. And yet, neither her bravery nor her strength was enough to make a difference.

The Samsara had climbed the wall despite her last stand, and it wasn't long after until the gate swung open behind the princess. Except it wasn’t humans who strode through, but demons.

Realizing all was lost, she’d disappeared from the field. No doubt leveraging Haste, Blink, and her other prana-empowered movement arts to flee.

A few demons pursued, but Raja Jagath prioritized the assault, riding through the opened gates to ravage the retreating soldiers. It wasn’t long before the retreat turned into a rout.

Until then, the casualties on the human side had been rather mild ignoring the debacle with Tia's S-rank magic. The damage from which Maiya had attempted to soften. Yet in any battle, it was the rout that killed the most, and it was no different here.

It didn’t help that nearly all the Altani mejai had managed to evacuate already, fleeing on their fast attack skyships, taking on none other than their own.

No, it was Ira’s Kin’jal Balarian Warriors who faced the brunt of the demonic assault. The very Warriors who’d stayed out of the fight under her orders.

Them, and the Matali military and what meager forces Sai could commit.

It was a massacre.

But she could not blame them for defending themselves. They had the overwhelming advantage in numbers, location, and preparation. Every general would predict resounding victory with odds so stacked in their favor, and if they were to make a stand, this was the place every general would have chosen.

Every general who’d never fought demons, that was, which was all of them.

The Asuras defended the fleeing humans where they could, but hampered by Maiya’s orders to not harm demons or humans, they fought with both hands tied. With their smaller numbers, there was little they could do but staunch the bleeding.

“The offer still stands, you know,” Cirayus said from the rail beside her. “Say the word and the Asuras will drive the demon army back within the hour.”

“Oh, I know,” Maiya said softly. “Just as we both know that our true enemy would like nothing more than for me to take any side in this battle.”

It was the deepest of ironies that she commanded the most powerful weapons the realm had ever seen and was simultaneously powerless.

It wasn’t just the Vimana or even her army of Asuras, either. Her own personal strength was beyond the comprehension of most.

The orb vault the Imperium had gifted her gave her a level of power that could sunder an entire army on her own. S rank robs of every affinity sat charged and waiting to unleash their wrath.

Far more powerful than any normal S rank, she could channel the explosion into a single beam, cutting apart a thousand Rajas or Samsara Warriors in an instant.

In the blink of an eye, she could massacre more demons than the whole human alliance had managed all night.

Or she could turn her back on humanity, killing just enough to get them to retreat as Cirayus had suggested.

And then what?

Should she aim at the humans, every Gargan she’d kept out of this war would march to battle under her banner and she would be labeled a traitor to her race.

Should she aim at demonkind, every demon in every clan would know that the Akh Nara's wife had slaughtered demons out of the sky, and the Pagan Order would have its proof that no human could be trusted. That humanity's Saint was the monstrous enemy they had warned of all along.

The Fateweavers hardly even need intervene to ensure it. They'd set the stage so perfectly, after all, that intervention wasn't even necessary at this point. It was what Jagath, the human generals, and even Tia failed to understand.

That there was no happy outcome here. Not by chance, but because those who saw far more than any mortal ever would had worked to make it so, turning the appropriate levers of Fate.

As terrifying as all that was, however, it paled next to when she thought of Vir coming back from wherever he was to find a demonkind that hated her.

The weapons that could turn the battle were the same weapons that would lose her not just the war, but the good graces of demon and humankind both.

All as the enemy desired, of course.

So she kept her orbs inactive and the Vimana at bay. She ordered her Asuras not to harm a soul. She’d bound her arms and her legs. She’d blindfolded her eyes and gagged her mouth, and as much as she was disgusted with herself for doing so, she still believed it was the least bad decision.

She’d just never known that choosing the lesser evil could feel be so absolutely dreadful.

Out past the gate, Ira's Balarian Guard continued to show superhuman bravery even when others fled around them. Five thousand Kin'jal who were, even now, honoring the oath they swore to take no demon life. Holding the line in the middle of a rout and breaking demonic charges where they could .

They dragged the wounded to safety, paying the ultimate price for it. Or worse.

The attacking demons had begun to unleash their Warrior Chakras, even when there was no need to use them.

She could see it from all the way up here. Humans falling to their knees or dropping their weapons for no apparent reason, their fellow soldiers fleeing in terror only to suffer the same fate.

Unless someone offered them release in the form of death, they would continue to live a crippled existence. Washed and taken care of by people who loved them.

Nothing but an empty husk of their former selves and no hope of recovery.

Maiya would know, wouldn’t she?

By some miracle, Vir had made it back from that abyss. But he was the Akh Nara. With the benefit of his predecessors and a special soul that survived reincarnation.

These poor people did not have that benefit, and so Maiya did one of the very few things she could.

She offered them that small mercy. With a signal from her, every one of the Vimana’s precision weapons swiveled onto targets far below.

Not the grand cannons, but smaller, more targeted weapons that fired at every human whose souls had been shattered.

The lances flashed for just an instant. There was no sound, nor would there be any pain for its targets. Not compared to the agony they were going through.

They simply died, and with each one she killed, Maiya herself died a little inside.

Every decision she made lately only seemed to worsen the situation. To the point where she began to wonder if she should intervene in anything at all.

She’d not seen Vir for months, and she knew he’d made little progress to a solution. Every day they drifted apart, Maiya became more vulnerable. More exposed to the enemy's designs.

And yet, rejoining him jeopardized the realm.

What, then, was she to do? How did one solve an impossible problem?

An Asura behind her cleared his throat. “Orders, Lady Maiya?”

The wall was lost and everyone who could be evacuated had been.

What the demon was truly asking was whether she would finally take action.

Whether the Akh Nara's wife would the bring the weapon of the gods down on the army to stop this madness.

His eyes were full of expectation. Certain she was holding something back. That she had some grand plan to put an end to all of this for good.

He was left sorely disappointed.

“Order the Asura back,” she said, the words sounding like acid in her mouth. “We’ve done all we can here. All we can do now is pray. Pray that demonkind doesn’t eradicate humans in their mad conquest. That Roshan and his band can hold them back as he claims.”

She stepped back from the rail and walked past the wide-eyed demon, closing her eyes as she did.

For if she saw the look of disappointment on his face, she might have broken down right then and there.
