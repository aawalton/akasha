Chapter V.XLII (5.42) - Renegotiation

The fallout from Anata’s field trip hadn’t been as bad as Kizu initially feared. Rui had been inspected five times over by three different medics but nobody found anything wrong with the boy. He was asked by both the medics and Ms. Reads to stay home and be monitored for the next week. Meanwhile, Anata and Mae were put on a more obvious academic suspension for several days. Neither of the girls seemed to have minded the punishment. In fact, it seemed more like a punishment for Kizu than the girls. Every bit of his free time was spent babysitting them. Which he didn’t mind really, but it limited his studies. He knew himself well enough to know that he would hyper fixate on some projects and that would leave him oblivious to childish underhanded shenanigans.

That was finally over now. As of today, they were back to school and he could dive back into the meat of his studies.

“This is it,” Kizu said. He leaned back on his chair and swirled a vial in his hand. Nobody else was here with him. Just him and Zayne. Not even Mort was present. “It’s time to get whatever knowledge I can from you.”

After several weeks of training and instruction by Roba, Kizu felt confident in his mental compartmentalization capabilities. He could take on the bulk of Zayne’s memories and not lose any part of his identity to the soul. He’d already sorted away the bits of the ancient soul’s past that had been gifted to him about astronomy and successfully pulled them out during his last paper test session.

Kizu took in his workspace. He’d scrubbed out his cast-iron caldron and it sat over an enchanted heating stone designed to act as a replacement for a fire. All the ingredients had been carefully stored away.

Beyond the brewing station, his garden had been meticulously well maintained up until this morning, when he’d uprooted the final ingredients Aoi had helped him grow. Now, there were two fist-sized holes in the soil where the soul altering herbs had been.

This should do it. He trusted in both his brewing skill and his new enhanced memory compartmentalization spell.

Kizu closed his eyes and drained the vial.

This time there was no middle stage where Zayne met and spoke to him directly. When Kizu next opened his eyes, he held a gorgeous metal lamp that gleamed gold in the bright sunlight. In his pack were a hundred more just like it.

His heart was set on this course. The genies had taken the wrong one of them. Lahel would have been smart and played along with their plan. Kizu knew that would have been the intelligent decision. What he had planned was a stupid risk that jeopardized everything they’d built. But he wasn’t Lahel. When someone stabbed him, he stabbed back. No questions asked.

Not that it had been easy, engraving the runes into the inside of each lamp. Failure meant explosive consequences.

Kizu’s hand tightened on his spear shaft as he used it to help him take the final step up to the edge of the canyon’s cliff. He held no doubt as he approached his destination. His decision was made. Every warrior knew how to best bet his life. It was mandatory. You learned to gamble well or you died.

His heart beat steady, though his mind pulsed with rage as he stepped down the winding path that led to the genie’s meeting point. The powerful entity had demanded he meet it where the canyons converged.

It wasn’t alone. Even as Kizu hiked down the switchbacks, he could feel the attention of the unfathomably powerful creatures locked onto him.

These canyons were a landmark all humans knew to avoid. Just stepping foot in them meant risking life and limb. They were structured akin to if someone dropped a clay dish and attempted to glue it back together, with the canyon beds acting like the glued paths. Chamosh, the mythical flying dogs, made their nests in the canyon beds and often would tear apart any intruders with their teeth and claws. They moved silently and only howled after the kill, to declare the flesh their meal.

Kizu suspected the Genie wouldn’t let him get gobbled up before he traded them the lamps, but he couldn’t be entirely certain, so he kept his spear ready and his eyes trained on the narrow canyon passages. If he saw one coming, he felt confident he could take it down before it ripped out his throat. If he encountered a pack of the beasts… well, he planned to die with dignity, spear in hand.

Thankfully, it never came to that. Kizu arrived at a small pond with a single olive tree, the center of the network of canyons. Genies surrounded the pond, all of them watching his approach. They didn’t use their eyes. Those were just an illusion. A manifestation of an avatar. No, these creatures existed in a state of existence that transcended what Kizu could understand through his mortal senses.

Still, they weren’t omnipotent gods. Just the offspring of one.

“You wish to deal with us?” one of the Genie said. Kizu couldn’t say if it was the same as the one he’d spoken to in the cave or one of its brethren. He supposed it didn’t matter regardless.

“I’m not here to negotiate. I already explained my trade to you. You must offer me a means in which to fighting back against Sekai’s children in exchange for the artifacts I’ve recovered.”

“You wish for magic. A demand that requires a steep price. Let us see the goods before commencing the exchange.”

Holding up the lamp he carried, Kizu presented it to the Genie who was addressing him. He felt it caress the metal with unseen fingers. It purred in delight.

“All of them. We must view all the spoils to be satisfied that your payment is of equal value to what you demand.”

Kizu set his overloaded pack on the rocky canyon floor. He removed each lamp and carefully lined them up on the dusty ground. A hundred gorgeous metal structures, subtly distinct with their unique designs.

“This does not only offer us safe refuge, it can accelerate our capacity for power,” one of the Genie whispered.

“We can finally reclaim our territory from the Dragons,” another said.

“Still… the cost demanded.” One of the Genie avatars had its face twist in revulsion as it examined Kizu.

Kizu kept his own emotions in check, his face dull.

“My friend, Lahel,” he said. “Return her to me.”

A Genie snapped its fingers and a stone sculpture materialized in the center of the pond, startling a family of frogs and sending them hopping away.

Kizu stared at the stone. Finally, his emotions broke.

“You… you monsters,” he whispered. He moved into a fighting stance.

“That is hostile behavior,” a Genie warned. “Careful or your deal will be made void.”

“I returned her to you,” another said dismissively.

“You killed her.” Kizu said darkly. His knuckles turned white as his grip tightened on his weapon.

“Her soul is even intact. Simply sealed in the stone encasing. What? Did you expect me to feed and care for a mortal all this time?”

“So, you will return her to the way she was?” Kizu relaxed just a bit.

The Genies all laughed. The booming noise ricocheted and echoed in the canyons around them.

“That was never promised.”

“Would you prefer we alter our deal? Your companion’s life in exchange for your treasure? That certainly can be arranged.”

“In fact, if all you want is a means in which to deal with Sekai’s children, that can be arranged. It need not be the exact tool you requested. Other methods may be applied.”

“How about it? We offer you your companion’s life, and what you originally sought out.”

“Not magic,” Kizu said, understanding. “You’re twisting our arrangement to rob my species of magic.”

“Your plan of attack is selfish and flawed. We discussed it. This is a more certain path forward.”

“And less taxing on your species?” Kizu guessed.

“Perhaps.” The Genie sneered. Based on his tone, Kizu suspected it was meant to be a smug smile to showcase confidence or something intending to put him at ease. This Genie lacked the control of its avatar to pull something subtle like that off, so it instead showed Kizu what he suspected the Genie truly felt. They all loathed the idea of forming a pact with him, but they were greedy for his treasure and needed to follow through because of the original deal made. Genies always abided by a contract. They simply wanted this contract officially altered. However, Kizu could still push the original deal forward and they’d be forced to follow through. Magic for his species. If he did, they probably just intended to kill him afterwards in vengeance. However, that was not something he was particularly concerned about at the moment.

“Explain your plan,” Kizu said.

“You must journey to each of the continents that house one of Sekai’s children.”

“There, a sacrifice must be made.”

“Once carried through, a seal will form, banishing your foes down into the deep reaches of Sekai.”

“The children will gnash their teeth and rage, but you will remain untouched by their influence.”

“Your understanding of the exact task will be branded into your mind upon the deal’s completion, so worry not about a lack of comprehension.”

Kizu considered this for a long time. With magic, humanity could strike back directly. They’d have the potential to beat back the monstrosities and inflict vengeance. This new reiteration of their deal was just shoving them aside. And there was a massive risk to it as well, it required him to travel across the world to end Sekai’s reign. It wasn’t nearly as appealing to a warrior. As the silence stretched, the Genies clearly understood his hesitancy as one spoke up.

“We will even offer you immortality,” the Genie said, sweetening the deal further. They most definitely did not want to give humanity magic.

“Fine,” Kizu decided. “Three conditions in exchange for the lamps. First, the ability to vanquish Sekai’s children. Second, Lahel’s return to her previous state. Third, immortality and safety from your kind for both me and her. Promise me these three things, and our contract can be made void and forged anew.”

“DEAL,” every Genie chorused the word at the same moment and it echoed across the canyons. Kizu felt the pact settle on his heart.

Their avatar forms faded as the Genies surged forward in a wave of pure magic, grasping for the empty lanterns. Not one even considered for a moment that Kizu might have ulterior motives. They likely perceived him incapable of keen thought or deception. Another stupid mortal with a sharpened stick.

If any had held themselves back from their greed and taken a moment to consider, Kizu would have surely faced an existence far worse than death for the rest of eternity. A gamble.

The word from the Genie still echoed around him as the stone statue cracked. By the time Lahel broke through the cocoon that sealed her away, silence had taken the canyons.

“Zayne?” Lahel blinked and wiped a crusty bit of stone from her brow. “Where are we?”

The lamps, now scattered throughout the ground, all remained still. Only the glow indicated their prisoners.

The star signs meticulously engraved into the interior of each lamp were a magic from beyond this planet. One few had the capability to interact with. Genies, however, were the species who felt the stars stronger than any other. They were beholden to the laws of the heavens.

And so, rather than respond to Lahel’s query, Kizu embraced her. He held her in his arms and let out a gasping cry. All the emotions held back, melted into sobs of relief. He’d actually done it. He and Lahel were both safe. They would be forevermore.
