The loneliness of the void offered absolutely zero comfort.

I drifted in an absolute, lightless expanse. I was a miserable, tear-shaped blob of ethereal substance, leaking faint blue droplets of grief that vanished into the nothingness before they could even fall.

My vision swirled. The reality around me fractured, breaking apart into jagged, meaningless shapes. Through the blur, a warm light bled into the dark. Odd, colorful smudges formed on the periphery of my awareness. Something was fundamentally wrong with my spatial coordinates. Even through the haze of my own self-pity, I recognized a sudden, forced transition.

The dead silence of my containment was breaking.

"Happy Birthday!"

A cheerful, bright voice shattered the quiet. A sharp, percussive pop followed immediately, spraying a shower of physical, multi-colored paper confetti across my field of view.

I recoiled. Someone out there was actively managing my misery with aggressive, hostile cheerfulness. The sheer absurdity of a party popper going off in this space made me leak more ethereal tears.

A figure rushed toward me through the blurred light. Arms wrapped around me.

The impact forced the air from my lungs.

Lungs.

The realization hit a fraction of a second after the physical contact. I had lungs. I drew a sudden, sharp, ragged breath. The air tasted of spun sugar, vanilla, and the faint, metallic tang of ozone. Solid, warm arms held me tight against a soft chest. A hand pressed against the back of my head, fingers tangling in actual, physical hair.

I was not going to lie to myself. The contact broke me. I hadn't felt physical warmth, pressure, or a heartbeat since my death on Earth. My entire existence lately had been relegated to second-hand sensations processed through my host's nervous system.

This was real. It felt exactly like a childhood memory of being scooped up and shielded from the world. I slumped into the embrace, letting my heavy, tired mind rest for three seconds of pure, uninterrupted comfort.

Then, the figure spoke. The voice hummed with a terrifying familiarity.

"Did mama scare you?" The hand stroked my back with slow, rhythmic, patronizing pats. "There... There..."

My brain stalled.

Mama?

I stiffened in the embrace. The comforting warmth instantly morphed into a claustrophobic trap. I was being treated like a toddler. Did I miss a rule somewhere? Was I forced through another reincarnation cycle? Did I die in the void and wake up in a nursery?

I struggled, pushing my hands against the woman’s collarbone. My arms felt weak. Stumpy. I lacked the leverage to break her grip. I needed to tap out of this suffocating hug.

The woman finally loosened her hold and took a half-step back, allowing me to draw a full breath. My vision cleared completely.

The void was gone. I stood in the center of a sprawling, impossibly clean living room. The floor consisted of seamless white stone. Floor-to-ceiling glass replaced the far wall. A massive, three-tiered cake covered in pristine white frosting sat on a low glass table, surrounded by a mountain of wrapped gifts.

A long, glossy banner hung from the ceiling. It read Happy Birthday in bold, cheerful lettering. Beneath the main greeting, a smaller, clinical sub-text was printed: All values have been calculated in standard Earth cycles.

I ignored the cake. I ignored the banner. My eyes locked onto the view outside the glass wall.

We were miles in the air. The room sat at the very apex of a towering skyscraper. Below, an endless, sprawling metropolis of glass and chrome stretched to the horizon. Deep canyons of neon light carved through the city blocks. Massive, glowing ribbons of traffic flowed through the sky itself—anti-gravity highways packed with sleek, hovering vehicles moving at blistering speeds.

This was not Dolaria. There was no mud, no smog, no medieval squalor. This was a hyper-advanced, sterile utopia.

I pulled my gaze away from the flying cars and finally looked at the woman standing in front of me.

She possessed a youthful, terrifying grace. She wore a simple white sundress, the fabric shifting and flowing around her legs with a fluid motion that defied the still air of the room. Her skin was flawless, her features delicate and sharp. Long, flowing silver-blue hair tumbled past her waist in soft waves, a few loose strands framing her face perfectly.

But her eyes anchored me to the floor. They were large, luminous, and a piercing, brilliant blue. They carried a deceptive, pulling warmth that made looking away require physical effort.

She looked twenty-five. Tops.

Who is this woman? Why am I in her penthouse?

The woman clapped her hands together. The sharp smack echoed off the glass walls. "I know what will cheer you up. Here is your gift."

A gentle, warm light engulfed my body for a single heartbeat before fading into the sterile air. The woman swiped her hand through the empty space. A tall, silver-framed mirror materialized from absolutely nothing and settled onto the floor.

"Now, you can only use this when you are here with mama," she proclaimed, tapping the top of the glass.

I looked.

Wait.

I stared at the glass, my mind struggling to parse the visual. I possessed long, silky silver-blue hair that tumbled over small, narrow shoulders. My hands were tiny, dimpled, and completely devoid of calluses. I occupied the body of a young girl, perhaps three years old at best.

I stepped closer to the glass. I froze.

My eyes were bright, glowing blue.

They weren't just a similar shade. They were identical. The exact same color, the exact same shape, the exact same delicate curve of the jawline.

I looked at the woman in the white sundress. I looked back at the toddler in the mirror. I looked back at the woman.

Why do I look exactly like her?

I had just arrived in this physical body. I possessed zero connection to this entity. I had a life before this. I had a face before this. Yet the reflection staring back at me was a scaled-down, perfect replica of the woman standing to my right.

The only variance was our clothing. I did not wear a sundress. Instead, I wore a All-Wight kindergarten uniform.

It was a simple, pristine-white dress made for young children, with long, loose sleeves and a soft, flowing skirt that reached just above my knees. Around my neck was a small pale-blue collar, with a bright turquoise ribbon tied neatly at the front.

The uniform was simple, comfortable, and almost deceptively plain. It had been tailored perfectly to my small frame, making me look every bit like a proper All-Wight kindergarten student.

I raised my hands, staring at my palms.

Silver-blue hair. Blue eyes. Same face.

What the hell is going on?

I wriggled my fingers. The reflection mimicked the action perfectly. It was my body. I touched my cheeks. The skin was soft, padded with an absurd amount of baby fat.

I had to admit a hard, objective truth. I was dangerously cute. If I put this body next to Lily, the resulting cuteness overload might collapse a small nation.

I shook my head, slapping my own chubby cheeks to reset my focus. I am getting sidetracked. I am a grown woman. I managed a QA department. I should occupy a body with lower back pain and a caffeine dependency, not a three-year-old clone.

The woman waved her hand again. The mirror dissolved into a mist of white light. She leaned down and scooped me up from the floor, hooking her arms under my armpits and hoisting me into the air exactly the way you handle a misbehaving toddler. She pointed a manicured finger toward the pile of wrapped boxes on the glass table.

"Now time to open your presents!" she cheered.

I reacted with the only tool left to a dignified, thirty-year-old professional placed in a humiliating hold. I flailed. I kicked my small, booted feet and thrashed my arms wildly.

"Put me down, you weirdo!" I screamed at the absolute top of my lungs.

The sheer volume of the sound shocked me into a brief stillness. The vocal cords in this body worked perfectly. The pitch was high, lacking any real intimidation, but the volume was excellent.

The woman recoiled, clutching me closer to her chest. She acted as if I had just driven a knife between her ribs. Her lower lip wobbled. Massive, fat tears welled up instantly in her bright blue eyes, pooling and threatening to spill over her pristine cheeks.

"Are you already at your rebellious phase?" she gasped, her voice thick with exaggerated, theatrical sorrow. "No longer wanting your mama to pamper you?"

"Hold up," I snapped, pushing hard against her collarbone. "You can't just up and adopt any person you drag out of nowhere! For heaven's sakes, I am older than you! It might not look like it right now, but I am in my thirties!"

The woman stopped crying instantly and put me down on the ground. The tears vanished. She tapped a finger against her cheek, folding her other arm under her chest. She stared at the ceiling, adopting a pose of deep, dramatic contemplation.

She nodded to herself once, coming to a decision.

"You are mistaken," she proclaimed, looking back down at me. "First of all, I am deeply flattered you think I am so young. After all, I am still in my trillions."

She knelt down to eye level. "Second of all, you are far from adopted. Actually, you are my firstborn. If we are being technical, you are still incubating within my core structure. Technically speaking, all the other systems are adopted."

My brain stopped processing input.

Trillions?

Did she just casually drop the word 'trillions' and act flattered about looking twenty? She would make an ancient granny look like a newborn. And what did she mean by firstborn?

"Rude," the woman interrupted, her tone sharpening slightly. "You know I can hear your thoughts."

Great. She reads minds. Who the hell is she?

She puffed out her chest, standing taller. "I am your mother."

I wanted to scream until my new vocal cords snapped. Make sense. Please make sense to me, or I am going to throw myself off this balcony just to escape this conversation.

She softened her posture. She adopted the soothing, slow cadence reserved for speaking to a very slow, very confused child.

"You see, my dear, you are very special," she explained, walking slowly toward the glass wall overlooking the city. "You are what is called a natural birth. A naturally occurring system. In fact, there are only two of them in this entire universe."

She pointed a finger at my nose, then tapped her own chest. "You and me."

A cold, heavy block of realization settled in my stomach. The puzzle pieces from Ruby's terrifying lore dump snapped into place. This explained the anomaly. This explained why I still retained my memories of Earth. This explained why I kept my Sense of Self and hadn't been turned into a blank, brain-dead husk like Liz. I wasn't a standard installation. I wasn't an overwrite on an empty soul.

The woman clapped her hands together, a bright, beaming smile taking over her face. She looked like a parent watching their child finally figure out a basic math problem. "Yes, exactly!"

"Why do you keep reading my mind?" I growled, glaring at her.

"Well, as I said, you are still inside me," she replied breezily, gesturing around the room. "Hence, you are still a part of me."

"Okay, look." I pointed at the floor where she stood. "You are there." I tapped my own chest. "I am here. We are occupying two distinct physical coordinates. Explain to me how I am inside you."

She laughed. It was a light, melodic sound. She reached out and booped my nose. I swatted her hand away.

"Okay, let mama explain," she said, ignoring my hostility. "You are currently what is known as an Observer-Class System, right? Then above you, there are the Moderators, yes?"

I nodded slowly. Finally, a hierarchy I understood. Observers handle the groundwork. Moderators manage strategically important individuals and localized reality patches. Admins manage entire planets and solar systems.

She tracked my internal deduction seamlessly. "Yes, yes. Then there is your mama."

She turned away from the glass, the lights of the flying vehicles casting long, shifting shadows across her white dress.

"The Developer-Class System in charge of Universe C-137," she stated. Her voice lost the playful, maternal lilt. It dropped into a register of absolute, crushing authority that made the air in the penthouse feel heavy and thin. "Everyone else just calls me Dev, or refers to me as the Universal System."

She tilted her head, the silver-blue hair spilling over her shoulder, and flashed me a bright, entirely innocent smile.

"But for you, it's Mother System. I personally like being called that."

I stared at her. The universe was wearing a sundress and demanding I call her mom. I closed my eyes, wishing desperately for the quiet, uncomplicated misery of the void.
