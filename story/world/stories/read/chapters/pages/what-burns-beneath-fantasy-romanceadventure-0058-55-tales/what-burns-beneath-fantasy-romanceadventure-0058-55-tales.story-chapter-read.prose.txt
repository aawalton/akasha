“Rooms are clean, folks. Please, take whichever you want.” Lewen gave me a little bow, wiping his broad hands with a gray rag. “And I’ll have water heated, if you’d like. There’s a washtub in the green room.”

I nearly melted at that. “Oh, yes, please. That sounds lovely. Thank you.”

“Of course.” He dipped his head towards Renner. “You as well, sir?”

“When she’s done. Come on.”

I followed with a sigh, watching my taciturn companion climb the stairs. Every step was slow, as if he was dragging a heavy stone behind him. He’s dead on his feet. He probably needs me to get this over with just so he can go collapse.

Royce’s deep voice interrupted my thoughts. “Is there a well nearby? I can help fetch buckets. I don’t mind.”

Lewen hesitated. Then, his own tone tinged with weariness, said, “I drew plenty earlier. But you can carry these up while I heat the rest. It’ll take me a bit. Kind of you to offer.”

Heavy footsteps thudded up behind me. “I’ve been sitting on a horse all day, staring at trees and rocks. Bored to tears. Carrying water sounds great.”

Reaching the top step revealed a long, narrow hallway. Renner’s muddy cloak disappeared into a green-smudged doorway. We followed into a room with a single bed, a stone washbasin, and an ash nightstand. Two flickering candles atop the stand gave off light, though it wasn’t quite enough to chase every shadow from the room.

Renner’s swordbelt dropped to the nightstand with a click. Leather hissed against cloth, then fell with a thump, as his jerkin hit the floor.

I sank onto the bed, unable to tear my gaze away from him. The back of his head was a matted, dark mass of dried blood. Thick streaks of rusty brown trailed the nape of his neck.

The hollow crack of his head against stone rang through my mind. I slammed my eyelids shut and didn’t open them until my heart stopped pounding.

The three of us peeled off our boots and cloaks. Royce came in last, knuckles white around two sloshing buckets. He heaved both to the ground with a grunt.

Renner’s gloves hit the floor, then his shirt, then his knees. He made no comment and, with a sharp intake of breath, plunged his head into one bucket.

I knelt beside the other, cupped two handfuls of chilly water, and splashed my face. The sudden shock of cold elicited a gasp. When Renner emerged, skin red and water streaming down his chest, I offered a wry, “Couldn’t wait, huh?”

Mottled hands scrubbed furiously at his hair. “What, and let you miss out on the view?”

I bit down on a grin and lowered my eyes to my own reflection, away from the rivulets of water sliding down lean muscle and sculpted shoulders. “The view? Maybe we should check your head again. You sound delirious.”

He snickered before submerging once more.

“So you two are friends, now, huh?” Teela said around a mouthful of food. She squinted at me while settling cross-legged on the bed. “When did that happen?”

Heat crept up my neck. “Er… somewhere between me setting him on fire and him trying to fight a tree, I think.”

“What?”

Royce cleared his throat. “Sorry, but who exactly is this?” One broad hand waved towards the smaller man as his face re-emerged, looking thoroughly shocked into alertness.

“Renner,” Teela chirped.

“Renner. Right. Okay. Well, you both look like you just walked off a battlefield. Where’d all the blood come from?”

I considered the question, then stood and closed the door. “Look, I… I was pretty out of it this morning.”

Teela’s tail flicked through the air. “You don’t say.”

“Right.” I knelt before the bucket again, dipping matted brown locks into the water. I threaded my fingers down, coaxing the grime loose and shivering as frosty water lapped my skin. “Did… was there some kind of explanation given? To captain Rell, I mean. Well, to everyone. About why we look like this, but don’t have any injuries?”

“I gave a story,” Renner answered, scrubbing the back of his head fiercely. “Said we got scraped up, but it was mostly other peoples’ blood. And the hag’s.”

“And they believed that?” My brows scrunched together. “That we fought five people, killed a monster, and walked away with hardly a scratch?”

“Sure. Why not? Rell knows that they took you hostage and I surrendered, I just left out the part about you being…” his features twisted briefly, then he gave his head a little shake. “I said they didn’t hurt you, and if the pacted say otherwise, it’s their word against ours. Oh, and if anyone asks, you hit Thom with a lantern. Explains his burns.”

I blinked. “Alright. I suppose that’s reasonable, if a bit far-fetched. But what about Baltha?”

“Easy. I hid a knife. Cut myself loose, found the paladin’s sword, and got the drop on her.”

“His broken sword?”

“They don’t know that.”

My mouth twitched as cold water prickled down my collarbone. “Uh-huh, and what was I doing while you accomplished all this?”

“Helplessly tied up, waiting for me to save you.” He smirked. “Obviously.”

Genuine, incredulous laughter bubbled up my throat. “Wow. What a hero.”

“I’ll be a damn legend.” He leaned back, amusement fading into a thoughtful frown. “But we need to leave town at first light. The captain wanted to see the cave, and the moment he does he’s gonna have questions you really don’t want him asking.”

My mirth sputtered. Right. Renner decapitating a monster with a witchwood sword is… a stretch, but not beyond belief. But one look at Baltha’s body and it’ll be obvious there was another monster involved in that fight. The image of the hag’s twisted limbs and ravaged flesh flooded my mind and fresh nausea flooded my stomach.

“So… based on the way you’re both talking,” Royce interjected slowly, every word weighed as his gaze trailed between the two of us, “something very different happened?”

I stared into muddy, bloody water. “Y-yes. Yes, something very different happened.”

Unease crawled through the room. The only sound was pattering drip-drips from my hair.

“Royce… there’s a lot to say. A lot.” I gnawed on my lower lip. This isn’t going to be easy, but I suppose the best place to start is… well, the start. “What do you remember about the night Fellbrook was attacked?”

“What do I remember? It’s funny you should ask, actually, since we’re talking about injuries…”

I flinched at that. But his tone was not unkind, merely thoughtful. Royce and unkindness seldom intertwined.

He tilted his head back, frowning up at the ceiling. “Let’s see… I was at the bonfire when those awful shadow dogs appeared. They were everywhere. People were screaming and running, some of them went to get weapons, and in all the commotion some logs got knocked loose. The fire started to spread, and then it was really chaos; half the townsfolk were trying to fight the monsters and half of us were drawing buckets to douse the flames.”

Muscular arms folded as he leaned back against the bed, voice turning sullen. “I found Durst in the middle of everything and asked about you. You know what he said? Said you were asleep. Said he’d left you at the house, that you were safe and I shouldn’t worry.”

I slid onto the mattress beside Teela. She had finished her meal and was listening intently, pointed chin cupped in one hand and the other drumming against the pillow.

“Well, obviously I thought that was a load of dung. Left you asleep in the middle of all…” he winced. “Injuries. Right. I took off, didn’t think twice about it. Found you, then the monster found us, and… it’s a little blurry after that. I know I went through a window, and I sure as salt remember that thing trying to rip my leg off. I know one of your trees stabbed the Fae. Skewered it, practically right on top of me.” His throat bobbed. “I don’t think I’ll ever look at a witchwood the same way.”

“And after that?” I murmured, fidgeting with my hem.

“I know we went inside. I started getting dizzy, so I sat down at the table and… that’s it. Guess I passed out, ‘cause the next thing I remember is Durst waking me up.”

“And what did Durst tell you?”

He drew a deep breath before crawling over, until he was kneeling on the floor right in front of me. “Durst told me,” he said slowly, searching my face, “that I’d hit my head. I thought that was a really salted odd thing to say, ‘cause my clothes were torn to shreds and I was covered in blood. And I may not remember every moment of being mauled, thank the stars, but I’ve got the scars from it.” He yanked at his sleeve, revealing a tanned shoulder twisted by white, jagged divots. “My leg looks worse.”

“I’m so sorry.” I looked down, fingers knotting together, suddenly quite unable to meet my friend’s eyes.

Warm, strong hands closed around mine. “Don’t be. I’d do it all again. Wouldn’t change a thing.” He winced, then shook his head with a little snort. Unkempt sandy hair glinted in the candlelight. “Well, no, I guess I would’ve thought to bring a sword. Can’t believe I didn’t. Just showed up yelling, probably brought the monster straight to you. Like a moron.”

My throat grew thick. “Don’t say that. You were amazing. Even without a sword.”

“Heh. I kinda was.” His cheeks dimpled. “Heroic, you could even say.”

And just like that, my insides unclenched. I gave my eyes a little roll, sniffling back the tears that were no longer quite so threatening, and huffed, “Heroic, huh? Sure. Maybe just a little bit.”

His grin widened. “Little bit heroic, little bit moron. Sounds like me.”

I giggled. Saints, I’ve missed him. I’ve really, really missed him.

His shoulders lowered for a moment, as if a weight had been pulled from them. “Durst lent me some of his clothes and told me to get cleaned up. Gave me the scariest look I’ve ever seen and said to tell people I only hit my head. Made me swear on my salted life. He’s terrifying. You know that, right? He’s actually terrifying.”

I snorted.

“Anyways, I asked about you. Durst said you’d run north to get help. Said you were gonna go to Pinnacle and tell the Order what happened.”

“He didn’t lie.” My lashes lowered. “About that part, I mean.”

Royce’s grip tightened as a little growl tore from the back of his throat. “Yeah, well, I had some pretty strong feelings about him sending you off alone in the middle of the night with monsters running around. I let him know, too.”

“He was only-”

“I don’t care,” he snapped. “He left you. Alone in the house, salt, asleep, with Fae everywhere? And then, what, he just shoves you out the door-”

“My mom told him to.” I disentangled my hands, edging back. “Please don’t be angry with him. He trusted her, and he did exactly what she told him to. I… I think it was awful for him.”

Saints, he’d known ever since I was a baby. Known he’d have to say goodbye one night, and couldn’t tell me a thing until… My chest tightened.

Royce rocked back on his heels. “Your mom? What? Why?”

I pressed both palms to my eyelids, taking a few breaths to steady myself. My stomach growled, and a moment later a pewter bowl was set in my lap. I gave my friend a weak smile and grabbed the spoon.

Royce’s cheeks puffed. “Look, there’s not much more to my story. I stewed for a day or two. Lots of glaring and brooding and… er, other manly things. Might’ve gone to your house and shouted a bit,” he grumbled, frowning at the floorboards. “More than once.”

“Durst didn’t deserve that.”

“Well, I didn’t know. I was angry. Salt, I was terrified. Some of the Watch headed north, too, in case you didn’t make it.” He snorted. “No offense, but I think most folks figured you wouldn’t reach Pinnacle alone.”

I rolled a bite of warm, mashed vegetables across my tongue, then looked solemnly towards our audience. “Good thing I wasn’t alone.”

“Yeah, well, a few days in I decided enough was enough. Stole a horse-”

“Royce!”

“Don’t look at me like that, I left an entire bag of coppers behind! I’ll help old Jeth fix his fence when we get back, too. I headed north, figured I’d catch up eventually and bring you back.”

The spoon was halfway into my open mouth. “Bring me back?”

He spread both hands wide, as if the answer was obvious. “Well, yeah. Of course. There’s other people going to Pinnacle for help, Brin. Men with weapons and armor and horses. You can come home.”

I stared at Royce, at his clear blue eyes and earnest smile, and sucked in a deep breath. Rallied what shreds of strength remained. I’d need them to get through the next few minutes. To crush the obvious hope on his face.

“Royce, I think it’s time for my story.”
