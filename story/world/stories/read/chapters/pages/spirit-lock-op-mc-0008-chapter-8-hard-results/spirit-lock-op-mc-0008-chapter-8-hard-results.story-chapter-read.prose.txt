Seven days. That’s how many days Lina had to pass by Hayden's training spot after lunch. Each time, she heard laughter or gossip, but the laughter grew lesser and lesser. She originally thought it was because the joke had ended, but the area was increasingly charged with excitement—just a different kind of excitement. Crazy sentences flew around like, “You think he can match Goren?” and people actually debated it, saying things like, “Goren can bench six plates, so probably not now. But give ‘em six months? I think Hayden could take a teacher.”

Lina forced herself not to look. Not just for Kayline, but her own curiosity. So she endured, and today was the day she would finally find out.

It was worth it.

The scene she saw with the other students left her stupefied. Perhaps it wasn't that impressive to others, but it was to Lina.

Hayden was still practicing punches.

No kung fu.

No flying kicks.

No, “Waaaah!”

The same punches—on repeat, without the slightest variation.

But they weren’t the same punches she saw a week ago. They were as solid and refined as Hayden’s forearms—and abs. Somehow, in seven days, he had turned his flat stomach into abs. Sure, there was no fat there, so it didn’t take much to show muscle—but abs were abs. His chest was minimal, but it was growing, and while she was fairly certain that she never considered forearms attractive, seeing the difference in his was… kinda attractive.

“See?” Kay said, sneaking up behind her.

Lina jumped in startlement. “See what?”

“He’s hot. Notice his face?”

Lina studied Hayden’s face with mixed emotions. It was… complicated. It was still full of acne, but like his muscles, the improvement made it strangely attractive. Maybe “attractive” wasn't the right word, or perhaps it was attractive, but as a trait rather than something visible. She didn't know, but it was something.

Last week. Hayden’s face was a topographical war map marked with red and white pins. Now, half of his acne had cleared up, and his jaw had become more defined. If that wasn't “attractive,” the word needed a far broader definition.

Lina must’ve made quite the face because Kay said, “Riiiiiiight? It’s pretty cultish, but… I’m starting to believe.”

“Believe in what?”

“That he’s good in bed.”

Lina's eyes snapped open. “Kay!”

Kay giggled. “You’re so easy to mess with.”

“Yeah, only because I can’t tell if you’re serious.”

“Oh, I am. Not about bed, that was a joke. But if he wins the tournament, I’m definitely going to ask him out.”

Lina looked between her and Hayden a few times. “You are joking right?”

“Come on.” Kay grinned and dragged her away by the wrist. “No more peeking till the tournament. Otherwise, you won’t see those juicy, juicy gains.”

Lina tried to comply—but she couldn't.

It was impossible to ignore Hayden's gains during the last week. He had become a phenomenon, a collection of social concepts that drove every conversation at Galma.

“Hey, are you hungry?”

“Not really. It’s not even eleven.”

“Yeah, but if we don’t get there by noon, Hayden’ll have eaten everything.”

Ha ha.

“Did you hear? Goren is practicing punches now.”

“Wait, seriously?"

“Yeah, seriously. But like, how do I put this? When Hayden does it, it looks like he knows what he’s doing. When Goren does it… It looks like…”

“He’s severely constipated?"

“Yeah! No joke. His face gets all red and his cheeks start jiggling and… it’s too much.”

Ha ha ha.

“Of course I’m not gonna win. I just want to hit the guy once for the rest of us.”

Ha ha ha ha ha.

Everyone was part of the ongoing joke, but at some point, it stopped being a joke. Lina walked down the forest path to the dining hall one day and saw a large crowd gathered around Hayden's training spot. There was no laughter—just reverence.

As Lina approached, she heard someone say—

“You know, I thought it was a joke, but… I actually think he’s going to win this thing.”

—and she broke down.

Lina pushed through the crowd and saw something incredible: Hayden wasn’t just throwing punches and kicks—he was fighting his horix. Or rather, he was training the spirit by throwing a flurry of punches and kicks at it. It was so high-speed and intense that the crowd gasped when Hayden threw a surprise flying kick. His boot connected with the spirit's tiny body, and it rocketed into a tree with a devastating bang.

Women covered their mouths. Men took a step forward to yell. Then, Gluttony shot back with a vengeance, attacking Hayden like a battering ram.

A woman suddenly snorted with laughter, shattering the silence. The spectators turned to her, likely wondering if she was seriously going to mock him.

“I’m sorry, it’s just…” She covered her mouth with her wrist. “It’s just… we were laughing about flying kicks all last week, but when he actually throws one… we’re impressed.”

The students exchanged surprised glances and then burst into laughter.

Even Lina found herself laughing.

There was no longer a doubt in her or anyone else’s minds—Hayden would win the martial tournament. Judging by Gluttony’s demonstration, he might take points in the spirit category as well.

Rosen came to that same conclusion after watching the recording. He collapsed onto his bed and tried not to think about it. His brain felt like it was made of frayed wires, and they were sparking over his bathtub. That analogy didn't exactly make sense, but it generally communicated how he felt—and it was definitely warranted.

Next week, Rosen would be disinherited from the family business, and it was all Hayden's fault. After all, Hayden screwed him, and now he was about to sweep the martial tournament.

Rosen tried not to be defeatist, but no one could stop him.

Goren was strong, but he didn’t know martial arts. That's why Rosen could win. But Hayden? He was different.

Not only did Hayden know martial arts, but the rate at which he was infusing cora into his muscles was terrifying the teachers. Two weeks ago, the kid couldn't bench press a wet noodle; now, he was running 10Ks and rocking triple plates. The only explanation was infusion—and infusion was terrifying. It wasn't an equalizer in his case—it was overkill. If Hayden hit Goren in the gut, it would probably rattle eighty percent of his vital organs. If Hayden hit Rosen—it could kill him.

And Hayden would hit him. There was no doubt about that.

The guy's martial prowess was the real deal. It didn't make sense, but that video Rosen just watched wasn't altered. Hayden was landing hits and dodging faster than Rosen could keep up with.

So yeah, Hayden would hit him—it might kill him—and then he would lose. Then he would be disinherited and sent off to some C-rank shit hole for the next three years.

The worst part was that losing wasn't even the worst part.

It wasn't even close to the worst part.

The worst part of Rosen's situation was the video.

Not a video. There were plenty of those. Students recorded Hayden nonstop, then used AI editing tools to make him look like an ancient martial arts master who had been preserved in a cryo chamber, only to be set loose in the 29th century.

That was funny. The video wasn't funny.

It was titled “Hayden’s Redemption,” and it was a professionally edited hit piece.

It started out cheesy, sporting a blank screen with text that read:

“After years of merciless bullying, our young hero had become content with living a quiet life. But that changed when he saw his heroine being harassed.”

Footage Lina recorded on the day of the burning ceremony flashed onto the screen. The video was silent and skillfully edited to make Rosen look like he was snapping at Kayline, even though he was just saying that he wasn't blocking Lina's path.

Hayden watched Rosen coldly.

The screen cleared, and more text appeared.

“Too weak to step in, our hero felt fury for the first time and vowed vengeance. At first, he was laughed at…”

The video showcased a montage of students laughing at Hayden in the woods, in the gym, and in the cafeteria. Rosen was part of that mockery.

“But people quickly realized that our hero had an unquenchable desire to put the harasser in his place.”

What came next was an unbelievable training montage of Hayden transforming from a scrawny dweeb into an increasingly attractive man with refined muscles and a bold personality. The story was well done, an underdog tale that captured the growing crowd sizes and captivation.

Hayden was officially a popular phenom. That was bad enough—

—without Rosen being the villain.

But he was.

People actually believed he harassed Kayline. Once people realized who she was, things quickly went downhill from there. The day that video came out, Rosen was ridiculed, heckled, and scorned for it.

Rosen didn't care about the ridicule—he worried about his family.

Kayline's father was more powerful than many multigalactic leaders, as he controlled the fate of businesses running intergalactic trade routes in one of the four largest multigalactic empires. Ritrix Iska made Rosen’s father look like a child. He was not the type of person that Rosen wanted to make enemies with.

It was a catastrophe. The only reason Rosen wasn’t looking over a bridge was because the video was a work in progress. After all, the “hero” had yet to beat the “villain.” That said, it was fated to happen—at the “Redemption Festival,” for fuck’s sake. The naming couldn't be more symbolic.

I’m so screwed… Rosen thought. No… I can’t let this happen.

As much as he dreaded doing it, he called his father.

“Yeah, Dad. Yeah… I know I’ve been wasteful, but… Yeah. I know it’s overdrawn. I know… I know… but dad—listen. We have a serious problem.”
