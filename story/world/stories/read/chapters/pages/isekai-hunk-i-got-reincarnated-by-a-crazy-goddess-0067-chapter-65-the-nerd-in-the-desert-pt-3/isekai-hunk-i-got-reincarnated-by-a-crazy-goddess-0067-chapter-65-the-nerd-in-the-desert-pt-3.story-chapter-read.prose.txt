The dragon’s roar grew louder, reverberating through the desert as Sigurd hoisted Ada onto his horse.

“Watch the armor!” she protested, wincing as a metal plate jabbed her side.

“Watch your attitude,” he shot back, mounting behind her. With a swift kick, the horse bolted into the sands, racing away from the approaching doom.

The desert around them was vast and awe-inspiring, painted in hues of amber and crimson as the setting sun bathed the landscape. Towering rock formations loomed like ancient monoliths, their jagged peaks reaching skyward. Massive canyons cut deep into the earth, casting long shadows that seemed to stretch endlessly. The air carried the faint scent of sagebrush, mingled with the dusty, baked aroma of the arid land. Overhead, the sky transformed into a brilliant gradient of orange and purple, dotted with the silhouettes of hawks circling in search of prey.

Ada clung to the saddle, her muttered complaints barely audible over the wind. “This is exactly why I don’t leave my workshop. Every time I step outside, it’s dragons, explosions, or defective scrolls.”

“Sounds like you’re cursed,” Sigurd said, steering the horse toward Grimroost. His tone carried a hint of teasing, though his eyes scanned the horizon warily.

“Probably,” Ada admitted with a sigh. “But you’re the one who dragged me into this, so now you’re stuck with me.”

Sigurd glanced back at her, his brow furrowing in thought. “You know, you should start your own business.”

Ada blinked, caught off guard by the sudden suggestion. “What?”

“Think about it,” Sigurd continued. “You’ve got the knowledge, the skills, and plenty of experience with magical tools. Make a name for yourself. And here’s the key: offer a warranty system.”

“A what now?” Ada’s skepticism was palpable.

“A warranty,” Sigurd explained patiently. “Back where I… come from, companies guarantee their products. If something malfunctions due to a manufacturing defect, they replace it, sometimes free of charge. If the product breaks from wear and tear, they offer to repair it for a small fee. It’s a way to build trust and show customers you stand by your work.”

Ada stared at him, her pink eyes narrowing. “That… actually sounds reasonable. But do you really think people would go for it?”

“They will if you market it right,” Sigurd said, his voice steady and confident. “Nobody else in the magical artifact business does it, right? You’d have a competitive edge. People would pay a premium for reliability and accountability.”

Ada considered this, her expression shifting between doubt and intrigue. “Huh. Maybe you’re not as brainless as you look… wait? What’s your name?”

“It is,” Sigurd said with a nod, his tone steady. “Sigurd Svobodna, war hero, a Goddess’s champion, and current baron, at your service.”

Ada’s eyes widened, and a slow, exaggerated grin spread across her face. “A war hero and a baron and a goddess’s champion? My, my, aren’t you the full package? Handsome, well-built, noble, and divinely favored. Gods, I’m practically swooning over here!” She clasped her hands dramatically over her chest, fluttering her eyelashes with exaggerated flair. The glint of mischief in her pink eyes made it clear she was laying it on thicker than dragon-hide.

Sigurd raised an eyebrow, unimpressed. “Subtlety isn’t your strong suit, is it?”

“What? Can’t a girl appreciate a good-looking hunk when she sees one?” Ada quipped, her grin turning sly. “But seriously, you’re offering me business advice? Is this the part where you tell me you’ll personally fund my little venture?”

Sigurd tilted his head, considering her words. “Actually, yes. I dragged you into this mess—even if it’s one you partially created. Financing your business could be my way of apologizing, and I actually think is a good idea. A warranty system and a baron’s endorsement? Sounds like a solid start.”

Ada’s jaw dropped, her playful demeanor faltering for a moment. “Wait, you’re serious?”

“Completely,” Sigurd said. “It’s a good idea, and you’re clearly capable. You just need the resources and a little direction.”

Ada stared at him for a long moment, her expression a mixture of disbelief and cautious excitement. “Well,” she finally said, her voice quieter but no less sharp, “maybe you’re not as insufferable as you look. I’ll think about it.”

“Glad you think so,” he replied with a smirk. “Now, let’s focus on getting back to Grimroost in one piece. Then you can start drafting your business plan.”

"By the way, will sex be included as part of the financing?” she asked, scratching her chin with exaggerated thoughtfulness.

Sigurd’s frown deepened. “Heck no, forget about it. I’m a father!”

Ada gasped, her hand flying to her mouth in mock shock. “A father? Oh no, not the ‘responsible family man’ card! What next, you’re going to tell me you bake cookies on weekends and host charity banquets?”

“Not quite,” Sigurd replied dryly. “But I do enjoy a good sparring match and keeping reckless witches alive.”

She clicked her tongue, shaking her head dramatically. “Damn. Figures. The hot ones are either married, dead, or emotionally unavailable. My luck just keeps getting better!”
