“I’m glad to see Mike and Cora are enjoying the new city,” I said after returning to our rented apartment in Sorenguard.

We’d considered moving alongside the Arkenesis couple, but opted to wait until we finished our IRT training first.

Arkenton was still a little larger than my ideal home, but the novelty of the steampunk setting was enough for now.

“It is,” Stephen agreed. “It’ll be fun to explore the new dungeon there with them.”

“I agree. Mike is probably already doing research on the floor we’ll be challenging to prepare,” I said with a grin.

“I don’t doubt it,” Stephen said, reaching for the information tablet. It was the primary reason we spent any time in the apartment instead of just hanging out in the sanctuary.

We’d almost certainly rent a delver apartment at the Dungeon Complex in Arkenton when we moved there as well, since it was basically the only way we could retain network access.

If we really wanted to, we could probably afford a single networked tablet (mostly because of the compensation I received by managing Portal transfers), but I just couldn’t stomach the thought of spending nearly all of our savings on something we could get basically for free by living in a specific housing complex.

Seeing Stephen settling in, I brought out Aerie and Lana. Both of the kits were asleep, so I placed them in my lap as I snuggled next to Stephen.

A little while later, I moved to the small workstation I’d set up in the apartment and started enchanting some of the bags and jewelry pieces I’d picked up for just that purpose.

Now that I was focusing my efforts on a single type of enchantment (albeit done two different ways), I found that I was able to complete far more pieces than before. What used to take several hours, I was now able to complete in just over three.

“Done already?” Stephen asked when I stood and stretched.

“Yes. I’ve already gone through my stock. I’ll need to pick up more base items before I can make any more.”

I wouldn’t have time to go shopping before class since it was scheduled to start so early, but I’d make time to stop by my item suppliers in the next few days.

I had a couple of crafters from whom I purchased bags and jewelry to make my storage devices, and they likely had a stockpile waiting for me already.

“Why don’t we do that after class?” Stephen suggested.

“That sounds perfect.”

“Alright. This is the last week of classroom instruction,” Veridia stated when everyone arrived for class the next day. “You all should have a good understanding of the overall mission, so we’ll be specializing your training from here on out. Group assignments are posted on the board.”

I looked at the board behind her, noting that each instructor had been assigned between three and five students. Strangely, I didn’t see my name anywhere on the board.

“You’re probably wondering why your name isn’t listed,” Veridia said, stepping next to me. “It’s because you and I will be working together this week. You are the only Space Magi in the class, so what you need to learn is different from the others.”

From my other side, Stephen said, “I’ll meet you here after class?”

After I agreed, Stephen hurried to join his assigned instructor. He was in one of the larger groups – one that I was fairly certain was dedicated to those with strong Archery skills.

“We’ll be heading to a more private location,” Veridia said as I stood up. “If you’re ready, I’ll teleport us now.”

“I’m ready.”

In a particularly skillful display of spatial magic, Veridia moved us from the classroom to a well-appointed office. Her skill was so smooth that I hardly felt the sensation of movement.

In fact, I suspected she might be even more skilled in that way than I was. It was abundantly clear from that one action that the woman had dedicated centuries to mastering spatial magic.

If she wasn’t already a Space Magi, she was likely darn close.

“You look surprised,” the golden-haired woman said with a smirk.

“I am,” I admitted. “The transfer was exceptionally smooth.”

“Yes. I suppose it should be after nearly two millennia of practice.”

As a gold-ranker, Veridia’s lifespan should be somewhere between 1500 and 3000 years. The fact that she’d admitted to living almost 2000 years but didn’t appear to have entered stagnation (based on her youthful appearance) meant she was probably pretty close to reaching platinum rank.

“I also had an excellent teacher,” Veridia added with a hint of melancholy as she took a seat on one of the two couches. She motioned for me to sit on the opposite couch, and I quickly obliged.

“As I’ve mentioned in class, Incursion Response Teams can function without a Space Magi as long as they have the right equipment and skills. In fact, three of your classmates will be granted skills only found in the Portal Ascender Dungeon, which will allow them to isolate and retrieve new dungeon cores in much the same way you eventually will.

“The slotted skill method is rougher on young dungeon cores than manual retrieval and inevitably results in a complete reset of the dungeons. But that is better than allowing demons to destroy the cores.”

“But the method is basically the same?” I asked.

“In broad terms. The biggest difference is that genuine Space Magi are capable of sensing and retrieving an entire dungeon as opposed to just the core. They are also able to create temporary dimensional spaces to store said dungeon in, which allows them to be transported to a new location.

“Unless they have a separate skill allowing them to create dimensional spaces, retrieval teams without a Space Magi will have to rely on specially crafted dimensional storage devices with exceptionally high storage capacities.

“As I’m sure you know, such devices are extremely expensive and can only be made by a few high-evolution crafters. Luckily, most of those crafters are associated with the Porter Guild, so we are able to get them at a reduced price.”

“Even then, dungeons are basically separate dimensional spaces, so placing a dungeon core into dimensional storage has to be extremely risky,” I said uncertainly.

I had always been taught that you should never place a dimensional storage item into another since any destabilization could lead to a catastrophic failure.

Then again, my demesne was basically a dimensional storage space, and I often stored multiple dimensional storage items within it.

“It would be if they were active,” Veridia agreed. “Part of the process also involves placing the core into slumber, which prevents it from destabilizing whatever storage device they are placed into.”

“Is that still the case if I’m taking an entire dungeon instead of just removing the core?” I asked.

Veridia smiled. “No, which is why I said the method of retrieval is only similar in broad terms. We have a lot of information to cover if you have any hope of learning the process by the end of the week.  To start, you should read this,” she said, handing me a thick tome.

I took the book with a sigh. It was a good thing that I’d invested so heavily into my mind during my evolution.

“You look tired,” Stephen commented when we reunited several hours later.

“I am tired. I spent most of the day reading and still didn’t finish,” I replied. “I’m supposed to finish before the next class, which means I either need to spend most of my night reading or implement a bit of time dilation to get through it.”

Veridia had actually suggested I take a quick trip into the dungeon and study in the safe zone to make use of the dungeon’s inherent time dilation. She didn’t know that I could do the same thing within my demesne.

Or maybe she did.

Not about my demesne in particular, but if she knew that I was the Originator of the Sorenguard Ascender Dungeon, she probably suspected that I could use time magic to get through the book by morning.

“Yeah? Would you rather I hang out in the apartment so you can study?” Stephen offered thoughtfully.

“That’s sweet of you to offer, but I don’t think it’s necessary,” I assured him before pulling us both into the sanctuary. “I’ll crank up the time dilation in here and activate Temporal Aura to multiply the effect. I was also thinking—”

I stopped mid-sentence. I hadn’t told him that I could enter and exit the dungeon through my demesne yet, though he was aware that I could exit at any location with a Dimensional Anchor.

“What?” he asked, tilting his head.

After giving it some thought, I decided to go ahead and come clean. I was terrible at keeping secrets from people that I interacted with regularly, especially when they were important to me.

“So, you know how I can exit the sanctuary at any location with a Dimensional Anchor?” I waited for him to nod. “Well, that includes the anchor I have in the dungeon. However, I can only maintain a single Dimensional Anchor within any dungeon.”

“Does that mean you could enter and exit the dungeon directly?”

“Yes.”

“How does that work?” he asked, causing me to shrug.

“I don’t know. It’s something about the way the sanctuary works. I still can’t teleport between the dungeon and the outside or open a portal between the two.”

“That’s interesting,” Stephen said thoughtfully. “Are you considering popping over to the dungeon to double the effective time dilation you can access?”

I nodded. “That was my thought.”

“That could be very helpful. It’s too bad you can only set a single Dimensional Anchor within any dungeon.”

“I know,” I said with a grin. “However, it’s probably better this way since I’d feel bad about cheating the city out of taxes. I try to follow the rules whenever I can.”

I couldn’t help it. I’d been raised to be a rule-abiding citizen, and those lessons had stuck with me even after all this time.

That didn’t mean that I never broke the rules. The fact that I had a Golden Oak in my garden was proof enough of that.

For now, I was letting the valuable tree grow freely, only harvesting its acorns when they were fully mature and fell to the ground. I made sure to leave enough for Aerie, Lana, Mario, and Luigi to have the occasional treat, but most went into storage for use in the future.

I’d probably end up selling most of the resources that I gathered from the tree, but I also planned to use some of them for Enchanting and maybe give a bit to Cora to use in Alchemy when her skill level was high enough to justify it.
