I stared silently at the door.

I studied every miraculous carving: the way it caught light in what was almost a sparkle of the air. Anything not to think about what was about to happen. I was going to kill someone in cold blood. Not in the Dream, or in an Instance or any of the other times I had been willing to kill someone. Nate and Maggie had been traitors and dangerous, and the last Instance had been a desperate struggle. This would just be me going down, plucking someone’s brain out, and sanding down every piece of them until they fit into one of my monstrous creations.

My mind stuck on Nate and Maggie; the anger I felt towards them back then. What I did next would be worse, but I’d do it. Damn it, I’d do it a thousand times over. I took a deep breath and reminded myself what I had gained upon killing a part of an Instance Holder. My grief and hesitance would not absolve me. A crying wolf does not make it less of a murderer. I steeled my resolve, and a small part of me that wanted to maintain some thin veneer of innocence—that wanted to walk the path as someone who broke all which was unfair—shriveled into nothing.

[Are you ready?] Ananke asked me.

I nodded, and purged the concept of tiredness from my vessel. Energy returned to its… my body.

[Yes.]

[Good. Now look through the door.]

I turned the knob with a steady hand and was surprised by what I saw. It wasn’t the chaos of the other door. The spaces shared similarities, but this one didn’t overwhelm me at all. Tiny motes of light undulated and bowed as if on the surface of water. It was hard to count them, a wave of chaos sweeping across and blocking several from my view, only for the mote to appear elsewhere; one would blink out, another would slowly aspirate into being.

[The worlds of the lower layers.]

Ananke had stopped using static to communicate to me. Now I just heard her voice in my head. It was easy to distinguish her from all the background noise of my own mind; her presence felt far more rigid and defined than any stray thought I mustered.

[There’s less than I thought.] I sent to her.

I didn’t get a good count. Less than a hundred, more than thirty by my best guess. The number made me frown. For all the talks of many worlds and the fact Instance Holders were basically Gods, I had expected something more vast, incomprehensible. Like the chaos through the door. For all the strangeness peering into the lower levels filled me with, it didn’t fill me with anything but pity.

[Invading a world in anything but the lower levels is difficult. You must find an entrance, else be strong enough to break through any defenses. That is not wise. Instance Holders prefer subtlety. A direct confrontation would mean much destruction. If one makes too much of a splash, I would have to step in. You understand?]

I answered tersely.

[Vaguely, yeah. So I can’t just go killing every Instance Holder I meet?]

[You may, if you so wish, as long as you do not make too much noise.]

That didn't make any sense. She was all powerful, a little noise would be nothing to her.

[Why? It can’t just be because you don’t like loud things.]

[Noise thins the veil of the Dream. If it breaks, I would survive. No one else would.]

Right. Top of the list. Don’t make too much noise.

[Just warn me if I’m getting close to that, yeah?]

[A fair request, and one I have granted before. Very well.]

I stared out at the thin sea of motes and gestured forward.

[You said for everything but the lowest layer I’d need to find an entrance. Not here though? So how do I go?]

[Some rules first. The lower layers are farms for minds first and foremost, of many types. They are not cultivated much, but even old Instance Holders make their way down on occasion, to get a personality or specific type of mind. Your harvesting must be limited. A piece of me enforces that. Entering the lower layers is as simple as trying, and being allowed to. You will be ejected when you’ve harvested too much. Lastly, for you specifically, Dorian.]

I perked up, focusing more on her words at that.

[Do not view those things down there as people. View them as impressions of people. They do not have the Presence to be considered even close to sentient.]

My breath came out shaky and I swallowed hard. [Thanks.] I finally decided on. [That does help.]

[On your own you’d have to travel and search through each world in the lower levels, but there’s a nice fit for the monster you made. Step inside when you’re ready.]

My feet carried me forward and in an instant I found myself looking down from on high through a fish-eyed lens at a lake, and a series of cabins that reminded me of a summer camp. I couldn’t help but let out a small laugh.

[I mean. I did model my guy after Jason, so it fits.]

Without waiting for a response, I willed myself to descend. It felt like drinking thick water. I stumbled back, repulsed in all manners physically and emotionally.

[I errored slightly in my explanation. You may explore these worlds. From the outside visually, or through an Instance. Only your constructs may enter themselves in that case. Anchor your body to the barrier.]

I did so with a thought. The how of it I didn’t set in stone. This was all training, so I’d practice with impressions. I was tethered to the barrier. The ‘how’ wouldn’t matter unless someone else observed the tether.

[Good. Now imagine your creation, think of them at the bottom of the lake and them crawling out of it. Since this is your first time, I will put this section of the world in a loop. You will repeat it until you’ve harvested everyone in the camp in one loop. In short, I will make this place an Instance for those below. You will learn the same in time.]

The time loop part sounded a lot like Jeren’s [Title] ability turned up to eleven. I thought of him now. I couldn’t remember if he was from the lower layers or just the layers lower than mine. I pushed it out of my head for now and did as Ananke suggested.

I imagined my monster in the water and felt a tether connect me to my creation, the familiar feeling of a small portion of myself becoming more independent across space filled me. Once the creature was created it became less imagining and more piloting with simple commands. The first command I gave it was up. I spawned the guy at the bottom of a lake, couldn't exactly do much down there.

My creature surfaced, clothes soaked through. He made his way to the shore with heavy steps. Campers pointed immediately. Someone screamed. A gunshot rang out. I felt the bullet embed itself in my skull as my creation fell back. I willed him to get up, but nothing happened.

Time reverted and my creature was back at the bottom of the lake.

[What the hell was that?] I asked, trying not to let my annoyance creep in. [I should trump those guys with Presence, why wouldn’t he get up! He should be invincible.]

[Another lesson. Instances play by the rules. Outside of them, in someone else's worlds, I do not care what you do, or how you break it as long as it is not noisy. Inside Instances, the rules will be obeyed.]

[Still doesn’t explain why that all just happened. I should’ve been able to bring him back up. I believed I could and nothing happened!]

[Your belief matters not in an Instance. The creatures may, to an extent. Your monster is a shell, piloted by you, with only the meager Presence it took to make its body.]

[Still! Why’d they shoot on sight. He could have needed help and they didn’t even ask him a question before panicking.]

[I put this section of the world in a time loop. I will not always be here to do that. Sometimes Instance Holders leave survivors or do not harvest their desired mind. Tales of monsters like these are known to the inhabitants.]

My blood slowed and a cold sensation crept in. I steadied my voice, not letting a single drop of emotion touch it. The idea of having fell for another trap infuriating to me.

[I thought you said they weren’t people.] I thought the words through gritted teeth. It wouldn't matter right now, but if she was lying even after I had steeled myself and cooperated I was going to make as much noise as possible.

[Even ants can recognize patterns.]

I grumbled my compliance. Less certain than before, but I pushed it to the side. It didn’t matter. This was happening.

[Each failure I will give you another piece of advice. Try your best. They are not free, and I do not have forever.]

Ignoring the comment, I took a second and thought about the entire process a bit more. Impressions mattered. I could build them into my monster. I started building my monster at the bottom of the lake again. The mask first. I wanted to go for something twisted. I felt it fit to be somewhat honest that the creature was just a facsimile of a person than an actual one. So, I made the mask too small for its face and then carved out what was underneath, replacing it with deep nothingness. I bulked up his body, 6'5", corded muscles covered by thick clothing and algae from the lake. Thick blood. He was twisted; what powered him was not anything that could be killed with bullets. The blood did nothing. He could live in the lake without air after all. I decided not to give him a weapon. Tools were a human thing. If I wanted to play into the whole, no longer human, twisted by something greater theme, stripping him of his use of tools seemed apt.

This time I waited until night, until the beach was empty and everyone but a sparse few had gone to bed. I had him slowly breach the depths of inky black water. He walked strange on land, more like he was crawling on all fours on the lake floor than anything else.

The movements reminded me of a muscular Gollum, and an idea for a different monster popped into my head. Next time, if I couldn’t get the Jason archetype to work.

My creature scuttled along the ground, passing by a worn out sign reading the words “Camp Lakeside” in carved out letter.

A ways off, in a fire pit further down the shoreline, my creature spotted his first target.

He began creeping forward, drawn straight there with no care for subtlety.

A mindless beast, nothing more ... yet it cackled to itself as it drew to that which had been denied it: life.
