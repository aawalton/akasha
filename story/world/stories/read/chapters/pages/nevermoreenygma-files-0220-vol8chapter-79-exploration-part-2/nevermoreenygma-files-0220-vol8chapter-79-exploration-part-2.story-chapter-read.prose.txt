Chapter 79: Exploration (2)

The second day of exploration was strange, bringing new surprises.

Among them was confirmation of what they had witnessed when they first entered.

The pyramid had begun to change again.

Some of the paths they had already traveled were no longer accessible, while new ones had appeared where there had once been nothing but straight corridors. The place really did move, despite the fact that they couldn't see any mechanisms anywhere.

Lizbeth seemed familiar with the new corridors. Through gestures, she explained that it was normal for parts of the pyramid to change every few days, almost always at random. It took them several minutes to understand her explanation without putting too much pressure on her. She genuinely wanted to make herself understood, but her broken English didn't always help, and they didn't want to push her too hard.

The corridors no longer descended into the depths of the monument. Now they seemed to slope gradually upward, as though trying to lead them back to the surface. But still the route was treacherous. Each ascent branched into other passages, which in turn opened into new chambers that multiplied into endless galleries where they went up and down.

Moving forward didn't mean making any progress at all.

It was as if the damn building really was larger on the inside than on the outside.

"This… makes no sense," Mai murmured at one point, glancing back with an exhausted expression. "We should have climbed much higher by now."

Shin didn't answer immediately but of course he had noticed it too. "I know. But we're still going up."

"Are you sure?"

Shin hesitated for a second. "…I think so." He didn't sound convincing at all.

Lizbeth kept trying to lead them back to places they’d just been. This prompted a little whimper from her. She didn’t seem to want to leave the place where she’d spent so much time. It took them two hours to calm her down. They didn’t want to frighten her, but the mere thought of staying in that place for another day didn’t appeal to them at all. Mai reckoned it was because Lizbeth had been through the same thing herself, and seeing them so desperate, wandering through the corridors without knowing what they might find, frightened her.

Shin felt a bit helpless because he didn’t know how to react to Lizbeth. It pained him to see her in that state, but that pain went a little deeper than simply feeling sorry for her because she’d been locked up in that place for so long.

No.

There was something else. But no matter how hard he tried, he couldn’t figure out the answer.Even so, thanks to Mai’s patience, they managed to persuade her to carry on.

Sometimes they found drafts of air.

They were faint, but constant. Breezes seeped through cracks in the stone, brushing against their skin like a promise, only for that promise to be broken when they reached a dead end.

It gave them hope. But it also confused them. The air didn't come from a single direction.

It appeared from different points, crossing their path, vanishing, then reappearing farther ahead. Sometimes it blew from behind them. Other times, from above.

"It's as if…" Mai began, frowning. "As if there's an exit right in front of us. We just can't see it. Could this place be closing the doors on us as we go?"

Shin nodded slowly. "At least we have fresh air, even if we don't know where it's coming from."

Lizbeth seemed more calm and let them investigate on their own. She seemed to find it amusing to watch them, but she stopped them firmly whenever they were about to enter somewhere they shouldn't, or when they chose a path she apparently knew well enough to recognize in advance.

Shin spotted chitinous shells in several places, along with skins covered in a kind of fine, soft fuzz that looked as though it had been torn from something. He only understood what they were later, and the realization terrified Mai.

He had found several spider husks about the size of their heads, their legs curled inward.

Lizbeth grimaced too. She scolded Shin, telling him that it was "bad" and that they shouldn't go that way.

Yes, Professor Lizbeth.

During that time, she’d started to relax a bit more and would occasionally smile at him, revealing her white fangs. She seemed to be whispering something to Mai, which made Mai laugh too. Whilst the situation wasn’t exactly funny, it was at least reassuring to see them together. Just as Mai was calming Lizbeth down and encouraging her to move forward, Lizbeth, for her part, seemed to be calming Mai down as well. Well… I don’t blame her, Shin thought.

Being naked with a stranger couldn’t have been easy at first. But as the hours passed, they’d grown completely accustomed to the situation.

During that time, they explored chambers that didn't seem to belong to the same place again.

Or even the same style, material and place.

One gallery had walls covered in engraved cubes, stacked on top of one another in patterns that followed no obvious logic. They didn't form any recognizable structures, but neither were they chaotic.

They were… something else.

In another chamber, circular, the ceiling was pierced by thousands of small square holes, as if it had once held fitted pieces that were now missing.

Mai raised the lamp.

"Why do you think it was built that way?"

"No idea," Shin replied. "The question is… who did this?"

Farther ahead, a corridor displayed reliefs of enormous blocks floating above ancient cities. Nothing supported them. No ropes, no pillars.

They had to watch their steps carefully there, too, as rocky formations jutted from the floor like needles, reaching toward their counterparts on the ceiling. But at that moment, the carvings had caught their attention more than the hazards underfoot.

Those cubes stirred something in Shin's mind, though he didn't know why.

They simply… floated.

Lizbeth stopped in front of one of them. She touched it with her fingertips. Her expression changed ever so slightly.

"…Box…" she murmured, as if the word were difficult to remember. "No… box…"

Shin looked at her.

"A cube?"

Lizbeth blinked, then nodded, though she didn't seem entirely sure.

Shin turned his attention back to the walls. And then he noticed something.

"The triangle…" he said, more to himself than to the others. "It's barely present here."

Mai stepped closer, examining the reliefs more carefully.

He was right.

The recurring symbol was no longer the triangle. It was cubes. Large ones. Small ones. Whole or fragmented. Arranged in three-by-three patterns, with even more divisions on each face. More and more repetitive patterns.

They covered the walls, the floor, and the ceiling.

As if they were the true, invisible structure underlying this section of the pyramid.

"So… it changes," Mai said. "Depending on where we are."

Shin didn't answer, but his expression grew more serious.

Lizbeth seemed to recognize some of these symbols. Sometimes she stopped in front of them and touched them carefully, as if trying to remember something that kept slipping away.

But she never said anything clearly. Only fragments. Just isolated words and fleeting impressions.

But still, they continued, until they reached a smaller chamber.

More carvings, and something else.

White mice scurried through cracks in the floor, slipping easily between the stones, appearing and disappearing as if they were part of the place itself.

Mai stopped.

"We could…" she began, taking a step forward. "We could catch some."

She didn't finish the sentence, because Lizbeth moved first.

She stepped between Mai and the mice, held out an arm, and shook her head.

"No…" she said with difficulty.

She pointed at the mice, then at the walls. She shook her head again. "No…"

Mai blinked in surprise.

"No… catch them… eat them?"

Lizbeth shook her head again, quickly and firmly.

Shin frowned. Then he remembered the reliefs. "Wait. I think she might be right."

Mai looked at him. "Why?"

"I saw them before…" he replied. "On the walls. The mice… they were always near doors, staircases… important places. Do you remember the warning at the entrance too?"

Shin couldn’t quite make sense of it, but that warning was certainly unusual. He didn’t know why, but something told him that mice in places like that could only be a nuisance. What was the point of that warning they’d found before going in? He’d also seen a mouse right in front of him when he woke up. Why would the builders have put them in various places?

Mai brought a hand to her chin, thinking, then nodded. She looked around.

"Does that mean if we could follow them, we might discover something?" She looked at the little animals again. "Then…?"

"They're not prey," Shin said. "Or not just prey. Besides, as long as we have fish, we don't need to catch anything else. Do you really want to eat a mouse?"

Mai's stomach growled.

Lizbeth nodded emphatically, as if confirming every word.

"I try… but… n-never could… find exit. I try… I try. One day, c-c-catch... and e-eat one. Since that day, things get worse. I siick… for d-days," she said sadly.

The mice stopped for a moment, watching them with their tiny shining eyes.

Then they vanished into the cracks. As if they had never been there.

It was possible that the mice were important, but it wasn't as though they could follow them through cracks in the walls anyway.

The three continued through the pyramid's labyrinth, following invisible currents of air and the symbols carved into the stone, while the little perpetual lamp burned in their hands.

They searched for more mice, but the animals had disappeared completely.

Instead, they found a kind of lizards in one of the corridors. They stood about a meter tall, with a leathery frill around its neck that could flare open. When the creatures spotted them, they let out a hissing roar.

A group of five lunged at them.

Lizbeth caught one and, before it could bite her, snapped its neck with astonishing ease. Shin had to deal with two others. He smashed their heads against a wall.

Lizbeth looked pleased.

"T-they... tasty!"

It was best not to ask how Lizbeth had found out, but Shin guessed that they had caught their lunch for that day.

Fortunately, they had found some dry wooden pieces wedged into the ceiling, which made it easier to cook the meat and give it a different flavor.

That day, they ate the meat of one of the lizards. Shin cut it up using the particles of his armor to form a blade.

Following Lizbeth’s instructions, Shin cut off a lizard’s thighs and skinned it while letting it bleed out.As he did so, Shin asked himself several questions. He knew how to handle the blade attached to his armor, but he was doing it almost mechanically. Where had that experience come from? It bothered him a little, but he decided not to say anything. After a while, when the meat seemed cooked enough, he decided to try it.

It wasn’t a delicacy. But the taste of the meat wasn’t actually bad.

Mai was a little reluctant, but she said nothing after tasting the first bite.

As Shin tasted the meat, an image flashed through his mind. A plump bird with a crest. "This tastes like something called chicken."

When Lizbeth tasted the cooked meat, her eyes lit up. Of all the things that had happened, at least that lunch seemed to make them happy, and he was happy, too.

It might have been exhaustion, but to them it tasted like a delicacy. They had to eat with their hands, which was a problem because the rather dark-colored meat, once roasted, turned out to be juicier than it looked. According to Lizbeth, those lizards had a layer of fat on their backs, and that was the reason. The part on the outside, near the fire, seemed rather tough and crispy, but when they bit into it, the flavor flooded their mouths.

Lizbeth seemed to be the one who enjoyed digging in the most, and she ate quite a bit, even though the wait was making her impatient.

For no apparent reason, Shin had a problem after eating, which meant he couldn’t get up for about half an hour and had to cross his legs slightly to hide his issue. Could it have been because of the meat? Mai looked a little flushed and was scratching her chest a bit, but she seemed to calm down quite a bit after drinking water from the second bronze head. Lizbeth, on the other hand, seemed perfectly normal.

After eating a sumptuous meal, the team continued their journey, saving a few pieces of meat for the road. The bronze pot with the rods made a good container for the pieces, but it would be better to reheat it a little later, even if it got a little burnt.

And so, exhausted once again, as the hours passed, they decided to take a break. It was the second day and they hadn’t stopped and sleep had overtaken them once more.

They chose a new room that didn’t seem quite so cold and rested. They huddled together as best they could in a hidden spot and set up a trap using the light, lying down on the opposite side. That way, whoever was on watch could see if anything was approaching.

But soon it became a routine.

Waking up, having whatever breakfast they could manage, and setting off, stopping to hunt something, and then finding a place to sleep.

Over the following days, as they explored the place, Lizbeth took charge of finding dry herbs and wood, however little she could gather. Shin was a little puzzled by the wood. It seemed very old, but not yet petrified. The problem was getting it to burn. The dampness inside the pyramid made the process take far too long.

And time inside the pyramid began to lose its shape.

Sometimes they seemed to lose all sense of it. After walking for a while, they would suddenly realize they had been moving almost on autopilot. The same thing happened when they rested.

"Huh? Didn't we fall asleep somewhere else?" Mai asked once.

At the sound of her voice, Shin woke up too. He was supposed to be keeping watch, but he had dozed off.

But she was right.

When they awoke, they found themselves in a small, vaulted chamber decorated with carvings of beings fighting with clubs against… something that looked like cat people? Aside the strange carving, Shin was certain they had fallen asleep in a corridor.

Somehow, they were beginning to feel as though they had spent more time inside than they could remember.

Neither Mai nor, especially, Lizbeth seemed concerned about their nudity anymore, and Shin had stopped paying attention to it as well. They had more pressing matters to worry about.

They could feel themselves changing, too, with the passing hours and days.

The days were no longer marked by the sun—which didn't exist down here—but by the accumulated fatigue in their muscles, the hunger that kept returning, and the brief periods of rest they managed to steal from the stone. Lizbeth seemed to know where to find food, but after they had traveled quite a distance, she began to grow somewhat more and more reluctant.

When another three days had passed, they had been trapped inside for almost a week. It might even have been longer but, by their count, they had spent at least six and a half days in the pyramid.

And that wasn't all. As the days passed, they had begun to feel increasingly strange. It wasn't depression, exactly, but an acceptance that what was happening was normal. Mai had mentioned it to him, and what they feared was that if they gave in to that feeling, and to everything else happening around them, they would eventually give up.

Lizbeth insisted that she hadn't given up. She had searched for ways out, but she had never ventured into that part of the pyramid.

Would the three of them spend the rest of their lives here?

And something else had changed.

Lizbeth felt it first.

In the preceding days, she had guided Shin and Mai with almost instinctive confidence. She had shown them which mosses were safe to eat, which insects to avoid, and where to find water. But now she was restless. Her body remained tense even while they walked, and her eyes kept scanning the passages, as if she expected something to emerge from the darkness at any moment.

She no longer walked ahead of them. Now she stayed close.

Sometimes she even took Mai's hand or grabbed Shin's arm without realizing it, as though she needed to make sure they were still there like in the first days.

Mai noticed.

"Liz…" she murmured once, gently squeezing her hand. "It's okay… we're here with you. We're not going anywhere."

Lizbeth didn't answer. But she didn't let go of Mai's hand, either.

Shin watched her in silence. He didn't like the change. Because it meant something simple.

They had entered a different part of the pyramid, and there was a chance she had never been here before.

Perhaps, once she had found shelter and learned how to survive, she had never wanted to venture farther inside. If she had shelter and food, why take the risk? But if that was the case, why had she never managed to leave through the entrance, which was more closer?

They had asked her, but she had made it clear that she had tried many times, only to find the entrance sealed every time. When she had looked on the verge of tears, they had decided not to bring up the subject again.

Still, Lizbeth's and Shin's instincts had saved the group on several occasions.

Twice, while passing through tunnels, they had come across two more of those red-headed serpents. They had avoided detection by mere inches, remaining perfectly still and holding their breath. Shin wondered whether these serpents had the same kind of stone inside them, but he had no intention of finding out at the risk of endangering the two girls.

On another occasion, Lizbeth had yanked him backward by the hair, then leaped toward them and covered their noses with her hands, trying to drag them away.

A cloud of green luminous spores was floating in the air.

Lizbeth warned them that breathing it in could cause lung problems. She had suffered from it several times herself.

On several occasions, Lizbeth had gone pale and dragged them away with even greater urgency. These disturbances came from certain openings in the floor, covered by black stones three meters in diameter. Each bore an engraving of something difficult to make out. Eyes and a mouth that seemed to emerge from wind or clouds.

The stones were covered in hieroglyphs, but they couldn't translate them.

Lizbeth tried to explain by drawing in the dirt, but what she described was almost as difficult to understand as the black stones themselves. Something enormous, apparently. She mimed it by hopping on one leg while making gestures as if it were attacking them.

Eventually, she gave up, looking dejected.

Shin and Mai reassured her and thanked her, promising they would avoid those things.

The corridors had changed, too.

The walls were no longer covered solely in cubes or repetitive symbols. Now the reliefs were more… chaotic. More difficult to interpret in some way.

In one gallery, they found a scene that covered an entire wall.

A city. Or what remained of one.

The structures were strange. Leaning towers, impossible geometries that seemed to stand at angles defying all logic. And above them… something was descending.

It had no clear shape. It was a mass.

A tangle of curved lines, scattered eyes, and appendages that seemed to follow no recognizable anatomy. It wasn't attacking with brute force.

It was simply there.

And the city was collapsing beneath its presence.

"It's not a creature…" Mai murmured, her voice low.

Shin didn't look away. "It is."

"But it has no shape…"

"Maybe that's its shape."

In another chamber, the floor was covered in carvings depicting enormous fissures splitting open the earth. Elongated figures emerged from them, stretching toward the sky as everything around them broke apart.

There were people, too. But they weren't fleeing. They seemed drawn toward the fissures.

As though the cracks weren't an act of destruction, but rather a call.

Lizbeth avoided looking for too long and tightened her grip on Shin's arm.

"Not… g-good…" she murmured. "Mind goes cloudy."

"I know," he replied quietly.

Farther ahead, they found another scene.

A sea. Dark and turbulent, and crowded with strange shaped ships. But those ships floated above the sea, as though suspended in the air. Beneath the water lay something the carvings never fully revealed, accompanied by inscriptions in a language Shin had never encountered in his memories and couldn't recognize.

Only fragments.

A silhouette far too large to belong to a single creature, stretching beneath all the ships at once, as if the entire ocean were its body.

Mai looked away. "I don't want to see any more of that…"

"Then don't look," Shin said, without any harshness.

Perhaps Lizbeth was right. Those things could play tricks on the mind if someone spent too long studying them. But he couldn't tear his eyes away. There was something about those carvings.

Lizbeth gently tugged at him. "No… go on…" she said, pointing ahead before turning toward another corridor.

"Let's go this way."

The skeletons.

They weren't part of the original pyramid. They were intruders.

Like them.

"More…" she said, barely audibly.

The architecture had changed.

Compassion. And urgency.

The crossing was slow. The silence was almost absolute, broken only by their hearts beating in unison, so loudly that they could hear one another's.

When they reached the other side, none of them spoke.

They passed through a new passage, which widened to about three meters across and ended at a dead end.

Standing before them was a tall, ancient double stone door beneath an enormous arch.

It had been carved from a different kind of stone than the rest of the pyramid. Its surface was smoother, more uniform, as if it didn't belong to the same structure.

Higher up, another carving ran along the arch. At its center was a triangle. On either side were depictions of places that looked identical at first glance, but proved different upon closer inspection.

On the left, mountains and a river wound through a forest, with a path leading toward the triangle.

On the right, there were mountains and forests, but also a surface that could have been a sea, covered in spirals beneath storm clouds.

Shin stared at the triangle. It was barely visible, but inside it were a square and a circle. Everything within it was outlined as though it were a labyrinth.

An inscription had been carved above the arch.

Shin brought the lamp closer.

They weren't strange hieroglyphs. The letters were Latin.

"Gravitas mentem sequitur."

Mai read it aloud in a whisper. "Gravity follows the mind…"

Neither of them spoke for several seconds.

They had probably spent seven days inside the pyramid.

And that door… seemed to be the first thing they had encountered that had been deliberately constructed to stop them.

Even though it was now slowly opening toward them.
