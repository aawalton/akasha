Chapter 109The tower door swung shut behind him and the yard opened up the way it always did at midday, loud and full and moving in every direction at once.Thorne stood at the top of the steps for a moment and let it settle into him before he went down. He read a yard the way he read a tree line. Not for danger first, though that came without asking. For balance. For where the weight of the place was sitting at this hour, and what would move it.A great deal had moved it lately. The Hoshin had come through the gate and stayed, and the settlement had swollen around them the way a river swells after rain, faster than the banks were built to hold. Where two weeks ago he could count the heads in the yard, now he could not. Carpenters worked a new longhouse frame near the eastern wall, their hammers falling out of time with one another. A line of women carried water from the well in shifts. Children he did not know the names of yet ran between the foundations of buildings that did not exist yet. The cookfires had doubled. The smell of them reached him on the steps, smoke and barley and something with too little meat in it.That last part he had noticed before he noticed anything else. The pots were thin. More mouths than there was flesh to fill them.He came down the steps and crossed toward the smithy, and that was when he saw Zelgra waiting near the corner of the storehouse, watching him come.She was not doing anything. That was the first thing wrong. Zelgra always did something. She leaned on tables, she folded her arms, she planted her hands on wood and let her weight say what her mouth had not gotten to yet. Now she stood with her thick fingers loose at her sides and her shoulders set in a way that did not match the rest of her, and when he met her eyes, she held them for only a breath before they slid off toward the carpenters and came back.He had hunted enough to know what it looked like when something large was trying to make itself small. It never worked. It only told you the animal was uneasy."Zelgra," he said, and let a smile come up easy. "How goes the mining?""Same." Her voice came out level. "Busy."He waited. She did not add to it. The drumming that her fingers usually did against a tabletop happened against the side of her own leg instead, once, and then she made it stop. Her eyes went to the well, to the gate, to the new frame on the eastern wall, and came back to him each time as though she kept losing the thread and finding it again.The silence stretched. He did not fill it. He had learned that a thing left quiet long enough will say itself."I have been below for a long while," she said at last. The words came slow, picked up one at a time the way she picked up ore to judge its weight. "Down the shafts. Day on day. The air down there is the same air, breathed over and over." She stopped, and her jaw worked, and she seemed to dislike how much she had already said. "I would like to be outside the walls. For a day. That is all.""We are running a hunt this afternoon," Thorne said. "Come on it."She blinked."We need the meat," he went on, before she could decide it was charity. "The pots are thin. You have seen the gate these past weeks. We have far more people than we had flesh to feed them, and the gap is widening." He tilted his head at her, the same way she tilted hers when she was pricing something. "You came to us through that forest alone. The story they tell is that you took down a monster single-handed on the way. A caribou the size of a cart, head and all, slung over your shoulder when you walked in.""It charged me." She said it as though that settled the matter, but the corner of her wide mouth had moved. "I did not go looking for it.""And it lost." He let the smile widen. "I have men who train for years and would not have made that swing. I would rather have you outside the walls than in the dark counting stone."For a moment she did not answer. Then something in the set of her shoulders eased, the small loosening of an animal that has decided the ground is safe to put its weight on."I would like that," she said. "I would like to blow the dust off my axe for a good hunt.""Within the hour," Thorne said. "Meet me at the main gate."She gave him a single nod, deep and final, the way she closed a deal, and turned back toward the storehouse with her stride already longer than it had been.He watched her go a moment. Then he crossed the yard toward the barracks, because there was another thing to settle before he could leave, and he could already hear it starting.If you discover this narrative on Amazon, be aware that it has been stolen. Please report the violation.The new soldiers were coming out.He reached the barracks yard as the last of them stepped into the light, and Valrik was already there, standing where he always stood, helm under one arm, his weight square. The level two infantry filed out behind him and fell into line without being told. They looked much as the level ones did, the same broad cut, the same plain armor catching the midday sun, only larger. A hand taller, maybe. Thicker through the chest and the forearm. The system had simply taken the same man and grown him.They fell into line for inspection and went still.Thorne came up beside Valrik and the two of them walked the line together. Valrik went first, slow, his boots scraping the packed dirt, and he stopped in front of each soldier in turn and looked hard into the man's face. He did not say anything. He stared into one and held it, and then moved to the next and held that one, his eyes going over each one the way a man checks a blade for a flaw he expects to find.None of them flinched. Not one. Every soldier kept his eyes forward and his jaw set, and the steel in each gaze matched the steel in the face around it, and when Valrik passed there was nothing in any of them that gave."This week's drills should be interesting with these additions," Thorne said.Valrik gave a short sound that was almost agreement.Thorne looked down the line once more. "We need to ready the troops for the next mission.""I will hand pick them myself," Valrik said."I can work with the forge to outfit these ones with the extra gear.""No." Valrik did not look away from the line. "I will handle it. I have been away too long. I want to get reacquainted with the army."Thorne understood that, and did not push it. A man came back from where Valrik had been and wanted the weight of the work back in his own hands. "Perfect. I will head out for a long needed hunt.""I have been meaning to talk to you about that." Valrik turned, and his face had its usual stillness, but his eyes were working on something. "It would be more efficient if we drilled the teams to watch for game. You could spend more time killing and less time locating targets. We need to be more efficient if we are going to feed all these new people."Thorne thought about that. It was sound. It was the way Valrik thought, the way the man broke a problem into men and motions and the spaces between them."Maybe we need another Thorne," he said.The corner of Valrik's mouth moved. Half a smile, gone almost before it arrived. "Maybe."Thorne left him to his line.He went to the main gate, and Zelgra was already there waiting, and she had made herself ready in earnest.She had tied her black hair back in a single thick braid, pulled hard off her face the way a person does when they mean to fight in it rather than be seen in it. Her light green skin had caught some color from the sun already. Her shoulders, broad to begin with, looked broader stripped of the merchant's coat, all the muscle of years of labor sitting plain on her now. The war hammer sheathed across her back. In her hands she held a spear, the haft fresh, the head clean, turning it slowly so the point caught the light.Thorne looked at her and thought, not for the first time today, that Zelgra might be worth more outside the mine than in it.They stood eye to eye, which few in the settlement could do with him."New addition?" he said.She paused a moment, then followed his eyes down to the spear in her hands. "Oh. You mean the spear. Yeah. I had the forge make me one. Thought I would try it out.""You ready to get your hands dirty?""Looking forward to it."Thorne smiled. Then the smile held still on his face, because something in the back of his attention had caught, the way undergrowth catches when a thing has moved through it that should not have."I got a message from Nicola, there might be activity due south," he said. "Not far from a logging crew we have running out there."He stopped."Wait. No. There might be trouble. We have to move."
