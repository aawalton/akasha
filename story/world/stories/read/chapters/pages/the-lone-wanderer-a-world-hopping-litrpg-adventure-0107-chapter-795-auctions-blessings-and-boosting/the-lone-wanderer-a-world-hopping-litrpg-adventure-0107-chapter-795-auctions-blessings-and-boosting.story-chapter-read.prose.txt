Percy’s clone returned from the Sapphire Pavilion a few days later.

He had once again failed to meet up with Obatala during his brief stay on Gallimus. While the titan had supposedly expressed his interest in receiving him personally, it appeared that he had still been busy with other matters.

Percy hadn’t been very surprised, nor offended by that. Even a decade was but a blink of an eye to a deity who had lived for millions of years. A single meditation session could easily last centuries, so it wouldn’t be strange if Obatala was still working on the same project that he had been working on during Percy’s last visit. Either way, Percy wasn’t in a rush. He’d eventually get the chance to chat with the titan if he kept hitching a ride to the Sapphire Pavilion’s venue once a year.

Aje had been chosen to escort another batch of Green-borns to the auction. Percy didn’t know if the goddess was given that task every year or if she had simply been sent a second time due to her familiarity with him.

Tiberius and Duwa had also been allowed to join the group. The former had hoped to meet up with his people if they managed to reach the venue safely, while the latter had simply jumped at the opportunity to spend time with an old friend. Percy had possessed a new host, so as to pay the Gallimians back for their assistance by granting another Green-born the Moirais’ Decree.

The auction had been relatively uneventful. There had been no grand prize like the invitation to Mithril on offer, so the Attendants had only managed to attract a fraction of last year’s participants.

Percy had bought a single karmic compass which led to a planet that he was equal parts excited and terrified to visit. It wasn’t an exclusive compass, and there had been as many as ten copies sold in the same auction, so winning one had only cost him a few dozen tokens.

The lesser spring it pointed towards didn’t seem to have any valuable resources. Even more strange was the fact that the compass had been auctioned off by none other than the natives of that planet. Most people dreaded the idea of leaking the coordinates of their homeland to outsiders, but the residents of the lesser spring had been driven by desperation to do precisely that, consequences be damned.

Their world was apparently infected by a moderately powerful demonic infestation. It wasn’t a Cardinal Devil, and the local deities claimed to have done a decent job containing the corruption, so their planet seemed unlikely to meet the same fate as Amenthes or Robari.

However, the gods hadn’t managed to eradicate the infestation. The best they’d been able to do had been to keep pushing it back whenever it reared its ugly head, buying themselves time and temporary relief. Eventually, they’d decided to request help from outside.

Since the planet didn’t possess anything worth stealing, they hoped that other factions would either help them out of pity, treat the infestation as a training opportunity for their demigods, or leave them alone entirely.

Percy obviously didn’t think that he was capable of saving an entire planet either, nor did he expect to gain any tangible treasures from the trip. However, he wouldn’t mind a chance to finally put his Saint’s Eye and Ningirima’s Mercy to the test.

His most recent brush with corrupted creatures had occurred during the special event in the second round of the void tournament, and he hadn’t been very satisfied with his performance.

If he ever hoped to rescue Marnok from the Cardinal Devil, Percy couldn’t ignore an opportunity to sharpen his tools against a relatively weaker and safer infestation. While he didn’t know whether the sailor was even alive at this point, and while he had more urgent matters to worry about than his eventual return to Robari, Percy understood that he would inevitably encounter more demonic infestations in the future. Being able to fight against them effectively was an important skill to develop regardless.

Other than his dirt-cheap purchase, Percy had rounded his spending up to another five thousand tokens in common affinity-granting treasures for the rest of the nobles who had stuck with him. The alliance might no longer be as useful as he would have liked, but he wanted to reward his subordinates for their loyalty and compensate them for their losses, even if most of them had only remained by his side due to the terms of their spectral contracts.

Finally, he had asked Aje whether her people could escort him to additional branches of the Sapphire Pavilion. Unfortunately, she had explained that this was the only venue close enough to Gallimus.

Percy guessed that he could probably purchase a karmic compass leading to other branches directly from the three-handed Attendants, though that wouldn’t exactly solve his problem.

He’d need to possess a dying mortal located either at or on the way to the second venue. Replicating the circumstances that had allowed him to establish a regular transit to the first branch would be a tall order, as he was unlikely to find another survivor of divine banditry like Tiberius and a trustworthy acquaintance to vouch for him among the attendees like Duwa.

In the end, Percy had been forced to accept that he would only be able to participate in a single auction per year for the foreseeable future. It was better than nothing, as it still provided him with a steady – if somewhat slow and unreliable – flow of karmic compasses and affinity-changing treasures.

Once he was done assimilating his counterpart’s memories, Percy sent out another soul clone on a random adventure. He was afraid to mess with the wise gnomes at the moment, and his other compasses didn’t sound like they would help him in the short-term either, so he decided to keep focusing on his new affinity for the next year.

Two months later, he was pleasantly greeted by an expected but somewhat early notification.

[Congratulations! You have acquired a new blessing: Blessing of Frost!]

‘Already? How many droplets did you use?’

‘Only around three hundred,’ the Huehuan replied while metaphorically scratching the back of his head.

Percy frowned, feeling like a complete idiot. They’d suspected that Micky needed less Clear mana than the Violet Tenefyrians, but this had been even faster and cheaper than expected. They could have saved a lot of time by cutting that trip short. Then again, they hadn’t been using their soul clones too efficiently lately, so it didn’t really matter.

‘Store the rest in the seal,’ Percy instructed his friend. ‘I’ll hand them to Nesha the next time I see her.’

Pulling up his Status, he examined the blessing section once more.

___

Blessings [3/5]:

___

‘Simple and straightforward, like Zoris said,’ Percy acknowledged with a faint smile.

‘What should I do next?’ Micky asked. ‘I could try replicating the Thess’kalan ritual now that my body’s made of more potent mana.’

Percy considered it for a few seconds. ‘Perhaps you should spend a couple of days getting used to the changes first. Also, it might be better to wait until you get a mutation from your third affinity as well.’

‘That’s not a bad idea, but I’ll need to awaken my new core before my beast affinity reaches Violet, otherwise we’ll have to wait until White for the mutation,’ the Huehuan pointed out.

Percy nodded. He would have to spend a handful of years granting new cores to the Starry Knights, and he hadn’t even started yet. He’d rather complete the first steps of his training with the mind affinity before investing most of his waking time possessing wasps. At least he was fairly confident that Micky’s first core still needed more time than that to advance.

‘Go back to practicing with your sentries,’ he suggested. ‘I’ll lend you an extra mind or two to accelerate your training as soon as I’m done upgrading my mindset.’

Other than his training with Nephthys, Percy had already mastered Circulation with his third core, though not at a level where he could maintain it effortlessly. That didn’t bother him too much, because he knew it was only a matter of time until he got there. Between the Insomnia trait and his mind clones, keeping the spell perpetually active should be easy enough, even if it still required a modicum of attention.

Unsurprisingly, the mind pattern followed the shape of his nervous system, manifesting as a network of thin, branching lines that spread to every corner of his body. The pathways were asymmetrically concentrated inside his head where his brain was, though his skull mostly obscured the glow.

The orange light of his augmented mana was only visible at a circular spot in the middle of his forehead where his core pressed against a slightly thinner layer of bone. It made Percy look like he had a third eye while the spell was active – which would hopefully be forever. It also made him resemble Nephthys, funnily enough, due to the pyramid-shaped gem lodged above and between her compound eyes.

Percy might have to rely on his Cloak’s hood or go out in public even less often than usual if he wanted to keep his new affinity a secret. Some people might eventually wonder why he wore a hood or a bandana wherever he went, but nobody would guess that he was hiding a third core just from that.

Hopefully, he’d be able to maintain the element of surprise until his clash with the Great Houses.

After freeing up one of his mind clones and redirecting his efforts toward mindset creation, Percy only left a single mental pocket in charge of the external part of the boosting art. With any luck, it would only take him a few more months to master that as well, after which he could start constructing the seals for the Cradle.

Percy tried to supercharge his Enhanced Cognition with his excess mana, though the mass-produced mindset refused to accept it. Apparently, it was too poorly designed for even a basic function like that.

Sighing, Percy went right back to work – or rather, it might be more accurate to say that one of his minds simply rejoined the other eight. His minor failure only motivated him to upgrade Enhanced Cognition that much sooner.
