The pain had taken my whole body.

Then the pulses came slower.

I knew this part. I had been through it more times than I could count. The body giving up. The world thinning.

The darkness took me.

When my eyes opened, there was no dawn.

There was no light at all, at first. Only a sense of standing upright on ground I couldn't see.

Then the sky resolved.

Stars.

More than I had ever seen, in either life. They filled the black from edge to edge. There was no moon. No clouds. No horizon where the sky ended and anything else began.

I looked down.

Mist covered my feet. Pale. It rolled outward in every direction without thinning, without rising above my ankles. I couldn't tell whether I stood on stone or on nothing.

Not the river.

The last time something like this had happened, there had been a black river and a skeleton grinning over it.

I turned.

A table stood a dozen paces away.

The surface was smooth and dark, worn at the edges like something that had been touched by hands for a very long time. It sat alone in the mist under the stars.

Eight chairs, seven occupied.

The figures sat very still. Every face was hidden behind a mask.

One of them I knew.

A skull. Bone-white, the grin too wide, the eye sockets filled with two gold coins.

Pride.

Hatred came up my throat so fast it tasted like bile. My jaw locked. My heels dug into ground I still couldn't feel.

I walked to the table.

Seven masks turned to follow me. The eighth chair waited between a figure in a heavy dark coat and a slender one in white. I put my hand on the back of it and pulled it out.

Mana Perception opened before I reached the chairs.

The pressure hit me all at once.

I could not measure them. There was too much. Mana filled every part of them, dense beyond anything I had thought a body could contain. The Sultan had been a storm behind an open gate. Beside these seven, he was weather on a distant horizon.

My hand locked around the chair. The pressure bore down on every part of me, vast enough that for one absurd moment I expected my bones to give.

This was not a difference of Circles. It was another order of existence.

They felt more like gods in human form.

Across from me, one of the masked figures had its face pressed flat against the table. A plain mask without features I could make out from this angle. Its shoulders rose and fell in a slow rhythm. A soft, rasping snore came from beneath it every few seconds.

Nobody seemed to find this remarkable.

"Welcome, Kaspar."

The skull's jaw didn't move. The voice came anyway, exactly as I remembered it.

I looked at the coins where its eyes should be.

"Where am I?"

"The end of time."

The skull inclined slightly, as if that explained everything.

"A refuge. A place set aside for those who have earned a seat at this table. Nothing reaches it. Nothing leaves it unless we permit."

"Then why am I here?"

"To talk."

The voice came from my left.

A woman's voice. Warm. Close enough that I felt it against my ear before I heard it in the air, the way I had felt it on the ridge.

I turned.

The slender figure in white sat with her hands folded in her lap. Her mask was blank porcelain, smooth and featureless, with one exception. Where the mouth would be, a single mark. The print of painted lips.

Take my hand.

"Lust," I said.

"Yes."

I hit the table.

My right fist came down on the stone with everything I had, and the impact went up my arm and into my shoulder, and the whole table jolted. One of the silent masks flinched. The snoring stopped for a single breath, then resumed.

I looked down.

The hand was whole. Five fingers. The ring finger and the little finger curled against my palm where they belonged. Above them, the wrist, the forearm, the elbow, the arm. Skin without a mark on it.

I flexed the fingers.

They obeyed.

It meant nothing. I understood that even as I stared at it. This was not my body.

I pointed at her.

"You," I said. "You fucking knew."

The words came out in a rush.

"I was supposed to go back."

My voice cracked on the last word.

"You knew."

I stood.

The chair went over behind me. It made no sound when it hit the mist.

"You made sure I'd live through it. You made sure there was no way back."

I put one foot on the table.

Seven masks watched me climb.

"You killed him. You didn't hold the blade but you killed him, and you made me watch—"

"Sit."

The chair was under me again.

There was simply a moment where I was on the table with my hand reaching for a white mask, and a moment after where I was seated, upright, my palms flat on the stone and my spine pressed hard against the chair back.

I strained.

Nothing gave.

Lust unfolded her hands and laid them on the table in front of her.

"You are the one who made sure you would live through it," she said.

I stared at her.

"You chose Body Reforging. You forced mana through your body until flesh and bone remade themselves. You chose to become harder to kill. Every time you grew stronger, you moved a little further from death, and made the return you wanted harder to reach."

"So this is my fault?"

"The reason you survived is your own strength. The reason your return failed is that you did not die." The warmth was still in her voice. It made everything worse. "What happened to him was a misfortune. I am sorry for it. I know that means nothing to you now."

"It means less than nothing."

"Bringing you here was necessary."

I said nothing.

She tipped her head toward the snoring mask.

"Death would have sent you back. But this place—"

She lifted one hand and turned it, palm up, toward the stars.

"—does not open for the strong. It opens for the desperate. For the moment when a soul has nothing left and no way back. That is the only door. That is the only state in which you were ready to come here."

"So you arranged it."

"I took the chance when it came."

"Go to hell."

"Kaspar—"

"No. You've said your piece. Here's mine." I leaned as far forward as the force would let me. "I'm done. I'm done listening to any of you. Every single thing that has gone wrong since I woke up in that fucking cave came from listening to you."

I looked at the skull.

"You. You gave me a countdown. Grenzheim. Elves. A hundred and eighty days. So I go south. Because of you, I saw humans crossing no-man's-land. And because they live, the ritual happens, and the grid drops, and the elves come across, and half of Wendt's people die and I end up on a beach watching children drown in cages."

The coins gleamed.

"If I hadn't gone south, I'd never have been in that trench. There's no ritual. No attack."

"Because of that attack, I become a Knight. I go to Qalathar. I burn Selim's warehouse to the ground, and because I do, an alchemist in the desert loses the only buyer he had, and two weeks later an Imperial officer walks into his life and puts him in a room with a chained Titan."

My voice dropped.

"Hayyan never touches that aquifer if Selim's alive. The Titans would never come. This whole shit only happened because I did what you warned me about."

Silence around the table.

The skull laughed.

It was a soft sound. Almost fond.

"Yes," it said. "You were a puppet."

I stared at it.

"You expected me to deny it?" The gold coins tilted. "You went where you were pointed. You struck what you were shown. Every step you have described is true. I could add a dozen you have not noticed."

"Then—"

"But you misunderstand the strings." The skull leaned forward a fraction. "The five vessels were bred for that hill long before you drew breath in that body. The elves built that ritual because it was time. Had you let those five die in the mines, another five would have crossed a month later, at a different sector, and there would have been no one in the trench who could read a human mana signature and no one on the hill with a sword."

I opened my mouth. The skull didn't stop.

"The aquifer. Yes. Selim's death gave Sigrid her alchemist. Had you not killed Selim, she would have found another. A year later. The Princess began that project because the war is ending, boy, and she intends to hold the leash when it does. The Titans were always going to walk toward Qalathar. You made them walk early. You made them walk while there is still a Sultan on that wall who can be reasoned with and a man in the Empire building a weapon that might break their hide."

The coins caught the starlight.

"Everything you set in motion was already moving. You did not cause it. You accelerated it. The floods arrive sooner, yes. They arrive while humanity still has something left to meet them with. Every year of delay would have been a year of decline. Wait long enough and there is no Kaspar at the wall. No Iron Cross that can hold a ridge. No one left to be a puppet at all."

"You're saying I should thank you."

"I am saying you were useful. Just as we are useful to you."

I sat back.

I looked at the skull and its coins, the white porcelain, the gray one drooling on the stone, and the four who hadn't spoken.

"Why," I said, "should I listen to a single word any of you say?"

Nobody answered.

"You sit at the end of time. Nothing reaches you. Nothing leaves." I gestured at the stars, the mist, the impossible quiet. "You have nothing to lose. Humanity burns and you watch it as a spectacle."

I looked at Lust.

"You don't care. None of you do. You act like gods. You look down from very far away and you see your little rat running through the maze, and when it turns the right corner you laugh, and when it dies you bring it back to the start."

I stood again. This time nothing pushed me down.

"I'm not your rat. And I'm not going to spend the rest of my life pretending you're trying to help me."

Lust rose from her chair.

The movement was so fast that the porcelain mask blurred. Her hands hit the table where mine had, and the stone rang.

"Go to hell yourself."

The warmth was gone.

"Nothing to lose. Nothing to lose. You sit in that chair for one hour and you decide you understand this table. You have no idea what any of us went through to be here. Not one of us was born at the end of time. We came the same way you did. Bleeding. Alone. With every person we ever loved dead behind us and no door back."

Her voice shook.

"You lost a friend and an arm today. One. I watched you break over it. Good. You should. Now imagine that for every seat at this table. Each of us watched everyone die."

She reached up.

Her fingers found the edge of the porcelain and pulled.

The mask came away.

Dark hair, pinned back for court. A sharp, symmetrical face I had last seen on the tribunal dais, watching from above while her Magister tried to hang me.

Princess Elara stared at me across the stone.
