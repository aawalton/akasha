Day 372, 1:45 PM

The outer gods have stayed true to their promises. A massive wave of minor abominations rushes down the tunnel as soon as we reach the first floor. Edna’s army of treants blocks their path, and it’s time to part ways.

“I want to come with you!” she says, and she means it. She really wants to die fighting the invaders.

“No. Please,” my voice shakes, “stay alive and do everything in your power to find happiness, whatever happiness means for you.”

“It would make me happy to kill them.” She’s telling the truth, but such pettiness should be beneath her.

“You can do much better. I need you to live for the both of us.” I smile an honest but sad smile. “We killed them twice already, that should be enough.”

She opens her mouth to protest, but I kiss her. A dumb thing, I shouldn’t have done it, and yet she doesn’t push me away while Gila’s frown conveys her distaste. Finally, I break away. The clock is ticking, and I have a deadline.

“Stay alive, and if you don’t mind, watch over Lucy.” I wanted to ask her to show me her real face, the unaltered version from her youth, but I don’t have the strength. I might abandon everything if I discover she really is Manny. To hell with the world and the greater good. We could live in eternal loops.

Edna nods, and I’m sprinting through the hatchery, heading down to the next floor. As I descend deeper, beyond the third floor, I notice each floor has several small creatures weaker than minor abominations. The creatures transport eggs, bring chunks of food for the hatchlings, and as I dive deeper into the abyss, they transport larva between floors.

I guess the abominations ate them in the two previous runs, but that’s the extent of my focus. Danger sense spell remains silent, and my thoughts drift to my life on Everrain. What did I do? What was wrong and what was right? What are the implications of redos, and how does something as massive function? I don’t know anything beneath the power of a god which would force back time for an entire universe, so the eldritch creatures might be correct, I am a god in the making.

That comes with a serious responsibility, and the first question I need to tackle is how I will treat those around me? Exploiting and hurting them just because I have the power is distasteful; I would become the kind of bastard I was striving to eliminate from the world. And after the whole thing with Edna potentially being Manny, I will never find peace. Every person I meet might be Manny’s shadow. Her frail, defenseles reincarnation.

That would mean each person I harm could be the one I love, but I will need to set boundaries. Whom I can kill, whom I can’t. What about human experimentation? I need a solid limitation there, otherwise, whatever lines I lay, I would cross them at one point, given a strong enough incentive.

So, no human experimentation. None at all. That is the only boundary I can know I won’t be able to push by saying there’s no difference between A and B.

I use the nearly seventeen hours of descent to formulate my moral code. I forgot the Tarantulaman’s uncle’s name, but he’s right, great power means great responsibility if you don’t intend to become a tyrant. And with my power I could easily become a tyrant, but that runs both against my nature and my class. Anarchy assumes a world without rulers, one in which people are decent human beings and everyone does what they need for the betterment of mankind as a whole.

Will I be able to create such a world? Is creating one through policing everyone missing the point? I’m not certain. On one hand, it creates a higher power, on the other, I’m just a human being doing what I can for the betterment of my species. Am I human?

I would like to think such philosophy is beyond me, but my intellect and wisdom both can devour, deconstruct, and digest all those simple questions in the blink of an eye, and the answer I have is horrible in its simplicity - ‘it depends’. Everything depends on the context, the situation, and the parties involved.

I wonder whether that’s a universal response, or one generated by my limited intelligence blown out of proportions. What would the outer gods say? Would they have a concept of mercy? Considering they wish to devour a whole world, my money is on them not having it.

Which brings my thoughts back to them. What kind of society do they live in? Do they have a society? Are they like solitary beasts, sailing the void, as they called it, looking for morsels? Does that make them any different from fish in an ocean? What makes us any different?

So many questions, and no answers.

I step past the final hatchery, everything operating as if I’m not here, the workers and the larvae ignoring me. Without the threat of invasion, the eggs produce a variety of creatures - moths, worms, centipedes, caterpillars, spiders, and countless others.

The chamber beneath is full of dead specimens, kept in stasis by magic rather than vats and such mundane implements. Hadriuse is only partially covered in gold, and the crack at the center isn’t gushing with as much golden light as it was the last time we entered.

“Welcome,” Hadriuse said in the otherworldly duet. “Please, sit.”

A pair of chairs and a table materialize between us. “Do you mind if I take a look at your real bodies?”

Hadriuse’s head tilts in a way which would have snapped human necks. He smiles, revealing all fangs, and his eyes turn black, with more eye-slits opening all over his face.

“We guess you can try,” the duet says after the transformation doesn’t even make me blink. “Be warned that peering into our forms might break mortal minds. We don’t exactly conform with the anatomies and the concepts of space you find natural.”

I nod and slowly follow Hadriuse towards the rift.

“What kind of pact do you have in mind? How does it work? Can it really bind you into fulfilling your end of the bargain?”

“Well, no, there is no form of contract which can enforce something, only penalize the breaking of conditions. All we need to do is make the penalty severe enough for both parties, ensuring they wouldn’t dare act in bad faith. And even if they do, the other party would benefit more than they would lose.”

“It’s a fairly standard procedure,” the other voice chipped in through the same mouth, while the first one was still talking.

“You mentioned the seventh law of magic. How many are there?”

“Twelve, but more details after the pact is sealed. It will mark you across time and space. Until such measures are in place, it’s a bad practice to make agreements with time hoppers.”

Another piece of information, eldritch pacts stick across time.

“Did the world lose some of the energy it had or did it come to any sort of damage because I was looping time back? Do I need to compensate you for that in any way?”

“No damage other than annoying us and any others who can see beyond time in this small dimension.”

That’s another good piece of information. Other than moral implications, there are no consequences of redo loops. However, they are a dead giveaway for any eldritch entities living in the same universe as myself. The good thing is that they have no definite way of knowing I’m in the same world as them.

But what of my friends and family who get to live after I’m gone? Are those years an illusion or something they really experience? Does it impact their souls?

The golden crack is but two steps away, Hadriuse stops and gestures towards it with an evil inhuman smile on his lips. I check Redo. Red, six hours twenty minutes remaining. I guess now is as good a time as any.

I don’t dare look through the portal, the eldritch creatures obviously believe my sanity would snap from seeing them, and I have no reason to play their game. Mana within my body stirs, a song fills my ears, even if I don’t voice it.

It’s one of a dark whirlpool, spinning and sucking in countless people, all of them are me. The other visualisation is one of a heart, a powerful heart beating, determined to see the end of time. The heart, so full of power and potential, bursts.

My chest explodes and blood gushes out of my mouth. I can see shock written across Hadriuse’s face, then we all explode. Griff Rider, archmage Hadriuse turned puppet, and the vile gate out to devour Everrain, all three disappear in a blink.
