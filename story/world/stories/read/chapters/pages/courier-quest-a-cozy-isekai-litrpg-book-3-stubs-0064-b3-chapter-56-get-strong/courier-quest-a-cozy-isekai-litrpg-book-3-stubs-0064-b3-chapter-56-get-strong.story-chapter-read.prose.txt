Trevor’s screams didn’t last after the first landing. He had seen the gouge created by the yeti’s sudden stop and had thought the worst, but it turned out to be a non-issue. Despite being manhandled in such an unexpected way, Hans’s fur was surprisingly soft and they didn’t hit the ground with nearly as much force as he expected. In fact, it was an almost gentle ride.

It didn’t stop the fact that he had essentially been kidnapped, but the little things helped him keep calm.

Hans continued jumping towards the peak. At one point he had to leap straight up, grab a rocky protrusion jutting out from the cliff face, then climb the rest of the way. He did so with expert precision. His feet worked with the same level of dexterity as his hands, so he didn’t even have to let Trevor go.

Even as the air got colder and thinner, the courier’s body didn’t register the change. He knew that, logically, it must have been inhospitable, but the magic Shaman Brown had put on him was working wonderfully. It continued even as they passed through the clouds and emerged into the sunlight, damp but unharmed.

Trevor’s breath hitched as he caught sight of what lay above. It looked like something out of a fairy tale. A radiant palace composed of a vibrant blue ice sat atop the mountain. Tall spires rose into the air on its four corners, and a path of stone led to its open gates. They had bypassed the normal way up with Hans’s leaping charge.

He took a moment to check his bond with Clay. They were far away and that dulled the sensation, but they were feeling alarmed. Trevor frowned. Hans had made his familiar worry and he wasn’t sure how his wife was faring, either.

“Welcome to dungeon,” the yeti said as he landed in front of the palace. “Should get quest. Usually now.”

Even I didn’t expect this, but it doesn't matter. You’ve received this quest automatically for gazing upon the dungeon of Froslan Peak.

Inside is one of the coldest places in the known world. You have been warded against it, but I come bearing a warning. Shaman Brown’s spell only protects you from environmental factors. Take that as you will.

“Yeah, I got the quest,” Trevor said as Hans gently set him down. He put his hands on his hips as he looked up at the yeti, doing his best impression of a disappointed Adabelle. “And that was incredibly rude of you. It’s not okay to kidnap me.”

The other Summoned tilted his head. “Not kidnap. Compete. Fight dungeon, man to man.”

“Did I tell you that I was ready to go?” the courier asked sternly.

Hans frowned. “No.”

“Did I tell you that I wanted to go?” he continued.

“No,” the yeti answered, lowering his head.

“Or that it was okay for you to touch me? Carry me? Jump with me?” Trevor finished.

“No,” Hans said slowly before pointing over the courier’s shoulder. “But, dungeon! Excitement! Can compete now.”

“I’m not going in there without my party,” the courier retorted, crossing his arms. “We are on this trip together, and they were very excited about going into the dungeon.”

“Warrior girl and rock.”

“Yes, warrior girl and rock,” he confirmed with a nod. “She and I just got married, and we’re on our honeymoon together.”

Hans grunted, looking between the dungeon, Trevor, and the path down the mountain. He appeared to be conflicted. As if he didn’t know what to do. The courier wasn’t even sure if the yeti realized he had done something wrong, just that someone was mad at him.

“Okay,” he said, looking back at Trevor. “Hans sorry. Can see where wrong. No permission, shouldn’t take. Sorry. Can forgive?”

The courier stood up a little straighter. Perhaps he did realize the errors of his ways. With a smile, Trevor reached out and patted the big guy on the arm. “I forgive you this time, just make sure it doesn’t happen again. I’ll need a minute or two, okay?”

“Waiting. Okay,” Hans agreed.

“Man, if only Adabelle had the other half of the Two-Way Blackglass. She could just use the Couples Cube to call me,” he mumbled under his breath.

Just as he said that, something new entered his inventory. Trevor smiled as he retrieved a piece of parchment from the space. Its ink wasn’t even dry yet. He was glad his wife was as smart as she was beautiful, and he read it quickly before turning to Hans.

“I’ve been told that Mayor Nester wants you to return right this moment,” he informed the yeti.

Who tilted his head again, confused. “Huh? How know that?” Hans asked. Trevor showed him the parchment, and the yeti squinted at it with a grunt. “No can read.”

“You… can’t read?”

“No. Never learned.”

“Wait, really?” the courier asked incredulously, arching an eyebrow. “Then how do you interact with the Quest Master?”

“Show Hans pictures. Answer based on that. Get strong,” he announced proudly, pounding a fist against his chest.

Trevor frowned sadly up at the yeti. The more they spoke, the more he realized that the guy was just a big puppy focused on the exciting, shiny thing in front of him. Prone to giving in to impulse rather than thinking things through. Even though the village seemed to have a positive view of him, how much did they have to change to accommodate his presence?

Sighing, the courier put his hand down. He retrieved a pen from his inventory and wrote a note telling them that he was safe before returning the parchment to the section that Adabelle could reach. It disappeared a few seconds later. That Couple’s Cube was going to be handy.

But for now, he made a decision.

“Do you want to learn to read?” Trevor asked, looking back up at the yeti.

“Want to,” he sulked, flexing his fingers against the icy ground. “Can’t. Tried learn. Too hard. Don’t like letters. They mock Hans.”

“And if I could teach you how to read magically?” the courier offered. “All you would have to do is not break a magical item.”

Hans stood up a little straighter. “Trev teach Hans how read?” he asked, strangely hopeful.

“A little bit, yeah. It’ll—”

That was all it took for the yeti to sweep him up into a giant hug. Trevor’s back cracked from the force, though he was too sturdy now for it to injure him too much. Regardless, he was very happy when he was released.

“Is that a yes?” Trevor asked, coughing a bit.

“Please please, and thanks,” Hans said before sitting down in front of the courier like a kid waiting for story time.

“You’re welcome,” he said before removing his necklace.

The metal fish pendant hung an inch below the black cord it was magically bound to and had three orbs slotted into it. Trevor had long since absorbed the languages the spheres had imparted on him, and only wore the item because he liked it. The Language Fish had been very good to him, but he now knew enough that he would be comfortable almost anywhere.

Hans scooted forward and watched closely as Trevor removed the orbs. “This is called the Language Fish. It will allow you to learn and, more importantly, remember how to read,” he explained as he worked. “Each orb is keyed to a different language, and I’ve got six of them. The message I received was written in Lucern, which is my home’s native language, but that won’t really help you.”

“Okay,” the yeti said. Trevor wasn’t sure if he retained all that.

He nodded anyway. “I’m going to set you up with Mercantile, the language of merchants, and then both Dwarven and Elven. They’re ancestral rather than belonging to any one country, but they're very common as a base. When we get back, I’ll talk to Mayer Nester and see if there’s a better one we can use, and I’ll leave the other orbs with him.”

“Okay. Sounds good,” Hans said, nodding.

When Trevor offered him the Language Fish, the yeti lowered his head expectantly. “Yeah, you probably would break the string on contact, wouldn’t you?” the courier said before putting it on the other Summoned.

Like most magical items, it grew to accommodate Hans’s larger size. When he straightened out, the fish pendant disappeared right into his fur. There was a big grin on his face.

“Hans read now,” he grunted, though it almost had the feeling of a giggle. “Hans smart. Can read.”

“Well, you can read when you get back. I told them it would be a moment when I wrote back to them, but you have to go.”

The yeti tilted his head. “How told them? How get words?”

“I’m a messenger, so I can send and receive messages,” he answered, simplifying the reason for him. There was no telling how much he would understand about an inventory. “It said that you must return to Froslan Heights, otherwise you’ll be denied fruit for a week straight. You also have to—”

“Oh no!” Hans yelled, standing up and putting his hands on his head. “No fruit? Hans love fruit!”

“Yes, but—”

In his panic, the yeti stood up and, without preamble, jumped backwards off of the cliff. Trevor sucked in a breath as he watched the other Summoned disappear into the clouds. He heard a crash seconds later from the landing, and shook his head.

“You were supposed to take me with you,” the courier sighed, turning around to look at the palace. The gates to the dungeon were wide open, almost as if trying to invite him in. “Yeah, I know better than to go in there alone.”

Summoning a recliner from his inventory, Trevor sat down and admired the scenery. There were more mountains peeking through the clouds, but not many. None of them were as tall as the dungeon’s, nor did they have the majestic blue ice whether in palace form or not.

His admiration came to an end as he heard footsteps coming from behind him. At first, Trevor sank deeper into his chair to hide. He closed his eyes, took a deep breath, and tried not to think about the fact that he was alone. After all, he’d gone up against elementals, giant turtles, and pirate ghosts. He could handle an ice golem.

And, if not, the Feather of the Graceful was always an option. There was plenty of space to glide out there.

The initial rush of fear passed as he went through his options just as Adabelle had drilled into him. Knowing that he couldn’t be seen even if his recliner was very out of place, Trevor summoned a mirror from his inventory. He used it to get a good look behind him, trying not to make any part of himself a target.

A blue ice sculpture walked towards the gates. It had to have been at least twelve feet tall. It was a monstrous humanoid with an open, fanged maw and hollowed out eyes. Despite looking like it was made of a solid piece, it moved completely unhindered. The only sound was its footsteps.

Two details seemed more important to Trevor than the others. The first was the long, spear-like icicle in the golem’s hands. It was currently at rest, as if the monster was on patrol, or perhaps it had heard Hans’ yell.

The second was the heart in the center of its chest. Unlike the rest of its ice, this was almost transparent with a white outline. A visible cold emanated from it, which was saying something considering where they were.

Steeling himself, Trevor slipped forward off of the recliner and, while crouching, changed into his armor. Fighting this thing wasn’t going to go well for him, but he could at least try before escaping. He had the tools, it was just a matter of will.

That and stacking the odds.

“Quest Master,” he whispered, checking the mirror and trying to gauge how much time he had to work with. It wasn’t much, but it could be enough. “Very quickly, give me my skill options. Please.”
