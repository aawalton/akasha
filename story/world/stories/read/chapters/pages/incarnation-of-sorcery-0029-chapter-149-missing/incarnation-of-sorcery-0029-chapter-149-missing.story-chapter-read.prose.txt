Ryland thought that he’d have time to enjoy the ceremony. After how the last week or two had been so hectic, if not for him then at least for the institute itself, he figured they ought to get at least some form of rest.

But no. That was not to be. It seemed their students had decided to simply go up and take a hike.

Apparently, not of their own accord.

Ryland looked around after High Academ Zevo’s surprising revelation. Everyone was so busy setting up the ceremony to make it all look fun and wholesome. He could feel the Rhellians’ relief and excitement.

The streamers were colourful, the podium had been polished bright, and everyone was busy and happy taking care of last-minute preparations. They were all about to begin.

Apparently unaware that there was more trouble afoot.

“Can you explain the entire situation?” Ryland asked. “I find it hard to believe that some institute students simply… disappeared.”

As Zevo answered, Ryland listened intently as he described what the current situation really was.

It seemed that several students had been reported absent from a few classes. Upon further investigation, the institute administration had discovered that the students weren’t present anywhere on campus.

Naturally, this had led to a lot of concern. For there were no records of the students following the right procedures before sudden departures or even letting anyone know. It was almost like they had been kidnapped from thin air, though of course, the institute refused to consider that as a possibility.

“Is that a possibility?” Ryland asked, eyeing Zevo keenly.

“I highly, highly doubt it,” the High Academ said without a single ounce of hesitation. “Would you believe students could be nabbed from nowhere within Arcoryx Academy?”

Ryland cast [Translocate] and poked his hand through the little rift. “I wouldn’t call it an impossibility.”

Zevo sighed. “Mages of your calibre aren’t just rare, Marionys Ryland. They’re non-existent. Besides yourself, of course.”

“What else have you tried, then?”

The High Academ went on to explain the frantic search the institute had performed while ensuring no undue panic arose at the situation. As discreetly as possible, they had checked with the families of the missing students only to find they hadn’t returned home. There were no active field trips they could have accompanied.

They had also discovered proof of their departure. A few of the guards had seen furtive figures moving about, cloaked and hidden from recognition. Their builds matched that of the students’, however.

This was aside from the surveillance Glyphs the institute had checked.

Those discoveries were the main reasons they weren’t assuming some fantastical kidnapping of any sort. Sadly, their magical surveillance was limited to the institute’s boundaries. Anything beyond it was out of their purview.

For the time being, the institute had sent out search parties into the local neighbourhood but had yet to find any sign of the students or their destination.

“But it isn’t out of everyone’s purview,” Ryland said meaningfully.

The High Academ was smart enough to catch on to what he meant. Zevo cleared his throat, clearly uncomfortable at the notion. “The Great Demiurge knows all and sees all, yes. But he also knows when to intervene, if needed. We have already sent in a request for his divine attention. But in the meantime…”

“In the meantime, you’re hoping I’ll provide a clear answer and Mavar’s intervention won’t be necessary. Because you fear the consequences of it.”

Zevo frowned heavily. “There is nothing to fear!”

“If you say so.”

The High Academ didn’t add anything further. Ryland hoped it was less because he was miffed at the insinuation of the Demiurge being furious at the institute and more because there wasn’t anything further to add.

“Alright, then,” Ryland said. “I’ll see what I can find. Do you have any specific leads for me to look into?”

Zevo shook his head, though there was some clear hesitation there. “Not exactly. Except for a curious coincidence we’re not sure about. All the missing students are… well, they’re all the ones who had gotten sick with that illness you dealt with.”

Ryland blinked. “Ah, I see.”

“Then, as I suspect, there is some sort of foul play going on here, isn’t there?”

“I should think so, yes.”

Ryland caught Zevo’s mannerisms changing, his expression hardening with resolve and paling with fear at the same time. In all fairness, Ryland had revealed concerning information when he had dealt with the illness afflicting the students. A curse placed by a powerful mage. An “illness” that was actually a Tier [24] Inscription.

“No leads yet?” Ryland asked. “About how that Inscription came about?”

Zevo shook his head, shamefacedly looking down. “I’m afraid even the surveillance Glyphs were unable to properly locate who could have caused it. We were hoping to bring in more powerful investigators, but they would need the students, who have…”

His eyes widened at the same realization that Ryland himself had concluded.

“Yes,” he said. “It seems there may have been lingering effects from the Inscription that even I hadn’t foreseen. Ones that have caused the students to depart the institute before they could be fully interrogated.”

Zevo cursed lightly. “I don’t know where to begin. I was hoping you’d have some clue…”

Ryland decided to deliberate on the matter for a while. “Give me a moment. There are links here hidden from view, but if we can investigate them, then I believe we will find the source of all our problems.”

Viren dashed after his new friend. To any outside observers, this would look like a friendly game of tag. Two friends running after one another, despite maybe being a bit too old for such childish games.

If only it was something so simple.

“Hey!” Viren called out. “Tezar! Wait a moment.”

The older boy didn’t even look back. Viren had started following him almost ten minutes ago. At first, it had been a sneaky chase. For weeks now, Viren had been curious as to why his supposed friend acted so oddly at times and hesitated every time he was about to reveal something important.

His suspicions had heightened during the incident with the illness, where it had felt like Tezar knew something critical about the whole situation but was refusing to speak up about it.

That was so frustrating, Viren wasn’t even sure how he had allowed himself to bear it.

Especially since he wasn’t bearing it any longer. Not at all. He was determined to corner Tezar once and for all and determine just what in all the Realms had the older boy so scared. The mark of a hero was never letting anyone exist in such a state of panicked distress, and Viren wouldn’t stop until he had pulled off at least some of the burdens on Tezar’s shoulders.

A spell circle ignited on Tezar’s armoured robes. He was suddenly accelerating away as he rounded a corner.

“Hey!” Viren shouted. “Two can play that game, then. [Ignite]!”

Spell circles popped up on Viren’s back, emitting a stream of compressed heat that made him charge forward much faster. His new Tier [7] Glyph of Ignition was coming in handy. He rounded the corner, nearly as fast as Tezar, and found nothing there. The hallway was empty.

A suspiciously wide-open window caught his eye and he rushed over.

“Stop running, you idiot!” Viren called to Tezar hurrying away through the bushes two storeys down.

That was a rather tall jump. But Viren didn’t hesitate for even a second. He leaped over the windowsill and crashed into the bushes too, his fall slowed down just enough with another cast of Glyph of Ignition under his shoes. Then he was dashing away with blistering speed too.

Tezar really thought he could give Viren the slip. He was swiftly changing directions, dashing in between various pillars and through the alleyways between buildings. No matter how much Viren shouted, he refused to slow down, much less actually stop and listen to what Viren had to say. This was starting to get ridiculous.

At any other point in time, Viren could have tried to cut across his quarry and stop him that way. He had learned how, after all.

The Excursion Club had gone through exercises like this. Eternally chasing one’s target wasn’t very efficient and had a very high chance of the pursuit ending in failure. Instead, the club trained its members to cut around the target by predicting the path to be taken and putting an end to the chase that way.

Unfortunately, this required the pursuer to possess at least some level of informational superiority over the target. Viren was on entirely unfamiliar ground here. It was Tezar who held the advantage. If Viren tried to do something clever, he’d just get lost.

Thankfully, there was a different tactic that the Excursion Club had also taught—a second chaser.

Acrypha dropped out of nowhere to land right in front of Tezar. The boy cried out in surprise, unable to stop his momentum before he collided with Acrypha. It was very gratifying to see someone else bearing the brunt of feeling like they had collided with a solid column of rock.

“What in the—” Tezar was cursing as he got to his feet rather quickly. “Where did you come out of?”

Acrypha advanced on him with the implacability of a glacier. “Isn’t the more important question where are you running to?”

“I’m not running anywhere. You two are the ones trying to follow me!”

Viren was more than done with all this foolishness by now. He advanced, and before Tezar could think of doing anything even stupider, he had his hand on the older boy’s shoulder.

“That’s enough, Tezar,” he said. “Drop the act. We’re trying to help. You’re trying to run.”

“You don’t know what you’re dealing with!”

Viren squeezed the older boy’s shoulder harder. “Exactly! That’s why we’re trying to learn. Get that through your thick head, already!”

It didn’t look like they were going to get through to Tezar at all. Even with Acrypha at his back and Viren making sure he couldn’t run, the reluctance that Tezar seemed possessed with was combined with an obstinacy that honestly surprised Viren.

“Our plan worked last time,” Acrypha said. “We helped fix the illness. We proved we could do it, right? So why are you still running away?”

“That’s right!” Viren was having an incredibly difficult time not shaking Tezar. “What’s gotten into you? Even after we cured—”

“You didn’t!” Tezar pulled himself free from Viren with a rough tug. “You didn’t prove anything. You didn’t fix anything. You have no clue what you did, and now, because of you, they’re gone.”

“What?”

Acrypha came right up to Tezar, her expression turning stormy. “What do you mean they’re gone? Where did they go?”

Viren wasn’t even sure what Tezar meant. Was he saying that all the students Ryland had healed had simply… disappeared or something like that?

“I don’t know where,” Tezar said miserably. “I just know that they left. Maybe for good.”

“I don’t get it.” Viren shook his head. He could understand why Tezar was acting so weird, so concerned. So panicky. “Why would they run? Where would they go?”

Tezar shook his head. He was starting to look like he was regretting not going with the rest of them, that he might as well have joined the runaways and escaped from it all. Viren still felt like he really should shake the older boy, but his own worry about where all those students could have run off to was getting to him.

This could be bad. They had heard rumours that the illness was a lot worse than it had seemed, that it might have consequences none of them could foresee easily. Not even Ryland.

So was this new development related?

Tezar pulled free from both Viren and Acrypha like a spooked deer, once again intent on running away as fast as he could, but he didn’t get far. Ryland was coming down the other path. In the sudden onslaught of panic affecting them all, the power of the Glyph of Tranquillity he had cast was ensuring that the extremities of their feelings didn’t find any real purchase.

“Sir,” Viren said. “Were you here because you heard the news?”

“That the students are missing?” Ryland asked with a slight wince. “Yes.” His eyes landed on Tezar. “I recalled that you had a hand in getting me to assist with the last matter. So I assumed I could perhaps glean something from you about the current situation.”

Right. If Viren could see that Tezar clearly knew something that frightened him, then of course Ryland would have noted it too. He wasn’t as dogged as Viren about pursuing it, perhaps, but it didn’t necessarily matter. Ryland had a certain presence that ensured he wasn’t going to be rebuffed or denied that easily.

Especially not when he had a High Academ of the Rhellian Institute backing up his approach.

“I…” Tezar swallowed. He was still looking for a way out. His whole body screamed that he was still determined to run away, to make an escape before things spiralled out of his control. Before he said something that would put him in serious peril.

Something that the two far older mages appeared to realize.

“It’s alright,” High Academ Zevo said. “You don’t have to reveal anything you don’t wish to, Tezar. But we suspect you at least saw the defectors leaving, yes?”

Tezar grimaced at them being called defectors. “Yes, sir.”

“Did you see who they were leaving with?”

“I didn’t, sir. As far as I saw, they left by themselves.”

“Evading many of our surveillance Glyphs and most other attention whatsoever.” Zevo’s eyes had screwed in suspicion as he took a step closer to Tezar. Suddenly, Viren wanted to take a step forward too, to place himself between the High Academ and his friend. “Why didn’t you go with them?”

“I… why would I go with them?”

“Surely, they wouldn’t allow someone who knows all their secrets, someone who’s aware of everything, to remain behind as a liability, now would they?”

Tezar took a horrified step back. “I don’t know anything like that sir!”

Zevo didn’t approach. Didn’t decry Tezar’s actions. Viren was forced to wonder what all their reactions would have been had there not been a Glyph of Tranquillity shining down on them all.

“The High Academ was telling the truth,” Ryland said. “Despite his demeanour seemingly going against it. You don’t have to reveal anything you don’t wish to, Tezar. But if you have a lead, if you can spare anything to go off of, then we will be grateful.”

Viren couldn’t decide who he ought to shake in his current situation. Why was Ryland being so delicate about the whole matter? If Tezar really knew pretty much everything, then why weren’t they basically apprehending the boy and pulling out every bit of information they could use to address whatever was going on?

Of course, he didn’t want them to hurt Tezar. There were magical methods of extracting information without resorting to torture.

Viren had a feeling he was missing something here. Something vital and important. Maybe, once Ryland and Zevo were done, he could perform his own little interrogation of Tezar.

“I…” Tezar closed his eyes, then took a deep breath. “I don’t really understand this whole departure thing. I don’t know why they would leave now. But… I know they met someone before all this started. Before they became ill, that is.”

“Who was it?” Ryland asked. His physical reaction was more muted, but they were all leaning in, desperate to hear what Tezar had to say.

“It was a priest,” Tezar said. “Someone from an old cult. A ruined temple. He… he called himself a follower of the Recreator.”

“Ah,” Ryland said, straightening. “A strange location, but not one I’m unfamiliar with, at least.”

“Thank you,” Zevo said quickly. “The Temple to the Recreator. I wasn’t expecting that location either, but now that we have a lead, I will immediately begin investigating.”

Ryland nodded. “I will leave you to it, then.”

“What are you going to do?”

“I’m going to find your students first.”

“That’s what we intend to do!”

“Yes. But I don’t need to travel to a location to search through it, you see. [Omniscience].”
