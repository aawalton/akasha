Chapter 174 / 19: What the Yuk

While he waited for Yuk to finish building themselves back up, he mended his gambeson; his shirt was beyond repair.  He then looted the wights of their tokens.  Looking over the heavy, blue-metal discs, Joe spoke.

“Hey, Yuk?  Do you want two of these?”

‘Sure, but could you hang on to them for us.  One of the drawbacks of being what we are is we can’t use items.  We have a spatial storage skill, but since we don’t have a real body or a home, it’s always packed full.’

‘What do you mean you don’t have a home?’ Joe inquired through the link. This seemed like a personal question, and so the silent connection felt more appropriate.  ‘You live on the streets?’

‘Close. Under them mostly.  There are lots of cellars nobody uses.  Storm drains work too in the dry seasons.  We can’t be around people too much.  They would start asking questions about me, and that would be bad.’

‘You said you’d explain this to me at some point.  We aren’t going to get much more privacy than this,’ Joe sent, waving his arm to indicate the deep underground tunnel they were hiding in. ‘Is now a good time?’

‘Uhh.  I guess.’ Yuk replied hesitantly.  Joe felt a strange probing brush against his mind.  It was as if Yuk was trying to gauge Joe’s feelings.  Connected as they were mentally, it was almost a tactile sensation.  In turn, Joe could feel waves of worry flowing off the strange being.  Yuk was scared.  Scared that what he was about to say would turn Joe against them.

Behind that worry was a massive well of loneliness.  After Joe had lost his brothers and parents, he had felt bereft and unfettered.  That feeling of being alone in the world had been crushing, but he really hadn’t been fully alone.  Friends came by occasionally, he could volunteer in the children’s ward, and the hospital staff made sure to care for his sense of self as much as they did his failing body.

Yuk had nothing even close to that.  Even though they had the guild, they kept the members at a very long arm’s length away.  Even Myllo and Edror, with whom Yuk had shared his secret, were not close to the skittering raggamuffin.  Here was someone who had been completely alienated for their entire life.

Sensing their need, Joe shifted his position, carefully easing his way into the slowly gathering swarm.  The thousand tiny feet tickled everywhere, but Joe could feel Yuk calming.  This was as close as Joe could get to showing his acceptance of the odd youth, kind of like giving them a hug.

‘To start with, you have to know something about the first days of Illuminaria.  The early world was not nearly as complex as it is now.  Magic back then was vastly more powerful, but it was crude.  Nowadays, The One Above has refined power into thousands of precise skills and traits.  Back then, the beings could hurl around raw tempests of magic, but they never could use it to craft a pot or mend one.’

‘Sledgehammers versus scalpels?’ Joe prompted.

‘Exactly.  The heroes and monsters in the First Era were powerhouses of an order that the world has never seen again.  These are the figures of legend: Zhellex the Unkillable, Tybault the Mountainbreaker,  Char Magmakin.  They faced terrors of equal proportion: Salmara’roog, the First Dragon,  Thadun Hellforger, Ymurg Icebringer.

‘Among these ancient evils was Khimbar, the Great Devourer, the Ravenous Swarm, the Locust King.  They are what I am, but infinitely bigger.  Whole lands were stripped bare by the coming of the Great Devourer.  The Ravenous One was stopped only by an incredible sacrifice.  A man known only as Birdsing drew Khimbar into the Firelands far to the west with his melodious singing.  There among the lava-filled calderas, Char Magmakin embraced the pure essence of {Fire}, becoming a twin to the volcanoes around him.  Every living thing in the Firelands burned.  Birdsing was slain.  Char ascended to another form of existence.  Khimbar was wounded to their core.  But they survived.

‘Now the primordial terror sleeps for centuries at a time.  They wake, strip the world bare around them, and return to slumber once more.  Each sleep has been shorter than the last.  We have read that scholars foretell that someday the Locust King will fully awaken once more, and that may be the end of life on Illuminaria.

‘Even in sleep, the body of the primordial feeds on anything foolish enough to venture too close.  Including the dead.  It is said the only thing the Necromancer fears is Khimbar.   The sleeping primordial horror is what has thwarted the legions of the dead from expanding to the west.’

Yuk paused, and Joe slipped in a question. ‘So you are worried that because you are like Khimbar, people will freak out?’

‘It’s worse than that.  So, remember the story of Mira and the Falling Star.’

‘Where she has the vision to get her people out of Glandyrth before the Necromancer comes into power and destroys everything on the other side of the mountains?’ Joe asked.

‘Yeah, that one.  Well, the falling star was a real thing, not just a vision.  It landed in the Firelands, tearing up the Ravenous One even more.  That’s a good thing; means they will take longer to fully heal and awaken.  It also scattered bits of Khimbar all across the South Lands.  We think the rest of those pieces crept their way back to the Crawling King, but not us.  We were hurled far, far to the east, and the spirit of the Baerrok Mountains is very powerful.  It is strong enough to block all of the legions of the dead from crossing into the Hornlands.  It also blocked us from returning to the Locust King.

‘For over a hundred years, we were nothing more than a mindless, insane swarm, trying to get across those peaks, too stupid to think to go around them.  Then, about ten years ago, something changed.  It’s like we woke up.  We became aware of ourselves.  The first thing we did was fly as fast as we could away from those mountains and whatever beyond them that had been calling us for so long.  We didn’t know anything about Khimbar in those days.  We just knew something far away wanted to consume us.

‘Eventually, we ran into Edror.  The old guy has an ability to find rare monsters, and I was a unique being.  He almost killed me, but suddenly I knew how to speak, and we weren’t a monster anymore either.  We couldn’t talk well, but it was enough that Ed stopped and saved us instead of finishing us off.  He brought us back to Fort Coral and the Guild.  It took a while, but he and Myllo figured out what we are and how we came into being.’

‘You just learned how to talk in the middle of a fight?’ Joe interrupted, having gotten stuck on that weird fact.

‘Yeah.  That was the first time we felt Mairree’s blessing. The second time the god of camaraderie threw us a boon was when we were able to make it to you and your fire after Cauldrakon tore us apart.  When we noticed we couldn’t identify you, we figured you were like us; shielded by the [Terrible Secret] trait.’

‘Uh. Not quite, bud.  My trait is called [No One].  It’s a changeling trait.’

‘You’re a changeling?  We heard you were a newcomer?’

‘I guess I’m both.  I was a human back on my old world, and then I was made into a changeling when I was brought to Illuminaria.’

‘Cool,’ the uncanny youth exclaimed.  ‘Youth’ worked if you started counting Yuk’s years after their awakening, which Joe did since Yuk felt like a teenager to Joe.

“Dude.  Your parents are a primordial creature and a celestial event.  You can’t get much cooler than that.’

Joe felt a wash of stunned surprise through the connection they shared, which shifted into shy amusement.

‘We never thought about it like that …’

‘Got to learn how to spin the worst.  That might be the only good part about my first bout of dying.  Taught me to look for any glimmer of good amidst the mountains of crap.’  Joe eased himself to his feet so as not to squash any bits of Yuk.  He cracked his back and stretched.  “So.  How are you doing? Should we try to figure out how to get out of this mess?”

‘We’re better. Much better. We’ve pretty much assimilated everything right around us, but these caves are loaded with insects. We should be back to full size as soon as we start moving again. So what’s the plan? How are we getting out of here?’

“Good question. I have no idea where we are in relation to where we left the others,” Joe admitted. “Do your bugs know the layout of the caves?”

‘It doesn’t work like that. Once they become part of us, they stop being what they were,’ Yuk’s mental voice explained. ‘Even then, they’re just bugs. Unless you want them to lead us to their hive, they don’t really have much sense of direction. Complex thought is not an option.’

“Great. What do you remember of the run?” he asked, moving to the intersection of where the dead-end they had been hiding in met the longer tunnel.

‘Uh? We were reduced to a pair of crawlers hiding under your hairy coat sleeves. We have no idea which way you went.’

“Well, this is not good. I have the worst luck with directions.  I always get lost.  Okay.  I’m almost positive we came from that way,” Joe stated, pointing down the passageway. “Hopefully, I’ll remember things as we go, or maybe I can track my own scent.”

Quietly creeping down the tunnel, they stopped when they reached an intersection and peered in both directions.  Unfortunately, the rocky paths quickly faded into darkness. Joe deactivated the [Glowstone] to see if there was any light out there. His eyes swam in the complete blackness until he couldn’t stand it anymore. Reactivating the hovering magic lamp, Joe kept it at the barest amount of light he needed to function.

He closed his eyes and smelled the air. It was full of musty and chalky aromas. His scent was also there, but since it was coming off his body as well, he couldn’t tell which passage he had taken to get to where they were.

“Well, damn it,” Joe grumbled. “I think I turned here, but I’m not at all sure which way we came from. Let’s give that way a shot.”

They turned left and quietly moved through the stone burrow. Yuk made no more sound than a chitinous rustle while Joe managed to level [Whisperstep] one more time. Unfortunately, nothing looked familiar in the scant light.

“What’ya think?  Keep going?”

He felt a telepathic shrug as Yuk’s only answer.  Since his now fully reformed teammate had no better suggestions, Joe had nothing to do but push on through the labyrinth of passages, cracks, and small caverns. On and on, the pair crept, lost in the dark depths below the blighted pyramid of the Erlking.
