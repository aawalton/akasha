After training the first morning, Caleb finally found his way over to the forge. Finding it was easy, he just had to follow the sound of hammering until he smelled the familiar scents of a smithy. The smithy had been set up on t he outskirts of camp near the other less than desirable features such as latrines.

A man in his 30s with the light brown hair typical to most of the legion. his features were hard to make out however as he was completely covered is coal dust and soot from the forge. His face was clean shaven, and green eyes shown brilliantly against the black of his face.

“You must be the new smith,” the man said as Caleb approached.

“How did you guess?” Caleb asked, looking himself over.

He was wearing the standard legion outfit he found in his tent, and hadn’t put any of the ranks or insignias on that had marked him as an Equite.

“You showed up without a broken sword or piece of armor,” the smith said. “No one comes here unless they want something. I’m Drusus Tettius Ferrarius”

“Caleb—Caleb Kavilson”

Caleb still wasn’t used to introducing himself to new people, let alone the instance the imperials had with full names.

“Is it now?” Drusus said.

“What does that mean?”

“Just that, that name isn’t very imperial. I suspect you’ll be Caleb Something Draconis before too long. Important people don’t keep unimportant names for long in the empire.”

“I’m not important,” Caleb said, though he knew that was a lie. “I’m just a smith that joined up to save my village from being recruited.”

Drusus laughed.

“I know what you did at the battle—heck, I’ve had to repair the damage you did to all the unenchanted blades and armor. If you’re just a smith, then I’m just a dragon rider and I only see you’re dragon around here.”

Caleb looked around at that and saw Bog chewing on a piece of scrap iron.

“You’re dragons been around a few times already. We worked out an understanding. I give him scraps, and he doesn’t steal my finished work—I’m not getting the better side of that deal.”

Caleb smiled at the man’s joke.

“I’m sorry. I can get him to stay away.:

“Don’t do that. I enjoy the company. He’s sort of like a dog you know?”

“I’ve noticed,” Caleb said, and then to Bog called “Come.”

With a sigh, Bog got up from where he’d been loundgin behind the portable forge and sat next to Caleb where he continued to naw on a piece of a broken spear.

“So what brings you here?” Drusus asked, “Seeing as you didn’t realize your dragon was.”

“I wanted to get some time in on the forge,” Caleb said.

“I’d love to take a break, but I need to get this pile of armor repaired before the end of the week.”

Drusus gestured to a wagon loaded high with damaged lorica and helmets.

“How much of that did I do?” Caleb asked, wincing a little.

“Only about half of it.”

“Sorry about that.”

“Don’t apologize,” Drusus said. “When the equites go out, a little collateral damage is the best we can hope for. But… try to be more careful on our equipment next time.”

“How about I do some of the repair work?” Caleb suggested. “I just want time on the forge, there’s nothing specific I need to do.”

“That depends,” Drusus said. “Are you any good?”

“I was recently raised to journeyman,” Caleb offered.

“How recently?”

Caleb winced once again, more visibly.

“This week.. but, here is my journeyman’s piece.”

Caleb pulled out his hammer from the ring it sat in on his belt and offered it to Drusus.

He took the hammer reverently as he saw the pattern of it.

“No apprentice made this,” Drusus said, not believing Caleb.

“Well, technically I made it after I was elevated, but I count this as my real piece,” Caleb admitted. “How about I let you watch me work, and you decide if I made it or not. Just one piece.”

Drusus agreed, and Caleb got to work.

The firth thing he did was remove the bellows from the forge to create a second inlet in for air.

“What are you doing?” Drusus protested.

In answer, Caleb surged the wind and the forge blazed with heat.

“Bloody magic,” Drusus cursed. “Of course.”

Caleb smiled, as that was the least of his tricks.

He spun up vortex, splitting the air. He sent a mental request to Bog to manage the heat of the flame and then used his Imperium to pull the first lorica armor to him. Even before the metal got to him he knew which plates were damaged. He quickly placed the armor over his anvil with the rivets holding the offending plate in place over a hole in the anvil, drew a punch tool to his hand from Drusus’ neatly ordered rack of tools and punches the rivets out securing the plate with a few Arcane Power Attack assisted blows.

This plate was damaged beyond repair, and was bad quality to begin with. Caleb wasn’t sure what aspect it was, but the quality of this plate was inferior to the rest.

Must have been a repair he decided as he set it aside.

Drusus already had a large number of plates set out and ready. Each was approximately the size of the plates that made the lorica and they were all earth-aspected steel. Asking Drusus he learned that outside of some very specific magical regions, earth was the most common. Caleb inspected the armor in the card and found it was all mostly earth.

Aside from the steel being the easiest to come by, the Terra—the race of men with an earth bloodline—were the empire’s most common enemy. In most locations the army wore dissonant or earth-aspected gear, only deploying specific metal equipment on legions sent out on specific missions. If the empire had expected a Frigidi attack to occur, cold-aspected equipment would have been issued.

Caleb worked to forge an earth steel blank into the proper form to replace the damaged plate. As he worked it, he purged the mana from the plate as much as he could, but since there wasn’t much work to be done on the forging itself, he was done with the plate before he could even get a minor earth aspect to appear.

Drusus, in awe of the speed at which Caleb had worked, offered to replace the rivets to finish the repair, and Caleb moved to the next.

On and on he worked, his mana draining slowly through the process as he leaned heavily on his Imperium to manage multiple things at once. He placed as much steel in the forge as it would hold, and monitored each piece for the correct heat, nudging and rotating when need be. He also stayed in front of the anvil working as he pulled the armor over to himself to repair. Normally his working pace was a slow constant drain on his mana with Vortex and his mana fueled hammer blows, but with the additional work he was losing mana at a faster rate.

Bog helped where he could. He managed the flame with his Imperium Ventorum, he also was really good at removing the brass rivets from the armor, swallowing them down like little treats. It was the first time the dragon had eaten brass—an alloy of tin and copper—and he was quite fond of it. Caleb had to stop him from eating more than just the damaged ones.

“We need that!” Drusus had complained when Bog ate the first piece but Caleb had assured the other smith they’d get it back eventually.

At the end of the day, Carb had Bog—reluctantly—spit out a molten glob of brass for Drusus to collect.

With the extra mana work, Caleb wouldn’t be able to work through the whole afternoon as he planned, but as he was watching his mana steadily tick down he recalled his newest skill Mounted Warrior.

He sent a request to Bog and the dragon’s tail flicked around to touch Caleb’s ankle. Caleb felt renewed as he was given access to the dragon’s large pool of mana. With a small mental effort, Caleb switched Vortex to be controlled by Bog’s mana, and returned to his work without fear of running out. His dragon’s mana was useless for Imperium controlled work, but perfect for any skill. He then powered his Arcane Power Attacks—when he used them—and his Vortex in this manner.

All the while, Drusus stared in awe. At the flurry of activity, a crowd gathered to watch the spectacle. The soldiers off duty kept a fair distance to protect their own hearing from the whirlwind of activity but even still Caleb could hear them talk.

A name was repeated among them, and Caleb suspected that Drusus had been right.

Turbius. A high tongue name for whirlwind. Thinking back to the battle, and his current flurry of activity, he couldn’t argue that it didn’t fit.
