The door was three steps away.

I didn’t make it that far.

Boots scuffed against thick carpet, cloth rustled, and a muscled arm snapped past my head.

I threw myself aside with a scream, but the blow was not intended for me; he caught the door just ahead, slamming it shut. The frame trembled.

Saints, that wasn’t enough! Not nearly enough!

What would happen in the waking world? Would my companions wake to find my body mangled in the bed? Would my fate be a gruesome, horrific mystery? I buried my face in my hands, shoulders hunched, bracing for whatever retaliation would come.

“I have been,” every word was punctuated by a deep breath alongside the sound of something horribly wet, “Very patient. You-”

A blaze of heat tore through my chest. The world spun sideways.

As if from very far away, I heard his voice raise. Shouting, furious, threatening… me? Surely it was me, but... the words were strange. Unfamiliar, as if he were speaking a foreign tongue. He sounded very angry.

Well. I’d be angry too, if someone stabbed me. Should’ve… should’ve kept going. Pushed harder. Stabbed him better. Ashes, I think I’m going to faint…

The ceiling spun. Was I on my back? Soft, plush fabric cushioned my spine. That impossibly handsome, cruel face appeared above.

“-done yet. Stay. Stay.” He was breathing hard.

“What…” From somewhere in the distance, there came a tapping. Very light, like fingers drumming against glass. Something creaked.

A thousand pinpricks of silver glinted down as the room settled into focus. “That’s better, mortal. So you did lie. And now you’ve actually attempted to harm me. You brazen little-”

“Brin.”

He paused.

My throat felt scorched. Dry lips parted and I rasped, “My… my name isn’t ‘mortal’.” Warm tears slid from the corners of my eyes. It doesn’t matter what Horace said. If this is… if I’m about to… he should know my name. I doubt it will haunt him forever or anything so poetic, but…

The Fae tilted his head. One hand, smeared with crimson, lifted to press against his side. The ethereal cloth beneath was wet and torn.

“It’s Brin.” I closed my eyes. There. That’s it, then. I wonder if it will hurt? I hope he makes it quick. Teela and Renner will be sad- or, Teela will, I’m not sure about Renner. Although he did say I was pretty, so surely he doesn’t hate me entirely. But they’ll be alright. And Durst… ashes, Durst…

“Brin.” He chuckled. Silk around steel. “Get up. Try again, if you’d like.”

My eyes snapped open.

His mouth held the ghost of a smile. He lifted one bloody hand, dangling the gleaming bolt over me. It was drenched in vivid scarlet.

I clambered, still dizzy, to my feet. Try again? Surely he doesn’t mean…

He stepped closer. “Mortal… Brin.” My name came out slowly, as if he was tasting every letter. It sounded dark and decadent on his tongue. “Five nights, now, you have been entirely at my mercy. Yet I have not harmed you, nor threatened you.”

I drew back. “You-”

“I have not harmed you, nor threatened you.” Another step forward. He stretched his bloody hand out, offering me the weapon. “I have, in fact, only attempted to speak with you. I’ve been quite patient, dare I say even gracious, in humoring your fits of anger and terror and grief throughout every interaction. Yet you remain convinced that you’re in mortal danger. And, apparently, that you need to attempt an extraordinarily ill-conceived assassination.”

“I…” I couldn’t tear my eyes off the bloody silver. Saints, what sick game is this?

“Take it. Stab me a few more times, if it will offer you some measure of comfort.” His lip curled, too-sharp teeth bared. “I’ll do my best to hold still.”

“You’re… you’re insane.”

“No, I’m exasperated. But perhaps if I let you try to kill me for a while, you’ll finally be willing to have a conversation.”

“Have a what?”

“Five nights. On the first, I saved your lover.”

“Friend,” I whispered. My back hit cold marble.

He drew closer. “On the second, I tended your wounds. The third… we did not interact, but I am fairly certain my pets saved your life.”

I shook my head in disbelief, eyes widening. “You mean… the howling in the mist? They’re what chased the other Fae off?”

“Chased… No. Not most of them, at least. But you’re already terrified, so let’s not go into detail about their fates. As for last night… perhaps I did you no tangible favors, but you’re the first creature who’s tried to damage my house and gone without consequence. I’m certain you’ll be the last.” Glittering eyes narrowed. “And you left a mess on my carpet.”

I swallowed. “And… and the fifth? Tonight?”

He nodded towards the iron table. “So far, I’ve tried to give you a book. The chest is yours, as well, if you can calm yourself enough to listen to my offer.”

“Your offer? Look, anything kind,” I forced steel into my voice, despite how thoroughly rattled I was, “You’ve done is only because you want something from me. After you tried to destroy my home and nearly killed someone I care about!”

The Fae scoffed. “Your home is perfectly fine, and what happened to your friend was an accident. The hound either disobeyed me, and paid in blood for it, or… well.” His expression grew dark. After a moment he murmured, voice velvet again, “Your secrets can wait.”

I shivered, acutely aware that I was trapped against the wall. “I don’t have any secrets.”

“Liar.”

I grabbed the bolt.

Steel-bright, silver-lit eyes glinted. “Ah, there’s that boldness. Good. I’ll hold still, as promised. Let me know when you’re satisfied.” His expression returned to one of bemusement as he moved in, until he was just a breath away. Hands spread wide, as if in surrender.

I gripped the slick wood. Stared into his striking face, jaw clenched and muscles taut… and slowly lowered the weapon.

One corner of his mouth curved up. “Mm. Better, still.”

I let out a breath as he withdrew, sagging against the marble. My heart was racing.

He huffed a little laugh. “A word of advice, Brin. The weapons mortals fashion to combat Fae are… inadequate. Echoes and ash, with only the memory of wanting to kill us. The power to destroy someone like me is absent.”

I examined the bolt, brows knotting together. I suppose I shouldn’t be surprised. Durst’s arrow went straight through his chest, and… well. Here he is. Alive, and telling me to keep stabbing him. “So this can’t kill you? But… it works on other monsters?”

“Lesser Fae, yes. For someone like me… unless you manage to bring an entire wretched forest with you, I’m in no real danger. Pained and in an increasingly foul temper,” he added dryly, glaring at the weapon, “But safe.”

I gnawed my lower lip, eyeing him warily. “Why are you telling me this?”

“Because you’re afraid of me,” he said simply, as if that explained everything. He chuckled at my ensuing silence. “I’d like you to understand your value, mortal. Why I’m not going to hurt you. You have, in the waking world, something incredibly powerful. Incredibly unique. Something that could kill me.”

“I knew it!” The triumphant hiss burst out before I could stop myself.

He laughed. It was a deep, rich sound. “Part of a singular creation. A weapon that destroyed one of the most powerful beings to ever exist. And you’re just… walking around with it.” He gave his head a bemused little shake.

I bristled. “I’m taking it somewhere safe.”

The Fae’s smile widened. “I expect nothing less.” He reclaimed his seat at the table and took another sip of wine.

After a moment’s debate, I cautiously eased myself into the other chair and placed the bolt between us.

His gaze traced the movement. “You’re right to try and protect that relic. Whatever human decided to affix it to a statue…” He sneered, giving his head a small shake. Night-black hair swayed. “But, I digress. Since you’ve finally calmed down, I’d like to explain why you should return it to me.”

“I’m not going to trust anything you say. You have to know that.”

He drained the glass, then reached for the clear decanter and poured more. The amount of liquid in the crystal didn’t seem to lessen.

Magic. It must be. A thrill of excitement ran up my spine. If something so mundane as a carafe of wine was magical… what other wonders could this place hold?

“Brin, you’ve made your mistrust of me painfully clear,” he indicated the bloody bolt.

“Was that a joke?” Before he could answer I flinched, half-rising from the chair, as frosty air whispered across the back of my neck. I shivered, glancing around. The sensation faded.

“I’ve been known to make them. Look in the book.”

I opened it slowly. The first page was smooth and butter-yellow, with tidy handwriting scrawled across every inch. My fingers stretched of their own accord, sliding across the page. My head dipped as I inhaled warm leather and earthy parchment. Familiar, comfortable smells. Cherished smells. I let my eyes drift closed, sighing.

“I can give you some privacy, if you’d like.” His mouth twitched, one eyebrow slowly rising.

Heat crept up my throat. “I… Books can be wonderful things.” I ducked my head, swiping a tangle of hair behind one ear.

“You’re not wrong. That one is nothing to be enamored with, though.”

I squinted at the tiny handwriting. “‘Dictation: twelfth day of Elian. Absent: lord Embriel, lady Frith. Discussed: lord Sybion requests aid with boundary restoration. In favor…” I furrowed my brow, entirely at a loss. “What is all this?”

One slender finger traced the goblet. “Dictation, as it says. It’s incredibly boring, but the records from our most recent meeting are important.”

“‘Our’?”

“High Fae. Rulers and regents of fiefdoms within the Night Court. Among whom there has been some dissent, as of late.”

My mind churned. It occurred to me that I was being given information that perhaps no human had ever heard. Fae didn’t just… tell people things about their realm. Or at least, if they did, those pieces of information never made it into the stories of horror and bloodshed.

Assuming he’s telling the truth.

“These names…” I weighed every word carefully, peering up through my lashes. “A Fae told me that names… mean something. That they should be guarded.”

“A Fae?” The goblet settled with a sharp clink. Pale arms braced against iron as he leaned forward, searching my face hungrily. “Tell me. Who was it?”

The intensity of that look made me shiver. “He was outside the other night. In the trees. He… he looked like an owl, and said his name was Horace. Well, no, that’s not right, he said… er, that the name Horace would suffice. Whatever that means.” I dragged both hands through my hair, sighing. “And I’m fairly certain he wasn’t an owl.”

He fell silent, lips pursed. Finally, in a voice laced with disdain, “Denizens of this place are fickle, Brin. It sounds like someone was trying to frighten you.”

“It worked,” I muttered.

“Not a difficult achievement.” The Fae smirked as indignant heat flooded my face. “If you’re worried about me knowing your name, don’t be. Magic of that kind no longer exists in this place, if it ever even did.”

“Oh.” My breath rushed out in relief. “Good. That’s good.” Again, assuming he’s being honest. I thought for a moment. “So what’s your name, then?”

He took a long drink, dark eyes glinting at me over the rim of his goblet. “Mm. I didn’t say such magic doesn’t exist in other places. You may refer to me as ‘my lord.’” He inclined his head, black brows arched. The very picture of arrogance.

I blinked. “I’m… not doing that.”

He looked mildly offended.

I sucked in a breath… just as there were several odd sounds from behind me. The creaking of a wooden floorboard, a heavy exhalation of breath… then a muffled whimper. I twisted around, eyes wide and hands clammy. There was nothing there. Frosty air kissed my face.

The Fae’s deep voice reclaimed my attention. If he’d noticed the sounds, he didn’t show it. “We’re getting distracted. Towards the end- I marked the page for you.”

I shivered, fighting down the feeling that something was very, very wrong, and turned to a crease in the book.

“Dictation: third day of Belfain. Absent: none. Discussed: lord Embriel led raid. Southern human settlement. Defenses minimal. Seven claimed, no retaliation…” I trailed off, eyes going wide, and finished in silence. As best I could discern, the page recounted an attack on a town. What followed was a heated argument between, presumably, High Fae. Some demanded this lord Embriel be punished or locked away, while others praised him.

I looked up, unable to find a voice for the thousand questions whirling through my mind.

He smiled wryly. “In case you’re wondering, I’m one of those in favor of leaving you humans alone.”

I closed the book. My fingers were trembling. “Why… I’m not saying I believe any of this, of course. I barely even understand what I’m looking at. But why are you showing me this?”

“For context. There’s a feud going on in this realm.” White teeth flashed in a grimace. “A pointless one. Some of my kind want to leave and claim a more permanent stake in your realm. They’ve been growing bold; amassing acolytes and information from the outskirts of your larger cities. Attacking towns, as you read. Sensing what defenses they should be wary of.”

My pulse quickened. His claim made a terrible kind of sense, when paired with what Teela and Renner had told me about how pacted and Fae were making appearances all throughout the realm. After two decades of near-peace…

“Why now? Things have been… since the Nightmare, there haven’t been many monsters in my world. It’s been peaceful. What changed?”

He examined the ceiling for a while, seemingly lost in thought. Then, very quietly, “What do you know of my kind?”

I hesitated, wondering if any information I shared could somehow be… twisted. Used against me, or used against some other poor human. It’s not as though I know much, anyways.

My silence stretched between us until he murmured, almost gently, “You need to answer my questions, Brin.”

I felt the blood drain from my face. Salt, what am I doing? Just… sitting here, talking with this… this evil, ruthless creature?

“Or Royce dies,” I whispered. My eyes fell to the bloody bolt. For one wild moment, I imagined picking it back up.

His silence spoke volumes.

“I know your kind prey on mine.” My words fell like a stone; frigid and unyielding. “I… I know you’re monsters. Cruel. Bloodthirsty. That some of you keep humans as pets and playthings. And some of you make wicked deals with us, because I suppose helping a dying man out of sheer kindness is just unthinkable for someone like you.”

Endless silver regarded me calmly. “Kindness so often leads to regret.”

I slammed the book shut. “What a terrible way to see the world.”

Another chuckle. “It’s very fortunate we did make a deal. Otherwise your dreams would be banal and boring, and I’d have no way to give you this.” He flipped open the iron chest.

I gasped. A pile of jewels, bright and large and sparkling in a kaleidoscope of vibrant hues, lay before me. I saw diamond necklaces, bracelets of woven sapphire and emerald, ruby earrings and gilded topaz tiaras. Every gem I knew of, and many I didn’t recognize, sparkled flawlessly.

“I am not familiar with mortal residencies,” he smirked at my doubtlessly awed expression, “But I noted your home to be… ah, absent of finery.”

I had a hard time tearing my gaze away from the mound of jewels. But I managed to do so and growled, “Durst provided for my every need.”

“Needing and wanting are two very different things, Brin.”

I bristled. “You can’t bribe me! You haven’t even explained anything, nor given me any reason to trust you! And you never answered my question before. Why-”

The table shivered beneath my palms. I flinched, withdrawing them, as the chair and walls followed suit. Very distantly, and yet from somehow just behind me, I heard a voice.

“Get up! Wake up!”

Is that Renner? He sounds… odd. Frightened. That can’t be right.

The room twisted. Colors bled into a swirling, vibrant palette. I looked up just as the Fae lifted his sparkling goblet, as if in a toast. The wine shone a brilliant shade of garnet.

His eyes glittered with those endless, beautiful stars. As the world melted away I heard a soft, “Until tomorrow, Brin.”
