Sure, he had plenty of clones still up there, but it seemed that trouble was waiting as well. Right where they’d delved into this mysterious and frankly fascinating network, there were now some people hovering around that spot. Masked figures, fully covered and clothed. Likely the people from the lake, Henry assumed. He could have confirmed with an Identify, but he didn’t want to give himself away, and because he didn’t want to deal with any of it, Henry opted to continue exploring this underground world, which already netted them a pretty wild experience.

It was fun down here. The caves were full of life, and every time he expected to reach a dead-end or to be pointed back up to the surface, a new, massive cavern would open up, one with its own flora and fauna. It was mostly mushrooms and insects, and most weren’t worth hunting, but it was still fun, so as they strolled, Henry told Maurice of the legends of dragons in his old world. How in some cultures they were depicted as calamities while in other traditions and contexts, as gods, and considering what the two of them had felt from that one, the god bit certainly felt like it had some truth to it.

Had there been a real, world-traveling dragon that had visited Earth? Was that how the legends got started? One thing was for sure, Henry was looking forward to getting some answers. But in the meantime, he left five avatars tucked away in the surroundings of that lake, just so he could find the dragon in the future. Maybe it’d give them a hand when things went bad. Or maybe he’d spar with it someday. After 40 or 50 more levels, perhaps. For now, he just wanted to keep tabs on it.

Still, every few minutes, Henry would take a second and picture the majestic creature once more. That had been a damn dragon. He wondered what level it was. If it was actually still A-rank or if it was beyond that.

And speaking of levels, this spot should work, he thought as the two came into yet another massive, high chamber. It wasn’t as well lit as the tunnels and chambers deeper below, but the two of them could see just fine in the dark. This area’s walls were lined with tunnels, while the floor parted around a wide gash that led down… well, somewhere. Henry was curious to explore the cave network, but he wasn’t that curious. As for this space, it was a little busy, as Henry saw plenty of antennas swaying in a few corners and tunnels, but most of what he was seeing was C or B-rank, so after finding a small, compact crevice in the wall, Henry turned to Maurice.

“Alright, this spot should do. Still good with the plan?”

Maurice nodded, though he wasn’t very enthused, and after hesitating for a short moment, he scratched the back of his arm as he gave Henry a worried look. “You sure you don’t need me to watch over you? It might take you a minute…” the crab trailed off, but Henry just shook his head.

“It might. But you shouldn’t feel it much, and this way, even if something bad happened, you’ll be safe. Who knows what could show up down here,” he muttered, glancing around at the cat-sized ants watching them.

With that settled, Henry popped Maurice in his Maw and made himself comfortable in the crevice, shedding the human form before using a handful of clones to mimic a stone wall as he closed the exit. In the darkness of the improvised chamber, Henry let out a slow exhale before he glanced around at the stone surrounding him. Once upon a time, he remembered being claustrophobic. He remembered how much trouble that’d given him early on, especially with his small form and how often he needed to hide. When had that gone away?

Maybe it’s because I’m never really trapped, he thought to himself, his attention skimming over what his hundreds of avatars were perceiving. It hadn’t been that long since he’d arrived in this world, but he’d changed so damn much. He’d progressed fast, but then again, this world was brimming with potential. It was all around them, ready to be plucked.

Maybe that’s part of the equation, somehow.

Henry turned his attention to the beacon still shining in his sight. Find the Path was constantly active, showing them the direction of the two remaining orbs they had to recover.

Henry hoped the one nearby wasn’t too far. And he hoped this evolution would help with everything they had to do. From recovering the orbs, to figuring out if the clones he’d sent with Velistraine were still active, to figure out if she was still alive, and to finally have any hope of clearing the mantis-shaped ominous cloud that loomed over this world.

For now, at least it’ll help us with the more dangerous A-ranks. It should make the upgrades of our Aspects easier since we don’t need to run as much, he mused, and then, closing his eyes after one last look through his hundreds of clones, he turned his attention inward, and began with the tiger’s core. If it wasn’t enough, then he’d burn the eagle’s core as well.

Henry inhaled, then consumed the core, drawing its electric potential and energies deep into his core.
