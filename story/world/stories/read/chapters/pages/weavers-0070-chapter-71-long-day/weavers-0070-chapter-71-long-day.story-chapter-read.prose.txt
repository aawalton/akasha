Chapter 71 – Long Day

The Obsidian Gate yawned open with a sound like cracking stone and rushing wind. Its ancient glyphs pulsed dimly beneath the curling ivy, casting flickers of pale light onto the snow-dusted ground. Beyond lay the Shadowgrove, veiled in mist and shadow, a forest that waited.

Ezra stepped through first, boots crunching softly on brittle frost. The transition was immediate, one heartbeat he was in the academy clearing, surrounded by students and professors watching from safe perches. The next, he was swallowed whole by the Grove.

The light dimmed, warped by a canopy thick with tangled branches and silver-veined leaves that arched like cathedral spires overhead. The cold air pressed in, sharper here, tinged with old rot and distant smoke. It tasted of raw, unparalleled strength. Like something sat hidden, out of place.

Sounds came differently now. Muffled, distant, twisted. The rustle of leaves became whispers, unintelligible but persistent. The calls of birds took on a warped cadence, too deep and too slow. And beneath it all, the low, near-subsonic thrum of ancient threads pulsing through bark and root, as if the forest itself remembered why it had been sealed.

A soft shimmer beside him signalled Marcel’s arrival. He emerged through the gate, blinking against the sudden gloom. He scanned the surroundings in a slow, practiced motion, fingers already toying with the coin at his knuckles.

“This place gives me the creeps,” he muttered.

Ezra didn’t respond. His eyes swept the undergrowth, mind already dissecting routes, possible ambush points, chokeholds. “We won’t be alone for long.”

“No,” Marcel agreed, stringing his bow with a practiced ease. “But most won’t risk fighting this early. They’ll be laying traps. Forming temporary teams.”

“Good.” Ezra stepped lightly, cloak shifting around his ankles. “Gives us time to test the ground.”

They moved like shadows between trees, keeping to the ridgelines where the terrain gave them an edge. Ezra felt the pulse of his heartbeat slow, syncing with the rhythm of the woods. Every breath was deliberate. Every step chosen. He knew the kind of magic that lived in places like this. Beasts of all shapes and sizes, ready to devour.

The Shadowgrove was a twisted snare of gnarled trees and fog-choked ravines, where moss hung in sheets like funeral veils. Paths seemed to shift behind them, and more than once, Ezra glanced back to find the trail gone.

“The Grove’s moving,” he murmured.

“Yeah.” Marcel’s voice was low. “It must be alive or something.”

It was watching. Ezra could feel it, the same way he sometimes sensed Time bending around anomalies. The same prickle behind his eyes when someone was about to die. The Grove had rules. It simply didn’t keep to them itself.

A flicker of motion on the far slope made Ezra halt. He raised a hand. Marcel froze beside him.

Below them, nestled in a sunlit clearing that hadn’t existed seconds ago, prowled a creature.

A Pocodrake.

Four-legged and armoured, its back bristled with obsidian spines, each one gleaming with faint iridescence. It moved like it had no bones, muscles rippling beneath scaled skin. Its eyes were a vivid, unnatural green, sentient, cruel. Steam curled from its nostrils, and when it exhaled, the breath stank of scorched stone.

Ezra’s lips thinned. “That's graded considerable, right?”

Marcel rolled his shoulders, pulling an arrow from his quiver. “Yea, a nasty one. We got this though.”

Ezra gave a sharp nod, already slipping to the left, boots brushing only lightly over the loam. Marcel mirrored the motion to the right, melting into the undergrowth like he belonged there.

Despite the oppressive stillness, Ezra felt a pulse of adrenaline building. A familiar heat behind his ribs. This was different from duels, from tests. This was real. The kind of fight where a misstep didn’t mean some kind of punishment, it meant dying face-down in the dirt.

He felt Marcel move. A flicker in the corner of his perception. Not sound. His sight.

Then the arrows flew, three quick strikes in succession. The first thudded into the Pocodrake’s shoulder, the second bit into its leg, and the third was deflected with a twang by a twitching spine.

The beast shrieked, an awful, grinding noise that made Ezra’s teeth ache, and spun toward the source.

Ezra surged forward, boots pushing off a root, blades gleaming in either hand. He aimed low, for the soft gap behind the beast’s forelimb. The Pocodrake turned far too fast, tail lashing out. Ezra ducked under the swipe, dirt and frozen leaves exploding as it struck the ground.

He rolled, came up slashing. His dagger met flesh and parted it, a shallow cut, but enough to sting.

The Pocodrake reared back, eyes flaring.

Above it, Marcel dropped from a branch, slammed another arrow into the base of its neck, and vaulted away just as the tail snapped upward.

Ezra didn’t hesitate. His breath caught. His heart hammered.

Now.

He darted in, feinted left, then vaulted off a nearby stump and landed squarely between the spines. His left blade drove deep into the shoulder seam. His right hand reached instinctively for his threads, drawing the time just enough to thicken the air around the creature’s limbs, slowing its movements.

The Pocodrake bucked and screamed in distorted time.

Marcel’s arrow struck its eye, and the world went quiet.

The beast crumpled with a shudder.

Ezra landed hard beside it, panting. His heartbeat pounded in his ears, louder than the wind. Blood splattered his gloves, seeping into the leather.

“First mark?” he asked.

Marcel checked his crest, where the academy’s pad glowed faintly. “Yeah. Registered.”

They didn’t celebrate. This wasn’t a victory. That was only the first of many.

They moved deeper, the Grove growing darker with each step. The air thickened. Trees leaned in like eavesdroppers. Ezra felt a crawling itch in his spine—some sense that this place wasn’t passive. It was alive, it had to be, there was no other explanation.

A new sound echoed ahead, wet, dragging on endlessly. Ezra lifted a hand. Marcel nocked an arrow.

From the fog stumbled a hulking Ghoul. Seven feet tall, stitched from mismatched flesh, one arm replaced by a bone shield fused into its skin. Its face was a jagged mass of scars and metal staples, and sigils glowed beneath the surface like veins of fire.

Ezra’s jaw clenched. “Necromancer work.”

“I swear that shit’s illegal.” Marcel’s voice was tight.

“Doesn’t matter now.” Ezra adjusted his stance. “Same plan. I bait, youget rid of it.”

The Ghoul charged. Ezra barely dodged the cleaver it swung, rusty and massive, it carved a trench in the earth. He slashed at its leg, fast and sharp, but the monster barely flinched. Its attention locked on him with eerie intent.

Above, Marcel released a volley, arrows enchanted with Mind disruption. They fizzled on impact.

The Ghoul shrieked, turned.

Ezra dashed in again, shouting, raw, guttural sound, to keep its focus.

Marcel’s next arrow found its mark—deep through the roof of the creature’s mouth. It faltered.

Ezra leapt, driving both blades into the back of its neck. With a violent jolt, it went still.

Marcel was already moving to check its sigils. “It's marked... somehow.

Ezra didn’t speak. His breathing slowed as he stood over the corpse. In his chest, a knot tightened. Someone wanted this to escalate. They weren’t just testing strength, they were measuring limits.

They kept moving. The Grove shifted again, sunlight filtering through golden branches one moment, then frostbitten wind whipping through skeletal limbs the next. Time, here, was a suggestion.

They faced a Bone Serpent that tunnelled beneath the ground, erupting upward with venom-dripping fangs. Ezra’s calf was grazed, but Marcel downed it with a precise shot through the exposed gill beneath its lower jaw.

Then came the Arctocrall. Twin-headed. One maw spewed freezing wind, the other howled with psychic noise that clawed at their minds. Ezra slowed time just enough to deflect its charge, and Marcel’s illusions disoriented it until a blade to the throat silenced both heads.

The final battle of the day was a Shatterstag, its translucent hide reflected their movements in broken patterns. Marcel lured it into a trap with phantom doubles. Ezra collapsed a tree atop it, then drove a blade into the brittle chest before it could regenerate.

Night settled slowly. Not darkness, just a deepening blue that bled between trees like ink in water.

They found shelter beneath a massive elm whose roots formed a natural alcove. Ezra crouched there, sharpening his blades, fingers steady despite the exhaustion tugging at his limbs.

Marcel leaned against a stone, eating dried fruit. “You think anyone’s close?”

“Maybe.” Ezra wiped his blade clean. “Doesn’t matter.”

A silence fell.

“I’m not letting you fall,” Marcel said softly, almost too quiet to hear.

Ezra didn’t look up. But the words struck deep, landing somewhere beneath the armour he always wore.

After a moment, he said, “That goes both ways.”
