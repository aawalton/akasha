Merlin was levels above what he’d been a few months ago.

Ever since becoming a fugitive, burdened with living his life with as minimal interaction with the outside world as possible, he’d also lost the benefits of being in Prestige Academy. Most especially his close proximity with mage facilities and the constant tutoring that had helped bolster his skills.

Which was why, after his arrival in Nigeria, he had no choice but to take the extreme route—venture into the countless dungeons present in Singularity Mage. He had basically become a berserker, mad with venturing into dungeons to reap their rewards, clearing the easy ones and already more than halfway through the mid level ones.

And, of course, it had been very fruitful. Despite the fact that he had only gained a singular Perk during all his expeditions—which made him wonder if he had a maximum slot to fill, or his Perks also functioned in the same way as his Attributes, that if he got to a certain level his growth speed would be cut down—he had learned a skill that would be very useful for a fugitive like himself. His Attributes were also all past the fiftieth mark, pushing him into superhuman, or, more precisely, super-mage territory.

With his Strength being at level 66, he had become the kind of person monsters with the builds of giants feared coming up against. A single punch of his generated shockwaves, regardless of if he hit something hard or just punched the air. If it was the current him who had fought the Ice Wyverns back during his date with Janeth, they would have posed very little problems to him. Honestly, they probably would have caused no problems at all. Not with Janeth fighting with him. And besides, his Resilience was now at level 70, which made attacks that used to be life threatening to him useless. Sharp attacks, like a knife stab, were shallow and drew very little blood from him, and even some magical acts posed little issues for him.

He was not only stronger, he was now more durable.

As for his Perception, it was at level 51. It was the lowest of his Attributes, as it had been for very much the whole time since Merlin had gotten the System, but it was still one of the most useful, and it had honestly made good progress. It was because of its current level that Merlin was able to sense and trace the mana residue of those he had infiltrated Club Quilox for, and which of the VVIP rooms housed them.

To get there, on the other hand…would have been troublesome for Merlin if his Agility had been anything lesser than the current level 70 it was currently in. He had become a god of stealth. His movements blurred to the eyes of others, his steps were extremely light that they made not even as much of a sound as a pin dropping to the floor, and that was why he was able to walk past the cluster of guests, stewards, and even the guards watching the entrance to the upper section housing the VVIP rooms, without being noticed.

As for his Mana Power, to put it simply, deconstructing the spells of the Nora he had left a few months ago would be a walk in the park for him.

It was now at level 60, and even though this made it harder to level up because gains would now be quartered, it was still a milestone that Merlin was very happy to achieve.

At this level, not only could he deconstruct the Advanced Tier I and II spells of an A-Class Mage with very little stress on his mana, he could also deconstruct the Advanced Tier I spells of an S-Class Mage. As for the B-Class Mages and below, none of their spells were problematic to him. He could deconstruct all of them, and even more than one at once.

But, of course, there was a limit to how much he could push his mana, and the knowledge he had gained on his abilities after leveling up told him that the stronger he got, the more the damage to his body was if he went against his limits. Basically, if he stubbornly did more than what his mana could handle, like he was accustomed to, it would not just end with him being unable to lift his arms or passing out. It would be a lot worse.

Merlin was not rather keen on finding out what those consequences were, as he was not keen on getting caught at the moment, so he pushed the thoughts in his head away, and focused on the mission at hand.

So far, everything was great. He was now a few steps into the corridor of the VVIP section, a wide space that smelled of luxury, with a velvet carpet that ran the whole length of the corridor draped along the floor, and dim light spilling from the ceiling above to bless the area with a rather sensual feeling.

Merlin was almost dragged into a state of mind that beckoned him to relax and enjoy the moment, but he snapped out of it quickly enough, and just in time to see the door of the room he was approaching flip open to spit out someone.

The person was a young boy who looked so young Merlin guessed he wasn’t even up to fifteen. He was dressed casually in a hooded sweater, but had the hood down, wore a schoolbag, and was counting some notes of money while licking his lips. His cheeks were a little sunken, matching his wiry frame that made his clothes exceptionally baggy on him, and he had a gaunt expression.

Shock bled onto Merlin’s face without wasting even a second, and that forced him to freeze on the spot.

For him to remain stealthy by relying on his Agility’s level, he had to constantly be in motion. The second he paused, he made himself noticeable to anyone who had even a modicum of sharpness. The guards behind him, however, seemed to lack that, but the gaunt boy was different.

With a twitch of his eyebrows, the gaunt boy sharply looked up, his fingers freezing upon the notes he held.

However, there was no one besides the guards to the end of the corridor in his line of sight.

“Hmmm…” he mumbled, tilting his head slightly with a frown. Then with a shrug, as if telling himself that he didn’t care, he went on counting the money he held as he kept walking. He was halfway towards the guards when he grinned and said, “Fifty thousand. Jackpot.”

Merlin, who had hurried behind the boy after his slip-up, watched silently as the boy walked out of the corridor, handing a thousand naira note each to the guards and walked into the elevator.

Immediately, a serious question plagued his mind. Why was a boy far younger than him in a club renowned for not easing their entrance rules, and not even in the lower sections, but in the VVIP section?

But Merlin instantly answered his very own question.

The bag, the money, the interaction with the guards, and most especially, the room the boy had walked out of. The answer was as clear as day. He was involved with the distribution of the Berserker drugs, as Merlin called them.

A similar distaste to the one Merlin had had when he’d found out about Nikolai tampering with drugs stormed his tongue, and he clicked it subconsciously. Luckily for him, it wasn’t loud enough to draw the notice of the guards, but it was not like that was even something he should be worried about any longer. He was pissed. Even if the adults were deranged, they should have known better than to involve a child in their nonsense. Because of that, Merlin was certain that when he walked into the VVIP room currently behind him, he would use every single second he spent in there to relieve his anger.

He took out the pair of glasses in his pocket and wore them, then turned towards the door. Immediately he pushed it open, along with the thick earthy scent of tobacco slamming into his nostrils, a ruckus of murmurs mixed with the loud burst of music that had been kept at bay by the soundproof door rushed into his ears.

Any other person might have been disoriented by something so jarring, but Merlin simply walked into the room as everyone froze in their steps, bathing himself in the purple and blue light alternating between themselves to paint the room in a mildly ethereal hue.

As Merlin shut the door behind him, his eyes perused the space. It had the width of a mansion’s master bedroom; several tables and couches were all over with the former bearing mounds of pure white powder at their edges. The ladies holding straws close to the mounds of powders looked up as well, freezing as they stared at Merlin, but he didn’t mind them at the moment, or what they were doing. He was not a detective. What he was more concerned with were the black pills scattered at the center of the tables, with some already sealed in transparent vacuum bags.

The berserker pills.

“Hey! Do you know where you are?!” one of the men littered around asked, straightening on his feet from one of the tables he was hunched over.

Merlin’s eyes shifted from the drugs to the man, then towards the rest of the crowd, counting them in a second. They numbered twenty, excluding the girls, who were five. Then his eyes settled on the singular person who made the count tick up by one. He was seated in the middle of two ladies, his head tilted slightly as he watched Merlin with even more curiosity than the goons.

Their boss.

Merlin decided to save him for last. He turned towards the ladies and nudged his head towards the corner of the room.

“Move over there,” he said.

The ladies stared at him for half a second, their brows furrowed. They were lost. But Merlin wasn’t going to repeat himself. It was not like they were exonerated of their involvement here just because they were ladies. Giving them that option was just him being considerate because he knew some, if not most of them thought they had no choice. The sad truth was, everyone had a choice.

The goon who had asked Merlin a question a second ago ran out of patience. He reached for a knife on the table and stepped forward. Which was stupid, in truth. There was a Grimoire around his waist; he was a mage, just like the rest of the goons present, so why was he relying on a knife?

Merlin knew the answer though. Not being in possession of a Grimoire made it hard for people to consider him a mage, so in a fight like this, they would naturally limit themselves. He was glad for that. He was physically stronger than any mage, and he was undercover. So he was happy to simply rely on punches and kicks to get his job done.

“All right then,” he started, taking his stance. “Let’s get this over quickly and hand you all over to the police, eh?”

His words became the nail in the coffin. The goon rushed forward, thinking nothing of the possible disaster that would come out of killing someone, lunging his knife straight for Merlin’s abdomen.

It was extremely easy to disarm the fool. Merlin didn’t even have to use any of his skills. He simply shifted to his side, slapped the wrist of the goon with as much restraint as he could summon—which didn’t do much, as the man’s wrist dislocated—and the knife dropped to the floor. Then he grabbed the man’s mouth before he could cry out in pain, keeping his screams in, and punched him unconscious with a hook to the space between his ribcages.

As the first of the goons dropped to the ground, the rest wore a blank look, confused as to what had just happened. In the next second, their expressions changed as they came to a collective realization. Unlike their counterpart, they decided to completely rid Merlin of his life with a barrage of spells, not taking into consideration the enclosed space and the damage that could come from it. They all reached for their Grimoires.

Merlin, however, had no intention of drawing too much attention, so he couldn’t have that.

He activated the Perk: Reader’s Bookmark, and made use of [Flash Step] to rid every single of the nineteen minions left of their Grimoires in half a second.

It was at that moment that the ladies came to an understanding of the situation they were in. They tried to run out of the door screaming, but Merlin shook his head and once again pointed them to the corner. They gladly obliged.

He then turned back to the goons, who had settled for physical weapons, and forced them all unconscious without so much as breaking a sweat.

By the time he was done with them, their boss was already up on his feet with a nasty look on his face, and a nasty spell shaping at the center of his palm. A spell nasty enough for a C-Class Mage, at least.

Honestly, the level of skill of the man was appalling. His mana took too long to shape, and the spell was so slow in coming to life that Merlin had to take a quick glance at the Grimoire of the man to realize that he was a water mage.

Regardless of the fact that Merlin would have no problem deconstructing such a weak spell, he had no intention of drawing unwarranted attention and destroying the club of someone who was just running a business. So he crossed the length of the room with a single step, blurring in front of the boss, who flinched at that, and clamped down hard on the man’s wrist. When the man didn’t want to stop the conjuring of the spell of his own accord, he opted to break his hand.

A loud shriek swirled around the room, but couldn’t escape its walls. The boss fell on his knees, tears streaming down his eyes as his expression turned from the one of curiosity he had had at the start, and that of anger just a second ago, to one of pleading.

“What do you want?” he asked. “I-I’ll give you anything. Just let me go. Please!”

Merlin was about to say he didn’t need anything, when he decided that wasting such a good gift would be stupid of him. So he squatted, aligning his eyes with that of the boss who was on his knees, and asked, “That kid who left here a moment ago. What’s his name?”

The boss’s eyes twitched. “What? What do you want with him?”

“Answer.”

“Issa,” said the boss immediately. “His name’s Issa.”

Merlin sighed. There would most definitely be a lot of people with that name. He needed something more to go on with.

“Surname?” he asked.

The boss shook his head. “I swear on my dead mother, I have no idea.”

Merlin studied the man’s face for a moment and decided that he wasn’t lying.

“Well, that’s a bummer,” he said as he placed a hand on the man’s shoulder. “You didn’t give me all I wanted.”

Before the man could say a word in protest, Merlin swiped the back of his palm at the man’s neck, knocking him unconscious. Then he stood up and sat on the couch, heaving out a deep exhale. He pulled out his phone in the next second and called Kyu-Ri.

“Hope you’re not drunk?” he asked as soon as she picked up.

“I saw, you little brat,” Kyu-Ri said in retort. Her tone was a little loquacious, but she still seemed to have a major part of her wits about her. “I’ve called. He’s on his way.”

Merlin nodded and hung up. Then he turned towards the ladies shivering in the corner.

“My apologies,” he said. “I would have let you all leave, but I don’t want the guards at the entrance alerted. Bear with me until the police arrive, will you?”
