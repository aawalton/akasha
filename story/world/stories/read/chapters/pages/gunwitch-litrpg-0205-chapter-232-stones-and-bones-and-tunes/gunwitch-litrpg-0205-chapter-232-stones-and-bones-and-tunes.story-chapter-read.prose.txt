After everyone woke up, I asked them all for their attention, because I had an announcement to make. Even Vasil popped his shivering head out of my satchel, being careful not to expose too much of himself to the cold winter air.

I cleared my throat. “I have gathered you all here to—”

“We were already here,” Bogumir rudely interrupted. “You didn’t gather us. This is where we made camp.”

I narrowed my eyes at him, but didn’t take the bait. “I have gathered you all here to inform you of something important. I am going to form a council. I realize that it might be in everyone’s best interest to get multiple perspectives on my potential build. Since I am responsible for your safety, I’d like to explore all angles before I commit to certain skills.”

Vasil nodded. “That is a wise decision. That you acknowledge this shows how much you have grown not just as a person, but also as a leader. Yes, a leader makes the ultimate decision, but not before listening to her advisors. This ensures nothing is missed or forgotten.”

“Thank you, Vasil.”

“Mmhm.”

“And I have already decided who will be on my council,” I said, clapping my hands excitedly.

“It’s us, isn’t it?” Bogumir asked.

It was totally going to be them, but I didn’t like that Bogumir already guessed it, so I feigned ignorance. “What—Why? Why do you think that?”

“Because we’re your only friends.”

“My only friends,” I scoffed. “I have plenty of friends. Hundreds of friends.”

“Name one other friend.”

“Jelka.”

“Jelka,” he repeated. “Sounds made up.”

“She’s a viwa. We met her in the forest.” I turned to Kasia. “Tell him I’m not making her up.”

“Um…” Kasia said, rubbing her arm. “Jelka is not made up. She did help us. I, uh, don’t know if she’s a friend, though.”

“Ola!” I raised a finger. “Ola is a friend! And Phisto!”

Bogumir squinted, clearly trying hard to decide if they did qualify as friends. “Hmmmm.”

“Skelly!”

“Alright, alright. I’ll give you Phisto and Skelly. I don’t think Ola is a friend. More like an employee. I don’t think she’d want you to call her a friend. She takes pride in working for you. But does that mean Phisto and Skelly will also be on the council?”

“I don’t think that would be in my best interest. I don’t think that would be in anyone’s best interest. Phisto would just tell me to invest all my stat points into Chaos and take Chaos-related skills. Skelly, well, he’s Skelly.”

“True.” Bogumir nodded. “So we are a council of four.”

“Yeah. Now that’s out of the way let’s get to our first order of business. I have forty-two unspent stat points, four unspent skill points, and four unspent skill upgrade points. Now I don’t think I have any good offerings to upgrade my skills with, so we will need to keep an eye out for those.”

“Or chop up some more people.”

“Or chop up some more people. But only bad ones. Maybe we can sneak a hand or two out during the tournament. Maybe we can steal some of their kit.” I prodded Vasil’s head. “I’m looking at you, Vasil. Use that tongue. Take a camouflage skill if you have access to one, so less people will see you do it. We need to get seriously decked out for what I have planned.”

Vasil cocked his head. “And that plan is?”

“Don’t worry about that. Worry about relieving whoever is fighting of their precious gear.”

“But if we are part of the council, we should be aware of your plans, so we can advise you.”

“Not this one, because you will think it’s stupid or you will tell me not to do it.”

“Especially a plan like that!”

“Don’t worrryyy about it.” I waved him off. “Look guys, let me get the priorities straight. I need skills, and I’d like it if those skills benefitted not just me, but all of us. And I also need something that will allow me to turn my [Stunt Double] tangible.”

Bogumir rolled his eyes. “So she can do your hair?”

“Among other things. But yes. Also my hair. And Kasia’s hair. We could form a circle and we could all do each other’s hair. But that’s just a bonus. Not the main reason. There are much more important reasons for that.”

“Sure,” Bogumir said, but didn’t look like he believed me.

Even Kasia didn’t look like she believed me. At least she didn’t say anything about it.

“Anyway,” I continued. “I was thinking of skills that help me build stuff… or people. You know, empower them. Create bricks, then give them those bricks to build their own houses.”

“Riiight,” Bogumir shook his head. “You do not build bricks. You steal bricks, or you forcibly enforce the concept of a brick onto something entirely different.”

“What?! I don’t steal! …often. And I don’t know what you mean by that. I build stuff. I mean, I could build stuff. I made that [Push] bullet, didn’t I?”

“Very useful, yeah. Especially since practically no one uses firearms in Silesia.”

“That’s besides the point. I could do other stuff like reverse engineer runestones, craft essences, craft artifacts made out of scavenged body parts, heck, maybe I’ll even create a completely unique skill that no one has ever even thought of.”

“One that you also haven’t thought of.”

“Yet.”

“You lack the patience for something like that.”

I sighed. “Worst council ever.”

“If it was possible to reverse engineer runestones, don’t you think people would have already done so?” Bogumir asked.

“What makes you think they haven’t? Would you go around telling everyone if you did?”

He hesitated. “No…”

“See! For all we know there are plenty of people who can do that already! And even if there aren’t, they aren’t me. I am me, and by Mēnē, if I say I will do something I will do it.”

“You have to give her that,” Kasia agreed. “She does what she say she will. Even if it sometimes sounds a little…”

“Stupid? Reckless? Idiotic? Counter productive? Suicidal?” Bogumir said.

“Um… I was going to say ambitious.”

I pumped my fist into the air. “Ambitious! That’s right! No one ever got anywhere by doing what everyone else does. No one ever got anywhere by playing it safe. You know, Bogumir, I’d start to doubt myself if you actually agreed with me on something. It’s good that you are like that. It just makes me all the more confident that I am doing the right thing and that I will succeed. The more people who think I can’t or shouldn’t do it, the more likely it is that I should.”

Bogumir shrugged. “It’s not like I don’t want you to succeed. We are on the same team.”

“And don’t you forget it!”

“So all this time you’ve been talking, did the thought ever cross your mind to check if you’re actually able to reverse engineer runestones?”

“Uhh…”

I quickly opened my System and checked if I could do anything with runestones.

[Runic Scholar]

Type: Crafting

You may sacrifice a runestone for a chance to learn its schematics.

You currently do not have enough believers to acquire this skill.

[Runic Crafter]

Type: Crafting

You may craft runestones as long as you have the required schematic.

You currently do not have enough believers to acquire this skill.

Alright. So it can be done. Just not at the moment. Annoyingly, it also didn’t say how many followers I needed. Classic System.

“Actually,” I said. “I did check that. And I could, potentially, craft runes at some point. Unfortunately it depends on how many followers I have.”

“How many does it require?” Vasil asked.

“It doesn’t say.”

“Classic,” Bogumir said.

“That’s what I thought.”

Tapping my chin, I pondered my options for a few moments. “So I can’t make runestones. What if I could, you know, create something of my own? Like runebones?”

Kasia frowned. “Rune… bones?”

“Yeah! You know, like stones, but instead of stones it’s bones. We could take them off inquisitors, or monsters, or whatever.”

“Right,” Bogumir said, rubbing his face. “You just want to casually take a couple of bones from an inquisitor and create your own magical alphabet?”

“Right! Isn’t that a wonderful idea?”

“It is certainly a unique idea,” Vitek said, shrugging. “And pretty witchy.”

“Ahem.” Vasil cleared his throat. “And how did you plan on acquiring these bones?”

I gave him a good glare for asking such a stupid question. “We take them the same way we take everything else. We kill them and then instead of taking just the head and hands we take, I don’t know, the toe bones? The finger tips? We could even take their teeth! That’s not so strange is it? I’ve seen people in Graecia walk around with necklaces made of teeth. We just wouldn’t be making necklaces out of them, it would be rune teeth?”

Bogumir scrunched his face.

“What? We could name it something catchier.”

“And I’m sure you have plenty of names already thought out?”

“Hecatunes,” Vitek helpfully suggested.

“Hecatunes!” I snapped my fingers. “That’s perfect!”
