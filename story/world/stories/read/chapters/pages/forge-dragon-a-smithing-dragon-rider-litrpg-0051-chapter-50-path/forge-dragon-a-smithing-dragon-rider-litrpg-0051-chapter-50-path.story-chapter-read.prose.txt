“I think my half-sister is behind this—somehow,” Mettius said as they ran through the forest. They’d forgone horses as Mettius had acquired stamina potions that would allow even the uninitialized to tap into that resource to fuel their exertions for the day. But, as with most potions, they didn’t work on animals.

Bog trotted alongside them, matching their steady pace while Avis flew high above and ahead watching for danger. With the gaining of her Beast Bond, Kelia and Avis could now communicate intent and emotions in the same way Caleb and Bog could, which made using the bird as a scout much easier.

“Why do you say that?” Kelia asked.

“She has shadow magic like what was described. It’s a class our father—may-be-reign-forever—can give to members of his court, but he only ever gives it to his children.”

“Why’s that?” Caleb asked.

“It’s an assassin class,” he explained. “A very powerful one.”

“Would your siblings have a vested interest in killing your father?” Caleb asked.

Mettius hesitated before answering.

“I can’t explain the workings of the succession but let’s just say killing the current emperor would only decrease your chances of ascending to the throne.”

“How does it work?” Caleb asked.

“I literally just said I can’t explain it,” Mettius deadpanned.

“It was worth a shot,” Kelia told Caleb, and Mettius smiled a little at that.

As they ran, the lorica and helmet Caleb wore rattled and clanged like crazy, so much so that he it was beginning to grate on his nerves. He combatted this by exerting his Imperium over the gear to hold it steady before realizing his has a skill for essentially this. He activated Immutable Metal and the constant noise of their passage grew significantly quieter but his movement became harder as his arms could no longer swing freely and the normally flexible armor was unable to bend with his running.

He spent no small amount of time activating and deactivating the skill so that the sound of his movement surged and then stopped, making a much more annoying sound than he would have if he’d simply done nothing.

Slowly through his efforts however, he was able to alter his control over the metal so that if he tried to move it, the metal would move with him, but he still allowed the skill to restrict movement imposed by outside forced. In this case gravity, but he suspected in combat would mean he could keep the skill active while fighting, only having it activate when an enemy landed a blow.

Immutable Metal (uncommon) has evolved into Metallic Guardian (rare).

Metallic Guardian: Use your willpower to selectively reinforce metal and prevent it from deforming or moving. This skill improves in efficiency with willpower, acuity, and your level in this skill.

Caleb smiled to himself with satisfaction at the skill evolution. He’d not even leveled the skill a single time despite having used it in two battles, and now he’d evolved it to a rare skill with a half hour’s effort.

Caleb kept the discovery to himself, and they ran enough hour before stopping to rest and eat.

“Mettius,” Caleb asked over a quick meal of dried rations, “How common are skill evolutions.”

Mettius turned to Caleb with suspicion.

“Why? Did you just evolve a skill?” Mettius asked.

“Yeah, it seemed easy, but back home, no one really did it and I’ve done four now.”

“It isn’t easy, but it’s not impossible. Four is… excessive. How many skills have you created?”

Caleb pulled up his status screen and counted.

“Two,” he said, “But both of those have already evolved.”

Mettius nodded knowingly.

“Let me guess, your level 5 skills were all kind of not great fits for you combat style.”

Caleb nodded.

“You’re going to stray from your path soon,” Mettius said, as if Caleb should know what that means.”

Sensing the confusion, he sighed and continued, “You’re going to get offered a new custom class.”

“Is that a bad thing? You make it sound like it’s a bad thing.”

“It’s great if you have the talent to back it up—which I suspect you do. You will receive a class evolution, raising the rank of the class and creating a new one.”

“That sounds like all upside,” Caleb pointed out.

“This class,” Mettius continued. “Will not offer truly new skills. Ever. You will only be offered skill evolutions when you reach milestone levels. And these would only be ones you were close to earning already. Any new skill you may be offered would be one you were about to unlock. The milestones just accelerate your progress.”

“That doesn’t seem too bad,” Caleb said. “Increasing the rank of a class might be worth it.”

“You’re speaking from a perspective of privilege—even if that privilege is earned through your skill. Most don’t have the skill to progress on a new path. Most never even create a single skill. They level the skills they are given using the system and records to guide them, but that becomes a crutch. You see it when people reach the peak of well-trodden paths. The Legionnaire’s path ends at level 70. None who follow the path that far have the skills to forge their own path once that is the only option left to them.

“If you forsake the path you’re given early, you can eventually walk a new one, creating a new class others could follow.”

“Is that what you’re trying to do?” Kelia sked.

“Me? I’m just trying not to die, but… yes, that’s the path I mean to follow to survive. Let’s get moving.”

At the not so subtle indication that the conversation was at its end, Caleb put what he’d not eaten back in his pack and checked that Bog was okay to continue. The dragon couldn’t drink the potions Mettius had, but his endurance was up for the task and he had his own means of drawing on his stamina to extend it further. Caleb cold tell through their bond and his Mounted Warrior skill that Bog was drawing on his stamina to sustain him.

Kelia checked over Avis as well before they headed back out. Caleb hadn’t seen the hawk up close since the battle and the wounds she’d taken took to have already healed. Where once she’d been a pure brown, now she had a blue-white cluster of feathers on her wing where she’d been injured. It seemed that the hawk had gained more from her bond with Kelia that just resistance to the cold and Caleb was curious to see what would come of it—even if he did still hate that bird.

Since the bonding, Avis seemed to have forgiven Caleb for the great crime of stealing Kelia from her—even if Caleb had known Kelia longer than the bird had been alive. He suspected the bird now felt through the bond how Kelia felt about Caleb and in turn how Kelia felt about Avis’ disdain for him.

Avis and Bog still did not get along, and the bird gaining a cold affinity wasn’t helping the matter.

With everyone ready, Avis took off into the air and the four resumed their trek across the ground.

Hours later as the sun began to set, Avis notified Kelia that they were close.

“The wall is around the next ridge through another half mile of forest,” Kelia relayed with specific detail that made him question how her bond might differ from his.

They stopped to discard anything they wouldn’t need and continued on at a more measured pace, Caleb doing his best to silence the steel on himself and Mettius.

They’d only just rounded the ridge when the world went black and darkness took over.
