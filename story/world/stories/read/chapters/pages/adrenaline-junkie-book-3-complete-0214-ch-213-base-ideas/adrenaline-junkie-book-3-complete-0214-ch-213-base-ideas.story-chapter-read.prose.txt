"Ah, that was fun," Archie sighed as he felt the stiffness that had plagued his fists was now absent after having a good tow in with both the Amphislimes and Clawserpents. “Truly, the cure to everything is a good fight.”

He tilted his head back, letting the hiss of evaporating slime and charred wood settle into silence. The enlarged Amphislime’s body sagged lifelessly between the smoldering limbs, bits of gelatin hissing as they dripped to the forest floor.

Without wanting to waste more potential material, he flicked his hand and wove mana strings outward like fishing lines. The strands latched onto the Amphislime core and the remaining of its bubbling corpse that stuck to it, and the sprawled, half-mangled bodies of the Clawserpents. With a second thought, they all vanished into his Spatial Storage Cube that he was now carrying in a pouch tied to his waist.

Clapping his hands together, Archie began to scan the canopy, having activated Vital Sight to be aware of all around him in case something or someone else wanted to join in after the Amphislimes and Clawserpents. However, much to the misfortune of his rising adrenaline, nothing and no one approached him. Only the faint echo of distant shrieks and the occasional crash of some beast moving too far off to bother with him.

For once, the coast was clear.

“Awww, just when things were getting good,” he muttered, childishly kicking the tree trunk beside him high above the ground. After deactivating Adrenaline Rush, he was moving again, not a heartbeat later, darting from branch to branch in the same direction he had been running before the slimes and serpents derailed his momentum and teased him with a half-dozen fights.

But after traveling over no more than a dozen trees, Archie slowed his speed as his brows began to furrow, as something felt as though it were tugging at the back of his mind.

Holding himself steady on a tree’s trunk, his lips pressed into a line as a realization overcame him. “…Wait a damn second here... Why did I even ask where I could find this place?”

For a few seconds, he just stood there awkwardly before reluctantly reaching into his Spatial Storage and pulling out his Compass of Desire. Upon coming into contact with it and flipping it open, the needle turned crimson and spun around before pointing towards north northeast.

“...Ah. Right. I had that…”

Vul’s voice hummed from the back of his Soulscape, smug and completely unhelpful. While Volos and Arsenic were trying to reenact the fight he had with Vul’s help of creating weakened controllable statues similar to Amphislimes and Clawserpents for them to fight.

“Don’t,” Archie cut him off immediately. “Don’t even start. I can already feel the stupid bombardment coming. Save it. I already know that I’m a dumbass for even asking for directions when I had this.”

Archie exhaled through his nose, flicked the compass shut, and stuffed it back into his Spatial Storage.

“…Shut up, Vul,” Archie muttered under his breath as he heard a laughing noise akin to molten rock meeting water echo within his Soulscape.

Archie cut through the treetops with renewed speed as he was finally allowed to travel from branch to branch without interruption from ambushes, stray arrows, and spells from E-Grades, and the surprisingly often random E-Grade humanoids flying through the air after being flung away from a low D-Grade or strong E-Grade.

Following the direction of the crimson needle from memory and adjusting the direction he was running in, Archie was finally rewarded with the forest opening into the Primeval Forest that Dina spoke about.

How did he know?

In a clearing up ahead, Archie slowed his tree-hopping until he came to a stop, his boots finding firm purchase on a thick branch. He narrowed his eyes, taking in the sight before him.

A stone monolith loomed over a massive clearing in the forest; the monolith was unmistakably shaped like a hog – its broad snout jutting forward, tusks curling halfway from the rock, every contour worn into place by wind and time. But strapped across its back was a dome of jagged ridges, looking oddly similar to a tortoise shell but without the patterned shapes atop it.

Archie let out a whistle before saying, “…Huh. That’s… yeah, that’s actually pretty damn accurate, Dina.”

He chuckled to himself and reached into his Spatial Storage and pulled out his Aetherglass. With a flick of his thumb, the device shimmered awake, its surface blooming with a quick display of Aether Inc. before going into his. Clicking the camera icon, Archie raised it up, aimed it, and snapped off a quick barrage of shots at the hog-shelled monolith.

But that was just step one.

Archie bent his knees and launched himself into the air. The forest blurred beneath him as he soared forty meters up from where he previously stood, marking him to be at least a hundred and thirty meters above the ground, before his boots slammed down atop the hog’s granite snout.

Without pause, he leapt again, springing upward until he landed on the wide curve of the tortoise shell at its back. There he straightened, balancing easily on the uneven surface, Aetherglass already raised again to take even more photos and a couple of videos.

The Primeval Forest of Creavenboor sprawled in every direction. Massive trees stretched skyward, their crowns swallowing the sky above, with the only place untouched by their leaves being where the hog stood, as even it towered over them.

Archie panned the Aetherglass slowly, capturing it all with incredibly steady hands as this wasn’t just for him, though part of him wanted to save every second of this sight so he could look back on this moment sometime in the future.

But mostly he’d done this in regards to the Quests he would receive from Paleos upon telling him he had a bunch of videos, samples, and other types of documentation in regards to what he saw and did in a System Event.

Omniversal Solutions would pay well for footage like this, especially paired with whatever came of the dungeon itself. Full documentation of the environments, fauna, and hostile creatures that he’d seen would go a long way for him; both in a monetary sense as well as a bureaucratic sense as well and hopefully it would give him enough of a boost to bring him to the next membership tier.

Once he finished taking enough photos and videos from high above, he lowered the Aetherglass and switched it off before tucking it back into his Spatial Storage.

Archie stood still for a moment longer, gazing down at the monument beneath him. Hmm, should I try to create my own massive base, one that can move? Archie thought to himself. Should that be my forging goal while I’m in the System Event?

“It doesn’t need to be something walking,” Archie muttered thoughtfully. “Maybe something like an aircraft carrier that flies in the air… Yeah, and it would make sense because by then I’ll have made my mana bike able to fly. Yeah, that sounds good to me, I’ll just need to find a large deposit of Gravity Gems and or ore to build the bottom or whatever structure it needs to keep it afloat.”

And then, without hesitation, he dropped from the shell’s back, plummeting like a stone until he bent his knees on impact and exploded forward into motion once more.

Archie came to an abrupt halt, boots digging into the bark of the branch as his eyes widened at the sheer size of the thing looming ahead.

“...Okay, how in the hell was I not able to see this big ass tree before?” Archie muttered aloud, genuinely baffled. He stopped dead on his branch, craning his neck back. The thing was colossal – at least double the size of the hog-shelled monolith and quadruple the size of all the other trees.

“Was there some sort of obscuring visual barrier around this tree that made me not see it earlier when I was atop the big ass stone hog?” Archie asked himself as he took notice of a donut-shaped platform nearby him on the massive tree.

Leaping forward for a better view, Archie cleared the sixty-meter gap with ease and landed on the polished planks of a massive platform built into the tree’s upper half. From here, the scale of how massive the tree was truly hit him. “Because this tree has to be at least a kilometer in diameter alone…” he thought, lips quirking with half-disbelief.

But it wasn’t just the tree that caught his attention. It was also the people.

Noise rolled across the platform – shouts, laughter, bargaining, clattering steel. Archie found himself staring at the sheer density of adventurers crowding around the centerpiece: a massive set of ornate double doors carved directly into the trunk of it. Their surface was layered in intricate designs, curling beastial shapes going through various stages of what he believed to be a story. It reminded him faintly of the mosaics he’d seen prior to entering the boss fight of the Dungeon of the Infernal Minotaur.

The craftsmanship of the doors alone was leagues beyond anything he’d ever crafted, which, to be fair, he’d never once thought about aesthetically designing his weapons and armors with stories or designs such as those the doors had. “But, maybe I should try and do that,” he murmured to himself.

Looking around the platform, he could see groups of people whom he believed to be adventurers, judging by their gear and similar-looking badges a couple of them wore on their waists, lingered in varied-sized groups ranging from pairs to squads of ten.

Within said groups, he could see humans, elves, dwarves, and a whole bunch more that he’d never seen before – some were scaled, horned, feathered, and even one of them had all three of those features with an addition of having blue skin.

Not everyone was there to do adventuring or delve into the dungeon, though. A ring of stalls encircled the platform, each manned by merchants, traders, and crafters hawking their wares. The tang of alchemical brews bit at Archie’s nose from one stand, while another flashed racks of polished blades and gleaming armor. Food vendors shouted over the noise, peddling skewers of spiced meat and bowls of steaming stew.

The whole place felt like a bizarre festival.

Archie’s eyes narrowed, drawn to a certain group of individuals also on the donut platform – guards.

A handful of them were stationed directly in front of the double doors, hands resting on the hilts of their weapons and ready to act at a moment’s notice. More stood along the edges of the donut-like platform, keeping watch over the surrounding area. A few others patrolled between the groups of adventurers, ensuring that the commotion some were causing did not escalate beyond verbal sparring.

Archie’s lips pressed into a thin line as he saw a group of five each pay five Silver Coins to one of the guards before they were allowed to approach the double doors. “I wonder what they’re doing here? Do I have to pay some sort of fee to enter the dungeon?” he thought, tilting his head slightly.

But I’m broke as shit right now… he mentally complained to himself just as he used Identify on the double doors that the group of five opened and disappeared within.
