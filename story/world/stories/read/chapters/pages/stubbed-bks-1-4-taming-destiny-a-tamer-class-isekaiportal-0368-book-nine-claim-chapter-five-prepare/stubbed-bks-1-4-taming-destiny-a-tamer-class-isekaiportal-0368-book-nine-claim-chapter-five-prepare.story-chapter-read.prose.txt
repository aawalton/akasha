Kyrian is present and has outright declared that his intentions are death and destruction. He attacked me with Water-Shaping, I tell my network grimly. Prepare for battle.

Layton and Josiah are ready, River reports a moment later.

Lord Forestheart is too, Hunter confirms.

Valence stands prepared. Happy’s contribution.

Captain Sor’az as well, Iandee offers.

And Captain Tolly confirms his readiness, Catch tells me finally.

I hate spreading them out like this, but the communication benefits of our Bond network are just too much of an advantage to give up. I haven’t yet offered an Alliance or Tame Bond to any of the lords – I want to get more of a measure of them first – but by having Noir deliver a samuran to each ship who will stick closely to the lord or captain in charge of it, we can get most of the advantages. And since all of the samurans’ abilities are restricted in range, they wouldn’t be able to play much of a part in a naval battle like this.

It’s frustrating – a good portion of our fighters and my companions will be almost useless in a fight like this unless it comes to fending off a boarding party. But if we’re boarded, all of our other attempts will have failed and we’ll be on the point of being utterly defeated. And given Kyrian’s stated intentions, I can’t let that happen.

Ivor and I float in the air between the two fleets, waiting for Noir and Sirocco to come and join us. It will be better for us all to act together.

Already, I see my allies shifting into the formation we decided on before I headed off to speak to Kyrian.

Not wanting to lose all momentum, the six ships have split apart and are circling around in opposite directions. At the same time, they reposition so that my ship, Elarion’s, and Layton’s are in the lead with Valence’s, Josiah’s and Sor’az’s following in the more protected position. We have the advantage of numbers – we need to use that effectively.

I can see Josiah’s and Layton’s efforts at work – the weather mage has joined his cousin on the ship I lent him. Together, they’re forming a cloud over Kyrian’s fleet’s heads, streamers of steam drifting up directly from the water surface. Unlike his father, it seems that Kyrian isn’t able to control all the states of water as the streamers are being allowed to pass by without interference.

That doesn’t mean he’s doing nothing, though. All three ships have shifted to press closer together with Kyrian’s ship in the lead for now. The waves beneath their ships boil with what looks like tentacles or sea serpents made out of clear water. These creatures pull the fleet of enemy ships forwards far more quickly than the ships’ sails would alone.

The stretch of water between our ships, though once vast, is rapidly decreasing, and the waves are becoming increasingly choppy. Gazing at it with my magical sight, I see the clash between the water magic of the mages on each fleet that’s causing the unpredictable movements of both waves and current.

I cross my fingers and silently wish them good luck. We’re not hoping to be able to turn the sea into our battleground – it’s almost certain that our enemies have more and more powerful water mages than we do. But if they can stop our enemy from turning it into their battleground, that will be enough.

Near the prow of his ship, I see Elarion standing, his pale hair snapping in the strong sea breeze. His gaze is on the water ahead of the ships and his expression shows his intense concentration.

Following his gaze, I see small chunks of ice beginning to form and nod in satisfaction. Besides the number of water mages our enemy has, who could completely mess up our ability to manoeuvre, our next biggest challenge is our enemy’s shields. Until we’ve got through the ones keeping attacks at bay, we won’t be able to do much to the ships themselves.

The chunks of ice that Elarion is supposed to be seeding everywhere will do little to the shields preventing magical attacks, but they will help drain those keeping physical impacts at bay.

Two forms join us in the sky and I send greetings over the Bond to Sirocco and Noir.

Come on. Let’s do our bit before the storm gets going.

Receiving determined agreement in return, we sail back through the air, heading straight towards Kyrian’s ship.

The closer we get, the more loudly the shouts of anger and challenge become. Kyrian, or whoever he has commanding the fighters, has clearly whipped them into a frenzy because now all I see on their faces is anger.

Good luck, I tell my companions, just as I leap off Ivor’s back, my Shaped air-wings catching the air to allow me to fly independently. High enough that we’re almost crossing into the territory of the air beast lords, I’m out of almost everyone’s attack range.

Ivor and Noir split apart, heading each towards their own ship. Sirocco stays with me. Ignoring the shouts emerging from below, we begin attacking the shields.

Sirocco uses her Fire-Wing, focussing on sending multiple attacks rather than powerful ones, more as distraction than anything else. I, meanwhile, pull out two large flasks from my Inventory and, uncorking and then tipping them up, I soar over the ship from prow to stern.

The viciously green substance in the bottles pours out as a liquid, but quickly becomes gas that my Air-Shaping forces downwards.

As soon as it comes into contact with the shield, it latches onto the magic, coating it and then spreading like a fungus. And, like a fungus, its objective is to soften the shield slightly – make it more vulnerable to our attacks later. Sirocco’s Fire Wings immediately start sinking deeper into the patches of air that shimmer faintly green. I grin vindictively.

Pulling out another two flasks, I prepare to do another run.

Unexpectedly, something grabs at my wings and drags me downwards with alarming speed. I glance upwards – nothing can be seen. Activating magic-sight, I see better what’s happened: they have an Air Mage of their own. Damn.

I reach out to the tendrils of magic in the air, battling with the will behind them as I fall like a stone. The Air Mage is far better at Air-Shaping than I am – as expected. I would guess they also have more actual mana. But his or her will is significantly weaker and, though it earns me a headache, I manage to rip their claws free from my wings before I crash into the shield.

But it’s cost me a lot of height and my next few seconds are frantic with my need to dodge every attack that comes my way. Magical missiles, arrows, even a person who seems to have an incredible jumping ability and whose spear comes within a foot of impaling me.

That one regretted his actions, though – I enhance my muscles and punch him in the chest as I dodge his attack just enough that he goes sailing past. His momentum plus my own sends him spiralling into the water, his chest caved in. He sinks and doesn’t come up again.

I fight my way upwards, triggering my magic-sight even though it makes me feel intensely nauseous to move while it’s active – but it’s the only thing allowing me to avoid the air controlled by the Air Mage that seeks to ensnare me again. Or, failing that, make the air so tumultuous that I’m likely to fall anyway.

By the time I’ve cleared the mage’s reach, my heart is hammering and my stamina is down by quite a bit.

Sirocco, warned by my experiences, has also stopped attacking the shields on Kyrian’s ship. It’s a pity, but there’s no way I want to start losing people – or myself – in the opening gambit.

Still, I vindictively drop a few more little ‘presents’. These aren’t made for ship warfare like the flasks were, but they’ll cause some issues one way or another – hollow and fragile bone spheres that contain poisonous gas.

They don’t contain magic, so the magic wards don’t stop them. And they’re not falling fast enough for the physical wards to see them as a threat – instead, they treat the capsules like raindrops, directing them to open rain storage barrels.

I grin wolfishly as the capsules break apart on impact as intended, spreading their poison across the deck.

Unfortunately, the captain isn’t stupid, and soon sets the wards to repel all impacts, so my capsules start bouncing off the physical wards. I stop dropping them – they’re not heavy enough to do much damage and I’m sure I can find better uses for them.

Seeing that the Air Mage is busy clearing the deck of my little ‘gift’, I risk another fly-by, trailing the shield-fungus behind me. Then, before he can turn his attention back to me, I head off to help my companions with other ships. With any luck, I can spread some of that poison gas on them before Kyrian’s ship gets in contact.

I was heading towards Bladestrike’s ship, but a call for help from Noir makes me quickly veer off course. Aiming for House Siren’s ship instead, I see the issue – Noir is being attacked by several flying beasts. Two of them look a bit like Lithos, Nicholas’ winged serpent Bonded, while three of them appear more like flying monkeys.

They’re all smaller than Noir and, with a quick Inspect, I see that only the winged serpents are Tier two, but together, they’re severely hampering his ability to fly. The winged monkeys are clinging onto his wings while the serpents are attacking his head and underbelly, respectively.

Pouring mana into my wings, I shoot forwards through the sky, summoning my spear to my hands while I do so.

The winged serpent attacking Noir’s underbelly only realises I’m there when a spear strikes straight through its wings. It hisses at me, but I grab it quickly by the throat and stop it from biting me.

Withdrawing my spear from its wings, I stab again, this time piercing and destroying its brain. It droops limply over my hand and I tuck its body into my Inventory – I can get the Core later.

The winged serpent, seeing what I did to its companion, hisses angrily and dives for me. A sharpened sawblade of air chops it into two pieces which also get added to my Inventory.

Meanwhile, Noir makes short work of the winged monkeys clinging onto him and they fall to the water in pieces. A howl emerges from the deck below and I wince briefly at the thought that I’ve just killed someone else’s companions. Then I consider that there was far more rage than pain in the cry, and that if I hadn’t killed them, they would have taken Noir down, and I feel far less sympathy.

Pulling out another couple of flasks of shield-fungus, I go for a fly-by. Noir, like his brother, has been given a storage bracelet with a small space just big enough for some potions, and does the same thing. My air-magic catches the liquid-turned-gas and presses it into the shield – without an Air Mage on this ship, it’s a lot easier.

I also deliver a few of my poison capsules, but these just deflect off the shield from the start – the captain on this ship is obviously more savvy than the one on Kyrian’s.

Glancing upwards, I see that the cloud that’s formed over our head has become very dark, very puffy, and very dangerous-looking.

We need to go, I warn my companions. We don’t want to get caught in this. It’s unlikely Layton and Josiah have enough control over it to protect me.

The thunder that comes almost immediately after adds perfect emphasis to my words. None of my companions argue and we decide to skirt the clouds on the way back.

Just as well – we’re only halfway back to our ships when the heavens open.
