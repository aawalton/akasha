The [First Man] was often a little too polite. The only thing that made it tolerable was that as long as he spent long enough in the conversation, the man would eventually slip away from his politeness to something more informal.

“It is most… dare I say, eloquent,” the man replied.

“Oh suck a flopper,” Dorthna returned, “I talk like a child sometimes.”

Intentional was not what Dorthna would call it. The thing with life was that no matter how powerful you became, a being was always susceptible to adaptation of different kinds. Some, like talking like a child, snuck up on them when they spent too much time with children.

“So, tell me, Shet,” he said, looking at the man’s pants, “before we continue, what are those about again?”

Dorthna chuckled a little, knowing the answer to his next question. “Did you find him?”

The man shook his head. “Nope. But on to more important things. I thought you and I were the only ones who can access this place.” He looked around, at the darkness and the creatures clawing about within it. “How did a child get here? And why don’t you look bothered?”

“I don’t look bothered because I know the child. Good kid. Holds back a little too much, if you ask me,” Dorthna said. “As for how he got here. I think he’s built something of a connection to a creature that was once here.”

Shet’s brows furrowed. “A creature left the void after being sent here.”

“Oh, come of it,” Dorthna laughed. “It’s difficult but not really impossible.”

“And was there a reason you let him open the door even if a little?” the man asked. “You could’ve stopped him.”

“I needed him to feel the fear that you feel now. The perfect being must learn to overcome that fear, or to at least survive it. As you do.”

The man came to a stop next to Dorthna, and Dorthna knew that he would venture no closer as long as the door remained open.

“Tell me about the search for Melchizedek,” he instructed, seeking to distract Shet while he also consorted with his own thoughts.

Regarding what had happened here, he needed to know if God was finally paying attention to him once more. He needed to know if God was finally taking him seriously.

Shet paused, thought about the question. “He is nowhere this side of Existence, if that is what you are asking.”

“It is not what I am asking,” Dorthna confirmed. “What else?”

“Salem is still empty.” Shet slipped his arms into his pocket and shrugged. “A Melchizedek still sits on the altar.”

“Nothing new there.” Dorthna knew that the last place to look for Melchizedek was in Salem. “He always felt claustrophobic there. He said it felt like God was staring daggers at him.”

“I just didn’t… I wasn’t…” Shet’s lips pressed into a thin line. “I didn’t think he would be there.”

“Because he’s not,” Dorthna said with a sigh. “People would think that being one of the oldest beings in Existence, you would be able to pay attention to words and not jump to conclusions. Melchizedek said that it felt like God was staring daggers at him. God is not in Salem any more that he is in your not so humble abode.”

“My abode is humble enough.”

“There is a part of Existence I haven’t checked, but that’s because nobody checks there.”

“The Order of Melchizedek continues to spread, even to worlds that have not awoken to mana. At this point, God is the most popular of all beings even to those unaware of him. How he did that is beyond me.”

“It’s his faith stats.” Even thinking about it annoyed Dorthna. “Since its so high, concepts that have to deal with faith are powerful when he uses it. It’s also the reason you can’t find him.”

Shet frowned a little. “I hate it when [Priest]s abuse the stat.”

“Well, that doesn’t count for Zed since you could as well say that he created the stat. All he has to do is believe well enough and hard enough and things happen. Only those with free will are completely immune. And you know how that is.”

The faith stats were always an annoying thing. Dorthna remembered getting into an altercation with the [Priest] once. When Melchizedek realized that there was no calming him down, the idiot had believed that the planet would kill itself along with everything in it except him.

“How far has the Order of Melchizedek spread?” he asked.

“Ninety percent of Existence as per my last check,” Shet answered.

Dorthna didn’t like the sound of that. “Is that in population or in planets?”

“Population,” Shet answered. “A ration of about two hundred thousand to one when it comes to followers to leaders.”

Dorthna wasn’t surprised. It was like that almost everywhere. The leaders were always far fewer than the followers.

“That’s good to know.” He sighed with the conclusion of the conversation. Like he had expected, he had gained nothing from it. “Oh, I almost forgot. Who made the request?”

“Atlas.”

Dorthna paused. That wasn’t right. “Isn’t he still powering… well, basically everything?”

“He is,” Shet agreed. “He sent an emissary.”

An Emissary? Atlas had no emissaries and no friends. It seemed Dorthna would have to look into some things. Firs, he would…

No, he dissuaded himself. You are not who you used to be. He looked at his open hand and flexed it. Not yet, at least.

It made him remember something else with a smile. “Tell me, what are those are the top like now that I’m gone?”

Shet chuckled. “At first they were happy when the angels finally got you.”

Then you escaped and now they are all panicked. Tiamat wonders if you will finally come for her for looking to enslave the world of the [Dying Light].”
