On Ivor’s back, I fly towards the ships pursuing us. My allies didn’t like this idea. They definitely didn’t like this idea, and nor did Sarran. But Elarion weighed in on my side and together, we managed to convince the rest of the group. I can’t deny that I’m a bit wary of Elarion’s support – I’m uncertain about his motives. But since it works in my favour this time, I’m not looking a gift-horse in the mouth.

The thing is that, though it puts me at risk, we’re taking precautions to reduce the danger posed to me. Riding on Ivor’s back, for one thing – I have more options available to defend myself or return an attack if I’m not having to concentrate on controlling my own flight. Using my new Skill Willpower Leverage to boost my Constitution by almost twenty percent is another – I’ve never been so tanky.

But even if my allies didn’t like me to be the only one venturing out practically alone to speak to those controlling the ships sailing after us, they all recognised the necessity of the conversation.

The fact is that a battle is most likely inevitable, but since they are nobles, we must give the other party a chance to establish their intentions. If they can argue we attacked unprovoked, then we and our Houses could be the ones receiving censure when we eventually return to Moriax – or even during, if the Torrent has a means of transmitting information back to the mainland.

In fact, that could even be the Torrent’s intention – to provoke me into firing on them first, and then use that to discredit my House. Certainly it would fit with the lord’s most recent scheme to make Titanbend look bad by being incapable of finding the rift that was causing beast attacks to target Zlona.

So, I definitely need to give Kyrian – or Lord Torrent, if that’s who’s present – a chance to speak before taking action. But there’s no way I want to let our ships come close enough that we can talk. Hence why I’m flying towards him.

Remember, if anything goes wrong, just get out of there, I remind Ivor, nerves playing a little in my belly.

The alcaoris sends reassurance my way, adding in an image of returning to the ship with me still on his back.

Yes, exactly.

I have Stealth enabled and have done my best to press it over Ivor too – combined with his camouflage armour, it’s made us pretty much invisible. Between startling the people on the ship and letting them prepare a ‘surprise’ for us, I definitely prefer the former. So, when we drop it, a ship’s length away from the lead ship, the cries and sudden activity on its deck are expected.

A young-looking fighter even shoots a crossbow at us. Its bolt flies faster than Ivor can dodge, but my Bonded’s tattoo shield easily deflects it down to land harmlessly in the ocean below. I tense, magic running through my system in preparation to react to further attacks, but a more experienced-looking soldier orders the younger to stand down.

“Announce yourself and your business!” an official-looking man calls up. I wonder if he’s the equivalent of Sarran – he’s not dressed like a warrior.

“Lord Markus Titanbend, here to speak to the expedition leader,” I announce, using air-magic to carry my words more clearly down to the deck. Interestingly, I feel a hint of resistance to even that much. Switching to magic-sight, I spot the bubble-like shield that surrounds the ship. It seems to be similar to the ones that envelop my own – it reacts passively to any magic, and actively to magic with harmful intent.

This, of course, is another reason for me, personally, coming closer – I get to do that recon trip after all.

“Heir Titanbend, If you would be so kind as to wait at that distance, I will see if Heir Torrent wishes to speak with you.” His tone is interesting – a mixture of fear, politeness, and dislike. I wonder what this man has against my family – I doubt his dislike can be towards me personally. It’s not like I’ve done much outside the territory of Azaarde. Unless he’s related to Dexil or someone in his gang, of course.

“I will wait, but not for long,” I warn the man. After all, it’s not like I can just hover in the air – Ivor has to keep pace with the ship that’s travelling at a decent speed. If his intention is to tire us out so we struggle to fly away afterwards, I’m not going to play into it.

“Of course, Heir Titanbend. I will inform my lord.”

With that, the soberly-dressed man disappears down below decks. I take advantage of the pause to inspect the ship as much as I can, using all the tools at my disposal. My Inspect Item Skill struggles to work at this distance, though it can give me information about the bigger objects – and the ship itself. My combined Inspect Skill is another matter – though I set it not to scan any humans invasively, I receive a good deal of information about the ship and who’s on it.

The two ally ships are a little too far away for me to use either Inspect Skill with much result, but my magic sight and, of course, my regular sight, enhanced by borrowing Sirocco’s vision, give me some useful information for if – when – this comes down to a fight. The most important thing I learn is that, unlike among my fleet, there is no uniformity among the ships.

The ship to the right, the one that has Bladestrike’s flag flying proudly below Torrent’s, is definitely the poor man of the bunch, equivalent perhaps to Josiah’s ship. It’s obviously old: repairs are visible; weapons are less so. Its shield is dimmer than those on the other two ships, and overall there is less enchantment magic carved into its planks.

I recall Josiah’s hesitation about using magic to increase the speed of his ship and wonder how that’s affecting this ship. Because, from the water-magic I see suffusing the waves around the three ships, they’re definitely using something to speed themselves. If the water-magic is causing strain to Bladestrike’s ship, then that makes it even more obviously the weakest link in the group.

Among the other two, the ship beside which we’re keeping pace is definitely the most well-equipped. I’d say it was custom built for this expedition, much like my own ships. Not unexpected – the earlier official’s words confirmed that Kyrian is here, so it makes sense that Lord Torrent would want to make sure his son had the best transport available.

The planks of the ship are just as blinding in magic-sight as those of my own. I’ve already seen one shield, but if there isn’t at least another to protect from physical attacks, I’ll eat my hat. And there are far more protections besides – I might be able to push through the ship’s fire resistance, especially if I have Aingeal helping me, but it would be challenging even without the shields.

And in the meantime, I’d have to defend against the ballistas and magical cannons that are obvious on two different decks, and the weapons of the large group of fighters already assembling on the top deck. Not an easy target.

Nymphbay’s ship, by contrast, is far lighter. I suspect that little water-magic is speeding them – there are no cannons that I can see, and the ship is both the smallest and the one with the cleanest lines. It cuts through the water like a knife, enchantments obviously acting much like the ones on my ships to reduce its water-resistance at the prow.

If Kyrian’s ship is the heavy-hitter, House Nymphbay’s is the nimble gnat. I would suspect that its defences aren’t fantastic – most of its enchantment seems committed to increasing its speed and ability to avoid attack. That doesn’t make it weak, though, and with the intensity of green magic suffusing the ballistas on its top deck, I would suspect that they pack an unexpected punch.

“You wanted to speak to me, commoner?” Kyrian’s voice meets my ears, his tone full of arrogant hostility. “Impertinent, but I suppose a little entertainment would do me good.”

The Heir of House Torrent has emerged from the darkness of below decks, his bright blue eyes burning like two bunsen flames. He’s clearly making an effort to appear calm and unconcerned, but I see how his hands clench; how a muscle ticks in his jaw. Young, Brash. Clearly he hasn’t changed much since our dinners at the palace.

“Kyrian,” I start, creating a current of air between the other heir and me so our words are each carried to the other’s ears.

“That’s Heir Torrent to you,” the other man sneers. Clearly, without the need to put on a performance in front of the other Great House heirs – and indeed, the rest of the hall – he’s willing to give his true feelings space. Or perhaps, I think as I glance around at the onlookers, this is just a different kind of performance.

“Then it is Heir Titanbend to you,” I reply steadily, coldly. “Not ‘commoner’.”

“But that is what you are, is it not?” the other man needles, his voice dripping with spite.

I gaze at him for a moment. He’s itching for a fight, for an excuse. No, this was never going to end peacefully, but I still need to try – for the record, if nothing else. As Nicholas indicated, Torrent will try to throw the book at me if I end up killing him.

“Why are you pursuing us?” I ask instead of responding to his provocation. I don’t need to prove anything – not to him, and not to my allies.

Kyrian laughs, a hard, brittle sound. But he doesn’t deny it.

“You wish to play the ignorant?”

“I wish to be clear about your intentions.”

“My intentions?” he laughs again incredulously. I notice more than a few hard looks coming from the fighters around him, though there are a number of uncomfortable ones too. Division in the ranks? “Is that what you told my father when you attempted to kill him?”

“To kill him?” I ask, surprised. “At no point have I tried to kill your father.” Though if it had happened accidentally, I wouldn’t have exactly shed any tears.

“Impaling him on a spike isn’t an attempt to kill him?” Kyrian demands hotly.

I shrug insouciantly, wondering if Kyrian has just realised that he’s confirmed his father’s presence in a way that would surely be enough to see him in front of a judicator. Perhaps he feels safe this far off Moriax’s shores.

“I was just trying to…pin him down.” My lips quirk up briefly at the memory. Kyrian must see the small twitch in my expression as he goes red and he storms over to the rail, clenching the wooden form until his knuckles whiten. The waters beneath the ship roll unnaturally, the ship lifting just a bit higher than it should.

“You dare laugh at what you did to him? To a man worth ten, no a thousand times more than you!” Kyrian declares furiously. “You ask my intentions? Death! And vengeance for the wrongs you have done my family! The lands you’ve stolen from us will be paid for by your blood!”

“I haven’t stolen any lands from you!” I protest, unable to let the accusation stand unchallenged even if it lets him lead the conversation. “Your father was the one to attempt to destroy a city under Titanbend’s protection.”

“All my father did was study a rift! And for that, you attacked him, nearly killed him, and then had him brought before the king and forced to pay in lands and gold for the privilege!” The ship begins to pitch and roll, the fighters on its deck struggling to stay on their feet.

I blink, then shake my head wordlessly. Kyrian is deluded. Or severely misinformed. Either seems equally likely at this stage.

“Kyrian, I have nothing to do with whatever decisions the king made. And I doubt that Nicholas did either. If he levied penalties, it’s because he believed your father deserved them.” And a curl of satisfaction goes through me that Nicholas’ prediction was right – that Ionith wouldn’t let such an egregious interference in another lord’s duties stand, even without evidence to convict a Great Lord.

“Does he not?” Kyrian sneers. The soberly-dressed man who stands at his shoulder, holding himself in place with one hand clutched onto the railing, flinches at his words.

“My lord…” he murmurs tentatively. Kyrian ignores him.

I frown at Kyrian’s loaded statement.

“What do you mean?”

Kyrian’s sneer deepens.

“As if you don’t know.”

“I don’t.” I reply neutrally.

“My lord, I really think you should–”

“Shut your mouth!” Kyrian snaps back at the man and he recoils a step. Then he turns that burning and oddly knowing gaze back on me. “A bit suspicious how the king so favours the Titanbends, is it not. Offering you tila before your match with Heir Fell. Turning a blind eye to your transgressions – and bringing the axe heavily upon those who go against you. Like he’s…bound to always support your side.”

I take in a sharp breath at the implied accusation.

“Accusing my family of committing treason without evidence is a dangerous play, Heir Torrent,” I say, my voice colder even than the sea wind that rips at my clothes. “And one that bears heavy consequences when found to be false. I would be very careful what you say next.”

Kyrian’s face pales and then reddens again. I think I might see a hint of hesitation, maybe even regret in his eyes, but the flash is gone a moment later, replaced by arrogant pride.

“I’m saying what everyone thinks,” he snaps. “What no one else dares to say.”

I exhale slowly, reminding myself of why I’m here.

“Given the history between our Houses, I did not think it likely your intentions were peaceful, and your own words have proven I am right. But we are not enemies – yet.”

“Of course we are enemies!” Kyrian returns angrily. “Have you not listened to a word I’ve said?”

I ignore him.

“We are not enemies yet, because you have not personally done anything against me – nor I you,” I tell him levelly. “Turn away, travel to the Lost Continent by a different route. This does not have to end in bloodshed. But if you attack, we will respond. We will answer death with death, destruction with destruction. And vengeance with justice.”

Kyrian’s face has turned an odd blotchy colour, and for a moment, he’s so incensed he’s unable to speak.

“Justice? Justice! I’ll give you justice!”

The choppy sea beneath the ship abruptly smooths, returning the ship to a calm state once more. But underneath me, my magic-sight spots water-magic gathering in a large ball.

Ivor, be ready to move, I warn him.

A moment later, a column of water surges upward, splitting and reshaping mid-rise into a massive, translucent serpent’s head. Its jaws yawn wide, cloudy fangs of compressed water glittering as they snap toward us.

Ivor roars, wings flaring and dodging out of the way of the strike as I tighten my grip on Air-Shaping and use it to form a shield between us and the water beast.

We soar upwards to clear its reach and the last thing I see as we head higher into the sky is Kyrian’s face twisted in furious, vindicated satisfaction.

If he’s determined to have a fight? So be it.
