How the heck was that much unknown?

As soon as the question crossed my eyes, they burned just for a second, and the notification changed, revealing new information.

[Flock of Wings & Teeth, Pack Hunting, Level 240, Predators, Unknown.]

That helped, but not in a way that gave me any additional info. Well, that wasn’t really true. At least I knew they weren’t so high a level I couldn’t see any info about them, and it was just a skill of some kind.

I jerked to the right as another did a fly-by, trying to cut me with the edge of its wing. It missed, but I returned the favor with my claws as the creature passed, slicing right through the thin membrane.

It wobbled in the air.

Another dove with its beak open, then something shot out of its mouth, spraying all over my armor. It stunk.

Bad.

My eyes watered, but it didn’t hurt.

Instead of doing whatever the thing had expected, I swiped at it, golden lines forming in the air and cutting through its beak.

A third dove at me, along with a fourth.

Taking a deep breath, I used Sovereign's Call. All of them paused in midair, and started to fall.

I held it for as long as possible, which went by faster than I’d have liked. Two hit the ground, but the other two broke free and glided down.

[You have gained experience from combat.]

[You have gained experience from combat.]

The giant pupper, which had been watching from a crouch, leaped after them like they were balls to play with.

I dipped down, letting my shadows cross over one of the carcasses on the ground. Chicken, and something else less pleasant, filled my mouth as I darted back in the direction of the chain.

Havi, Manee, and Sile all sat on the floor with their backs pressed up against the wall panels.

“Well, that was something,” I muttered, as I landed in the center of the wooden boards, before sitting against the only open panel.

“Wasn’t expecting fliers?” asked Sile.

I just shook my head.

[You have devoured part of a Flock of Wings & Teeth, and gained insight into Flight.]

[You have gained experience from combat.]

Well, two more of them were down. Plus, the one I’d eaten had given me a little bonus to my flying skills. I’d take that.

Barking echoed across the plains.

“The dog seems to like them,” I added, with a little chuckle.

The platform inched up link by link without a sound, but the boards vibrated as it went. The energy near the chain at my back itched a little.

“Scary creature,” muttered Manee, still smiling. “Seems to like you…”

“It's young, very young.” Again, I wondered how something that high level felt so much like a child. “I’m not sure it's from this world, to be honest. It might be stuck here.”

Havi frowned. “My father spoke about anomalies, things that slip through the cracks. This entire world feels that way.”

As soon as he said crack, my eyes went to the tear in the sky. No one else saw it, which worried me. My perception and insight couldn’t be that much higher than everyone else's.

I tried to trigger my earpiece, but nothing happened. It was gone from my ear.

Dang.

I didn’t have a way to contact the others, except using my skill to pull them to me, and I didn’t want to do that. Not unless I really, really needed to.

None of them said anything after that. We just sat in the darkness taking a moment to relax. By the time we reached three quarters of the way up, something made my skin tingle more than the chain did.

The darkness suddenly lightened, again like someone turned on a dimmer switch. No sun in the sky, no stars, nothing. The crack flared with green and purple light, but it didn’t touch any of the landmasses.

From this high up, the crack appeared almost on eye level in the distance. The green and purple energy didn’t reach any higher.

Yet, it reminded me of something. It itched at the back of my mind. Then, it all came back. My divinity trial, and the high-concentration mana area. The spires of rocks and the orb in the sky.

I had closed that tear, and then run into the dragon. It was right before I was yanked out.

This tear reminded me of that.

I stood up and crossed the platform, leaning closer. Mana flickered around the edges of the tear, and my second sight took over. The light radiating out across the land came from that tear.

I wanted to get closer.

“Are we going to target the lifts, or the council itself?” asked Sile, out of nowhere.

It caused me to pull back from the edge. I turned to face him.

Havi stared up at the underside of the mountain. “The mountain is a large area to search. We have to assume they will have guards.”

“Finding them might take time, unless Alex can pinpoint them,” added Manee.

I pulled up the bounty list, and started skimming through the names. “The problem is, I don’t know who actually sits on the high council. I don’t even know how many people there are on the high council.”

The list of Forgers with bounties had shortened since we’d arrived on this world. More than we took out were missing, but not by much. No one had been added.

“At least the energy is getting better,” said Manee, taking a deep breath. “It's easier to breathe.”

I turned to look at the construction of the platforms. Some mana was used with the gears, but not much else. If those were destroyed, they wouldn’t run.

“Destroy the gears and they won’t work. We could each target the three other platforms, then all meet back up here to go down this one.” I moved closer to the gearwork and pointed at the rod holding the main gear in place. “This axle is what's holding the entire thing together, but it was created by a higher level being.”

“So we could destroy the platforms, but what about those below?” asked Manee. “We passed those who weren’t working with the Forgers. This could be a haven for them from the shifts below.”

I closed my eyes. I hated choices like this.

“Our goal is to stop the Forgers. Those below are survivors. We need to focus on stopping the council.” At least, I hoped so. The number of bounties left on this world were too many to take out alone. A good amount of them needed to be below.

The platform crossed the underside of the mountain, and the crack vanished from view with the floating rock in front of it. All of us readied ourselves, yet no new enemies appeared on my map.

We rose up, and the platform hovered in the air revealing not a single person in sight. Just an empty stone platform.

All of us quickly climbed off, and the platform only paused for a couple of minutes before beginning its run back down. Jagged rocks blocked most of the landscape, but the stone platform led to a set of metal rails.

“Is this for a train?” I asked myself. They twisted off behind more of the spires. Everyone else searched the platform, while Sile vanished beyond some rocks.

I leaned over the edge and glanced both ways before triggering second sight. The tracks vibrated with some sort of energy that wasn’t mana. It slowly increased as I watched. A slight rushing sound came from one direction.

There wasn’t anyone on my radar. “I bet they aren’t expecting anyone until the platform, which is the next run, and wherever this goes, that's where the people are.”

At least, I hoped so. The Forgers’ technology ranged from hovercarts to magic lifts to, now, trains. Though, I wasn’t sure if the hovercarts could actually run here.

The train continued on its slow path down the rails. The tall jagged spirals just kept going, blocking most of the view except what was on the tracks right in front of us. Nothing appeared on my radar.

Strange popped out and sniffed out every inch of the wooden platform we all stood on. No one said anything as the tension increased. We waited.

Time ticked by so slowly, as literally nothing happened. The scenery didn’t change. The speed on the rails stayed constant, and we saw no one.

Finally, we came across another platform, but it was empty. The chain gleamed with its strange energy, but that was it.

Sile and I crept forward at a slower pace, careful to not dislodge any of the gravel at our feet.
