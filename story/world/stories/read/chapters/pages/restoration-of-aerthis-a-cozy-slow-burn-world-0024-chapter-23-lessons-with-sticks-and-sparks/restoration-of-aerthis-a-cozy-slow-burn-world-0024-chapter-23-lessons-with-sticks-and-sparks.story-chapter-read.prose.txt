The sun hadn’t even crested the rooftops when Starpuff decided her cheek needed immediate prodding.

Tap.

Pause.

Tap tap.

A third one came with what could only be described as enthusiasm, followed by a gentle pat to the forehead like it was bestowing divine purpose.

Elynara groaned into her pillow.

“You are the worst spirit alarm.”

Starpuff trilled proudly.

She cracked one eye open just in time to see it do a smug loop near her ear, glowing just bright enough to make blinking unpleasant. With a sigh that was mostly affection, she rolled upright, stretching both arms toward the ceiling. Her back gave a satisfying pop. Her shoulders only protested a little.

Progress.

The dull ache in her limbs had faded overnight into something manageable—still there, but less like a complaint and more like a grumpy reminder. Which meant today was perfect.

The day.

She had a plan. A real one. And it was shaky, yes. Possibly foolish. But it was hers. Each step forward lately had given it shape. Teaching. Charging objects. Helping others reach the spark of mana. There were still a thousand things she didn’t know, but for once, the path ahead wasn’t empty.

And if she was going to walk that path—really walk it—then she needed to be ready.

Today, she was hitting one hundred capacity.

Her fingers flexed once in the morning light. Then, slowly, she settled cross-legged on her bed, letting her staff rest across her lap. A breath. Then another. Eyes closed.

Mana in.

Mana out.

Each pull filled her chest with a familiar, humming warmth. She didn’t force it. Just let it build. Slow, steady cycles, like breathing in color. Like weaving strands into thread.

She reached her current limit easily. Ninety-four. Then, as always, she pushed—just a little. A small stretch. A test. The resistance rose like a tide, gentle but firm, pressing back against her reach.

But she had learned how to lean through it.

Her will pressed against the edge again. Held. Released. Mana out, mana in. Then pressed again.

It was always like this now. Each cycle faster, but every point needing more. But not impossible.

Not yet.

She kept going. Breath by breath. Cycle by cycle.

Ninety-eight.

She felt anticipation then, her chest thrumming with mana.

Ninety-nine.

She grit her teeth and pushed one last time.

A breath. A pull.

And it was done.

A soft jolt ran through her chest. Not quite pain, not even discomfort. Just a shift. Like something had finally given way and let her through. Her core buzzed, full and dense.

One hundred mana capacity.

Starpuff did a celebratory cartwheel above her head, its glow turning briefly gold. It looked very proud of itself.

Elynara, however, collapsed backward into her pillow with a soft groan.

“Ow,” she mumbled, staring up at the ceiling. “That hurt in ways I can’t describe.”

She grinned anyway.

Totally worth it.

The number meant little, technically. Arbitrary, as all thresholds were. But it felt like something. Like a mark in time. A flag planted. A quiet declaration that she was ready for what came next.

The path was long.

But she was walking it now with just a little more power to give.

Her musings done, Elynara slowly readied up for the day, Starpuff harassing her every step, that rascal really knew no bounds!

She tried catching it with mana, but it just ate its was out. And was floating around looking very smug.

Really.

Getting out she soon made it to the breakfast hall, it already buzzed with too much energy and not enough tea.

Elynara had already cleared half her bowl by the time Mina stumbled in, hair clinging to one side of her face and her robe slightly mismatched like she had lost a fight with both sleep and gravity. Pufflet waddled beside her, tail flicking, looking equally offended to be conscious.

“You’re cheerful,” Mina muttered, collapsing into the bench across from her.

“Well, we’re going on a trip today. And I hit a hundred,” Elynara said brightly, pushing the last bite of bread into her mouth.

Mina blinked blearily.

“Of what?”

“Mana capacity.” She beamed. “A lovely round number. Very aesthetic. I feel victorious.”

Mina stared at her like she’d just confessed to counting tree bark for fun.

“You’re weird.”

“Flatter me more.”

They ate in companionable disarray, surrounded by the sounds of the orphanage waking up. Kids ran in and out of the hall, half-dressed, half-fed, and wholly chaotic. Word had clearly spread—again—that the Fire Queen and Mina were going on “an actual trip,” and the sheer volume of attention was… excessive.

One kid clung to Elynara’s leg. Another solemnly asked if they could bring back a monster tooth. A third wanted “a rock that glows but only on Tuesdays.”

It took three hugs, two dramatic goodbyes, and one heartfelt demand for souvenirs before they were released.

Elynara promised to return in one piece. Mina promised to keep Pufflet from licking anything arcane. Pufflet sneezed once, then burrowed into Mina’s cloak like it was deeply offended by being given rules.

They made it as far as the church gate before Elynara suddenly veered left, tugging Mina with her.

“Wait—aren’t we supposed to—?”

“Shopping.”

Mina narrowed her eyes.

“Why does that sound like a threat?”

“It’s not. It’s a favor. For you. And technically for Pufflet.”

The girls reached their destination quite quickly, Elynara stopping for a moment to take it all in.

The market still smelled like rain. Damp earth, wood dust, and that sharp, warm scent from the baker’s cart two stalls over. The vendors were already in full swing, canvas awnings fluttering gently as people filtered through.

Elynara, however, had the energy of a guided missile. She wove between stalls like she knew exactly what she wanted—which was only half true, but Mina didn’t need to know that.

First, a travel pouch. Sturdy, leather-bound, but lined with soft fleece on the inside. Pufflet immediately curled into it and refused to leave.

“See? He loves it.”

“I think he’s nesting.”

“Same thing.”

Next came the snacks. Dried pears. Nut-sugar bars. Something that smelled kind of like cinnamon bread but looked suspiciously like sugar pretending to be fruit. Mina tried to protest the growing pile—Elynara drowned her out with enthusiastic humming and a very selective sense of hearing.

And then it happened.

A gleam caught the corner of Elynara’s eye. A glint of green set against a curled silver stem.

A hairpin. Shaped like a leaf, just beginning to unfurl.

She picked it up without hesitation.

“Try this.”

Mina looked at it like it was a dagger.

“Why?”

“Because it’s adorable. And I think it would look great on you. And if I don’t use these coins soon, I’ll do something reckless, like buy a decorative wand sheath.”

“You don’t even have a wand.”

“Exactly.”

Mina sighed like someone being emotionally mugged.

“I can stop you.”

“You can complain. Which is different.”

Despite herself, Mina let her fasten the pin into her hair. It caught the light, glinting softly against the darker strands.

The look on her face wobbled somewhere between a pout and something else. Something smaller. Quieter.

“You’re the worst,” she muttered.

“And you’re glowing.”

“I’m not.”

“You are.”

“I will throw Pufflet at you.”

“Bold threat. He’s cozy right now.”

They left the market with their arms bumping every third step, Pufflet dozing in his new mobile home, and Starpuff zipping overhead with dramatic loops like it was the herald of shopping victories.

Elynara didn’t stop smiling the whole way to the guild, and even Mina seemed to be in a good mood now. The snacks must have helped!

It hummed with its late-morning rhythm—just enough movement to feel alive, just quiet enough to hear the shuffle of papers and clink of gear from the back room. Elynara slipped in with a spring to her step, Mina just a half-step behind, carrying her newly christened travel bag like it might run off if she let go.

Starpuff barreled in first, trilling loudly as it zipped over the job board and then did an overly dramatic loop above Alicia’s desk.

Alicia addressed them without looking up.

“What brings you here today, Elynara?”

She dragged Mina over to Alicia’s desk before answering conspicuously:

“We’re here on scholarly business.”

That earned her a raised brow when Alicia finally glanced up—followed by a second, slightly higher one when she spotted Mina.

“You brought a sidekick.”

Elynara gave an exaggerated huff.

“I brought a student. There’s a difference. She learns. She assists. She has great instincts and decent aim with a bucket.”

Mina flushed but gave a polite wave, the corner of her mouth twitching upward. Pufflet peeked from her bag and sneezed like it was trying to make a statement.

Alicia gestured them forward and pulled a parchment from a short stack already waiting at her elbow. She slid it across the desk, fingertips tapping the top edge.

“Same farm region. Close to where you stayed before. They requested you by name—monster signs, minor patrols. They’re also asking for spirit check-ins.”

Elynara’s eyes lit up, and her fingers drummed against the side of her satchel.

“Of course they are.”

From the far table, Marcus made a very quiet, very smug hmmph. She glanced toward him just in time to catch an exaggerated pantomime of a dramatic stagger followed by a mock collapse. She ignored him with masterful professionalism.

Instead, she turned back to Alicia, tone suddenly too casual to be innocent.

“So… I was thinking.”

Alicia’s pen froze.

“Oh no.”

“Hear me out,” Elynara said, already pulling out her notebook. “I’ll take Mina with me. We stay with the same family—Rina, Finnie, all of them. I do the patrols, yes. But also…” She flipped open a page, showing a rough sketch of circles and lines. “I visit every spirit I can find in the area. Map out where they are. How active, what condition the area is in. Everything. A proper field survey.”

For a moment, Alicia didn’t reply. She leaned back just slightly, eyes narrowing in thought.

“You want to make a spirit registry.”

Elynara nodded.

“More than that. I want to make a map. I figure if I keep track of how the spirits are faring, how far apart they are, we will know where to focus the offerings and charms. And maybe… maybe knowing they are there, people will take them more seriously.”

Alicia tapped her pen twice against the desk, then pulled out a blank slip of parchment.

“If you bring back a legible copy with coordinates, notations, and confirmed locations, the guild will file it as auxiliary research. Small bonus payout per confirmed site. No promises beyond that.”

“Deal.”

Elynara turned slightly and gave Mina a nudge.

“Also, she’s my field assistant. She has a badge now. It’s called a hairpin.”

Mina went very still under the menacing attention of quiet professionalism incarnate but gave a tiny bow—one she seemed to instantly regret when Alicia offered her a formal handshake.

“Looking forward to working with you, Miss,” Alicia said, voice dry but polite.

Mina blinked, then shook her hand quickly, ears pinking up. Pufflet sneezed again from the bag, like it had a quota to hit.

“Well then,” Alicia said, already writing into her ledgers, “best get going. The weather is clear. No reason to tarry.”

After thanking Alicia, Elynara pulled Mina with her to leave the guild.

And so they marched out—the Fire Queen, her apprentice, a floating spirit with zero discipline, and a sleepy puff-beast still chewing on a corner of its own travel pouch.

The doors swung shut behind them with a breeze and a thud, the guild echoing faintly with the sound of a new quest in motion.

Their steps took them towards the gates quickly, Elynara determined, and Mina quietly following.

The road out of town crunched softly beneath their boots, worn smooth by the slow insistence of wagons, travelers, and the weight of years.

It wasn’t muddy anymore, just damp enough to give beneath each step with a satisfying give. Elynara didn’t speak right away. The town faded behind them in the hush of morning mist, and ahead—nothing but fields, sky, and the world stretching open like a page not yet written.

The clouds hung low but friendly, streaked with gold in the breaking light. A breeze tugged gently at her scarf, cool enough to bite if she stood still, but perfect once her legs found their rhythm again.

Beside her, Mina walked in quiet strides—shoulders just a touch stiff, steps careful, like she hadn’t quite convinced herself this was really happening. Her eyes flicked between the horizon, the fields, and Elynara, as if checking whether the world would stay kind a little longer.

They passed the outer post without fuss. The guard on duty—definitely not Josh, unless he had shrunk, changed species, and decided to care about posture—gave them a curious look and a brief, cheerful “Stay safe out there!” Then went back to his tea. Or staring into space. Possibly both.

And just like that, they were past the gate. Out. Free.

Mina stopped walking.

“We’re out,” she said, voice small but full of something like awe.

Elynara grinned.

“Welcome to the outside.”

Mina blinked once, then reached down to unhook her satchel’s side flap. Pufflet tumbled out in a blur of fur and enthusiasm, hitting the ground like a springy rock and immediately scampering toward the nearest clump of weeds with the single-minded intensity of a creature that had never sniffed a wild plant before.

Starpuff, of course, had been out and abound for minutes already, trailing green light and smug confidence in a lazy loop overhead. It dipped toward Mina’s hair like it was considering nesting, thought better of it, and zoomed ahead again in victory spirals.

The road itself was quiet. Just enough sun had broken through to warm the air, and the scattered clouds let the light dapple across the patchwork fields in shifting stripes. They didn’t talk much. Not because there was nothing to say, but because the silence felt right. A gentle hush in which footsteps could breathe, and birdsong didn’t need to compete.

The trees on the horizon were still distant, but their outlines already held a promise. One of shaded paths and spirit lights and whatever else might come.

“Hey,” Mina said, holding up a very small stick. “Think he’ll chase it?”

“He tried to eat chalk once,” Elynara replied. “His standards are nonexistent.”

Mina snorted and twirled the stick. Pufflet, somehow catching the motion from across the road, skidded to a stop and stared with absolute focus.

“Ready?” Mina crouched, narrowed her eyes. “Fetch!”

The stick went flying. Pufflet sneezed dramatically in its direction, did a tiny, confused hop—and then sprinted past the stick to chase a beetle instead.

“I stand corrected,” Elynara said solemnly. “Clearly, he only chases worthy foes.”

Mina laughed, for real this time. A warm, bubbling sound that rose above the wind and set something inside Elynara relaxing that she hadn’t even known was tense.

They walked on like that. Throwing sticks. Occasionally attempting to teach Pufflet to sit (he did not), or to roll (he just flopped sideways and blinked). Starpuff joined in once, briefly, then decided its job was to judge from above and resumed lazy loops like a flying crown.

The fields rolled by, quiet and gold. Wind danced through young leaves. Birds sang in passing. And with every step, the weight of the town fell further behind them, replaced with something gentler, clearer. Like possibility.

The road didn’t feel long, but for that moment, it belonged entirely to them.

The sun had climbed high enough to soften its bite, spilling warm light in long, sleepy stripes across the road.

The road mellowed with them.

Elynara glanced sideways. Mina still walked close, her earlier energy subdued now to a thoughtful sort of pace.

Elynara smiled. Time for part two.

She reached into her own bag and pulled out another slim length of wood—smooth, polished, and faintly humming with warmth. The mana it exuded still wobbled a bit under her fingers.

Without a word, she passed it into Mina’s hand.

The younger girl blinked at it, then at her.

“Here, I charged it with mana,” Elynara said quietly. “Try again. Just… walk with it. Feel it. Don’t push too hard.”

Mina looked down, frowning slightly.

“What if I trip?”

Elynara held out her other hand, palm up, the answer already waiting in her eyes.

“Then hold on.”

Mina hesitated only a moment before slipping her fingers into hers.

They walked like that for a while—quiet, steady, the rhythm of their steps syncing naturally. Mina’s gaze dipped to the wood in her hand, brow furrowed in quiet focus. She was trying to do as Elynara asked her to. To feel and listen, letting her breath fall into step with the soft thrum of magic nestled in her fingers.

Elynara let the silence stretch. She felt the curve of the road ahead more than she saw it, let her awareness drift in the calm. Her voice lifted, soft and low, into a hum. Some tune from her memories with no words she recognized. Just something old and quiet that felt like warmth around a campfire or stories whispered under quilts.

Mina didn’t speak, or look up. But her hand didn’t let go, either.

Even Starpuff, ever dramatic, had slowed to a lazy spin above them—trailing faint wisps of gold now instead of green, like it, too, had been lulled by the hush of the road.

Eventually, Pufflet gave a tiny snort and dragged himself from where he’d been trailing behind. He paused, flopped theatrically into the dust, and looked up at them with the weary resignation of a creature who had gone on exactly five steps too many.

Elynara chuckled and scooped him up, carefully tucking him into the cloth-lined bag that had unofficially become his personal transport. His nose buried into the folds with a pleased huff. Mission complete.

Mina said nothing, but her mouth twitched at the corners.

The rest of the path stretched out in soft curves, the landscape opening up around them like a book returning to a favorite page. And just beyond the bend of the road the familiar fields stretched out.

Elynara gave Mina’s hand a gentle squeeze, and Mina, still focused on the mana in her palm, squeezed back.

It took them another hour of walking before the girls arrived at their destination.

They reached the gate by late afternoon, just as the shadows began to stretch and the roofline dipped in soft golden light. A thin breeze kicked at the grass ahead of them, and before either could step up to knock, the front door burst open with a dramatic swing, and out stormed Rina—apron flapping, cheeks flushed from either baking or sheer excitement, possibly both.

She marched out like she had been waiting all day.

“Well well! Look what the fields dragged in today!”

Elynara didn’t even manage a word before Rina swept over, arms open, grin wide.

“And you brought your little girlfriend this time too, how sweet—look at her, she’s adorable.”

Mina froze mid-step. Elynara choked on her greeting.

“She’s not—wait—Rina!”

But the older woman was already beside them, crouching just enough to give Mina a quick, effortless hug. Mina didn’t resist, though her eyes darted toward Elynara with betrayal.

“You didn’t tell me she was this precious. Oh, you’re helping in the kitchen later. Anyone that cute has to earn their snacks.”

“I—what?” Mina’s voice barely cleared a whisper.

Elynara sighed.

“She’s not—ugh. Hi, Rina.”

“Hi, she says.” Rina reached out and ruffled her hair with one hand. “Still stiff as ever.”

The house hadn’t changed much. Same creaky front step. Same sun-warmed tile near the kitchen. The same laughter spilling in from the far end where the kids were likely already arguing about who got more beans in their stew.

Rina didn’t ask why they came. Just waved them inside, pointed at a clean towel, and said there was still dough to knead if they felt like being useful.

Once the bustle settled and Mina was comfortably trapped in light conversation with Finnie, Elynara finally brought up the reason they had come.

She pulled the parchment from her satchel and explained the patrol request. The need for another sweep. The map she was building, and why.

Rina glanced at the note, then back at her.

“You’re making a map of spirits now?” Her smile didn’t falter. “You really are one of those don’t-sit-still types.”

“It’s useful,” Elynara replied. “And no one else is doing it. Not sure if they even could”

That earned a nod and a slow, one-armed hug. No hair ruffling this time—just a hand resting on her shoulder.

“You’re a good one,” Rina said simply. “And you’ve got my full blessing. Just don’t forget to eat.”

They helped with the rest of the evening prep. Pulled weeds by the far fence. Fed the chickens. Mina got introduced to the more cooperative cow. Pufflet tried to befriend a bucket.

Then Elynara went to check on the shrines, Mina in tow.

Knowing their location, the trip there did not take long. Elynara knelt near the pond one, and offered a handful of mana to the eagerly waiting spirits.

Behind her, Mina stood with arms tucked in, peering at the pond.

“Are you... asking them something?” she asked.

“More like checking in.” Elynara stayed where she was, hands resting on her knees now. “They’re alright. Quieter than last time, but I think that’s good.”

“You can tell?”

Elynara nodded.

“Sort of. I think I’m starting to learn how to read them better.” She turned slightly. “You brought the piece?”

Mina dug in her satchel and took out the same wood she was using to train during the walk. Elynara supplied it with more mana, and pushed Mina forward, showing her what to do.

She held it out, palm up, and the curious spirits floated over, nibbling on the offering.

They didn’t rush like Starpuff. Just soft dips of motion and slow curls through the air, weaving faint trails above the wood. The smaller one darted first, the larger following a few moments later with a more reserved pulse.

Elynara watched them with practiced calm.

“They like it.”

Mina squinted.

“I can’t see anything.”

“They’re still there.”

“I figured.” She kept her hand steady, though her other one twitched like it wanted to help. “Feels weird. Not scary. Just... weird.”

“That’s good. They’re just curious, not hostile.”

The spirits fed slowly, draining the stored mana in soft flickers. When the thread thinned, they drifted back again, curling past the stone in a slow orbit before settling near the shrine base.

Elynara waited until they moved away, then reached out and brushed a thumb along Mina’s hand.

“You did well.”

“It didn’t feel like much.”

“It was enough for them.”

She took the wood back and checked the feel - there was still some mana left - before handing it back to Mina.

Then they went to check on the second shrine, repeating the process, only turning back as the light dipped lower, sunset brushing gold across the field edges.

Pufflet popped out from a thicket with something suspiciously stick-like in its mouth. Starpuff reappeared with a smug loop above her head and poked her hair for no discernible reason.

Elynara didn’t bother reacting.

The walk back was short. By the time they reached the house, the windows glowed soft and the smell of cooked grain and herbs rolled out from the open kitchen hatch.

Elynara handed off her staff and helped set the table. Mina joined a minute later, brushing dirt from her sleeves and pretending her arms weren’t still a little stiff from earlier.

They ate in a quiet corner while Rina talked over the day’s plans with her husband, and the youngest two kids chased Pufflet until he gave up and rolled under the bench.

Elynara didn’t say much until they were both tucked in for the night. The guest room was the same one she remembered. One bed now shared with two small bags, a curled-up Pufflet, and one blanket that definitely wasn’t sized for three bodies and a sprite.

She sat up for a while longer, notebook balanced against her knee.

Mina peeked over once. “You’re writing about today?”

“Mapping where the spirits are. Notes on their behavior. Details for the guild copy later.”

“You always work this late?”

“Only when I have something worth noting.”

Mina shifted, turned toward the wall, and mumbled something she probably meant to keep quiet.

Elynara smiled anyway. Then put finishing touches on her notes, before wiggling into the perfect position.

And soon the sleep took her.
