Chapter 41: Loot and Triage

While no one had been in the mood for a fight so close to the previous one, no one could deny the thrill of conquest and taking someone’s cards. Even Luthor, as much as he tried to be above it, couldn’t help but breathe a sigh of relief and satisfaction upon seeing exactly what they needed.

[Healing Touch: Rare Hybrid. Call forth your inner light and heal those with moderate wounds.

Spell Effect: Heal moderate wounds and energize the target, encouraging regeneration. 3 Mana/1 Charge. 3 Weekly charges remain.

Special: This takes up a slot in both combat and noncombat decks.]

Once they’d found it, Luthor had immediately made room and equipped it, grateful that everyone’s raise to Affinity Level 4 had given them more slots. He burned a charge on healing Ambrose’s head injury. The concussion didn’t leave immediately, but seemed to get better as everyone went through their cards.

Everyone waited with bated breath when Ambrose equipped a card named [Darkness] in his one open slot. After a second, he laughed and said, “It changed. It changed into a fucking imp. Look.” He held out his hand, and it appeared.

[Umbral Imp: Common. Summons a hazy, shady imp at your side, capable of causing havoc without ever being seen.

Special: Stealth. This creature starts out stealthed until it attacks or uses an ability.

Special: Once every other round, this creature can create a fifteen foot radius magical cloud of darkness centered around it for fifteen seconds, blocking all sight and muffling sound.

Strength: D. Defense: D. Speed: C. Magic: B. 1 mana]

Luthor didn’t know what to make of it, other than acknowledging that Ambrose’s Wildcard had turned it into something powerful. For all they knew, the Wildcard might’ve even made the difference between life and death against Vanderborn. It was strong, useful, and it vexed the shadow wizard. All things considered: perfect.

“Is that a bad thing?” he asked.

“Not really,” said Ambrose with something of a sigh. “It’s been really useful so far, but…”

“Cheer up,” Raeva said, coming up behind him and throwing an arm around his shoulders. “The imps suit you, and I’m honestly a little excited to see how weird it gets. We can’t all be winners here.”

The elder Ransa had picked up a simple, but fairly strong card of her own.

[Hotfoot: Uncommon. Blaze with personal speed, and let it out in controlled bursts!

Spell Effect: You gain 3 charges of enhanced speed and perception, usable in one second bursts. Lasts until all charges are gone, or thirty seconds pass.

Special: Immediate casting time. 3 mana]

“I guess we can’t,” said Raeleq, who was the only one who didn’t pick up a usable new card. There had been no Wild wizards in that group at all. “That’s okay though. I’ll find something soon.”

Luthor nodded his way, and then looked at Jessica, who remained mostly silent as she contemplated the card in front of her. He debated saying something, and decided it was important to keep things moving and not let his friend get too caught up in her head. “What’s wrong, Jessica?”

She jerked as if surprised. “Nothing. It’s just…When we all hit Affinity Level 4…My max mana didn’t increase. It’s still only three mana. And this card, it’d cost all of it. I’d be able to use it and my cantrip, and that’s about it. I’m just…kind of sick of not being more useful.”

Before Luthor could say anything, Ambrose spoke up. “Not useful? Don’t be stupid. That last fight, you used two spells to kill the guy who neutralized me and Raeva. Someone as smart as you only needs two spells at a go.”

Jessica shrank a little, hiding behind her dark hair. Eventually, she nodded, and the card disappeared into her deck. A second later, she displayed it for them all to see.

[Lethargic Lash: Rare. Reach out with a stream of temporal magic and slow the target down.

Spell effect: Target enemy slows down 70% for 2-10 seconds, based on the power difference between the caster and opponent. 3 Mana.]

The rest of the cards had been a mix of commons and uncommons that weren’t especially useful to any of them, but would probably be good to trade for supplies, if it came to that. No one, not even Ambrose, had been greedy enough to try to claim more than one or two out of the pile. With that done, Luthor decided it was time to move on, before time worked against them.

“Alright. Now, we don’t have too much time. I want Jessica and SNAC to inspect the damage to the ship. I know you’re new to flying ships, but you also have the most experience out of all of us, and your affinity might work in your favor.”

Jessica nodded grimly. SNAC was currently busy scrubbing blood off the deck. A task he assured them he was used to. No one had asked further.

Continuing, he nodded to his best friend. “Raeleq, Raeva, I want you to scout the area around the ship, and see if there are any threats or things to worry about. Do not go far, but at least see how bad the slope is, going back up.”

Raeleq nodded, and Raeva released Ambrose to give an exaggerated shiver. “I love it when he takes charge like this.”

“Hey, I was going to make that joke,” Ambrose half-heartedly protested. “What am I supposed to do, then?”

“Help me take stock of our resources and put together some packs for traveling. Unless that’s beneath you.” Luthor’s lips twitched, but he kept a mostly straight face.

The shadow wizard waved his hand. “It is, but that’s okay. I might as well be helpful in more than just fights.”

“For a change,” Raeva said, nudging him.

Before it could become a thing, Raeleq went to the railing. “How, uh. How are we supposed to get down?”

The Wanderer happened to have several rope ladders meant to be thrown over the side. The weird angle they rested on made it a bit awkward from the sides, but the aft of the ship proved mostly perfect, and half the group climbed down, while SNAC went to inspect the damage from the inside rather than leave the ship. That left Luthor and Ambrose to go below deck, and into the cargo hold.

It was odd, Luthor reflected as they went down, that the ship, dim as it was inside, was still better lit and less oppressive than the darkness of the world itself. Their little magelights made it relatively easy to see, and the wood walls around them kept them protected and hidden, rather than being in a wide, open, dark cavern where anything could be lurking.

He wasn’t the type to be afraid of anything, but this was a matter of practicality. The unknown wasn’t to be feared itself, but it sure had a way of causing complications.

“So, what are we putting together?” Ambrose asked when they arrived in the tightly packed hold, where barrels and chests were packed in tight enough to be hard to move. “I’ve gone traveling, obviously, but I’ve never had to rough it. I’d always anticipated being more of an urban wizard.”

Oh, this was going to be fun. “We’re going to need water, some basic rations, blanket, matches or a flint and tinder, and some glowstones, just in case. The real trick is going to be finding packs, but if this vessel was really used for Vanderborn to help conquer worlds, I think we’ll be fine.”

“Do you?” Ambrose said, ducking his head under a low beam. He leaned against a keg of beer insolently. “Vanderborn has all of my flaws and then some. Do you think that he’d really care about supplying others with things he doesn’t himself need?”

“I do.” Luthor turned from him and opened a chest. It was full of bottles of different recovery potions. He tapped the chest and said, “He might be an arrogant, bloodthirsty tyrant, but there are enough rooms for a full crew. Chances are, he set this ship up to be a mobile base for himself and strike teams he’d take with him or transport from world to world. He’d make sure they had what they needed.”

Ambrose frowned, but nodded. “It’d be nice if they’d fucking label things, but I suppose they just used SNAC for that. Are we sure we need him to check the damage? While we put together these packs, the imperial assholes could be getting away.”

“Pretty sure,” Luthor said. In a lot of ways, he was grateful for Ambrose. Most of the others would listen to him without blinking, but he always had to poke and prod and make sure that others weren’t being too optimistic or complacent. The man was still a pain in the ass sometimes.

“We need to think both long-term and short-term. We fought four people, and the ship wasn’t big. Whoever was left was the one flying the ship, and maybe two others, tops. They might have had a worse crash, or injuries. Either way, they aren’t going to get away, if only because if we find their ship, [Reggie] should be able to track them by scent.”

Ambrose paused. “He can do that? Huh. And there’s always my imps as well. Okay, I see your point.”

“Good. Now shut up and look for something useful for me.”

They shared a quick laugh and started going through the chests and crates.

It took a few minutes, but aside from the medical potions, they came across pre-packaged rations, some waterskins, and everything else Luthor listed, except for the translation cards. Luthor methodically put together the first two packs, layering everything in just right to cradle the potions in the blankets and stack the waterskins on top for easy access. Ambrose joined in after, and they ventured to the lab at the bottom of the ship, laden with bags.

It was the first time Luthor had been there, and he immediately saw the value in it. If only for the open area in the middle where they’d be able to run drills, practice using cards, and other things. For when they weren’t immediately under attack again.

The only downside was the hole in the starboard side, about eight feet wide and three feet tall, where a rock or stalagmite had ripped it open. SNAC crouched, running a too-many-fingered hand over the wood.

“Shit,” said Ambrose, letting the packs slide off his shoulders to the floor. “How screwed are we?”

SNAC looked up. “I believe the damage isn’t as bad as it looks. While it will take some materials and time to repair it, the main enchantments keeping the ship moving are mostly intact. I can begin, with tools and spare parts we have in storage. The greatest issue we have is that Stonskarr requires an extra enchantment we don’t have, due to the heavier, underground nature of the world. If we wish to fly out of here, we’ll need that part.”

Luthor took a deep breath, while Ambrose cursed again. It was pointless to get mad or upset about it. He had a feeling there would be a problem like this anyway, so it was just another task to add to the pile. “We’ll get it. What’s the part?”

“It is called a Levi-Box, and Bedrock Spire should have them, to keep other Imperial ships afloat while operating here.” SNAC stood up and turned to them. “They will also have resources to repair the wing and hull while we are here, past what I am capable of doing. Assuming that it doesn’t jeopardize the secret mission.”

He swore the golem sounded suspicious, but that was a problem for later. “Then we’ll have to go there. C’mon, Ambrose. We need to get everyone together.” Luthor shifted the packs and climbed through the hole in the hull.

Jessica waited for them outside, sitting on a rock and looking despondent. Before he could ask her how she was doing, the Ransas returned, with a small, glowing ball of fire orbiting Raeva’s head much like Luthor’s own light spell. They met in a semi-circle next to the hole, where Raeleq immediately spoke.

“The slope is steep, and there’s plenty of creatures around, but they didn’t seem to want to come anywhere near us. I don’t believe this place is safe, but it isn’t going to kill us immediately.”

“There’s that, at least,” said Ambrose.

Raeva continued, “Whatever we do, we need to do it fast, though. I don’t like the feeling of this place, and the survivors might be getting away as we speak.”

Luthor shook his head. “According to the golem, the nearest Imperial Outpost is two days travel south. We have time, but we also have a bigger mission than expected. The first thing we do, obviously, is we find the fallen ship and we stop them from snitching on us. From there, the prudent thing to do would be to get as much information from them as we can. Because in order to fix the ship, we might need to head into enemy territory and see if we can procure some materials and then…”

“And then what?” Ambrose scoffed. “Repair it ourselves? Think SNAC’s good for that, or has anyone here taken up airship repair?”

“SNAC can do it, and I can help,” Jessica said quickly. “I don’t have much experience, but I’m going to learn. I promise.” She shifted as if she wanted to say something else, but stopped.

Luthor nodded and looked over to the big slope they’d need to climb. The ship emanated just enough light to see the slope, and the occasional dim glows going upwards, but this place was so dark and ominous. He wasn’t scared, he was wary.

“Then I think we need to get a move on. SNAC, can you engage stealth mode, to keep others from finding us? Will you be okay on your own?” They couldn’t afford to keep someone back, but he didn’t want to say so. Jessica would’ve volunteered, and he wanted to keep her close.

The golem nodded solemnly. “I can, and I will. There’s also another defensive field I can activate that will deliver a mild shock to any animals that get close, but I am afraid that it drains magical power, and we will not be able to replenish it without either a fuel source, or getting moving in the air again.”

It was another thing to worry about, but acceptable.

“Okay, said Luthor. “We have some provisions, we’ve got a direction, and we have our mission. Does anyone have any reason to linger?”

They shook their heads.

Luthor smiled, feeling his scar stretch. “Then let’s move out.”
