The Tier three proves willing to stay, but it remains tense – as well it might. The battle of domains might be over, but that doesn’t mean that a physical battle won’t follow – and it is clearly far warier of us than it was to begin with. In addition, I suspect from some of its twitches that it isn’t used to having so many pairs of eyes on it.

Bastet speaks for us. I have to rely heavily on my Bond with her and Animal Empathy to understand the communication passing between them, because it certainly isn’t based on words. Instead, it’s built from emotional impressions mixed with body language and the odd vocalisation.

As we’d suspected, the original attack was to warn us away, to frighten us into leaving. Now that I've seen it and felt its domain, I can more easily identify what kind of beast it is – one who relies on an assassin’s tactics rather than head-on confrontation, or domain battle if forced to defend its position.

Since it’s reached Beast Lord level, I can see that those have served it well. If it has the intelligence level that I think it has, it most likely looks for weak points among those stronger physically than itself. For example, while it wouldn’t surely be able to take on Kalanthia in open battle, if it was able to threaten Lathani, Kalanthia would have probably chosen to move away rather than risk her cub’s safety, like she did with the samurans.

Due to recognising its intelligence, I hesitate for a moment when Bastet reaches out to me to ask what her verdict should be. She’s won the domain battle and the beast has chosen not to move it to a physical battle – that implies that it’s accepted the loss of the territory to her.

A beast that intelligent would be an excellent ally to have – or a dangerous enemy. The main issue is determining where its loyalties lie – and without having defeated it personally, I’m not sure whether I’d be able to Dominate it and be certain that it’s on our side. I do make a mental note to test whether my companions can bolster my Willpower in the way I was able to bolster Bastet’s, but this isn’t the moment to test it.

And there’s also the factor that it’s already killed four of our people. Their faces flash through my mind, Layton’s words of Talin’s unfulfilled romantic dreams repeating in my ears. There have to be consequences for that.

It’s with a touch of reluctance that I pass on the message to Bastet, that she then transmits to the beast. It’s a plain one: leave and live; stay and die.

I give it the option because I’m not certain we would be able to kill it here and now – what I’ve gathered from its skill set is just too geared towards escape. Attacking it here and now would make it an outright enemy, and might risk it sticking around to pick us off out of revenge. If it has the option to leave, it might see that as an opportunity to gain a different territory – to start anew somewhere else and not risk its hide against us who have already proven capable of defeating it in one type of battle.

It hesitates, but ultimately makes the choice I suspected it might. To live another day.

Of course, just because it’s agreed doesn’t mean that it will leave, but Bastet makes it very clear that if we see any traces of its presence in her new territory, we will hunt it down, and we will kill it.

I cross my fingers behind my back at that, privately wondering whether we’d even notice if it was sticking around if it doesn’t attack us. But then, if it doesn’t try to kill any more of us, that’s a win in my book. And without warding the whole edge of the territory to track who is coming in and who is leaving – which is probably something we will need to do eventually, at least for beasts over a certain level – we cannot be sure it’s left. Or if it comes back even after leaving.

Still, it appears to take Bastet’s warning of never to hunt our people again seriously. And in return, it gives us a warning of its own.

In the absence of humans, the territories of the Lost Continent appear to have developed a hierarchy that does not exist on Moriax. The Tier threes fight over territories, but above them are Tier fours, who act as overlords to the Tier threes. Apparently, this particular territory falls under the oversight of a Tier four that lives in the mountains.

The Tier three indicates that although Bastet does not strictly need to introduce herself, the Tier four may very well come to visit to see who exactly has taken control of this region – and to demonstrate why she should not grow too arrogant.

The impression shared by the creature does not fill me with enthusiasm: a strong sense of overwhelming power and potent magic. But I suppose that’s typical of a Tier four. The roc certainly fit that bill too.

By the time we separate, the Tier three is practically twitching and it flickers into invisibility as soon as Bastet indicates that the conversation is done. I don’t like knowing that I can’t see it, but by extending my Inspect Skill outwards, I am at least able to feel it moving very rapidly away from us.

“We’ll need to keep a watch out for that creature,” I say darkly as I gaze in the direction it went. “I’m not certain it will leave the territory as it’s agreed to and anything that’s capable of being so undetectable is a potential threat.”

Seeing as the coast seems to be clear for now, I decide to check the notification I can feel waiting for me.

Congratulations!

You have earned a new Skill: Willpower Share

“Huh.” Interesting. I request more information about the new Skill.

Willpower Share

By pushing the boundaries of what is possible with the link between you and your Bonded, you have discovered that you are able to use your formidable Will to support them in battles of their own. While you cannot conduct the battle for them, you can aid them in a manner proportional to the depth of your Bond with them and your familiarity with their souls.

Having read the description, I can only wonder one thing – did I force the System to make up a Skill for me? This isn’t the more generic stat-share that Nicholas spoke to me about. In that case, it operates much like how Transformation does – relatively clinically. This, however, is far more conditional and makes it clear that it only worked because of how well I know Bastet.

In the end I shrug and wave away the Skill description. If it helps me help my companions, I’m not going to complain. And maybe I’ll be able to leverage it to share more than just my ‘formidable Will’ with them. If beasts like this Tier three attack us, we might need it.

On that note…

“Did you get it?” I ask Lathani and Catch as they emerge from the forest around.

Lathani doesn’t reply verbally, but the way she practically prances over speaks volumes. Catch, moving more sedately approaches and hands me a glass cylinder that contains a dark red liquid.

“Well done,” I praise them with a smile. Catch nods at me stoically, though I sense that he’s pleased, while Lathani licks her shoulder, practically preening.

We discovered on the ship that Lathani is capable of opening small portals through which she can press her paws without actually stepping fully through them. They must remain within one continuous shadow which limits their range, but we also discovered that it isn’t only her paws that can pass through them. Others can use the portals as well, though objects by themselves, oddly, tend to get lost or not go through at all. We’re still trying to test exactly what happens with those.

In this case, she and Catch slipped away and moved behind the Tier three while it was occupied in the battle of domains. Then Lathani opened the portal while Catch used a syringe – tipped with a numbing agent – to act like a mosquito and surreptitiously draw some of the Tier three’s blood.

With this, we’ll be able to create defenses specifically against this Tier three, and hopefully also develop alarms that will alert us if it comes near the settlement.

Just a backup plan, in case it decides not to honour our mercy.

*****

While the success against the Beast Lord is definitely a step forward in settling the new continent, it comes with its own set of challenges, one of which becomes immediately obvious the next day when a great tawny-coloured beast that looks a bit like a maned wolf almost ambushes Bastet on a trip to the forest. Its domain is far weaker than the previous Beast Lord’s had been, and she defeats it relatively easily even without my input.

After that, she’s challenged at least once daily – twice on the third day. On the fourth, we’re unpleasantly surprised when a rhinoceros-like beast just charges straight at our battlements, bellowing a challenge to her and uncaring of what stands in its way. That domain battle is almost as difficult as the one with the gorilla-like Lord had been – the main difference is that this one is defence-focused meaning it’s drawn out and exhausting. But Bastet makes me proud when she demonstrates that she’s learned – by using a similar approach to the humanoid-like Beast Lord, she tires her opponent while preserving as much of her strength as she can.

After being defeated, the rhino turns and trundles down the slope far slower than it arrived and we all breathe a sigh of relief that it didn’t decide to press the challenge. Still, that is a wake-up call – three people who were in the wrong place at the wrong time end up in the healers’ care. Realising that the beasts will come for Bastet wherever she is, convenient for us or not, we set up perimeter alarms specifically for Tier threes coming within a certain distance of the settlement. We probably needed to do that anyway, but the high level beasts regularly coming close is really impacting our attempts to develop our farming and animal husbandry. If only Bastet could hide as well as the previous Beast Lord could.

Unfortunately, with us so visibly established in the forest, Bastet can’t avoid challenges nearly as easily. And Kalanthia, when consulted on the matter, makes it very clear that Bastet must either face the challenger, force it to withdraw the challenge, or remove it from the board – permanently – if she wants to be acknowledged as the Beast Lord of the territory. And that’s important because if she’s not, then this territory will become a target for all those Tier threes who are already lords of their territory and who are keen to expand.

Our scouts, human or otherwise, have indicated the same thing – for now, the most powerful Tier threes in the nearby territories have stayed within them, but fresh tracks and spoor near the boundaries make it clear that they’re keeping a close eye. We definitely don’t want to risk them thinking we’re an easy target – dealing with the less-powerful hopefuls that have been challenging Bastet is one thing; fending off experienced Territory Lords is quite another.

Following further advice from Kalanthia, Bastet takes up running daily patrols, accompanied by her little pack of raptorcats, and usually also Sirocco, Artemis, Orion, and Lathani. They can’t walk the borders of the whole territory in a single day – it’s too big for that, stretching to the foot of the mountain to the east and almost the same distance to the north-east and south-west along the coastline. It doesn’t reach the grove of trees marked on the original maps I received from Nicholas, reaching only about halfway, but I’m pleased with the size and the resources we’ve found within – if we can hold it.

Being the territory’s Beast Lord is definitely more work than we expected it to be – and more disruptive. Still, it does have advantages. One major one is that everyone in our settlement is considered to be under Bastet’s protection. Therefore, only the beasts confident of surviving her reprisals will attack us as long as we remain in her territory. That means most beasts will challenge her first to a battle of domains to measure their strength against hers in a conflict that won’t automatically end in the death of one participant.

In the second week after news spreads, she’s tested on that – a Tier three that she had narrowly defeated in a domain battle in the first week attacks one of our patrols not far from where she is patrolling.

I hear the news with my heart in my mouth – I’m too far away to be of much help and teleporting her out of danger would only cause more issues. Instead, I have to listen to the commentary from our other companions as they are forced to watch the fight from the sidelines.

When Bastet returns, bloody but victorious, I immediately pull her aside for healing, quietly wondering to myself whether the whole thing is worth it. Then I remember that Bastet taking the attacks head on means that the rest of us don’t have to. And that, though there are many Classers who would be capable of holding their own against a Tier three, especially in a group, there are far too many vulnerable people who could not.

But as I see the deep bite wound that came only millimetres away from ripping into her jugular, I find the argument unconvincing.

When another beast pushes the boundaries and Bastet has to kill it – I’m actually present, but watching the flaring of abilities and fur flying in all directions from the sidelines is worse than hearing it from afar. It takes all my will not to interfere, but I know I mustn’t – if I do, it will just announce Bastet’s weakness and she’ll have more beasts testing her. At least being close means being able to heal her immediately.

At least the regular practice means Bastet quickly becomes skilled in Domain combat and using her abilities. And, as two weeks turn into three, the number of challenges rapidly begins to dwindle.

Little by little, the forest turns into something watchful rather than actively hostile. By the end of four weeks, I marvel at the change. It feels like we’re slowly making our mark, like we’re becoming part of this place.

Of course, just because the challenges have died down and no Tier three has tested Bastet’s protection in a week, that doesn’t mean the forest is safe. Tier threes might be dime a dozen in a way they certainly aren’t on Moriax – and weren’t in the samurans’ world either – but they’re not the only threats in the forest. Tier twos are even more plentiful than Tier threes, and they're largely unconcerned with the shift in territorial power.

Although Tier threes can technically control Tier twos, it requires enough effort that most don’t bother trying to control an entire territory’s worth of them. That generally only happens if the Tier three possesses an ability that makes doing so both possible and relatively easy.

As a result, we still have to contend with the strangely powerful Tier twos of the area. Fortunately, our Classers can handle them with increasing ease as their levels go up, and even some of our non-Class fighting forces are capable of defeating them if they have sufficient numbers on their side.

The last four weeks have certainly been a baptism of fire for our fighters. They did their best during the journey in the battles we fought there, but, here on land, they’re now able to show their real mettle.

In the last few weeks, I’ve done two different Stone awarding ceremonies to grant Classes to those who demonstrated exceptional courage or exemplary conduct in battle. Seeing so many of their peers earn stones has enlivened motivation that has been flagging from increasingly short rations and a number of other unexpected difficulties that have appeared across the board – the farming is still the biggest challenge, but all the crafters are finding that they’re having to adapt to the new materials and environment in surprising ways.

It’s also been a baptism for more than just the humans. As we reach the end of the fourth week, an event I’m both nervous and excited over is due to take place: the first time the samuran hatchlings will be taken out into the forest proper. And given their propensity for getting into trouble in the settlement, I’m more than a little nervous about what might happen in the still-dangerous environment of the forest.
