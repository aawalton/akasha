Moonfall couldn’t be more different from Fairwood. There are no trees here, no places to forage for plants and herbs. But that isn’t to say that the desert is lifeless. Shrubs are scattered across the plains, which bloom with surprisingly vivid red and yellow blossoms. There are cactuses—cacti? Whatever—all over the place, too, though these are way bigger than the kinds I’d seen on Earth. Some of them must be two or three stories tall, and wide around as a car. The spines are as long as my arm—I’ve seen more than one Blackcloak member use them as weapons.

I take a zigzagging path between the cactuses’ pools of shade, Checking my surroundings as I go. Echo helps me find a handful of smaller creatures, from birds to lizards, but it’s the big ones I’m after. I flex my fingers, itching for a fight.

After a while, I catch sight of some tracks. It’s not obvious footprints or anything—which, yeah, I was kind of expecting from all the movies I used to watch—but a clean patch of cracked clay. The dust that usually covers the baked desert floor has been swept aside, and I have a strong suspicion I know what did it.

I activate my Soft Step skill and continue forward more carefully, eyes peeled for any hint of movement.

Even then, it takes almost an hour to find what I’m looking for.

[Target identified,] Echo reports.

I go still as I catch sight of the creature, and a fluttering thrill tickles my fingers. Perfect. Not only will this get me the level up, but it will fetch a good price at the Guild, too.

I’ve seen shells from these creatures traded and sold in the city, but I hadn’t seen a living one until now. It’s smaller than the remains I’m used to seeing, so this one must be young; that said, it’s still the size of a horse.

The animal looks a lot like an overgrown scorpion, but the shell is a faint green and pink, and its jagged edges and sharp angles flash in the sunlight like it’s made of gemstones. It’s currently distracted by the carcass of a star drake—giant lizards that the inhabitants of Moonfall like to use as mounts. It snips pieces of dehydrated flesh from the corpse with its sharp forelimbs, and its tail is curled up over its back, showing off the second set of pincers.

[Check,] Echo says. [Level 26 Crystal Scorpid. These opportunistic hunters and scavengers will eat any prey they can manage to subdue. Unlike their smaller counterparts, crystal scorpid’s have been transformed by ambient Life arcana such that the pincer on their tail allows them to absorb the mana from any prey that has been caught in its grasp.]

Lovely. Luckily, I’m not overly dependent on my mana, anyway. My potions and poison can do everything spells can do, and then some. The question is, how to take this guy out?

It’s a couple levels lower than me, which is a good sign. But that body looks pretty tough, and I doubt my knives will be able to get through—at least, not without weakening it, first. I’ve got another acid bomb on me, so maybe I can use that to get through its shell, and my knives can finish the rest. And if that doesn’t work, well… I’ve always got options.

I carefully slip the acid bottle from its leather clasp on my bandolier, then switch it to my bad hand as I draw my butcher’s knife with my good hand. No one in the Guild has commented on my peculiar choice of weapons, yet. If I could use anything other than cutlery half as well, I definitely would—but my class has given me such a huge bonus when I use kitchen knives that there’s no point in fighting with anything else.

I’m almost not even annoyed about that anymore.

Just as I raise the acid bottle and get ready a throw, the scorpid freezes. I also stop. Did it catch sight of me? I thought the cactus I was hiding behind provided enough cover. Maybe it smelled me. Shoot, I didn’t check which way the breeze was blowing. The scorpid skitters around, partially facing me, and its pincers flex in the air.

No point in procrastinating now. It’s either going to come for me or make a break for it—either way, I need to move first.

I dash from my hiding spot and launch the potion at the scorpid. My Knife Throwing skill has grown to Level 15 from daily practice, and my potion lobbing abilities are nearly as good. The creature moves with lightning speed, dashing to the side—but the bottle still shatters against the clay only inches away, spraying the creature with searing green droplets. I’m running toward the creature before the spray has even settled.

[36 points of Acid damage dealt.]

[Minor Poisoned status afflicted.]

A flash of movement darts from my right. I course-correct before I’ve even registered the cause, dodging to put the scorpid between me and the new threat. The creature hisses, moving left, then right, like it can’t decide which way to strike. Still in a full tilt run toward the creature, I don’t have the time or space to skid to a stop; I either have to fight or redirect my run to the side. But that might open me to an attack. I make the split-second choice to continue my assault and deal with the new threat once I can make it out.

I can’t say I was expecting to hear it laugh.

A boy bursts from the other side of the clearing with a loud crack, like a gunshot, and races toward the scorpid as he laughs. I’m so surprised by this that I nearly falter, and the scorpid whips its tail in my direction.

Nearly, but not quite. I block the attack with my butcher knife, and as I expected, the tough shell skids over my blade like I’ve struck rock. But its side is peppered with steaming holes from my acid, and as I duck under the arc of its tail, I turn the momentum of my knife to slam into its side. I feel the knife bite into its shell.

The scorpid shrieks, and it whips its tail back to take a second strike at me. I kick the scorpid’s side and use the leverage to yank my knife free, stumbling away. I raise my blade to deflect again—

With another loud crack, something wraps around the scorpid’s tail, yanking it to the side. The boy pulls back on his whip—a whip, that must have been the sound—grinning madly as he yanks the scrabbling scorpid away. He seems more amused than surprised when he finally makes eye contact with me.

“Hey, what are you doing?” he calls, his upbeat tone jarring against the threat of the situation. “You trying to steal my kill?”

“What?” I say, too baffled to think of anything else.

The boy flicks his wrist, and the whip lets go of the scorpid’s tale. It skitters around to face him with a hiss, jabbing out with one of its pincers. He backs away, laughing as he snaps his whip at the pincer, deflecting it to the side. Laughing. Like this is all a game.

“You know: KS. Kill steal,” he calls, still dancing out of the scorpid’s reach. “Or, I guess you don’t. Nevermind, don’t worry about it.”

I gawk at the boy. Those are videogame terms. I haven’t heard anyone else here talk like that. Now that the scorpid is out of my range, I spare a moment to give him a Check.

[Name: Álvaro]

[Species: Human]

[Class: Tracker]

[Level: 28]

[Attack: 58]

[Agility: 46]

[HP: 100/100]

[Mana: 75/75]

[Affinities: Nature, Necromancy]

[Role: Monster Hunter]

He looks like any other human I’ve met in Moonfall. The boy appears to be in his late teens—about my age—with light brown skin and long, black hair pulled back to keep it out of his face. But there’s something he has that no other human I’ve met does.

A Role.

I shake the surprise from me and jump back into the fight. With the scorpid distracted by Álvaro, it isn’t hard to dash in behind it and take out its legs. I go for one rotted with my acid, first, and it collapses to the ground with a squeal. Its tail flails around blindly behind it, and I duck beneath to go for another leg, this time landing the blow in the joint. I sever it clean through.

Echo is tracking points of damage as I deal them, but my mind is spinning with thoughts of Álvaro. We both have Roles. Is it a coincidence? Or is he just a god, like Shirasil, in disguise? No—that can’t be it. When Shirasil was disguised as Lisari, she didn’t have a Role. And the terms he was using were so out of place. Then is he really someone like me?

Someone from Earth?

Álvaro again snags the scorpid’s tail with his whip, and this time he yanks it sharply to the side, and I hear something in the shell crack. Then he races forward and jumps, clearing the scorpid’s pincers as he lands on its back in a squat. He glances at me with a grin.

“What’s the matter? Dazzled by my amazingly good looks?”

I’m still too surprised to be grossed out. “Are you from Earth?”

Álvaro’s smile vanishes behind a look of genuine shock. The scorpid thrashes beneath Álvaro’s weight, and the boy scrambles to hold on but is thrown from the creature’s back. Despite its injuries, the scorpid lunges after him, its pincers extended. This time, Álvaro’s bravado is nowhere to be seen as he rolls over the hard clay, grunting as he tries to scramble to his feet.

He won’t make it in time.

I draw another knife and throw it at the scorpid’s back. It strikes home, sinking into a joint behind the creature’s head. The animal screams and thrashes, and I rush after it, another knife already in hand. Now it’s my turn to jump and land on the creature’s back, but instead of using the opportunity to brag, I plunge the second blade into the scorpid’s joint, and using both knives, I sever its head.

The body collapses to the ground, its tail sending up a puff of dust as it smacks into the clay. The scorpid’s body twitches for a few seconds, then goes still.

[Level Up!] Echo declares. [New skills available.]

I smile to myself. Just as planned. But I’ll have to check out my new stats later, because right now I have a monster hunter to deal with.

I round on Álvaro, who’s looking up at me with round, surprised eyes. Okay, now it’s time to brag.

“First time fighting a scorpid?” I ask, as if it wasn’t my own.

He wipes the look of shock off his face, replacing it with an easy smile just as quick. “You seriously from Earth?”

I guess that answers that question. I climb down from the carcass and pick up Álvaro’s whip that he dropped when he was thrown off the scorpid’s back. I toss the handle back to him, and he catches it.

“I wouldn’t be asking if I didn’t know about it,” I say, regarding him warily. I don’t really consider him a threat, as he’s two levels lower than me, and I just witnessed how well he handled—or didn’t handle—the scorpid. But just because he’s from Earth doesn’t mean he’s a friend, either.

“Wow.” Álvaro’s face lights up as he starts to coil his whip back up. “I wasn't sure if there was anyone else besides me and my sib. That’s awesome! Have you met any more?”

“No,” I say, a little unsure how to feel about this. I’m not alone. There really are others here like me. But what do I even do with that? It doesn’t change what I have to do. “But I think there are others. I don’t know how many, but Shirasil mentioned other ‘Travelers’. I guess he meant people like us.”

“Sweet,” Álvaro says, securing the whip at his waist. “Who’s Shirasil?”

I frown. “Just some jerk. Um… what are you doing?”

Álvaro is heading back over to the scorpid. He reaches down and touches the carcass; everything except the severed head abruptly vanishes.

My jaw drops.

He turns back to me and wiggles his fingers dramatically. “Magic!”

“You have an Inventory, too?” I ask.

“Aw.” Álvaro looks disappointed. “That would have been way cooler if you didn’t know what I did.”

I shake my head. “Are you taking this seriously at all?”

He tips his head. “What do you mean?”

I gesture vaguely around. “Laughing while you run into a fight. Nearly getting killed by that scorpid. Calling it a kill steal—”

“Technically, you did,” he says.

“—And now just shrugging off the fact that we’re both from a different world.” I helplessly splay my hands. “You’re acting like none of this matters.” Shouldn’t this mean something? Shouldn’t this be a bigger moment?

He scratches his cheek as he thinks. “I mean, you died, right?” he asks. “That’s how Nye and I got here, anyway.”

My stomach goes sour. “Yeah.”

“So, why not enjoy it?” he asks. “We got a second chance at life! We should have fun with it. Who knows if we’ll get another one. Live life to the fullest and all that, right?”

His words stir memories of when I first appeared in this world. How ecstatic I was to have a second shot. How grateful I was to have a healthy body.

All that was kind of overshadowed by Maru’s attack.

I just shake my head. “I’ve got too many problems to worry about to have fun.”

“Really?” Álvaro says. “That’s a shame. But hey, if you want to go monster hunting sometime, let me know!” He grins. “It’s my specialty.”

Is he for real? He’s so carefree. So naive. “Do you understand what kind of danger we’re in?” I ask. “With the gods?”

“Oh,” Álvaro says, his smile fading a bit. “Yeah. Nye told me a bit about that. I heard they’re looking for us, or something.” He shrugs. “I guess there’s nothing I can do to stop them. All the more reason to have fun while we can, right?”

I glower at him. “You might not care, but I intend to stop them. I won’t let them keep getting away with stuff.”

Álvaro rocks back on his heels, eyebrows lifted. “You want to challenge gods? Right on! Let me know how it turns out.” He gives a two-fingered salute. “In the meantime, I’ve got a monster to turn in.”

“Hey,” I stop him as he starts to turn away. “I killed that scorpid, you know!”

“You did,” he agrees. “Even though I spent all morning tracking it. But now I can sell the body and satisfy my Role Requirement. I’d call that even.”

“That’s not even at all!” I object. But Álvaro just grins.

“Hey, come on. I didn’t leave you with nothing, right?” He points to the ground behind me.

I look; the scorpid’s head sits by itself on the ground. “That’s not—” I turn back to address him, but the boy is already gone. He can’t have gone far—probably just hiding behind a cactus—but I’m not in the mood to chase him down and fight over the scorpid parts. I got what I came here for, anyway; my Level Up.

Letting out a heavy sigh, I snatch up the scorpid head and start trudging back to the city. My mood sours as Álvaro’s smile and laughter dance through my head.
