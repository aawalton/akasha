Less broken bones and escape attempts that way, she claimed.

Or something. I had no idea how to greet people, it wasn’t like there was a protocol.

“Good,” the man said. “Officer Branston. Let’s get this over with quickly and bloodlessly, shall we?”

We didn’t shake. Instead, he started to powerwalk towards the store, opening the doors with his shoulder. The two cops inside made room for Officer Branston and I to approach the man, but kept their guns up.

I thought about the data Vera gave us. The man was a D-tier Supe by the new informal lettering, with enhanced strength estimated at 3x baseline as a camera field guess. The cameras had recognized him and tripped the supervillain alarm, but I didn’t think of him as an actual villain. I just wondered what he was doing, what he was thinking. Having superpowers meant you were handled differently, often lethally. It was a small wonder the cops hadn’t pulled the trigger on him, and most of the reason could be reduced to they aren’t sure if you are hiding more powers, and could still be able to kill them.

Harsh but true.

I took a step.

“If he tries to run, cut him off. He won’t be running into the police,” Nadia said in my ear.

He wasn’t going to run. He looked frightened and frozen, his mouth open under the hood of his dark hoodie.

“Keep the civilians in sight, and pin him.”

“Slow and steady,” he told the man. “Palms always visible.”

He guided his hands from above his head into the cuffs. They locked with a click.

I relaxed a fraction. Nothing bad happened. It was over.

“Take him—”

That’s when he snapped. And my first thought wasn’t that anyone had mis-tiered him. The letters were brand-new, unofficial, and nobody knew yet what a D could break. Still, though. Perhaps a D could break handcuffs.

Then the Supes came, and they handled it and then delivered their pitch. I was the Supe now.

Back here, there were no cameras, no phones. Only her. The spare pair of handcuffs the officer had given me dangled from my belt.

“Now,” she said into my earpiece.

Time stopped. When it resumed, the wannabe villain was on the ground, cuffed again, out cold. We had practiced ways to knock someone out without giving them brain damage, but this was my first application to a real human. Frozen time made everything so much easier, and now I stood above his motionless body with the racket from the sales floor rushing in its wake, blaring sirens and guns pulled.

There were five seconds where I felt alone with my actions before the police officer arrived, followed by the other two, and they began to haul the man away. I finally realized, as they did, that being here was the right call and I had been naive in thinking my (or another hero’s) presence was overkill.

But in those five seconds that preceded that, another thought gripped my mind.

His power. His… super strength. Stronger than it looked, and I could just… take it. In fact, my nanomachine swarm almost made it a no-brainer. I could hoard powers. Why not take his, improve it, make it better, and then put it to use for good?

After all, given what I’d seen, I was sure I could use it better than he ever did. With me, it would be useful.

All I’d have to do was touch him. Nobody would even know. But I didn’t. I almost did, impulsivity getting the best of me, but I felt the weight of the little piece of Nadia still stuck in the nanites’ output slot—her fae, elf looks—and stopped.

She saw my hesitation and nodded, pride on her face. Then she was again my little loving girlfriend, no trace of the cold fighter, and she was hanging on my arm and swooning over me.

“They found odd components, pieces and parts on him and in a bag he left behind. There, they also found chemicals and bleach,” Vera said after we gave our statements to the police. “I’ll handle the rest of the paperwork, good job team.”

Her smile was thin but warm.

“Why the odd loot, though?” Nadia asked. “It’s not what I would steal if I were trying to get by.”

I recalled our own shopping trip the other day, when we bought cameras and equipment to fortify our home.

“You don’t have to follow any leads if you don’t feel like it,” Vera said, towering over her tablet, adjusting her glasses. “But if you want to, you can. I will, of course, help. I already pulled some information from our Civic Response Center databases. We will know more as they question him. Unless, of course, you want to take the lead on that too?”

Nadia shrugged. “I’ll let the police handle it. Neither my Adrian nor I know anything about interrogation techniques.”

“Well,” Vera said with a smirk. “I suppose we have found something that you two can’t do. In this case, let me treat you to a coffee while we wait. I have a hunch it won’t take long and, between you and me,” she leaned in. “Halford’s CRC has quite a generous allotment of money they give to managers to coddle their favorite heroes with. And you just happen to be mine.”

“Only one,” she corrected. “Tactically competent civilian girlfriends are generally not part of protocol, but I suppose I can make an exception.”

Minutes later, we were in front of a door in a run-down part of town. Rows upon rows of squat condos crisscrossed by access roads, surrounded by withered and struggling trees that hadn’t seen maintenance or proper care since they had been planted. The houses all looked the same under the grey overcast sky, terracotta bricks that were just fake covers peeling away and revealing rusted concrete pillars beneath.

Up the stairs, third story, then last door to the right. The elevator was busted, its lights flickering, and the landing was dark, but as soon as we got near the door a sensor picked us up and a powerful floodlight blasted us.

Well, Nadia and I. Vera was in the car, keeping tabs on us with the earpiece and watching the road. No movement.

The guy who sent the robber who had pretended to be a shoplifter.

Good. My power wouldn’t have helped if he decided to turtle.

“Yes?” Kimberly stammered out. “Nate said it’s close to expiry.”

Because of course, I would be leaving behind a corpse if I ever took a power from someone. That’s a fact. No witnesses, or it’s a one-way ticket to the dissection lab.

“Now,” she grinned wide. “I recall asking for some jackhammering. Will my prince deliver?”

“Hmmm,” I pretended to think about it.

She looked at me with wide, wet, teary eyes. “Please, sir?” she begged.

I lifted her up and made her yelp. “Get ready, woman. Your begging won’t make me stop, not until you are a shivering mess.”
