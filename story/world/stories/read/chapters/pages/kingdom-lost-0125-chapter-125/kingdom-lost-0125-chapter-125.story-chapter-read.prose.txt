Chapter 125

Riley was let in for the brief celebration of the win over the Clawborn depot. She walked through the gate and smiled to join the revelry. She moved through the men, all cheering. Some of them patted her on the back, some raised their weapons in the air, all cheering and shouting, even Valrik.

Two soldiers came up from behind Riley and picked her up as she almost stumbled backward. Each grabbed a leg and hoisted her onto their shoulders. Surprise flashed across her face, and she grabbed a shoulder with each hand to steady herself. Then her surprise turned to laughter as the men cheered even harder and louder, "Warden! Warden! Warden!"

Thorne raised a sword and shouted, "Victory!!"

Riley was smiling. Thorne and Valrik were happy. Everybody was enjoying the win.

As the men continued to chant and Riley smiled, she caught sight of one of her fallen just outside the circle. He lay on the ground, bleeding, clearly dead. Her laughter was lost, and her face sobered. She turned to the soldiers holding her up and said, "Please put me down."

The men quieted, and she cleared her throat. "I couldn't have done this without you. This was a clear victory. Not just for us, not just for the Hoshin, but for the future of our community. Let's take a moment and remember the ones who gave their lives so that we could have this moment, and hope that they'll join us soon."

Everyone bowed their heads for a moment. A silence fell over the group as the moment hung for as long as was needed. Once it was finished, Riley raised her head and said, "Okay. Let's get to work."

And then Riley started to think. Her face hardened, and she began to work through it tactically and strategically, doing a postmortem of the situation.

Riley started to take a tally of the troops and how many she had left, cold after the battle. She had about thirty-five soldiers left, a mix of different types: cavalry, infantry, and ranged.

She was more interested, though, in the fights that had happened behind the gate. For example, the three-on-one, three of Riley's troops against the one level four Clawborn infantry guard, and the five-on-one against another level four Clawborn guard.

What she was most interested in was the battle report the system generated in her head once the battle completed. She found that the three-on-one between her men and the Clawborn had cost her two troops, whereas the five-on-one had cost only one.

She took that as an interesting comparison of her level two troops against the level four Clawborn, for future reference. She also wanted to discuss the new weapons and how they had fared from the troops' perspective.

She asked Thorne, "How do you think the battle went?" and called Valrik over as well.

Thorne took a moment to compile his thoughts, looking down at the ground, eyes moving like he was reading the air. Valrik, on the other hand, said the new weapon had worked well, except there was a danger to their own men. When it was used, some of them had suffered a consequence too, and that would need to be addressed. Valriks eye contact was unwavering, almost steely.

As Riley listened, her heart started to beat harder in her chest. She thought about how she could solve this issue, something she would put eyes on later.

Riley said, "You're right, Valrik. This is something that deserves our full attention when we return. I have a few ideas we can go over then."

Thorne said there would be a benefit on defense if there was a way to block the attacking troops' line of sight. He was thinking in terms of the Clawborn attacking their community, taking the perspective of defending rather than attacking, drawing from his experience in this clash.

As Thorne spoke, Riley contemplated and had an idea. She put it on her mental checklist to be addressed later, but the idea of being able to block the enemy's sight had resonated with her, and she thought it had real value.

As Thorne finished his thoughts, Valrik jumped into the conversation. "It's clear we need the numbers," he said. "If we're going to defeat a higher tier enemy, and if they come with numbers, we'll need more than just a few of these weapons. We'd need them on a grander scale."

Riley nodded. "Agreed."

Then Thorne said, "We need to collect the dead for transport."

Riley turned and said, "No. First, search the depot completely. If there's anyone left, they need to be taken into custody. Word of this cannot reach the Clawborn yet."

"And the storehouses?" Valrik said.

Then Riley said, "Thorne, you'll head the search of the compound, starting with the storehouses, then the rest. And Valrik, you're with me to take a closer look at the spoils."

***

Riley and Valrik opened the door to the first storehouse. The door opened inward into a dark room. They both stepped in, and the light from outside shone into the storehouse. It illuminated what was inside better than the internal light did. Riley's eyes widened, and so did Valrik's. They both stretched their necks and tilted their heads as they looked all the way up to the ceiling, and from the front of the storehouse all the way to the back.

Valrik stayed standing just inside the door as Riley walked forward, holding her breath as she brushed her hands across the gigantic piles of wood, then made her way around toward the back where the gigantic pile of stone was.

She slowly turned and looked at Valrik, and Valrik just nodded.

"How much do you think is here?" Riley asked.

Valrik looked for a second and said, "Where's Nikola when you need him? I have no idea. This is more than I thought there would be."

Riley looked down at the stone in her hand and rubbed across one of the large pieces at the base. She looked back at Valrik. "How many communities like the Hoshin are enslaved to produce this kind of resource stockpile? I can't even imagine how long this has been accumulating. We really need to get a handle on what's happening across the north."

Valrik looked back at Riley. "Agreed."

Valrik said, "Both storehouses are already sorted, and there's a lot more here than we can transport even over many days, even weeks."

Riley turned as Valrik was talking and stopped with her back to him. She started to take in the magnitude of what they'd just accomplished. Still facing away, she said, "Agreed. We'll need to allocate the lion's share of the troops and most of the horses to handle all this. I'll put you in charge of this." She paused.

Valrik looked from side to side at all the resources, his eyes hardening like he was figuring out how many hands and days it would take to move all of it. After a long pause, Valrik said, "Warden?"

Riley said, "This battle; it was fought by you, and the others. But it was different, this time, for me."

Riley’s breathing shallowed and her blood ran cold.

Valrik stopped looking around and focused on Riley's back.

Riley continued. "The new weapons are working, and they will narrow the gap." She paused again, turned, and looked at Valrik. "But when they fall, when the soldiers fall..."

Valrik said nothing and softened his gaze as he now looked into Riley's eyes.

Riley said, "It's not the same as before. I know they will come back. It's..."

Valrik said, "What they'll have to go through to come back."

Riley's eyes looked away from Valrik, down at the floor. She said, "What you had to go through, it stayed with me. The idea, I can’t get that out of my head. In the coming battle, I can't hesitate. Not even once, Valrik. If I do..."

Valrik responded, "Then more will fall."

"Yes."

Riley turned her back to Valrik. Her breath stalled, and her left hand grabbed her right shoulder. The silence hung between them.

Then Valrik said, "Warden."

Riley's head turned to the side, and she looked down.

"If you hesitate, even for a moment, then the fallen won't have anything to go through. They won't be coming back."

Riley's hand dropped, and she look forward and down at the ground. Valrik lifted his head and walked around Riley so he was facing her, then Riley looked up at him. He said, "You command, we follow. This is the order of things."

Riley looked at Valrik, and he nodded once. She paused, then half nodded back.

Suddenly the door opened to the storeroom, and Thorne walked in. In a louder than expected voice he said, "Warden, we'll need to move soon. We're running out of daylight." Thorne slowed as he approached the two and looked at Valrik, then Riley, who were still looking at each other.

Riley finally broke eye contact and looked at Thorne.

Thorne said, "What?”
