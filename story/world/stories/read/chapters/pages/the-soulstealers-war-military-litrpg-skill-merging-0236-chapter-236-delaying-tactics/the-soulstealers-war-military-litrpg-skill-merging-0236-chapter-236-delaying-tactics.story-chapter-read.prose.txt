“Keep moving!” I yelled. “Draw them out to the side!”

Burr wanted to speed up, and finally, I let her.

We rode parallel to the orc front lines. An arrow thudded into my shield, but I wasn’t nearly as exposed as some of the others farther down the column of riders. A Dupe took an arrow to the throat and fell off his horse, and another’s horse crumpled. An arrow struck its upper thigh.

Scowling, I tucked my chin, then pointed my spear toward a clump of orc archers. I angled [Mass Hammer] with my mind, then dragged it sideways through the clump, flinging most of the archers off their feet. It bought us time and cover.

But dealing with the fell-fox riders was more my concern. There had to be a hundred of them. They were wrapping around the edge of the orc line, circling around the outside to meet us. I locked eyes with a rider at the head of their column. He was a shaman, covered in pelts and pale leather, and his pupils glowed bright yellow in the night. Twin stars bobbed up and down, far lower than they ought to be.

We had to get in front of the fell-fox riders. If they cut us off, they could push us back and trap us against the orc lines. We’d all die, pressed by riders on one side and spears on the other.

As we rode, I locked onto the shaman and assessed his tier with my Presence skill. He was only a Titanium-tier. I was about to hit him with my main Art, but before I could aim, he pointed his staff.

Something chimed, the air trembled, and a beam of decay raced at me. It was pitch black, and the only reason I could track its trajectory was because it flew in front of the orcs’ ranks, which were slightly less dark.

Holding my shield up, I caught the beam. It melted the wood of the disposable shield with rot. The metal frame rusted and the shield boss crumbled into flakes.

So much for not needing a tougher shield. I cast it aside as we rode, then activated my Art.

The shaman leading the charge slammed to the ground, falling off his fox. He tumbled, and his neck bent backward awkwardly. His fox tumbled over him, and the clawed feet of the riders behind him trampled over him.

He wouldn’t be getting up from that, even if he was a Titanium.

A different orc took the lead. He was just an Iron, wielding a crude axe, but he was getting closer to me by the second. His fox was taking longer bounds than Burr was. At this rate, the orcs were going to cut us off. I pushed Burr to ride faster, leaning into her mane and whispering, “Come on…come on…”

The orcs crossed in front of us like they were drawing a line atop a T. I made a split-second decision, targeting a fox-rider’s flank ahead of me, and sprang out of Burr’s saddle with the help of my Art.

I flung myself through the air, launching forward, then reversed another Art and slammed myself down in the middle of the foxes. I hit one, shattering all its bones on impact. More importantly, I slammed into the soft summer dirt with enough force to create a flame-less explosion. The flying chunks of dirt cleared a path through the middle of the orc riders, splitting their formation in half and giving my riders a corridor to charge through.

Burr kept charging, even without me on her back. As she passed, I used my enhanced base stats to jump back up onto her saddle, then continued charging.

We passed through the fell-fox riders in a matter of seconds. The dust from my attack cleared, and I glanced over my shoulder.

The fell-fox riders scrambled to a halt, trying to change directions to catch up with me. They would have to accelerate again, and though they may have been faster than the regular horses of the other Dupes, we could lead them away a little longer.

As we charged, I angled Burr to the side and shouted to the lieutenant with me, “Lead them away! I’m going to pull back and guard our rear! After a few minutes, turn and circle back! We will need to help the others!”

He nodded, then pointed his spear forward and led the riders farther across the valley’s ridge. I picked up the pace again when the rear of our cluster passed. A fell-fox nipped at the horse’s heels, but I closed the distance and jabbed my spear through his head. As the rider fell, I used my Art to fling the fox back into the other orc riders who were trying to follow us. It tumbled through them, knocking others off their foxes and causing others to trip.

We continued along the ridge for about a mile until the head of the column turned about and raced back toward the larger orc army. We led the fell-fox riders in a wide loop, then aimed behind the front lines. If we could cut behind the front phalanx, where the orcs weren’t nearly as dense, we could do much more damage.

As we turned, an orc rider approached me from behind, aiming his spear at my back. I turned in my saddle, doing my best to deflect it, then drew my crossbow off my shoulder and blasted him off his horse.

Yeah, blast probably wasn’t the best word to use when it came to using a bow, but considering how far the orc had flown, it felt appropriate. He tumbled across the grass and shrubs, tripping a rider behind him.

As we rode, I reloaded the bow, all while holding my spear in the crook of my elbow. Burr whinnied in warning, and I leaned to the side, steering her away from a fell-fox rider’s enormous hammer.

Once I had the bow reloaded, I picked off the hammer-wielding rider, then increased our pace again, aiming for the front of our column.

I reached it just in time. The orcs covered the land in a gradient as they poured toward their siege formation. We aimed for the thinnest part of the gradient—while at the same time, the other larger force of our riders slammed into the other side of the orc army, completely undisturbed by fell-fox riders. They hit the distracted front lines from the side before they could even get their spears up.

I aimed to meet them in the middle. We wouldn’t stop the orc army completely, but we’d delay their advance, which was all we needed. Burr’s hooves trampled orcs. A beast bounced off her chest when she smashed into him from the side. Another tried to jab a spear into her, but I pulled my foot out of the stirrup and kicked it away.

Where the others just rode, I couldn’t afford to. I extended my awareness as far as I could, watching to the east for a Black Captain or something worse. I had no idea what to expect with a Hangman, but we’d get his attention soon.

For now, I saw no signs. I pointed my spear down, skewering an orc archer, then flung him to the side, knocking him into a Steel-tier troll who was trying to intercept us. It didn’t kill the troll, but by the time the troll got into position, we were already past.

Was this actually going to work? Two hundred riders could outmaneuver five thousand orcs?

I cut off that line of thought. Even if the Hangman didn’t turn up, there was no way we could win this fight.

They’d eventually group up around us and overwhelm us. Most of the Dupes under my command right now were Irons and Steels—comparable to orcs. More of the beasts bounded forward, flinging themselves at us. Some of them didn’t even carry weapons, and attacked with nothing but bare hands. One tacked a rider off his saddle, and another latched onto a horse’s hind legs and started biting at it with his teeth.

Still, we needed to buy more time. The bells of Hyrdford were still tolling, and people shouted in the distance. Farmers and civilians were still pouring through the gates. If we didn’t hold on a little longer…

As soon as we could, I angled west again, aiming to regroup with the other riders. I shouted, “Lieutenant! We need to split the orc lines in half! Turn west, and we push from behind!”

I couldn’t slow down. The other column, however, had embroiled themselves in the thickest swathe of the orc army. By now, they were barely moving.

I aimed just beside them, then used our column’s speed to smash through the rear of the orc ranks.

The orcs were already spinning around by the time we arrived, but they didn’t lower their spears soon enough. Burr bounded over the front line with a massive leap, kicking off an orc’s helmet as she jumped, and when she landed, I used my Art to clear a route ahead of us, allowing us to maintain our speed.

In seconds, the front line crumbled away, orcs scattering in all directions. There was nothing nearby to hit with my spear, and I couldn’t afford to let go of the reins again to reload my bow.

We surged out from the front lines, riding back toward Hyrdford. I was tempted to call it there, while we were ahead. It would still take plenty of time for the orcs to fix their formations, wouldn’t it?

I almost pointed my spear back at Hyrdford, but before I could, I caught a glimpse of glittering arrowheads racing through the sky toward us. There were hundreds of them, glittering indigo in the light of Welkinmere.

“Shields!” I shouted.

How had they gathered that many archers? And that fast? Then, specifically positioned them to stop us from escaping.

Around me, the riders raised their shields. Since I didn’t have one, I used an Art above my head. I reversed its direction, sending arrows flying back up into the sky. Nothing hit Burr or I, but a few riders behind me fell. Arrows slipped through the gaps in their armour or hit their horses.

We had to take out those archers if we wanted to make it back.

First off, they were exposed. They were an easy target.

Secondly, if I let them advance on the city, they’d be able to pick off civilians from a distance as they tried to get through the gate.

Thirdly, if I wanted to get back to the city without losing half our riders as we turned our backs to the archers, we’d need to take out that cluster.

So far, there was no sign of a Hangman. But the longer this went on, the more a chill ran down my spine. Something was going to happen. It was here.

But I couldn’t do anything about that right now. We could still take out the archers, then run.

I clenched my jaw, then pointed my spear at the archers. “Turn!” I shouted. “They’re our next target!”
