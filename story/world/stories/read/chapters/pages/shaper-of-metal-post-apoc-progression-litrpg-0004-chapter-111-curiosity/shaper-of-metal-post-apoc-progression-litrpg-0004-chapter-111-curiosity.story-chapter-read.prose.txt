Chapter 111: Curiosity

As it turned out, everyone on the actual team, aside from Jack, had networks they could tap into to pry for information on their rival, Team Irresistible Style — which probably wasn’t surprising considering they’d all lived a long time as a Non and he was new, even compared to Bo, who got a late start but had more than caught up by way of the sort of infamous club she’d joined. If the D-Heads did anything, it was hang out with each other and skirt around the rules doing questionable shit. They were great information sources.

With Ira on spot duty to more or less babysit the rascal, Augur — at the very least she could raise an alarm to the rest of them — Jack was kinda free for the morning. He spun ideas and strategies in his head and aimed to get a bit more solo training in while he could.

He realized he had an older message waiting for him through his Mini-Mem, before the recent excitement and even while he was still asleep. It was from Lindsay: <Thanks so much for the flowers, Jack. They’re beautiful. But a bit too much, too! It’s fine to dial it down a little! Cool! Thanks again! <3 >

Jack frowned at the reply, utterly bewildered by the words, seemingly laced with anxiety. Did she like them or didn’t she? ‘Too much.’ Well, it was a lot. But they were platonic flowers! What did it matter?!

Grumbling in annoyance to himself, Jack exited his quarters. As soon as he stepped out into the hallway, he froze. Waiting right next to it, leaning against the wall idly with her arms crossed and one foot pressed against the wall, was Délight. No aura, no brightness, not particularly noteworthy — at least to him — aside from fairly luxurious waves of thick, jet hair.

She turned her head sideways, met his eyes, and smiled faintly. “Hey there, soldier.”

Jack glowered at her. “Whatever this is, Délight, it won’t wor-”

“I wanted to apologize for my prior behavior. I did go too far, and I’m sorry for that.” Jack’s face must’ve shown how much he bought it, because she looked away and sighed. “I don’t expect you to even accept the apology. I make it anyway because I owe it. I… waited for a reprimand, a correction, a penalty… that never came…” She trailed off, eyes focused down the hallway.

Jack frowned. He was tempted to just ignore her and walk away to go about his business. That would be a pretty satisfying sting delivery, wouldn’t it? If he were petty enough. He wasn’t sure if he was… it was a near thing. She wants to see if I’m curious. Dangling the bait. That thought made Jack’s muscles tense in the almost-motion of finally walking off. But he hesitated.

I just can’t help myself, can I?

Jack took a breath, crossed his arms, and studied her with his chin lifted slightly. Her eyes shifted back to his, as if waiting for his cue. Jack grunted and obliged. “So what did you do? Figure it out yourself?”

“I took it to Mother,” Délight replied. “Wanting an answer. But she didn’t give it, even then. It was then I realized I already had the answer.”

“What was the question, exactly?”

“Why a reprimand didn’t come. Whether I was in error, whether I seriously wronged you.”

“And?”

She held his eyes. “I think it’s more of a decision. Determining the truth myself. Letting go of something. The past, in part. Uglier sides of me. The ugliest. Would you believe it isn’t all ugly?”

“Of course.”

“A work in progress, in any case.” A wry smile. “You know, you aren’t the first to ignore the passive aura; see the real me.”

“Was it merely the passive?” She didn’t answer, looking away. He frowned at that. “It scares you? Losing that control you have over others?”

She shrugged slightly. “Is it control? How people see you? I still wonder. Have you seen people with the gift of gab? Natural charisma, or stunning symmetrical beauty, or both? Those others love to love. The world is their oyster. They might as well be holy to those they’ve so effortlessly charmed.”

“You think that and what you do are the same?”

“Mostly. It doesn’t work quite the same.”

“A feather doesn’t work the same as a knife if you stab someone with it, either. It’s either the same or it isn’t, Délight.”

She sighed. “Just like with normal people and relationships, I can’t predict exactly what it does to someone. It can be more intense. But look at the long list of people who hate my guts for the evidence of uncertainty. Anyway…”

Obviously, she doesn’t want to continue down this track. He didn’t add anything more, waiting on her.

She fidgeted, glancing his way. “It scares me to be exposed. But… I realized it didn’t as much as it used to, when you slapped me in the face with your particular version of immunity out of nowhere. Even when what I saw in your eyes scared me worse than ever. I felt so… small. Later, I got over it; I let it go. Once upon a time, I wouldn’t have… I’d have gotten vindictive. Angry. But I was just tired of that, somehow… over it. Seeing the energy as a waste. And I even thought… ‘good for him,’ at one point.”

“You did?” Jack was incredulous. “Why?”

She shrugged again, looking down at the floor in apparent deep thought. “The camaraderie of who we are, and even perhaps seeing my own evils. Happy… that someone else was free. The past, it catches up. It’s become like a weight on my back, and some of the things I’ve seen in the field… It’s like it demands more out of me. Demands me to change, and dispense with being a child. Who I’ve been, that’s insufficient. I don’t feel like it happened in one moment, but… I’ve been walking through a threshold while still clinging to the old me out of nostalgia. Around friends, especially. I’m duplicitous and caught between, going through rote motions that a part of me sees from the outside, and I-... mmn — sorry. It’s getting too deep, right?”

Jack blinked and considered it, then shook his head at her seriously. I’m not going to naysay someone having some self-introspection and doubt about their crummy behavior toward others. If she’s maturing? Good.

Délight took a deep breath, sighing. “I guess what I’m trying to say is… I’m truly sorry for what I did, and I don’t want to act like that anymore. I’ve had enough crises in the way I’ve been, now, that I’m actively going to stop. Be more… who I want to be.” Her eyes seem to hold an inner determination, though her words were dispassioned. It had the sound of something she’d had boiling inside, and the top was finally lifted off the pot into reality. A hint of some relief that it had escaped.

“I guess the problem,” Jack replied calmly and insistently, “is you’re not telling me exactly what you did. Did you dial up your ability on me or not?”

Her eyes flashed to his with sudden despair, and her voice was similar as she replied, “I don’t know.” She pushed off the wall, but still had her arms wrapped around her. There was a long, pregnant pause as she shook her head. “You think it’s black-and-white to me? Want drives everyone’s powers. I could easily tell you I technically used no active abilities out of conscious control, but what difference does it make, Ferro? What does it matter when I might’ve done it subconsciously? The ebbs and flows, the touches, the insistence in the words, it’s all murky as hell. Where do I draw the line?” She shrugged with her hands out wide.

Jack held her eyes firmly the whole while. Whatever it was he saw in her eyes, he knew her angst was authentic. “If you say you didn’t mean to do it, then so be it, Délight. That’s good enough for me. I forgive you.”

Staring at him, her mouth hung open slightly. The moment hung, too. Her eyes watered over slightly.

Jack shook his head. “Look, don’t beat yourself up over it. We aren’t perfect. You regret it, you don’t think you did it intentionally, you agonized over it, you asked others about it… I mean… that tells me enough. Both of us know in this world, people like us, already riding on the borderline, aren’t going to get thrown in the brig for doing borderline shit. We’re too vital. You’re making an effort to ride clean? Good enough. Count yourself lucky for everything you’ve gotten away with to date, stick to this new philosophy you got going on, and walk on, soldier. We’re good. Okay?”

Délight managed to collect herself, though she kept staring at him. She swallowed and nodded. “Okay. Yeah. Thank you…” She trailed off, looking like she wanted to say something else but couldn’t find the words.

Not so surly, now, eh? She’s quite awkward without her ability to make things predictable.

Jack nodded back curtly. “I gotta run. Good luck in the match.”

“You… you too.” She rubbed her neck.

Trying not to chuckle — not in mockery but at the hysterical absurdity of seeing this side of her — Jack turned away and walked off. He wasn’t entirely sure what was hitting her so hard, but maybe it was that a peer she knows isn’t being manipulated straight up forgave her trespass. That could genuinely be a first-time experience for her, if the ‘immune’ were rare and avoided. She might’ve been certain he’d hate her guts no matter what she said.

Well, that’s a good thing. Beneficial surprises for being mature will probably help her along.

Jack wanted primarily to train on Wall Art, his Augment techs, practicing his new stunt techs, and deploying and utilizing clouds more, against simulated enemies. Not necessarily in that order…

Split was pretty great on the attack as well as for utility. He practiced large, 3kg dart creation, just to utilize Split, making a series of smaller copies, able to rapidly make that into three smaller 1kg darts, and then each of those into three smaller versions, thus spawning nine darts in a fairly fluid motion, if with a slight delay, like a series of flickers. He could do it with a long wave of his hand, fanning them out in front of him — kinda cool in and of itself. It was faster than making them one at a time, not to mention much less resource-intensive after the first model. He could divert to making another one of those while splitting the first, and thus multitask a grand spawning.

He practiced that with spheres, too, remembering his efficiency at follow-up attacks with them. They were a trade-off, as he couldn’t exactly have a sphere and a Fragile Spike improvement at the same time. But, he could start from a thrown sphere, and branch out with Fragile Spikes. Mass was mass, but he’d gotten better at using smaller projectiles. It was a difficult timing maneuver, but he could Throw darts from a moving thrown sphere. When it worked, it was nasty, being another order more difficult to detect and avoid.

While he was practicing on it anyway, he gave simulations ‘displacement’ type effects in his own cobbled-together imagination of how Hiccup’s powers might work. That way, he could attempt the double-timing trick of hitting such a target while it wasn’t displacing. Follow-up attacks seemed perfect for countering her.

Ideally, I get an angle on her while she’s fighting Highfive. Crack! This team is like us. No healing, and not even much defense reinforcement. Whatever injury we sneak through can weaken an enemy for the whole rest of the fight.

The team would want to keep Délight and Chime protected, but on the surface, that seemed difficult. Defiant’s speed had to be part of it. And…

Ah! Chime’s power might have defensive components. Of course. Kinetic blocks, essentially. Common to those types of Blasters.

If true, it made him a smidge like a Controller, just that everything had to emanate outward from him, and he might not be crazy on his Control stat. All shit he’d have to ask about, so he knew, in any case.

Onward to practice with clouds and Nettle Storm. His prior practice was already feathered with doing things within the particulate fields — in his head, increasingly thought of as clouds. He couldn’t depend on it, and thus couldn’t train exclusively with it, but it vastly improved accuracy in acute timing scenarios. He felt he could hit evasive foes if he stacked all these elements together. At the least, he had a chance. Admittedly, his techniques were awkward at times.

He simulated the properties of a protective body suit to test Nettle Storm and its ability to pierce through. An immediate attack did not pierce, but if he focused for a short period of time on one area without interruption, the material wore down enough for nettles to slip through the divided fabric. The longer he could focus, the more could slip within.

He tested on different spots, and the best remaining locations that needed more flexibility were the inside of various joints. The most exposed, least initially noticeable locale was the back side of the knee. Because the material was designed not to harden in situations where doing so could screw up someone’s movement and get them killed, the knee was faster to pierce. The neck was highly desirable as a target, but was sadly a spot where the memory material decided it was best to harden against damage and tended to be more stubborn. Not impossible, at least.

Attacking two targets at once in the knee areas was feasible, in an overall area of effect storm that was quite annoying even before he managed to get metal inside a suit. With enough time spent clandestinely sneaking in metal, he could consolidate it into a nasty little blade capable of injury, not to mention likely to cause massive panic when detected inside a suit. His Interpret 2.0 made controlling it blind quite improved from before, basically not much different than an average person moving something with their hands.

As it happened, the back of the knee was a pretty great stabbing and slicing target already, with important nerves and such ripe for the plucking. A serious wound there would stun and cripple a person’s mobility, likely sending them to the ground. So, he stuck with training on causing damage right in the general area he penetrated. He knew he could spend a little more time on ‘collection’ right over the femoral triangle. As he was no assassin, he’d be guessing a bit to attempt driving a knife in the region, but if he struck ‘true’ in a match, the System would simulate a femoral artery strike, possibly with nerve damage for extra debilitation. Not an instant out, but certainly one they could go heavy on defense after, expecting an eventual bleed out.

Naturally, this was assuming a very ‘squishy’ target, but there were literally three of them in the match. Hiccup was perhaps the most dubious there, possibly a bit tougher, and possibly able to ‘displace’ herself away from the knife.

Hmm. Good thing to think about, isn’t it? I can’t choke her out if it works like that. I’m better off with very quick injuries against her.

Because of the sheer utility of having Nettle Storm battering people without any recourse for avoidance inside its zone, Jack practiced simply condensing more metal to ‘tag’ someone on the surface and get a metal band on them. Namely, the neck. If he didn’t need to penetrate the material to take someone out, why bother?

Well, it took time to form enough material for something strong enough for use, compared to a tiny blade. He had better luck forming a blade and using that to pierce the suit. The time frame was longer than his ‘batter the back of knees’ technique, though. The blade needed to be slightly bigger and much more refined. Stronger.

A riskier, but more efficient plot was to simply use the storm as a screen to move a pre-existing denser piece in — or, as he found, a small collection of denser pieces that reformed on-site. That was the best way to get a choker on someone.

Alright. I’ve got to work through clouds as much as possible. We’re on defense, anyway, so I should be able to saturate the battlefield.

To that end, Jack doubled up on cloud generation practice. Forming them, moving them, anchoring them, multitasking their maintenance while doing other things. Anchors was a goddess-sent for this, as he had a large number of instances he could lock in over an area for modular coverage. He could shift a zone, lock it, and move on to something else, or even do it simultaneously.

Going a bit over on the time he’d planned toward this segment of things, Jack had to finally cut it off and get around to Wall Art and Augment practice…

Both of Corroline and her teammate, Ten, are completely unified in endless, burning hatred of Délight. I decided to broach the situation directly to Ten. She said their team wouldn’t even try them. Corroline apparently punched her in the face once! Wish that was on video. So, I also pointed out the matter of their tender, supple young male members, and she admitted that it was worthy of concern, particularly the softboy, Abundance. I decided I’d talk to him personally about it.”

“Nope.”

“As if I’d need to?” Bo asked, unmoved. “Hmmph. Anyway, I got nothing new and juicy on Defiant. Sorry.”
