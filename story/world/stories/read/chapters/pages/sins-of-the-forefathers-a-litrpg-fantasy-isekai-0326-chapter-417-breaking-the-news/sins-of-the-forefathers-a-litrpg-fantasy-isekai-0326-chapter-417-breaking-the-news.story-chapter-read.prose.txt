After our little moment in the office, I took Aveline back to my apartment here in the Bastion. Both so she could have her breakfast from Rachel, and so she could recover a little bit. The sight of her Mother’s face after so long had been a little…overwhelming to my daughter, and I didn’t blame her. Lina had barely looked up from the pictures ever since Isolde had handed her. I’d had to gently remind her to be careful with them, as a still shocked Rachel had set a plate of hot food down in front of the little girl.

It wouldn’t do to get anything on the photos, after all. In retrospect…I probably should have had them framed before I left. But in my defence, I had been really busy organising the expedition. After a hurried, muttered conversation with Rachel, she promised to get the pictures framed on my behalf.

Well, after the woman had her own words with me.

Probably a bit deserved, frankly.

After promising Aveline I’d be back for at least a little bit more time with her, before I had to leave, I floated back over to my office. No fool, Isolde was already expecting me. What I wasn’t expecting…was for her to have switched places.

Isolde had migrated from her position behind the desk to sit in the chair before it, watching my Sprite with a calm, expectant gaze. I paused, for a moment, in the doorframe to take the scene in…and then shook my head with a wry smile.

Thank you, Isolde, but that wasn’t necessary. I had never once lost faith in you, in all the months we’d managed the Polaris Reach together.

Still, I accepted the message in the spirit it was intended, and floated my way over to the high-backed chair behind my desk. Sitting on the rim of the backrest, I crossed my tiny legs and nodded at Isolde.

Out of respect, I decided to match her serious air. “I’ll have your report now, Commander.”

Isolde nodded sharply at that and didn’t waste any time. “Blutstein is surviving, Marshal, but that’s the best I can say. We’re not quite thriving down here, but we are managing. The arrival of the Gnolls turned out to be a deciding factor in that. Without their Warded Gigant, I’m not sure how well we would have been able to navigate our way through the crisis. We would have been in the midst of a famine if not for their once-weekly trips out to the surrounding farms. As it is…the Lord Regent has still implemented a full-on rationing scheme. Food is a resource we’re wary of wasting, and that has gone over with…middling reception. Particularly among the upper class.”

I grimaced at her delicate wording. “I can imagine,” I said with a sigh. Across a distance measured in hundreds of miles, I somehow felt a migraine coming on to my flesh brain. “Has there been significant unrest?”

In other words, I was asking if it was still safe in the city. Both for her and for my daughter.

No fool, Isolde immediately understood. “No,” She said firmly. “Rest assured, we have it under control, Nathan. At least, for now.”

In other words, hurry the Hell up.

I nodded at her, before broaching the subject I was most interested in. “And the Order?”

Isolde’s eyes tightened, but that was the only movement on her face. “The bulk of the Order is with you, Nathan,” She pointed out. “For those that are still here with me…we’re managing well. Most of the time, we’re coordinating with either the Gnolls or the Army. The strangely Ward attracted monsters that you’ve likely already encountered keep us busy, most of the time.”

I met her eyes, trying to project my seriousness. Despite the limited expressiveness of my Sprite, I think she understood by the grimace on her face. “That’s not the Order I’m talking about, and you know it. Have you made any progress on my last request before I left?”

In response, Isolde took a deep, chest-inflating breath…and shook her head, to my disappointment. “Very little,” She admitted grudgingly. “You’re lucky you caught me when you did, Nathan. I might not have been here if I hadn’t remembered today was little Aveline’s birthday. As you asked, I venture out every week on the Gigant during their supply runs with the rest of the Throng. Each time, I urge them to go a little farther, to reach another farm or settlement just beyond our last voyage. All so I can question the residents, and so I can do a little investigating on my own. What I’ve learned about what became of Headmaster Greycton, the majority of the Order of the Eclipsed Dawn, and our own classers…is very little.”

I resisted the urge to curse aloud by a hair.

God-damnit.

Only weeks before the onset of the Skyfall, there had been alarming reports of a gathering horde of monsters out in the countryside. It wasn’t all that unexpected, in the aftermath of the Break Stone Incident that had occurred during the Construct War. Tens, perhaps hundreds of thousands of freakish, mutated monsters had been born all at once from that incredibly foolish plan from the Loyalists. The majority of them had already been culled by the time those reports surfaced, but that event’d had global significance beyond the materialisation of so many Monster Cores at once.

It had shifted the balance of Vereden’s Aether towards a higher density. More monsters were spawning, and of a higher strength than had been observed in years past. There had actually been Academy-led studies being done on the phenomenon before the Skyfall. I’d seen some of the preliminary reports that had crossed Grey’s desk. My mentor had been perfectly willing to share them with me.

All of that to say, it hadn’t been exactly surprising to hear about the horde forming in the Herztalian countryside. As a result of it, a coalition between the Polaris Reach, the Eclipsed Dawn, and whoever else among the Martial Orders was available had been formed with the sole purpose of exterminating it. That expedition had been very popular among the Classers of Herztal. That many monsters, gathered in one place, just begging to be culled?

Easy Status fodder. An excellent chance to replenish the depleted ranks of our Classers in the aftermath of the Construct War.

Only…

A few weeks after they had departed to track down the horde, the Skyfall had hit.

And we had never received word of what had happened to any of those people. Before I’d left on our far more ragtag expedition, I’d tasked Isolde with trying to find any hint of them.

We needed those people. I needed them.

Especially Grey. I really, really didn’t want to fight Fynneas without the legendary Shadowed Sun by my side, the consort and secret Envoy of Elys, Great Spirit of the Moon, herself.

Even when Alveron had proposed that I permanently slay his grandsire, he’d done so with the assumption that I would do so with Grey at my side.

I don’t know how they would get to the northern lands of Velancia if Isolde could find them. Especially after all of the trouble our expedition had gone through just to get there ourselves. I just had to have faith that some piece of esoteric knowledge or power of Grey’s would allow him to get there.

We. Needed. Those. Forces.

Without them, I might just be leading the expedition into a suicide mission.

I was knocked out of my split-second reverie when Isolde spoke next. “However…” She said slowly. “Very little isn’t nothing.”

My heart leapt into my throat for a moment, and I leaned my Spritely form forward, silent with anxiety.

“Just last week, we managed to reach the farthest we’d gone so far,” Isolde continued. “A small farming hamlet by the name of Theyrl. The village elder in those parts confirmed that, a few weeks before the Skyfall, a large force of extremely well-armed Classers passed within a league of the town. They were initially frightened of them until a squad of scouts from the force appeared within the village square, and offered to buy whatever supplies Theyrl could spare. While the purchased supplies were being loaded onto a cart, they asked the Classers who they were.”

“They replied they were part of a Blutstein force assembled to hunt monsters.”

I let out a shuddering breath.

Finally. Any word at all was welcome. While that was only confirmation that they had been alive at all before the Skyfall, that was at least something.

“Did they say where they were going?” I asked breathlessly. “Anything at all?”

To my alarm, Isolde grimaced and looked away from me. When she spoke again, her words sounded like they were being dragged out of her. “Yes,” She said reluctantly. “They did. They said they were going to Warthorton.”

I furrowed my brow, mentally pulling up a map of the continent as I did. Flicking over it, I finally remembered just where ‘Warthorton’ was.

And the bottom dropped out of my stomach.

“Warthorton?” I asked hoarsely. “That Warthorton?”

Isolde nodded quietly for a moment, but didn’t speak.

Desperately, I shot into the air from where I sat on the back of the chair, and zipped over to a bookcase on the left side of the room. “Where is it, where is it…” I muttered rapidly, rifling through the stack of parchment I had bee-lined to. After a moment of frantic searching, I found it.

A map I owned of the continent, rolled up tightly. It wasn’t anything special, nor was it very detailed…

But it was enough.

I flew back over to the desk and struggled with the rolled-up parchment, eventually managing to undo the oversized tie, laying it out flat. When I did, I shot a glance over my shoulder at Isolde. “Where’s Theyrl?”

My second in command sighed and stood up from her chair. She leaned over my tiny form, casting a long shadow over me, as if she were a giant and I an ant. The Princess scanned the map for a moment before resting a finger.

On a small, unmarked section of the map…a few inches away from the large dot marked ‘Blutstein’ on the west coast.

The strength went out of my conjured legs, and I stared blankly down at the map with unseeing, numb eyes.

Nevertheless, Isolde wasn’t done. “Theyrl was about here,” She said quietly, and then began to move her finger. "And Warthorton..."

And move it and move and move it…until her finger rested on another large dot on the map.

On the east coast of the continent.

I stared at it, silent at the implications.

Warthorton was the single largest Herztalian port on the eastern coast of the continent. It was neither as large nor as prosperous as either Blutstein or Elderwyck, but it was not a small city. The western seas that those two cities lay in were considered warm water ports, but Warthorton was a colder port. A bit more northerly, it technically lay between half-way point of Blutstein and Elderwyck, if you drew a straight line across the continent. The Navy in particular had a strong presence there, far more than they did in Elderwyck.

It was an important place…and there was no connecting point between ‘Theyrl’ and Warthorton. There were no major settlements that could possibly house a force as large as that. If that expedition had truly been on the way to Warthorton, having tracked the horde to that location…

If they had been caught out in the open on that large, barren stretch of the continent.

They were dead. The Skyfall would have killed them.

Silence fell between Isolde and me for a moment, as I processed that possibility. She had no doubt already come to terms with it, while for me…

It was just bringing back old fears.

Isolde broke the quiet in my office. “It’s possible that they could have come across another unmarked settlement on the way,” She said softly. “After all, Theyrl was unmarked as well.”

I let a long breath escape the non-existent lungs of my Sprite. “If they found another warded location. If they were close enough to one. If they were under the wards when the Skyfall hit. If it was large enough to hold thousands of additional Classers.” I shook my head. “If. That’s a lot of ifs, Isolde.”

“…it’s all we have.”

I closed my eyes and tried to think. “How fast is the Gigant?” I finally said.

“Nathan, no,” Isolde said immediately. She knew me well enough to guess where this was going. “It can’t be done. We can’t risk it. Going as far as Theyrl was already a stretch, and we’ve made the decision we won’t go farther for the duration of the emergency. Trying to find Grand Marshal Greycton’s forces along that route would be too great a risk. We depend on the Gigant now, Nathan.”

I let my tiny fists clench and met her eyes. “We have to. Isolde…there’s something you should know.”

And there, in my own office, I told Isolde about what I had learned.

About how the Skyfall was getting worse…and how we only had a few more months to resolve this…

Before Fynneas’s unbound Mantle killed us all.

Needless to say, she didn’t take it well…but she agreed to my proposal. Isolde was a practical, sometimes cold-hearted woman.

While the northern expedition was fighting off a Savoy assault on Rhoscara, the southern forces would take the Gigant out…

And find those soldiers.
