Ezekiel Thorn let out a long sigh.

In truth, he did not mind that his mark had been washed away. He did not mind having fought the damned innkeep and his husband, he didn’t mind needing to trail through the undergrowth of the third floor. All of that was perfectly acceptable to him, because all of it was proof.

Proof that he was free.

However long he crawled around the dirty paths of the third floor - he relished every moment of it. Even when he lost his way to track the rookie, having to spend days trawling through the muddy forest, constantly faced with challenges far greater than the average climber. After all, that was only fair, right?

All of that he took in strides. But when Respitia contacted him again, demanding to know how he had not succeeded yet, how he’d lost to someone so much weaker than him, how he’d failed in his mission, and that he was a pathetic, worthless wretch of a man… that made him sigh.

It was a little tiring. He sighed, again.

The mage, another scithian, laid a cool hand on his shoulder. Her eyes were the same pale blue as the crystals behind her back, looking like the ocean on a bright day. “Are you ill, old man?” she asked, fake concern in her voice. “Perhaps you ought to let me test a few spells on you?”

Ezekiel brushed her hair from his face, strands of blue-white that felt cold to the touch. “No, Serra, I do not need your assistance.”

“Shame,” she said. Then she leaned back, stretching, pushing her gloved hands far above her as her wings spread wide. She gave a satisfied hum as her spine popped once, and then turned to the woman in armor. “Igrys, come here. I need to test something.”

The tank, for her part, looked up and huffed in annoyance. She had black hair and red eyes, pale skin and wide shoulders. “Pay me,” she said simply.

Serra pouted in reply. “Fine, then I’ll just cast it on you over there!” she huffed.

For a moment, Igrys’ eyes went wide. She rose to her feet, quickly brandishing her shield. A moment later, a spell smacked against its surface, covering it - and the woman behind it - in a thick layer of clear ice. Serra smiled proudly. “I call this one the popsiclifier!”

Their spellblade, a feryran woman with reddish hair and dark eyes, smacked the mage on the back of the head. Her skin was covered in the bio-metal plates that was common for her species, which meant that even the small smack was probably uncomfortable. Not that high-level climber bones were much softer than metal.

“Owwww,” Serra whined anyway. “What was that for??”

“Stop casting spells on our party,” Railles, the spellblade, chided her, with a gravelly voice. Ezekiel eyed her for a long moment, finding no concern, and instead, a face filled with annoyance at the inconvenience. He smiled. She was the most like him, then.

He didn’t care in the slightest if anyone in his temporary team died. They could all fall off the tower if they wanted to. No, they were bound by convenience. Serra owed him a favour. Igrys and Orri, their sumeen clothmancer-diviner, were paid handsomely. And Railles, the grey-skinned spellblade, was here because Respitia had traded her a major request to the tower for it.

As Serra stuck her tongue out at the spellblade, Igrys broke from the ice, asking if she could strangle the mage. Gently, Ezekiel had to turn her down, and request Serra stop her wanton experimentation. Then, finally, when the bickering died down, he turned to the quietest member of their party.

“Any news?” he asked the clothmancer, Orri.

The sumeen hummed, then slowly tilted its head in a manner not unlike a bird’s. A shiver ran down Ezekiel’s spine as all six of those sight-holes fixated on him. He never liked the sumeen. They were too… alien. Barely people. But he could work with one at least.

Orri hummed again, noting his discomfort but disregarding it. The clothmancer replied easily. “No, something is shrouding them right now.”

“That makes no sense,” Serra supplied instantly. “There’s nothing on a floor this low that should stop your scrying.”

“And yet, here we find ourselves,” the sumeen replied casually. “With my scrying stopped.”

Ezekiel huffed in annoyance. “What’s blocking you, then?” he asked.

“Mana,” Orri replied. “A dense, thick sheet of mana. An amount that would burst my vessel a hundred times over. An incredible amount of it, even.” Their voice was calm, melodic as always, and yet, Ezekiel tensed at its description.

There was only one thing that could mean. “Tower’s Wrath,” he whispered. “But… who? Which other Eye is pursuing Ion?” That last question was leveraged at Respitia rather than anyone present, really. He grit his teeth when the uptight creature he served refused a reply. “Damn it,” he cursed. “I was looking forward to the fourth floor.”

Now, he’d have to fight someone there. Great, just great.

Flamando, once the mana subsided, found himself on the fourth floor. Instantly, he slammed his fist into the ground, shattering it. “Damn it!” he cursed. “Falling piece of falling shit. Tower’s Wrath. Tower’s damned Wrath!” He slammed his fist into the ground again, sending bones and debris flying.

The fourth floor. The Grindstone, the Graveyard. Bones littered the fields around him, the tower having deposited him miles away from any fully fledged city. Of course it had. It wanted him to cool off. To put his limiters back in place.

He could feel it, too. The dense knot of mana behind his head, like an eye, watching everything he did. As if the tower was chiding him for his loss of control. Flamando tightened the magic inside his flaming head, forcing the sphere that he used as an anchor for vision to compress.

It turned from red to orange and yellow, then glimmered with a faint green, tightly webbing into itself. Flamando took a long moment - the equivalent of a deep breath - to calm himself. Just the fourth floor. They’d be here soon. And then he’d find that rookie and toss the falling bastard off the side of the tower.

Until then, at least, with what this floor was, he didn’t need to worry about boredom. There were always plenty of bodies to be shattered deep in the Graveyard. As soon as the rookie came up, it was over. Ion would be dead. Dead, damn it.
