Chapter 30: The Hunt Begins

Molech was leading the largest faction, and had been hunting us for a while now. His group had swollen to nearly thirty initiates, a small army of killers united under his reptilian banner. Other factions had been hounding him since the beginning, trying to keep him in check, forming temporary alliances to prevent any single group from dominating the games completely. It had been the one thing keeping Kari, Vathwin, Zoey and I alive during those early weeks.

I had thought of it as necessary then, staying with my small group while larger predators stalked each other through the dungeon's twisted corridors. But it was another lie, another comfortable fiction I told myself to avoid facing ugly truths. I stayed with the group because I had been afraid to die. I didn't mess with any of the other factions because they protected me, gave me cover, let me hide behind their strength while I slowly gathered my own.

Weakness. Pathetic, but carrying its own truth like all the best lies do. I had been a coward, plain and simple. Too scared to strike out on my own, too weak to claim what I wanted through strength alone. The man who had built houses and worried about mortgage payments, still clinging to life in the thing that wore his face.

An observation I would need to meditate on later. Pain and shadow and the void between what we tell ourselves and what we actually are. For now, I had prey to hunt, and hunting was honest work. Pure in its simplicity, uncomplicated by the lies we tell ourselves about noble motivations.

Luckily, they weren't hiding. In the opposite direction of the chasm where I had undergone my trial, past the twisted forest where alien trees grew in impossible spirals, was a camp erected from fallen logs and stone. It sat in a natural clearing surrounded by an outcropping of black rock that provided both protection and a commanding view of the approaches. Smart positioning, the kind of tactical thinking that kept people alive in a place designed to kill them.

Keth'Val was a chosen initiate, marked by his patron just as I had been marked by Vash. He didn't run the largest faction, or even a big one, but nor was it small. Perhaps a dozen initiates called his camp home, enough to handle most threats through sheer numbers if not individual skill. Enough to be dangerous, but not so many that I couldn't handle them if I was smart about it.

I stood amongst the alien trees, my respirator breathing like a wraith stalking through the night. The sound echoed softly off the twisted bark, a mechanical rhythm that felt more natural to me now than my old human breathing ever had. Above us, the crimson Flux storm swirled with concentrated aspects, shadow and void and primal energy dancing together in patterns that hurt to look at directly. We were in no danger down here, the storm was too high up, and it appeared every few days like clockwork, part of the dungeon's mysterious ecology.

I watched the camp with the patience of a predator. Every so often, patrol groups would leave through the makeshift gate, venturing into the forest before returning hours later. They carried dead Flux beasts slung over their shoulders, their point totals slightly higher than when they'd left. A steady but slow accumulation of power, the kind of careful advancement that kept you alive but never made you truly strong.

I considered striding into the camp and killing everyone present. The thought had a certain appeal, the directness of it, the honesty of overwhelming force applied without hesitation or mercy. However, I tempered that impulse with what little wisdom I'd gained through pain and experience. There was no doubt I was powerful now, my bloodline giving me advantages most initiates could only dream of. But numbers were still a factor, still a force that could overwhelm individual strength if applied correctly.

I didn't think I was so powerful as to stop ten or more initiates at once, especially not in an enclosed space they knew and controlled. I was no great tactician, had never studied warfare or strategy beyond what Vash had beaten into me about single combat. But I did possess common sense, and common sense said that walking into a fortified position alone was a good way to die stupidly.

Arrogance was also a weakness, a lie of its own that I would come to learn intimately in the days ahead. The belief that power alone could overcome any obstacle, that strength made you invincible. No, the best thing to do was be patient, hunt their patrols, whittle them down one group at a time. They would stop sending them out eventually when the losses became too heavy, but I hoped to make the situation a little more manageable by then.

I waited for another group to emerge before following them, tracking their aspects through the Flux like a bloodhound following a scent trail. I didn't bother being stealthy in the traditional sense, didn't worry about broken twigs or footprints. Instead, I simply kept my distance and masked my own Flux signature, letting my bloodline's natural authority over the energy hide me from their primitive sensing abilities.

There were four of them, a manageable number for someone of my current capabilities. I could feel their individual power levels through the Flux, none of them particularly strong, all of them well within the range I could handle even if they worked together perfectly.

Alien trees swayed in blood-kissed air, their branches moving to rhythms that had nothing to do with wind. The crimson light filtering through their twisted canopy gave everything an otherworldly quality, as if we moved through a fever dream made manifest. This place had never been meant for human habitation, had been designed by forces that thought in scales and colors we couldn't comprehend.

Not all of the initiates were human, I noted as I drew closer to observe them. There was a snake-like creature whose scales shifted color as he moved, camouflage that would have been perfect in a normal forest but stood out starkly against the alien vegetation. Another looked like living rock, his form crude but powerful, joints grinding against each other with every step. The rest were human, though all had developed the predatory eyes I had come to associate with those who had survived long enough in this place to matter.

The group was talking amongst themselves as they moved, their voices carrying through the strange acoustics of the twisted forest. Snatches of their conversation reached me through the trees, fragments of information that painted a picture of the politics back at their camp.

"Keth grows paranoid," one of them was saying, a woman whose voice carried the flat tones of someone stating an obvious fact.

"Well, I would be too when your second in command tries to stab you in the back," another replied with bitter amusement. The kind of laugh you heard when something wasn't funny but what else were you going to do about it.

"He seems to think Molech will try to raid us soon."

A snort of derision from the rock creature, a sound like gravel sliding down a mountainside. "Let him try! That lizard's gotten too big for his own good. Someone needs to cut him down to size."

Interesting. Internal strife, paranoia, the slow dissolution of trust that happened when people were forced to live in close quarters under constant threat of death. All weaknesses I could potentially exploit, though not today. Today was about culling numbers, not gathering intelligence.

When they were far enough away from their camp that screams wouldn't carry, I made my move.

My Resonance Blade ignited with its familiar hum, white light touched by crimson spilling through the alien forest like captured starlight. I tapped into the Flux within me, letting the primal aspects surge through my bloodline, granting me speed and strength beyond normal human limits. The power felt clean, honest, uncomplicated by doubt or hesitation.

I surged forward, closing the distance between us in a handful of heartbeats.

The snake initiate died first. He turned just as I reached striking distance, forked tongue tasting the air, predator's instincts warning him of danger. His eyes widened in the moment before death, pupils contracting to slits as some primitive part of his brain recognized a greater predator approaching. It was too late, even with the enhanced reflexes his species granted him. I had caught him completely unprepared, focused on their conversation rather than potential threats.

My blade decapitated him in a single clean stroke, the resonance energy cauterizing the wound even as singed green ichor painted the air in arterial sprays. His body toppled forward while his head rolled away into the underbrush, reptilian features locked in an expression of terminal surprise.

The rock initiate tried to use the Flux, his crude form moving with surprising speed as he began channeling power into some kind of defensive ability. I could feel him gathering void aspects, preparing to create barriers or perhaps harden his already stone-like hide against my attacks. It might have worked against someone else, someone who fought by the normal rules.

I reached out and touched him, my fingers making contact with the jagged surface of his shoulder. He felt exactly like what he appeared to be, living stone given form and purpose. Then I activated my Consume Flux ability for the first time, pulling his gathered power into myself rather than letting him complete whatever technique he'd been attempting.

The sensation was indescribable. Like drinking liquid lightning while swallowing molten metal, the stolen void aspects flooding through my system with violent intensity. It wasn't quite experience in the traditional sense, but the aspects nourished something deep within my bloodline, feeding hungers I hadn't known existed.

The rock initiate made a sound like churning, hot gravel, his voice box grinding against itself. Perhaps that was surprise, perhaps confusion at finding his power suddenly gone. I didn't know or care, already moving back and whirling my blade into a modified chi strike that sheared him in half at the waist.

Living stone revealed its limitations when faced with concentrated Flux energy. His upper torso toppled backward while his legs remained standing for several seconds before collapsing into rubble. No blood, but something that looked like molten lava leaked from the wound before cooling into black glass.

Reaching out through the Flux, I found the mind of one of the remaining human initiates and froze her in place with my Command Presence. She stood motionless, eyes wide with terror, as her companion launched himself toward me with a resonance blade that manifested like a massive claymore.

There was no weight to the energy weapon, so he could swing it with impunity, bringing it around in great sweeping arcs that would have bisected a normal opponent. But his Flux aspects sang to me through my bloodline, making his intentions as clear as if he'd announced them verbally. I could see where each strike would fall before he'd even begun the motion.

I stepped inside his guard and cut off his right hand at the wrist. The appendage dropped to the forest floor, fingers still twitching around the hilt of his now-fading weapon. The initiate howled in pain and shock, staring at the cauterized stump where his hand had been. A howl that was cut short as my blade removed his head with surgical precision.

My respirator calmly filtered every breath as I turned my attention to the final initiate, still frozen by my power. Through the Flux, I reached into her chest and crushed her heart, applying pressure until the organ burst like an overripe fruit. She dropped like a sack of meat, blood frothing from her lips as her body's systems failed in cascade.

Power flooded into me from their deaths, four more lives converted into advancement, four more steps along the path I had chosen. But when the energy settled into my bones and sinew, I realized it wasn't enough to trigger another level increase. The disappointment I felt at that realization was sharp and immediate, and I acknowledged it as a new part of who I was now.

A man disappointed at not gaining enough power from killing four people. The observation should have horrified the person I used to be, but all I felt was a clinical interest in my own transformation. How far I had come from the man who had worried about mortgage payments and coached little league. How completely that person had been stripped away, layer by layer, choice by choice.

I looked down at the four corpses scattered through the alien forest, their blood already being absorbed by the hungry earth. Tomorrow there would be nothing left of them but stains on the ground and whatever memories their faction-mates carried. In a week, even those would fade, replaced by more immediate concerns about survival and advancement.

Such was the nature of this place. Death was common, forgetting was survival, and only the strong left any lasting mark on the world.

I was becoming strong. Soon, I would be strong enough to take whatever I wanted from whoever had it.

Starting with Keth'Val and his carefully constructed little kingdom.
