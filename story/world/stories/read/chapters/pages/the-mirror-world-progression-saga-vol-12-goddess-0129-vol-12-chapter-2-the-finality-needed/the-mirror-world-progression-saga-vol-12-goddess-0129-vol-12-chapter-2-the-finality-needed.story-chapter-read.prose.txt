The gallows humor only broke up the seriousness of things briefly, as Azure was still all nerves, a false smile ending up on her face as she wrung her hands.

Orswyth cleared his throat as he met Tashome’s eyes and said, “We’ll make it as aesthetically pleasing as possible, I promise.”

“And painless,” Azure said insistently.

“Indeed,” Tashome said placidly.

Samantha rolled her eyes. Pain has become irrelevant to me, anyway. Despite that fact, she instead said, “I can use [Create] to make it unseen if you don’t like it.”

It was Azure’s turn to roll her eyes. “As if I, of all people, wouldn’t still perceive the difference? Besides, that doesn’t seem to stop your eyes from bleeding.”

“That is different. The eye is a deliberate change, the bleeding some sort of spiritual version of a psychosomatic twitch.”

“Eh, whatever! Let’s just get this over with, already.”

“I thought you’d never ask,” Orswyth said, as he produced a small box from his satchel.

Azure stared at it like a viper. “Here? Already? Now?!”

An almost collective sigh filled the room. Tashome finally replied, “The location doesn’t matter for this. All the laborious aspects are concluded, most of it by Orswyth, and we just need to finish this last technical point, though it will require a brief… dose of my best to facilitate the sacrifice.” He turned to Samantha and nodded.

Samantha gave Azure a supportive smile before floating over, as Orswyth and Tashome moved to stand next to each other, and the others began a simple ritual circle creation meant to stabilize and prevent interference.

“It’ll help if you’re stable and still, Samantha,” Tashome said. “Not flying, at least.”

Nodding silently, Samantha activated [Create] and fashioned a complete crystalline carapace around herself, encasing her like a cocoon, leaving only her face free and holding her quite securely to the floor. She dropped her hovering powers, naturally. “How’s that?”

Tashome shrugged. “Overly thorough and very sufficient.”

Orswyth made the little box open, and Samantha gasped as the intense aura bathed her. The orb revealed inside was simply magnificent, both naturally and in the spirit world, glittering with many-colored sparkles, and its numerous intricate facets catching the natural light. On top of this, portions of its surface shimmered like a shifting pool was hidden somewhere within it, while lightly ‘peeking’ out.

“Incredible,” Samantha breathed, spellbound.

“Thank you,” Orswyth said with some amusement.

Tashome, who’d — as usual of late — been cloaked entirely over his body, threw the cloak back, revealing yet more fully covering clothing. When he took off his glove, he unveiled a hand covered in enchanted bandages made entirely of golden thread. He then undid a brace over his whole arm that fell to the floor, revealing the bandages all the way up to his shoulder, and thoroughly wrapped. Everyone watched with wide eyes as he began unwinding it, and the act was much more — it was exposing something very powerful.

I know what’s coming. I’ve been overlooking this for quite a while. We don’t have much longer until the end, anyway. He can go nuts at this point, and I bet he knows it. I just hope he can restrain himself from crossing that final line.

Tashome revealed an arm that seemed a cross between charred wood and frozen tar. Down the length along the outside, symbols made of brilliant gold were embedded in it, and spiderwebbing networks of the gold thread interwove within, like visible nerve lines. But they existed most principally as something Samantha understood was a binding principle, to restrain the rest.

A baleful, terrible aura emanated from it that saturated the room. Something like Bast’s ‘halo,’ this was a type of chaos counterbalanced by order. In Tashome’s case, it was perhaps getting much closer to ‘evil’ than chaos, and the ‘order’ certainly wasn’t ‘good.’ It took her a moment to conceive of the best analogy, but it finally came to her.

Runaway growth simply in containment. That’s exactly what it is. Redberry wouldn’t like this type of growth, either.

It wasn’t quite a cancer, but the word ‘malignant’ was relatively appropriate.

“That control element is whatever is left of the lich’s book staff,” Samantha declared. “It was all gold. You fused multiple artifacts together. Probably all the ones we couldn’t figure out or utilize.”

As various exclamations resounded around the room, Tashome nodded in confirmation. “Yes. I know most of you haven’t seen this yet. Let me just say, I didn’t exactly intend this result, but this is it, and there is no changing it. I experimented with our darkest artifacts. They sat unused and discarded, their secrets on the periphery, their whispers torturing me no matter the barrier or distance. They wouldn’t let me be... so I poked where many would say I shouldn’t have.”

“You can say that again, Mister Fucking Dipshit,” Estara said, glaring at him with her arms crossed.

Samantha winced at her cussing like that. Good thing she had limited peripheral vision, or she’d see Azure or Ash glaring at her like it was her fault — which it pretty much was.

Tashome, as was typical, shrugged. He flexed his awful hand as he looked at it. “It’s destiny, it seems. Despite its appearance, the pluses are extensive, and not just in power. I feel more… stable than ever.” His brow knitted together thoughtfully as he stared at the hand. “As if my work has a new filter, sparing me all but a tiny portion of the damage I’d otherwise deal myself. This… emergency work, this necessary ingenuity of my very survival… has become my magnum opus. I’ve attained… victory. And the whispers?” He closed his fist. “Well, they’re not gone, but I’ve pushed them back.” His eyes met Samantha’s. “One last time.”

Samantha had no critiques. “I ask for nothing more. We’ve all dived into our individual obsessions to some degree, and managed to deal enough not to totally lose ourselves. Now we’ll all come together one last time.” She smiled. “In Orchestration.”

To her surprise, Tashome gave her the rare gift of returning that smile as he nodded. Then, it was gone in a flash, and he took a deep breath as he said, “It is time to begin.” With that, he dropped down a pair of heavy goggles over his eyes. “If you haven’t already, ensure you’re protected from blinding light. I’m not sure if it will show from inside the artifact, but better safe than sorry. Samantha: keep as still as possible with your eyes focused dead ahead.”

Samantha waited and watched as the resplendent stone orb was moved closer to her eye. Orswyth closed his own, holding out his hand and focusing in deep mental spaces. She felt a spiritual shift. Meanwhile, Tashome held out his black hand, showing another golden symbol embedded in the center of his palm. The aura from it, and basically from him as a whole, intensified and concentrated around her, saturating the air with heaviness and charged heat.

Their two powers, one subtle, one flagrant, synthesised together as they ‘approached’ her own soul. She felt the necessity of attunement from her, of ‘permission’ with absolute rejection possible.

Naturally, she gave it, surrendering herself to the process and linking herself spiritually with a feeling like exposing her own throat to predators.

But this was part of the game of predators, after all, and she chose to trust these aeons' long rivals, as so many times before, to attain the heights they forged together.

The stone became aligned with Tashome’s hand, directly between it and Samantha’s left eye. It then began to heat, and its inner power seeped out and made it shine, casting the room in a vast, colorful panoply, like thousands of facets to a tiny, stained-glass work of art.

Beautiful.

It happened like a striking cobra, like a lightning bolt too fast for most to properly fathom — but Samantha was plenty fast enough.

Masses of black thread-like tendrils erupted from Tashome’s fingers and enveloped the stone, then carried around and past it, stabbing and digging into Samantha’s eye and quickly ‘taking’ it. What that entailed was something between absorption and replacement, between destruction and creation. It was certainly one thing, above all: transmutation.

In any case, her old eye was gone in a flash in every fashion, from her Avatar to her spiritual form. She felt this sacrifice intrinsically. She saw nothing but blackness in it.

There was pain — extreme, spiritual pain as a stronger echo of the physical — but Samantha didn’t even twitch as she remained detached from it. It was ignored.

She felt a bit of amusement, as Tashome had bold-faced lied to Azure, knowing the pain was irrelevant to Samantha.

Even as her eye was ‘changing,’ the stone orb was carried over through the mass and pressed up to her eye socket with great force. The tendrils ‘pushed’ up and sheared bone simultaneously, to wedge the stone into the waiting matter, now like a primordial goo desperately seeking definition.

The stone provided it, and the remains of her eye absorbed and became one with the stone with a million little alterations and inner flashes that bewildered even her. But she sensed the construct attach, synthesize, and cement.

The artifact, ‘Light of Finality,’ has been added to your inventory.

Tashome’s tendrils retracted quickly after this ‘strike,’ shooting back across the distance and reforming into his arm, with a disturbing series of shifts and ripples. But all too soon, his arm was as it was.

Initial feedback from the eye was just chaos, but gradually, it became more discernible. The world was all color and light through it, in endless varieties she didn’t know the names for, and lacking shadows or darkness, making depth very difficult. But with both eyes open, her other eye could fill in the blanks. The combination created something like a filter or lens of stained glass — the old world with new outlines. Instead of darkening, this filter was too bright. Not an exact explanation of it, but it was the closest she could relate it in her head.

The eye was very much a hindrance to basic optical vision due to range-finding issues, growing worse with distance, but she also felt there were new fine details, particularly up close.

Doesn’t matter. I don’t need natural vision anyway. I didn’t make the sort of sacrifice poor Ana did. I gave up an eye, not sight.

It felt very heavy and hot inside her eye. She felt the power radiating through her, and its profound readiness to open when needed.

It’s what I need to finish this. I’m ready for him; I have to be.
