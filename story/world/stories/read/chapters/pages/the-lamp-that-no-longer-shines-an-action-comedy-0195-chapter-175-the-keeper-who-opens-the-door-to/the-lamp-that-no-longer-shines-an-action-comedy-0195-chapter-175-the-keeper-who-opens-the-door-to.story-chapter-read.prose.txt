[Time]: Summer Break, Day 48 — 2:30 PM[Location]: The Crown of Ovelia · Administrative Control Room

The black banners continued their slow, suffocating descent across the broadcast screens, burying the arena in shadow that belonged to a dynasty claiming its throne.

Inside the Administrative Control Room, the ambient temperature shifted.

The radiating barometric pressure of a sovereign reclaiming her territory, conducted through a single, unhurried act of standing.

Tasia stood up.

The quiet, watchful restraint she had maintained through nineteen hours of vigil evacuated her frame in a single breath. What replaced it was the Dragon Empress: grey eyes stripped to their operational core, cold and immeasurably deep.

"The Crown of Ovelia is our sovereign airspace," Tasia said. Her voice was quiet, but it registered against the control room's spell-glass with the undeniable weight of martial law.

"The Witch Authority maintains an explicit, standing restraining order against Celestial Witches carrying weapon-grade karmic payloads within Inner Sea jurisdiction. The Grand Witch of our realm was just forced to absorb the full karmic load of a Celestial's Wondrous Item within our municipal borders."

She looked directly at Nino.

"The White City has the right to invoke emergency protective custody and implement temporary control measures over all relevant parties. We act first. Administrative paperwork follows."

A hunting license, Hathaway's tactical processor translated, a cold shiver moving up her spine. She just authorized a black-ops strike under municipal environmental hazard law.

Alucard took a long, slow breath.

When she exhaled, the hollow, dead-eyed exhaustion of the eternally tormented corporate employee sloughed off her frame like dead skin.

The woman underneath was something else entirely. Lethal. Hyper-compressed. The grey eyes burned with the specific, incandescent fury of an executioner whose shift had finally begun.

"Finally," Alucard hissed, the word vibrating with contained violence. "Finally we can settle the score."

She turned toward Alice.

"Varro." The name hit like a whip. "The match is over. The causal density covering this stadium has dispersed. It is time to demonstrate why you hold the title of The Zenith of Divination, or that title is going to be weeping in its display case."

Alice produced a short, dry sound that barely qualified as a laugh."No need to worry about my professional credentials, Alucard," Alice murmured.

She lifted her head.

The playful, unhinged warmth in her liquid-teal eyes was gone, replaced by the terrifying, hyper-focused geometry of a master diviner cutting directly through the fabric of spatial probability. The ambient pressure around her plummeted. The air tasted of ozone and burning copper.

She read off eleven-digit three-dimensional coordinates with the flat, flawless accuracy of a machine reciting a hardcoded address.

At the secondary console, Nino's hands blurred over the haptic keyboard, forcing the panopticon array to snap directly to the specified vector.

The central monitor locked. The feed rendered.

Nino froze. Her fingers stopped dead.

Hathaway watched the room react before her own visual cortex had finished parsing the image.

The confident, knowing smile on Alice's face was severed clean, leaving behind a blank, scraped-out mask.

Tasia's sovereign composure shattered, the blood evacuating her cheeks in a single, cold rush.

Alucard recoiled, all the predatory momentum collapsing in a single frame, one arm thrown up in the instinctive flinch of a veteran blindsided by a jump scare with zero atmospheric build-up.

From the far corner, Victoria's fingers tightened on the console edge. "Why..." she whispered, the word hollow as a bell with no clapper.

Hathaway forced her eyes to the screen.

Someone was at the provided coordinates. But it was not Evangeline.

The coordinates pointed to an observation terrace nestled amidst the staggered white marble architecture of the city. It sat adjacent to a municipal clock tower, bathed in the rich, golden slant of the late afternoon sun, the kind of unremarkable civic balcony a pedestrian might casually glance up at while crossing the street below.

Perched on the stone railing with her legs hanging loose over the open air below, her cloak lifting in the warm afternoon wind, was a tall, slender Cat Witch.

She had a thick mane of naturally curly long hair. She wore a pristine, slim-cut white shirt, accented with fine vertical gold-pressed stripes perfectly matched to the crescent-white fabric. Below it: a black JK-style pleated skirt cut to mid-thigh, woven with dark floral motifs outlined in the same gold thread.

Her long legs were sheathed in black hosiery, and spiraling upward across the fabric, in hot-stamped gold bands, were runic lines.

Hathaway's developer instincts parsed the archaic syntax etched into the hosiery automatically:

Worship the Queen of Cats.Bow down, look up, and crave.More profound than the erudite. More obscure than fables.

She wore round-toed black block heels. Over her shoulders: a structured tailcoat layered beneath a sweeping black cloak. Resting atop her curling hair was a half-tall silk top hat, adorned with three pitch-black phoenix feathers.

The overall aesthetic weaponized sex appeal and dashing theatrical authority with the unconcerned certainty of someone who had never once needed to justify the aesthetic to anyone. A female stage illusionist who happened to be devastating. A modern gothic high schooler with an overwhelmingly dominant personality who had simply been allowed to dress herself.

Hathaway had never seen this person before, and yet something below the logic layer understood completely what she was looking at.

A flock of white doves wheeled through the warm sky above the clock tower. A single bird banked away from the flock, gliding down toward the terrace.

On the screen, the Cat Witch raised one hand, unhurried.

The dove fluttered down and landed gently on her outstretched fingertip.She lowered her head to look at it, the golden light catching the edge of her top hat.

A single click rang out in the dead silence of the control room, sharp as a gunshot.

Alice Varro had set her fountain pen down on the console.The Mistfall Hive Lord turned her back to the monitors and began walking toward the blast doors without a word.

Hathaway's body moved before the thought assembled. She stepped into the path, one hand catching the sleeve of Alice's coat.

"Wait, even if—"The words stopped.

She looked at Alice's face. There was zero humor in the teal eyes. No creative hunger, no authorial arrogance, no fanatical devotion dressed as academic neutrality.

"This matter is already under the Witness of the Keeper," Alice said quietly. "A story cannot have two narrators at the same time."

She gently removed her sleeve from Hathaway's grip and walked out. The blast doors sealed behind her.

Hathaway stood in the blue light, her hand still extended toward the empty air where Alice had been.

She knew Ovelia ran multiple Aspects, each independent of Ovelia herself and each classified to the level of state secret. Allison was the only one anyone ever saw; the rest were not discussed.

She turned back to the room."Who is she?" Hathaway asked.

Tasia kept her eyes fixed on the floor, the syllables tasting like ash."Her name is Avenka. She is the Aspect who governs [Possibility]."

The silence that settled had weight and temperature.

"Ovelia once reversed the river of time to resurrect the dead," Tasia said, her voice barely carrying. "And Avenka... she is the Keeper who opens the door to the non-existent."

Alucard's body yielded. She leaned toward Tasia with a slow, gravitational inevitability, the motion carrying equal parts need and offering of support, neither one resolving into the other.

Against the far wall, Liandra's hand moved. Her fingers reached into the empty air beside her, curling inward, grasping at phantom resistance. Her hand fell back. She closed her eyes.

Hathaway's developer brain spun up and immediately crashed.

Resurrect the dead. The concept refused to compile against the established physics of this universe. Witches didn't die, not in the way that required resurrection. They had conquered the Underworld. "Death" for a Witch was a bureaucratic inconvenience, a mandatory vacation to the afterlife before filing the return paperwork.

What kind of dead person requires reversing the river of time?

Time magic was a known quantity. She had watched Heidi Lucent reverse a ruined gown's worth of spilled Frostmead back into a decanter. The [FreshRevert] function built into any domestic food container, used to restore a cold bento to its just-cooked state, was localized entropy reversal. Standard domestic hardware.

But if you ran [FreshRevert] on a cooked steak and kept pushing the revert sequence backward, you never recovered a living cow.

You were applying a rollback to a compiled asset without the original source code. The biological architecture corrupted.

What emerged was a screaming, distorted abomination that defied the laws of nature because the laws of nature had a hard prohibition against crossing that specific boundary. From dead to alive. That was a server-level taboo.

And Ovelia reversed it anyway. And the Aspect who opened that impossible door is on that screen, looking at a bird.

Alucard sank back into her chair and buried her face in both hands.Tasia placed a hand on her shoulder, and left it there.Liandra remained against the wall: not looking at them, not moving toward them, but not leaving the room either.

At the secondary console, Nino stood up.

She smoothed the front of her jacket. The mechanical composure locked back into place, her expression settling into its habitual precision.

She looked at Hathaway. Then she looked at Victoria.

"My work here is done," Nino said quietly. "You two still have things to do, don't you?"

Victoria Wellington rose from her terminal.She looked at Hathaway and extended her hand.

Hathaway's developer brain, which had been running risk assessments and throwing fatal errors for nineteen consecutive hours, shut down completely. Her body moved faster than the logic could reconvene.

She reached out and took Victoria's hand with desperate, unhesitating certainty.

It was just the two of them now. The author had left the building. The hunting party had been dissolved by something that outranked their jurisdiction entirely.

Whatever terminal sequence was already executing somewhere out in the shadows beyond the spell-glass, they were going to walk into the blast radius and witness it.
