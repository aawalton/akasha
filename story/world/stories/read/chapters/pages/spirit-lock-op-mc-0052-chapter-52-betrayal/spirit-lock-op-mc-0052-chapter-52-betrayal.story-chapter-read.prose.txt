Carian shot toward Hayden the second he outed her. She aimed to inject him with a tranquilizer, but dodged when a Clear Line tendril shot out of his hand.

It looked harmless—but her primal instincts told her to dodge.

She evaded it—and then dodged a second. Then, she dodged left, only to run into a tendril that came out of nowhere.

It barely grazed her, but her cora channels haywired on contact, sending cora shooting in and out of her pressure points.

What the hell’s this? Carian screamed within, vision blurring. She jumped backward, but her legs wobbled when she landed—unsteady.

Hayden struck before she stabilized. She managed to dodge two punches, but when she blocked the third, he injected a clear line tendril into her arm.

That’s when the horror began.

Carian’s core pulsed, and her consciousness disconnected from her body.

“Koben,” she muttered, calling for her shadow cat—but he didn’t come. What’s going on? she wondered.

Hayden approached with slow footsteps, pulling a thick, clear line out of his fingertip. He wrapped it around his hand twice, and the wire developed sinister blades like razor wire.

Carian tried to run, but Hayden wrapped the line around her neck and dragged her into the alley with it. She was an A-rank rifter with a tora-reinforced body, the name for the specialized cora that came from killing and harvesting beasts. Normal attacks shouldn’t have been able to harm her, but the blades sheared through the protective layer and cut into her skin as he dragged her out of sight.

She gasped for breath, mind-numbing. That’s when he spoke.

“You have one—and exactly one—chance at life,” Hayden warned. “You will tell me three things as concisely as possible. First, I want you to tell me who you are. Second, I want to know what you want. Finally, I want to know what you’ll do if I release you. Think carefully. If you lie once, stall, or say something foolish, I’ll turn this alley into a grisly splatter painting. Do you understand?”

Carian gasped for breath, saying, “Y-Yes.”

“Good. You may begin.”

“I’m… part of… Crimson Sick… les,” Carian wheezed out. “It’s… a guild. Our leader… is trying… to buy… Junolyx. And if you… came to us… Mayle would… lose her… leverage.”

“If I joined you—or died,” Hayden corrected. “What will you do if I let you go?”

“I’ll… tell… Karva… that you… beat me… with a… clear spirit,” Carian said, hoping honesty would sway him. “If I do… she won’t… kill you. You’re too… valuable.” Her voice became distant as her brain lost oxygen.

“So your solution is to reveal my defenses and accelerate capture attempts?” Hayden asked. “How foolish.”

-

Hayden had no mercy. Keaden set Hayden up, and he almost got kidnapped or assassinated as a result. Belia Alicibria was out for blood, and he was in the middle of a political battle. This wasn’t the time to be virtuous. If he spared her, he’d lose his trump card and accelerate further attacks. By contrast, if he disposed of her, he would suffer no problems as long as he could remove the evidence—something he could do with disturbing absoluteness.

That’s why he pulled with conviction.

Unfortunately—

“Stop,” a deep voice said.

Hayden didn’t release her when Keaden, who was perhaps the most powerful person at Junolyx, told him to stop. He didn’t even release her when the seven-foot man reached him, towering over him like a muscular tree. But he did release pressure and change the Clear Line into something that plugged Carian’s wounds.

Hayden had no choice but to do so. Unlike Carian, Keaden had seen his Clear Line attack, so he would be on guard against it. That meant that while he would win in close combat, he would lose the moment Keaden used ranged attacks.

“I said stop,” Keaden repeated.

“And release my secret?” Hayden asked. “If she does, everyone who learns of it will attack Junolyx to get to me. If they get me, Mayle will lose her leverage and this institution will collapse.”

“As opposed to killing her and giving the Sickles a pretext to attack?”

“Oh, so you knew she was a spy?”

Keaden’s eyes sharpened. “Let her go. If you don’t, I’ll make you.”

Hayden shot Keaden a chilling gaze as he retracted the line, dropping Carian’s unconscious body to the ground.

Keaden used healing magic to heal her neck. Then, he studied Hayden with a sinister glint in his eyes.

“I’d proceed cautiously,” Hayden warned. “Mayle’s inside—and your ruse couldn’t be more blatant.”

Keaden clenched his jaw and jerked at Hayden. Hayden shot a clear line at the man, who wisely chose to dodge instead of attack.

“You’ll destroy this building fighting me,” Hayden warned. “And I’ll stream it.”

Keaden flexed his muscles. “You’ll fall for this.”

“I’d reassess this situation carefully,” Hayden warned. “Your mind’s deteriorating.”

“What’d you say?”

“You’re suffering from an illness,” Hayden said as he ascended a staircase. “Think twice before you act.” He entered his home and immediately woke Mayle.

“What’s going on?” she asked groggily.

“Your jealous ‘guard’ didn’t protect me from an assassination attempt, and now he’s helping the attacker escape,” Hayden said. “If you don’t hurry outside, she’ll escape.”

Mayle shot up. “What?”

Hayden nudged his head to the door. “Alleyway, twenty feet to the left.”

-

Mayle ran out of the building in her undershirt, praying that Hayden wasn’t correct. Yet when she got to the alley, she saw Carian riding her shadow cat out of the alleyway as Keaden gave chase.

“What the hell happened…?” Mayle muttered as she stared at the blood in the alley.

“That’s Carian Raina’s blood,” Hayden said as he approached. “At least two liters’ worth. It’s strange she’s conscious, isn’t it?”

A static buzz bristled across Mayle’s skin. “Wait… you did that? To Carian.”

“Correct.”

She opened her mouth and paused. “How?”

“I destabilized her core when she attacked, then I shaped my line”—he transformed a line into razor wire—”into something that could cut through her infusion lining. Both usages will send the Crimson Sickles into a rage to capture or kill me. Thank Keaden for your troubles.”

“No…” she whispered. “Keaden wouldn’t betray me.”

“In his mind, no,” Hayden said. “But there’s a thin line between good intentions and cold betrayal, Mayle—and he crossed it.”

He walked past her.

“Where are you going?” she demanded.

“To a safe house.”

“No. You’re coming with me. I’m an A-rank, and he won’t touch me.”

Hayden conceded and followed her to her home, meditating in lieu of sleep while Mayle sat at her dinner table.

Two hours later, there was a knock on her door.

Mayle opened it and stared at Keaden. “You’re fired.”

Keaden looked at his bloody clothing and back. “What?”

“You just tried to kill Hayden against my orders—that’s betrayal. You’re fired.”

“I didn’t betray you. Look, I don’t know what Hayden said to you, but—”

“Where were you when Carian showed up?”

He opened his mouth, but didn’t answer.

“She had been there for three hours.”

He swallowed. “Did Hayden tell you that?”

“He took a video of her shadow, and I didn’t see a single guard on my way down the street.”

Keaden looked away. “Did he tell you what he did to her?”

“He strangled her with razor wire. I saw the scene. There’s no way she woke up after losing that much blood unless she had a booster.”

Keaden laughed. “How can you gloss that over? He strangled a teacher with razor wire.”

“An A-rank rifter who attacked him—an A-rank that confessed. There’s no court in the universe that wouldn’t exonerate him for self-defense. But they will find you guilty of attempted murder and conspiracy.”

“He tried to kill her after she was unconscious!”

“Do you have that on video? Because from the evidence, it seemed like you showed up and he released her.”

He trembled in anger. “Mayle. Listen to me. Actually, don’t listen to me. Look around. Everything’s collapsing. Your life’s in danger. You’re facing prison time.”

“So you thought getting rid of my leverage was the solution?”

“No. I wouldn’t do that. I…” His face creased in strange angles.

“Oh… So you made a deal with Belia? The Sickles?” Her lips curved into a grin. “That’s betrayal. Now get the fuck out of my home before I send you to prison.”

“Prison? You’d do that to me? After seventy years of service?” He raised his voice, only to fall silent when professors surrounded him. He laughed. “You think I’d hurt you?”

“Yeah, actually. And that’s sad, isn’t it? To have a confidant for seventy years, only to have them betray you? Someone who you trusted, only to have them become murderous and possessive?”

“Possessive? Mayle, don’t listen to Hayden’s lies. He’s manipulating you.”

“No. The person who thought he could get away with something so blatant that a fifth grader could see through it is manipulative. You’d have to guilt and gaslight me to extreme degrees to have gotten away with this betrayal. It makes me wonder how many years you’ve abused my trust. It’s sickening.” She laughed sharply. “Now leave. If you don’t, I’ll prove that I keep up with training.”

Mayle slammed the door.

Keaden pounded on it, screaming, “Mayle!” like a snarling animal—rabid and lost for control.
