Lira was recovering from the muscle aches from her new exercise regimen when an idea came to her. She was copying the Close Wounds incantation into a new notebook, since she wanted all her Life spells gathered in one place. When her eyes landed on Hano, used in both Mend Bones and Close Wounds, she started considering other uses for Connection.

She put the quill down and thought about the concept, wondering what else she could use it for other than mending pieces back together.

Connection between two people? Friendship, or a love spell? No, she was not going there.

Her eyes landed on the paper she had been writing on, the material she handled more than anything else in her life. She had a spell that moved ink on paper, a spell that manipulated paper, and a spell that retrieved paper back into her hand. Every one of her runic enchantments used paper, and even her ink reserve was stored in paper.

If she could conceptually connect two sheets of paper together into one, so that whatever she cast through one would also be cast through the other regardless of distance, then she could activate enchantments while remaining safe.

The idea was simple and not that unique of a concept. It was similar to a spell she had read about in fairy-tale stories, where wicked witches used dolls to curse someone from a distance by linking the target to a facsimile of themselves.

But Lira wasn’t about to use something as complicated as dolls and tap into esoteric magic when paper was a more suitable medium for her anyway.

She could link two sheets of paper together and use the link between them to extend the range of her spellcasting. Her limit was always visualization, and with the existence of a magical link, she could always visualize the Connection rather than the location.

It didn't take her long to come up with the words of the spell either.

Kisar. Asatan. Hano.Symbol. Paper. Connection.

Three words, which made it a cantrip, and she already knew every one of them well enough that she did not expect the imprint to give her trouble. The part that needed thought was the justification for the link. She could not link two sheets that had never been one, since she needed something she could picture well, so she decided that her starting point needed to be two pieces of paper that had once been part of a whole.

She decided that the initial linking would have to happen before the separation, on a single strip of paper with Hano runes drawn at each end, and only after the spell took hold would she cut the strip in the middle, leaving each half with one rune.

"Pika. Selan. Tureth."

As she entered meditation, she did not think about the sheets rejoining themselves, like with Mend Bones. What she wanted was a conceptual state of connection, not a physical pull toward reconnection. She thought of a letter written on multiple pages because the content needed more space, with all the pages sharing the same envelope, and how the reader would consider them one letter without ever thinking of them as separate. She thought of a ledger and its duplicate, both kept in separate locations and still counted as the same record. She thought of a sales contract where both sellers and buyers would end up with a copy of the same document. She thought of how paper symbolized connection through sending letters, how the duplication of knowledge through scribal work was also a form of connection, and how physical separation didn’t mean an object stopped being one.

Click.

She opened her eyes, then copied the Hano glyph from her father's lexicon onto both ends of a long strip of good paper.

"Kisar. Asatan. Hano."

Nothing happened that she could see, which was what she had expected. Reaching for the scissors, she cut the strip down the middle and set the halves on opposite ends of her table. Then she waited for a moment, making sure they wouldn't creep toward each other. She wasn't surprised when they didn't. She wanted a conceptual connection, not a physical one, and with that settled, it was time for the real tests to begin.

First, she drew a dot of ink on the left half and watched the right half. Nothing appeared there. The link did not copy what happened on one sheet to the other. The two sheets could hold different content and still be considered linked, just as two pages could hold different content while still being part of the same paper.

Next, she spilled a small pool of ink on each half and cast.

"Asatan. Tir. Kisar."

She visualized the ink on both sheets shifting and writing her own name in a plain hand, which appeared on the left sheet and the right one at the same moment. They looked almost identical, though the pool on the right had been slightly smaller, so the last letter came out thinner and unfinished.

The next test was to influence one paper through the other without seeing it. Normally, when casting a spell, she needed to visualize the exact location she wanted to affect, and distance would interfere with the efficiency, which was why she had needed to enter Salvek's office and see its layout before she could absorb the contents of his journal.

She left one sheet on her desk, then walked to the space between the history and geography sections, checked that the library was still empty, closed her eyes, and turned on the spot until she was thoroughly confused. She stepped forward to the nearest shelf with her eyes still closed and wedged the paired sheet between two books, then made a half turn, returned approximately to where she had started, and turned on the spot again.

Finally, she opened her eyes, returned to her desk, and picked up the first linked sheet.

"Asatan. Tir. Kisar."

She imagined the ink shifting from her name to the number 123, willing the change to happen on the paired twin only. Nothing happened to the sheet she was holding.

"Yorad. Asatan. Kaldris."

She focused on the twin of the sheet in her hand as she cast the Paper Summoning Spell. A sheet of paper flew from the geography section, and Lira extended her hand and caught it. On it were the Hano rune on one side and the number 123 on the other.

Success.

She repeated the test, blind turns and all. This time, the aim was to activate a runic enchantment from a distance. She started by magically drawing a fire rune with her ink spell and then cast, "Kisar. Fila. Coopar."

This time, rather than summon the paper, she went looking for it and found it wedged between two history books with warmth radiating from it. She let it run for a minute before using the ink spell again to collapse the rune into a shapeless blot, and the heat went out.

Next, she put on Mage Sight, since she wanted to know whether this spell would be stealthy or visible to anyone watching, just like the Focused Hearing spell.

"Pika. Maja. Syneth."

A thin line of disturbance ran between the two halves, faint enough that she had to look twice to notice it, and it was there whether she cast anything or not. She moved one sheet to the far end of the library, and the line stretched with it, a pale filament crossing the reading area at the height of a table. The thread was barely noticeable, if she was honest, thin enough that she doubted anyone would instinctively feel it the way Samy had felt her dust-gathering spell. Though anyone casting Mage Sight might spot it if they knew what to look for. Even now, the disturbance from the heating plates helped obfuscate it somewhat, but not much, since they were the only other magical effect in the library. She doubted anyone would notice it in the busy noble gardens.

She observed the line while she cast a spell across the link.

"Asatan. Tir. Kisar."

She turned the ink into a fire rune, and the disturbance line thickened slightly before returning to normal.

"Kisar. Fila. Coopar."

The line thickened more than the first time before returning to normal as the heat rune activated.

Lira frowned as she considered it for a second. The line of disturbance seemed to fluctuate when the link was actively being used. She considered adding runes for mana obfuscation and stabilization, but in the end, she concluded that they would not be very useful, since they would cover the two sheets but not the line between them.

This meant she had to be careful when using the spell, so she should probably use it under the garden cacophony of magical disturbance, just like she had done for her Focused Hearing spell. She would only keep the link alive in places that were already thick with other magic and drop it everywhere else.

Speaking of dropping the spell, that was the next thing she needed to test. Let's say she wanted to spy on Kavard or Kelroth and managed to sneak a paper from a paired sheet inside their belongings. That would mean she had a way to extend her runic casting directly over them. Now the question was, could she break the link, thus stopping the line of disturbance pointing between her and her target, then reactivate the link without touching the piece of paper that was already deep within enemy lines?

Using her ink magic, she smudged the Hano rune on her own sheet, picturing only that rune and not its twin, and the line of disturbance slowly started to thin until it was indistinguishable from ordinary mana. She fetched the far sheet and found its Hano rune untouched, only inert, an ordinary mark of ink with no magic behind it.

"Asatan. Tir. Kisar."

She redrew the Hano rune on her end and cast the cantrip again.

"Kisar. Asatan. Hano."

Through her Mage Sight, she saw that the line of disturbance had returned between the two sheets, exactly as before. The link was restored, which was a relief. She was afraid that she would have had to start over from a single strip of paper once more.

The link could be killed and relinked at her end. She could cut it, restore it, and cut it again without ever going near the twin. The only way to lose a pair for good was for someone to find the far sheet and destroy its rune.

Lira leaned back in her chair, thinking of the sound-mimicking enchantments, two runic spells she had made a few weeks ago, one that mimicked life, like the sound of a cat, and another that mimicked metal, like the chimes of a bell. With this new spell, she could now activate them from a distance or after waiting for the perfect timing, in a way that would let them work as a distraction rather than forcing her to activate them where she stood.

She considered creating a runic version of Fireball and exploding Kavard from a distance before shaking her head.

Too messy.

Still, Lira stretched in satisfaction, proud of the spell she had come up with. She closed her eyes and did some of her breathing exercises, focusing on her soul projection, trying to see if she was depleted of magic after this series of tests. She didn't feel too strained. Maybe her new Breathing Spell was also helping her mana recovery, or maybe those cantrips didn't cost that much to begin with. Either way, she was happy with the result, especially when combined with the next few runic spells she had in mind.
