Lexie hugged her father for what felt like an eternity.

She couldn’t believe that he was really back.

Early too. She inhaled his warm scent. Somehow, he still smelled like cinnamon and nutmeg. Like home.

She forgot about everything else for those minutes, forgot about her questions for him and all her plans for the day. All the worry she'd pushed to the back of her mind finally drained out of her.

She was just so happy he was home.

Aiden held her up, too, patting her back and murmuring into her hair.

Eventually, Lexie let her father go and kicked her feet so he would let her down.

"How come you’re back so early?” she asked after her feet touched the ground.

“Well, they couldn't complete the interrogation because of the problems on Earth,” Aiden said. “Not too long after I landed, Earth embassies received an alert that all off-planet reserves were being summoned back, including some of the Lightlarks. All other extraterrestrial missions have been halted to deal with the impending world war. Jerry also cancelled the interrogation and requested that, since Earth was in an active emergency, they would need all the superpowered help they could get. I guess I technically count as superpowered help, since I do have hero training, and I was working with Vacek. They had no choice but to let me go a few seconds into testing."

“Ha,” Lexie said. “I bet the Ambassador was pissed about that, wasn’t he?”

“He wasn’t pleased,” Aiden looked smug in admitting it. "But he had no choice in the matter as the Oath does dictate that in cases of large-scale human emergencies, like when there's a threat of world war, all heroes and helpers can be summoned back on Earth irrespective of prior contracts with the Fae. That was one of the concessions that the First Mage secured, and Vacek made sure to never bargain away.”

“Hmm,” Lexie said. “So there's really going to be a world war?"

“Shouldn’t you be telling me?” He seemed amused. “You’re the one on Earth with access to the news channels.”

“Yeah, but I rarely watch the news. Every time I turn it on, my face pops up, and it cringes me out.”

Aiden grinned. “You should be enjoying your newfound fame. You know that was one of my favorite parts of being a Hero."

"Yes, but that's because you're arrogant and an attention hog."

"Oh, and you're oh so humble, aren't you, bumble bee?" He tweaked her nose. "I saw the video they took of your return, and I’m so happy I repainted our cottage three years ago. The previous paint job would have looked embarrassing on TV." He scratched his chin. "Max could have mowed the front lawn a little, though."

Lexie gave him a look that made him chuckle.

“I’ve expanded the scope of our protective wards. The PHORBs won’t be able to get close anymore, so feel free to take a walk by the lake if you want.”

“Did you do the same thing for Emma’s place? I thought I felt a ward there.”

“More or less.”

She raised an eyebrow. “Did you do it, or did your golem do it? We can talk about that now, right?”

Aiden’s smile slipped. He looked reluctant, but he wasn’t changing the subject, which meant that he was no longer being surveilled.

“It’s true, isn’t it? You created a golem of yourself before you left.”

“Yes.”

Lexie nodded. “I thought as much. That was who saved me in Indigo, right?”

“Yes. The tag in your brain tells me when you’re in severe danger or on the verge of passing out. Then I trigger the golem to wake up and teleport to wherever you are, in incognito mode.”

“I see. It's not an automatic trigger?"

"No. The golem doesn't act on its own. It can't, by its very nature, unless it's extremely sophisticated."

"Oh, okay. But why didn't you tell me it was there?"

“I didn't want to tell you in case you got asked about it. So then you would have plausible deniability." He raised an eyebrow. "I don't know if you've noticed, but you've become terrible at lying."

“It's not my fault," Lexie grumbled. "Still, you should have told me. We shouldn’t have secrets between us anymore.”

“Yes,” he nodded. “I know.”

“Speaking of secrets… You must already know that I talked to the Alchemist. And you must know what I talked about.”

“Yes.” He took a seat at the kitchen table, and Lexie sat too, ignoring her now soggy cereal to focus on his explanation.

The conflict in his expression told her what he would say before the words came out of his mouth.

“What he told you is true. I did strike a deal with him, and our lives are inextricably linked until the deal is done.”

Lexie wasn't surprised by the news. Deep down, she'd known it was true. If it hadn't been true, he would have found some way to let her know already.

She wished he'd let her know in the beginning. It was scary how close she'd gotten to unknowingly killing her own father.

“Why?” Lexie asked. "Why did you make the deal with him?"

“It was the only way to save you. The Alchemist and Vulcan shared visions. That was one of the reasons they worked together. In those visions, you died.”

“Well, yeah, because Vulcan was actively trying to find scenarios to figure out how to kill me.”

“No. Out of curiosity, the Alchemist looked into hundreds of thousands of futures where Vulcan does not kill you, just to figure out what would happen. In most of them, Vulcan dies, but you don’t survive much longer either.”

“I don’t understand.” Why would she die if Vulcan wasn't trying to actively kill her?

"Vulcan wasn’t the biggest threat to you,” he said. "Vacek was.”

Lexie’s mouth opened and closed.

Of course, logically, it made sense, though a part of her rejected the idea of Vacek intentionally hurting her. It didn't fit what she knew about the man.

Nevertheless, they were in a death game after all, and there could be only one Chosen left standing. Vulcan had presumably gotten rid of all the others, so with him gone, it would be between Vacek and Lexie, both of whom had the highest amount of fate points. Though Lexie was powerful, the other man was crafty and had the power to sell Lexie out to the Fae the minute that he thought he could no longer control her.

Or he might use her and lead her to her death as he did with her mother.

“So you with Vacek," Lexie ventured carefully. "Did you….?”

Aiden’s jaw clenched slightly, but there was still a curious lack of guilt in his eyes and his tone when he said. “Perhaps I could have tried harder to save him.”

That wasn’t exactly an admission of guilt, but maybe he wasn’t quite ready to accept what he’d done yet. Or maybe he was concerned that if he did admit it, and the Fae looked through his memories at some point, they would use that against him.

Then again, couldn’t he just take out the memories that he didn’t want them to find? He’d said he could do that, right? But maybe the Fae would notice a significant gap in his mind and flag it.

Which begged the question, if he did hurt Vacek, how did the heroes and the Fae not see it in his mind? Or maybe he'd made it look like he'd tried to save him when he didn't.

“I watched so many scenarios with you hurt, Lexie. I couldn't risk even one of them coming true,” Aiden said. “That’s why I made the deal with the Alchemist because without him, you wouldn't make it to adulthood.”

"Okay, but now we've got what we wanted, right? I’m safe now. Both Vulcan and Vacek are dead.” Which left Lexie and Tate as the two Chosen standing. Sure, there could be more. In fact, there were probably more.

But none on Lexie's level at the moment.

Hold on.

If there weren't more Chosen, and Lexie wasn't officially a Chosen...did that make Tate the last official Chosen standing by default?

That couldn't be right. At least it didn't feel right.

He wasn't nearly powerful enough, and he was still sickly, although somehow his disease was progressing at a slower rate.

Aiden shook his head. “That was only my end of the deal. There’s one more thing he wanted from me.”

“What?"

“A key, found only in the Dark Tower.”

Lexie froze. It couldn't be the same key she was thinking of…could it?

It had to be. The Alchemist must have made a deal with either Yasycht or the Mountain himself.

“What does this key do?” Lexie asked quietly.

“The key, I believe, belongs to the Guardian Janus, who was essentially in charge of transitions. Most people think that just means things like travel or seasonal transformations. But it deals with bigger things, shifts like cosmic regime changes. It's somewhat a reality-altering power that abides by certain rules. Janus was also the Guardian responsible for keeping the other Guardians’ authority in check. They often call him the Decider of Kings and Impartial Judge. Capturing him was essential to capturing the rest of the Guardians. And his Key, when accessed, could unleash some of his power."

"That's incredible."

"Yes. But it's also incredibly hard to get. The key is found on the sixth level of the tower itself and can only be accessed through sacrifice and passing the Guardian’s test.”

“So you think that the Alchemist wants it because–"

“He wants to break the Fae Oath, and this is even better than Oathbreaker."

“I thought you said the Fae Oath was the most powerful magic in the world.”

"It is. But with the Key, the Alchemist might be able to transition us into a world where the Oath never existed.”

Lexie inhaled sharply. “Like time travel?”

“Possibly, or possibly more complex than that. The Key can access a lot of different realities, and with Janus powers, we can shift our Earth into a new reality pretty easily.”

"That's..." Lexie couldn't even fathom it. "Wow."

"Yeah. Wow."

“But doesn't that go against a bunch of the other cosmic principles? Like Fate? And Balance?"

“That's one of the reasons why the Key is so hard to get."

“Wow,” Lexie said again. "So you have to go into the Tower and get the Key?"

"Either that, or find a way to break the Oath another way.”

Lexie looked down at her fingers. A cosmic, reality-defying, oathbreaker. That was what everyone was fighting about.

“It’s just…” Lexie bit her lip. “I was also supposed to retrieve a key.”

Aiden raised an eyebrow. “What?”

“When I was in the dungeon, I spoke to the Mountain where the Oracles get their visions from. She was the one who told me that you would be in danger soon, and that was why I came back. But in order for her to help me, I had to make a deal with her. The Deal was to either kill Vulcan or to retrieve the Key that Yasycht wants. Yasycht is a Great Old One, an ancient, powerful Eldritch Lord of the Doorway. He's probably similar to Janus in that he deals with transitions, and he's the one from whom I derive teleportation." Lexie cleared her throat. "Anyway, he’s trapped in another realm, so I assumed that they wanted this key to free him, but the Mountain said she wasn’t going to give it to Yasycht and that when she was done using it, she would put it back in its rightful place. So I wonder what she needs it for." Lexie shrugged. "Maybe to free herself from the Yasycht? Or is she trying to free Janus from the Tower?"

Aiden listened thoughtfully, rubbing his chin again. “Well, the Oracles always worked with Janus in some capacity. Interesting. So you think it’s the same Key?”

“I have no clue, but there’s no other Key I know. And the Mountain said that if I ever saw the Key, I would know it. She seemed sure that I would encounter it at some point, so I’m guessing…yeah. It’s the same key.”

Aiden licked his lips, his eyebrows furrowing into thought.

"But I’m pretty sure that I don’t need the Key anymore, since I fulfilled the other terms of the contract. So that’s a good thing, I guess. I don't have to go to the Tower now, and technically neither do you."

"Yes." Aiden exhaled. "But breaking the Earth's Oath the old-fashioned way is just as difficult."

"I have a few ideas." She thought about it. “Oh, by the way, did you know that Governor Azure had a temple of Janus behind his bookshelf? His family apparently used to be one of the worshippers."

“Hm. I suppose that makes sense. He’s one of the ones who was working with Silas, according to Vacek. That means, by extension, he was working with the Alchemist."

"Seriously?" Lexie gaped. "Wait, Vacek knew that Dewie's dad was working with the bad guys, and he just left him to it?"

"No. He used that information to blackmail him into letting Dewie work for him.”

“Oh.”

“He also kept the Governor in place to monitor his movements, using him to catch the other conspirators."

“But what if he'd helped tip them off before Vacek could get to them?"

“I guess Vacek must have handicapped him some other way.”

“Do you think that Vacek knew about the Temple?” Lexie said. “I entered it, and while I was walking in there, it felt weird. Like almost familiar. And then these gongs started going off, and somehow I got out of there without even knowing."

Aiden frowned. “Strange.”

“Yes, I was thinking of going back there to investigate. Governor Azure said that his family used to be the Waystriders, and they helped execute Janus' will, whatever that means. Have you heard of them?”

Aiden shook his head.

“They used to have some kind of soul power, but it got stripped from them when one of their ancestors did something stupid." Lexie paused. "You said our family didn’t have any deals with the Guardians, right?”

"Not that I know of. We were descended from a low-ranking Baron, with no special ties to the Guardians or the Fae. Why do you ask?”

“I just got curious about our family tree.”

"Hm. I can't speak for your mom's side, but from my side, you have two grandparents and maybe some cousins twice removed."

"How come we never visit my grandparents?"

"To be honest, it's because my mother died while I was in my last year at the Academy, and my father and I never really got along. After I left home, he never tried to reach out to me, and so I figured he wouldn't want me reaching out to him as well."

"Why? Did he hate you?"

"No. I don't think so. It's just complicated." He inclined his head. "I suspect that he thinks I'm not his real son."

"Seriously?"

"Yes. We're very different people. He's a lot sterner, duty-bound. Some might say humorless. We look different, too. Neither of my parents had magic, so they weren't equipped to deal with someone like me. After I grew up, I think he started to suspect that I might not be his and that I looked down on him or something." He shook his head. "He was wrong on both counts. I actually got a DNA test, and I am his son. And I've never felt any type of contempt for him. As a matter of fact, the older I get, the more I understand him, but I wouldn’t say we were very close at all.”

"I'm sorry," Lexie said.

"It's okay. That's life."

"Is he still alive?"

"I assume."

"And you don't have siblings?"

"Nope."

"Bummer. It would be nice to know if I have any other Uncles apart from Max and Luke. Oh, speaking of Max, do you know that we ate pizza every single day for an entire week?"

Lexie went on to catch her father up on everything she'd been up to, but most of her discussion centered around complaints of the lack of meal variety Max provided.

Aiden saw the complaints for what they were and said wryly, “Is that your way of not-so-subtly asking me to make you something to eat?”

"Maybe," Lexie circled her toe on the floor. "I mean, if you want to."

"Hm. How about a nice pot roast? With cookies and brownies for dessert?"

Lexie’s mouth was already watering, but she swallowed her saliva and said, “If you’re not too tired, that is."

Aiden laughed at her coy words, and Lexie was too happy to push back at his teasing.

She hung out with him in the kitchen like old times while he started cooking. He also told her about his experience on the Orc planet, the ship making an emergency landing, and the fact that they hadn't actually met any Orcs.

At some point, the front door opened, and Tate walked in holding some books.

He grinned widely when he saw Aiden. “You’re back!”

“Hey,” Aiden washed his hands and went over to give him a hug, which Tate accepted awkwardly. “How are you? You know I left a few messages for you, but you didn’t call me once while I was gone.”

“I was pretty busy. I figured if you had to tell me something urgently, you would tell Lexie.”

"Or you could have just answered my calls. Did you miss me that much that you couldn't bear to talk to me?"

He shrugged carelessly, but it wasn't fooling anyone.

“Dad’s making pot roast,” Lexie said, swinging her feet with excitement. “He’s also introducing me to his golem later."

"Thank goodness." Tate exhaled, putting his books down on the kitchen table and sitting. "About the pot roast. I don't know or care about the golem thing."

Aiden laughed.

Max also came in like ten minutes later, and rather than greet Aiden, he said, "Thank the fucking System, you're back. I'm out of here."

"Where are you going?" Aiden called.

"Home," Max said as he jogged upstairs to get his stuff. "Oh, and do me a favor? Never ask me to babysit those two demons again."

"Demons?" Aiden frowned at his daughter and Tate. "Just what did the two of you do to him?"

"Nothing," Lexie and Tate said in unison, earning Aiden's suspicion.

As the delicious, meaty, umami scent filled the kitchen, Lexie also told Aiden about going through Vulcan's memories. She figured that might give them some clue about her mother and also about anything the Alchemist might be planning.

"We can go through it together?" Lexie said. "The Mom parts that is."

It took a long time for Aiden to nod when she made the suggestion.

"After lunch," he said. "We'll go through it after lunch."
