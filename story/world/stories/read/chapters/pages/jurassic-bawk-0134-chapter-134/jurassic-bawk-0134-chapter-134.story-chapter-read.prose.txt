The first sensation that Phatagin experienced as consciousness returned was one of mild discomfort.  It was hot even for a creature that had been born in the sticky climate of coastal West Africa.  At least it was a dry heat.

The second thing the pangolin noticed was the sound of flames crackling nearby.  After a few moments, the sounds of burning were joined by loud cracks as the strangely-armored eggshell that had protected the pangolin during his Class Evolution fell apart.

Phatagin emerged to find himself alone in a hellscape of howling flame.  The temperature continued to rise around him, but his newly increased Durability protected him from being scalded by the growing inferno.  Ignoring several messages from the System, he investigated his surroundings.  Power flowed into his eyes as the pangolin activated his mana sensing Skill, but this proved to be a mistake as the sheer amount of environmental energy overwhelmed his vision and sent pain lancing through Phatagin’s optic nerves.  He cried out, his voice barely audible over the roar of flames.  Another message from the System arrived, but the pangolin accidentally dismissed it as he panicked.  The world suddenly went silent.

As Phatagin slowly recovered from the shock of pain and being temporarily blinded, he realized two things.  The first was that he had been transported elsewhere by the System, since the air had was now cool and still and the charred dirt under his claws had been replaced with smooth tile.  The second was that, during the brief moment he’d glimpsed the firestorm with his mana sight, he could make out the form of a rooster at the very center.  Charlemagne.

What sort of monster is he becoming? the pangolin wondered in awe as he processed what he had just witnessed.

Thankfully for Phatagin, a System message arrived outlining his rewards for completing the first Floor of the Delve.  They were good.

Very good.

Brett sighed as he leaned back on his sofa, alone for once with his thoughts and a stiff drink.  The past few months had been especially grueling, starting around the time his Champion had returned from the Systemic Games.  The debrief session with Ndiogou was both frustrating and alarming.  The knowledge that the System existed in dimensions that he could not perceive was especially troubling.  Not for the first time, the human deity wondered what exactly they had wrought.  Aside from that startling revelation, there was also the time dilation factor to ponder.  Ndiogou had only experienced two days at most in the demiplane, or set of connected demiplanes, that hosted the Systemic Games.  But on Earth, well over a month had passed during his absence.

It was a well-known fact that time flowed differently depending on the energy state of the observer.  In the physical universe where Earth resided, an object needed to travel nearly at the speed of light or experience incredible gravity to achieve any time differential worth measuring.  Oh, sure, pre-System astronauts traveled fast enough and experienced low enough gravity that they could, with the aid of atomic clocks, track the difference between themselves and an observer on the ground.  Brett frowned to himself as he recalled the NASA Twins Study, where scientists estimated that astronaut Scott Kelly had aged 5 milliseconds slower than his twin, Mark, over a period of 340 days.

Deities also experienced time dilation within their realms.  Contrary to expectations, the more power that went into the creation of a demiplane, the slower time passed.  This was generally not really a concern for immortal beings with few responsibilities, especially since most demiplanes were almost identical in size.  In fact, it was within GOD headquarters that time passed most slowly, as it was both the largest demiplane and had substantial energy reserves prepared in case of hostile intrusion.  Even so, time passed only about 1percent slower there.

The time dilation Ndiogou had experienced was closer to 2,000 percent.

What sort of being could generate that kind of power?  Was it the System itself or was it a deity (or perhaps a pantheon of deities) that ruled over a veritable multiverse of life?  And, if such hideous power existed, why hadn’t its owner conquered this universe already?  Clearly, the System knew about Earth’s existence: it had chosen Ndiogou’s Party to compete in its game.  So why hadn’t someone at sent over an envoy, a greeting, or even a threat?

It made no sense.

And, of course, this issue just had to crop up at the worst time.  While Ndiogou and his friends were off having a grand adventure, the coalition that Brett had spent so much time and effort to build had been slowly falling apart.  Deities that had hitched their star to humanity’s rise were jumping ship left and right.  Notable among the losses were Angus, the deity of cows, and Flockheart, the deity over chickens.  Both had established enormous Settlements where co-opted humans served as traders, grew crops, constructed buildings, or even cleaned.  What was worse was that the System had blessed such an arrangement, giving his followers the appropriate Skills and Classes to allow for meaningful progression.  In fact, one of the highest-leveled humans in the world was an Exacting Dairy Extractor, able to milk hundreds of cows in a single day.

While these losses stung, Brett still had Canius and Felissa, although he had to confess that canines and felines weren’t as useful as they had been before the arrival of the System.  But at least they were edible.

Speaking of canines, Ndiogou and Sirius had been ordered to team up again, this time leading a trio of pale foxes specialized in tunneling.  Their mission was to travel to the Sahara Desert and figure out what Formua Integralis was up to.  Something was up with the deity of ants and termites, and Brett was committed to getting to the bottom of the mystery.  There were reports from all over the world of empty tunnels being uncovered: tunnels that appeared to have been created by giant insects.  That is, except in the Americas.  In the Americas, most sightings of these six-legged insects suggested that a mass migration was underway to the Amazon rainforest, although there was not enough data yet to say for certain.  And Brett didn’t have any Champions in the Amazon…the entire place was a death trap for humanity almost as bad as the continent of Australia.

Brett took a sip of his drink and shuddered.  He had little desire to remember how the System Integration had gone south down under.

Instead of dwelling on that particularly nasty bit of the past, the human deity thought of the near future.  The upcoming 3.0 update had a lot of great features in it that Brett expected would benefit humans more than any other species, particularly a buff to crafting Skills and Classes, the introduction of Alchemy and Potion-Making, and the re-introduction of electricity.  For too long, physical strength was the only metric that mattered on Earth.  It was time for humanity to once again flip the table through artifice.  Thinking about plans for the future occupied a happy few minutes as the deity dreamed big.

First would come raw combat prowess: magical arms and armor rivaling anything granted by the System or that could be found in a Dungeon.  While some humans, like Raul Sanchez, had experienced success creating their own equipment, Brett envisioned a return to the factories and assembly lines of the past.  Human artificers could apply their highest-leveled crafting Skill to every mass-produced item, yielding incredible benefits.

Once humanity had re-established martial dominance across the world, transportation, medicine, and science would be next.  Transportation was a top priority because no one knew where the next planet-wide emergency would crop up, and Brett hated the idea of having to depend on an overgrown chicken to protect the Earth.  Instead, several strong Parties armed with powerful human-made Items and supported by Potions would be deployed to the location of any Challenge as quickly as possible to maximize humanity’s gains.  Being able to reinforce population centers would also serve as a deterrent to assaults from species that remained hostile to humans, like the American badger and the entire group of gastropods that humans had dubbed “Conidae”.  Earth’s oceans were no longer safe for humans, but Brett had a plan to deal with that…eventually.

Medicine would continue to be important, as the limits of current medical knowledge would soon be transcended through Alchemy and the power of Potions.  By using magic to blur the laws of biology, the human lifespan could be extended, lost limbs restored, and power temporarily bestowed on even the feeble.  And that was just the beginning.  Some Potions were even said to provide permanent boosts to Attributes, although these were not nearly as effective as those provided by the System.  And there were even hints that those recently slain could be revived, provided that some life force clung to the body of the deceased.

These advances in power, mobility, and medicine would all march to the unceasing drumbeat of science.  The effects of mana would be studied and quantified.  Efficient methods of achieving Body and Soul Tempering would be codified and distributed.  Even the prerequisites for Skills, Special Abilities, and Classes could be uncovered with enough effort.  In one thousand years, Brett expected most humans would be raised along a carefully defined growth plan designed to maximize their utility.

As to who decided what was the most useful, well, the deity would test groups of humans against each other to see whether a top-down approach, with leaders determining which path a child should take, worked better than a “laissez-faire” style, where the child was allowed to choose for themselves.  Maybe Brett could organize some tournaments between the different schools of thought.  After all, one thing that humanity did exceedingly well was to fight among themselves.  Even his Champion, Ndiogou, had gone on a revenge spree once he had found strong allies.

At the time, Brett had been worried.  The world’s strongest group had fallen on Niamey like a lightning bolt, slaying anyone who dared stand up to them.  But, just as he had written the city off as lost, Ndiogou suddenly withdrew, his goal having been accomplished.  What was even more incredible was that the human’s mental state had stabilized after.  In Brett’s experience, humans suffered terribly from killing each other.  It was another thing that he would have to have his future scientists study.

Brett sighed as his thoughts reluctantly shifted back to his to-do list.  He fully expected humanity to take advantage of the coming changes to pull further away from the pack in the global hierarchy, but each step needed to be babied along, or the whole thing would collapse.  Being able to make Potions was meaningless without a safe place to do it, making safe places required the protection afforded by weapons, armor, and/or Settlement Tokens, and there was always the threat of a Challenge like the asteroid looming on the horizon.

In order to fully benefit from the update, humanity would have to recreate the industrial revolution from scratch, starting with mana-based electrical engines and working up to complex machinery that would reduce the need for basic Skills and physical labor.  That way, despite their comparatively small numbers, humans would be able to exert an even more outsized influence over the world around them.  That suited Brett just fine: he had always been a quality over quantity type of deity, and the strategy had served him well over the past several thousand years.  Humanity would never be the most numerous group on Earth, but that was all right with him.  After all, a ruler had to have subjects.

Brett took another long pull from his drink and wondered just how long the rooster and his pangolin sidekick would be competing in the “Systemic Games”.  Despite how quickly the world was changing, it would take a few years for humanity to truly regain the upper hand, and, in any event, Charlemagne was not a creature to be taken lightly.  The deity could only hope that nothing bad happened to the rooster’s farm while he was gone.  That, and assign multiple Champions to guard it.

No matter how long it took for Charlemagne to return, his flock would stay safe.  It was better for everyone that way.
