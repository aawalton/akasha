Ace couldn't believe his luck.

In fact, he didn't believe it.

Rachel claimed she had detailed information about each area in Floor 1. Pros. Cons. Risks. Benefits. The very idea seemed impossible, and he glanced down at the squiggles she had traced across the cave floor.

Everything about this felt like a setup. Like another way for the System to raise their hopes just to dash them later.

But it was worth a shot.

"Let's see what you've got, Rachel," Ace said.

Though she hesitated, Rachel eventually knelt by the sketch and gestured for everyone to come closer. They clustered around her, everyone giving the map a wide berth so as not to mess up the delicate lines. It had been carved into a patch of dirt surrounded by the cave rock, shaped almost like a perfect outline of a body.

He tried not to think about how the hole got there, or who might’ve died in this cave.

Ace knelt at the map's southern edge, his enhanced vision cataloging each feature. It took a moment, but he eventually recognized the tactical thinking behind Rachel's cartography—she had marked potential ambush points with subtle depressions in the soil. Crosses marked other areas, though he wasn't sure what they signified.

The map wasn't just geographic—it was strategic intelligence rendered in dirt.

"Not bad," Marcus whistled. "You got the contour lines and everything."

Rachel shrugged. "I memorized the terrain features we've encountered. It helps—"

"—to obsessively catalog every detail as a coping mechanism?" Marcus finished.

"To stay alive," Rachel corrected with a scowl.

Ace studied the map, his training kicking in as he assessed potential routes, choke points, and defensible positions. "Walk us through it, Rachel. Start with our immediate surroundings first."

"Like it says on the Crucible Map, we're in a spot called the Barren Highlands." Rachel pointed to a cluster of squiggles that must've been hills. "Low-Level zone, typically housing creatures between Levels 10-25. Rogue vampire clans stake out small territories in the cave networks, but they're disorganized and mostly composed of tribes of three or four. Corrupted stone elementals live in the higher elevations."

"So let's stay the hell out of there," Olivia muttered.

"Lower Leveled adversaries mean less valuable loot, but correspondingly lower risk," Rachel continued. "Perpetual twilight, occasional blood-rains. The rock spires sing in the winds."

Olivia raised an eyebrow. "Sing?"

"More like wail," Tara clarified. "Heard it yesterday when I was on watch. Like dying animals."

"Wait, can we go back to the blood rain?" Marcus asked. "The fuck?"

Rachel shrugged again. "That's what the System implied."

Marcus wrinkled his nose in disgust.

Ace ignored the man's expression and tapped his finger on the Highlands. "So all we'll find here are Low-Leveled vampires and stone elementals that can crush us?"

"Basically."

"Lovely," Tara muttered.

Ace's gaze darted to her as their healer leaned forward, studying the map with focused intensity. The dim light caught the new angles of her face—sharper now, more predatory than when they'd first arrived.

These people weren't just fellow survivors anymore. His subconscious mind had categorized them into their roles, and everyone added something useful.

Tara—their medic and his second-in-command.

Marcus—their morale officer and psychological warfare specialist.

Rachel—intelligence and tactical analyst.

Olivia—well, Olivia was still an unknown variable, but she was decent at killing things, and that was a plus.

The weight of their survival weighed on him like a fist to the chest. They looked to him for guidance. For leadership. For decisions. They had followed him through hell, and there was no telling what they would face next.

Here he was, making choices that would determine whether they lived or died—and what parts of their humanity they would inevitably sacrifice along the way.

Bit by bit, they were coming together—as a team.

"Next is the Gloomvale Woods." Rachel tapped on a group of cloud-like clusters that were apparently trees. "We already know this place intimately, of course. The dense forest absorbs light. It houses a wide range of inhabitants, from Levels 10-55. It’s home to ancient ruins and lost vampire artifacts. If you don't mind sifting through the perpetual mist and parasitic trees that feed on blood, of course."

"Jesus," Tara whispered. "Glad we didn't encounter any of those."

"What's this?" Olivia tapped one end of her staff on a stretch of open plains to the east of their position.

"The Bloodfields," Rachel answered.

"Why does everything here sound like something out of a horror movie?" Marcus muttered.

"We're in a death pit," Tara said with a wry laugh. "Are you expecting unicorns and rainbows?"

"Wouldn't mind them," Marcus admitted.

"Focus," Ace chided. "Rachel, tell us about the Bloodfields."

"Prime hunting grounds for a human EXP solute," Rachel replied, her voice dropping to a clinical monotone. "Farming communities. Enforced blood harvests. Roving creatures range from Levels 15-30, though vampire clans can hit 60."

"And let me guess," Marcus said. "They kill humans out in the plains? That's why it's called the Bloodfields?"

"Nope," Rachel corrected. "The grass bleeds when you step on it. Literally seeps red from the soil. Attracts predators for miles."

"This place is so messed up," Marcus said under his breath.

"And this?" Ace asked, tapping a group of inverted "V" shapes that reminded him of crudely drawn mountains.

"That’s the Ironweep Mining District," Rachel replied. "Silver and iron mines. The System said something about how metals are important. We already encountered silver, of course” —she shivered at the thought of their Forgeborn battle, as did nearly everyone else— “The problem is that I don’t know what each metal does yet. The humans who live here work under vampire overseers."

"Wait a minute." Ace ran his finger along a thin stretch of the same marker that curved into the Gloomvale Woods. "I know this range because I've been studying the Crucible Map in detail. This is the Shadowmarket Warren."

"Yeah." Rachel gulped, and her body stiffened with fear.

Ace frowned. "What did the System tell you about it? There's nothing on the map to go off of."

"It's not good," Rachel confessed. "It's a literal underground black market built in the massive caverns. There's plenty of monsters wandering around in the tunnels, but the settled areas have safe zones. Monsters usually reach Levels 25-45, with the cities' crime lords pushing 80."

"So, don't piss anyone off," Marcus quipped. "Got it."

"Mixed races, outlaws, merchants," Rachel elaborated. "Dangerous as hell, but the best place to fence loot and gather intel."

Ace rubbed the stubble on his jaw, lost in thought. As dire as it sounded, they would probably need to make their way over there at some point. An area to trade and gather intel sounded useful.

"These are the Crimson Academy Grounds," Rachel continued, tapping on a crudely drawn house that reminded Ace of a kindergartener's finger painting. "These are elite training facilities for vampires. Levels 30-50, but the instructors are way higher. The System implied that there are gardens full of poisonous plants used in alchemy."

"Vamp school," Marcus said under his breath. "Wonder how you get in?"

"The System didn't say," Rachel replied.

"And this?" Ace pointed to a swampy region marked with skulls. "I take it the bones aren't a good sign."

"Nope." Rachel swallowed hard as she stared at the skulls she had drawn into the landscape. "That's the Mistweald. Levels 40-70 for the standard monsters. Apex predators go higher, but that’s all the System would tell me about what we would face in there. It’s a misty swampland with ancient city ruins sprinkled throughout. The mist preserves the last moments of those who die within. Their final screams are said to echo endlessly."

"Hard pass," Marcus muttered.

"We will probably have to go there eventually," Ace pointed out.

He and Marcus shared a glance, and a hush settled over those gathered around Rachel's map.

"This… uh…" Rachel cleared her throat and pointed to a big cross at the edge of her drawing. "This is Duskgate Harbor. Water creatures in this area hit Levels 25-45. There's a trade hub and naval shipyards." She tapped a large stretch of nothing. "And finally, the Forgeborn Wasteland. Abandoned zone and heavily contested between vampires, dragon shifters, and the human rebel faction known as the Forgeborn.”

“What were they doing all the way out here, then?” Ace asked. “The ones we fought were in the woodland. That’s pretty far from home.”

“I assume they send out hunting parties regularly,” Rachel explained. “They also have secret military bases littered throughout Floor 1, from what I could gather. The Forgeborn Wasteland is just their homebase.”

Lovely.

“There’s more, of course,” Rachel said with an irritated scoff. “The Wasteland is also home to illegal weapons facilities and a whole lot of bloodshed. Pretty much everyone there—except the humans, of course, who can't cultivate their power—are at least Level 45, with most pushing Level 65 or higher. Anything less, and you can expect to die a quick death."

Marcus crossed his arms over his chest. “And a painful one, I’d wager.”

“Probably,” Rachel admitted.

Ace sat back on his heels, absorbing the information. Every location presented risks and opportunities, strengths and weaknesses, potential gains—and, of course, potential losses.

"Recommendations?" he asked.

"We should stay put," Rachel said immediately. "This position is defensible, and the risk-reward ratio for venturing elsewhere—"

"That's not an option, Rachel and you know it," Tara cut in. "Besides, what little food we could find is almost gone. Blood doesn’t exactly sate the physical hunger. It just heals us when we’re hurt. I don’t know about you, but I’m sick of those damned cave mushrooms.”

“It’s better than Carnage Fiend bark,” Marcus quipped.

“I know it’s frightening, Rachel,” Ace said, his voice calm as he tried to soothe the woman’s nerves. “But we all need to reach the Crucible. Victor's probably on his way already. I think he got a head start."

Rachel sighed in frustration, and her hand tightened into a fist. Her eyes glazed over as she stared down at her map, apparently lost in thought.

Victor was a threat.

They couldn’t deny that any longer.

The way Victor had slaughtered the Forgeborn leader, the way the man had ripped out his throat while the human was still alive—it was too much. The casual brutality. The growing darkness behind the former merc’s eyes. Victor was changing faster than any of them, embracing the transformation rather than fighting it.

And, as of this moment, he was winning.
