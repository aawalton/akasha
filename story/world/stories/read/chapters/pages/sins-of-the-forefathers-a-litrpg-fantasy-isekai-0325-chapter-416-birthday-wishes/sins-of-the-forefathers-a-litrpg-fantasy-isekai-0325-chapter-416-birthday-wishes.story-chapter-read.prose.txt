“…and I’ve been helping out in the infirmary, when I can,” Aveline chattered away happily to my current Sprite-based form. We had been simply talking to each other for nearly half an hour now, ever since I had successfully made a connection to this Mana construct. I hadn’t realized just how much I had truly missed the act of just speaking with her every day. Not until I was face to metaphorical face with her.

Up till the moment I’d left, I had spent nearly every day in Aveline Montblanc’s presence. I would wake her up for either class or lessons from home, I would cook for her, I would clean up after her, and I would even put her to bed after a story. Yes, hiring on Rachel as our official housekeeper and nanny had lifted the burden on me to a degree, but I’d had no desire to be an absent Father. Or, well, parental figure, up until I accepted what Aveline was to me. I had strove to be a good-ish influence on her, taking an interest in her day, being present for her, being supportive.

I hadn’t realized, though, that for all I had tried to support her, Aveline had been giving me a reason to be supportive. To be more human, even though I felt like something else most of the time. It felt like a profoundly obvious thing to realize, but…I just hadn’t spelled it out for myself up to now.

Becoming a Father truly did change you. I guess it wasn’t just something the greybeards tsked at you about.

As such, I’d just been…soaking her in. Her words, her presence, and the simple, recognizable hum of what I could only describe as her soul.

Honestly, I think Aveline was trying to do the same. I was a little guiltily gratified to realize she had missed me as much as I had missed her.

“And what are you doing in there?” I asked her, idly drifting through the air over her bed. My blue ember wings left sparks in their wake, as I floated above my daughter below. This was a different kind of flight than I was used to, and it was…kind of fun, honestly. I think the tiny, insect-like wings of fire were mainly for show.

Aveline looked up at me from where she was laying back in her bed, her eyes, so strangely tinted in an emerald similar to mine, tracking my fae-like form with curiosity. “Not much,” Aveline answered. “I mainly fetch supplies for Healer Velaron. Sometimes I take notes that he says out loud.”

I made a humming, acknowledging sound in my tiny throat. It sounded like the buzz of a bee trapped in bottle, to me. It made sense that Lina had retreated to a medical setting, in my absence. Not only had her Mother been a scientist, but her secret Father had been a doctor, once upon a time. Over months of careful questioning, I’d learned that Cecily would often leave Aveline in Traver’s office when she was busy. Lina didn’t even see anything strange about this. After all, wasn’t it normal to entrust a child to the care of its family doctor? Few people were meant to be more trustworthy, after all.

Heh.

I still hadn’t made a decision on if I would tell Aveline about her paternal ancestry, one day.

But I fully understood Aveline taking comfort in a more curative setting.

Still…I couldn’t linger here forever. I was already extremely lucky that the massive Mana drain needed to connect me to this sprite wasn’t a continuous drain. Once the initial cost had been paid, it seemed like the connection would remain open until I closed it. Problem was, my real body was still sitting in the middle of a Rhoscaran rooftop thousands of miles away. I could even still feel it, bizarrely. It was like…the sensations of my true self were a shadow over the muted ones of this Sprite. I could sense my slow, even breaths, and faint, dead breeze against my skin. It was…odd, and a bit uncomfortable I didn’t think anyone would come across me, and I sure as Hell wouldn’t have pulled this stunt if there were still saboteurs in the city. But I should still return to it soon, if only to continue my preparations for the siege.

Before I go, though, I still had a few things to attend to.

I flipped in midair, sending bright shower of heatless embers floating down over Aveline. When she stopped shielding her eyes, she opened them to find me smiling down at her. “Well! I think it’s time for presents.”

Aveline blinked at me curiously, sitting up from her crisp white sheets. “Presents? What do you mean? There’s no way you could have a present for me.”

Despite her visible doubt, I could still detect the hint of excitement in her voice.

My own smile took on a mischievous edge. “Oh? Are you so sure about that?” I teased, putting my tiny hands on equally tiny hips. “Well, I guess we don’t have to go see Miss Isolde and get it from her, now do we?”

That caused Aveline to gape at me in pre-teen indignation. “Are you telling me you left a present with Miss Isolde before you left?!”

Now Aveline sounded properly riled up.

In response, I just blew on nails composed of crimson red thorns, and buffed them over the surface of my leafy chest nonchalantly. “I’m just that good.”

Plus, it was a gift to me all by itself, to see you so childishly annoyed. You really had come a long way, my love. Once upon a time, you were far too quiet and muted to even think of being offended that I’d indirectly kept a present from you for months.

“Let’s go get it, shall we?” I winked at Aveline exaggeratedly.

In response, she tried to muster up a glare, but the excitement of it won out, and she hopped out of bed quickly. She scurried over to the door to her room and nearly slammed it open in her enthusiasm, rushing out and into my small personal quarters here in the Bastion. I floated after her, suppressing my laughter as her feet slapped against the tile.

Aveline raced past the small kitchenette, where I could see Rachel standing with her back to us, already working on a breakfast for the two of them. The stocky, black-haired woman didn’t even turn around as my daughter raced past her with a quick, “I’ll be back in a bit!” before she reached the exit and began tugging on her shoes resting off to the side of it.

“Mmm,” Rachel hummed, stirring something sizzling in her skillet. For a moment, I lamented the fact that this construct didn’t have a sense of smell. My fellow former slave was a shockingly good cook. “Be back before yer food gets cold.”

Aveline merely nodded rapidly, not that Rachel could see, before she raced out the door and over to my office just a few feet away. As I followed after her, I sounded out a few nonchalant parting words. “We won’t be long, Rachel. Good to see you.”

Rachel hummed again, paying more attention to her skillet than me. “Alrigh’, Nate.” As I floated through the doorway, I heard her stirring pause. “Wait. Nate?”

I looked over my shoulder to find a deeply shocked Rachel staring at my floating form just outside. I stifled as a drop of butter fell from her limply held spatula, and splashed onto her apron.

Instead, I just winked at her.

And then the door closed behind me.

Shaking my head, I floated over to the open door of my office, where I could already here the excited voice of my daughter pestering the second in command of my Order. As I flew inside, I took in the sight of the room. Before I had left, I’d lent the usage of my office to Isolde, as she was going to need the extra room to properly lead what was left of the Polaris Reach in the city, in my absence.

The paperwork alone would have buried her own desk.

I was strangely comforted to see that my office hadn’t changed much. Isolde had the large, shuttered windows hanging open, letting the grey light of the Skyfall inside, illuminating my massive wooden desk. It stretched nearly across the breath of the back wall in front of the windows, and on either side of the adjoining walls stood rows of bookshelves, stuffed to the brim. My own personal, growing collection of Mystical texts and reference materials, really. On the wall across from the desk sat my workbench, where, more often than not, I was working on my Enchanting and Alchemy via Aetherial Melding. It was dusty, though, and that I was unsurprised by. Isolde didn’t much care for anything beyond the bare minimum of Profession work.

And then there was my second herself.

Isolde looked…tired, if I wanted to put it politely.

Exhausted and sleep-deprived, if I didn’t. There were deep, dark bags beneath the Princess’s forest green eyes, and her normally impeccably maintained blonde hair was instead held back in a loose, frayed ponytail. Her Polaris cloak thrown over her shoulders was looking a little lived in too, as if she had been sleeping in it.

And yet, for all of that…Isolde was still startlingly alert.

Her eyes snapped onto the form of my Sprite, analyzing it in a flash, as befitting a former intelligence officer. I’d told her about this Sprite and its capabilities before I’d left, so an almost visible wave of relief rolled over her face before she mastered her expression.

I just smiled at her, with my…slightly indistinct features. My face was only defined by a mass of blue flame, after all. “Hello, Isolde. Good to see you.”

“Nathan…” Isolde breathed, closing her eyes for a moment. She shook her head, and I was granted the rare sight of the Princess actually smiling. “It truly is good to see you. With no word, I had almost begun to believe…”

She trailed off, but she didn’t need to continue. I understood. There had always been the possibility that our preparations would be insufficient and the Skyfall would claim us. Or that we would fun into a force too great for the expedition to handle.

It…really had been a desperate gamble.

“We’re alive,” I confirmed. “Minimal casualties on the journey there, and we’re currently inside the walls of Rhoscara. We’ve been here for several days now.”

Isolde nodded in further relief, before pausing and furrowing her brow. “Only a few days?” She murmured. “That’s…slower than our predictions.”

My eyes flickered over to Aveline, watching us with curious, intelligent eyes. For as excited as my daughter had been about her gift, Aveline was sharp. She could tell when something important was being spoken about, and could parse the implications better than most adults I’d met.

“There have been…complications, yes,” I said carefully, to Aveline’s small pout. “We can talk about it in a bit. For now, it’s somebody’s birthday, and I believe I left something with you?”

Isolde blinked rapidly at me for a moment, before flickering her gaze over to Aveline in realization. “Ah. Yes, that’s right,” She said, bending over and rummaging through one of the deep drawers on my desk. From within it, she pulled out something…small.

Relatively. It didn’t even look like a gift at all, really.

Instead, it looked like an envelope. Something no different from all of the other methods of containing and separating out stacks of parchment. These were a little thing I had introduced into the bureaucracy of the Order of the Polaris Reach.

But this didn’t contain almost illegible reports from field Classers. Instead, it had something I liked to think was more…important.

“Here you go, Miss Aveline,” Isolde said kindly to my daughter, handing her the envelope. Said daughter took it in a confused manner, glancing up at me and blinking with a tilted head. I merely smiled enigmatically, as well as I could as a thorn and flame five inch tall construct. “Happy twelfth birthday.”

She remembered? What was I kidding, of course Isolde remembered. “Go on, open it,” I said softly.

Avline looked back down at the envelope and slowly opened it up, reaching in and taking out the contents. As soon as she laid eyes on them, she immediately choked up, staring at them with wide, already watering eyes. Her hands trembled to the extent that she nearly dropped them. However, she maintained a careful grip on what I had prepared for her, and I completely understood why.

It had been most of year, by now, since she had seen her Mother’s face.

Inside of the envelope were pictures. Not just paintings.

Real pictures.

I floated down to eye level with Aveline, and looked at them with her. There was one of Cecily, a small, slight woman with very long blonde hair and emerald eyes standing professionally in front of a whiteboard. There was one of her bent over inspecting something kind of Netherim technological device resembling a microscope. There was even one of her seemingly deep in conversation with Travers.

Not the Lich, but the man as he had been, heavyset frame, brown hair, glasses and all.

Most importantly, though…

There was one of Cecily and Aveline standing together, seemingly posing for some kind of picture together. Both of them appeared to be dressed in their equivalent of Sunday best, in long dresses. For once, Cecily seemed to be out of her omni-present lab-coat, and had her hand resting on the shoulder of a beaming Aveline.

This was the picture that Aveline stared at so desperately, tears running down her face.

“I found them in the archives of Glee,” I said quietly, referring the Netherim archive given to me by Travers. Almost glacially, Aveline’s gaze tore away from the photos to rest on me, floating only inches away from her. Her gaze was desperate and fragile as her eyes drank my Sprite in. “They must have been taken with a camera of his. I…was fairly surprised he even had a print function, but I managed to create proper photo paper after some experimentation and fed it to him.”

After many, many hours of trial and error, that is. Turns out, industrially produced, specially treated paper was hard to create.

Who knew?

I smiled at Aveline as she started openly sobbing in a combination of happiness…and a grief that would never truly leave her. “Happy Birthday, Lina.”

Carefully, ever so carefully, Aveline set the photos on my desk and cupped my Sprite in her hands. She brought me up to rest against her cheek, in the best way she could approximate a hug. “Thank you, papa,” She whispered in a broken, joyful, happy tone. “I wish you were here so much…”

I sighed regretfully, nodded, and reached out to lean against her young cheek, deeply thankful that neither my thorns nor flame could hurt anyone unless I wished them to.

“Me too, Lina…” I whispered back. “Me too…”
