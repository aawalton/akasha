The door slid seamlessly shut, cutting off the duke’s rage in an instant. I turned to find Estan staring at me slack-jawed.

“What are you… why would you…” he gaped like a fish, seemingly unable to understand why I’d enrage someone he had spent a lifetime living in fear of. Maybe he couldn’t comprehend someone acting counter to his deep-seated cowardice, or else he was just monumentally stupid—could never be sure with nobles.

My new storage ring, given to me that very morning, deposited Resolution into my hand with a neat thud. There was no time to don my armour, but for these two, I wouldn’t need it. Bone-pale carapace soon sheathed my left arm, and I let a grin slip onto my face as I advanced.

Credit to the soldier—he reacted far quicker than Estan. Drawing his sword, he advanced on me with a blitz of speed. It must have been a skill of some kind, but it wouldn’t save him. He was a 1st tier, chosen for the task of informing the duke because he was fast and wouldn’t make much of a difference in the final battle, anyway. But now that left him alone in this cavernous room with me: a lamb to the slaughter. An ironic smile slipped across my face.

The amulet that restrained my attributes fell to the floor just as the soldier reached me. I simply raised my shield to intercept his sword, and braced for impact. There was an echoing clang as his helmet pinged off the flanged edge of my shield, and a wet thud as he fell to the ground below me. I hadn’t moved an inch.

I shook my hair out of my face—must have gotten caught on some part of his armour as he crashed into me—and with a contemptuous flick of my spear, I opened his throat. Blood flowed along with the gurgles, and soon enough my eyes were meeting Estan’s. His arms trembled, fingers gripping weakly around the hilt of his sword as he held it up between us.

I made to stride his way when I heard the first impact behind me. A thud. Then another. Loud, as if the cavern itself was shaking. That could only be the duke attempting to break through the door, and the evidence of his power made me wince. This vault was made of material so dense and unyielding that even my mana couldn’t penetrate more than a few inches, and he was making it tremble.

It had only been seconds since I’d locked him out, and by the sounds of it, I only had seconds left before he broke in again. I cursed and took off running. I tried to veer around Estan, but he moved to block me. First brave thing I’d seen him do, but he looked so uncertain about it that I wasn’t sure which of us was angrier at the move. I tried going back the other way, and he did the same. I was only a few feet away now and it was clear he wouldn’t let me past, so I grit my teeth and leapt upwards, spear jabbing out at his face. He flinched back, sword at least staying on point, so I tucked my shield up into position and fell directly into the blow.

The point of his blade skittered off my shield, and I tackled him around the waist, carrying him into the obsidian wall behind. He let out a breathy “oof!” on impact, and then he screamed as my fist splattered his nose. He shoved me off as I wound up for another strike, and I was shocked by the power. Clearly, this man had somehow made it into the 2nd tier, and was heavily invested in strength, too, if I was any judge.

Considering all of the fighting I’d been doing in the last year; I was a very good judge. Estan though? He’d not been half as busy. He clutched his ruined nose and moaned, glaring accusingly at me as if he was personally insulted that I’d hit him and it was somehow unfair of me to do so. Did he think this was a duel? A gentleman’s agreement? The shock and blame was so obvious on his face that it genuinely threw me for a second and we both just stood there staring at one another from a few feet away.

No noise, just our heavy breathing and the pitter-patter of his blood hitting the smooth obsidian beneath our feet. My eyes widened. Where was the thudding?

I heard a roar of anger from behind, so loud it physically hurt, and turned in time to see the door shiver. A gap no more than half a foot across appeared in the doorway, the rictus grin of the duke appearing behind it as he strained to hold open the impossibly strong door of this ancient place. A swirl of purple light slipped between the door and the frame, and I caught a final glimpse of the duke’s blazing glacial eyes before the door slammed shut once more.

My heart wanted to pound out of my chest, but instincts honed from dozens of life and death battles prevented me from breathing out a sigh of relief. Instead, I twisted to one side, avoiding a desperate—and quite frankly sloppy—lunge from Estan. He put his entire body into the strike, and the telegraphing was so obvious that I had no trouble turning his blade aside with the haft of my spear.

My shield came up, the two spikes protruding from the edge where my knuckles would sit slamming up into his belly. He screamed at the impact, and then again when I kicked him from the shield. A flare of mana from off to one side had me turning in time to witness the small ball of purple light—a raven, I now realised—bloom into the form of Varice, floating in the air by the doorway.

She alighted gracefully, a slight bend to her knees, before standing tall and wandering over. Unhurried, unbothered. This was bad. Varice didn’t feel like a 3rd tier—she’d not demonstrated any overpowering aura yet, in any case—but she was clearly more powerful than me. The easy confidence that she strode through the library with now only cemented that suspicion.

I backed away, stooping to grab Estan by the back of his fine robes and drag him back with me. A smear of blood stained the floor beneath him, the red barely visible against the obsidian blocks in the dull lighting provided by the mage-lights high above. I let Resolution return to its travel size and slipped it into my belt, instead withdrawing the hatchet from my storage ring.

I held the axe-head to Estan’s throat as I backpedalled. “Stay back!”

“Oh, come on,” she said, exasperation filling her voice even as she gestured at the man I was dragging around. “He’s a walking corpse.”

I looked down at the blood sheeting from his stomach. His skin was pale, and his head was lolling to one side, eyes rolling in panic. Occasionally he’d look up at me and seem to come to his senses, hands slapping weakly at my own where I gripped his shirt, but then he’d cough or retch and his head would loll once more, eyes again sightless and fixed on something beyond.

“On second thought, perhaps just a corpse? He’s not doing much walking, is he?”

I frowned at the words. “I’ll fucking kill him,” I grunted. “You know I will!”

“Go ahead.”

“But he’s your boss’ son,” I said weakly as my back hit the wall.

“He’s an entitled letch, and the Marchlands will be better off without him,” she replied. I couldn’t even disagree. “You’ve just given me a good enough cover to get it over with.”

She kept walking, and I knew I had only moments left to make a decision before it was made for me.

“Fine,” I grimaced, then heaved Estan’s body directly at her. I spun around the edge of the low wall, disappearing into the first ‘room’ of the grand library. There was just enough time to see ghostly chains appearing in the air to catch the body, Varice continuing her unhurried walk forwards as Estan was wrenched aside. The chains, emerging from strange fractures in space, bunched and twisted, and Estan let out a final scream as he was literally ripped apart.

I vaulted the display cases and fled through the library, hearing the wet sounds of torn flesh thudding to the ground behind me. What sort of monster had I trapped myself in here with?

***

I fled through displays as varied as I could possibly imagine. One room contained a large aquarium filled with colourful coral and fish. Were their patterns supposed to represent knowledge? Was this simply a display of the environment of some strange locale known to the Ashkanians? Did the movements of the shoals correspond to a universal truth about how to reach the 4th tier in a couple of seconds? Because if not, it was entirely useless to me.

Varice thundered after me, her steps light but her presence anything but. She hung in my mana sense like a star. Blazing. Burning. A beacon of power that signalled my end rather than my salvation.

Her powers were less direct than I’d feared—she had a support class and was clearly optimised for spy work and information gathering rather than pure combat. It was the only reason I hadn’t been caught so far. But she was still at least a peak 2nd tier, and her capacity for violence wasn’t meagre—Estan’s shredded body could attest to that. Well, the man couldn’t attest to anything anymore, but that was precisely the point.

I slipped into another open room, angling ever rightward with every room I shot through, hoping to reach the wall of the great cavern. As if that would help. I was out of ideas, but movement was good. Movement was freedom—it meant I was still breathing, and every second that was true was a victory.

Another display case in this room; scrolls with dark ink detailing a strange, hieroglyphic language. In the final case, a sword. Massive—far larger than any practical weapon I’d seen, it even put Vera’s executioner’s blade to shame—and gleaming with such a silver shine it looked like moonlight was perpetually kissing its surface. Along that same surface, in golden filigree, was carved more of the same hieroglyphs.

On a whim, I smashed a shield-clad fist into the display case and snatched the sword in one hand. Couldn’t wield it well with the shield-tip extending just beyond my clenched fist, but I could carry things without issue. My telekinetic bond with my weapons was growing, and it took but a moment of focus to create that connection with this new blade before it was bobbing along beside me. My control was still delicate—nothing close to the ability to wield it in a fight—but I could at least move the weapon around, call it to my fist or throw it far away. On a somewhat wobbly arc, it had to be said, but progress was progress.

As I ducked around the edge of the room and into the next, I pushed the weapon against the wall at roughly neck height and willed it to remain there, spending a small portion of my mana to imbue it with the necessary rigidity. Perhaps I could catch her by surprise?

I couldn’t. I heard a raven cawing in warning and the blade go spinning into the wall or floor with a musical sound. Followed by Varice’s laughter.

“I will admit,” she said, slightly out of breath but not sounding much perturbed. That alone was enough to perturb me, honestly. “You’re a canny bastard. But you can’t run forever. Eventually I’ll catch up, and then you’ll die just as Estan did. Why make it harder? If you face me now, you might even win. No such chance once you’re exhausted…”

What a convincing argument—too bad it was coming from my enemy. She’d obviously spent too much time dealing with an idiot like Estan if she thought I’d listen to my opponent in a battle. I continued on, lips pulling back in a snarl as I leapt over a few rows of books and careened into the next diamond-shaped room. She wasn’t entirely wrong—I would have to face her soon—but I’d give myself every advantage I could before I did so.

Just as I could escape through these semi-enclosed rooms, so could she. There would be no point risking my life to land a debilitating blow if she could just flee to recover her strength immediately afterwards. I needed to pick my battleground carefully and make sure to use my environment to its fullest.

It was simply unfortunate that Tsanderos did not seem keen on accommodating me. As I crossed into the next area, preparing to leap over or dash around whatever obstacle was waiting in the centre of the diamond room, I found myself instead snared in a world of blood and snow. White and red blurred my vision, and something covered my head, grasping at my mouth and nose and threatening panic.

Stumbling, I tore the thing off my head and realised that my mind, in its frantic whirling, had conjured drama from nothing. The room was just like the others; a simple diamond of nearly enclosed walls with a cultural display in the centre. This one held what looked like criss-crossed washing lines holding up sheets of cloth. They were the colour of fresh snow and had flowing stitching across each one in varied patterns. Not language per se, for I recognised no characters. But there was clearly something being communicated by the tapestries, and it felt familiar.

In my haste I had rounded the corner and careened face first into one of the hanging sheets, but there was little time to curse myself for the clumsy error. Varice was hot on my heels. I scuttled beneath the next row of cloth, jumping onto the obsidian dais off to one side that contained information about the display. It flashed the name of the exhibit—‘Language of a lost people; the keys to the past’—but I paid the glowing script no mind. Tucking my legs up beneath me and hanging off the structure with one hand, I used A Frozen Pyrre to draw my hatchet and send it skittering along the floor by the room’s exit.

When Varice rounded the corner, she was greeted by a seemingly empty room, white sheets fluttering in the breeze of my passage, and a single one crumpled on the floor. She heard the clatter of my hatchet on the other side and continued her pursuit, sparing no glance to either side as she ducked beneath the waving tapestries of a culture long ago lost.

Her mistake. I acted, and the room echoed with the sound of pain. Three distinct screams tore from Varice’s throat: the first, when Resolution’s tip punctured her thigh, the second, when Shatter Point activated and broke her femur, and the third, when the Heart of Winter pulsed with mana, freezing flesh and blood alike.

Varice collapsed, skidding across the dark floor as her leg gave out. I leapt from my perch, aiming to end her before the fight even began, but she was not a simple bureaucrat—no quill-scribbler or petty scribe. She might not be specialised for combat, but she clearly had experience in the field, for she was rolling to her feet even as I descended upon her.

A ball of blinding purple feathers shot out at my face, and in the heartbeat it took me to raise my shield and deflect the phantom raven, she had vanished. I circled, wary. Spear held close, shield even closer; like a bristling porcupine with only one remaining quill. My eyes darted around, the fluttering curtains hanging from the lines above catching my attention and setting my heart to stuttering with every movement. Where was she?

An acid green glow lit the room behind me, and I turned in time to see the air split apart. Varice leaned against a doorframe, an archaic stone room visible behind her. The rest of the vault’s library spread out to either side; all dancing cloth and smooth obsidian, but she was standing within a portal to another world. No, not a world—a new liminal space. I could see bookshelves lining the walls, a rickety wooden desk, and a winding stone staircase ascending above.

Predominant in the doorframe though was the spymaster. Her pale face was twisted into a grimace, one hand clutching at her ruined left leg. Her eyes were bright though, blazing with anger and widened by pain.

And in her other hand, she clutched a small crossbow. Loaded. Pointed at my chest.

Shit.
