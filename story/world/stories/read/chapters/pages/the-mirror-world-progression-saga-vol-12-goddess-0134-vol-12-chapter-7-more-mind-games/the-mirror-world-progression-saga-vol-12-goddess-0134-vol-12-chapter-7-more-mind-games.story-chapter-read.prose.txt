Eltridge ended up not being fun at all very quickly. It was, in fact, a horror show.

From the first engagement with the defenders, who appeared to be improved, smarter, faster zombies, it was found that they were not zombies at all. Samantha raised the alarm immediately when she felt souls within them. Pivoting in their strategy, they quickly captured a few and found signs of extensive mind control so severe that it had reflections in the body. Certainly, they felt no physical pain. There weren’t many experts in the field to help with this, sadly.

Samantha tried unraveling it herself, and immediately, the soul of the person just shredded apart without recourse, with shocking finality and a quickly decaying body. Worse, she got a bundle of impressions in that instant, that of terror and agony unleashed from a binding — an echo of what they must’ve felt being made like that. When they died and passed on with a bare flash of cognizance for what they’d become, it was a relief, a mercy. They wanted to be free of it.

Perhaps what would’ve happened to me a time or two.

As horrifying as it was, Samantha calmly gave the order to stop capturing them, at least for the majority. They’d do so for a portion, to see if they could counteract it over a longer period, but she understood what the enemy had done here: make better soldiers out of conscripts, and now without the necessity of necromancer controllers. There was no ‘hive mind’ here. They were just contorted and twisted souls, barely stable enough to remain. Any manipulation strong enough in the spirit unwound it immediately.

The biggest motive of all this was glaringly obvious: they were made specifically not to turn against their masters. There was no way for the alliance to recruit them.

I’ll tell myself our necromantic efforts didn’t cause this development.

Feeling a bit sick inside, Samantha nonetheless made it her business to execute these tortured souls. With each she felt pass on in relief from despair, her anger, her desire to rip apart who was responsible, grew and grew.

She was fairly certain who it was. Who else but the Sage of Truth was left, of their psychics and mind mages? He had always been the most powerful. Now he was their enemy’s last recourse.

This was a coward’s idea, and he’s been a coward throughout the war, never risking himself. I guess he’ll be a coward to his last, probably hiding deep in their inner sanctums for the war, constantly fleeing. Perhaps he’ll attempt to ambush me in there, after the rest is over? Or will he run away? Far away, as many must have, or perhaps simply to his master, to beg for protection. Beg him to finally save them.

For her role in Eltridge, Samantha did exactly what was prescribed from the beginning: she blew stuff up. But it was very hard to restrain herself from her full powers, in this case. She probably went a little overboard with Seal of Devastation, but could the enemy even tell the difference? At least she refrained from her newer things.

Eltridge ultimately fell, as it was destined to from the beginning. However, the casualties were much higher than they’d experienced in a long while for a large battle. The mind-zombies staged better ambushes and tended to frenzy when pressed, rather than go continuously even-keeled like other enemies.

They put their soul into it, as it were… perhaps literally.

This was just a test run. Guinea pigs. I’d bet they consider it successful. Wonderful news for the innocent people who will suffer. Damn it. Damn them!

After the battle was decided, the majority of the city was a smoking ruin, and reconstruction efforts were begun. In some cases, they found whole buildings with enchantment preservations. After copiously confirming it was safe, the town’s old commercial quarter was occupied, for whatever reason, left almost abandoned and untouched. Samantha herself analyzed the area before approving it. It was barren of traps, and fighting had left it almost pristine.

Inside a prominent inn’s cabinet, otherwise barren of products, a single white square of cloth lying flat was found.

Interesting. A clandestine white flag from the town leadership, perhaps? With plausible deniability, I’m sure.

There were very rich, semi-independent interests in the old city. Mercantile nobility, more or less. The buildings themselves were ancient and priceless.

Perhaps someone pulled strings to leave it, thinking of a future here, whoever wins? Yeesh. Finally, some reasonable attitudes discovered amongst these fuckheads. Fine by me. I’ll honor such preservation. I never wanted to destroy anything north of Matrazere, aside from the Sage’s twisted ‘unscape’ or whatever it is. I’m glad someone at least considered the possibility.

No evidence of ‘experimental labs’ or the like had been found; the zombified people apparently brought from elsewhere, likely the Capital itself, not far north. Not only that, they’d almost certainly been brought long after the city had been evacuated.

Deniability. If they plan armies of them, though, wouldn’t they know about it already in the Capital?

When she put the question to the Brain Trust, Dax had a response ready. “They have hundreds of thousands of soldiers outside the city. The Dominion is really good at rigidly adhering to set patterns of behavior, with ritual discipline. They could easily have troops segmented away from each other, and the culture is not to ask questions. There’s a saying in the Capital: ‘Question what’s going on and the question is put to you.’ In other words, keep your mouth shut or expect a knock on the door from the Inquisitors. Considering what they’re facing, the soldiers might be fine with ‘advanced zombies,’ so to speak, being sent in the vanguard in front of them.”

Samantha scoffed. “You mean, if they won’t be turned into them as well?”

“Right. I don’t think this will work like Matrazere with a grand spell over everyone. There will be mixed troops, including plain old humans, albeit with artificial buffs to morale. Matrazere’s people were still expendable in the grand scheme. The rest of their entire army isn’t viewed that way, not here. They can’t survive without them afterward, not in the way they’ve always known here, having their brand of control over the populace. That’s my assessment, anyway.”

“Mmn. Makes sense.”

“I have another question inspired by this that you won’t like.”

“Hit me.”

“Will you consider using the ‘rip everyone’s souls out’ maneuver, if you can just target these types? I wouldn’t even bring it up, but I know you’ll think of this eventually.”

“Damn. Yes, and… I can likely tell the difference, if you were wondering. Not absolutely, but…” She trailed off, wondering where Dax was going with it.

“That’s what I was afraid of. Perhaps they’re baiting you. They may have debuted this just so you see.”

“Shit. But what if they know that I know that they’re baiting me? Perhaps it's simply a bluff to dissuade me.”

“Or a double-bluff, to reduce the chances of you targeting the whole army. In other words, you decide to do it after this convoluted thinking because you tend toward bold actions.”

Ash chimed in here. “Such convoluted thinking is sadly not far-fetched for my former masters. It could’ve been done for no other reason than to tangle up your strategies.”

Samantha sighed. “Yeah. They must think they got me soooo figured out, huh? Well, I was actually thinking we wouldn’t need it anyway.”

Dax huffed. “I thought it was a tool in my toolbox?”

“Do you need it?”

“Probably not. But if things change, it could be useful.”

“So, what, I’m reserving both uses of Channel Domain?”

“We have plenty of things to unleash. We might as well. The only exception is if we think this will end up taking a lot longer than planned. If you use that while your crown is activated, the cooldown is only four hours. Not relevant in most battles, but if we go for multiple engagements, it could be.”

“Fair point.”

The conversation died naturally at that point. They continued mopping up in Eltridge, triggering remaining traps and proceeding methodically to avoid problematic ambushes that might still be waiting. In any case, they had no full casualties, only injuries here and there. Samantha double and triple-checked everything she could think of as potential plots after they set a garrison up, but she found nothing.

Still paranoid, she ordered minimal living agents inside the walls and to have a separate camp. This was a fairly normal procedure, but Eltridge was a large city and had locations worth occupying. Regardless of how annoying it would be, she hedged their bets for safety and moved on.

At least they’d finally taken the place. She connected with Ash mentally and asked, “Are you looking? Does it feel good on some level?”

Ash sent a hesitant nod. “A little. I can’t say I care much about revenge now, if I ever did. I just wanted out. You already accomplished that back then. This city is just… the end of one road, and the beginning of another. I’m glad it wasn’t fully destroyed, in the end. Hypothetically speaking, there is much to rebuild.”

“So, not super special, but a little special. What about the Capital?”

“That I look forward to conquering. Not for revenge, per se. It’s more that it is time for it to fall. Time for it to be remade on better foundations. I know the ideal is probably impossible, but one step up is one step.”

“Indeed, it is. We don’t have many more to make, Ash.”

“I’m glad I’m making them with you, Mother.”

Samantha smiled. “Me too.”
