Chapter II.XX (2.20)

This time Mitsuko decided to battle the troll from afar. From the bluff next to the crumbling bridge, Mitisuko hurled stones while the troll exchanged the attacks with projectiles of his own. Occasionally Mitsuko sent them back at him with a cast of Mend, but mostly she used it as an opportunity to practice dodging and aiming with Deteriorated projectiles.

“Toll!” the troll bellowed. As he screamed, two stones embedded themselves into his flesh. One in his gut and the other in his throat. The troll snatched up the rotting carcass of a bitelas and hurled it in Mitsuko’s direction. The corpse split into pieces as it slammed into the stone beside her, splattering Mitsuko in rotted gore. The smell hit her stronger than any physical blow. And worse, it wasn’t damage she could Mend her body of. Gagging, she Mended the dead body and let the magic drag the dead bitelas back into the muck below.

Another stone to the neck and the troll buckled over, clawing at holes in his loose skin. Dark blood oozed out.

Mitsuko pulled on the troll’s clothes with a quick cast of Deteriorate, this time careful as to not rip them off the troll. He stumbled at the sudden pull. Now off balance, when the next stone embedded into his neck, the troll fell to the muddy ground.

A flick of her wrist and her blade appeared in her hand. Flipping it into reverse grip, she threw herself off the cliff and cast Deteriorate on the blade. Her descent lasted a fraction of a second. With a swift blur, Mitsuko’s sword thrust deep into the back of the troll’s neck. Her legs also broke as they impacted his back, but a quick cast of Mend solved that issue. Her feet had landed with enough force to break through the skin. Exposed muscles and shattered ribs spewed out at her heels.

“Toll,” he gurgled. A few death throws and jerky motions were all the troll had left in him before he succumbed to the release of death.

“I wonder if the souls of people can exit the barrier into the outside world,” Mitsuko mused as she stepped away from the newly made corpse. “Are they trapped in time as well? Or do they deny the Prismatic Spiral’s power like how the sun is commanded by a Titan? But wouldn’t that create multiple copies with every loop? What happens if a necromancer attempts to speak to the dead?”

An interesting line of thinking, but not one she deemed worthy of a question from the sages. While she was currently in possession of a small wealth of questions, Mitsuko was under no illusion that they could all disappear with just a twenty minute conversation. The issue of where souls went after death while trapped in the Prismatic Spiral was interesting, but not something she thought likely to be relevant to her success.

A few hours later, Mitsuko was back on The Selcouth Sable, practicing Retrospection down in the cargo hold.

In her last loop she’d managed to cover a good chunk of the cargo, but a lot remained to examine the history of. And since this trip was expected to be shorter, one way or another, she wanted to get back to work on her training as quickly as possible. She climbed into the center of the cargo and managed to shove aside enough barrels and crates to make a little nook for herself.

“What are you doing down here?” Familiar purple hair appeared on the other side of a barrel. “You looting the cargo? You know, you could probably just ask the captain for anything down here. He’s equal parts in awe of you and terrified of you. You could request his first born and he might sign it over to you.”

“I’m training my Retrospection spell,” Mitsuko explained. She pressed her shoulder against a crate and shoved, creating enough of a gap for Holly to slip into her little space.

“That one lets you see into the past history of an object, right?” Holly sat down cross-legged beside her and snatched up a trinket. A small fabric doll with strands of twine for hair.

“Yes. I can see recent events that have occurred,” Mitsuko said. “Actually… do you want to give it a try?”

“What do you mean?” Then Holly’s face lit up. “Wait! In a past loop did you discover I have some measure of affinity for temporal spells? Can you teach me?”

“Sorry, not quite. I unlocked the ability Retrospection Others recently and haven’t yet tried it out.”

“Close enough!” Holly raised the doll in her hand. “Hit me! Let me gaze into the past!”

Mitsuko raised her hand in Holly’s direction and did as her friend requested. She cast the spell. Holly’s face froze in place. Seconds passed. The gnome blinked and shook her head.

“Woah! You didn’t get me and the doll at all!”

“What?”

“You hit my shoes, Mitsuko! I just saw the history of my little feet walking about in my pair of shoes for the last month. It really puts into perspective just how much we all walk around. Poor shoes have been through a lot in just the last month. Such a fascinating spell! It reminds me of a divination ritual I learned back as a student. But that required a lot more effort than a simple wave of a hand. We needed complex ritual circles and a ton of our blood to power it.”

Mitsuko eyed the worn leather shoes tied to her friend’s feet. Why had her spell targeted that instead of the doll in her hand? After a minute of consideration, Mitsuko settled on a hypothesis.

“That’s the object on you with the most vibrant history over the last month or so,” she concluded.

“You think I saw what I did because my shoes are the most exciting thing on me?” Holly considered that and looked over herself.

Mitsuko shrugged. “You switch out your clothes daily. The doll’s been gathering dust in a crate. Nothing else on you has been as active as your shoes. They have the most story to tell.”

“Does that usually happen when you cast the spell?” Holly asked.

“No. But I think that’s because I have more direct control over my willpower when it’s me that’s receiving the information. Let me try again with more focus and intent.”

Once again, Holly’s facial expressions froze for a few seconds. Then her jaw worked a bit. Awareness snapped to her and she dropped the doll with a yelp.

“What did you see?” Mitsuko asked, curious by the response.

“This doll is…” Holly shivered. “It has some unpleasant memories.”

Mitsuko knelt down and picked it up. “Cursed?”

“Not quite. Take a look for yourself.”

Mitsuko turned the cloth doll over in her hands. A spike of worry pierced her heart for a moment as she thought about what sort of awful things the doll’s previous owner might have suffered through. She was so tired of watching children suffer.

Then again, pushing herself with the spell in interesting ways would mean gains that could help her protect. What did the past matter if she witnessed something horrific when it could serve as a foothold for a better future?

She cast Retrospection.

At first there was a quick replay of the events that had played out between her and Holly. Then into the crate the doll went. Consumed by darkness for weeks. Maybe even a month. There was shuffling and the crate opened. Instead of the child Mitsuko expected, there stood an old crone of a woman with greasy hair that fell over her hunched shoulders in strands. Her face was marred by warts. She smiled at the doll and her tongue flicked over her teeth like a lizard.

“Hello, girl. You’ve finally found my little message. Very good. I’ve had an eye on you for many years now. I even kept your rib. Time to finally pay your tax on my time. A toll if you will.

“First. You must fix my acquaintance over there. He’s struggled enough, I daresay. Poor lout. I’d do it myself, though I can’t be bothered. Don’t like all the salt in the sea. And that thing on those islands doesn’t like me much either. We might tussle.” The old woman chortled and cackled.

“Next, I’ve sent an old friend’s daughter to act as your guide. Go give those geezers what they want. Deliver this doll to them. It should be enough to gain their friendship. And payment enough to act as a guide to your missing temple.”

“Finally, if nothing else, remember this. For its sins, the entity your sages siphon deserves harsh justice. Do not listen to its lies or sweet kiss. Resist and look deep into its iris’ abyss. Break, shatter, destroy and it will be endless. Of this, I promise.”

More cackling and the old witch carried off the doll.

Mitsuko’s awareness snapped back to the modern world. The doll fell from her shaking hands and into her lap.

“That was—that was,” she whispered.

“A witch,” Holly confirmed. “She commanded me to hand the doll over to you.”

“What?” Mitsuko’s head whirled over her friend. They met eyes and Mitsuko’s mouth suddenly went very dry. A shiver ran down her spine. “I didn’t see that. In fact… I shouldn’t have been able to understand her at all. When I used the spell in the past, all the words were backwards. I couldn’t comprehend what was being said.”

The witch’s cackle echoed in her ears.

Mitsuko pushed the doll away with a foot. Its button eyes glinted in the lamplight.
