Anya choked on her coffee, feeling it threaten to drip down her chin as she stared at Fay in shock. She could tell Seb wasn't any better off, coughing as he tried to clear his throat.

"Fay!" she said the moment she'd swallowed.

"Yes?" Fay answered, her tone clearly trying for innocence despite the grin she couldn't hide.

"We have not spent the last six months trying to replace Fay with a fae, or two fae, or any number of fae."

"Half of what you told me yesterday was about them. We spent the afternoon with two of them and you had them promise they wouldn't interrupt today."

"We're classmates," Anya said. "We train with them to practice dueling, to learn how to handle other fighting styles."

"They walk into your room as though they're there every day."

"That's not-" Anya began before deliberately taking another sip of her coffee.

"I'm Fay, they're fae," Fay said as though it made everything obvious.

"Fay," Seb said with the slightest sigh. "Once was funny."

"I know."

Anya grabbed a piece of bacon off the plate between them before making sure no one was staring. They'd chosen a table in the corner of the dining hall for privacy, but she hadn't planned on Fay managing to make them both choke on their drinks with a comment that had been perfectly-

"You were waiting for the right moment to say that."

"Yes," Fay admitted. "The parallel was too perfect to miss, especially if I got you both."

"To think I missed you," Anya said as she shook her head with a smile.

"It's what sisters are for."

"It's what I would have expected from Sandra."

"Except she's not Fay."

"Please," Seb said, his cup hovering at his lips. "Stop."

Anya saw the instant Fay gave up. First the grin started to twitch and then fully dissolved into laughter. Seb was only moments behind her and Anya joined in as soon as her mug touched the table.

You have to admit it- Seb sent.

No. I don't have to.

Then why are you laughing?

I'm- Because- Fine!

She knew Seb was laughing at her. By this point she was probably laughing at herself as much as at Fay's pun. She hadn't expected it, she hadn't thought of it, but the moment Fay had said the words she came up with made her realize just how accurate it was, even if it wasn't true.

Six months had been too long. Much too long. They all knew it and they all needed this. The laughter, the time where nothing mattered beyond the silly words they got to share.

"Thank you," she said when they all finally calmed down.

"For?" Fay asked.

"Being Fay."

She felt as much as heard Seb groan.

Hush, she sent.

Fay didn't answer, her smile telling Anya everything she needed to know about what the words meant to her. Telling her she'd needed to hear that she wasn't being replaced, that she was still family.

"Are you jealous?" she asked.

"What? No. Of course not," Fay answered.

"Fay?"

"Just, you're here and I'm at the Holding. You're making friends, spending time with Gwen and Cara. They aren't part of the Holding, they aren't like Alex or Sandra or Briana or Yshe or-"

"Fay," Anya said softly. "They aren't you."

"But…"

"You're still our sister," Seb said. "We're here to learn, we trust you to make sure nothing goes wrong while we do."

"I know. And I know you're not replacing me. I know they're just new people, new friends, new family members I guess?"

"They are. At least I think of them that way," Anya said. "They're different. But they're… family feels right. Not close though."

"They are," Fay said. "Valene suspected your father's ancestry when you first met. He wasn't sure, he told me just in case."

"Wait," Anya said. "You knew all along and didn't-"

"Anya," Fay said quickly. "I couldn't. You don't talk about the Fae until someone has exposure to them and is aware. You've seen how words matter with them?"

"Yes," Seb said. "Strange Energy pulses when they use full names. Or mention certain topics."

"Exactly. Names matter. Use the wrong, or maybe right, word and certain fae will become aware of you. They'll know who you are because the words you used mattered."

"So you knew but…" Anya said.

"I wasn't sure. I mean I suspected. Valene suspected. But it didn't matter, there wasn't any evidence."

"Gwen?"

"Yes, but more what you said about Cara," Fay answered. "The lifesworn geas is something we, that is Human Seers, know enough about to know that her reaction to you is more than enough proof for me."

"Then why won't they?" Anya said even as she felt Seb figuring something out.

"Politics," he said.

"Politics," Fay agreed.

"But I'm only part fae. I'm not someone important to them."

"Your father might have been," Fay said.

"Who was he then?"

"I don't know," Fay admitted. "I don't think Valene does either."

That didn't help. She didn't care about whatever her fae heritage was, what it made her in that family. She had her Holding, she had her family, she had Seb. Why did… Well she knew why. She'd grown up with Court politics. She'd been taught what they meant, even if at a smaller scale than anything Gwen had ever hinted at.

"It doesn't matter," Anya said. "I won't let it matter. Not unless they can prove to me it needs to matter."

"Bern is going to make it matter," Seb said. "He insists Anya isn't family. He snaps every time he hears Gwen mention it."

"That's his problem, not mine."

"For now," Fay agreed. "And I'll try to see what I can find. Even if I have to get Valene to give me more access."

"Fay…" Anya said.

"No. You're family."

"Thank you."

"Were the beds in the tents comfortable?" Seb asked, his tone deliberately calm.

Seb!

I want to know. And it's not as though we were going to solve anything more today.

"Yes," Fay said. "Not the way a mattress is, not the way even your couch was. But they were comfortable, they were home."

"You hadn't ever mentioned home," Anya said.

"I know. I told you I left. I told you about how some of the culture works after my birthday dinner. But…"

"Do you want to?" Seb asked.

"I want Bri to know too."

"Bring her next visit?" Anya suggested.

"If she wants to come."

"Maybe she can figure out how to make the coffee machine in our rooms make good coffee," Anya said.

Hey!

"Doesn't seem to matter, Anya still burns the coffee."

I do not. Not on purpose.

I didn't say on purpose love. Coffee making just doesn't like you.

The coffee just never came out right, the water somehow too hot, yet no hotter, or the grounds tasting burnt when they were the exact same batch as Seb had just used without difficulty.
