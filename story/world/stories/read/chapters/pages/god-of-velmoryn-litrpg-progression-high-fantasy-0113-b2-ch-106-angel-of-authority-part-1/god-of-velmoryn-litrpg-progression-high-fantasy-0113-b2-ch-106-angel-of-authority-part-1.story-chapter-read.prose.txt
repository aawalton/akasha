The creature was enormous. Not larger than Aurelion, but still at least twice the size of an average human. Its body was unnaturally lean, pale skin stretched so tightly over its bones that it looked as though there was barely any muscle beneath it at all.

A tattered dark robe hung from its frame, open down the middle and doing little to conceal its breasts or the gaping hole where its abdomen should have been.

It had no head.

Or rather, its head was not attached where one should have been.

Instead, the creature carried four different heads in its four arms, each with long, greasy black hair and a face hidden beneath a piece of lace cloth. A single letter had been embroidered onto every veil with crimson thread.

That’s English…

Recognizing the letters caught me off guard.

I already knew Athena’s believer spoke English, so it was reasonable to assume that many other humans did as well, but this was the first time I had actually seen English letters anywhere in this world.

I need to know what kind of creature this is.

Guidance.

[An Angel of Justice]

An Angel born from the Authority of Justice. Its Purpose and Rank cannot be revealed and must be discovered through observation and interaction.

I was still reading the information Guidance had given me when Aurelion charged.

With a powerful flap of his wings, he shot forward and crossed the distance almost instantly, but just as he came within striking range, something stopped him.

An invisible force.

It was as though a barrier separated him from Noah’s angel, except neither Aurelion nor I could see or perceive it.

Aurelion retreated immediately, caution replacing the confidence he had shown only moments earlier.

In response, Noah’s angel finally moved.

It raised one of the arms carrying a head whose veil had the letter “J” embroidered across it.

“Must… Set… The… Rules… First…” the head rasped.

“Rules… Are… Absolute…” another whispered, this one marked with the letter “A”.

Aurelion hesitated.

His glaive might have been capable of cutting through almost anything, but cutting something he could neither see nor perceive was an entirely different problem. For a moment, he simply stood there, then straightened and lowered his weapon slightly.

“A duel to the death,” he said.

WHY?

I barely stopped myself from shouting at him.

Why did he always have to take everything to the absolute extreme?

His objective had already been achieved. The portals were destroyed, one of the creatures of darkness had been killed, and Noah had not opened any new gateways since Aurelion’s attack. It was entirely possible that he had already reached whatever limit was preventing him from simply flooding the place with more portals.

So why did Aurelion have to challenge an angel we knew absolutely nothing about?

Unlike this creature, the only true surprise Aurelion possessed was his glaive, and unless Noah was genuinely an idiot, he must have already realized that spells, barriers, or any tangible protection that could be attacked would not stop it.

Every advantage now belonged to Noah’s angel.

It knew what Aurelion could do, while neither my angel nor I had the slightest idea what kind of opponent stood before us.

“I… Accept,” the head marked with “J” rasped.

The moment those words left its mouth, space distorted around both angels.

A sphere of brilliant light formed around them, bright enough to rival the sun, and expanded until it completely enclosed Aurelion and Noah’s angel.

At the same time, a blue Window flashed before me.

[Warning!]

Your angel, Aurelion, has accepted a trial of Authority.

He will be summoned into the dominion manifested by the Authority of Justice and tried according to its laws. If Aurelion passes the trial, the Angel of Justice will suffer the consequences instead.

Trial of Justice: One’s conviction shall be weighed against a given premise.

Any attempt to intervene will render Aurelion unjust and subject him to the consequences of failure.

Fuck! How many fucking times? When will he learn that charging headfirst into danger is nothing but foolish and never ends well?

I was furious. Aurelion’s endless desire to prove himself had once again dragged him into a completely unnecessary risk. I was still in the middle of mentally tearing into him when his voice reached me.

“Worry not for me, my Liege. I will not lose!”

You better not.

I did not dare answer him. Any direct communication might be considered interference, and after that warning, I had no intention of testing how loosely the trial interpreted its rules.

Instead, I turned my attention back to the Window and willed it to show me what was happening inside that strange space.

Nothing happened.

The Window remained fixed on the mortal world, showing the human army led by Noah’s inquisitors as it slowly advanced into the forest. Their progress was painfully slow, mostly because the carts carrying the wyverns were difficult to push through the deep snow.

Their numbers were higher than during the previous attack, but still far lower than I had expected. I had not counted every single person, but Noah seemed to have sent an army of roughly twelve thousand this time. About half were frontliners, or rather, melee combatants; more than a quarter were archers, while the rest were mages and priests. Strangely, this army didn’t have any cavalry either.

However, there was a big problem. Judging by their direction, they were not going anywhere near the place I intended to flood once the dam was broken.

I needed to fix that. Focusing on my connection with Amon, I  whispered directly into his mind.

“You need to lure the army closer to the river, or my plan with the dam will be useless.”

Amon was in the middle of a strategy meeting with Mirion, Dariel, Tekla, Lyle, and Othrien. The moment he heard me, he raised one hand and silenced the others.

“Alright, I’ll pass the order. I have already sent several groups ahead. We will exhaust them with guerrilla attacks, and by the time they reach Orrvyn’s wall, I’ll have them completely demoralized,” he whispered back, a faint, wicked smile appearing on his face.

I liked that he was confident. I liked that neither fear nor worry had managed to settle into him before the battle had even begun.

But he looked a little too relaxed.

That, I did not like.

“Do not become overconfident,” I warned him before splitting off a small portion of my consciousness and attaching it to the Window.

That fragment would continue monitoring the army and Eriavel while the rest of my attention remained with Aurelion.

Even if the Window could not show me what was happening inside that strange space, I could still observe through my angel’s senses. The method would severely limit my perception compared to the wide view the Window normally offered, but it was better than nothing. Besides, there was no chance I was going to stand back and ignore Aurelion while he was probably facing the greatest challenge of his life.

I shut down my own senses and focused entirely on the connection between us.

Finding it came as naturally as breathing. Well, as naturally as breathing came to mortals.

The thread tethering Aurelion to me was brighter and stronger than any other. I followed it carefully, allowing my awareness to sink into my angel’s senses.

This was one of the most unpleasant sensations I was capable of experiencing as a god.

Whenever I silenced my own awareness and tried to see, hear, and feel through someone else, it felt disturbingly close to losing control of myself, as though my own existence had been pushed somewhere distant while I remained trapped inside another person’s dream.

A lucid dream. But a deeply uncomfortable one.

Aurelion was in a space that reminded me of my own realm, except there were no gray clouds or crimson mists stretching into the distance, only an endless darkness that seemed to swallow everything around him.

He stood before Noah’s angel with his glaive embedded in the nonexistent ground beside him, thick chains wrapped around the weapon as though it had been sealed in place.

By the time my senses aligned with Aurelion’s, he was already listening to one of the heads, unable to move so much as a finger. His entire body felt impossibly heavy, as though some immense, invisible force was pressing down on him from every direction and pinning him where he stood.

Aurelion glanced toward his glaive and willed it to return to him.

Nothing happened.

He tried moving his fingers instead, believing that if he could understand how the restriction worked, if he could break even one of the invisible shackles binding his body, then perhaps the rest would follow.

“You… Cannot… Free… Yourself,” the head marked with the letter J rasped.

“He does not respect justice! He is innately unjust! We should punish him. We should punish him with death!” another head shrieked, this one marked with the letter E.

Its screams were quickly silenced when the head bearing the letter P spoke.

“Where was I? Ah, yes. You used divine power against mortals, creating a gross imbalance of force that under no circumstances can be considered a just action.”

Unlike the others, this head spoke with the clear, composed voice of an ordinary man, each word delivered in a flat, emotionless tone.

“According to the absolute rules established by the Pantheon, a divine being may not kill a mortal belonging to another deity unless its own life is under threat. Your life was clearly in no danger, and therefore your actions can only be considered criminal and must be punished according to the law.”

“DEATH TO THE CRIMINAL!” the head marked with E screamed.

The pressure bearing down on Aurelion immediately intensified. Before, it had merely prevented him from moving, but now it became strong enough to hurt, crushing against his body as though the darkness itself were trying to force him to his knees.

“Stop… It!” the head marked with J rasped.

The pressure immediately eased, returning to its previous weight.

“The… Ruling… Cannot… Be… One-sided…”

The head, dangling from the angel’s hand by its long, greasy black hair, shifted as it turned toward Aurelion.

“Speak… Accused.”
