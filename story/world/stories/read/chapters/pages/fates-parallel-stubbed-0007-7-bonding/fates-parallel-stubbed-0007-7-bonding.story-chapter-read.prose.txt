She trudges across a snowy field, lost and afraid. Her tracks disappear in the snow behind her. Eventually, she sees a light in the distance. Following it, she finds a traveller sitting at a campfire, who offers to share the warmth of his fire. She reaches out and sees the blood on her hands—the traveller is dead, their corpse staining the snow red with blood. She turns and flees, leaving the snow red in her wake.

No matter where she flees, she always arrives back at the camp. More death, more blood, until finally the entire field of snow is red. Once again, she is lost. Once again, she sees a light in the distance. A cat with pure white fur stands out against the bloody field—has it always been there, invisible against the snow? She hesitates...then reaches out.

Lee Jia woke with a start. Her head was throbbing painfully and her throat was completely parched. She sat up and slowly took stock of the unfamiliar room, quickly realizing it was the bedroom. The other bed was empty—apparently An Eui was already up. As Lee Jia trundled blearily towards the door she realized it wasn’t just her head that hurt. Her entire body was sore from yesterday’s excruciating workout. Once again she dreaded the fact that she would have to do it again. Tomorrow? It felt too soon.

Entering the main living area, she soon spotted her roommate. An Eui sat on one of the couches with her knees pulled up to her chest. The posture reminded Lee Jia of their first encounter. Lee Jia’s stomach growled fiercely when she smelled the rice cooking on the stove. An Eui looked up at her and scowled.

“Go take a shower. I can smell you from here.”

“Shower?”

Lee Jia hadn’t heard the word used to describe anything but rain before. An Eui gave her a flat look and pointed to the bathroom.

“There’s a formation on the ceiling in there, you’ll figure it out. Go bathe.”

Why didn’t she just say that in the first place? Lee Jia shrugged and did as she was told. The stone floor of the bathroom was already wet, presumably from An Eui using it earlier. Sure enough, Lee Jia’s mana sense detected a formation on the ceiling that she hadn’t noticed before. Lee Jia tried pushing mana into it as she had with the stove and the formation activated, creating a small shower of rain contained within the room. Lee Jia was immediately soaked—including her uniform which she hadn’t thought to remove yet. She turned the water off in a panic, but it was too late.

“Agh! Damnit, An Eui you could have warned me!”

Lee Jia heard her roommate’s cackling laughter from the other room as she tried to wring the water out of her robes. When her clothes were as dry as she could get them, she hung them out of the way of the shower. Now properly disrobed, Lee Jia reactivated the formation and let the water wash away the dirt, sweat, and grime of the previous day’s gruelling training session. Curiously, Lee Jia tried tasting the water—it wasn’t very good, but she’d had worse and it relieved both her thirst and the headache.

Lee Jia’s mind wandered as she bathed. She recalled the strange dream from last night. It had felt like she was looking through someone else’s eyes, like the fragmented vision she’d had when she awakened with An Eui. Were they related? Did An Eui have a similar dream? It occurred to Lee Jia that they really should talk about it, but she wasn’t sure how to broach the subject. An Eui was prickly at the best of times and it was hard to tell what would set her off.

The soothing effect of the cool water on Lee Jia’s tired muscles reminded her of the training from the previous day. She shuddered when she recalled what had happened when she ran out of mana. Had she almost died? What would have happened if Ienaga hadn’t—Lee Jia blushed and touched her lips. That had been her first kiss—no, it was just first aid, right? And Ienaga was a woman. Surely it didn’t count! Lee Jia chose to believe that.

Finished bathing, Lee Jia disabled the formation and shook the water from her hair, ears, and tail before wiping the rest from her skin as much as she could. She was surprised to find that her uniform was already entirely dry. For that matter, it didn’t seem to have picked up any of the sweat or dirt from her training. Looking closely with her mana sense, Lee Jia saw complex formations woven into the fabric. Lee Jia was impressed and bewildered by the garment. In her old life, an artifact like this would set her up for life if she could sell it—here, it was given freely.

Once dressed, Lee Jia returned to the living area and sat near the fire to warm up. The rice had finished cooking, and she served herself a heaping portion. She hadn’t eaten at all yesterday and she was ravenous. Lee Jia looked askance at An Eui as she ate. For the second time she found herself wondering just what her roommate did with her free time—hunt, kill, survive.

Lee Jia frowned. She had thought the intrusive thoughts had stopped, but that definitely hadn’t been her. An Eui noticed her staring and glared back.

“What are you looking at!?”

Lee Jia averted her gaze.

“S-sorry! I was just—thinking about something.”

An Eui narrowed her eyes, gazing suspiciously at Lee Jia.

“You’ve been getting them too, haven’t you?”

“What!?”

Lee Jia stared wide-eyed at An Eui. She didn’t need clarification about what her roommate was referring to.

“Memories, right? Dreams? Weird thoughts popping into your head?”

Lee Jia nodded solemnly in response to An Eui’s words, and stared down at her rice bowl—suddenly less hungry than she had been a moment ago. To her surprise, An Eui didn’t seem angry. Rather, her expression looked sad and hurt as she drew her knees closer and buried her face in them.

“I knew it. You’re terrified of me. You probably think I’m a monster.”

She is a monster.

“No! It’s not like that! I just—”

Lee Jia felt guilty. She had been afraid of An Eui—worried that she would slip up and get a knife in the gut. But that had been before she saw the fragmented memories and heard the intrusive thoughts. She felt like she understood An Eui a bit better now, and she wondered if the opposite was true.

“What about you? You saw my memories too, right?”

An Eui sighed and nodded.

“I saw them—the orphanage, Boss Lee—I can’t believe you’re still using that fucker’s name.”

Lee Jia blushed a bit. Her past wasn’t something she liked to dwell on, and clearly An Eui had gotten a more complete picture than Lee Jia had. An Eui continued.

“I thought we were similar at first. Sure you were naive, but I assumed it was just a difference of experience. It’s embarrassing how wrong I was. If anything, you’re more experienced than I am, and you still have that positive outlook. Now I know why—you’re a good person, born into bad circumstances. I’m the opposite.”

An Eui’s expression twisted into a self-deprecating smile even as tears began to well in her eyes.

“I was born into fortune. Everything you ever strived for in life, I had growing up as a matter of course. I shouldn’t have ever wanted for anything, but I’m such a fuckup that I managed to lose everything. Then after a short year of hardship I had the arrogance to look down on someone who’d gone their whole life without any of the things I took for granted!”

An Eui was now weeping openly, and Lee Jia’s body moved ahead of her mind as she swept her roommate up in a hug. An Eui let out an uncharacteristically cute squeak of surprise and weakly tried to push Lee Jia away. Lee Jia only tightened her embrace.

“You’re not a bad person An Eui. A bad person wouldn’t be feeling so much guilt, and regret, and self-loathing. A bad person would be ruthless and unrepentant—would have tried to take advantage of my trust instead of pushing me away!”

An Eui struggled weakly in Lee Jia’s embrace, sobbing quietly as she protested.

“I—I’m not—hic—I don’t—don’t deserve—”

Lee Jia shook her head and held An Eui tighter.

“That’s not true! You were looking out for me, weren’t you? When I chose to go to initiation and attend the lessons, you didn’t join just because you thought I was right. You were there to watch my back. Even in a place swarming with mages, you were ready to risk your life to protect someone you’d just met!”

An Eui shook her head in denial, but stopped struggling as she wept into Lee Jia’s shoulder.

“I know we’ve only known each other for a few days, but I feel like I know you better than anyone I’ve ever met. And not just because our minds got smashed together or whatever.”

An Eui let out a half-chuckle, half-sob and Lee Jia smiled.

“We’re like a pair of mice stuck in a viper’s nest—we really do need to look out for each other. But even if we didn’t, I’d still want to be your friend.”

An Eui giggled lightly and wiped at her eyes with a sleeve.

“Ancestors, Lee Jia, you’re so pushy. Fine! I don’t know what you see in me, but we can be friends, ok? Happy now?”

“Only if you agree to use my given name from now on. My surname is just a borrowed one, anyway.”

An Eui nodded.

“Alright, Jia, same here then—you can just call me Eui from now on. And...thanks—for what you said, even if it’s not true.”

Eui averted her eyes in embarrassment as she muttered her awkward thanks and Jia beamed at her.

“I meant every word of it!”

“Still, isn’t it a bit weird how much free time there is?”

Jia and Eui were chatting on their way to their first magic class with Magus Hwang. Eui nodded in response to Jia’s question.

“Qin basically isn’t teaching us shit, and Hwang’s lessons are only twice a week. Meanwhile Ienaga’s got us running ourselves into the dirt every other day—it’s pretty unbalanced.”

“Do you think it’s just a difference in the instructors or are the disciplines that different to learn?”

“Both maybe? I get the feeling Qin doesn’t want to be here at all, but martial arts training definitely seems to be more intensive than the others. I don’t know what to think about Hwang, yet. I think there’s something weird about him, but I don’t trust mages.”

Jia considered that as they arrived at their destination. It was a utilitarian looking two-storey building with two entrances. Entering the so-called ‘lecture hall’, they saw that the inside was taken up by a single giant room of tiered benches facing a large dais and podium. Staircases on either side of the room allowed access to the higher tiers of seating.

“I don’t think there’s enough room for all the students in here.”

It was hard to imagine hundreds of people squeezing into such a space, as large as it was. An unexpected, but familiar voice answered her.

“It’s not. Magus Hwang split the students into three groups. I attended one of his other classes on the first day, regrettably I missed the second one yesterday, hehe.”

The signature nervous chuckle marked Hyeong Daesung as the speaker, who had just arrived behind the girls. Lee Jia greeted him with a wave.

“Good morning, Dae. Why join multiple classes? Also doesn’t that mean some classes are on Ienaga’s days?”

Lee Jia couldn’t imagine trying to attend a class on the same day as one of Ienaga’s training sessions.

“Indeed it does! I suspect that’s why he runs two per group per week. As for attending multiple classes, hehe. Well Magus Hwang has something of a reputation among the colleges for running his lectures like discussions, and going on wild tangents. No two lectures are the same.”

“Oh.”

It didn’t occur to Lee Jia that Dae would have been attending one of the colleges back home, or that he might already be familiar with the instructor. An Eui, who had been quiet until now, voiced a question that had also been on Lee Jia’s mind.

“If you were already in one of the colleges, why did you come here?”

“Oh! Sorry, hehe. Didn’t see you there. I’m Hyeong Daesung, but most friends just call me Dae. Nice to meet you Miss…?”

“An. You were saying, Hyeong?”

Lee Jia cringed a bit at Eui’s rude treatment of Dae. It was easy to forget just how much her friend disliked mages.

“R-right, hehe. Well, Miss An, the academy is a unique opportunity to study the disciplines and cultures of other countries. My master, Do Hye, was one of the architects behind its creation. For various reasons he was unable to come here himself, so I am here to learn what I can and gather data in his stead! I’m sure the others have similar reasons, but relatively few of the disciples here are from Goryeo like us.”

Lee Jia had been wondering about that.

“Why is that? We’re in Goryeo, right? Shouldn’t there be more half-spirits?”

“Well, yes and no. The land we are on was technically part of Goryeo, but the treaty has redesignated it as an extra-national territory under joint jurisdiction of all three signatory nations. By both population and territory, Goryeo is the smallest nation. We also have far fewer immortal practitioners than the other signatories. The demographic of the disciples here reflects that.”

Lee Jia had no idea what Dae was talking about. At least she understood the part about Goryeo being smaller than Qin and Yamato.

“I see. Well, we’d better get seated before Magus Hwang arrives.”

While Lee Jia had thought to part ways from Dae at that point, she was surprised to find that he joined them as they found a place to sit. Well, she supposed that his company would probably be useful. He had certainly helped quite a bit with Qin’s ‘lesson’, such as it was. In fact, that thought reminded Lee Jia of something.

“By the way Dae, have you made any progress on that transcription of Qin’s lessons? You were right about the jade slips, it’s hard to make sense of all the information at once.”

Dae gave Lee Jia a confused look.

“What do you mean? You should focus on the notes I gave you first. You won’t be able to read the jade slip until you can control...your—h-hold on a minute, hehe.”

Lee Jia watched with interest with her mana sense as Dae’s entire aura seemed to move as though it was part of his body. His aura reached out to touch hers, but her mana simply flowed around his as though it was a rock in a river. Dae’s jaw dropped open in shock.

“Y-you’ve already awakened your mana sense!? You can control your aura? But it’s only been—”

Dae furrowed his brows and began mouthing something silently to himself. Lee Jia tilted her head curiously.

“Is that bad? I thought you said that your notes would be enough to learn it. Did we mess something up?”

Dae scratched his chin thoughtfully.

“Uh, no. I don’t think so, hehe. It’s just fast—I was trying to calculate the difference in mana density under the academy’s gathering formation to the average baseline and compare that to known data on the correlation between mana density and the average speed of—”

“Dae! I have no idea what you’re talking about. Plain language, please.”

Lee Jia interrupted before Dae could start rambling again. Dae looked abashed and scratched the back of his head.

“S-sorry, hehe. I get carried away sometimes. Awakening should have taken a long time—a few weeks, usually. For an extremely talented individual with access to high mana environments and mana stones, at least a few days. To awaken so quickly isn’t just unusual, it’s unheard of!”

“It certainly is!”

All three students jumped in surprise as Magus Hwang Sung spoke up from behind them. Once again he had appeared from out of nowhere. Evading every one of Lee Jia’s senses, including her newfound mana sense. To her surprise, she didn’t see an aura around Magus Hwang at all.

“Good morning Miss Lee, Miss An, Master Hyeong. It pleases me to see you all getting along.”

‘Master’? Just who was Hyeong Daesung that the instructor spoke to him so respectfully?

“Your progress is indeed quite staggering. To think you would awaken before I even had a chance to teach you anything, hoho! I suppose I have Do Hye’s boy to thank for that.”

Dae bowed respectfully to Magus Hwang.

“My apologies, Magus, for overstepping.”

“Oh, not at all, not at all. You’ve done me a service, if anything. Though, I must say, it’s quite a coincidence that two such monumentally talented unawakened would both happen upon tokens of invitation, then just so happen to arrive here with them, and then just so happen to encounter the sole apprentice of Grand Magus Do Hye and receive tutoring on his techniques before I even had a chance to begin their education. Why, if I didn’t know any better, I’d think the old schemer was up to his tricks, eh Master Hyeong?”

Magus Hwang’s smile seemed a bit stiff as he spoke. Dae chuckled nervously and averted his eyes.

“Ehehe, well I wouldn’t know, Magus. Fate can work in mysterious ways, can it not?”

“Yes, and so can your master. Ah well! No matter—whether it’s the machinations of fate or that clever old snake, I’m interested to see how things play out. It was nice speaking to you three, I’ll be beginning the lesson shortly.”

Magus Hwang took his leave and headed down the stairs towards the podium. Lee Jia looked askance at Dae. Most of it had gone over her head, but she couldn’t help but feel like there was something more significant about her arrival here that had something to do with Dae or his master.

“What was that about?”

Dae scratched his cheek and considered Lee Jia’s question.

“Eh, my master—well, he’s an important figure. I’m surprised you haven’t heard of him, actually. He’s the foremost mage in the country and also our prime minister, second only to the queen herself. He has a reputation for being controversial in both his politics and his academics.”

Before Lee Jia could inquire further, Magus Hwang’s voice rang out through the lecture hall.

“Greetings, students! As you already know, I am Magus Hwang. By now you will have received tutelage from both of the other deans, which I am sure has been very insightful.”

Lee Jia heard Eui scoff, and she thought she saw a small smirk on Magus Hwang’s face as he continued.

“It is my privilege to not only teach you the ways of Goryeon mages, but also to elucidate for you the theories behind mana, qi, and ki and to guide you as you experiment in finding the ways in which they might complement and enhance each other! Now then, without further ado, let us begin your first real lesson!”
