“Dios, what a mess!” Victoria rubbed her eyes with the heels of her hands.

“You can say that again.” Steve said. He didn’t even bother to open his own eyes, reclined as he was on his bedroll inside the shelter. “Do you think they’re finally calming down?”

It took but a single glance at the constant milling of the Hiscariat inside the Outpost to make up my mind on that. “Nope. I think they’re actually regressing. Looks like they’re circling back from anger, amping up for another round of disbelief.”

“Can you blame them?” Two-Steps-Left sounded forlorn. “One of our largest taboos, broken by a Holder. And for what? For nothing else than the fact that he wanted to prove himself.

I grimaced. I really couldn’t blame them, no. This was a tiny community, where everybody knew each other. Today had probably been more drama than the Outpost had ever seen. And once the secret was out, the hits just kept coming.

Oh, this wasn’t some major conspiracy shit like we’d expected. Those fears were the real reason for all our cloak and dagger-bullshit. Nobody know that Two-Steps-Left was involved. We’d waltzed in on our own, demanding restitution, while she had a very rough verbal discussion with Fair-as-None, the black-robed judge and executioner, convincing her that yes, this was very real and needed dealing with.

While the rest of us were busy riling up Fortune-Unfolds and keeping him guessing, she’d paid attention to all Holders, and looked out for every hint of manipulation. How she knew was anybody’s guess – she hadn’t told Two-Steps-Left, and I honestly didn’t care – but she knew.

For the first attempt, where he’d manipulated Care-of-Ways, she’d almost missed the talent use. But the second attempt against Set-Alight removed any doubt. That final one against me was just icing on top of the mind-control cake.

Once he died and his active talents were destroyed, the skeletons started spilling out the closet. Honestly? It was all so goddamn shallow. Fortune-Unfolds wasn’t planning some hostile takeover or anything. He just wanted to be respected, to have his usefulness as a scout leader reinforced. Except, the stupid fucker decided that there were no limits to the tricks he could pull to get there.

Two-Steps-Left had stayed with us, updating us as the news started coming in. Care-of-Ways? An easy target, because she had no friends. Over time and constant reinforcement of his talent, he’d have gotten her fully on his side, even without reapplying his talent.

When we arrived? We messed up everything he’d worked for. If we actually did succeed in joining humanity’s forces to those of the Hiscariat, we’d invalidate all his work, possibly even remove the need for real intelligence.

It was so goddamn ironic that he couldn’t see that we’d have loved to learn everything he and his people had to offer, and give him all the credit for it. Instead, he’d decided that we needed to go.

“Why are people so goddamn shortsighted?” I growled to myself. As the others started answering, I waved them away, getting off of my own bedroll. “Don’t answer. I’m going out there again for a bit. Might as well take whatever Mental Resistance I can earn from this.”

The moment I moved out from underneath the shadesail, the thrum of mental speech surrounded me once again. I still didn’t quite understand how it was that they seemed to forget about our presence the moment we entered a shelter. If it was some sort of mental effect, that sounded very hypocritical. Meanwhile, if it was some type of social conditioning, these people were freaks.

Nearby Hiscariat turned their heads to stare me down. Some had hatred in their eyes. Others sorrow. A few – the worst ones – looked at me with compassion.

I didn’t need that. It wasn’t like I enjoyed what had happened. But I was not a victim. I’d fought back. There was a difference.

Still, I wasn’t going to act like I understood what the Hiscariat were going through today. They’d been through a rough patch, and the waves were still going high. Though, from the feel of things, they were moving on to another thing. Instead of the raw sensations of anger, disbelief and disgust that had been predominant earlier in the day, now it felt like they were moving on to sharp, naked grief. They were mourning, not only the loss of a Holder, but also everything that had led to this point.

When I re-entered the shelter, I was emotionally exhausted. The fact that my Mental Resistance had increased to eleven didn’t quite justify the undiminished mental assault I’d exposed to. I opened my mouth to say as much, only then recognizing Drift-Ashore standing inside, patiently waiting with her tentacles calmly placed at her side.

“Liam. Good. Our debates have concluded.” She said without preamble. “After the…” she hesitated. “proof of aptitude and durability that you and your kin have provided, we have decided that it is for the best of both our species to band together, in order to eradicate the Ceaseless and share the boons of the Torn Divide between us.”

I blinked. “Are we just going to gloss over…” I waved my hand vaguely at the Hiscariat all around the Outpost. “everything that’s happened?”

“We are.” She bowed deeply. “We apologize for everything that has taken place since your arrival here. Yet, we need for your kind to move on, allowing our people time and space to heal.”

I snorted. “Are you listening to yourself? Do you hear how ungrateful you sound right now?”

She held up a tentacle, cutting me off. “Yes. I realize. However, you do not understand. The collective literally will not be able to move on and heal properly, until you are gone. Not unless you desire to spend the next few days inside that shelter?”

“Are you having me on? This has got to be a joke.” I looked at the others, watched Two-Steps-Left as she softly shook her head.

“Not a joke.” She answered solemnly. “Only, I implore you not to judge us from what you have seen so far.”

“You are making that rather hard, love.” I rubbed my brow, trying to come up with a response that wasn’t just swearing. “You might not realize, but the time since we got here hasn’t exactly been all cake and fucking sunshine for us either. And now you’re tossing us out on our arses with nothing to show for it, and asking us to be happy about it?”

“Oh.” Her mental voice sounded ashamed. She blinked owlishly. “I do apologize. I try to adjust, but we are really not used to people unable to keep up with the mental communications.” She lowered her head, as if in concentration, before gazing straight at me. “Before you arrived, we were starting to worry about the survival of the Telvan Outpost. The Ceaseless were keeping us penned up, holding us back from establishing the supply lines needed to feed ourselves. Now, the prospects have changed, and so will we. The Hiscariat would seek a formal alliance with humankind. An alliance offered as equals, with either species holding the same level of power. We offer reparations for our transgressions, both those who attacked you in honest mistake, and the horrible misdeeds you have suffered from Fortune-Unfolds. The faster you come to our aid, the more we will be able to renumerate you.”

I had not seen that coming.

Drift-Ashore continued as if she didn’t even notice. “We will listen to your wishes regarding reparations. Clearly, there will be limits to what we can offer, but… would your kind be interested in Mage training and enchanted artifacts? These are topics at which we excel.”

I was pretty sure my mouth dropped open at this point. Artifacts, enchanted by powerful Crafters and training from Mages above Level 30? Ever Steady was going to be pissing rainbows in sheer celebration. And it wasn’t even going to cost them anything we hadn’t already paid for.

“Deal.” Victoria said with a pleased grin on her face.

Steve nodded happily from his spot on the ground.

I didn’t even bother hiding my eye-rolling. “Listen, we talked about this. We aren’t heroes or diplomats.”

“Really now? So, we didn’t just survive a lot of hardship, against all odds, and helped push humanity to a better place?” Victoria asked.

“That’s… absolutely not the point. We- Listen-”

“You said ‘listen’ already.” Steve offered happily. “Actually, let me fix this for us all.” He cleared his throat and leaped nimbly to his feet. With a half-bow, he addressed Drift-Ashore. “Those sound like wonderful offers, and we are keen to accept. We don’t actually hold the authority to accept anything on behalf of humanity, but we would be pleased to take the offer back to our leaders and return with their response.”

She inclined her head in response. “Well put.”

Steve raised his palm toward me, forestalling my outburst. “There is one change we would ask for.”

“Speak.”

He met my gaze and, with the most provocative twinkle in his eyes, said. “Since our group is, obviously, going to return and be in the front ranks when we take on the Ceaseless. How about we put our group right front and center of the lists of both enchantments and training?”

“Of course. We would have done so regardless.”

“Well, there we go.” He brushed his hands together in satisfaction and raised an eyebrow. “Cutthroat enough for you, boss?”
