The suite’s bedroom had a movable wall that covered up its window just like the living room. This one was probably going to be down most of the time, but the newlyweds had gone to check the bed after cuddling on the couch. Clay had been more than happy to claim the top of the strange circular chair and laid there belly-up.

The mattress and pillows had been made with demons in mind so they were comfortable for Adabelle’s horns, allowing her to lay on her side without issues. After testing the bed out, they laid together and watched the islands come and go.

“Is it going to be like this the whole way?” Trevor asked before kissing the back of Adabelle’s head. “Just island after island? I’m not complaining, I think they’re pretty. It would be nice if this kept up.”

“From what I remember, no,” she answered. His hand was resting on her stomach, and she moved hers on top of his. “They go on for a while, but sometime later this evening we should reach the open ocean. There are some island nations every so often, but it sounded like we wouldn’t be landing at any of them based on Braidley’s explanation.”

“That kind of sucks,” he remarked before shrugging. “I can see why, though. If we can make it straight through, then there’s no reason not to. Especially if they have a race to catch.”

“You just want to visit as many places as possible,” Adabelle said, snuggling back into him. The spade at the end of her tail, which was wrapped around his thigh, lazily waved in the air.

“Yeah, but I know you’re the same way,” Trevor shot back, poking her stomach. He found the squeak she made to be adorable.

She was quick to intertwine her fingers with his so he couldn’t do that again, turning to give him a chastising look. All that did was earn her a grin. Adabelle sighed and rolled her eyes, trying not to show her amusement. He saw right through her.

“I mentioned that my parents went on cruises, right?” he asked.

“You did,” she answered. “I was a little surprised because you don’t generally like to talk about your family.”

“Well, I figured I could talk about how cruises worked back on Earth,” Trevor offered, shrugging.

“If you’d like,” Adabelle said with a smile, turning further. “I am certainly curious, and it’s hard to know how much of what Wayne tells us is actually true.”

Trevor looked at the glow of her eyes before returning her smile. “I never got to go with them, but I saw a lot of pictures and heard a lot of stories. They had to take a flight to get to the ship because it was so far, but their favorite ones took them to the Caribbean, which was basically a big string of islands leading from one continent to another. It was a lot like Paladona, actually. Weather-wise, I mean.”

“It sounds like it would be a very popular destination,” Adabelle replied.

“Definitely,” he confirmed. “Because of the climate, it was something they could do year round, though I’m not sure what they did for hurricane season. Not really well-versed in their weather. Anyway, not important. What I was getting at is that the big thing about the cruise was that the boat would dock at a few islands every time. They’d get to get off, wander around, and visit exotic locales. It would have been cool if this one did, too.”

The demonkin nodded as she thought about it. “That would have been cool, yes. The Red Dream seems more like an airship that goes straight through, but that doesn’t mean we’re not allowed to ask if there are others that will visit some of the islands. While we can’t commit to something during this trip, there’s always the future.”

“So long as my wife lets me take her on a vacation,” Trevor teased.

“Being the mayor of a town on the rise doesn’t afford me a lot of time off, but at least I’m doing better,” Adabelle pouted.

“You are, and I’m proud of you,” he replied easily. Then, he sighed. “That does sound nice, though, and I’m obviously here for it. Especially once my camera’s done being made. Man, I used to love looking at those pictures they brought home, thinking about going on cruises myself someday. I always got so jealous. They always looked like they were having so much fun, and some of the places they visited were so beautiful.”

Adabelle shifted so she could watch Trevor’s face carefully as he gazed out of the window. “It sounds like we’ve figured out where your wanderlust comes from,” she said cautiously.

He blinked at the realization before chuckling to himself. “Yeah, I guess so.”

“If you don’t mind me saying,” she started before biting her lip in thought. Trevor looked into her eyes. “It sounds a bit lonely.”

“What do you mean?” he asked, looking genuinely confused.

At first, Adabelle wasn’t sure if she should walk it back or not. Their honeymoon was supposed to be a celebration of their life together in this world, and she wasn’t sure how he felt about the topic. She warred with herself for a few moments before deciding to continue. Her husband was opening up about his past, and she didn’t want to miss the moment.

The noblewoman moved so that she was laying on her back to see him better.

Trevor tilted his head. “You’re going to miss the scenery if you’re facing this way.”

She kissed the tip of his nose. “This is more important.”

It looked like he was about to argue. In the end, he chose not to. “Alright, if you insist.”

“I do insist,” Adabelle replied, putting a hand on his chest. “And what I meant was that it sounds like they went on quite a few of these cruises without you. They didn’t take you on trips with them?”

“Well, yeah, but mostly to go visit family,” Trevor answered with a half shrug. “We’d go on long car trips, like eighteen hours one way, to go see them. Normally during the summer, when there wasn’t any school. We had plenty of fun hanging out with my cousins, aunts, and uncles. I remember…”

Adabelle noted the frown on her courier’s face as he trailed off, and her tail unwrapped from his leg to lace between the fingers of his hand on her hip. “You remember?”

“My aunt was usually the one who took us to theme parks, movies, and whatever else was around,” he said slowly. “My uncle would sometimes come, but it was usually her and whatever other kids were visiting that week.”

“The same aunt that saw the fairies?” the demonkin asked.

“No, but I love that you remembered me telling you that once,” he said, offering her a small smile before looking back out of the window. “Now that I’m thinking about it, because I think I’d forgotten, yeah. My parents didn’t really hang out with my aunt and uncle much when we were visiting. They disappeared for a couple of days at a time.”

Adabelle shimmied so that her face was leveled with Trevor’s, and she leaned her forehead against his. She didn’t close her eyes, though. “That’s what I meant by lonely, though I only meant in the context of these cruises,” she gently explained. “I wasn’t expecting the problem to extend to other trips.”

Frowning, the courier thought things over. “They… went away a lot,” he admitted slowly. “Three or four times a year. Usually when I was at school, aside from visiting family, which was always during summer. Then there were work trips, which could take a while depending on what was going on, which happened more frequently.”

Trevor’s eyes stared into hers, but they weren’t focused. It seemed like he was looking through her, remembering the past that he usually kept bottled up.

“It’s not like I was really alone, though,” he continued. “I’d stay with my grandmother, or have a babysitter until they thought I was old enough to watch myself. I was pretty mature for my age, so that didn't happen until I was about eight or so. None of the other kids had that, so I was pretty excited that I was responsible enough to watch myself.”

“Trevor,” Adabelle said softly.

“Yeah?” he asked, focusing on her eyes.

“I won’t claim to be an expert on the parenting methods of Earth, but leaving their eight year olds unattended for any number of days does not sound like a winning strategy.”

“I might have been nine, if that helps. You know my memory of back then is pretty fuzzy.”

“As you’ve said,” Adabelle replied with a nod. “And no, it doesn’t help.”

Trevor gave her another half shrug. “I don’t know what to tell you. It seemed normal to me. They would always bring back pictures and souvenirs from their trips, and I always thought it would be so cool to go on adventures of my own some day.”

“You didn’t want to go with them?” she asked, frowning.

“Well, yeah, of course I did,” he replied sheepishly. “I remember bringing it up once, and mom said something about not having enough money for three, so I just kind of accepted it. They worked hard, and the apple doesn’t fall far from the tree, so I figured I’d be able to go with them after I grew up and got my own job.”

“That’s eighteen for you, right?”

“That’s when we’re considered adults in my country, but I got a job at the tail end of fifteen. The guy who would be my first boss got the years wrong and he thought I was sixteen, but ended up hiring me anyway. I was pretty proud of that since it meant I nailed my first interview.”

“And were you able to go on your parent’s vacations with them once you were able to afford it?” Adabelle asked. Trevor didn’t answer, and that was all she needed. The demonkin wrapped her arms around him, pulling his head to her chest. “We don’t have to talk about this anymore. I’m sorry I brought it up.”

“No, it’s okay. I brought it up; all you did was make an observation,” he said, making himself comfortable.

“Still, I wasn’t expecting to dredge up bad memories. I’m sorry.”

“That’s twice now. One more time and I’m gonna pull out the apology jar.”

“That’s only for frivolous things, which this isn’t,” she replied. “From the sounds of it, this was a painful revelation for you.”

“It was,” Trevor admitted. “But that was just my normal.”

“Just because you didn’t realize it hurt you until now doesn’t mean it’s okay,” Adabelle said firmly. “I won’t say that I understand your family dynamics because my parents were overbearing and planned out as much of my future as they could, such was the burden of my station, but it sounds like you came from the opposite and that almost sounds worse to me.”

“I… don’t know what to say to that,” he replied quietly. “That’s just how my life was. My parents would work, go on trips, and I’d stay home. I’d work, get the best grades I could, and do my own thing. It’s just how life went. Besides, it’s not like it matters.”

“Trevor, you came to this world through a ritual that chose you specifically because you needed a break. You were really anxious back then. This was probably a big reason for it.”

“Maybe,” he allowed. “But it still doesn’t matter.”

“I disagree,” Adabelle said, frowning in anger. “If they were here, I would give them a piece of my mind. From the sounds of it, they left you for weeks at a time and didn’t even bother to get someone to watch you after you turned eight. Eight years old, Trevor, and all alone.”

“I was mature for my age,” he offered.

“That doesn’t make it better,” she almost snapped. Holding him a little tighter, she took a breath. “I apologize for my tone. You’re not to blame here, and I know that. The thought of you so young and alone just infuriates me. You were their child. It sounds like they went out of their way to leave you behind without anyone calling them out on their abandonment, and you’re making excuses for them. I’m upset for you, Trevor, because you always seem to give people who mistreat you a pass while caring so much about others.”

“And that’s the reason why it doesn’t matter,” Trevor said as he scratched her back. Her tail guided him to the best place to dig his nails into, and he did so.

“Your parents left you behind and it’s okay because I’m upset?” Adabelle asked, calming down slightly at his touch. Her back arched as he got a particularly good spot.

“I didn’t say it was okay, I said it didn’t matter, and yes. How you feel is a part of that,” he answered as he looked up at her. “Look, I was a mess when I came here. I was nervous, I stumbled a lot, and I didn’t really slow down until I was forced to. No one’s denying that, especially me. But coming here was the best thing to ever happen in my life. You, more than anything else, are the best thing to ever happen to me.”

Trevor took a deep breath before pulling away from his wife. He reached up and cupped her cheek. “I might have wanted it more than I realized, but I’m getting the care now that I craved back then, and that is what matters. The family that I’ve made here are what’s important, and I want to do right by you. My past made me the man I am today, and I might not be as well adjusted as some people out there, but there’s no changing it now. All I can be is the best version of myself, which I can do thanks to all of you.”

Adabelle watched her husband carefully as he spoke, searching his eyes and the soft smile hanging on his lips. After a moment, she matched it. “I’m glad you are who you are, Trevor. I’m very fond of my courier,” she told him without reservation. “Are you okay, though?”

“I should be,” he answered. “There’s a lot to unpack there and I’m sure that I’ll be thinking about it for a long time. You’ll be there for me if I need help, right?”

“You know you don’t even have to ask,” she replied, chastising him in a lightly teasing tone. “I’ll always help you, son of Ander. No matter what.”

“And that’s all I need, lady,” Trevor said, leaning forward to kiss her. She returned his affections, and they placed their foreheads together again. “That got a little heavy.”

“I wasn’t sure if it was wise of me to pry or not,” Adabelle admitted softly.

“You’re allowed to, whenever you want. I’ve got nothing to hide from you. Things I can’t remember might be a different story, though.”

The demonkin turned around so she could see the window and snuggled closer to her husband. “That’s okay. I’ll just have to make sure the memories you make with me are unforgettable. It might be tough, but I’m up for the task.”
