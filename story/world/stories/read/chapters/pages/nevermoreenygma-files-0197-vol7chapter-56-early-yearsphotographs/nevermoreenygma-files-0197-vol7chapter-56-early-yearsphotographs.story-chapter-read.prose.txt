Chapter 56

Early Years/Photographs

After a period of trial and training for a month, Mai was accepted as one of the operatives best suited for dangerous missions. It really was no surprise to anyone. Her reputation from Wishper preceded her. Even though she had been away for several years, the fact that she had stayed active—conducting investigations in Tokyo and doing daily workout—made the training easy for her.

But Nevermore was not Wishper, nor an small investigation office.

Wishper had been a rapid intervention force, an organization operating from the shadows to save lives and eliminate threats before they grew too large in the hidden world. Their methods were direct: rescues, infiltrations, target elimination, and dismantling of dangerous groups.

Nevermore, on the other hand, was another story.

Its objective was different.

It was almost as if they were creating a research institute with an attached military structure, though without the rigidity of an army. It was not just about combat or protection, but about understanding and preparing for what was to come.

Wishper had been an organization marked by violence, even if with good intentions.

Nevermore did not rely on force only—but that did not mean there was no danger.

It was a strange balance—a group of scientists, soldiers, explorers, and, why not, weirdos who simply wanted to do the right thing.

Every mission was a different scenario for the active agents, and so every assignment could demand different skills.

Everything depended on the situation.

A Nevermore agent was not simply a soldier or a researcher, but an extension of the island’s departments. They could act in coordination with scientists and specialists or work alone, relying solely on their instincts and training.

Missions were coordinated by the two Aeons of Nevermore, distributing assignments according to risk level and the capabilities required.

If the mission involved recovering a dangerous artifact from ruins, field agents had to work closely with archaeology specialists, understand excavation protocols, and recognize the signs of potential hazards. Many relics of the past and OOPArts were not only valuable, but also deadly, whether due to traps, toxic organisms like fungus or other type bacteria, ancient curses, or because they contained technology that should never have been reactivated.

On a mission to rescue a fey, esper or mages, speed and strength were essential. Some feys were powerful but unstable, while others were targeted by organizations that wanted to capture or eliminate them. An agent had to move stealthily or engage in direct combat without losing sight of the primary objective: ensuring the target’s safety and removing them from danger.

In those cases, the mission had a structure that had clearly been copied from Whisper, and Mai had to admit that there wasn’t much difference. The problem lay in what to do with the culprits. That part of the hidden world was always the most brutal, because it stemmed simply from the nature of the humans and fey involved, and it almost never ended well.

When investigating a Dark Event, the mission might require a different type of talent: infiltrating an neighborhood, organization, site, assuming a false identity, blending in, and gathering information without raising suspicion. It demanded intuition, self-control, and a capacity for deception. A single mistake could mean death—not only for the agent but for civilians and for everyone who depended on that information. The challenge was always to solve the case or to mitigate the causes as much as possible.

Yet not all missions were highly dangerous. Sometimes, a Nevermore agent simply supported research departments, assisted in exploration logistics, or accompanied a group of scientists to ensure their safety. Other times, it was enough to collect testimonies, analyze clues, and connect threads that allowed the island’s strategists to form a clearer picture of ongoing events.

It was a group of explorers as much as warriors to put it in a romantic way.

And while the world burned in conflicts over resources and territory, Nevermore moved behind the scenes, trying to figure out the next challenges that would come, helping those in need, operating in missions of research, rescue, sabotage, and infiltration.

It did not take Mai long to adapt.

Perhaps it was the way every member contributed something different, the way there was no crushing hierarchy but a network of cooperation.

Or perhaps it was the mystery.

The fact that the organization did not have all the answers, and its purpose was to discover them.

Little by little, mission by mission, training by training, Mai ceased to be a newcomer and became a full-fledged operative.

And with that, a long period of adventures and dangers began.

***

She had never planned to stay too long.

She accepted Nerea and Miria’s idea more out of curiosity than conviction. Even Jinat, when she consulted her, had said, “Go, spy a little, see if these geniuses of mystery have anything to hide,” with an ironic smile on the video call.

And Mai, in her usual style, responded that if they kicked her out after a month, at least she’d have a good excuse to travel for free and sample lab coffee.

But once inside, things changed. It was not what she expected. They were not fanatics or conspirators—they were a bunch or weirdos, yes, but methodical. Almost too methodical. People who took the impossible seriously, who discussed anomalies with the same casualness others used to talk about the weather. And slowly, Mai realized there was something in that noisy, messy, order and disorder that felt… nice.

Thus, what had been temporary became permanent.

The rest—the years, the alliances, the missions, the ruins, the names that came and went—simply followed their course.

From the moment she joined Nevermore, Mai watched the organization slowly expand beyond Siren Island.

Was not a chaotic growth, nor an explosion of power, like that of large corporations or warring governments. It was meticulous expansion, designed to endure over time, without drawing too much attention. First, small facilities at strategic points: Tokyo, New York, Cape Town, Istanbul, Buenos Aires. Then, alliances with groups that shared common interests. Mai knew it was all part of a larger network, an effort to maintain balance in a world just beginning to recognize Dark Events as a real threat.

One of the first allies to establish close contact was the Armitage Foundation, an institution born in the 20th century and linked to Miskatonic University. Unlike Nevermore, which had emerged in 2050 as a modern response to paranormal phenomena, the Armitage Foundation had existed for generations, accumulating knowledge in the shadows.

Its researchers and scholars had not only documented strange events, but had actively participated in their containment during the last century, dating back to the world wars. Even Leon had been involved with them, as had Rein and Noki's father.

The current team, however, focused mainly on academic pursuits. For Mai, it was strange to work with a group so ancient yet so disconnected from Nevermore’s operational mindset. While they viewed Dark Events as puzzles to be solved with patience, Nevermore’s agents knew there was not always time for answers. Sometimes, you could only react before it was too late. Mai had heard Rein and Leon complain, more than once, that they missed the twentieth century. Back then, the Miskatonic group had always been more daring when it came to conducting research. Academic policies had changed, and now they were more like librarians than anything else.

Over time, other alliances emerged.

The Wishper Team became one of the most reliable sources for understanding movements in the underworld paranormal community.

More recently, sometime in the 2070s, the Pyrene Organization appeared—a group that, though still without a defined place in the hierarchy of the hidden world, was rapidly gaining notoriety. Mai observed these alliances at a distance. For some reason, Rein and Noki were happy about it. Apparently, they were old friends with the leader of that organization, a fey named Mari.

Meanwhile, to the public, Nevermore remained just a research institute. Governments knew the truth but kept it hidden. Ordinary people barely paid attention, too caught up in economic crises, political conflicts, and a planet slowly trying to recover its breath after decades of suffocation.

On internet forums, conspiracy theories would arise, but the organization itself had a department dedicated to monitoring, manipulating, and debunking any information that got too close to the truth. They made sure the idea that feys, espers, and mages worked in secret to contain the unknown still seemed like madness.

But in the hidden world, the story was different. Mai had heard the whispers among the fey clans, the silent disagreements that surfaced whenever Nevermore’s name was mentioned in certain circles. Some saw the organization as a threat to their independence, others feared it was trying to monopolize the representation of feys in the human world.

But the hidden world had never been unified. It was too decentralized, too fragmented by histories, grudges, and differing priorities. For everyone to align under a single banner, would have to occur that neutrality would become unthinkable. And so far, it hadn’t happened. The Hydra incident years ago had been only a temporary union.

Something even worse would have to happen for them to finally come together.

And so, Dark Events continued to appear. That was what the agents were there for.

Mai knew this better than anyone. The world did not stop for anything or anyone.

And in this new stage of missions, adventures, and mysteries, she had not stopped either.

Yes, she thought she would only be there for a few months.

A few months that soon turned into ten years.

***

The missions in her case were never simple.

Not all of them were battles. Not all involved explosions and chases. But all were dangerous.

Sometimes, the enemy was human. Sometimes a fey.

Sometimes, it was something that could not even be named.

Yes, she had managed to survive for ten years, but just when she thought her sense of wonder had been exhausted by everything she’d seen in previous decades, the new challenge posed by the Nevermore Initiative served as a wake-up call, making her realize just how far-reaching these new threats could be.

The Dark Events had only just begun to awaken.

By 2076, Mai was on a mission in Southeast Asia when she saw the news about the first recorded Dark Event outside Earth.

During a break between meetings with Initiative agents, she turned on the screen in her room and saw the images broadcast from the Moon: in Mare Imbrium, rocks floated in the void, completely ignoring gravity. Scientists attempted to explain it, analysts speculated on possible causes, But it soon became clear that there was no explanation for it.

An entire research facility was destroyed by that event, making it the first instance of a Dark Event outside Earth. Old books were dusted off from the archives of Miskatonic University that spoke of species that had arrived on Earth in ancient times and told other stories claiming that there were hidden planets in the solar system.

By 2077, Mars was next to fall under the shadow of the inexplicable. While at an operations center in Europe, Mai saw the reports from Utopia Planitia. An unknown fungus was spreading uncontrollably, covering tens of kilometers in days. Experts debated whether it was a threat or an opportunity, since by altering the composition of the air, it was generating a thin layer of oxygen. On the streets of Earth, people were divided: some feared it was another Dark Event, others saw it as the next great step in terraforming. Mai, not taking her eyes off the news, thought only one thing: the universe did not stop for anyone, nor did it intend to do them any favors.

And between missions and the new world, another five years passed.

During those first fifteen years of adventures, Mai finally felt that she had found her place in the world.

The missions, dangers, and dark secrets she had faced did not change the fact that she had built a family within the organization.

Rein and Noki, always together, were like her younger sisters, though technically Rein was the older one. Mai never argued it, knowing she would lose the debate.

Oxy appeared from time to time, with her carefree attitude. Sometimes it seemed she knew too much. Sometimes she disappeared for months and returned with a simple smile, as if she had never left.

Leon, with his cigars and his habit of changing his last name, was always there to make heavy jokes or be a reliable presence in difficult moments. He was not exactly a brother, nor a friend in the traditional sense. He was simply Leon—or Leo-pon—and that was enough.

Over time, Van joined the group. Another fey, strong-willed and inexhaustible, as if always seeking one more adventure. Mai respected her, though on more than one occasion she had to endure her endless jokes.

Dr. Kohinata was a unique figure within Nevermore. Eccentric to the core, always lost in her own theories about life, genetics, and feys. The frog-fey could talk for hours about the biological development of extinct species while forgetting she had a scalpel in her hand. Mai had learned not to move too much when the doctor spoke and operated at the same time.

And then there was Camila, a wolf fey with a strange combination of medical quirks and an devotion to the team’s wellbeing, as well as to the budget for conducting experiments. She was capable of forcing one to take a break if she believed you needed it, and more than once Mai had found herself trapped in a room, unable to leave until Camila was satisfied with her health condition.

Over the years, Nevermore’s family grew. Not everyone survived. Not everyone stayed. But Mai held on to those who remained with her.

Despite everything, she still returned home whenever she had the chance.

The wars that had once consumed the world had subsided, though environmental problems persisted. The Aeons were working on an ecological plan to repair the damage, but it would take time. Humanity had proven to be good at destroying. Rebuilding was much harder.

As long as there was someone fighting for it, as long as a Dark Event didn't occur that swallowed up the sun, Mai felt that there was still hope.

***

It was during this period that Mai began to uncover aspects of Nevermore’s history and the secrets surrounding the organization’s members.

Shin Aogami.

A name that appeared everywhere, in conversations with Rein, Noki, and the others, like an noisy echo that never really faded.

Shin’s name was not mentioned solely with affection, but with a complex mix of respect, nostalgia, and occasionally a certain teasing. Every member of the organization seemed to have their own nickname for him.

Sometimes he was “dad,” other times “that bastard,” also “brother,” “friend,” “daddy” (as Noki called him), “the alien,” and even “the crazy one.” The nicknames were many, and although they apparently reflected the diversity of relationships, they all pointed to the same figure—a figure who had left an indelible mark on the lives of everyone who knew him.

From what Mai could gather, Shin had been much more than the adoptive father of Rein and Noki. During the 20th century, he had been a fey who traveled the world, someone who over the years had earned both allies and enemies in equal measure. He acted for his own reasons, driven by a quest that took him beyond any notion of right and wrong.

Throughout his life, Shin helped many, but not always in conventional ways. He had been in the most remote places always with a clear objective in mind: to find a solution to his curse, which had accompanied him for decades since his arrival. And to be with those he cared for. However, he had also earned the animosity of several groups, which had put him in dangerous situations and forced him to make difficult decisions.

Shin was a member of Tempus Fugit, one of the first to investigate Dark Events after World War II. At that time, the organization consisted of a select group of people: few people from Miskatonic, Leon, Oxy, Van, and Gehirn. Though Gehirn’s figure was not well-known, he had been the financial backbone of the organization. Always contributing quietly, Mai thought. Gehirn was like the trust fund of the hidden world.

Tempus Fugit did not only investigate these strange phenomena, but was also deeply involved in studying ancient secrets of the world, along with the Armitage Foundation or looking for, the more old, Fog Society. Tempus Fugit was one of the first organizations to attempt to unravel the mysteries of the Dark Events.

Mai began to understand that the creation of Nevermore was not simply a coincidence. Shin had been one of the fundamental reasons the organization took shape. Rein, Noki, Leon, and Gehirn had been very close friends of his, but beyond the adventure of investigating strange phenomena, the idea of helping others was always there. Nevermore did not merely study Dark Events, it also became an organization aimed at offering help to those in need.

In many ways, Nevermore carried Shin’s legacy: an organization that, like him, not only search and fought against weird stuff but also offered a hand to those who needed it most.

However, in 1999, Shin Aogami simply vanished.

There was no goodbye, no farewell—just an inexplicable void that sank deep into everyone who knew him. No one knew what had truly happened. Some said he had simply disappeared on a travel, leaving no trace, as if the world itself had swallowed him. Others thought Shin had been pulled into the Dark Events, or perhaps his quest had taken him to a place beyond the reach of the living. Rumors swirled, but the truth was never clear.

What Mai could understand was that, even though Shin was no longer physically present, his influence remained alive. Rein and Noki were the most visible witnesses of his legacy. Both of them, though pained by his absence, never lost hope that one day their father would return. Rein and Noki carried the weight of that hope in their hearts, like a lighthouse that never went out.

But what truly surprised Mai was the bond between Lizbeth and Mimi, the two women with whom Shin had shared his life.

Although Lizbeth and Mimi still kept in touch with Rein and Noki, the two feys mothers no longer shared the same life they once had. Over the years, their relationship had waned, and although they still met occasionally, those visits were sporadic and distant.

As Mai listened to all this through the years, she realized that Shin Aogami’s past was not a complete story. Mai did not ask more about Shin Aogami. In that moment, she understood that some stories never close—they simply keep opening, and she was not yet ready to delve fully into those dark passages of the past. After all she still kept her own stories opened too.

The legacy of that man remained alive, and Mai did not know if she would ever be able to understand it fully. But what she did know was that, although his name resonated through the organization, Nevermore’s family continued forward.

That... was until she had the chance to see Shin, Lizbeth, and Mimi through old photographs.

***

Due to a recent restructuring, a large portion of the agents had moved to the southern side. There was a plan to build a city in the north where the base had once stood. Mai herself had to move to one of the newly constructed buildings in the south, and a new, expanded base was already under construction.

In addition to the mini-bases and stations, Nevermore was now almost everywhere in the world. A few months ago, Mai had visited the Tokyo station, where even Nerea was interested in returning to action. Mai, for her part, had brought several things for her new apartment, along with visits to old friends.

Rein had also built a small house near the complex, closer to the sea—a quiet, secluded place. Noki, on the other hand, preferred to sleep in the Technology and Development wing, where she had made many friends.

During Rein’s move, she and Noki took charge of the boxes full of memories. Apparently, those two sisters liked to store memory deposits in various parts of the world, and now those memories had been brought to the island.

As Mai helped Rein unpack, she found the albums.

While Rein and Noki were putting things away, they didn’t notice her.

Something inside her stopped.

It was as if an invisible force had drawn her toward those memories that were nor hers. And that was when she saw the photo.

Shin—the man who had been at the center of so many stories told over the years—was there, with a serious, yet relaxed expression.

Tall bastard, was he some kind of giant fey?

Beside him, a girl with long blonde hair rode on his back. Her laughter was so clear, so full of happiness. And that woman’s smile… Mai didn’t know how to explain it, but something inside her clicked. Lizbeth. She knew who it was. It didn’t make sense, but deep down, Mai knew that woman was Rein’s mother.

How? She though.

The pain she felt was not immediate. It wasn’t like a direct blow. It was a small, subtle imbalance, something deep in her chest that told her, in a nonverbal way, that this image represented something more. As she flipped through the pages, the photos of the couple—Shin and Lizbeth—followed one another, stolen moments from a full life, captured in laughter and embraces that bore no mask of sadness.

But then, one photo caught her off guard even more. Mimi, the other girl, also beautiful, with a tail barely visible in the picture, an image so innocent it almost made her stomach knot. Mimi, a woman who was also part of that triangle.

But it was seeing Lizbeth, Mimi, and Shin together in those photos that truly took her breath away. Those images started to hit her like an invisible hammer in her chest. Mai felt a pang in her heart, something she could not describe. She couldn’t put a name to what she was experiencing, but she felt a burning in her chest, an unknown, overwhelming emotion that seized her without mercy.

She was alone in Rein’s room, hands trembling as she held the album, unable to look away from those faces, from those foreign memories.

Something began to invade her.

She felt strange, as if that image had uncovered a part of herself she had never known, that made her feel warmth, warmth in her face, as if the air in the room had thickened. Her breathing quickened for no reason, and for a moment, she thought something had changed inside her.

That burning surprised her, and deeply confused her.

What she had felt in other fleeting relationships could not compare. Never before had something affected her this way, never had she felt that pain that seemed like a hot knot in her chest.

She closed the album and left the house to get some air, making sure the two girls didn’t see her face.

“Is something wrong?” Rein asked when she saw her come out.

“No, I’m fine.”

In the days that followed, Mai could not stop thinking about those photos. They repeated over and over in her mind. The faces of Shin and Lizbeth, the joy in their eyes, the evident love between them—something so pure, so real, so… impossible? Why did it hurt so much to see it? She didn’t understand. Her mind struggled between fascination and guilt. What was that feeling that had settled in her heart? It was painful and at the same time delicious, and sometimes, when she thought of the photos, her eyes would uncontrollably grow wet.

It was during especially quiet nights, when the island sank into the calm that precedes darkness, that Mai could take no more.

Her body tensed, and, unable to resist, she was seized by an inexplicable urgency. She remembered those faces from somewhere—the smile of Lizbeth, Shin’s warm embrace—and without thinking, she touched herself. She imagined them entangled, giving themselves to one another, and Mimi appeared too in her imagination. The sensation was a shock of emotions, a mix of pleasure and confusion that left her breathless. The image of them in her mind consumed her, and a heat ran through her body that she had never felt before. She imagined herself there with them too.

Why?Why?Why?

She didn’t know why she had done it.

It was as if something inside her, something she had repressed for years, had been released at that moment. She didn’t understand why she had felt so kneeling before those images, before that borrowed happiness, but deep within herself, Mai knew that these photos were not just images—they were the manifestation of an emotion so deep she could not ignore it.

Afterward, she felt incredibly ashamed. Remorse struck her.

She thought of Rein, of Noki, of the memories of Shin and Lizbeth that now formed part of her own memory. She felt a profound confusion, but something inside her, something she had never felt before, told her she could not escape these feelings. She had touched something—she had touched a desire, a need that she herself did not know existed.

Mai had no answers, but one thing was clear. That burning in her chest, that heart-wrenching sensation, could no longer be ignored.

She didn't know. She couldn't explain it. She could only feel it.

She felt like she knew them.
