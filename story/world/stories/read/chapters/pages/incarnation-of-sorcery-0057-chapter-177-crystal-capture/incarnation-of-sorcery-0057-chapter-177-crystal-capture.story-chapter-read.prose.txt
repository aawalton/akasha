The one silver lining of being trapped by a Great God was that Ryland was no longer being annihilated by the sun. A pocket of the Overrealm had been forcefully created around Ryland—and around Viren too—which had blocked out the star.

Thankfully, it had happened after Ryland had finished casting his main set of spells to move the titanic ball of gas, so he didn’t need to worry about failing the Archdragon’s task.

Especially since the spells would fade now that the link to any energy powering them had been severed by the manifestation of this Overrealm pocket. By the time they ran out of power, the star would have moved enough to satisfy Archdragon. Or that was Ryland’s hope, at least.

Even if he did fail, the appearance of a Great God was probably a good enough excuse.

“Ah, I’ve found you at last,” the Great God said. His voice was vaguely masculine, but it rippled with an ethereal, echoing quality that made it a lot more dubious. “Marionys Ryland.”

The divinity arrived in their presence with a rain of bright crystals, each one reflecting the pocket of the Overrealm with such clarity, it felt as though there were Realm pockets trapped within the crystals. The Great God was within the crystals, his disembodied reflection flitting between the crystals as though he couldn’t exist outside of them.

Ryland’s only answer was casting [Translocate] so that he appeared right next to Viren. After how Acrypha had been kidnapped, he wasn’t about to allow the same to happen to his other charge.

“Sir.” Viren’s voice was shaking, just as he was. The poor boy had to be overwhelmed by the series of events, despite going through all the madness that Mavar had inflicted upon him. First a planet-sized dragon, then a quest to move a star, and now a Great God himself had come to wipe them out. Of course, he was shaken. “Is that really a Great God?”

“Really a Great God?” the divinity said with mocking disbelief. “I am Jahaevo of the Augment, Shaper of Reflections, Monster of Mirrors, and the object of your worship.”

As he spoke, his form seemed to solidify within the crystals. His divine raiment turned bright as though they were constructed with threads of light, the head of the sceptre in his right hand turned into an orb of opalescent magic, and his form crackled with sparks of red lightning. Jahaevo presented a figure of incalculable power, even when he was a mere reflection.

There was a time when Viren had been happy to mouth off against people far beyond him, against personages who could have made him pay for speaking to them in such a blasted fashion. But, right now, he was too flabbergasted to summon up any sort of defiance.

Ryland, once again, didn’t blame him at all for it.

“I have no quarrel with you, Great God Jahaevo,” Ryland said. “You’re wasting your time.”

He was well aware that he had a great quarrel with practically all the Great Gods. However, if there was one thing any all-powerful and immortal being loved, it was their eagerness to hear their own voice while enforcing their superiority over others.

“There is no need to play pretend, Ryland,” the Great God said. His iridescent eyes sharpened as they flickered to Viren. “Unless you’re ashamed of revealing the truth to the boy.”

That brought Viren out of his little stupor. “What truth?” Some of his defiance returned. “And why would I believe you? I trust Ryland more than anyone or anything else.”

The words warmed Ryland’s heart, though he was still troubled to hear them spoken so brazenly. They needed to deal with Jahaevo carefully. At least for the time being. It was why Ryland had started off by saying he had no intentions of jumping into battle against an eternal representation of a Concept.

He needed a bit more time before he could get started on that.

“So, it is true,” Jahaevo said, still with that calculating look. “You truly haven’t spoken of everything you’ve done to us, you traitorous monster.”

Ryland deliberated for a moment. He recalled all his past conversations since meeting Viren that fateful day. “Well, to be honest, I don’t think you can blame me. I tried multiple times, yet I saw that everyone was getting quite tired of what they liked to call long-winded and unnecessarily convoluted explanations, so unfortunately, I never got around to explaining everything, although I did manage to—”

“That will be enough,” Jahaevo said. The Great God sighed. It made several crystals shatter, though they quickly reformed. “No wonder you’ve failed to mention anything. But if you must know, boy, this creature is the reason why we haven’t been able to draw more Calamities unto ourselves.”

Viren had an obvious calculating look where he was trying to tie different pieces of information together. He wasn’t surprised by what the Great God had said, though. Maybe that said a lot about Ryland.

Hmm. He supposed it did make sense that the strongest person Viren knew would be the one to challenge the very Great Gods themselves.

“Calamities?” Viren asked. “But you’re a Great God. Why would you need Calamities?” He turned to Ryland, who appreciated that Viren’s confidence was returning, because how or why else would he be so blithely questioning things if he was concerned about the Great God? “I thought it was all about Concepts or something like that?”

“It is,” Ryland confirmed. “But like people, the Great Gods are divided in their intentions regarding what to do with those Concepts. After all, Calamities are failed representations of those Concepts. Many Great Gods, such as Jahaevo of the Augment, wish to extend their influence very directly beyond the Overrealm. As such, they want to use Calamities as bridges.”

Even as Ryland explained things to Viren, he was slowly starting to picture how and why the Flourinus Order had come to the Overrealm.

“Extending influence using the Calamities…” Viren said. It sounded like he was starting to understand much the same thing as well.

“And this mad mage has the gall to dare and stop us,” Jahaevo said, eyes flashing with fury. “Us, the Great Gods, the ones who deserve to rule every Realm and every Plane in existence. Yet this fool going by the name of Ryland would dare to stand against us.”

“It’s hardly been just me,” Ryland said. “Over the aeons, you and your kind were part of several incidents, and there were others who stopped you. Your plans were always foiled, all because your own hubris makes you fail to consider any threat that could ever hope to stop you.”

“No one said you could speak your mind, cur,” the Great God growled quietly.

True to his threat, what passed for air within this Overrealm pocket now turned sharp as glass. Ryland felt as though he was stuffed inside a box full of broken shards, where any single motion would cause his skin to tear and spill blood. And the scariest fact was how the sensation appeared to bypass his powerful barriers.

A Great God truly was an incomparable force to be reckoned with.

Despite that, Ryland forced the words out anyway. He didn’t care what he suffered, he didn’t care if his body was lacerated by divine energy. The words needed to get out. Jahaevo had to be stalled.

“A millennium ago,” Ryland said, his mouth immediately tearing. He ignored his healing Inscriptions started counteracting the damage. “You and your kind were the ones who left semi-permanent breaches all across the cosmos, leaving behind pools of divine energy that all the Realms warred over.”

“A millennium ago,” Viren said, eyes wide as he started to consider the scale of things. “That sounds like the Realmbreaker Wars, but they didn’t happen that long ago.”

“Indeed. The similarities are striking because the Great Gods have had a hand in many such incidents, including the Realmbreaker Wars too. The very origin of the system of Realms we currently have was because the Great Gods spurred their followers to create conflict among the various races, so they were forced to depart for and live on different Realms. The Great Schism, as it was called.”

Viren’s eyes widened in recognition. Clearly, he had heard of the incident, likely in his history classes.

“Do you know what sort of incidents sparked the Realmbreaker Wars in the first place, Viren?” Ryland asked.

“Religiously motivated assassinations,” Viren said quietly, putting two and two together to understand the true origin of the Realmbreaker wars. “But why? Do the gods want to extend their influence just because they want us all to keep killing each other?”

“The Great Gods might be venerated as divinities that are unknowable and all that,” Ryland said. “But in truth, they share a lot of similarities with the very people they wish to worship. One of these similarities is their desperate need for domination. For greater influence and control. For giving in to their unchecked ambition.”

“Right… I saw that when one of them fell in love with you, sir.”

Apparently, that was quite the wrong thing to say. The crystals grew more numerous as the world turned harsh. It felt as though they were being inserted into solid glass, losing the ability to move of their own free will while also turning into a strange and ethereal representation of light.

This was the power of a Great God. This was the strength of a being who didn’t need conventional magic to rewrite not just local reality, but the very beings nearby according to his wishes.

Thankfully, the apparent transformation wasn’t absolute. If given enough time, Ryland might have been able to find transmogrifying Glyphs at work. They would just be made of divine energy Glyph Prints rather than regular mana Glyph Prints.

But the point remained that Ryland’s manasoul was still intact. His body might be losing its regular form, but his Inscriptions were sticking and so was his capacity to channel magic.

Plus, the time he had gotten away from the sun had allowed his healing Inscriptions to fix all damage.

“I’ve been silent long enough,” Jahaevo said, every word a thunderclap that hurt to hear. “I will be doing the universe a great favour when I finally get rid of you, Ryland. All the Calamities you’ve killed, all the Incarnations you’ve pacified, all the Great Gods you’ve set back over and over. You will pay for all your transgressions.”

Ryland was already casting spell after spell. Just like with the sun, he was layering barrier after barrier on top of each other. “[Absolute Timefort], [Awakened Avatar], [Animus Aegis], and [Eternal Bastion].”

It wouldn’t completely stop whatever the Great God intended to do, but it would at least lessen the effect. Just as it had with the sun.

“Do you want another Concept then?” Viren asked. It seemed his old audacity really was returning because his next few words shocked even Ryland. “If you get one, will you let us go?”

Ryland glanced to his left. The boy was admirably holding himself together. It was quite the turnaround. All the recent incidents had almost made it look as though he had been about to be overwhelmed. Yet, he was trying to delay things even now. He had picked up on Ryland biding his time and was still trying to keep things going until it really was time to act.

The Great God almost looked like he was considering Viren’s offer, but then he laughed. That noise made more of the crystals shatter, and the barriers around Ryland and Viren started cracking under the sheer pressure of a divinity’s laughter. The level of power was awestriking. Even a shadow of his presence was affecting them all so negatively.

“How bold of you to try and bargain when you should be begging and grovelling,” Jahaevo said. “Perhaps I should make you kneel before I annihilate you.”

It seemed their time was up. They had to act. Ryland needed to act.

“[Shrieking Survival],” Ryland incanted.

The spell empowered by his Soul Glyph of Genesis took effect in the world around them. Tiny replicas of the Soul Glyph overtook every bit of divine energy near Ryland, quickly taking on a semblance of life. One that proceeded to assault the Great God in the blink of an eye.

Taking on a Great God needed quite a few things. One of them Ryland had already explained to Viren—lots and lots of planning and preparation.

One of the other vitally important things was the element of surprise. Of course, this required a lot of other sub-elements. For instance, the ability to think quickly, the ability to be decisive, the ability to be utterly confident even when odds were deplorably low.

So it was that Jahaevo was taken aback. Not just by the fact that Ryland was fighting back, which wasn’t really that surprising, but by the way he had chosen to retaliate.

He was turning the Overrealm itself against Jahaevo, after all.

Sadly, like Ryland, the mana he had channelled and shaped into those little representations of life, those horned beasts and aggressive birds and assaulting serpents, all froze in place. Just like Ryland’s own, their reality turned to crystalline glass that they couldn’t navigate.

“Futile and worthless,” Jahaevo said, his words shattering against Ryland like shards of broken glass. “Your paltry magic can do little.”

“It’s because you’re taking advantage of his weakened state,” Viren said. “You came after Ryland took care of an entire sun and—”

He choked, then coughed out blood.

A spike of worry drove through Ryland’s heart, but he was already casting a spell to address the situation. “[Lifeward].”

His Soul Glyph of Genesis again came to the rescue, imbuing his target with vitality. Viren was soon no longer coughing blood. The taste of it clearly had him both disgusted and frightened too.

“Pathetic worms,” Jahaevo said. “Now, shatter.”

It was a testament to the Great Gods’ prowess that Ryland still hadn’t broken free from the crystalline trap he and Viren were locked into. And now, said trap was about to explode with them inside. Viren’s eyes had widened as he began experiencing the exact same pressure that had started destroying Ryland.

But it was time for the trump card to come into play.

“As ever,” Ryland said. “Your hubris will be your downfall, o Great God. [Gods’ Tangle]. [Omnipresence].”

Jahaevo didn’t look like he believed that for an instant, but Ryland didn’t need him to believe anything. All he needed was the rift, which promptly cracked open at the edge of the pocket of the Overrealm.

And through that rift, Archdragon Vorkorax burst into the Plane of the Great Gods, maw already heating up to spew fire.
