“Please proceed down the left-hand hall, Mr. Swift,” a gravelly voice said from the walls. “The meeting room will be the third door on the right.”

Sylvester strode through the passages of the Obsidian Tower, his cane clacking on the white stone floor. The upper floors of the tower were quieter than the first five, but junior mages in their colored robes could be seen everywhere. When he passed, they stepped aside, offering a small bow out of politeness.

He paid them hardly any attention.

“Finding the room would be easier with divination,” Sylvester grouched. “Or signs.”

“If you remain at the tower, you will grow familiar enough that you won’t require assistance,” Tårn replied without inflection. “And we suppress divination as a security measure. The Obsidian Tower works on many confidential projects. If we let information leak, our clients would be rightly angered.”

“Reasonable,” he accepted. Sylvester grabbed the knob of the door. “And thank you for your guidance, Tårn.”

“Serving the mages of the tower is both my purpose and my pleasure, High Warlock Swift,” Tårn accepted.

The silver-haired man pulled open the door.

It’d been three days since he’d taken up residence in the Obsidian Tower. The month of ice, Men-Glacius, was now behind them. The second day of Men-Pluvia had dawned. The Exposition was in full swing, the alchemy contest already forgotten. In its place was the battle magic tournament, where mages faced each other in duels. That violence, however, paled before all the magical wonders displayed in the park.

But none of it was Sylvester’s concern. His mind was focused on a different puzzle: how best to crack the nut called the Obsidian Tower. Part of the plan was already in place. The rest….

His crimson eyes gleamed.

“You’re Magister Swift, I take it?” A tight and irritated voice rang out.

The speaker was a tall, pale-skinned vampire. He had burgundy eyes, deeper and darker than the warlock’s jasper, and a mess of black hair, cut short so it hung around the ears. Sylvester studied him for a moment, then doffed his hat, offering a bow.

“And you must be Grigore Druță,” he replied. “Tower Head Breggen mentioned you in passing. You are the Aidonopol Tower’s expert on biological alchemy.”

Sylvester’s lips turned up in a smirk. Grigore glared, one hand tightening into a fist. High Mage Druță was a regal vampire, a bloodline with a tolerable affinity for magecraft and an innate ability to create certain classes of chimera. He wasn’t, however, particularly good at his job. Grigore was a mediocre talent when it came to actual study.

How, then, did the regal vampire cling to his position? Seniority, of course. According to Gavin, Grigore had crossed into the third consolidation eighty years ago, an achievement bought by wealth rather than natural ability. Still, a third consolidation mage was powerful. To hold a valuable asset like High Mage Druță demanded a compromise.

Tower Head Breggen didn’t hesitate to fund the vampire’s hobby. That was, unless he found an all-around superior asset. Like, say, a fourth consolidation magister.

Politics. It was impossible to escape them.

“That’s right,” Grigore Druță confirmed. “I head the department of chimeric studies. Over the years, I have produced a large number of warbeasts for the Tower’s clients.”

“Impressive,” Sylvester said blandly. The silver-haired man opened the notebook in front of him, skimming through the pages. “One would wonder why Loke is looking for a consultant, then.”

“That’s – ”

Grigore’s words cut off when the door opened a second time. A laughing voice filtered in through the hall. Loke Breggen stepped into the room, quickly followed by a red-skinned magia. It was Jarl Zayd Rahal. The great mage surveyed the room with haughty arrogance, hands clasped behind his back.

“Wonderful! Magister Swift is already here,” Tower Head Breggen said. “This is Magister Rahal, but I believe the two of you have already met.”

“We’ve crossed paths,” Sylvester confirmed. He nodded his head in the magia’s direction. “At the alchemy contest.”

The jarl’s dark gaze turned to Sylvester. Slowly, his third eye opened. Then, disdainfully, Zayd looked away, striding across the room to take the seat opposite.

Magister Rahal’s apprentice was not so polite. Delano MacIntyre entered shortly after his master. When the leshen spotted the silver-haired gentleman, his lip curled into a sneer.

“Tower Head Breggen, don’t you think it’s an insult to have him here?”

“We meet again, Mr. MacIntyre,” Sylvester said easily. With a genteel smile, he turned his eyes toward the jarl. “Your apprentice is quite rude. I think a good chiding is in order. Hell is not as well-known for its courtesies as the Federation. If he continues to mouth off like that, sooner or later he will invite trouble.”

The leshen bristled. “How dare a High Warlock speak like that in the presence of a – ”

“Quiet,” Zayd said firmly, his expression dark. “High Warlock Swift is a magister. Afford him the appropriate respect.” His gaze shifted to the dark elf. “However, my apprentice is quite right. It is understandable that Magister Swift is here, as he arrived first. But you should end your invitation immediately.”

The Great Mage’s voice carried a hint of warning.

“So you noticed,” Loke laughed.

“For weaker mages, fate magic is difficult to discern,” Sylvester explained. Then he gestured to the room. “But you are in the presence of experts.”

He smiled, his eyes briefly meeting Zayd’s gaze. Sylvester, too, had noticed the wish clinging to his body, the spell identical to the one hanging on Magister Rahal.

“Tårn, you heard them. Dissolve the wish,” Loke ordered. The dark elf took a chair at the head of the table. “I hope neither of you gentlemen was bothered by my actions. We needed a trusted expert, and a fate spell seemed suitable for the task.”

The air shifted. Sylvester felt the threads of fate uncoil as the hidden energies dissipated into the air.

“No need to apologize, Loke,” Sylvester said, leaning back in his chair. “For those on our level, wishes are a matter of convenience. I use them myself from time to time.”

“Since we’re all settled, let’s begin,” Loke Breggen said. “Grigore, please explain our issue to Zayd and Sylvester. We’ll see if either can provide insight.”

With a sour expression, the tall and reedy vampire stood.

“My team is studying a novel territory phantasm which we call ‘The Heart,’” Grigore Druță introduced. He waved at the table, where selected papers introduced the research. “On behalf of our client, we carried out a successful germination of the phantasm. We then observed how it feeds, how it interacts with the world, and how it communicates with the subordinate phantasmal bodies.”

Subordinate phantasmal bodies. What an excellent euphemism for the demons with the System they had kidnapped. Sneering internally, Sylvester skimmed through Grigore’s notes. He wasn’t reading so much as using his eyes to translate the psychic imprint. Once the documents were stored on his local System client, he used his digitized soul to consume them faster.

“With the completion of our initial study, the client requested that we return The Heart,” High Mage Druță continued. “Currently, we are attempting to safely reverse the germination process. I am quite confident we can complete this within – ”

“I assume the phantasm in question is a chimera,” Magister Rahal suddenly stated. He lowered the notes he’d been reading.

Grigore glowered. The regal vampire, however, didn’t dare to berate the Transcendent for his interruption.

“That is correct,” the tall and skinny man confirmed.

“And The Heart cannot be something your client created themselves,” Sylvester added. He crossed his legs, then leaned back in his chair, fingers tented. “Otherwise, you wouldn’t be forced to turn to outside experts.”

Grigore’s burgundy eyes burned into Sylvester’s jasper. The warlock grinned.

“You are correct,” Loke Breggen confirmed. The dark elf smiled. “There is no need to hide it. Our client took The Heart from one of their enemies. We were commissioned to complete the initial study of the phantasm. However, our client now fears that their enemy has tracked the territory phantasm to us. They want to ship The Heart to another plane immediately.”

Sylvester gave a nod of understanding. “And High Mage Druță cannot promise delivery in a timely manner.”

“I just need – ” Grigore began.

Tower Head Breggen held up a hand, silencing the vampire. “We sent out a wish, fishing for the alchemical masters best able to quickly and safely reverse the germination process. We found you.”

“I need to see the phantasm myself,” Zayd asserted.

Sylvester’s face was utterly blank. This was a request he didn’t dare make himself, lest the Obsidian Tower grow suspicious. Yet, here he was, being handed his dearest desire on a silver platter. The Obsidian Tower’s use of fate magic truly encapsulated the saying: ‘be careful what you wish for.’

“Impossible,” High Mage Druță said. “The client insisted that their project remain strictly confidential. It’s already pushing the limit to share these details with outsiders. The Heart must not be seen by anyone.”

“Arrogance,” Delano sneered, his moss-green eyes sharp. “Do you want my master’s help or not? Or are you pinning your hopes on a warlock who couldn’t win an alchemy contest against a mage five times his junior?”

“I don’t need anyone’s help,” Grigore snapped. The vampire’s burgundy eyes turned to the dark elf. “I said I could resolve all the issues within a month. There’s no need to bring these men into it.”

“The client wanted The Heart last week,” Loke returned. “They already have the subordinate phantasms and are growing annoyed at our delay. Our reputation is on the line, Grigore. I know I’m stepping on your toes, but Magisters Rahal and Swift are known experts, confirmed by fate. I will not have the client call in one of their experts instead.”

High Mage Druță’s expression tightened. Sylvester’s gaze flickered. The kidnapped members of the Bluelight were in Heaven’s hands then. Wonderful. Absolutely wonderful. Freeing them was going to be a massive headache. And that was assuming they were still on Tartarus.

The dark elf’s silver eyes swept to the red-skinned magia. “Is it absolutely necessary that you observe the territory phantasm yourself?”

“Yes,” Zayd said without hesitation. “It is obvious from reading these papers that Grigore Druță has little to no understanding of the phantasm’s inner workings. If I cannot see the beast, then I won’t risk my reputation by giving advice based on flawed data.”

The regal vampire trembled. “What are you implying?”

“That you’re incompetent,” Delano mocked. The leshen wore a gleeful smile. “I wonder, did your client demand The Heart’s return out of concern for safety, or because this idiot’s work was so shit they had to find another house?”

The regal vampire jerked at the insult. Zayd, however, didn’t so much as twitch. Either the magia didn’t care how Delano behaved, or the great mage was using his apprentice as a foil.

“How dare you,” Grigore spat, drawing himself to his full height.

“Enough,” Tower Head Breggen interrupted. The dark elf turned to the warlock. “And what are your thoughts, Sylvester?”

“I’m in agreement with Magister Rahal,” he replied evenly. “High Mage Druță’s specialty is the macro combination of phantasmal bodies. However, reversing the germination of the phantasm may well demand an understanding of the beast’s microstructures. Chimera can be delicate, especially when forced through a process for which they were never designed.”

Grigore glared. Sylvester held up a hand to indicate he wasn’t done.

“Furthermore, I cannot help but note that much of this data was recorded by Grigore’s assistants,” he said, offering the vampire a polite smile, “who clearly don’t grasp the fine details.”

Loke turned to the vampire. Grigore grimaced. The problem Sylvester had noted was very real. Due to spirit speech, written works relied on the scribe’s personal understanding. If the scribe didn’t know the material well, it was very easy to leave out key details.

Of course, that problem could be resolved by reviewing the original notes. But High Mage Druță surely knew Sylvester was offering him a polite fiction.

“An oversight, Tower Head Breggen,” Grigore said through gritted teeth.

“Then I see no reason to hold back,” the dark elf decided. Loke stood. “Magister Rahal, Magister Swift, follow me. I will show you The Heart. I do hope the two of you will be discreet.”

“As project head, I must file my objection,” High Mage Druță interjected.

“Enough,” Loke interrupted. “I don’t want to hear another word about it. We approached the magisters. They didn’t approach us. Both are known residents of the Fey Federation with long histories. They are not men with whom our client would be concerned.”

…

Well, Sylvester supposed that was technically true. He was most properly a woman with whom their client would be very, very concerned.

The dark elf took the lead, Zayd only a few steps behind. The red-skinned magia strode out of the room, both hands clasped behind his back. Grigore was next. Sylvester trailed the group, walking slowly to give himself enough time to scribble a message onto the mirror pad hidden in his soul.

Delano was waiting for the warlock out in the hall. The taller leshen sneered down at Sylvester, his moss-green eyes narrowed into a glare.

“Give it up,” Delano snapped. “You can’t compete with my master.”

Sylvester paused in his steps, crimson eyes turning to the apprentice. He let out a low chuckle.

“Ah, what desperation for affirmation, Mr. MacIntyre,” he replied. He raised his cane and shook it gently. “I think you will find the gap between Class VI/High and Class VII/Low is much smaller than you imagine. With your boldness, I find myself curious. Is your master practiced in the art of battle magic? I dearly hope so for his sake, given how freely you waggle your tongue.”

“You think too highly of yourself, old man,” Delano scoffed. “Magister Rahal will teach you the immensity of heaven and earth.”

The leshen strode off. Sylvester shook his head. What a fool. What a fool indeed. Delano was utterly blind to the three worms of rot he had planted near the leshen’s heart. As a senior figure, Sylvester lamented the naivety of youth. The world was cold and cruel. Why were the young always forced to learn this the hard way?

Wearing an amused smile, he picked up the pace. In his soul, a page burned.

Elroy,

By happenstance, I’ve been invited to examine the dungeon core. We won’t find a better opportunity than this. Prepare your forces to attack, then send a message when you’re ready. Also, make sure to inform Gavin. It’s time to start planting blood curses among the Tower’s key personnel.

If anything changes, I will let you know.Good hunting.

It was a five-minute journey from the eighth floor to the fifth basement of the tower. Layers of security had been placed underground. Keys and passwords were required to bypass the protections. In addition to a handful of combat-ready mages and a couple of warrior-class demons, the Obsidian Tower had an abundance of golems. Specifically, on each basement floor there was a giant construct golem that looked like a spider. They were powerful, Class IV machines. Not only were they armed and armored with spell catalysts, each was equipped with three to five talismans.

The sheer firepower present was enough to threaten Sylvester himself. The spider golems weren’t much of a challenge, of course. But if he were careless, he might die.

His fingers itched to tear one of them apart. What an exciting piece of equipment.

The warlock didn’t linger for long. Soon they reached the fifth floor basement. Here, the Obsidian Tower had built a special laboratory, all to study The Heart. The pulsating core was found in the room’s center, its thick, fleshy limbs extending toward the walls, floor, and ceiling. Some of the wooden roots pierced through stone to reach deep into the earth. Others wrapped around exposed, wooden ether channels to feast on the energy within.

Grigore had learned the hard way that dungeon cores were meant to interface with an artificial structure.

“Fascinating,” Sylvester said out loud. “There are no obvious inclusions. It would be difficult to tell that this is a chimera through visual inspection.”

He was, indeed, fascinated. Every dungeon in the Cloud Island Wilderness had been designed by Sylvester personally. The Heart, however, had been created by the Promethean cult Last Beacon. Living flesh was an unusual choice. As of eight years ago, nearly all the dungeons used multifaceted crystal instead. That composition allowed for better ether management and greater processing power.

This wasn’t to say a flesh-based structure was without merit. Living hearts were better-suited for organic growth. Sylvester had tried the design again recently, when prototyping a dungeon with living veins, arteries, and tunnels. But Last Beacon didn’t use his recipe. If they had, the Obsidian Tower’s basement would’ve been transformed into a labyrinth.

“It’s a high-level synthetic creation verging on phantasmal genesis,” Zayd agreed. The magia strolled around The Heart, his fingers trailing over roots which were as much wood as flesh. “The nether code has undergone drastic modification. A work of such complexity demands many years of effort. If you can duplicate the creature, I would like a copy for my collection.”

“I will put in a word with the client,” Loke Breggen offered. “But I don’t think they will be willing to share.”

The dungeon core wasn’t alone in the room. Large, mechanical instruments were strapped around each of The Heart’s roots. Desks and side rooms were available, places where the mages working under Grigore could observe and study. Near the main door, which was a heavily enchanted slab of iron, was positioned a spider golem. There were even chutes in the ceiling for figment golems, should they prove necessary.

“Make way,” Grigore ordered, gesturing toward the junior mages. “We have two magisters who want to look at The Heart.”

Drawing closer to the dungeon core, Sylvester tapped one of the metal bands strapped around the root with his cane. “What is all this equipment for?”

“We use them to read The Heart’s ethereal interactions,” one of the junior mages piped up. “It took several weeks, but we isolated the method by which the phantasm communicates with its subordinate phantasmal bodies. Unfortunately, all we could do was trace the communications. We have yet to decipher what was being sent.”

“Quiet,” Grigore snapped. “They only need to know about The Heart’s physiological structure, its recovery mechanisms, and the germination process.”

“Interesting,” Sylvester commented.

He set a hand on a root near the beating heart. Sylvester didn’t even hide his attempt to probe the dungeon with ki and magic. What did go unseen was the blue screen that popped up in front of him. A quick security handshake and he was logged in. A few seconds later, Sylvester had The Heart running a series of self-diagnostics.

“If I stay at the Tower, I might try my hand at cracking the code,” he mused out loud.

“It would be wonderful if you did,” Tower Head Breggen encouraged. “Do either of you gentlemen require anything from us?”

“All this equipment has to be removed,” Sylvester said, waving at the items attached to the core. “They’re interfering with the phantasm’s natural state. And they’ll only get in the way when we reverse the germination.”

“That’s completely unnecessary,” High Mage Druță said. “Those instruments do nothing more than read The Heart’s ethereal interactions. They won’t influence your divinations at all.”

“They’re rife with causality magic,” Sylvester said in disbelief.

It was getting ridiculous. Yes, his request was agenda-driven. With the instruments gone, Sylvester could safely communicate with the dungeon core by remote. But his demand was very reasonable. Grigore was off his rocker if he thought causality-based sensors wouldn’t distort their divinations. In fact, those instruments were doing worse than that! They were interfering with the dungeon’s ether cycle!

“If you’d read the damn papers, we wouldn’t be in here violating the contract and wasting our time,” High Mage Druță said in retort, his burgundy eyes burning.

“Grotesque incompetence,” Jarl Rahal commented. He turned to the dark elf. “You’d be well-advised to remove Grigore from this project immediately. Even passive sensors can engender changes in the environment, distorting our understanding of the specimen. What we have here are active instruments.”

“How dare – ”

“Quiet!” Tower Head Breggen said, slashing his hand through the air angrily. “Recall, Grigore, that you are speaking to a magister and a great mage who is a foreign noble.” The dark elf put on a pleasant expression. “Since Magisters Rahal and Swift are in agreement, we will have the instruments removed. You over there, hop to it.”

Several of the junior mages got to work. Sylvester stood off to the side, watching. The moment the sensors were gone, he pinged the dungeon interface, reading the diagnostics.

From what he could see, they’d never succeeded. But knowing that for sure would take a much more serious review than he could muster in a short period of time.

As he worked, Sylvester kept an eye on those around him.

Zayd stood back while the junior mages disassembled the equipment. Only when he was satisfied the job was done did the jarl approach the dungeon core anew. Magister Rahal cast an aggressive series of spells, probing for secrets. The Heart pulsed in response, its faint intellect recognizing the intrusion.

A sly smile tugged at Sylvester’s lips. With the dungeon core’s database rebuilt, he sent a piece of software across the network, then executed it on The Heart.

The dungeon went to work. Roots started to shrivel, blood essence drawn from its appendages into the core. It didn’t take more than a minute before the effect was obvious.

“What did you do?”

High Mage Druță’s angry roar startled half those present. Magister Rahal turned his head in the regal vampire’s direction. The magia had one hand behind his back even as he worked magic with the other.

“I said, what did you do?” Grigore repeated, stomping toward the great mage.

“What did he do?” Delano sneered. The leshen stepped into the regal vampire’s path. “How dare a mere third consolidation mage speak to my master like that? Get on your knees and slap yourself three times, and maybe he will forgive you.”

Grigore’s eyes blazed. Sylvester let out a soft chuckle. He could see the energy stirring in the vampire’s body, ready to strike. Right before he could, the warlock stepped forward, setting a hand on High Mage Druță’s shoulder.

“Calm down, Grigore, and take a closer look,” he said smoothly. “The roots are withering because The Heart is drawing blood essence into its central core. What we are seeing is most likely an instinctive response to danger.”

The pale-faced vampire trembled with rage, never noticing the worms of rot wriggling through his flesh. Teeth gritted, Grigore turned. Then, angrily, he shrugged Sylvester’s hand off his shoulder.

“Don’t touch me,” High Mage Druță snapped. The tall, thin man stomped over to the core. “I will check this myself to ensure no damage was done.”

The silver-haired man stepped back, hands raised to show he’d meant no ill. Slipping Grigore a curse had been tricky. Spells like worms of rot became more elusive as the caster’s level rose, but their rate of improvement didn’t match the speed at which a demon’s senses sharpened. Grigore was a skilled mage. He’d be far more sensitive than Baron Naopte.

And Zayd Rahal would be more sensitive still. With crimson eyes, he studied the magia. Sylvester analyzed how best to strike the great mage down.

“My apologies for Grigore’s behavior,” Tower Head Breggen said, sliding up beside the warlock. “He has been irritable as of late. And thank you for calming him down. I’m sure Grigore will thank you later as well, though definitely not in words.”

“I have always preferred a professional environment,” Sylvester replied smoothly as two worms of rot slid down the inside of his pants. The curses wiggled over the dark elf’s shoes before climbing him like a mountain. “Drama only complicates matters.”

“I truly hope you stay at our tower then, Magister Swift,” Loke said with a smile.

Sylvester gave a polite nod. Inside his soul, dark inky words filled a blank page. The script was written in Elroy’s sharp and clear lettering. He wondered which mortal language the werewolf had used. Such distinct letters certainly hadn’t been taught in Loge Sombre.

Nadia Wielock informs me that her forces are already in position. Gavin says he’ll move after we start the attack. As I write, Les Griffes strike at the Dinții Adâncului. Be at the ready, Mrs. Swallows.

Excellent. It was all Sylvester could do to resist the wicked smile that threatened to split his lips. His hands played happily on the handle of his cane.

Officially, the Thirteen Cauldrons was launching the attack. Les Griffes du Gouffre was a criminal gang. If they were fighting in the streets, most of their men would be arrested by the guard. Nadia’s coven, however, had political cover. The mountain witch had allies in the Dark Parliament who were willing to back her. What’s more, while legally they were in the wrong, socially they were in the right. Even if Demon King Zagan was willing to go against that political bloc, there was the International Exposition of Magic to consider.

The Exposition had brought a huge number of mages to Aidonopol. Some of those mages were witches. The last thing anyone wanted was a flight of one hundred thousand angry witches, especially when several of those witches might be archdemons themselves.

The walls shuddered. Rumble, rumble. A sound, like distant thunder, passed through the stone.

Loke’s silver eyes turned up. “Tårn, please explain the sound I just heard.”

“Tower Head Breggen,” the tower spirit replied. “I regret to inform you that we are under – ”

Tårn’s voice cut out in mid-sentence. A great pulse of law magic swept through the Obsidian Tower. The spell froze Tårn’s communication network solid. Gavin’s jammers had taken effect. If everything went to plan, the goblin had loaded the package first. In which case, things were about to get exciting.

Sylvester broke out in a grin.

The Thirteen Cauldrons didn’t stand a chance against the Obsidian Tower. Loke’s mages outnumbered Nadia’s witches. The walls, the defensive enchantments, and the golem army only served to swing things further in the tower’s favor. To tilt the odds, Sylvester and Gavin had the job of disrupting the fortress from within. Nadia’s army of chimera would sway things further.

“Magisters Rahal and Swift, I must head upstairs immediately,” the dark elf said. “Grigore, make sure The Heart is well-guar– ….”

Phoot, phoot, phoot, phoot, phoot.

Loke’s voice trailed off as crystal orbs fell from the chutes in the ceiling. The spheres paused in midair, stopping at chest height. Each orb was heavily enchanted. Strips of metal wrapped the spheres as though they were armor. Ether flowed through the metal, thin channels glowing bright.

Earth magic stirred.

In a blink, stone was conjured from nothingness. Rocky bodies formed around the golem cores. Legs came first, then chests, arms, and heads. In two seconds, the transformation was complete. Six stone knights, molten halberds in hand, stood in various corners of the room.

They were not alone.

A metal shape stirred to life. The legs of the spider golem unfolded, the construct’s body rising. The machine was massive, weighing ten times more than an ordinary demon. Lines of red light crawled over its body, flows of ether fed into the enchantments by its core. Sylvester could feel it breathe, primordial energies sucked in from the surrounding air.

The metal spider turned. A shimmering bubble of force snapped into place. On the construct’s back, two turrets turned. Molten metal formed inside the tubes.

Loke’s silver eyes went wide. “Guard the – ”

Tower Head Breggen never finished his shout. Two pods on the spider golem’s back sprang open, flinging out a pair of garnet talismans. The stones shattered, unraveling into an inferno. Runes wrapped the raging flames, shaping them into a pair of blazing balls which streaked across the room.

Sylvester was already moving.

Time slowed when he called upon his mirrored lake sutra. Aware before anyone of the coming danger, he flashed his hand through a series of seals. Worms of rot slithered, hiding in the recesses of the room. On his lips rang runes entirely different. The great swirl of water and realm he released helped to disguise the faint signature of blood.

“~■, ~■, ~■~■.”

A pair of barrier frames took form, followed by an aqua shell. As water rushed around him, Sylvester stepped back. Space twisted. The silver-haired gentleman seemed to slither as he suddenly appeared in front of the dungeon core.

B-BOOM!

The meteor talismans unleashed their fury. Twin detonations washed the world with fire. Searing, orange-red flames sizzled when they met their elemental opposite. The energies canceled, annihilating Sylvester’s defense even as water snuffed the flame. Steam wrapped the warlock. Loke, Grigore, and Zayd were almost as quick. All three had conjured defenses of their own. The leshen, Delano, was a bit slower, having called forth a shield of wood too flimsy to withstand the assault.

Luckily for him, Magister Rahal had neatly placed himself in front of his apprentice. The shadow cast by his tall figure absorbed half the force.

As the fire faded, every golem charged toward the mages. Molten bolts shot from the turrets on the spider construct’s back. Loke skipped to the side, evading one molten streak before catching another on a glacial scale. Zayd took a balanced approach. First, he secured his defense with a sphere of water. Next, he released a wave of earth ether. Five stone teeth erupted from the ground.

Grave maw’s mighty jaws snapped closed on the metal spider.

But not before the golem could react. The metal construct triggered a talisman in retort. A shell of water suddenly wrapped its giant frame. Stone smashed through the clear liquid, releasing a wild spray. The teeth crushed into the glassy barrier next, force magic shattering. Finally, grave maw gripped the golem’s steel body, metal twisting its grip.

Yet, the steel spider survived. As the rocky figments fell away, the construct launched itself across the room. Too late. Its brief stagger had given Magister Rahal all the time he required. The magia spoke, his hand rising in an upward gesture. A spike of wood slammed the golem straight up into the basement ceiling, the thick column piercing through the golem’s core.

“~■, ~■.”

Left hand still in motion, Sylvester cast spells of his own. His cane flicked left. Blazing flames flew across the room. BOOM! A fiery explosion shattered a pair of stone knights. Another swish sent a buzzsaw of air screaming to Sylvester’s right. The maximized wind scythe tore through another set of figment golems, hardened stone providing little in the way of defense.

He glanced left and right, the runes for glacial scale frozen on his tongue. All clear. The golems were defeated. But this didn’t stop him from finishing his last bit of magic. Fire, earth, and water coiled around the Disk of the Depths, catalyzed into the element abyss. The spell took form, dark scales crawling over Sylvester’s pants and suit.

“Well, that was exciting,” he said after a silent moment. He gestured. Water ether moved according to his will, snuffing the flames feasting on the wooden desks and the dungeon core’s roots.

“Of all the amateurish things, how did you fools lose control of your golems?” Delano spat.

Tower Head Breggen gritted his teeth. “I must head to the upper floors and see to the defense. Grigore, keep The Heart safe.”

Without waiting for a response, the dark elf ran out of the room. The tower rumbled. Sylvester could hear the sounds of fighting on the next floor up through the door and the ceiling. A faint smirk teased at the warlock’s lips. Whether the victims were golems or mages, it was victory from his perspective.

“Don’t you three dare cause any trouble,” Grigore warned.

The regal vampire hurriedly tried to put the laboratory in order. The room was a wreck. Blackened scars were left on the floor, the walls and the ceiling. Tables, chairs, equipment, and precious paperwork had been reduced to scraps and embers. Only three of the six figment golems had endured the onslaught, their cores retreating through the chutes.

A pity. Sylvester had been aiming to destroy the golems permanently.

Under High Mage Druță’s direction, the five surviving junior mages put the room back into order. Finally free, Grigore turned his attention to the magisters and The Heart.

“Fortunately, it wasn’t seriously harmed,” the regal vampire breathed out in relief.

“I protected the core with my body and my barrier,” Sylvester said. “Unfortunately, the roots took quite a beating.”

While the pulsating heart of the dungeon remained undamaged, the exposed roots had been splintered, then blackened into charcoal. From those wounds oozed thick, sap-like blood. His crimson eyes tightened. The limbs had to be severed quickly, or they’d weaken the dungeon’s core.

But that, in and of itself, was a tricky process.

B-boom. Rumble, rumble.

The sounds of fighting grew more distant and less intense. As Sylvester had planned, Gavin had uploaded a hack into Tårn’s network before turning on the jammers. They had scrambled the golem’s IFF, resulting in a defense system that struck at defenders and attackers alike. Until the jammers were removed, Tårn had no means to fix the mess.

Hopefully, Nadia and her Thirteen Cauldrons would make good use of that time.

“We must excise the carbonized wounds before trying to heal them,” Zayd noted. The magia’s hands were clasped behind his back as though nothing had happened. “Unless verified, I would not trust a synthetic phantasm to heal anything but superficial injuries on its own.”

“No one touches the territory phantasm until Tower Head Breggen is back,” Grigore said in retort. “I don’t care what you think of your expertise. I’ve worked with The Heart longer than you have. I won’t allow any form of experimental treatment.”

Sylvester stepped forward before the argument could turn into a fight.

“We respect your experience, Grigore,” he soothed. Sylvester put on a friendly smile. “But I have a question. That root over there appears stunted. Did you, perhaps, cut it short previously?”

Zayd’s gaze swayed, focusing on the wooden limb that had grown toward the roof. Currently, the root was wrapped around a strangely low ether channel. Sylvester knew well that his dungeon cores had flaws. One of the core faults was their crystal spindles. Those threads spread through the roots. Once damaged, they never healed right. The only fix was juice from a blood fruit along with a bit of manual intervention.

Of course, High Mage Druță didn’t know that.

“We did,” Grigore confirmed, his expression turning tight. “That was before we learned The Heart was meant to interface with an artificial ether transmission network.”

“Then, I fear Magister Rahal is correct,” Sylvester concluded. “If left to heal naturally, this synthetic phantasm will be crippled. Indeed, if it continues to bleed, it might die. Your position on experimental treatment is reasonable. But alas, it is not tenable. If we do nothing, a worse outcome awaits.”

High Mage Druță pressed his lips into a thin line. The tall and pale man looked unhappy with where he was going.

“I’m willing to listen to your proposal,” Grigore managed after a long moment.

Zayd Rahal gave a nod of appreciation, then stepped forward. The great mage pinched the root, crumbling carbonized wood between his fingers. Beneath the ash could be seen thin, shimmering threads. Sylvester’s eyes narrowed. Oh? What a brilliant man. He found it so fast.

“I believe this is the tissue of concern,” Magister Rahal explained. “Compared to the other essences, these threads are more complex. Further, if you look where they are splintered by the damage, you see no sign of recovery. The bulk pulp of the root, by contrast, is healing.”

“What you have is a suspicion,” Grigore replied. “Unless you have a way to restore the spindles, your observation is worthless.”

“I think I know what Magister Rahal is trying to say,” Sylvester interrupted. “If we can isolate the vulnerable tissue, we can skip the intervening steps and proceed to a reverse germination. This eliminates the need to heal the roots entirely.”

Incidentally, that was the best course of action. Unless the Obsidian Tower happened to have a blood fruit on hand.

“Madness,” High Mage Druță spat. “You want to risk The Heart on a careless proposition?”

“It’s hardly careless,” Magister Rahal replied. “If we dissolve the wood pulp, we can use it to brew a potion. With that, we can sustain The Heart regardless of injury for two days, possibly three. The core of this phantasm has already withdrawn the bulk of its blood essence, which has created the perfect environment for a reverse germination. Eliminating the majority of the root tissue while preserving the spindles will only further the outcome.”

“And what will you do if the reversal process fails?” Grigore asked. “If the spindles are as important as you say, The Heart will surely die when the potion runs out.”

“Not necessarily,” Sylvester said. “If, after a day, we cannot find a path to reverse the germination, we can facilitate the recovery of the roots. Zayd’s plan is bold, but I like it.”

“I don’t,” Grigore returned.

“My only concern is the reverse germination,” Zayd said bluntly. “I have no desire to stay for an extended consultation. Fundamentally, you have two choices. First, we can dissolve and reform the roots. The Heart will require a month to recover, to ensure its health and stability. Or, we can take advantage of the situation and secure the phantasmal core immediately.”

The red-skinned magia turned to the vampire, his gaze firm. High Mage Druță glared.

“I think I have made myself clear,” the regal vampire declared.

Ding!

The System’s bell rang in Sylvester’s head. What a beautiful sound. The worms of rot had finished their incubation, which meant he didn’t need to entertain these fellows a moment longer.

“Gentlemen, gentlemen,” Sylvester inserted, wearing a wild grin. “Regardless of which path we choose, we must deal with the issue of the damaged roots. And, as it just so happens, I have a lightning element spell that I think would be excellent for dissolving the wood pulp.” A bit of ether stirred around Sylvester as he dramatically swirled his cane. “The basic sequence goes like – ~■!”

Lightning ether surged out of his palace, giving an instant’s warning. Runes joined with the energy, sending ragged chains of electricity crawling up his cane. Magister Rahal’s reactions were impressively swift, his ki hardening on instinct. BANG! With the tilt of the cane’s handle, brilliant light sparked. A jagged streak crossed a single meter before crashing into Zayd’s chest.

The lance scattered off an invisible barrier.

The great mage’s armor served its function, sparks cascading over the magia’s robes in a thousand streamers of light. But the force behind Sylvester’s lightning lance was too great. The barrier shattered like glass. Electric threads crawled through the gaps, perforating Zayd’s torso.

Snap!

Thankfully, his assassination plan had a backup. The warlock snapped the fingers of his left hand. Magic pulsed. Worms of rot suddenly came to life. The vicious figments tore through flesh, feasting on the blood of Grigore, Delano, and Zayd alike.

All three men staggered.

Sylvester seized the opening like a vicious predator. He stepped forward with force, ki spiraling through his body. The physical energy rose through his feet, his chest, then his arms. “■.” As his cane shot toward Zayd’s face, he added a flood of wind ether and a coil of seventy-four runes.

Wind scythe. Cra-chink!

He was an instant too slow. Before Sylvester’s cane struck, a plane of frosty ice took form in front of him. Wind hit the frozen water. The warlock jerked to the side, his scythe ricocheting off the glacial scale’s surface. Despite his dodge, his spiral thrust continued forward, smashing through the cracked and gouged surface of Zayd’s shield.

But only barely.

Zayd stumbled back, his fumbled retreat growing graceful with each following step. The jarl glared, his tongue casting words unheard. “■ – ”

Sylvester smirked. “~■.”

Howling air wrapped the transformed body of Chrome Tempest. A second wind scythe flew free as the silver-haired warlock made the smallest swish. As the buzzsaw screamed forward, Zayd triggered some catalyst on his person. Water swirled around the magia just in time to intercept the second scythe.

Spla-lush!

Water scattered in a broad spray, deflecting Sylvester’s magical blade. Lightning crackled again, this time cascading along Zayd’s staff. Thanks to the mirrored lake sutra, Sylvester had a single moment to contemplate his reaction.

“– ■!”

Tank it!

He made his decision in a snap. Sylvester’s worms of rot had wreaked havoc. Delano stumbled back, spitting up a mouthful of blood. Pale and pained, the leshen clutched his chest. Zayd’s apprentice was a dead man. He just didn’t know it yet. High Mage Druță likewise staggered, reeling as worms ravaged his flesh, but Sylvester judged he would survive.

For safety’s sake, Zayd had to die before Grigore got his feet back underneath him.

BANG!

Then the bolt struck. Terrible light tore across the room once more. The lightning lance met Sylvester’s hardened ki, tearing straight through it. Next was Spring Blossom’s barrier, the mystic protection augmented by a layer of black scales. Unfortunately, the warlock’s armor was far too weak. The barrier shattered, triggering the effect: safety glass. A second pulse of protection echoed. It wasn’t enough. A third of the energy pierced straight through Sylvester’s flesh, too scattered to reach his core.

Teeth gritted against the pain, he stepped back. Space twisted around him, placing the silver-haired man neatly on the laboratory's ceiling. Runes sang on his lips. A third wind scythe was added to the fray. “~■.”

With a grimace, Zayd conjured another shield of ice. He had no choice. Even as Sylvester’s third blade whirled forward, the first had finished ricocheting around the room to hook in from another angle. Spla-Crack! The two scythes struck at nearly the same instant. The third blade cracked off Zayd’s frozen shield while the first tore through his weakened aqua shell, only to be parried by the magia’s staff.

Yet, the chain of destruction wasn’t done. The second of Sylvester’s three blades bounced off the ceiling to fall down on Zayd’s head like an axe.

He didn’t see if it hit, because right then Sylvester finished his final handseal. Great flows of ether ran through the Disk of the Depths, catalyzed into the advanced element abyss. A terrible hand of black covered half the room. Then, with brutal abandon, it crushed the magia to death.

And not a moment too soon.

A shock wave of psychic energy smashed in from Sylvester’s right. In the last second, the warlock hardened the essence of his mind. The blast blew through him nonetheless. He staggered, stars dancing in his eyes. Grigore Druță had finally found his feet. The regal vampire threw himself into the air, a blade of blood forming in his hand.

“■,” Sylvester scoffed.

Crack! A wall of frosty ice appeared in front of the warlock. The vampire’s blood sword sent cracks running through the ice. Then – fluupt, fluupt – two scythes swung in from odd angles, slicing Grigore into parts.

“That’s why you should never slack on the combat practice,” Sylvester tutted toward the dissolving corpse.

He took a step forward, landing lightly on the floor. Screaming wind flickered around the room, harvesting the remaining lives. As the spells dissipated, silence draped the basement laboratory. The only thing heard was the muted rumbles of the battle above and the steady th-thump of the dungeon’s giant heart.

“I do love it when a plan comes together,” Sylvester chuckled. Cracking his knuckles, he got to work.
