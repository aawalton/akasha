Barrels of holy water were wheeled out before the orderly formation of vanguard troops waiting patiently. They were still some distance off from being a true elite force, but Aphexia’s and Gorn’s training was starting to shine through.

“Listen up. Coat your weapons in the water. This will prepare you for the fight ahead. We’ll have porters bring the barrels along with us in case we require more,” Carmack said.

“You heard him, men!” Commander Gorn said. “Get moving!”

The vanguard troops sprang into action at the word of their commander. But it wasn’t chaotic or disorderly. They formed lines and began coating their weapons in the holy water, stepping out of the way once they were done.

“You’ve done a fine job,” Carmack commented as he admired their coordination.

“Just a little something I picked up from back home. We were always bringing in untrained labor to help with the harvest. Without procedures, it becomes a mess quickly. People working atop one another, getting in each other's way. I just applied those same principles here that kept us working efficiently.”

To think a farm boy taught a military force organization skills. It seems Lydrus was in quite a rough state before it fell.

Each man was armed with a spear, sword, and shield. They were still armored with their chainmail, since the salamander scale suits were not yet ready. Not only that, but they all wore aether crystals in their helms to provide light.

“So, you’re dragging me along as well?” Aphexia said, stepping up to Carmack’s side.

“I intended to, yes. Unless you’d rather remain here?”

“Please, no. I need to get out of this place. I think I’m going crazy being locked up in here.”

“Good. I’m not sure what we’ll find as we delve deeper into the tunnels. Your presence will be appreciated. I want to give the men a chance to prove themselves, but if things go bad, we’ll have to step in.”

“Yeah, yeah. I know.”

“Alright then, let’s move out!” Carmack called out, and Gorn ordered his men into action.

Returning to the zombie-infested tunnels, Carmack lowered the wall he had built with a cast of “Earthshaper.”

The undead creatures turned their dulled, lifeless gazes toward them the moment that the tunnel was opened, and began their meandering charge.

“Can your men handle this, Commander?”

“We’ll be fine, won’t we, men?”“Yes, sir!”

“Formation, march!”

The tightly packed spearmen pressed forward, but this time, when their spears met the zombies, they hissed and sizzled. Smoke rose from the walking corpses as they impaled themselves on the spears. Within seconds, burned wounds appeared across their bluish-gray skin, and the zombies began to fall to their true deaths.

But the mindless undead were not deterred by losses. The spearmen jabbed and pushed, rotating between one another as they tired. The pile of lifeless corpses grew with every second that passed. But their numbers seemed limitless. Slowly, the line of spearmen was pushed back against the relentless undead horde funneling out from further down the tunnels.

Nonetheless, Commander Gorn’s visage remained unchanged and unbothered. He was stern and confident, commanding his tiring men to retreat to the back as he sent fresh reserves in to take their place.

The formation operated like clockwork, compensating for the skills and levels they lacked.

Once fresh men arrived at the front, they pushed forward once more. The zombies fell back, slaughtered en masse against the spears and skills unleashed by troops with full mana pools.

This back and forth continued for some time. The men taking breaks consumed stamina and mana potions as they recovered at the back of the formation.

The troops called an array of skills to aid them in battle, from spectrally enlarged weapons, to calling upon holy powers, and even strength and stamina bursts. The variety of skills constantly flashing to life as the vanguard warriors displayed their techniques against the unyielding passes of zombies was a far cry from what they had once been.

They looked like a real military formation, not just ragtag guards who could not be trusted to hold their positions against goblin raids.

They still need work, but there can no longer be any doubt that these men have the potential to be something special, Carmack mused as he watched on.

An hour into the battle, the zombie horde was finally starting to thin. Noticing this, Commander Gorn pressed his advantage. Calling for a renewed offensive, the vanguard pushed forward amidst orderly shouts from sergeants responding to Gorn’s orders.

Pushing forward, the spearmen split the mindless zombies. Once their numerical advantage had been mitigated, it was easy to cut the horde apart. The mindless zombies marched into their weapons, and vanguard squads surrounded the breakaway gatherings of undead and meticulously dispatched them.

No longer needing to crush the zombie skulls, it was almost too easy for them to deal with the zombies. Whether by sword or spear, the undead creatures could barely take one or two strikes before falling victim to the holy water coating them.

Pushing the horde back through tunnels and chambers, Carmack and Aphexia trailed behind them, leaving Commander Gorn to his job.

“Okay, they’re not entirely useless,” Aphexia said.

“The two of you have done well,” Carmack approvingly stroked his chin. “I must congratulate you. These men have clearly improved in strides, and you’re as much to thank for that as our honorable commander.”

“Bah. This is more discipline than technique. Wait until they fight an enemy that doesn’t just mindlessly wander forward.”

“Perhaps. But training inspires confidence, and confidence turns morale.”

Aphexia glanced up at Carmack. Maybe he was right, but she certainly wasn’t about to admit it.

The battle continued, transforming from a battle into an industrial culling. The vanguard worked their way through the adjoining tunnels, putting down the straggling undead.

But as they walked deeper, the strange mist that hung heavily in the tunnels dragged Carmack’s attention back to its otherworldliness. Turning to where he felt the mana-rich mist permeating from, he sniffed and began to walk.

“What’s with that look on your face?” Aphexia said.

“This way,” Carmack said. “Come, follow.”

“Huh? Where are we going?”

Aphexia hated it when the hero was cryptic. It brought up a strange feeling… excitement. She felt like a young adventurer again, following her old mentor into a dungeon.

“Just follow me.”

“Carmack!”

“Fine. I sense something up ahead. This mist is full of mana. If I had to guess, I’d say it is related to these undead creatures somehow.”

“That wasn’t so hard, was it?”

Carmack raised a hand, and she fell silent without thinking. Did he just silence me? The nerve of that man!

“Look,” Carmack pointed ahead.

The mist thickened into an impenetrable cloud of white that completely soaked up the crystal light. Passing through the fog, several zombies lunged for them in the adjoining tunnel, but were easily dispatched.

Carmack raised a hand and closed his eyes. The magic within the mist was palpable, but also disturbing. It hung with the essence of death.

“Do you feel that?” Aphexia asked. “This… is this what you were sensing?” A tremor of dread traced the adventurer’s spine for the first time in years. The mist brought on a terror that reminded her of that dungeon… the one where he had died.

“Yes, I have sensed it since we reached these tunnels. It’s rich with mana.”

“Maybe we should head back. Who knows what’s ahead?”

Carmack picked up on a subtle quiver in Aphexia’s tone that betrayed a hidden fear.

“We’re fine, Aphexia. Trust me,” Carmack said, extending a hand.

“You think I’m a child?”

“Sorry… I just thought…”

“Let’s just get on with it!”

“Okay.”

Passing into the next chamber, Carmack led her with total confidence. Irritation swelled within. She couldn’t believe that she had let her guard down and that he had noticed.

Eyes widened, Carmack’s mouth fell agape. Within the next room, writhing undead were baked into the walls themselves, their hands and mouths moving and groaning. The fog masked much of the room, but with a simple hand wave, it could be cleared to get a better sight of the undead.

“What is this horrible place?” Aphexia muttered in disbelief, forgetting her internally squabbling.

“Foul magic,” Carmack shook his head. “Necromancy.”

Crushing groaning skulls under his boots as he entered the chamber, Carmack made for the room’s center. Every inch of the room seemed to be crafted from the very undead themselves as if they had entered the heart of undeath.

Motes of hazy purple light drifted up from the room’s center, penetrating the thick white fog. As they got closer, it appeared the purple was coming from a skeleton buried in the earth.

“A corpse?” Aphexia said.

“It appears so,” Carmack raised his hand over the corpse and channeled magic into it. Even in death, his mana struggled against the deathly power of the corpse.

This… whoever this was had extraordinary power.

He realized immediately what was happening. There was no spell or any mastermind behind the zombies. The dead were being raised by the corpse’s residual deathly mana itself. However, he was alive, and whoever this was wasn’t. If he really wanted to overwhelm and destroy the corpse’s residual power, he could.

But did he want to?

“Aphexia, would you mind calling Commander Gorn in?”

“I’m your messenger now?”

“Please, Aphexia, would you mind calling him in?”

Aphexia frowned. Even the way his deep voice carried her name sounded pleasant, and it made her want to shove straw into her ears.

“Damn it. Why’d you have to go being so polite?” Aphexia sighed and turned to leave. “Fine, I’ll get him.”

As Aphexia left, Carmack strolled across the chamber. With an extended hand, he magically inspected both the glowing skeleton and the room itself. There were no artifacts or magical items. The skeleton possessed no core nor means of producing mana, and yet it did.

To leave such potent mana behind after death… It’s truly amazing.

“Hero, you called?” Commander Gorn said as he entered. “What in the goddess’s lifegiving bosom is this?”

“Amazing, isn’t it?”

“That’s one way to put it. It’d be horrifying.”

“Yes, that too. By the way, how have your men been dealing with the undead? Have they grown from it? Gained levels?”

“Grown? Yeah, of course. Everyone I’ve spoken to has gained at least one level. It’s been great for them actually. We’ve almost completed our sweeps of the tunnels, as well. They’ll probably gain a few more levels by the time they’re done.”

“Good to hear. And did you take any kills?”

“Only a couple.”

“And?”

“I gained a small, depreciated amount of experience. I’m only an early Iron rank. I doubt they provide any experience for mid or high Iron rank. What is this all about, Hero? Does this have something to do with this… this horrible room?”

“In a way, yes. I think we just found the missing piece of our puzzle,” Carmack said.

“Ahh—hero? Is that it? That-that skeleton, it’s glowing.”

“Never mind that, Commander. Have your men scout defensible positions. We’ll move out and continue onto the mine within the hour. You’ll leave a small contingency behind to guard this.”

“Guard the skeleton?”

“It’s much more than that, Commander. I’ll go over it another time. For now, let’s move out. We still have a mine to liberate.”

Carmack fortified defensive positions at several key points in the tunnels. A dozen guardsmen were left behind to man the stations, and they continued on to the mine.

The march lasted for several more hours before the dull, repetitive thuds of pickaxes striking earth sounded in the distance.

“From here on out, we must be cautious. The enemy has been described as powerful and vicious. We have much work to do and cannot afford losses. Please proceed with caution,” Carmack warned.

“I’ll order the men into defensive formations,” Commander Gorn said. “We’ll be ready at your command.”

“I suppose I’ll keep an eye on them,” Aphexia shrugged. “And hopefully keep these novices from getting themselves killed.”

“Good. No need for unnecessary heroics.”

Creeping up through the passages, Carmack used his mana to sense paths into the cavern ahead that held the mine. He sensed four tunnels within reach and instructed Gorn to separate the vanguard into teams. Carmack led one, Gorn led another, Aphexia a third, and the final group was ordered into a defensive position with a sergeant placed in command to observe the encounter and move his men into position where necessary.

Communication was limited, so Carmack ordered them to wait until he sprang his attack and then to follow his lead into battle.

He had already heard enough about the insectoid slavers from Mae that there would be no mercy shown. Slavery had long been seen as a foul practice on Avalock, and Carmack’s moral compass did not sway from this. If this were to be their land, then slavers would need to be shown a lesson.

But there was also the promise of the slaves themselves. Mae said that they were all humans, and while they were no doubt it poor condition, if they could be freed, feed and provided medicine, then they could be nursed to good health.

With the plan settled, Carmack led his group up through on of the tunnels. They winded up and onto a higher platform.

Carmack had already sensed the lifeforms within the cavern ahead. He intended to take the battle to the high ground. It wasn’t just a tactical decision; he sensed that was where they would find the yaddiks themselves.

Mae had advised that the slavers were held up on the higher ground, and he had no reason to doubt the information. It made sense from a tactical standpoint.

Turning around the tunnel’s final bend, their information was confirmed as he locked sight on the insectoids. They were as described. Four-armed insects the size of men standing on two feet with four arms. Their carapaces looked formidable, and almost all of them carried scimitars in every hand, with wispy anteneae extending from their faces like moustaches.

“Okay. Prepare yourselves and follow my attack,” Carmack said softly as he counted the enemy numbers around roughly twenty. Of course, there were many huts scattered around the cavern ahead, and he knew that more yaddiks could be hiding within. That said, he doubted they could possibly number more than fifty, based on the camp’s size.

Stepping out into the cavern, he raised his hands and summoned the second circle spell “Wave of Spears,” and shot stone spikes the length of a human forearm towards the closest yaddiks.
