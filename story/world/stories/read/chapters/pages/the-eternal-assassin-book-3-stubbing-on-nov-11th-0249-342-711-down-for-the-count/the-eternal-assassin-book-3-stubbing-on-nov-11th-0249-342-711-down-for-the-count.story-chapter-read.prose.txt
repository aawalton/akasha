“…Did we live?” Mako called out weakly as everyone groaned and struggled to free themselves from the pile of bruised and tangled limbs.

“No. You’re in hell,” Cora snapped, and Mako yelped from what Asher felt pretty confident in assuming was a crack of electricity. “Would you get off me? You’re lying on my broken arm!”

“Is everyone alright?” Asher asked, cheating by slipping into the astral to free himself from the pile. Weirdly enough he felt fantastic. Whatever Lanra had done, his tritessence reserves had gone from empty to almost half full in an instant. And a good thing too. Whatever magical explosion had been about to hit them, it was so powerful it had actually impacted his own Recall skill. Moving everyone had been harder than ever before, which explained the giant pile of bodies in the center of his and Samantha’s bedroom.

“I can’t move my legs,” Moxy called out weakly, and Asher sucked in a breath of worry for a moment before remembering what had happened and frowning at her.

“You’re hilarious,” he drawled as she cracked up laughing. “Rulfar, Samantha, Kroult? You guys alright?”

“That was… a bit bumpier of a ride than usual,” Rulfar said, managing to extract himself and Kroult as Asher helped Samantha up. “Nothing broken here.”

“I am intact as well,” Kroult confirmed, brushing off his robe. “I have never witnessed anything like that. I fear what effects such a wave of tritessence will have on the countryside.”

“Good thing it was already abandoned,” Moxy pointed out as Rulfar picked her up and placed her in one of the chairs at the table. “Hey, looks like I’m not the only one who can’t move.”

Confused, Asher took a look at the pile, his heart skipping a beat as he realized what she meant. Everyone else had managed to get to their feet by now, finally revealing the fact that Lanra wasn’t moving.

“Lanra?” Mako asked, holding the old wizard carefully as he gently tapped a hand against her cheek. “Lanra!”

“Moxy, can you…” Asher shook his head as he remembered the state she was in, before shifting to their second healer. “Samantha, can you help?”

“I’ll try,” she nodded, crouching down beside Mako. “Open her mouth.”

Carefully, Samantha poured some water from her finger into the old woman’s mouth, making sure she swallowed it instead of choking on it. After having evolved Soothing Spring, the skill had gained a new effect. While spraying a specific part of the body had a more powerful healing effect, consuming the water itself functioned as something of a weaker potion of Lesser Restoration. The benefit being that unlike normal potions, where their efficacy decreased with each one drank in a single day, her water could be consumed again and again without worry.

Yet even as she continued feeding Lanra one mouthful of healing water after another, the woman showed no sign of waking up.

“I don’t see any visible wounds,” Cora said, frowning as she ignored her own busted arm to examine Lanra. “What happened? She was fine just a few moments ago.”

“I think I know,” Asher said, his voice growing somber as he noted the deathly pale color of Lanra’s skin. She wasn’t trembling, but he’d bet anything she’d start the moment she regained consciousness. “She’s gone into tritessence debt. I’ve seen it once before. Wizards can borrow tritessence they don’t actually have yet from their souls in an absolute emergency.”

“What? Lanra’s never said anything about such a technique!” Mako argued.

“It’s incredibly dangerous,” Asher explained, feeling all but certain that had to be what happened as he crouched down and took hold of the woman’s hand. It was just as cold and clammy as Richard’s body had been, and he blinked when he realized even while she was unconscious, she was still trembling ever so slightly. “This isn’t good. It looks like it’s worse than when it happened to Richard. I don’t think she had time to figure out how much tritessence to borrow and lend me, she just grabbed a giant chunk and shoved it into my soul. Seeing the size of that explosion, she probably erred on the side of caution and overdid it to make sure we’d survive.”

“What do we do?” Cora asked as she turned toward him. “I take it potions won’t work?”

“No. Richard said the soul needs time to catch up on its debt. He wasn’t able to use any of his spells for a few days, but Lanra’s in even worse shape.” Making a snap decision, Asher got to his feet. “Let’s bring her to Richard. Maybe he can do something to help.”

Not wanting to freak Richard out with a sudden parade of people flooding into his room, Asher’s team decided to stay behind and rest after everything that happened. Mako and Jameston worked together to carry Lanra between them, while Cora drank a potion of Lesser Restoration and grimaced as her broken arm slowly healed itself.

Asher briefly considered pulling Lanra into his rift to make transporting her easier, but he didn’t dare do anything unusual to her in her current condition. For all he knew his Personal Rift would somehow make her even worse, so he simply directed the others on where to go as they raced through the palace.

“Richard!” Asher shouted, slamming a fist into the wizard’s ornate door. “Emergency! We need help!”

There was the sound of screeching, followed by banging that gave Asher pause even in his panic, before the wizard threw open his door with a snarl on his face, his normally well-kept appearance in a strange disarray.

“Asher, I don’t know—”

Richard took one look at Lanra’s condition and froze, his eyes going wide as they took in the trembling and unconscious elderly woman. The often self-centered and aggravating wizard barely even hesitated, throwing his door wide open as he motioned for them to come inside.

“Place her on the table within the circular couch,” Richard instructed, sweeping his hair out of his face as he made sure they followed his directions to the letter. “What happened?”

“We were both pretty much out of tritessence, and then there was a giant magical explosion,” Asher began. “She put her hand on my chest, and suddenly I was flooded with tritessence. Enough to save us and get us out of there. Once we were safe, we realized she was unconscious, and I recognized the symptom of tritessence debt from when you threw me into the wild realm on my own and couldn’t get me back out.”

“He did what?!” Mako shouted, finally tearing his eyes away from Lanra as he stared at Richard in disbelief. “And you brought Lanra to him for help?!”

“Of course he did, I’m the only chance she has of making it through this alive,” Richard said, ignoring Mako’s stare as he rolled up the sleeves of his robe and looked down at Lanra’s trembling form. Asher could only assume he was doing something important, but as far as he could tell, Richard was just staring at his fellow wizard without moving so much as a muscle. Seconds passed by one after another without him doing much of anything. Just as Cora opened her mouth to say something, Richard grimaced.

“I won’t lie, it’s not pretty. She strained her soul something fierce borrowing so much tritessence in such a short time, and then strained it even further getting it over to you so quickly. Either one of those actions probably could have resulted in this happening, but together… I’m not very skilled when it comes to soul magic, but I’ll do what I can.”

“Is there anything we can do to help?” Cora asked, and Jameston nodded along vigorously, no longer willing to use his Mind element to speak now that they were back in the Noala Kingdom.

“Unless any of you are skilled enough to transfer tritessence and change its attribute from one person to another, you would just get in the way,” Richard said bluntly before laying his hands on Lanra’s chest and closing his eyes. “Now be silent. This is incredibly delicate work, and I don’t need any further distractions.”

As Mako, Cora, and Jameston stood there silently, watching Richard get to work, Asher briefly contemplated trying to use Permanence on the old wizard’s body before scrapping the idea. This wasn’t blood loss or some sort of toxin. Tritessence played by its own rules, and he somehow knew intuitively Permanence wouldn’t be able to do a damn thing to help her condition. Soul damage was in a league all on its own, and as much as he wanted to do something to help, he knew leaving it to Richard was the smart thing to do. Laying a gentle hand on Mako’s shoulder, Asher pulled him out of the room, motioning for Cora and Jameston to follow suit.

“Come on,” he said after closing Richard’s door for him. “There’s nothing we can do for her right now. Trust me, if there was any way to speed up what Richard was doing or make the process easier with our help, he would have demanded it even if it were dangerous. For now, all we can do is wait and hope for the best.”

Asher didn’t want to tell them that things were almost certainly going to get worse before they got better. Richard had looked like absolute death when Asher had returned from the wild realm, and his tritessence debt hadn’t been nearly as bad as Lanra’s. Lanra had gotten to this point in only a few minutes. Asher didn’t even want to think about what she was going to look like as more time passed. Even if he couldn’t do anything to help, at the very least, he could keep her team from seeing her in that condition.

There was only one thing he could think of, and after placing the three of them in the lounge room his team often used for their strategy meetings, he pulled out his sending stone. But right before he could press Donvath’s rune, he blinked as the rune lit up on its own and his stone began vibrating, indicating that Donvath was calling him.

“Come to my office,” Donvath said curtly before Asher could say a word. “I told you before, sending stones aren’t secure.”

Shaking his head at the High Prince’s blatant display of time shenanigans, Asher did just that, nodding at the royal guard as he entered Donvath’s office. As soon as the door was closed, Donvath sighed and shook his head.

“Sorry, Asher, Gran is elsewhere at the moment. I don’t have the faintest idea where, but her position requires her to be on the move almost constantly. Lanra is going to have to pull through without her.”

“There’s nothing you can do?” Asher asked. “No special resources you have at your disposal as the High Prince?”

“She’s already being tended to by the kingdom’s lone wizard,” Donvath pointed out. “Unfortunately, soul damage is a pretty unique thing. Gran is the only one I know of capable of fixing that, and even for her, it’s not easy.”

“I figured that was the case,” Asher admitted, plopping himself down at a side table as he replayed the scene in his head. “The sheer power contained in that magical explosion… I’m not even sure if I would have been able to regenerate from it fully if I’d gotten caught up in the blast. I get the sense it would have scoured my soul as well. If Lanra hadn’t done what she did, we probably all would have died.”

“There’s a reason she was chosen to lead the Eight Crests’ one and only team of realm wanderers,” Donvath said as he pulled out a bowl of small candies and placed them in front of him. “Their country is large enough that they actually have two or three wizards last I heard. But it takes a special kind of person to lead a team into dangerous situations and get them safely back out again. She’s tough. She’ll pull through.”

“Is that what you see in the future? Or is that what you think I need to hear?” Asher asked, giving Donvath a pointed look.

“It’s what I think I need to hear,” Donvath said simply, picking up one of the candies and popping it into his mouth. “Lanra has already proven herself to be an invaluable member of the coalition, and things are only going to get scarier the more liches we take out. It’s only a matter of time before they decide to retaliate, and we’re going to need all the help we can get to keep the damage to a minimum once they do.”
