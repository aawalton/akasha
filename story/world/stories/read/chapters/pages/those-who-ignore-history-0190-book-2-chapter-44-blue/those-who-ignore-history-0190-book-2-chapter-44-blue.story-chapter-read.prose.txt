The Gate was… well, blue.

Not regular blue. Not sky blue, midnight blue, ocean blue, crystal blue, or any other blue that had the decency to look like something my brain could reference. No—this was the bluest blue to ever blue, a color so intensely saturated it felt like it had weight. Like if I reached out, the hue itself might drip off my fingers.

“It’s blue,” V announced, hands on hips, stating the obvious with all the theatrical gravitas of a prophet descending from a mountaintop.

“It really is,” Cordelia agreed, completely deadpan—except she was actually reading all our thoughts, so her comment felt uncomfortably precise.

I rubbed the bridge of my nose. “Right. Thank you. Both of you. Wonderful contributions.”

The Gate pulsed again, sending a ripple of mana-charged air across the clearing. Even the breeze felt thick—humid, warm, carrying the scent of wet stone and something faintly sweet. Fire, Earth, and Water miasma radiated off the arch like heat off a forge.

Tropical. Dense. Probably dangerous.

I turned to the group I’d chosen for this run—V, Cordelia, Ten, Fractal, and Fallias. Wallace and Sven were back at base, Wallace whipping a regiment of my technically-an-army into shape, and Sven managing everything else so I didn’t have to think about numbers or supply chains or the fifteen other things that gave me headaches.

“Alright,” I said, straightening. “As we agreed. V—you’ve got rear guard.”

V bowed with a flourish so deep it counted as stretching. “Ah yes, the noble art of watching our backs. A sacred duty. A sensual duty.”

Cordelia didn’t glare. She simply turned her head five degrees in V’s direction. Somehow, that was worse. V immediately pretended to adjust his coat instead of continuing whatever monologue he’d been about to deliver.

“I want salt mines on our trail,” I continued. “Layered. Unpredictable spacing. Anything follows us out of that Gate, I want it regretting its life choices.”

V saluted with two fingers. “Consider their feet ruined.”

“Good.”

I turned to the two smallest members of the group, who were also somehow the most terrifying.

“Fractal, Ten—you take the vanguard. Scout ahead. Ten, you’re in charge of terrain and threat identification. Fractal, you’re our messenger. If anything changes, you come straight back.”

Ten cracked her knuckles, eyes gleaming. “Finally, something fun.”

Fractal chirped brightly, puffing her feathers in pride. “Message delivery is my specialty, Alex! No problem, no problem at all!”

Her enthusiasm made the Gate’s oppressive aura feel a little less heavy. Just a little.

“Cordelia and I will handle any immediate hostiles,” I said, stepping back so the projection on my wristband could adjust and highlight our formation path. “Standard pincer, fallback triangle if we get swarmed.”

Cordelia nodded once—professional, controlled. “Understood. I’ll maintain full spatial awareness.”

Which meant she’d be in my head, senses overlapping with mine. It was comforting and unnerving all at once.

“And Fal-Fal…” I pointed at the tall dragonoid girl sharpening her spear with slow, methodical confidence. “You handle finishing blows. Anything that breaks formation or survives our first pass, you take down.”

Fallias grinned—showing just a bit too much fang. “Understood, Alexander.”

The Gate pulsed again, deeper this time. A low hum rippled across the clearing, making the hairs on my arms stand.

“The miasma signature from this thing is absolutely loaded,” I said. “Fire, Earth, Water. Expect a tropical biome. Heavy humidity, thick vegetation, unstable ground, steam vents, volcanic pockets—maybe even tsunamic flux zones.”

Fractal’s feathers flattened at that. “Ooh, hot rain world.”

Ten rubbed her hands together. “Perfect.”

V flicked imaginary lint off his sleeve. “My hair will suffer, but I will endure.”

Cordelia closed her eyes for a moment, likely double-confirming my readings. “He’s correct. Mana pressure is consistent with volcanic tropics. Combat visibility may be compromised.”

I glanced at everyone, did a mental roll call, then asked, “Everyone have a water crystal on them?”

Five heads nodded.

Fallias tapped hers where it hung on a chain at her waist. “Yes.”

Ten held hers between two fingers. “Yup!”

Fractal proudly pulled two from her pouch. “I brought extras!”

V produced his with a flourish, because of course he did.

Cordelia simply placed a hand on the hilt of her sword. “Confirmed.”

Good. Hydration crystals were mandatory; tropical realms liked to drain your fluids faster than you could blink.

I took a breath—slow, steady, grounding.

This was routine. A Gate run. A new realm. A potentially hostile environment. Dangerous monsters. Unknown territory.

Routine.

But as the blue light washed over us again, painting everyone in shades of impossible sapphire, something tightened low in my stomach. Anticipation, maybe. Nerves. The familiar edge between fear and excitement.

Fractal drifted closer, bumping her shoulder against mine—light, small contact, but it steadied me.

“You okay, Alex?” she whispered.

“Yeah,” I said. “Just thinking.”

“You always think,” she giggled. “You’ll be fine.”

Cordelia stepped to my right, posture perfect, expression serene. “We’re ready when you are, Walker.”

The title settled something in me.

Not Alex. Not kid. Not the boy who made mistakes on his last mission.

Walker.

I lifted my hand, letting the Gate’s blue glow paint my fingers.

“Alright,” I said. “Formation alpha. Vanguard enter first. Let’s move.”

The Gate flared, the mana hum deepened, and one by one, my team stepped forward—into the bluest blue that ever blued.

And I followed.

We emerged into a forest so enormous it felt like stepping into the hollowed-out ribcage of some ancient god.

The trees weren’t just tall—they were colossal, rising in vast pillars that vanished into darkness far above the reach of sight. Their trunks were wider than townhouses, bark rippling in layers like old volcanic stone. The canopy was so dense that not a single sliver of ordinary sunlight penetrated it.

And yet… the forest wasn’t dark.

Everything glowed.

Bioluminescent insects drifted lazily through the air like floating lanterns. Flowers pulsed with soft, rhythmic light—violets, greens, blues, colors I didn’t even have names for. Moss painted the tree roots in swaths of silver-gold luminescence, and strange fungal pads dotted the underbrush, lighting up with each step we took.

Despite not a shard of natural light reaching the ground, the entire forest was lit like the world just after dusk. Not bright, not dim—just a perpetual, alien twilight.

The air, though… The air was brutal.

Thick. Wet. Heavy with humidity so intense it felt like the atmosphere was trying to crawl down my throat. Every inhale dragged with a weight I wasn’t prepared for, and each step squelched faintly on the damp soil.

Sweat beaded on our skin almost instantly.

Ten wiped her forehead with the back of her arm. “Okay, wow. This place needs… vents. Or a breeze. Or a very large ice cube.”

Fractal shook out her feathers, which were already starting to clump. “It feels like a bathhouse without the bath.”

Even Fallias, who was part reptilian and generally unbothered by heat, looked mildly put out. “It’s… dense,” she admitted with a slight nose scrunch.

Cordelia, of course, looked pristine. I suspected she simply refused to sweat out of sheer willpower.

I took a careful look around the clearing we’d appeared in. “Okay… Ten. Fractal.”

They straightened immediately.

“Go,” I instructed, pointing north. “Try to make it out a mile in that direction. I want a quick survey—terrain, wildlife, threats, anything unusual. Stay together.”

Ten saluted sharply. “On it.”

Fractal hopped once, wings flaring in a shimmer of bismuth light. “Messenger mode activated!”

“Don’t call it that,” I sighed.

“It sounds cool,” she protested before fluttering upward.

I turned back to the clearing. It wasn’t safe—nothing in a fresh Otherrealm ever was—but it had features we could use. The massive roots formed natural barriers. The fungal pads reacted to movement but didn’t seem aggressive. And the narrow funnel of open space gave us a controllable perimeter.

“It’s not safe,” I said aloud, “but it’s at least defensible.”

V flopped dramatically against a tree root, fanning himself with a leaf the size of a shield. “Defensible is good. Defensible is sexy. I accept this.”

Fallias snorted. “You accept breathing.”

Cordelia’s eyes glowed faintly as she expanded her awareness outward, letting clear mana threads drift like fine mist. “The ambient mana density here is extremely high. It’s thick enough to distort long-distance senses. We’ll need to rely more on direct scouting.”

“I hate realms like that,” V muttered.

I hated them too—but I didn’t say it. Instead, I adjusted the straps on my robe and forced myself to take deeper breaths, trying to acclimate even as the humidity clung to my lungs like syrup.

“Alright,” I said, settling myself. “We wait here until Ten and Fractal report back. Stay sharp. This realm… feels old.”

I couldn’t explain it. But the forest did feel old. As if the trees had been here longer than anything else I had ever seen. As if they were watching.

“Does anyone else feel watched?” Fallias asked, her hand drifting toward the hilt of her blade. Her voice wasn’t frightened—just alert. Focused. Exactly what I was feeling.

“Yes,” Cordelia said immediately, exhaling through her nose. Her tone was perfectly flat, perfectly unimpressed. “I feel watched, but I’m not sensing who, what, or how. It’s as if the sensation itself is being… filtered.”

She tilted her head, thoughtful. “Technically, we are non-hostile Others until the local Dominus makes contact and designates us as Visitors. So maybe the Dominus in question is the one observing? That would be the logical answer.”

V clicked his tongue sharply. “Too easy, Sunshine. Way too easy. When has anything gone logically for us?” He threw a dramatic hand toward me. “The rabbit is with us again! Which means fate absolutely will go wild.”

I groaned. “Stop calling me that.”

“You literally hop between crises,” V retorted. “And sometimes you make little squeaky noises when startled. Don’t deny it.”

Fallias tried to hide her laugh poorly. Traitor.

But V wasn’t wrong about the sentiment. When fate wanted to turn the world into a stage play, it tended to involve me in deeply uncomfortable ways.

“If fate was going to go wild,” I muttered, rubbing the back of my neck, “we’d need my father here for that.”

Cordelia’s lips curled into a small, knowing smirk. “Right. His Arte was… Fortune manipulation, wasn’t it?”

I snorted. “Not even that. He was just… lucky. Or unlucky. That’s literally it. No technique. No skill. Just raw, absurd, impossible luck stuck to him like a parasite.”

V snapped his fingers. “Which is an Arte in itself, Tea-Pot.”

“It’s not,” I said firmly.

“It might be,” Fallias countered lightly, stepping to my side. “If it affects reality consistently, it might still qualify as an Arte even if he never realized it.”

“Or,” I said, “he’s just genuinely that stupidly fortunate.”

Cordelia raised a brow. “Is there a difference?”

I opened my mouth to argue, hesitated… then frowned. “I… don’t think so?”

“There isn’t,” V confirmed smugly.

But the humor settled only briefly before the forest’s pressure crept back in around us. The sensation of being watched was still there, still sharp and prickling along the edges of my mana lines.

Watchful.

Patient.

Older than anything in the forest itself.

Fallias’s tail flicked, restless. “It’s not like a person watching,” she said quietly. “It’s… deeper. Like the whole forest is paying attention.”

Cordelia nodded grimly. “Yes. Something ambient. Something pervasive. Like a presence woven into the realm itself.”

I swallowed against the thickness of the air, my voice dipping lower. “Then let’s hope it stays curious.”

Because if it wasn’t curiosity?

It was hunting.
