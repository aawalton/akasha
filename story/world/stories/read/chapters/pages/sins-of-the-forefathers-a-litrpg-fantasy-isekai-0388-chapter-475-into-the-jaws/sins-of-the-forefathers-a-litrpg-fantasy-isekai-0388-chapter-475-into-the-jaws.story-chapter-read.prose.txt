This would be my first true military assault.

A strange thought to have. Good thing I wasn’t actually nervous about it.

Up until now, all of the larger-scale military actions I’d been involved in had involved being on the defense. Not to say I had never really assaulted a location before, but those had been smaller scale, or not really involved with something like a military. Caer Drarrow didn’t count, I felt like the aborted battle for Helstein didn’t count, and the infiltration of the Netherim Bunker beneath Mt. Goryuen didn’t count. Those were all small-scale, Classer-level attacks with a focused objective.

This…this would be a full-scale assault. I almost wanted to call it a siege, and yet that wasn’t quite right. There was no intention here for us to take and hold Karag Korvok. If we took any buildings once inside, it wouldn’t be to hold them, only to establish a front line.

No, we were going to smash our way through the heart of Karag Korvok and accomplish a few very important objectives, all of which had a similar theme.

One.

We were going to be rescuing the captured people of Karag Varla that these animals had absconded with.

Two.

That included Azarus and hopefully Gren as well. My dumbass friend had gotten himself into a bind once more, and it was my job to get him out of it. If that meant I had to rampage up to whatever flesh forsaken throne this ‘Ul-Korveth’ ruled from and kill him…well, that would just be a bonus.

Three, and much as I hated to think of it this way…

The most important thing was finding wherever the Hell Nehushtan was hiding in this mountain. I was going to get whatever method he had to breach the War Wards of Smaragd. Even if I had to personally slaughter every last cannibal in this mountain, I’d do it. This was, in the grand scheme of it all, more important than anything else. We needed to get the promised method of breaching those wards if we wanted to finally deal with both the Skyfall and Fynneas.

Of course, all of that rested on the fact that we had to fight our way through a Hold filled with madmen who wanted to literally tear our skin off our bones, and then gnaw on them. I really had chosen probably the hardest thing I possibly could, to break my teeth on commanding an assault on an enemy city.

Still, it would be good practice. Soon, I was going to need to do this again. No matter how dangerous, no matter how fraught, and no matter how hard the assault upon Karag Korvok was going to be…

Smaragd would be harder.

Two weeks, Core reminded me in a whisper.

I had, roughly, two weeks to resolve the Skyfall, or else I risked outright genocide.

Not going to happen.

So here we were. At the bottom of a pit that had been tunneled by a pack of madmen bent on religious fervor about cannibalism.

It was…not quite as precarious as I had been expecting it to be. To my shock, after I had fully descended with my men to the floor of the pit, we found that the Korvoks had done a fairly good job in shoring up the walls of the tunnel they had used to reach Varla in the first place. It was easily large enough to accommodate at least five people marching shoulder to shoulder. Although, honestly, marching was probably the wrong word for it. The footing down here was precarious as it was, to the extent that everyone who was still among the living had to be careful not to stumble over their own feet. Still, I had confidence that the dark walls that led off into the distance weren’t about to crumble all around us.

Which made me wonder…

Had the Korvoks intended to come back? This felt like a much more permanent passageway than we had been anticipating.

…none of that mattered now.

At my order, light Skills, Spells, and Arts flared into being all around us, illuminating our surroundings. Before, the light of Varla’s great light stone had barely pierced this far down.

Now, we had enough to travel by. And we would be traveling for a bit, I believed. From what we had learned from the Varlayan’s holed up in the Hagia Dynamis, the distance between the two mountains that held Karag Varla and Karag Korvok was somewhere in the range of two to three miles.

With a force of our size, carefully maneuvering the Korvoks tunnel would likely take us several hours.

We’d do it anyway.

I ordered the column forward, now that we’d found our footing, and on we marched.

Into the darkness.

…………………………………….

The Aether was changing. As we marched down the dark, silent tunnels mined by the mad Korvoks, the atmospheric Aether always present within the air of Vereden was…shifting.

This was, to an extent, to be expected. It was very rare to encounter someplace where the Aether in the air was entirely neutral. Almost always, something was influencing the elemental balance of environmental Aether. The simple presence of a single tree was often enough to shift Aether towards a more Terrestrial balance, much less an entire forest. Aether-heavy artifacts such as Ward Stones, or even light crystals like Varla’s great false sun, also contributed to the balance of Aether, if not in a more conceptual direction than elemental.

It was often hard to put words to the sensation of Aether, to one’s Aetheric sense.

That wasn’t the case here. The Aether was becoming so dense with it that I could taste it on the air.

Blood.

So much blood.

As our column navigated the tunnel carved by the Korvoks, the air became thick with the sensation of blood. The humidity around us began to gradually climb, and so did the temperature. Before long, I was almost startled to discover that the atmosphere down here was starting to remind me of the dense jungles I had traversed on the island of Gorenzan. And yet, this moisture in the air was wrong. It was thicker, odder than normal humidity. It felt less like natural mist clinging to my skin, and more like dispersed oil, thick and viscous.

And the scent. There was a smell in the air, something that filled me with a grim certainty.

Iron.

It was…rare for Aether to become so saturated with something that it began to influence the physical realm in such a manner.

It didn’t bode well.

And then, two more senses were assaulted, and in a manner I didn’t expect.

At first, I wasn’t sure what it was. A sound began to reach us as we marched through the tunnel. It could have been anything, I initially told myself. The shifting of the stone, the rushing of air from either end of the tunnel. It was, at first, a soft sound. Something that pulsed down the length of the shaft, coming from the distant destination we sought. Tremors slight enough through the stone to on barely dislodge dust.

A thump, as if a great drum had been stuck. A pause, for several seconds…and then another thump. Slow, rhythmic pounding. It wasn’t loud, not in the sense of a noise. It was more like a sensation, a sense of pressure as if reality itself was being forced to pulse at a perfectly even timing.

It reached my inner ear in the same way that sound did, and yet, there was no noise. It was as if it were the opposite of a sound wave. Something that displaced air in the same manner, and yet ate the sound that was meant to be and never was.

An almost anti-noise.

It set my teeth on edge. It was almost like I could feel the distorted wave tickling at the edges of my mind, at the borders of the self that was Nathaniel Hart. Middle almost instinctively checked my rudimentary mental defenses I had cobbled together during my time in Mind Magic class.

Nothing. It felt like something was trying to reach me, but I couldn’t find anything.

I didn’t like it one bit.

Finally, something happened that caused everyone in the column still among the living to tense and ready themselves.

A light appeared at the end of the tunnel, far in the distance, only barely visible beyond the long, long line of Rafael’s undead. Even in normal circumstances, that would be enough to cause a sense of battle anticipation to hit us my soldiers.

But even beyond that, this light gave me pause for consideration. Unlike the bright, warm, nearly daylike light given off by Karag Varla’s artificial star, this light was different.

It was a deep, glowing crimson. It almost seemed to ooze out of a distant opening, congealing across the walls of the tunnel like questing tendrils of ichorous blood.

Backlit by the malevolent glow, I saw the floating form of Rafael, only barely visible above his command…pause. He turned in midair, and I saw his flaming blue eyes turn to stare at me. Across the distance, his voice reached not only me, but I believe nearly everyone in the expedition. Certainly, soldiers all around me tensed up in sudden surprise, as if it was.

“Prepare yourself,” I heard, drifting on the stagnant air. “The first of my assistants are breaching the tunnel. Resistance is light at the moment, but that will change soon.” He paused for a moment. “Steel your will. This place is…not as expected. You will see why, soon enough.”

I didn’t like that, and for a single, weak moment, I wished I was anywhere else. It was only a fleeting thought, and I tried not to hold it against myself. But I was absolutely certain I was going to be greeted by sights straight out of a description of Hell. I was so tired of being assaulted by sights of depravity, but…

But somebody was going to have to deal with it, and it might as well be me.

Be us.

My will firmed, and the column slowed. Rafael turned away and focused on the ever-shortening gap between us and the exit of the tunnel. The undead were still pushing their way through and into the heart of Karag Korvok, but the speed at which they did so was slower.

The good thing was that there were quite a few zombies in Rafael’s risen army. There were so many of them that they possessed the inevitable momentum of an avalanche. Whatever resistance was being encountered couldn’t possibly hope to stem the tide forever. Not when a legitimate Lich was controlling them with the implacable precision of a conductor for his orchestra.

Not when hundreds breached the tunnel every minute.

Still, those were long minutes indeed, as my forces and I waited our turn to enter the battle. All the while, we grew ever closer to the malevolent light, and the ever-deepening pulse of noise that ate noise, pounding harder and deeper to batter against the walls of my mind…

And then I was through, and I beheld a sight of horror such that I had never encountered. Of a kind that I had never even dreamed, in my darkest of nightmares, could have been possible.

The Korvoks had created such a landscape of monstrosity that not even the demons and devils of my Terran upbringing could have prepared me for it.

Dimly, I realized that the site through which they had begun their tunnel through the mountains and into Varla was at the very wall of their Hold. We had exited out into the open air surface of their…home, and not into another pit such as the one we had descended into. We seemed to be on a large platform at that wall, from which they must have staged their invasion, because there was a large descending ramp that led into the Hold itself.

I almost regretted that. It allowed me to properly absorb the rolling sight of the hold before it, and I wish I didn’t have to.

As I had expected, Karag Korvok wasn’t lit by a warm, soft light such as had been found in Karag Varla. Instead, that same oozing crimson light from the tunnel bathed the entirety of the hollowed out mountain in its malevolent glow. It cast shadows of such darkness and depth that I expected all manner of evil to crawl forth from within them, and there were many shadows to be found within the cannibal’s home.

Because the entire cityscape of the Dwarven Hold of Korvok was dotted with spires. These were nothing like the myriad, cheerful towers of Blutstein, or the clock and belltowers of lost Rhoscara. Instead these were tall, thin, and sharp. Needle sharp, in face, terminating in very real pointed peaks, sharp enough to skewer a god upon. These needle spires were densely packed enough that it was hard to even see through them, as if they were the sharpened teeth of a hungry shark upthrust through the stone of Vereden. Rows and rows and rows of them...

And each and every one of them was covered from base to sharpened tip with flesh.

Or rather, skin. To my horror, I don’t think it was dead skin at that. The surfaces of these flesh-covered spires pulsed in time and in rhythm with a great heartbeat, slow and sinister. This must have been the sound and presence that had crept upon me within the tunnel.

And yet…somehow, someway…

That wasn’t the worst of it.

There was a Calamity pinned to the ceiling of the mountain.
