“Sal, grab me some ground fireroot, would you?” Iski sets her taster spoon aside, squinting at the pot of stew like it’s committed a crime. “Still missing something.”

“Sure.” I head over to the spice rack and grab the bottle by muscle memory before I’ve even checked the label. Iski nods appreciatively as I hand it to her and she seasons the pots.

“So how long are you staying?” she asks without looking up. She doles out another spoonful of broth and hands it to me. “Here, what do you think?”

I blow on the spoon before trying it. It’s rich and spicy and citrusy in all the best ways; it tastes exactly how I remember. It tastes like home. “It’s good. And a few days, maybe. I’ve got plans with some friends at the end of the week.”

Iski plants a hand on her hip as she looks up with me. “Friends, huh?”

I set the taster spoon down. “Yeah.”

“Multiple?”

“Two.”

She makes a grunting sound she uses when a loaf of bread turns out to her liking. “Next time you visit, double that.”

“Four?” I repeat, bewildered. “Friends don’t just drop out of trees!” Although, actually, Cyros kind of did.

“I should hope not! That would mean it’s random.” Iski wipes her hands off with a rag. “Gotta put some effort into it. And I know nothing can stop you when you put your mind to something.”

I grumble under my breath, trying not to smile. As Iski hops up onto a stool to reach the counter, I think I catch a hint of a smile from her, too.

Instead of arguing the point, I sweep the scraps off the counter and toss them into the flames beneath the cauldron. The fire sprite that lives there eats them up, burning the pieces to charcoal in mere seconds.

For a while I was super jealous of Iski’s familiar and wanted my own. Who wouldn’t want a pet flame that would eat things for you? As I’m crouched by the hearth, watching the sprite eat, the refiner on my wrist catches the light. I suppose now that I have Calx I do have my own strange and hungry little entity. Although it seems sleepy before all else.

Funny how things work out. I wanted magic when I first got to this world; I wanted a magic pet soon after. I wanted to go adventuring and I wanted to grow stronger. And I’ve achieved all those things—just not in the way I expected.

“I’ve got everything else covered in here,” Iski tells me. “Why don’t you check on the tavern?”

I step outside, happy for a little relief from the kitchen’s humid heat, and find Gugora tending the tables. He beckons me over, and I start helping to collect mugs and plates. I used to hate this part of working at the Starlight. It doesn’t seem nearly so terrible now.

The room smells like people and beer. I barely even register the puddles of spilled drink as I step around them, and dodging drunk gesticulations also seems easier. I find myself listening to the crowd without actually hearing the conversations, searching for keywords or tones that might stand out. All that assassin training wasn’t for nothing, I guess. The me that worked here half a year ago almost seems like a stranger.

“... the cash is kept behind the counter?”

My ears prick, and I grab a rag to wipe off the tables, casually circling in the direction of the suspicious conversation.

“Might be locked with a spell,” their companion replies.

The conspirators don’t stick out from any of the other customers, just a felis and a harpy in travel clothes. They’re sitting a little apart from the rest of the guests. I give them both a Check before turning away.

[Jota: Level 21 felis Rogue]

[Paemus: Level 19 harpy Thief]

“I’ve got tools for that,” Paramus says.

Like hell you do. Twentyone and nineteen? This’ll be a cakewalk. The fight with the lindwyrm pushed me all the way up to the brink of thirty-two, and I’ve been itching to force the level-up.

The two stop talking as my cleaning takes me too close for their comfort, so I begin to move away once more. It still takes them a minute before they start conspiring again. It occurs to me that I shouldn’t be able to overhear their conversation with normal human hearing—especially given the noise of the tavern. Maybe my level-ups have to do with that? I thought I was imagining it, but I think my vision has grown sharper as well. I hope it’s a System thing and not a champion thing. That would be annoying.

The thieves debate between trying to take it while Gugora is distracted with other guests, or if they should wait until the room is empty. They eventually settle on the latter, planning to sneak back down in the middle of the night.

I grin to myself. Guess I’ll be spending the night here, too.

The rest of the evening passes without event. I fall back into old routines, surprising myself by how much I can do out of muscle memory. I help Gugora take care of the tavern and its customers, and I shepherd plates to and from the kitchen. During any moments of downtime, I help Iski with food prep—not just for tonight, but seasoning meat for future meals and making dough for tomorrow morning’s bread. If there’s a heaven, this is what it would smell like.

Gugora doesn’t ask me if I’ll be spending the night. He just hands me a key charm and tells me when to be up tomorrow morning. I give him a quick hug before bed—nothing nearly as embarrassing as my hug earlier that afternoon—and retire to my room.

It’s the same room number I had when I lived here. I open the door and I’m met with the smell of age; a room left uninhabited, unable to soak up the smells of people. It’s not covered in dust—they must clean it every so often—but it also clearly hasn’t been used since I left. A folded stack of vaguely familiar clothes sit on the chest. I crack it open, and find a dozen empty bottles I’d been using for my potion experiments. Some paper and charcoal are tucked in the drawer next to my bed.

My bed.

I laugh quietly, shaking my head. What kind of innkeeps don’t use one of their rooms? They could have just put my stuff somewhere else. It’s silly they left it like this for me.

My throat tightens painfully, and I quickly make myself busy preparing for bed.

Of course, I won’t be sleeping until much later. First I need to deal with those two wannabee thieves.

The room isn’t big enough for me to remove my Apothecary in, so I sneak out a window and remove it from my Inventory in the clearing behind the Inn. I sort through my belongings—removing my bedwear while I’m at it—and try to pick my poison. They’re ten levels lower than me, so it shouldn’t be much of a fight, but there are two of them, which means they could potentially get the drop on me. Especially with Rogue and Thief classes; they probably have some sort of stealth abilities similar to my own. Not to mention, I know it can be dangerous to underestimate someone who is low level: Maru could attest to that. I’m not going to pull out all the bells and whistles, but I won’t underestimate them, either.

I noted which room number Gugora gave them earlier, so I crack my door open and watch theirs, waiting. It’s about two hours after the inn has gone quiet and all the lights have been snuffed out that their door swings soundlessly open.

They must be using a spell, because none of the inn’s doors are that quiet. Sure enough, as the two shadows step from their room, the boards don’t creak beneath their feet, either. When they pass out of sight, heading for the stairs, I silently follow.

I’m not using spells of my own; just my highly-leveled Soft Step skill, and the habits I’ve picked up from the Blackcloaks. Note any light sources. Stick to the shadows. Take care where you place your feet. Feel the movement of the air, listen for any sounds, analyze what you can smell.

I think back to my clumsy attempts at sneaking around the inn before. Now I’m basically a ninja in comparison.

When I reach the balcony at the top of the stairs, I can make out two shapes down in the tavern, huddled by the bartop where Gugora stores payments. I creep my way to the bottom of the stairs.

Okay. So I’m not totally a ninja quite yet.
