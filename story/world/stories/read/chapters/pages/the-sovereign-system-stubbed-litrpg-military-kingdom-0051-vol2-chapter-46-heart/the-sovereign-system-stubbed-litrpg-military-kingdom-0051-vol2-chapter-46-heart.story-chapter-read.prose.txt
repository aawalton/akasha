The southern edge of the Marxx Kingdom bordered Lhair, but due to the absence of nearby fortresses, few skirmishes had ever broken out in this region.

Still, that did little to ease the anxiety of the ten thousand soldiers now marching quietly through enemy territory. Unarmed and weary, they were painfully vulnerable to any ambush.

It was only the second day since retreating from Xiandong Fortress, and their supplies were already running dangerously low. Beyond a few handfuls of dried rations, the army had nearly exhausted its stores of water.

But then, the Wu River came into view.

A silent wave of relief washed over the column at the sight of it. Though not a single man cried out or cheered, their steps quickened. No one dared make unnecessary noise—they knew better than to tempt fate.

The low roar of rushing water met their ears as they neared the riverbank, echoing faintly through the sparse trees that dotted the land. Luke’s tense shoulders eased just slightly when he confirmed the area was empty. No signs of villagers or scouts. At the very least, they could rest. Even if only for a moment.

“The area’s too exposed,” he muttered after a quick sweep of the surroundings. “Tell the men to drink their fill, then we’ll follow the river north before sunset.”

His voice was low but firm, and the nearest messengers nodded before hurrying off.

It didn’t take much convincing. Though still wary, the soldiers moved with quiet urgency—kneeling at the river’s edge, drinking deeply, and refilling their leather pouches. The cold water offered them some measure of comfort, if not strength.

A few hours passed.

The sun dipped low on the horizon, painting long shadows across the plains. Luke’s brow furrowed. They were still too exposed. The edge of the forest remained a two-hour march away, and the open terrain around them left little to the imagination—if an enemy scout passed by now, they’d be spotted instantly.

He stood still for a moment, jaw clenched in thought.

Do I risk camping here in the open… or push them further, hungry and tired as they are?

The men had already gone two days without proper food. If he pushed them too far tonight, the consequences might spiral. But staying here was dangerous. Unforgivable, even.

“General, do we keep going?” Commander Bridges asked, voice low and uncertain.

Luke hesitated—but only for a moment.

“Yes,” he said, nodding. “We can’t afford to be seen. They’ll have to endure a little longer.”

Bridges nodded silently and passed along the order.

The march resumed.

They moved under a shroud of darkness, progress slow and arduous. Clouded skies and a new moon meant visibility was abysmal. It took them three grueling hours to finally reach the tree line—but they made it, intact and unseen.

Luke immediately ordered a halt. No fires, no lights. The risk of discovery was still too great.

They’d press on in the morning. If fortune favored them, they would reach one of the small fishing towns along the river by midday. With any luck, it would offer both food and a place to settle—at least temporarily.

But that was a problem for tomorrow.

Tonight, sleep was a luxury few could afford. Inside his cold, makeshift tent, Luke tossed beneath a threadbare blanket. The wind howled softly through the branches overhead, rustling the fabric of his shelter and gnawing at his concentration. Every sound, every creak or whisper in the dark, felt like a potential threat.

He barely drifted off when a harsh whisper jolted him awake.

“General!”

Luke sat up sharply. Pain shot down his back, muscles spasming from the sudden motion. He clenched his teeth, swallowing the cry that rose in his throat.

“What is it?”

“We’ve spotted campfires deeper in the forest,” Bridges said, voice low and urgent. “The Deputy General’s gone to investigate, but he told me to wake you and fill you in.”

Luke blinked groggily, rubbing at his tired, itchy eyes. Sleep clung stubbornly to him, but the weight of the situation snapped his mind into gear.

His heart began to pound.

The worst-case scenario was becoming more likely by the second: enemy soldiers this close could mean disaster. With no weapons and their strength waning, a surprise engagement would be catastrophic.

“We’re in Lhair territory,” he muttered, running a hand through his disheveled hair. “They’re probably Lhair forces…”

“How long since we arrived?”

“Few hours, sir,” Bridges replied.

Midnight, Luke estimated, nodding to himself.

He drew in a steady breath, pushing down the rising dread. “We wait for the Deputy General to return. We’ll decide what to do once we have more information.”

Bridges saluted quietly and slipped away into the night.

Luke sat in silence for a long moment.

We always knew this was a possibility, he reminded himself. It’s not ideal, but it could be worse… at least we weren’t caught in the open.

Still, he knew the truth. Avoiding this confrontation entirely would be near impossible. With ten thousand men at his back, they couldn’t exactly disappear into the forest—even with the cover of darkness.

Stepping out of his tent, a cold breeze brushed against his skin, causing him to shiver. Overhead, thick gray clouds twisted across the sky, pregnant with the threat of rain.

If the storm broke tonight, it would make morale plummet.

And none of the men had tents.

Only him.

Tristan returned twenty minutes later. Even in the darkness, Luke’s enhanced vision—courtesy of Eagle Eye—allowed him to make out the grave expression carved into his friend’s face.

Luke’s eyes widened. His frown deepened.

“From the east?”

They had also come from the east, carving a rough southwest path into Lhair before following the Wu River north. If either party had traveled a little slower—or faster—they might’ve crossed paths on open ground.

“Did you see any flags? Any insignia?” Luke asked.

“No,” Tristan admitted, shaking his head. “It was too dark. But based on the direction, I’d bet they’re Lhair.”

Luke remained silent, thinking.

“That doesn’t make sense,” he muttered. “Xiandong is the only fortress within a hundred miles. The next closest belongs to Selis.”

His brow furrowed.

“But why would Selis troops be this deep into Lhair territory?”

“Could they be in an alliance?” Tristan asked, though even he sounded doubtful of the idea.

“I don’t know,” Luke admitted, rubbing his temples. “But it’s not impossible. What troubles me is why they’d come this far west. There’s nothing here worth the risk.”

The two fell silent. Speculation was dangerous when facts were scarce. Regardless of their reasons, one thing was clear—they couldn’t afford to be discovered.

“We’ll have to deal with them tonight,” Luke decided quietly. “If we wait until morning, it’ll be too late.”

Tristan nodded grimly. “I’m sure I don’t need to remind you, but for my own sanity, I will—we’re unarmed. If we rouse them, they’ll butcher half our men before we can even react.”

Luke exhaled sharply, pressing his thumb and forefinger against the bridge of his nose. The constant fatigue, hunger, and lack of sleep had dulled his mind like rust on a blade. Every thought felt sluggish, heavy.

The obvious option would be to strike first—to ambush while the enemy slept—but ten thousand exhausted men couldn’t move silently through dense woods. Even the faintest noise could spell disaster.

And most of their soldiers were still asleep themselves, worn to the bone.

“Get Bridges and the other officers,” Luke ordered at last. “We’ll go over our options.”

Tristan gave a curt nod and left.

As the tent flap fell shut, Luke muttered under his breath, “It’s just one thing after another…”
