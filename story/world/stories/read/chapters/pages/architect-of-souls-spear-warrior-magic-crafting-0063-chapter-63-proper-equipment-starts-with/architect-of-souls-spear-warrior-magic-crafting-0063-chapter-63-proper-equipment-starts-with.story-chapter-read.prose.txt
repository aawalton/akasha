When his uncle mentioned preparing to delve, Ferris immediately perked up.

Jade rolled over, feeling his excitement over the bond. The slime remembered that Ferris had said delving meant more magical monster parts to eat.

“Tell me specifically,” Uncle Winton continued, “what are the requirements to join the clan’s groups?”

Ferris thought for a moment.

“I haven’t asked for exact rules, but I have discussed them with other retainers working on qualifying. I will need both an offensive and defensive ability that passes an examination, a rift magic ability to exit a rift in case of an emergency, and be able to pass an obstacle course under a time requirement.”

Uncle Winton considered for a second.

“For the rift magic, it would be best to use Clan Hollowfield’s techniques, as you will be part of their groups. I can’t help you with the obstacle course; if I could, I never would have brought you here. But,” Uncle Winton raised a finger, “I may be able to help with the other two.”

“How?” asked Ferris. Clan Bluereef didn’t have any knowledge of attack abilities for slime familiars, although maybe they knew general defensive abilities?

“Go ask about the exact requirements,” Uncle Winton replied, “see if it would be acceptable to use a magical weapon and armor. You have years of training with a spear; it should be sufficient if the weapon is not mundane. Same for the armor. Back home, everyone uses a magical skinsuit to start delving. Developing a defensive ability that can protect and regulate their temperature underwater simply takes too long.”

Ferris was surprised by the idea, although on reflection it made sense.

He’d seen many delvers back home wearing a dark blue skintight layer covering their whole body. It went from the top of their head down to the tips of their toes. Gossip claimed it made it easier to swim and kept them warm. Wearing one was a sort of rite of passage that Ferris, and all the other children, had envied when growing up.

For the offensive ability… how often were new Adepts in his situation? He’d trained for years with the spear, but didn’t bond one.

Someone like Brix would likely begin training with a backup weapon, but it wouldn’t be her main focus. Plus, she’d likely start with a mundane weapon, rather than magical equipment. The time to learn to wield it beyond a basic level—plus the cost of the construction and resources—would be better spent improving her bonded cauldron instead.

It could be argued that the cost of a magical spear might be better spent on Ferris’ internal cultivation, but at least he wouldn’t need to learn how to use the weapon. In addition, with a familiar bond Ferris would likely rely more on a non-bonded weapon than someone like Brix.

The armor made sense as well, although perhaps more generically to all new Adepts. Cyrus had said that the clan wanted to make sure new delvers could survive, and magical armor would do that just as easily as an ability. It was always better to develop an internal ability that would stay with you forever, but just because it was wasteful didn’t mean it didn’t work—at least if Clan Hollowfield allowed it.

Ferris had seen Uncle Winton wear armor in the rift, but not the skinsuit. The man was advanced enough that he’d probably replaced it with an internal ability. Ferris wasn’t sure if there were tradeoffs. Logically, it would be better to have every kind of protection possible. Although it could simply be that the man felt his resources were more useful elsewhere…

“I talked with the clan treasurer back home. They officially allocated you the resources typically used to create a bondable weapon,” Uncle Winton said. “I’ve been considering how best to recommend you use it. Magical equipment could let you focus on the healing ability, and not worry about two more abilities beyond it. It would be tight, but I think we could afford both a spear and armor within budget.”

Ferris looked at Jade, who had climbed over into Uncle Winton’s lap. The man appeared to be petting the slime. Jade was a sucker for anyone that fed them.

“Uncle,” he began, organizing his thoughts, “the fact of the matter is, I didn’t bond a spear. At this point I am not sure what style of fighting I will pursue. A spear is appealing, as it is what I wanted all my life, but I worry that it would only hold me back as I outgrew it. Would I look at it and just be disappointed in what I can never have? Would I build a fighting style around an external weapon? Will I forever practice a style I cannot master?”

Uncle Winton watched him, the man’s face giving no insight into his thoughts. Eventually, his uncle simply nodded and replied. “Do what you think is best. Think on it and let me know.”

Ferris was torn. He wanted the spear, really wanted it. He didn’t intend to just sit back and let Jade fight, so he would need some kind of weapon. He fought almost every night in the soulspace with a spear. It was just… too close to what he had always dreamed of, yet not the real thing.

The armor, at least, was more reasonable. Ferris had some ideas for how to protect Jade with Assimilate Object, but no current methods to protect himself. There were likely abilities he could purchase from Hollowfield, but if buying armor was an option, then Ferris would take it. He already had many more abilities he needed to develop compared to the average new Adept.

In fact…

“Uncle,” Ferris said, “if we are going to purchase armor for me, would the clan’s skinsuit qualify?” Wearing something that all new delvers back home wore... Ferris didn’t know when he would be able to delve underwater rifts, but he definitely intended to start long before his retainer contract was up, and this would help. He was missing out on so much of his home’s traditions. How would he fit in once he returned?

“It should be fine,” the man smiled and nodded, the glint in his eye showed he could see Ferris’ thoughts. “We have someone back home that makes them for us, so the costs are cheaper than buying outside the clan.”

“Thank you,” Ferris said with a slight bow of his head.

Uncle Winton waved his hand, “It is no large matter. Now!” He exclaimed, “I have a few messages from your family.”

They spent a good amount of time gossiping about home until it was time for Uncle Winton to leave. Ferris still felt a pain of homesickness watching the man go, but he was getting used to the feeling. This was the third time he had done it, after all.

There was still some time before lunch, so Ferris took Jade to run another errand needed for that afternoon’s meeting with Serena. He wanted to test what happened when entire chunks of the slime’s body were chopped off. The slime ranch had a magically enhanced wide blade they used, but Ferris would need to find something cheaper.

He’d heard that the second branch of Hollowfield specialized in forging, so he set out for their district.

Ferris had been going to the third district every day for work, but he’d never gone further to the second. He heard that they worked hand in hand—the second branch forging weapons and armor from magical materials, and the third branch enchanting them.

His first impression on passing the threshold was that the second district was hot and loud. Large chimneys were billowing smoke up out of every other building, and there was a constant low-grade rumble of noise in the background. Occasional bangs, crashes, and other sounds broke through the ambient clatter.

Ferris was able to find a shop that was selling mundane tools and cookware near the center. They had a small selection of weapons, but the focus was on metal household goods and tools. Anything magical would be sold from the merit store, and there wasn’t a large need for mundane weaponry.

He was able to find an axe with a large, wide head. It should work to completely sever a part of Jade’s body, preventing the slime from reforming around it like a smaller blade. The mundane metal would dissolve if it was left too long in Jade’s body, but it wouldn’t be a problem if they were quick enough. In the end, it would be worth it as long as they got through the tests.

After he purchased it, Ferris and Jade returned to Lily House for lunch. They ate a quick meal, then set off for the training hall to meet Serena, the axe strapped to Ferris’ back.

They were earlier than usual, as Ferris wanted to prepare for his tests of Jade’s memory without Serena. An attendant showed him to a room, then left. He set the axe down next to the table and settled in to wait. It was the first time he’d gotten there first, which was a pleasant change of pace.

Ferris pulled out his journal and spent some time giving Jade a new set of numbers and various other information to remember. Afterwards, he pulled out his own bag of carrots from Mother Lily and began chopping.

When Serena arrived—still well before the appointed time—her eyes scanned the room with practiced efficacy, briefly taking in the carrots and the axe before landing on him and Jade sitting at the table next to the usual cot. She was carrying a large sack over her shoulder that clanked when she moved. She set it down with a bang in the corner.

“Greetings, Ferris and Jade,” she said, walking over to the table.

“Greetings, Serena,” Ferris replied, still chopping carrots.

“You are early today.”

“Yes, I wanted to be first for once.” He gave her a grin.

Serena looked back with an amused smile, gestured to the axe, then asked, “Does this mean you have a plan for today?”

“Yes,” Ferris said, a little embarrassed to have to admit failure to her yet again. “I am afraid that we cannot start working on Acid Resistance today, as I was unable to acquire the merit points required for it. I should be able to have it before our next meeting, four days from now.”

She was probably as disappointed as he was, but she had the grace not to show it.

“In the meantime,” Ferris continued, “I’ve been thinking about our tests last time.”

The tests that hadn’t prepared him for the yellow scarabs. That had forced him to make decisions with Jade’s life on the line, without knowing what would happen.

He looked her in the eyes.

“I think it is time we got more serious.”
