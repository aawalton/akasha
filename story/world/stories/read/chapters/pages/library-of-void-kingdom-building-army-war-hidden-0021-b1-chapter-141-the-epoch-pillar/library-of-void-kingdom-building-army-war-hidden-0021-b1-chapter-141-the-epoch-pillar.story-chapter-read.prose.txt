Vector's eyes traced over the status window a second time, just to be certain — to confirm that he had truly achieved Basic Mastery of the Astral Knife Art in a mere three hours. When the words remained unchanged, he exhaled a breath he hadn't even realized he'd been holding.

A broad smile broke across his face. It wasn't only the System's confirmation that lifted his spirits; he had practiced the knife art once more immediately after exiting the Simulation, and the results had been just as unmistakable in the real world as they were on the interface. With both confirmations aligned, a wave of pure elation washed over him. A Martial Art, mastered in three hours. He couldn't begin to imagine what someone else would think if they ever found out — though even accounting for time spent within the Simulation, the true figure was closer to ninety hours. Still, the achievement felt nothing short of extraordinary.

Most of all, he wanted to shove this progress right under his grandfather's nose. The old man never missed an opportunity to boast about how he had mastered the Basic Spear Art in three months, and at the grand age of twenty-one, no less. But if he ever learned that his grandson — still twenty, though half way to twenty-one — had already mastered two Martial Arts, the expression that would dawn on that proud face would be worth every drop of sweat.

System…? Then again, he owed a share of that pride to the System, and he knew it. But was that really so different? Luck, opportunity, circumstance — these were all facets of strength, just as much as raw talent or grueling discipline. Without the System, his progress might well have mirrored his grandfather's, steady and unremarkable. With System, the ceiling felt limitless.

Still basking in that quiet satisfaction, he let his gaze linger on the interface a moment longer — and that was when something caught his attention. The Basic Stats had shifted. Strength, Agility, and Dexterity had each climbed by 0.1, modest but undeniable. Vitality, however, had surged the most, rising by 0.5 — from 71.5 to a clean 72.

He queried the System, and the answer came swiftly. It was the work of his physique, the Void Body. While he had been immersed in the Simulation Space, his body had been quietly and continuously absorbing the ambient Chaos Energy, and that absorption had steadily fed into his Vitality, with the other stats rising in correspondence.

The moment he understood this, a part of him wanted nothing more than to live permanently within the Simulation. But reality, as it always did, reasserted itself. There was work to be done in the real world, responsibilities that couldn't be sidestepped, and so he pushed the thought aside — though not without a deep and eager anticipation for that evening's simulation session.

It wasn't a matter of preference so much as necessity. He had every intention of pushing further in his Body Refinement stage, but the System had cautioned him clearly — his body required three to four days of rest before he could safely resume that particular training. And even if his body had been ready, the elixirs, required for it, presented their own obstacle.

It wasn't that he lacked the elixirs and materials for the Second Stage; he had more than ten complete sets gathered already. The problem was that those herbs were no longer sufficient for what lay ahead. What he truly needed was something rarer — something that, in Body Refining, almost no one ever sought out. It was an optional herb; one theoretically reserved for Fourth Stage warriors seeking to harden their bodies beyond ordinary limits. The Vajradanti. It was said to render one's bones comparable in hardness to diamonds — perhaps even harder. But the reasons so few pursued it were equally well known: its near-total absence from the market, and the soul-crushing, excruciating pain that accompanied its use. Even Sebastian, seasoned as he was, had not yet made up his mind on whether to take it, when he would reach 4th Stage. Vector, however, did not have the luxury of deliberation. It was necessary for his further progress in his Body Refinement.

His family, the Astrums, possessed the Vajradanti. But to claim it, he would have to travel to Elara, the capital city of Avalon — his home. After the immediate matters here, in Vantara, were settled, that journey would have to be made.

Today was the 3rd of Solstition. The Lionheart's assault was expected on the 17th of Inviction — roughly sixty days away, given that a month in Ethereum spanned forty-five days rather than thirty. The window was generous enough, but the trip to Elara would need to happen sooner, within a week probably, rather than later otherwise time would be constrained. Without the Vajradanti, he would be stranded at the First Stage for weeks, and that was a stagnation he simply could not afford.

Vector's gaze swept across the vast plain stretching northward, where the distant glimmer of the Saraswat River cut a shining line across the horizon, just barely within reach of his vision. Arthur and Leo walked ahead, leading the way with familiar ease, while Sebastian kept pace beside him, with Marcus, Tristan, and Rhea rounding out the group at their flanks.

It had been Arthur's idea, offered casually over breakfast that morning — a guided walk through the terrain surrounding Vantara, a chance to get a proper feel for the land before anything else demanded their attention. No one had objected, and so here they were, north of the village at last, standing before the very lifeline that sustained it.

The Saraswat River.

Where the other sides of Vantara were hemmed in by dense, rugged forest and the imposing shoulders of mountain terrain, the northern expanse opened up into something altogether different — broad, breathing plains given almost entirely to cultivated land. Endless fields rolled out beneath the morning light, dominated by golden wheat that rippled and swayed in slow, unhurried waves wherever the breeze chose to pass through.

Under Aethel’s light, the crops shone so brilliantly that the fields almost seemed coated in gold itself.

“Young Master,” Arthur said, breaking Vector’s quiet observation, “this is our primary source of food. It still isn’t enough to fully sustain the village, but if used carefully, it serves us well enough. These wheat crops were planted near the end of winter and are almost ready for harvest.”

Vector nodded faintly as his eyes swept across the fields once more.

From what he could judge, the wheat would likely be ready within ten to fifteen days. Villagers moved throughout the farmland, tending crops and inspecting irrigation channels. Fortunately, Vantara’s proximity to Ironfang Hill City allowed them to compensate for shortages in food and daily necessities through trade.

After crossing the fields, the group arrived near the banks of the Saraswat River itself.

The water appeared remarkably deep.

As Vector’s gaze followed the river eastward, his expression gradually stiffened.

Shock flickered through his eyes.

The sight before him should not have been possible.

The river passed directly through the mountain range.

Not around it.

Through it.

He understood the geology well enough to know how rivers carved canyons — centuries of relentless force wearing stone down grain by grain, finding the path of least resistance around obstacles, never through them. A steep mountain face was not an obstacle a river navigated head-on. The water would skirt it, bend around it, find another way. That was simply how it worked. And yet the Saraswat did none of those things. Its path cut clean and deliberate through the sheer rock, in a manner that no natural process could account for. It was either man-made, or something far beyond ordinary human capability had been brought to bear here — and from what Vector could see, the latter seemed far more likely.

He had barely formed the thought into words when Rhea's voice rang out beside him, sharp with surprise and curiosity.

"Chief Arthur — that river's path...?" She extended a finger toward the steep eastern mountain face; her expression caught somewhere between disbelief and fascination.

Vector was equally eager to hear whatever context existed — whether local lore, recorded history, or something passed down through the customs of Vantara. By every measure of Ethereum's current power and technology, what he was looking at should have been impossible.

Even by Earth's technology, such a thing would have bordered on absurdity.

Unless…

Supernatural power truly had been involved.

Arthur smiled faintly, as though he had expected this exact reaction.

“This is also the reason Vantara managed to survive within the Naga Forest for so long,” he explained calmly. “According to the lore passed down by our ancestors, the Saraswat River never originally flowed through this place. Its old course was along the northern side of the Ironfang Mountain Range.”

Vector had already reached the same conclusion.

But the more important question remained:

How had the river’s course been changed?

And why? And Arthur, as though sensing the questions forming in silence, continued without prompting.

“But,” Arthur continued, pointing toward the split mountain face in the distance, “someone cut the mountain in half with a single swing of a sword, creating that steep divide. Then, with another strike, they carved the canyon itself and redirected the Saraswat River through it.”

Silence followed.

Vector stared toward the mountains, trying to fully process the implications.

If his memory was correct, Ironfang Hill City lay somewhere in that direction as well, and the next words confirmed what he had already begun to suspect.

“The river was redirected for the sake of Ironfang Hill City. The city itself was established because of the mines within the mountains. Redirecting the river improved both mining efficiency and access to water.”

Hearing it laid out so plainly, Vector became increasingly convinced that the story was not merely folklore. Lore didn't emerge from nothing; there was always a root, always a reason buried beneath the lore.

And since coming into contact with the System, he had come to understand that such a feat — reshaping a mountain with a single strike — was not merely legend if the person wielding the blade had advanced far enough into Origin Energy cultivation, which was due to some unknown reason absent currently in Ethereum.

Otherwise, even he would have dismissed the tale as nothing more than exaggerated myth.

After the exchange settled into a comfortable silence, the group turned westward, moving into the dense foliage of the Naga Forest. From there, they continued in a broad arc — south along the periphery of the village wall. Compared to the northern region, the southern forest was noticeably thinner, most likely due to constant human activity over generations.

The southern side also housed the main gate, the same one they had passed through the previous day, and the road that connected Vantara to Ironfang Hill City.

Maintaining their path along the village perimeter, they pressed on eastward until, finally, they reached the eastern perimeter — where the Ironfang Mountains rose abruptly from the earth in a sheer, imposing natural barrier.

Arthur gestured toward the mountain, clean like a wall.

“This marks the eastern boundary of the village,” he said.

Vector stood there for a moment, turning the full picture of the terrain over in his mind. What he had observed across the entire periphery made one thing abundantly clear: Vantara was not equally vulnerable on all sides.

The north was protected by the river — a natural barrier wide, deep, and swift enough to make any coordinated assault from that direction an exercise in futility.

The east was effectively sealed by the sheer mountain face, terrain so unforgiving that even 2nd Stage Warrior would struggle to traverse it.

So, it made launching an attack from either direction would be nearly impossible—especially for a large military force.

Which meant only one viable invasion route remained. The southwest. And coincidentally, Carnatic City also lay in south direction.

The closer Vector studied the mountain face, the more his certainty solidified into something unshakeable. The cliff was unnaturally smooth — stripped entirely of the jagged protrusions and irregular formations that stone accumulated over centuries of natural weathering. It was too clean, too deliberate, too absolute in its geometry to have been shaped by wind or water or time. Whether or not a single sword strike had truly split it apart as the legends described, one thing was beyond dispute: no natural process had produced this wall.

And that, in turn, settled something else in his mind. What Arthur had said at the bank of the Saraswat was not the embellished mythology of people trying to make sense of something they didn't understand. It was history — true and specific — preserved in the only form that extraordinary truth could survive in: as a story most people were comfortable not believing.

He continued to study the face in silence.

Then a thought surfaced, slowly at first, before sharpening into something concrete.

A mountain dwelling.

If the interior of this terrain could be hollowed out — worked methodically from within — it could become something not unlike the hidden sanctuary his family had carved into the Agastya Mountains. That refuge existed in near-total secrecy, known exclusively to those who carried the Astrum bloodline. Not even Sebastian and the others had ever been made aware of it, and that invisibility was its greatest strength.

But what he was imagining now was something of a different nature entirely.

A concealed stronghold — hidden from the outside world, yes, but known and accessible to the Astrum Forces themselves. A military base that the terrain would protect better than any wall his people could raise with their own hands.

And Vantara, it seemed, might offer exactly the right foundation for it.
