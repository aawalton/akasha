The key… all this time I had thought it would be an item—a trinket for someone to wear, very much like the black ankh I had taken from Hyena. Not once had I imagined that the picture the goddess had given me might have been a literal representation.

A tattoo, I would have laughed if things weren’t so depressingly serious.

I knew that Max couldn’t have betrayed me. I didn’t remember seeing him at all in my previous run. It had to be someone else, a group or organization of participants. For a moment, the goths came to mind. It was undeniable that Eleonor had helped me, but the same could be said of the people who had betrayed me before.

“Serp, how is she?” I asked as I stood above Max.

The unfortunate wretch was trying to crawl away with two swords in his head. Every few seconds Anubis’ boon would activate, pulling him back from the verge of non-existence.

Alive, if that’s what you’re asking, the serpent blade replied. I was very careful about it.

“Can she talk?”

Probably. Not at the moment, though. The only thing she can do right now is breathe.

So, Eleonor was out. I’d have to ask her more on the topic when she came to. On that note, I’d have to have a similar conversation with Sparta as well. Currently, I had seen both of them naked and didn’t remember spotting any key-shaped tattoos. That said, I hadn’t been looking.

I bent down and pulled one of the divine blades out of Max’s head.

“What’s the meaning of the tattoo?” I asked.

The man let out a deep groan, not responding to me in any meaningful way.

“Zap!” I said sharply.

A new wave of electricity went through the participant’s brain.

ANUBIS boon activated.

MAXIMILLIAN SWANSEN has been reconstructed.

He writhed in pain, using what little strength he had left.

“What does the key mean?” I pulled the other blade out, then thrust it through his leg, pinning him down. There was no way I’d completely deprive him of punishment. The only thing I needed was his head to function a bit less. “Tell me that and I’ll let you go,” I said loudly and clearly. “Or kill you. Whatever you prefer.”

Right now, he was probably pingponging between the two.

“Don’t, and I’ll keep zapping you for the next hour.”

Our whole fight had lasted minutes. The notion of having to endure all that and more for an hour made him shiver like a leaf in autumn.

“It’s a gift!” he said, his words almost reaching the level of a shout. “A gift to the chosen.”

I waited for a few seconds. When nothing followed, I ordered the blade to zap him again.

“It’s the mark of freedom!” Max was quick to add. “It lets us leave the Arcana.”

“No one leaves the Arcana,” I said, even if I didn’t fully believe it.

According to the goddess in my past run, there was a way, although she had never said anything about a key, not one like that in any event.

“There is. That’s how we know about your sister.”

What?! If he was telling the truth, it meant that the siren had played me. Concentrating, I tried to think back to my fight against her. Parts of it were rather vivid, but a lot of detail was missing.

Shit! I thrust the second divine blade into the soft floor, inches from Max’s face.

I should have seen that coming. Just because the siren was the enemy of my enemy, that didn’t mean she wasn’t my enemy as well. She had tried to torture and petrify me, and there was a good chance she would again. Had she had a key this whole time? There was no other way she could know about the outside world.

“We were promised an escape!” Max continued talking, misinterpreting my action. “Freedom from the Grand Arcana and a pass to rule.” He tried to drag his body forward, but the blade in his leg prevented him from doing so. “That’s all I know. I never got to use it.”

“The siren,” I changed the topic. “Why were you hiding her?”

“What?”

“The creature you keep locked in the underground cell,” I said louder. “Why is she here?”

“She’s being punished. She’s out of control. Can’t follow orders, plays with her prey instead of doing what she’s told.” He groaned again.

I was tempted to zap him just to vent a bit of anger, but chose not to.

“She defied the deities, so her key was taken away. She’s trapped here with the rest of the rats… It was supposed to serve as punishment, but she enjoyed it too much. Dozens of hopefuls killed and turned to stone.”

“She turned them to stone?”

“The Stoneweaver was her card.” Max spat in front of him. “I was given it to keep her here. No one was supposed to find out.”

That bitch had definitely played everyone. During our last meeting, she had created the distinct impression that she was willing to change sides. That had been nothing more than an act to get her free. I had no idea when she had devised it. Could it be that was the reason she had let herself be killed during the underwater section of the cruise ship trial? I couldn’t put it beyond her.

One look at me and she had decided to roll the dice. Maybe she had even used her ability to predict it, then set a chain of events in motion that brought us here. The worst part about it, there was nothing I could do.

Even if I left Max and rushed for the well, there was no way I’d reach her in time. The statue guardians were no longer under anyone’s control. And even if they were, the rest of the group would arrive and kill the unfortunate participant long before I even managed to leave the aelf castle. Everything had been planned to the last detail. We had a fall guy who barely knew a thing; meanwhile, someone who was a lot more familiar with the deity opposing the rule-masters and the significance of the keys, was about to escape by my hand.

“Which is the deity behind this?” I went to the reason for our trial.

“I can’t…”

I briskly pulled both blades out of the ground, then sank them into his skull again.

“Zap.”

ANUBIS boon activated.

MAXIMILLIAN SWANSEN has been reconstructed.

“Who is it?” I pulled them out.

“I’m not allowed!” Max screamed, desperation filling his voice. “They made sure I can’t! No one who knows the truth can. It’s the price of knowledge.”

“What truth?”

Mentally, I was still reanalyzing everything I’d gone through during this run based on the new information I had just learned. There were too many connections between things. Even now I had no idea whether a singly monolithic organization was involved or were there multiple competing groups loosely under the same banner.

The siren princess seemed to be a wildcard formerly working with the chosen of Anubis. Carl and his brother were also part, possibly even before entering the Grand Arcana. Maybe because of Eleonor, the goths seemed to be separate. Death and dream deities didn’t mix well. And then, there was Sparta—the love of my past. She was the only person I had mentioned my plan to escape, so she could very well be involved with what happened to me afterward. Was she part of any side? Or was she a free agent like the siren?

“What truth?” I repeated, sensing that the silence had gone on for a bit too long.

“We’re all—”

Red mist seeped out of the ground quickly, enveloping Max in a cocoon before he could finish. Instinctively, I stepped back.

I recognized the mist. It wasn’t a skill Max had, nor was it a new enemy. One might argue that it was a very old enemy, one that I had known for over ten years.

“Oh, Maximillian,” Harlequin’s voice filled the room. “You had such high promise, and yet you turned into such a disappointment.”

The red cocoon rose into the air. The man inside was twisting about like a caterpillar trying to break out of its own skin. It was pointless. Nothing could escape Harlequin’s clutches. The scene very much reminded me of what had happened to me at the very end of the last run. The red-and-yellow monster had stood above me when I had been in a similar state and expressed his regret of my fall.

Did he know anything about the keys? In the past he had denied it, but it was only because I had asked the wrong question.

“Congratulations, participants!” Harlequin’s voice boomed again. “You have completed the trial with flying colors. It is unfortunate that no deities were able to see your performance, but I must say with a high degree of certainty that all rule-masters were absolutely thrilled.”

The bundle that was Max faded away, leaving only air behind.

“It also pleases me to say that most of you survived this ordeal, which was substantially more than I was expecting,” he continued. “You have truly distinguished yourselves from the trash that dragged you down during the initial trials.”

Red mist swirled around my feet. Looking at Serp, I could see it starting to envelop him as well.

Quickly, I unsummoned the weapon. Eleonor remained on the floor, unconscious. The wound in her leg had just started to bleed. Not that it mattered. Once we were back in the preparation hall, all our wounds would be dealt with, hopefully for free.

“You have achieved everything I had hoped,” Harlequin said as the mist surrounded me. “Now, let me welcome you back.”

The redness intensified. I returned the divine blades to my inventory and waited. My thoughts wandered back to the times I had spent with Sparta during my previous run. Back then, the only thing I needed to worry about was getting killed.

When the mist cleared again, I was back in the hall. The walls were still painted red and yellow. Also, Harlequin was there, sitting on his throne.

Maintaining my poker face, I looked at the other participants. All of their eyes—with the exception of Marcus—turned gold for a few seconds before returning to their usual colors. They were just like devices connecting to a Wi-Fi after a power outage.

Many had gained a few additional cards since I had lost sight of them in the aelf castle. Clearly, the Gatekeeper wasn’t the only opponent they had faced while I had been dealing with Maximillian.

“There you are.” Harlequin’s mask turned left and right as if it were a steering wheel. “My magnificent semi-dozen. All of you did splendidly.” He looked at each of us in turn. While the mask faced Sparta and Reginald, it stopped for a few seconds longer than the rest. “Most of you, at least,” he added.

Even a successful mission wasn’t enough to erase the hatred between the rule-masters. I had no idea how long this attitude would last. If the past was any indication, Harlequin could hold a grudge for years, or forget about it in a day.

“Before you receive your much-deserved rewards, there is some sad news I must make you aware of,” the monster said, leaning back in his cushioned throne. “Starting tomorrow, this group will be dissolved.”

I used the Observer card to gauge the other’s reactions. None of them seemed in the least surprised, even Marcus. After knowing so much about the trials so far, I suddenly felt like the only one who hadn’t received the memo.

“It was to be expected,” he let out a fake sigh. The stillness of his mask made it all the more disingenuous. “This was merely a rule-masters’ trial, which means for all intents and purposes it doesn’t count. There will be no deity rewards and no explanations. Let me only reassure the powers that be that the matter has been dealt with swiftly and efficiently, with only a third of the participants dying in the process.”

Always lying, I thought. Even to the deities.

The matter was far from resolved. If anything, we had only scratched the surface. There was no telling who stood behind the mess, what their goal was, or who the others involved were. Not only that, but we had set an insane siren free—something that I felt would come back to bite us in the ass. Also, I strongly doubted that Carl had died. The cockroach had always been resourceful, to levels I couldn’t imagine. If he really had a “key” there was a chance that he could go in and out of the Grand Arcana at will. There was no telling how many groups he had been part of, all the time pretending to be a witless piece of shit. After what I did to his brother, he was bound to have it in for me.

“Tomorrow, the Grand Arcana returns to normal, and our valued participants will join others of their caliber as they proceed to their official sixth trial.” Harlequin stood up, extending his arms as if talking to an audience.

No one dared to make a sound.

“Finally, as a token of my thanks, I will personally allow our survivors to claim the cards that were in the possession of the miscreant that broke the pact of the Grand Arcana.” He raised his hands, as if rousing the invisible audience. “Those who fail to claim a reward will receive placeholder cards instead.”

Of course they will. I tried not to laugh.

Even after everything, he was already back to his old game. My only choice was to bear it and keep on playing.
