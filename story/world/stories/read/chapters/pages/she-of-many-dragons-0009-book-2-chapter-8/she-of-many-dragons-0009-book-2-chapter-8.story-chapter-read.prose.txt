"Perhaps you can eat coins?" Numi said helpfully. She reached into her mouth and pulled out one, only a little dripping with saliva, from her stomach's storage space. "You never know until you try."

"I do not need to try to know I want food, not coins," Iggy snapped.

"No," Natura agreed, "but you can certainly eat plants."

"There are edible plants around here?" Alice asked.

"Of course," she said. "I'll show you."

The nature dragon was as good as her word and was able to save the day—or rather, their meal. The forest was fairly devoid of animal life except for the eels, but it did have tuberous roots and mushrooms Natura assured were not poisonous.

To effectively forage, they had to leave the area surrounding the river. The eels could flop out of the water and eat anything else within quite a range.

Iggy and Prim grumbled about their meatless dinner. But though it lacked protein, it still made for a better meal than anything Alice had as a General Laborer. Mushrooms speared on a spit and cooked over an open fire tasted rather nice, as long as one did not accidentally drop them into the flames.

That night as they lounged around the campfire, Iggy advised a rapt Aconite on basic battle tactics. Already, the newest sub-aspect felt like a part of the family.

Alice smiled to herself.

****

It took two more days to clear out the river of the eels. The next morning after the Eel Mother had been defeated, there was a brief surge in the local population.

Natura explained that more had traveled from either upstream or downstream—likely sensing that the Eel Mother was in trouble.

Working together, the dragons wiped those out, too.

The real test came when Natura once more grew seeds along the riverbank using her Enriching Soil skill. This time, nothing came boiling out of the water to eat the shoots.

In fact several hours later, a small herd of deer, their muzzles terribly scarred from eel bites, were spotted tentatively drinking from the stream. They were thin beasts with dull coats, and incredibly nervous about the water. It was easy to imagine they only braved the eel infested waters when forced by extreme thirst.

But they were not harassed either and were allowed to drink their fill. The eels were truly gone.

"I could stand to eat some venison," Iggy commented, also watching the deer.

"Let them be," Alice said, "but I believe that our work is done here."

"There will be more in different forks of the stream," Natura said with a sigh. "But this, at least, is clean."

The next morning they continued traveling through the forest. The sights did not improve. As they went, the tree bark along the trunks and branches blackened until the trees themselves sickened and died.

Alice didn't like this country at all, but she wasn't ready to turn back. Instead, she focused on encouraging her dragons to level their skills.

Numi hunted out any treasures, though interesting things were thin on the ground. She mostly came back with copper coins, greened by age.

Prim concentrated on improving her illusions. Iggy scouted ahead with Spark and kept a strict lookout for danger. His job was to keep them safe.

Alice needed to level up in order to hatch the egg. But unlike any other classes she had heard of, she could do nothing herself. Her strength and levels came through her dragons.

And as the days went on, she had to admit that Natura's skill for enriching the soil was much more helpful than she'd thought it would ever be.

The little dragon was interested in leveling it quickly and went from blackened tree to blackened tree to press her claws at the base and work the soil using her power. However, she skipped many trees as well.

When Alice questioned why, Natura shook her head sadly. "Many of these are too far gone to save, and it's getting worse as we go deeper into the forest."

"You're helping the soil," Prim said, cocking her head, "but I don't see any changes in the trees."

"Because trees are not grass shoots. They take time to heal. If we come back in a few weeks, we'll see new growth, but..." she shook her head again, "many are too far gone."

"What did this?" Numi asked.

"I'm not sure. It's not a fungus. It feels like this is the result of something beyond normal nature. It's as if all the nutrients have been leached away."

That was concerning, but not so much that they wanted to turn back. Natura insisted that whatever was killing the plant life would not jump to animals.

Also, except for a few scattered old stone buildings, there was no sign of human habitation. If the roofs were still good—or good enough to keep the rain off them—they used the old buildings to rest in at night.

Numi diligently scoured every nook and cranny but was unable to find more treasures left behind or any hints of what made them leave.

“I just keep finding more skeletons,” she told Alice in disgust. “What’s worse, the graves have all been raided already. See?”

She took Alice to the base of a tree outside the building they’d slept in last night. She swept some of the loose leaves away. Underneath was an open shallow ditch serving as a grave. The bones were visibly cracked and weathered.

“I found other eggs, too—or their shells. Whoever dug up all the skeletons smashed them.”

Alice swallowed and reflexively touched the deep pocket in her skirts where she kept the egg. She wished she had a more appropriate way to carry it, but she needed her only bag for their supplies. She’d lined her pocket with soft moss, and the egg did have the advantage of her body heat. It would have to do for now.

“We haven’t found anything other than dead forest for over a week now.” Alice gave a sigh. “It makes me wonder if I’m doing the right thing here… if we should just return after all.”

Prim, on her shoulder, fluttered her wings. “Back to the city with Orris? I’d rather not.”

“It would be a risk,” Alice admitted. “I’m not sure if he’s exactly friendly or not but…” She trailed off and shook her head. “No, I suppose I’m not willing to turn around just yet.”

But we will soon if it looks like things are not growing more promising, she silently thought.

Sure enough, however, as they traveled on the forest grew more bleak around them. Now every tree was visibly blackened and dead. It made for a grim scene, like walking through a graveyard. Even the vegetables Natura found for their meals were growing thinner.

“We will go one more day,” Alice told her dragons that next night. “Then we are turning around.”

She felt like a failure, but what else was she to do?

****

Halfway through the next morning, Prim, who sat on her customary place on Alice’s shoulder, suddenly raised her wings so abruptly she knocked one into Alice’s ear.

“Everyone stop!” Prim cried.

Instantly, they all did. Iggy, who had been flying ahead, quickly doubled back to join them.

“What is it?” Alice asked.

They had just crossed over a shallow, muddy stream. While it didn’t have any blood eels, it didn’t have any visible life in it, either. The blackened forest ahead was so long dead that all the trees had completely shed their leaves, leaving behind thick debris on the ground. There weren’t any animals to trod it all down.

All was still and quiet and clear ahead.

For a long moment, Prim didn’t say anything. Her sharply pointed head faced straight forward.

“There is an illusion just ahead of us,” she said. “What you’re seeing is not actually there.”

Everyone looked back to the forest.

“It just looks like trees,” Numi said. “Dead trees.”

Alice frowned. Numi was right. “Can you tell us what is ahead?” she asked. Privately, she wondered if they were just about to come across another hidden city.

“I cannot tell,” Prim answered. “Only that my own illusion skill tells me that it is false.”

“Then those dead trees are hiding something!” Iggy sounded thrilled. “I will go and flush it out!”

“No,” Alice said. “Send Spark to do it.”

Her tone brooked no argument and it wasn’t until the words were out that she realized she had just made a cold, calculating decision. Between Iggy and his sub-aspect, she would rather put Spark in danger. He was more expendable to her.

None of her dragons seemed to fault her for it. Iggy only looked a little put out that he couldn’t play the hero.

Spark was more than happy to rush forward. He flitted ahead, the electric blue that ran through his scales seeming to look brighter in the gloom.

As soon as he entered the trees, it was as if a branch suddenly curved down to try to snatch him out of the air.

No, not a branch, Alice realized with horror. Now that the ‘branch’ was moving, its part of the illusion fell away to reveal a spindly, bark-like hand.

Spark had been anticipating danger, and at the very first sign of it he had tucked his wings to dive straight down. That saved him from being snatched.

Only, another thin hand reached to grab him from his dive, and when he avoided that, there was another, and another.

The entire illusion abruptly shattered and fell away. Alice saw dozens of man-sized creatures standing in and among the trees, with long, branch-like limbs and heads filled with needle teeth. Each was connected to a tree via a vine that went from their stomach. It looked like an umbilical cord.

Instinctively, Alice drew back though the things were dozens of yards away.

“Tree wraiths!” Natura shrieked. “Oh, they’re terrible parasites! No wonder all the nutrients have been sucked away from the forest.”

“How do you always identify these things?” Numi asked, but Natura ignored her.

Perched on Natura’s back, Aconite was spitting fury at the wraiths with the rill around his neck fully extended in threat.

“No, Aconite,” Natura said to him. “Venom will not kill them. Only fire wire.”

“That’s what I wished to hear!” Iggy cried and then dashed forward with his burning breath and claws.

Spark had managed to weave out of immediate danger, but he had not fully retreated out of the way. He twisted sharply in the air and landed on the back of one of the wraiths. Lightning arced between them and the creature burst into flame.

Those flames immediately caught the thick, leafy debris underfoot. That took another wraith by surprise and burned it whole.

Iggy roared a challenge and raked his long flaming claws along the barky skin of a wraith.

A wraith jumped down from a tree to try to grab the red dragon, but could not get a grip on his Ultimate Armor. Iggy easily broke free and roared his fire breath right in its face. Droplets of flame splashed off and caught the surrounding dead brush as well.

In fact, it looked like the flames had been waiting just for this moment. Ground cover of dead leaves, shin high deep in places caught even easier than paper, and the dead trunks of the trees welcomed the fire like an old friend. Flames raced upward and then used the thin wind to jump from tree to tree, claiming more wraiths.

The parasites started the shriek in high-pitched voices, like the wind whistling through the branches.

“Iggy! Spark! Stop, the fire is catching!” Alice yelled. The battle had only been moments but it was far too late. With horror, she realized her two combat dragons had no skills to contain what they’d just unleashed.

She looked down at Natura, wondering if anything in her suite of nature skills could prevent this growing disaster.

However, she only looked on with satisfaction. Catching Alice’s eye, she nodded. “No, the fire is very good. These terrible wraiths must be destroyed.”

Prim raised her wings in agitation. “Perhaps, but we cannot get caught up in this. What do we do?” It was a mark of how unsettled she felt that she asked Natura directly instead of taking charge herself.

Natura stood on her hind legs and sniffed. “Let’s move to the east. We must get out of the wind.”

By now the fire seemed to generate a wind storm of its own, which only allowed it to spread faster. Flames caught high branches like kindling and the new embers spread to trees further away, that caught just as easily.

Wraiths were unhooking themselves from their trunks and trying to flee, but any that were in the direction of the wind could not move fast enough.

Alice recalled Iggy and Spark, who promptly joined them.

As it turned out, Iggy did have some very limited effect on close-by flames. When he flapped his wings, he could somewhat push the fire away. But the growing wildfire was too strong for him to affect directly.

It was Natura who led the group out of the way of the rapidly spreading fire. Unfortunately, the wind was whipping up in all directions and prone to shift in a breath. Between one step and another, they were caught in a gout of choking smoke and heat.

The soot was blinding. Alice couldn’t see her hand in front of her face, much less her dragons. All she felt was heat.

Natura gently grabbed her hand in her teeth and pulled her further on.

Deep in her pocket, she felt the egg start to rock.

It wasn’t hatching quite yet—she knew that with the silent wisdom of her She of Many Dragons class—but she clearly felt how it reacted to the fire… to the destruction.

She and her dragons found their way out of the worst of the smoke. Natura looked back over her shoulder at the fire and gasped, “We must hurry!”

Alice looked too and felt her heart quell in terror.

The fire had grown significantly in the last few short minutes. It now leapt from tree to tree behind them like a living thing. The sounds over the snapping of dead wood and falling limbs were incredible. She couldn’t tell what was made by the fire and what were the dying tree wraiths.

Iggy suddenly darted to the side to rake burning claws at another wraith that had detached itself from a tree and was stumbling toward them.

“Alice, we must get away!” Prim yelled into her ear, and Alice realized she’d been rooted to the spot with terror.

“No, wait.” Her voice was rough from the smoke. She drew the dark egg from her pocket. “It wants the fire.”

Despite Prim’s protests, she walked forward toward where the fire was advancing and laid the egg in a nest of leaf litter.

Coughing from the smoke, she returned to her dragons.

“Mark the spot,” she told Natura.

Then Alice and her dragons ran for their lives.
