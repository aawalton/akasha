The problem with the Verdant Expanse had started on Day 3.

Day 3 was when the event’s World Activity scoring had directed enough players toward botanical resource collection that the Verdant Expanse—the largest open flora zone on the server—became the most trafficked area in the championship. Players arrived with collection quotas. They harvested efficiently. They did not ask the plants if they had feelings about this.

The plants had feelings about this.

Specifically: the emotional resonance from the Glimmer-Phina wedding had dissipated unevenly through the Verdant Expanse for weeks, settling into the flora in proximity to Livia’s regular presence. Those plants had developed the capacity for something that functioned like awareness. Not intelligence, exactly. But awareness. Memory. Reaction.

And now several hundred players were harvesting them for event points.

On Day 3 the response had been passive: plants moved when touched, roots tightened, some species had produced a faint luminescence that players found attractive and collected more aggressively. By Day 4, active resistance had begun. Thorns that had not previously existed. Vines that redirected. One cluster of forget-me-nots had organized themselves into a ring formation that Thorne had photographed extensively and labeled "defensive perimeter, unprecedented, see Yellow Tab Seven."

Day 5 had been the first injury. A mid-tier player named Garrett had tried to harvest a specimen Thorne had flagged as "emotionally significant, do not disturb" and had taken 847 damage from a root spike that came from nowhere. He had posted about it on the forums. The post had forty-two comments, most of which were variations of "lmao skill issue."

On Day 6, the forum tone changed.

[SERVER NOTICE — DAY 6 — VERDANT EXPANSE]

[WARNING: Flora in World Activity Zone 4 (Verdant Expanse) demonstrating anomalous hostile behavior.]

[Recommended: Reduce harvesting activity. Approach with caution.]

[Note: Behavior is localized. Cause under investigation.]

The notice went up at 8am.

By 9am, fourteen guild parties had entered the Verdant Expanse specifically because of the notice.

The recommended approach was caution.

The server’s player base interpreted caution as: unusual mob behavior, probably has good drops.

I have been here nine months.

I understand this now.

Thorne found Livia at breakfast with the expression of a man who had been in the field since 5am and had come back specifically to say something.

“They’re going to make it worse,” he said. He set his field notebook on the table. Yellow Tab Eight, Nine, and Ten were flagged. “The eastern sector is in active defensive formation. Three species in the western approach are showing coordinated response patterns I have never documented before. If mass harvest parties push into the center cluster—” he stopped.

“What’s in the center cluster?” Livia asked.

Thorne looked at her with the expression of someone who had been waiting for exactly this question.

“The oldest flora in the zone,” he said. “The ones that have been here longest. If any of them have retained memory from before the wedding resonance, if any of them understood what was happening when it settled into them—” he paused, —“they will not respond well to being raided.”

The oldest flora.

Memory retention from before the wedding.

Being raided by mass harvest parties.

This is going to be very bad.

By noon, the Verdant Expanse was generating the most dramatic server activity feed Livia had seen since the Ironfall pact dissolution.

It started with individual reports. Garrett’s root spike had been written off as a freak occurrence, but by 10am there were eleven similar incidents across the zone. Mid-tier players hit by environmental hazards that had not existed during any prior server build. Root spikes, yes, but also: a vine formation that had locked a warrior’s movement for forty-seven seconds during what should have been a routine harvest. A mage who had cast AOE fire at a cluster and found the cluster’s response was not burning but a coordinated counterattack that had stripped her concentration buffs entirely. A rogue who tried to stealth through the eastern perimeter and had found, to his evident distress, that the flora tracked him anyway.

Then the northern approach.

The center cluster was older growth—larger specimens, denser canopy, the kind of flora that had been rooted in a place long enough to have opinions about it. They had been quiet during the early days of the event. They had watched the harvest parties move through the outer zones. They had not intervened.

While all of this was happening, Thorne had gone back into the Verdant Expanse.

Not to harvest. Not to document—or not primarily. His combat log was not dramatic. He was a Lv.94 Feral Archer; the plants that had nearly killed Yuki presented him with tactical challenges rather than existential ones.
