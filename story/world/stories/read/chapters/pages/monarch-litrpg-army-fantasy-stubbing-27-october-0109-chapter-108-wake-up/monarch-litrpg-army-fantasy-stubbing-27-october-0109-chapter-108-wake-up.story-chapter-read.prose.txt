Rayne didn’t know what was going on.

All around him, there was only darkness. Not the kind that came with night or shadow, but something far thicker. It felt as if he had been submerged in ink itself, every direction swallowing what little sense he had of the world. No matter where he looked, there was nothing. No light. No sound. Just an endless void stretching in all directions.

And yet, he was moving. Or at least, he thought he was.

His legs carried him forward step after step, but there was no ground beneath his feet that he could truly feel. There was no resistance, no texture, nothing to anchor him to anything real. Still, he walked.

Time had no meaning here. It slipped through his grasp no matter how hard he tried to hold onto it. He was certain he had been moving for hours—maybe longer—but the thought itself felt uncertain. There was nothing to measure it against, nothing to confirm if even a single second had passed.

He didn’t know how he had gotten here. He didn’t know where he even was. And he didn’t know if there was a way out.

Still, he kept moving forward, even if that direction meant nothing.

At some point, a faint warmth brushed against him. It was so subtle he almost missed it, like a distant memory of sunlight grazing his skin. His body reacted before his mind could process it, turning slightly toward it, chasing after that small flicker of sensation.

But the moment he tried to reach for it, it vanished. Gone, as if it had never existed at all.

The emptiness that followed felt heavier than before. It settled somewhere deep inside him. For a moment, a strange thought crept into his mind—one that made his chest tighten slightly.

Was he dead?

Rayne paused. The question echoed in his mind. It didn’t feel like a passing thought. It felt real. Too real. And for some reason, it made him wince.

The next moment, something shifted.

Images flooded his mind without warning. They came suddenly and without order, one after another, like fragments of something broken.

He saw himself dying.

In one moment, he was something small and fragile—A fox. One simply running through a forest when something massive crashed into his body. Bones snapped under the force of a charging bull, his form crushed into the ground without resistance.

Then the vision changed.

Lightning split the sky apart, and he was there again, this time as a human. He could see his body locking as the bolt struck him down, tearing through flesh and bone in an instant.

Then again, the scene changed to display an endless sea of flames that swallowed him whole, heat so intense it should have driven him mad. Somewhere above, something massive loomed—a dragon, its presence overwhelming as its breath consumed everything.

Scene after scene passed through his mind showing him death after death.

Yet, as Rayne stood there, watching them unfold, he felt nothing. There was no fear, pain or resistance.

The deaths didn’t feel like something happening to him. They felt distant like echoes of something that had already happened long ago. Like memories he no longer cared about.

He simply watched them, but then, something changed.

The deaths that had felt distant until now began to grow more personal. The strange detachment that had wrapped around his mind started to crack, and with it, a pressure built inside his chest. It wasn’t pain, not exactly, but something heavier. Something that made it harder to breathe.

And then, one scene appeared.

Rayne froze as he saw himself standing in a library. Shelves filled with books stretched across the walls, quiet and undisturbed, as if nothing was wrong. For a brief moment, everything felt almost normal.

Then the door burst open.

A man rushed in, a rifle already raised in his hand.

The silence shattered instantly. Gunshots rang out one after another, loud and deafening as bullets tore through the air. Screams followed, echoing across the room as people dropped to the ground. Some didn’t even have time to react before they were already bleeding out.

Rayne saw himself moving, his body already turning as he ran. Panic drove every step forward, his breath coming fast and uneven as he tried to find some hide behind a shelf to get away from the chaos unfolding around him.

Then the gun turned toward him.

For a single moment, everything slowed, and then the shot came.

Rayne watched as the bullet struck his neck. His body jerked violently, his steps breaking apart as he stumbled forward. His hands rose instinctively, trying to block the wound, but it was already too late. His strength left him all at once, and the ground rushed up to meet him.

The world tilted, blurred, and then everything went still.

The scene didn’t fade immediately. It lingered, and this time, Rayne felt it.

His breath turned ragged, uneven as his chest tightened painfully. It felt like something was squeezing his heart from the inside, refusing to let go. His hands trembled at his sides, fingers twitching as a cold unease spread through his body.

His legs gave out, and he dropped into the darkness.

Unlike the other deaths, this one wasn’t distant. It wasn’t something he could watch without care. There was no separation between him and the memory.

It was his.

That had been his death.

The realization settled heavily over him, pressing down on his thoughts as the image finally began to fade. And when it did, no other deaths followed.

Instead, something else came—Memories.

They surged forward in a flood, clearer and more vivid than anything before. He saw fragments of his life on Earth—classrooms filled with quiet chatter, long corridors he had walked through countless times, late nights spent studying without thinking much of it. Faces appeared one after another. Friends, people he had laughed with, his family. Moments that had once felt ordinary, yet now seemed impossibly distant.

He watched them in silence, unable to look away. And as they passed through his mind, a thought slowly formed.

If that had been the end… if that moment in the library had truly been his last…

Then what was he now? Was he just a soul?

The thought lingered in Rayne’s mind as he sat in the endless darkness. For a brief moment, it felt like the only answer that made sense. This void, this emptiness that stretched in every direction without end—it felt like a place no living being should exist in. A place meant only for those who had already crossed the line between life and death.

Maybe it was hell. As soon as he thought that, a chill ran through him.

But the feeling didn’t last.

Something deep inside him resisted the idea almost immediately. A quiet certainty that this wasn’t the end. That this darkness, no matter how endless it seemed, wasn’t where his story stopped.

There was more to it. There had to be.

Rayne steadied himself as his thoughts began to settle. He had already died once. That much was undeniable, but he was sure that there was more that had happened. But what was it?

As the question lingered, more visions appeared in his mind. This time, they weren’t of death, but of something far more familiar—His life in the Valerian army after his death.

He saw himself waking up among corpses, the cold ground beneath him and the stench of death filling his lungs. He saw the early days that followed. The training, the battles, the constant pressure to prove himself.

Each moment played out clearly before him.

He saw the fights he had survived, the merits he had earned, the slow and steady climb from someone expendable to someone who could no longer be ignored. Faces appeared in fragments—people he had fought beside, those who had come to rely on him, even if only in passing.

Then the dungeon appeared. He saw the march forward with the rest of the warband, the battles against the undead, and trying to kill the necromancer.

He saw it all.

And as he did, something within him shifted. A part of his identity that had felt distant until now—blurred and out of reach—suddenly settled into place.

He remembered everything and the name “Rayne Frayser” settled into his soul. But that clarity brought another question with it, one that he had already asked of himself before.

Had he died?

The last thing he remembered was the fight with Fredrick. How he had survived his attacks, and how he had killed him even as the poison was moving towards his heart. He recalled forcing down the rare-grade potion in his throat in a final attempt to survive.

Had it not worked?

Rayne’s expression hardened as the thought settled. No, that couldn’t be it.

His life couldn’t end like that. Not after everything he had gone through. Not after enduring the weight of being called a traitor, the battles, the pain, and the constant shadow of death that had followed him at every step..

The refusal rose naturally within him, steady and unshaken.

Rayne lifted his head and looked around at the darkness once more. It hadn’t changed. It was still endless, and suffocating.

But this time, it didn’t feel like he was cursed to be trapped here. A feeling inside him told him that, but he refused to accept it.

Without hesitation, he stepped forward again. If there was a way out of this place, he was going to find it.

Rayne kept walking through the darkness, but this time there was no hesitation in his steps.

The void around him hadn’t changed in the slightest—it was still endless, still silent, still without shape or form—but something inside him had settled. The confusion that had weighed on him before was gone, replaced by a quiet certainty that he needed to keep moving forward, no matter what this place was.

As he walked, his pace naturally increased. What started as steady steps turned into a quicker stride, and soon enough, he found himself running.

It didn’t take long for him to notice something strange.

No matter how long he ran, he didn’t feel tired. His breathing didn’t grow heavy, his muscles didn’t ache, and there was no slowing down.

It was as if his body had been freed from the limits it once had. The realization came to him slowly, but once it settled, it changed the way he saw everything around him.

If exhaustion didn’t exist here, then this place didn’t follow any real rules. And if that was true, then the darkness was probably nothing more than an illusion.

That thought alone made him push forward even faster.

His focus shifted away from the darkness itself and toward everything outside of it. He wondered if his party and warband had managed to escape, how many had survived, and whether the fight against the necromancer had truly ended.

The questions built up inside him, each one adding more urgency to his movement as if running faster would somehow bring him closer to the answers.

Then, without warning, everything changed. The ground beneath him gave way.

One moment it had felt stable enough to run on, and the next it melted beneath his feet like it had never been there at all.

His balance broke instantly, and before he could react, his body dropped into nothingness.

He reached out instinctively, trying to grab onto something, but there was nothing around him to hold onto. No surface, no resistance, just empty space stretching endlessly in every direction.

He was soon falling.

A rush of air surrounded him as his body dropped through the void. Panic rose in his chest as he tried to steady himself, but there was no way to control the fall. And then, as his gaze shifted downward, he saw something that made his entire body go cold.

For some reason , a troll warlord waited below.

its head tilted upward as it watched him descend. Slowly, almost deliberately, it opened its mouth wide, waiting for him. The sight froze him in place. He tried to move, to twist his body, to do anything that might stop what was about to happen, but he couldn’t.

There was nothing to push against, nothing to change his direction.

He could only fall. The warlord’s jaws soon closed around him. At once, pain erupted out and he screamed, thinking this—

Rayne’s eyes snapped open.

Air rushed into his lungs as he took a sharp, ragged breath, his chest rising and falling rapidly. For a few seconds, he didn’t move at all, his gaze locked onto the ceiling above him as the remnants of the darkness still clung to his mind.

It took him a moment to realize that what he was seeing now was real, and everything else had been nothing more than a nightmare.

He laid in a tent. The familiar shape of it settled his thoughts quickly, and as he looked around, recognition followed almost immediately.

It was one of the healers' rent. Relief flickered faintly through him as the realization took hold. He had survived.

His eyes moved across the space, taking in the rows of injured soldiers lying around him.

Some were covered in bandages from head to toe, others missing limbs entirely, their bodies resting in uneasy stillness. He didn’t recognize any of them, but they had probably gotten injured back in the dungeon.

As he observed the scene, a healer moving between the beds suddenly locked eyes with him. She froze the moment she realized he was awake, her expression shifting in surprise before she quickly told him to stay still.

Before he could respond, she turned and rushed out of the tent.

Rayne blinked, confused by her reaction, but didn’t dwell on it for long.

Instead, he tried to sit up, his body responding slower than he expected. There wasn’t much pain, but there was a stiffness that made every movement feel heavier, like his body had forgotten how to move properly.

It took a few seconds, but he managed to sit upright, deciding not to push himself further in case he wasn’t supposed to.

As he settled, his focus turned inward. Almost immediately, he felt the presence of dozens of notifications at the back of his mind.

He tried to pull them out to see what he had gained in the battle against Valtheris and Fredrick, but before he could, the flap of the tent opened again.

The healer returned, and this time, she wasn’t alone.

Rayne’s gaze shifted toward the entrance as Axel stepped inside. The squad leader looked at him carefully, his eyes scanning him from top to bottom with clear interest before a faint smile formed on his face.

Without hesitation, he pulled a chair closer and sat down in front of him.

“You truly are a grave walker,” Axel said, his tone carrying a mix of disbelief and amusement. “So hard to fucking kill.”
