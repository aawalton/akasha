Blood dripped from Ashton's chin. It was not his own.

The one this blood belonged to was currently laying on the ground, twitching ever so slightly while trying to cling onto remnants of life. Ashton glanced at the thing that this Marked had been carrying around. It was a skull, picked clean of any flesh that may have been on it just a few days ago. It must have belonged to the person that wrote the post in the community, asking questions about this 'disease'.

During the entire fight, if it could even be called that, this Marked never let go of the skull. Even now, they were just staring at it as the light faded from their eyes.

"Well... let's hope you can be together again," Ashton said as stabbed the barbell down onto the carrion crow's head.

Ashton continued walking. He was waiting for the Observer to actually say something about that, and join in by bickering with the other two like usual. But the Observer stayed silent. Maybe their focus was momentarily elsewhere. They hadn't been speaking for a while, even before that 'bet' with the dickhead trio.

But well, it really didn't matter all too much whether they were around or not. He knew that they would come back soon enough, at the very latest once Ashton figured out how to actually form a summoning contract with a constellation.

So, for now, Ashton just stretched. He had to start running again immediately if he wanted to make it to that place and then back to Calibur in time.

Not that he really wanted to do this, but if Millie was right, then that might be exactly what he was looking for.

But the more Ashton ran, and the closer he got to familiar, yet changed, streets that he hadn't even gone near in so many years, the more his throat tightened. This area was ruined, just like most other parts of Drakewood, but he could see the skeleton of his childhood in the ruins.

The playground that, despite being older than almost any other in town, had that one really cool climbing wall. The café that Ashton's dad dragged him to almost every Saturday morning, where he would sit and play games on his phone while his father read a few chapters of whatever book he was currently obsessed with.

And now, the forest path that Ashton walked down on his way to the bus stop every single school day. The air felt thick in this forest. So thick and dense that it was almost like pushing through mud.

His run soon turned into a jog, and then into a walk. Until finally, Ashton just stood there, in front of the dilapidated ruins of an old house that stood here for more than a hundred years.

Ashton couldn't actually 'see' the house yet, but he knew what it looked like. He could feel it.

He walked through the overgrown, rotten wooden fence gate and climbed up the steps to the front door. It was already hanging off its hinges, with leaves and sticks and mud having been dragged inside over it.

There were rubbish bags and shards of glass and old bottles of booze all over the place. The kitchen cabinets had been torn down, and the fridge was pulled forward and leaned against the counter right across from it.

The wooden stairway had completely collapsed in on itself, but plants were growing out through the hole. Ashton didn't even want to know what it was like upstairs.

He walked through the hallway that felt just so much more narrow than he remembered, and soon got to the living room. An old fireplace was filled with random garbage, and some sleeping bags were laid out in here, though they must have been in that exact spot for months already.

The glass of the garden door was smashed in completely, but Ashton still pulled it open. It fell of its hinges the moment he tried.

Ashton walked over the creaking floor and walked into the garden, and down the steps of the deck. The steps definitely cracked and gave in a little when Ashton stepped on them, so he hurried a little, and soon stood right at the entrance to this overgrown garden surrounded by a thin fence and numerous old trees.

Straight ahead, there would be an old pond at the edge of the property; it's what he was aiming for. Because now that he was here, he could tell that Millie was right.

Despite how ruined this place was, and how rotten everything here was, including the last memories of his home before it was ripped awa from him, Ashton could sense nothing but the purest mana that he had felt on earth thus far.

Just that mana alone made this all a little more bearable. It felt like he was in some oasis of a never-ending desert, or in the most beautiful sun-flooded grove of an ancient forest. That wasn't an exaggeration, either. He had been in both of those places, and the mana felt weirdly similar in quality, even if not quantity.

It was more subtle here, like a piece of fabric just barely touching your skin as it passes by.

"Poppy," Ashton said. The pixie climbed out from his pocket, and looked ahead. "Do you see anything here?"

The fairy nodded. Slowly, with clear uncertainty, but she nodded. A weight was lifted from Ashton's shoulders immediately as he began to walk forward.

The loosely cobbled path in front of him was overgrown with old, tall grass, with some stalks reaching up to his chest. Ashton pushed it all aside with the bottom of the summoner's staff, until he noticed a change.

He couldn't see the colour of the grass, but as he walked forward, the it just seemed... healthier? There were more flowers around as well, and bees and butterflies were buzzing and fluttering from flower to flower. And these weren't monsters either; they were just regular, normal bees and butterflies. Well, they might be a little larger than normal, but that was just what happened when mana was introduced a world.

It was the same with the flowers. There were many here that Ashton didn't recognise to be from around here, and they were definitely much larger than normal.

But it was nice, anyway. It was all healthy and balanced, and just seemed like the way a world was supposed to change when mana came to it, rather than what was happening because the Interdimensional Council decided to tag along.

And then, before he knew it, Ashton stood at the edge of the pond. He could tell that the veil was shifting and flowing differently right here, right above the pond, but he wasn't able to 'see' anything beyond that.

"Poppy, treasure pixie..." Ashton said, offering his hands to both of them. They climbed onto them, but were just looking completely ahead and not focusing on him at all.

As soon as the two fairies were out in the open, something happened. The veil in front of Ashton started to shift, like a whirlwind was forming right around the centre of the pond. It was compressing, forming a large sphere, like a bubble ready to burst.

But it didn't burst. It slowly folded open, like the buds of a flower.

"So this is how sanctuaries stay hidden..." Ashton whispered in amazement. He knew that there was something special to these places, but he didn't expect the veil itself to be hiding the sanctuaries. Well, it certainly made sense.

And as the sanctuary revealed itself to Ashton, there were a few things that he saw immediately. First, about a dozen pixies came flying right at Ashton. They were staring at him with quite a bit of disdain, but they were excited to see two more of their friends arrive at this place safely.

They probably didn't really like Ashton because of the way he looked right now. Pretty dishevelled, and covered in blood... Well, nothing he could do about that.

And while the pixies were excitedly looking at the dress that Ashton had made for Poppy, which she was smugly showing off, he was focused on something else entirely.

First, he carefully let down Poppy and the treasure pixie. There were some fae that quickly came to take care of them. Not just pixies, either. The other two sort that he saw were sprites, small, shapeless fae that were often describes as 'shadows that were too bored staying shadows', and a single brownie was here as well.

Seeing a brownie here was actually a bit surprising. They were usually tied to old buildings, and would rather die than abandon their home. Did this one use to live... here?

But this wasn't even the most surprising part, or what he had been focused on since the sanctuary was revealed.

First, he noticed that the messages from the constellations simply stopped. They were in the middle of some random conversation that Ashton didn't really care for all too much, but it stopped, and he was pretty certain it wasn't because they were too stunned to keep talking.

And the other thing was the being that was clearly the cause for a sanctuary with the power to do that in the first place.

At the centre of the pond, surrounded by water lilies and duckweed, sitting on their own large lily pad, was a nymph. She was wearing a long dress that spread out like the petals of flowers, and looked like it was almost a part of her body.

Even while presumably kneeling there, she was towering over Ashton. It felt like this pond shouldn't be the place where a nymph of her stature belonged. Then again, it wasn't necessarily about the size of the body of water, but more about its essence and how well-connected it was to magic and the veil.

And from what he could tell, this was a particularly powerful fairy.
