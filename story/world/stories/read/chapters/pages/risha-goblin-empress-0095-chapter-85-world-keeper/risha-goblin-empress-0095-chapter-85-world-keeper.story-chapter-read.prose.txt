Risha rose from her furs in a cold sweat, the image of a dead goblin’s eyes seared into her brain.

She heaved for several seconds, before the sound of birdcall hit her. She looked down at her hands, scarred and callused, but there was no blood on them.

She closed her eyes, and took one last deep breath, relaxing her muscles one by one before she crawled out of the small tent her people had made for her.

A kobold was already starting on breakfast across from her, gently dropping spices into a large pot.

Risha looked past the kobold to the distant wolf dens.

The forest quickly gave way to rolling hills, just beyond which, more mountains stood tall.

The more Risha discovered about the land she lived in, the more she realized that in every direction there were simply more mountains, or the ocean. She’d yet to follow the sun where it rises, but she suspected that even there, there would be some great natural obstacle.

A voice brought her back to the present, as a young Brown Fox offered her a bowl of soup.

Risha thanked the fox, and the fox shyly bowed her head before hurrying to feed the others.

The others were also slowly rising, the two wolf packs hurrying to get themselves ready for the last leg of their journey.

Sadly, the goblin who’d been accompanying them had continued along the road, leaving her without the usual company of her own kind. Not that she minded the kobolds, she found that she actually had a lot in common with them, and she enjoyed their company. Still, the goblin was one from her village, and there was an understanding there.

Kliks’s soft leg on her cheek brought her attention to the tiny spider. Risha gave the spiderling a soft scritch before taking another bite of her food. Sun-Chaser, Mist-Prowler and Wind-Whisperer sat next to Risha, the first to eat before the other wolves.

The food was good, and the flavor helped to bring Risha away from the nightmare that had ruined her sleep.

After breakfast, they finished getting everything away and continued on foot.

Already, Risha could spot scouts in the hills, retreating to inform the packs of their approach. No warning had been sent ahead of time, and the caution of the wolf den was clear to see as several packs quickly gathered to watch the approaching warriors.

Risha entered [Pack Leader: White] as she walked to the front of the pack. Her movements smoothed, and her walk lengthened as the aura of a kobold settled over her.

More and more kobolds came out of their dens to watch their approach, many of them seeing their goblin alpha for the first time.

Risha spotted a few wolves still in their baser form, many too young to evolve, but she hadn’t had time to make sure that every wolf in the dens had evolved.

Risha’s pack spread out behind her, their heads held high as they approached a tall hill, a single cave glowing with a golden light.

Ten older wolves stood there, every one of them watching her as she approached, some were Lupus, others Vulpine. A single Jackal stood at their head, his golden fur radiant in the setting sun. Streaks of gray made their way through his fur, and he carried a presence with him that Risha could feel as she walked towards him.

Risha stopped several paces away from the Golden Jackal, and the two studied each other for a long time. The Elders looked at her with various expressions, ranging from hatred to pride, but she didn’t have eyes for them, only the Golden Jackal mattered..

Finally, Risha bowed her head the smallest amount, “Your newest alpha has come to greet the Elders.”

A Brown Vulpine stepped forward, her voice cutting through the tension. “This Elder does not recognize you.”

“Peace, Storm-Watcher.” The Golden Jackal spoke, and the others looked to him. He tilted his head towards Risha in the same manner she had greeted him, “I am World-Keeper. It is a pleasure to meet the one who grants voices to even the lowest of kobolds.”

“I am Risha, Mother Of Monsters.”

“What are you here to give us, Mother Of Monsters?”

“The future of the pack.”

“What are you here to take, Mother Of Monsters?”

“The burdens of the past.”

“And why should we give you our burdens?”

Risha paused, that wasn’t in Sun-Chaser’s script, and the small smile on the Keeper’s face told her he knew full well it wasn’t. “Risha will take your burdens, whether you choose to give them or not. That is Risha’s responsibility as your alpha.”

The Jackal watched her, expressionless, “I do not recognize you as alpha.”

Risha’s breath caught.

“Not yet. Follow me, Mother.”

Risha walked to the kobold, past the other Elders as they continued to watch her. There was a clear divide among them, one she could see now that she was closer.

They did not move as the Keeper led her into the cave. Golden stones lit the way, illuminating a small stream that flowed its way through the cave.

“What do you think of our home, Mother? Had you thought on what it must look like, our bastion from the orcs?”

“The hills are beautiful under the sun,” Risha watched the Jackal, his peaceful expression unreadable, “But I find the Elders prideful.”

“Oh?”

“I have brought your people freedom from our lesser forms, and yet some have made their decision before they even see me.”

“There are some who believe you destroy the natural order. They are right, and wrong at the same time. That is not pride that speaks, but fear and a desire to protect their people.”

The Jackal paused in front of a door, vines twisting through it and small golden flowers glowing with the light that seemed to suffuse the cave. He pushed the door open, revealing a forest garden. Trees climbed to the rocky ceiling, the golden stones shining down on them. Flowery vines, herbs, plants, even small animals made their way across the soft floor in a carefully taken care of home.

“Welcome to our sanctuary, Mother.”

Risha stepped forward, the peaceful energy of the garden putting her at ease in a way she wasn’t prepared for.

“Even when the orcs nearly saw us to extinction, they could not breach this sanctuary. It is our home, our guardian in the worst of times. Tell me, Mother, why do you judge pride, when yours consumes you?”

Risha turned to the Jackal. Despite his words, she did not feel angry at him. It was a surprisingly genuine question. “Pride does not consume me.”

“And yet you have not greeted us in the year that you have commanded our children to their deaths. You fight in wars that do not call you to them. Even now, you do not give us warning, but appear on our doorstep, expecting us to show our throats. How many of us died for you across the mountains?”

“Too many.” Risha responded, her voice hard. “But they followed me willingly, and saved lives.”

“How many died in your latest war?”

“That number breaks Risha.” Risha narrowed her eyes, a snarl covering her face as tears threatened to fall from the corner of her eyes, “Do not use it against her so callously, Keeper.”

The Golden Jackal stayed silent for a long moment, then continued calmly, “What right do you have to ask for their deaths?”

Risha fully bared her teeth, her nails drawing blood from her hands as she clenched her fists. “Risha does not force them, you mud-coated mutt. Risha fights for the freedom of all our peoples. Risha would fight that war on her own, if she must. Your people follow Risha because they choose to, they die for the freedom of all children. Do not use their deaths as a weapon, mutt.”

The Golden Jackal watched her, not reacting to the aggression as he weighed her in his eyes. “I do not use it as a weapon, Mother. These are questions I must ask, questions that must be answered. Our loyalty cannot be seen as something to use freely. You cannot ask us to bow simply because you killed one wolf who himself was blind to the needs of our people. I do not wish to trade one tyrant for another.”

Risha let out a shaky breath, and unclenched her fists, blood dripping to the leafy floor. “Risha would rather find death, than do to others what was done to her own people. What was done to her.”

The Jackal looked down to the ground, “It is good to hear your words, Mother. It is a weight that I am glad to see gone. There are still more questions that need answers, Mother. May I ask them freely?”

“You asked freely already.” Risha narrowed her eyes at the Jackal.

The Jackal bowed his head slightly. “Yes, but that was before you earned my respect.”

Risha paused, caught off-guard by the bluntness of the words. “Y-yes. You may ask.”

“What future do you see for our people?”

Risha considered the words, and the Jackal was fine with letting the silence extend as he waited for her answer.

“Risha sees a world where monsters are free from bonds of slavery. Risha sees a world where kobolds can learn and grow to respected heights, where they are masters of their craft, masters of their sword, masters of their magic. A world where no child has to fear for their next meal, or for their life.”

“An ideal that seems impossible.”

“Everything Risha has done looked impossible, until it was done.”

The Golden Jackal laughed, and turned away from her, covering his mouth. “I see why so many choose to follow you, Mother. Very well, there is still much to discuss, but I believe you will make an excellent Alpha.”

As he said, there were many more questions. They stayed in that sanctuary for hours, discussing futures, mistakes, and the Jackal’s people. Questions about the monsters Risha chose to trust, the ones she chose to become spirits.

Despite the time that passed, Risha did not find herself becoming weary. The Jackal’s conversation flowed like the water of a stream, unending, and yet always refreshing.

When enough time had passed, the Golden Jackal stood, and motioned for Risha to follow him.

They left the Sanctuary, and walked through the cave into the darkness of the late night. Despite the hour, not a single kobold slept.

Her people waited for her, standing there silently. Across from them, the Elders still stood where she’d left them. They watched the Keeper with curious gazes as the two of them emerged from the cave.

The Keeper looked over them, his gaze commanding in its own subdued and subtle way. “I, World-Keeper, Keeper of the sanctuary, and third of the Golden, give Risha, Mother Of Monsters, my support as the next Alpha. You may make your own decisions now, Pack Elders.”

Surprise filled the kobolds watching from the nearby hills, but none were as surprised as the Elders. Many looked angry, some looked pleased, but surprise still seemed the major emotion.

Risha watched them, recognizing the battles still to come. She needed them, but she did not need all of them.

She would destroy their challenges.
