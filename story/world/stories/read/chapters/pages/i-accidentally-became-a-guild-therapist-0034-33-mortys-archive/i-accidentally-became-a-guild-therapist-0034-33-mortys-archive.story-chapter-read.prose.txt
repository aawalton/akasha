I ACCIDENTALLY BECAME A GUILD THERAPIST

Chapter 33 – Morty’s Archive

ARC III: THE WORLD REMEMBERS

SYNOPSIS: Day 9. Three clips. One empty corridor with no timestamp. Morty shows up when the crystal goes dark.

Day 9 started the way most event days started now: with Alaric’s voice carrying through the guild hall before Livia had finished her first cup of tea.

“Taming event announcement,” he said, appearing in the kitchen doorway with his tablet already open. “Week three. Mount acquisition challenge. Every participant starts with zero. No transfers, no purchases. You find your mount, you tame your mount, you keep your mount.” He paused. “The mount options are…” he scrolled, “…extensive.”

“Extensive how,” Livia said.

“Tab Nineteen,” Alaric said, and turned the tablet to show her.

She read Tab Nineteen.

She put down her tea.

“That’s a sentient cloud,” she said.

“Option fourteen,” Alaric confirmed. “There are thirty-one options. I’ve flagged the ones that have attempted to eat previous riders as red. There are seven red ones.”

“Alaric.”

“You don’t have a mount,” he said. Simply. The way he said things that were facts. “This is the most efficient acquisition window in the event calendar. Tab Nineteen has full species profiles, temperament ratings, and a compatibility assessment I built based on your session data.”

He set the tablet on the table and left.

Livia looked at Tab Nineteen for a moment. Then at her tea. Then at Tab Nineteen again.

I don’t have a mount.

I have never thought about this before.

I have been in this world for nine months and I have walked everywhere.

Tab Nineteen exists.

She closed the tablet. That particular problem had its own time, which was not today.

[AETHELGARD RESONANCE CHAMPIONSHIP — DAY 9 — MORNING UPDATE]

[Individual: Livia 14,203 • Yuki 13,441 • Thorne 6,102]

[Guild: Bloodbath & Beyond 40,117 • Eclipse Covenant 37,203 • Crestfall Keep 33,441]

[Voting Portal: Yuki 118,203 • Livia 89,441 • Blorbo 24,108]

[Week 3 Event: Taming Challenge. Begins: 48 hours. All participants start from zero. Mount options: 31.]

[The system notes: option 14 is a sentient cloud. The system notes: the system does not recommend option 14. The system notes: the system is aware this recommendation will not necessarily be followed.]

Guild gap: 2,914 points. Down from 3,087 yesterday. Narrowing.

She noted it and moved on. Today had other work.

The crystal was on her desk where she had left it since the morning after Goldmere, nine days ago.

She had looked at it every day. She had not watched it.

This was not avoidance, exactly. It was more like… she had known it was there and had been waiting for the right quality of quiet. Sessions in the morning, event updates at noon, the guild’s ambient chaos in between. The crystal needed a different kind of time. The kind where she could sit with whatever it showed her without immediately having to do something about it.

Tonight was that kind of time.

She set it on the table. The lamp was on. The armchair was across the room, neutral gray, not currently needed for anyone. The guild hall was its late-evening self — most members logged off or in their rooms, the main hall quiet, the kind of silence that felt earned rather than empty.

She held the crystal for a moment.

Then she played it.

The first clip was the planning room.

Not the planning room as she knew it — her lamp, her armchair, the two chairs angled just so. The planning room as it had been four months and eleven days before she arrived: guild-issue furniture, nothing placed with intention, the window letting in afternoon light that hit the table at a flat, functional angle.

Miles was alone.

He had moved one of the chairs. He was looking at it from the doorway, evaluating the angle with the particular quality of someone who had been thinking about this for a while and was now testing the thought against reality. He moved the chair two inches to the left. Looked at it again. Moved it back. Moved it one inch further right than its original position and left it there.

He sat down in the other chair. The chair across from where he had just placed the first one. He sat in it for approximately forty seconds, looking at the empty chair, at the empty room, at whatever he was imagining occupying it.

Then he stood, walked to the window, and tested three positions for a lamp that did not yet exist.

Livia watched this without moving.

He was thinking about the light.

Four months before I arrived.

He was thinking about whether the light would be right for someone sitting in that chair.

He did not know my name. He did not know what I would look like or how I would work or whether I would be any good at the thing he was preparing the space for.

He just… made sure the light was right.

The clip was three minutes and forty-seven seconds.

She had known about this footage. Morty had told her. Miles had told her to watch it. She had thought she was prepared for it.

She had not been prepared for the lamp positions.

She had not been prepared for how long he sat in the wrong chair, looking at the empty one, in a room that did not yet have anything of her in it.

Without thinking, she reached out and touched the Goldmere lamp on her desk. The warm, non-intrusive light she had lived inside for nine months. The light she had bought because it felt right, and had never once asked why it felt right, because it just did.

He had stood in an empty room and tested the angle.

Before she existed here.

Before there was a her to test it for.

Filed. Everywhere. All at once.

The second clip was the main camp bench. Two years ago, based on the timestamp.

Bron was sitting on it. Not the guild hall bench, not the planning room bench — the old camp bench, the rickety one in the main hub where Livia had sat on her first day and doodled a frowny face in her notepad. He was in full gear, which was unusual for someone just sitting. His shield was propped against the bench beside him. His hands were on his knees.

He had been there for a long time before the clip started, judging by how still he was. Not the stillness of rest. The stillness of someone who had run out of motion.

T.S. had said: a man sat on a bench in the main camp for two hours. I walked past him four times.

This was that bench. This was that man.

Livia leaned forward slightly.

Bron’s hands were on his knees. In his right hand, almost hidden by the angle, something small. She could not make it out from this angle. A coin, maybe. Or a token. Something small enough to hold without it being obvious he was holding it.

He was looking at nothing. The camp moved around him — players passing, NPCs running their loops, the ambient noise of a server in motion. Bron sat in the center of all of it and was completely absent from it.

After a long time, he put the small thing in his pocket.

He stood up. Picked up his shield. Walked back toward the guild hall.

He did not look back at the bench.

Two years ago.

Before Livia.

Before sessions, before breathing exercises, before anyone asked him what was wrong.

He sat on a bench for two hours holding something small and then put it away and went back.

I have had forty-three sessions with Bron.

I have never asked about the small thing.

I don’t know if he still has it.

She wrote in her notepad: Ask Bron. Not directly. But find the way there.

The third clip had no timestamp.

This was unusual. Every other clip in Morty’s archive had a timestamp — date, time, location tag. This one had nothing. Just footage, slightly different in quality from the others, the way old recordings sometimes looked different. Not degraded. Just… earlier. Like the server had been running on slightly different parameters when it was captured.

It was a corridor. The east corridor of the guild hall, the one that ran between the planning room and the main hall. Night. The overhead lights were at minimum — the server’s ambient night setting, just enough to see by.

Nobody was in it.

Livia watched it.

For eight seconds, nothing. An empty corridor at night.

Then, in the far left corner of the frame, at the edge of what the camera could catch: movement.

Not a player. Players had a specific quality to their movement, a smoothness that came from being rendered at full resolution. This was different — lower resolution, slightly stuttered, like something moving at the edge of what the server was willing to render. Like something that was technically present but had not been assigned full processing priority.

It moved through the frame in two seconds. Left to right. Gone.

Livia played the clip again.

Second time: she caught more. The shape of it was vague — not a person, not a standard NPC model, not any creature type she recognized from nine months in Aethelgard. It moved like something that knew where it was going but was uncertain about whether it was supposed to be going there.

She played it a third time.

The third time, she noticed something she could not have named on the first or second pass. Not a visual detail. Something else. The footage carried a quality she had felt before — not seen, felt. The same ambient warmth as the center cluster in the Verdant Expanse. The same frequency as the amber light that had stopped at her feet. Not identical. Not quite. But related, the way two notes from the same scale were related even when played in different octaves.

She did not understand it. But she recognized it.

That was the part she could not explain to herself yet.

It moved like something that had been doing this for a long time in a server that did not officially know it existed.

T.S. said: I move through the guild building sometimes. Navigation. Not surveillance.

T.S. said: I have been on this server for seven years.

T.S. said: I was created as a tutorial NPC. I was never deployed. I have been here since then.

She looked at the corner of the frame where the movement had been.

This clip has no timestamp.

Morty kept it without knowing why.

T.S. doesn’t show up on camera — Morty said so. Three years of documentation and he’d caught them twice, maybe.

But this isn’t T.S.

T.S. moves like someone who has learned to be invisible. This moves like something that is invisible because it hasn’t been given the processing budget to be fully visible.

This is something else.

Something that was in the guild hall’s east corridor at night, at some point before Morty’s archive has a record of.

Something that moved like it had somewhere to be.

She closed the crystal.

The room was quiet.

The lamp was warm.

She wrote in her notepad:

Clip 3. No timestamp. East corridor, night. Something moving at the edge of render priority — not a player, not standard NPC, not T.S. Something that moves like it hasn’t been assigned the processing budget to be fully real.

Note: the footage carries something I have felt before. Same frequency as the amber light in the Verdant Expanse. Not identical. Related. Like something that comes from the same direction.

I do not know what direction that is yet.

Morty kept it without knowing why.

Intuition keeps things that logic hasn’t named yet.

I don’t know what this is.

But it was in this building.

And it knew where it was going.

She did not hear Morty come in. She rarely did.

He was just… there, in the doorway, the way he tended to be there — present without announcing it, the skull and the star-tipped crown and the layered coat, the recording crystal on its lanyard. The crystal was off. It stayed off.

“Playback indicator,” she said, without turning around.

“Yes,” he said. “I’m sorry. I wasn’t—”

“I know,” she said. “Come in.”

He came in and sat in the chair across from her — not the armchair, the regular chair. He sat in it with the careful posture of someone who had not been invited to sit in many places and was still recalibrating to the fact that he was now in one of them.

They sat for a moment.

“The planning room clip,” Morty said. Not asking. Just acknowledging that she had watched it.

“Yes.”

“The lamp positions,” he said.

“Yes.”

He nodded once. The skull ornaments on his belt shifted. “I didn’t know what I was filming at the time. I film corridors. I film common areas. Miles was in the planning room alone, which was unusual. I framed it and…” he paused, “…kept it.”

“You keep a lot of things you don’t have a reason for yet,” Livia said.

“Yes.”

“Is that what the archive is? Things you don’t have reasons for yet?”

Morty was quiet for a moment. His magenta heart-eyes were looking at the space between them, or past it, or at something that wasn’t quite in the room.

“The archive is…” he started. Stopped. Tried again. “I joined this guild because it was interesting. I said that to you before. That’s true. But the other reason— the one I didn’t say— is that I’ve been in a lot of places that weren’t interesting. And in those places, I documented things, and when I looked at the documentation later there was nothing there. No through-line. No…” he searched, “…weight.”

“And here?”

“Here the footage has weight,” he said. “Even the things I don’t understand yet. Especially those.” He looked at the crystal on the table. “The third clip.”

“You know about it,” Livia said.

“I kept it,” he said. “I keep things I don’t understand. The third clip I have watched seventeen times and I still don’t know what is in it. But every time I watch it I… feel something.” He paused. “I’m a Pastel Lich. Feeling things is not always a reliable data source. But I kept it anyway.”

“What do you feel when you watch it?” Livia asked.

He considered this with genuine care.

“That something was in this building before any of us were,” he said. “And that it’s still here. And that it was…” he stopped.

“Waiting?” Livia said.

Morty looked at her.

“Moving toward something,” he said. “Not waiting. Moving. Like it had been given a direction and was following it.”

Moving toward something.

Not waiting.

Filed.

They sat in silence for a while. Not the silence of two people who had run out of things to say. The silence of two people who had said the things that mattered and were letting them settle.

Outside the planning room, the guild hall was quiet. Late. The event leaderboard somewhere updating in real time, Alaric’s Tab Nineteen existing whether or not she thought about it, the Verdant Expanse still standing in the eastern zone with its pre-server flora and its amber light and its secrets that had touched her feet and retreated.

Livia said: “Can I ask you something?”

“Yes,” Morty said.

“Three years in this guild. 847,000 followers. You document everything.” She paused. “What do you do when the camera’s off?”

Morty was very still for a moment.

Not processing. Not performing. The specific stillness of someone being asked a question they have not been asked before and are discovering, in real time, that they have an answer.

“I read,” he said. “Old patch notes. Dev logs from before launch. Things that most players don’t care about anymore.” He paused. “I’m looking for something. I don’t know what. But the archive and the reading and the… all of it is… I think I’m trying to understand what this world is made of. Not the content. The…” he searched again.

“The intent,” Livia said.

He looked at her with the expression of a skull that had just been given a word it had been missing.

“The intent,” he said. “Yes.”

The crystal sat on the table between them, off, no playback indicator running.

Morty did not reach for it.

Livia did not reach for her notepad.

They just sat there, two anomalies in a world that did not entirely know what to do with them, in the warm non-intrusive light of a lamp that had been chosen because it didn’t intrude.

“The third clip,” Livia said finally. “Moving toward something. Do you think it found it?”

Morty thought about this for a long moment.

“I think,” he said, “it’s still moving.”

After Morty left, Livia wrote in her notepad.

The intent. That’s what he’s looking for. What the world is made of, not what it contains.

Me too, I think.

The third clip: something moving toward something. Still moving.

The pre-server plant: amber light that stopped at my feet.

The system choosing not to log things. Three times, now four if you count tonight.

Miles and the lamp positions.

Bron and the small thing in his pocket.

I’ve been in this world nine months and I’m starting to think the question isn’t why am I here.

The question is: what was already moving toward me before I arrived?

And what does it mean that I’m not afraid of the answer?

She closed the notepad.

On the event feed, half-visible at the edge of her HUD, a new notification had appeared while she was writing:

[AETHELGARD RESONANCE CHAMPIONSHIP — WEEK 3 EVENT PREVIEW]

[TAMING CHALLENGE: Begins in 47 hours. Mount options: 31 species. All participants start from zero. Compatibility algorithm active.]

[Note: mounts respond to emotional resonance of their potential rider. Forced taming attempts will fail. The system notes: this is consistent with the event’s broader scoring philosophy. The system notes: some participants will find this more natural than others.]

She read it.

Then she opened Alaric’s Tab Nineteen.

She read it properly this time, all thirty-one options, and by the end she had circled nothing because nothing felt right yet and crossed out seven because of the red flags and was looking at the remaining twenty-two with the specific expression of someone who had just been told they had to choose something that would reflect their character and was only now taking that seriously.

Thirty-one options.

The system says they respond to emotional resonance.

Alaric built a compatibility assessment based on my session data.

I am going to look at this assessment with my whole chest.

She scrolled to the compatibility assessment.

It was very thorough.

It was also, she thought, probably accurate in ways that were going to be uncomfortable to confront.

Tomorrow,” she thought. “I will confront that tomorrow.

She did read the first entry, though. Just the first.

Top Compatibility Match (Score: 94.7%): Thundercloud Jellyfish — Option 22.

Rationale: responds to calm emotional presence, does not require combat engagement, communicates via ambient resonance rather than verbal cues, has never eaten a rider (unlike Options 3, 7, 11, 15, 17, 19, and 24). Rider sits inside the cloud body. You will be dry. Probably.

Alaric’s note: ‘Probably’ is doing significant structural work in that sentence. I stand by it. The jellyfish has excellent references.

Secondary note: I showed this result to Miles. He looked at it for four seconds and said nothing. I am logging this as ambiguous.

Livia looked at the screen for a long moment.

“I’m going to be a cloud,” she said, to no one.

No one answered.

The lamp was warm.
