“You know, I think in most stories where someone has the ability to clone themselves, the clones do the mundane tasks instead of the main body,” Gio complained.

“Well, unfortunately for you, one of us is chained to the depths of a dungeon, the other one fractures reality at a single touch, and for that matter, both of us can’t exist in the real world without fusing with you, mister real deal. So shut up and go to class while we stay here and do your homework for you.” Trio said.

Gio shot his imperious second reflection a dirty look, dejectedly knowing that he was correct, but feeling a little chafing around the idea of being elsewhere while his alternate selves continued work on his domain project. Even as they spoke, two of Rio’s clones were returning with freshly pilfered reflections of two spells that would be crucial to the success of their venture.

[Monolith Field] (Tier: Greater Rare)(Schools: Conjuration, Alteration, Earth) (Type: Domain) Take possession of an area of realspace. Summon and control earthen constructs to restrain or hamper hostile movement. Hostile mana shaping is slightly debuffed in this area.

​[Wizard’s Domain] (Tier: Upper Rare)(Schools: Enchantment, Arcane) (Type: Domain) Take possession of an area of realspace. Inside this area, Spells, Skills, and other abilities of the Arcane subschool will be amplified and discounted. Hostile Arcane spellcraft is inhibited within this area.

“Monolith Field was a hard one to find. Not a very popular spell. I got it off a girl in Shimmerstone Street who was… celebrating. By the looks of things, she won’t even be opening her spellbook for the next… twelve hours? Maybe more. I think the Inquisitors were about to haul her off to her dorm room to sleep it off. In any case, I doubt she’ll be noticing that the reflection of her domain spell will be missing for a few hours.” Rio said.

Gio’s chaotic reflection turned to him. Ever since the dungeon, Rio had been a bit shimmery because of the spellflower, manifesting as an iridescent glimmer around the edges of his being, especially his hands. Gio still felt the bracing touch of the ‘forbidden magic’ (whatever that meant) upon his mind… but the brunt of the burden had been taken over by Rio. Gio swore to himself that he would not be experimenting with the spellflower for quite some time, and would likely have to bring it up to his mentor eventually just to be on the safe side.

“You’re still here? Go to class. We’ll still be at it when you get back.” Rio huffed.

“Fine.” Gio sighed. “But… I guarantee that I won’t be able to learn anything with how distracted I’m going to be just thinking about the domain.”

Gio was wrong.

Alexander Anaximenes stood at the front of their usual classroom, a wide-domed lecturing hall with tiered wooden seats. He was as dapper as always, wearing a black vest over a clean white collared shirt that matched the shade of his stark white ponytail and beard. He held a purple needle of arcane mana up to a wobbling barrier of matching color, watching as it melted apart under his ministrations.

“So, as you can see… an improperly built foundation will cause the entire structure of the domain to fail. Your grip on the spell must be absolute. Unshakable. A domain is meant to be an extension of the caster’s will, and as such, your domain must be able to survive a reasonable amount of interference without failing.” Professor A finished.

A boy that Gio had not spoken to outside of basic pleasantries stood on the raised platform beside Professor A with a dour expression, having just had his [Wizard’s Domain] thoroughly picked apart and found lacking.

“Mister Beauxclerk, do you have any questions?” Professor A asked.

“Uhh… If I… want to improve my ‘grip’, as you put it- how do I… do that?” The boy asked.

“Repetition will help, but rote memorization alone will not help you solidify your foundation. I recommend subjecting yourself to outside influences- have your classmates or sparring partners attempt to interfere with your domain while you attempt to ward them off. Only by working on your shortcomings will you learn to overcome them.” Professor A instructed.

It seemed like the entire class of 257 students was rapt, with many who weren't usually so studious furiously taking notes. Parker Beauxclerk looked down at his shoes, visibly downtrodden and anxious for the exam that would occur two weeks from today. He was a young noble with almost-handsome features, if not a little bit of an awkward teenager. Gio didn’t recognize the Beauxclerk name, but from the way other students treated him, it was likely that he was of above-average wealth, likely some Gold or Silver Ring court-noble family who would have no trouble seeking employment after his 8 years at the CRA… if he could only manage to survive that long. Parker seemed to wilt under the unimpressed gaze of the other students, though nobody would dare voice any of their opinions, lest they be called to demonstrate their progress in front of the class.

Professor Anaximenes seemed to notice this, so he clapped the athletic-looking young man on the shoulder.

“The feat of managing to manifest any amount of a domain at all is impressive, young man.”

A few paces away from Gio, Minette Pedini seemed to shrink backwards in her chair. The diminutive blonde girl looked to be on the verge of tears. Gio briefly locked eyes with Vito, as the dark-haired shadow mage also noticed Minette’s state.

Is Minette… okay?

“You’ve already fought more than half of the battle.” Professor A continued. “All you need to do is take the next two weeks to shore up your shortcomings, and I’m certain that you’ll manage a passing grade. Please note that I will be one of your examiners, along with the rest of the first-year professors. We know what the expectations are, and the standards are not a mystery. I’m not just guessing when I say that you’re close to a passing grade. I am definitively letting you know that you are so close to it.”

The boy seemed to perk up at the professor’s words and took his seat.

Professor Anaximenes turned to the rest of the class. Minette dried her tears with a leaf-patterned sleeve that poked out from her school uniform, putting on a brave face in front of the professor.

“I know that this examination is daunting to many of you.” The professor accurately spoke. “I plead with you to remember that for the most part, you all have what you need to succeed.”

The white-haired man took a long moment to look both Gio and Minette in the eyes, causing a few of their more observant peers to take note, which in turn caused Gio to shift uncomfortably in his seat.

“While the school and its faculty may only provide… a certain amount of the resources required to succeed here… we are free to provide all of the knowledge we are able. With that in mind, please, if you have any questions, please raise your hand.” Professor A spoke.

Gio raised a hand, drawing attention from the class around him. Perhaps earlier in the year, he’d have received scoffs and rolling eyes… but not today. The class seemed to anticipate the top student’s question, especially since many were familiar with his unique situation.

“Mister deGloria.”

“Professor, why is it that so many domain spells have names that seem to allude to either a place or a kind of building?” Gio asked.

A few murmurs sounded throughout the class. Professor A paused to stroke his well-maintained facial hair before responding.

“You must be doing some very thorough research if you’ve noted that particular quirk.” He said.

Gio shifted slightly in his seat.

Surely it’s not… that hard to notice, right?

“And what a difficult question to answer, to boot.” The professor sighed. “But after what I just said, I suppose I was simply asking for one of my students to challenge me. Very well. I shall attempt to explain.”

Gio scooted forward in his seat and readied his pen.

“The requirement to demonstrate a ‘domain’ is not a new one.” The professor began. “Before the first coalition of forces invaded Vitrium, the ‘infrastructure’, if it could be called such a thing… let us just say that there were not any classrooms that you or I would recognize. The kind of learning that took place here was not structured with mortals in mind. In those early days… those who wanted to learn here had to demonstrate that they could walk as the spirits do. It is said that Saint Baphelus, although he was not the first to do so, was certainly one of a small number of human mages to meet this challenge and survive. And so… the first true mages had to craft themselves the first domains. They attempted to replicate the powers of the spirits… but they found that difficult to do so. Does anyone have any ideas why that might be?”

Minette raised a hand. Many heads turned.

“Ah, Miss Pedini. Please.”

Minette cleared her throat, giving a sanguine smile as she answered.

“Because… the domains of the spirits… their realms… are foreign to us. Spirits embody things like harvests and dawn… and humans can’t inhabit those things. The spirits do not think in patterns and logic like people do- they just are. They do not live as we do, and we can not live in the way that they do. It’s kind of like trying to fit your head inside a birdhouse; we’re just too big and stupid to do it.” Minette said.

Her voice had gained a bit of heat. Venom.

She’s… been having her own struggles with her domain, hasn’t she?

Professor Anaximenes looked at her. “Indeed. The way that the spirits work… the essence of true magic… the reason that any of us are here… is that we do not fit the mould.”

Gio pondered the professor’s story.

So… the original domains… weren’t meant for human mages. For human hands… wait… hands?

“Are domains modeled after physical places because the first true mages had to build them?” Gio asked.

Professor Anaximenes smiled.

“Indeed.” The professor crowed.“The prototype domain spells that the ancient true mages made were almost all based on places that were built by human hands. Think of houses… castles… temples… monuments… you get the picture. As Miss Pedini had said, while a mortal mind may not easily wrap around the concept of taking ownership over a plot of sunlight, you can certainly imagine… a house, through which that sunlight may enter… perhaps through tall windows? So that one might bask in the afternoon glow on a cushion, wrapped up with a book?”

Gio envisioned the space behind the couch in his childhood home. In the summer afternoons, there would be a brief period before sundown when the light would reflect off the wall that divided the Copper Ring from the Obsidian Ring, the outermost layer of the Ringed City. This afternoon light bathed the carpet in orange radiance and warmth, creating a private little sanctuary for a younger Gio. The memory was plush and gentle.

“I… think I get it. It is about visualization. It’s a tool to make the experience of taking possession of reality… more human. More tangible for the average mage.” Gio noted.

“Well… that’s how it started, at least. In the modern era, with so much authority lent to us from- actually, scratch that. I’ve said quite enough already. In any case, did I answer your question, Giorgio?”

“Yes, sir,” Gio said.

Interesting.

Back in the mirror dimension, Gio, Rio, and Trio sat at their triangular workstation, their altar to the process of learning magic. Gio sat in his corner, a perfect reflection of his bedroom. Rio’s desk teetered along a faultline-like seam where the cream carpet of the dorm spiraled into fractals, guided by the Aspect’s idle repositioning of some floating mirrors. Trio listened in from a shimmering portal to the serene interior of his dungeon, a boundary of which he could not cross.

“So, with Professor A’s explanation in mind… we could begin to piece the components that we have together while envisioning a kind of… building? It might help with the visualization aspect.” Gio said, explaining his thoughts to his reflections.

“Well, for it to help with the visualization process… we need to have a solid grasp on the visual part of that idea. What kind of building would work with mirrors and water?” Trio asked.

“What about… a funhouse? You know, like the wacky mirrors where you look fat with a lumpy head.” Rio said.

“A funhouse. What a great way to get taken seriously…” Gio deadpanned. “We might as well learn how to do clown makeup and run away to join the circus.”

“Hmm… It’s got potential, you know.” Trio said.

“Not you too!” Gio pleaded.

“Think about it for a second before you disregard the idea. It’s an established place where mirrors are present. There’s also some interesting stuff that we could do with the distortions of carnival mirrors…” Trio said.

“I see that, I really do… I just… don’t want my first domain spell to be so blatantly foolish, you know?” Gio rebuttled.

“Something something beggars something something choosers,” grumbled Rio.

“What about… just a hall of mirrors? Similar in nature, but less… funny.” Gio replied.

“Oooooh! Like a spooky maze of reflections… and then maybe I get to pop out and scare people?” Rio hummed.

“Hmmm… that could work. That’s going to have some more… misdirection-related connotations, you realize.” Trio said.

“Misdirection I’m fine with… kind of comes with the territory, I’m afraid. But I’d rather not get a reputation as a straight-up fool.” Gio replied.

“Yeah, I suppose if your first domain was a funny face generator, people would probably notice.” Rio chortled.

“The only funny face around here is yours, glow-stick.” Trio sniped.

“Now, now. No fighting… we’ve got a lot of work to do.” Gio said.

With the plan finalized for his domain, Gio and his reflections settled in for an endurance-testing race to the finish line. By the end of that first afternoon, Gio had disassembled both of the stolen reflections for the pieces that he was most interested in- namely, the part of [Monolith Field] that summoned structures, and the part of [Wizard’s Domain] responsible for amplifying and discounting spells, skills, and abilities that used arcane mana.

Gio was very interested to see how the monolith field spell dealt with the complex task of conjuring rock formations, imbuing those formations with warding magic, and then tying those wards into the domain portion of the spell as it formed. There were plenty of parts that he did not care about, such as the extensive sections that dealt with the disruption of enemy earth shaping, as well as the sections that dealt with the type and texture of the rocks to be formed. What he found underneath all of that erroneous earth-centric magic was a shape. The true shape of the monolith-conjuring domain spell was a hypnotic pattern of pillars that would fill the entire domain, spreading their effects uniformly throughout the spell by chaining together. Each pillar was like a column that could support a small section of realspace, propagating the control effects of the spell by interlinking with other pillars to form a kind of boxy web-like structure.

It was… not exactly what Gio was looking for, but it was a start. Being a mirror mage, Gio would need reflective surfaces if he wanted to leverage his powers over reflections as part of his domain. He planned to accomplish this by having his domain automatically summon mirrors as part of the shape of the spell, similar to how the pillars summoned by [Monolith Field] would propagate the earthen magic of that spell. He also imagined that it would be useful if his domain had some synergistic element where his mirror-based skills and magic were discounted and enhanced within it, just like [Wizard’s Domain] did for arcane spells.

Gio took the task of slotting those new elements into the prototype of the domain spell personally, inking those sections by hand with a custom blend of ink.

As he slotted the sections together, Gio felt something begin to take form. With the sigil at its core, each symbol he added felt like a stone, no, a brick, placed into a foundation that would grow into a place that he could call his own. Gio felt the seedbed of his domain and realized, in that moment, that all his work would come to fruition. He placed his inking brush onto the parchment and allowed his domain to come into being as it flowed through him.

The remaining days passed quickly.

Over the rest of the school week, Gio would go to class in the mornings, asking Professor A for his expertise when necessary, and taking notes on the things that the professor would say to other students when they demonstrated their domains. In the afternoons, Gio and his reflections worked. Chandrika would come and force Gio to have at least one meal every other day or so with their friends, but everyone knew that Gio’s work was important, so they mostly left him to his devices.

By the time four days had passed, Gio and his reflections had fully renovated the sections from the two other domain spells to the point where Gio was certain that none would be able to tell where those parts came from. He almost wondered if it was ever really necessary to use the spells as reference, because of the amount of effort that it had taken… but he ultimately decided that they had been useful reference materials, even if he could have probably survived without them.

By the end of the first week, Gio had finished programming certain logic and instructions into the spell that would govern how his domain reacted to his commands.

And by the start of the next week of classes, Gio’s spell was ready for practical testing.
