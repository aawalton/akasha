“I’m not really an NPC,” I said quietly, as I slowly chewed the fresh bread they gave me.

“I kind of figured that one out,” said the Korean guy — Lee Hyun-woo.

“I’m sorry for lying to you,” I added. “And… thank you.”

“It’s fine,” he said. “I’m sure you had your reasons. How are you feeling?”

“I’m… better.” I was awake, so technically, it wasn’t a lie. Also, most of the dizziness was gone. But I was still… well, like before.

Then I looked at the new girl. “Are you… a player?”

“I’m not one of those weird robot people,” she said. “My name’s Samar. I’m Egyptian. Fourteen. Just arrived yesterday. Was sure I got sunstroke or something and started hallucinating. I don’t know what I would’ve done if this nice dude hadn’t found me. Who are you? Your name is John? You don’t look much older than me.”

“My name’s actually Geshem,” I said. She was talkative. I liked her. “I’m sixteen. From the U.S.”“I haven’t really introduced myself either,” Hyun-woo said. “I’m twenty-two, a computer science student from Korea.”“I’ve also got a friend here,” I continued. “Juliette. She’s… we’re both not in great shape. That’s why I went to the market — to get food. We’ve got a room at the Baron’s mansion.”

“We’ll help!” Samar jumped up. “Come on, Hyun-woo, get the bread ready. Don’t worry, he makes the best bread. Never ate anything so good back home.”

They packed a big bag full of pastries and baked goods and helped me get on my feet. The three of us walked slowly through the streets, back to the mansion.

“She doesn’t look too well,” said Samar when we entered the room and saw Juliette. She touched her forehead. “She has a fever.”

Fever? Shit, I hadn’t even noticed. But now that I looked at her, she was pale. “Don’t worry,” Samar said, already moving into action. “We’ll take care of her.” She dipped a cloth in the water bucket and placed it on Juliette’s forehead. “Got six brothers and sisters back home, and I’m the second oldest. My big sister’s an asshole, so she doesn’t help. My parents work all the time, so I used to take care of people. What happened to you two anyway?”

I hesitated. “We… went to the forest. Got stung by a creature, poisoned. And... a good friend of ours died.”

That was all technically true. I just didn’t mention that the creature who poisoned us and the friend we lost were the same guy.

Juliette stirred, eyes fluttering open. “Who the hell are you?” she asked in a dry voice.

“Samar. Egyptian. Fourteen. Nice to meet you!” she beamed, wiping Juliette’s forehead again.

“Fourteen is way too young,” Juliette muttered. She coughed. “Doesn’t this place have some kind of age limit? Are they gonna start summoning toddlers next?”

“Hey! Fourteen is old enough,” Samar said, offended. “What even is this place? And how do I get home? Hyun-woo said you guys might know something. I mean, it’s cool here, but I gotta take care of my little siblings. It’d be fun to bring them here, though.”

Samar and Hyun-woo both looked at me.

I was exhausted again, but I forced myself to speak. “We don’t know much ourselves,” I admitted. “We got here about twenty days ago. We learned a few things the hard way. This place is dangerous. We had a third member in our group. But he got killed in the forest. And… we don’t know how to go back. We’re not even sure it’s possible.”

There was a long pause as they processed that.

“Well,” said Hyun-woo, “we don’t have to talk about it now. We can figure things out when you two are better.”

“Thank you,” I said quietly.

Juliette drifted back to sleep. I crawled back to the couch and closed my eyes. It felt… a little better, just knowing someone was taking care of us. Maybe we’d push through this somehow.

The next day was… better.

Juliette’s fever dropped a bit. We managed to stay awake for a few hours. We sat up and ate fresh bread with Samar and Hyun-woo. And I actually… enjoyed it. I don’t think I’d actually enjoyed anything since… since Bob.

Later, after our new friends left, Juliette and I stayed up and talked a little, just the two of us.

“They’re nice,” I said.

“I like them too,” Juliette said. “But seriously. Fourteen? This system is just cruel. She’s like… eighth grade?”

“She’s from a poor neighborhood,” I said. “Doesn’t go to school. Gotta help out at home.”

“Oh.” Juliette frowned. “Still. It’s messed up. She’s just a kid. You’re too. Sixteen is way too young for this.”

“Well,” I said, “it’s not like this system got our permission. It just kidnapped us. So yeah — total jackass system. We can agree on that.”

“Totally.”

She leaned back against the wall, her expression thoughtful. “You seem better today.”

“Yes,” I said. “I still feel like shit — physically and mentally — but better. Yeah.”

She was quiet for a beat, then spoke with a new seriousness.

“I think the reason we crashed so hard… was the grief plus the withdrawal.”

I nodded. I had already come to the same conclusion.

“It was ten days since Bob last stung us,” she continued. “All the physical symptoms, the exhaustion, the mood crash… that was withdrawal. I’m sure of it.”

I had already figured that out. I just didn’t want to think about it.

She looked me in the eye. “Look, we can’t take another dose of the suricata poison. You know what it does to our brains. How addictive it is. If we keep using it... it’ll be the end of us. Especially in this place. Tell me I’m wrong.”

When she put it right in front of me, I had no choice. I had to confront it.

And of course, she was right.

If I became a scorpion surikata addict in this fucked-up place, I wouldn’t last long. And I still wanted to live. Despite the depression. Despite everything.

“You’re right,” I said. “I know you’re right.”

“Promise me, Geshem,” she said. “Promise you’ll never let them sting you again. And if one of them ever does — if an enemy surikata stings you — you kill it on the spot. Before it takes effect.”

It was harsh. But there wasn’t another way.

“I promise,” I said, and I meant it.

She gave me a look.

I leaned over, stretched out my hand, and held up my pinky finger.“I pinky promise.”

We hooked pinkies.

“Good.” She said.

We sat in silence. Then I spoke, voice shaking.

“I keep thinking about them. The tribe. The leader, the kids. When the poison is gone completely… will we go back to thinking they’re just monsters?”

I felt tears coming. “I don’t want to hate them.”

Juliette was taken aback. Her eyes teared up too.

“I don’t think so,” she said, her voice cracking. “I really hope not. I don’t want to hate them either.”

We didn’t say anything else for a while. Just sat there. And eventually drifted to sleep.

The next day, Juliette’s fever was gone completely. We had a full meal with Hyun-woo and Samar, talked for hours, and even managed a short walk to the bakery. We bought some fruit at the market.

The day after that, we decided to go back to work.

We were still weak. Emotionally weird. But we could handle it.

Also, we both had two warnings for skipping five straight days, and we figured one or two more days would get us fired — and Juliette would lose her room.

The work was hard for me, but I also found I kind of missed it. That weird sense of normalcy in this totally insane world. I made some awful panties and skirts — but only awful by my usual masterpiece standards. They were still good enough for Ysabel.

After the day’s work, we decided to go to the tavern to… sort of celebrate our recovery. We survived this. We were fine. And that was a huge reason to celebrate.It was also another one of our many unofficial, unconfirmed dates.

“Let’s toast to…” I didn’t have anything ready. “…to us being OK-ish.”“I like that,” she smiled, and toasted, “We are OK-ish!”

We drank. It was good.

But I had something else I wanted to talk about.

“I’ve been thinking a lot about the tribe,” I said. “I think it’s safe to say the poison’s out of our system. Our feelings now… they’re back to normal. And even with all that, I still like the tribe.”

Juliette stayed quiet, letting me continue.

“I feel different about them than before. It’s not the same ‘primal’ connection. But I still feel like they’re my friends. The leader. The kids. They’re not monsters. Not in my book.”

I paused.

“The way I feel about Bob is… more complicated.”

Yeah. Still not ready to unpack that one yet. It was fucked up situation.

“Do you… feel the same?” I asked her.

She took her time.

She exhaled, then nodded slowly. “I’ve been thinking too. I don’t hate them. I don’t feel… manipulated anymore. But I need more time to sort through it all.”

“Sure,” I nodded. “I get it. It’s… all of this is insane.”

There was a pause. Then I said, “I’m going to visit them tomorrow. The new hideout. Just to see if they’re okay. And maybe bring some supplies. They probably need it.”

“I understand,” she said. She nodded, thoughtful. “But I’m not coming. Not yet. I’m not ready. Is it safe for you to go alone?”

“I feel good,” I said. “And I’ll be careful.”

“Okay,” she said. “Just… don’t get any cravings for suricata stings. And tell Alice and the kids I said hi.”

“I will,” I promised, knowing they probably wouldn’t understand. But it was nice of her to say.

The next morning, I bought supplies at the market and headed into the forest.

And for the first time in a long time, I was myself again—and felt damn good about it too.
