Sparkling traces of mana snaked through the sky as if it were cracking as something slammed heavily against the empty air. Almost immediately, the chatter and commotion of the busy market square began to quieten, and people slowed. Some turned, hearing the sounds; others followed their gazes.

“Did you hear that?”

“What was it?"

“It came from over there.”

Thud…

“There it goes again!”

...Thud

Each time it sounded as if something was trying to bash through where the cracks were appearing, the air bulging around whatever it was. And with each strike, the mana in the air glowed brighter, and the cracks spread further.

“A-a-I know what that is…” A merchant raised a trembling finger, pointing at the cracks as they stretched out. "It's a… It’s a Gate!”

Everything came to a stop. Soot-stained miners pulled oxen to a halt, their wagons filled with coldrock. Parents grabbed their children, some ran, and some whispered and pointed. Window shutters slammed shut, and doors were bolted.

“Make way for the guard!” Came shouts as the lord’s men weaved their way through the stilled crowds as a rumbling panic gradually grew.

THUD

“Oh no. No, no no… NO!” One man screamed, turned and fled, and within seconds, dozens had joined him, sparking a stampede that quickly spread through the town.

“Make way for the guard!” The captain shouted to little effect as a throng of people pressed against them, forcing the guards to get handsy as they barreled through the masses.

THUD!

Rex, who was only a small boy, stood there amongst the chaos, his mother at his side. He remembered looking up at her. He remembered the woman he had known as fearless, ready to delve into the darkest mines. And he remembered how absolutely terrified she had looked that day.

“Ma!” He called, pulling on her rag dress.

THUD!!

“Ma, ma. Ma!”

“Huh?” His mother gasped as if pulled from a trance.

“Ma! What’s happen–”

“Get out of the way!” A man sneered as he pushed both of them aside and continued to run.

His mother staggered backwards and looked around. “No, this can’t be.”

“Ma!” Rex gave her another tug.

“We’ve got to go. NOW!”

Suddenly, her entire demeanour changed. The fear was gone, replaced by a wild, fierce desire to survive. She grabbed him by the shoulder, her fingers squeezing into him.

“NOW!”

She didn’t wait for a response, grabbing his hand and pulling him as she began to run, and almost knocking him over in the process.

“Ma!” Rex called, flailing as she pulled him through the crowds.

She didn’t answer. She just kept running. Moving as fast as she could, pulling Rex along with her, up until they reached the crowd of writhing bodies pushing and pulling against one another. It was a wall of people tightly compacted as they fought to get into the narrowing streets that exited the market square.

THUD!!!

The panic exploded into a melee. Screams as people pushed each other, fists flew, and men yanked people out of their way, and threw them to the ground savagely as they fought to escape.

His mother joined, trying to find a way through the mass. As she battled against writhing bodies, a man’s elbow flew backward, slamming into her nose and knocking her over with a fountain of blood.

“MA!”

Her nose was bent, and her face was covered in blood. But that wasn’t even the worst of it. She had fallen badly, and the moment she tried to put weight on her right foot, she fell back to the ground.

Rex tried to help her back up, but she groaned in pain and shook her head.

He was about to scream at his mother to get moving. His chest beating through his thoughts. But just as he was about to, he realized something.

Wait, the banging… Is it gone?

Rex tilted his head, turning to look back the way they had come. People were screaming and shouting all around him as they tried to shove each other aside. But he just listened. Trying to hear the sound that had started it all.

Suddenly, a flash of bright mana spread across the sky, and then quickly retreated as if sucked back into wherever it had come from. A moment later, the air pulled on him, as if it were trying to drag him into it. Rex gasped as the air was sucked from his little lungs, and he went dizzy for a moment, falling to the ground.

He looked up just in time to see a shockwave explode out from where the thudding had been sounding from, tossing the closest people into the sky, and knocking the rest over.

Rex was flung onto his back, staring up into the sky. And he saw a glimpse of it fly over. A great wyrm. A linker of worlds. It was one of the massive, scaly beasts that opened the Gates, binding worlds together in the process and unleashing the hordes of monsters beyond.

“A… a dragon?” He muttered as he watched its tail whip through the sky and disappear into the dark, sooty clouds above.

Groaning, he sat up and saw it. A rippling portal—the Gate. And climbing out of it were horned and furred demonoid beasts. They carried massive axes and halberds, and without a word, they attacked.

The press of bodies grew even more frantic as they fought to get away. And what followed was no battle. Just slaughter. Terrified townsfolk were cut down without mercy, nor the means to defend themselves.

And with every passing second, more of the demonic, hooven beasts trampled through the Gate, joining the massacre.

“Rex, run! Run!”

His mother was still on the ground, her face bent in pain, and her hands grabbing her ankle.

“Ma! Are you okay?”

“Don’t worry about me! Just run! NOW!”

Rex shook his head.

“Dammit, Rex! GO! GET OUT OF HERE! NOW!”

Staggering backward, he shook his head. He couldn’t leave his mother, but in that moment, she looked possessed. Feral, even. He turned and did as she commanded, but he didn’t get far into his flight before he spotted them. A dozen men in armor. Their weapons were at the ready, and they charged fearlessly into the fray.

“Look, ma! We’re saved! Lord Ferris’ men are here!”

But only a moment later, his jaw dropped. The horned monsters carved a path straight through the lord’s men almost as quickly as they had arrived. Even their “Classed” captain, who could lift a man over his shoulders and throw them like a ball, barely lasted a few short seconds against the beasts, cut down as he was surrounded.

Crossbowmen on nearby walls hurried to take up position, and reinforcements were already charging down from the barracks. But it all seemed so pointless. When they neared, they were met by balls of flames shot from the beast’s mouths, and heavy polarms that crushed straight through their armor.

“H-hopeless… It’s totally hopeless.”

Rex dropped to his knees. His mother was still screaming for him to run. But what was the point? They didn’t stand a chance. Who would stop this horde of monsters? It was inevitable. They would overrun their entire world, given time.

His mother was screaming at the top of her lungs as one of the beasts charged toward him, axe held high, and ready to strike. But with only meters before reaching him, an arrow blasted straight through the beast’s neck, decapitating it with a resounding bang.

“Huh? What the?”

Eyes wide and mouth agape, Rex turned in the direction from where the arrow had flown. There was another Gate, but instead of demonic beasts, Classed adventurers were stepping through it.

Portals, or temporary Gates, were expensive, but channelers could summon them with the right reagents and enough mana. A call must have gone out, and a hefty contract offered by the Lords of Dorog, calling on adventurers to save their world.

But there were only a dozen of them against an army. Rex could hardly believe it, blinking in disbelief. But the numbers didn’t seem to bother the adventurers in the slightest.

One of them charged straight out from the newly formed Gate. It was a warrior, and they crossed the market grounds in a flash, slamming straight into the horned beast, creating a shockwave that knocked half of them over. And he didn’t stop there; he continued, barreling straight through several of them with his shield, swinging a mighty hammer with each step.

Skills… Those are real Skills.

It was the first time Rex had seen real adventurers with Classes fighting. And the battle was only just getting started. Behind the warrior that led the charge, more arrows flew out, each finding a target, and each target they found dropped dead.

But it was the mage that really took his breath away. From the portal, out walked a slender elven spellcaster clad in silken robes. In her pale hands, she held a delicate-looking staff, and when she raised it toward the incoming horde, lightning burst free from it. But not just any old lightning. It was magical, and when it struck the first beast, it continued onward, skipping to the next, and the next after that. Within a second, the spell had traced a path of deathly destruction through dozens of the demons, frying them in an instant.

But the lightning was far from all she could do. Before the spell had even finished, she was casting another. Cobbled streets turned to thick, gelatinous mud, sucking in the legs of the beasts that found themselves caught in its grasp.

With another wave of her hands, vines seemed to materialize and grow out of thin air. They wrapped around the wrists of other beasts, disabling them as more of Ferris’ guards joined the fray, leaving them easy victims to their blades.

Rex had never seen anything like it. He had heard of Classed heroes from across the Gates. He had heard of their exploits and how they could travel to distant lands, fighting monsters and conquering Gates.

But seeing it in person was another thing entirely.

Only moments earlier, the army of demonic creatures had looked entirely unstoppable, and now he almost pitied them.

And at the heart of it all was the elven mage. She was unstoppable, and every spell she cast turned the battle further in their favor.

Rex couldn’t pull his eyes away from her. She was beautiful and deadly all at once. As his eyes were locked on, she turned and looked straight at him, holding his gaze for a second before addressing her comrades. “Secure the town. I’m going after the wyrm,” she said.

Then, she turned to the portal she had just come from and whistled. A moment later, a pegasus flew out, and she leapt up into the air as if she weighed nothing. The two of them seemed linked somehow. The pegasus flew toward her, she landed gracefully on its back, and then they took off into the clouds.

Did that actually just happen?

Rex blinked and shook his head. The adventures were incredible, and for a moment, he forgot about all the death and destruction around him.

I think I want to do magic.

He looked up as fiery explosions and sparks of magical energy lit up the clouds. But she never did come back down. And Rex never did learn of what happened to the elven mage, nor did anybody else, as far as he was aware.
