Still, the recap is helpful. To simplify it all down, magic is split into seven primary aspects; fire, water, air, earth, life, dark, and light. Though some mages end up mixing two or three of the primary aspects to create compound aspects. For instance, Calder’s metal magic was a mix of fire and earth magic. The aspect thing is pretty simple, but mana is where it starts to get complicated. It was definitely right in mana being one of the key pieces to understanding magic in general.

Very simply, each ‘point’ of mana has some aspect assigned to it. Otherwise known as an affinity. Light-affinity mana, or just light mana is an example. But mana can have an affinity for any primary aspect, and even compound ones. Additionally, not all mana of one affinity is equal. Each point of mana also has an attunement to that affinity, hence the whole shifting process.

The core problem with mana is that it is rarely in a pure form. Most mana is generated inside a being’s mana pool, which is tied to whatever their alignment is.
