Kyle and Duroc took some well-deserved time to recuperate and triage the worst of their injuries while the chaddling legionnaires finished binding the remnants of Mangalica’s forces.  While their War Chieftain was alive, they fought to the death, but seeing his defeat at Duroc’s final attack broke what little morale they had left.  The battle had been an expensive one, with nearly twenty percent of the normal chaddling variants having been destroyed.  They could be replaced, with time, though it still marked a notable decrease in his fighting force, particularly given his wretched condition.

The wound on his back was by far the worst injury remaining, with the nasty cuts left by the hodag still causing persistent pain.  The bigger issues facing Kyle were the backlash from channeling so much of the conceptual Death and the strain on his mana network from overcharging it with power from his nascent staff.  He was no stranger to this type of damage, and it always required longer periods of rest to heal properly.

Duroc was much worse for the wear, as Kyle couldn’t do anything about the missing ear at all.  The injury in his side punctured something important, and though Kyle knit up the internal bleeding with Healing Impetus, the orc would be out of commission for quite some time as he went through the healing process.  At least, that’s what Kyle kept trying to convince him.

“I agree to no fighting.  Well, limited fighting, and only if necessary,” Duroc argued.  “But there’s no better opportunity for us to go and seize his capital.”

As the one who had fought Mangalica and landed the killing blow, Kyle wasn’t about to argue his friend’s rights to the spoils.  After all, Duroc made it clear to his retinue that the hodag’s remains were Kyle’s to do with as he saw fit.  However, he was still worried about the consequences of overexertion.

“Look, I’m not saying that you can’t go, but if he has any traps in his office, or had any more C Grade hodags locked away, things could get bad, quickly.  Would taking another couple of days to recover really change all that much for you?”

“From a military standpoint, no,” Duroc admitted.  “But when we think about the contents of his office, and any notes related to how he accomplished what he did, that’s another story.  The longer we wait, the more likely somebody else is to end up in there and either take it for themselves or destroy it.”

Duroc knew that was the angle Kyle was most interested in, even if he hadn’t expressed it directly.  And the thought of somebody else making use of the same methods sent a chill up Kyle’s spine.  The sacrificial path held undeniable power, and was in violation of virtually all of the Collective’s rules around such rituals.  The higher-ups would want to track down any leads to stamp it out.  If there was something in Mangalica’s notes that could get them leads on Zierlan, the Collective could deal with him.  At least, that’s what Kyle hoped.

“Just be careful,” Kyle said, not pushing the conversation any further.  “Skippy is going to want to spar both of us here soon, and you know how annoyed he’ll be if we aren’t in good condition.”

Duroc laughed and thumped Kyle’s shoulder.  “That’s as good a motivation as any.  Say, any chance I could borrow your summons when we march on Manglica’s territory?  It could help things go more smoothly.”

Kyle considered a moment, then shook his head.  “I’m afraid not.  No guarantees how effective they’d be over that much distance.  Plus, having them in reserve back at your village gives another line of defense in case something happens while you and your troops are out.”

“Fair enough,” Duroc said, wincing in pain as he stretched.  “I’ve sent for the others to march out, would you at least do me the favor of escorting my non-combatants back to the village?”

“Is it a favor, or am I being paid?” Kyle asked with a raised eyebrow, though the grin on his face made the joke clear.

“A favor to me, and compensation from the Collective,” Duroc replied.  “If you stay a few days to recover and consolidate your gains, I’ll have you take back anything relevant we find in Mangalica’s quarters, and we’ll split the bounty evenly for whatever they deem it’s worth.”

“Wait, we can get paid for that?”

Duroc shrugged.  “It isn’t widely advertised, but as a War Chieftain we get some insights.  There’s a bounty system for turning in anybody following paths that veer too far into unorthodoxy, and a C Grade heritage would net quite a nice payout.  There are quite a few resources that my village could afford with our half.  Potentially even another D Grade chef…”

“Then it sounds like a fair price for me to guarantee your people’s safety on the road home, and for auxiliary security for the village until you return,” Kyle agreed.

The reorganization was swift, with the chaddlings bringing prisoners and the hodag’s remains along with the non-combatants of Duroc’s group, while the military forces made a beeline for Mangalica’s territory.  For Kyle’s part, he kept Adaptive Regeneration active to keep patching up the deeper injuries, and tried to look menacing.  They kept a brisk pace, the weaker members of the party being carried by spare chaddlings which allowed them to move through the night.

Fortunately, they arrived at the village without incident.  There were scant few guards remaining, though they still had the resources necessary to log and process the prisoners one-by-one as they were shackled and locked in rooms with mana-isolating formations.  The hodag body had been sent to some of Duroc’s finest leatherworkers to be broken down for Kyle’s return trip.  There wasn’t much left, but he hoped the scales and bones could either be fashioned into something useful, or sold for a reasonable sum of credits. Finally, he set the chaddling legionnaires to work, splitting them between leading patrols across the village and guarding the prisoners.

With all the logistical work completed, Kyle settled into his chambers, and took a well-earned nap.  When he woke, he had a good breakfast, and then settled in to prepare to enter meditation.  He had his Level 60 skill upgrade to complete, the D Grade evolution for his World Tree Tender class, all leading up to the move to mid-tier C Grade and a final skill upgrade.

“C.H.A.D.D., let me know if something urgent comes up.  I think I’ll be in meditation for a while.”

The world around him faded as Kyle dove into his Center, seeing the familiar sight of the symbol of the Unbroken Survivor.  There was one orange light floating in the space, representing his skill upgrade.  Here we go.  With a thought, he selected it, and then focused on Parasitic Resonance.

As usual, three options appeared before him.  However, they were joined by an indistinct humanoid outline that inclined its head at him.

I must say, this is quite a novel experience, Phaegos said.  Am I to take it these represent your upgrade options?

Why are you here?  Wait – how are you here?   Kyle thought at the entity, feeling a frown grow across his face.  The entity had made it clear when they’d first met that its original plan was to consume Kyle before emerging, though that apparently wasn’t an option for it anymore.  The fact that it could show up during a skill upgrade unbidden was more than a little concerning.

If it makes you feel any better, I certainly didn’t choose to be here.  If I were to hazard a guess, though, I am here because the skill in question is the one most directly tied to my existence, and the one that draws most significantly from the Progenitor.

So this is the only skill you’ll be showing up for?

Probably, at least for now.  Iron Will and Adaptive Regeneration are also connected, though to a much lesser degree.  In any event, it’s not as though I have any control here.  I’m just here as a spectator and consultant.

Then I suppose we should get to it, Kyle thought, turning his attention to the first option.

Reaper’s Resonance

Kyle saw a vision of himself extending tendrils of Parasitic Resonance, each tinged with conceptual Death.  Indistinct creatures appeared before him, and a light brush with each tendril was all it took for the insidious Death to creep in, growing unnoticed within the victims until the moment that Kyle released its constraints, ravaging each enemy from within.   The active connection was even deadlier, flooding his target as he wrested control of their mana network, releasing the built-up decay in a single decisive blow.

It was clear to Kyle that this upgrade was leaning heavily into the offensive applications of his skill, which he’d been using more and more often.  It took what was currently a trump card, and wove it into daily application.  From what he could tell, Death would be tightly connected to his skill moving forward, narrowing its uses in non-combat situations.  If he could master the Timeless concept, Kyle thought he might be able to mitigate those downsides somewhat, but that was still a long way off.

Not getting any interruptions from Phaegos, he focused on the second option

Devourer’s Domain

The vision this time was odd.  It started by showing blossoming tendrils of Parasitic Resonance reaching out as far as they could.  Three became six, then twelve, then twenty-four, until soon his entire field of vision was covered by tendrils in all directions.  As one, they moved, then merged.  From there, the vision became more standard.  He saw himself walking through a field teeming with living creatures and plants.  Some withered as they were drained away, while others grew and adapted, changed by his will.

The upgrade here was straightforward.  Parasitic Resonance would become a true domain skill, expanding in all directions without the need for the tendrils to form a connection.  The abilities seemed to work with greater efficiency than before, and required less conscious effort.  It was a middle-of-the-road upgrade, taking everything about Parasitic Resonance and improving it without adding additional concepts into play.

Phaegos was still quiet, so Kyle moved on to the last option.

Resonant Creation

Kyle saw himself in the middle of a barren wasteland, holding a small acorn in his hands.  Tendrils of energy reaching into the atmosphere and deep into the ground, draining them of mana as the seed held in Kyle’s hand grew and sprouted, roots digging into the ground as it matured in the blink of an eye.  Then, the tree withered and died, with dozens of others springing up out of the destroyed trunk, propogating until soon the entire waste was replaced with a lush forest.

As he focused, he could see a faint hint of golden energy flowing through each tendril.  It’s connected to the World Tree Seed, he realized.  He’d been puzzled about how the skill seemed to do so much with so little energy, and that explained it for him.  It drew power directly from the Between to supplement the power it absorbed.  It still had the ability to drain energy, but was an upgrade that was clearly focused on the crafting and connection component of the skill.
