Zane's expression was ice cold.

Malzareth scrambled wildly, bucking against the harpoon, but it was no use—it'd been nailed to its own throne.

It screamed as Zane stepped in.

And it went for broke.

It threw up all manner of barriers—basic, flat planes, hastily constructed. It tried reality-warping, but it couldn't break free of his weapon.

He shattered the essence-barriers with ease, fists clenched, and struck it, and struck it again, slamming its heads against the now System-colored ground.

It kept flooding the air with constructs, like a reflexive defensive reaction. Constructs that were quite useless.

Zane’s hands found his chains, and he wrenched them.

His chains lit up white.

And the dragon’s efforts grew even wilder.

It started thrusting even more constructs at him but they were so feebly made it looked like a child hitting a grown man—they simply shattered against his body.

The chains closed in, and its scales started to break—

It wrenched its heads back, eyes shining, and reality warped for a full click—just enough to shift it outside of his spear.

Scars ripped open in its soul as it did, some kind of backlash from the effort.

And even that success cost it, since golden blood erupted from its chest. It didn't care. It took to the air on ungainly wings—but it'd only made it out of the worst of the bind. Not all of it.

Zane’s chains were still attached to its other limbs.

There was a flicker of irritation in Zane's expression before he brought it crashing back down.

Here his own Supreme flickered out. But at this point—with the damage it'd taken, and how much essence it had left—he wasn't concerned at all.

It'd hardly built back up when he hammered it with a Size-filled fist, flattening it. It tried again, and he flattened it once more.

And then the rest of his chains caught up to it, binding it tight.

By this point its soul was flickering.

But there was no sign he'd broken its spirit; it kept fighting, kept thrashing—but he'd realized it wasn't about fighting spirit anymore. It was fighting like it'd lost all sense.

He hammered it with two more fists, noticing that when he struck its heads he disrupted its Skills.

Then he wrapped its necks with steels, flooding them with gravity, stacking gravity cores—nailing its heads to the ground...

And there, he unleashed what he intended to be its final beating.

First, after strike, after strike—

It screeched unintelligibly as he slammed it.

At one point it managed to wrestle up a head—You can't—it snarled, before a Size-filled fist bounced the head off the ground, shutting it up nicely.

The rest of its words came rather garbled, rasped—

It'd lost the ability to resist.

It still had the will, it seemed, but he realized whatever it'd done with that last big reality-warp had tapped out its Bloodline. It ceased warping entirely.

It could only use whatever essence it'd regenerated—what little it had left...

He wasn't certain it was necessary to beat it down like this.

But after what it'd just done to him, he felt it only right that he return the favor.

He struck it until he was certain it had absolutely nothing left.

And then he weighted his fist as heavy as it could go—all heft. Filled it with a mile's worth of size, and then rested that fist on Malzareth's entire form, crushing it utterly flat.

That done, he switched out his chains.

Seconds passed. There was nothing it could do.

He sensed they both knew it—could both sense what was coming.

Malzareth started to laugh.

It was trembling; there were tears in its eyes, but they weren't tears of sadness.

It definitely wasn't in a stable state.

What are you?

And then—What are you? TELL ME!

Zane did not answer. It started giggling, then snorting with laughter again.

It could only watch as his axes gathered essence.

As he loaded them heavier and heavier...

With the state of its wounds, it lost life-force by the second. What little it regenerated couldn't even make up for it.

It blinked, stilled.

Your pagoda, it rasped. It is shattered. That... that’s what saved your life—it's a Skill. It's given you a Skill.

He didn't confirm it.

How close? it said. It suddenly seemed quite intent on this; it fastened onto the idea. How close was I?

"You can rest easy," Zane said. "You were not.”

It seemed taken aback.

And at that point, he decided this was enough.

The light on the Axes reached its peak. He weighted a chain, holding down its necks, binding the dragon tight.

And then he raised his fists, and his Axes took their positions. High up, to either side of those fallen heads.

He decided.

But just as he made that decision, as though responding to it, a string of brilliant green Fate shattered within Malzareth's soul.

Its golden blood shone in the Astral Plane.

Its eyes shone too, and Zane's eyes narrowed—a life-saving Skill? He was certain it was a reaction to his Axes.

But this Skill, if it was a Skill, didn't flood Malzareth with power.

Malzareth's reaction was curious—it seemed bewildered for a split second, and then—

No, it snarled. He didn't think it was capable of mustering such hatred. You have no right—!

Time stopped.

Time stopped for the rest of the world, and he was still thinking—like his soul was in a time bubble unto itself. He saw his essence frozen mid-air, his body on the verge of the act...

A presence descended.

A pair of eyes, directly above Malzareth's fallen form. Vast eyes sitting in the midst of a spatial tear.

Eyes of pure silver that seemed to Zane almost like tilted crescent moons. They gazed pitilessly over the world.

A new aura poured into the Throne Room—an aura he instantly knew was currently a vast distance away; a tiny fraction of its presence was here, but it still felt several clear steps above Malzareth's own.

Its presence struck his Ironclad Mind, and was halted.

Zane met its gaze. He knew, instinctively, what this creature was.

The Origin of all Monsters glanced down at its son.

He wondered, for a second, what it might do. He felt strangely calm in that moment.

Its eyes were filled with regal disdain.

And then it was gone.

Time resumed.

Malzareth screamed and thrashed as wildly as it possibly could—finding new life out of seemingly nowhere; it managed to lift a neck off the ground.

That was all.

Zane put his whole body to the task.

The Axes descended in a single shining cross.

Its three heads fell to the ground, severed cleanly.

And then the System confirmed it.

The Monster Overlord of the Dragonspire Galaxy has been slain.

Title earned!

Champion of the System [Unique]

The Overlord of a Galaxy has been slain, and its slayer has conducted a perfect clear of the Galaxy's Corruption. A singular Title must be granted.

The Champion of the System is the highest title recognized by the System, granted for exceptional meritorious service to the Universe itself.

+500% to all stats. System administrator privileges unlocked when applicable.

He couldn't bring himself to analyze it in that moment.

He titled his head to the sky and let himself understand what, precisely, he’d just done.

Not long after the floors above were broken through as Striker and the Carved Saint descended.

At the same time the Throne-portal shone, and Reina rushed through. She caught him in her arms.

***

On a middle floor of the Superdungeon, Haxorax set aside his scrying glass with a slight smile.

In the World Tree—"He's done it!" Evan couldn't wait to see Zane again—and even from up here in their upper-bough house, they could hear the cheers lighting up the boughs below—not just for Zane, but for Reina and the rest of the force.

In the Steelheart Conclave—"The champ's godsdamned done it!!"

In the Luminous Faction there were folks who still remembered the Zane who'd conquered Washington State all those decades ago. Plenty had been in their twenties or younger at the time, and duels like his fight against Marcus Blackwell or Asterion the Minotaur—fights Zane himself rarely thought about—were essentially formative memories.

Those were the folks who never lost faith in Zane, even as they saw him hammered in that mini-Universe.

Needless to say—the Faction was filled with celebrations.

In the next two weeks the united Dragonspire Army wound down, and Reina had a great deal of wrap-up work to do. Folks headed back to their respective factions, or, in the case of Haxorax and his dragons, their ancestral home.

They had little left to concern themselves about.

Corruption was not utterly wiped out in the Universe. There were still vast seas of Corruption just outside the borders of Dragonspire—and creatures that inhabited them, like the End-Dragons in the End, would likely still stray in from time to time.

What Zane accomplished on that day didn't represent an end to conflict, forever.

But it was perhaps the first time that chaos cycle that Dragonspire was truly safe.

***

A week later, Zane, Reina, Evan, Avery, and Chomper found time for a picnic in an upper bough of the World Tree—his friends had gone on their own little journeys these past few months, journeys he finally had time to catch up on.

The future was still filled with line items for Reina. The scale of the rebuilding she’d oversee was quite massive. And then there was the council on future of Earth which they'd decided to host sometime in the distant future.

The System Store had vanished.

Slightly disappointing to Zane, but he'd kind of expected it. It was a wartime resource, and he knew running it expended a tremendous amount of the System's energies.

If it'd been active in perpetuity, with the resources he had access to—like that upgraded Time Chamber... it would've made life quite easy.

The System's energies, Reina said, went back to rebuilding itself. Over the next few weeks the Superdungeon conducted significant self-repairs.

***

He also received news from Haxorax a few weeks later. The war seemed to have strengthened his position in dragonkind.

But more—Lyxandor and his splinter group had left the Galaxy.

Reina had been somewhat concerned that if the former King of Dragons saw a chance—any sign of weakness in Dragonspire after the war—he'd take it.

She theorized that he'd seen Zane's final performance and decided against it. Rather wisely, she thought.

As for where Lyxandor went, no one knew. He'd left from Dragonspire's northwest edge, fording into the unknown.

***

Zane visited quite a few cities in the coming weeks.

Everywhere he went—from the cities on Earth to the cities in the Great Factions, to the Tribes on Stormhaven, even—was decked in splendor.

All across Dragonspire, celebrations in various forms lasted the next two months. Some lasted far longer.

The name Zane Walker was cemented forever in legend.

In ways not even he knew at the time.

He wasn't the first from Dragonspire to achieve Universal renown—he knew the person who did, as it happened—but he'd later learn that very, very few folk in the history of the Universe had the Title he'd just gained.

And none had gained it at just True God.

And finally—last, but certainly not least...

Reina and Zane got some time to themselves.

This involved their usual activities. But it also involved activities they'd put off for long enough.

***

Months later—flowers bloomed on a hill on the outskirts of the Luminous Faction; spring had just arrived, and a soft wind graced it all.

It was a day and a place of little significance, except to the folks assembled there.

Today this hill played host to a ceremony.
