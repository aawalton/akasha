Dae’s lessons were a welcome distraction from Lee Jia’s troubling thoughts about Eui’s past. With her newfound knowledge, she blasted through the reading lessons at a pace that had Dae staring in shock.

“Ancestors, Miss Lee! How did you manage to pick this up so fast? Even for a mage this is rather incredible.”

“Eui and I—”

Lee Jia paused. She wasn’t certain if she should talk about the joint meditation technique she had discovered with Eui. If nothing else, it was an edge that they had over other disciples, and while Lee Jia considered Dae an ally—perhaps even a friend—she didn’t think it was wise to share that detail just yet.

“—we’ve been studying together.”

Dae nodded excitedly.

“Excellent, excellent! It’s rare to find someone so diligent in their studies, hehe. Um, that said it seems to me that while you’ve done an excellent job learning the strokes for these characters you’ve been neglecting to actually practice them, so let’s focus on that for today.”

Lee Jia groaned at the prospect of spending the rest of the afternoon transcribing characters over and over until she could consistently replicate them to Dae’s exacting standards.

“Practice makes perfect, Miss Lee! The mind may remember what to do, but it’s the body that remembers how it’s done. Even old masters continuously practice calligraphy to keep themselves sharp. Come to think of it, I wonder if your awakening as a martial artist is why you’ve been able to pick up calligraphy so fast.”

Lee Jia grimaced at Dae.

“What!? This is fast? It feels like it takes forever just to get a single character good enough for you to let me move on.”

Dae chuckled and shook his head.

“Miss Lee, I had previously simply assumed it was talent, but it took me days to draw that character to my master’s satisfaction. I suspect you’ll be drawing it proficiently within the hour. Besides, most of these characters share smaller components or similar strokes. It’s slow at first, but you’ll find that it gets faster as you go.”

As much as she tried to focus on the calligraphy practice, it was extremely tedious and her mind couldn’t help but wander. Lee Jia thought about what Dae said about the body remembering—he had implied that being a martial artist made her learn calligraphy faster. Did that mean that to internalize her calligraphy practice it would be better to meditate on her Ki?

Another thought occurred to her, and she thought back on her martial arts lessons. The basic movements had taught her how to move with the flow of her ki, which in turn empowered those movements, making them more graceful, more precise, and more powerful. Could she do the same with her calligraphy?

“Miss Lee!? What in the ancestor’s names are you doing?”

“Sorry, Dae. I was trying something different.”

Lee Jia’s first attempt made a huge mess and Dae panicked as he tried to clean it up. The forms of Ienaga’s bujutsu were too big a scale for calligraphy, unless she was writing on a wall or something. She would have to try to enact the same principles with smaller movements.

Dae looked askance at Lee Jia as her next attempt stayed on the page, but made a huge mess of scribbles that only vaguely resembled the character she was working on. Working with the flow of her ki was taking her too far off course and it was hard to strike a balance between moving the way she wanted to and the way her ki flow dictated.

Dae continued to watch curiously as Lee Jia attempted to redirect the flow of ki in her hand to empower the movements necessary to replicate the character. It took a surprising amount of concentration and physical effort, and she had broken a sweat by the time she managed to finally replicate the character over an hour later.

Dae picked up the finished sheet and examined it with interest. As he did his eyes widened, then widened again when he examined it with his mana sense.

“Miss Lee, this is perfect! Not only have you managed to scribe the character within an imperceptibly small margin of error, you’ve also infused it with mana. This is effectively a talisman, albeit one that doesn’t do anything except harmlessly dissipate its mana into the air, hehe.”

Lee Jia hummed with interest. She had only been trying to see if she could use ki-empowered movements to write more easily, but the end result had been very different from her expectations. The character came out perfectly, but it was physically taxing and imbued the character with her ki in the process.

Experimentally, Lee Jia drew out three more of the same character and Dae’s jaw dropped as she did so with the speed and grace of someone who had done it thousands of times.

“These are all perfect! And you did them so fast! How are you doing this?”

“I um, I just wrote them with martial arts I guess? Hard to explain, but I used my ki while writing.”

Dae’s tail was wagging excitedly as he held the blank talismans. The character written on them simply meant ‘market’ and was too vague a concept to manifest any sort of phenomenon. Dae tried activating one anyway and it simply disintegrated harmlessly.

“Magus Hwang and Master Do were right. The potential of interdisciplinary study is incredible! I really need to work harder on my second awakening…”

Dae mumbled the last part to himself as Lee Jia went back to practicing her calligraphy the old fashioned way. The ki method was a bust as far as she was concerned, it was way harder and she’d probably run out of ki if she kept it up long enough.

---

On the way back from Dae’s lesson, Lee Jia decided to check in on Takeda and Eunae—she was worried about Takeda’s wound from yesterday. It was Seong Eunae who answered the door, and she clasped her hands, beaming happily at Jia’s right shoulder.

“Ah! Jia’s come to visit, how lovely! Please come in.”

Eunae ushered Lee Jia in and seated her in the usual plush chair by the tea table. Eunae had already set out snacks and tea by the time Lee Jia realized that Takeda Rika was not present. She broke out into a cold sweat at the thought of being alone with the princess. Lee Jia hadn’t realized that the situation was so disturbing to her, but now that it was real she was as nervous as she had ever been.

“U-um, h-how—how’s Takeda’s wrist?”

Lee Jia cursed herself for stammering so badly. Eunae had always been good to her, why was she so scared now? Eunae didn’t seem to take any notice as she responded.

“Almost entirely healed, now. The constitution of a martial artist is certainly impressive! She’s out taking lessons with one of Magus Hwang’s classes, in case you were wondering.”

Lee Jia nodded and reached for her teacup, but gave up on the idea when her trembling hands threatened to spill the drink. Eunae had a look of concern on her face as Lee Jia tried to take a steadying breath.

“Are you ok, Jia? Is something wrong?”

“U-um, I—”

Lee Jia stammered as she tried to figure out what to say. How do you tell someone you’re terrified of them because they can enthrall your mind with a glance? In the end, she simply ended up mumbling an excuse.

“I’m just uh, nervous about the duel.”

Eunae’s furrowed brow indicated that she wasn’t convinced by the answer, but she didn’t press further.

“I see. Well in that case, I may have some good news! One moment, let me go get something.”

Eunae stepped into another room briefly, and came back carrying a small plate of white jade, about two handspans in diameter, with a formation delicately engraved onto it and what appeared to be some kind of gold inlay on the engraving.

“I promised I would give you something to help, right? This is an elemental converter—a common tool used by young mages to aid in the creation of talismans. Rika told me you had your natural affinity checked, right? I’ve modified this one to use the lightning element.”

Lee Jia peered curiously at the plate, through her mana sense she could see the intricate infusion of mana flowing through the delicate engraving.

“How does it work? Did you make this yourself? It looks really complicated.”

Eunae shook her head and laughed melodically.

“No, this would be beyond me. I just made a tiny modification here, see?”

Eunae pointed out a place where the engraving seemed a bit rougher, and without the gold inlay. Despite the physical differences, the mana seemed to imbue that portion of the formation without any issue.

“As for how it works, you place an imbued talisman in the center here, then activate the formation with your own mana. As you feed mana into the formation, the mana imbued within the talisman will be converted. It’s a shortcut to allow first stage mages to create elemental talismans.”

Lee Jia’s ears perked up and she stood up excitedly.

“You mean I can make my own attack talismans with this!?”

One of her biggest worries for the upcoming duel was that other than the basic combat martial arts training she was receiving and Dae’s freebie talisman, she would have no means of attack. This item, combined with her new calligraphy martial arts technique—which sounded extremely silly, when she put it like that—would let her build up her own supply of attack spells.

“Ideally an attack spell would be imbued and activated by the same element, but imbuing the talisman with lightning mana and activating it with unattuned mana works as well. I should warn you that this isn’t very efficient. It takes a lot of mana to reattune even one talisman.”

“This is exactly what I needed, Eunae! I didn’t even know something like this existed! Dae never mentioned anything about it. Why doesn’t everyone have one of these?”

Eunae scratched her cheek and glanced away awkwardly.

“W-well, they are only useful to first stage mages. College doctrine usually doesn’t expect mages to be creating their own talismans until the second stage or later. In battle, line mages are assigned to static formations or armed with generic talismans. You would only find something like this among the high nobility and even then it’s considered...extravagant.”

Eunae slowly turned redder as she spoke, clearly uncomfortable to be flaunting her status, which seemed to be a sore spot. Lee Jia froze as she came to a realization, her nerves had been eased a bit by her excitement but her anxiety returned with interest.

“E-Eunae...is this thing rare or expensive?”

“They are uncommon because there is little demand for them. I’m not sure if it’s expensive, my aunt had this one commissioned for me as a birthday gift.”

Lee Jia quirked her eyebrow curiously at Eunae, it was rare to hear her talk about family. Despite her better judgement, Lee Jia asked a question with trepidation.

“Eunae...who is your aunt, exactly?”

Eunae broke into a nervous sweat and looked away in a miserably failed attempt to look innocent.

“T-that’s not important, Jia. This is my gift to you, alright? As a thank you for helping Rika and for showing me that awakening technique. A celebration of our friendship!”

Lee Jia frowned down at the plate of white jade and gold. Now that she looked at it, it was obviously expensive. The materials alone would cost more than Lee Jia could even imagine. A horrible suspicion came to Lee Jia’s mind and she couldn’t stop herself from confirming it.

“Eunae…is your aunt the matriarch of your clan?”

Eunae visibly cringed at the question, rubbing at her face in frustration.

“Please don’t think differently of me, Jia. I’m just Eunae. Not Seong Eunae, not Princess Seong, not the Queen’s niece. I am just me, alright? This is the only chance I’ve ever had, perhaps the only chance I will ever have to just be Eunae, do you understand?”

Lee Jia’s head was spinning. She had been thinking of Eunae as a princess, but that was much closer to the queen than she had even imagined. What was she doing here? And a gift from the queen!?

“Eu—um, I mean—I can’t accept this. It’s too much, I’m sorry.”

Lee Jia turned to leave, but she was interrupted by Princess Seong.

“Wait! Jia, look at me.”

Lee Jia’s blood ran cold. Her entire body was trembling as she began to edge towards the door—could she escape a mage like Princess Seong? What would happen if she was caught? She remembered that piercing blue gaze, that empty feeling as if nothing mattered anymore. Eunae spoke again, her voice barely above a whisper.

“Lee Jia, please. I know what you’re thinking, and I promise it’s not a trick.”

Lee Jia stood still as her thoughts warred in her head. Eunae hadn’t given Lee Jia any reason to distrust her except for that first day, which she had said was a mistake—not an accident, careful phrasing. She’d been nothing but friendly, even insisting on given names—too friendly, trying to lower your guard. There had been ample opportunity for Eunae to enthrall Lee Jia before now, if she was going to do so—not Eunae, Princess Seong. Then finally, Lee Jia realized that her misgivings were not entirely her own—they were Eui’s. She took a steadying breath, gathered her resolve, and turned around.

Eunae was on her knees, hands clasped together and held against her chest. Her eyes were closed, but tears ran down her cheeks. She must have sensed that Lee Jia had turned around because she immediately kowtowed, so deep that her forehead nearly touched the floor.

“Please! Before I came here, I was alone. My family hated me—I had no friends. You and Rika are the only people who have ever trusted me, the only people with whom I could just be myself. Please don’t let my family take even that from me.”

Lee Jia realized that she and Eunae were more alike than she thought. They had both been alone before coming to the academy. They had both been happy to make new friends. Lee Jia couldn’t imagine the hardships a princess would go through, but if she had learned anything in her time here it was that people were the same in any social strata. Lee Jia let out a heavy sigh and sat back down.

“I’m sorry, Eunae. I was just a bit overwhelmed, I wasn’t thinking clearly. You’re right, it doesn’t matter who you are or who your family is. You’re my friend, and I shouldn’t have forgotten that. I guess I still have a lot to learn about friendship.”

“Jiaaaa!”

Eunae practically tackled Jia with a hug as she sobbed openly. Jia returned the hug awkwardly as her shoulder was soaked by tears and some other, less dignified liquids.

“Eunae, you’re getting snot all over me.”

Eunae half-chuckled, half-sobbed in response, but didn’t release Jia.

---

Eunae did eventually compose herself, and after taking a few moments to clean up, there was no sign of her earlier breakdown. In the meantime, Eunae tried to convince Jia to accept the elemental converter.

“It was a backhanded gift, you know.”

Eunae explained as Jia enjoyed the snacks, more comfortable now than she had been on any of her previous visits.

“I’m not actually very talented. I may be the least deserving person of a place in this academy. I’ve always tried to make up for it with hard work, and my family has tried to make up for it by spending vast resources on my development as a mage. The elemental converter was a thinly veiled insult, a way of suggesting I’d be stuck in the first stage forever.”

Jia nodded. Even royalty could be petty and vindictive, they just expressed it a little differently.

“It’s fine if you take it. I have no use for it anymore, and the only people who would trace it back to me are close family members.”

A thought occurred to Jia as she reluctantly accepted the item.

“You said these weren’t in very high demand because they are only useful to first-stage mages, right? But isn’t this place about to be swarming with first-stage mages?”

Eunae put a finger to her chin as she thought about it.

“Hmm, I suppose you’re right. Perhaps try to keep it safe somewhere.”

Jia gave Eunae a flat look, but in the end she accepted that this really was exactly what she needed right now. She was a bit eager to try it out, actually.

“Eunae, can I borrow a calligraphy set and some paper? I want to try this out.”

Eunae tilted her head curiously.

“I don’t mind, but you need to infuse the talisman first, you know. I can see that you’ve been diligent about your mana training, but I’m not sure it’s going to be as simple as you think it is.”

“Just let me try it?”

Eunae shrugged and fetched the materials for Jia. As she began to inscribe the talisman, Jia realized she had a problem. She had learned to adjust her ki flow for a specific character earlier, but the talisman had several characters that she would have to replicate the same way if she wanted to make use of her ki in calligraphy. With a sigh, Jia went to work relearning how to write those characters using her new technique.

Thanks to her practice earlier in the day, it went a little bit more smoothly. It still took her almost an hour for each character to learn the right ki pattern but once she had, it was simple to replicate it again. It took a few hours to finish learning the entire talisman but once she did, she could rapidly reproduce them as long as her ki supply lasted. Eunae watched the entire time, her half-lidded gaze focused intently on Jia’s hands.

It took more out of her than Jia had expected to finally produce a basic attack talisman. Her ki reserves were nearly empty and she was sweating from the exertion of her ki. However, she now had a fully infused talisman without having spent any of the mana in her aura. Eunae examined the finished product with interest.

“You used ki to infuse the talisman at the same time as you wrote it? That’s an interesting technique—you’ll be able to produce a lot more of these than I expected. It normally takes quite a bit of mana to infuse a talisman, but this technique of yours is faster and much more efficient. Where did you learn it?”

Jia chuckled awkwardly and scratched her head.

“Ahaha, I figured it out myself this morning while I was practicing writing.”

Eunae’s eyes widened as her head jerked up from looking at the talisman and she nearly stared Jia in the eyes before averting her gaze.

“I think I might be coming around to Rika’s way of thinking about people of exceptional talent.”

Jia turned away, embarrassed as she tried to change the subject.

“Enough about that. Let’s try out the elemental converter.”
