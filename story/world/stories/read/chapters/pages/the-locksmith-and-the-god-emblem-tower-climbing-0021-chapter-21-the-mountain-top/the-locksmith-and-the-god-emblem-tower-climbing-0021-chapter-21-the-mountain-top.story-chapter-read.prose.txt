It was cold. Tundra level cold. Goldman carried Manes around like a backpack, his body fighting against every cutting wind attack that came from the mountain. Not only him, of course. Harry used his sturdy doors as platforms with each step he took; he could no longer feel the tips of his toes, and thought that avoiding the snow might help.

He was walking better with the crutch, but still felt like he was slowing the group down. Daniel strolled through the snow as if this was just another day. Brun, surprisingly, followed in her father’s footsteps. That, somehow, made Madeline pissed off for realizing she had less physical capability than an elderly woman. She then used Harry as an excuse for being so behind, as she could "help him" in case things got tense."

Everyone followed Vela, who used the power of his Emblem in his candelabrum, a flame that broke through the blizzard falling over them. Its light, like a beacon, kept everyone on the same path, each at their own pace.

They had been in this situation for almost an hour, all of them tired.

I hate winter. Harry thought as he stumbled over his doors, each step harder than the last, but he persevered.

It wasn’t winter that he hated, but rather the consequences of his self-destructive plan. When he was in the capital, he used his locks and his cleverness to take down enemies. Besides that, the enemies, for the most part, were normal guards. Rarely an Emblem user. That certainly wouldn’t be the case in the Logos Tower. Dangers were above, much greater than even Dracno, and he couldn’t lose a hand and a leg every time he fought. He needed to replenish his tools and learn to work in a group.

He looked at Daniel walking alongside Brun, the two of them not the least bit concerned with those who were slower. He could hear Madeline’s grumbling at his side while she forced herself to walk straight. Was this the best he could have? No, but it was what he got. He had to make the best of it.

He could have carried me instead of that infernal child, right? Harry added inwardly as he saw the silhouette of Manes and Goldman.

That was the third floor. Or level. Or area. Whatever they called it, for Harry it was one more step toward fixing his life and those he loved. Though one thing kept pounding in his brain; did they all want their lives changed? That was a question he should ask himself more often, but that would be for the future.

They felt the stifling air and the wind disperse as they followed Vela. Suddenly the light guiding them all stopped, and then he heard Daniel shout.

“…Now!” His voice was distant, and soon the flame was gone. Damn it, where’s Madeline? Did I get lost?

Harry looked from side to side, walking in an endless blizzard. He could still hear voices muffled by the wind, almost as if they were lost souls. He decided to hurry and generated several doors as a path. Despite his disability, he was almost running as he chased the voices.

One moment his eyes were narrowed, straining to see a foot in front of him, and in another, he was mesmerized by the view. He was near a ledge, the blue ocean giving contrast to a gigantic mountain that stood in the center. The Locksmith felt the pressure of the wind disappear and another force pushing him downward; he was falling.

Harry stared at the sky for a second then turned around, seeing an abandoned structure below him. Realizing he had a few moments, Harry put his hands behind his head. He closed his eyes as if he were going to sleep

Despite the concerned shouts of his companions up above, he filtered out only the relaxed voice in his head. This was why he stole. This was why he threw himself off cliffs and lost limbs while fighting. That feeling, that adrenaline, that itch in his soul he didn’t even know he had. His freedom was different from Madeline’s, but the principles were the same. He fled, fought, ran, and jumped. Was that all he used his gift for? No. He had goals. Before the potential of his life, however, that goal seemed empty.

No. It isn't empty. It's a calling. The young thief opened his eyes and shook the hedonistic thoughts out of his mind. He then conjured a door right below him. Harry used his Emblem two more times until he stabilized on a door in front of the group. Madeline was relieved, Brun frowned, and Daniel kept the same expression of someone who recently smelled shit.

He felt a pull on his broken leg so strong he almost passed out. He thought he had said the last sentence out loud when Daniel pulled him closer to the group, but he realized that was just the viking’s way.

His crutch, however, fell down the mountain. Son of a bitch… The Locksmith thought as he fell into the snow in agony.

“You made Brun worried.” Daniel said flatly without looking down.

Madeline helped him get up. “You don’t need to fake pain…”

He looked indignantly at the girl, who broke into another laugh.

He was sulking over the lack of his tie and the pain he felt, still throbbing in his leg. Even so, he stayed on his feet while everyone admired the view. Harry also got a better look. No. Not a better look, but a more stable one.

He now saw that even near the edge, this mountain was frighteningly high. It was the same reaction as when he saw the size of the Tower when he entered the loop. I’ve never read anything about something so big besides the Logos Tower… Even so, he wasn’t a climber, so he was probably lacking in that department.

Clinging to the mountain was a decrepit building. Possibly abandoned, it had holes near the top. It was rectangular and tall, reminding Harry of old hotels where he spent his nights alone or with some girl.

A hotel at the peak of a mountain? He amused himself with the absurd idea, itching not to jump all the way to the place, but after seeing Daniel’s reaction, he thought better of it.

He looked down and saw the stone path that led to the hotel. It stopped abruptly in the middle of the snow. Trees surrounded the place. Some of them, however, were upside down or cut in half. Strewn against the snow, only the abandoned structure remained intact, if you ignored the holes and the whole haunted air of the place.

“Brun,” Harry was the first to comment on the view. “Are you sure we’re in the right place?”

“Why do you keep doubting me?” she replied, visibly irritated. “It will take time until I can understand where's the hatch and if we even need to climb. It’s not like I came here in the summer when I was little; all of this is new territory.”

“…So we’re going to have to go down then.” He looked down with a smile and then added, “Who wants to go first?”

Black bands slid over the snow, forming a kind of rope. This was Manes' Emblem. Although his power was stronger based on the lack of light around him, he could still exert a certain amount of force over his Shadows. It was strong enough to hold the weight of Harry, Madeline, and Vela.

Coming from his wrist, the bands, and Manes himself, were also held by Goldman. His hand was buried, with the tips of the bands sticking out. They then descended several meters, but didn’t even come close to touching the ground. It was a safer attempt to lower everyone without problems, and although the tension of the bands could hold a lot, it didn’t seem to be the most viable option.

The second option was to use Goldman’s Emblem. As Harry had already witnessed, the gentle compass man had the ability to create poles on the ground and on certain body accessories of the same polarity, making him literally avoid the ground, and thus fly. The problem, however, was that the radius of his ability wasn’t wide enough to create a safe path from where they were to the ground. He would have only a few moments before hitting the ground to soften the fall. If he were alone, Goldman would have no problem performing the feat, but with other lives on his back, he was hesitant.

That left the methods of Harry and Daniel. They could frenetically jump along with the Locksmith or be at the mercy of Daniel’s arrows. Surprisingly, it was a difficult choice for part of the group.

After a few minutes, Vela decided to take the first step and went with Harry. As he was more used to the young thief, and had already followed his steps more than once, he saw no problem.

Amid the mountain and the snow, the two of them descended to the ground, near the abandoned structure. They looked like two flies teleporting from one side to the other, rapidly descending as nothing more than blurs. Then a tired Harry appeared, his leg still hurting.

For it to work, Vela quickly followed onto the Locksmith, who then created horizontal doors as platforms. When they were balanced, they jumped a bit further down. For Vela, the trip was smooth; he basically just walked across five or six doors until his foot touched the snow. For Harry, however, due to his state, it was much more exhausting.

After a few trips, they all reached the ground. Brun had gone with Daniel when Harry went up; the viking didn’t go back up to help anymore.

According to the scholarly lady, this was possibly a good place to use their C-Coins; very close to the supposed hotel entrance.

Either way, they were all safe, Harry praying that the loop would come soon so his pain could be washed away. He knew, however, that it would take a few more hours for that to happen. Harry then looked up and barely saw the top of the mountain, a sharp blade coming from the ground.

We’re all gonna die. Harry thought, but it was just his fear talking. Probably.
