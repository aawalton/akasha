Arthur stood motionless outside the King's dining chamber, a perfect statue of a loyal guard. Beside him, Yudursh was just as still. The heavy oak doors were warded, their magic a silent, shimmering curtain in Arthur's mana sight, blocking all sound from within. He could only wait, his mind a cold engine of calculation, focused on the princess. A single misstep from her inside that room could draw the King's unwanted attention. Nothing good ever came from being noticed by the powerful, especially when you had no allies and no way out.

When the doors finally swung open, Ysara stormed out. Her face was a mask of cold fury, but her eyes held the glint of unshed tears. She didn't look at them, simply sweeping past and heading down the corridor toward her personal wing. They fell into step behind her, their footsteps silent on the polished marble. They followed her through the grand, empty halls until they reached the doors to her private chambers. She stopped, her back still to them.

"You are dismissed for the night," she said, her voice tight and strained. Without waiting for a reply, she entered her chambers, and the doors closed with a soft, final click.

They walked back to their own quarters in silence. Just as Yudursh was about to enter his room, Arthur spoke, his voice the quiet tone of Kaelin. "A question. Are we confined to the palace when off-duty?"

Yudursh paused, turning slightly. "No. Our duty is to the princess, not the palace itself. We can move freely within the city. Just be back before the morning shift." He gave Arthur a weary look. "Be careful. The capital is not the forest."

Arthur gave a curt nod. The moment Yudursh's door closed, his mind was already in motion. He had a new objective. He needed assets, eyes and ears within the palace walls, and he had a candidate in mind. But to move freely and untraced, he needed a new face.

He left his luxurious quarters, navigating the palace's corridors with a scout's practiced ease. He didn't need to evade the royal guards, but he moved with a quiet purpose, his path deliberately avoiding the main thoroughfares. He slipped out of the palace grounds through a quiet servant's gate, his movements designed not to raise alarm, but to go unnoticed and unremembered. His target wasn't one of the elite royal guards, but a common city guardian. He made his way toward the massive outer walls of Valoria.

He spent the next hour observing the guards patrolling the city walls. He wasn't looking for the best or the strongest. He was looking for someone whose physique matched his own. He found his target an hour before the shift change: a young, lean guardian with dark hair, roughly his height and build.

Arthur followed the man when his duty was over, a silent shadow trailing him through the winding streets of the city's inner district. The guardian lived in a small, neat house in a quiet neighborhood. Arthur waited, his patience a cold, familiar cloak. He watched as the lights in the house went out, but he didn't move. He waited another hour, pushing a sliver of mana to his ears to enhance his hearing. He listened as the man's breathing slowed into the deep, rhythmic pattern of sleep. Only then did he slip through an unlocked window. Activating his mana sight, the dark room lit up in a landscape of faint blue energy, allowing him to see as clearly as if it were day. He moved with absolute silence. He found the guard's uniform, neatly folded on a wooden chest at the foot of the bed. He took it and was gone, leaving no trace of his presence. He returned to his own luxurious room and slept.

The next day was quiet. The princess remained in her chambers, and Arthur's duty was to stand outside her door, a silent, motionless sentinel. After weeks of constant struggle and isolation in the forest, the simple structure of guard duty felt like a welcome change. The food was warm, the bath had been hot, and the bed was soft. Being around people again, even as a silent observer, was a stark contrast to the lonely vigilance of the wilderness. It was a chance to operate from a position of comfort rather than pure survival.

That night, he left his quarters again, dressed in Kaelin's simple, dark attire. He moved through the palace corridors and out the same servant's gate as before, a familiar shadow in the sprawling complex. Once clear of the palace grounds, he found what he was looking for in a forgotten side street. A weathered wooden sign depicting a needle and thread creaked above a derelict shopfront, its windows boarded and its door hanging off a single hinge. Inside the old tailor's shop, surrounded by the scent of dust and decay, he changed into the stolen guardian's uniform and pulled a simple cloth mask over the lower half of his face. Now a nameless city guardian, he returned to the servant's gate he'd used before and found a dark alcove to wait. He watched as a handful of off-duty servants left the palace grounds. A few minutes later, he spotted Seren. He let her get a head start before melting into the night to follow her as she made her way home.

Her home was a forgotten hovel on the outer edge of the capital, a one-room hut made of scrap wood and patched canvas that spoke of a life of constant struggle. He waited in the shadows, watching as she settled in for the night. Through a grimy window, he saw her greet a small boy. From the looks of it, they were the only two who lived there, making her a perfect, isolated target.

He waited, a patient predator in the dark. He watched them share a simple meal. Soon after, the boy curled up on a small cot in the corner, getting ready for sleep. Seren took their two bowls to the side of the hut, her back to the entrance as she began scrubbing them in a bucket of water. This was his moment.

He slipped through the main door, his footsteps making no sound on the packed-earth floor. The single room was cramped and bare. He moved past a small fire pit and a pile of rags that served as a bed, toward the child's cot in the corner. He covered the boy's mouth with one hand, his other resting on the boy's neck.

Seren entered a moment later, her expression weary from a long day of work. Her face, which had been set in a mask of tired resignation, instantly contorted into one of pure terror as she saw the masked guardian standing over her sleeping son.

"Be silent," Arthur whispered, his voice a low, dangerous rasp, "or I will twist his neck and kill him before you can even scream."

The maid dropped to her knees, her hands clasped before her in a desperate plea. "Please, honored Guardian," she whimpered, tears streaming down her face. "He is all I have. Take whatever you wish, but please, do not harm him."

Arthur pulled a small, corked vial from his belt. "I want your obedience," he said, his voice cold and flat. "Tomorrow, you will mix the contents of this vial into the princess's morning tea."

Seren's face went pale. "No," she whispered, shaking her head. "I can't. I would never..."

"You will," Arthur said, his fingers tightening slightly on the boy's neck. The child stirred in his sleep. "Or your son will pay the price for your loyalty." He placed the vial on a small table. Before Seren could process his words, he effortlessly scooped the sleeping child into his arms.

"Do this," Arthur's voice was a cold, final command, "and he will be returned to you, unharmed. Fail, and you will never see him again."

He turned and walked out the door, carrying the boy and disappearing into the night. The last thing Seren saw was her son's tear-streaked face, his mouth clamped shut by the guardian's unyielding hand.

The next morning, Arthur was on station outside the princess's wing, his face the calm, professional mask of Kaelin. A few minutes later, the doors to Ysara's chambers burst open.

Her first instinct was to call for Yudursh, her steadfast shield. But this was a matter of internal palace security, a potential poison plot. To exclude Kaelin, her scout, would be a breach of protocol. It would draw questions. She couldn't afford that. Steeling herself, she made the call. "Yudursh! Kaelin!" she shouted, her voice sharp with a mixture of fear and anger. "To my chambers, now!"

They entered to find the princess standing beside a sobbing Seren. Ysara's face was pale, her eyes wide with disbelief. "Seren has just told me the most disturbing story," she said, her voice trembling slightly. "Last night, a guardian broke into her home. He threatened her son, tried to force her to poison me... and then he took him."

She looked at Yudursh, then at Arthur. "I want every city guard in Valoria questioned. I want this man found."

Arthur stepped forward. "That will not be necessary, Your Highness."

He reached up and pulled the cloth mask from his belt, the same one the maid had seen the night before. Seren gasped, her eyes widening in horrified recognition.

"It was a test," Arthur said, his voice still the quiet, level tone of Kaelin. He looked at the terrified maid, his expression unreadable. "A test of your loyalty to the princess." He then turned his gaze to Ysara, whose face was a storm of confusion and dawning horror.

He looked back at the maid, who was still kneeling on the floor, trembling. "You passed."

Disgust shimmered in Ysara’s eyes, but she said nothing.
