Today, there was tension in the council chamber of the Magi.

That much was nothing new. There was always tension in here. The usual sort ran on raw animosity, bruised egos, and the good old spirit of competition.

But today it was heavier, and a different kind.

Adom felt it the moment he came through the doors, and could not place where it came from. Maybe it was him. His nerves had been frayed all week, the training leaving him to feel every threat a half-step before it came, and a week of that was enough to make a man find danger in an empty room.

Or maybe it was the man beside the Archmage, who was smiling, whose eyes had found Adom before he'd cleared the doorway and had not let go since.

He had good dark robes, hands folded on the table, and his head tipped a little to one side. The Chancellor of the Empire, Lord Mephtilem.

At the thought of his name, the demon's smile widened, just slightly, just for Adom.

Or perhaps it was only his imagination.

But he had just decided the Chancellor might read minds, and on the chance that he did, Adom thought it wiser to assume the man was reading his right now, and to keep the inside of his head as tidy as the outside.

"Magus Sylla."

The voice found him before he'd taken three steps into the room. It was a young man's, or sounded like one. He never lifted it past conversational, and it reached the far wall regardless.

It suited the rest of him. A stranger would have put the Chancellor at thirty-five and not looked twice. The guess would have missed by a wide margin. People laid it at the door of his blood: both his parents half-elven, by the common telling, and elven years were slow to show on a face. The pointed ears had skipped him, at least. Whatever else ran in the ‘man’, it stayed well out of sight.

"And Magus Merlin. The very two I'd hoped to find."

Mephtilem didn't rise. He turned in his chair, an easy half-turn, and opened a hand toward the empty seats as though the room and everyone in it were his to seat.

"Come, sit, both of you. You've walked the length of that dreadful corridor only to be stared at by a roomful of people who've known your faces for years. I won't compound the cruelty by standing on ceremony."

Down the table, men who had never once in Adom's memory let a council pass without an argument sat with their hands folded and said nothing at all.

Adom took his chair. Merlin took the one beside him.

"I've been following the council's work," Mephtilem went on. "Yours in particular, the two of you, and closely. Closely enough that I daresay I could sit your examinations, though I'd ask you not to mark me on the result." He laughed at his own joke before anyone else dared to. It was a big laugh, and it carried to the corners of the chamber. Half the table came in behind him, a moment late, every one of them watching to be sure they'd judged it right.

Adom marked the ones who didn't. Gaius at the head, unmoved. Beth, the still needles in her lap. Merlin beside him, who gave it no more than a courteous tilt of the head. And Nox — this one with some surprise — who sat with his jaw set and gave the joke nothing at all.

"I'll say this plainly, because it is not said here half so often as it ought to be. The empire is glad of this council. And His Majesty—" the smallest bow of the head "—asked me to bring you his regard, by name."

The eyes returned to Adom, and warmed. "He reads of you more than he reads of half his own generals. With a good deal more pleasure, he tells me."

Adom inclined his head. "You're very kind, Chancellor. His Majesty more so. We'd be glad if you carried him our thanks."

Gaius let the courtesy settle, then set both hands flat on the table and rose half out of his chair, which from the Archmage counted as calling the room to order.

"Then we're all present," he said. "We can begin." He had none of the Chancellor's ease. His was an old man's voice at the end of a long week, and the room went quiet to make space for it. "You've been brought here on no schedule of ours. The Chancellor is with us on an official matter, and an important one. I'll let him put it to you himself."

He sat, and gave the floor back with a small turn of the hand.

"You're kind, Archmage." Mephtilem dipped his chin to Gaius, then let his gaze travel the table, gathering every eye before he spent a word on any of them. "I'll not keep you long from your work. The Emperor would not have sent me at all, but for a matter that has lately cost him sleep."

The brief pause that followed felt measured out to the ounce.

"It concerns one of your own. A matter that has reached the palace and troubled it greatly." His hand lifted, and a single finger leveled at Adom. "And it touches you, Magus Sylla."

Every face at the table turned to follow it.

Adom kept his hands where they were and his breath steady, careful not to let the cold going through him reach his face.

"Connected to me?" he said carefully. "Forgive me, Chancellor, but what is it you believe I've done?"

"Done?" The Chancellor's brows climbed, all delighted alarm. "Oh, no. No, no. Don't look so stricken, Magus, you'll frighten the table. You've done nothing at all."

He then leaned in a fraction, and the edge that came into the smile never once touched his voice. "Unless you have. In which case, now would be the moment to unburden yourself."

Adom's heart was going hard enough that he half-believed the man could hear it across the table.

"No," he said.

Mephtilem held his eyes a breath past comfort. Then he laughed, and just like that the danger drained out of the air.

"I jest. I jest." He waved it all away. "Forgive an old court habit. One spends too long among schemers and forgets how it lands on honest men."

And then, in the same breath, with no movement in the smile at all:

"No. What brings me to your council is not anything you have done, Magus Sylla. It is what was done to you." His hands folded again. "The attempt on your life."

For a moment Adom only stared at him.

Of all the ways he'd played this council out on the walk up, the Chancellor of the Empire arriving to fuss over his safety had not been one of them. He turned it over and found no grip on it anywhere.

It made no sense. Which, with a creature like this one, meant it made a great deal of sense, and Adom simply couldn't see the shape of it yet.

He gave the only answer that was safe to give.

"That's kind of His Majesty." Adom heard his own voice come out easy, and was faintly surprised it had. "But the matter was dealt with, Chancellor, the same day. Both of them were taken alive. Last I knew, they were in the healers' ward, under guard and mending. There was nothing left to worry over."

"Ah." Mephtilem's head tilted the other way. "Yes. About that."

He let a small, regretful quiet open up before he filled it.

"They died, I'm afraid. Of their injuries." He feigned sympathy, arranged for the occasion. "A pity. One does so like to ask questions."

Adom held still.

It did not fit. He'd quietly kept a thread on those two himself. Bruised and broken after what he'd done to them, certainly. A long way from dying, though. He'd checked more than once.

"...When?" he asked.

"Yesterday, I'm told." The Chancellor waved it off, the whole of it, with a small turn of the hand.

The two people alive who could have named who sent them. Gone the day before the Empire came to ask why…

"But this is beside the point. The dead are not what brings me." Mephtilem folded his hands, and his voice settled, graver now. "What troubles His Majesty is the why. Two trained weavers, in daylight, in a public street, spending their own lives to end one of yours, and no one in his Empire can tell him the reason. That is the part costing him sleep."

His gaze went round the table, gathering all of them in, not Adom alone.

"You must understand how the Emperor holds this council. The magi are the pillars the Magisterium stands on, and the Magisterium is the magical body of the whole Empire. Its strength is the Empire's strength. So when one of you is set upon in the open, like a merchant relieved of his purse, it is no private misfortune. It is a wound to the realm." A small bow toward Adom. "And His Majesty does not take wounds to the realm lightly. The safety of every soul at this table sits very near his heart."

What the fuck? Adom thought, but kept as little of it on his face as he could.

Okay. The man was good. He was so good.

And that was what he kept colliding with. The regret pitched just right, the pause held just long enough, and had Adom walked in here knowing nothing, he'd have swallowed it whole and thanked the Chancellor for the trouble of telling him. He was that good a liar.

But Adom knew what he knew. He'd set guards on those two himself. Picked men. And the pair were dead regardless, the day before anyone came asking — which meant someone had walked past Adom's people unnoticed and closed two mouths before either could open. He had a fair guess whose hand.

"Which is precisely why," Mephtilem went on, as if he'd heard none of what Adom hadn't said, "His Majesty has resolved the matter be better kept. Going forward."

He lifted one hand and snapped his fingers.

It came from the General.

Magnus had drifted to the wall at some point Adom hadn't clocked, and his shadow stretched long across the floor in the slanting light. As the snap faded, it began to come apart.

The dark peeled up off the stone. It took an edge, took a shape, and stepped clear as a living person. Then another behind it. Then another, each separating from the last, rising, resolving, walking free, until ten of them stood in a row on the council floor where a breath ago there had been only the light and Magnus's shadow on it.

Men and women both. Plainly dressed, unremarkable, and perfectly still. They came to attention as one and waited, and not one of them had made a sound.

No magus at the table moved.

That was worse than the ten figures. A room of the most influential mages in the Empire watched armed strangers unfold out of a man's shadow, and not a single one of them shifted in his chair.

Adom looked to Gaius.

The Archmage watched the row without surprise.

Perhaps he'd been told at the last minute, Adom guessed. Handed it already decided, with the throne's seal on it and no room to refuse. The one thing left to him was to keep his face still, and he was doing that now.

"These are the Shadows." Mephtilem let a note of ownership into the words. "A new elite body, raised this past decade, answerable to the throne and to no one else. You'll not have heard of them, and that is the point of them."

His hand opened toward the silent row.

"Each is trained past anything the old orders ever turned out; the best the Empire could find, and better still by the time it was done with them. One stands equal to a Star Knight, and is schooled to bring down a mage besides. Knight, weaver, blade — there is nothing they have not been set against."

He had a small, satisfied smile that was frankly annoying. "They live for the one purpose. To keep the soul they are given breathing."

His gaze landed on Adom.

"And His Majesty has decided that each magus of this council shall have one. From today."

It was Draven who asked, from his place near the head. "Following us how, exactly, Chancellor? To what degree?"

"Every degree." Mephtilem looked pleased to be asked. "Your Shadow goes where you go. When you walk, it walks in yours. When you sleep, it stands your door. It won't eat at your table or speak unless you wish it to; you may forget it is there, and most of you will. But it will be there. Awake, aware of every corner of the room, from this day to the last one it is needed."

He spread his hands. "Day and night, a single set of eyes that lives only to see you safe. Take it for what it is, I'd ask. A gift, from an Emperor who will not lose one of you."

A gift.

Adom sighed in his mind, as understanding dawned on him.

Mr. Biggins did mention they suspected what Adom might be, and a man under that suspicion earned a watcher or three. Well, here was the watcher, he supposed.

They couldn't be sure he was the Architect. If they had known what he was, truly known, the matter would have been settled somewhere quieter than a council chamber. But they had a suspicion and no proof, and a one-circle magus, the youngest the Empire ever raised, was not a thing you buried on a guess. Guess wrong and you'd thrown away an asset for nothing. Guess right and the answer went into the ground with him, still unlearned.

So it would appear they built a reason to set eyes on him instead.

He realized the two assassins were probably thrown away on purpose. Sent to fail, and to die failing, so a frightened Emperor might do the only decent thing and wrap his youngest magus in a protection he could not turn down.

A protection that would stand at his door by night and walk in his shadow by day, and study him, hour upon hour, for the one slip that turned a possible Architect into a certain one.

It was a wildly self-centered way to read a room. Adom knew it. Every magus here was getting a Shadow, and not one of the others had a phoenix hidden in his house and a coup folded in his pocket.

He tried to make it about all of them, but he couldn't. Every line of it ran back to him.

"And you, Magus Sylla?" The Chancellor turned the warmth back on him. "What do you make of it? A great deal to arrange for a man with no say in the matter, I know, but that is the nature of being cared for, isn't it? One doesn't get a vote." He laughed, delighted with all of it. "I do hope you'll forgive the imposition."

"Not at all, Chancellor." Adom smiled. "It's a kindness. Please thank His Majesty for me. I'll sleep better knowing someone's at the door."

And while he said it, behind the smile, in fluent and leisurely detail, he thought about exactly what the Chancellor of the Empire could do with his gift, his Shadow, his borrowed face, and his freshly murdered witnesses — a private oration that took in several anatomical impossibilities, two farm animals, and a use for the pegasi outside that would have seen him arrested in eleven provinces.

So much for keeping the inside of his head tidy.

Across the table, the Chancellor kept smiling at him, fond as ever, and it occurred to Adom that the man might well be doing the very same thing, behind the very same face.

Fair enough, he thought, and gave a gracious nod.

Whatever you just called me, right back at you.
