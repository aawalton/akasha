Maurice saw sweat beading down Fabian’s brow as his mana coalesced within the vial, and one little glowing drop fell into the dregs of glowing liquid right as the leather-ball hit him on the nose.

Fabian reeled with a startled yelp, then stared at Maurice as the ball floated back in his hand.

“What the hell, Maurice!”

“What? You told me to distract you so you can work on your focus.”

Fabian rubbed his nose, his glowing vial still in his hand. “I thought you were going to do the same thing as Arisia. Not hit me in the face with a ball.”

Maurice’s brows knitted. He didn’t know why Fabian expected him to play by his expectations and in any case, he wasn’t sure how to do the poking thing Arisia did yet. “Isn’t this better? The whole point is for you to maintain your focus. What’s the point if you’re just ready to ignore a specific thing? In fact, I should just use this. I should spray you with water too. And shove you. And try to dispel what you’re doing. Right?”

Fabian’s lips pressed into a line and Maurice grinned. He knew he got him, though before Fabian could come up with a response, Ash stepped into the little cabin, arms held out ahead of her as if she was transporting some priceless artifact. One hand had a little mana-wrapped shard of familiar shadows, and one looked like a one-time ability.

“Told you,” Maurice called out as he hopped off the cramped bed so she could sit. She looked up at him and smiled, somehow still disbelieving. “He didn’t even hesitate.”

“I mean, yeah. Why would he?”

Both teenagers shared a look and chuckled. “Anyway, Fabian, can you tell Velistraine Henry wants us to slow a little? He said he has something to do.”

Henry smoothly dodged the jagged, coral-studded rock that flew at him. It flew by like a meteor, unimpeded by the water but still carrying enough energy to nearly boil its surrounding and in exchange, a dozen crackling Trickster’s Shadows slammed into the hill-sized crab. Like the darts of a taser, the clones latched on to it, then unloaded a powerful, recently upgraded Lightning Discharge that lit the crab up like a Christmas tree. The monster screeched in pain, slamming its massive claws, and destroying more of its surroundings as its eyes scanned for the real Henry.

But with everything Henry was throwing its way, that might not be easy.

While the clones on its back dodged the frenzied strikes, Henry’s projected Octominds peppered it with Toxic Rays from a distance, all but the two who’d chosen to go for its eyes. The crab’s struggle grew more frantic, but that was expected. Eventually, one of the massive claws hit its mark, destroying one of his clones, so Henry used that as a trigger and blew up all the others.

Henry was glad to see that Spiteful Detonation had grown in power. Now it was enough to get some blood out from an A-rank, but the difference in rank was still too large. But it would do. Henry might not punch hard enough just yet, but he would win this.

He would consume this crab.

Unlike the lobster, this one actually wanted to eat him. The only thing he sensed out of its mind was anger and hunger, even through its innate resistance. So Henry didn’t feel like holding back and considering he’d had a couple of days to rest and relax, he was ready. And he had a plan.

Toxic Ray isn’t using any of my venoms, Henry thought. Which is a problem. But my Venom Production skill is producing the different toxins I’ve copied. So how to fuse the two without losing my ability to inject the venoms through Bite?

A brown orb of dirt-colored light condensed in front of the crab’s mandibles, making the ground shudder and break down into clumps and clods of rock that slowly then quickly accelerated toward the orb of light. The debris condensed, shining with deep, earthy colors before it detonated toward Henry and his summoned army, clipping a few clones and one of his Octominds and punching a hole straight through the projection. One of the rocks even managed to reach Henry, but while it would create definitely create welt, it wasn’t enough to get through his defenses.

Unfortunately for his Octomind, that impact was enough to destroy its projection, and Henry sensed the mind retreat fully within Henry, disappointment wafting out of it.

“Don’t worry about it, little buddy. Just help me out with the clones,” Henry told before he created some distance and watched the crab swat at the opportunistic clones, returning most of his brain power to his venom upgrades.

I would have to somehow take the blueprint of the venom before it’s synthesized, then have the Toxic Ray use that, he mused as he sent another batch of clones forward, then followed it up by empowering the two Octominds that were only a few paces away the A-rank’s eyes. They had slunk behind him every since the fight had started, invisible, and now they were ready to attack, so Henry gave them a boost of Predation, and watched as they went ham on the armored eye-stalks.

Naturally, the crab didn’t like that, but Henry was keeping it busy enough that it just didn’t have enough arms to deal with everything.

So how do I make Toxic Ray into what I have in mind? he wondered as the crab was electrocuted once more. Do I try to fuse the two skills, or somehow link them? Do I break them down in smaller chunks and work everything from the ground up?

Henry hummed as he created more and more Shadows. Ten. Twenty. Thirty. He layered them behind him and pelted the crab relentlessly as he considered his options. The easiest option was probably linking them both, but keep them semi-independent, and also, to make it so Toxic Ray would be based upon Venom Production. Then again, breaking everything down could give him some much needed flexibility.

Oh! If I break down Venom Production and isolate the part that analyzes and memorizes toxins, I could have both Venom Production and Toxic Ray use it as a reference.

Henry liked that solution, but even though he said he’d work on it right now, he didn’t expect to finish it mid-battle. This wasn’t just a skill upgrade but actual manipulation and splicing of a particularly complex ability of his. But that didn’t mean he couldn’t start on the work right now.

I could work on analyzing both. And that’ll need a little time, as for this friend right here…

Henry had to focus a little on the fight. While his Shadows were doing damage and he was managing to tweak his skills a little to push them forward, it will take a while for him to be done here at this rate, and he couldn’t ask the crew to just stop for him, so when the next batch of Shadows rushed the giant crab, Henry slipped among them, darkening himself to match though he was definitely larger than them all.

The Shadows detonated, enraging the crab further, while Henry latched himself near the back of its thorax, which had been cleared from the moss thanks to the explosions. The cleared patches were brown, pocked with barnacles, which didn’t bother Henry much. He settled on one of the patches, then used his arms to wrap around the edge of the shell. He could have enlarged himself to match the crab’s size, but this was easier. With his current stature, he could apply the abilities he wanted to work on while making himself difficult to reach and address.

Even if the crab had noticed his arrival.

The crab was not happy about having him latch on to it. Especially when the two Predation-empowered Octominds were trying to tear its eyes out. But unfortunately for the Ravager, its arms couldn’t reach that far back so when Henry began grinding his beak into the shell, it went wild, throwing itself against the reefs and showered itself with magic-empowered rocks, though considering Henry was at an incline, he didn’t even need to make any effort to dodge the rocks. He just made himself small and layered a thick coat of bone above him, and focused on piercing the shell. Henry channeled more of the essence of Predation into his beak, gnashing and biting for what felt like less than a minute before he tasted the crab’s blood.

Half the battle was won.

Using the hole in the shell, Henry injected his venoms in the monster’s system, and while Lightning Discharge had been effective from outside the shell, he’d bet it’d much more effective from within. Still, Henry had to keep an eyes on his surroundings and for that, he assigned the Octomind that had lost its body. For the rest, he divided them over mainly two tasks: three were tasked with spreading their senses inwardly and observing Venom Production’s process. To help, Henry accelerated the production by using his mana, and he also made sure to alternate the venoms so the Octominds could sense the changes a little better. For the rest, one was left to scout and keep an eye on dangers, while the rest helped with the fight.

Two of those were already hounding the crab’s eyes. So business as usual there.

Henry continued his injections, and by now, he could sense how his personal toxin eroded the crab’s strengths and resistances, which was being made worse by all of Henry’s arms and Bites stealing copious amounts of its mana. To add insult to injury, that mana was then near instantly converted into toxins, which were then sent back into the crab’s body. Add to that the ionizing, blinding bolts of lightning that scoured it and made its limbs freeze every so often, it was fair to say the crab wasn’t having a good day.

It screeched, slamming its claws on its own shell, but Henry was too far back, and while he would have liked to shorten its suffering, it really was incredibly tough. To the point that he didn’t believe his usual brute-force method would be effective. No empowered swings or stabbing would do the job fast, and of course, he’d open himself to retaliation. This method was safest, even though he was being flattened against sharp rocks here and there.

Lightning Discharge wasn’t just physical when I fought against the lobster, Henry recalled, admiring the newfound power of the skill. Or maybe that was just because of how intense it was?

Henry considered the skill as the giant crab’s movement grew lethargic. Maybe the lobster’s lightning had been so powerful because of its Aspects?

Yeah, that was probably it. It must have had something lightning-themed. That’s why I was having trouble moving and controlling my body before flushing out the essence, Henry recalled as he delivered another dose of his own malicious mana into the monster’s system, this time empowering it with Predation before he followed it up with another pulse of lightning though this time, he didn’t just blast it willy-nilly.

No, this time, Henry held on to the skill nexus and didn’t let the discharge leave his body. Instead, he pushed more and more mana into it, more than the skill usually took on its own, and when it reached triple its normal cost, he even felt a little bit of pressure into the nexus, so he let it go.

For a fraction of a second, Henry went blind, while the spot from which the skill had exploded felt sore.

The whole shell of the crab and Henry’s own body lit up as the blue arcs of electricity surged wildly, to the point that a few bolts somehow curved back toward him and zapped him. But it was nowhere as bad as it had been within the crab’s body.

The taste of cooked crab permeated Henry’s beak and arms. He was doing damage, and he would win. But to be honest, he hadn’t expected to be making some new discoveries today.

You learn something new everyday.
