I honked twice before the gate opened. It was the same as the secret door inside the warehouse. A completely ordinary-looking wall split into two, and each part slid to either side.

I rode inside through a tunnel just big enough to squeeze a car through, dimly lit just enough to see inside. It opened into an open space. Various luxurious, ordinary, and extraordinary-looking cars and bikes stood there. I parked the bike in an open spot, yanked the keys out, swung them around my index finger on my left hand, whistled, and walked to the door.

Pushing the door open, I walked into the base. It looked exactly like it did before, except for one minute detail.

Cerise wasn’t on her computer.

She had laid a carpet on the floor and was sitting in a lotus position. A candle with a jasmine scent burned in front of her.

Is she doing some kind of aromatherapy to calm her anger?

She let out a deep breath and slowly opened her eyes. She extinguished the candle with her finger and stood up. Then she turned and looked at me.

She gave me a slight smile.

That’s creepy.

“Clark,” she said in a voice that was trying to sound creepy. “You are back.”

“Ah, um, y-yeah. I am back.”

“Why did you hang up on me?”

This is scarier than the monsters in the Rift, scarier than Johan, and I am sure she isn’t even using a skill.

“Ah, y’know…”

“I don’t know, so please tell me.”

“Look, I was tired from the fighting, and then Johan wouldn’t tell me anything. And I wasn’t really looking for you to give me an earful either.”

“I see,” she replied, really calmly, something I wasn’t expecting at all. “Okay, I understand. I am not going to give you an earful, so can you please explain what happened there and who this Johan is?”

Hurray for aromatherapy. I wish she could be like this every time instead of being pissy.

“Yeah, so…”

I told her how, just after the connection cut off, I was ambushed and then had to fight more than a dozen guys myself. I also told her how I broke my finger doing that.

“That skill is strong, but just as dangerous to yourself as it is to your enemies,” Cerise said while pulling out a container from one of the machines. She poured its contents into a glass.

“Yeah, that’s why I keep it as a last resort.”

“Here.”

Cerise handed me the glass she had just poured. It smelled sweet and was golden, just like the Golden Hearth Nectar I had given her. Just a bit duller.

“What’s this?”

“The potion. I replicated it. It isn’t as potent as that. Still, it should be strong enough to mend your finger, but not completely heal it. You will still need to rest to heal it completely.”

I stared at the liquid.

“Is it tested?”

“Yes.”

“But you just took it out of the machine.”

“I had already produced a small batch and tested it. This is the mass-production batch.”

“That means this batch isn’t tested.”

“It is literally the same. You don’t need to worry.”

“Listen, I am sorry about hanging up the call on you, but you shouldn’t make me your guinea pig.”

“Just drink it.”

The anger was starting to creep into her voice. Better not antagonize her even more.

“Here’s to me not dying.”

I took a gulp of the drink. The sweetness melted onto my tongue. I felt a tingle in my finger.

I took the cloth off my finger. It was healing. Slowly, the cracks in the bone filled and the tears sealed. The purple was gone.

I curled my palm and then stretched it open. The finger still hurt a bit, but it was now minuscule, only hurting when I moved it.

“Just so you know, this is on par with a lower Gold-tier potion.”

“How much do you think a bottle will sell for?”

Cerise said a number which was equivalent to me eating two instant noodles a day for a decade.

“I’m gonna be rich.”

I celebrated a little too openly.

Even Cerise laughed at that.

“Okay, now tell me what happened after that. About Johan.”

I told her about the naked man in the crate, how every other crate had some illegal goods but none of the people we were looking for. I told her how Johan used his skill on me just after waking up and how rude he was.

“There are so many Johans in the database, but none matches your description.”

Cerise was already on her computer, scrounging through the government database. Her powers amazed and terrified me. She could be anywhere, wherever there was internet.

“Of course it was a fake name. He gave me a fake name, and so did I.”

“So what was he doing there?” Cerise asked.

“Hell if I know. He kept saying he couldn’t remember anything else other than his name. So I dropped him near the police station, but he vanished instead of going in.”

“This looks like a completely different matter from ours. Let’s not waste our time on it. Anyways, I have another question for you, Clark.”

“Hmm?”

“Where’s my drone?”

...I am fucked, aren’t I?

I heard an earful, and it would have kept on going if Toby hadn’t come back.

“What the hell happened to you?” I asked the moment I saw him.

He was bloody, his clothes were torn, and he had cuts all over. It looked like he had come back from fighting a war himself.

“Cerise, quick, give him a potion.”

“No need,” Toby said, exhaustion evident in his voice.

“What do you mean, no need? You have lost so much blood. It’s a miracle you’re still standing.”

“Don’t need it.”

He unbuttoned his Hawaiian shirt and showed me his back. It had a nasty wound, a cut from a sword running down his back. Pink flesh was visible, and it was knitting itself back together.

A self-regeneration skill.

“Here, go take a bath. You reek.”

Cerise pinched her nose with one hand and gave Toby a new set of clothes with the other.

Toby grabbed them.

“I will when I’m healed.”

“No, now. I can’t tolerate this bloody smell.”

“Do you know how much it hurts when water goes into your wound?”

“I do not, and I do not want to know.”

Toby sighed. He did not go to the bath. Instead, he went to the other side of the room, picked up a deodorant, and sprayed it into the air.

“There. It shouldn’t reek of blood now.”

“Better.”

“What happened to you?” I asked. “How did this happen?”

“It was a trap,” Toby answered.

“And now, because of you, they will know that we’re on their trail.”

“Yeah, that’s on me. Mine was a bust. What happened in yours? Did you find the others?”

“No, mine was a bust too. But something interesting happened.”

“Oh?”

That piqued his interest.

“Tell me.”

So I told Toby the story.

“Any idea who this Johan guy might be?” I asked Toby.

“Neh. Johan is probably a fake name, and I can’t remember any Walker with long blond hair and golden eyes.”

“I think he was from a rival gang. He was kidnapped, and I just helped him escape,” I said.

“Your theory sounds very plausible,” Toby agreed.

“Are you guys done? Stop wasting your time talking about the blonde. We have a guild to infiltrate,” Cerise said.

“So what’s the plan?” I asked.

The base went dark.

Then a hologram of the Phoenix Guild’s headquarters emerged in the middle of the room.

“Tonight, when we…” she explained the top-of-the-line infiltration plan.

After the briefing—which I wasn’t happy with at all—I lay on the couch, finally getting that well-deserved rest.

It was early morning, and the party was at night. None of us had slept the night, so we decided to catch up on it now.

I wanted to go home and sleep in my own bed, but Cerise said it would be counterproductive.

Like hell it would.

“You asleep?”

I peeked up from the couch to find Toby standing in the doorway with a towel on his head.

“No. What’s up?”

“Nothing. Just wanted to talk.”

He walked towards me, and with him came a cool breeze and the smell of roses.

“Nice body wash.”

“It’s Tina's. Don’t tell her. She’ll kill me.”

I sat up to make space on the couch. He sat beside me, handing me a can of beer. I took it.

“Nothing like a can of beer after a good night of fighting on the verge of death and a refreshing bath.”

Two snaps of the cans opening echoed through the room, followed by the sound of gulping.

It had been some time since I had a beer, as I had been surviving on instant noodles. I missed the taste of it.

For a moment, neither of us spoke as we took in the moment.

“The last couple of days must have been hard on you.”

“Eh, not so much. Though I would very much prefer fighting Rift monsters over listening to Cerise’s nagging.”

“We are the same on that account,” Toby chuckled. “But don’t be too hard on her. She suffers from serious anger issues. She has been trying to get better and is already a lot better than she was a year ago. If you had met her back then, you would have either killed her or yourself.”

“That bad?”

“Yeah, that bad.”

“How did you survive that?”

“I have just known her for a long time. And me knowing what made her that way is also a big part of that.”

I looked at Toby, expecting him to continue. To tell me what it was that she had gone through.

“That isn’t something I should tell you.”

He stood up.

“If she tells you someday, know that she trusts you more than she trusts herself. Anyways, goodnight, Clark. It was fun sharing a drink with you.”

“Likewise.”

I watched Toby as he left the room, waving me a last goodbye before closing the door behind him.
