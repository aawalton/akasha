The leader was a handsome designer baby. He sported long black hair in a ponytail and a bright, white smile. He was clearly not an average ruffian; he was the Digital Universe's idea of an “elite,” and he had his eyes locked on Lina.

His name was Rosen.

“Hey, Lina. Stuck on pimple patrol again?”

Lina blushed. “Don’t look at me like that. Loricks ordered me to get him.”

“Right, right… That man must’ve done some pretty heinous things to be compensating with such”—Rosen looked Hayden up and down—“Charity.”

Hayden glanced between them. “Is there a reason I’m part of this conversation?”

Rosen’s face creased in strange angles. “Excuse me—what?”

“I asked why you’re barring my path. Because if you need me as a prop for your ‘flirting’... that’s pitiful.”

Rosen turned to his friends to ensure he had heard Hayden correctly. Their expressions matched his own.

“I can’t believe this…” Rosen chuckled. “Hayden Reskco, the lowest of the low, is calling me ‘pitiful’…”

“I’m the lowest of the low?” Hayden looked at the insignia on Rosen’s outfit. “We are at Galma, right? Galma? The place where coreborns go when they’ve been rejected from government universities?”

“Coreborns” referred to individuals born with a “core”—the organ necessary for breathing a gas known as cora. Cores not only made humans physically superior, but they let them house mutualistic endosymbiotes with seemingly supernatural abilities. The symbiotes were called spirits, and they gave humans the power to shake worlds.

Coreborn wasn't a derogatory term, but elites always found alternative names for the same traits. So Hayden's words implied Rosen was no greater than normal folk—and it made him clench his fists.

“What?” Hayden asked. “Did you think your core made you special?”

Rosen planted his foot and reached for Hayden, but Hayden dodged with a single step, sending Rosen stumbling forward. As Rosen turned to attack, he saw a woman jogging toward them. Judging by his starstruck expression, she was famous.

As Hayden would later learn, Kayline Iska was Lina's fraternal twin sister. They looked similar, but her hair was lighter and she had hazel eyes instead of blue. Her parents must have gone for a softer look when they edited her genes.

Kayline lifted her eyebrows at Rosen and then looked at Lina. “You're late.”

“I know,” Lina whined. “I left early, but—”

“This idiot slowed you down?”

Lina’s gaze was fixed on Hayden, so she said, “Yeah, exactly.” She looked up and saw Kayline staring at Rosen.

Rosen took a second to process the insult and then said, “Wait! I wasn’t slowing her down. I was just—”

A loud bell tolled. Kayline looked toward the sound and said, “Come on, we’re seriously late,” before pulling Lina away by the wrist.

Rosen saw the red lit bracelet and turned to Hayden. “Were you recording me?”

Hayden caught a glimpse of Lina's red lit wrist. So that's what that meant.

“You set that up, didn't you?” Rosen demanded. He took a step forward, but ground to a halt when he saw Hayden's chilling gaze.

Rosen stepped back. “What are you…?”

Hayden stepped in front of him. “We’re late.” He then sent Rosen’s friends a warning glance. “So don’t interrupt me.”

Once he was certain they got the message, Hayden continued up the mountain without resistance.

The ceremony was being held in the meadow from his memories. Multiple stages had been erected for future tournaments, and they were circled by hills that the students were standing on.

Hayden saw a man on stage he recognized. He couldn't remember who it was, but he remembered the man's ex-military build and dusty brown crew cut. He looked so standard and yet so familiar. It left Hayden frustrated to not know who he was.

Lina knew exactly who the man was. It was Loricks Raskin, the president of Galma Remedial. He was important, but she couldn't care less about him. She was way too busy getting scolded by her sister.

“Don’t tell me you like that guy,” Kayline said, sending Rosen a sidelong glance.

“Of course not!” Lina said hurriedly. “I think Rosen’s a pig.”

“Then why'd it look like you were all buddy-buddy?”

“We’re not ‘buddy-buddy.’ It’s just… we’re annoyed that we’re stuck watching over… him.” Lina subtly motioned to Hayden.

Kayline raised an eyebrow. “What’s wrong with him? I thought he was kinda cool.”

“You thought he was cool?”

“Yeah. He stood up to that idiot like a boss.”

Lina turned to Hayden in confusion and found him staring at the stage with an ambivalent expression. “Yeah, I guess so. But he’s not like that normally. He's usually so—”

“Uh oh,” Kayline interrupted. Loricks was glaring at her. “I gotta go. I'll talk to you later.”

Lina watched Kayline join the tournament staff and then turned her attention to Hayden. Was I wrong about him? she wondered.

Lina genuinely reflected upon it and came to a definitive conclusion—

Nope.

Hayden was just as bad as she thought—if not worse. He was hideous, antisocial, weak, and craven. He was a bootlicker—a worm that hid in the president's office. Just escorting him had turned into a strange humiliation ritual, as Hayden had become universally despised for shirking hard labor. And even though she got dragged through the muck just for walking around with him, Hayden was never thankful. Oh no, he was never thankful. Not to her—not to anyone. He was just sullen, terse, and rude—in that order.

Lina had the right to hate people, and Hayden was twice as hateable as he needed to be to hate. That was the God-honest truth.

He must’ve woken up on the wrong side of the bed or something.

Hayden did wake up on the wrong side of the bed—and he was indeed irritated. He hadn't even been up for an hour, and somehow he made two enemies simply by exercising maturity.

Hayden noted Rosen's warning glare and suppressed the desire to roll his eyes. I can't take this seriously, he thought.

It felt like he had walked into a preschool and was expected to fit in. That was a humiliation he refused to endure, so he ignored Rosen's damning stare and waited for the ceremony to begin.

Loricks walked to the microphone. He lifted his hand, and the crowd hushed into a deep silence. Everyone could feel the gravity of the speech before it even began. Then he spoke.

“Every year the rifts become more frequent… but the number of people closing them has remained the same. Coreborns are rare, and the ones talented enough to wield spirits are even rarer. Those willing to face deadly beasts within the rifts are rarer still. And when there aren't enough talented rifters, the rifts break, spilling alien beasts and lifeforms into our towns and cities.

“You read about it every day—you’ve probably seen the videos. But it doesn't mean much to you. You haven't experienced it. You haven't seen beasts tear through cities, or the way alien plants spread disease. But I have. When I was a rifter, I saw more of these scenes than I could count—and I refused to see more.

“That's why I started this program: to give brave, talented youth the resources and opportunities to apply to rifter academies. Some of you couldn't afford tuition; others failed entrance tests. A few were suspended and wanted a second chance. Whatever your story, you committed yourselves to a year of hard labor, training, and study. Now, you will be rewarded.

“Today you will receive your first catalyst. Those who successfully unlock their core will receive a spirit. And once you're trained with your spirit, we'll get you into entrance exams. This is your first step—the day your journey begins!”

Never before had Hayden heard such ironic cheering. He wondered if he also cheered like that—cheered when they announced they were maiming cores. He probably did. The thought gave him chills.

“But don't grow complacent,” Loricks warned. “If you burn your gate incorrectly, you'll never be a rifter. You may even suffer health complications. So take this event seriously. Okay?” He scanned their resolute faces. “Okay. Now that that's out of the way… let The Burning begin!”

The excited students spread out in the meadow, sitting on the grass as instructors walked around to help them.

Hayden walked to the back, hoping to avoid notice. To his annoyance, Rosen followed him.

“Can I help you?” Hayden asked.

“No. I just want to be in a spot where I can watch you fail.”

Hayden turned to him. “If you prioritize watching someone fail over your own success, you shouldn’t be surprised that you’re at a remedial labor camp.”

Rosen’s eyes flashed with rage. He looked around, likely hoping no one was monitoring him. Unfortunately, Kayline was, so Rosen scoffed and sat down.

Hayden followed suit.

The two sat in silence for less than a minute before Rosen said, “I’m not here for lack of skill, you worm.”

“So it’s behavior then?” Hayden asked. “That's worse.”

Rosen vibrated with anger. “Just shut up.”

Hayden obliged. He sat in silence, watching instructors walk around with glowing flowers. Each flower contained a specific pattern of cora. Once someone “burned” the pattern into their core’s “main gate,” it amplified the speed and power of their core.

The flowers held an ideal pattern—but it was incomplete. Using it was like having a hand with two fingers and a thumb; it was better than having no hands, but it made things more difficult. Hayden overcame that limitation through partially taboo methods, but it came at the cost of damaging his core.

He wouldn't make that mistake again.
