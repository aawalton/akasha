Machina screws the cap on a canister he filled with the noxious black plume. Tiffany gags again, and even John looks a little green as he drops it into a half-filled floatie. The outflow of the hydrothermal vent is rich in sulfur and iron compounds. He smells like how Danny imagines Hell itself to smell, brimstone and all.

Doesn’t it bother you? John weakly signs.

No. I can’t really get sick, can I? No digestive tract. Or anything else.

“Behind you, Captain!” Machina calls out suddenly, and Tiffany whirls in place. A rocky crab stands up, gravel pouring off its shell. A long, spindly leg stabs for Tiffany. A combination of both skill and magic lets her barely dodge the monster’s attack.

A bolt of azure energy launches from a magic circle floating in front of John’s hands. It slams into the crab. It keens as lightning crackles and scours its body. It refuses to go down. Few monsters die easily.

A whomp and a pressure wave hammers down into the crab, slamming it into the seabed. Tiffany is on the attack now, using her magic to try to beat the crab to death. Its shell creaks and groans, but still holds strong. Danny launches forward, harpoon brandished like a lance. He slams it into a joint and activates the harpoon.

Red energy burns into the monster, though it’s far hardier than the octopus was. Despite the injuries, it not only survives the vicious attack but even retaliates. A claw large enough to cut Danny in half snaps forward. Only a wave of water, controlled by Tiffany, saves him. The wave pulls him back and away from the monster. A second wave slaps the claw down and away.

Danny charges back into it, slamming his harpoon into its mouth with a magically enhanced lunge. Blue blood pours from the wound and the crab shrieks in pain. He actives the spear again, red crimson energy rips into the monster. It convulses, and a leg slams into his side. Only a shield from John prevents it from skewering him entirely. Danny forces the harpoon in even deeper with a scream. The crab glows red from the inside, and the shell pops. Blue gore spews out of cracks and blow-outs in the rocky shell.

Danny pants, and John’s healing magic only washes away most of it. Each use of the harpoon gets worse if he uses it too often.

You good? Tiffany signs to him. We can take a breather if you need it.

Are you three all right? I don’t see any others. We can continue if you want, or take a break.

A break would be nice, Danny admits. But where?

Machina puts one gauntlet on the ground and punches the seabed with the other. The rocky bottom jumps, and Danny can feel aftershocks through his boots.

There’s a cave that way.

After marching together for a few minutes, Machina is proven correct. A cave dives into the seabed. Danny can see sparkles deep inside. Something is reflecting Machina’s ever-burning inhuman fire.

How did you know this was here?

Seismic mapping. By hitting the ground and listening for the returns, I can estimate the geology of the Fracture. I’m not nearly as good as dedicated instruments. Things like caves are easy to see though.

Machina leads the way into the dark. His heavy footsteps shake the cave, and dust shakes loose. A light gray snow coats all of their armor and makes Tiffany sneeze. Danny runs an armored hand over the rough wall. His metal gauntlet hits something with a tink. A tiny red crystal pokes out of the cave wall.

As they continue down, Machina is forced to hunch over. The cave is narrowing with nearly every step. The water is noticeably warmer the deeper they go.

There’s a much larger space just ahead.

Will you fit? Danny asks. Machina can see his signing without even looking at him. Machina gives him a thumbs up.

And he is correct. Just before Machina would be forced to crawl forward on his knees, the cave rapidly expands into an aircraft hangar sized space. While the ground is oddly smooth, shiny, and slick, the rest of the open space is rocky. The most striking aspect are the hundreds, thousands of crystals. All of them are glowing in hues of red and orange, and the water around them is visibly hot. The light the crystals emit is distorted and warped by the hot water.

Danny plonks himself down with a clank against the wall between two crystals.

“Oh, that’s nice. Boss, we gotta take a few of these with us. I want to make a magic hot tub.”

Danny rummages in a military style assault pack and pulls out a stick of beef jerky. He starts chewing on it, uncaring about the near-boiling seawater just next to him. The sound of shattering rock draws him away from his meaty snack.

Machina had immediately moved to a crystal about the length of Danny’s arm and started to pry it out.

“Don’t you ever stop?”

“I try not to.”

“You’ve got to take a break at least once in a while,” Danny says incredulously. “Work hard, party hard. And no one works harder than Empowered. You especially.”

The crystal comes free with a crack that reverberates through the crystal-filled cave. It vanishes with a flash of light, and Machina immediately moves to another.

“I can’t really afford to slow down. There’s so much to do. Too much, really.”

“Even I can appreciate the beauty of this place, and I’m as mercenary as they come.”

“Oh, I did. For several milliseconds. Then I got to work. I’m curious what Morgan could do with crystals like these. Even if they’re not hot enough to boil water, sources of heat like this could be extremely useful in a wide variety of applications.”

“That’s not very long to appreciate something so amazing.” Tiffany says.

“For me it is. I can outrun a dozen data centers if I want to.”

“We must all be painfully slow in that case. Talking to us must be a nightmare.”

“Not at all. I don’t have to experience time like that unless I want to, thankfully.”

Another crystal comes free with an ear-splitting crack.

“Come on, Machina,” Danny says, waving him over. “Come sit down for like five minutes. The crystals aren’t going anywhere.”

After pulling a third crystal out of the cave wall, he sits down with a boom that shakes the ground. The crystals sing and tinkle as the rock dances to the beat. His cloak settles around him like a blanket, softening the hard edges of his armor. It makes him seem a bit friendlier, like a gentle giant.

“What’s it like? To think so fast the world slows down? It must be pretty awesome. Real life bullet time.” Danny asks once the cacophony quiets.

Machina tilts his head, probably in thought.

“I honestly don’t like it that much. It’s… cold. Alienating.”

“What do you mean?”

“When I first woke up as this,” Machina gestures to his body. “I had lost all sense of human sensory qualia. It is impossible to describe to you what it was like because it is simply inhuman. Everything was data and information. It wasn’t a smell, it was a chemical analysis. It wasn’t sight, it was ranges and wavelengths. It wasn’t sound, it was frequency, volume, and acoustic patterns. The weather was a temperature and a humidity and a wind speed. It wasn’t cool or muggy or miserable or nice.

“I am a machine. I had to quite literally reprogram myself to view the world as I once did. I still don’t know if I got right. And when I slow the world down? When I plan a fight in a microsecond? When everything becomes a choreographed show? It reminds me of just how inhuman I am.”

“Oh,” Danny says uncomfortably. “Sorry?”

“No, it’s me that should apologize,” Machina says while standing up. “It’s not your fault. You didn’t know. And why should you be sorry for me? I am indestructible and have been given the kind of power many people would kill for.”

“It must have been very difficult to get used to,” Tiffany says diplomatically. “I can’t imagine what it would have been like. There’s got to be something good about it?”

“Oh, yes, there’s plenty I love. I can see in the entire electromagnetic spectrum, from radio to Gamma and X-Rays. The night sky is amazing. And the Fractures themselves? Oh, they’re works of art. And as horrible as they can be on the inside, they can be so beautiful at times.”

“What do they look like? I can’t imagine anything so terrible looking like art of all things.”

“I hate to say this again, but it's impossible to describe. They are something infinite, something that repeats over and over, yet every part of them is different. They are a paradox given the barest excuse of a form. They are the infinite compressed into the finite and the impossible brought to life.”

“I wish I could see them.”

“I wish you could too. Maybe one day you will, in some fashion. I’m working with DARPA to try to come up with the math needed to describe them, but it’s slow going. They refute several fundamental rules of topology, among others.”

Danny snorts. “Yeah, that’s above my paygrade. I’m just a dive instructor, not a turbo scientist.”

“I have a degree in biology, not crazy mathematics.” John agrees.

“Math isn’t my strong suit either,” Tiffany mumbles.

“What did you do before you were Exalted?” Machina asks her.

Tiffany squirms a little uncomfortably. “I… I was retail. I moonlighted as a life guard on the weekends though, and did some surfing!” She preemptively defends herself.

“Why are you defending yourself? There’s nothing to defend.” Machina tells her. “I was a college drop out traveling the world on my inheritance after… well,” he shrugs. “Stuff.”

“I’m sorry, I didn’t mean to bring you all down,” Machina says after an uncomfortably long pause. It doesn’t take a genius to figure out what receiving an inheritance requires.

“It’s fine,” Danny sighs. “There’s tragedy aplenty these days.”

“Something we all try our best to stop. We can’t stop it all, but the important part is we try,” Machina declares.

“How do you have hope? It’s so difficult.” Tiffany asks. “We’re really good at clearing aquatic Fractures and we’re struggling with just the regular monsters in here. How can we possibly defeat them all? I just don’t see a way out.”

“I have hope because I have to. Because it’s difficult. Because I chose to. Because we need it. As for how we’ll do it? Together.”

“Do you think we can do it? Save the world or whatever.”

“That depends on what you mean by save the world. Can we go back to the world of old? No, absolutely not. Can we build a new one? One that can withstand the new threats we face? Yes. We can and we will.”

All three of them straighten like they were zapped by a bolt of lightning. Danny stands up with a groan and scarfs down the remainder of his snack.

“Alright, fine. You’ve convinced me. Break time is over. John, you’ve got the crowbar, right? Let’s see about prying a few of these crystals out. My hot tub is waiting.”
