Princess Tiyana Matali—Supreme Commander of the allied human army, Saint to her people, and strongest human alive by no small margin—stood out in the thunder and the driving rain was clad head to toe in Artifacts more powerful than any human had ever wielded.

Her armor was a shining golden work of art retrieved from the deepest depths of Hiranya’s ancient vaults. Armor that not even the rain could touch, which no blade could cut and no magic short of S rank could leave a mark upon.

Underneath that armor, Tia wore no fewer than a dozen rings, bracelets, and necklaces. Every Artifact Imperator Andros had once worn now adorned her body, and then some.

Then there was the talwar that hung at her side. An Artifact worth all the others combined. A mythical blade with a glowing blue edge that hummed with unbridled power just begging to be unleashed. Excavated by her dear brother from some ancient Matali crypt, draining her country’s coffers just to give his sister one more advantage.

Princess Tiyana Matali, nigh immortal with all her vast prana reserves, S rank magic, and countless Artifacts, stood out in the rain… and awaited her end.

For fifty sleepless days, she had toiled to exploit every precious moment the Akh Nara had given them. In that time, she’d done what no leader in all of human history ever had, let alone a Matali leader.

Not only had she assembled the mightiest army humanity had ever mustered—over two hundred thousand strong—it was also the largest allied coalition to ever stand on one field. And the most motivated.

From the Altani Council’s Prime Mejai themselves to humanity’s foremost Talen wielders all the way down to the lowliest Kin’jali recruit, every soldier on the field had already resolved to lay down their lives. For the realm and for their families.

There wasn’t a soul on the battlefield who wasn’t aware of what life under demonic rule would look like. Not a soul among them who thought death was the worse outcome.

Which meant they would fight well beyond their limits. An unknowable advantage to their already overwhelming numbers.

She thought their chances were quite good, all told. Humanity learned from its mistakes, and the mistakes that turned their most powerful magic into their greatest disaster at Khantagati Pass would not be repeated.

Even with their Samsara, the demon army numbered less than a tenth their number. The Asuras had not, as she’d predicted, joined the initiative. Nor were either Vimana anywhere even in sight.

As for the immortals, they numbered a hundred and eight. If they were all that was left, Tia doubted very much they’d continue the war.

No, humanity had quite a solid chance, she thought. But at what cost?

For while humanity might have a chance in his absence, the cost paid in blood today would leave the realm scarred for two generations or more. Worse—it would cross a line that could never be walked back. It would pave the path for future wars in the blood of all those killed.

Of brothers buried and fathers slain, fanning the flames of hatred between the two peoples for a millennium to come.

If there was any peace to ever be had between their peoples, if there was any hatchet to be buried, the two armies could not fight here today.

A sentiment she had been sure the Akh Nara shared. Right up until he murdered his wife in cold blood. Now? It was why she stood out in the rain, gazing into the dark shadows lit by lightning. Now, all she could do was hope. Hope and pray.

That the Akh Nara would come. That he would heed her words and avoid a tragedy.

The shadows, it would seem, were listening.

For when the skies lit again and thunder reached her ears, the black robed form of a gray demon emerged from a shadow in front of the demon army a thousand paces away.

Suddenly, the loudest thing wasn’t the storm or the rain, but the cheers and battlecries of twenty thousand demons heralding the arrival of their god.

Heralding the end of this period of mourning, and the beginning of the end.

Several demons rushed forth. Tia tensed, and for a moment, she feared that the battle would begin without even giving her a chance to reason with their leader.

A single extended palm from the Akh Nara, however, halted even the most eager of demons in their tracks.

The Akh Nara met their gazes, his eyes alone forcing them back to their ranks.

Then, without even so much as addressing his army, he began walking. Towards Tia. Crossing the thousand paces that separated them.

Clutching the pommel of her sword like a lifeline, with her heart hammering in her chest, Tia walked out to meet him.

Every step toward the center of that open field felt like an eternity to Tia, and as she approached, her resolve was steadily worn away by what she saw.

For the Akh Nara wore not the nigh-impervious armor of Aspect of the Demon God, or any armor at all. He wielded no weapons, and he was clad in a simple black robe that even now was becoming soaked by the rain.

Has he not come to fight, then? Tia wondered, growing fearful. If the Akh Nara simply watched… if he intervened only when the armies had spent themselves and humanity was vulnerable…

No. Tia shook her head, nipping that delusion in the bud. It did not matter if the Akh Nara acted first, last, or anywhere in between. If he took to the field at all, her army was doomed. The future was doomed.

Vir alone was greater than the strength of the human and demon armies combined and multiplied tenfold, and while she doubted even now that he would attack humans directly, he hardly needed to.

His mere presence on the battlefield was enough to rile up demonkind, whose cheers and battlecries upon the arrival of their god could be heard even above the roar of the storm.

It was a good thing, then, that Tia didn’t need to win. She merely needed to get the living god to agree to a duel.

“Hear me, Vir!” she shouted above the din of the wind and the rain, more for her people than for him. Even a whisper would be picked up with hearing as prana-enhanced as his.

Her soldiers were not graced with that particular boon, and so voice transmission and amplification orbs had been installed throughout the ranks of her army. Thousands of them, all relaying her words so that her people could hear.

And they did need to hear. If her plan were to have any hope of working, she needed her army to accept the outcome. Only then would demonkind reciprocate.

“The armies of humanity and demonkind have assembled this night expecting a battle,” she announced. “One that will, if fought, go down in the victor’s history books as an era defining battle.”

Vir simply stared. She’d hoped for some sign of guilt or empathy with her plight, but he betrayed no emotion at all, as cold as a hard rock face.

No, not quite… It was almost as if he had the look of a man who’d made up his mind about something. Who’d resolved himself to his fate.

Just like me…

Tia shook off the feeling. No one who’d survived a decade in the Ash would be so easily rattled. She pressed on.

“It will be a glorious battle sung by the bards, but we both know just how high the mountain of corpses will stand by the time this battle is done. On both our sides. The Akh Nara I knew would not have wanted that.”

Still no response. Not even a twitch in his gaze.

“Tell me!” she bellowed. “Is this what you want? The deaths of a hundred thousand souls on your hands? I think not. I’d like to think that the one I have come to respect is still in there, despite…”

Despite your coldblooded murder of your own wife, Tia didn’t say.

“So fight me,” she declared. “Commander to commander. Duel me here and now, to the death, and let this dark page in our peoples’ histories be ended by its conclusion. What say you!?”

Vir—no the Akh Nara—stared at Tia in silence, and for a moment, she thought he might stay that way, ignoring her words to the end.

Demonkind’s deity did finally respond, just not with words, and certainly without something as mundane as voice amplification crystals to make himself be heard.

No, Vir’s voice echoed in Tia’s mind, and by the reactions of the soldiers behind her and the demonic warriors across the field, she knew she was not alone. That somehow, his thoughts were being sent to every soldier gathered there. Human and demon alike.

Just two words. Two words, spoken with the sort of finality only a deity could muster, which would seal Tia’s fate and set the course of the future.

“Demonkind accepts.”
