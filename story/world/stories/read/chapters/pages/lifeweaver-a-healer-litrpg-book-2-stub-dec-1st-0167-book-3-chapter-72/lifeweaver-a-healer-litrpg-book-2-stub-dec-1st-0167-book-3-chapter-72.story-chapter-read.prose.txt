Alan's guild, Integrated Solutions Group, was a traitorous bunch of assholes even after their guildmaster died. Either that, or they didn't know, which Luke found an unlikely proposition. What a mess. Rather than anger bubbling to the surface, he found himself going cold as he pondered what to do about the guild's treachery. First things first, he needed to get out of this dungeon and inform the others. If one of the largest guilds in the US was working with the enemy, everyone needed to know.

"What's wrong?" Leom asked, standing right behind him and trying to peer out of the crack in the door.

"Nothing," he grumbled, not wanting to alienate the female Singer who seemed so happy to give up information when he asked for it. Perhaps he might even extract a little more. "What about the portals? How did you open those?"

"Oh, that's easy. Custodian did that. It took some doing, I heard, but we talked her into it in the end. The joining is coming, after all."

"Custodian? Joining?"

The quizzical look on his face must've tipped her off that maybe, just maybe, she shouldn't answer every question asked by one of the people her kind were invading. Even so, she kept going as if she couldn't stop herself. This time, though, he felt she held back some.

"The one who brought the system to us. An... ancestor of ours. An ancient Singer. She's helped us through the transition. As to the joining... It's where different systems... meet."

He nodded, doubting he’d get more out of her if he pushed. "Fair enough. Ready?"

She nodded.

Luke didn't trust Leom. That much went without saying, but he also wasn't sure what to make of her. A Lifeweaver, true, but she saw herself as a healer and nothing else. The weakness with which she attempted to fend him off confirmed her story, but he wondered if that was all she saw herself as. Was this person the Singer version of Dot? For the moment, it sure seemed like it. Speaking of Dot, it was time to go get her.

Stepping into the corridor, Luke leaned back to avoid being rammed by a gurney racing from right to left, a couple of human-looking monsters pushing it in front of them. The person in the bed was an actual human, he realized, and Luke called out.

"Stop!"

They didn't stop.

Following them at a sprint, it didn't take him long to catch up and dispatch the rude monsters. Two monster cores richer, he leaned over and examined the patient. He was Integrated with the narrow mana channels of someone who didn't use much mana. Some sort of fighter, then. By the state of him, the last fight hadn't gone so well. A long vertical slash from his left shoulder all the way down to his hip oozed blood. Judging by the ashen face and slowed-down vitals, this one didn't have long.

By the time Leom caught up, Luke had finished healing the Integrated. A glow rose and surrounded the patient, and a second later, he disintegrated into the same motes of light that took the dead Integrated. Except this one wasn't dead.

"What the hell?" Luke breathed, taking a step back.

Leom stepped past one of many dead human-looking monsters on the floor, leaning over the now-empty bed. "What happened? Didn't you heal him?"

"I did," Luke said. He looked over at her, seeing her confused expression. "That hasn't happened to you before, either?"

"No. Never."

He was about to get moving again when he stopped and drew in a breath instead, steadying himself. Finding the weave of this strange place took some doing, but Luke managed it. Searching for echoes proved futile, though, since sensing the weave of The Greater System was out of reach without some serious effort, and Luke didn't feel safe draining himself of mana.

"Can't find him."

"What?" Leom asked.

He glanced over at her, then shook his head. "Never mind. Let's go find my friend."

"What do you mean?"

"Another Lifeweaver. She's here."

It was her turn to give him a searching look. "How do you know?"

Luke saw movement at the end of the corridor and started toward it with a shrug. "I just do."

As they walked toward what turned out to be more human-monsters, he decided to probe Leom with another question. "Do you know any other Singer Lifeweavers?"

"One," she confirmed. "He was assigned the class at the same time as I, but with the ineffective healing and range issues, he was granted a special dispensation to attempt turning it into a more fighting-oriented role. Haven't seen him since. From what I understand, there are a few others being trained after an event in a dungeon, but I don't know any specifics."

She stopped, eyes widening. "Maybe you know something about that? I've heard rumors saying people from your Earth were involved somehow."

Luke cleared his throat. "Nope. I don't know anything about that."

It was clear she didn't believe him, but Luke ignored her accusatory glare as he approached a new set of monster-humans.

A young-looking woman with disheveled blond hair grabbed his arm and pulled him toward a room. "Doctor Quinn! Good, we need you!"

Indulging the scenario a little, Luke asked her, "What has happened here? Where are all these patients coming from?"

She gave him a sharp look, then frowned. "How are you feeling, Luke?"

"I'm fine," he said, giving the absolute stranger, a monster at that, his best winning smile.

"If you need a break, you have to tell us. No one will blame you after what happened."

"What happened?"

The not-nurse looked over to one of her not-colleagues and shook her head. "Maybe we need to notify Dr. Saints?"

"Just tell me what's going on," Luke barked as they went through a set of double doors and into an OR. Interestingly enough, they bypassed the whole process of cleaning and changing into gowns for whatever mock-operation they wanted him for.

"You're scaring me now, Luke," the nurse said, her voice straining to keep calm. "There's a war happening. You know that."

Luke looked over his shoulder at Leom, then turned back to see the patient in the bed waiting for him.

"Ray?" Luke asked, unable to believe what his eyes were telling him. He hadn't sensed his friend's echo before, but now he did. "What happened?"

Ray gave no answer, of course, since he was unconscious. Also cut in half, so that wasn't so great. A vertical cut around the height of Ray's belly button had sheared him right in two. At least both pieces were present. The bed was stained red with massive amounts of blood, and if the previous patient had been near death, Ray was a breath away.

Weaver's Mercy kept that breath where it belonged and bought Luke time to get started. Since his friend's legs were right there, it was better to give reattaching them a go.

"What are you doing?" the nurse asked as Luke grabbed the severed lower half of Ray and placed it in the right position. Rather than answering, he got started, bringing out four threads of mana. This would take a lot of stitches and a heap of mana, no matter how careful he was. With Ray's life hanging in the balance, there was no room for mistakes.

"We'd better call Dr. Saints in here," the nurse murmured before disappearing through the doors. Five nurses still remained, but Luke ignored them as he worked, ignored them until that turned out to have been a bad idea.

Lancing pain shot up his side, bringing him out of his concentrated effort to heal Ray, and he looked down to see a male nurse-monster having shoved something sharp straight into him. It hurt. A lot.

"What the hell?" he blurted, a little shocked that he hadn't seen it coming.

That's when Leom cried out in pain. Two nurses were on the Lifeweaver Singer, slashing at her with scalpels. Ray's healing was still at a stage where he wouldn't survive if Luke diverted much of his attention. A skill he picked up a long while ago but hadn't had the chance to use yet came to the rescue.

Lifeweaver's Sacrifice [Toggleable - Mana Cost: Low]: Absorb damage from anyone touched by your threads.

Bracing himself even as he cut the throat of the monster-nurse stabbing him, Luke was still not ready for his face being sliced open when a scalpel cut Leom. A thread connected them, and that was enough for the skill to work. It took almost no mana, either, but the downside was pretty clear. It hurt. It hurt a lot.

"Kill them!" Luke growled as he turned back to Ray, resuming the healing. As if healing his friend wasn't grueling enough, he now needed to heal himself at the same time, while sustaining even more injuries. What a pain in the ass.

Grunting from a scalpel slicing open his arm, and another being thrust into his throat, Luke steeled himself and finished healing Ray. As the motes of light came for the now-restored patient, Luke slapped his cheek. Hard. This was too good an opportunity to pass up, and he doubted Ray's appearance was pure coincidence.

"Ray! Hey, Ray! Wake up!"

Ray's eyes fluttered open, then were squeezed shut before he opened them again. "Whu?"

"Listen to me!" Luke shouted, having just repaired his own throat after one of the monster nurses sliced through Leom's. "ISG is working with the Singers!"

Ray's eyes widened just as the motes of light came for him. Luke wasn't sure if he'd heard or not, or if he was going back to Earth, or if that had even been the real Ray. This dungeon was like a nightmare.

Now, it was time to find Dot, figure out what the hell was going on, then wake up.
