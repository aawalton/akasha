Sitting on the porch, he opened the rewards one after another. The standard rarity one—the one that didn’t specify “Rare”—dropped a package of AAA batteries. For a second, his heart leaped, but then he saw the third A, and it dropped again. No good; they wouldn’t fit in the Magical Wand. He still put them away for later, in case he stumbled upon another battery-powered weapon… or ran into an electrical engineer or electrician willing to jury-rig them into his Magical Wand.

The ordinary reward was whatever. It wasn’t important. The Rare reward was the one that had the chance of being something worthwhile. He pulled it out of his inventory. Bright light glowed, then faded, revealing a small star-shaped trinket. Eric frowned, picking it up. He turned it over in his hand. It was a little hunk of plastic on a chain, the large, chunky plastic star shaded a kind of powder blue with sparkles in it. He didn’t like it, but he had to admit it: it matched the Magical Wand in a lot of ways.

Star Sparkle Bracelet

Be a pretty pretty princess with this charm! Grants +5 MAG and +10% mana

Well… sure. He slid it on without hesitation. Whatever they were trying to do with this weird loot, they could go on and keep doing it as long as it granted good bonuses. He’d prefer if it was a little more masculine, but at the end of the day, what mattered were the numbers, not the way it looked. It was honestly a little funny that the drops seemed to be trying to humiliate him when he was literally the only person on the planet. Who was he supposed to be embarrassed around? Besides, it fit neatly under the sleeve of his jacket. Ninety-nine percent of the time, it’d go unseen anyways. I guess if I was a dude bro, it would be mortifying? Never really got into that, so I don’t mind.

Still wet and disgusting, he slogged upstairs to the nearest shower and cleaned himself off, then wandered into the teen’s room to claim a new set of dry clothes. With that done, he returned to the porch. He spent most of the afternoon practicing Explosion, testing its upper and lower limits. If he used just a tiny amount of mana, it barely did more than a pop; if he gave it Firebolt amounts of mana, it burst, not quite as big as one of the little gnomes, but enough to give anything pause and break open wooden boxes and the like. When he ran out of mana, he stood on the porch and stared out at that thick golden mist. Last time he ran out of mana, he rested until it returned. But was there a faster way?

“Cy,” he said.

“Yes?” Cy asked. He’d washed himself in the sink and hopped out on the porch with Eric, then flopped down beside him to dry off and watch him practice Explosion. Eric had offered to dry him with an Explosion, but Cy had wisely turned him down.

“Is there a way to gather mana actively, rather than just taking a break or drinking a mana potion?”

“Is there a way! Is there a way. My primary school teacher would weep to hear you ask that question. What a failure of the education system. What a tragedy! Not knowing how to gather mana. Honestly.”

Eric waited out his tirade, crossing his arms in annoyance. “So there’s a way?”

Cy harrumphed. He lifted himself up and gave himself an adorable little shake, then hopped over, totally undercutting his pompous attitude. Eric lifted his hand to hide a smile, annoyance forgotten.

Unaware of how cute he was being, Cy sat down stiffly like a strict governess in a period drama, his back as straight as a rabbit’s back could be. “That’s right. There is a way. A way even the infants of my world know. First, sit down. Cross your legs if you like… but it’s more important to be comfortable.”

Eric complied. He sat down against the wall, using the wall to support his back, and crossed his legs.

Cy hopped over and sat stiffly again. “Now rest your hands in a comfortable position, close your eyes, and breathe. Don’t sleep, but clear your mind. Breathe evenly. Feel the flow of energy in the world.”

Eric peeked an eye open. “You want me to meditate?”

“Oh, this primitive world knows what meditation is? I assumed you didn’t know, since you don’t have mana. Yes: meditate.”

Ignoring the casual insult, Eric closed his eyes and breathed evenly the way Cy asked him to. It was harder to clear his mind, but he did his best. Without his Mana Sight, it was harder to sense mana around him, but he tried anyways. He felt… nothing. A grand total of nothing at all. He was sitting there like an idiot, accomplishing nothing but taking a load off. Resting was restoring his mana, but he wasn’t restoring it any faster than that.

A tiny fluffy paw rested on his leg as Cy called his attention. “Once you can feel the mana flowing through you, call out to it. Draw it into you. It isn’t much faster than simply resting at first, but as you get used to the technique, you should be able to do it more easily. Eventually, you’ll be able to do it without meditating, then subconsciously, and you’ll only need to meditate when you really need to draw in mana rapidly.”

Eric nodded. He sat there, calling out to the mana. Nothing happened. Nothing continued to happen. He sat there and thought about nothing and almost fell asleep, but that was all; no more mana flowed into him.

Man, this sucks, Eric thought. Why were his eyes closed, anyways? He could see mana. Wasn’t it disadvantageous to close his eyes?

It’s not working. Why not give it a shot with my eyes open? Eric mentally shrugged and opened his eyes.

Mana flowed all around him, right there, swirling in the fog and densely packed in the safe zone around him. He trained his eyes down as hard as he could, then breathed in and breathed out. Mana flowed in as he inhaled, and flowed out as he exhaled, passing through him without being trapped at all.

Oh. I’m breathing mana in all the time, I just can’t feel it, and I’m not capturing it. How do I capture it? He breathed in again and reached out, trying to sense the mana inside him with all his heart. Faintly, he felt a heat or warmth, but he wasn’t sure if he was imagining it or not. For the rest of the time, he focused on that heat, trying to trap it inside him or store it somewhere in his body. It felt like he gathered mana a little faster than before, but with his recently expanded mana pool, he couldn’t be sure. It could have just been placebo effect.

He unfolded his legs and stood. I’ll have to work on that. As a mage, mana was the most important resource for him. He desperately needed a way to refill it without just sitting and waiting or relying on mana potion drops. Mana potions were rare enough right now that he didn’t want to waste them willy-nilly. If that changed, he could drop meditation on the floor, but assuming that wouldn’t change, he definitely wanted to get a better handle on refilling his mana. If he could eventually do it on the fly the way Cy had indicated, that would be invaluable.

Cy sat primly beside him. As he stood, the little rabbit opened his eyes and gave himself a shake. “Not bad, for a first try. You have room to grow, for certain. Keep practicing.”

“Of course,” Eric said. He went to start working on Explosion again, then paused. “That aura thing you did in the dungeon… could you teach me that, Cy?”

“Mmm…” Cy squinted, propping his chin on a paw. “I’m not sure you’re at that level yet. I could try…”

“Give me something to work with. I’m not going to master it today, no, but if you let me get started on it, I can work toward it.”

“An admirable attitude.” Cy hopped over and stood on his hind legs, extending his forepaws in the universal symbol of “uppies.” Eric picked him up and set him on his shoulder. “The traditional way to teach a young mage to feel their aura is for their teacher to brush their aura, to introduce them to the way aura feels. If I were at my full strength, I could touch your aura from the other side of this valley. Unfortunately, I’m reduced to such a pathetic state that I need to be in physical contact with you to reach your aura.”

“That sucks, man.”

“Indeed. Ah, to tell the truth, I even had to hijack your aura earlier to protect you from that gnome… It was fortunate that I was your familiar and you were untrained in aural arts, or else I might not have been able to do that at all. Close your eyes once more… or do not, I suppose. You seem to do better when you look at things.”

Eric nodded. He craned his neck to look at Cy.

“Here… hold me in your hands. That should make it easier on you, no?”

Eric held his hands up to Cy, and Cy hopped into them. He held the rabbit out in front of him instead, waiting.

Cy closed his eyes. Mana stirred inside his body, and a faint sheen of gold shone around his entire body, maybe a quarter inch extended beyond his fur. “There. Do you see that?”

“I do,” Eric confirmed.

“Excellent. Now I’m going to touch your aura.” Cy furrowed his brows. The gold light around his body shifted, then pushed, extending toward Eric. It touched his arm, and warmth blossomed there, as if someone with particularly warm hands had just touched him.

“Huh,” Eric said. He pushed his mana around his body, trying to mimic what Cy had done while holding that sensation in his mind. From the warm spot on his arm, the warmth extended over his entire body, and thin gold light shone on his skin—but only for a heartbeat. He released the breath he’d been subconsciously holding, and his concentration snapped. The gold light vanished back into his body.

Cy opened his eyes and nodded. “Not bad, not bad. You manifested your aura on your first try; that’s not bad at all. Practice manifesting it until you can manifest it for minutes at a time, and when you have that much control, come back to me. I’ll teach you the next step toward hardening your aura and protecting yourself with it.”

“Got it. Thank you, Cy.” Eric set him back on the ground.

Cy hopped one step away, then lifted his head and preened a little, clearly pleased. “It was nothing, nothing at all.”

Eric remained on the porch until nightfall, practicing all his new techniques: Explosion, mana recovery, and aura control. When night fell, he retreated into the house, had himself a fantastic warm dinner, then retired to bed. He could have stayed up later, but since his current hypothesis was that the fog started at noon, he wanted to get out ahead of it. If he could tackle his first dungeon at dawn, he might be able to manage a second before the fog set in at noon. That, and it would go a long way to confirm whether the fog was triggered by time, by him clearing dungeons, or by something totally unrelated. He didn’t need to know that, he supposed, but he wanted to know that, and that was almost as important.

He didn’t appear in tonight’s episode, either. He wasn’t bitter about that at all.
