Chapter 209 / B3-54: Therefore We Are

Yuk was clearly having a rough day. When Joe opened the door to make it easier for the swarm to gain entry, the whole skittering mass was significantly smaller than what Joe had been expecting.

“You okay, bud? You look like you’re missing half of your critters.”

‘Every time we built ourselves back up, whoosh, another flood surge washed a chunk of us away again. We gave up on [Brood Cycling] and decided to either wait out the storm here or at the guildhouse. We’ll rebuild ourselves after the rains let up.’

Joe initiated a party and checked his friend’s levels. Yuk’s resources were full, though their maximum health, stamina, and mana were about half normal because each depended on the swarm’s size. Joe realized that if Yuk ever grew as big as Khimbar, the primordial horror that created them, those maximums could reach tens of thousands.

‘Whoa! That’s crazy,’ Joe thought to himself privately, as Yuk began to speak telepathically.

‘Now we’re torn about what our epic reward should be again. Puqmup went through the list of things that we could ask for, and we were going to ask for [Saint Rooker’s Funeral Bell]. It’s one of the very few physical items we can use because it’s impalpable … means it works without physical contact,’ the tatterdemalion clarified. ‘But now we’re thinking we may go back to our first idea and ask for a residence in Fort Coral. We’re tired of getting washed out of basements and drains,’ the swarm sighed telepathically. ‘There is a granary in Rockpoint, not far from here, that has a caretaker’s quarters nobody lives in. It’s been warded up tight ever since we got here. Puq was pretty sure that the city would give us residency there.’

“Well, before you give up on the bell, I have a present for you … for us really. Come take a look,” Joe blurted eagerly. He used a paw to grab the long map case that held Myllo’s initial sketch of the Turtleback and bring it across the room to them.

As he unfurled the plans, Joe realized he wasn’t sure if he was more excited about showing off his brainstorm or what this idea would mean for his friend. Having lived through Glen Cumha together, bound mind to mind, Joe had experienced the utter depth of loneliness that was Yuk's existence. There was very little Joe wouldn’t do to lift that isolation off the exceptional outcast who had quickly become so important to him.

Joe had so many more connections now than he did when he died on Earth. He was falling for Kenda. Mo was his boon companion. Har’hoo was like a big sister he had never had. Wen and Tez were great friends. Yuk was something more personal.

When Jan and Keith had died, part of Joe was lost. Being their big brother was a fundamental part of who Joe was. In those last years without them, an ache had weighed on him every day, drawing him into depression. He had only managed to understand that malaise with the help of a hospital therapist years later.

That hollowness had persisted until now. Being with Yuk filled the void. Joe felt the same protective love for his fragmented friend as for his little brothers. He would stand in front of a charging bear for any of them.

“This is what I requested as my epic reward,” Joe exclaimed. “It’s a houseboat, and the top floor is all yours.”

‘Are you kidding us! That’s an amazing idea. Wish we had thought of this. So much better than some old caretaker's apartment. We’d be roommates?’

“For sure! Granted, with me, you also get Mojo, the spirit pack, and Finn. Myllo and I even worked out a couple of extra rooms for guests,” Joe added. “I’m sure Mahq and some of the rest of Howling Voice will stay over from time to time. This is likely to be one noisy boat. You okay with that?”

Through their telepathic connection, Joe could feel Yuk was drowning in joy, so much so that Joe felt sympathetic tears prick at the corners of his eyes. The idea of being surrounded by others was not scaring Yuk this time. The oddball may come to rue the chaos of a full house someday, but in this moment, all Yuk saw was that they would belong somewhere.

They’d have a family.

‘WE’RE IN! This is so cool. We’ll have to lay off the centipedes, but there are plenty of flies, water-skitters, and such that we can absorb to get on and off the boat. Does it have a name yet?’

“I was thinking the Turtleback, since our hull will be made out of the shell of a turtle dragon. What ya think?”

The long pause told Joe enough. There wasn’t a negative feel coming through the [Parasitic Bond], but the name did not foster much excitement, either.

‘It’s ok. It’s pretty good, but we could do better.’

“Like what?”

‘Um … Turtlebark, cause of the tree … nah … um … Fort De Tort,’ Yuk mused; the name made Joe chuckle. ‘Shellboat? Ooh. I like shell ... THE SHELLADON! Da da dun!’

“Ok, you’re being ridiculous, Bud. No Shelladon, Shellasaurus, Shellinator type monikers.”

‘Just winging names here, Joe. You’ve had way more time to think about this than we have.’

“Fair enough. Continue spitballing.”

‘We’d ask what spitballs have to do with this, but we’re going to assume it’s an Earth expression.’

The mass of insects shifted from side to side, reminding Joe of a boxer loosening up before a fight. ‘The …. Shellowship … you know cause we’d all be friends together.’

“Nooooo,” Joe drawled, shaking his head. “Hard veto. I like puns, but that’s bad even for me.”

‘Hold on. We’ll get a good one. Give us a sec. The Shellreaver. Nah. Too piratey.’

“How about the Shellraiser?” Joe suggested.

‘What would we be raising? Shug pups?’

“Yeah. That doesn’t translate well unless you know the movie.”

‘We could do Shellden, cause, you know, all the dog-like critters you seem to attract.’

“Reminds me of a whiny know-it-all,” Joe stated, shrugging his shoulders. “Even so, it’s not bad.”

‘Then how about the Shellter? With two L's. A fortified place. Somewhere safe.’

“That’s better,” Joe admitted, rolling the name around in his head. “You know what, I really like that one. Top of the list so far.”

They bantered names back and forth for a while. After close to half an hour, no suggestion beat the Shellter. Joe grabbed a pen and scribbled the ship’s new name on the parchment.

“You know what? I think I’m going to get this framed,” Joe stated, looking at the concept design for their new home.

‘Ooh, ooh, ooh!’ Yuk hooted telepathically. ‘Forget the bell, we know what we want for our reward. The Shellter is going to need some offense if we are going to be floating around in the bay with sea lions, shallows squids, and reef rays. We’re gonna trade in the epic reward to have Myllo make us one of his big-ass cannons! Shelly’s gonna get some teeth.’

“You sure? It’s an epic item. Isn’t there something better you need?” Joe questioned.

‘Completely sure. The bell was the best item we could find, and even then, it was just an okay fit for us. We’ll have to ask Myllo to make the cannon one that we can operate too. We never get to do stuff like that; you know, the whole lack of hands … and real body thing.’

“Sounds like a plan then. After the rain lets up, we can go talk to Myllo about a timeline for a cannon and see if we need to revise the plans to make a spot for it. Pretty sure the shipyard was going to start framing the decks next. They had just finished weaving the tree roots into the shell, last time I checked.”

‘Can’t wait to go and see it.’

They discussed the rain-delayed plans until the conversation shifted to their last few days. Joe skipped his curse-breaking blues and improving ward work. The grumblebees made a far better story.

“You probably just could have absorbed the whole lot of them and removed the infestation in one fell swoop,” Joe speculated, as he cut up an oshuu for Mahq and Mojo.

These deep-blue fruits were quickly becoming Joe’s favorite, tasting like mango mixed with berries. Their only downside was the dark-blue juice, which stained tongues and lips like blue raspberry candy from Earth or, in Mojo’s case, entire hands and face.

‘Probably not. Hive queens and hive cores make it tricky for us to assimilate the workers. It is harder for us to absorb a common ant than a dire beetle. We could have broken the queen’s dominion eventually, but it probably would have taken us longer than it took you,’  Yuk telepathically replied. ‘Why didn’t you just go full werewolf? Wouldn’t the higher resistances have been better, especially with your face exposed?’

“It probably would’ve been, but the weird thing is I don't actually control the [Skinwalker] skill. You know that feral feeling you get sometimes through our [Parasitic Bond]?”

‘Yeah. What about it?’

“For some reason, that is not me. Well, not fully me. It's a sub-pattern. I call it the wildness. Why I have a sub-pattern is a mystery to me, but the wildness controls some of my skills, like my claws and [Skinwalker].”

“That’s cause your spirit doesn’t understand mirrors, Joe,” a small voice chimed in from across the room. Joe turned to Mahq, who didn’t seem to be paying any attention to Joe. The boy and Mojo were playing some made-up game with a bag of colorful buttons that Joe had bought the shug monkey a few days ago.

‘What the crick does that mean?’ Yuk asked.

‘No idea, Bud.’  Joe switched to his spoken voice for clarification. “OK, Mahq. You lost me on that one. What are you trying to say?”

“When you look at yourself, you do not accept all the bits that make up you. Instead of mirrors, you see windows; windows with someone else on the other side of the glass,” Mahq explained, never looking up from what he and Mojo were doing. “You have your head spirit that you think of as you. Your gut spirit should have been you, too, but you made it into a wild spirit inside you. You could have fused the spirit of the death mark into yourself, but instead, you made it a passenger. You have a habit of taking parts of you and labeling them as not you. That is why your spirit is all split up.”

Joe’s hands faltered to a halt. He stood at the kitchen counter, the knife in one hand while the blue juices stained the amber paw he was using to hold the piece of fruit. In his head, he was miles away. Or years away.

When Joe was young, he often held full conversations with himself, far beyond ordinary self-talk. According to his parents, his tone and mannerisms changed between the different sides of the conversation. It was odd enough that they took him to a therapist, Dr. Pearlson. After a few weeks, Doctor P explained that Joe naturally compartmentalized his feelings. His vivid imagination then personalized those different viewpoints. Dr. Pearlson assured his parents that Joe would likely grow out of it.

Yet, he never really did. Joe always felt like he had a chorus of thoughts in his head. Sometimes this was great, allowing Joe to pick from multiple options. Other times, it was distracting as hell. This was the main reason Joe could so easily get lost in his own thoughts.

When playing D&D and other tabletop RPGs, it was a huge asset. Joe donned the personalities of his characters much like a method actor, not just wearing someone else's affectations but almost becoming that character. When he played djinni-kin Caliban, the world was full of wonder and danger, leaving Joe's heart racing as he vacillated between curiosity and dread. He was stoic with Capaluchu, not just acting stoic, but Joe himself became so spartan in his feelings that sometimes he had to actively shake off the ent-like Capaluchu persona to join in on the side jokes.

Joe thought back to the last years of his life on Earth and realized that this tendency was present there, too. His cancer had always been something trying to kill him. While deep down he knew the illness was a part of himself, in his gut, it was something else. Something alien. It was an enemy that had already taken his family away. Something he had to fight.

Ever since those sessions with Dr. P, Joe had been aware of this quirk of his personality, but having it manifest into actual changes to his pattern here on Illuminaria was a bit of a shock. Joe thought the wildness was a remnant of Sougath, which he had never been thrilled about. Yet, as he sent his feelings to explore that feral part, Joe realized the vile Night Skinner had nothing to do with the feral id. The wildness originated from Joe. It was part of him, yet not completely so. Somehow, Joe had created a spirit that was both him and not him at the same time.

“Um. So what do I do about this?” Joe asked the young spirit savant.

“Why do you need to do anything about it?” the boy countered, flicking away a button, much to Mojo’s chagrin. “Having more than one spirit is weird, but it’s you, Joe. You are what you are.”

With that, Mahq's attention was gone again, refocused on his game, where Mojo was making checker-like jumps with a yellow button across the grid they had drawn in chalk on the floorboards.

‘So the consensus is … you’re weird. Welcome to the club,’ the swarming collective stated smugly.

Joe looked around his apartment, taking note of those present: his skittering best friend, the changeling monkey-pup, the baffling young druid, and the pack of spectral fey hounds. While he was still reeling a bit from this revelation, it was nice to know that if oddity was going to be his new norm, at least he was in good company.
