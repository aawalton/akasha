Thank God the Thaumaturgy ritual outright empowered objects, or else I would be lost as Hell. I had left the chalked circle behind in my search, but had taken the splinter along with me, safely enclosed inside of an emptied-out compass. If this lead turned out to be false, I’d just return to that rooftop and fire up the circle once more, reorienting the splinter towards another target.

But I don’t think I would need to. I was…a little shocked at just where the splinter was leading me, as it slowly oriented in a particular direction. I shouldn’t be, though. This was a perfect hiding spot, and I think I understood just why the Rangers had missed our Elven saboteurs, up to now. This particular area of Rhoscara was extra deserted, even in comparison to the rest of the city. And it wasn’t because of the martial law in effect.

It was because of the dead.

I stood before a large set of intricately decorated, black metal gates and contemplated them. There wasn’t anyone around for quite some distance, according to my blood sense, and so I wasn’t scared of being spotted. Still, there could be hidden spotters out of my range, so I made sure I was still under my invisibility as I considered my destination. I was…reluctant to go inside, even though I was still determined to see my path through. It’s just that every single time I seemed to enter a place like this, I ended up in a fight for my life. And yeah, I was here to fight in the first place, but…

I was so goddamned tired of the undead.

These were the gates…to the Rhsocaran cemeteries.

The various peoples of Vereden had equally varied ways in which they cared for their dead. Humans, to my sensibilities, had the most logical solution to handling the dearly departed. In a world where it was possible for the dead to reanimate and become hostile to life, I thought it was entirely rational to burn them as we did. Why risk grandma rising from the grave to take a nibble on your carotid artery when you visited her grave, when you could just keep her ashes in an urn instead?

Practical. Sane.

Unluckily for me, the rest of Vereden wasn’t sane. I could forgive the Orcs, I suppose. They were from another planet altogether. As I’d already experienced, they dug spiralling, maze-like catacombs where their ancestors were laid out on slabs to slowly dessicate. Something to do with their obsession with blood and death Magic, to my understanding.

From an off-hand comment I’d heard from Renauld before, the Gnolls apparently left their dead in the middle of nature, for their bodies to be reclaimed. Just…laid out in a nice, pretty-looking patch of the woods. A bit disrespectful, to me, but whatever. Those wouldn’t be at risk of rising from the dead. Not when they were probably gobbled up by a hungry monster the minute the grieving family clattered away in the Throng.

The dead still possessed a form of Aether, after all, and monsters were always hungry for it.

But the Dwarves…

The crazy bastards just had cemeteries like I would have found back on Earth. Huge plots of land within the walls of their cities, where the dead were laid to rest within the soil. Only…they didn’t bother with coffins.

They were just shoved into a hole in the ground and had dirt shoveled over them. Something about giving back to Vereden itself, in a way similar to the Gnolls.

What a hazard that was. It was apparently another tradition, as expected of the Dwarves, from the Age of the Gods. It was how Yorgun liked things, and even after the schism from the mountain holds, the Velancians hadn’t changed that. They weren’t total fools about it, of course. Each burial plot was enchanted with a mild Spell of decay, to help speed things along and prevent the goddamned zombies. With a year or two, a buried Dwarf would be totally absorbed by the soil, apparently enriching it quite a bit. It was…apparently common to then take that soil and sell it to farms, for the use of plantations that already used slave labor.

That had to be some weird karma.

As a result of this bizarre practice, Dwarven cemeteries were a bit different. There weren’t individual grave stones for families to visit, and they weren’t even typically allowed into the plot itself. Rather, there was a large, carefully maintained board posted on the outside of it that listed all of the residents within who were currently…decomposing. This was where families would usually gather to pay their respects, until their loved ones were fully absorbed by the earth.

The only thing actually visible on the grounds of the cemetery…

Was a small stone building, barely larger than a hut. That was little more than an entrance, though. It apparently led underground to where the traditional Dwarven occupation of ‘gravemender’ was practiced. In other words?

The half Magi, half groundskeeper position. Respected by the populace…but shunned, nonetheless.

The door was hanging wide open. I could see it from here, the wooden surface of it banging in the wind against the stone of the entrance.

I wondered…had anyone checked up on this particular gravemender, since the start of the Skyfall? If I asked around, would any of the scared residents of Rhoscara mention having seen him go to the markets?

I suspect that the answer, if they even bothered to speak to a total stranger like me, would be no.

I cracked my neck, crouched, and leapt over the gate in front of me, sailing into the air high enough to clear it. I touched down on the rich green grass on the other side, keeping my balance perfectly as I did. Remaining motionless, I strained both my physical and my mystical senses for a moment, checking to see if my movement had either disturbed something…

Or alerted them.

After a few seconds of stillness, I concluded I hadn’t. Maybe I was stereotyping these Elven raiders too much…

I was starting to suspect they might be a bit sloppy. Capable when it came to a raid, maybe. But when it came to covering their tracks in an urban environment? The wide open door to the underground quarters told me they were…less than capable. It might have only been a matter of time before the Rangers would have found them, especially with my soldiers helping.

That just told me I should move faster. I didn't want my kills stolen.

I kept an eye out for potential watchers as I crept across the lush green grass of the cemetery. Even beyond the…unusual vitality I could see in the leafage, something about it struck me as strange, but I couldn’t quite lay my finger on what. I put it out of my mind, though, focusing instead on my internal Mana stores. Just as internally, I grimaced at what I found. I wasn’t going to be able to keep the enhanced version of Thorn Cloak running for much longer if I wanted any juice in the proverbial tank when it came to the actual fighting. The outright invisibility offered when I powered up the stealth Skill was very Mana hungry, and I’d already abused it plenty today.

Time to dial it down. I mentally removed Starfire Veiling and let the mote of the Skill drift away from Thorn Cloak. Instantly, the black cloak I wore sprouted the crimson thorns of my primary Terrestrial affinity, and I faded…somewhat back into the visible range. This would have to do.

Besides. I think the unenhanced version of the Skill offered a marginally better amount of physical protection, anyway, if it came to an outright fight.

And…

It looked like I might just need it.

I froze only a few feet away from the banging door of the underground entrance. My blood sense had abruptly started registering something. Six heartbeats were thumping through my metaphorical ears. Five strongly, one…less so, from what I could tell. The thing is, though, I had quite the range on my blood sense these days. If I had only just started to sense these hearts…

This entrance might be a bit deeper than I had thought it would be.

Troubling.

My understanding had been that these types of cemeteries just bore a residence for the on-site gravemender, and not a full-on set of catacombs. Was there something different about this one?

I guess I’d just have to find out.

I waited until the wind had blown the door to the underground open once more and then dashed through the entrance before it could close once more. Abruptly, the thin grey light of the Skyfall faded away…

To be replaced by absolute darkness.

I knelt quietly as my eyes adjusted to what seemed to be a stone step at the top of a staircase that led downwards into the earth. When they did, I couldn’t help but frown.

This was…quite a bit more decorative than I would have expected. The narrow, downwards sloping hall I had found myself in was far more elaborately carved than what seemed appropriate for a mere gravemender’s home. Delicate, almost floral abstractions were etched into the walls, almost seeming to flow downwards in a guiding manner. There were outright rails on either side of it, as well, something I hadn’t expected. It seemed to be in nearly immaculate condition, as well, no cracks or signs of decay visible at all upon the stone. Except for one thing.

I reached out and dragged a single finger down the wall to my right, inspecting what was left on my glove.

For as well-maintained as it was, there was a thin layer of dust upon the stone.

Hmm…

Cared for, and yet recently neglected. Interesting.

And then it hit me, just why I had found the grass outside distracting.

It was too tall. Maintaining the grounds was absolutely within the purview of a gravemender. If this cemetery’s mender had still been active…that would have been cut.

A vicious smile crossed my lips then.

Found you.

I crept down the stairs, employing every single ounce of experience I had in stealth to do so quietly. I maintained an almost laser-focused stare on each subsequent step as I descended, watchful for anything that would give me away.

I didn’t want these savages to see me coming at all.

I stopped abruptly before I could go any further, eyes fixed on something before me.

Well, well, well. That was almost admirable.

Almost.

They had set a trap, and not a simple one at that. It was layered, from what I could see. There was the obvious wire that was just about at shin height on me if I had taken another step, stretching across the hall downwards. That would have triggered…absolutely nothing, I saw, as I traced its path. It was a feint, something a bit obvious to raise the confidence of whoever was on the lookout. The real trap was below that, and it was magical. Runic, from what I could faintly sense.

I couldn’t quite tell what it was, but I could sense where it was. If I let my foot touch the step below the wire at all, the trap would trigger.

I considered the matter, faintly aware of the six heartbeats below me, and focused beyond that step.

Why, I think that was another trap, just beyond it. I couldn’t tell what it was, but I could tell it was there. You paranoid little rascals. Just who were you trying to hide from, hmm?

I bet the name started with ‘R’.

No matter.

I reach out one hand and cast out a Thorn whip. It was a bit shocking, just how easily the barbed length of vine attached to the ceiling of the tunnel. Honestly, I think the Skill was getting stronger over time. It certainly wasn’t that sharp when I got the Skill with my Class. I definitely wouldn't have been able to penetrate stone with the barbed tip of it.

I carefully reeled myself up and over the trapped section of the hallway. On my forward momentum, I released the Skill, sailing a good distance down into the depths of Vereden. Twisting in midair, I landed in a silent crouch upon what seemed to be a landing of some sort.

Safe.

Just ahead of me, the tunnel had flattened out. I was no longer traveling downward, having reached the bottom. Only…

The heartbeats I could sense were ahead. More than that?

There was also the slight glow of a fire lamp I could see in the distance.

I was tempted to crack my neck, but restrained myself. Instead, I began to creep forward down the hall, making sure to stick to one side of it.

Slowly, carefully…I approached the den where my prey were hiding. I didn’t blink as I started to hear faint voices speaking in low, guttural tones not far from me.

But my heartbeat did pick up in anticipation.

I even think I recognized the language they were speaking. I couldn’t understand it. But I had heard it before…from Kierla.

In other words, Elvish.

Ever so carefully, I glided forward until I reached the end of the hallway, where it turned abruptly right. I flattened myself against the wall beside the bend and slowly peered around the corner. What I saw caused my breath to catch in my throat.

They had a prisoner.
