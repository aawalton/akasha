Chapter 283

Earlier.

“Icarus, I need to lease the Flight skill.”

Moments later, Travis felt the Origin System begin to alter him at a fundamental level. A dialog box appeared, and the rare [Flight] skill became available to him. He jumped and activated it, soaring up into the sky above the Valley to oversee the ground operations from a vantage point.

Icarus’s voice spoke directly into his mind. The difference between their tone and behavior between when Michael was present and when he wasn’t was stark, but Travis was slowly getting used to it. In fact, his relationship with the enigmatic AI was improving nicely, in his own opinion. To the point Icarus sometimes told him jokes!

“How is the skill behaving?” they asked. “Any interference with your pre-existing Card system? I wouldn’t want you to fall down and break all your bones.”

Travis looked at the ground below. He was easily a good half mile up in the air. “I think I would survive,” he said. “But no, no problems as far as I can tell.”

“Good,” Icarus replied.

In the brief pause that followed, Travis scouted the whole place. The usual mists shrouded the mountains in the distance, hiding their far sides. The sun was still high in the sky, casting short shadows.

Below him, the portal was busy spilling hundreds of men and heavy machinery. There was a slight breeze but no clouds, and for a moment Travis imagined himself alone in the dungeon. In his imagination, there was none of the urgency of the real world, nor the Gaze and its tainting effects.

Thinking about it reminded him of its existence, or its lack thereof. Since the Misty Valley was now Michael’s Domain, any person authorized to be here was also protected from any outside harm, Gaze included.

“You do know that you aren’t forced to lease skills, right?” Icarus said.

Travis kept watching the men as they worked on beginning to build a new base of operations inside the dungeon. “I’m aware, yes. I just can’t bring myself to make them permanent.”

“I assure you there will be no repercussions. They will feel completely natural as if they were yours. And don’t worry, you will also be able to remove them without pain or discomfort should you choose to. The Origin System is designed to be stingy when giving skills, but when it comes to taking them back it never refuses free gifts,” Icarus said.

Travis chuckled. “I know all of this. I have admin access when I am at the Central Dashboard. I saw the code.”

“Ah,” Icarus’s voice seemed to deflate. “You can read the code?”

“After staring at it for a while, it’s like it begins to speak to me.”

Silence followed, and then finally Icarus spoke again, but their voice felt distant. “Interesting…”

“I don’t need to tell you the reason why I don’t make them fully owned or whatever the name is,” Travis said after a short pause of his own. “I know I did not create the System, and that I do not own it but… I can’t bring myself to take things from it. Even if it’s just a single skill I am lending, let alone that I own. As long as I am using it, it doesn’t contribute to the power of the System. In fact, it draws power from it. Icarus, when I sit at the System console, I feel at the center of the universe. The feeling is intoxicating, powerful, dangerous. Every time I go there and see the new operational numbers, the new things the System can do, the new Domains we control… I feel a jolt in my brain, a high no drug can even hope to replicate. I see the numbers go up, and I know that I am a part of them, a big part of them. I can’t be the reason they go down. Tell me it wasn’t you manipulating my mind and I will believe you.”

“It wasn’t,” Icarus said. “I promise. Michael is very much against mind control, as you know, and even I am changing my mind about it. At least, when it comes to our people.”

“Thank you.”

“The reason the Origin System works so well with people is because it is built to tap into the reward and pleasure center of the human brain, Travis. You have unlimited XP and access, which should nullify most of that, but your brain found a little exploit by linking your growth to the growth of the System as a whole. And it is right to do so! Your total access means that the more power the System has, the more power you have. It just goes to show that I didn’t need to manipulate you to be sure you were a nice little slave to the System.”

Travis found himself blinking as he stared at the Valley from far up into the sky, feeling like he was falling. “That’s a lot to take in,” he said. “Is this the true reason Michael gave me access?”

“Nope,” Icarus said. “Nor was it me who told him to. He just did it because he trusts you. Everything I’ve said is simply a consequence of how the System is built, something you also have had a part in. Oh, look at that, the President is about to deliver his speech. Want to watch?”

“Sure?” Travis said, still a little discombobulated.

A hologram window appeared. It was almost impossible to tell whether it was an actual hologram or just a mental artifact that Icarus was beaming directly into his mind through the Origin System, and he decided that he didn’t want to know. The existential implications were too much for even him, and instead he focused on the things that brought him pleasure. Like, for instance, when he was the one controlling people and things.

“Good speech, Mr President,” he muttered to himself as he listened.

He even found himself taking mental notes, even though he was only half paying attention, thanks to a leased enhancement to his mind that allowed him to focus on two things at once without trouble. He was still testing it, and it felt strange, but he liked its usefulness and he planned to upgrade it to fully owned soon enough. Some things were worth paying the price for.

“I’d say the President did a good job, didn’t he?” Icarus said after the speech was over. “I’m giving him the next quest.”

Travis hummed distractedly as he frowned and leased a skill to zoom his sight into an area of the Valley.

“You’re not paying attention to me!” Icarus said in a sing-song voice.

“Yeah, I know,” Travis said. “I’ll review the quest later when I can go to the Central Dashboard. Give me a minute, Johanne is about to open a portal and David just told me he bets fifty gold coins that she causes an explosion. I’m betting a platinum that she doesn’t.”

Icarus laughed. “Well, looks like you are enjoying the instant communications module of the System. It will be shipped wide with the version 0.5 later this week. Oh, there she goes aaaand… looks like you won the bet!”

Travis chuckled. “Of course I did. There was no way you’d allow an explosion to happen here, right?”

Icarus said nothing.

“Right?” Travis repeated himself, this time with an undertone of worry in his voice.

“Um, speaking of things and explosions…” the AI said. “There’s a dossier you should review.”

The file popped up in Travis’s vision, and he took a moment to appreciate the beautiful formatting that followed the proper Candle Light specifications to a T. Then, when he got to the actual contents, his face darkened.

“Let’s see… a whole class of students vanished in thin air in Korea three hours ago?”

“There’s camera footage too. Watch,” Icarus said, pulling up the video. “I enhanced it, and added our data from the satellites. One moment they are all there, then a flash of Faith, and poof, the classroom is empty.”

“Demonic energy?”

“Traces of it, yes,” Icarus said.

“I need to get there, immediately.”

“I agree. I’m authorizing you to lease an Override to Gold Tier to one operator of your choosing so that they can fly there quickly. Once there, have them deploy a portable domain.”

“I’m also going,” Travis said.

“You can do whatever you want, Travis. You are the CEO. I'm just saying, don’t waste time flying there when you can send someone else. While you wait, I would like to hear your thoughts on the President's situation. Can you come to the Central Dashboard?”

“Coming.”
