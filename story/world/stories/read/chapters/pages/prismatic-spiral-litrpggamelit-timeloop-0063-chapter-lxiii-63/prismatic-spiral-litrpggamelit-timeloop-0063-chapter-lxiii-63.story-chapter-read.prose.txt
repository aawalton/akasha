Chapter LXIII (63)

Rather than the mute silence Mitsuko expected from slicing off her ears, her hearing was filled with a muffled roar.

She took a step up the stairs, dripping blood. No longer able to hear the song, she likewise couldn’t hear her own footsteps or even her blood as it splattered on the stone steps.

“Absolutely foolish,” Sterling said, his voice garbled but still discernible, despite the wound. He likely bypassed some sort of mortal communication due to his link to her soul. She recalled both him and Rodrick speaking to her mentally before. In a way, she wished that wasn’t the case. The gurgling roar was a peaceful oblivion for her to work with. But, despite how annoying he often was, hearing Sterling’s voice would likely still be a boon in her upcoming battle.

“It will let me finish this,” Mitsuko said. But her own voice was lost to her in the roar of blood. She pocketed both of her ears, intending to reattach them after she faced off against the guardian. They were so tiny, a child’s ears. She’d cut through them cleanly and quickly. The holes in her head still burned with acute pain, but slicing off the cartilage and tiny bones had been easy. The quick jolt of pain and dull throb on the side of her head was nothing in comparison to the agony of sawing through bone and muscle sinew while cutting off her leg in that earlier loop.

The reminder that the pain was temporary helped considerably. All she needed to do was finish off this guardian and she’d piece herself back together with ease.

Mitsuko took a step forward and had to catch herself, using her now bloodied sword as a cane.

“Your balance is off due to the damage dealt to your inner ear,” Sterling said. “While less substantial than what you did to the outer ear, it will likely make movement a fair bit more difficult. At least until you get used to it.”

She steadied herself. Another complication. If the guardian was more like the Mauve Island guardian than the Ashen Island guardian, it wouldn’t be much of an issue. Of course, if this guardian was too much like Bupre…well, that likely wasn’t the case. The children referred to her as ‘Mother’ which implied an adult woman.

Step after step, Mitsuko ascended up to the fifth floor of the temple.

As she made her way up, she found she no longer needed the glowing moss. The bricks on this floor’s walls were transparent, allowing light to spill into the open room through the gaps in the vines that hugged the pyramid’s exterior.

A woman stood in the center of the room, a wooden flute at her lips and her eyes closed. Flowers were woven into her brown braids and her clothing was made of leaves save for her bottom half which appeared to be covered in thick, furry pants. An odd pairing, but Mitsuko’d seen stranger styles in her travels. Only once she stepped closer did Mitsuko realize her mistake.

While the guardian’s arms and face appeared human, Mitsuko realized the guardian wore no clothing below her waist. It was natural fur that ended in cloven hooves. Accompanied by an extra backwards knee, like a four legged animal. It took Mitsuko a few seconds to realize what this species was. A faun. A creature of legend, rumored to have been brought to extinction.

A blond, curly haired girl sat cross-legged in front of the faun, her head bobbing along to the music, unheard by Mitsuko. She figured through context this must be the acolyte Hallie. The child the guardian had put the most effort into controlling.

Chunks of debris and plants lay strewn across the open floor. Rocks and flowers in equal measure surrounded the guardian. And, on the far wall, Mitsuko saw the final staircase, leading up to the sixth floor. That would be where the sage rested.

Mitsuko wished she had spatial magic. The ability to teleport right in front of the guardian and cut her down with a single slash of her blade. Alas, she had to instead rely on her feet to carry her forward.

As she did, the guardian opened her eyes and lowered her flute. Her lips moved, but Mitsuko heard nothing, still deafened. The guardian frowned. In front of her, the child Hallie’s head cocked slightly to the side, as if confused about where the music had disappeared to. She appeared oblivious to Mitsuko’s arrival, her attention solely dedicated to the guardian.

“Your ears are missing. Willing to mutilate a child’s body in a brash attempt to reach me? Barbaric.” This time the guardian’s lips didn’t move. “You lack the spirit of a child. Instead you claw back the hands of time in an effort to spit in my good work. A disgusting action as to be expected of humans. An infiltrator and a disgrace to what you once were.”

“You’re one to talk,” Mitsuko said, her words inaudible to herself. “You’ve lured out all these children from their homes and plan to banish everyone on the archipelago into a frozen oblivion. You’re nothing more than a thief stealing their lives from them.”

“Only those not under my protection will be caught in this moment of time,” the faun said. She reached down and patted Hallie’s head. “So long as I remain, so too will the children under my care. Remove me from the timeloops and you sentence these young souls to a fate worse than death. A moment in time of infinite proportions."

That made Mitsuko hesitate. Could she gamble these lives so easily? This guardian had basically created a hostage scenario where she put children up to shield her from harm. Not that different from whoever set Bupre as the guardian on Mauve.

“Cease with the needless prattle of false goodwill, Anemone,” Sterling said, breaking Mitsuko’s internal debate. “Your actions mirror those in your life. Self-serving and egotistical. Hardly a surprise, as you had no opportunity for growth. This version of you must have been created from the days before your execution by my hand.”

Mitsuko's attention shifted to the black cat beside her. Sterling sat on his haunches, his green eyes fixed on the sage, Anemone.

“Pilroth,” she hissed. “You pathetic piece of trash. You are the one who ended my last life? I should have known someone as cold hearted and selfish as you would be the one to slay me. How many children’s corpses did you leave in the wake of your destruction?"

“I hardly bothered to count,” Sterling said simply. “I saved those I could from your delusional rampage. Sadly, not even my power can Mend death.” Sterling finally met Mitsuko’s eyes. “At least, not under those circumstances.”

“Will your timeloop bring back your family? Does it reach far enough back?” Anemone taunted. A sneer now marred her otherwise beautiful face. “Pity how things played out. I’m especially sad that I was already dead and lacked the opportunity to witness the downfall of the Novryss family. At least in this life I’ll have the vindication of watching your failure.”

Beside her, Sterling bristled. Mitsuko noted his claws were extended, his body trembling. For once, he said nothing, simply seething in rage.

“Your music’s magic won’t hurt me,” Mitsuko said. She stepped forward, standing between Sterling and the guardian. She raised her bloodied blade, the action requiring two hands with her small stature and the blade’s balance.

“I am aware,” Anemone replied. “I hoped you would attempt something more mundane, like clogging them with wax. I have methods in which to thwart such measures. But you instead maimed your very body, changing how you absorb noise. Troublesome. But I won’t let you take these pure souls away from me. You aren’t the only one with clever tricks.” The faun raised her wooden flute back up to her lips. “I dedicate this song to Pilroth. Now die.”

The guardian blew into the instrument, though Mitsuko heard no notes.

Rather than stand back and take a defensive stance, Mitsuko opted to take the lead in this dance. Cut off the source of the music and end it quickly.

As she dashed forward, the ground shook underfoot. She narrowly dodged out of the way as one of the chunks of rock debris in the room flew past her head. The rocks slammed into one another and assembled in the center of the room. The plants growing off the stones weaved between the cracks to tie it all together. In mere seconds, a massive golem stood before Mitsuko. Anemone stood on its shoulder, with Hallie at her side.

Mitsuko stared up at the artificial monster. Anemone continued to play her flute, presumably using it to control the construct.

Mitsuko cast Mend in an attempt to dissemble the creation and put a stop to this all instantly. She felt her spell get blocked and shrugged off. Her magic wouldn’t end this fight so easily.

Thankfully, Mitsuko was more than a few temporal spells. She readied her stance, positioning her blade forward between herself and her foe.

Mitsuko, before all else, was a bladedancer. And it was time to dance.
