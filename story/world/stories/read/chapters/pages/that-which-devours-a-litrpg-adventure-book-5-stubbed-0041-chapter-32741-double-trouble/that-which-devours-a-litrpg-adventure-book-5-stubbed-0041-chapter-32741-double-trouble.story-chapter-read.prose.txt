“Dad’s on a hoverbike, and on his way back here,” said John. He stared at the map on the screen in front of him.

Alex’s father wasn’t my concern. Something had happened on that floating mountain. Something that the voices couldn’t speak about.

My chest tightened, as I leaned a little closer.

“What about Alex?” I asked, staring at the second dot, searching for any sign of her.

“Dad said she pushed him away, and she landed on the chain,” he said with relief. “I found her without a problem. She needs a moment, then we need to get off this world.”

“The mana is going to keep increasing,” I said quietly.

When we’d first arrived, there hadn’t been any, and whatever had happened up there, it now slowly leaked from the mountain. The shifts we saw from afar were now nothing compared to the sudden changes from mana hitting the land and water.

Creatures were appearing that I hadn’t seen before. Some were small, and some much bigger, and some of the bigger ones flew.

“Can I speak with her?” I asked, as my concern grew. She was hurt. That much the voices knew.

John went quiet. Then, he spoke super quietly, “She passed out on the hoverbike. I don’t want anyone to see her like that.”

A hurt Alex was dangerous. She needed to eat.

“We need to feed her, away from the others,” I said carefully. I didn’t know how much she’d actually told her family about what she was, or what she actually needed.

“We will. I won’t let her do something she regrets.” He nodded at me, then swore. “It’s losing energy rapidly.”

“What is?” I asked, taking a step back, already knowing the answer.

“The hoverbike.” He swore again.

Abby appeared at the back. “Hellion is docking.”

A warning blared from the dashboard. “We need to leave,” said John. “The shuttle can’t handle much more of these mana swings.”

“Head to the safe area near the portal,” I said. I kept my voice level, not letting my concern be heard.

John gritted his teeth, then the shuttle started moving. “We’ll just ride these waves out.”

“Can we let Hellion out?” asked Abby, looking between the two of us. “He can help Alex.”

“He can stew for the moment,” said John with a slight growl. It was unlike him.

I set a hand on his shoulder, and he flinched.

“It isn’t his fault,” I added, trying to soften his reaction a little. “Alex does what Alex does, she gets that from somewhere.”

John let out a sigh. “The portal better help, otherwise we need to leave.”

I swallowed hard, as the whispers increased. Usually they weren’t like this during the day, but here it was, like day and night didn’t exist, no matter what the light outside showed.

Alex, on the hoverbike…

I woke with a sour taste in my mouth, as something nudged my side twice in a row. I ignored it, and almost let the darkness take me away again, when something touched my face.

My eyes snapped open to find Strange’s head right next to my eyeball.

“Alpha!”

The sound ripped through my mind, almost like I was hung-over, but that wasn’t possible. I tried to remember what happened.

The hoverbike rested under me, with the shield still up.

It tilted at a downward angle, and Strange crawled between me and it.

“Strange, what happened?” I asked, as I hit a button and nothing happened.

“You slept.” His answer didn’t help me.

My fingers flexed on both my hands, with only a little pain on the one that had been injured. But it didn’t feel right. I brought it closer to my face and the area that had been injured by the mana still appeared charred, plus the biggest problem. My skin wasn’t its normal color. A dark tint lingered.

The hoverbike suddenly rolled under me, and we tumbled to the right.

I tried to use the map, but pain stabbed through my head.

That wasn’t good.

Something had gone wrong.

We crashed to a stop, thankfully right side up.

The shield still blocked my view of the outside. The bike didn’t have any power at all, and I didn’t dare try to use any of my skills at the moment.

John would need to forgive me.

My spear appeared in knife form, and I stabbed at the shield. It shattered into nothingness, revealing a dark sky and a massive form staring down at me with two heads.

It barked, making my ears ring.

Strange vanished into the darkness.

“Shhhh…” I said carefully, letting the knife go back into my inventory. “It’s okay, pupper…”

It didn’t move, just whimpered.

“Oh, I’m okay,” I said, as I climbed off the bike. A line across the grass made it clearer what had happened. I’d crashed. Though, I didn’t know why.

Dust covered the area where the bike had hit the ground. The giant, two-headed dog sniffed in that direction and whimpered again, before looking at me. It sniffed again, then sneezed, sending dust everywhere, along with dampness.

I shivered and tried not to think about it as I resisted wiping down my arms

“Now, where are…” My voice trailed off as I spotted a cluster of burning tents in the far distance. Somehow, we were farther out in the plains than I thought we’d be.

Still, I knew how to get back to the portal area, and even a few spots to rest on the way. The trek might take me some time, though.

I started walking.

The massive dog stayed nearby, moving much faster than me. He darted off deeper into the plains, then looped back to check on me, then darted off again.

He was bored and lonely from what I could see, he really wanted packmates. That much was clear.

Strange darted out near his feet, making the dog’s heads pause while one sniffed him, and the other tried to lick him.

The quest notification still hummed in the corner of my mind. I had another mana lens to track down. I needed to check my map, but wanted to wait a little. That pain in my head worried me.

I also hadn’t passed out before like that. Not in a long time. Then again, I had been pretty sure I’d lost a finger or two while grabbing the chain. I pushed those thoughts away, not wanting to think about what I’d done to my body. It seemed to be fixed now though with some modifcations, and that was what mattered.

Still, here I was walking around without my armor on, with only my spear in my inventory.

Food. Once I got some food, I’d be fine.

“Hey pupper, where can I find food?” I asked.

The dog cocked one head at me, while the other followed the tiny trail left by Strange as he darted along its feet.

He nodded and turned to face not the on-fire tents, but something farther away in the darkness. Then he started padding away from me. The other head whimpered again as they left the trail for me.

I followed, and Strange joined me.

It didn’t take long until the pupper stopped at the edges of the water. He stayed very quiet, but one head motioned to the water.

Something dark swam through the water.

My spear appeared in my hand, and I lengthened it. Some fish would be tasty.

Before I could do anything, both heads lunged into the water. It went everywhere. A wave slammed into me, soaking my clothes and cleaning off way too much gunk.

When I cleared my face, I found the dog biting this massive fish in half. One piece slammed into the ground with a thud, bigger than me.

The two heads fought over the rest of it.

I darted forward and cut a few massive hunks off of it before tossing some of the raw flesh into my mouth.

Each swallow helped as energy flowed from my stomach. I ate bite after bite, until my stomach wouldn’t take any more.

The dog's head sniffed at the rest. “Go for it,” I said.

It tossed the leftovers into the air, and the two heads tore it to shreds making a mess. Then, they sniffed out each little bit and licked it up.

Part of me wanted to curl up and sleep, but I couldn’t. We needed to make it to the portal area where everyone should be meeting up. After that, I needed to finish this quest.

I sat on a rock and took a moment to just let my stomach process all of the fish. The usual burst of energy that happened when I ate didn’t come. Instead, my head felt a little warm.

“So, I damaged my head,” I said quietly to myself. That wasn’t good.

Head injuries were the worst.

Once the warmth passed, I triggered my notifications to figure out what had happened. The levels I’d gained from the Lord showed up, and then the unstable mana warning, popped up. Nothing else.

Taking a deep breath, I tried to open my map. It appeared without a problem. The quest marker didn’t show up. I twisted the thing to show up and down instead of around me. Then, it popped into place.

In the Below.

I freaking knew it.

I leaned back and let out a sigh. Dealing with bone horrors wasn’t on my list of things to do. At least it meant the backtracking to the portal wasn’t completely useless. The gap between the two landmasses would be my best bet to get to the Below area.

Finally, I got to my feet.

“Alright, let's get moving,” I said to Strange, then paused.

I hoped she knew a little more about what he was, and could help him out.

“She will know a good place for you to go so you can have a pack,” I added.
