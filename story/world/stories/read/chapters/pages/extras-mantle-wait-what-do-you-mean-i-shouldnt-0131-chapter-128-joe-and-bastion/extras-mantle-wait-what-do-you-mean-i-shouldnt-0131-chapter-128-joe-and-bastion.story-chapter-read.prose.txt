The lower levels of the bastion stank of blood and burnt essence.

Joe stood in the corridor outside what used to be a sealed maintenance tunnel, watching Illiana direct a crew of volunteers through the process of replacing walls and various systems that Jin's fight with the cultist had melted into slag.

And he had to give it to the young Winters… the kid had really gone all out, like the thing with the collapsing building was one thing, this was… well, amazing.

Structural damage to the walls, essential conduits reduced to dripping metal, and entire wings turned into rubble. And that was just on the floors Joe could see. The destruction spread across multiple levels like someone had taken a hammer to the bastion's spine.

He leaned over the massive hole that punched straight through from the twenty-eighth floor to the thirtieth.

"Damn," He whistled low. "Jin really doesn't do anything by halves."

"No, no, no—left conduit first, you idiot!" Illiana's voice cracked through the enclosed space like a whip. "If you overload the right channel before stabilizing the left, the whole array will cascade, and we'll be scraping your charred remains off the ceiling!"

The Master Artificer's yells carried through the corridor as she barked instructions to people who clearly had very little idea what they were doing, but they were all the bastion had at the moment.

Joe allowed himself a small smile.

Illiana was in her element, and he respected her knowledge of the craft enough to know she'd pull it off. After all, not many people progressed their skills to Master rank while still being an Overmortal ranker.

The single-minded dedication she must have poured into her craft was awe-inspiring.

He turned away from the repair crew and pulled a spatial ring from his finger—one of the dozens Jin had simply dumped on him after the raids—and let its contents spill across the floor in a controlled cascade.

He didn’t dump more than one quantity of the items in the ring. This was so that the people could easily find what they needed to use for the repairs.

Materials, ores, essence crystals, refined metals, enchanted components, and alchemical reagents, all of them piled up in front of him, and with a wave of his essence, the scattered resources arranged themselves in neat rows. Categorized by rank, sorted by likely use.

This was second nature to him by now, all thanks to his own master ranked skill.

"Sir?"

Joe looked up.

One of the volunteers—a young man with soot streaked across his face and exhaustion bleeding from every pore—approached cautiously.

Josh. Joe remembered the name.

"Catch!" Joe said and hurled the ring.

Josh let out a surprised yelp and barely managed to catch it, fumbling the ring twice before securing it against his chest.

"Well, you're gonna have to up your game if you want any chance of hitting on that lovely lady over there." Joe grinned, pointing at another volunteer across the corridor. A girl with hazel hair and brown eyes who was currently wrestling with a damaged essence conduit.

“Shush!” Josh's face went bright red. "How did you—"

"That’s not what’s important!" Joe slung his arm around Josh's shoulders in a gesture that was half-friendly, half-conspiratorial. "What’s important is for you to know life is short, my fellow survivor. Who knows what'll happen tomorrow, yeah?”

“You look bearable enough. Just go for it. Show her you can take responsibility even in situations like these."

"But... but..."

"No buts!" Joe separated, clapping Josh on the shoulder once for emphasis. "Now. See the pile?"

"Yes." Josh looked at the organized resources, his expression shifting from embarrassment to focus. "Although all I can understand are the metals. They're arranged by melting points, I think... I recognize them from my time as an apprentice to a blacksmith."

Joe gave him an amused yet reassessing look. "Then maybe your chances aren't so slim after all."

"Thanks..." Josh said, voice shy but warming slightly.

"That ring's a spatial storage containing the bulk of the resources laid out there," Joe gestured at the neat rows. "Give it to Illiana." He paused, "And what's the status on the corridor?"

Josh nodded, tucking the ring safely into his chest pocket before consulting his clipboard. "We've cleared the debris from corridor nine, but the structural supports are compromised. Do we proceed with repairs or mark it for demolition?"

"Repairs," Joe said without hesitation. "We need every functional corridor we can salvage. Reinforce the supports with whatever you have available and move on to the next section." He waved Josh toward Illiana. "Now hurry to her and tell her I'll do the sweeping down below."

Josh nodded and hurried to reach Illiana.

"Act confident!" Joe called out.

Josh paused mid-step, took a deep breath, and adjusted his posture.

Then he squared his shoulders and walked a bit slow, but with a lot more confidence now.

"Haa... at least people are feeling better after this win." Joe chuckled as he watched him go. “Good to see not everything is all doom and gloom.”

He turned his attention deeper into the bastion.

Down to where Jin had fought the Underlord priest and somehow walked away breathing.

"One insanity after another with him," Joe muttered and snapped his fingers.

A door opened to his right—plain weathered oak, his usual style—and Joe stepped through.

◈◈◈

The lowest floor of the bastion was not in good shape.

Once at the core administrative level, a site of ancient history and architectural wonder. Now turned into an abattoir.

Blood stained every surface. The walls were carved with ritual circles that pulsed faintly with dying power.

Joe's lip curled in disgust.

It was hideous work. Even before Jin and the priest's fight had torn through the chamber, the ritual had been a mess.

To Joe—who was an arcanist and had dabbled in ritualism—this was utterly offensive.

That priest likely didn't have a single artistic bone in his body. All he cared for was efficiency. No elegance. No respect for the craft.

The civilians who'd been strung up like offerings were long gone. Taken to medical. Given whatever comfort could be offered after experiencing that kind of horror.

The dead had been handled with care.

But the ritual traces remained.

Joe had requested they stay untouched until he could examine them, and now—standing in the heart of the cult's work—he understood why his instincts had screamed at him to look closer.

"They were trying to siphon power from the bastion," he said to the empty chamber. "And likely divert it to the veil."

He already knew the bastion ran on a ridiculous amount of power. The core generated it from the earth itself through mechanisms Joe only half-understood.

The bastions were considered archaic knowledge, relics of a past the current empire wanted desperately to forget.

Ever since the new emperor had risen after the coup almost a century ago, he'd been vocal about disregarding the past—ancient customs, old knowledge, traditional power structures—all of it cast aside in favor of blazing a new path forward.

A new his path to be exact.

Genius, the emperor might be.

Wise, he was not.

"Morons," Joe cursed under his breath. "The whole lot of them."

He snapped his fingers.

A spell circle blazed into existence, and a wave cascaded outward with him at the center.

Wherever the wave touched, it washed away blood, corruption, and the lingering traces of the cult's horrific activities. The ritual circles charred and crumbled. The stains dissolved.

When the wave faded, the chamber was spotless… at least spotless of all hideous things. The destruction and debris were still there, but all of that was for Illiana to fix.

Joe was here for more important matters.

This was the reason he'd tagged along with the father-daughter duo for the expedition into Silver Spire in the first place.

"Okay," Joe exhaled slowly, his gaze fixing on the structure rising from the center of the room. "After three years, I'm..."

He stopped speaking.

The crystal pillar stood before him.

The heart of the bastion, the keeper of lost order.

The pillar rose twice his height, translucent and bioluminescent, pulsing with faint internal light that cast prismatic patterns across the curved walls. The surface was covered in runes—hundreds, thousands of them—layered in formations so dense they looked like circuit boards etched into living crystal.

Joe counted silently as he approached.

One thousand and forty-seven distinct rune formations.

"And the crown says in the olden days, people lived in caves." Joe shook his head, voice bitter. "Idiots."

He stopped three feet from the pillar and snapped his fingers.

Space twisted.

A miniature door materialized—different from his usual weathered oak. This one was ornate and carved in patterns of the sun, moon, and the earth—and Joe reached through.

His fingers closed around something smooth and familiar.

Joe bit his lip and closed his eyes. Complicated emotions he'd locked behind a door in his mind threatened to spill over. Memories that clawed at the edges of his consciousness, demanding attention he couldn't afford to give.

It took him a full five minutes to suppress the tide, and he finally pulled his hand out of the door.

In his grasp was a plain dark band. Unassuming. No decorations, no visible enchantments. Just dark metal that had been worn smooth by time and use.

Joe stared at the band for a long moment, his thumb tracing its edge.

Again, memories tried to surface—faces he'd lost, fate he'd survived, promises he'd made and broken—but he pushed them down.

"No time," he whispered.

Joe slipped the band onto his right wrist and took a deep breath.

Then he reached out and touched the crystal pillar.

The moment his fingers made contact, the runes on the band flared to life—bright enough to leave afterimages burned into his vision—and the crystal pulsed in response.

Light raced through the pillar's interior. Holograms materialized around him, illuminating pathways and connections that had been dormant for decades.

And a voice spoke directly into Joe's mind.

SYSTEM INITIALIZATION... FAILED.

AUTHORIZATION CODE MISMATCH.

ACCESS DENIED.

Joe's jaw clenched. "Envoys are granted certain rights, Keeper."

All the holograms froze mid-rotation.

A pause, and then the information flow resumed, faster this time, carrying weight and scrutiny that pressed against his mind like a physical touch.
