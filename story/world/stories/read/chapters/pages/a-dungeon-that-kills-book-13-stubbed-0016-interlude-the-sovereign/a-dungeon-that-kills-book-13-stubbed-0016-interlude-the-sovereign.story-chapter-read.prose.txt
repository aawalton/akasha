Ekon found himself thinking more and more about the Sovereign with each passing day.

And honestly, how could he not?

The first time they met in person was when he was cornered by the masked men and only moments away from being captured. Then, suddenly, that being descended from the sky like a wrathful god who came to pass judgment upon the world below. Effortlessly, he slaughtered his enemies to the last man, and such an overwhelming display of power had etched itself into Ekon’s mind, an unforgettable image that only deepened the more he thought about it afterward.

Then, he learned that the Sovereign was actually the master of the strange dungeon in Daelin, a mysterious entity who commanded countless powerful subordinates who would carry out his every order without hesitation. And this was not something Ekon had merely heard about. He himself had witnessed the Sovereign’s military might with his very own eyes during the battle by the great tomb. Even though the forces deployed there were probably only a tiny fraction of what the Sovereign had at his disposal, they were still formidable enough to put him and his party into mortal danger. Had it not been for Anastrasza’s help, none of them would have escaped that place alive.

And then came the final revelation. He learned that the Sovereign was in fact the Dark Emperor, the legendary tyrant who once ruled the world three centuries ago.

Yes, that Dark Emperor.

There was no one in this world who had not heard of that name. His story had long since spread across the continent, repeated endlessly in so many books, songs, and cautionary tales told throughout every kingdom.

Before he became the feared ruler history remembered, the Dark Emperor had once been nothing more than a simple adventurer. Driven by his boundless ambition, he gathered followers and led a massive rebellion against the King of Lyndor, an uprising that attracted not only common adventurers but even several Adamantite whose names alone inspired awe and fear across the land. Together, they stormed the royal palace, crushed the king’s soldiers, and captured the royal family.

What followed was one of the darkest events in recorded history, and one that marked the first step in the Dark Emperor’s path of infamy. He had ordered the king and his entire bloodline to be publicly executed in an unimaginably brutal spectacle, a horrifying act that had earned him the dreadful nickname “the Impaler.”

Afterward, he seized the throne and proclaimed himself the new King of Lyndor. Yet, taking over this kingdom was not enough to satisfy him. Once his rule had been secured, he immediately turned his gaze outward and began to wage relentless wars against the neighboring kingdoms. He secured an alliance with the distant kingdom of Brefjord through marriage while vanquishing his enemies one after another as his armies marched east. And, after he captured the great city of Voskryn, he had abandoned the title of king. There, from the ashes of many conquered nations, he declared the birth of an empire, with Voskryn as its capital and himself as its sovereign.

The rest of the world did not easily submit to him, of course. They called themselves the Last Alliance, a vast coalition of the southern kingdoms and the broken remnants of the western realms, united under a single desperate cause. With their combined strength, they believed they could challenge the rising empire of the Tyrant.

Or so they thought.

What none of them could have known at the time was that they were not marching toward a battlefield, but a carefully constructed slaughterhouse. It was, as later scholars would conclude with a mixture of awe and horror, a trap of unimaginable scale, one devised by the Dark Emperor with a patience that bordered on inhuman. He had deliberately allowed the alliance to form and their armies to converge into a single unified front, so that he could wipe them all out with a single move.

On that fateful night, just before the Last Alliance could engage the Imperial Army in what would have been the defining battle of the age, a clash that was said to decide the fate of the world, a firestorm erupted, descending from nowhere and everywhere at once. Three hundred thousand soldiers, men who had marched under countless banners, men who believed in the righteousness of their cause, had been instantly reduced to ash. When dawn came, there was nothing left of the Last Alliance but silence and scorched earth.

For generations afterward, scholars and historians had been debating how such a catastrophe could have been possible. Some believed it was the result of careful planning and logistics: that the emperor had coordinated thousands of mages to assemble the trap in secret, before luring his enemies straight into its jaws. Others claimed the truth was far more terrifying: that the Dark Emperor himself had been the catastrophe. In this version of events, he had appeared alone above the assembled armies and personally rained death upon his foes.

In the end, it did not matter. Whatever the truth, only one fact remained. After that day, all resistance ceased to exist, and everyone bowed to the Dark Emperor. Even the nomadic tribes of the eastern steppes submitted and called him their Khagan.

Ironically, the Empire didn’t even last a full year after this absolute victory. One night, a group of assassins who would later be remembered as the Six Heroes infiltrated the Dark Emperor’s castle and succeeded in killing him. With his death, the Empire fell apart.

And now, Ekon was sitting inside a dungeon that belonged to that very same man.

Ever since the moment Anastrasza revealed this truth to him, his thoughts had been in constant turmoil as countless questions turned over and over within his mind. How could the Dark Emperor have survived the assassination? What had become of him during the three centuries that followed his supposed death? Or had he truly died, and been resurrected only recently? And above all else, what was he going to do now? Would he exact vengeance against the descendants of his enemies, or would he seek to conquer the world once more and rebuild the empire he had lost?

Driven by this growing obsession, Ekon found himself paying increasingly close attention to everything Haku had told him. Of course, he had been careful. The bird-man must not suspect anything. So he only listened, and never asked questions. Even when there was something that sparked his curiosity, he forced himself to remain silent. Instead, he simply memorized it, so that if he ever heard the same details again in a different context, he would be able to piece them together.

There was someone he had heard quite a lot about recently, a Lady Galatea. Whenever Haku spoke of her, he never failed to praise her beauty. And, according to the bird-man’s stories, the relationship between the Sovereign and this woman had been... complicated.

On the one hand, the Sovereign had gone to extraordinary lengths to accommodate her whims, and no request of hers was deemed too extravagant or unreasonable to be granted. Haku had told Ekon about the place where she was residing, a so-called “cathedral,” and from his description, it was without a doubt a structure of impossible grandeur. The Sovereign must have poured immense resources into its construction, just to please Lady Galatea. And apparently, this project was hardly unique. As it turned out, even the colosseum Haku had once mentioned was her idea as well.

Yet, on the other hand, it seemed they frequently argued with each other. In fact, just a few days prior, they had a huge fight in her bedroom, one intense enough that the Sovereign had allegedly considered banishing this consort of his from the dungeon.

I need to be careful, Ekon thought. If he ever encountered this Lady Galatea, he would remain respectful. She was, after all, someone who stood by the Sovereign’s side. But at the same time, he would maintain a careful distance, so that if their relationship ever turned sour, he would not be close enough to be caught in the fallout.

A knock came at the door.

Ah, dinner.

“Come in,” Ekon said.

The hinges gave their usual groan as the door opened, and as expected, Haku stepped in with his tray of food. He set it down, and conversation followed naturally afterward, drifting across many different topics.

As always, the bird-man was deeply fascinated by the lives of those in the outside world, so he asked Ekon about all sorts of things. He wanted to know how kingdoms governed themselves, how trade routes shifted from season to season, what kinds of stories parents told their children, how common folk viewed the Forgotten Gods, what fashions were popular in major cities, what foods were eaten during celebrations, and countless other things that were utterly trivial compared to the horrifying cosmic truths Ekon had recently been forced to learn.

Ekon, on the other hand, listened attentively whenever Haku said anything involving the Sovereign or the dungeon.

“...by the way,” the bird-man said, “we’ve just found the hideout of those masked men who attacked you—”

“What?” Ekon gaped. “You found them? Where are they?”

Yes, this was a topic that he could, and should, ask questions about.

“North of Daelin, inside a Folded Chamber,” Haku replied. Ekon frowned, and the bird-man quickly noticed his confusion. “A bounded space disconnected from the normal world. Think of it as a miniature dungeon.”

There’s actually something like that?

“What is the Sovereign going to do about it? Is he going to attack that hideout?”

Haku shook his head. “My lord deemed it too risky. There’s no way to know what exists inside a Folded Chamber without physically entering it first. And my lord does not wish to gamble his agents on such an uncertain mission. There aren’t many of them, you know. Most of us are bound to the dungeon and cannot leave it.”

“So... he’s just going to ignore it?”

“We will continue observing them for now. There’s no need to rush. My lord is a patient man, after all.”

Ekon frowned. The Sovereign could wait, but he himself could not. He had no intention of living in this cell forever. On the other hand, his enemies were out there, beyond these walls. The moment he left, he would be in grave danger.

“How about this?” he said. “Let me take part in this mission. No, let me enter that Bounded Chamber first. That way, the Sovereign wouldn’t have to risk his men.”

This was reckless, yes, but if the alternatives were either to remain stuck here for who knew how much longer or to be hunted down like a dog, then he would rather bring the fight to his enemies, with the Sovereign by his side.

Haku rubbed his beak. “I don’t think that’s a good idea. There’s a chance you will be attacked the instant you enter. Not that I doubt your abilities, but sending you in alone is not something my lord would approve.”

Ekon couldn’t really argue with that. It was a reasonable concern. He was just about to ask if the Sovereign could dispatch a couple of his agents to come with him, but he stopped himself. Doing so would defeat the entire purpose of his volunteering.

“Wait,” Haku said. “You have your party, right? How about bringing them in too?”

“I’d rather not drag them into this.”

The bird-man shrugged. “From my perspective, they’re already involved, whether you like it or not. You know those lunatics have been indiscriminately kidnapping the pyromancers, right? Even the smallest connection is enough for them to target someone. And your friends—just by being your friends—have a mark on them. It’s only a matter of time before they’re taken too.”

Ekon slowly nodded. Haku was right. And that meant he couldn’t afford to remain idle here. He might be safe, but the others were not.

“I understand,” he said quietly. “In that case... tell the Sovereign that my party will act as the spearhead for the attack. Please, help us destroy our enemies.”

“I cannot guarantee anything,” Haku said with a smile, “but I will do my best to convince him for you.”
