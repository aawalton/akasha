“Junior Brother, aren't you spoiling Aunt a bit too much?” Ling Yinran asked with a sigh.

Ning paused mid-step, holding a tray of freshly prepared dishes.

“Spoiling?” he repeated, looking at her in confusion.

Ling Yinran gestured toward the dining table, where several carefully prepared spiritual dishes had been arranged. Each portion was well-balanced and rich in spiritual energy, with a small plate of sweets sitting beside them, clearly having received a little extra attention.

Ever since learning the fundamentals of spiritual cooking, Ning had been experimenting with various ingredients and preparation methods, gradually improving both the taste and nourishing properties of his meals.

“You wake her up in the morning, remind her to freshen up, prepare her meals, make sure she gets enough rest, and even clean up after her experiments,” Ling Yinran listed, counting on her fingers. “At this point, are you her disciple or her caretaker?”

Ning glanced at the table before turning toward Ling Xin, who was sitting nearby, happily enjoying her sweets without the slightest hint of guilt.

"It's just basic maintenance,” he replied calmly.

“Maintenance?” Ling Yinran's lips twitched. “She's a Foundation Establishment cultivator, Junior Brother, not some delicate spirit beast that needs constant attention. You're just making her even lazier.”

Ning sighed helplessly. “It's my duty to help Master. Besides, if I don't remind her, she skips meals. If I don't clean up after her experiments, the entire residence becomes a hazard zone. And if I don't organize things…”

He paused and glanced toward a nearby corner, where faint scorch marks from one of his master's previous experiments still decorated the walls.

“…Well, you've seen what happens.”

Ling Yinran opened her mouth, seemingly intending to argue, but eventually closed it again.

She couldn't exactly refute that.

"Still feels like you're spoiling her,” she muttered after a moment.

Ning shook his head. “I'm just making sure Master is satisfied so I can cultivate in peace.”

Ling Yinran stared at him.

That was unexpectedly reasonable.

Meanwhile, Ling Xin, who had been quietly enjoying her sweets throughout the conversation, finally decided to contribute.

“Ning is a very good disciple,” she declared with a satisfied nod before popping another sweet into her mouth.

Ling Yinran turned toward her aunt. “You're enjoying this way too much.”

Ling Xin blinked innocently. “Hm?”

Ning decided to ignore the usual banter between aunt and niece. Since his master was in a good mood, this seemed like a suitable opportunity to ask about something that had been on his mind.

“Master, is there any way for me to obtain an Earth-grade technique? I've heard that cultivating one can help with breaking through to the later stages of Qi Condensation.”

“An Earth-grade technique?” Ling Xin paused to consider his question. “There are several ways to obtain one. You could purchase it from outside the sect or try your luck at an auction, but such techniques are extremely expensive. Whenever an Earth-grade technique appears on the market, powerful clans and high-level cultivators usually compete fiercely to obtain it.”

She took another bite of her sweet before continuing.

“As for the Scripture Pavilion, the sect has quite a few Earth-grade techniques in its collection. However, gaining access to them requires an enormous amount of merit points. An ordinary disciple would have to make some truly significant contributions to the sect to afford even one.”

“I see.” Ning nodded thoughtfully.

Although Earth-grade techniques might not seem particularly rare at first glance, especially considering how frequently they appeared as rewards during major sect competitions, they were actually extremely difficult for ordinary cultivators to obtain.

After all, the fact that a powerful sect could afford to offer such techniques as prizes didn't mean that its disciples could easily acquire them through ordinary means.

“It's a pity, though,” Ling Xin added. “Both Yinran and I had to sign binding contracts when we learned our Earth-grade techniques. Otherwise, I could have simply taught you one of mine. Such troublesome rules.”

Unlike cultivators born into powerful clans, Ling Xin had originally entered the sect as an ordinary disciple, much like Ning himself. However, her exceptional talent had attracted the attention of a Purple Mansion cultivator early in her cultivation journey, allowing her to become a direct disciple and gain access to resources that most others could only dream of.

Because of that, she had never really struggled to obtain high-level techniques.

“Actually, I still have some merit points saved up,” Ling Xin continued casually. “If you want, I can exchange them for an Earth-grade technique for you.”

She was quite satisfied with her new disciple, after all. Ever since Ning had arrived, her daily life had become much more comfortable, and as an added bonus, he somehow managed to counter Yinran's lectures with perfectly reasonable explanations.

What more could a master ask for?

“There's no need, Master,” Ning replied, shaking his head.

Although he appreciated the offer, he preferred not to rely too heavily on others for matters like this. Favors had a tendency to accumulate, and even when offered freely, accepting too many of them could eventually become a burden.

More importantly, it wasn't as though he had exhausted all his alternatives.

He still had a few ideas worth exploring.

“Junior Brother,” Ling Yinran suddenly spoke up, “if your goal is to reach the Seventh Stage of Qi Condensation, you should know that an Earth-grade technique isn't your only option.”

Ning turned toward her.

“To reach the Seventh Stage, you need to open your Ren and Du meridians and awaken your spiritual sense, essentially releasing the 'god' within your mind,” she explained.

Naturally, the 'god' she referred to wasn't some heavenly deity. It was the embryonic consciousness of the soul, something that existed within every cultivator but remained dormant in most people. Awakening it required a level of mental awareness and control that many cultivators struggled to achieve.

Ling Yinran glanced at her aunt before continuing.

“If you're only concerned about breaking through, there are other methods available. In fact, Aunt already has one that could help you. Hasn't she told you about it?”

Ling Xin frowned slightly at being called out.

“Of course I have! I've already prepared a method to help Ning break through to the Seventh Stage. It's that super-secret technique I mentioned before.”

She then smirked at her disciple.

“But you still haven't reached the required level of flame control for alchemy, so I'm keeping the details a secret for now.”

“Okay,” Ning replied calmly.

Ling Xin blinked.

“Eh? That's it? Aren't you curious?” She leaned forward, her expression turning rather suspicious. “How about this? If you agree to try one of my special pills, I'll tell you everything right now.”

“I'll learn about it eventually anyway,” Ning replied without hesitation. “There's no need for the pills, Master.”

He had absolutely no intention of becoming a test subject for his master's latest creations, especially when she had a habit of enthusiastically describing their benefits while conveniently neglecting to mention any potential side effects.

“You haven't even tried a single one of my special pills!” Ling Xin complained, gritting her teeth. “How can you call yourself my disciple at this rate?”

“By staying alive,” Ning answered calmly.

Ling Yinran nearly burst out laughing.

Of course, Ning wasn't entirely ignorant of what his master was hiding.

He had already done some research into the alchemy lineage, and although he hadn't managed to uncover the complete details, he had learned that certain techniques within the lineage involved using pill refinement to overcome cultivation bottlenecks.

Considering his master's insistence on improving his flame control and learning alchemy before revealing her secret method, Ning suspected that the two were connected.

That was enough for him to make an educated guess. No matter how mysteriously Ling Xin tried to present her technique, he wasn't particularly tempted to trade his safety for an early explanation.

“Haha! It seems Junior Brother isn't just spoiling you, Aunt,” Ling Yinran remarked, finally unable to contain her amusement as she watched Ling Xin sulk over yet another rejected pill experiment.

Ling Xin puffed out her cheeks in dissatisfaction, while Ning quietly returned his attention to the dishes on the table.

As always, the atmosphere on Spiritual Flower Peak was anything but dull.

...

As usual, Elder Shen was lounging comfortably in his chair, looking completely at ease with a book held loosely in one hand. An unsuspecting disciple might have assumed he was studying some profound scripture befitting his status as an elder, but Ning knew better by now.

“Greetings, Elder Shen,” Ning said, bowing politely as he approached.

Elder Shen calmly closed his book and turned toward him. “Ah, there you are. I've been waiting for you.”

Ning paused. “Hmm?”

“You haven't submitted the next volume of your book in quite some time,” Elder Shen said sternly. “I was just about to have a word with you about that.”

Ning fell silent.

Here it comes.

“I've been waiti-” Elder Shen abruptly cleared his throat. “Ahem! I mean, do you have any idea how many people are waiting for the next volume?”

His expression was grave, as though he were discussing some urgent matter concerning the future of the sect.

Ning forced a polite smile. “I've been busy settling into the inner sect, Elder. Between adapting to my new environment and keeping up with cultivation, I haven't had much time to write.”

“I understand, I really do,” Elder Shen replied, nodding solemnly.

Then he leaned forward and lowered his voice conspiratorially.

“But do you have any idea how popular your book has become? There are already seven different versions circulating in the outer sect alone!”

Ning blinked. “Seven?”

“Copycats! Shameless copycats!” Elder Shen snorted indignantly before beginning to count them off on his fingers. “One changed the setting to a demon sect, another made the protagonist a female cultivator, and there's even one where they added dual cultivation every other chapter, completely ruining the pacing!”

Ning stared at him.

That last one sounded unfortunately predictable.

“And don't even get me started on the writing quality!” Elder Shen continued, growing increasingly worked up. “There's no buildup, no tension, and barely any character development! They just rush straight to the 'interesting' parts without laying the proper groundwork. Where's the artistry?!”

He slapped Ning on the shoulder with an expression of heartfelt appreciation.

“Your work is different. It has depth!”

Ning wasn't entirely sure whether he should feel proud or concerned about receiving such praise from a respected sect elder.

“I'm glad Elder enjoys it,” he replied carefully.

“It's not about me! I'm speaking on behalf of the readers!” Elder Shen insisted, although his expression made it abundantly clear that he was probably the most invested reader of them all.

Ning wisely decided not to point that out.

“Besides, do you know how much effort I've put into promoting your book?” Elder Shen continued, leaning back in his chair. “I've even recommended it to several of my old friends.”

For some reason, Ning suddenly had a very bad feeling.

“Elder…” he began cautiously. “May I ask which friends?”

“Oh, just a few acquaintances from the other peaks,” Elder Shen replied casually. “Some elders from the Artifact Hall, a couple from Beast Taming Peak, and a few others.”

Ning fell silent. So it had already spread that far.

I'm cooked.

Don't get him wrong, seeing his book become popular was certainly a good thing. In fact, the income it generated was already approaching what he earned from his higher-tier spiritual farming, which was quite impressive considering how little effort and investment the books required.

However, what worried Ning most was the possibility of his identity being exposed.

He wasn't particularly concerned about the embarrassment of being discovered as the author of such books. After all, compared to the many ways a cultivator could lose face in this world, writing a few questionable novels was hardly the worst offense.

What truly concerned him was the possibility of being forced to write them endlessly.

Just imagine transmigrating into a magnificent cultivation world filled with immortals, ancient inheritances, and countless opportunities, only for your grand adventure to end because you accidentally introduced a popular new genre of literature.

The next thing you knew, some powerful senior had locked you in a basement, demanding ten thousand words a day while threatening to withhold your spirit stones until the next volume was finished.

Hmm, that could actually be an arc in a novel.

Ning paused at his own thought.

Wait, why am I giving myself ideas?

Unfortunately, Elder Shen had no intention of allowing him to contemplate the dangers of his newfound literary career.

“Anyway,” the elder asked eagerly, leaning forward once more, “when can I expect the next volume?”

Ning suppressed a sigh. “I'll write it when I have some free time, Elder.”

“No, no, no. You need to understand how to prioritize things properly.” Elder Shen shook his head and raised a finger, adopting the solemn expression of a senior about to impart profound wisdom.

“Cultivation is important, of course, but writing can also be considered a form of cultivation. By expressing your thoughts and emotions through literature, you temper your mind, refine your understanding of human nature, and stabilize your Dao heart.”

He nodded slowly, seemingly growing more convinced of his own reasoning with every word.

“A cultivator who neglects his sentiments will eventually find himself troubled by inner demons. Your writing is a means of achieving balance. You mustn't abandon such an important path!”

Ning nodded thoughtfully. “…I see.”

He did not see at all.

Elder Shen was clearly bullshitting.

And as someone who frequently practiced the very same art, Ning could recognize a fellow practitioner almost immediately.

Unfortunately, Elder Shen was only getting started.

“In fact, I've even prepared some notes!” the elder announced proudly, producing a jade slip from his storage bag. “A few suggestions for possible developments in the upcoming volumes.”

Ning instinctively took a step back.

“E-Elder, I believe creativity should be allowed to develop spontaneously.”

“Nonsense!” Elder Shen waved away his objection. “Look, I've already outlined three possible routes for the story.”

Routes?

Ning suddenly had a very bad premonition.

“One focuses on emotional development, another introduces greater conflict between the characters, and the third…” Elder Shen paused, lowering his voice slightly. “…explores more intimate interactions.”

Ning immediately raised his hand.

“Elder, I just remembered that I came here to select a cultivation technique. I'm afraid I shouldn't delay any longer, so I'll discuss the matter with you another time.”

Before Elder Shen could respond, Ning placed a bottle of Frozen Breath Grain wine on the table as usual, offered a hurried farewell, and made his way deeper into the Scripture Pavilion.

Elder Shen blinked as he watched the young disciple disappear around the corner.

Then he glanced at the bottle of wine and smiled.

“He'll be back soon enough.”

With that, he uncorked the bottle and took a satisfied drink.

...

Deeper inside the Scripture Pavilion, Ning headed toward the shelves containing cultivation manuals.

As usual, he paid little attention to the techniques with grandiose names and even grander descriptions. There had been a time when he genuinely wondered whether some hidden gem might be concealed among them, a technique whose seemingly exaggerated description was actually telling the truth.

Unfortunately, after encountering manuals with names like Immortal Sword Cleave, which supposedly allowed a cultivator to slay a Golden Core expert with a single strike despite being at a lower cultivation realm, he had gradually abandoned that hope.

At this point, he was more inclined to believe that whoever wrote those descriptions had a promising future in the advertising profession.

So, Ning headed straight toward the section containing incomplete manuals.

Most disciples avoided this area, and for good reason. An incomplete cultivation technique could be difficult to practice, and without the missing portions, its usefulness was often severely limited. Some might even contain dangerous flaws that made cultivation risky.

However, that also meant the manuals here were cheaper than their complete counterparts. Some were even classified as Spirit-ranked techniques, despite their incomplete state.

Ning had visited this section many times before, so he already knew what he was looking for.

After searching through the shelves for a short while, he finally located a familiar jade slip.

[Soul Ferrying Scripture]

He picked it up and examined it with interest.

In truth, this hadn't been his original choice.

What Ning had initially wanted was the Earth-ranked Heaven Shattering Fist, the very same technique Xiao Fan had displayed during the Heavenly Origin Tower trial.

Unfortunately, the sect had acted rather quickly. According to what Ning had heard, the moment Xiao Fan demonstrated the technique's first level, the elders realized its potential and promptly removed the manual from this section.

A pity.

After all, anything Xiao Fan used was practically guaranteed to be special.

Ning could only imagine how much attention that display must have attracted.

...

[Soul Ferrying Scripture]

Among the many incomplete techniques available in the Scripture Pavilion, the Soul Ferrying Scripture was somewhat unusual. Its underlying principles and philosophical foundations were largely intact, with the missing portions primarily involving the circulation of qi through specific meridians, the timing of each circulation, and other practical details necessary for cultivation.

This was precisely why Ning had chosen it.

Of all the incomplete martial arts he had examined, only two were related to the soul. The other was the Howling Soul Shaking Technique, which was so heavily fragmented that even its fundamental principles were difficult to understand. Compared to that, the Soul Ferrying Scripture was clearly the more suitable choice for the experiment Ning had in mind.

After settling down with the manual, Ning lit a stick of Mind-Stabilizing Incense and began reading through its contents. With his superior mental strength and the incense helping him maintain his concentration, it didn't take long to finish studying the available material.

“As expected, it's quite incomplete,” Ning muttered, examining the sections where the instructions abruptly ended.

Fortunately, the sect had a convenient system for handling incomplete manuals. Each one included brief descriptions of its missing portions, allowing disciples to recognize corresponding fragments if they encountered them during their travels and exchange them with the sect for rewards.

After reviewing the manual once more, Ning put it away and made his way toward an open field. Although the Soul Ferrying Scripture didn't require much physical movement, he had learned long ago that experimenting with unfamiliar cultivation techniques was best done somewhere spacious, preferably far away from anything expensive or easily destroyed.

Finding a suitable spot, Ning sat down in the lotus position and took a deep breath of the fresh air.

There was something he had wanted to investigate for quite some time.

Currently, Ning's deepest secret was that he wasn't originally from this world. He was a transmigrator, someone who had somehow crossed over from Earth and found himself living in a world of cultivation.

His second deepest secret was his supernatural ability, Know Thyself, along with the mysterious panel that accompanied it. Of course, Ning wasn't the sort of person who believed every secret had to remain hidden forever. Aside from his origins as an otherworlder, he was perfectly willing to reveal certain things about himself if the benefits were substantial enough and the risks could be managed.

But today, he wanted to see whether he could develop his supernatural ability further.

As far as he understood it, Know Thyself had two primary functions. One was its ability to help maintain the stability of his spirit, although that aspect was much less obvious during everyday cultivation. The other, and by far his favorite, was the panel's ability to display his progress and help him determine whether he was advancing along the correct path.

Ning absolutely loved that function. In fact, he had become somewhat obsessed with watching the numbers on his panel increase, finding an almost unreasonable amount of satisfaction in seeing his efforts translated into visible progress.

Or, as he preferred to put it, he loved watching his numbers go brrr.

However, the panel's usefulness had always depended on his ability to make progress in the first place, and that raised an interesting question. Could he use it to determine whether an unfamiliar or incomplete cultivation method was correct?

“It all comes down to this,” Ning muttered before closing his eyes and beginning to circulate his qi according to the instructions recorded in the Soul Ferrying Scripture.

The first five circulations proceeded smoothly, but upon reaching the sixth, he encountered the incomplete portion of the manual. Although the remaining instructions provided some clues about the intended pathway, several crucial details were missing, leaving him uncertain about where the qi should flow next.

Under ordinary circumstances, this would have been the point where a cultivator either searched for the missing fragment or abandoned the technique altogether. Ning, however, intended to see whether he could deduce the missing pathway himself.

Naturally, he wasn't foolish enough to recklessly force large amounts of qi through unfamiliar meridians. Instead, he carefully reduced the amount involved and began testing his deductions, relying on Know Thyself to monitor his internal condition.

With his first hypothesis in mind, Ning guided a thin stream of qi along the pathway he believed was most likely to be correct. The qi moved through his meridians, and a faint disturbance rippled through the surrounding air, but even after waiting for several moments, his panel showed no change.

“Not this pathway,” he muttered, taking out his notebook and crossing off the first possibility.

His initial attempt had failed, but that was hardly surprising. If reconstructing incomplete techniques were so easy, the Scripture Pavilion wouldn't have an entire section dedicated to them.

He adjusted his hypothesis and tried again, only to receive the same result. The third attempt failed as well, followed by the fourth and fifth. Sometimes the qi circulated smoothly but produced no meaningful result, while other attempts revealed subtle inconsistencies in its movement, forcing Ning to stop and reconsider the pathway or timing.

Each failure was carefully recorded in his notebook, along with the corresponding circulation route, qi flow, and any unusual sensations he had experienced. As the hours passed, his notes gradually filled with rejected hypotheses and possible alternatives, yet Ning showed no intention of stopping.

By the time the sun began to set, he was still sitting in the same field, occasionally opening his eyes to record another failed attempt before returning to his cultivation. Night eventually fell, and the stars emerged overhead, but even after spending the entire day experimenting, he had yet to produce the result he wanted.

“Three hundred and twentieth attempt,” Ning muttered, his voice carrying a hint of exhaustion.

Taking another breath, he gathered a small amount of qi and began circulating it according to his latest hypothesis. The first five circulations proceeded as usual, and upon reaching the incomplete section, he carefully guided the qi through a slightly different pathway, adjusting its speed at several points based on what he had learned from his previous failures.

A faint tremor passed through the surrounding air, but Ning ignored it, keeping his attention fixed on the panel floating before his eyes.

Then, just as he was preparing himself for another disappointment, the numbers finally changed.

[Soul Ferrying Scripture: Starting (1.3333 → 1.3334)]

For several seconds, Ning simply stared at the panel, almost unable to believe what he was seeing. Then his eyes widened, and he shot to his feet, throwing both arms into the air.

“IT WORKED! YES!!!”

After hundreds of failed attempts, the number had finally increased. It was an almost laughably small improvement, barely enough to register even with the panel's increasingly precise measurements, but to Ning, that tiny change was worth more than all the progress he had made throughout the day.

Because it meant his hypothesis had worked, or at least that he had managed to deduce one correct step.

Ning laughed for a while longer before finally collapsing onto the grass, utterly exhausted but wearing a satisfied grin.

As his cultivation improved, the panel had gradually begun displaying his progress with greater precision, allowing him to notice improvements that would otherwise have been practically impossible to distinguish. Until now, he had mostly treated that feature as a convenient way to measure his advancement, but this experiment had revealed another possible application.

What if he could use the panel to reconstruct incomplete cultivation techniques?

One of the greatest limitations Ning faced as an ordinary cultivator was his lack of a powerful family background. Unlike descendants of established cultivation clans, he had no inherited library of secret arts and no ancestral techniques passed down through generations.

Even within the Pure Qi Sect, acquiring powerful techniques required considerable merit points or exceptional contributions, and anything genuinely valuable was unlikely to remain freely accessible for long.

Fortunately, Ning might have discovered another way.

The greatest difficulty in reconstructing an incomplete technique was uncertainty. Even if a cultivator managed to deduce a plausible circulation pathway, how could they be certain it was correct? A smooth circulation didn't necessarily mean the technique was functioning as intended, and a mistaken pathway might produce no immediate reaction or even lead to hidden problems that only emerged after prolonged cultivation.

Without the original instructions or some reliable means of verification, a cultivator could spend years experimenting without ever knowing whether they were making genuine progress.

Ning's panel, however, offered him something ordinary cultivators lacked: feedback.

If his proficiency increased, even by the tiniest amount, he would know that his experiment had produced genuine progress toward mastering the technique. Of course, that didn't mean every successful attempt would immediately reveal the complete circulation pathway. He would still need to compare results, identify which adjustments had worked, and gradually reconstruct the missing portions through further experimentation.

But having a reliable way to recognize progress changed everything. With enough patience, he could potentially reconstruct certain incomplete techniques through trial and error.

“Well… assuming I have enough time to waste on them,” Ning muttered.

There were, naturally, several drawbacks. First, the process was painfully slow. He had spent an entire day testing hundreds of possibilities, only to achieve a minuscule increase in proficiency. Completing an entire technique could easily require months or even years of experimentation, depending on how much was missing.

Second, his method had clear limitations. The panel could help him verify practical progress, but it couldn't simply conjure missing knowledge out of thin air. If a technique lacked its fundamental principles, philosophical foundations, or essential explanations, Ning would have no reliable basis from which to begin reconstructing it.

For now, techniques with relatively complete foundations and gaps primarily involving qi circulation seemed the most promising candidates, and even then, success was far from guaranteed.

Still, Ning was more than satisfied, especially since the technique he had chosen was related to the soul.

One of the greatest obstacles to reaching the Seventh Stage of Qi Condensation was awakening spiritual sense, and among the various methods available, cultivating certain soul-related techniques was considered one of the more orthodox approaches. Unfortunately, such techniques were exceptionally rare, especially those suitable for cultivators who had yet to awaken their spiritual sense.

That was precisely why Ning had gone to such lengths to obtain and experiment with the Soul Ferrying Scripture. Although his master had already assured him that she possessed a method to help him break through, Ning preferred to have an alternative prepared.

After all, there was nothing wrong with having a backup plan, especially when the primary plan involved his master's alchemy.

“Speaking of which… Xiao Fan is even luckier than I imagined,” Ning muttered, gazing up at the stars.

Even with the assistance of his panel, reconstructing a single incomplete technique would require an enormous amount of time and effort. Yet Xiao Fan had somehow managed to comprehend the first level of an Earth-ranked technique and put it into practice.

And Ning had a fairly good idea of why.

Luck. Pure, outrageous, heaven-defying protagonist luck.

Back on Earth, Ning had read enough cultivation novels to recognize that protagonists came in all sorts of varieties. Some were reincarnators carrying the memories of past lives, others were regressors who had returned to an earlier point in time, and some possessed mysterious systems or ancient inheritances.

From what he had observed so far, Xiao Fan seemed to belong to the classic Waste Wood Flow.

These protagonists usually started with terrible talent, no resources, and little to no background. They were looked down upon by their peers, humiliated by arrogant young masters, and constantly underestimated by practically everyone around them, yet somehow, they always managed to stumble upon opportunities that others could spend entire lifetimes searching for.

Why? Because they all possessed one overwhelmingly powerful advantage.

Luck.

The sort of luck that made one wonder whether they were Heaven's illegitimate children.

A priceless treasure might appear beneath an ordinary rock, an ancient inheritance could be discovered in some forgotten cave, and whenever they encountered an impossible problem, the solution would somehow present itself at precisely the right moment.

As far as Ning was concerned, Xiao Fan could very well have comprehended the Heaven Shattering Fist through a similarly ridiculous chain of coincidences. Perhaps he had coincidentally chosen the correct circulation pathway on his first attempt, coincidentally adjusted the timing perfectly, and coincidentally managed to comprehend the technique's first level without making a single mistake.

Yup. That's a lot of coincidences.

And even if Xiao Fan had relied on some mysterious cheat to accomplish it, wasn't obtaining such a cheat in the first place another manifestation of his absurd luck?

Ning narrowed his eyes slightly at the thought, but he had no intention of investigating further for now. There were certainly things about Xiao Fan that he didn't understand, and perhaps one day he would uncover the truth behind his friend's extraordinary fortune.

At the moment, however, he was far too exhausted to concern himself with someone else's protagonist privileges.

Besides, after spending an entire day attempting to reconstruct a single circulation pathway, Ning had developed a newfound appreciation for the luxury of simply lying down and doing absolutely nothing.
