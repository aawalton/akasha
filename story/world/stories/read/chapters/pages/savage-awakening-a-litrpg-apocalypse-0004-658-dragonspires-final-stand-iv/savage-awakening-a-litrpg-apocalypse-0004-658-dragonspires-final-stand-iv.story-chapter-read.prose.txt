Malzareth hadn't taken a single strike so far.

Despite the appearance of the dragon and its clear power, Zane couldn't find it in himself to respect it.

He wasn't certain exactly why. Maybe it was just a matter of character... it was also Noughtfire's attitude toward Malzareth, he recalled.

He still had faith in the plan.

As the fighting progressed, though, there seemed to be kinks emerging in its execution.

Command seemed to think that the power differential between them and Malzareth was higher than anticipated—and now Malzareth's spears were making a clear difference on the battlefield. After the spears gathered a few hundred shards of Destruction, they'd drive down without warning, nearly catching a few elites. If the spears didn't land they'd sink into reality itself and act as 'buff stations' for the Summons around them—refilling their essence reserves and shard endowments.

At times the spears simply burst without warning, turning into pillars of light. Which did manage to catch a few elites.

"It's toying with us," said the Huntress. She phased into lightning, avoiding one such pillar.

"We need to make it uncomfortable," Thalia agreed. They started coordinating on a private line on just how to do that.

The dragon continued to defend with relative ease. Dark essence shields popped up all around it. It easily deflected a series of light pillars from the Constellation King while blocking a hail of daggers from the other side. Malzareth was only untouched because it seemed to clock every little essence action on the battlefield. Its perception and its raw mental capacities were as advertised.

All its downed Summons were quickly replaced too—an endless stream of high-tier Monsters kept marching out of its Universe.

It kept up its musings all the while.

There is, in this Universe, a phenomenon known as the Fate Sign, it said. It is the Universe's acknowledgment of an event so significant, so influential in Fate, that it warrants an acknowledgment from the highest Law itself, which is the Universe. Such a sign graced this Universe at the instance of my birth... it precipitated a civil war in Father's Court over whether I'd be permitted in the line of succession.

Such a sign has never graced this little training Galaxy.

And you still believe that I shall fall here. It seemed amused by the thought. That I, with a Fate like mine, am merely the stepping stone in the path of another... that such a thing is even a possibility within Fate. Do you understand that this was merely a game for me? This Galaxy.

It was silent for a few seconds. These words are wasted, it decided. It is like explaining beauty to a—

At this point a nearly invisible blade stroke somehow broke past the pack—a blade stroke containing well over 800 shards of Destruction—and almost caught Malzareth in the head.

Malzareth summoned a crystalline wall to block it, like a dark version of the Mirror Dragon's strongest shield. Then its eyes narrowed at the attacker.

"You are less interesting than you like to believe," Striker said dryly.

The Sage laughed, quite approvingly—"Shut the hells up, godsdamn!"

He thrust for Malzareth's head too.

Then two of the thickest Destruction-spears dislodged from the array above and struck at the two of them. Two pillars of immense light clipped the air they'd just occupied.

I shall get to you, Malzareth promised Striker.

Then it turned its eyes on the Sage too—I shall get to you too.

The rest of the elites weren't quite as light-hearted as Striker or the Sage, who seemed light-hearted in pretty much any occasion. They were still quite focused, by the looks of it. They seemed to mostly ignore what Malzareth was saying.

On the outside, Zane was currently driving a shining wedge through the Monster hordes—hordes that were now focused just on shattering this spatial cage.

The cage wouldn't hold for long. It was significantly less durable than the Greatwards. Zane estimated, jaw clenched, that it'd hold a quarter-hour, maximum, before it was finished.

Dragonspire's side seemed to feel the urgency.

The pace of their attacks started to speed up.

A few more attempts to break through the Summons' lines took place in those next few minutes. The Constellation King and a few Guild folk tried ramming through the center, which almost worked, but didn't quite. Zane saw Noughtfire and Striker actually manage to take down the Prime-level soul dragon—but another Prime-level dragon, almost identical to the first, exited Malzareth's Universe just as the first went down.

There seemed to be a quota on the number of Prime-level summons Malzareth could keep active at any one time. It also came as something of a surprise to Command that it could re-summon a Prime with this kind of ease.

Outside the spatial cage Zane still kept up a chain of Star-Crushing Slashes, trying to clear as much of the perimeter as he could manage.

He actually managed to take down quite a few Greater Endbringers in this time—twelve, in just that quick span—because their focus simply wasn't on him. A stray thought flickered at the back of his mind; it was almost a shame the Credits wouldn't matter since this was the final battle.

You have still not forced me to try, said Malzareth. It seemed it'd decided to just ignore Striker and the Sage's words and carry on.

Then—This is not all you have, it said sharply. You have held back your Supremes. Your attacks thus far have uniformly failed. Why have you not used them?

If the question was directed at Reina—which Zane assumed it was—she didn't reply.

He just noticed she'd sent an avatar of herself into the Cage, standing near the back.

Perhaps it's to drain my reserves, it mused. Perhaps it is an attempt to draw out my own Supremes first.

Reina's eyes narrowed slightly, and it seemed to realize it'd guessed at least partly right. There was quite some satisfaction in its tone.

Did you believe that exchange—that War's opening skirmish—means you've outwitted Lord Malzareth?

Malzareth spoke with the same, seemingly detached curiosity. You truly believe this, it said. Such pride. You think you have this situation perfectly under your control. You have a perfect illusion in your mind... but hubris is a fatal flaw. Especially so in those who lack the faculties to justify it.

Zane's brow furrowed. It could be talking about itself, he felt.

You are out of your depth, woman, it said. What is about to happen to your kind lies squarely at your feet.

Zane also wasn't sure why it was singling out Reina all of a sudden; it seemed to despise her more than it despised even Noughtfire or the Sage, which felt a bit inexplicable to him.

"Let's move to phase II," Reina said into her necklace piece. It seemed she'd elected to simply ignore Malzareth. "Diamond Force—"

Your attempt to draw out my Supreme. The plan cannot proceed without it. Is that correct?

Reina went silent, frowning.

Let me save you some time.

And then, all six of Malzareth's eyes exploded with silver.

Six massive images of its eyes superimposed over the eyes themselves.

Then a presence descended over all eighteen elites in its presence. A presence Zane could only partly feel, as distant as he was—but even he was struck by a sudden, instinctive urge to kneel. Like it was impossible to do anything except kneel.

It broke over him as quickly as it'd come, though; he felt the sensation for a split second, and then it was like it could no longer touch him at all. He knew his Title had activated, stopping the command.

Inside the cage, a grayish light stabbed through the souls of all those present, and silver hardened over their souls like a shell... even Reina froze, a look of surprise on her face. Her body outside was affected too; she went still.

In the same instant, light-blue System shields popped up over the gathered elites. Everyone except Reina's avatar, which didn't need protection—and Reina's true body had bodyguards outside.

These temporary System shields, automatically activated, would each be among the most valuable life-saving treasures a Faction could offer, pre-War.

Was this part of your plan? said Malzareth, rhetorically. It appears I have moved out of turn. None of the elites could answer.

Its eyes moved over the shields. Draw out my Supreme, and wield System shields to outlast its window... should I be impressed? It is a child's idea of clever.

It turned its gaze away from the elites. It didn't seem interested in attacking them in this state; again this seemed to surprise Command a bit. They'd expected it to at least try to break the System shields, which they expected was nigh impossible.

This entire scheme hinges on your ability to place me under threat. Plainly, you cannot. And if you cannot, then you have simply abandoned your armies and your home—left Dragonspire itself vulnerable... for very, very little. It is a gamble which must succeed. And it has not.

It seemed to be enjoying itself.

Perhaps I shall act out of turn a little more.

It turned its attention to its army, which was massed outside.

Does it appear that I need your protection? it said scornfully. You may cease your attacks. Your energies are better directed elsewhere.

Then it cast its gaze outside—at the rest of Dragonspire, in its entirety.

Crush them.

***

This was a scenario Dragonspire had considered and planned for. Just an unlikely one.

Granted, it wasn't something they imagined occurring under these specific circumstances. Malzareth did catch them off guard, and it certainly wasn’t an ideal scenario at all.

Still, even as Malzareth gave the command, Dragonspire's armies started a full-scale, coordinated retreat. New last-line-of-defense traps sprung up along the front lines, trying to halt the Monsters' advance... but ultimately the fate of Dragonspire's armies, in this moment, would rest on just one thing.

The capacity of Dragonspire's cornerstones to hold and fight back, so the rest of Dragonspire's army would be spared.

All down the lines, the rest of Dragonspire's portal charm-holding elites threw themselves into this fray at once.

They knew the kinds of forces they were up against.

In that first few minutes of the crush alone, a dozen Hegemon rankers perished, like that. But they knew what they were fighting for, and each life spared tens of thousands. Reina's true body was whisked away under Silver Wind protection.

Malzareth's Supreme still held, still freezing the T4+ elites inside the spatial cage. They could do nothing but watch as some of Dragonspire's finest gave themselves to the battle. If they didn't fall, they were forced to give ground, one by one—still fighting hard as they did.

There were two points of light left—one in the north-northwest; the other far in the southeast; the one in the southeast was the #19-ranked Constellation Princess, who was forced to retreat—she said over charm that she hoped to slow the enemy's advance as she fled.

Then there was a single point of light remaining.

A light that seemed to have taken on the entire burden of the defense of the northern lines, as their other defenders succumbed or retreated...

The elites, and Malzareth, watched in silence as 6 Mirror Drakes, 4 Iron Gold, and nearly a dozen other Class-I and II Greater Endbringers threw themselves at just one man.

A man who cut them down, one after another.

Around him—for clicks and clicks—was a brilliant graveyard.

There were signs of heavy Star-Crushing slashes all over the place, and the light of Supernova Law lingered on the corpses.

Zane Walker stood there, bleeding from wounds all down his chest, arms, and back—a wound slashing over his brow, so that he had to wipe the blood out of his eyes again and again; his chest heaved, over and over.

His fists were clenched tight on two chains, and his Axes were pyres of pure light.

Malzareth was silent. Its thoughts were inscrutable.

And then—

Stegar, it said. Subdue him. Do not irreparably break him. I plan to repurpose this body.

Prime entered the fray from either side. Four of Stegar's bodies engaged him, even as another twelve Greater Endbringers attacked him at once.

There was a silence as they all watched... and then—

Is this a joke? said Malzareth. Cromm. Finish this farce. Break, do not kill.

The Meat King grunted and joined the assault.

And this time, they managed to break through.

The Meat King specifically was able to break Zane's body in ways that should've easily halted him—put him out of commission for weeks, if not permanently.

It should've halted him.

It broke his body, but in some ways, when it came to Zane... that was the least important thing to break.

The resistance it was facing—the sheer heart Zane was displaying—seemed to surprise even the Meat King.

And then—with visible incredulity—

Ghoul Doctor.

"Yes, Master." The Ghoul Doctor bowed.

Three cursed needles, down Zane's spine, with passionless accuracy.

One at the neck, two in the mid-back. And then, a stack of over twenty Iron Gold—the entirety of the remainder of their crew, after what Zane had just done to them—piled on top of him.

And then, at last...

The man was still.

In the end, three Primes, and nearly fifty Greater Endbringers, and it was done.

Malzareth turned back to the elites, visibly slightly irked.

It still gathered itself; it took a breath to do so.

Then it addressed Reina's frozen form again.

Sit with this feeling, it told her. It is a minuscule fraction of the emotions I was made to feel, an infinitesimal unit of what I was made to lose.

And then it looked at her face; it seemed surprised—I do hope I have not broken your spirit... it seems I truly have overestimated you.

The Supreme's bindings visibly weakened on her in the Astral Plane, letting her speak. And?

Reina wielded this privilege to level a glare at the dragon that would've made most elites flinch.

"It took you all that to take down one man," she said.
