Trevor had offered for Croal to drop them off back at the villa mostly so that the dragon would know where they were staying, but also because they had made such a spectacle that the people of Paladona would absolutely swamp the newlyweds if they saw them return on top of their largest citizen. Adabelle hadn’t argued, either, and they soon found themselves waving off their new friend from their private beach.

Even after Croal had flown away and returned to his caldera, presumably to speak with High Priest Worth and the other members of the clergy about the rest of the ceremony, they remained standing on the beach. The waves had returned in the dragon’s absence, lapping gently against the sand. It was a nice day, if a little hot, but the breeze felt good against their skin.

“Trevor,” Adabelle said first, breaking the silence.

“Yeah?”

“We just rode a dragon.”

“Twice!” he exclaimed with bubbling enthusiasm.

“We did it twice!” she repeated with a laugh. “We rode a dragon twice!”

“That was amazing! Paladona looked so good from up above.”

“And then the air bubble underwater? We could see so far. That must have been a little magic, too.”

“It had to have been,” Trevor agreed. “The water here is clear, but not that clear, you know?”

“Oh, I know,” Adabelle replied.

Silence once again descended on them. Clay moved onto Trevor’s shoulder, still in the form of a long dragon, and stared at the island with them. All three perked up when Croal’s head rose above the caldera, but he lowered it again not long after.

“I gotta say, I’m kind of at a loss for what to do now,” the courier said sheepishly, scratching the back of his head. “What can we do to top that?”

“A few things come to mind,” Adabelle said as she moved closer to him. She rested her chin on the shoulder opposite of Clay. “But you keep saying you want to go offer a platinum to the alumrae, and now might be your best chance to do it.”

“We’d have to go all the way back to town,” he replied, wrapping an arm around his wife’s waist. “Plus, yeah, I definitely said it, but I also don’t want to abuse a luck mechanic like that. I’m plenty lucky as is. And that last bit was because of Glyrphizith, not the alumrae. It could have easily happened to you if you were alone.”

“I wouldn't have met Croal if I were alone, but what you say is partially true,” the demonkin said. She glanced back at the villa. “Do you want to go back to the city? Or stay here?”

Trevor thought about it for a moment. “Stay, I think,” he answered thoughtfully. “We haven’t really had a proper beach day, and I’ve got at least four different meals from Paladona for us to try.”

“Then I’m going to go change. Will you set something up for us?”

“You’ve got it, wife lady,” he replied with a smile.

She gave him a look at the new nickname before kissing his cheek and heading inside. Trevor followed after her, but only far enough to grab two chairs and a table that had been put on the side of the villa. They were meant for the beach, and that’s where he moved them to.

“You want to stick around or explore?” he asked Clay, reaching up to scratch under his familiar’s chin.

The earth elemental thought about it for a moment before jumping off of his shoulder and belly flopping onto the sand. Trevor laughed at the sight, feeling the amusement through his familiar’s bond, and they slowly disappeared into the beach. He could still tell where they were thanks to the connection they shared, and the familiar was digging pretty deep.

The courier idly wondered what they would find here considering what they had brought back last time as he set up the table and chairs. Activating Quick Change, his body became cloaked in shadows. Trevor’s clothes disappeared in an instant, replaced by a pair of red swim trunks. The shadows vanished once the process was over, and he began applying sun screen.

It didn’t take long for Adabelle to return. She wore a light blue bikini with a white sarong and smiled as she approached. Her eyes lingered on his chest, something he had come to expect from her, before sitting down.

“So, what are we having?” she asked, not looking up at his face.

“Ma’am,” Trevor said, gesturing upwards. “My eyes are up here.”

“That’s right. It would be rather odd if they were anywhere else,” Adabelle replied, her gaze stubbornly unmoving.

With a chuckle, he shook his head. Trevor offered her the sunscreen after putting some more on his hand. He was done with it except for his back, anyway. Once she took the bottle, he moved behind her and started rubbing the places it was harder for her to reach with anything but her tail.

“I’m thinking rice, beans, and that really nice looking pork we stumbled across,” the courier said as he rubbed sunscreen on his wife’s back. “Maybe some croquettes on the side?”

“That sounds lovely,” she replied. “Thank you, by the way.”

“No worries,” he said, smiling as he finished up.

Sitting down across from her, with his back facing away from the sun so he wouldn’t have to worry about the lack of sunscreen, he summoned the dishes they had purchased during their wanderings yesterday. The smell immediately overpowered the sea breeze, causing his stomach to rumble and his mouth to water. Silverware and drinks, a local citrus tea, came next, and they started to eat.

“That was amazing!” Lora’s voice interrupted as they put the first bites into their mouth. She had appeared by the villas and was rapidly approaching them.

“Good afternoon, Lora,” Adabelle greeted after she swallowed her food. “We weren’t expecting you.”

“Yeah, sorry, sorry. I saw Croal heading this way with you two, then leaving without you, and I’d thought I’d pop in to check on how you were doing. Really good, obviously.”

“Oh yeah, it was a blast,” Trevor responded as put his fork down. “We did kind of volunteer you to deliver a message back to him in the future. You know, eventually, when you revisit Paladona. He’ll have it for us in the morning.”

“Just a message? Yeah, that shouldn’t be a problem,” Lora agreed with a nod. “What’s up?”

“Croal wants to meet with Glyrphizith,” Adabelle answered. “He smelled her on us and heard us talking about her while he was performing the ceremony. Became really excited about the potential opportunity to meet another of his kind.”

“Aww, that’s actually kind of cute,” the teleporter said with a giggle as she patted the ribbon at her side. “Yeah, I can do that. Easy peasy, lemon squeezy. I know he lost his mother before his first century and doesn’t travel a lot, if at all, so that should be good for him. You know, if she wants. I don’t know much about Glyrphizith except that she’s apparently a sweetheart.”

“That’s exactly what she is,” Trevor confirmed with a smile. “Would you like to sit? I can get a chair for you.”

“Nah, I’m good,” Lora answered with a shake of her head. Instead, she lifted her skirt slightly and knelt by the table to rest against it. “Like I said, I just wanted to check up, and tell you about Surtla.”

“Ugh, Surtla,” the courier groaned. “Did I tell you he was rude to my wife?”

“You did.”

“Alright, good.”

“I think it’s safe to say that we’ve got the upper hand now, so you can let that go, honey,” Adabelle said.

“Maybe,” he grumbled, making no promises.

“You’ve definitely gained the upper hand, even if I weren’t already going to talk to the boss about him,” Lora said, shimmying forward slightly as if excited to dish out some gossip. “So, as you probably guessed, I did invite him to the Hello Sea so he would be out of your hair. He jumped at the chance to get closer to the boss’ liaison.”

“We did figure that out, and thank you,” the demonkin replied as she smiled at her husband. “I’ve also found out what it takes for the son of Ander to hold a real grudge. He’s done it before for laughs, but I think this one might actually stick.”

“I’ve held grudges before,” Trevor complained, pouting. “They usually just don’t mean anything to me so I forget about them quickly. It’s different when the people I like are treated poorly. And don’t think it doesn’t go the other way. You’ve gotten indignant about the way I’ve been treated before, too.”

“Yes, but I’m actually mean,” Adabelle teased, resting her elbow on the table and her cheek in her hand as she watched him. “Unlike you, who’s just a little snarky sometimes.”

“If anyone makes you act mean, then they deserve it,” he replied with a shake of his head. “Like Surtla. Lora, you said something about us having the upper hand now?”

“I sure did,” she answered with a laugh. “Anyway, eat while I talk. I don’t want to interrupt your meal, and I’ll be out of your hair soon enough.”

“Sure,” Trevor said as he resumed eating, and Adabelle joined him.

“So, yes, Surtla had a lot of things to say about you,” Lora started, resting her chin on her arms as she looked up at them. “They weren’t very flattering. He complained about a super rare drop that you wouldn’t give up. Kept yapping about that darn hat, saying that none of the other Summoned refused to give up their extra rewards before. You were completely unreasonable, based on his tale.”

“I’m sure some have parted ways with their trophies, but I doubt that no other Summoned had declined his offer before,” Adabelle said between bites.

“That’s what he claimed,” the other woman replied with a shrug. “When he tried to bad mouth you two and your work ethic, clearly trying to get word back to the boss to ditch you both, I hit him with the all-time finisher.”

“And what was that?” Trevor asked.

Lora reached for her bag, pulled out a picture, and put it on the table. The newlyweds leaned forward to get a better look, and Adabelle laughed. “Oh, I would have loved to have seen his face when he saw this.”

“Me, too,” the courier said as he picked it up.

The picture was from their wedding. Specifically, when Klar was handing Trevor the rings. The minotaur was standing there with a big grin on his face as plain as day.

A similar, if somehow bigger, smile was plastered on the groom, too. Trevor knew that was exactly the kind of goofy grin Lailah was going to tease him about.

“The expression on his face made me wish I had a camera myself,” Lora giggled. “You went from ‘just a guy who worked for the boss’ all the way up to ‘oh no, the boss actually cares about these people’ in one fell swoop! The way he started stammering and tripping over his words to run things back would have been impressive if I wasn’t already tired of the guy.”

“Sorry for making you put up with him,” Trevor said with an apologetic smile.

“No worries!” she replied with a dismissive wave. Suddenly, she straightened up, still kneeling at the table, and offered a sloppy salute. “It’s my duty to make sure you have the best honeymoon, and if that means putting up with a bit of a downer then I will happily do so. Not that I think he could have ruined your mood after that. Riding Croal? An actual dragon? I’ll be honest, I feel bad for the next leg of your journey.”

“I don’t suppose you would be willing to tell us about it, would you?” Adabelle asked. Prying was not beneath her. “We will be taking off tomorrow, and it’ll be something to look forward to.”

“You just want me to spill the beans,” Lora accused.

“Yes,” the noblewoman admitted. “That’s why I asked.”

“So bold,” she giggled. “I suppose I can tell you, but you gotta really want to know.”

“I wouldn’t have asked if I didn’t want the answer. Trevor?”

The courier quickly swallowed his food as Adabelle and Lora looked at him. “Yeah, I’m curious, too.”

“Alright, but only a little bit,” the Summoned allowed before leaning forward conspiratorially. “Okay, so get this. Tomorrow morning, after Croal appears hopefully early because we have a tight, tight, tight timetable, I’m taking you to Garend!”

“Oh, cool!” Trevor started strong, then gave her an apologetic smile. “I don’t think I’ve heard of that place. What’s Garend?”

“It’s the easternmost country on the continent,” Adabelle answered as she leaned back in her chair. “It’s small and was made as a place where rebels and outcasts could go if they wanted more freedom. For the past hundred and fifty years or so, though, it’s become a major hub for trade and transportation since they’re the closest to Aletander.”

“Aletander?” he asked to confirm, and she nodded. “And that’s the country of the demons? The human nations used to be at war with them?”

“That’s right on all counts,” Adabelle said, smiling. “They’re across the ocean on another continent, but they’re also amongst the most technologically advanced societies in the world. Now that the war has been over for so long and the hostilities have ended, they needed a place to trade that they could trust. Since Garend was completely neutral while everything was going on, mostly because they were too small to do anything, they became the gateway between the west and the east.”

Trevor nodded appreciatively at this information. “Okay, that is pretty cool. Not so much the war part, but everything else.”

The noblewoman turned towards Lora. “So if we’re going to Garend, it seems as though our next logical stop would be Aletander. The question is, are we teleporting across the sea? Or taking a ship?”

“Dang, they warned me that you were a sharp cookie but I didn’t think you’d get all that from knowing one place,” the Summoned laughed. “I was saying it was a shame that you just got done flying on a dragon because you’re not going on any regular ship. No ma’am and no sir. You’re going on an airship!”

“Airship?” Adabelle repeated, surprised. “Those are rare.”

“And awesome,” Trevor added, giddy at the thought. “I used to love the concept of airships. Still do. That sounds like a great trip.”

“It will be, because it’s not just any airship,” Lora said, standing up and stretching. “The boss and I got you a suite on the premier demon airship, The Red Dream! The most luxurious airborne anything this side of anywhere! You’ll board tomorrow, spend two full days traveling, and arrive on the fourth. I guarantee you ain’t never had an experience like this.”

“Except with the dragon,” Adabelle stated.

Lora pouted. “Except with the dragon.”

“It sounds really great, though,” Trevor offered.

“Yeah!” she agreed enthusiastically. “Alright, I’m going to go before I give anything else away. I’ll see you two in the morning!”

In a flash, she disappeared, leaving nothing but a set of footprints in the sand. Trevor and Adabelle looked at each other. A dragon was a dragon, but an airship was an airship. They both started talking at once, excited for the next day.
