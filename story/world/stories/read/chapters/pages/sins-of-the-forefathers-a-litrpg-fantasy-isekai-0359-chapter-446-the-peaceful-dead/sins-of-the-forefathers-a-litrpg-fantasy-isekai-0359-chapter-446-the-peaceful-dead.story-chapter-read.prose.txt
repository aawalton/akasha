By this point in my time on Vereden, I had been in more than one Netherim ruin. In both of those places, the architecture had been fairly familiar to me. To my Terran-born sensibilities, even through their decay, they had looked futuristic. I had even seen an example of Netherim construction that had been maintained by those strange little…robots near the core of the bunker. Smooth white panels of a metal I was unfamiliar with, fluorescent lighting, combined with a level of technological advancement to render me amazed.

That was Netherim construction as I understood it.

This…this was something entirely different.

Maybe five minutes in Sylvia, Fade, and my exploration of the darkened, Death Aether-filled space, I stopped us abruptly in the middle of the hallway. Sylvia and Fade continued on for a moment, only to turn and look at me in puzzlement. I was barely paying attention to them.

Instead, I was gazing at the wall, laying a confused hand on its surface.

“This…wasn’t made by the Netherim…” I murmured out loud, as I finally gave voice to my suspicions.

Because there was no way that this place had been hewn from Netherim hands. And I do mean hewn, in the roughest sense of the word.

The wall I had my hand resting on…wasn’t made of the same durable, shining white metal as the bunker had been, once upon a time. It wasn’t even the corroded, half-falling-apart steel found in the cursed section of the bunker, or the neglected halls under Hollow Hill.

No, the tunnel we found ourselves in was nothing more than a roughly carved-out stone passageway. It was barely even shaped like a proper tunnel. The entire length we had walked down so far was nothing more than an uneven hole through the rock that looked like it had been carved with pickaxes. All three of us needed to be careful as we walked down it, just so we weren’t sliced to ribbons by jagged hunks of stone sticking out of the wall. That was made all the more difficult by the fact that this ‘tunnel’ was barely wide enough for us to walk down one by one. If Sylvia and I had tried to walk side by side, we would have suffered a death by a thousand cuts.

This wasn’t a secret Netherim repository like I had thought it was.

But if they hadn’t created this place…who had?

Goddamnit, I should have known. The door itself was a clue. It had been too roughly hewn, too unremarkable to be something made by the Netherim.

“I was wondering that,” Sylvia murmured, carefully stepping closer to me and unintentionally echoing my own thoughts. “None of this has either matched your descriptions or what I saw beneath Hollow Hill. Is this Dwarven instead?”

I immediately shook my head, dragging my eyes away from one particularly sharp spike in the wall. “No, think of the height of the tunnel. We may only be able to walk single file, but it’s still high enough for us to walk comfortably. If the Dwarves had created this at any point in time, the ceiling would be lower.”

“But who else would have done this?” Fade asked from behind me. I didn’t need to see him to read the confusion in his voice. “I mean, there was a Dwarven town right above this place. If not them, then who?”

Not Humans, they had never advanced this far north to own this territory, in all their fractious history with the Dwarves. Not the Elves, either. I had a hard time imagining them ever building this deep. Then…

“Could this be something done by the Spirits?” I whispered, almost to myself.

“What?” Fade startled at my words.

I took a deep breath, turning troubled eyes upon the wall once more. “There are only a few options for who could have done this. One, this could be something created by the Lost-”

Fucking Gnomes, long since genocided by a Calamity.

“-but I doubt that, considering they were meant to be just as small as Dwarves. The only other option is the Spirits. Neither the Human, the Dwarves, or the Elves could have reasonably created this place. So that leaves…”

“Spirits,” Sylvia finished for me quietly. “Nathan, I don’t know much about them. What kind of Spirit would live underground like this?”

I let out a long, slow sigh of realization as I heard Fade’s breathing pick up behind me. If I didn’t know any better, it almost sounded like he was trying to rein in anxiety. Still, I didn’t feel anything abnormal over our bond, so I had to assume he had it under control.

But I really understood just why he might have been anxious.

“Orus,” I said quietly. “The Lord of the Mountain. One of the five Great Spirits…and one of the two who betrayed us all to help bring about the Skyfall. That’s…that’s my guess.”

Silence fell among us, then. A few still moments went by before Sylvia broke it. “Ah.” She said carefully. “We should go, then…shouldn’t we?”

As if on cue, I heard the rumbling of stone behind us. I carefully looked over my shoulder, and through the glow of my light Skill, was just barely able to see movement as the stone door slid closed.

Morbidly, I couldn’t help but notice that the movement was much smoother going down than it had been going up.

There was no point trying to reach it before it closed. Not only were our environs hostile to even the idea of swift movement, we also wouldn’t have been able to reach it in time anyway. When at last the door slid closed, the resulting silence in the tunnel was much more sinister.

At least, to me it was.

“T-That isn’t good…” I heard Fade mentally stutter, sounding far more shaken than I ever had before.

Ah, so it wasn’t just me.

I took a deep breath and steeled my nerves. “One moment,” I murmured, bringing two fingers up to rest on my forehead. “Let me see if I can find out anything about this.”

I concentrated upon a very specific link, something wholly different from my Familiar bond.

“Anima?” I broadcast along it. “Do you have a moment?”

Silence. No response after several moments of waiting. This wasn’t completely out of the ordinary. Sometimes Anima was just busy with…whatever it was she was doing, down in the core. Contemplating magma flows, maybe? I don’t know, but the point was she sometimes took a bit to answer me.

However, she had never failed to answer me after I had called for her more than once.

And I just called for her three times in a row, growing more concerned as I did.

Still, no response.

For some reason, Anima couldn’t answer me. Maybe she was just too busy to spare a moment. After all, it was…plausible that she was speaking to Nehushtan once more. She was the one who had set up our little rendezvous, in the end.

But my instincts were telling me that she couldn’t respond. Either she was being prevented from doing so, or the far more likely option…

I couldn’t reach her from here.

I took a long, deep breath, held it for several seconds…and breathed out whatever anxieties I might have about the situation.

Time for your game face, Nathan. You had stumbled into a situation here.

It remained to be seen if it was malicious or not.

“I can’t reach Anima,” I said calmly, opening my eyes.

Fade’s breath hitched, and I almost absentmindedly reached out to rest my hand on his head. All the while, I was meeting Sylvia’s eyes as they narrowed in a suspicion that I shared. “Enemy action?” She asked shortly.

“Unknown, proceed with caution,” I replied, matching her tone.

The both of us understood how to act under pressure. Hell, we had, together, done so for months within the city of Elderwyck.

It was time for business.

And that business was finding out just what the Hell was going on.

It was entirely possible that this was enemy action, and the three of us had just wandered into a trap. I was only guessing that this had something to do with Orus. And if I were being honest…

I didn’t even really know if Orus was an enemy. Whatever reasons he had, whatever drove him to cooperate with Anguis to unleash the Skyfall…I’m not sure it was something as simple as hatred for Fynneas, as Anima seemed to believe.

If we wanted to escape this web, we were going to have to find the spider at the center of it.

I couldn’t help but wonder if that spider was even alive, though. I hadn’t forgotten the Death Aether that had brought us here in the first place.

Our course set, Sylvia resumed her vanguard position, and Fade and I fell in behind her.

We were all just a bit more cautious, though.

We ventured on, through the bladed and spiked tunnel.

………………………

Eventually, we broke through. All tunnels had an end eventually.

At least…

Hopefully.

Not a one of us expected anything that we found on the other side of the tunnel. It was crude, but I could make out just what it was.

This was a home. Or at least, it had been one, once upon a time.

And the residents were still here. Just…

Not among the living. Upon a massive, crudely carved stone dais on the far side of a large, craggy, irregularly shaped cave, there they were.

Dozens and dozens of desiccated skeletons. There must have easily been over a hundred of them. Each and every one of them sat upon the rim of the dais, skeletal legs folded underneath them in a crisscross position. Before their exposed ribcages, all of their hands were folded together as if they had been praying. There was so little moisture within the air, down here, that they were preserved to a startling degree. The scene was so oddly serene, despite the sharp taste of Death Aether upon the air, that I could almost imagine their eyes closed in peaceful repose. That is, if they’d even had any.

All soft tissue upon the remains had long since been whisked away. Once upon a time, I think they might have each been wearing a pure white robe as their only garments. Time and the passing of ages had reduced them to scant rags that did little to hide the scraps of paper-thin skin that still clung to long-dead bones.

To a last, each of them…looked to be human.

Or human-ish.

I might just be wrong about how this place had nothing to do with the Netherim.

But, by far what was the most interesting thing about the scene, even with the presence of so many of the peaceful dead…was what lay in the center of the dais.

“What is that…?” I heard Sylvia whisper to my right. All three of us had been struck dumb by the scene at the other side of the cavern and had stopped to stare at it. To a one, all of our gazes were fixed on the same thing.

The much larger skeleton that the upright ones surrounded.

Large was an understatement, really. What was in the center was huge, easily taller than three men standing on each other’s shoulders. It was a testament to just how big the space we had entered that its head didn’t brush the ceiling. The remains were of something that was very much not Human, or even bipedal, really. It was an animal of some sort, but an animal writ large. Just like the rest of the dead, though, it was sitting up on its…haunches, I suppose, but the more accurate word would be hipbones. Its posture was perfectly straight, its hind bones folded beneath it, and its forelegs resting peacefully in front of it. The long-dead creature’s skull stared out at the cave from atop a thick spine, perfectly straight, its black, empty eyesockets big enough to fit a boulder inside. Unlike the preserved, desiccated remains of the others, this was a true skeleton. Every last inch of it had been stripped perfectly clean of flesh, or even fur for that matter. The stark white of the bones almost seemed to glow from the light of our Skills, reflecting across the distance in a way not even the other remains did.

I just…couldn’t tell what it was.

“Some…kind of dog?” I said, unsurely, briefly glancing down at Fade almost involuntarily.

To my relief, Fade shook his head. “No way. That’s not a dog, or even a wolf. It’s almost rodent-like. At least, that’s what it looks like to me.”

“No…” Sylvia said in a thoughtful whisper. “That’s still not quite right. You…wouldn’t typically find true rats this far underground. Wouldn’t it be a…mole instead?”

I blinked and turned back to look at the skeleton. I…suppose that could be true. I wasn’t exactly an expert in rodent physiognomy, but it made sense that a mole would be underground. Even if it was a mole of incredibly unusual size.

But most importantly?

All of the Death Aether that we had sensed, the Death Aether that had been strong enough to escape whatever seal had been on this cave and enter into the larger caldera…

It wasn’t coming from the Human skeletons.

All of it was coming from the creature in the center.
