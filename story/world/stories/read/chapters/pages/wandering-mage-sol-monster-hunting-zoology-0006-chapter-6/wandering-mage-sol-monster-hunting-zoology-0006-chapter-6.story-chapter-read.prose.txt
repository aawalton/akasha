Tressa crashed through a tangle of branches before slamming onto the forest floor. Stars swam across her vision as she lay there in a heap, groaning for breath while leaves danced gently down around her.

“Nhmmmm…”

She blinked and pushed herself upright. Pain flared through her back and one arm, but nothing felt broken. At least, not immediately.

“Ouch…”

Through the shattered branches overhead, the massive black bird circled. It was one Tressa had never seen before. She wondered just how far she had flown from Falidorn. It seemed traveling while airborne could take her quite far quite quickly. She had never attempted to do it like that before. She was just so mad that she had zoomed off without thinking it through.

“At least I’m still in one piece,” Tressa muttered. “Mother would kill me if I came home any worse than this. I can’t even imagine what Maikia would do to me…”

After a frustrated screech, the bird gave up and flew away.

Tressa let out a shaky breath and carefully picked herself up. Everything hurt, especially her back and arms. At first it seemed she had nothing but bruises, scratches, and a torn sleeve, but as soon as she put pressure on her right leg, pain tore through her ankle.

She dropped to a knee with a hiss of pain. Looking down, she saw a long cut running from mid-calf to her ankle, blood already soaking her sock, and her boot was torn, perhaps from the bird’s talon.

“Shit.”

She dug through her bag, tore a strip of cloth, and wrapped the wound as tightly as she could. Once it was secured, she pulled her damaged boot back on and tied it with what remained of the lace. It would hold. Probably.

Then she searched for her staff, spotting it propped against a decaying log. She limped over and felt relief loosen in her chest when she saw that it hadn’t broken. She ran a hand over the smooth wood and smiled despite herself.

“You’re alright. Thank the goddess.”

For a moment she just stood there, leaning on the staff and listening to the forest. Rustling leaves, distant birdcalls, the hum of insects. Evening was beginning to settle now.

“Damn, it’s getting late. Don’t think I can make it back before… dark…”

Her words trailed off as the thought grabbed hold. What if Alderman was already back inside the city walls? What if he was on his way to tell her father, or worse, report directly to the council what had happened?

What would happen to her once she went back? Would she be apprehended right at the city gates and arrested?

“Shit…” she said again.

Would Alderman really do that to her?

She looked around at the thick underbrush that surrounded her. Deep, dense moss, old trees, and patches of half-melted snow clung to the shadows beneath roots. This was a far denser, rougher stretch of forest than the areas near Falidorn’s walls that she was familiar with.

“I have no idea where I am,” she muttered. It seemed traveling with Levant could take her faster than she realized. But she should still be far from the Thimberman mountains where far more dangerous monsters were known to roam.

A slight sound caught her ear, and she looked around trying to find it. After a second, she realized it was buzzing. A low, angry buzz.

Tressa’s breath caught as she realized what it might be and crouched lower in the underbrush, then slowly looked up.

“Oops…”

The tree she had crashed through held a hive of torrent bees.

They were overhead, buzzing in an angry swarm around their damaged hive. Tressa dropped all the way to her back under the thick undergrowth, making herself as small as possible. If even one of them marked her with its pheromones, she’d be swarmed and likely killed in seconds.

Luckily, they hadn’t noticed her. Yet. Her best bet would be to crawl through the undergrowth until she had some distance from them and then make her escape.

She checked for her satchel, confirming she hadn’t lost it in the fall and it was still tied shut. But then, an idea struck her, and instantly a smile bloomed across her face.

She fished out her sketchbook and metal quill, then lay flat on her back, safely beneath the undergrowth as she began to sketch. She watched the hive through the gaps in the leaves, realizing that yes, she would never get such a nice vantage point to observe torrent bees from again! This was a rare opportunity, and she couldn’t just let it slip away!

After roughing in the structure of the hive, she moved on to jotting some notes. She could fill in more details later. Then, she searched for a bee that had gone still and found one perched on a nearby branch.

They were large—about the size of her fist—with heavy bodies and beak-like mouths. She quickly sketched it, paying special attention to the beak, then added some notes.

Torrent bees used those beaks to carve into bark, harvest syrup, and hollow out the tree beneath their hive. What looked like a nest built onto the outside of the tree was only part of it; the true heart of the colony lay buried deep in the wood, where the queen’s chamber would be hidden and couldn’t be any safer.

Her eyes lit up as she imagined it, and she shuddered, suppressing an excited squeal from bubbling past her lips.

Goddess! I wish I could see inside!

Nobody really knew what the inner chambers looked like. Or at least, the records in Falidorn’s libraries lacked such details. These amazing creatures only had a few paragraphs dedicated to them in the general encyclopedia, along with some warnings! Such a travesty!

But Torrent bees were far too dangerous to study up close, and their honey was both rare and notoriously difficult to steal. Their beaks could chew through leather and even pierce through armor, and their stingers carried a necrotic toxin nasty enough to rot flesh.

“Oooo, this is so exciiiiting!” she whispered as she scribbled faster.

She would have stayed there sketching for hours, but soon the day was coming to an end and she was losing her light. Still, she had several pages full of drawings and notes by the time the bees began retreating back into their damaged hive for the night.

The first fireflies began flickering through the darkness between the trees, and the sounds of the forest shifted into the soft chorus of crickets and night insects.

I should go now… she thought. The bees were all up for the night, but… her leg was still badly injured. She’d be able to make her way back more safely by daylight. Besides, she’d be able to observe how the torrent bees started their day once morning came.

At some point, still clutching her book against her chest, Tressa fell asleep right there, far below the hive of monstrous bees.

**

She woke at dawn to a cold drop of water landing on her ear, making it twitch as she groggily peeked an eye open and rubbed her brow. Another drop of dew slid from a leaf and splashed her cheek.

The forest was already stirring. Birds sang through the canopy, and the cool air of early morning clung to the ground in thin, drifting mist.

And as for the torrent bees, she heard buzzing. Lots of buzzing. Much more than the day prior.

Tressa rubbed her eyes and sat up slowly, wincing as her injured leg protested. The torrent bees were already awake, swarming around the hive overhead.

“Early start today, huh guys?” she said with a smile as she felt around for her sketchbook and quill.

But something about them seemed… off.

The bees weren’t just busy. They sounded furious.

She frowned and peered through the leaves just in time to see a blur of brown shoot out from a nearby branch.

Something small and fuzzy dove straight toward the hive and flew past it with a sharp ripping sound.

A chunk of the outer comb tore free as the creature snatched it and immediately flung itself away from the tree, gliding through the air toward another trunk several yards away, then running along a thick branch and jumping to another before disappearing into the canopy.

Tressa’s eyes went wide.

“Wait—what is that thing!?”

A moment later, another one came.

The second blur of brown dove from above, its claws hooking the hive as it passed. It ripped off a sizable piece and glided away just as the bees swarmed towards the disturbance.

Tressa scrambled for her sketchbook again, completely forgetting the danger.

“No way…” she said in shock as several more of those brown blurs assaulted the hive and quickly retreated.

Her quill scratched furiously across the page, capturing as much of it as she possibly could.

The creatures looked like Barn Squirrels at first glance, but they splayed out as they leaped, revealing thin gliding membranes between their arms and legs. Their bushy tails fanned out behind them and seemed to be used to steer them through the air.

She quickly sketched one as best she could, but she was sure she wasn’t very accurate, as they had moved so fast. She’d have to find wherever they lived and get a good vantage point on them in a calmer moment.

Tressa’s brow furrowed as she wrote faster.

Strange. The squirrels were actually getting away. Normally, torrent bees would have attacked immediately. They were notoriously aggressive, and one whiff of alarm pheromones should have turned the entire hive on them. Yet, the squirrels were not being chased at all.

She tapped the end of her quill against her lip.

“They must have some way of repelling the pheromones…” she whispered.

Her eyes drifted back towards the gliding creatures darting between the trees.

“Oil in the fur, maybe?” she murmured as she scribbled another note. “Or some natural scent that masks them… or a counter-pheromone!? I suppose even some sort of mana field could do the trick!” She was giddy with excitement over this new mystery. Though she couldn’t see any mana around them, that didn’t necessarily mean none was there. If it was a low enough source, her eyes wouldn’t be able to pick it up.

Another squirrel dove. This time, the bees reacted faster. A buzzing cloud of swarming bees collided with the creature.

Tressa gasped as the creature screeched and flailed midair. It barely made it a few yards before dropping out of the center of the swarm, limp and soundless—its death coming almost instantly.

It hit the forest floor with a dull thump somewhere beyond the brush.

“Fascinating,” Tressa whispered as she jotted another note. She was considering crawling back later in the day to find its body so she could study it. That would also give her a chance to study the effects of the torrent bee stings.

As she was writing, something spun through the branches and landed right next to her with a thud.

She turned her gaze absentmindedly, at first thinking it was a snapped branch or something. But then she froze.

A torn chunk of honeycomb was lying in the leaves just feet from her hand. Golden honey glistened in the morning light.

And the smell of it…

Her eyes flicked upward.

“…Oh no.”

The swarm shifted, thousands of wings turning at once as many bees peeled away from the hive and dropped towards the forest floor. Toward the lost chunk of their hive—toward her!

“Shit! Shit—shit shit!” Tressa yelped, scrambling to her feet.

She tried to run, but her injured leg immediately screamed in protest. Pain shot up her calf as she staggered forward through the brush.

“Levant!”

A quick burst of air magic shoved her forward like a sudden gust of wind. She half-ran, half-limped, half-floated through the trees as the swarm surged after her, the buzzing growing louder and louder. She didn’t want to risk flying high, where a tree branch she might not notice could instantly end the chase—not to mention she simply wasn’t skilled enough with her flying spell to trust it yet!

A bee slammed into her shoulder. Its hooked beak bit into her sleeve, and she cried out, swatting it away as it tore free with a ripping sound, but it only got her clothes.

But then another clipped her arm, this time its beak finding flesh with a sear of pain.

“Ah! Damn it!”

She shoved herself forward again with another burst of Levant, skipping over roots and fallen branches. The forest blurred around her as adrenaline and magic carried her ahead.

But the bees were faster.

One struck her back, its beak biting through the cloth of her shirt. Another slammed into her thigh. She hissed through her teeth as she grabbed the one that chomped her back, tearing its beak free from her flesh just as its stinger jabbed at her but barely missed. She threw it away and nearly lost her balance when her injured leg buckled.

“Lavant!” she cried once again, with another burst of speed skipping her far forward.

“Come on, come on!”

Through the trees ahead, she spotted pale morning light reflecting off stone—a cliff! Below it, there might be water!

She pushed harder, using another burst of Levant to fling herself over a fallen log. The swarm was almost on top of her now, the air thick with their furious buzzing.

Then, something stabbed her shoulder. A stinger!

She screamed.

The pain burned like a red-hot needle as she stumbled forward, swatting wildly with her sketchbook at the bee clinging to her by its stinger before it tore free and dropped away.

The cliff edge came into view, and Tressa didn’t even slow to plan the jump. She just threw her satchel to the ground and took a leap of faith.

She plunged down twenty feet to a river below, and cold water swallowed her in a violent splash. Bubbles roared past her ears as she sank beneath the surface, the shock of the icy water barely registering for how high her adrenaline had spiked.

She tumbled along the river floor, a jutting rock striking her, before she managed to get her legs beneath herself and kicked down hard, bursting back up.

She broke through the surface, flailing and gasping for breath.

Above her, the bees swarmed along the edge of the cliff, furious but not following. Without the trail of their pheromones, they quickly lost track of her.

Tressa reached for a tree root, and between that and getting her feet down on the rock below, she easily managed to pull herself free from the river, where she collapsed on the muddy shore, gasping and panting for breath. She rolled over onto her back, her leg screaming with pain from its forced use despite its injury.

“Ooooow… ouch, ouch, ouch,” she whined between the pain of her leg, the wounds from the bees, and her other bruises and scratches.

The lace on her boot had come loose again, and the bandage beneath was soaked through with blood. She tightened the leather cord around it as best she could and leaned heavily on her staff.

Only then did she notice her shoulder burning. Her stomach dropped.

“No… no no…”

Hands shaking, she inspected the wound, pressing her fingers around it. It was red and irritated around the bleeding center, but the flesh hadn’t gone hard the way she had read about.

She let out a long breath of relief. The sting was shallow, and the swelling already looked wrong for a full torrent bee strike. Maybe even the river had washed out enough of the venom, or it could have been a young worker without necrotic venom.

“Lucky…” she muttered to herself.

A full venom sting from an adult torrent bee would have meant necrotic rot spreading through the flesh within minutes, and there’s no way she could mistake that. If it had, she very likely would have lost use of her arm in the best-case scenario, without immediate treatment. Worst case was something she didn’t even want to think about.

She flexed her arm carefully. It was still painful, though.

Using her staff for support, she slowly forced herself upright.

“Levant.”

Air gathered beneath her feet, lifting her gently off the ground. She flew up to the top of the cliff, checking carefully for any lingering bees but finding none, and then snatched up her satchel and sketchbook.

From there, she rose above the trees just enough to see the morning sun climbing in the east; the thimberman mountains’ snow-capped peaks were far to the north, still mostly streaked in shadows in the misty haze of distance.

“So… southwest should be Falidorn…”

Below her, the forest stretched endlessly in every direction. She had never made it this far before. She was out of reach of anyone from within the city walls.

Instead of fear, and despite the pains in her body, a small grin spread across her face.

“I don’t think there is anything about those squirrel things in our libraries. I wonder what other creatures are out here?”

Her eyes sparkled as she drifted slowly through the air.

She’d heard stories of trees that walk, and monsters so old even gods might struggle to fight them.

Falidorn, the idea of going home, and what might await her there if Aldmern sold her out, faded from her thoughts as curiosity pulled her deeper into the wild forest.
