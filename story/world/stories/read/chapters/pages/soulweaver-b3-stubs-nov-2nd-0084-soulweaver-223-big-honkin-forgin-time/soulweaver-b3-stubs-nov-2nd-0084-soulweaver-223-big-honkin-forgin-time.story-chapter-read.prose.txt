We made it topside with just minutes to spare. The sun had set some time ago and the last of the blue in the sky was already turning black. The streets of Akihabara stretched off in every direction.

It would’ve been nice… if it weren’t for the groans of zombies entering their night phase.

“We need shelter,” Aerion said, her hand on her sword. “Now.”

I scanned the buildings around us, looking for something defensible. Hotels were always a good bet, and Tokyo was chock full of them. Multiple floors meant we could get above the zombie swarms, and the rooms usually had decent doors we could barricade if needed.

My eyes landed on a building with distinctive signage. “There. That APA Hotel.”

I once again put Aerion in my inventory and I swapped back into my Grace armor, dashing across the street, before the zombies noticed. Their snarls were getting more feral and aggressive, and their enhanced senses would soon follow. We didn’t have much time.

The hotel’s automatic doors were shattered and the glass crunched under my boots as I entered the lobby. Not quite trusting the whip, I pulled out Light of the Fearless instead, ready for resistance, but the ground floor was thankfully clear, and I let Aerion back out.

“Never thought I’d be glad you had that bizarre space,” Aerion said with a shiver. “It’s served us quite well, regardless of how… weird it is being stuck in there.”

“You can say that again.”

Aerion squinted with me, before realizing I’d uttered another Earthism.

“Stairs or the elevator?” Aerion asked.

“Elevator,” I said with a shrug. “Less chance of encountering any zombies in the stairwell. “But first, we’ll need a keycard.”

Luckily, the systems at these hotels were highly automated, and the skeleton of a receptionist had his keycard, which I used to grant access to the system. Thankfully, it had an English option, and I soon had myself a brand new room key, dialed in for the highest floor.

We got into the elevator and I punched 15. The doors slid shut silently and we rode it all the way up.

The top floor was as clear, and I flashed the card on the sensor of the corner room… which was quite a bit smaller than I’d expected, despite Japan being rather famous for these sorts of accommodations.

“It’s a box!” Aerion said upon entering, gesturing at the space that was maybe ten feet by twelve feet, most of it taken up by the bed. “How do people live like this?”

“Tokyo’s one of the densest cities on Earth,” I said, sliding the key into the receptacle, which turned on the lights and the TV, which fired up an AI maid who blathered on about all the hotel’s amenities. It even showed us our checkout time. How convenient.

“Land is at a premium, so everything gets compressed. You should see the capsule hotels. They’re literally just pods you sleep in. You can’t even sit up in some of those.”

“That sounds horrifying.”

“It’s part of the experience! Back on Earth, this city had thirty million people living in it.”

She shook her head, gazing at the room in wonder. “I still don’t understand how you lived in such a world. So many people, so little space. And no magic…”

“Pretty amazing what technology can do, but we had our fair share of problems. Some would say more problems than a world like Axius could ever dream to have.”

I glanced back at Aerion and almost regretted doing so.

“I’m covered in zombie gore and I’m pretty sure some of it got in my mouth at some point,” she deadpanned. “For that matter, you don’t look much better.”

“Shower together?” I said, jerking my thumb to the bathroom door.

“Let’s,” she said. “And pray the drainage system is up to the task.”

The bathtub and bathroom were both tiny, but we did somehow manage to squeeze in there together.

Thankfully, the heating systems were still chugging away, and stood under the hot shower far longer than we probably ought to have. I scrubbed until my skin was raw, then scrubbed some more for good measure, and by the time we were done, about a year’s worth of zombie grime had swirled down the drain.

The little room had also filled with steam, which set the smoke alarm off before I silenced it with a good smack.

I even used the opportunity to wash my armors off, too.

Aerion collapsed naked onto the bed with a groan that was equal parts relief and exhaustion.

“Joining me?” she asked seductively, her gorgeous body on full display.

“Literally want nothing more,” I said, making a face.

“But…”

“But I gotta make sure the hotel’s clear. Don’t think I can sleep right without clearing the place.”

“Fine,” she said, rolling onto her stomach and kicking her feet up, giving me the most seductive look she could. Which was to say, achingly seductive. “After we play.”

Every ounce of restraint I had evaporated in the fact of that, and before I knew it, I was on the bed and all over Aerion.

— —

Some indeterminate amount of time later, I managed to pry myself of Aerion’s hug and get into my armor. Aerion was snoring happily, and I let myself out as quietly as I could, though I needn’t have bothered. She was out cold.

I could hardly blame her. We’d been running nonstop for hours, fighting zombies, coordinating train crashes, and generally pushing ourselves to the limit. She’d earned the rest.

As for me, my Vigor suit pretty much eliminated that need altogether, so I took the opportunity to check every floor.

The hotel was empty, which was rare for the dungeon. Most buildings had at least a few zombies shambling around.

After convincing myself we were safe, I barricaded the fifteenth floor before migrating to the rooftop, monitoring the streets below.

Akihabara at night was surreal. Just like on Earth, this place was lit up like Christmas with gigantic LCDs everywhere advertising maid cafes and anime posters plastered on every square inch of free wall.

The only thing missing was the crowds of people popping in and out of the various anime goods stores and electronics boutiques.

The otaku mecca of the world.

This Akiba, though, was filled with undead. Which, now that I thought about it, was the exact setting for a number of B rated anime.

I thought back to that meeting with Cosmo, when he’d brought me here. At the time, I’d thought he’d simply cooked up the setting out of the blue like the bar and that beach we’d visited. Maybe based on what he thought would relax me, maybe snooping in on my memories to arrive at that particular choice.

But now that I was really here? Was that really a coincidence?

There was one way to check. To go back to that store where he’d made me pick out those Aerion figures. I’d always thought that was weird, but I’d chalked it up as more Cosmo being Cosmo at the time.

What if it wasn’t? What did that mean?

I didn’t even begin entertaining that thought. It was probably nothing. Just Greg paranoia.

Just that my instincts said otherwise… and my instincts were rarely wrong about this sort of thing.

“Fuck.”

I wanted to go explore right now. To [Spacefold] to the street and start checking every building until I found that store. I knew the general area and Akiba wasn’t that large. Hell, I could probably be back by morning.

But wiser Greg put an end to that stupidity. If that store really was there, it’d be there in the morning, and I wasn’t going to abandon Aerion to navigate the night horde when I was supposed to watch over her.

So instead, I pulled out my forge, as hot as it was when I stowed it last.

I brought out my Voidsteel Alloy Shards with the [Frag Out] ability. The ones that were still stuck at F-0. Time to change that.

The shards themselves were solid, and I’d learned a lot about my ability since I’d made them It was time for an upgrade.

I tossed all dozen of them into the forge, watching the Voidsteel glow red, then white as they coagulated into one lump. Once they were molten, I added Mythril to the mix. Not much, just enough to change the alloy, mainly to get the System to think it different enough . The two metals merged surprisingly well, creating an alloy that reminded me of liquid mercury with veins of silver running through it.

On a whim, I sprinkled in a bit of the Frostsilver I’d collected. I wasn’t even sure what it would do, and since it was the least exotic material of the bunch, I made sure not to add too much.

The alloy took on a slightly blue tint as the Frostsilver mixed in, which I thought looked quite pretty.

These would end up being too small to insert a Soul Crystal into, so the chances of them developing stats was almost nil, but I could crush a crystal and sprinkle in the dust, impacting the ability they manifested.

Which was exactly what I did, selecting a [Rare] crystal from a monster known for its Essence manipulation abilities, mixing the dust evenly through the ingot before folding the steel over several times to incorporate it.

Like kneading dough. Except with exotic metal, and at very high temperatures.

I then brought out the red hot ingot and cut off a thumb-sized piece. Instead of shards this time, though, I opted for a different shape. A better shape.

With [Launch], I could apply spin to anything I fired. Much like a rifle, doing so would not only stabilize them, they’d let them fly much, much farther.

Assuming I could aim them at that distance, of course, but I had a solution for that.

My Soulweaving interface not only allowed me to make them almost as perfect as if they’d been machine made, it let me guide the properties they had.

Instead of spreading my focus across multiple stats like I usually did, I concentrated entirely on Wisdom. The bullets didn’t need to be fast or durable. They just had to be smart. Smart enough to course-correct mid-flight. Maybe even smart enough to home in on targets when my shitty aim was inevitably off.

The result was a pointy, solid tipped bullet somewhat larger than that of a .50 caliber round.

The Essence cost was only slightly more than what I reclaimed from the shards, but what I got in return was incomparably better.

[Exotic Alloy Big Honkin’ Bullet] (Epic)

It was getting to be a mouthful naming all the stuff you put into this, so I shortened it. Shoot ‘em up, Grug!

Essence Cost: 27

Condition: 500/500

Stats: — None

Abilities: — [Search & Recall] (Foundation-0): Projectile adjusts trajectory mid-flight to track designated target. Projectile returns to Spatial Inventory once its velocity reaches zero.

“Holy shit!” It worked!

No, as my eyes read over that ability, I realized it worked far better than I’d hoped it would.

Not only did this solve my aiming problem, it solved another problem I realized existed only after creating them.

Bullets would be much faster than shards. They’d fly a lot farther too, which would easily put them outside of my recall range for my Spatial Inventory.

With these, I no longer had to worry about that. Even better—I no longer had to spend any mental effort stuffing them back in. Now I didn’t just have to not aim, I didn’t even have to think about stowing them! I could focus entirely on [Launching] them.

Laughing like a maniac,I set up a makeshift target on the far side of the roof made from some loose metal plates I found and [Launched] one of the bullets at a forty-five-degree angle away from it.

The bullet fired faster than I could see… and went wide of the target by a mile, ricocheting into the darkness before popping back into my inventory a few seconds later… presumably after it hit the ground and came to a stop.

I glanced at its condition, breathing a sigh of relief when I found it had only lost two points from the fall. Those exotic metals weren’t for nothing.

I tried again, firing it about ten degrees off. Still no dice. The bullet missed.

But when I got to within about five degrees, the magic happened. The bullet unquestionably curved mid-flight, bending through the air like a heat-seeking missile before punching into metal plate’s center of mass.

Well, I supposed that made sense. The ability was at F-0, after all.

I couldn’t stop grinning. This changed everything. No more needing pinpoint accuracy with every shot. Now I just had to aim in the general direction and let the shards do the rest. And as [Search & Recall] leveled up, the tracking would only get better.

I spent the next hour testing them, firing at different angles, different distances, seeing how sharp a turn they could make. The current limitation was about a seven-degree correction. Anything more and they’d miss. By the time the ability hit Divergence, these things would be damn near impossible to dodge.

Between the new bullets, my chain whip, and my flying swords, I was becoming a walking artillery platform. I only wish I could make more. Even with my levelups, I was pushing up against the limit of my Essence.

The only question was whether I could control all of them at once without overloading my brain.

Well, that was a problem for future Greg. And maybe a specialized Cunning suit? But Cunning wouldn’t improve my processing power, that I knew of. Maybe that was another trait of Wisdom? Something to experiment with later, for sure.

I glanced at the horizon and the skyscrapers that covered the land all the way up to it. This city truly was a Cave of Steel. A marvel of human innovation. Some people hated all that concrete but I kinda liked it. It was impressive as hell, though it’d have been moreso if we hadn’t ruined the planet to make it.

The sky was starting to lighten with the predawn blue. Soon the zombies would enter their daytime fugue again, and we could move safely.

I stored the forge and my small arsenal, then headed down the stairs to check on Aerion. Still sleeping peacefully, her naked chest rising and falling in a steady rhythm. She looked so different like this. So relaxed and vulnerable and not anything like the fierce warrior genius who could bisect zombies and disarm bombs without blinking.

I let her sleep another hour, until the first rays of actual sunlight broke over the horizon. Then I leaned down and kissed her on the lips.

“Wake up Aer. It’s morning.”

She stirred, mumbling something incoherent, then blinked awake. “Morning already?”

“Yep. And we’ve got places to be.”

She sat up, stretching with a yawn. “Your mysterious hunch?”

“My mysterious hunch,” I confirmed, barely containing my excitement. “Come on. I promise, it’ll be worth it.”

“It better be,” she grumbled, but she was smiling as she pulled on her armor. “You look like a child on Giving Day.”

“Er, is that coming up?” I asked, seriously hoping that wasn’t what I thought it was.

“It is,” Aerion said smugly, before frowning. “But it is difficult to know precisely, what with the altered time flow in the dungeon.”

“Right. Yeah, tough luck, that,” I said, scratching my head. “Anyway, if I’m right, this should be a fun, easy morning in a part of the city I think you’ll get a kick out of.”

“And if you’re wrong?”

I frowned. “Let’s hope for both our sakes I’m not.”
