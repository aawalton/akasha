Storm-charged wind whipped Edge’s hair across his face as he stepped into the passageway beyond the black door.

Foebinder and Magebane were waiting on the far side. His metallic allies followed in his footsteps as he crossed the short tunnel leading to the stretch of coastline beyond. When he emerged into the light, he noticed two major changes to Skill-Eater’s domain, which was part of his core but not an aspect of his inner world.

The first was the egress’s location. Back when Skill-Eater and the dark passenger were fused into a single entity bound by the chains of oblivion, the black door was embedded in the giant’s chest. Now that the lord was free and the Ancient was confined beneath the waves, the tunnel emerged onto a rocky cliffside running along the shore. It would have been strange if this place wasn’t merely an abstraction his mind could comprehend, but given the bizarre rules that governed this realm, he didn’t give it much thought.

The other new addition was the structure he spotted in the distance. Since Skill-Eater was unchained and her memories had been partially restored, he’d assumed she would reside in a palace befitting one of the most powerful entities on the planet, maybe an ornate temple like the one where he had unsealed her reliquary. Instead, the path led to a modest dwelling that was about the size of Trapper’s lodge.

Edge walked along the cliffside and approached the building. As he drew near, he could make out more details. The walls were formed from an alien substance that had the grain of wood but the appearance of stone. Crystal tiles lined the roof, and an assortment of flowers and herbs were growing in clay containers hanging from the window sills.

The door was open, and when he stepped onto the front porch, a familiar voice rang out. “Welcome, Edge. Come in.”

He wasn’t sure what customs she followed, but tracking mud inside her home didn’t convey the respect he felt for the mysterious entity whose heart beat in place of his own. He took his boots off, set them against the wall, and crossed the threshold.

He was intensely curious to find out what was inside, as this was his first time entering an alien abode whose owner was in residence. He had explored a few ruined structures built by the natives of this world, but other than some stone furniture, everything had been destroyed by time and the elements long ago.

While that was interesting in its own way, Skill-Eater was from a planet that wasn’t connected to the portal network. That meant Edge was one of the first humans to ever lay eyes on artifacts from her culture—possibly even the first, since the lords’ memories had been sealed away when they were transformed into cores. She might well be the only one who remembers this part of their past, although there’s no way to know for sure.

The entire building was a single, open floor. Waist-high partitions ran between the kitchen and common area, and paper screens separated what he assumed were the bed and bathrooms. Some of the surfaces were painted, highlighting the natural beauty of the materials. Crystal sculptures were suspended from the ceiling, throwing multicolored checkers of light across the interior that shifted from one gorgeous pattern to the next whenever the wind blew.

The air was filled with the scents of alien spices that had no earthly equivalents. At first, he thought it was incense, but when he saw Skill-Eater sitting at a table in front of a boiling pot, he realized the ravenous lord was brewing tea. Today, her raven hair was drawn back into a single braid, and she was wearing a sky-blue robe that accented her slender arms and fine features.

“Sit, my scion. Be welcome in my home. This is a place filled with happy memories. It feels so liberating to reconnect with my past—remember the person I was long before my family traveled to this world.”

Edge pulled out a chair across from the lord. Whatever the furniture was made from was unbelievably comfortable. He let out a soft sigh of appreciation, enjoying a moment of companionable silence before moving on to weightier matters. When the tea was ready, she poured him a cup, then slid a plate of cookies across the table that hadn’t been there until that moment.

Both were amazing. He savored the novel flavors while enjoying an interaction with Skill-Eater unlike any that had come before. “Thank you. These are delicious. How are you feeling now that we’ve evolved to stage three?”

“A great deal better. I still need to sleep a fair amount of the time, but I am awake more often, and my mind is clear as a cloudless sky. I haven’t felt like myself in centuries, and coming home again, even in this realm of concepts and intent, means more than I can put into words.”

“I think I know what you mean. My central chamber took the form of my father’s workshop, a place I used to treasure as a child until he and my brother died. I never thought I would see it again, but now it feels like I’m a little closer to those happy days and the memories of the people I love.”

“Indeed. We are well-matched in many ways. I believe the parallels to our pasts are what brought us together in the first place—the reason I emerged from unfathomable darkness at your touch and encouraged the reliquary to unseal. I look forward to conversing with you about matters great and small, but I can sense your eagerness to join your friends. Let us address the matter at hand and save the simple pleasure of conversation for another time.”

Edge nodded his agreement as she poured him a second cup.

“We still have a long way to go to restore my true strength—at least two evolutions, and likely several more. Additionally, the tome wasn’t the only artifact sealing my powers and my past. While many memories have returned, my knowledge has gaps, and my mind is filled with fuzzy places. It’s hard to know what I don’t know, but one aspect is glaring. Though I can picture the voices, personalities, and history of my family, I cannot recall their appearances or the nature of their powers.

“There are a few exceptions. I remember a great deal about Lore-Weaver, as you have interacted with her scion on numerous occasions. I can dredge up a few details about Death-Walking and Sheltering-Hand, since you spent some time in the presence of their scions, who you know as Corpse and Shield. I also have a few memories about Benevolent-Monarch, which means you were in proximity to him as well. The man was masking his mana signature at the time, but in retrospect, I believe King must be a scion too.”

“That is new information.” Edge rubbed his chin. “I suspected that was the case, but it’s good to have confirmation. If you remember anything about their powers or the nature of the lords they bear, please let me know. It could be vitally important for our future interactions.”

“I will. Sheltering-Hand is a consummate protector. His magic preserves that which he holds dear and that which his duty dictates. Benevolent-Monarch inspires and empowers followers who have pledged their service. That being said, I don’t know how their magics will manifest in their present forms.”

“On the subject of scions and lords, I’d like to learn more about Corpse and her core. I know her magic can control the dead. The squid could even raise minions of its own. Do you remember any additional details?”

“A few. Death-Walking views death as an alternative to life, not its end. At the height of his power, he could restore his followers to unlife with only a fragment of their physical remains. As a result, he didn’t try to protect his people like most of the lords, and his actions led to the demise of many, making him reviled and worshiped in equal measure. You have seen the least of what his magic can do. He can alter the forms of the creatures he commands, making them far more powerful in death than they were in life.

“I should be able to recall more if you unseal another relic or interact with additional scions, particularly if you witness their unique magic up close, which your kind call ultimate abilities. Finally, I can sense that you passed other scions at a distance, although I can’t be certain how many or which lords they bear.”

“That was probably when I had that scare with the Wardens’ captains and lieutenants, but there’s always a chance I encountered another scion elsewhere in World’s End. That reminds me, Sakura discovered signs that there are two Ancients in the free city, although one trail is old and the monster might have left the settlement in recent days.”

That turned their conversation toward the lords’ mortal enemies. Skill-Eater would try to help him track and identify the monsters in human form. She also confirmed his theory that they were trying to create a permanent bridge between the dimensions.

“This won’t end until we destroy the monster possessing Realm-Traveler’s body. Unfortunately, I doubt his mind and soul remain housed within his flesh. I only sensed a trace of his former self when we spotted his mutated form on the Ancients’ homeworld, though that glimpse was too brief to be certain.”

Edge spent a while longer conversing with the ravenous lord, getting a better sense of her true nature now that the dark passenger was out of the picture. It wasn’t listed anywhere on his profile, but he was certain their bond was just as important as his relationships with his avatars.

Skill-Eater was wise, pleasant, and mysterious. He was looking forward to developing a true partnership now that he knew she wasn’t responsible for the insatiable hunger that had overwhelmed him during the early stages of his adventures and had no intention of hijacking his body or subduing his mind.

He was getting ready to leave when he sensed Rue paging him through his Companion bond. It seemed that the golden fox wanted to meet the master of skills. He couldn’t see any reason why that would be a bad idea, so he gave his permission, and the beast materialized by his side.

He had no idea what to expect and was delighted when the two of them got along well right off the bat. Skill-Eater played with the beast while he sipped tea, feeding the fox treats while stroking his fur and singing songs in a long-lost language. She told Edge Rue’s magic was potent, and it wouldn’t be long before he evolved to stage two.

When his daily time limit was up, they bid her farewell, and he stepped back into his body.

Before he partook of the drinking and dancing underway in the inn’s common area, he summoned his Guide and pulled up his profile, taking in the changes his evolution had wrought.
