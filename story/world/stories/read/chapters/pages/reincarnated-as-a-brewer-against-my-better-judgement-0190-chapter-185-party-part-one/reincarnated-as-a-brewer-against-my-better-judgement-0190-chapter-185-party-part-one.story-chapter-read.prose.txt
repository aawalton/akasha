It was the day of the party; though it was mid-morning, Alric headed for the muster yard, six guards in tow. The staff had been told the day was theirs, and some were sleeping in. Alric left early, assuming there would be last-minute issues to deal with.

Walking along the road toward the muster yard, he realized just how badly he had underestimated everyone when he could hear the people from there before he could even see them.

The cold had made a concerted effort overnight; mist lay across the river and drifted to lower parts of the field. Alric’s own breath was visible; he could see frost on the dry grass.

He pulled his cloak a little tighter as he approached the already busy field.

Alric scanned for Pavise but couldn’t see him anywhere. He made for the center of the field and the beer wagon, which was already attracting a congregation. People clustered around fires with food, sat at tables, and shouted across the field with the sort of volume usually reserved for emergencies or auctions. The beer wagon was already in business; a server was pouring beer for someone who looked as if they’d been waiting since last night.

Has a barrel been tapped? Already? At maybe nine in the morning?

Alric slowed down slightly as he passed the beer wagon; several were laughing and waving tankards around.

One man was holding a sausage in one hand. Clearly preparing for this. Another stood nearby, taking a deep pull from a tankard, having decided beer was breakfast.

They really did mean all day.

He felt a flicker of admiration for the dedication before his thoughts were interrupted, “Alric!” Pavise called with his deep voice.

Alric turned to the shout. Pavise stood by one of the fires, waving at him. Four other adventurers were standing with him. All of them were holding big bowls.

“Pavise, how long has this been going on?” Alric asked. He gestured with his thumb to the beer wagon.

Pavise glanced over at it. “Not sure, just after sunrise.” Pavise gestured to the men around him; none held tankards. “Free beer does that. We are all preparing to fight your beer dragon.” Pavise lifted the bowl with a smirk; one of the other adventurers holding a bowl sniggered.

Alric saw the wisdom in that. He turned to his guard and had them arrange to eat in shifts; four peeled off toward the food. Alric noted the general grins. “I’m going to get food too,” Alric said to Pavise, who nodded and fell into step with him.

'You don’t need to babysit me, you know?' Alric said, frowning. He didn’t mind the company, but the idea of depriving Pavise of all his friends felt strange.

Pavise shook his head. “I need a refill. I’m more worried about everyone coming to give you hugs than anything else.” Pavise said, showing his nearly empty bowl.

Pavise pointed to another adventurer with a grin. The man held a tankard in each hand; he sat in front of a fire with a focused expression, as though pondering philosophy. There was no food anywhere near him.

As they walked toward the innkeepers, the smells of the field came through. Several long fires were already burning; above them, meat roasted on spits. Sausages were everywhere; bread was on wooden boards near the fires to keep warm. Alric could faintly smell eggs, but roasting meat and smoke dominated everything else.

Further down the row, a small cluster of people were around Berrin's space. Berrin saw Alric, and he waved happily. Alric waved back; he felt genuinely content.

“Pavise,” Alric said, stopping and just taking it in.

“Hmm?” Pavise asked, around a mouthful of porridge.

“Thank you, truly,” Alric said, gesturing to the field and everything.

Pavise swallowed; he glanced around the space before he gave a shrug. The grin he’d been wearing faded slightly when he spoke. “I don’t think you understand it sometimes, Alric. But you gave us something good.”

Alric opened his mouth to speak, but Pavise continued,

“This will probably be the best night of the year for all of us.” Pavise used his spoon to gesture to a small group sitting down at a table. “Been a rough year, everyone says that every year, but most of us watched a friend bleed. Some did the bleeding.” He pointed at the adventurer with tankards they had passed, “Some had to bury good people, most have little money right now.”

Alric swallowed and felt himself shrink slightly.

“But at least, for today we can either forget about that or remember it.” Pavise explained, the solemnness spoiled by a blob of porridge falling off his spoon as he gestured to the same group as earlier, “Some will treat this like a wake and good for them. Some just want to eat too much, drink too much, and throw their name away. You gave them that.” Pavise put his spoon back in his bowl and patted Alric on the shoulder.

Alric nodded slowly. He saw out of the corner of his eye that two of his guards were nodding at Pavise. “I’m glad,” Alric said after a pause. It sounded feeble even to him, but there was little else to say.

“Speaking of drinking!” Pavise said, the grin returning in full swing. He pointed to a signboard next to the beer wagon.

The signboard hadn’t been visible on approach; Alric tilted his head. There was a large 16 written in text that dominated the top center; underneath that was… Something. It had four legs of different sizes, a thick neck, mismatched wings, and was blowing what must be fire. It resembled a deeply unfortunate chicken more than anything else.

“Is that a?” Alric said, tilting his head the other way.

After a pause, Pavise added, “It’s your beer dragon. We must slay it today.”

“And the sixt…” Alric began but then remembered this discussion when he first proposed the party to Pavise, “You can't be serious?”

Pavise folded his arms, dunking his elbow in what was left of his porridge, and nodded to it. “Everyone needs to drink sixteen to empty the barrels you said?” Pavise said, his expression deeply smug.

“Hang on, that was an average,” Alric said, pointing to it.

Pavise shrugged, “My guards all heard it. Everyone considers it their obligation. You need the barrels, we all do our share.” Pavise nodded to someone who waved at him before turning back to the board.

“You know the number is way higher? There are many who won’t drink.” Alric said, trying to fight the overwhelming smugness.

Pavise unfolded his arms, noticed his elbow had porridge on it, and frowned. “OH, I knew that, which is why I made sure your soldiers heard that we considered sixteen to be our minimum.”

Nearby, Alric heard a cheer and distinctly made out the words sixteen.

“And then, I made sure your brewery people heard that both the soldiers and the adventurers didn’t think they could handle it,” Pavise said, the smugness returning.

Alric stammered for words. 'You are a terrible person,' he managed, though the effect was ruined by his own laughter and that of his guards.

Pavise shrugged and nodded to someone heading to the beer wagon. “I’ve been called worse.”

“Pavise, lad, we got a problem.” A voice with an accent Alric knew well called out. Both turned toward it.

“Oh, what problem is that?” Pavise said to Stromni.

Stromni gave Alric a grin and a wink as a greeting before turning back to Pavise. He held up a small burlap sack; in his other hand was a white lump and a tankard.

“Chalk?” Pavise and Alric asked together.

“Aye, anyone can say they ‘it sixteen, but none can prove nothing.” Stromni said, stuffing the sack under his armpit and miming making a line on his tankard, “Need ta mark people’s tankards, who’s pouring?”

“Oh, that’s excellent, good idea, come this way,” Pavise said, heading for the beer wagon.

Alric stood there for a moment, then decided someone responsible should probably be nearby and pretend to ignore official decisions, so he followed them.

Four people were serving beer to anyone who approached; when Alric caught up, Stromni was already explaining his system to them. Every time a tankard was refilled, the server made a chalk line on it. One line, one beer.

“No makin’ ya own marks.” Stromni added, handing over the sack and chalk. He pointed at nearby adventurers, “Server does it.”

“What if they forget?” Someone called out.

Stromni turned to the voice. “Then ya remind em before ya walk.”

“But I’ve already had one?” Someone else called.

“If the server remembers, you get a line, but they have the chalk, they set it,” Pavise said to the voice with a mock glare.

The adventurer threw both hands up, “That’s unfair, I’ve had two!”

“I’ve not seen ya face, have any of you?” One of the servers called out to the adventurer first before turning to the other servers. They all shook their heads, “Ya got a face I wish I could forget, it happening.”

People nearby scoffed as the adventurer put down his hands and pouted. Alric just stood watching the exchange; now the competition had rules, record-keeping, and a soon-to-be inebriated judiciary.

Stromni handed over his own tankard; the server grinned, poured to the brim, took the chalk, and added a thick line near the bottom. Stromni had a wide grin when he took it from the server, then he glared at everyone nearby, holding his tankard close to his chest. “None o’ ya touch this tankard.” The dwarf said, still mock glaring.

“Why would anyone want your tankard?” Someone shouted.

“Cause then I lose me marks, and I am gonna win this thing,” Stromni shouted back. This got a cheer, followed by another cheer of sixteen.

“Oi? What about Zane here?” Another shout came; a small ring formed as many people pointed.

“That ain’t a tankard! That’s a bleedin cup!” Stromni said, pointing accusingly, “counts as a quarter tankard at best.”

“No one said how big,” Zane said with a big can’t-help shrug; the effect was spoilt by someone pushing a large tankard into his chest, which he took with a pout.

The arguments continued while Alric remembered his own tankard. Seren had arranged a nice set of tankards, if headquarters ever needed to entertain with beer. Alric had liberated them earlier; Seren already knew she would have to arrange more.

Alric took out one of the tankards from his item box. Tankard wasn’t the right word; he would have called them a stein. These in particular could double as weapons in an emergency. They were made of good wood; metal held the sides together. A wooden lid hung on a metal hinge with a thumb latch to lift it. There were four in total, one for himself, Rein, Pavise, and Calvern.

Alric held one out to Pavise, “This is yours.”

Pavise shifted back slightly but took it. He lifted it and turned the stein, looking at it. He lifted the lid with his thumb and checked the handle.

“This is nice, thank you,” Pavise said with a grin.

Alric nodded and gestured toward the beer wagon, retrieving his own stein. He and Pavise handed them over for a fill; the servers accepted them with the sort of grin usually reserved for accomplices.

“Mark em!” Came from Stromni. Nearby people picked this up as a chant, ‘mark em, mark em, mark em.”

Pavise rolled his eyes. Alric retrieved his beer but waited for Pavise. Once they both had beer, they turned to the chanters. Pavise turned his stein to show people his mark, which got a cheer. Alric did the same and got another cheer.

'I’m not competing in this,' Alric said, glaring at Stromni, who grinned and held up one finger. 'This whole thing is ridiculous.'

“Aye, it is,” Stromni said. He came forward, knocked tankards with Pavise, and both held them out to Alric.

Alric rolled his eyes, bumped drinks, and took a sip, to another cheer.

Oh, that’s good.

The brewery didn’t make IPA often, but Alric had always held with the idea of balance that his old world pushed with IPA. A strong hoppy profile had to be matched by a strong malt. The malt was a mix of different toasting levels. It just worked.

“Aww, that’s great.” Came from Stromni, whose shoulders slumped. Pavise nodded and held out his tankard to the dwarf. They knocked and drank again.

Alric just grinned into his stein as he took another sip. Then he noticed something else about the beer: it was strong.

“Three!” Someone shouted from the beer wagon.

“Ya damned liar, that’s two.” Came another voice

“I had one at sunrise!”

“Doesn’t count!”

Alric and Pavise started laughing as more people argued.
