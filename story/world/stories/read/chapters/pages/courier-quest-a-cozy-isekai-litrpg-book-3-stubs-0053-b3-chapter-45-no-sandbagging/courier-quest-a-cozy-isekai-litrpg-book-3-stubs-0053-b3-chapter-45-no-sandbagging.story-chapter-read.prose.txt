“Hold on, there’s no way…”

Trevor stood in awe as he looked at the main feature of the room they had been taken to. Two of the Sammersons, both teenagers by his estimate, were sitting in the middle of the room. They were playing with controllers that weren’t made in any style he had seen before, but they were unmistakable. The kids were observing the wall on the far end.

There were no screens in this world aside from what the Quest Master gave. Lora had said that Sammie the Gamer had never gotten them to work, and Adabelle had made a comment about a possible grudge against the system.

As it turned out, there simply was no need for screens. Sammie had come up with a different solution.

The padded stage filled up the entire wall with curtains blocking the ends. There were two platforms above the floor, separated by about ten feet, and then another one higher up that was directly in the middle. It was such an iconic setup that there was no way Trevor couldn’t recognize it.

Two polygonal humanoids were the main focus on stage. They were beating the ever living tar out of each other.

The fighters were covered in brightly glowing protective gear. One was teal, and the other was yellow. The background fluctuated as they traded blows with the latter color taking up over three quarters of the wall.

Trevor watched with a grin as yellow grabbed teal, threw them on the ground, and then kicked them. The sound that it produced was immensely satisfying, almost like the sound of a dodge ball hitting someone, before the prone fighter rolled to their feet. They jumped onto the platform, evading a strike, before coming back down with a kick.

The teal section of the wall started gaining more ground as the fighter pushed back against the yellow one with a combo of fierce kicks. It appeared as though they were some kind of kickboxing character considering not a single punch was thrown.

Yellow brought their arms in front of them as a shield, alternating between standing and crouching as the strikes came, but the chip damage was still coming through as they were pushed back. Just before the fighter was about to get pushed back beyond the curtain, they stopped blocking and crouched again.

The other fighter attacked high, having clearly expected the jump to get up and over, leaving them vulnerable for a moment. It was a bad read that could have won them the game. Instead, yellow grabbed teal.

One of the kids shot up, throwing their hands in the air as they celebrated. Even without their input, the yellow fighter rolled backwards and tossed teal out of the arena. There was a soft thud, as if they had landed on a cushion, before the victor stood up and began robotically throwing their hands in the air repeatedly in celebration. The background changed to be completely yellow.

“Don’t pout, Hedry,” the victor said, patting her brother on the shoulder. “You might win next time if you stop being such a disaster.”

The boy, who seemed the younger of the two, placed the controller gently back onto the couch before trying to skewer his sister’s hand with his jagged horn. It didn’t work.

“Hey now, no fighting outside of the game, especially when we have guests,” Stammy chastised. The kids were quick to stand up, revealing the same flight suits as their parents.

“Welcome to the Playground!” they said in unison, one much more cheery than the other, and she stuck her tongue out at her brother.

“Thank you. It’s a pleasure to be here,” Adabelle greeted, though her eyes were on the stage and the fighters inhabiting it.

They had moved to the center under the highest platform. Neither of them were glowing anymore, having gone dark just like the background, and seemed to be waiting.

“That looked like a pretty fun show. What do you call this?” Trevor asked.

“This one is Ultimate Fighter Rushdown,” Dee explained. “It was one of the last ones that Sammie the Gamer completed, and eventually became her favorite one to play. She had patched it over twenty times to get the mechanics just right and add more character settings.”

Trevor tugged at Adabelle’s sleeve in his excitement, and she rubbed his back with her tail before turning back to the Sammersons. “This is a pretty wondrous work of art. Can you run me through it? Perhaps while my husband here gives it a chance?”

“Of course!” Stammy answered. “Jedfry, I’m sure Trevor’s already got the gist of things, but can you get him the manual anyway? Don’t want to withhold any information.”

“On it, dad!” the smallest child said, gesturing for the courier to follow him to the table.

Trevor passed Clay to Adabelle, who cradled them in her arms, before heading towards the controllers. He, too, listened to the explanation.

“This here is one of Sammie’s greatest inventions, incorporating both technology and magic in almost equal value,” Stammy said, clear pride in his voice. “The fighters move based on inputs from the controllers there, which you saw Lilsian and Hedry playing when you came in.”

“Got it all warmed up for you,” his daughter chirped.

“Exactly. Anyway, it’s all one big piece of art,” the curator continued. “The fighters accumulate damage in the form of imaginary numbers as they take hits, as indicated by the background, which tells you how easy it is to push them around. If they’re pushed off screen or one color completely overtakes another, then a victor is decided.”

“That is certainly an interesting way to do things,” Adabelle replied as she gave the stage an appreciative glance. “If accumulating damage makes it easier to push the other fighter around, then what happens if you’re winning?”

“You become more firm, so you’re easier to combo,” Lilsian answered with a nod, receiving a questioning glance from the noblewoman. “It’s a game of balance. You get hit further away if you’re losing, making it easier to lose outright. If you’re winning then you have less of a chance to get destroyed but there’s a comeback mechanic built in if you aren’t careful.”

“I see…” Adabelle said before glancing at her husband. “I assume you know what that means, honey?”

“Sure do,” he replied as he flipped through the old, colorful, and unexpectedly thick manual.

Trevor zoned out as he went through it, committing what he could to memory. Parts of it were, thankfully, in English. The whole presentation even reminded him of the game manuals of old where they used to explain the story, moves, and items. Long before companies seemed to almost unanimously decide to get rid of them.

Regardless of what he had become used to, he was engrossed as he read it.

Sammie the Gamer had thrown three different systems into one to make Ultimate Fighter Rushdown, but he was here for it. There was only one model of fighter, but every color had a different moveset programmed in. There were thirteen of them, ranging from styles like pure rushdown, grappling, zoning, and everything in between. There even appeared to be a stance fighter added at the end.

The manual was even more helpful than he expected it to be. It had nothing to do with the technical side of how things worked, instead showing off the character’s movesets. Trevor had access to everyone’s weak and strong strikes, which were dependent on control stick position, aerials and grapples. They were alien to him, but still somewhat familiar. None of these were so far out of his experience that he couldn’t wrap his mind around them.

Trevor nearly jumped as Adabelle placed a hand on her husband’s back, and he gave her a smile. “This is going to be a lot of fun.”

“Honey, did you hear anything Stammy had to say after you answered me?” she asked.

“I… did not,” he admitted with a chuckle, turning towards the curator. “Sorry. This is a really nice manual.”

“No worries. We get that a lot,” the man said happily. “What I was telling your dear wife was that we have a little challenge for you Summoned in honor of Sammie the Gamer.”

“Color me intrigued,” Trevor replied before trading Adabelle the manual for Clay, who oozed in his arms. She began to read it.

“Quest Master, if you will,” Stammy requested, glancing up.

[[New Quest! The Sammie Gamer Gauntlet.

You think you’re hot stuff. I see you there, reading that basic strategy guide, coming up with things you think will work. Your brain is just going wild with the possibilities you have been presented with.

Good, you’re going to need it.

The Sammersons have lived in the Playground all their life. Sure, some move, others spend more time outside, but taking care of this place is something akin to a generational duty for them. They take it seriously, not just because it’s expected, but because they genuinely enjoy it.

And that has made them good at what they do.

Stammy and Dee might not be as good as they used to be thanks to age, but their children are all little monsters. They have been practicing against each other for weeks knowing that you were on your way. The question is, will it be enough?

You will fight the family one by one in a best of one. Win or lose, you move on to the next. Since Boel isn’t currently here, you only have five chances.

Defeat at least two Sammersons in order to win this quest.

Reward? Bragging rights and a little trophy.

Plus, a level up because you’re only one quest away!

Since you are still on your honeymoon, and I am still magnanimous, you may continue to piggyback off of your significant other and vice versa. Between you and me, I think that might be the only way Adabelle wins this.]]

Trevor looked at his wife. She had an expression of intense concentration on her face as she perused the manual. The fact that she was taking this so seriously made him smile.

“You’re right, Quest Master,” he said teasingly. “My wife is very cute when she’s confused.”

The demonkin looked at her husband. It took her a moment to digest what he said before arching her eyebrow. “Is that something the Quest Master said?”

“No, I was making two very different statements,” Trevor answered before nodding towards the book. “Should I play some rounds while you’re reading through it? Or do you want to jump right in?”

Adabelle offered him a small smile as she shook her head. “I think I’m going to need more time, but you go ahead. It seems like you’re ready.”

“You’re welcome to start against Jedfry if you like,” Stammy offered. “If we give you too much time to practice, then the Quest Master won’t like it too much.”

“Free win, though,” Hedry muttered, which earned him an elbow to the ribs from his sister. “What? He’s got a quest and he isn’t confused by the manual. Jed’s going to get destroyed.”

“Nuh uh,” the kid said as Trevor looked down at him. He couldn’t have been more than nine or ten. “You just wanna try on the headband first.”

“Yeah? And?” his older brother scoffed.

“It doesn’t really matter who I play first,” Trevor said with a smile. “I’ll be fighting all of you at least once anyway. Including Boel? I haven’t actually seen him yet and the Quest Master mentioned he isn’t here.”

“Oh, don’t worry about him. He’s getting another one ready. That one’s a team game for when Lora comes back,” Dee explained.

“That sounds great. I’d say I’m looking forward to it, but I’m very excited about this one,” he replied before looking down at Jedfry. “Ready to go?”

The kid nodded, a look of determination on his face, before running off towards the couch to grab the player one controller. Everyone else moved to join the boy, but Trevor caught Stammy’s eye before he could get too far. Adabelle, still reading, hung by her husband.

“So, I don’t really like picking on kids, but how hard should I be trying here?” he asked quietly. Lilsian’s ears twitched at the question, but none of the others seemed to have their father’s exceptional hearing. “Like, do they have a lot of experience playing against Summoned?”

Stammy, to his credit, stifled a laugh. “No, and no mercy,” he answered.

“You’re sure?”

“None of my kids are quitters, but they’re very perceptive. If they think you’re holding back for their sake, then they won’t like it.”

“No sandbagging, got it,” he replied with a nod.

Stammy patted him on the shoulder and joined his family.

“I don’t think these kids understand what they’re about to go through,” Trevor said to Adabelle as they followed. She looked up from the book.

“You’re very confident,” she replied.

He shrugged and waggled his eyebrows. “This is kind of my thing.”

Jedfry offered the courier a black headband as he sat down. It activated from just a touch.

[[The Equalizer has copied your stats. It will last for ten minutes before it needs to be renewed.]]

“That’s pretty neat, I didn’t feel a thing,” Trevor remarked.

“Nope. It’s completely harmless,” Stammy said as he put his hands on his son’s shoulders. “You’ll be just fine, Jedfry. Play like you normally do.”

“Dad, I’m not nervous,” the kid whined.

“I know, I know. Have some fun anyway, okay?”

“Against this rando? There’s no way he’ll win,” Jedfry replied. Trevor laughed, and the kid pouted. “What’s so funny?”

“Nothing, nothing. Sorry,” he said, shaking his head. “Please help this rando out and go easy, eh?”

“Nah.”

“Fair enough.”

As Jedfry put on the headband, Trevor put Clay onto his shoulder and leaned back on the couch. His familiar rested between him and the furniture, watching the fighters on stage with interest.

He grabbed the controller for player two. It was a flat disc that had a surprisingly comfortable grip on the back. There were buttons on the shoulder as well as four on the face. Opposite of those were both a joystick and directional pad. He mashed the buttons, getting used to where they were.

The fighters on stage lit up white. Jedfry was the first to react, swiping the joystick to the side and cycling through the colors. Trevor gestured for Adabelle to bring him the manual, and he skipped ahead a little bit to the setting he had been eying.

There was a method to this. He didn’t really want to drop a game after Stammy told him no mercy, but it was still going to be his first and he didn’t feel right picking on a kid this young. A quick look at the stage showed that Jedfry had locked in on the grappler character, as evident by his half of the screen showing green. Trevor hit the joystick until his fighter glowed red, and he locked it in.

“You mind giving me a minute at the beginning of the match to see the fighter move?” Trevor asked.

“I guess,” Jedfry said, though he didn’t look at the man when he did.

“Cool. Thanks. Good luck, have fun,” he returned, offering a handshake. It was accepted, and the game began.
