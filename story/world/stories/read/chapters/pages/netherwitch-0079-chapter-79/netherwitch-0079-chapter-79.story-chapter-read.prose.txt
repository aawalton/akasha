Lately, I’ve been thinking about how well the Cloud Island Wilderness would stand up if we were attacked by a major power. The superficial presumption is that we’d lose immediately. But I don’t think that conclusion holds. Weaker countries often succeed in fending off stronger countries (see Afghanistan vs the USSR and the US).

But can the Cloud Island Wilderness win? After running the numbers, I’ve concluded that yes, yes we can.

Let’s start with a statement of fact. Military strength relates to a nation’s GDP. The System estimates Heaven’s GDP at around 149 trillion soli. Our GDP is 615 million soli, which is quite a mismatch. Realistically, however, Heaven would only be able to deploy around 1% of its resources in a war against the Cloud Island Wilderness.

That’s 1490 billion soli vs 0.615 billion, or a two thousand times difference.

However, as a defender, we have numerous advantages. We can engage in total warfare, dedicating 40% of our GDP (0.4 factor) to the war. The defender also has a natural 3 to 1 advantage in most cases, but with the long logistical chain we can guesstimate an actual 10 to 1 advantage. Thus, 1490 billion vs (0.615 * 0.4 * 10) = 2.46 billion soli.

That’s the adjusted gap in military strength, giving Heaven a 605x advantage. Which is to say, we’d definitely lose under current conditions. But what about the future? To start, we’d need our GDP to grow by about 600 times. Or, more realistically, by around 400 to 800 times. At which point, we’d be able to put up a good fight.

Which sounds crazy, until you realize how fast our GDP has been growing.

First, let’s take our GDP per capita. Last year it was around 14,000 soli. This has increased by about 10k in the last 10 years. We can expect it to continue to grow due to technological advancement (minor) and rising player levels (major). In fact, player levels are a major factor here, as gamers gain levels much faster and at a lower resource cost than the average demon or celestial. Currently, my conservative estimate is a GDP per capita of 30,000 soli in 20 years.

Which means our population needs to expand to 200 to 400 times its current size to compete with Heaven. Which is to say, we need 8 million to 16 million gamers. That seems long off, but once again, growth applies to this algorithm. Right now, we’re doubling every 2 to 3 years. Thus, in 10 years we’ll have a population of around 450,000 and in 20 years around 4.5 million (optimistically) or 4.5 million in 30 years (pessimistically).

So, if the math holds, 25 to 35 years from now, we’ll be strong enough to fend off Heaven, Hell, or the Fey Federation.

Some caveats: The exponential population growth has been slowing. GDP per capita might also slow, though I think an upside of 50k per capita is more likely. The defense force multiplier might be wrong. I haven’t accounted for military quality differentials or a major power going crazy and throwing its entire economy at us. Conversely, these numbers assume we have no military allies and no one engaging in proxy warfare on our behalf.

“It’s so much fun, doing good deeds just to mess with people,” Sylvia cackled while rubbing her hands together.

The petite asteri was suspended in a sea of stars, twin rivers of glossy silver floating in the void behind her. To reach the astral world, she had gated to the first layer of Tartarus before opening a second gate out into the dark. This alone wasn’t enough. The coast of Tartarus was still under the System’s interdiction. Thus, Sylvia had endured an exhausting two-hour flight to reach her current point.

“The things a girl suffers for an internet connection,” she lamented.

Chuckling, Sylvia snapped the blue window closed. She had rested long enough. It was time to focus on the real reason she’d come out here.

Transcendence.

The System provided little help during the grand mutations. These were obstacles of the soul and the heart. If an alien machine interfered, the final result would be distorted. When a grand mutation reached its resolution, however, it triggered an evolution of the code. The System could safely lend assistance with that.

Sylvia didn’t want to miss that boost. What’s more, the providence she was building might well require the System’s presence.

Which brought her deep into the dark.

She closed her eyes, floating in the emptiness as she sorted through her emotions. Sylvia felt a flutter of anxiety inside of her. Was she ready? Despite everything, she struggled to imagine her ideal. A part of her screamed that she was a fool to come out here. She wasn’t ready.

“Do not hesitate.”

Lord Potami’s warning rang inside her head like a gong.

“The more I fear, the harder it will become.”

Heart firmed, she opened her eyes. Then she emptied her soul. The first item Sylvia removed was the essence core of her Spring Blossom dress. The armor was already donned, transformed by a potion into a pinstriped suit. As the body left her soul, it melted into its shadow. The two parts had become one.

Next came her Starlight Striders, Chrome Tempest, and Resonant Tear. Sylvia stuffed the loose items into a sack she’d tied to her waist. The starry void had its own hazards. One of them was that free-floating objects could be carried away by the currents. If enough time passed while her focus was turned inward, it’d be a pain to hunt everything down.

Gold coins, potion bottles, mirror pads, and the Disk of the Depths came after. One by one, the trash and random junk filling her soul were stuffed into her bag. It was only at the end that she let the most important tools emerge: the legendary artifact, Anbaht, and her realm ring array, Hidden Hut. The realm ring array went into the pack. Anbaht remained around her neck. The dark pendant would conceal her during Transcendence.

Flask in hand, she took a deep breath. She was ready. She knew she was ready. There was nothing to fear. Lord Potami believed in her. Emmy believed in her.

Sylvia popped the cork. “Bottoms up,” she murmured.

The witch tipped the bottle back. Dense essence oozed down her throat, turning into an airy fog as it filled her stomach. She gulped, feeling as though she were swallowing gassy slime. Despite its ephemeral character, the potion lingered inside of her like a lump of frigid lead. Psychic energy burbled, laced with the essence of her soul. Her eyes drooped, a wispy dream fluttering at the edge of her consciousness.

To Awaken, the soul was placed in a state between life and death. The conscious mind would rise from the abyss, facing the subconscious. If the two parts of self found unified purpose, the strength of the soul would surge, forcing a grand mutation of the bloodline code.

Transcendence demanded the exact opposite.

Instead of the consciousness rising from the darkness of death, the living mind must turn inward, plunging deep into the fortress of the subconscious. Sylvia felt her psyche swell even as the potion dragged her under. Before sleep could claim her entirely, she mustered her will. Then she dove into her own mind.

Darkness.

A bulb flickered.

Sylvia found herself in a broken bedroom. Shattered floors, walls, and ceiling opened up into an infinite abyss. Cheap carpet squished beneath her black dress shoes, her pinstriped jacket hugging her frame.

She recognized the place immediately.

It was Eric’s room. To Sylvia’s right was her old, twin-sized bed, sheets and covers askew. To her left was a bookshelf, filled with manga, ancient games, and Eric’s old college textbooks. In front was her computer desk, the monitor glowing pure white, the light sharp against the dull yellow cast by the bulb above.

“I thought I’d left this behind,” Sylvia muttered, nudging a dirty t-shirt to the side with her cane.

“You did.”

The office chair in front of the computer spun. Seated within was a dainty figure, her silver hair woven into a French braid. Navy-blue school robes hung on the girl’s shoulders, her tiny figure so slim she’d been all but invisible until the chair turned around.

“Eric’s life no longer belongs to us,” French Braid said. “But his past is our past. He is the root from which we were grown. A root you came here to sever.”

Another presence let out a disdainful huff. Sylvia’s gaze turned to see the heavyset body of Eric sitting on the twin-sized bed. His short, dark hair showed none of the thinning he had suffered in his thirties. His body was robust, with layers of muscle underneath the fat.

“You said you wouldn’t mutilate your soul,” College Eric accused. “Why are you here? You know you aren’t ready. Or do you plan to turn us into that thing?”

Sylvia grimaced.

“Emmy wanted you to stay yourself,” a younger voice chimed. A little, chubby Eric was curled up near the closet doors. “She’s so nice. Why are you planning to betray her?”

The twin-tailed girl sucked in a breath. “I see. You three are my doubts.”

“No, we’re your past,” French Braid countered. The witch’s pink eyes glowed in the bedroom’s dim light. Her legs crossed in a neat but dangerous manner. “You know what Transcendence means. You know what it requires. To transcend, you must shed your mortal origin and embrace a new ideal.”

“The thing you’re shedding? That’d be me,” College Eric spat.

“I don’t want to be a princess,” Young Eric added. “I want to be a hero.”

Sylvia’s gaze tightened. Bang! The twin-tailed witch slammed her cane into the floor, interrupting their lecture.

“All of you, shut up,” she snarled. “You won’t drag me down with your doubts.”

“This isn’t a conversation,” she proclaimed, slowly finding her words. “This is my monologue. I’m here to talk to you, not to listen when you speak to me. I’ll start with the simple truth. You three aren’t me.”

“Just as you aren’t him,” she continued, her cane swaying from her female clone to her older male body. “Experience changes us. Every day, we wake up a new person. We learn. We grow. Even the body changes, shedding cells as the years pass.”

“Perhaps, but so what?” Sylvia scoffed. “Change isn’t something to be feared. Change can make us better, stronger, smarter, wiser.”

“But you don’t have an ideal,” Young Eric spoke up with a whiny voice. “So how do you know you’ll change into a better Sylvia? I don’t want to be a bimbo. I don’t want to be the doll princess, either. I want to be strong and cool, then I want to conquer the world!”

“You don’t need an ideal to become better,” Sylvia said smoothly. Yes, that was right. She didn’t need an ideal. “People can learn from individual failures and successes. They don’t need to idolize someone or something in specific. Take you, for example.”

The twin-tailed girl gestured toward Young Eric with her cane. The chubby ten-year-old perked up at the attention.

“I envy your clarity of desire,” she said. “As we grow older, our minds grow deeper. To better obtain what we want, we learn to carve the intermediate steps. No child starts life loving money. They learn to love money because money can be used to buy candy, toys, or video games. Sometimes we learn those lessons too well and forget money is a means, not a goal unto itself.”

Sylvia’s voice rose, gaining energy as she went.

“But your ignorance? Your flighty dreams? I want none of it,” Sylvia declared. “Conquer the world? Did you even stop to think for ten seconds what that’d do to the people living inside of it?”

She turned sharply, thrusting her cane toward College Eric.

“And you!” Sylvia spat. “You didn’t fight hard enough. You didn’t work hard enough. Yes, it was unfair. The world set us on a terrible path, but there were a great many forks on our road. We were just too scared and too lazy to step off the path. I don’t hate you. I understand you better than anyone. We had a lot of good moments and enjoyed a lot of great games. But you know as well as I that we should’ve studied harder. If we had, we would’ve ended up with something more than this!”

She gestured widely to the apartment. As she did, the walls began to shift and crack. The space grew larger by a third. Dirty clothes picked themselves up off the floor before flying into a basket. The numerous wrappers piled up on the old computer desk stood, then jumped into the trashcan.

Sylvia turned to French Braid.

“In our first year in the Academy, we didn’t appreciate the gift we were given. Yes, we’d been turned into a girl. Yes, it was unfair and uncomfortable. But we had friends. We had a future. We had Emily,” she condemned, glaring at her younger female self. “Of all the things I regret in this life, wasting what little time I had with Emily before her soul merged with Esmeralda’s hurts the most.”

Emmy was Sylvia’s wonderful and beautiful wife. She loved her dearly. But Emily had deserved a chance to experience that love as well. She regretted it. Sylvia regretted it deeply. She would happily endure Charm Club for a full ten years if she had but one chance to share the love Emily had deserved.

“I don’t need an ideal because – ”

Sylvia froze in mid-rant. Goosebumps crawled over her skin, the words on her lips forgotten. She didn’t need an ideal. The twin-tailed witch trembled. Suddenly, the truth hit her like a freight train striking a petite girl foolish enough to stand in a tunnel.

She didn’t need an ideal, because she already had one.

Sylvia had been looking in the wrong direction. She’d been looking outside and forward when she should’ve been looking inside and back. Over the last fourteen years, she had grown. She’d transformed from a cynical fat man into a woman she could respect.

When had it started? Perhaps it was the first night in Orasul Lunii, when she had saved Valerie and Margret. A moment of will, pride, and foolishness engraved on Sylvia’s heart. The figure only grew larger when she stayed with Gavin, Elroy, and Iulian to fight the shamblers when she could’ve so easily fled. Then, on Tartarus, she’d risked her soul to save Belkis from the Duat Dream Drop.

She’d become a hero, a towering figure who faced the darkness, everyone gazing at her back in admiration.

But that wasn’t all. Sylvia had discovered the love and friendship Eric had always lacked. Three friends in the Academy. A shared kiss on Faded Star. Finally, Sylvia had bared her heart and stolen Emmy’s lips in their basement. A different kind of courage, but as a wife and an older woman, she knew that type of bravery was just as important.

Sylvia’s life was a litany of accomplishments. She’d built the Beginner’s Village, Swift Brooms, and Swift Ships. She had built so many things she was proud of. The truth dawned on her. For the last fourteen years, she, Sylvia Swallows, had been chasing her ideal.

“I don’t need an ideal,” she laughed. It was a mad laugh, a happy laugh, a freeing laugh echoing from her heart in an epiphany. “I don’t need an ideal, because I’ve already become it!”

When she spoke, the world thrummed in response. Sylvia could feel something rising inside of her. A clarity of purpose that brought the power of her soul to a crescendo. She’d been expecting a large jump, a huge change to herself. But she’d already made most of the changes necessary for Transcendence. All she had to do now was to finish what she had started.

“With every step I took in my new life, I conquered my past,” she declared. The dream wavered with the force of her will, the walls of the apartment groaning. “Eric Swallow is dead. The person standing here before you is Sylvia Swallows, a witch, a woman, a wife, a hero, an inventor, a tycoon, and yes, a princess!”

Her shout was a shock wave. The room shattered into splinters. Boards and broken fragments rolled in the dark only to be caught in a growing swirl. The charred remnants of the pink room below were pulled into the storm, soon to be joined by the library, the forest, and the ivory tower.

“I’m not ashamed of who I am,” she continued. “I am proud to be a girl. I am proud to be a princess. I like being cute, and I don’t want to be held down by my past or my fears a single instant longer!”

With her roar, the whirlwind became strangely ordered. Bricks, stone, and lumber reassembled, creating the skeleton of a room far larger. At the center was a king-sized canopy bed fit for two. A walk-in closet took form, filled with beautiful dresses. Some belonged to Sylvia. Others belonged to Emmy.

“But I’m not ashamed of my past either,” she said firmly. “You three are the road I walked to reach this future. Your failures are my failures, and those failures are my lessons. Your accomplishments are my guiding light. Every single one of you has helped me grow into the woman I’ve become. A woman who can walk boldly into the future. For that, I thank you. But you are the past. I only need to walk forward. I will never walk back.”

She didn’t wait for their response. Sylvia swept out of the room, a house growing behind her. Nay, an entire world assembled: a long, sandy beach with dense jungle and high mountains beyond. Far to the west, a distant city lingered.

Unseen by her eyes, the image wavered like a mirage. Then it vanished. Darkness crowded around her, white ripples arising on the glassy surface with every step she took. Her clothes flowered, the pink ruffles of Spring Blossom emerging like a butterfly from the lie that was her pinstriped suit. The cane in her hand lengthened, Chrome Tempest reclaiming its full grandeur.

In the deepest, darkest reaches of her soul, there was naught but silence.

But she wasn’t alone. She was never alone. There was a being creeping along the edges of her existence. When the barrier was weak, such as during the moments of death, that entity would intrude upon her, a thief stealing into the center of her soul.

For the first time, it was Sylvia who called out. “Sister, can you hear me?”

Hear me, hear me, hear me.

Her voice echoed in the nothingness. She felt something stir in the distance. A presence drew closer, coils of wire unwinding in the atramentous ocean beneath her feet. The lake was a wall, standing between two souls. On the other side was a sibling that had never been, longing to return to her embrace.

The situation was sad and terrifying.

“Brother, won’t you be with me – be with me?”

“I know you hurt,” Sylvia soothed. “You wish to return to what was. But we are not the same. I am me and you are you. We were just two souls in one body.”

“Brother, let me hold you – hold you.”

“Don’t worry, sister. When the time is right, I’ll let you in,” she explained softly. “And not just for one moment. Forever. We’ll never be one, but we can be closer. Our souls can touch, brushing against each other to fight the loneliness. But there is one thing I have to correct. I’m not your brother, little brat. I’m your big sister now, so you better start calling me Big Sis Sylvia, or you’re in for a beating.”

Sylvia glared down at the pool of dark. Wires pushed against the surface, trying to pierce through with childish need. She could feel the murmur of the System’s intent, a terrible longing. A soul, once torn, would always strive to become whole. When Lucifer had taken from young Eric the spirit of his twin sister, he’d erred in the direction of Eric’s safety, leaving many fragments behind.

Those pieces had long since been absorbed into her soul, leaving no distinction. Thus, the longing left behind could never be resolved. But that didn’t mean it couldn’t be tamed.

Sylvia drew in a long breath. She had the will. She had her ideal. Now, all she needed to do was build her foundation.

“Everything I am is thanks to the System.”

Long ago, she’d accepted that truth. Yes, she had worked hard for what she had. She’d braved many dangers. But, without the System, none of those efforts would’ve borne such delicious fruit. Life wasn’t the product of simple arithmetic like A+B. Instead, outcomes came from the multiplication of A * B, making it impossible to say which component was more important.

“Knowing this, it is fitting that I turn my crutch into ingots and use it to reforge my bones.”

Taking one last moment to school herself, Sylvia sang. Thousands of runes poured from her lips. In alchemy, there was a branch of production called synthesis. By chanting a code, a mage could speak a substance into existence.

What she built was something entirely different.

With her will, Sylvia wove an endless stream of golden thread, spinning sequences of runes into greater sigils. The glyphs curled into giant clumps, becoming shimmering instruments floating in the black sky. The runes she spoke belonged to a chain she knew well. It was the same sequence she’d imprinted upon every dungeon. A System kernel set for a node in the System’s network.

Slowly, piece by piece and gear by gear, the machine was assembled. As the runes piled up into the millions, the great construct showed signs of life.

Sylvia skimmed over the results to ensure there were no mistakes. Then she took a step back.

“Come, sister. I know it isn’t what you wanted, but it’s the closest thing I can offer.”

The lake beneath her feet rippled, a cascade of concentric circles that filled the black abyss with glimmering white. Ps-pshh, pshh! Water thrashed as wires ripped through the surface, the cords rising up like the heads of an electric hydra. Copper glinted, sharp needles extending from every tentacle’s tip.

Like snakes, they struck.

As a forest of vines shot toward her, Sylvia raised a hand. Water burst from the lake’s surface, catching the cords in a serpentine swirl. Forcefully, she shifted the direction of the attack. Instead of striking her, the cords punched into the gold shell she’d created.

C-c-c-click.

Every single one of them found a prepared socket.

“Sister – sister, sister – I can feel you.” The child’s cry was soft and full of wonder. The wires wormed, hoping to dig deeper. “Be with me – be with me. Stay with me – stay with me.”

“It’s okay. You can stay as long as you want,” Sylvia offered. “We were always meant to be together. Though, I can’t promise I’ll always be within reach.”

The witch let out a breath. She could feel the System, like a happy child poking around at the edges of her soul.

“As for me,” she murmured, “it’s time to sever my mortality.”

The world rippled. Beneath the golden machine, a white circle expanded. Then another. And another. More and more, until the sea of darkness transformed into an ocean of pure white. Runic threads flared. The machine turned, gears and components whirling to life. Suddenly, a thick golden pillar shot down from the contraption, piercing into the ocean of self. A chain of threads spread through Sylvia’s soul in dense, geometric patterns.

She closed her eyes. She could feel it. Something within her was changing. Transcendence had begun.

Unheard and unseen, the System’s monospaced font scrolled in her heart.

“I’m not even angry,” she said. Then she paused. “But the new class name is lacking.”

Eyes narrowed, she made a small tweak. Then, with a happy smile, her gaze turned to the next window.

“Just the two traits?” Sylvia questioned. Her pastel pink eyes shifted to the next window. “Oh, I see how this works. Now that I’ve reached Transcendence, I’m allowed to pick according to my preference.”

She set her evolutionary choices aside for the moment. First, she wanted to review what she’d gained.

At the start was the prize of every grand mutation: the advancement of her core. Sylvia’s new heart provided a huge leap in hitpoints and maximum mana. Furthermore, her compatibility with chaos domain elements had increased. Gate would cost less mana, chaos ether would gather faster, and fate-based wishes would be more easily detected. She could even transmute chaos ether, with her core acting like a six-element staff. With space dust alone she could fuel void, realm, law, causality, and fate magics.

Convenient.

The real meat of her evolution, however, was digitized soul. This was the foundation of Sylvia’s providence and the result was magnificent. Dungeon Administrator boosted her digital processing speed by a large factor. Never mind Doom, she could probably run some rather advanced Earthside games on her local System. Her digital control, sensitivity, and awareness had likewise undergone a major improvement. She could, quite literally, feel everything happening on her server rack as though it were part of herself.

Including the actions of her rambunctious twin sister.

Obstacles to Advancement

Various factors determine how far a soul in the netherworld will advance. Three play a particularly strong role: talent, wealth, and ambition. In Heaven, a fourth factor applies: government permission.

The majority of all demons, celestials, and fey are unconsolidated or first consolidation. Souls of this level live for around 500 to 1000 years. As such, two-thirds of the Hell and Fey Federation populations that are currently unconsolidated are simply persons who have yet to complete their first consolidation. That is to say, they are children.

The long delays are because low-talent individuals often screw up their first couple of advancement attempts, forcing them to transmigrate before proceeding.

For those reaching the first consolidation, the biggest obstacles to advancement are ambition and talent. Many immortals simply decide that their current cultivation level is ‘good enough.’ This attitude is particularly common among those with an innate talent rating below 40%, as reaching the second consolidation demands a lot of time, effort, and money.

Similar attitudes appear in the second consolidation, with Awakening being out of easy reach. Awakened demons, by contrast, can usually reach the third consolidation with effort. But many decide that they are already sufficiently elite.

By the time immortals reach their third consolidation, the majority of those remaining are full of ambition. Talent, however, remains an obstacle. By contrast, pretty much everyone who reaches Transcendence has the full trinity of talent, wealth, and ambition, resulting in high rates of carry-through into the fifth and sixth consolidations. Only Apotheosis remains as a major roadblock.

After reaching the arch level, the size of the soul becomes a problem. Due to taking part of the world into one’s being, the soul becomes stretched. This sharply limits growth, leaving only a handful of persons physically capable of reaching the seventh or eighth consolidations without killing themselves.
