Samantha was already asleep by the time Asher got back to their room, and he did his best to quietly clean himself off before slipping under the covers beside her. She shifted slightly and wrapped her arms around him without waking, and he smiled to himself as he gently ran his fingers through her hair. Despite the dozens of miles he’d run into the deadlands, fighting against zombies, Servants, and the King of Death himself, Asher wasn’t as tired as he would have expected. Something about Kroult’s story was still resonating with him, and he shivered at the realization that the man’s situation could very well have been his own if Zygrul hadn’t had his own agenda and he hadn’t gotten lucky with his Spatial element. He’d come quite a long way from that initial, invisible cell he’d been summoned within, but he very well could have remained trapped and studied for what little remained of his natural life.

Shaking the thought free from his head, Asher pulled up his interface, taking stock of his recent gains from defeating the King of Death and so many of his powerful servants. He’d gotten quite a lot of level ups recently, another new class, and even evolved his very first skill into the third tier.

Asher contained his urge to whistle as he grinned at his current skills. The fact that over half of them had already hit level 30 was mind boggling to him, especially seeing as he had only been in the Stage of Bleed for barely a week now. It had been entirely because of Judgement lagging behind all his other skills that he’d only recently entered this next stage of elemental advancement, and he was still waiting on his very first blended skill to manifest between his different elements.

Patience is a virtue, he reminded himself, scratching his chin as he took in his other low skills. At this point, his skills were starting to show some concerning gaps. The fact that he had so many skills already nearing their third tier while a few of them were still in the mid to low twenties just went to show that he wasn’t doing as good a job balancing himself as he could have been. It came as no surprise that all of his combat-focused skills were so much higher, seeing as he practically lived and breathed fighting these days. That said, he really needed to devote some time to rounding out his lower skills.

Judgement was a tricky one, because despite the fact that it was a passive skill that triggered pretty much anytime he looked at someone, he really only noticed a person’s aura if it were notable. Such as if they were a remarkable fighter, or had an impressive history or resolve that they lived by. He needed to continue meeting powerful people in order to level that skill, which meant people watching was actually something he should focus on.

Recall was another interesting one as he utilized the skill plenty enough, but he was pretty sure he needed to continue Recalling to new places and in different ways to keep the skill leveling quickly. Distance wasn’t as much of a factor as location, which meant he couldn’t just keep bouncing back and forth across the kingdom. He needed more variety than that. He had a few ideas as to how he might be able to work the skill, and he had a feeling he and his team would be paying a friend of his who really liked getting scratched behind the ears a visit before long.

The last of his lagging skills was Permanence. Before evolving the skill, there really hadn’t been a lot he could do for that one. He used the skill to borrow skill effects from artifacts and keep them on his person even after giving the artifact up. The skill slowly leveled over time from its passive portion, and that was pretty much that. Currently, Asher had the tier-2 Hidden Presence skill, the tier-2 Shard Detection skill, and the tier-1 Lesser Realm Sight skill all locked onto his person with it.

However, Permanence now had a second-tier effect all of its own that he was still experimenting with.

Tier 2: Bring permanence to the world around you

Out of all of his skills, this one was the one he was both most confused by, and at the same time most hopeful for. He’d originally evolved the skill within the wild realm and gained the power to stabilize the realm around him. While that effect had been massive and Asher didn’t regret doing so for a second, that second-tier effect was a bit more confusing here on the material realm.

At the moment, he was running a simple experiment with the help of Wilna, the craston librarian over in Whikoga’s library. After handing her an ordinary piece of fruit, Asher had focused on his skill’s newest ability, desperately willing the fruit to refrain from aging. Wilna had then suggested he leave the fruit with her for a week or so as he went about his ordinary life and she kept an eye on it with her tier-3 Identify skill that allowed her to observe the age of anything she looked at. If everything worked as he’d hoped…

He wouldn’t have to fear watching everyone he loved die around him from old age while he remained eternally in his early twenties.

Asher shivered as he pulled Samantha closer to him. The hunter smiled and nestled her head against his chest as she kept sleeping, and he tried to calm his racing heart as he realized he was going to have to have the conversation he’d been putting off before long. He and Samantha had decided ages ago that they would tell the other everything. No secrets. No lies.

But Asher hadn’t actually told her what Wilna had revealed to him. That due to his Eternity element’s passive, he was no longer aging. She’d given him ‘the talk’ about how he’d eventually have to witness everyone he knew and loved grow old and die, and despite his insistence he’d find a way to extend his immortality to those he cared about, Wilna had simply given him a sad smile and promised she’d be there for him when he needed her. Asher promised himself he’d tell Samantha what was going on as soon as he actually had a way to extend that immortality to her, only…

What if she didn’t want to join him?

Asher stared up at the dark ceiling, feeling Samantha’s head rise and fall along with his chest as she slept peacefully. Even if his experiment bore fruit, in a quite literal sense in this case, there was no guarantee she’d agree to joining him in immortality. Asher already knew from Samantha’s own admittance she was looking forward to growing old together and even starting a family of their own one day. If he could only keep one person’s age static, would Samantha be willing to remain young with him forever if it meant watching Brian grow old and die in exchange? If push came to shove…

Who would Samantha choose? Her lover that she’d known for all of a few months? Or her brother who she’d raised for the past eight years after their parents passed away?

Asher feared he already knew the answer to that question, and that scared him more than any fight. He’d rather get torn in half a hundred more times by a thousand greater demons than have Samantha reject his offer of immortality. A tiny part of him that was terrified of that potential rejection whispered that he should just use the skill on her without even saying anything and simply act like he was doing her a favor in a few years when she finally noticed she wasn’t aging along with the rest of the world. While he’d never do something like that to her without asking, Asher hated himself for even having the idea in the first place.

Realizing he wasn’t going to be getting any sleep, Asher sighed as he quietly extracted himself out from underneath the sleeping hunter. Slipping into his Personal Rift to get dressed, he used Astral Dip to phase through the palace wall, letting himself drop to the grounds below before catching himself with his Boots of Levitation.

Switching his class over to assassin, Asher stuck to the shadows and utilized the class’s silent movement as he evaded the regular guard patrolling the palace grounds and made his way into the city. Ironically, despite everything he’d been through over the past twenty-four hours in the Deadlands, because the King of Death had actually killed him for a few hours, Asher felt fully rested. He darted from rooftop to rooftop with rapid applications of Distortion, careful to ensure that he didn’t step on any loose tiles and tumble to a rather embarrassing death. If anyone knew how many times The Reaper had nearly broken his neck when he first started practicing running across rooftops in this way, his reputation would probably be a little bit different.

Beyond ensuring he didn’t break his neck, Asher didn’t really focus on anything as he ran. He didn’t think about the ten remaining liches making up the Council of Death that still wanted to scrape the element of Eternity from his soul. He didn’t think about remaining young while everyone he cared about died around him. He didn’t even think about the Head of the Assassin’s Guild who had been eerily silent ever since Asher had dismantled their deadliest skill and bested all ten of their Knuckles at the same time. Despite the Head supposedly operating out of the capital, Asher hadn’t seen any sign of the Guild in all the time he’d been staying at the royal palace. Seeing as the Head had sworn they would kill him for discovering the secret behind their power, their complete radio silence was more than a little concerning. Even so, Asher pointedly didn’t think about that.

Not until a serrated spear of pure darkness shot out from the shadow of a nearby chimney, shooting straight through where he’d existed a split second ago. By now, Asher’s first reflex to any sudden movements he wasn’t expecting was to slip directly into the astral realm. And while that instinct wasn’t a great one to have in the wild realm where his skill didn’t work, here on the material, it had saved his life more times than he could count.

The moment the attack phased through him, Asher stopped bemoaning his fate, his daggers already in hand as he quickly scanned the surrounding rooftops for any sign of his attacker. While he couldn’t rule out the Head and any of their Knuckles just yet, the spear hadn’t looked like the same one that Wolf used. The Head didn’t exactly have a monopoly on Shadow elements, and Asher felt pretty certain if they had been the one to come after him again, their attack would have been a lot more dangerous than a single spear thrust.

Expecting his attacker to try and slip away, Asher blinked as three people manifested around him in a large, loose triangle, doing the exact opposite of running as they slipped out of the shadows to reveal themselves. Appearing on adjacent rooftops, they were clearly trying to keep their distance from him. Each one of them wore jet-black clothing clearly designed to blend into the shadows of the night, and each one had their faces completely covered other than a small slit for their eyes.

“This is an unexpected surprise,” Asher admitted, returning to the material as he looked between their hidden faces. Other than the fact that one of his assailants was larger and had more of a bulky frame than the others, he couldn’t tell the three apart at all. Identify and Judgement weren’t giving him anything to work with either. “Those are some powerful skills you’ve got there in order to keep yourselves hidden. Or artifacts, I guess. I’ll figure out which is the case after I kill you.”

Yet again, his assailants surprised him. Rather than launch another attack, engage him in banter, or turn and try to flee, they did something he hadn’t expected in the slightest.

In perfect sync, each of the three strangers crouched down and jammed a small spike of metal into the rooftops beneath their feet. Asher didn’t have to wonder what exactly they were doing for long, as the moment all three spikes were in place, a pyramid of almost-invisible energy spread out between them, trapping the four of them inside. A quick attempt at using Recall confirmed his suspicions, and Asher frowned as he realized he’d been cut off from the rest of the world. Whatever those spikes were, they were enchanted with Spatial users in mind. He’d already suspected that this wasn’t just some random attack, but it was nice to have his suspicions confirmed. Whoever these people were, they’d come prepared.

Prepared to kill off a Spatial element user.
