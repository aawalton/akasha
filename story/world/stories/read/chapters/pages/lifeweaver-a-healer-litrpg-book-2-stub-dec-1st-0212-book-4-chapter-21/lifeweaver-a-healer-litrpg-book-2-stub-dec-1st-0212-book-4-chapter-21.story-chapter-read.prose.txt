After another moment of cursing and threats, along with some arm-pulling, Cage left the cell with Dot in tow. Straining Weaver's Perception, Luke followed their heartbeats to make sure they were well out of hearing range. Once they were gone, he waited a little longer. Both of them were Lifeweavers, after all, and he couldn't risk either of them noticing anything. With how careful the Singers were, judging by the restraints, gas, lights, and mana draining, he didn't doubt they had some sort of monitoring equipment in the room. That meant he needed to be both quick and decisive with his next actions.

When Cage entered the cell, or right before it, an orange light had glowed on the door. That glow reminded Luke of something. A way out, perhaps. The device they had him hooked up to, the one draining his mana, didn't touch the void mana. That simple fact made him think that maybe, just maybe, it was tuned to normal, unattuned mana. What the Singers didn't know was that Luke had access to not only that and void mana, but a third kind. He didn't dare touch the void mana, but what about infernal mana?

Killing Ezezal and finishing the system event awarded him a new title, one with a little more weight than his previous goblin-slayer one.

Demonic Avatar: Your expertise in the art of killing demonkind, as well as your ability to draw on their lifeblood, has granted you this title. Increased resistance to infernal mana. Ability to regenerate infernal mana alongside untinted mana and use it as your lifeblood.

Remembering back to when he'd used infernal mana, he couldn't help but make a face. The pain searing him from the inside was not something to scoff at. No, it wasn't as bad as suffering under the void mana, but it wasn't a walk in the park either. Under the present circumstances, though, perhaps he could stand a little more suffering. Thus far, he hadn't even attempted to use infernal mana, but as he considered the idea, knowledge on how to do so poured into him, as if he had downloaded instructions straight into his brain. It was weird and disconcerting, but just what he needed in that moment. The title granted him increased resistance to infernal mana. That would help against the pain, he told himself as he willed his meta-heart to switch over from untinted mana, as the title named it, to infernal mana. It wasn't one sort of mana or the other, but rather allowing infernal mana to regenerate as well.

Luke's meta-heart burned, but that wasn't all. Infernal mana poured from the meta-heart into the physical heart. He gritted his teeth against the pain and discomfort. At first, it felt like the trickle of infernal mana would turn his insides to lava as it coursed through both his heart and mana channels. It didn't destroy them, but he felt them change and expand to allow the burning sludge to make its way through his body. Agony was a tidy way of summing it up. The regeneration was slow, and Luke blinked when he realized his mana bar wasn't filling up. Instead, there was a new orange bar alongside his health, stamina, and mana. At his current level, it seemed he couldn't hold much of it, far less than his untinted mana pool, but it still felt like his body would burst like a water balloon, spilling the demonic, orange sludge all over the floor. Looking down at himself, he even saw a soft, orange glow in the middle of his chest. Why had they stolen his T-shirt? Luke added it to his long list of reasons to make them pay as he struggled to get himself under control. Pain surged again when he attempted to use the infernal mana, but it worked, just like he had hoped it would.

The thread of mana that emerged from his right hand to slice through the restraints glowed orange and looked far more solid than the regular wisp-like threads, and with Weaver's Whip, the metal almost seemed to melt away as he cut it, offering no resistance whatsoever.

"Outstanding," Luke whispered, still steeling himself against the pain.

A few breaths later, the alarms sounded. The strobing lights and gas returned, as well as electric shocks through the restraints. Ignoring it all, Luke freed his remaining limbs and slumped down to his knees. He needed a good rest, but this was not the time for it. Not yet. Weaver's Perception told him of the guards coming before the door opened, and he pushed to his feet, readying himself to face them. The pair of Singers at the front wore armor polished to a shine. Seeing his own reflection in their breastplates made him pause for a moment to look at his distorted face in the metal. His eyes glowed orange.

Fixing the Singers with a glare, they seemed taken aback as well, and that moment of hesitation was more than he needed to deal with them. A glowing thread of mana entered the Singer to the left before he could as much as raise his weapon, and the screams that followed told Luke he wasn't the only one who felt the pain of having infernal mana in his system. Moving the thread from one Singer to the next, Luke soon had them all on the floor writhing in pain, and by the time he'd stepped out of the cell, Luke's inventory wasn't empty anymore. They'd taken Smudge's snacks. It was only fair that he used their meta-hearts to replenish his stores.

The all-white corridor outside the cell was empty, but Luke knew it would stay that way for long. More Singers were coming, and they wouldn't all be pushovers like the first ones that arrived. He didn't have much time. Doors flanked both sides of the corridor. With no discernible way of opening them, Luke cut the wall open. Every use of infernal mana was like stoking a burning fire within himself, but he didn't have much choice other than to bear it. Now that he was out of the restraints, his regular mana regeneration had set in, but it was still slow going. Rather than push the infernal mana aside, the two seemed fine with coexisting inside Luke's metaphysical self. The infernal mana broke through that barrier and into his physical body, while the untinted mana did not, at least not on its own, and he didn't have mana to spare for experimenting at that moment. Each point of untinted mana regenerated eased the headache and dizziness. That was something, at least. It also lessened the pain from infernal mana, as if soothing a wound, and for that, he was grateful.

"Ray!" Luke shouted, his voice much louder than intended. He felt stronger, more alive, than in a long time. Another effect of the infernal mana surging through his system? A likely culprit, if ever there was one. Ray didn't respond, so Luke cut him free and was about to enter his friend's mana channels to heal a wound. Catching himself, he pulled back the thread of mana. When he got his start as a Lifeweaver, the pain of healing had been a constant issue, but that had been nothing compared to what his patient would feel if he attempted to heal with infernal mana. No, infernal mana he would need to use for more destructive tasks.

"Luke?" Ray whispered, squeezing his eyes shut before opening them and blinking.

"We're getting out of here now," Luke assured Ray.

"Your eyes are orange."

Luke nodded and hauled Ray's arm over his shoulder. "I know, buddy."

"Also, you're leaking orange," Ray added with a chuckle. "Put a damn shirt on."

Looking down, Luke saw that Ray was right. Orange sludge ran down his stomach. It originated from a puncture wound a little higher up on his chest. One of the Singers from before must've hit him without Luke noticing, and the burning pain inside had masked the pain from the wound. Just to see how it would go, Luke stitched himself up with infernal mana. Yep. Pain. A lot of it.

"Shit," Luke grunted, seeing the blackened stitches following the healing. Those might never go away.

Weaver's Perception notified him of the coming Singers even before they rounded the corner to the jail corridor, and Luke leaned against a wall, breathing. "We need to leave now. Hold on, and I'll have us out of here in no time." He'd regenerated enough untinted mana now to make the leap, even if gathering the concentration to do so would be a struggle.

"What?" Ray said, breaking free of Luke's supportive grip. "No!"

"Why not?"

Ray took a step back. "We have a job to do here, remember? The portals, the choir-thing?" He held out a hand, palm up, then seemed to realize he didn't have access to his weapons. "Also, I need to go get my dagger."

"There'll be an army coming for us."

"Are you saying we can't take down an army?"

Luke chuckled but kept his senses primed on the approaching Singers. They were being careful rather than rushing in. Not a good sign. "Didn't go so well before."

"You look like a badass now with those glowing eyes. Just get me my weapons and my potions, and we'll get to work again. There are still a lot of these bitches for me to kill. And truth be told, I need the XP. Also, shouldn't we rescue your ex? She was in here before and healed me."

Ray held out his hand for Luke to shake in agreement. It was too late to argue about it. Singers poured into the cell. Luke reached for the hand, intending to use it and pull himself off the wall. Everything shifted as soon as he touched Ray, and color drained from the room.

"What?" Luke asked.

"Hold on to my hand." Ray grinned at him. "First time in stealth? Welcome to my world."
