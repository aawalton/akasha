[Becoming the Sovereign of a Lesser Deity]

The lesser deity Liraelwyn has offered itself to you. It wishes to accept your mark and transform according to your Authority.

Your current Rank is insufficient to accommodate another deity within your Domain.

You may still grant Liraelwyn your mark. However, it will not inherit the uniqueness tied to your Authority, and accommodating it later will become impossible. A mark willingly accepted by a deity can only be removed upon the death of its sovereign.

I quickly read the blue notification and returned my attention to Pintre.

Even if my worst fear, that whatever Pintre was doing might somehow harm me, had been eased, I still had to stop it.

If I allowed Pintre to accept my mark now, I would permanently eliminate the possibility of it ever becoming a true part of my Authority. I had no idea what that actually meant, or what kind of powers the accommodation mentioned by the system might grant either Pintre or me, but I decided it was worth waiting to find out.

For now, I simply needed to stop Pintre before it finished muttering whatever spell it was chanting.

I willed my divinity forward.

Crimson surged across my realm and enveloped the kneeling deity.

Pintre raised its head in surprise, a trace of fear appearing in its golden eyes as my divinity swept it backward and pinned it against the dark floor of my dominion. Only when the trembling of my realm subsided, and I was certain the creature had stopped chanting, did I allow the pressure to ease.

The crimson energy coiled around the half-halvyn for another moment before slowly fading.

Pintre pushed itself upright and sat there with its legs sprawled before it, supporting itself with both hands. It trembled as it stared at my manifestation without saying a word.

“I mean you no harm, Pintre,” I said, realizing that it was frightened and perhaps even felt betrayed. “If I had not stopped you, you would have ruined your chance to truly become part of my kingdom.”

It remained silent, its golden eyes wet.

“The method you used might have allowed you to accept my mark, but it would also have destroyed any chance of us forming a stronger bond later. Don’t you want to be able to enter my kingdom whenever you wish?”

Pintre still said nothing, but I noticed some of the fear in its eyes begin to fade. Now I only needed one final push. Something that would immediately seize its attention.

“Well, you may not realize it, but I always intended to accept you as part of my kingdom. That way, not only can you and I become friends, but you can also befriend my angel.”

My lips curled upward when Pintre’s long deerlike ears perked up and its eyes began to sparkle.

I glanced at Aurelion, my smile deepening when my angel visibly sulked but did not dare complain. He started toward my dominion with deliberately slow steps, as though hoping that if he dragged it out long enough, Pintre might disappear on its own.

“More… more friends,” Pintre muttered when it noticed my angel.

It was a strangely majestic sight.

The enormous half-halvyn deity stood before an angel no less imposing than itself. Both looked surreal, like creatures that should have existed only in myth, and yet they were undeniably alive, complete with their own quirks, desires, and personalities.

The moment I realized that, I shivered.

Just imagining the two of them bickering inside my realm made me wonder whether accepting Pintre would even be worth it. Aurelion was completely obedient, so having him around was not particularly troublesome, even if his new habit of training in my realm had already begun ruining my peace and quiet.

But Pintre alongside him?

I could not even imagine what kind of chaos that would create.

I’ll deal with that problem when it comes. No reason to stress about it now.

While I was lost in thought, Aurelion and Pintre had begun getting to know each other. Or rather, Pintre was trying to get to know my angel. Aurelion was dodging the half-halvyn’s questions as if his life depended on it.

“New friend plays with Pintre?”

“I have a duty to protect my Liege. I cannot spare time for such matters.”

“Liege? What is Liege?”

“…”

“Pintre is half-halvyn. Is new friend half-bird?” Pintre asked, staring at the crimson wings folded behind Aurelion’s back.

“No. I am my Lord’s angel and His creation!” Aurelion barked, raising his head proudly.

“Pintre is Mother’s creation.”

“…”

Watching such a profound conversation unfold was surprisingly entertaining. But brightening Pintre’s mood was not the only reason I had introduced Aurelion to it.

“Pintre,” I said in the gentlest voice I could manage. I made my manifestation lean forward, tilting its head slightly to the side. “I have an idea. Why don’t you introduce your new friend to Au… to my angel?”

I almost let Aurelion’s name slip.

To be honest, I still was not entirely certain whether revealing his true name was dangerous. But considering how much value the names of deities held, including Pintre’s, I saw no reason to risk it.

“Pintre cannot, not now,” the half-halvyn complained. “Pintre asks new friend. If she says yes, Pintre will introduce new friend to Velmoryn God and half-bird.”

Aurelion glared at being called half-bird but chose not to respond. I, on the other hand, was becoming increasingly suspicious.

“Alright. I will not force you,” I said. “But at least tell me this. When did you meet your new friend? Was it a few days ago… I mean, a few moons ago?”

“Yes. Velmoryn God also knows Pintre’s new friend?” Pintre chirped.

“I might,” I answered absentmindedly.

It was a very suspicious coincidence.

Only a few days had passed since I defeated the God of Beggars.

Could Pintre’s new friend somehow be connected to him?

Perhaps his inquisitor? An apostle? A priestess?

No.

I would not have missed the presence of an ordinary mortal, even if she somehow managed to avoid the velmoryn lookouts. Whoever this woman was, she had to be unusual. Perhaps she could even wield divine power.

That would explain both how she had managed to see Pintre and how she knew a method for allowing the half-halvyn to accept my mark.

But why?

What would she gain from it?

“Why is half-bird’s skin dark? His wings are red like sun. Why is his skin dark?” Pintre continued pestering Aurelion, already comfortable enough now to start poking at his armor.

Aurelion firmly removed the half-halvyn’s finger, though he was careful not to hurt it.

“This is not my skin. This is armor. I wear it so I can better protect my Lord.”

“Wooow,” Pintre exclaimed, its eyes widening.

It tried to grin, or perhaps smirk, but apparently had no idea how to move only one side of its mouth.

“Pintre’s new friend also loves dark.”

Loves dark…

An image surfaced in my mind immediately. Noah’s apostles. Women dressed in long black robes, their eyes stitched shut with crimson threads.

If that’s true, if Pintre was really approached by one of Noah’s apostles… I need to confirm it.

“Pintre,” I said. “I might know your new friend. Does she have red stitches… I mean red lines over her eyes? Does she wear a long black dress with red snakes painted on it?”

“Pintre cannot say!” Pintre replied solemnly. “Pintre’s new friend says Pintre must not tell anyone about her eyes!”

Fuck. That bastard really sent his apostle to trick Pintre.

No matter how I looked at it, the Night God should not have known about Pintre’s connection to me. But facts did not care about what he should or should not know. He was clearly using Pintre for something. Most likely, he wanted information about me. Perhaps even confirmation that I was truly in slumber.

Oh, Noah… you are so unlucky that I might be the most paranoid god in this world.

“Pintre, did you reveal your true name to your new friend?” I asked, already beginning to think about how I could turn this situation to my advantage.

“No. Mother said Pintre must not tell Pintre’s true name to anyone but new god,” Pintre said.

“Good. You should never tell it to anyone else.”

I nodded to myself, satisfied.

“As I said, I know your new friend. And as it happens, I have a surprise for her. One that will make her very happy.”

I leaned a little closer.

“Do you want to make her happy, Pintre?”
