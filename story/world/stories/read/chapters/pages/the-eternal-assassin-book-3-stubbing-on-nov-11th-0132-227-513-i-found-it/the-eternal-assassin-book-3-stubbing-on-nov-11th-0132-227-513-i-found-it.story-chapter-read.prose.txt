Thankfully, they ended up finding Rulfar without too much issue. Even with all the speed he’d had when she let go, Samantha had thrown him in a straight line, and there had been a mountain relatively close by to stop him in his tracks. It seemed the potion’s effect of slowing down his fall had kicked in rather fast as well once he got closer to the mountain, bringing him to a gentle stop rather than smearing him across the ground.

“Maybe warn me before you throw me half a mile at the incoming swarm of enemies next time,” Rulfar said, frowning at them as they touched down in front of him. “We never practiced that one in training!”

“Well now we can!” Asher grinned, laughing at the bloodhound’s annoyed look. Despite being an exceptional fighter, Rulfar was about the polar opposite of Moxy. Whereas Moxy was all about thinking on the fly and winning via whatever means necessary in a battle, Rulfar was much more rigid. He preferred sticking with tried and true methods, trusting in his extensive training to see him to victory.

Which made throwing him face-first at a bunch of monsters up in the sky a hell of a lot more entertaining, in Asher’s opinion.

“I’m sorry, Rulfar, I’ll give you more heads up next time I need to do something like that,” Samantha promised, looking around at the mountain they found themselves on. “This looks a lot different from Dormaul. There aren’t any trees or vegetation anywhere!”

“This is the Malani Mountain Range,” Rulfar explained, having apparently already gotten over being thrown as a living projectile. “We’re still only on the very edge, but these mountains stretch on for hundreds of miles. They form a rather effective buffer for the eastern border of the Noala Kingdom against the Eight Crests. Harpies are only some of the many threats one would encounter while trying to cross. Frigid temperatures as you climb higher up, far deadlier monsters as you head deeper in… It’s not surprising it’s regarded as a no-man’s land.”

“Let me guess. The lich is hiding out somewhere in there?” Asher asked.

“According to my skill, that’s exactly where it is hiding,” Rulfar confirmed, breathing deeply once more. “It makes sense. Even if it didn’t bother hiding itself with magic, only the bravest of explorers would even stumble upon it within the mountain range. Bravest and unluckiest explorers, I suppose.”

“You said the mountain range stretched on for hundreds of miles,” Samantha pointed out. “How close are we at this point?”

“We are close. Very close,” Rulfar nodded, gripping his battleaxe more tightly. “As I said, I cannot pinpoint the lich’s location precisely, but we are only around fifty or so miles away.”

“Fifty miles… I can make that in a single trip,” Samantha said, suddenly looking incredibly nervous. “This is actually happening, huh? I mean, I know we’ve been training for this, and I want the lich to pay more than anything… But to finally have it right in our sights…”

“I know,” Asher said, taking her hand and giving it a quick squeeze. “We’ll get that bag of bones if it’s the last thing we do.”

“The good news is that with how barren the mountain range is, the realm of ages is quite inactive out here,” Rulfar said, seeming to stare at nothing. Blinking, Asher quickly realized what he meant. After flying around for so long, where the realm of ages was practically nonexistent, he’d almost forgotten he’d used Permanence on their Rings of Lesser Realm Sight. Now, when he focused on the skill, he saw what Rulfar was talking about. The usual, shifting green pockets of time were far thinner and more spread out this far from civilization. It seemed the lich had inadvertently given them the perfect battle ground to face off against the greater demons.

“Well, that’s a nice surprise,” Asher said, smiling as he stopped focusing on the skill. “We might not even need plan P after all.”

“Which would be good, as we haven’t actually tested it yet,” Samantha pointed out. "You're still working on the new effect with Wilna, right? The craston in Whikoga's library?"

"Yep," Asher said, careful not to give away anything on his face as he nodded. "I want to be one-hundred-percent sure it functions how I think it does before I rely on it."

Asher hadn't exactly had a ton of free time since getting back from his quick jaunt into the wild realm, but that hadn't stopped him from experimenting with Permanence's newest tier-2 effect. Bringing permanence to the world around him in the wild realm was quite obvious, as it stopped everything from chaotically shifting around. Bringing permanence to the material realm was proving to be a bit trickier. He thought he could do something similar, albeit a lot more limited in scope. Though beyond any sort of combat application, the moment he'd had a second to himself, he'd immediately Recalled over to Whikoga and asked Wilna to help him with an experiment.

He wanted to see if he could keep a piece of fruit from aging even while he was far away from it, and the craston's Identify skill was powerful enough that she could see the age of anything she looked at.

“I still don’t understand why the two of you continue trying to give all our plans and maneuvers single-letter names,” Rulfar frowned. “It makes them far harder to remember than using a descriptive term.”

“I mean, you can call it plan Permanence if you want, but I’m sticking with plan P,” Asher shrugged. “Anyway, shall we carry on? No sense wasting time now that we’re this close!”

Getting nods from his two companions, Asher and Rulfar waited a minute as Samantha quickly flew around and collected the floating orbs of shards from the harpy corpses. While they might still be rich compared to most people in this world, purchasing all those artifacts had drastically depleted Asher’s fortune, and they would need quite a number of shards soon enough. A million shards per skill to evolve them to the third tier was just around the corner, and Asher wanted to be well prepared when it was finally time.

Shards collected, Rulfar popped one last potion of Feather as they took off yet again. Samantha had to flap rather hard in order to clear the first mountain range, and Asher marveled at the sheer height they reached as they flew past the summit.

“I see what Rulfar meant about it getting cold,” Samantha said. Only then did Asher realize he could feel her shivering against his chest. His own Complete Resilience was strong enough now that he hadn’t even noticed the drop in temperature.

He’d come a long way since nearly freezing to death battling against the awakened panther made of ice.

“If you dip into each valley as we fly, you’ll get a chance to warm up!” Rulfar called up, not seeming fazed by the frigid winds in the slightest. Asher didn’t know if that was a side benefit of his Bear element or simply because he’d grown up in a far colder country, but either way, Rulfar looked to be in his element.

Taking his advice, Samantha did just that, diving whenever they crossed over a mountain to take refuge from the frigid winds, before using Rapid Flight to quickly shoot up high when it was time to climb again. As they flew, Asher took in the scenery below them.

The Malani Mountain Range was a rather barren and empty region of this world. The mountains themselves were largely snow and rock, but the valleys at least had a bit of life to them. Rough foliage did its best to survive in the harsher conditions, and he spotted a handful of various lizards and goats running around here and there. He knew goats back on Earth would eat just about anything, but even he wasn’t quite prepared to see one of the goats dart forward and snatch up a lizard the size of a small dog in its mouth, tearing it to shreds as it feasted on its meal.

Note to self, assume everything in these mountains wants to eat us. Even the ‘normal’ wildlife.

They ended up being attacked by three more harpy swarms as they flew, but they were far better prepared this time around. Rather than hurl Rulfar like a living cannonball, Asher leaned down and yanked him into his rift whenever the monsters approached, freeing up Samantha to utilize her agility and wings to their full effect.

Asher managed to help out here and there with his daggers, but it seemed with each swarm of harpies that attacked, Samantha grew more and more confident in how to use her wings as the weapons they were. By the time the third swarm hit them, Asher was pretty certain he’d only taken out four or five harpies to Samantha’s thirty. The ability to use her wings both as shields against the monsters’ raking talons and as blades to slice straight through them made her a terror in the skies. He decided to focus instead on using Distortion to snatch up the shards that dropped from the dead monsters before they plummeted, ensuring they wouldn’t have to drop to the ground to go after them.

“Have I ever told you that the Wing element was one of the best investments I’ve ever made?” Asher teased her as monster blood slipped off her emerald wings and the last of the harpies fell from the sky. The hunter blushed at the compliment, before laughing.

“No, but you could certainly say so more often! Honestly, it’s been helping me get over my hang up about us being rich.”

“Rich for now,” he corrected, chuckling at her confused look over her shoulder. “Don’t forget, a million shards per skill for the third tier. That’s thirty-six million shards between the two of us. We’re not going to be rich for some time!”

“At least the greater demon corpses will give us a nice head start to building up that fortune,” she said, holding out her hands and motioning for him to pull Rulfar out of his rift once more.

They’d gotten lucky that sitting in his Personal Rift evidently didn’t count as touching the ground for whatever magic decided when the potion of Feather wore off, as Rulfar popped into existence once more, barely falling a few feet before Samantha grabbed his raised arms. Asher wished they would have figured that out before burning through a few more potions of Feather, but he supposed it was better late than never.

“Let’s hope that was the last of the harpies,” Rulfar said, closing his eyes and breathing in through his nose. “We’re close. Four degrees to your left.”

Following the bloodhound's instructions, Samantha began flying once more, going slightly slower now that Rulfar was correcting her angle more and more frequently. After another twenty minutes of careful flying, he said the words they’d been waiting since yesterday to hear.

“This is it.”

Samantha stopped flying forward, flapping her wings in place as they hovered there together. Rulfar had stopped them directly in front of one of the mountains, at the very edge of a snow-filled valley. Asher looked around for anything even closely resembling a tower or structure of any kind, only to come up emptyhanded. That wasn’t too surprising, however, as it could very well be camouflaged.

“This is as far as my nose takes us,” Rulfar said, looking around even as he spoke. “It is hard to explain, but the lich’s scent basically diffuses at this point, covering half the valley and the face of this mountain. I can’t pinpoint a precise location.”

“So it’s hidden,” Asher grinned, pointing for Samantha to take them down by the mountain. “Nothing Secret Seeker can’t handle!”

Once they landed, Asher pulled Moxy out of his rift. Rulfar had already filled her in on where they were and what had been happening when they stored him to handle the harpies, so the warrior was up to speed and ready to go. Giving the valley a quick scan, she laughed as she punched her fists together.

“Alright, I see plenty of snow, but no walking corpses. We sure we’re in the right place?”

“Probably,” Asher said, already beginning to wander. “Give me a few minutes to look around. If there are any secret entrances, Secret Seeker will point them out.”

“Don’t forget, we’re trying to retain the element of surprise for as long as possible,” Rulfar reminded everyone. “Most likely the lich has some sort of detection skill active and knows we’re already here, but no sense in not trying.”

“I’ll refrain from yodeling at the top of my lungs for as long as I can,” Moxy drawled.

Asher briefly thought it was odd that this world also had yodeling, before clearing his head and focusing on the task at hand. Seeing as mountains were naturally good at echoing, he supposed it wasn’t surprising people on both worlds realized how fun it was to shout at them.

The next hour was spent in shaky anticipation as Asher wandered all over the side of the mountain. Secret Seeker didn’t have the largest range, so he wanted to be certain he didn’t accidentally miss a spot as he doubled back again and again. It wasn’t until he was a quarter of the way up the mountain that he paused, his skill finally picking something up.

Secret Seeker – Lvl 28

“I got a hit!” he called back to his team, causing them to jump up from where they’d been sitting and rush over. Pointing at a seemingly normal outcropping of rock, he smiled. “There’s something up with those rocks. I’m thinking it might be a hidden tunnel to wherever the lich is hiding.”

“So, no tower this time?” Samantha asked, testing her bowstring and shifting her arrows around somewhat.

“Maybe not,” he shrugged. “If the mountain already existed, I don’t see any reason why someone wouldn’t just mine out a portion of it if they needed a secret base.”

“You all know the plan,” Rulfar said, motioning for Asher to take the lead as he moved to the back. “Imps or lesser demons, everyone goes wild. Greater demons, make sure you're in an area with low realm of ages activity. The lich…”

“Detain and make sure Asher gets the kill with Sever the Thread,” Moxy said, sounding a bit upset about that even as she nodded. “We know, we’ve gone over this a hundred times.”

“Just don’t forget, taking down the lich isn’t our only task,” Asher reminded them. “We also need to find a lead to the other members of The Council of Death. If we kill this lich and it ends up destroying its whole base or something, we might lose whatever leads it has, and then we have no way of finding the other thirteen until they attack first.”

“It rigged a part of its old tower to explode, so it’s very possible this base has similar traps,” Samantha chimed in. “Just be careful.”

With the reminders out of the way, Asher moved forward, stopping before the seemingly ordinary rock wall that Secret Seeker was directing him to. Taking a deep breath, he took one more step.

Walking straight through the illusionary rocks and into the entrance to the lich’s lair.
