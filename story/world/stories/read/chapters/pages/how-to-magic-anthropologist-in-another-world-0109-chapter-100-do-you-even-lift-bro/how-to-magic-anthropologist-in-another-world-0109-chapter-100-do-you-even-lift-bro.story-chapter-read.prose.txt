“...and she said, I kid you not: ‘Ever since Nada took over the Freelancer Guild reception, I am no longer the only competent bureaucrat at the helm.’”

A squeal of excitement came from the half-Kindred, half-Bloodline Ink Mage receptionist.

I was gossiping about Marina with her biggest fan, Nada.

After finishing my Soul Shaper training, I had spent the night with Nina, where we had spent most of our time in the Moon Room catching lectures on electrical engineering, as well as sound and wave theory.

After that, I headed to the head receptionist's office to catch up with Nada. She didn't seem to be aware that her idol, Marina, was actually three people rather than one, so I conveniently omitted that part from my retelling. It was not my secret to share.

“I can’t believe you get to learn Soul Shaping under Marina,” Nada sighed, giving me an envious look.

“What can I say? I got lucky.”

For the next few minutes, we continued talking about what it was like to work so closely with Marina. Nada was clearly enjoying every bit of gossip I could provide.

Eventually, though, Nada shifted the conversation.

“So, what are you doing today?”

Just in case, I reached into my bag of holding and checked the communication slate to see if she had sent me a message.

Nope.

I put it back in my bag of holding and extracted the other slate, the one linked to Nina’s. To my surprise, something was written there.

“Oh, I guess I figured out what I am going to do.”

Nada looked at the slate curiously.

“Do you rent private rooms here in the Freelancer Guild?” I asked, “And is Yon around?”

Yon and I arrived at Nina’s workshop and found a single barbell placed on the ground.

It was an unusual piece of equipment. The bar and the weights were not separable, but instead appeared to be made from the same continuous piece of metal, as if the entire thing had been forged as one solid object.

Nina approached us carrying a small notebook and a leather pouch.

“This came with it.”

I opened the pouch. Inside were three high-quality, fist-sized cores and around twenty smaller, marble-sized ones. The cores were all a brownish-beige color, and each had some sort of etched-metal ring attached to it, making it look slightly like a Christmas tree ornament.

The cores alone would probably have cost half of what I had paid for the entire thing.

As I opened the notebook, I watched Yon carry the barbell.

“This must weigh only around 100 kilograms,” he said.

I read through the instructions, and yes, it stated that the base weight was 100 kilograms (220 lb). Each small core would double the weight, while the large cores would increase it tenfold.

There was also a note explaining that the mana cores only affected the base weight. In other words, two small cores wouldn't double the base weight twice, turning it into 400 kilograms. Instead, each core would add another 100 kilograms, making it 300 kilograms in total.

I examined the barbell more closely, checking the seams where the cores could be inserted, while doing some mental math.

There were nine seams in each disk, one at the center and eight near the edges, plus five more in the bar itself.

Since the large cores were technically equivalent to nine small cores, and I only had three of those, that meant the maximum weight should be:

100 + 100 × (2 × 8 + 4 + 9 × 3) = 4,800 kilograms. That's 10,582 pounds.

If the enchanter was correct in his estimation, the maximum weight this barbell could reach was 4.8 tonnes, or roughly 5.2 US tons.

Which, let's face it, was a ridiculous number. That was the weight of an adult African elephant, or perhaps a delivery truck.

Back on Earth, I went to the gym semi-regularly, but even at the height of my Chunibyo-fueled training obsession, my maximum benching strength had never reached the base weight of this thing. It barely went past half of it.

Yon frowned.

“I really hope this does get much heavier than this, because 100 kilograms would do nothing for me.”

“We will see,” I said, placing the notebook and cores inside my bag of holding. “Let me try lifting it for a second.”

He placed the barbell down, and I crouched, grabbing the bar, then lifted it like a standard deadlift exercise. In its base configuration, it was surprisingly easy. I stared at the barbell.

Had I really gotten that much stronger after my training with Edmand, and then Katar, Kan, and Raik?

Or was the lower gravity messing with my calculations?

No. The gravity here was only slightly lower. Otherwise, I would have noticed much more obvious differences in fluid dynamics and similar things.

Then what was it? Is this the effect of my Kindred side?

The Seven Realms were supposedly closer to the various Axes of Creation, and the Kindred Realm was governed by the Axis of Alteration. If the theory was correct, perhaps my Kindred side was more active here than it had been on Earth. Maybe that was affecting my physical strength.

I shook my head. More research was needed before I could arrive at a conclusion. I put the barbell into my bag of holding before turning back to Yon.

“Come on. I rented us a training room in the Freelancer Guild. We should try it there.” I glanced around. “This wooden floor wouldn't be able to withstand the kind of weight you might eventually be able to carry.”

As we left Nina’s shop, I made a stop at the orphanage.

I paid four of the oldest kids to go and find Katar, Kan, Shingo, and Raik. If they also happened to see either Calr, Ja’a, or Vena, I asked them to inform them where we would be and what we were doing.

I handed each of them a bronze coin. The kids immediately started running.

“You know there’s a guild for that,” said Yon.

“Meh. Those kids could use the coins more.”

The room I rented was essentially an enchanted metal box. It had been designed for elite Freelancer sparring, with reinforced walls and floors meant to survive people throwing magic and each other around without having to worry about digging holes in the ground or breaking the cobbled road outside. Mana lights illuminated the room from above.

It was exactly what we needed, as five tonnes concentrated on a narrow area could do a lot of damage.

With that problem out of the way came the next challenge. If the floor was at risk, what kind of furniture could withstand the same weight?

A custom enchanted bench was in order, but for the meantime, I needed something that could work. So I summoned my spear and made a wish.

“Take me somewhere I can buy something capable of supporting this kind of weight.”

The familiar sensation of teleportation seized me, and I blinked. The next moment, I was somewhere completely different.

It took me a while to recognize where this was as I looked around. The warehouse was filled with crafting tools everywhere: hammers, tongs, chisels, metalworking equipment, and other things I couldn't immediately identify. I thought it was a forge, but now half of those tools were never used, while others looked like good-quality secondhand tools. This was the place where you got new forges furnished; I was probably somewhere within the Smithing Guild.

Now, why would my spell bring me here?

I looked around and found the obvious contender for a bench that could handle five tonnes.

The enormous anvil was the perfect width and height for what I had in mind, and was tall enough to boot. I didn’t really know why an anvil needed to be this big, but I didn’t care much about the answer either. I just bought it.

Having four apprentices carry it high enough for me to slip it into my bag of holding, I secured it there and made another wish. This time, I wished to go to the Sunless Reach to recharge before returning fifteen minutes later.

The shirtless Kindred sergeant spent the time I was away doing a warm-up kata, readying his back and shoulders for the heavy weights.

I immediately opened my bag of holding and had him grab the anvil out. The anvil itself was 700 kilograms, but he was able to place it on the ground without a problem, as the metal floor gave a deep clang.

“Now for the actual test.”

I took one of the smaller mana cores and inserted it into the central slot in the bar.

According to the instructions, the base weight should increase from 100 kilograms to 200.

I grabbed the bar, and it was immediately obvious that the enchantment had worked. An extra hundred kilograms made a significant difference to the weight, especially when taking into consideration my level of strength. I was able to lift it, but it was heavy. Not impossibly heavy, mind you, but heavy enough that I actually had to work for it.

I stared at the barbell with a dissatisfied look. Apparently, one core was all I needed for my current training limit.

That was rather inconvenient. It meant that after a while, when I needed to increase the weight, I would have to make another 100-kilogram jump...

I would have preferred something that could be adjusted in smaller increments. Five kilograms, perhaps, or at least ten. It would have been much more useful than jumping from 200 to 300.

Still, this was only a proof of concept. The important part was that the enchantment worked. The next version would be better.

I was already thinking about it. We could make a series of adjustable barbells with different maximum weights: one could cap at 200 kilograms for non-Kindred beginners. Another could cap at 500 kilograms for Kindred beginners and advanced non-Kindred users. Then one could cover 500 kilograms to two tonnes for intermediate Kindred. And finally, one could go from two tonnes all the way to ten tonnes for elite Kindred.

Or something along those lines.

The important thing was having smaller increments between each level rather than being forced to make hundred-kilogram jumps.

I slowly lowered the bar, and Yon picked it up and lifted it.

Then he put it back down, looking distinctly unimpressed.

“Did it double?” he asked.

“Yes.”

“Then maybe we should have started with a higher base weight. Only a 100-kilogram jump isn’t enough.”

I stared at him.

He stared back.

“Fine.” I decided to be petty.

I removed the single core. Then I started inserting the rest. The instructions said to space out the larger cores, so I made sure the magical attachments were symmetrical. Two at the center of each disk and one at the exact center of the bar, while the twenty smaller cores went into the rest of the seams.

I could tell the weight had increased as I stopped being able to even roll the bar on the ground.

Still, I kept going until the barbell had reached its maximum weight: 4.8 tonnes.

“Try that.” I stepped back.

“How much does it weigh?” Yon gave me a suspicious look.

“Try it first,” I grinned.

He crouched down and grabbed the bar.

He pulled.

The bar shook for a bit but didn’t lift. He adjusted his grip and tried again.

This time, the bar lifted. It was only a few centimeters off the ground. His arms and back tensed, and the chiseled muscles of his back strained to their maximum.

Then his hands suddenly slipped, and the barbell slammed back onto the floor with a deafening clang.

We both stared at it.

Yon looked down at his hands.

“My grip strength failed me,” he said, dumbfounded. “I did not think I had a problem with my grip strength.”

“My dude,” I said, “you just lifted five tonnes.”

I pointed at the barbell.

“The fuck are you complaining about?”

It was at that moment that Raik and Kan showed up.

They stopped just inside the training room, both staring at the barbell sitting in the middle of the floor.

“What is that?” Kan asked.

“A barbell,” I stated. “A training tool from Earth. Basically, you lift increasingly heavy weights to make yourself stronger.”

Kan walked closer and inspected it with obvious curiosity.

“And you had it made?”

“I explained the concept to Nina, and she had it made for me.”

“How much does it weigh?”

“Currently, almost five tonnes.”

Kan’s eyebrows rose as she looked at Yon.

“You can lift five tonnes?”

“Only a few centimeters.”

“It’s probably better if we lower the weight you need to work with, then raise it gradually; otherwise, you might hurt yourself,” I said.

“So it’s adjustable?” asked Raik.

“Yes,” I explained the basic enchantment to them, including how the mana cores increased the weight.

Halfway through my explanation, the door opened again, and Calr, Shingo, and Katar walked into the room, so I had to repeat myself for a bit.

“So this is basically a piece of equipment that lets you train against a controlled amount of resistance?” Calr asked.

“Exactly.”

“You most of all,” I said, looking at his scrawny frame. “All you’ve got is sprint speed. You need to put some muscle on those bones.”

“She is right,” said Katar. “We all could use something like this.”

“The problem is that this particular version was designed more as a proof of concept, and five tonnes is something only Yon could realistically reach.”

“Kan could probably outmatch me in time,” said Yon. “Her limit cap is probably higher than mine, considering who her father was.”

“Maybe,” she said. “But that’s years into the future, and even then it’s not guaranteed that I would be physically stronger than you. My talent might not lie in direct strength. I seem to have some affinity with metal that I need to explore.”

Yon nodded. He knew her father’s history better than I did, and he didn’t seem surprised by the revelation about her affinity with metal.

Raik looked at the bar again.

“So, are you making it smaller?”

I nodded.

“In time. For now, let me lower the weight to something more manageable.”

“No. I want to try to see if I can move it as it is,” said Kan.

She crouched down, grabbed the bar, and pulled. The barbell shook for a bit, not much, but enough for it to roll slightly across the floor before Kan let go.

I stared at her.

“I moved it.” She was grinning from ear to ear.

“You did!”

“Barely,” huffed Katar. “Let me try.”

“This isn't a competition,” I said.

“It is now,” laughed Yon.

Calr looked at the massive bar, then immediately moved toward the side of the room.

“I will arbitrate.”

“Is that your excuse to avoid trying?” laughed Kan.

“It’s called knowing your limit, unlike some,” he said as Katar struggled with the bar, unable to even shake it.

“This is impossible.”

Raik stepped next and, after a bit of struggle, he also failed to move it.

Finally, Shingo stepped forward, walking over to the bar. He looked different from the first time I had seen him. He was still enormous, standing around two and a half meters tall, but he had slimmed down considerably. The excess weight he had carried was halfway gone, leaving behind a much more defined frame.

The bar itself looked slightly narrow for him. Not to the point of being unusable, but definitely not designed with someone of his proportions in mind.

“You want to try?” I asked.

Shingo nodded.

He wrapped his hands around the bar, adjusted his stance, and pulled. The bar lifted, not by much, but almost as well as Yon had managed earlier. He held it there for a moment before lowering it carefully.

This led Yon to step up again and reconfirm that he was still the strongest. He was better prepared this time and made sure not to drop it.

I looked between the two of them.

“All right. That was something useful to know.”

“It helps me know roughly where everyone's strength is.”

I removed the remaining cores and started adjusting the bar. There was no point making everyone struggle against a weight they couldn't train with properly. The whole point of the exercise was to provide resistance that was difficult but manageable, so I started lowering the weight until everyone had something appropriate to work with.

After a few tests, we eventually settled on acceptable weights for everyone. Yon settled at two tonnes, while Shingo ended up with one and a half tonnes. Kan was next in relative strength with half a tonne, while Raik and Katar worked with 300 kilograms. I set my own weight to 200 kilograms, and Calr, after considerable complaining and even more reluctance, finally agreed to try the base weight of 100 kilograms.

He managed it... barely.

“I would have given you 70 kilograms if I could,” I said, frowning.

Once everyone had their weights set, we began doing sets of bench presses. The anvil served as our improvised bench, which was about as comfortable as it sounded, but at least it was sturdy enough that none of us had to worry about it collapsing under the weight.

I relegated the role of spotting to Yon, while he quickly took up his old role as instructor, freely giving encouragement and correcting our form. This might have been his first day using this particular tool, but his understanding of muscular training eclipsed mine by leaps and bounds. Shingo was the only one who could relatively safely spot Yon, so that task fell to him.

Calr, who was struggling with the base weight, had to be stopped by Yon after the third set.

It was fascinating, seeing the difference between Kindred and non-Kindred at play. Shingo looked like he could carry one tonne, and he did. Yon looked very strong, at an Olympian level of physical strength, but he did not look like he could carry two tonnes. It would have been simply impossible back on Earth.

Kan, on the other hand, looked like the average health-conscious girl. She was slightly fitter than me, but nowhere near as fit as Nakera. Yet somehow, she could bench press more than the Earth record holder.

Both Raik and Katar were super fit, almost as fit as Yon, yet they were playing in a league lower than Kan. Calr was just a regular dude trying weights for the first time and was acting like it.

My theory was that for Kindred people, the body form and muscle density started shifting more dramatically the closer you were to your talent cap. Since their adaptation only happened when pushing against a limit

I asked Yon if my assessment was correct, and he nodded.

“Once your soul can no longer carry the burden of making you stronger, your body starts carrying the slack.”

“This is why Shingo has been slimming down drastically in the past days,” added Kan. “He is reaching the limit of his soul strength.”

“He was at 9 SB the last time Ja’a checked,” added Calr. “And she suspects he is capped at 12.”

“So Kan doesn’t look like a mountain of muscle because she is nowhere near her limit yet?” I asked.

Everyone nodded.

“Take Sergeant Lanka, for example,” said Yon. “She does look like a mountain because she has been stuck at her limit for three years, waiting for an evolution.”

I remembered the sergeant’s body from the first time I entered Hano’s bath. Her body was at the peak of what was anatomically possible.

“When she goes through an evolution, would she keep her strength but lose her muscle mass?” I asked.

“It depends,” said Yon. “She could thin out quite a bit as her muscles densify, or she could evolve in a way that allows her to bulk up even more. She could become larger than Shingo, creating more space to gather more muscles.”

I sat down and pulled out my notebook, starting to write down everything I had learned from today's testing. I also wrote my feedback for the enchanter who had made it. I tried to be as matter-of-fact as possible in my report. I didn’t personally know the man, so I didn’t want to offend him in some way or another.

The first requirement was obvious: smaller weight increments. Five kilograms would probably be ideal, while ten kilograms would still be acceptable.

That meant different tools for different people, making the second requirement the need for multiple versions. There was no reason for a beginner's bar to have a maximum weight of several tonnes, just as there was little point giving an elite Kindred like Yon a bar that started at 200 kilograms.

The third requirement was a proper bench, something comfortable yet able to carry the added weight of the tools.

Finally, with this proof of concept ready, I started adding instructions on how to make dumbbells.

Then I looked at everyone continuing their sets.

I was waiting for my turn to do my sets when Ja’a finally showed up. She looked better than when I had last seen her, after she tried to look at a goddess’s soul. She scanned the room, then narrowed her eyes at me.

“Your soul has grown again,” she said.

“Ah, that’s probably because I tried Soul Shaping with Marina yesterday.”

“Since when did you have a connection with Marina?” asked Raik, narrowing his eyes at me.

“I may or may not have teleported randomly, only to end up at her house.”

“One day you are going to get yourself killed with that,” he sighed.

Ja’a flew swiftly before sitting next to me.

“So? You have seen the Margin Worlds. Tell me everything.”
