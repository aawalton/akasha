With every bounce, I could feel my exposed skin being shredded by the sand, but there was nothing I could do to stop myself as I hurled down the slope. I squeezed my eyes and mouth shut as tightly as I could, fighting to keep the grit out, but it still found a way in. Pain burned through my body, and every time a new wound was opened, the sand immediately embedded itself in the cut, feeling like a thousand tiny needles were stabbing into me and burrowing deeper.

Eventually, my body came to a stop. Agony pulsed through me as I lay there on my back beneath the setting sun, unwilling and unable to move. My head spun, and my heart pounded in my ears as fire gripped my chest. My lungs were crying out, begging for oxygen, but I refused to give in.

I couldn’t. Not yet.

There was too much sand still clinging to my face. The gentle breeze caused them to dance gently, the sensation torturous on my ripped and raw skin, but I knew I needed to do something about it. If I were to open my mouth or even risk inhaling through my nose, I knew the grains would trickle into my body and choke me. I needed to sit up before I could.

But part of me didn’t want to. Temptation pulled at me, trying to convince me to remain where I was. I could just lie there and let the sand be blown over me. It wouldn’t take long before that happened.

The sun was setting, and storms often tore through the desert at night. Patrol often had to go out to repair the holes that had appeared in the sturdy vinyl or clear the sand from the bubbles, lest they be reclaimed by the cruel and unforgiving desert.

It would eventually take us all, so why resist, a voice in the back of my head whispered, but I pushed the thought aside. That was why I had to fight. To stop the desert from taking even more people I loved from me. Family members and friends had been killed by the merciless earth, but Salvation would stop that. If I got there, they had a chance at survival.

My muscles screamed at me as I sat up. It felt as though burning metal was being pressed against my skin, searing the tissue below, and I almost gasped, but I managed to catch myself just in time.

Wetness tickled my arms as I slowly lifted my shirt to my face, trying to wipe some of the sand from my lips and then my eyes, but an endless amount seemed to have embedded itself in the lines of my face. I could feel it clawing at my skin, drawing more blood as my hearing started to fade, and the lack of oxygen was making me light-headed and weak. I knew I would not be able to last much longer without breathing, but there was still sand on my face.

Still, I forced out a sharp huff, hoping to expel any lingering grains. It felt feeble on my parched lips, but it was the best I could do, and finally, I let myself inhale. The sun-baked air rushed into my lungs so quickly that it made my head spin, and it was so dry that it threatened to choke me, but I couldn’t stop myself from gulping down another breath.

For a moment, I allowed myself to sit there with my eyes shut, breathing in the earth scent. It filled my nose, engulfing me in memories, and I was whisked back to the bubble and tunnels below that I’d lived in for my whole life.

Whenever the guards returned from a patrol, the smell clung to them. No matter how hard they tried to wash the sand from their uniforms, the scent remained, but that wasn’t the only time I’d smelt it. Every time someone from our bubble was sent out for their attempt, the dry, desert air would rush in, but I could smell something else too.

There was another aroma that hung in the air. It was subtle. Faint. At first, I wasn’t sure whether I was imagining it, but I inhaled again, becoming more and more certain that the gentle undercurrent of sweetness was real. It was a strange smell, an unusual one, but somewhere in the back of my mind, I recognised it.

Fresh images forced themselves into my mind, and I was transported back to the hydrofarm that I’d spent some time working in last year. I could see the rows and rows of green plants growing in bizarre plastic cylinders around me, which would feed us for a few months, and feel the moisture that hung in the air.

It must be coming from Salvation, I realised as hope blossomed in my heart. If I could smell it, that meant I had to be close to it, and it must still be standing. The others could not have infiltrated it yet. The city must still be safe, and that meant I had a chance. My bubble had a chance. I just needed to reach it before they caught up with me.

Anxiety crept over me, filling my body with fear. I had been so distracted by the pain that I’d forgotten about them. I could still feel the hum of the machinery approaching, but I forgot that they were hunting me down, and I was so exposed. I was just sitting on the sand, near the bottom of the dune, and I had yet to even open my eyes. They could have surrounded me without me even realising.

I opened my eyes, needing to check whether I was about to be ambushed, but I saw nothing before pain seared through my brain. The combination of the still-bright sun and sand that had found its way in through my eyelids blinded me. I blinked quickly, balling my hands into fists to help resist the urge to rub my eyes. That was a horrible idea, one I’d been warned against. It would only risk scratching my cornea.

Luckily, tears came quickly, washing the grit away, and I looked around again. My gaze scanned the steep slope behind me before travelling over the expanse surrounding me, and my heart leapt at the sight before me, but I forced myself to peer up at the skies. Still, I saw nothing. The source of the buzzing sound, that made my bones feel like they were shaking, was not as close as it seemed.

Satisfied that I was not about to be murdered, I looked away from the darkening sky and glanced down at my body. Nausea churned in my stomach as I took in my raw arms. The skin had been torn away, and blood was running freely down my arms. My shirt was covered in drops that had fallen from my chin, but I could also feel the fabric clinging to the cuts on my hips and back, where it must have ridden up as I fell down the dune.

It was already starting to dry, I realised. Although more blood was flowing from my body, the sun was quickly baking it, and I knew scabs would already be starting to form. Ripping my clothes away from them would be agony, potentially more painful than the cuts had been in the first place, but there was nothing I could do about that. Plus, it would be fine. The doctors in Salvation would be able to heal me.

My gaze slowly rose, returning to the spot I’d glanced at just briefly moments ago, and I felt the breath catch in my throat. A doorway stood in the distance, alone and unattached to any structure. There was no sign of ruins around it, no hint of the building it must have once been attached to, and no explanation for how it came to be. It was just an empty doorway in the middle of the sand.

There was nothing particularly notable about it. It didn’t seem to include any kind of technology capable of transporting me to Salvation. The only clue that it was special was the slight glow that emanated from it, bathing the patch of white sand on either side in golden light.

I scrambled to my feet, my eyes fixed upon it. Dizziness gripped me, almost sending me tumbling back to the ground, but somehow, I managed to remain upright and take a step forward. The moment I put any weight on my left foot, the breath rushed out of me. My knee buckled, and I stumbled, almost falling again.

Tearing my gaze away from Salvation, I glanced down, and horror washed over me. I felt my mouth fall open as I stared at the sight below me. Neither of my feet were unscathed. They were both covered in cuts from the sand, but it was my left foot that made me gag. I pressed a hand to my mouth, squeezing my eyes shut as I grappled with the nausea thrashing within me and tried to breathe deeply through my nose.

After a few seconds, my stomach had settled enough for me to try again. I opened my eyes slowly and glanced down once more. The entire top had been skinned, and pain pulsed through it in time with my heartbeat, but it was the shredded strands of skin, coated in sand, that dangled from my foot that made me look away again.

I kept one hand pressed against my mouth, praying that would help keep the vomit from escaping, and forced myself to stagger forward. With each step, I could feel the swinging pieces of my skin move, drawing my attention back towards them, and bile burned the back of my throat. A breeze swept over the desert, sending sand into my eyes and making me horrifyingly aware of more sensations. I could feel more ruined skin hanging from my arms.

My gaze returned to the doorway, hoping that would be enough to distract me from the disgusting realisation as I limped towards it. Despite how deceptively plain it looked, it was the most beautiful thing I’d ever seen, and I didn’t expect that. Of course, I’d seen pictures of it before. They showed us them in school, but I didn’t expect it to look quite so… ethereal.

I hadn’t expected it to look like anything, not really. There were so many rumours swirling around about the attempts and the task we could be set, and I was ashamed to realise that I’d started to fall for them. They were too convincing.

Countless other young people had been sent out with the same goal, and only a few had succeeded. No one had returned, obviously, so it was easy to believe what people were saying. It made sense that the governor had come up with an excuse to cast out the more disobedient youths or the ones who did not live up to their full potential.

After all, there was no other way to get rid of them, and it was a delicate balance to keep the people in the bubbles alive. Too often, a drought, famine, or disease swept through the bubbles, so part of me understood why the governor would want to cull those of us who would never provide enough of a contribution to justify the resources they cost.

But it wasn’t a trick. Blood and sweat trickled down my face and back as I drew closer to the doorway, and something flickered on the other side. I stared, unable to look away as the glowing curtain shifted slightly. The air in the open arch seemed to glisten like a mirage. I caught sight of something, of another world, and tears prickled in my eyes.

Whatever I was looking at, wherever the doorway led me to in Salvation, I could see that it was so green. A stretch of grass, something I’d only seen in pictures and blurry old films, was visible, and the sight drew a sob from my throat.

I had not expected how vibrant and alive it would be, I realised as I wept. It was so different from the desert surrounding me. There, the only plants were the dry, thorny bushes that had forced their way up through the sand, and even the crops we grew in the hydrofarm were not so bright. The leaves always had a sickly yellow hue to them, regardless of the plant, but the grass I’d seen for just a moment did not.

Determination gripped my heart. I was still twenty feet or so from the doorway, but I knew that I could do it. I could make it there, and the information the microchip in my thigh contained would help the people in the bubble. It would allow Salvation to contact them, to provide them with more seeds, more water, and the uncertainty that forever dangled over my family and friends would be removed. They would all reach old age.

A shadow fell over me, and I looked up as confusion gripped me. My gaze found a giant shape, hovering in the air and blocking out the sun, but it took me a moment to realise what I was looking at. I stumbled, almost falling, before coming to a stop. I stared at the huge thing that was steadily moving towards me, and one question ricocheted through my mind:

Which of the others had an airship?

I could not remember ever seeing one before. Or, the version of me who usually existed in that world had never seen one before. I was pretty sure of that. Beneath the shock and fear, I could feel the awe that had seized my mind as I gazed up at it, but that only made me more confused, and I could not shake the feeling that it should not be there. It was wrong for the airship to be in that world.

That realisation jolted me out of my frozen state, and I shuffled forward again. My gaze found the doorway, and I moved towards it as quickly as I could. As soon as I crossed it, I would be safe. I had to be safe. Salvation was much more advanced than the bubbles. That was what we had always been told, so surely they’d be able to fight the others off. Despite the airship, I had to believe that they were still stronger than the others.

A sound split the air, sending ice through me, and my head snapped up. A tremble immediately began in my chest, and I looked around as pure terror seized me. The airship was almost directly above me, but I couldn’t see them. I couldn’t see the people who’d…

I’d stopped, I realised as I glanced back at the door. The fear that had rushed through me when I heard the sound must have caused me to freeze, and I hadn’t even noticed it, but I lunged forward towards the doorway. I was still more than ten feet away, but I had to make it there. I had to escape.

My legs collapsed beneath me. The burning sand tore at my palms as I crashed into the ground, but I couldn’t hesitate. The people could drop or teleport or whatever from the airship at any moment, and I had to keep moving.

Tears dripped from my face as I crawled forward, my breath coming in pants. The buzzing of the airship’s engine was so close that I could feel the pressure thrumming against my skin. It whipped the sand beneath me into a frenzy, and I squinted, trying desperately to keep it from my eyes.

The muffled thud of boots hitting the sand came from behind me, and another shout erupted from behind me, causing goosebumps to break out on my ruined arms. They were too close, I realised as a sob escaped my lips. Even if I reached Salvation, I would be risking bringing some of them in with me. The chip in my leg would be recognised by whatever technology Salvation utilised, and if the others clung to me as I crawled through, the people of Salvation might think it was intentional. They could think that the bubbles had sent more than one person, and bring us all.

But I could not risk inflicting the cruelty of the others on Salvation. I would not just be damning them, but it would also mean the end of my bubble. Everyone I cared about would be left to wither away in the desert, and I couldn’t do that. I couldn’t let it happen.

My decision made, I came to a stop. Dizziness washed over me, and I took one last deep breath before letting myself fall backwards out of the world. More boots landed around me. I could still hear them, feel their footsteps radiating through the sand towards me, but it was too late. I was already gone.

Coldness engulfed me as my vision turned black, and I returned to the void in my mind. The sudden absence of the sun shining down on me, burning my flesh, left me shivering, but it wasn’t just the chill that was making me tremble. It was fear.

But it was just a coincidence, I told myself. The shout the others had made wasn’t truly the same as that horrid cry that had chased me through the corridors at the Academy. It sounded similar, but it wasn’t actually, and I was sure lots of people probably had calls like that. It didn’t mean anything.

A shaky exhale passed through my lips, and I wished that I could wrap my arms around my body and hold myself. It would make me feel better, more steady, but I was falling too quickly to do that. I could barely feel my limbs, much less control them.

Other worlds stretched out towards me as I dropped past them, but I recoiled away from each one. I was too scared to let any of them wash over me because I was terrified that it would be even worse than the last world I’d been in, that I’d also be being hunted, and I just wanted to go home.

Tentatively, I began to reach out, searching for my reality, but once more, it was beyond my grasp. I could feel it, hiding somewhere at the back of my mind, but that was all I could do. I had no way of moving towards it or making it come to me.

Equal parts frustration and fear gripped me. I was annoyed that I couldn’t control it, that I couldn’t force it to do as I wanted, but I was also petrified that it would remain out of my reach forever, that I’d be trapped in the cycle of terrifying worlds, and I’d never find my way home.

I didn’t know what to do or where to go. I was still falling, passing countless worlds, but I couldn’t stay in the darkness forever. Sooner or later, one of the worlds would seize me, and I wanted a choice. I wished that I could choose where I was going. Then, I could select somewhere that was safe, like Salvation was meant to be.

An idea flickered in my mind, but I did not immediately dismiss it. Salvation was meant to be safe. I had been taught that it was a haven, a place of technological advancement and security. No one longed for anything there, and surely, if I went there, I had to be okay. The others, or whoever they were, wouldn’t be able to reach me, and neither would anyone else who might wish me harm.

I couldn’t exactly go back to the world I was just in, though. That version of me would never reach Salvation, and she was probably already captured. I needed to find another way back into the world, another me I could become there.

But that idea scared me. I wasn’t sure how it worked, how I journeyed to the worlds or how to control who I became once I arrived. If I focused on that world too much, I could just return to the same body, and the thought of that almost convinced me not to even try.

There had to be another option, though, I thought as another world came close, and desperation clawed at me. There had to be another way that I could get to Salvation. That couldn’t be the only world that it existed in, so I just needed to find it. I had to find another reality that was similar to it, but, ideally in that world, I would already be there.

Something appeared at the edge of my mind. A tentative dizziness approached, offering itself to me, and I hesitated before beginning to examine it. I was worried that it could be a trap, that the world could be trying to draw me in. If I let it, I could open my eyes and find myself in somewhere completely different and even worse than any world I had visited so far.

But it didn’t feel like a trick. The vertigo that had started to wash over me as it loomed close wasn’t unpleasant. It was making my head spin, obviously, but the sensation was strangely… nice.

And I could smell the sweet earthiness that I’d noticed in the other world. The scent of an orchard, of blooming plants and flowers, filled my nose, and I felt a smile tug at my lips as I let myself fall forward.

The world slowly solidified around me, making the scent grow stronger, and I inhaled deeply. I couldn’t see anything yet. I wasn’t sure whether my vision had yet to adjust or if I was just somewhere dark, but I was surrounded by darkness.

I blinked, trying to figure it out, before lifting an arm and waving my hand back and forth in front of my face. A hazy shadow flitted in my vision, and relief pulled at me. I could see, I realised. I just needed to find the light switch or a torch, and I’d be fine.

But I had no idea where either would be. My memories had yet to reveal themselves to me, and I took a step forward, hoping that I’d be able to find something, but a dark shape appeared before me. I shrank away as it moved closer, and a hand reached out, closing around my throat.

Fear leapt within me as I clawed at the gloved fingers, trying to pry them away, but it was impossible.

“Oh, there you are,” a gruff voice whispered, sending a waft of bad breath over me. “I’ve got you now.”
