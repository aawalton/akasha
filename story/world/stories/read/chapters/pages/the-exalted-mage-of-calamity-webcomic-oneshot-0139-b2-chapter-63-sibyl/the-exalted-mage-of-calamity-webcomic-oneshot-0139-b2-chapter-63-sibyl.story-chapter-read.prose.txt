Everyone stared at Theo.

“What are you sayin’, boy?” Orban grunted.

“Well, I’m just wondering if there’s another way we can beat him without fighting him to the death. Something else that accomplishes the same thing?”

“Do you have a suggestion?” Veronica asked.

Theo turned to Phillus. “You said you sealed him inside the castle, right? Is there a way we can seal him inside something smaller? Maybe one of the castle rooms? Or make another prison and lure him into it?”

“I activated the seal according to Morgan’s instructions. I was not, however, the one who created the trap.”

“Was there a backup trap or some kind of contingency?” Theo asked.

“I do not know.”

Theo turned toward Veronica. “That ring on your finger is some kind of storage ring, right? Can you… I dunno, store the Last Gold inside it?”

Orban kicked him in the shin.

“A-ow! What was that for?” Theo yelped, clutching his leg as he hopped backward.

“That’s the dumbest idea I’ve ever heard,” Orban grunted. “Storage rings can’t hold living creatures.”

Theo frowned. “Okay, sorry. I didn’t know…”

Veronica hummed, placing a finger beneath her chin. She turned back to the Great Stag. “You said you didn’t create the trap. Does that mean Morgan did?”

“No. Morgan presumably helped with its creation, but she did not make it herself.”

Veronica raised a brow. “Then who did?”

Phillus remained quiet for several moments, seemingly reluctant to answer. Eventually, however, he relented.

“The last guardian of Avalon. The one I said would be reluctant to help. Sibyl.”

“The eccentric one?” Veronica asked.

“Yes.”

“Why would she be reluctant? Is she so heartless that she’d leave all the other guardians trapped inside the castle?”

The Great Stag’s expression flattened. “I, too, was one of those ‘heartless’ enough to leave them inside.”

Veronica crossed her arms and gave him an equally flat look. “Will she be as easy to convince as you, then?”

Phillus sighed. “Unlike me, who feared the destruction the Last Gold could cause, Sibyl is uninterested in such things.”

“Uninterested how?”

“She cares little about anything that does not concern her. What she craves is knowledge. Curiosity guides nearly everything she does. Even if the Last Gold killed everyone else on this island except for her, she might accept it without concern. If she learned something new from it, she might even consider the day worthwhile.”

“What an inspiring way to live,” Gwen chimed in. “But that doesn’t make much sense. If she’s really like that, why make the seal in the first place? Unless she just wanted to see if she could?”

Phillus shook his head. “She owed Morgan a favor. Without that obligation, I doubt she would have helped at all.”

“Then maybe we can make another deal with her,” Nasha said. “If she owed Morgan a favor, she sounds transactional enough. Maybe we have something she wants.”

Once more, Phillus fell silent, considering the idea.

He really did seem reluctant to involve Sibyl.

“I can take you to her,” he finally said. “However, I cannot guarantee she will see you, let alone agree to help.”

Everyone looked at one another.

“Doesn’t hurt to check, right?” Gwen said.

“Seems like a waste o’ time, but I’ll do whatever it takes ta reach that forge. Don’t care ’bout the method,” Orban said.

“It’s settled, then,” Veronica said. “Let’s at least try talking to this Sibyl.”

“Oh my god. You’re so cute! Can I hug you, please?” Gwen chirped.

She and the rest of the group were now at the bottom of a short green hill. Ahead of them was a stone path that led all the way to the top, where a cozy wooden house had been built. At the bottom of the steps, however, stood a short attendant. Someone Gwen found immensely adorable.

“N-no, rude lady!” the creature replied. Its voice was surprisingly low, with an almost whimsical cadence to it.

“And respect your elders!” it cried.

The… attendant in front of Gwen was a short humanoid creature, barely even reaching her knee. Its body was a pale cream color, and it sported a large brown mushroom cap. Just from its appearance, it was clear that it wasn’t a hat, but was instead attached to its body. It even wore a small white tie and had spectacles on its face.

“Aww, come on. You look so squishy,” Gwen continued.

“No means no!”

“Okay, Bylan, stop playing around with her and guide us up,” Perri said, flying forward.

Bylan, the small mushroom person, glared up at her. “I already said that Sibyl won’t meet you.”

“You didn’t even go ask her,” Veronica interrupted.

The mushroom man put his hands on his hips. “That’s because Sibyl instructed me not to let anyone meet her. You’re always coming to her so she can fix your problems. She doesn’t care about doing any of that, or about being ‘nice.’”

Perri sighed. “Alright. Sorry about this, Bylan,” she said before flying toward him.

“H-hey, what are you—”

Perri landed atop his mushroom cap and grabbed onto it. The surprising display of strength from earlier, when she tried to steal Gwen’s staff, showed itself once more. She grabbed hold of his cap and flipped him over in the air before placing him back down on the ground.

“H-hey! Turn me back this instant!” Bylan shouted.

Due to how top-heavy he was, and the smooth, rounded shape of his cap, his body balanced almost perfectly upside down. Whenever he kicked his legs or flailed his arms, his body would lean to one side before immediately swinging back.

“Perri! You evil fairy! Flip me back!”

Gwen leaned into Veronica’s ear. “Hey… is this alright?”

Veronica pursed her lips at the scene. She thought about flipping him back upright, but meeting Sibyl was more important. “Let’s meet Sibyl first. We’ll flip him back once we’re done.”

“What?!” Bylan yelled. He turned as best as he could and looked over at the Great Stag. “Phillus! Surely you can’t be with these hooligans?!”

Phillus looked at him with pity and sighed. “Let’s go.”

“Hey!” the mushroom continued yelling as everyone walked past him.

After climbing the stone steps, they reached the top of the hill.

The house itself was quite ordinary. A wide wooden structure with a sloped roof, a couple of windows, and a porch that overlooked the bottom of the hill. A few chairs sat outside, along with some unfamiliar trinkets. Flowers of varying colors grew along the crevices of the house, while large trees shaded the back.

“Sibyl! We’re here to meet you!” Perri shouted, flying forward and cupping her hands around her mouth.

They waited a few moments, but there was no response.

Everyone looked at one another until Orban grew fed up with waiting. He stepped up to the front door and kicked it a few times instead of knocking. “Oy! You got guests!”

Again, there was no response.

“Sibyl!” Perri shouted. “Come out! We know you’re—”

“Not interested!” a voice shouted back from somewhere deep inside the house.

Although there were windows, there wasn’t enough light inside to see anything within.

Perri paused. “We didn’t even say anything yet!”

Again, they were met with silence.

Phillus stepped forward this time. “Sibyl, we have come to discuss something important. Please, at least hear us out.”

Several more moments passed without a reply. Just as Perri was about to shout again, they heard footsteps and the click of a lock behind the door. Orban moved away and regrouped with the others as they watched the door open.

To Veronica’s surprise, Sibyl looked similar to a race she’d seen before.

Dryads.

They lived with the elves near Yggdrasil, though she had seldom interacted with them. However, the dryads back in Yggdrasil were vastly different from Sibyl.

The ones back home looked more elven, wearing many of the same clothes elves wore. Their skin was green but much smoother, almost like real flesh.

The woman in front of them was far more wild.

She was also humanoid. She had green, flower-covered hair. Thorns and vines traced along her body like a makeshift dress, with two broad leaves cupping her chest. Dark seams ran along her arms, like they were bundles of plants woven together. Earthy veins could be seen across her body; a few berries even grew along her limbs. The only smooth part of her was her face, which was otherwise beautiful, with green-tinted skin and completely black eyes.

Gwen looked at her with awe, while Theo coughed and turned around.

Sibyl glanced at him, then down at herself.

“What?”

“N-nothing.”

Her black eyes narrowed slightly before moving across the rest of the group. They stopped briefly on Perri, and then Phillus, before she let out a sigh.

“You dragged outsiders into my home.”

“We need your help,” Perri said, flying forward to meet her.

“Didn’t Bylan already tell you? I’m not interested. Especially if this is regarding the Last Gold.”

“The outsiders here plan on opening the castle and fighting the Last Gold,” Phillus said.

Sibyl quickly glanced at Veronica and the others before scoffing. “How foolish.”

“We know,” Veronica added, stepping forward. “That’s why we were wondering if you had any alternative methods of defeating him.” She gestured to Phillus. “He told us you were the one who created the seal to trap the Last Gold. As a favor to Morgan.”

“That’s right,” Sibyl said plainly. “It was a one-time favor. And I’ve already done my part.”

“Do you not care if your friends and everyone else are stuck inside?” Veronica asked.

Once more, she scoffed. “Friends? I don’t need friends. I don’t care if they’re stuck in there. They’re all probably already dead.”

That earned frowns from both Phillus and Perri.

“Is there any way we can make a deal with you?” Veronica asked. “Maybe an exchange of some sort?”

“No,” Sibyl replied, not even taking a moment to think it over. “I have nothing to trade or barter with outsiders.”

Before Veronica could respond, Sibyl was already turning away, heading back into her home.

Veronica frowned. Talking to the dryad was much more difficult than she imagined, especially since she didn’t even care to listen.

Orban clicked his tongue. “Tree hag doesn’t even care. Knew this would’ve been a waste of time.”

Sibyl completely ignored his remark.

“Let’s go, Veronica,” Nasha said, placing a hand on her shoulder. “Maybe the Last Gold won’t be as strong as we thought.”

As they were turning away, Sibyl froze.

“Wait.”

Everyone stopped and looked back at her.

“Your name is Veronica?” Sibyl asked, walking forward.

Veronica and Nasha glanced at each other for a brief moment.

“That’s right,” Veronica answered.

Sibyl placed a hand beneath her chin and inspected her, looking at her face from different angles. “Hmm… how common is the name Veronica where you come from?”

Veronica raised a brow but considered the question. “I don’t think I know another Veronica.”

“I know one. She’s an old lady who was a regular at the place I used to work at,” Gwen said.

“There’s a playwright back in Ronswick named Veronica. Never worked with her before, though,” Nasha added.

A few more moments passed as Sibyl studied Veronica.

Then, to everyone’s surprise, she agreed to help them.

“Alright. I’ll help you all on account of this girl here. However, I expect a favor later,” Sibyl said.

“What kind of favor?” Veronica asked.

“That will be determined later. For now, tell me what you want.”

Everyone looked at one another, clearly confused by what had just happened.

Veronica most of all.

She had never met this woman before, yet she had the distinct feeling Sibyl hadn’t been asking about someone who merely happened to share her name.

The problem was that no one should have known who Veronica was.

There was no prodigy named Veronica in this timeline. No Veronica living within Annesheim. No Veronica destined to become an archmage.

Here, she was a complete blank slate with a fabricated identity.

So who exactly had Sibyl been looking for?
