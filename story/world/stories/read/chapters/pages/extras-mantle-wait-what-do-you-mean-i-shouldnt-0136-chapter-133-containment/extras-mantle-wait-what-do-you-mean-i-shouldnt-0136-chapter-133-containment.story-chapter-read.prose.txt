Veric's back hit the wall, and there was nowhere left to go, because his body was no longer listening to him, and as if the intense pressure—like someone had quadrupled gravity itself—boring down on every bone in his frame wasn't enough, the hallucinations hadn't stopped.

They had only gotten worse.

The closer whatever was possessing Jin came to him, the more the faces multiplied. They weren't just memories anymore. They were here, crowding his peripheral vision in a silent gallery of the damned, mouths moving in accusations he couldn't hear, eyes bleeding the same black oil that streamed from Jin's face.

If it weren't for his training and his decades of experience in the art of mind resistance and how to break one, he would have long been mindbroken.

Jin took another step forward, and the pressure doubled, grinding Veric further into the stone as his bones groaned under the weight and blood began pooling between his teeth.

The posture was wrong. The gait was wrong. One leg dragged slightly behind the other, and the joints moved in sequences that didn't match how human bodies were supposed to work: knees bending a half-second before the hips caught up, the spine straightening after the shoulders had already shifted forward. It was like watching someone try to operate machinery they'd only ever seen from the outside, and the feeling of nauseating wrongness kept layering on top of the hallucinations.

Jin took another step.

Close enough now that Veric could see the details he wished he couldn't… the dried blood crusting under Jin's fingernails, the dark tears carving wet tracks through the grime on his cheeks, the way his mouth hung slightly open as if the jaw had forgotten how to close.

The white eyes fixed on him with an intensity that had nothing human behind it.

Move. You need to move.

And where in fuck are the people I called for!

He gathered what little power he had left and prepared to use his compulsion again—knowing full well it would buy him a second at most, and that this time the backlash might kill him outright—but he would be damned if he didn't try.

Jin raised his hand.

Veric knew “kneel” or “down” commands won’t work.

"HALT!" Veric croaked, pouring every scrap of conviction he had left into the word, and Jin's form froze mid-stride.

A door materialized behind Jin—plain weathered oak, edges sharp and defined—and Joe stepped through with his lord flames already blazing white-crimson around both arms.

Veric saw through bloodied vision that Joe didn't bother wasting time. The man took one look at the scene—Veric pinned against the wall, Jin standing like a broken puppet between them, the room papered in blood and madness—gave him a quick, grim nod, and his hand closed around Jin's collar while the other pressed flat against his own door.

Joe pulled, and both of them disappeared through the frame.

The pressure vanished like a cork popping free.

The hallucinations guttered and died like flames starved of oxygen. The faces dissolved. The accusations fell silent. And Veric—free of the crushing weight for the first time in what felt like hours but had been barely ninety seconds—collapsed forward onto his hands and knees, gasping.

Blood dripped from his eyes, his nose, his ears. His skull felt like it had been used as a drum.

The door hadn't closed.

Through the frame, Veric caught a glimpse of somewhere deep and dark before the sounds of explosions erupted from the other side.

“Ah, shit…” Veric wiped blood from his eyes with the back of his hand, hauled himself upright on legs that barely cooperated, and stumbled toward the door.

◈◈◈

Joe hit the chamber floor hard and rolled, releasing Jin's collar mid-motion to put distance between them.

In that brief contact—his hand against Jin’s skin, lord flames pressed close enough to read something underneath—Joe had confirmed what he'd dreaded the moment Veric's message reached him.

Jin had come into contact with something he definitely shouldn't have. Again.

What am I going to do with this kid?

But this time, he knew he was also partly responsible; his releasing the “Void Heart” must have brought attention and weakened the boundaries.

What a twist of fate, Jin got in contact with something right at that moment.

The core room spread out around them as Joe saw the crystal pillar standing dormant at its center from the corner of his eye. He was betting on its help.

But even with the majority of bastion systems offline, Joe could exert some of his authority as Envoy to trigger the containment measures hardwired into the bastion's bones. This place had been built for exactly this kind of threat.

Jin landed on all fours where Joe had thrown him, and for one heartbeat—just one—Joe hoped the impact might have knocked him out.

It didn't.

Wishful thinking.

Jin's head rotated toward him with that broken, twitching motion, and those white eyes locked on. The dark tears streamed in thick lines down his cheeks, and the pressure that had been crushing Veric upstairs bloomed outward through the chamber like a shockwave, settling over everything with suffocating weight.

I’m still in a weakened state from earlier and cursed with void poisoning… let’s just hope this won’t be too much.

Joe planted his feet, flared his aura to its full extent—crimson light erupting around him in a blaze that pushed back the encroaching darkness—and met those empty eyes directly.

One thing I know… this is going to be a pain in the ass.

Oh, how I wish Salvatore were here.

But Salvatore wasn't here, and Joe was alone in this.

Jin stood. That same marionette motion—ankles, knees, hips, spine—one joint at a time, hauled upright by invisible strings.

"Jin." Joe's voice carried across the chamber, calm and steady despite the crawling wrongness pressing against his mental defenses from every angle. "I know you're in there. Fight it, I’ll pull you out soon."

No response. The white eyes stared. The dark tears fell.

Joe raised his right hand—the one with blackened fingertips—and snapped.

Three doors materialized simultaneously around the chamber, each one angled to cut off Jin's avenues of movement, funneling him toward the center where the dormant pillar stood.

Jin walked through the first door, and the oak splintered and disintegrated as his body passed through it, and Joe grunted as the feedback from having his mantle's manifestation shredded hit him in the gut like a physical blow.

Brute force, then.

He snapped again, and a white book materialized above his open palm—thick, leather-bound, pages dense with formations and runes he'd spent years inscribing—Joe fluttered through the pages with practiced speed, found what he wanted, pressed his hand flat against the page, and a magic circle blazed to life beneath his fingers.

His lord flames fed into the spell, and a concussive wave of compressed force launched toward Jin with enough power behind it to stagger an Underlord.

The blast screamed across the chamber.

And missed.

Not by much. A hair's breadth. The wave passed Jin's left shoulder close enough to ruffle his blood-stained shirt, close enough that the heat should have singed his skin, but it didn't touch him.

Joe's eyes narrowed. He fired again—three rapid pulses from different angles.

The first veered left at the last instant, bending away from Jin's body as if repelled by an invisible field.

The second curved upward, deflecting off nothing at all, its trajectory warping in defiance of every law of physics Joe had ever learned.

The third simply dissipated. The essence unraveled two inches from Jin's face, breaking apart like a wave hitting a seawall made of nothing.

Dispersion? Anti-essence? Probability manipulation? Joe's jaw clenched as he ran through the possibilities. Some kind of conceptual deflection.

Just what principle is riding in that body? Thankfully, it’s not death.

The entity wearing Jin kept walking toward him. Steady. Unhurried. Closing the distance with patience that suggested it knew exactly how this would end.

I need to end this fast.

Joe dropped low and wrapped his lord flames tight around his body until the crimson light sat flush against his skin like armor. If ranged attacks wouldn't land, then close quarters it was.

He surged forward—faster than most Underlords could track, let alone react to—and drove a palm strike toward Jin's sternum. Not lethal. Just enough force to knock him back, create space, buy time to set up the containment.

The strike connected. So not a 100% deflection… oh no.

Or at least his hand should have connected. But his palm slid past Jin's chest by a fraction—a tiny miscalculation that shouldn't have been possible for someone of his skill level—his hand grazed fabric instead of striking bone, and the momentum carried him forward, off-balance, into range.

The white eyes met his.

Pressure erupted right between his eyes like a spike driven through his skull.

His mother's face. Bloodied and dying. Her hands on his cheeks.

"The seed must never wake up—"

Joe snarled, forcing his will outward, drawing on reserves of discipline, and through the haze of grief, he drove a solid hit into Jin's abdomen with everything he had.

The hallucinations fractured—spiderweb cracks through the visions, his real sight bleeding through the gaps—and Jin bent like a flipped C from the impact, doubling over the fist buried in his gut. But he neither fell nor was thrown off balance. He stood there, somehow without any reaction, in a comical C shape.

Joe followed up with a cross aimed at the jaw.

Miss.

He pulled back, adjusted, and raised his leg in a high kick.

Miss.

Punch.

Miss.

Low kick.

Miss.

Straight right, thrown with enough force to cave in a stone wall.

Miss.

Every single follow-up slid past its target by the barest margin—fingertips grazing cheekbones, boot heels whistling past kneecaps, knuckles brushing fabric without ever finding the flesh beneath. It was like fighting smoke that had learned to wear a human shape.

Growling in frustration, Joe clapped his hands together and poured his essence into a magic circle that bloomed at his fingertips, bright and dense and impossible to dodge because it didn't need to hit—it just needed to push.

A wave of raw force slammed outward, and Jin's body skidded backward across the stone floor, boots scraping furrows through the tile, buying Joe three precious meters of breathing room.

In his peripheral vision, a figure stumbled through the still-open door—blood on his face, one arm hanging uselessly at his side, eyes burning with the stubborn refusal to stay down that only career soldiers and complete idiots possessed.

Veric.

The investigator took in the scene with one sweep of his bloodshot gaze—Joe breathing hard, Jin already recovering his footing across the chamber, the dormant pillar standing between them—and whatever he saw in Joe's expression must have told him everything he needed to know, because the man simply nodded.

"HALT!"

Veric's mantle flared.

The compulsion slammed into Jin from behind—a lance of authority, bypassing the entity's defenses entirely because it wasn't targeting the soul. It was targeting the flesh. The muscles, the tendons, the involuntary obedience buried deep in the animal hindbrain that responded to commands spoken with enough conviction behind them.

Jin's knees buckled, and he froze as if time itself had paused for him alone.

Joe didn't waste the opening.

He slammed his blackened right hand against the floor, the dark band on his wrist flaring to life with blinding intensity, and spoke with the authority of an Envoy.

"Keeper! Containment protocol! Heavenly principles' presence inside an ally and potential inheritor!"

The dormant crystal pillar pulsed once.

CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL RHG-OMEGA-ONE-ONE-EC ACTIVE

ADEQUATE POWER SOURCE PRESENT

CONTAINING.

Lines of runes erupted from the pillar's base—ancient, brilliant, burning with blue-white fire that carried the weight of centuries—spiderwebbing across the floor in branching patterns that raced toward Jin's frozen form with unerring precision.

The lines reached him and became rings, rising from the stone, stepping off the floor like shackles forged from pure light. They wound around Jin's body—arms, torso, legs, throat—binding him in a vice of ancient power that predated every empire currently squabbling over this dying world.

Black lightning crackled from Jin's form as the entity fought back. Space flickered and warped around him, the same deflection field that had turned aside every attack now slamming against the bastion's containment protocols with desperate fury.

But the bastion held.

The rings of runes spread further, crawling across Jin's skin until his entire body was covered in blazing lines of script that wrapped around his essence signature, his soul architecture, the connection between host and parasite, and squeezed.

Jin screamed.

It was two voices at once—one human, ragged, desperate with pain. The other layer and wrong that made the stone walls vibrate and sent hairline cracks racing through the ceiling.

The entity thrashed against the containment with everything it had, and the pressure in the room spiked so hard that Veric was slammed flat against the floor like a man hit by a falling building, and Joe's lord flames were snuffed out like candles in a hurricane.

But the bastion held.

The rings constricted. The runes burned brighter. And inch by agonizing inch, the entity was peeled away from Jin's soul—dragged out of the cracks it had crawled through—its hooks torn free one by one in a process that looked and sounded like someone ripping barbed wire from living flesh.

Jin's body convulsed, his back arching off the ground hard enough that only his heels and the crown of his skull touched stone. The white in his eyes flickered… white, then grey, then a flash of the familiar silver-grey that Joe recognized, then white again.

One final pulse from the pillar. Blinding. The whole chamber rang like a struck bell, the sound reverberating through the bastion's bones from the deepest vault to the highest parapet.

And then silence.

Jin collapsed, limp and boneless, against the cold stone floor, and didn't move.

The dark tears dried on his cheeks, leaving faint stains like ash scattered across pale skin.

Joe hauled himself upright—his body screaming in protest, the chaos infestation in his fingertips throbbing in sympathy with the bastion's exertion—and quickly crossed the distance to Jin. He dropped to his knees beside the boy and pressed two fingers against his neck.

Pulse. Faint but steady.

Breathing. Shallow but present.

"Keeper," Joe said, his voice raw. "Status of the containment."

HEAVENLY PRINCIPLE SIGNATURES DETECTED.

INADEQUATE DATA FOR IDENTIFICATION OF THE PRINCIPLE. ESTIMATION: A PRINCIPLE RELATED TO “REALITY”

HP-OMEGA-ONE-ONE IS DRIVEN OUT OF THE HOST.

HOST VITAL SIGNS: CRITICAL BUT STABLE.

BASTION INTEGRITY DROPPED TO 91%. PLEASE BEGIN REPAIRS.

WARNING: SEALS PLACED ON THE HOST BUT THEY WILL DEGRADE WITHOUT MAINTENANCE.

Joe stared at the text floating in the dimness, each line carrying implications he didn't have the energy to fully process right now.

"Thank you," he whispered.

IT IS MY DUTY.

Joe closed his eyes and exhaled through his teeth.

Behind him, Veric had pulled himself into a sitting position against the far wall, looking like death warmed over and then left out in the rain. Blood covered half his face. One arm hung limp at his side.

"Is he alive?" Veric's voice was a croak, barely audible across the vast chamber.

"Yeah." Joe looked down at Jin's unconscious face. "He's alive."

"The thing inside him?"

"Gone. For now, at least." Joe ran a hand through his hair, exhaling slowly. "We need to have someone keep an eye on him around the clock. Who knows what pile of shit we're stepping in next the moment he is left alone."

“Agreed,” Veric chuckled, and the sound that came out of him was more wheeze than humor, strained through what had to be at least two cracked ribs and a concussion.

"Well," he said finally, letting his head fall back against the stone. "Never a quiet day with you lot."
