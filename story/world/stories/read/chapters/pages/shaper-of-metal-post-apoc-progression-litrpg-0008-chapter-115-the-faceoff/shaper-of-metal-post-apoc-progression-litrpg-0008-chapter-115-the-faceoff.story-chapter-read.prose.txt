Chapter 115: The Faceoff

Chime glared at Jack and scoffed. “Stop acting all tough, oldie. That was a straight-up bitch move.”

“Oh yeah?” Jack said, raising an eyebrow cockily and crossing his arms. “And who’s too much of a bitch to step to me over a little musk?”

“Tch!”

It was at this point that Inkblot slowly moved forward toward Chime, like walking doom itself. Chime tried not to stiffen in response. He was kinda stuck. He couldn’t suddenly shift to Jack now. The new challenger had entered the ring, and he had little choice but to respond unless he wanted to look like a hypocrite with the sudden ‘bitch move’ of walking away.

Haha — and so the trap is sprung! We’ll go with that as a planned thing when we look back. That’s how I remember it already.

Meanwhile, everyone was talking over each other at this point. Highfive definitely said something cringey about the ‘little man,’ though he didn’t catch Defiant’s response, and Bo and Hiccup were going off with emphatic attitude. He thought Hiccup said something about ‘your little carry-bag ho,’ but Jack was uncertain who that referred to. In truth… He’d never say it out loud or admit it, but their exchange was pretty adorable to him, personally. A guilty pleasure, perhaps?

“Can we just drop it? Please?!” Délight called, eyes flickering over everyone. Everyone aside from Jack ignored her, and when their eyes met, she widened them insistently, as if he could stop it.

Jack just shook his head with a subtle shrug. Sorry, Sister.

Délight looked to the heavens briefly and sighed, plopping her head in her arms on the table.

Chime and Inkblot were nose-to-nose in a preposterous stare-off. Chime’s expression remained sour, likely due to the smell. Chime said something, but Inkblot didn’t reply, somehow nonverbally mocking him for even speaking, somehow stepping it up to an even more intense stare-down. It was as if he’d moved a fraction closer, looming over the other guy, despite not moving.

A bead of sweat could just be seen on Chime’s forehead.

Hilarious shit. Chime is stuck with a guy who probably had to stare people down as part of his real, daily life, with actual deadly violence on the line. This is child’s play for him.

In that very moment, Jack chose to walk by… passing right next to Chime, but not quite touching him, lest he give an excuse to break contact. Just a polite stroll across the way, no need to even say ‘excuse me!’

Chime literally had to suppress a gagging sound.

Jack could almost do cartwheels with how self-satisfied he was to make a mockery of all this, and presumably still not have his team giving him the stink-eye about it, because it had obviously worked out quite well.

He walked up to the table, letting the taunts of the others wash over him as mere noise. He grabbed a fry off Hiccup’s plate and ate it.

Délight raised her head to look at him; she glowered slightly, though perhaps it was just her own glumness at the moment. “So, is this part of my punishment, Jack?”

Jack shrugged. “You could look at it that way. More accurate to say these are consequences. If you’d wanted a world where we sat together, held hands, and sang hymns, you’d have conducted yourself much differently years ago.”

Délight nodded slowly. “We all restart somewhere.”

“Spoken like a true Linewalker. Starting is easy, though, isn’t it? Walking it through is harder.”

“Mmn.” She looked down at her plate of some huge vegetable ‘pancake’ or something that she’d barely eaten from, with neat slices taken out. “Your cologne is alluring.”

Jack was stunned by that. He had a fry halfway to his mouth. His brow furrowed. “You’re shitting me.”

Délight shook her head, met his eyes anew, and smiled. “You should wear it more often. Perhaps half as heavy, though. Nonetheless, I’m tempted to do the facedown thing with you, but for ulterior motives. Nose to nose. I could stand on a chair. What do you think?” A teasing look and tone as she raised a brow.

Jack ate the fry in his hand as a convenient distraction to collect his thoughts. This girl remains dangerous, doesn’t she? “That would most likely sabotage your entire effort this evening.”

Délight laughed faintly. “A forbidden affair it remains, then.” Her eyes swept to the others, her humor fading. “My efforts are already ruined, but you’re right, aren’t you? I should stick to my guns.” She rose slowly. She took a deep breath and met his eyes one last time — wistfully, he thought — then turned and walked away without another word.

Jack opened his mouth once, but closed it. Well, it was mandatory to attend, not to stay, after all.

He glanced at the others, still quite occupied, though likely near to wrapping things up.

Hiccup suddenly laughed with great exaggeration and turned her head. “Délight, this stupid ho just said-” She cut off short when she saw her teammate wasn’t even there. “What the frag?!” Her voice became strangled. She glared at Jack as if he’d stolen and hid her somehow. “Where-”

“Awww, what’s the matter?” Bo taunted. “Did your daycare nanny run off? No one to change your diapers and schedule your nappy-boos?”

“Yeah, looks like Mommy doesn’t approve of your behavior, lil’ boppers,” Highfive added mockingly. “Now you’re gonna get it. Probably a spanking. Then again, you weirdos would like that too much, wouldn’t you?”

Hiccup turned to glare at both of them equally, fists clenched and unable to muster an immediate response.

Defiant sniffed derisively and walked away from Highfive, taking the sparse steps to get right in Jack’s face. Due to Jack’s position and facing, he had to practically get into the table’s space to accomplish this. As such, he bumped it hard, causing cups to spill onto the table. One of them even splashed right on both of them. Jack jerked away, but Defiant ignored it.

The Non was something at the intersection of baby-faced and sharply symmetrical. In contrast, his eyes were hard and fierce, giving everything he beheld a continuous smolder. A challenge. He was short and naturally slight, but obviously did everything in his power to build musculature on top of that. He was solid, for sure.

“Hey there,” Jack said with a smile as he brushed liquid off his pant leg. “Something I can do for you, champ? Other than behold your mighty greatness?” Words laced with mocking sarcasm, as he gestured at the spilled cups.

Defiant was unmoved. “Keep thinking you’re playing the winning hand in your little game, Ferro. You’ve got no idea of the storm coming for you tomorrow, but don’t worry” — he moved slightly closer and raised his eyebrows — “we’ll make it quick.”

“Will you, now?” Jack actually thought the opposite, but no need to advertise. “I guess we’ll see.”

“No. You won’t.”

“That’s my line,” Inkblot quipped, looking over.

Defiant broke eye contact to huff dismissively at Inkblot, and then turned away, sauntering around the table, his tough guy act a bit spoiled by the liquid stain on his pants.

Chime took the distraction opportunity to finally break away from Inkblot. When he saw his and Hiccup’s drink spilled, and Jack next to it, he threw his hands out. “What the hell, dude?!” He rushed over to pick up the cups, though obviously it was far too late.

A cleaning drone flew right over the table, brandishing its prehensile cleaning tube, clearly specialized in sucking up liquids.

Bo managed a quick quip. “Oh, look, another of Délight’s boyfriends!”

“Ohhhh!” Highfive called, laughing.

The drone spoke right over this, in a polite, mechanical voice. “Can I be of assistance in cleaning this unfortunate spillage, Junior Agents?”

“Yes!” Chime called with another dirty look at Jack. By now, Hiccup was turned around and eyeing the whole situation with an incredulous look, her arms crossed.

“Hey, man,” Jack replied, holding up his hands and then gesturing vaguely at Defiant, who was nonchalantly scarfing down his final bites while still standing. “He bumped the table.”

“Pff. Whatever. To hell with this — you people are ridiculous. Let’s go, babe. I’ll buy dessert.”

A strange dirge of any human noises descended as the drone turned on a loud vacuum to clean up the spilled liquid from the table and floor. Jack and his teammates slowly came together shoulder-to-shoulder, watching the couple leave without looking back, clearly agitated, from their general pace. Defiant lingered only long enough to look at them each, one by one, in a row, before silently departing as well.

When the drone was finally done and flew away, Bo was all grins as she sighed contentedly, hands on her hips. “That went well.”

“Did it?” Jack asked. “Felt more like a shitshow.”

“Mmn. They blinked first. We owned this match. We’re totally in their heads, now, and completely rent-free.”

“I feel kinda icky, not gonna lie,” Highfive said. “Way too bully-centric for me these days. Not that Defiant cracked from it, as you could probably tell.”

“He hates you big time, Fiver. Good enough for me.”

“Well, as long as you’re happy, Queen.”

“Yes.”

Highfive scoffed and shook his head.

“What if that plays into his hand?” Inkblot asked. Everyone turned his way curiously. “His Conceptual powerset. Defying someone. Being an underdog.”

“Shit,” Jack murmured with an exhale. “Well, the ship has sailed. If it does, we did it. We gambled for a psyche advantage. Bo might be right that we have that on the greater team, even if not on Defiant.”

“I mean, I could apologize,” Highfive said. “Claim it was all bogus, and I feel guilty. Take the sting right out of it all, become the good guy again. A-allegedly, you know?”

Bo made a loud ‘tsk’ sound, glaring at Highfive with supreme disappointment. “No! We don’t know how his powers work. You’re not going to take this away from me. Us, I mean.”

“Riiiiight…”

Jack chuckled at the exchange. “Forget it. It won’t make or break us.” He glanced at their enemy’s table, feeling somewhat bad that he’d interrupted their meal. Not for them, per se, just for wasted food worth far too much to waste. But he saw that nearly every day, amongst this privileged class. He was tempted especially by Délight’s barely-touched meal. It could be taken, right? Unfortunately, he didn’t trust that it wouldn’t somehow give her a weird advantage from her powers. Paranoid, perhaps. Yet painful, also, to leave so much. He turned away. “Let’s go finish our food and get outta here. Time is ticking.”

“Seriously, bro?” Highfive asked. “You’re still hungry after that?”

“Of course. Aren’t you? Aren’t we Nonnies always hungry?”

“Hmm. I could eat, probably…”

They only had so long until their time was up with the Sim Chamber, so they hurried to get the process going on duel practice.

They very briefly went back over their drill process verbally to cement things, and then triggered the countdown of seven minutes as they burst into action, everyone with immediate tasks to conduct. Jack rushed to distribute very hasty metal particulate fields everywhere, and further move metal into place for the makeshift traps, going by rote and ritual by now, with the others conducting their own roles or helping place the pieces.

The amount of area he had to cover with his fields was too vast to get a good field over the majority. Instead, he had a larger majority of less-finely-detailed fields in large zones. He did this by making a small, dense zone of somewhat larger bits, then diffusing them over a much wider area to the limits of decent usefulness. He picked out likely hotbed areas for finer details and technically more density, with a conventional field, which also might move if he needed them to.

While he worked, he knew the sims were over on their starting spot, which was some random direction beyond a blocking forcefield wall entirely surrounding the place at a far distance.

I guess they can’t detect anything through that. That’s a good thing, or Chime could ferret things out with sound somehow.

Near the start of the actual match, Jack was the first to rush back to the hidey hole room where Highfive was because he needed to finish the final metal layer on the inside, putting it over the big man’s sprayed insulation. Highfive was just finishing, and the room smelled strongly of acrid chemicals. But it looked great. Insulation coated everything, and he’d started with metal floor panels that were allowed to cure as he did other work, and then fitted into slots, allowing for a solid walkway with the rest foam-facing. It was a sea of blue foam.

“Damn, dude!” Jack exclaimed, smiling on the inside of his mask, as he immediately began finishing the cured sections, placing metal panels over them and fusing them to existing panels, one after the other, by rote. “Looks absolutely great. Like a professional did it.”

“Thanks!” Highfive replied, as he set the gun down and otherwise stayed in his little corner. His armor had bits of blue foam sticking to it. “Reminds me of the old days, running around construction sites. Less dirt and junk everywhere, though. Just before I hit my powers, I was helping more. Kinda liked it.”

“Nice. If there was dirt, I might feel at home, too. But there would need to be manure, too.”

“I thought you were a city boy?”

“Er, yeah! City dirt. City manure.”

“Uh-huh.”

The other two came in after finishing the last of any placements, and Jack soon finished the whole room’s metal encasement, including the ‘door.’ There was no door, really — not to anyone but him. The whole wall would fold out, or could be sent hurling at their enemies if he wanted.

Everyone turned their attention to the cameras. Each had a section divided equally, so they would monitor and notify Jack to focus on any given camera if there was a target available.

The simulated match began in earnest, with the defenders technically just standing around in a dimly-lit, steel-encased room; condensed firepower waiting to unleash at the proper time.

They got vague indications of Chime’s handiwork and a few very brief indications of movement behind rocky cover. Inside the room, there was barely anything at all, even when a wave passed directly through the fort section they were in. Meanwhile, cameras flickered when Chime’s vibrations went through them — thankfully, they held up to repeated blasts, showing he was not detecting them. There was zero question that he could target and destroy them if he located them. They were small, reasonably hidden, and presumably he wasn’t looking for them.

Chime blasted some areas copiously, apparently paranoid that they were hiding in some section of the wall’s many fortifications. It eventually had the air of frustration. There was no indication that he detected the sealed room by its oddity, which was something of a worry that Jack had. It meant that, at least in terms of the sim, Chime could not discern material details through so much intervening material. Probably because the fortress walls were thick and naturally sound-absorbing.

Hmm. So perhaps not paranoia about the walls, but hopefulness? He knows we’re in the fortress, but he really doesn't want us here. He’s gotta get inside to find us.

Getting inside the fortress wasn’t easy. Why would it be? They’d likely have to go through the gate unless they could get to the roof. Fortunately for Team Highlife, their enemy was not super mobile vertically. They could definitely do it by quasi-conventional means… if allowed to jerk around in the yard.

Yeah. Best not to let them.

Inevitably, the enemy team had to stage a break-in, unable to pinpoint or affect their rival from a distance whatsoever. Rather than approach the formidable and intimidating double portcullis gate, the team picked a spot in the wall, and Chime began blasting it directly from a distance, weakening it almost immediately.

Jack didn’t have much time to decide on the tactic. Let them in the walls? He could pelt them from afar and give them trouble, but they weren’t yet truly in range of serious likelihood for wounding. But the other element was, just how paranoid did he want them? They didn’t know exactly how their enemy was hidden. He could feign an attack to not make it too easy…

Ultimately, he decided to let them in. They might be wary, but they still wouldn’t expect directed attacks if it seemed their enemy was holing up and forcing an altercation inside the fort. That was more consistent with a team they could feel confident against, and risk less caution.

And it was a simulation! They could test different strategies. At least a few.

Chime battered the wall for a spell, went to cover, re-scanned, waited, then came back out to blast it again. He did his cautious maneuver one more time before coming out and finishing it, breaking down a wall section that could be climbed over.

Jack didn’t have anything fancy where they were coming in, but he had metal needles and a few larger bits under the sand in the approach to the gate, regardless of whether they took the short way or the long way around, and more hidden up top on the roof, to come raining down.

His best fields were hovering right over those patches, but the whole inside was covered. Aside from that, he had the cameras, which… Well, it remained to be seen how well things worked through them.

Defiant — clad in his full suit and helmet and blatantly a sim thanks to a weird color spectrum sheen — ran up to the wall, alone. Jack definitely didn’t bite on that particular bait.

The man arrogantly hopped over the rubble pile, seemingly without a care. He looked around, scouting things out. At one point, his helmet was turned directly toward a camera. He might’ve looked dead into it. Jack held his breath.

Defiant moved on without any apparent indication he’d seen it. He jogged through the yard, all the way around the fort, getting close to the gate.

Jack was clenching his teeth at this point. There were various things to notice here. But he didn’t get close and didn’t touch anything. Presumably, he would have trouble getting through the heavy gate, though supposedly, he could push through power-reinforced materials, particularly if bendable. Was there a limit to how much he’d try to crack this particular egg alone?

Apparently so. He jogged back the way he came, around the fort, toward the breach.

Ugh. I hate this tactic, though. Still, they might even go to this after getting attacked at a distance, if they deem it safer. Frag me. We won’t even know exactly how they’ll act from these damned sims — because their damned sims! The real deal could be different.

As Defiant approached the breach point, his team was sprinting for it. Each of them was a suited form with a strange, back-lit, unreal color, highlighting them as sims. Defiant stood on top of the rubble, his eyes shifting everywhere like a sentinel, ready to protect someone if there was an ambush.

Chime had a thick utility belt and a small additional harness on his back, carrying various implements. As he was moving over the rubble, he was using two glass rods continuously, hitting them very lightly and then running them down over each other, creating lots of weird, high-pitched harmonics.

Some kind of detection method?

Whatever it was, it seemed unlikely to penetrate walls. It filled open spaces, making far different sounds down different corridors.

And then the enemy team was over the rubble and moving around to the gate.

“Alright,” Jack whispered into the dark, antsy room. “Time to strike.”
