Ethan stepped through a rift, appearing in an increasingly familiar underground cavern. It was enormous, and actually existed within the Astral, being the place the Old Masters had chosen to hide their god after he was injured long ago. Since Ethan had healed the god and woken him up, the dark, sparse cave had been decorated with tapestries and art from other worlds, and was almost cozy.

Ethan had taken a number of trips to this strange place since Twilight had been found a half-year earlier, but he hadn’t seen a single Master. He’d stopped bothering to hide his fury at that fact, but he still tried to be respectful to the god himself.

The great hydra had come a long way since Ethan’s intervention, and had even sprouted a second head, although that was a long way from full restoration. The massive creature loomed over Ethan, the size of a luxury mansion.

“Greetings, Noctem,” Ethan said, bowing his head respectfully.

“Welcome, Chosen, thank you for answering my call. There has been…news.”

“Have your wayward children finally decided to fight for this world?” Ethan asked excitedly.

The hydra rumbled, deep in his vast chest. “They have reached their decision, and you will see the results soon enough, but they are not why I have called you here.”

Ethan’s eyes widened slightly at the answer. About time they got off their asses. “That’s excellent news,” he said. “But if not for them, why have you called me here?”

“Twilight wakes,” Noctem said.

Now that was news. Earth’s god of technology and innovation had been in varying states of semi-consciousness for some time. The twins could glean some of his stray thoughts, and occasionally even get vague answers to questions, but it was akin to someone slipping in and out of a coma.

But the Awakened had been working around the clock to bring their god back, and Ethan had often assisted with the healing where he could. Despite the apparent mechanical nature of the body Twilight had chosen in Atlantis, there were quite a few organic elements to him that the Awakened were ill-prepared to treat. Ethan’s rune had gotten quite the workout, and remained his most advanced ability, at Fourth tier.

“Has he spoken?” Ethan asked.

“Far more than that, we have been in communication. It is a shame that Dawn chose to leave before the Union was complete, though I understand her desire. Her time on Nexum was challenging.” An understatement, as she’d been tortured for thousands of years at the hands of misguided Chosen.

Noctem continued. “We are capable of combining our talents to greater effect, and have done so to gain a better understanding of the state of Potentia.” Ethan felt a surge of excitement. They had effectively no intelligence on that deadly world. Ethan himself had been the last one to visit, and that had been years prior.

“What have you learned?” he asked.

“Troubling things. We were not surprised to find that our brother’s power had led to corruption. His has always been the most volatile element of the Union, and he has been dormant for so long. Yet in the past, the power always belonged to him.”

“I’m not sure I understand, is this about The Throne?”

“The Throne,” Noctem rumbled. “This is not a creation of Dusk, but rather the culmination of thousands of years of desire made manifest. I am aware of what those touched by Potentia have experienced, the overwhelming need to reach for the greatest source of power on that planet, and dominate it. You are correct that this is the source of the problem.”

“But what’s changed? Demons have chased The Throne for as long as there’s been demons.”

“And yet they’ve never reached it,” Noctem said. “They could not reach it, for the power was never their own. No more than you could take my power, simply by entering this cavern. But some time ago, a tipping point was reached. Dusk’s grasp has weakened too much, and finally slipped entirely. It is a ripe fruit, merely waiting to be plucked.”

Tomo appeared in a flash next to Ethan. The demon remained every bit as loyal as he ever had, but his confidence and independence had grown since he’d sacrificed himself to protect Twilight. He actually spent time alone, meditating within Ethan’s soul, rather than always hovering just over Ethan’s shoulder. Ethan missed that, but was proud of his friend for growing, and remembering himself.

“Noctem,” the demon greeted with a nod. Confident indeed. “I chased The Throne for more time and more lives than I can ever recall. Are you saying I never could have succeeded?”

“That is uncertain,” the great hydra replied. “We cannot say exactly when the change occurred.”

“So what are you saying?” Ethan asked. “From what I’m hearing, it didn’t used to be the way we thought, but it is now? That doesn’t sound like it changes anything.”

“It changes everything, Chosen,” Noctem said, some annoyance entering his tone. “Even if the power could have been claimed before, at worst it would have created a terribly powerful entity, confined to Potentia. But that power would have been lost at the culmination of the Union. Returned to its true master.”

“And now?” Ethan asked, starting to grasp the real problem.

“Now, Dusk’s connection has been severed. If another being claims The Throne, they will be the one in charge when the Union takes place. And given that Dawn has left, and Twilight is severely weakened, this being would become the dominant force in shaping the world that is to come.”

Ethan’s mouth went dry. “So, if a demon takes The Throne, they’ll be able to shape our new world into their own image?” he asked.

“A demon, or a human, whoever masters the power.”

“Unsettling though that is,” Tomo said, “it is not so simple. Always the Throne has required gathering a significant portion of the power that permeates my homeworld. We also believed claiming it was possible only for Champions, who seeth with corruption. Was that also a lie?”

“No, child of Potentia, you have the right of it. To ascend The Throne, you must accumulate an extraordinary amount of power. The real mystery is why the demon Champions have not done so already.”

Ethan considered that, and an image of a woman in silver armor popped into his head. Still, the stakes may be higher, but he didn’t believe this changed his plans. “We already planned to go to Potentia, gather power, and take The Throne,” Ethan said. “Should that change?”

“Yes,” Noctem said. “The Throne must be returned to Dusk, so that he may take his rightful place in the Union.”

And there it was. A prophecy fulfilled. Ethan realized he wasn’t even breathing, and he had to force his face to remain passive. This was what he’d been warned of by his own reflection, by Strategis, another version of himself from a different, potential dimension. That Ethan Bishop had already gone through all of this, had already completed the Union.

Strategis hadn’t known all the details that Ethan had just heard. He’d never found Twilight or even known of Noctem, but he had reached Potentia. He’d even reached the Throne…and he’d restored Dusk to his rightful place.

That had been his mistake.

“You can only trust yourself,” Strategis had told him. “I’ll give you my power, I’ll even help you reach your goal–in my own way. But you must take The Throne. Only you. Promise me.”

Ethan had borne many burdens since being pulled to Nexum and becoming Chosen, but none so great as this. What Noctem couldn’t know, and likely wouldn’t believe, was that Dusk himself had become corrupted. The moment he’d retaken his power, he’d triggered the Union. Strategis had thought he’d won, that the world would finally be righted.

The world that came after was far worse than Potentia had ever been.

“We are nearly ready to transport you, as promised,” Noctem said. “I required Twilight’s calculations to overcome the shift in the dimensional tides. But soon, it will be time. Prepare yourself, Chosen. Your journey has nearly reached its end.”

Ethan nodded. Noctem was more correct than the god even knew. Ethan had been forced to rely on himself for years. The Union had practically become his personal quest, despite the billions of beings relying on its outcome. He would keep his promise, and finish it alone, even though Strategis had been forthcoming about one other terrible truth.

Taking The Throne, channeling that terrible power…would destroy him.

Emperor Gallow looked out across the vast ocean of the world that would be the death of his people. He stood alone on the observation deck, wishing for more quiet than the bridge provided, trying to lose himself in the swell of waves. Trying to pretend they were the silver seas of his homeland.

It wasn’t working.

He was presiding over the fall of his people, and he couldn’t pretend otherwise, not any longer.

There had been so many setbacks, all starting with what had seemed like the greatest thing to have happened to his people since they abandoned their home. Finding this world before the Union had taken place had felt like a sign, like it was finally time for his people to defeat the monsters that had ruined them, and take back control of their fate.

They’d found Twilight, and his Champions had feasted. Gallow had longed to taste that divine power himself, but it was not meant to be. He couldn’t leave this ship. He was connected to it on numerous levels, and it wouldn’t survive his absence. Being the Emperor came with many advantages, but even more responsibility.

Then his Champions died, one by one. They’d died fighting the fourth scourge, the great hydra and its army of fools. Gallow’s ship was brought down, so many of his best were lost. There were eighty-seven thousand of The People left alive, including the recently converted humans. Only seven were born on their homeworld, including himself. Seven.

And now, once again they’d lost the ability to reproduce. The monsters that had ravaged his people had done so again, leaving them truly impotent, and trapped on this world. Their ship and home, The Devorare lacked the power to break atmosphere, and was still too damaged to leave the system even were that not the case.

They would die here. It was only a matter of time.

But Gallow was old, and always had plans within plans. The breeding pits beneath the surface, the new conversion tanks the scientists were working on, and of course, the traitor. Yet for all that, he didn’t truly believe. Not anymore. He could taste their defeat, and knew this was the death throes of his people.

Bek had been waiting behind him for some time, respecting his Emperor’s desire for silence. But the head-scientist deserved better, and an Emperor had responsibilities, even as his people were wiped from existence.

“Speak, Bek,” he said, not turning around.

The scientist was clearly excited, as he raced through his news. “We’ve finally received confirmation from our agents on Nexum and Potentia,” he said, and Gallow did turn, despite himself.

“What have you learned?”

“The truth of the Union. The work of the scourge Dawn has already finished, and our own scouts have confirmed that Twilight is nearly ready as well. But Dusk is another matter entirely.”

“This…‘throne’,” Gallow supplied.

“Exactly, my Emperor. Our agents believe it truly exists. More than that, it is vacant.”

Gallow took an involuntary step forward. “Then it can be claimed?” he asked, not believing this could truly be possible.

“Not easily, but yes. The traitor can explain the rest.”

Gallow followed Bek to the laboratory. He knew the scientist was more than capable of explaining it all himself, but traitors were an entirely new concept for their race. It had never happened before, and the novelty demanded attention.

They walked the comfortingly dark halls until they reached the large area full of equipment that only Bek truly understood. Gallow passed countless experiments and scientists before finally arriving at a converted stasis tank. He could see the traitor within, nothing more than a single arm, upper torso, and head.

Bek rapped on the tank, and the eyes shot open. “Your Emperor would hear your words!” he screeched.

Jack Foster nodded vigorously. “Greetings, oh great one,” he began.

“Do not waste the Emperor’s time!” Bek commanded. “Tell him what you know!”

“Of course, of course. As you’re aware, I had access to the internal security of Atlantis. I couldn’t monitor it all at the time, but we’ve since decrypted it. More than that, my spy still lives, and has not been found out. She has been relocated to Atlantis, and serves the children of Twilight even now. I have learned much–I can still be of value, I–”

“Speak, traitor!” Bek demanded.

“Of course, of course,” Foster said. “The Throne, my Emperor. It’s better than we ever believed. It sits empty, waiting for someone to claim it. But there’s more! This Union has gone terribly wrong, and now Dusk’s power will have the greatest influence on the joined world. Whoever sits on that Throne will be able to create anything they wish!”

Gallow looked to Bek. “Limitations?” he asked.

“Few, if any,” Bek said excitedly. “There have been some efforts by Dawn, but they could be overridden. The Throne will give a single being total control over the finished product. It has to be you, my Emperor. You could change things! Change us! Restore us!”

Gallow was silent as he considered. “Is it so simple? Travel to the world and sit on this Throne?”

Bek slammed a fist against the tank again. Foster happily spoke up. “There is more, Emperor. To be eligible, one must find The Throne, as it is hidden. But more than that, the power of Potentia must be gathered together into one place, governed by a singular will.”

Gallow’s hopes fell. “I have no method to accomplish this. Even if I devoured every creature on that planet, the power would become my own. Our so-called gift purifies whatever we take.”

“There is a method,” Bek said, eyes greedy.

“It is my discovery!” Foster shrieked, but Bek just hammered on the tank once more.

The scientist moved to pull a large display closer to them, then brought up an image. “This is the solution to our problems,” Bek announced. “We’ll need to do something unconventional, but I’m confident it will work.”

Gallow looked closer. “Who is this…Prince Calevaro?”
