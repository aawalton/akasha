The C-52 banked gracefully through the cloud layer, the hum of its anti-gravity turbines a low, steady thrum against the cabin walls. Nyxia Black stared out the reinforced crystal window, watching the jagged peaks of Dragon Valley resolve through the mist. It felt like a lifetime since she had been here. The last time, she had been a student, an instructor, a pawn in a game played by older men. Now, she returned as a queen in all but name, carrying a death warrant in her hands.

The head of security, a sharp-eyed Dark Elf named Rian, leaned over from the opposite seat. He tapped a glowing point on his data-slate.

"We are entering the neutral zone, my Lady," Rian reported, his voice crisp. "Sensors detect multiple draconic flights patrolling the perimeter. They are not interfering, but they are tracking us."

Nyxia nodded, adjusting the heavy, silver-clasped satchel that held the declaration of war. "They know who we are. The silver lion on the tail is hard to miss. We stick to the itinerary. The landing pad nearest the Great Banyan. No deviations."

"Understood," Rian said, settling back into his seat. He looked tense. They all were.

The modified bomber touched down with a heavy, metallic sigh on the designated platform. The ramp lowered, and the scent of the valley—a mix of ozone, pine, and ancient stone—washed over them. Nyxia stepped out, flanked by Rian and two Mark IV-S Specter units holding position at the base of the ramp. She didn't need a heavy escort here. The sanctuary was guaranteed by the Dragon Kings. To bring an army to a diplomatic parley was an insult she wasn't about to commit.

Waiting for her at the edge of the platform was a figure she knew well. The Headmaster. He leaned on his gnarled wooden staff, his long silver beard blowing gently in the mountain breeze.

Nyxia approached, her posture rigid, ready for a formal, stiff greeting.

The Headmaster smiled, a warm, genuine expression that crinkled the corners of his ancient eyes. "Welcome back, Lady Wight."

Nyxia stumbled mid-step, her composure slipping for a fraction of a second. Lady Wight. The title felt strange, heavy, and undeniably real spoken aloud by someone else. She recovered quickly, offering a respectful nod.

"Headmaster. It is good to see you again," she replied, her voice steady despite the flush creeping up her neck.

"Walk with me, Nyxia," he said, turning toward the path that led to the Great Banyan. "We have a short journey before we reach the council chambers."

She fell into step beside him, the heavy satchel swinging slightly against her hip.

"Kaelen sent word ahead of your arrival," the Headmaster continued, his tone conversational, as if they were discussing the weather rather than impending global conflict. "He was... quite emphatic about your new title. He is a very proud father-in-law."

"He has much to be proud of," Nyxia said carefully. "His son has accomplished the impossible."

The Headmaster sighed, the sound carrying the weight of centuries. "Yes. He has. And that is what concerns me."

He stopped, turning to face her. His expression lost its warmth, replaced by a deep, weary sorrow.

"I have known Alarion since he was a boy," the Headmaster said quietly. "He was brilliant, yes, but he was also kind. He spent hours in the library reading about ancient clockwork toys. He loved his sister. He just wanted to learn, to build."

He looked toward the horizon, where the storm clouds of the Maelstrom Sea churned in the distance.

"The world took that boy and put him in a crucible. It burned away his home, his family—or so he thought—, and it replaced his kindness with a deep, cold darkness. He has built an empire out of grief, Nyxia. A machine that runs on vengeance."

Nyxia bristled defensively. "He is doing what he must to protect his people. To protect His Family."

"I know," the Headmaster said gently, holding up a hand. "I understand the reasons behind this war. Cinderfall broke the peace. They must answer for it. But when the dust settles, when the ash stops falling... the boy inside that armor is going to need a reason to put it down. He is going to need love and care to heal the parts of his soul that have turned to steel."

He looked her straight in the eye. "Keep him out of future wars, Nyxia. Or the machine will consume him entirely."

Nyxia swallowed hard, the weight of the Headmaster's words settling heavily on her shoulders. She remembered the boy in the courtyard, offering his hand, asking for a partner, not a weapon. She remembered the look in his eyes when he talked about feeding the starving peasants.

"I will," she promised, her voice a fierce whisper. "I swear it."

They reached the doors of the council chamber. Nyxia unlatched the satchel and drew out the heavy vellum scroll. The seal of the Silver Lion gleamed in the sunlight.

She pushed the doors open and walked inside to start a war.

…

Aboard the D-21, a destroyer serving as the vanguard of the 3rd Fleet, the order came through the encrypted quantum channel with a sharp, electronic chirp.

Authorization Confirmed. Target: Cinderfall Flagship. Fire at will.

The captain of the D-21 didn't hesitate. He slammed his hand down on the firing console.

Deep within the hull of the destroyer, magnetic coils whined, drawing massive amounts of power from the ship's localized fission reactor. The main battery, a sleek, rotating railgun mounted on the foredeck, didn't need to swivel clumsily into position. It didn't require agonizing calculations for wind speed or the unpredictable sway of the ocean. The targeting computer, linked directly to The Oracle, had already painted the Cinderfall flagship—a massive, gilded galleon sitting smugly in the center of the enemy formation—with a microscopic laser designator.

The railgun fired.

It wasn't a boom. It was a crack, a sound like a thunderbolt striking an anvil right next to your ear. The recoil shook the entire destroyer, rippling through the water and sending a shockwave across the surface.

A slug of charged adamantium, accelerated to a fraction of the speed of light, tore across the ocean. It covered the five-mile distance in the blink of an eye.

It struck the Cinderfall flagship dead center, right at the waterline.

There was no glorious exchange of broadsides. No boarding actions. The galleon simply exploded. The kinetic energy of the impact shattered the enchanted ironwood hull as if it were spun glass. The ship was ripped in half, a cloud of splintered wood, screaming men, and vaporized sea water erupting into the sky.

The sheer force of the detonation threw massive, jagged chunks of debris outward, smashing into the galleons anchored nearby. Masts snapped, hulls groaned, and buckled under the secondary impacts.

For ten long, agonizing seconds, the three gathered fleets just sat there, bobbing on the waves, trying to comprehend what had just happened. The pride of the Cinderfall navy had been deleted from existence by a single shot fired from beyond the horizon.

…

Aboard The Titan’s Fang, the Beast King gripped the wooden rail of his ship so hard his claws dug deep gouges into the petrified wood.

The elderly Wolf-kin shaman beside him swallowed hard, his ears pinned flat against his skull. "I... I thought they would be slow. All that metal. It must be for defense, I told myself. I thought they would have to close the distance."

"They don't need to get close," the Beast King rumbled, his amber eyes fixed on the distant, grey shapes of the 3rd Fleet. "They can kill us from where they sit."

A frantic buzzing sound drew their attention. The communication orb mounted on a pedestal near the helm flared with bright, urgent light. A heavily armored messenger rushed over, his face pale beneath his fur.

"My King!" the messenger shouted, reading a hastily scribbled note. "An urgent dispatch from the Dragon Valley ambassador. It was sent to all active military channels."

"Read it," the King commanded.

"The Cinderfall Hegemony is now an active warzone," the messenger read, his voice trembling. "House Wight has formally declared a war of annihilation against the Crown. All neutral parties are advised to vacate the territorial waters immediately."

The Bear-kin chief snorted, adjusting his grip on his axe. "A war? Over a bride? The fire-king must be fuming."

The messenger shook his head frantically. "There is no bride, Chief. The dispatch confirms... Lady Nyxia Black is already married. To Lord Alarion Wight."

The chieftains fell silent. They looked toward the shore, toward the looming, jagged silhouette of the Black Mage Tower, standing tall and ominous against the sky. It was the last line of defense Cinderfall would have looked to for support. Now, it was a dagger pointed at their backs.

Taking down a Tier 8 Mage Tower was a grinding, brutal process, similar to conquering a high-level dungeon. It required tens of thousands of lives, endless siege engines, and months of bloody attrition.

Cinderfall was pinned. The 3rd Fleet sat on the ocean, an impenetrable wall of steel, while the Black Tower blocked the inland retreat. They were caught between two apex predators.

"Turn the ship," the Beast King roared, his voice cutting through the panic. "All hands to the oars! Get us out of this cursed water! We are going home!"

…

On the bridge of The Axiom, Theramore lowered his binoculars, a look of profound disappointment settling over his scarred features.

"That was pathetic," he grunted.

He knew the math. He had sat in the academy classrooms and run the simulations. He knew a railgun slug traveling at that velocity carried enough kinetic energy to crack a fortress wall from miles away.

But seeing it in reality was different. It felt almost unfair. The Cinderfall flagship hadn't even had time to raise its magical wards.

"Commodore," Trisa spoke up, her fingers flying across her console. "The Cinderfall fleet is reacting. They are not retreating. They are attempting to reform a defensive line closer to the shore."

Theramore scoffed, a harsh, barking laugh. "Idiots! Absolute, barnacle-brained idiots! Their commander is completely incompetent. If I had just lost my flagship to a weapon I couldn't see, I'd be scattering my ships to the four winds to avoid a second strike. He’s clumping them together to make a bigger target!"

He slammed his hand on the tactical table. "Let's end their misery. The Lord Commander wants a beachhead secured, and I refuse to let a bunch of floating firewood slow us down. All destroyers, lock missile batteries. Fire a full volley. Stagger the trajectories."

He smiled, a wide, feral grin that showed too many teeth. This was what he had waited for. This was the moment the old world died.

"Make them dance, Trisa. Make them dance."

Across the vanguard line of the 3rd Fleet, the missile tubes on twenty-seven destroyers hissed open.

They launched simultaneously.

To the terrified onlookers in the Cinderfall fleet, and the rapidly retreating Beastkin, it looked as though the grey ships had suddenly caught fire. A massive, coordinated eruption of white smoke and blinding exhaust flames lit up the horizon.

Then, the projectiles appeared. Hundreds of sleek, guided missiles streak across the sky.

They didn't fly straight. They jinked, spiraled, and altered course in mid-air, a chaotic swarm of death designed to confuse the tracking spells of the Cinderfall mages.

The Imperial fleet, anchored miles away, immediately flared its golden defensive wards to maximum power, anticipating stray fire. They watched in horrified awe as the carnage unfolded.

The missiles slammed into the Cinderfall line. The sea caught fire.

Galleons were lifted entirely out of the water by the force of the plasma detonations, breaking apart mid-air and raining burning debris down upon their sister ships. Wards flickered and failed under the relentless, overlapping blasts. The ocean boiled, choked with smoke, splintered wood, and the screaming remnants of a proud navy.

The 3rd Fleet didn't wait for the smoke to clear. The massive grey ships began to move forward, cutting straight through the burning wreckage. The adamantium hulls of the destroyers barely registered the impact as they crushed the debris beneath their bows, relentless and unfeeling.

…

On the shore, the Cinderfall ground forces were scrambling into position. The coastal defense garrisons, heavily depleted by the recent conscription efforts, were lining up their heavy ballistae and charging their siege runes, panic evident in every shouted order.

A squadron of Griffin Knights, a rapid-response aerial unit stationed near the coast, took to the sky. They were a fraction of the size of the annihilated Garuda wing, a token force meant for scouting and light skirmishes, but today, they were all that stood between the sea and the mainland. They had heard the rumors. They knew what had happened to their brothers in the capital. They knew the sky had rained fire and the Garuda were no more.

But as they circled the coastline, their spotters strained their eyes and saw a glimmer of hope. The skies above the encroaching grey fleet were empty. There were no sleek, black metal birds tearing through the clouds. No trails of blue-white fire.

The enemy's aerial assassins, the ones who had slaughtered the Garuda, were not here. They were facing only the ships.

The Griffin Commander signaled his men, his voice tight with a desperate, reckless courage over the comms-stones. "Their sky is bare! The metal ships are large and cumbersome. We have the advantage of speed and agility. We will employ hit-and-run tactics. Dive through the smoke, strike the decks, and retreat before their mages can track us!"

They would stall the invasion. They would buy time for the King’s reinforcements to arrive. They would avenge the Garuda and restore the honor of the Hegemony sky.

They formed a tight V-formation, diving headlong toward the thick, oily smoke billowing from the ruined remains of their own navy. Their griffins screeched, lances leveled and crackling with magic, ready to strike the first blow against the iron monsters below.

They tore through the upper layer of the smoke, the grey, flat decks of the massive enemy carriers just beginning to resolve in the gloom. They pushed their mounts faster, diving straight toward the heart of the 3rd Fleet.
