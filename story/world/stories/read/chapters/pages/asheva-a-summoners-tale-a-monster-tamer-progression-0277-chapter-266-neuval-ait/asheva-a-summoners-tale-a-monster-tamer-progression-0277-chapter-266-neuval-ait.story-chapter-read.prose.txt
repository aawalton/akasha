Fire, Ice, Blood, Mystic, and Dark—the five elements that Ewan could call upon surged beyond normalcy of Morinfair, where the water element ruled, and their vexed rampage tested his affinity when Stormfalcon neared its destination. Among the five, only mystic behaved before him, bending their knees to his Endued-level affinity, and they eased off and settled in his premises on his command.

Neuval Ait, the island they headed for, cleared the horizon in the steep hours of the night, the peak of the waxing moons introducing them with their cascading moonlight. Yet, even before the face-to-face prelude, the island blasted them with frigid gusts and ghastly whispers, chilling the nightly warmth of Flamecrest—the months of growth. It was an ice field, with not a hint of green or brown marring its matted snowy beauty. And the waters around it had frozen in its awe.

….

Stormfalcon, Top Deck.

“Did you guys feel that?” Ewan asked, squinting at the blurry white strip of the island up ahead. The barrier of the natural rune parted its territory from the ocean, Morinfair once again lost its dominance of the area. But the deviation of the natural rhythm of the Anima concerned him more—even the natural runes could not overstep their bounds and stir the pattern of other elements.

“Dark-Anima is going nuts here, Boss,” Kidd said, popping a vein on his neck when he strained to control the Anima. They listened to him but almost grumbled. “And that ghost-like whispers, it sounds eerie, Willy doesn’t like it.”

“Those whispers are probably the trait of this rune,” Stefan said, his ears twitching. “Ewan, all the elements are raging.”

“Vraan, should I go and check the island out?” Lance asked as Stormfalcon halted at a safe distance from the island, rocking with the waves in the shallow water. When its anchors dropped and plunged into the sandy bed, the deafening clangs of its chains blared away and shattered the tranquility of the night.

“Will there be any treasure?” Kidd’s eyes glittered.

“No,” Ewan said, taking out his journal to cement his guess. “So much for stealth,” he muttered when the bold sentences in the book validated him, and the tug of the hub-connector also backed it up. “The hub stratum is either nearby or probably passed by here recently, and it stirred all the Anima in the area. The surge should settle down in a few years on its own,” he said, keeping the book away. “Nana, confirm it with your journal too, it should be in there.”

“So, no water rune again.” Nana sighed and checked her book, nodding with a hum when she verified it. “Do you want to head to the hub?” she asked. “What do we do with the ice rune though? Exchange the information for another rune’s location?”

“None of us can use it,” Kidd said. “Selling its location for the rune we want is the only way to profit from this. The guy who sold us this location only felt the surge in Water-Anima in the area, which is because of the hub…” His words slowly faded away as the absurdity of the situation bared itself.

“So, he only felt the surge in Water-Anima because of the hub, yet we found an ice rune in the exact location,” Lance said. “Is this luck or someone’s design?”

“Ewan?” Nana grabbed Ewan’s sleeve and looked at him with a worried curve in her brows.

Ewan chuckled. “I did not use it,” he said, patting her hand. “I won't use it unless it’s absolutely necessary, don’t worry.” He looked at the island again. “I’ll admit defeat if this is someone’s scheme because I can't see a pattern in gifting us the rune, unless it’s tampered with which is unlikely, so I’m going to chuck this up to sheer luck. We haven’t had any since ages, it’s about time the lady smiled upon us too.”

“So, off to the hub?” Stefan asked. “If we sell the rune’s information there, it should be more profitable. We’ll have a better chance of trading it for the location of a healing water rune, hopefully, in this area only.”

“We have zero experience with this, so we can't estimate how far the hub is now, not even from the connector’s pull,” Ewan said. “What do you guys say? Do you still want to go? It might take us years on the way, maybe longer.”

“You’re almost at the edge of the first surge,” Nana said, holding his hand, interlocking the fingers. “If you break through during the journey, will there be any issue?”

“Shouldn’t be,” Ewan said. “Minor breakthroughs in each step are mostly quite forgiving on the disturbance, I just need to be able to focus and concentrate. And our Stormfalcon flies pretty smoothly even in the middle of a hurricane, no?” He chuckled, looking at the four, and they gulped. “At best, we’ll stop for a few days when I break through.”

“I once got snapped in half when we met a towering wave,” Stefan murmured.

“I got thrown off the deck once in a tornado, and he was just cackling his ass off,” Kidd muttered along, flaring his nose at Ewan.

Ewan laughed. “Those were fun times,” he said. “So, what do you say? Head to the hub next?”

“There’s nothing urgent in this area that we need to stay for, apart from looking for the vestiges maybe. But it’s not a task of a day or two, so we can delay it,” Lance said. “I say we go for the hub, Vraan.”

“If we miss it now, it might drift far off,” Nana said. “We’re lucky it traveled to this area, let’s head for it. I really want a healing water rune.” She stressed her words, puckering her lips.

“My vats of Midnight Blend wine are almost ready, we can start selling the bottles along with the potions,” Stefan said. “Its effect is simple and straightforward; it should sell decently.”

“Depends on how the market on the hub is like, with the new restrictions of invitation and all,” Kidd said. “It might be better to target the taverns in Fallsard instead, those drunkards will lick the last drop of it.”

“Hmm, we can re-evaluate once we get there,” Stefan said.

“We both have a lot of Novas stuck there too,” Nana said. “We should withdraw them all.”

“It’s decided then,” Ewan said. “Nyte, fire the thrusters. Stefan, link the connector, use its tug to manage our direction. The rest of you, get busy, it’ll be a long journey.” The chains hauled the anchors up, but the howls of the thrusters smothered its ringing tolls this time, and Stormfalcon sailed for the hub, still kissing the Morinfair, as the icy island said its bye with the rush of a biting breeze.
