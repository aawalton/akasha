Music in the forest was wrong by itself. This was a forest, not a party. And right now a melody was flowing through it, far too carefree for a place where any tree could hide something that wanted to tear you apart.

After the music came another sound: voices. Several people were singing in chorus. Not well, but loudly and with obvious enjoyment. The singing sounded so cheerful and inspired that Sakura truly doubted reality for a moment.

Sakura and Gilbert looked at each other at the same time.

She was the first to ask, "What is that?.."

Pampli tensed too. His ears twitched, his head lifted higher, and his muscles tightened warily. Sakura immediately placed a calming palm on his side.

"Hide," she whispered. "Behind the trees. Quickly."

Pampli snorted in displeasure, clearly hating the very idea of pretending to be a huge bush again. But he still obeyed. The enormous dark body slipped between the trees with surprising agility and almost dissolved into the shadows, hiding behind trunks and low branches. Even now, it amazed Sakura every time how quietly a huge obsidian beast could move when he wanted to.

She looked back at Gilbert. He had already crouched slightly and moved forward, toward the narrow forest path visible between the trees. Sakura followed him. She tried to step as softly as possible. Sakura and Gilbert came closer to the source of the sound, but did not show themselves right away. They stopped behind a wide trunk, where it was easy to watch unnoticed.

Sakura still had not gotten used to the fact that her life now consisted of this kind of spying and survival, of fearing every unfamiliar sound and voice. The life of someone always on the run and always waiting for the next blow. Was she really made for a life like this?

Meanwhile, the song grew louder.

Then people appeared between the trunks.

A small group, six or seven people. They walked along the path without hurry, as if they were strolling not through a dangerous forest in Veyr, but through a fair on a peaceful holiday. One was playing something like a lute, though the instrument looked unfamiliar. It had a long narrow neck, a dark rounded base, and metal pendants that jingled softly as he moved. Two of them sang along with such feeling that it seemed they wanted every obsidian monster in the forest to come out to the sound of the song and burst into applause. Another man carried a bundle of bright bags on his back while tapping the rhythm on his thigh. They laughed, quietly picked up the chorus, and one of the men had clearly already drunk more than was required for a good mood.

Sakura frowned.

"They’re a little too... relaxed?"

Gilbert, without taking his eyes off the path, gave a small shrug.

"Free Wanderers."

"Who?"

"Free Wanderers," he repeated quietly. "Vagrants, traders, musicians, sometimes all sorts of frauds. Always a little touched in the head. Usually believe the road loves the bold, and death is a matter of mood. They often die too quickly on their journeys."

Sakura looked at the procession again, and this time she noticed more interesting details. Their clothes were brighter and more chaotic than those of ordinary travelers. Their cloaks were often covered in ribbons, trinkets, amulets, and patches. Giant colorful scarves hung around their necks, and there were feathers. A lot of feathers. Feathers in their hair, sewn onto their clothes, even attached to their bags. Flowers often appeared in those feather arrangements too, dried or coated in hardened resin.

"And they just walk around this dangerous forest singing songs?"

"Exactly."

"Are they normal?"

"No," Gilbert answered very calmly. "But they sometimes have useful things. Rare ones too."

Sakura narrowed her eyes, studying the people more closely. They were carefree and cheerful, like children. One woman had a bundle of small jars and leather pouches hanging over her shoulder, knocking against each other with every step. Another girl was riding a large black horse. One of the wanderers was pulling a low handcart on creaking wheels. Behind them trundled a larger wagon drawn by a donkey. It was covered with fabric embroidered with strange symbols, and beneath it Sakura could make out crates, bottles, and some other junk that could contain anything. As Gilbert had just said, useful things, or even rare ones. Or maybe just junk. Judging by the look of it, Sakura would have bet on the second.

"They look like they either know something we don’t," Sakura muttered, "or they just haven’t realized how lucky they’ve been to stay alive so far."

This time Gilbert did smirk.

"One doesn’t exclude the other."

Meanwhile, the song changed to a new one. The lute player sang loudly, repeating the same lines, obviously the chorus. The others picked up his words, still a little chaotically. There was something strange and almost absurd about it after all the prisons, laboratories, slime, escapes, and conversations about the Castle of Justice.

“Let the road take us on,Through the bright and through the rain.If we live to see the dawn,We’ll be laughing by the fire.Hey, step light while you breathe,Drink to the sun after rain.If death comes walking at our side,Let him keep up with the refrain!”

Sakura didn’t want to admit it, but she caught herself thinking that the sound of other people’s laughter and singing made something in her chest feel a little lighter. Almost like a reminder that someone in this world still existed who did not live only by fear and pursuit.

Then everything suddenly went wrong. As if the road itself had decided these insane singers had had enough fun for today.

The large black horse walking beside the wagon suddenly jerked his head sharply, as if something had hurt him. At first, Sakura did not understand what had happened. She only saw the animal shy to the side, strike the ground nervously with one hoof, and throw up his neck.

Then the horse truly bucked. It happened with such force that the girl sitting on him cried out and flew down, barely managing to let go of the reins. She hit the path shoulder-first, rolled through the dry leaves, and the horse was already lunging again. Sakura did not understand what was wrong with him, but the horse was crazed and blind with pain. He struck out with his front hooves, nearly crushing the fallen rider’s leg.

"Hold him!"

"Back! Back, you idiot!"

"Get Ria up! Get her up!"

The singing cut off instantly, replaced by chaotic panic.

The path exploded with shouting, neighing, and the clatter of overturned things. The wanderers tangled together, clearly trying to help the fallen rider and calm the horse at the same time. The animal kept panicking. He slammed his side into the wagon so hard that one of the crates flipped onto the ground, spilling its contents. The horse jerked again, and this time struck one of the wheels with a hoof hard enough that the wagon creaked and tilted sideways. The donkey brayed in terror, but the wanderer at the reins managed to hold him in place.

"What’s wrong with him?" Sakura breathed.

Gilbert narrowed his eyes, looking not at the horse itself, but slightly above him.

"Birds," he said shortly.

Sakura followed his gaze and only now noticed them. Small, almost invisible in the gloom, they darted around the horse’s head and neck in short nervous bursts. They were definitely too fast and too small for ordinary forest birds. Like hybrids of hummingbirds and bats, only with very long sharp beaks. One of them darted right at the animal’s face, and the horse panicked again, tossing his head and wheezing wildly.

"Someone scare them off!"

"I’m trying!"

"He’s going to break everything!"

That was exactly what the horse was doing. He kept losing his mind and wrecking the whole procession. The reins slipped out of hands, people only got in each other’s way, shouted too loudly, and that only terrified the horse more. The animal did not understand that they were trying to calm him. He only felt pain, noise, and frantic movement.

Sakura understood that within seconds, and she also realized that if this continued, the horse would trample either the fallen girl or one of the other wanderers. Or maybe all of them at once, together with their wagon and rare goods.

She had already stepped forward when Gilbert cursed quietly, but very clearly.

"Sakura, no."

But she could not stay aside. She simply could not, just like back then with Lir’s father, when they had found him dying in his own house.

Besides, Sakura seemed to have just discovered something new about herself. While everyone else saw a dangerous, huge, crazed beast, for some reason she saw only an animal in pain. In stupid, terrible pain. And because of that, everything inside her had already moved before reason had time to build any decent arguments against the idea.

Sakura stepped out from behind the tree straight onto the path.

"Hey!" one of the wanderers shouted.

Gilbert cursed out loud this time and stepped after her, clearly ready to pull her out from under the hooves at any moment.

But Sakura was no longer looking at him or at the people.

Only at the horse.

He was enormous. Dark, wet with sweat, with flared nostrils and maddened eyes. Red points left by the predatory beaks were already swelling on the animal’s neck. The horse’s muscles twitched nervously. He tore at the ground with his hooves and would not let anyone come close. Even though the birds were almost gone from around him now, that did not calm his panic.

"Everyone back!" Sakura shouted, surprising even herself with how firm her voice sounded. "Don’t touch him! And quiet! Please, quiet!"

The wanderers stared at her as if a peace-loving obsidian monster had just walked out of the bushes. But there must have been something in her tone, because they obeyed, even if reluctantly.

The horse jerked his head, turning sharply toward her, and Sakura felt her heart slam against her ribs. One more step, and he could knock her down so hard that only a cautionary tale would remain.

But she did not retreat.

Sakura raised both hands with her palms forward, slowly, without sudden movements. She spoke to the horse quietly and evenly, almost in a whisper.

"It’s all right... quiet... quiet... I’m not your enemy... that’s enough... no one is holding you... no one is hurting you..."

At that moment, something shifted softly inside Sakura. It did not feel like activating another skill of the Obsidian Hierophant. Rather, it was something passive and gentle. She felt the horse’s state too clearly, as if she had connected to him. As if a transparent thread had stretched between her and the animal for a second.

Then Sakura felt the horse’s pain and panic even more strongly. She even felt burning points near her face and on her neck, as if the birds had stung not only him, but her too.

Sakura understood clearly that the horse did not want to kill anyone. He simply did not know where to escape from all of this.

She sucked in a shaky breath, and in that same second another memory flashed through her mind.

It was not this world. Not Veyr. Something else.

A field.

A real, green, warm field from her past life. Wind in the tall grass and sunlight falling on her back. A warm horse muzzle near her shoulder. A calm step beside her, the rustle of a bridle, a hot, drooling breath into her palm. Sakura was walking slowly in that memory, almost happy, and beside her was a large strong animal that wanted nothing either, except a simple walk and quiet. Sakura did not remember the horse’s name or the name of that place. Instead, there were only feelings of calm, trust, and comfort.

She grabbed onto those feelings like a lifeline. And pushed them forward through her strange, new, still poorly understood ability.

The horse sharply threw up his head, then froze. His muscles were still trembling, his nostrils were flaring, his chest heaving. But he had stopped jerking, struggling, and panicking. He only looked at Sakura with wide eyes and breathed heavily, as if for the first time in those terrifying seconds, he had realized where he was.

"That’s it..." Sakura whispered. "It’s all right. Quiet. It’s already..."

Another stinging bird darted toward him, but this time one of the wanderers managed to wave his cloak sleeve, and the gray creature veered aside.

The horse shuddered. Sakura felt a fresh burst of anxiety and immediately stepped closer, almost right up to his muzzle. It was stupidly risky, and she knew it. Judging by the grim silence behind her and the stare burning into her back, Gilbert knew it too. But stopping now would mean breaking the fragile thread that had only just appeared.

"It’s all right. I’m here," she said quietly. "That’s it, good boy. Enough."

She touched his neck very slowly, and the horse endured the contact. He exhaled heavily into her face. It was the familiar smell of grass and horse.

The horse was truly calming down now. Slowly, panic left his body, as if water were draining out of melting ice. His head lowered a little. The tremor was still there, but it was no longer frantic.

Everyone around them remained shocked into silence, watching.

Sakura stood beside the enormous animal and stroked his neck with careful movements, still not fully believing that it had worked.

Behind her, someone breathed out very quietly.

"Well, damn me..."

Another voice, a woman’s, still shaking after the fall, whispered, "He... calmed down?"

Sakura did not turn around.

"Yes," she answered, still looking only at the horse. "He is now."

Only then did she let herself lift her eyes.

The wanderers were looking at her as if they could not decide who stood before them: a mad girl, a forest witch, or simply a person who understood animals very well. A little off to the side stood Gilbert, grim and ready for anything, but, it seemed, also slightly impressed, even though he was trying not to show it.
