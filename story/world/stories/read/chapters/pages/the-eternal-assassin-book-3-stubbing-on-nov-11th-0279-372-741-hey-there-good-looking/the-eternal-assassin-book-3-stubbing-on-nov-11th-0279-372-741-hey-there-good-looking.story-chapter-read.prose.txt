The moment Crescent removed the lich’s head from her body, the flames winked out in her eyes, and the pulsing red light of her staff flickered and died. The sphere of water they were suspended in crashed down to join the rest of the lake as whatever magical effect that had formed a crater in the lake’s surface and kept it from filling in on itself came to an end. Asher was tumbled and thrown around as if he were in a giant wash cycle, struggling to maintain his sense of direction as to what was up and what was down. He may have succeeded in killing the lich going after Poltar, but that didn’t mean he could celebrate just yet.

First he had to retrieve his void dagger before it sank to the bottom of the lake.

Already he could feel the Eternal Mark he’d placed on the void dagger’s hilt starting to wear away, the skill in too-close proximity to the sliver of void to remain stable. Before he lost it completely, he swam down with every fiber of his being, sending out a silent prayer to whoever was listening when he spotted the dagger still slowly spinning in the water. If it had been angled straight down it probably would have been halfway to the bottom of the lake already, but as it currently was, the hilt was doing an excellent job of slowing it down.

Ever so carefully, seeing as he didn’t want to accidentally erase his fingers, Asher used Distortion to reach out and grabbed the dagger by the hilt. His greatest trump card secured, he placed it back into its abyssal sheath, mentally thanking Helpha yet again for being such a skilled smith.

The moment his dagger was back on his person, he focused on his Eternal Mark back in Poltar and Recalled to Sefta’s house once more. When the world stopped shifting and he found himself standing on solid ground rather than the splintered remains of the city, he let out a sigh of relief. Part of him had been terrified that he hadn’t taken out the lich fast enough, but it seemed as if everything had gone at least loosely according to plan.

“Keep back from the city edges!” a powerful voice called out, and Asher turned just in time to spot a large, muscular man with what looked like a pure mithral trident of all things blast himself off one of the docks. The man transformed into a living missile, shooting straight through a sea serpent that had breached the edge of the water and was looming over the city itself. Even if he hadn’t noticed the sea serpent crest sewn into the back of his shirt, or the shocking resemblance the man bore with Sefta, the mithral trident was a pretty good indicator of who he had just stumbled upon.

It looked like the head of the Malrron family wasn’t just going to take a sea serpent invasion lying down.

And he wasn’t the only one. There were dozens of powerful warriors repeating his orders while they ran around the perimeter of the city, ensuring civilians were sticking to their homes as they grouped up and forced back sea serpents each time a new one breached the water. They worked like a well-oiled machine, and Asher could only look on in awe as he realized there really wasn’t anything for him to do here. A series of explosions from off in the distance had to be Moxy going to town on some of the sea serpents, and Asher snorted at the confirmation that the warrior was keeping herself busy.

Well, if the lich is down and Poltar is in good hands…

Time to see who needs a little help.

Ducking back into Sefta’s house, Asher sat down and focused on his Eternal Marks. The plan had been simple enough. After everyone had their assignments, Asher had made sure each of the other four teams had one of his Eternal Marks with them. The goal for the rest of the coalition was to protect the different cities that were under attack and save as many lives as possible.

Asher’s goal was to kill the Council of Death.

He’d been designated as the floater, which meant it was his job to warp around and help out where necessary and do his damndest to finish off the liches wherever they could find them. Seeing as these remaining few liches had been completely hidden before this surprise attack, they couldn’t let this opportunity go to waste. Somewhere among the remaining four liches was Lethinal, the supposed leader of the entire Council, at least according to Zygrul. While Asher was determined to eradicate the entire bunch of liches and wouldn’t be satisfied until he had, he knew it was critical that they remove the true head of the snake above all others. Kroult’s own story showed that the Council wasn’t above pressing their finger on the scale and helping less-morally-inclined wizards take the first steps into lichdom. Unless they wanted the Council to come back one day, Lethinal had to be dealt with.

According to Donvath, Juhstain and Dormaul were the other two cities that had been destroyed almost instantly prior to the High Prince rewinding time, so Asher focused his attention there. He wasn’t able to see too much of the space surrounding his Eternal Marks, but a quick glance over at the mark he’d placed on Rulfar showed that the bloodhound was still fighting. Rulfar’s gaze was locked on something far larger than himself, and he was roaring as he batted falling rocks out of the sky with his great axe. Seeing as Groln was a greater power and had already proven he could hold his own against colossal opponents from when he’d tackled The Giant, Asher decided to help the Eight Crests’ team first.

One flex of Recall later, and Asher popped into existence beside Mako. Scaring the hell out of the spearman and nearly getting gutted for doing so.

“Asher!” Mako shouted, his eyes widening as he lunged forward regardless. “Get down!”

Not missing a beat, Asher slipped into the astral, blinking at the shining spear that was stabbed straight through where his heart had been moments before. Glancing over his shoulder, Asher’s own eyes widened as he spotted the man who had so rudely tried to kill him.

Mako. A second Mako. This one with a faint, violet sheen surrounding his entire body.

Asher could only stare at the clone in confusion, before a wayward blast of electricity tore his eyes off to the side. There he spotted Cora fighting her own clone, the two battle-hardened warriors doing everything in their power to electrocute the other with powerful blasts from their fists. Jameston was standing a bit farther away, locked in a silent battle with his own glowing clone as the two of them presumably tried to liquify the other’s brains.

“We managed to disrupt the ritual to destroy the mountain, but the lich created these copies of us before we could put him down!” Mako explained, his words transforming into an attack of their own as his voice blasted outward in a sonic strike. In response, the glowing Mako conjured up some sort of shield made from smoke, the soft material absorbing the sonic attack and keeping him safe. “I don’t know what they are, but they’re not flesh and blood! We’ve been landing attacks, but we can’t seem to hurt them! But don’t worry about us, the lich is probably repairing the ritual and still planning to collapse the mountain! If he finishes…”

Mako had to stop the explanation as creeping tendrils of smoke blasted out from the smoke wall, snaking across the ground and going for his legs. In response, Mako’s spear began glowing a brilliant yellow before he swiped it across the tendrils, severing them right before he could be grabbed.

As the fight continued, Asher maintained his position within the astral, watching for a moment as he confirmed what Mako said to be true. While the clones seemed to contain the same elements and skills as the trio, physically, they didn’t move around quite as well. It was almost as if they had the skills and the knowledge, but not the muscle memory and instincts that came from years of fighting. As if proving this, Mako managed to land a deadly spear thrust and tried to remove the clone’s head from its body. But rather than flesh and blood, violet light spilled out from the wound and the clone leapt back, the hole in its neck vanishing instantly before it answered with its own sonic attack.

Clones of pure tritessence, Asher realized. It wasn’t the first time he’d seen constructs of pure tritessence, or even battled against them. The Head’s ten Knuckles, the master shadow assassins that carried out their bidding, had been made from tritessence as well. Back then, he’d been forced to drag all of the constructs into the astral realm with him in order to get them to deplete their own bodies and eventually fade away, but he had a far more simple way of dealing with them now.

Mako and Cora’s clones were leaping around and constantly moving as they battled their flesh-and-blood counterparts, but one of the three clones was standing completely still. Deciding that was as good a place to start as any, Asher returned to the material realm and sprinted over toward Jameston. The clone spotted him coming at the last minute as it tore its gaze away from Jameston and threw up a hand toward Asher, but the battering ram that struck his mind wasn’t nearly strong enough to break through Complete Resilience in just a split second. Realizing this far too late, the clone changed tactics, its body taking on a metallic sheen as it used Jameston’s Steel element to transform itself into what looked like a living statue.

Which did absolutely nothing as Crescent sliced through the construct’s neck, the masterwork mithral blade easily cutting through the thin layer of steel covering the clone’s flesh.

The moment fake-Jameston’s head was separated from its neck, the entire clone erupted into a brilliant swarm of glowing violet lights that quickly faded out of existence. Jameston shook his head and looked at Asher in confusion, and Asher decided not to waste time.

“My attacks can cut tritessence directly, I can kill them! Help me with the others!”

Thankfully, Jameston was quick on the uptake, and he nodded before joining him as they raced back to the others. While Mako had instructed Asher to forget about them and go straight after the lich, Asher had decided he’d have a better chance at taking the ritual master down if he had an elite team of realm wanderers fighting at his side. He’d seen first hand just what the Eight Crests’ team was capable of, and there was no telling what other traps or tricks a ritual-focused lich might have protecting himself.

With Jameston’s help, the remaining two clones were taken out easily enough. Asher thought it strange that the clones made from tritessence were even susceptible to the Mind element in the first place, but he wasn’t going to question their good fortune. While Jameston held the clones in place, Asher dispatched them both with quick thrusts of his daggers, slicing through their dense tritessence and disrupting their forms.

“No time for thank you’s, we need to stop the lich!” Mako said once his copy had fallen. “This way, the ritual was right in the center of—”

Mako’s head fell from his body in a gleaming flash of blue metal. It happened so fast Asher’s brain didn’t even seem to comprehend what was going on. More by reflex than anything, his second-tier of Comprehend kicked in and the world slowed to a crawl. As his mental facilities were separated from the horror and agony quickly bubbling up through his chest at the sight of the realm wanderer’s confused face, the man’s head not even having hit the ground just yet, Asher took in the monster responsible for killing one of the few people he had the pleasure of calling his friend.

Himself.
