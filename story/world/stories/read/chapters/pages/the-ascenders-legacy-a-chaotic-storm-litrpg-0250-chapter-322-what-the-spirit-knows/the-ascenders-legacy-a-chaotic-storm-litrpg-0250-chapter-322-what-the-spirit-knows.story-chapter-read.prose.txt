“You are about to change the course of the sect forever.”

The words of the First Seat hung heavy in the air as Aodhán, Crixis, and the other specials stood before the assembled High-Elders, whose combined presence pressed down against them all like a suffocating blanket.

Beside him, Crixis trembled, knees buckling from the strain of standing upright until Aodhán shielded him with his own aura—a feat that didn’t make things any better for his recovering spirit. But the High Elders had leashed their presence as much as they could. It was up to them to withstand it.

“We knew there were words hidden within the chaos of the Origin Plane.” The First Seat continued quietly. “One cannot advance so high without coming to that realization. But to filter out the noise so cleanly as this interface of yours obviously does is a feat even I am not capable of. This interface will change that for everyone—from the outer to the elder sect—and that is a gift we cannot thank you enough for.”

Aodhán bowed. “I’m just glad I could contribute to the sect.”

“And so are we,” High-Elder Mystral responded, smiling down pleasantly at him. “When High-Elder Zarxan brought you to the sect just a few days ago, I doubt any of us could have seen this coming. It is a pleasant surprise, which is why we have decided to add one more thing to your list of rewards.”

Aodhán glanced at High-Elder Zarxan, and Zarxan smiled. “I think you know the gravity of the knowledge you’ve just given us, but I doubt you fully understand its ramifications. Not only will this turn the tide of the war drastically, but it will also triple the power of the sect in the next year. That is worth more than a seat at Elder Gumi’s workshop and a couple thousand sect points. You deserve something better, and after a lot of deliberations, we’ve decided to give you one-time access to the elder pavilion—a treasure house where the most precious cultivation resources are being kept. You will have the chance to pick three things alone, but I assure you, if you pick right, those three treasures will be enough to change the course of your life forever.”

Aodhán tried—he really tried—but he couldn’t contain his excitement at the prospect of additional rewards. He could already imagine it: a pavilion filled with Epic and Mythic ranked treasures.

Maybe even blessed ranked treasures, and with the other treasures he could buy himself with the hundred thousand sect points, he was looking at a definite uptick of five tiers and at least one seal.

He smiled widely, his heart racing with excitement as he bowed deeply. "Thank you so much, High Elders. I really appreciate this."

Behind him, Seamus and Sophran grumbled jealously, but Aodhán's smile only widened as High Elder Zarxan continued speaking.

"You're welcome." He turned his gaze to Elder Vasmos, who stood beside them. "The project will begin tomorrow, and you get to test out the first interface created. Unfortunately, I couldn't convince Elder Gumi to take you on as an apprentice for the project."

"I suspected that would be the case." Elder Vasmos smiled sadly, then bowed, her expression brightening. "I'm very grateful, High Elders. Thank you so much."

The First Seat smiled. "You deserve it, Vasmos. You've done exceedingly well in your assigned role. This is our thanks." He turned to Aodhán and nodded. "Thank you, Aodhán. Your contribution to the sect will not be forgotten in a hurry."

Aodhán bowed again, unable to hold back his excitement at the thought of the pavilion. They soon finalized all the necessary details, and a few minutes later, the First Seat dismissed them. "Every other detail can be finalized without your presence, so you may all go. Zarxan's aide will see to your reward, Aodhán."

Elder Luseph shot him a look somewhere between amusement and disdain and promptly led them out of the chamber, all except Elder Vasmos, who was asked to stay behind for something else. The sudden absence of the suffocating pressure left them all gasping, but mostly Crixis, who was coughing and sweating buckets despite Aodhán's help.

Chuckling, Aodhán handed him a jug of crystal-clear water and tapped his back gently. "I told you—you didn't need to come."

Crixis shook his head, still coughing. "I couldn't miss an opportunity to see all six High Elders in their element like this. Even my parents have never had that honor."

"It certainly is an impressive sight," Sophran said from beside them, and Seamus grunted.

"Not quite as impressive as the reward Mr. Dragon here is walking away with." He groaned sadly. "Oh, the urge to steal your skin and wear it."

Mirelle chuckled, nudging Seamus on the shoulder. "Oh, you insecure little baby. I always knew you hated your own skin—just admit it."

Seamus glared at her. "I don't hate my skin."

"Well, you certainly don't like it enough if you want to wear Aodhán's." She glanced at Aodhán and smirked. "Though I must say, he does have very impressive skin."

"Okay, dial it back," Aodhán quickly interjected before things could escalate any further. "Besides, I would argue that you guys are walking away with a steal of your own. Once those interfaces are completed, you'll no doubt each get one. Which means—"

"We'll be getting our familiars soon," Darien grinned and shook his head. "Nymera and Dravion will hate themselves for missing this."

Sophran chuckled. "I already texted them, but they're inside a rift, so I doubt they've seen it." His smile widened into a daydream. "I hope they don't return until we've all gained our familiars. It'll be so great to rub it in their faces."

That caused an eruption of smiles on all their faces until a voice popped the daydream like a knife. "As lovely as it is to daydream outside the elder hall, I believe it's time you all descended back to where you came from."

It was obviously Elder Luseph, and though he’d made the statement with a nice smile, he couldn’t have made the special sect sound any more like an insult if he’d tried. His smile widened when they all turned to stare at him. “Elder Vasmos is currently busy, so I've been asked—much to my delight—to escort you all back down. Lest you stray."

"Elder Luseph," Mirelle spluttered, mouth agape. "That's an insult."

Elder Luseph snorted. "It is not an insult. It is a fact. Tell me honestly—do you not all want to wander around the elder sect like lost children? If I were to leave you here without an escort, would you go straight down or would you go sightseeing?”

Silence.

Elder Luseph grinned. "That's what I thought." He summoned a platform of violet electricity and gestured for them to board. "Come on then. Humor me."

Reluctantly, the specials piled onto the construct, looking thoroughly offended. There was, however, one matter still unaddressed, and just as Aodhán opened his mouth to raise it, Elder Luseph suddenly remembered.

"Ah!" He pulled a black metallic slip from his robes and extended it to Aodhán. "This is a passkey to Elder Gumi's runic institute. If you're going to be a member of her team, you'll need it."

Aodhán accepted the passkey gratefully. "Thank you, Elder Luseph."

He caught a flash of genuine respect in the elder's gaze as he nodded. "You're welcome, Aodhán. As for your sect points, those should have already been updated."

Aodhán channeled a strand of willpower into the ring and smiled at what he found.

Available Sect Points: 101,995 SP.

He and Crixis had spent 5 SP on their jaunt to the market yesterday for Crixis’s mother’s recipe. The thought reminded him that although he wasn’t quite hungry, he still hadn’t eaten anything of worth since his welcome party.

Crixis hadn’t complained yet, but Aodhán knew the boy couldn’t live off food as long as he could yet. And with that in mind, he decided that the moment they returned to the special sect, they would be stocking up the kitchen for the next quarter at least.

“Right then.” Elder Luseph stepped onto the platform. "I'll be back in a few minutes to take you and Crixis to the elder pavilion. Stay here. I will not come searching if you wander off."

Mirelle snorted and muttered something under her breath. Aodhán grinned. “Don’t worry, guys. You won’t have to miss me for too long.”

“Oh, save it, Brystion,” Seamus scoffed in mock distaste. “Galivant away. Nobody’s missing you, and we are most certainly not jealous."

Darien grinned. "That sounded very jealous."

Seamus glared at him. "It was not."

“Save the bickering for when I drop you off,” Elder Luseph cut in, leveling them both with a flat stare as the platform began to descend. "Your voices are giving me a headache."

The platform swept toward the special sect a moment later, their figures disappearing past the elder sect barrier. Fortunately, Aodhán didn't have to wait long before Elder Luseph returned, dusting violet electricity from his hands. When he found them still waiting exactly where he'd left them, he paused and sighed wearily.

"Oh, for storm's sake."

Aodhán smiled. "You asked us to wait, Elder."

Elder Luseph didn't respond. He simply closed his eyes, drew a long, slow breath, and exhaled through his nose like a man practicing patience he hadn't quite mastered.

"Fine," he said at last. "Come with me, both of you, and prepare to have your minds thoroughly dismantled. Metaphorically speaking, of course." He grimaced. "How I wish otherwise, but no. You get to keep them."

Crixis frowned. "Have we offended you in some way, Elder Luseph?"

"No," Elder Luseph gritted out, in a tone that suggested precisely the opposite. "Now come along, and do not touch anything unless I tell you to."

They followed him past the elder hall and several other meeting rooms until they exited the building and stepped into the elder sect proper. Without the runic enchantments of the elder hall to filter the essence, the essence density in the elder sect was suffocating, and it took Crixis several minutes just to find a way to breathe through it.

Unlike the special sect, however, the elder sect was loud and packed full with people. It was the most generalized stratum of the sect after all. Every single being within the Mythic class lived here, and by the time they reached the elder pavilion, Aodhán’s back ached from all the bowing.

The elder pavilion was a museum-like building made from reinforced stone and supported by a seemingly endless row of circular pillars. Three massive runic arrays floated above the building, an aura of immense power radiating from them.

“Do not touch anything,” Elder Luseph repeated his instruction, seriously this time, and from the intense power those rings were emanating, Aodhán was inclined to obey him.

They climbed up the stairs leading to the pavilion’s entrance, and Elder Luseph pushed the massive stone doors open as if they weighed nothing more than a feather.

Aodhán stepped in and promptly came to a halt as his gaze landed on the most impressive array of treasures he’d ever seen in his life.

“By the storm!” Crixis cursed beside him, and Aodhán nodded in agreement. He had been expecting something impressive considering the caliber of treasures the pavilion housed, yet his imagination had fallen short.

This was no pavilion. In fact, the closest thing Aodhán could liken it to was a grand library—filled with rows and rows of books—only this time, those books were treasures so powerful the very air shuddered from the weight of their essence.

The pavilion clerk stepped out from behind a row of treasures, her grayish-white hair billowing behind her in nearly translucent wisps. She was old, her back hunched with the weight only she could feel. She was only at the 112th tier, only a couple steps into the mythic class, and yet, she felt older. Ancient.

Her eyes brightened when she saw them, and she smiled. “Welcome to the elder pavilion, disciples.” She glanced at Elder Luseph. “I believe these are the boys the High Elders asked me to give access to.”

“Just the one, Elder Misha.” Elder Luseph said, gesturing to Aodhán. “He was asked to pick any three treasures of his choice.” He glanced at Aodhán, who was staring slack-jawed at the rows of treasures, and smiled. “I believe he’s been struck comatose by the variety of choice.”

Elder Misha chuckled. “Let’s see if we can’t remedy that. Come, child.”

She led Aodhán down the first row of treasures and smiled as he followed with his eyes wide.

“It’s always interesting to see anyone so impressed with the treasures, even if these ones are only just Mythic rank.”

Aodhán glanced at her. “How can anyone see this and not be impressed?”

“You misunderstand,” Elder Misha chuckled. “You’re staring at them with awe and respect. Not selfish desire and cunningness. The eyes of many who come in here are filled with the latter.”

Aodhán frowned, not quite agreeing with her assessment. He was indeed in awe of the power and number of treasures in the hall, but he certainly wasn’t selfless. If given the chance, he wouldn’t hesitate to raid this place dry. He might even take the shelves too just for the sake of it.

Elder Misha chuckled. “I see you don’t agree with me, but that only proves my point. Had your intentions been truly selfish, you would have felt guilty. You don’t.”

Aodhán thought of her words for a moment and shrugged. “I guess you’re right.”

“Oh, I’m always right.” Elder Misha chuckled, gesturing toward the first shelf as they reached it. “What do you think of this treasure?”

Aodhán glanced at the treasure before him, a cloudy blue pearl emanating a fierce aura of storm and rain. A handwritten note labelled the treasure as the Cloudbound Pearl. Underneath the name, however, was a price that caused Aodhán’s eyebrows to climb all the way to his hairline.

Rank: Mythic+

Price: 120,000 SP

“By the maker," he cursed, eyes wide. He had thought a hundred thousand sect points was a decent amount—it turned out it couldn't even cover the first treasure he'd come across in the pavilion. Should he have asked for a million?

"Aodhán?" Elder Misha asked, and he exhaled.

"It's so expensive."

"That doesn't matter," Elder Misha dismissed gently. "Remember, you get to take three freely. Would you like this one?"

Aodhán shook his head. "I want to see the blessed treasures."

Elder Misha smiled knowingly. "Most would have taken this without hesitation. But you hesitated. Why?"

Aodhán frowned, studying the pearl. "It's powerful. But it doesn't... call to me. Not in the right way."

Elder Misha's smile brightened, and she simply waved him forward. "Come on then. Let's see if we can't find something that does."

She led him deeper into the pavilion, past several rows of gleaming treasures, and with each step, Aodhán's eyebrows climbed higher and higher. They had long since entered the blessed ranks, and the prices had simultaneously crossed into seven and eight digits.

But even more striking was the fact that none of the treasures felt overpriced.

Lightning Singularity residue. Heart of Unending Tempest. Heaven-Scar Fulminite. Seed of Gathering Sky. Pearl of Fulgarum Resonance…

Half the names Aodhán barely understood, but their power—their aura—was undeniable. Each one hummed with an intensity that tugged at the greed in his heart, pulling it slowly toward the surface.

With each treasure they passed, Elder Misha would ask, "What do you think of this one?"

But the problem wasn't finding treasures that called to him—it was that everything called to him. They were all useful and powerful, capable of granting a drastic shift in strength. But Aodhán hadn't grown this powerful by absorbing every treasure he came across—okay, maybe that was a lie—but he was no longer the wide-eyed, enthusiastic teenager he'd been a few months ago. Well, he was still wide-eyed. But he was wiser now. More intentional. Moving not with desire but with resonance.

He wasn't searching for a powerful treasure he liked. He was searching for one his spirit recognized.

And eventually, he found it—a floating nucleus of pale crimson light no larger than a fingertip, endlessly shedding faint motes of mist and static. Thin arcs of lightning spiraled lazily around it, not striking outward but folding back into themselves, as though its essence was perpetually being born and restrained at once.

Each pulse of crimson light sent a hum straight through his spirit, like a string being plucked again and again. He stopped before it, feeling the resonance bloom into a steady vibration beneath his skin.

"You seem to have picked one," Elder Misha hummed quietly. "An unusual one. But very powerful."

He glanced down at its label and smiled.

Name: Skyfall Authority Seed.

Rank: Blessed.

Function: Merges with Aura to grant a domain of absolute dominion where all abilities are amplified.

Price: 270,000 SP.

His smile widened into a grin. “I’ll take this one, thank you.”

Elder Misha chuckled as she grabbed the treasure case and simply handed it to him. “Come on then. You’ve proved yourself to be very picky, and we don’t have all the time in the world.”

Still smiling, Aodhán placed the treasure in his spatial storage and followed Elder Misha as she led him to the next row where, surprisingly, he found another treasure that caught his attention, a wide smile blooming on his face as he read the label.

Name: Jetstream Dominion Filament.

Rank: Blessed.

Function: Drastically enhances long-distance storm manipulation and high-altitude combat control.

Price: 190,500 SP

He wasn’t quite sure he understood where his spirit was going with these resonances, but he suspected it had something to do with dominion and authority. He accepted the treasure from Elder Misha with the thought that perhaps the third treasure might shed some light of understanding on what was going on with his spirit.

But half an hour later, when a third treasure finally caught his attention, it couldn’t have been more confusing.

Name: Core of Tempestrine Authority.

Rank: Blessed.

Function: Grants an 8% increase in storm control and ambient essence alignment within one’s domain.

Price: 300,000 SP.

Another treasure with the word "authority" in its name. He would be blind not to see the pattern. And Elder Misha, despite her age, certainly wasn't blind.

"Well, that's an interesting coincidence," she muttered as she placed the treasure in his hands. "I'm excited to see what you achieve with this."

Aodhán smiled faintly. "Me too." His gaze lingered on the Core as he slid it into his spatial storage, and a small frown creased his brow.

His path was unpredictability.

So what did unpredictability have to do with authority?

***

Aodhán and Crixis stepped out of the pavilion a moment later, but Elder Luseph didn’t go with them.

“Give me a moment," he said, letting the door fall shut behind them before turning back to Elder Misha. "So. What did you see?"

Elder Misha mused. “I saw a spirit struggling to find its path.”

Elder Luseph frowned. “But he already has three. The High Elders diagnosed this themselves during his trial at the mountain. Unpredictability. Devouring Storm. The Path of Raging Tempest."

"Hm." Elder Misha's frown deepened thoughtfully. "Then you may have to add a fourth to that list. Every treasure he chose today was centered around dominion and authority."

"Perhaps he intends to discard the others in favor of it."

"Or perhaps," Elder Misha said slowly, her gaze drifting to the closed door, "he intends to merge them all into it." She glanced back at Luseph. "Authority as a cultivation path—have you heard of it before?"

Luseph shook his head. "I've heard of Superiority. Dominion, even. But not Authority. Could they be the same?"

Elder Misha's expression remained thoughtful. "I cannot say. But whatever the case, I suspect High Elder Zarxan will find this development very interesting indeed."
