As Olag, Alveron, and I stepped into a meeting room I had grown very familiar with over the last few days, a low murmur reached my ears. I paused for a moment at the entrance, while the others slipped past me.

I just…wanted to take the sight of everyone in the room in. This was very much so an all-hands-on-deck meeting.

What I’d heard called the Rhoscarans call the ‘Quattrain’ room was packed to the gills.

Dwarven soldiers stood lined up along the edges of the soberly decorated room, both Regular Rhoscaran Army troops, as well as Corvid Knights. Meanwhile, Rangers were thin on the ground in here, it seemed. There were a few in the room, but not many. I’m guessing most of the Ranger force was already deployed out into the city, settling into their assigned posts and waiting anxiously for their foes to arrive.

I did notice, though, that Captain Gastone was one of the few Rangers in the room. And he didn’t exactly seem happy about that fact. The highest-ranked Ranger I had yet to encounter had a slight frown on his eternally tired features, but his eyes were alert as they scanned the room from his position near a far wall. He, of course, noticed me right away, and his frown lightened somewhat as we exchanged nods before he went right back to his duty.

This was by far the best defended room in the palace right now, I was guessing, from the sheer press of armored might within it. I’d even been stopped briefly by a pair of the avian-themed, heavily armed knights just outside of the room.

Because inside was the entirety of Rhoscara's armed forces command structure.

There were the expected Generals, of course, of the Rhoscaran Army, to a one, all of them dressed in sharp red military attire. Medals galore hung upon their shining, unmarked breastplates, while I couldn’t help but notice that full, stylized beards adorned their cheeks. Nobles every one, my impression of these guys had been…mixed, ever since we’d arrived within the city. Some were competent and could actually follow along and accept advice whenever it was given.

Some were decidedly not.

Each one, though, had an entire cavalcade of underlings following along behind them. From scribes to advisors to officers, I think all of them had brought their entire command staff with them to this meeting. They lined the massive, rectangular table in the room, speaking in low tones that nonetheless added up to create a din. An intricately detailed map of the city spanned the breadth of the table, as I watched. Generals and officers were leaning over the surface of it, studying the representation. Some pushed around small pewter figures on the map with wooden rods, seemingly directing troops in…relatively real time. Whenever this happened, a Dwarven messenger would sprint out of the room, nearly bowling me over in the process. In fact, messengers were leaving and arriving, coming and going, at such a pace that I nearly caused a pile-up, just from a few seconds' pause in the doorway.

After frantically scuttling into the room, one final thing caught my eye. The star of the show herself…

The Prince of Ravens.

Elysael sat in a tall backed chair more akin to a throne that befitted her station, at the head of the table. The Dwarven Prince was dressed in what looked to me like a distinctly military cut blouse, jacket, and trousers, all of it in shades of red. I was a bit shocked to see an actual blade sheathed at her side, what seemed to be an elaborately detailed estoc with an intricate basket hilt. Even from across the room, I could feel the intense enchantment on the blade. As I watched, Olag reached his cousin and gave the rightful liege of Rhoscara a courtly bow. In return, he received a stoic, acknowledging nod, and the Captain-General of the Raven’s Claw fell in beside her, standing at parade rest.

And then Elysael’s gaze fell on me. I straightened up as I was evaluated by a cool gaze, and then given first an acknowledging nod of my nod, and then a very obvious hint. Her pale golden gaze flickered over to the open space to her left, on the exact opposite spot from Olag, and I understood.

Frankly, even a bit flattered.

But instead of approaching her, I let my own eyes trail over to rest on someone else first, and then back to the Prince. She understood immediately, and blinked once, slowly, in understanding, before she focused her gaze back on the preparations.

I’d be with you in a moment, Ely.

Slowly, I navigated my way through the crowd of Dwarves, aware of the eyes that inevitably fell on me as I did. It was hard to be circumspect in a room of people you had several feet in height on, in the end. But this wasn’t a room full of fops and useless nobles, even with a few notable examples among them. They had a job to do, an existential one, and soon focused back on the more important work.

In the meantime, I came to a stop at the side of Captain Gastone, and…awkwardly crouched down on my heels next to him. Now he was the tall one between us, and the Ranger Captain knew it, too, judging by the faint smirk on his lips.

It died quickly, though. The Dwarf was no fool, and could likely guess what I was after.

“We didn’t get much,” Enzo Gastone muttered to me, his voice mostly blending into the background of the room.

I sighed at his words, somehow unsurprised, in the end. “Let me guess,” I whispered back tiredly. “The Savoy had a dead man’s switch on him?” At Enzo’s blank stare, I rephrased the question. “A suicide method?”

The Captain led out a quiet sound of understanding. “Ah, only somewhat?. The Savoy did end up perishing from his interrogation, but that wasn’t why we couldn’t get anything out of him. He was just mad,” He said frankly. “Really, truly, terribly batshit insane.”

I blinked as Gastone continued.

“I have no idea how the Dwarf was even functioning,” The Ranger said, a note of bafflement in his voice. “One moment, he would speak like he was who he seemed to be. Your ordinary run-of-the-mill Savoy partisan. A particularly arrogant one, at that. But the next…he would go feral. Elf-like, even. He would start grunting and snarling at his interrogators like an animal, unable to really verbalize anything more than what seemed to be a desire for violence. Or, maybe for something to eat? It was hard to tell, really.”

“Why?” I asked, fascinated despite myself. Out of the corner of my eye, I saw Elysael raise a hand, and much of the chatter in the room died down. Not all of it, though. It was enough that I felt like I could at least finish my conversation with the Ranger before joining Ely on her left side.

Gastone scratched his head, watching the other Dwarven officers in the room congregate around the table. “Because it sounded like he was speaking in the Elven language,” He whispered to me. “None of us could understand him. I didn’t even know it was possible for anyone but an Elf to learn that one, with how Language Adaptation works. We never even got his name…before he bit through his own tongue, and drowned in his own blood.”

For some inexplicable reason, I felt a chill run down my spine. The Savoy had bit through his own tongue?

That was…barbaric. Not at all what I would expect from someone who didn’t have Fynneas’s madness crouching on their soul.

I shook my head, shaking my troubled thoughts from it, and then bid my farewells to the Captain. With that, I finally approached Elysael’s left and stood at ease next to the nearly throne-like chair. I did my best to ignore the stares I was getting from the other Dwarves in the room.

Yes, yes. I was taller than you, but that didn’t mean I was better than you.

Much.

“Gentledwarves,” Elysael said in a loud, clear voice. All at once, the chatter in the room fully died, and the assembled officers sat up straighter in their chairs around the table. The Prince slowly let her gaze travel along every face present for a moment before she nodded once, firmly. “Let us begin. General Braggok, are our forces along the wall in position?”

One of the Generals I didn’t care for outright saluted Ely. This was one of the ones from the Army, and I was unfortunately familiar with his oh-so-noble type. “Yes, Your Highness!” He blustered. “The majority of our valiant soldiers are concentrated along the river wall, but we have not neglected the rest of it! If those dastardly Savoy think they can pull a trick on us and circle around, they’ve got another thing coming!”

Dastardly. Really? Nobody spoke like that, not even in Vereden.

A quiet cough was the only thing heard in the room for a moment, while Elysael’s gaze bored into his. I saw a bead of sweat roll down his bald head from the force of her stare. Eventually, the Prince nodded. “Very well. General Vallance,” She said, turning her gaze onto another of the Generals. “How are our defences within the river itself? Was the operation successful?”

This was one of the good ones. Vallance was the General who commanded the Rangers, and he was a crafty-guy. The long raven-haired Dwarf nodded silently, his flat blue eyes betraying nothing. “Yes, Your Highness. The payload has been delivered to all requisite locations.”

That was a bit opaque, and I don’t think I was the only one confused. I saw more than one Dwarven officer look around for clues, only to be met with blank stares as well.

At the very least, Elysael looked satisfied. “Very good. And I assume your men are in their places?”

Vallance didn’t say a word, merely nodding again.

The Prince returned it and left the taciturn Dwarf alone. “Then, next we have the forces within the city. Hopefully they will not be needed, and we can repel the Savoy at the wall…”

I saw quite a few doubtful looks get passed around, and I didn’t blame them for that. There was just so many Savoy and Velancian soldiers coming that it was possible, even likely that some would get through into the city.

And then they could run amok. The Savoy weren’t coming here to capture Rhoscara, after all. No…

They wanted to utterly raze it to the ground.

So, someone was just going to have to be within it, and not on the walls to hunt those saboteurs down, wouldn’t they?

“Olag has volunteered his Raven’s Beak to be the first line of defense behind the river wall,” Elysael nodded to her cousin, who merely smiled at the attention he was now receiving. “They will act the net to catch those who vault the wall. Unfortunately, nets will always have holes…which means we require hunter-killers to chase them down. Marshal Hart, of the Herztalian expedition, has volunteered the services of his people to accomplish this.”

This time, I was the one getting all of the attention from the room. Even though I’d been at more than one of these little planning sessions, and I hadn’t exactly been a quiet observer, there were still people who might have missed me. Not everyone could attend every meeting every time, after all.

It was from those people that I was receiving the most incredulous looks.

“A Human, my Prince?” One of the officers asked incredulously. “We truly intend to allow a Herztalian the task of directly defending our dear citizens?” His back straightened like a bar had been shoved into it when Elysael’s cool gaze fell onto him.

“It is only because of the Marshal that we had advanced warning of this attack in the first place,” She said frostily. “And he will not be alone. Over half of the Rangers will be assisting the Herztalian expedition in its task. Furthermore, there are multiple rapid response groups from the Army that shall be stationed around the city. You would know this, Colonel Ambroise, if you had been present at more of these meetings.”

Suitably chastised, the Colonel looked back down at the table and remained silent. I briefly caught General Vallance’s flat gaze from across the map, and was a bit startled by the satisfied look I saw in it.

I’ll just…stay away from that, shall I?

I had enough on my plate as it was, dealing with my own internal problems.

“Now,” Elysael said with finality. “That just leaves General Raccant. General, have we been able to ascertain anything further about the enemy force?”

The General who was, nominally, in charge of defending the river itself sat up from his chair. This one was…okay. Not great, not terrible. Just kind of forgettable, really. He wiped a hand across his sweaty brow and shook his head. “Yes, Your Highness,” He said, a tad nervously. “In the last hour since they were spotted, we have…confirmed the number of enemy vessels, as well as their composition. I…well…”

When he didn’t continue, Braggok lost his patience. “Well? Out with it, man!” He barked.

Raccant barely reacted to the man. Instead, he just let out a shaky breath. “Our last count puts the Savoy fleet at eighty-two Great Cogs, sixty-five barges, forty-one War Galleys, and…approximately thirty-seven Heavy Warships,” He said, his hands shaking. “There are possibly some more taking up the rear…but we cannot see much more than specks, in the distance.” Raccant smiled sickly, in the dead silence that had fallen over the room. Nobody even dared to breathe. “At the very least, they looked to be smaller craft…but we think they were landing skiffs. A lot of them.”

“Over two hundred ships?” One officer asked hoarsely. “How many soldiers could that transport?”

I bowed my head and shook it, speaking up for the first time. “Over forty-two thousand, I’m guessing,” I said quietly, my Core having done some quick mental math, using our knowledge of naval doctrine as a base. The Velancians weren’t that different, after all.

Silence fell in the wake of my words.

“Gyre save us…”

I don’t know who whispered those words, but it evoked a strange reaction.

Elysael abruptly slammed her open palms against the surface of the table and shot up into a standing position. “No,” She snapped, glaring out into the room, more passionate than I had ever seen her. “The Gyre will not save us. Nobody will. No foreign army shall heroically march to our aid, appearing over the hills as if they were born of providence. No miracle shall rise from the depths of the river we have lived and breathed and died to defend. It has only ever been us. We are the heroes of our own story. We are Rhoscara! We are the proud, the unbent, the unyielding in the face of tyranny! It is us who have held the line against the barbarity of the world!”

She straightened up to her full height, jaw clenched tightly in fury. Not at the table, nor her own officers.

But at the world.

“Forty-two thousand? Then we shall stand against forty-two thousand, the same as we have stood against all who have thought to challenge the Raven Throne. If the Savoy wish to burn our city to the ground, then we shall make them choke upon the ashes. We will not yield in the face of adversity. We shall not fracture. And we will not wait for salvation.”

Prince Elysael of House Florens directed her gaze at every Dwarf sitting around the table, all of them spell-bound from the force of her conviction. Even I was swept up in the fervor.

Now this was true charisma. True nobility.

“We shall make it.”
