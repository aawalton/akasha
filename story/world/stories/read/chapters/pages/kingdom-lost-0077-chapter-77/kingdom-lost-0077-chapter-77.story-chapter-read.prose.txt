The burning remnants of the gate exploded inward as Thorne roared and charged through the shattered opening. Flames licked at the edges of his new armor, casting flickering reflections across the polished plates. He held his shield high and swung his sword in a wide arc the moment he crossed the threshold. Thirty raiders scattered in every direction, some still clutching water buckets, others half-dressed and stumbling from sleep.

Riley’s infantry poured in right behind him. Boots pounded over splintered wood and glowing embers. Heat rolled off the ruined gateway, and thick smoke stung their eyes. The soldiers moved quickly, slamming shields edge to edge and locking them. Long pikes slid forward through the gaps between the shields, their iron heads gleaming in the firelight.

From just outside the broken gate, Riley raised her arm and gave a sharp signal. The forty archers waiting in the treeline broke cover and streamed through the breach in disciplined groups. They took position directly behind the reforming infantry line. Riley stepped inside the compound herself, staying near the rear where the smoke was thinner. She gripped her hatchet tightly, her eyes scanning the chaotic courtyard.

“Archers fire at will!” she called, her voice cutting through the roar of flames.

Inside the compound, panic spread like the fires themselves. Raiders dropped their buckets and stared in disbelief at the disciplined formation that had just breached their supposedly secure walls. Shouts of alarm rose above the crackle of burning wood. “They’re inside!” one man yelled. “They broke the gate!” A handful of raider archers still stood on the wall walks and began loosing desperate arrows downward.

The war cry swelled from Riley’s soldiers. “For the fallen!” The sound rolled forward like thunder.

The archers released dozens of arrows, filling the air with a deadly hiss. Some shafts trailed fire, streaking like comets as the tar mixture burned. The raider archers on the walls jerked and cried out. One after another they dropped their bows and tumbled from the walks, hitting the ground with heavy thuds. The relentless barrage forced the remaining raiders to duck behind barrels, crates, and half-burned structures. Small fires bloomed across the courtyard wherever the tar arrows struck.

Thorne’s voice boomed from the front of the formation. “Advance!”

The troops moved forward as one, shields stayed locked, pikes extended like the quills of an iron porcupine. Behind them the archers kept up their steady rain of arrows, protecting the advance. Riley watched from her position near the rear, hatchet ready in her grip, her jaw set with grim determination as the formation pressed deeper into the smoky courtyard.

Thorne's voice boomed from the front. "Attack!"

The infantry formation pushed forward as one disciplined unit. Shields stayed locked tight while long pikes thrust outward in coordinated waves through the narrow gaps. Riley, eyes stayed sharp as she issued crisp commands. "Pikes level! Archers, keep the rain coming!"

Sound of dozens of boots marching in unison mixed with the constant whistle of arrows flying overhead. The two sides closed the distance. The first clash began with a crash of metal and wood.

The long pikes proved devastating at range. Several raiders who tried to rush the formation were impaled before they could get close enough to use their swords. At the same time, Riley's archers maintained a relentless barrage. Arrows arced over the shields and slammed into the raiders. Many raiders were forced to split their attention as they dodged or batted aside pikes while trying to shield themselves from the deadly rain. A number of raiders, mostly the lower-level ones, fell quickly, while others were skewered by pikes or dropped from arrows to the chest or throat. The glittering tips of pikes thrust forward again and again. The thud of arrows striking flesh or wood filled the air as raiders cursed and scrambled for cover.

The more experienced raiders, proved far harder to kill. They weathered the arrow storm better by using an overturned cart, building corners, and shields for cover before launching savage counter-attacks. When the infantry pikes overextended or when a soldier stumbled, these veterans surged into the momentary gaps. They hacked at shield edges, stabbed at exposed legs and joints in the metal armor, and dragged soldiers down with raw strength. Riley's troops began to fall in larger numbers. Metal plate armor and training saved some from instant death, but many collapsed with grievous wounds. They screamed, bled heavily, or simply dropped unconscious. For every raider that went down, roughly three of Riley's soldiers were taken out of the fight. Thorne fought like a demon at the front, his armor shrugged off blows that would killed multiple regular soldiers. He cut down one veteran raider after another in brutal melee.

Riley moved with the formation scanning for tactical advantage, while she witnessed the horrific cost firsthand. Soldiers she had trained fell around her; the blood mixed with the muddy ground while screams cut through the chaos. It was hard not be affected by seeing this first hand, but her face hardened, and her voice remained steady as she continued giving orders. The weight of every fallen man hit her hard, yet she forced herself to keep pushing forward, there was nothing she could do for them right now.

Seeing their numbers dwindling rapidly under the combined pressure of pikes and arrows, the surviving raiders realized they could not win in the open courtyard. Most of the experienced veterans began a disciplined fighting retreat toward the longhouse at the far end of the compound. They dragged some of their wounded with them. Riley’s soldiers still advanced but now looked visibly bloodied. The courtyard lay littered with bodies from both sides while the raiders fell back toward the building. Riley's forces pressed onward without pause.

With the raiders falling back, Riley moved fully inside the compound and took a central position within the battered soldier formation. She walked among her troops with her hatchet still in hand. Her voice stayed calm but commanding as she directed the next phase. Critically wounded men were left behind for the hospital to claim them.

“Stay tight,” Riley ordered. “Archers, cover all directions. We finish this.”

The infantry advanced slowly and carefully toward the longhouse at the far end of the compound. Infantry kept pikes and shields focused forward while the remaining archers scanned rooftops, flanks, and the rear for any sign of ambush or stray raiders. The ground lay slick with blood and spilled water from the earlier fire-fighting efforts. Black smoke and the awful smell hung in the air, while the sounds of crackling flames, the groans of the wounded, and the heavy breathing of exhausted soldiers filled the space.

The mood shifted from open battle to tense, cautious pursuit. Everyone knew the surviving raiders, mostly the tougher veterans, had retreated into the large building and would hold the advantage in tighter quarters, this would be where this battle would be won or lost. Archers loosed occasional arrows at any movement on rooftops or shadows between buildings. Riley felt the weight of the heavy losses already suffered, yet she pushed the feeling down to keep her focused. Thorne stayed at the spearhead. His Level new armor now showed visible dents and streaks of blood.

The formation reached the heavy wooden doors of the longhouse. Thorne stepped forward and tried to force the doors open with his shoulder and shield, but they remained firmly barred or locked from the inside. The troops came to a halt in front of the building. They breathed hard with weapons ready. Arrow support grew much more limited due to the close proximity to the structure. The shift from open tactical advantage with pikes and arrows to the brutal close-quarters fighting that awaited inside hung heavy in the charged air. Tension rose as the soldiers realized the tactical advantage of the open courtyard was now gone.

A group of soldiers rolled in a previously over turned cart and got behind it. On the count of three they pushed the heavy cart into the doors and on the second attempt forced the doors open.

A handful of raiders were left at the far side of the room, all at once Throne charged with the remain troops and attacked. The fighting spilled into the narrow hallways and connected rooms of the longhouse. In the tight confines, combat turned savage and personal. Pikes became useless. Soldiers fought with swords, shields, and knives in choking, blood-slick corridors. The surviving veteran raiders, delt more damage and excelled in the close space. They used the layout to their advantage, flanking from side rooms and ambushing small groups of Riley’s troops. Riley’s soldiers, even in metal armor, took heavy casualties. Many more fell and were, slashed, stabbed, or dragged down. The metal armor bought them precious seconds, allowing some to wound raiders in return before they collapsed. Thorne fought like a force of nature at the front. His armor absorbed blow after blow while he cleared paths for the others.

Riley witnessed the brutal reality up close as her soldiers fell around her, their screams echoed in the narrow spaces. The growing realization of how many had already been lost weighed on her. Her face showed cold fury mixed with barely contained horror, but she kept issuing sharp commands to hold the line and push forward toward the main hall.

The fighting gradually funneled into the large, smoke-filled central longhouse hall where the remaining raiders had made their final stand. Loot lay scattered across a heavy wooden table in the center. Only a handful of Riley’s soldiers remained standing alongside Thorne. At the center of the raiders stood a facially disfigured man. The left side of his face formed a melted ruin of old burn scars. One eye looked clouded white. He breathed in a painful rasp. He had Valrik held with a knife to his throat, used as a human shield. A thin line of blood had trickled down Valrik’s neck. The raider backed slowly toward a side exit, while he snarled at Riley’s remaining troops. The hall fell into a tense, deadly silence broken only by the crackling fires outside and the sound of heavy breathing. Riley’s few soldiers froze with weapons raised but unable to act without risking Valrik. As Riley's eye studied the man's disfigured face, her eyes hardened with recognition, she had seen this face before.

Riley stepped forward slowly through her remaining soldiers. Her hatchet hung low in her right hand. The troops parted for her without a word. She stopped beside an archer who still had the tar mixture in a satchel. Without breaking eye contact with the disfigured raider, she dipped the part of head of her hatchet into the sticky liquid. She slowly turned the weapon three hundred and sixty degrees so the tar coated every edge. In a calm but ice-cold voice, Riley spoke. “When we first met, your fellow raiders were killed and you were left alive, even though you intended to kill me and Garon. Then you and your men attacked while we slept. You slaughtered unarmed people. You made this personal.” She stopped turning the hatchet and looked directly into the raider’s good eye. “This goes beyond business.”

The disfigured raider snarled, his rasping voice thick with pain and rage.

“What? You think you did me a favor? You left me for dead. My face rotted, foul liquid seeping from the wounds and the only thing that kept me breathing was fire. Steel, heated white-hot, pressed into my flesh to burn the rot out. I can still smell it.”

Riley grimaced, the thought of the man’s skin burning turning her stomach, yet her face remained stoic.

“You attacked us. You thought we were unarmed. The mercy you were given was more than you deserved. You’re a coward, you butchered unarmed soldiers in the night, and now you hide behind one of my men like a shield.”

“This man has value to you,” he hissed back. “You’ll let us go, or he dies.”

The tension in the room thickened, heavy and suffocating.

Riley’s expression did not change. “Go ahead, then.”

The raider’s clouded eye widened in disbelief. “No… you won’t let him die. He matters to you.”

Riley’s voice was flat, unyielding. “His life won’t save you from me. You owe a debt of blood and I’m here to collect.”

Riley turned and touched the tar-coated hatchet to a nearby soldier’s torch. The tar ignited instantly with a fierce whoosh. Flames licked up the blade and in one smooth, powerful motion she hurled the flaming hatchet at the disfigured raider. In pure panic the raider jerked the dagger hard across Valrik’s throat. Valrik’s eyes widened as blood sprayed and he collapsed to the floor in a growing pool of red, twitched once, and went still. The flaming hatchet buried itself deep into the side of the disfigured raider’s scarred face with a sickening thud. Tar-fueled flames erupted across his head and shoulders as he screamed and staggered backward. He crashed to the ground dead.

Raiders and Riley’s troops alike stood frozen in stunned silence.
