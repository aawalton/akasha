As the day drew longer, the death count on Sanctuary skyrocketed. With the flanking groups in the east and west, the Radoch forces in the Forest dwindled exponentially. Across every other zone and continent, the invaders scrambled to join the main force on the Tropical Isles. Without working spaceships, most struggled to get past roving zombie groups and the natural terrain.

For Sally, none of this mattered. All she knew was the pain radiating across every part of her Aspect form. She was starving. Almost to the point of losing her mind.

To make matters worse, there was nobody to yap to.

She could sense Theo had rejoined Hunter and the rest of the Dangers and were carving through the flanking force. On the other side of the Forest, Max’s group was mopping up the disorganized fleet that had been pursuing Wolf. Both allowed her easy passage toward the land bridge to the Isles. Both caused her to waste away with nothing to eat.

In desperation, she had taken to consuming the terrain. Plants and trees couldn’t fuel her, but it lessened the need to snack on a zombie. One of her mouths had wilted away. Sacrificed for the greater whole. But her vines were sluggish. The connection to Sanctuary’s entire undead fading.

The only thing keeping her going was the knowledge that a banquet was right ahead.

If she could get there alive, she would eat them all.

Dent held his face in his hands, quiet for a few moments before he sighed for the third time. “That’s… a risky plan.”

“Aren’t they all?” Chuck shrugged as he swiped through three screens of data.

“You’ve seen what kind of weaponry they have. It might mean a quick and fruitless death for everyone. For good.”

“The stakes have been measured appropriately. There is a chance that Sally won’t be enough of a distraction, or that I might… falter.” He paused to glance at the swordsman. “We’ve all sacrificed for Sanctuary, so this is par for the course.”

“You’re up there, though.” Dent shook his head. “For sacrificng, I mean. What else will you give up for this world?”

“Truthfully, everything, if I had to. You know this. Luckily for our marriage, it’s not me giving something up this time.”

“Archie?”

The Architect gave a brief nod. “Despite giving up so much power to create the STAR+, the guilt he holds for creating this universe pushes him to give up more. He can’t quit being an Aspect, but… well, that’s not important right now. Our goal is saving Sanctuary.”

“It isn’t long until Sally meets the forward force sent from the Isles. Are you going to assist then?”

“No.” Chuck didn’t elaborate, but as the silence dragged out, he sighed. The control room felt cold and empty, even if little had changed compared to normal operation.

He had been angry that the wizard pushed to rescind some of his powers. At a time like this? The problem was Archie probably knew best, at least in the long term. Even though the world hadn’t yet been saved, the old man was thinking about the future of the Outsiders. What would become of the Sea once the dust had settled?

But of the specifics? A few factors that were simple and straightforward. Plenty more that were improbable and unique. Lots of work for a man trying to prevent an alien invasion.

This was Chuck’s life, though. He was a gardener, and Sanctuary was his greenhouse. In this manner, he understood how Archie wanted to watch over the Outsiders in the same way.

He paused, realizing his eyes were glazed over instead of focused. With a dry smile, he turned to the swordsman. “You’d better go get ready. I’ll be beating your kill count soon, and you’ll owe me a victory meal.”

Sally woke from a foggy dream. Warm gore ran down her gullet, rejuvenating her thick stems and roots. Her mind had wandered, lost, almost forgetting her sense of self. Decades of this malaise had passed until food graced her many maws.

And what a rush it was.

Vision sparked through her brain, relaying dizzying colors and muddied shapes all moving too fast. Skeletal dwarves stood in a semi-circle around her as hundreds of zombies helped defend their master. A giant hand lowered down from the heavens, full of pulped organs and broken limbs.

She ate it readily, gaining strength.

Despite being essentially unconscious, she had not only made it most of the way across the Forest, but maintained her Aspect form as well. The primary force coming from the Isles had finally met up with her. Not only had the battle started without her, but the Radochs were playing their hand completely wrong.

Guided by the unspoken desire of their Queen, her zombies were dragging down the weaker roaches as they were sent into battle en masse. They took bites from the fallen but left them for her to finish. It was almost a conveyor belt, with fresh corpses being delivered to her at an increasing pace.

Her plant-mouths drooled as she grabbed a Radoch body from the ground and tore it apart with a bite. This was it. She craved more. The pit in her stomach was insatiable.

Life’s Overabundance flooded through every part of her - a new Ultimate for this form. With each body she consumed, she grew a little larger. A new root or vine slithered forth. Gnashing plant-heads grew, hungry for more.

Within the cluttered throne room, the Hive King drummed his many fingers on the edges of his seat. A wide display hovered across the side wall, showing the progress of the war. His mandibles clicked in annoyance while a smaller Radoch tried to explain the situation.

“Fleet Commander Va-Ly’k has met with the enemy force. While his retinue and collection are more than a match for the plant-zombie collectable, the flanks are weaker than expected due to the eastern and western roadblocks by the other prime collectibles. Those few who we believe have come back from the dead.”

The Hive King huffed, casting a sideways glance at the wizard’s headless corpse now pinned to one of his walls. “We need to get to the bottom of this trickery. Do our scouting fleets have no information?”

“N-no, well…” The war advisor twitched nervously. “Our forces once blanketing the land have diminished, and those who remain are… vanishing.”

A claim that lacked confidence, but the display didn’t lie. Even as they watched now, several dots representing Radochs forces around other parts of the world were shrinking or vanishing entirely.

“It must be the one who is half-fused to the System.” The King clenched four fists. “How brazen to come out of hiding at this juncture.”

He had long known that this world had powerful fighters on it. Few managed to travel the Sea without being so - yet killing them seemed more practical, despite his race’s affinity for collecting the novel or the strong. It was meant to be a lesson for the rest of the universe - standing against the Radochs was impossible.

However, now that they had shown even greater potential…

“We have sent this world’s System to the Mother for analysis, but we are unable to determine a solid point of natural respawning.” A second advisor hesitated, not wanting to imply they had been wrong. The cost of losing their current energy source across the Sea was deemed acceptable, considering what accepting the STAR+ could bring for their race.

“As She wills it.” The Hive King raised a hand in reverence. “The collectables on this world may be beneath my wants, but they have done irreparable damage to not only my living hoards, but to those of my loyal Fleet Commanders. For this, I will collect them personally.”

With a groan that echoed through the chamber, the King rose from his throne. Every guard and advisor bowed as he passed, only to scurry on after him. It was only natural that the leader of this Fleet had the biggest collection. That meant the most powerful weaponry, dangerous monsters, and rare technology.

The door closed behind the last Radoch, locking tight to protect the collection of trophies decorating the walls. Nothing but the distance hum of energy filled the room for twenty minutes before a fluctuation shook the air.

A purple glow increased in intensity around Archie’s decapitated corpse. Power spluttered out bright sparks for a couple of seconds before a figure burst out, landing softly on the metal floor.

Astral displacement didn’t count as teleportation, he concluded.

It was the wizard, although he looked more like a cut-out of his silhouette. Swirling purple and blue edging highlighted the edges of his form, while his center was a pitch-black galaxy dotted with stars.

His faceless Aspect of Creation form glanced around the empty throne room. He raised a hand, and a bright white star bloomed into life within his grip.

Big Bang (Death) may have reset the universe, but without the vampire’s heart, his Ultimate acted differently.

Energy from the surroundings circled the air, syphoning down into the miniature sun in his hand. It was a little too on the nose that the skill’s true nature was a mimicry of the mistake that created this universe in the first place.

Then again, what better second step after poisoning the food source was hitting the nest itself?

>>BIG BANG<<

The infant star exploded.

Sally was aware of an explosion, but didn’t care about it. All she wanted to do was eat and grow. Her Aspect of Life senses told her several thousand lives ended in an instant, but that had been happening for a while, albeit on a lesser scale.

Global undead numbers had dwindled significantly, but so had the Radochs. Those left far from the Isles could no longer fend for themselves.

A life force she could easily pin as being Chuck kept appearing across the planet, and smaller alien forces disappeared instantly. Although he kept playing up the not-a-god thing, he could go toe-to-toe with any of the other Guardians on Sanctuary if he wasn’t distracted.

It was a risk for him to be out in the open, but that meant things were in the final phase of the war.

She could also sense Theo above anyone else. He was getting closer and traveled with the rest of her family. The Russet group came from her other side, and had been clearing up their fair share of bugs.

But things weren’t entirely going their way. Big Joe had lost an arm, and was limping. Three dwarves and hundreds of zombies had fallen along the way to the land bridge. She had punctured the Radoch forces like an arrow, and reached the large squads full of elites, led by Commanders, and supported by weird tech and powerful monsters.

By far the worst part of this was one thread of life that was stronger than the rest. More despicable. The Hive King, along with his personal guard. As the giant roach had survived the explosion that had destroyed most of the Isles—including the collector ship—that could only mean he was enraged and vengeful.

All she had to do was chew through this next section of Radochs and meet up with the other Outsiders. Then they’d have no problem-

The air above the army burst, and the Hive King appeared high in the air.

He pointed a finger at Sally and spoke alien words of doom.
