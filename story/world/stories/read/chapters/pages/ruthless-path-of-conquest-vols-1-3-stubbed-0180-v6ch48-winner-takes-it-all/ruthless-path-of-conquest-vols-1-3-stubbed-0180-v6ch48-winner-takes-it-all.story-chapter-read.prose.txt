It’s really him?

A relatively unimposing figure stood in between the System’s usual, masked agents. He was by all means tall, strong, and even had an intelligent-looking face, but that was all only compared to pre-System people.

“Hello, my name is Lev Kazanov,” said the man, repeating what the System had already explained. “I’m from Russia.”

The failed state? Interesting. Honestly, I would expect a Ruler from that rough part of the world to feel a little more intimidating.

But James’s senses didn’t register much of a hint of threat from Kazanov, aura-wise.

Amid the array of formidable-looking Rulers all around, the Russian hardly stood out.

The air suddenly filled with the sound of music. James looked around, but there was no sign of a speaker anywhere.

Of course, it’s magic, why am I even surprised anymore?

As the lyrics began to play, James frowned in confusion.

“Here come old flat top, he come grooving up slowly, he got ju-ju eyeball, he one holy roller…”

What? What’s going on? Did the System just start playing the Beatles? Or whoever is in control here…

“I have always been a fan of American style, classic rock ‘n’ roll,” said the Russian without preamble. “Since I won the melee, the soundtrack in this waiting area is under my control now.”

The Beatles were British, and yes, they’re great, although this is one of their more nonsensical songs… But this is kind of small ball stuff. Behold, I have added music to the setting! What else is he going to do? What else are they letting him do?

The melee winner was supposed to determine some elements of the rules for the Victors Tournament, that was his prize, but which rules that applied to was not clear.

“I thank all of you for indulging my little eccentricities,” the Russian said. “I didn’t really have that much to say to the System’s agents. They had an excellent plan for how to run this event, and I just offered a few additional guideposts.”

Out with it, man! James thought, slightly annoyed. What exact rules did you make up?

Kazanov cleared his throat. “So, the tournament will be one on one bouts set in a large arena—not nearly as big as the arena the melee took place in, but fairly sizable. There will be single elimination matches broken up into brackets, like a regular sports tournament.” He turned to the System agent to his left. “Is there anything important I’m forgetting, sir?”

Kazanov gave off an air as if he was trying to be humble, but James sensed an underlying smugness. It was understandable from the one who had actually won the melee, even if he had possibly used nuclear weapons or some similarly underhanded tactic to achieve it.

The System agent replied.

[Yes. Our distinguished champion has elected to propose a tournament with two separate divisions for those who were born as humans and those born as monsters. This means those who have completed Race Evolution to change from humans into non-humans will still be in the human division. In the end, there will be a human champion and a monster champion, and each will be rewarded identically. Those who perform well in either division will be rewarded depending on performance.]

That’s actually clever, James thought. I ought to be impressed that he got them to agree to that. It’s very good rule manipulation, and it’s massive. Frankly, the division separation is such an important rule that I’m almost certain he didn’t forget about it. He was just pretending to not recall that he suggested it, to try to avoid pissing off the monsters. But I wonder how far he could’ve taken something like that, if he really wanted to. Men’s and women’s divisions? Age-based?

Monsters far outnumbered humans in the post-System world. To create two lopsided divisions, with significantly unequal numbers of participants but equal prizes, was ridiculously unfair. It would probably sound legitimate to many of the humans listening in, but the monsters might feel very differently.

James looked their way, and he thought he detected a whiff of hostility directed at the melee winner. They understood the way in which they had been disadvantaged.

There were the former human monsters, like Cara and Count Aleph, who wore more neutral expressions—probably secretly happy they were in a separate division from some of the more dangerous monstrous Rulers, frankly, but unwilling to show that on their faces.

He couldn’t help but think back to the Panther Queen. His emotional regulation Skills had kept him from suffering too severe a psychological blow at the revelation of her death, but in a moment like this, he couldn’t avoid imagining how she would have reacted. There definitely would have been some sort of smug comment about general monster superiority.

His lips twisted into a bittersweet smile.

Following that thread, this could be an avenue for me to win more allies at some point, he thought. Or even right now. The sheer unfairness of the way the nonhumans are being treated by both our melee’s champion and the System itself at his behest… Okay, so I won’t go over and congratulate the winner. I’ll approach the discontented nonhumans instead. Stroke their egos a bit. They’ll be flattered that I agree with them this is a sign Kazanov didn’t believe humanity could perform well in fair competition. And this is a little way I can honor my dearly departed ally… an approach inspired by her.

A number of the monsters had shown appreciation for the Fisher Emperor at the World Leaders’ Summit previously. Maybe that was where his power base was meant to be.

[We will place the human division brackets on display at the front of the room, and the nonhuman bracket division brackets will be at the opposite end, so each individual can anticipate who their opponents might be. The first matches will start in five minutes.]

So, talking to anyone is out for now, then.

A giant glowing blackboard-like object appeared in the space the System representative had indicated, and bright white lines began tracing themselves across it in a familiar shape. Brackets.

At the same time, a timer appeared in the corner of James’s field of vision.

[00:04:59]

James began moving across the room to get a proper look at the board with the human brackets. As if he had issued an order, his monsters started parting the crowd of other competitors with their bodies, making way for the Fisher Emperor to pass.

I hope they’ll get to take a look at the nonhuman brackets.

He stepped past many faces he recognized from the Summit, only stopping for a brief moment when someone poked him in the side.

He turned his head and saw Sierra and the armored figure she had been with before. The figure that James suspected was her re-embodied brother.

“Good luck,” the young woman mouthed.

I guess neither she nor her companion are participating. Otherwise she wouldn’t be wishing me luck.

James smiled and used Monster Generation. He bit his thumb, drew a few drops of blood, and infused it with his raw mana.

Just like that, he had a blood slime.

He extended his hand with the slime, and after a moment, she accepted the creature onto her palm.

“We’ll stay in touch,” he said gently. “If you or he needs anything, just let me know.”

She smiled and nodded, and James continued walking.

There were other faces he nodded to, other individuals he wished he had time to properly speak to, but he didn’t have the time to exchange more than a few words with any given person. Most of them didn’t even receive that.

Finally, James found himself close enough to the giant board to read the brackets. He wasn’t actually right in front of it, but with his senses, he could read the small print from thirty feet away.

He frowned grimly as he spotted where his name fell. The section he was placed in felt like it had been chosen for him out of spite, by someone who knew him and wanted to cause him trouble.

In his bracket, there were a number of opponents whose names he didn’t recognize, but there was also Zhang Chāo, his closest ally who wasn’t actually one of James’s subjects. And in order to reach him, James would likely also have to defeat Han Jianguo.

This is such a fucking headache. What a mess…

Pú Tiě Shān, conversely, was nowhere near James. Perhaps in an opposite bracket. The same appeared to be true of the surviving Holy Kings.

I won’t have the chance for any more assassinations unless I defeat everyone in my bracket and they defeat everyone in theirs, he thought, groaning inside. I have faith in myself, but they have to win, too. Number of followers and fanaticism won’t help here, either. So I doubt it will happen for them.

He looked around to see if there was any System agent watching him, but he couldn’t spot any such person.

James couldn’t help but wonder if this was some sort of quiet sabotage as payback for him twisting the intent of the melee by turning it into a lethal, soul-destroying competition instead of a friendly space for feeling out future opponents.

Fair enough, if so, I guess. I may have gone a bit too far from their point of view, never mind that I didn’t really start it… Would the System really arrange these brackets specifically to sandbag me and my goals?

If that was what they did as a warning, he would have to ensure that he didn’t break future System events any more than was necessary.

James looked at the timer again.

[00:02:23]

I hope the rest of you are going and getting a look at your own brackets, he sent telepathically to all the monsters. Your responsibility now is to do as well as possible, to bring back glory for the Empire. No one had better lose in round one!

He sensed, at the edges of his peripheral vision, how the two monsters that had been nearest to him moved away from his position.

He smiled. Hopefully they’ll help each other with this preparation part, at least.

The monsters would be on their own for the actual Tournament, but if one got within sight of the brackets and simply broadcast the image to the others, that would do a lot to help the group’s odds of navigating the competition successfully.

James was realistic about his monsters’ chances. The nonhumans were in for a much stiffer competition than the humans were, by the design of the melee champion. They would not win or make it to the quarter finals or even the top sixteen. Not if the enemies involved were as powerful as James imagined.

After all, none of the monsters under his command would be a part of the Fisher Empire if they could have simply defeated him in a fair fight. And James didn’t flatter himself that he was stronger than the strongest monsters in the world.

Time ticked down slowly as he thought about the fighting that lay ahead. He had a plan for dealing with Han Jianguo already, and he had tried to make such a plan for Zhang Chāo. Realistically, though, he had to worry about the likelihood of defeat there. The vision of the future that Sister Strange had shown him weeks back had suggested not just a loss but death.

There was clearly some wiggle room in the predictions, but the fundamentals had not changed much. Zhang Chāo had the strength to kill James if the fight played out in the wrong way. He wouldn’t want to, given all they had gone through together in just the day they had known each other. It would be incredibly wasteful of their shared efforts.

But I do have to tread carefully. If I back him into a corner, and he uses that sword…

The Mandate of Heaven, Zhang Chāo had said it was called.

The blade itself was almost as deadly as the wielder taken separately.
