Samantha felt the control flow into her from the ‘simulation,’ felt the ‘nerves’ of her power as a deity grown into a vast network of many rich, raw materials. Despite the pressure of this moment, and her understanding that Ozra was a liar, and this was far more than ever let on… Despite it all, Samantha was calm. She even felt a tingle of excitement. She smiled.

This is what we were born for, Ashawein.

In this moment, she didn’t just draw on herself, but from the life of Ashawein, as synthesized with her own ancient understanding and artistry, blended with the modern, blended with the high-speed chaos absorbed from her experiences on Calrenazzod. Once again, though, she had time to do it, time to have fun doing it.

She began with beautiful natural landscapes, more or less fashioning a world this time, as a grand re-imagining of what she conducted on Calrenazzod. The core biome was simply idyllic nature, but she made more whimsical biomes, too — something she’d always loved and wanted to replicate was a ‘mushroom land,’ so she did, making huge colorful mushrooms hanging overhead, with vast smaller clusters every which way, and mud patches, and some fields of purple, wheat-like grasses. In numerous caves, the mushrooms were glowing.

Classic!

She populated it with weird, though somehow cute, fungal creatures and giant insects. They were quasi-realistic. It was a whimsical land, after all.

She fashioned ice locales, deserts, and mountains, all with an eye toward natural wonders like waterfalls, seemingly random stone structures, and stranger things, like walking trees and permanent rainbows. She made forests all of glittering crystal, and caves with wonders upon every step. She also fashioned treasures to be found, and set the stage for feats of challenge — like dungeons full of cool enemies!

She knew this was beginning to look a lot like a game world, and she didn’t care. If anything, it seemed like the ideal sort of world to live in compared to the reality she knew, and it was better than only some fluffy clouds to hang out on. She’d take some adventure most days, compared to that.

Some people might like Hard-Mode Heaven, after all. Ha!

Most of the default creatures were friendly, from small, cute things like cabbits to big grass-grazing cows, to colorful, flying giant fish-like things. Only those brave enough to dare the darker reaches would find the violence they craved, with certain ‘trophies’ as the incentive.

Layers, layers, layers. She added details and made new lands on a whim, growing the realm further and further out. Unlike a planet, her world was a continual plane, so each addition was like a paint stroke on a growing disk, more or less. Because she wanted a ‘front face,’ though, it was more of a half-disk.

And what if it never stops changing, never stops growing? What if it customizes continuously? Glorious!

Before she got too excited about that, she moved to the forefront of it all. Here was the grand beginning, and more or less the center. Only one thing could go here, only one place could be the most glorious thing outside of time: the City of Tomorrow.

Bigger and grander, so it went up; she made the walls and towers rise higher than ever. In greater numbers, she constructed the rollercoasters, theatre halls, palaces, and winding streets. Every construction she could imagine, pulled from Earth or pulled from the fantasies of her imagination, she layered into the ultimate city.

In the time available, life soon spawned in the place from within Ashamein’s realm. Furthermore, ‘lost souls’ previously cast around the world like ghosts gravitated to Heaven, some rediscovering who they were; others became someone new. In any case, their stories were their own, and the goddess was the aloof mastermind behind it all.

Once more, when adding the finer details herself became daunting, she began fashioning Angels to help her, delegating creation to them. Collectively, they added even lesser entities, basically ‘NPCs,’ much the same as she’d done on Calrenazzod.

This ‘paradise’ simulation never lacked for work from its creators, especially not from the numero uno creator. Samantha/Ashawein began to focus more on artistry and beautification, leaving the others to the work of adding new features, challenges, and so on — keeping it ‘fresh’ for the needier types.

Her greatest work was at the top of the great central palace. Here, she tediously made giant, life-like sculptures of the twenty-two Major Arcana of the tarot, as the pillars for the roof of the temple that served as the highest point in Heaven, and thus for all of the world, spiritually. The Fool about to stumble off a cliff, the Emperor on the throne, the Tower with its in-action calamities, she spent months, on and off, on the genesis. She was obsessed with getting it perfect, weaving spirit and passion into it rather than mere physical skill. It was Ashawein’s magnum opus, certainly.

‘It’ happened before the work was finished, smack in the middle of that tremendous work and toil. She was just standing back and smiling up at the awe-inspiring vision of the Wheel of Fortune, seen between the sculptures of clouds so realistic, it seemed they were floating wisps rather than stone. As she smiled in satisfaction, she felt a strange eruption in her spirit, a tug at her heart of hearts. Simultaneously, all of reality rumbled.

She heard a voice from deep within, yet far beyond. It was pained, and painful to hear, the notes like the strumming of a melancholic harp inside her. ‘This. This is what I wanted. More or less.’

As Ashawein felt weak and fell to her knees, struck down in complete shock and awe, she felt reality crack open-

No. Not reality, but an eye of it. A focal point, an awareness of it. The awareness of it, which meant the Creator Within.

She’d done it! But that eye was barely cracked open, as if still sleepy — as if it could close again, on a dime.

Ashawein blurted within herself, “Yes! Yes, Creator Within! We can do it together!”

Silence persisted for a moment, in which she wasn’t sure she was heard. But then there was a sigh. ‘No. That is a dead dream. A trillion times dead, even. I’ve finally left it all to die, as we deserve for the abominations we’ve spawned. It is the only end left to hope for. On to the next cycle of grief, perhaps. I can’t say.’

It was fully Ashawein’s time to shine here. She stood and shook her head up at the sky as she said, “I am but a small god compared to you, but I see, Creator Within. I see ahead, into potentiality, and there is an answer waiting.” She turned and gestured out to the world she’d fashioned, at large. At her idyllic Heaven. “And even here, you can see it, if only you look. Is it not the promise? Has the Machine Maker not succeeded, and proven you wrong? It churns, it runs, it lives! And do you even realize who has done it? Look, Creator Within, please, I beg you! Look!”

The eye seemed to open wider, and it was not what felt like a good thing. ‘Proven me wrong?’ Though the sudden turn might’ve been due to hubris, the eye nonetheless flickered to see out, and very suddenly, it widened, as a grand awareness bloomed. ‘Who is-?’ It flickered again.

This act, seemingly minor, was in fact monumental. That awareness had, in fact, flickered into the future.

On a dime, everything twisted and warped. All that would happen collapsed into a moment as the Creator Within, the Director, became awake and aware of a tediously arranged potentiality, and rather immediately, it became reality.

Samantha felt that awareness sweep over her, too. Not her as Ashawein, no. From there, from the past, to the future, everything fused and connected together.

Simultaneously with this, the Director mused to herself, lodged in her surprised moment of realization, and the echo reached Samantha. It seemed sardonic. ‘Ozra Bosk. Few Iterations have been the abnormality you became. You think yourself one in trillions, and then counting on another to be the same? Very well. A crude and cruel path, as one last gamble; a gap of hopelessness. Perhaps even from the beginning, there was no other way than your jarring little Orchestration.’

In some sense, and perhaps in truth, everything from that point ceased to be — one way or another. Reality, as existed by current rules, found an end somewhere, every moment at once.

Within the ‘challenge,’ memory unveiled to the end of Ashawein’s world. She continued her creation, even as in the world, she paid half a mind as her influence aligned a new and greater alliance to face the Pale Prince. She put together the ‘crew,’ as she had so many times before, and initially, her enemy was put on the back foot.

This time, she was assassinated quite suddenly by a dark knife in the back. Though the weapon was most certainly the artifice of Gerenna and the Pale Prince, he was not the one to deliver it.

She would never know who did.
