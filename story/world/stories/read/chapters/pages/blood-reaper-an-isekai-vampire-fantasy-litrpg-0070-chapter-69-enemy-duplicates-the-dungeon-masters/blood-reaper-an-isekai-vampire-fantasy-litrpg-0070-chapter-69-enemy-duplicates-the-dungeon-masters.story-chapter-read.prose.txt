The first tendril struck without warning.

A writhing shadow erupted from beneath Raven’s skin, lashing outward with the speed of a bullwhip. One moment the corrupted sentinel stood silent before them, the next his body convulsed as shadow manifestations tore free from his body. The ground where the tendril struck exploded in a shower of stone and dirt, carving a six-foot furrow in the lakeshore.

Ace dove to the left as another tendril whipped toward his head. The air by his ear whistled as death missed him by inches. The cold rush of displaced air tickled his neck—a grim reminder of how close the attack had come.

“Spread out!” he shouted, rolling to his feet as three more tendrils erupted from Raven’s torso.

The team broke apart in four directions, exactly as they’d drilled during their Highland training. Tara and Marcus sprinted right toward a rocky outcrop while Olivia executed a perfect backflip over an incoming tendril, landing in a low crouch twenty feet away. Ace drew Moonfall, its silver surface gleaming with cold light against the backdrop of the black lake. The weapon hummed in his hand, its energy resonating with his own.

Raven’s form changed completely in the seconds after his attack had begun. Where a broken, corrupted man had stood, something else now took shape—a humanoid figure wreathed in undulating shadows that boiled off his skin like smoke from burning oil. His eyes glowed with vile amber light, tracking each team member’s movement with unnerving focus.

“The Sisters send their regards,” Raven’s voice rasped, hollow and distorted as though speaking through water. “Nothing personal. Just the rules of the Crucible.”

Another volley of shadow tendrils exploded outward, this time in all directions. The black lakeshore erupted in a dozen places as the shadow weapons shattered stone and pummeled the earth. Fragments of rock sprayed into the air like shrapnel. Ace narrowly avoided two simultaneous strikes, his Phantom Step Resonance Boots giving him just enough warning through ground vibrations to sidestep at the last moment. The boots hummed against his calves, an extension of his senses rather than mere footwear.

Moonfall hummed in his hand as he attempted a counterattack, swinging at a tendril that ventured too close. The blade passed through the shadow construct like it was cutting fog, causing no visible damage. The sword’s energy flared in protest, its magic seeking substance where none existed.

Shit.

It looked like direct confrontation was suicide.

Time for a new plan.

“Defensive formation, boulder cluster at three o’clock!” Ace called out, pointing toward a collection of massive rocks near the lakeshore that offered some cover.

The team converged, ducking behind the natural barricade as shadow tendrils continued their relentless assault, cracking against stone with the force of artillery shells. Chips of rock exploded into the air as the shadows gouged deep furrows in their shelter. The impacts sent vibrations through the ground, into Ace’s boots, and up his legs, each one mapping the enemy’s position for him.

Ace pressed his back against the largest boulder, his breath coming in controlled bursts. The situation demanded immediate reassessment. Raven possessed overwhelmingly superior power—conventional attacks wouldn’t work. They needed intelligence, weaknesses, patterns.

“Look,” Ace said, his voice calm despite the chaos around them. “Direct assault is off the table. This fight requires intel first. Focus on observation. Find patterns, weaknesses, anything we can exploit.”

“And if he attacks us while we’re doing that?” Marcus asked.

“Don’t get hit,” Ace said.

“Awesome,” the telepath said with a huff. “Helpful.”

Ace just shrugged. It wasn’t like there were alternatives.

The cool stone against his back grounded him, keeping the tactical part of his mind in control while adrenaline flooded his system.

Closing his eyes briefly, Ace activated his hivemind ability, extending his awareness through the psychic network that connected him to the other members of his party. He felt their presence—three distinct consciousness signatures pulsing with adrenaline and fear, yet each maintaining combat readiness. Their emotions washed over him—Tara’s fierce protectiveness, Marcus’s buzzing mind magic layered with terror, and Olivia’s disturbing blend of excitement and artistic appreciation for the violence.

Stay connected with me through the hivemind, Ace ordered.

Through the mental connection, he felt Marcus’s acknowledgment—a sensation of focused concentration rather than words. The Mind Drinker tapped his crown, the purple crystal at its center pulsing with energy as he touched it. The gem’s light cast eerie shadows across his face, highlighting the determination in his eyes.

Ace felt the mental connection between them sharpen. What had been limited emotional awareness evolved into something more comprehensive—he could now sense their tactical observations flowing into a shared pool of awareness. The sensation reminded him of squad communications during night operations, but infinitely more intimate—no words needed, just pure information exchange.

It was weird as hell, but goddamn was it useful.

From Tara, there came a wave of protective determination. Her Echo Staff’s crystalline head glowed with crimson energy as she channeled her power. A translucent red barrier shimmered into existence around their position, not strong enough to stop the shadow tendrils entirely but capable of slowing them down. The air within the barrier felt warmer, charged with her healing essence that tingled against exposed skin.

Olivia’s consciousness, meanwhile, radiated controlled aggression and unparalleled focus. She shifted into a fluid stance, her movements liquid-smooth as she positioned herself at the edge of their shelter. Through the hivemind, Ace felt her intent to track attack patterns. Her eyes narrowed as she watched the shadowy tendrils dance, her body already memorizing their rhythm.

Ace manifested seven Duskblades that orbited around him like deadly satellites, ready for immediate defense should the need arise. Moonfall wasn’t helpful, sure, but daggers made of shadow energy stood a far better chance against Raven’s magic. The shadow-forged weapons spun silently, their edges drinking what little light reached their position.

“Sixty seconds of observation,” Ace ordered quietly so that Raven couldn’t hear. “Don’t counterattack unless absolutely necessary. Stay out of range, or—”

Before he could finish his thought, the next wave of Raven’s assault crashed against the other side of their rocky outcropping. This time, the blows carried force that Ace felt through the soles of his boots—concussive impacts that sent microfractures spiderwebbing through the boulders around him. The shelter groaned under the onslaught, the ancient rock pushed to its limits.

Ace peered through a gap in the clustered stones. Shadow constructs had formed in the air around Raven—duplicates of his own silhouette made from concentrated darkness that pulled all ambient light inward, creating pockets of absolute void. Five of these shadow-Ravens emerged from his body in wisps of black smoke, each one pulling itself from his flesh with an audible tear, like wet leather being ripped apart. Their featureless faces were little more than swirling vortexes of deeper darkness where eyes should have been.

“Well,” Ace said dryly. “That’s not good.”

“Talk about an understatement,” Tara muttered.

The new manifestations of Raven walked forward with unsettling independence, and each summoned its own deadly tendrils of darkness. They stalked the perimeter of the team’s shelter, their movements inhumanly fluid and their limbs bending at impossible angles. The corrupted duplicates left trails of dark residue that sizzled against the grass, eating into it like acid and leaving scorched footprints in their wake.

The temperature plummeted as Raven’s shadow energy intensified, and in less than a second, Ace’s breath fogged on the air. The cold burned his lungs with each inhale. A pressure descended upon them—not physical, but psychic—as the shadow constructs drained energy from their surroundings. Even Ace’s sense of sound dulled, and the waterfall’s roar became muffled.

Tara shivered violently, her skin prickling with goosebumps as her magic fought against the encroaching cold. From the corner of his eye, Ace saw frost forming on Marcus’s eyelashes, and the Mind Drinker’s breath came in sharp, painful gasps. Olivia’s exposed skin took on a bluish tinge, though her expression remained focused, almost entranced by the deadly ballet unfolding around them.

One shadow construct tilted its head at an unnatural angle, regarding their shelter with predatory curiosity. The darkness comprising its body rippled and contorted, its bones visibly shifting beneath its surface as it reconfigured itself into something more bestial. Its arms elongated into wicked scythes that dragged across the ground, carving furrows into the dirt.

The stone beneath Ace’s palm vibrated with a subsonic frequency that made his bones ache, a tactile warning from his Phantom Step Resonance Boots that the ground had become unstable. Shadow energy was corrupting the very earth they stood upon, transmuting solid matter into something malleable and treacherous. Hairline cracks appeared at their feet, leaking wispy tendrils of darkness.

Move, Ace ordered through the hivemind. Stay out of reach.

A burst of affirmation shot from each of his teammates, and everyone scattered. Information shot through Ace at an impossible speed, registering immense detail in mere seconds.

Tara launched herself to the right in a fluid roll that carried her beneath a sweeping tendril, the shadow construct’s attack missing her by mere inches. Through the hivemind, Ace felt her methodical assessment of the nearest shadow entity—her medical training cataloging how its form pulsed asymmetrically, the left side lagging behind the right as though the construct suffered from some sort of phantom arrhythmia.

Marcus, meanwhile, vaulted over a low boulder, his Mind Leech Crown pulsing with violet energy as he narrowly evaded two shadow tendrils that crashed into his previous position. Stone fragments exploded into the air, slicing shallow cuts across his cheek and neck. A burst of pain shot through his connection with the telepath, and the rush of agony sharpened his focus rather than diminishing it. The cold focus in Marcus’s mind contrasted sharply with the sweat beading on his forehead and the tremor in his hands. His eyes locked onto one particular construct that moved with subtle hesitation, its teleportation leaving behind more residual essence than the others—a sign of imperfect control.

On the opposite side of the lakeshore, Olivia flipped like water between attacks, her lithe form shifting mid-leap as shadow tendrils converged on her position. Her expression remained unnervingly serene as she navigated the lethal storm. Where Tara and Marcus sought cover, Olivia danced in the open, using momentum and perfect timing to stay perpetually half a second ahead of death.

The real Raven stood at the center of the chaos, his corrupted form pulsating with each attack. Dark fluid leaked from his eyes and mouth, leaving smoking trails down his chin. Given that he hadn’t moved since summoning the constructs, it appeared as though the strain of maintaining them all had rooted him in place. Despite this, Raven’s amber eyes tracked Ace specifically, recognition and something like regret flickering in the depths of his expression.

Ace stood his ground, the seven Duskblades orbiting his head like a deadly halo of shadow-forged steel. Each blade hummed with dark energy, their edges drinking in what little light reached them.

A tendril lashed toward his chest with killing force.

“Left,” Ace commanded under his breath.

The leftmost Duskblade shot forward with perfect aim, intercepting the tendril with a sound like steel striking wet leather. The impact sent a jolt through Ace’s shadow connection to the dagger, the feedback from weapon to wielder erupting into a coppery tang in the back of his throat. The blade shattered on impact but accomplished its purpose, and the tendril dissolved into wisps of corrupt shadow.

Three more tendrils whipped toward him simultaneously.

Ace didn’t flinch.

“Three, four, six.”

The numbered Duskblades flew outward in rapid succession, each intercepting its target effortlessly. The third blade cleaved through a tendril aimed at his throat, the corrupted shadow essence hissing as it contacted the shadow-forged weapon. The fourth Duskblade spun in a tight spiral, wrapping around a tendril before exploding in a burst of competing shadow energies. The sixth blade met its target head-on, both shadow constructs annihilating each other in a flash of cold light that left purple afterimages dancing across Ace’s vision.

His Phantom Step Resonance Boots hummed against his calves, tracking his opponents. A flash of warning shot up from his heels, and he pivoted at the last possible moment before something could take him out.

A shadow construct had materialized directly behind him, its claws raking through empty air where his spine had been a heartbeat earlier.

Again, Ace remained steady.

Calm.

Focused.

“Five, behind.”

The fifth Duskblade reversed direction without slowing and buried itself in the construct’s formless face. The blade sank deep before the entity teleported away, taking the weapon with it. Through that brief connection, Ace sensed something crucial—the shadow-blade had tasted blood within the construct, not just darkness.

It clicked for him, then.

These weren’t mere shadows but fragments of Raven himself, each one connected to the original by threads of corrupted essence.

Now that, he could use.

“Two, seven,” he commanded as more tendrils converged on him. “Come.”

His remaining Duskblades flattened and expanded, overlapping to form a temporary barrier of shadow-forged steel that absorbed the next wave of attacks. The shield held for precisely three seconds before disintegrating, but it bought Ace the moment he needed. His tactical mind grasped the significance of what he’d sensed: damage one construct severely enough, and the pain would reverberate through all of them, potentially creating a systemic vulnerability.

Instantly, the daggers returned to his palms. In a fluid arc, the shadow-forged metal sang through the air as he took aim—not at the nearest tendril, but at the nearly invisible thread of corruption connecting it to Raven.

Maybe this would work, and maybe it wouldn’t.

Time to find out.

His Duskblades writhed against his skin, hungry and alive, their edges jagged and uneven as they ached for blood.

Ace crossed his arms in front of his chest. He meant for the move to appear defensive, as though it were merely a ward against the next attack, but his target wasn’t the incoming tendrils.

With a sharp exhale, he slashed outward in an X-pattern, both arms extending fully. The twin daggers hummed as they cut through the air—then they sang with a high, keening wail as they struck the tether connecting the shadow construct to Raven.

It was a sound that shouldn’t have existed.

Shadow cutting shadow should have been silent, but the daggers shrieked like metal dragged across glass. The connecting thread resisted for a moment, stretching like hot caramel before the corrupted connections finally snapped. The severed ends whipped backward, leaking black goop that sizzled where it struck the ground.

A rush of painfully cold air swarmed Ace as the daggers absorbed fragments of Raven’s essence. The raw intensity of it all burned his palms with freezing fire that shot up his arms and into his chest.

Fifty feet away, Raven’s body contorted violently. His spine arched backward at an impossible angle, vertebrae visibly shifting beneath corrupted his skin. The scream that tore from his throat warped midway, dropping two octaves into something inhuman. Dark fluid erupted from his mouth, eyes, and ears in pressurized jets that hung suspended in the air for a moment before dissolving into shadow mist. The constructs nearest to the severed threads destabilized, their edges bleeding into the surrounding fog as their connection to their source fractured.

Feedback crashed through Ace’s nervous system—not pain exactly, but alien sensations never meant for a mind to process. A wave of corrupted shadow essence washed over him, cold and invasive, carrying nothing but raw emotion. Rage. Despair. Resignation. The sensation clawed at his consciousness, threatening to overwhelm him.

Ace staggered, the shadow daggers momentarily flickering in his palms as his concentration wavered. Blood trickled from his nose, and the honey-sweet tang bloomed across his upper lip.

The remaining shadow constructs pivoted toward him in unison, their movements jerky and uncanny, and they all charged him at once. They must’ve recognized the threat he now posed.

And there it was.

The weakness they had been looking for.
