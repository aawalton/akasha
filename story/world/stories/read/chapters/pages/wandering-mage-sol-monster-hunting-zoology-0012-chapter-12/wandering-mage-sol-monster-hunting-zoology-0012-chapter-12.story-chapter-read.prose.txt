“Hm, why doesn't she just float up there?” Briadon asked as he watched the elf girl climb.

“Perhaps she believes she is less likely to be detected like this,” Lisha answered.

“I just hope that elf knows what she’s doing. Her ankle is still injured.” He tilted his head as he watched her move from branch to branch skillfully, hardly making a sound. “Guess it's at least true what they say about elves and climbing trees.”

Lisha hit him once again.

Finally, Tressa reached the branch with the massive moth sucking on the tree's sap.

Once again, a rustling sound hit her ears, and she froze, looking over to the tree nearest them, and instantly dropped to the branch, lying low.

Perched on a branch in a neighboring tree was a bird camouflaged in with the fading light of the day. She swallowed hard as she took in the bird, hoping to see a long beak, only to find a smaller, sharper beak like that of a crow. Looking lower, its legs had scales instead of the standard rough skin found on most birds, with three toes facing forward and two facing back. She had never seen that before and wondered what kind of bird it could be.

It opened its wings, showing razor-sharp feathers and a random finger on the end like that of a bat. As it moved, it made the rustling sound again, and Tressa watched as the moths seemed to not care. To her, it sounded like wind in the tree leaves, so she figured that was the way the bird kept the moths from flying off. She observed its feet, wondering if those sharp talons could be used for climbing? She wished she had time to study it for a while, but she couldn’t do that now and only hoped she would cross paths with this type of bird again one day.

She bit her lip as she moved along the branch, and its eyes instantly moved to her. She froze where she was. Well, that seemed to have confirmed a theory. The bird responded to movement. She stared at its glassy eyes that gave a slight glint from the light the moths gave off. She stayed still as the sun truly began to disappear. The more the light faded, the more glow the moths gave.

“Shit,” she whispered. The bird had to be waiting for the moths to move. She watched as it stretched its neck up and shook its head, ruffling its feathers and once more creating that wind-through-the-trees noise. She had a wild idea of it having some kind of sonar sense, as its wings did have a bat-like feature, and disguised it with a common sound rather than a bat’s screech that could easily scare certain prey.

She did not have nearly enough evidence to support such a theory yet, but it was an interesting thought.

Slowly, she tore her eyes away and stared at the moths, taking them in.

Up close, the moth was even larger than she had realized, its body nearly the size of hers. Long tails trailed from the ends of its wings, and its colors shifted between brown, pink, and green in the dim light. The great false eyes stared from its wings, while soft fur covered its thorax.

Tressa knew to be careful not to touch the wings. The dust coating them carried a powerful neurotoxin. Typically, one wouldn’t be enough to paralyze, but it could still send her into hallucinations. She’d once considered using it as an extra ingredient for the Blue Mimic potion she’d planned on concocting, but she was worried it would be a little too dangerous, and she’d need to find a way to counteract the paralysis as well as safe experimentation methods.

Holding her breath, she inched forward, drew the knife, and held it up. She watched the moths for a moment, envisioning the strike. She would have to be careful about the moth and about the bird. She worried that once she moved too quickly, it might catch the attention of the bird.

“Tressa?” Lisha called up a little louder than Tressa would have liked.

The bird made a noise as it ruffled it wings and looked down at the two standing on the ground below.

“Shhhhh!” She pointed to the bird.

Lisha followed Tressa’s finger and swallowed.

“What?” Briadon asked as he watched Lisha stiffen.

“Scrollow bird.”

“What? One of them, where?”

“In the adjacent tree,” she whispered, trying not to move as they hunted based on movement.

He followed her gaze and saw the shimmering eyes in the darkness, glowing from the faint light the Farron moths gave off. “Shit.”

“I know.”

“If Tressa moves too fast, that thing will attack. How is she supposed to get the moth down?”

“I don’t know. I think she should abandon the attempt and just come back.”

“Shit,” Briadon said.

“Shit indeed,” Lisha agreed.

But Tressa wasn’t going to come back down empty-handed. She had deduced herself that it may hunt based on movement. The way it periodically rustled its feathers. That was clearly for a reason, and she had a plan.

She crawled a little closer, putting herself in striking distance of the moth. She let out a slow breath, watching the bird through the corner of her eye as she raised her dagger very slowly, being careful to time her movements between the shakes of its feathers.

She then lurched forward suddenly, plunging the dagger deep into the Farron Moth’s thorax.

The creature let out a thin, wet chitter as the blade sank in. Its wings twitched, but Tressa kept her grip on its lower body firm and her arms outstretched as she twisted the dagger, being careful to avoid touching its wings.

Then the wind-through-the-trees noise came again, and instantly there was movement in the corner of her eye.

She rolled off the branch, taking the moth with her, and saw the bird swooping down, talons extended to strike. They looked razon sharp and clearly could cut her open with ease.

Tressa held the moth towards the bird as she fell then opened her free palm and sent forth a pulse of magic, casting a basic wind spell.

The spell rustled harshly against the moth’s wings, pulling off the toxins and sending a gust right at the bird.

“Tressa!” Lisha cried in alarm from below, but Tressa ignored her.

She kept her mana flowing, and as the bird was less than a blink away from sinking its talons into her arm, she surged her mana, increasing the wind into a violent gust that threw it off track.

That much worked, as the bird was flung back, but that would only buy her a few seconds if the other part of her plan didn’t work.

As it extended its wings and tried to correct itself, she thought she really was going to be in for a fight now and likely an injury.

However, it sank unnaturally in the air, tried to correct itself, but it was clear that it was struggling. It tried to flap its wings, but it was out of sequence, causing it to roll through the air and hit a tree; then it seemed completely limp as it fell to the forest floor below.

“Yes! Take that!” Tressa cheered in Elvish.

“Tressa!” Lisha shouted below.

Tressa yelped and prepared to cast a wind spell to slow her fall, but Briadon snatched her out of the air, his big hands catching around her waist, and then he gently guided her feet back down to the ground.

“Tressa, are you alright?” Lisha asked, coming over to her instantly.

“It’s okay!” Tressa said, making the hand gesture.

“Briadon, quick bag that thing,” Lisha said, eyeing the moth still held in one extended hand.

“Right.”

He unfolded a bag from his belt and wrapped it around the moth rather than touching it himself, then tied off the top, sealing it inside.

Tressa was already snatching up her staff and throwing her satchel over her shoulder as she ran towards where the bird had fallen.

“What the hell is that crazy elf doing now?” Briadon huffed as he secured their bounty to his belt.

“Does it surprise you anymore?” Lisha asked as they calmly followed her.

They found her crouched over the corpse of the bird, carefully lifting one of its wings, sparkles dancing in her eyes.

“Ooooh, it’s sooo cool!” she awed in Elvish.

“Tressa, we should really get going,” Lisha said, but Tressa was hardly paying attention as she let go of the wing, then carefully poked the end of its neck feathers, finding they were rather sharp on the very end. Already, she was reaching into her satchel for the notebook.

“I mean, can we just leave her now?” Briadon asked.

“No! We’re not just leaving her!” Lisha said. She reached down and jostled Tressa’s shoulder, causing the elf to look up at her with wide eyes, her quill already touching the inside of a blank page.

“Tressa, we shouldn’t stay here. It’s getting dark, and we need to find a suitable camping place.”

“But research!” Tressa complained, getting the idea they wanted her to leave.

Lisha pointed up. “Um… dark… rawr,” Lisha said, making clawing gestures with her hands the way Tressa had done before.

Tressa giggled, but then looked around at the ever-darkening woods around them. She knew Lisha was right and frowned at the new creature below her. Then she had an idea, pointing at Briadon. “You um… Take it!”

“You want me to carry it?”

“Yes!” Tressa said, making an okay gesture.

Lisha laughed. “Just do it, Briadon. I doubt we are going to succeed at getting her moving otherwise.”

He let out a huff as he reached down. The large bird was comparatively small in his big hand. “Fine,” he complained. “Let's just get out of here already.”
