Alric leaned against the wooden rail of the grain section in the distillery, watching people work. The first washback was empty again. They had drained all the boiled water they used to swell the staves and help with cleaning. The important part was that all the staves around the washback were holding. The whole thing looked and functioned like an enormous barrel.

Since he arrived, the workers were scrubbing everything again. Parts that would have wort moving through, especially so. The troughs had been scrubbed, rinsed with boiling water, and the process repeated. The inside of the washback had undergone the same. People had climbed ladders to reach the upper sections. Even the wooden paddles that were to be used for stirring had been boiled and scrubbed; they might have been cleaner now than when they were made.

Alric grinned, reviewing that. The washback lid had been scrubbed as well, but it still had numerous openings. In the brewery, it was easy; a liquid seal solved the sealing problem at cask level, but at this scale it just wouldn’t work. Fermentation would be too aggressive with this volume.

He’d considered getting a piece of sailcloth made. It could be stretched over the washback to stop most things getting inside, but that would create a new set of exciting problems. The cloth could sag, or even the fermenting wort could splash onto it and cause more issues. For now, they were using cloth over the openings, but just the openings, largely in the hopes of keeping insects out.

Alric pulled his lips to the side.

No point guessing till we actually see this thing do a fermentation.

One of the workers called to someone else. Alric glanced at the troughs. They had added the lips Stromni suggested after the first test. The raised edges had stopped the worst of the splashing, but if wort dried on them, it could be another infection point later. More to clean.

While he was considering problems, his eyes drifted to the washback drain. They had made two drains. One so the washback could drain straight to the river; it was, however, about as effective as draining a bath with a cup. Having a drain ditch in the middle of the floor was a tripping hazard without proper grating, he knew. It was just too small.

In the end, they had to drain the washback via Maggie, which was less than ideal.

“Basket coming!” Someone shouted

Alric turned back to the washback to watch. One worker came forward with the thin basket of stones. The stones had been boiled to heat them, much like the bathhouse. They were then lowered into the insulating layer of water. Alric’s frown deepened. The inner and outer walls of the washback were the same height. So now there was a risk of splashing when moving the baskets. The solution was simple: the outer wall just needed to be lower. If it wasn’t structural, they could have just sawed off half of it, but that would now need to wait till spring. The outer wall could be low enough that the ladder wasn’t needed at all.

All small niggly little issues that he could only see once they were running. Alric had a list, and it was getting long.

He watched as the two workers who had put in the basket walked around the wash back, placing hands on various places and nodding.

“It’s not hot, right?” Alric called to the worker.

The worker shook his head. “No, Mister Alric, just warm. Warm all the way.” He said, gesturing to it. Alric nodded to him.

Still, the numbers are satisfying, at least.

Alric reviewed his notes.

3x 300L Boiler. 5x daily boil. 1500L per boiler or 4500L per day. Washback at 15,000L should take four days to fill- well, three and a bit; call it four.Eight days to ferment. Twelve days total.

He glanced over at the wall where Maggie’s room was. Maggie was three thousand liters. Maggie could turn the washback into low wines in ten days. The thumper helped a bit here, pushing the alcohol content up and catching some of the heavier flavors.

So, Maggie needs five days to work through the washback. An extra day for the cleaning, and then we need to do the spirit run and all the cuts. Twelve days.

Twelve days to make wash. Ten days to strip it. Two days for Spirit.

He nodded to himself. All of it was too orderly; he knew that, but he let himself enjoy it anyway. This was why he had insisted on two washbacks. While Maggie was processing one washback, they could fill the other.

The two days off can be an issue sometimes, though. Just need to plan around it.

Alric knew six boils were possible in a day, but it wouldn’t change anything other than overwork the staff. Five boils made a ten-hour day, so they staggered the start times. All the staff was staying in a lodging house in the warehouse district with some of the other brewery staff, but the distance wasn’t enough.

Alric glanced over at Stromni coming up the stairs. The dwarf had a big grin and was holding up his belt.

“Ya brewers making the nice smellin’ soup?” Stromni said, pointing at the boilers.

Alric grinned; it was the same smell as the brewery, just slightly less herbal, as this did not use hops. “It’s called wort.”

“That. That’s what I came ta ask.” Stromni said, nodding, “I been hearin’ wort, mash en wash. Mara said mash earlier, them’s call it wort.” Stromni waved a hand vaguely at the brewery workers. “Then ya keep callin it wash in the washback. I don’t speak brewer”

Alric grinned and pointed to the nearest boiler. “Alright, so technically. Grain mixed with hot water is mash, but it may as well be porridge.” Alric explained. When Stromni nodded, he continued. “So, we use a cloth sheet or bag, it holds the mash. We don’t have a big mash sitting around. Some setups work that way.”

“Never seen another setup, lad, so that bag is mash?” Stromni asked, looking over.

Alric wobbled his head to and fro, “Malt and water together would be mash. Don’t worry about mash. We’re making Wort.”

“Aye, clear as day that is. I reckon it’s easy now.”

Alric chuckled and pointed again. “When we pull the bag of grain out of the boiler, what's left in the boiler is wort. It’s a sweet, grainy water.”

“So bag out, en ya ‘ave wort?” Stromni said, nodding like he was adding a new fundamental principle of the universe. Then he pointed to the wash back. “So when ya pour it in there, it’s wash?”

Alric shook his head. “Still wort.”

Stromni slowly turned his head to Alric.

Alric laughed, “So, when I add my special ingredient—”

“Yer mysterious weirdness.”

“My important mysterious weirdness,” Alric said, holding up a finger. “When I add that, we leave it for eight days. Then and only then is it wash.”

Stromni narrowed his eyes but repeated it, “So mash don’t matter, them boilers make wort, ya do some mysterious stuff, then we have wash.” Stromni said, but then was quiet for a moment. “Feel like ya just making it more complicated than ya need.”

“I don’t even disagree. I didn’t make these rules. Wort goes in, sits for days, wash comes out.” Alric said with a smile and a half shrug.

“So what got ya glaring earlier?” Stromni asked, looking at the washback.

“One fly. It’s winter, so less of an issue, but one fly in there can destroy a whole batch,” Alric said, pointing to the openings of the washback. “If we're lucky, it will stink, and we will throw it out. If we aren’t, we mix it with other low wines and get something really bad at the end.”

Stromni glanced across and started rubbing his temples. “Ya made it so fragile?”

“No,” Alric said, shaking his head. “We will figure out something else in spring. I have a few ideas. Everyone has this issue, theirs are just smaller.”

Stromni shrugged; workers began calling, and people moved away from the washback. Alric and Stromni sharpened their attention, watching the boilers. Two workers began tipping the first boiler. Despite the issues, excitement stirred in both of them.

“Take it slow!” Stromni called, but Alric could see him bouncing on his heels. The workers nodded and continued.

Hot water poured into the wooden trough, sending steam rising. The flow gathered as they tipped further. They couldn’t see the flow from their angle, but a small splash happened around the first lip; it was significantly less. Then, at the second lip, it finally dripped into the washback before gaining momentum and splashing down loudly.

The air filled with the scent of warm malt. Both Alric and Stromni took a deep breath through their noses. Their grins widened.

“That smells great,” Stromni said, watching it.

“It does,” Alric said, still watching.

“Ya sure ya just want to fill ta half?” Stromni asked, his eyes still glued to the liquid going into the washback.

“I am. It gives us enough to do a few tests with Maggie.” Alric said.

“After the brandy, though?” Stromni asked.

“Whisky is not like brandy. Whisky is going to foam. We need to see how it behaves. We also can’t take too much malt from the brewery.” Alric said.

Stromni nodded, but neither of them was looking at each other. They watched until the flow slowed and stopped. The workers moved around, occasionally bumping into each other as they found their rhythm.

“You know.” Alric began, “All of this is brewing. If I added a herb to that, we’d have beer. It’s the same process, but everything past here.” Alric said, pointing toward Maggie, his finger following the trough before making a vague gesture toward the headquarters, “I just know the theory.”

“Aye, well ya know more than I do. The fun ‘ere is figuring as we go.” Stromni pointed out.

“That is true, but I just don’t want you thinking we won’t make mistakes. We will.” Alric said with a shrug.

“Aye, lad, I know it well.”

Alric leaned forward as the second splash began and more steam came out of the washback. That made him realize he hadn’t figured out a yeast strain yet.

Something clean, reliable, and forgiving. Temperatures would fluctuate a bit; the amount of sugars would be something he couldn’t measure.

US-05

He’d used it to make beer before but knew distillers used it as well. It was familiar and dependable. One less thing to worry about.

He realized as he stood there that right now, everything was going according to plan. The washback held; the troughs moved the wort properly without spilling much on the floor. Even the stupid insulation jacket was keeping it warm.

Tomorrow, when there was enough wort inside and the wort had cooled, he would add the yeast.

Then we will see how my maths matches up with reality.
