Chapter 78

Exploration

Exhausted and surprised by the short battle, silence settled over the bridge once more, broken only by the sound of the river flowing beneath them, and the water running through the two narrow streams along either edge of the bridge.

The tension hadn't disappeared, but it had changed for Shin and Mai. It was no longer just fear of the unknown, but a kind of constant vigilance, as if each of them were trying to understand the other while they moved through a place that offered no answers.

"We should keep moving," Shin said, picking up the bronze heads and taking the lamp in his hand.

The newcomer stayed between them, while Mai tried to comfort her a little. She still seemed frightened. The wound had disappeared completely, but something else appeared to be hurting her far more. She kept sobbing and staring at Mai's hand, the one helping her walk.

Shin watched her for a moment, but now that they had encountered threats, he was more alert. Where had that girl come from? Why was she in such a state, and how long had she been there? But more than that, why did looking at her feel as though something were crushing his heart? Who was she? Why hadn't any memories returned?

That night, they took shelter in a narrow passage and barely spoke.

In truth, only two of them could.

The girl who had emerged from the darkness didn't seem to remember how to use words properly. Whenever she tried to say something, her voice broke into awkward, fragmented sounds, as if language were something she had forgotten and was now trying to rebuild from scratch. But, one thing was clear: she was deeply nervous. She kept looking at them and touching them, as though her eyes couldn't believe what they were seeing. Did she think they would disappear if she looked away?

Shin and Mai exchanged a brief, worried glance.

How long had she been there?

Shin studied the girl's features. Something about them told him something, too.

"Use English," he told Mai, speaking in English himself.

Mai looked at him strangely, then hesitated before nodding. Shin suspected that the association came naturally once the concept was introduced in the brain.

At the sound of those English words, the blonde girl's eyes widened in surprise. Perhaps she knew the language. It was only a theory, since she looked different from Mai, but something inside him told him that Japanese wasn't the language he should use to speak with her.

"Can you… talk?" Mai asked carefully, keeping her voice gentle.

The girl barely nodded.

"What is your name?"

She opened her mouth and pointed at herself. "Ei… li…" she tried, then stopped. She frowned, as if even that small effort frustrated her. "Li… zz…" she tried again, with greater difficulty. "…beth…"

The sound came out incomplete and broken, but it was recognizable despite her little pointed teeth.

Mai tilted her head slightly. "Liz…beth?"

The girl nodded quickly, as if Mai had finally found the right answer. She took Mai's hand, nodding and crying.

"Liz…" she repeated, a little more clearly this time, though still uncertain. "…zy…"

She began to cry like a child, leaving the two of them at a loss for what to do. They had only just met her, and they had no idea how she might react. She had leaped to their aid and saved them, giving them a clue in the process, but how were they supposed to deal with her?

Shin watched in silence, while Mai simply held the girl's hands. Her fingernails were long and unkempt, but Mai wasn't afraid. Those were the hands that had rushed to save her. Even if it was only a simple gesture, she wanted to comfort her.

"Lizzy… or Liz… beth," Mai murmured, trying out both possibilities.

The girl nodded again, with a little more certainty, though her expression remained unsettled, as if her name were the only thing she could still hold on to inside her mind.

"That's all right," Mai said with a faint smile. "Liz, then. It's a pretty name."

Lizbeth lowered her gaze for a moment, tears in her eyes, her body trembling, but she didn't seem uncomfortable. If anything… she looked relieved. If that was her name, perhaps she hadn't heard it spoken in a very long time.

Through gestures and stammered sounds, she asked Mai to repeat her name. Mai glanced at Shin with concern, but did as she asked. The girl's face lit up as tears rolled down her cheeks, listening to Mai say her name again and again.

For now, that was enough.

Despite her difficulties, she managed to make herself understood. And although her words were often unintelligible, the girl had a peculiar voice when she pronounced syllables and meaningless words. It was almost hypnotic, lilting and fresh as a spring.

"Can I ask you something, Lizzy?" Shin asked.

The girl nodded nervously, her ears twitching attentively when she heard Shin say her name. He truly didn't want to overwhelm her, but he needed to ask.

"How long have you been here?"

She stared at him. Tears welled up in her eyes, and then she burst into uncontrollable sobs, clinging to both of them.

"L-Looong… looong tiiime," she managed to blurt out between sobs and sniffles.

Mai and Shin spent the next several minutes trying to comfort her and persuade her to loosen her grip. She had latched onto them. It took several gentle strokes from Mai, running her hand through Lizbeth's filthy hair, matted with mud and moss, before she began to calm down.

Only then did Shin try again, asking his questions slowly and carefully.

Lizbeth pointed whenever she wanted to indicate a direction. She shook her head when something wasn't safe. Sometimes she crouched down and traced simple shapes on the ground with her finger, crooked lines representing paths or spaces.

Above all, she knew the pyramid. Not as a complete map, but as someone who had lived inside it. When Shin tried to ask her about an exit, her answer was clear.

She shook her head, her expression darkened.

She didn't know. Or perhaps… there was no exit she knew of. Maybe she had tried to find one many times.

But when he asked about the creatures, her reaction was different. Her body tensed once more, and her eyes darted about.

She shook her head again.

This time, more emphatically.

"N-No…" she managed, struggling to speak. "No… w-woorse thiiings…"

They needed no further explanation. There were things inside the pyramid worse than the serpent. And that was more than enough.

Shin and Mai exchanged a glance. The prospect that the girl had never managed to escape was rather discouraging for the future of all three of them. But that didn't mean they would give up.

Lizbeth looked at them and must have noticed their worried expressions. She took both their hands and urged them forward.

She took the lead.

As they started moving again, Shin placed the black pyramid inside one of the bronze heads. It was a small trophy from their battle. He would have time to study it later.

Or so he hoped.

Lizbeth led them into one of the side tunnels, narrower than the main passages. Shin had to stoop even lower than usual to get through. The air was thicker there, heavy with moisture. They followed one bend after another as he tried to memorize the route. The place was becoming a labyrinth with every hour they spent inside, but they continued to follow her.

The next tunnel sloped gently downward, its walls more irregular and less carefully worked than those of the larger chambers. The smell of wet stone was constant in that part.

During one of their hurried stretches, Mai turned toward Shin.

"Are you okay…?" Shin looked at her in surprise. "You walk funny."

"My leg's bothering me a little. It's nothing."

"Hmm."

He hadn't lied, although the real reason for his discomfort was between his legs. Whenever he walked quickly, the constant rubbing of his testicles became uncomfortable. But he wasn't going to mention that. He was more interested in the discovery that there were other people inside the pyramid.

Even if there was only one.

After a while, Lizbeth stopped and pointed to a crack in the vaulted ceiling.

Water dripped slowly from it.

There wasn't much.

But it was clean.

Shin held one of the pieces from the bronze heads beneath the dripping water, collecting enough to avoid wasting any.

"This will do," he said quietly.

Mai nodded, visibly relieved and blushing.

Her skin was fair and pale enough for her veins to show through. Her blonde hair fell like a cascade of gold. She had firm breasts with rosy nipples and a soft abdomen.

They didn't want to seem like perverts, but she really was beautiful.

"C-come," she told them in that strange-sounding voice.

The tunnel continued downward for several minutes. Lizbeth moved with a confidence that contrasted sharply with the emotional fragility she had shown only moments earlier. She didn't need a light. She knew every bend, every change in elevation, every loose stone.

Shin suspected that wasn't the first time she had made that journey.

Eventually, they reached a much larger opening, beyond which stretched an enormous circular chamber.

There were four pools.

Each was several meters in diameter, carved directly into the stone floor. The water was so dark that it looked like an open hole leading somewhere else. Even the tiny droplets falling from the ceiling produced no visible reflections.

Shin surveyed the room at a glance. The carvings seemed to depict forests and rivers. Men and women emerged from the rivers and swam in their waters, or appeared to be playing among the trees while carrying instruments. Other humanoid figures rose from rivers with wings spread toward the sky. Still other walls depicted the sea and its marine creatures—whales, octopuses, and schools of fish among which humanoid figures swam.

Mermaids and mermen, Shin thought.

Lizbeth pointed at one of the pools. Then another. Then the first one again.

"W-water," she said. Her pronunciation was still clumsy, but now it was much clearer.

Mai approached cautiously. "Did you drink from here?"

Lizbeth nodded. "M-Muuch. W-Wheen I a-arriveed" She placed both hands on her stomach. "Very… thiirsty."

Lizbeth's smile faded. Her eyes drifted toward the depths of the pool.

For several seconds, she didn't answer. Then she raised a hand and pointed at the water.

"I-It… a-attacked mee." The words came out almost in a whisper.

"Something came out of there?" Mai asked, taking a few steps back as Shin went on alert. Lizbeth nodded much more emphatically. She crouched beside the edge and moved her fingers through the air, mimicking something climbing out. "Hand…" She slowly raised her arm. "Liike this." Then she bared her teeth.

Mai blinked.

Lizbeth touched one of her fangs with the tip of her finger. "I-It biit mee."

Shin frowned. "It bit you? What bite you?"

Lizbeth nodded. Then she opened her mouth. Her teeth were small, but definitely more pointed than before. They weren't the teeth of a person who had simply gone too long without proper care.

They were tiny, but clean white fangs.

Mai studied them in surprise. "Were your teeth always like that?"

Lizbeth shook her head. "Beefore… no." She touched her teeth again. "A-after bite."

Shin exchanged a glance with Mai but said nothing. They thought they understood what they were hearing. Lizbeth was telling them how she had ended up here.

Lizbeth pointed toward the pool again. "It… p-pushed mee." She brought her hands to her throat. "Water." Then she closed her eyes. "Much."

"Something tried to drown you."

Lizbeth breathing quickened.

Mai approached and took one of her hands. "You don't have to keep going if you don't want to."

Lizbeth shook her head. She didn't seem frightened. "I… e-escape." She pointed upward, then gestured with both hands as if she were swimming.

"And the thing that attacked you?"

She fell silent.

"The creature?"

Lizbeth nodded. "S-spasms." Her whole body shuddered as she mimicked them. "A lot." She hugged herself, then let her arms fall to her sides. "D-Died."

Shin looked at the water again. "It died after biting you?"

Lizbeth shook her head. "A-After." She held up several fingers. "Maany hours… came out… water. Singing… looking at mee. U-Until died… crying."

"Singing?" Shin asked.

Lizbeth nodded.

Shin looked at the carvings around the chamber.

Then Lizbeth pointed toward an area of the floor, away from the pool.She led them several meters farther.

There, among damp stones and remnants of rotting vegetation, lay something that at first looked like an irregular rock formation.

Shin crouched down.

It wasn't rock. It was a skeleton.

Mai covered her mouth with one hand.

The remains retained a humanoid structure, but even after so many years, it was difficult to imagine that had once been a living creature. Two dark cavities stared into nothingness. Its teeth were pointed too.

Lizbeth stayed behind Mai. She didn't want to get any closer.

Shin, however, knelt beside the remains. Small stones and larger rocks had been arranged around the skeleton. Had Lizbeth put them there?

"How long?"

Mai looked at the remains. "I could have guessed that."

Lizbeth watched them in silence.

Shin continued examining the remains as new ideas and concepts emerged from the darkness of his mind.

"Decomposition depends too heavily on the environment to serve as an accurate clock. There's constant humidity here, very little air circulation, and probably a lot microorganisms. All of that could accelerate or slow down the process. But even so…" He turned it between his fingers. "Years have passed."

Shin felt something sink in his stomach.

"How do you know that?" Mai asked.

Shin turned to look at them. "I have no idea."

It was the simple truth. All of it had come to him while he studied the skeleton and its surroundings in the wavering light of the oil lamp.

"How many?"

Shin shook his head slowly. "I can't be certain." He looked at Lizbeth. "But I'd say more than six. Six years… maybe." Lizbeth didn't seem to understand the number. Shin continued. "It could be seven. Or ten. Maybe even more. We don't have enough points of reference."

Mai looked at the girl. Six years. At least. This girl had been alone here for all that time.

Lizbeth finally approached the corpse.

"Ate me."

Lizbeth pointed toward another part of the chamber. There were dried remains of small creatures, shells, and tiny bones piled up in one corner.

The way she moved.

And perhaps her teeth, too.

Shin looked around the room and towards the doorway. It was best to be prepared for any danger, but at that moment he noticed that the path in the distance seemed to vanish, as if it were fading away. In the room where they were, a light mist also seemed to be gathering at the top. And at that moment, the ceiling looked to him as though the crystals emanating from the rock were like an image of a starry sky.

Condensation? he wondered.

At one point, he noticed something about Lizbeth and leaned closer to get a better look.

Lizbeth was asleep.

But she was crying silently.

Tears slipped down her face without changing her expression very much.

Shin sighed softly.

He rested a hand on her head and gently stroked her hair, careful not to wake her.

Then he slipped his free hand into the bronze head with the pinecones and took out the pyramid fragment. It was nothing more than a carefully detailed piece of stone. It didn't appear to be anything else.

Lizbeth stirred, but she no longer seemed to be crying. She had calmed down and wrapped one arm around his stomach and the other around his back.

It was a little uncomfortable, considering that she was naked, but it was probably best not to think about that.

Shin sighed as he watched the lamp's flame.

He didn't know who she was.

He didn't know who he was.

Or who Mai was.

Or why they were in that place.

The questions kept piling up, unanswered.

He and Mai shared one certainty: they had somehow vanished from somewhere else.

Earth.

But in that moment that wasn't the most urgent problem.

What mattered was finding a way out.

Before the pyramid turned them into something else.
