“Right,” I say with a clap, interrupting the building argument between Valence and Elarion over exactly which wine would be best right now. Given that we don’t have any of it, the question is irrelevant and, though it started off as friendly fencing, families have just been brought in which is never a good sign.

Silence falls as all pairs of eyes come to rest on me. I glance over at the scribe sitting in the corner, a writing board providing a firm surface on which he’s placed his parchment and pen. The latter begins scribbling away busily as I begin to speak. “Let it be known that it is the third day of the twelfth month of the year, and Explorer is in ascendance. Please take note as well that this is the eighth council meeting since we made landfall.”

I glance around the table and smile at everyone, doing my best to make it seem genuine, despite my worries.

"Welcome, everyone," I continue, then gesture at the side of the table. "As you may have noticed, we have some guests today who have some matters they wish to raise for our attention. Healer Thessalonor, would you like to begin?"

Thessalonor steps forward, bowing his head briefly to me, then to the others.

"Thank you, my lord, my lords. I would like to request that more focus be placed on finding alternative methods of treating sickness, rather than relying solely on my healers. We are simply beyond capacity as it stands, and it is impossible to train our apprentices fast enough to meet the demands on our time and effort. My fellow healers and I are working twenty – sometimes even twenty-four – mark days, and we are only barely keeping up with the cases brought to us."

"Why are you so overburdened?" Layton asks in surprise. "Are so many people truly that sick? I hadn’t been aware of it."

"With all due respect, my lord, I believe you spend more time among the Classers than with the common populace," Thessalonor replies politely. "Classers are far hardier, especially those who have committed points to Constitution beyond thirty, as most Warrior types do. Apart from the injured who come to ask for our help after returning from a patrol, the vast majority of cases we treat are among the common people."

“What kind of cases?” Elarion asks, his eyes narrowed.

“Fevers, allergies, general malaise. The city planning did well to ensure that waste is disposed of in ways that do not risk contaminating the food or water supply – an outbreak of disease would push our capacity over any possibility to manage. However, nutritional deficiency does not help – people become sick more easily and it takes longer to treat them.”

Valence shifts a little uneasily.

“I hate to suggest this but…do all cases you treat really require a healer’s care? Back home, most would never see a professionally-trained Healer in their lives. Perhaps your outreach efforts – and our policy of allowing all people to access healers freely,” he sends me a quick glance, “may be causing this strain on your resources. I understand it would be…challenging to change things now, but perhaps we could provide you with assistants who will turn away the least urgent cases, allowing you to prioritise better."

I inhale sharply, knowing that this suggestion won’t go down well. I have found in Thessalanor a companion spirit in the desire for everyone, no matter how much money they have or the circumstances of their birth, to have the opportunity of a healthy, happy life – if they chose to seek it. Still, I stay silent, allowing Thessalanor the opportunity to speak for himself.

Sure enough, Thessalonor’s face tightens slightly and when he responds, there’s a cold edge to his polite tone that wasn’t there before.

"Heir Fell, you are correct that the average non-classer on Moriax does not tend to consult a healer. Indeed, due to limitations of appointment availability and financial difficulties, they do not do so even in serious cases where their health is at considerable risk. However,  with all due respect, I would not consider that a valuable model to follow. The result of such inaction is often permanent harm – or death.”

“But if you’re overburdened with cases, can’t you turn away those with just a cough or sniffle,” presses Valence.

“We already spend the minimum of time on those with minor ailments, Heir Fell,” Thessalanor continues frostily. “However, many illnesses and detrimental conditions first present with minor symptoms. If we simply turn away all those with a cough or sniffle then we may fail to spot a serious disease or malaise until it is far more advanced, and thereby more difficult to treat.”

“I’m not–”

“Indeed,” continues Thessalanor, speaking over the heir, the rudeness making Valence flush angrily. “Given how limited our population is, I would venture to say that our non-Classers are still not consulting us enough, conditioned as they are to the idea that Healers are a last resort.” His glare is sharp enough to pin the Lesser House heir in place – I’m glad not to be the recipient of it.

“I have never dissuaded anyone from seeking the healer,” Valence objects irritably.

“I can believe that,” Thessalanor replies with irony. “In fact, if we look at the healers’ records, I think you would find that Classers approach us disproportionately frequently considering that they are significantly in the minority. Especially those in hunting parties who should ask their comrades with healing Skills for help before approaching one of us.” Valence doesn’t quite manage to hold his gaze at the pointed comment, instead glaring at a spot just above his shoulder.

Noting how neither of them seem inclined to move from their positions, I decide to step in for the sake of the meeting.

“Heir Fell, Healer Thessalanor, I believe we have taken the point. The healers are overburdened, but deterring people from visiting them isn’t the answer. Healer Thessalanor, do you have any suggestions of how this can be solved?”

“Increasing the number of healers, removing the exacerbators of the illness such as better nutrition for everyone, or finding more efficient means of treatment," he answers promptly.

“You might as well ask for the moons,” Valence comments, just a little too loudly to be ignored. Thessalanor sniffs, not deigning to glare at him – unlike most, I wouldn’t say it’s just that he doesn’t dare.

“Valence does have a point. All of those are worthy objectives, but you indicated the situation is urgent.” I pause for a moment, thinking back to hospitals on Earth. “Perhaps we can ask for some volunteers to help? They could do some of the more menial tasks that the healers do like preparing bandages or poultices. With more instruction, they might even be able to identify the most common ailments and give out basic treatments themselves – after a time of monitoring by a healer, of course. That would then allow the healers to focus on tasks that only they can do.”

I warm to the idea even as I explain it. I know there is the risk of something being missed, but if the healers become too overburdened, the threat to the whole settlement will be far higher.

“That…could work. Thank you for the suggestion, my lord,” Thessalanor replies slowly.

As he steps back, Layton leans forwards with a frown.

“Is our food situation so serious that it’s already making people more vulnerable to illness?”

I grimace, then nod.

"I hate to say it, but we’ve just about run out of fresh produce. We are now entirely reliant on meat and fish sourced from the local area – and those seem to be reducing in quantity as well," I say, looking toward Josiah and Valence.

Valence looks down, the final traces of his earlier angry flush disappearing, while Josiah looks faintly worried.

"We’ve been trying," he replies quickly, "but the fish just aren’t biting. We’ve been discussing leaving the bay to try new areas. That was what I wanted to speak to everyone about in this meeting."

"It’s the same problem for us," Valence adds. "The local beasts seem to have learned how to avoid us, and fewer and fewer are falling prey to our traps. I fear we’ll need to move into another territory to source enough food for our population."

"Why aren’t we producing our own food?" Josiah demands. "Isn’t that what the farmers are here for – animal husbandry and growing crops?"

Elarion leans forward with an intent expression.

“We are doing our best. We have been testing crossbreeding local plants with our Moriaxar equivalents, as well as attempting to find local plants that we – and the animals – can eat safely. If we do not find good options soon, then we will need to slaughter the non-carnivore beasts and eat them before they lose too much of their weight, or return them to the transport cages to buy us time.”

“We have been making some progress,” I contribute, trying to reduce the current of panic I can feel building around the table. It won’t help any of us if the members of the council believe all is lost. Several pairs of eyes come to rest on me, clearly hopeful. “The Energy absorption tablet functions outside a living body. The downside is that it has to be in something liquid, warm, and highly acidic, meaning that the food afterwards isn’t terribly palatable. And the alchemists are continuing to work on adjustments to improve the treatment.” I nod at Sera Kerst, who is waiting patiently to the side of the room.

I almost hear the sighs several people visibly release.

“Is that why my patrols haven’t been receiving enough healing potions recently?” Valence demands, turning his ire on the Alchemist next.

I raise an eyebrow at her, River standing beside her in silent support.

"Sera Kerst, would you like to respond to Valence’s question?"

She steps forward and bows her head to both of us. "Of course, my lord, Heir Fell. The truth is that we are needing to ration potions due to our current difficulties in producing them.”

“What difficulties now?” demands Valence with a weary note. I have to wonder whether his anger is mostly to cover up fear. Honestly, I wouldn’t blame him – I’m more than a little worried myself. I knew creating a settlement would be a challenge, but I wasn’t expecting the land to be as hostile as it is to all of our attempts.

“Our difficulties are much the same as those the farmers are facing. Just as they are struggling to produce crops that do not poison their consumers, we are struggling to produce potions that function as they should. Indeed, our discovery of which plants can be used to treat Energy poisoning remains one of our only successes to date – though I assure you that is not for lack of trying.

"Like the Healers, there are few properly trained alchemists among our settlement population – and even fewer trained in research. Most of us are skilled in using, creating, and prescribing established potions that have been refined over centuries, not in discovering new combinations or developing new methods. It is a different skill set.

"We are doing our best, but that is why I am here: to request that some of the mage researchers – those who specialise in research – be assigned to our team to assist us in that capacity."

“That would mean reassigning them from my domain,” Elarion replies, his eyes narrowing again.

"Can we afford to do that?" Valence asks his fingers tapping at the table fretfully. "Everything I’ve heard today indicates that we need our farming to be effective if we are to survive."

"Can we afford not to?" Layton counters, though I see the tightness at the corners of his eyes. "So much depends on the Alchemists – or do you want to find out what happens when we run out of healing potions? Because I assume those are also among the ones that have not yet been rediscovered."

Sera Kerst bows her head slightly to Layton. "The most powerful healing potions – those suitable for Classes above level twenty – we have managed to replicate. However, for everyday healing potions, we are still struggling to find a combination of ingredients or a process that allows them to be safely consumed by the less resilient."

She glances toward Thessalonor. "I suspect we are facing the same issue as with food. The plants in this area are simply too powerful, and none of the methods we have tried reliably reduce their Energy content to manageable levels."

I’ve been listening to the debate, but now I step forward again.

"I suggest that we reassign the mages at this point," I say.

Elarion sends me an unreadable glance.

"Farming is important, I agree, but unless we have the right kind of potions to hasten plant growth, then finding crops to grow is only half the solution. Meanwhile, developing a repeatable strategy that allows our Alchemists to supply the full range of potions our people need seems to me the more urgent priority, especially given how overwhelmed the healers are already. Losing access to their more effective materials isn’t going to help in the slightest."

I pause, then continue.

"At the same time, food is still a pressing issue. Josiah, I agree that you should take the fishermen out to sea – but ensure you take sufficient precautions. That may mean bringing non-research mages, especially Water Mages, or sailing with multiple ships to give you more options if something goes wrong.” I can only hope that this will help the continued dissatisfaction among the ship crews rather worsening it.

“And Valence, if you need to leave the territory, so be it, but be careful – here, we have Bastet’s influence that stops the more powerful beasts from attacking. In another territory, that won’t be the case.”

"As for me, I will work with the healers to try to reduce their workload where possible, and to continue exploring ways to mitigate energy poisoning – ideally allowing people to consume local food without requiring two days of treatment afterward."

I look around the table.

"Does anyone disagree with this course of action?"

As Elarion leans forward to speak with discontent written on his features, I can’t help but lift one hand to my face and rub at one temple to ease the burgeoning headache there. And to think – I was hoping Tiria and I might be able to go for another walk after the meeting tonight after the success of the previous one. Fat chance – I’ll be lucky just to get enough sleep.
