Four hours after slaughtering the giant remipede, Gael and the rest of the Saint’s Hands were already sailing north. The Fishermen had needed less than an hour to gather twenty of their stranger vessels from across Wraithpier, and Gael had spent most of that hour learning that there were apparently several categories of fishing ship beyond ‘boat’, ‘large boat’, and ‘absurdly expensive floating fortress Fergal would never let him forget buying’. The Fishermen’s ships were narrower things built for harsher waters, with reinforced undersides, strangely curved bows, engines buried deep beneath their decks, and enough mechanical contraptions welded along their railings that Gael had given up identifying a while ago.

Almost a hundred Saint’s Hands were packed aboard the little fleet, most of them six-armed veterans carrying chitin-edged blades, harpoons, and boarding hooks. One Fisherman helmed each vessel, which was all the crew they required. The ship Gael, Maeve, Jin, and Vivi were on was one of the larger vessels in the fleet, commanded by the salt-hatted man as they streaked across Wraithpier’s northern canals.

Buildings whipped past. The hull skipped over waves hard enough to make loose crates hop, and every time they rounded a bend, half the Saint’s Hands leaned instinctively toward the opposite railing while the Fisherman at the helms laughed and turned harder.

“We’re nearly at the northern shore!” the salt-hatted Fisherman shouted over his shoulder. “Last chance fer ye southern grave-crows to decide the sea’s got enough corpses already! Ye still want out there?”

Gael sat cross-legged against the railings, and he raised his bottle without looking up. “Yep! Just keep going!”

“Fine by me! Dead men pay no dockin’ tax!”

The rest of the Fishermen barked something that may have been laughter, may have been a curse, and threw the wheels around another corner. Gael returned his attention to the more important matter: blood. Specifically, the irritating necessity of possessing enough of it.

Maeve had used an obscene quantity of hers vaporizing the giant remipede, and Jin had dumped enough of his into Gravesend Deep to convert half the lake into thick mud. Accordingly, all four of them were now seated in a line against the railings, forcing all sorts of foods, medicines, concentrates, and whatever else Gael could justify as blood-replenishing assistance into everyone’s stomachs. A wicker basket between the four of them contained black pudding, liver paste, redroot biscuits, dried bloodfruits, and even several stopped bottles containing liquids so chemically dense one of them had begun corroding its label outside the glass.

Oh, the taste of these foods and medicines wasn’t anything to applaud, but they did need to replenish their blood, and they had to do it quickly. So Vivi stared at the vial of raw chemicals in her hands, took another heavy gulp of it, and immediately coughed and hacked into her sleeve.

“These are… dreadful,” she choked, her voice raspier than he’d ever heard it. “How is it even possible that each one tastes worse than the last? Why does medicine always have to taste like… coins soaked in burnt onions?”

Jin sat beside her, chewing through a dense slab of some liver-and-herb loaf with the expressionless determination of a prisoner who knew complaining would only lengthen his sentence, but Saintess, even Jin couldn’t completely hide the disgust every time he swallowed one of Gael’s medicines. A tightening of an eyelid here, a clenching of a jaw there, it was obvious enough he hated the foods just as much as Vivi did. He just didn’t say anything about it.

… Weaklings, Gael thought. Both of you have yet to become true Bharnish, I see.

Because Maeve, meanwhile, had already finished two cakes, one full bottle of chemicals, and most of the salted meat without visible distress. Years of living with Gael had destroyed whatever ordinary standards she once possessed for medicinal flavor, so he grinned as she uncorked another dark vial with her teeth, drank half, and passed the rest to him. Gael accepted it happily. Their bloodshackle pulsed faintly green between them as he continued feeding fresh blood across to her.

“Oi, doc!” Evelyn shouted from the vessel beside theirs, and Gael craned his head mid-drink to see her, Liorin, Cara, and Fergal leaning against their ship’s railings. Rebux, Bran, and Nixy were on their ship as well, though they were considerably quieter. Probably because they were freaked out of their minds sailing out onto the open sea. “Are we really doin’ this today? We just blew the biggest bloody Myrmur we’ve ever seen into soup hours ago! All of you look half-dead already, so… would it kill us to eat lunch first and then invade the mad scientist later?”

“It might!” Gael called back. “The Breeder sent that remipede after us because he knew we were getting close to him! He wanted it to kill us, obviously, but it was probably also meant to just distract and delay us while he prepared to evacuate his lab! If we give him even one more night, he might just disappear on us! We have to get his ass before then!”

The four of them on the other ship didn’t look completely comfortable with rushing headfirst into another battle so quickly, but they understood his logic. The four of them on his ship, however, were the picture of complete resolve and determination. Jin, at least, kept his eyes narrowed as he scarfed down his final medicinal loaf of bread and pulled his gas mask back up.

“I’m ready,” Jin said, voice low and firm. “We’ve chased him long enough. Let’s take the man down.”

Vivi looked at him for a moment, then straightened beside him despite still looking slightly pale.

The far northern docks appeared ahead several seconds later, and Gael’s amusement faded by half a degree when he saw what waited beyond them. Wraithpier ended almost abruptly there. The last crooked houses and piers gave way to black seawater stretching beneath a sky so thick with storm clouds that midday didn’t even look like midday. Heavy rain slashed sideways across the open sea. Thunder rolled beyond the horizon. Waves rose high enough that even from within the protected canals Gael could see the white crests smashing into one another with enough force to probably dent even his floating fortress.

Slightly nervous, he looked back at the salt-hatted Fisherman and gave the man a grin.

“Question!” he called. “How, exactly, do you guys plan on sailing us through that?”

In response, the salt-hatted Fisherman reached beside the helm and pulled a large lever. Something enormous immediately thumped under the deck, and every person aboard lurched to the side as the ship tilted back abruptly.

The fuck?

Are we… taking off?

Gael’s stomach remained behind for approximately half a second before the ship lifted off the canal, hovering several meters above the water while hidden thrusters beneath the hull screamed against wind and spray. Maeve caught Gael by the shoulder before he toppled backward, and Vivi grabbed Jin. All four of them quickly looked over the railings, then, to see all nineteen other ships in their fleet were slowly rising into the air, the Saint’s Hands above them every bit as shocked and surprised as they were to see the hovering ships.

“... We ain’t barbarians, y’know?” The salt-hatted Fisherman laughed. “Full speed north, Fishermen! Let the sea throw fists at someone else!”

With that, their fleet accelerated into the storm, and soon they left Wraithpier completely behind the curtains of rain. Rain hammered Gael’s mask. Wind clawed at his coat and hat, threatening to rip both away from him, but somehow he managed to find his footing in the storm and staggered to his feet, gripping the railings with one hand.

“We’re looking for an underwater laboratory!” he shouted to the Fishermen. “It shouldn’t be too far from shore, otherwise resupplying the place would be miserable, so if you lot would circle around until we spot some unnatural lights under the sea, that’d be—”

“Unnatural lights?” the salt-hatted Fisherman cut in.

“Yeah! What about them?’

“I’ve seen some before.”

Gael blinked. “You have?”

“Aye. There’s been a weird glow under the sea for a few months now, over thataway.” The Fisherman pointed northeast, and to back him up, several Fishermen on the vessels behind them also nodded and shouted they’ve been seeing the same lights recently. “We thought maybe it was just a giant lanternfish. We was gonna investigate a while back, but then Old Marn caught a thirty-year-sturgeon and we all got busy. Wanna check it out?”

Gael stared at him for several more seconds before nodding vigorously.

“Take us there!” he shouted.

The fleet changed course immediately. Not long after, all twenty vessels slowed and clustered above one section of the sea, about three or so kilometers off the northern docks of Wraithpier. Once the ship stabilized, Gael and the rest of the Saint’s Hands immediately leaned over the railings and squinted down through the rain.

At first he saw nothing but churning black water, but then he pinched, turned, and adjusted his night vision lenses.

Far below, faint points of light glimmered beneath the sea. Straight lines of light. Repeated intervals of blinking lights. Pale, there even seemed to be shadows of artificial geometry down there. Obviously, they weren’t natural formations.

Good thing we enlisted the Fishermen’s help, huh?

I thought we might have to look for the lab all by ourselves with nothing but vague recollections in our heads, but…

Gael grinned and turned around to clap.

“Strike teams one to six! Get ready!”

Preparation became immediate noise. Of the hundred Saint’s Hands scattered across the fleet, sixty of them moved and jumped towards the enormous diving bells suspended along six of the twenty vessels, weapons strapped down and armor checked. Fergal himself leaped over to Gael’s vessel, and then he, Gael, Maeve, Jin, and Vivi climbed into the diving bell attached to their ship. The other five teams, each led by one of Fergal’s Five Fingers, entered their separate bells and locked the hatches behind them quickly.

While the others climbed into the giant iron bell, Gael paused at the hatch and looked back at Cara, Evelyn, Liorin, as well as the children from Miss Umbrage’s clinic.

“Remember: you have to stay topside and coordinate the fleet!” he shouted. “If anything suspicious tries surfacing, just assume it’s the Breeder and make his day extremely unpleasant!”

Cara nodded. “Try not to drown!”

“Perfect!” Then Gael looked down at the salt-hatted Fisherman. “And you’ll protect us, won’t you? Look after the rest of my men, and…”

He trailed off, though, when he saw the Fisherman had already dragged a chair towards the railing and cast a fishing line into the storm. Across the fleet, the other Fishermen were also doing exactly the same thing.

“Aye, aye, whatever you say,” the salt-hatted Fishermen muttered, waving him off. “Now go and stab yer mad doctor. Fish’re bitin’.”

Gael smiled. Then he climbed inside his giant bell and sealed the hatch.

The cramped iron interior contained a few benches, a few straps, several portholes, and approximately no instructions on how to operate the damn thing. But then Gael spotted a lever by the side of the hatch. Maeve, Jin, Vivi, and Fergal spent several moments trying to figure out how to buckle themselves in their seats before Gael pulled the lever, and the bell—with a heavy groan and screech—plummeted from the side of the Fisherman’s ship.

Everybody immediately discovered why the straps existed. Gael hit the floor laughing while Maeve landed against a bench. Jin struck the wall shoulder-first, Vivi sprawled across him, and Fergal somehow remained mostly upright through sheer refusal to participate in gravity.

“Couldn’t have given us a warning first?” Maeve said, scowling up at him.

“Nope.”

Black water swallowed them quickly outside the portholes, but the humor didn’t last long. As they began sinking through the bubbles and oily waves—with the other diving bells plunging in after them one by one—shadows started moving around them. Huge ones, and there were dozens of them.

One of them growled outside the bell. It came through the water as a low, warped snarl that vibrated through the iron shell and made everyone inside go still at once. Gael immediately whipped his head towards the nearest porthole just as another shadow slid past beyond the glass, too large and obviously too fast to be any ordinary mutated fish. Then another appeared beneath them. Then three more above. Within seconds, the darkness around their diving bells were completely saturated with moving silhouettes.

Maeve pushed herself fully upright and stared through the porthole, all traces of irritation gone from her face.

“Myrmurs,” she whispered.

Then the sea above them flashed. A heavy harpoon tore through the water and punched straight through one of the approaching Myrmurs, twisting the creature sideways in a burst of black blood. A second harpoon followed. Then cannon fire began slamming down from the hovering ships above, distorted thunder chasing each shot through the water as shells and barbed shafts ripped into the swarm before any of them could close on the bells.

The darkness erupted into violence. Carapaces shattered. Bodies spun away trailing limbs. One enormous crustacean Myrmur lunged for their bell and had its head blown apart less than five meters from the glass, so Gael leaned closer to his porthole, fascinated as several people dived into the sea moments after the first volley of cannonfire ended.

The Fishermen. Of course. And they came down laughing.

Gael recognized the anglerfish-hatted Fisherman as he kicked through the water faster than any human had any right to move, rammed a spear under the shell of a swimming Myrmur, and used the creature’s momentum to fling himself toward another. The eel-hatted Fisherman was nearby, ripping at a segmented monster with a hooked blade and looking so delighted that the lack of breathable air seemed to be the only thing preventing him from singing. More Fishermen dived in afterwards, some with weighted nets, and others with harpoons, but every single one of them appeared to be having the best afternoon of their lives as they hacked and slashed the swarm of Myrmurs apart.

The Fisherman bared their rotten teeth and waved at Gael as his bell continued descending, so it was the least Gael could do to grin and wave back up at them.

“Fucking weirdos,” Fergal muttered, staring up at the fading Fishermen.

“Fortunately for us,” Gael agreed with a solemn nod.

“How are they even swimming this far down after us? And that fast? Most of them don’t even have Symbiotic Systems like us.”

“I guess they’re just built different?”

But below them, the laboratory was finally becoming clear.

On the surface, Gael had only seen scattered lights through the dark, but now the structure itself rose into view beneath their sinking bells: a vast complex of glass domes, reinforced ribs, industrial corridors, protruding chambers, pipe networks, and metal platforms clinging together beneath the sea. Artificial lamps burned along its exterior in harsh white and red rows, making sections of the surrounding water glow faintly while shadows of more Myrmurs moved between the structures. One protruded section of the lab sat directly beneath their descent path, broad enough to take the impact of the bell. Probably. Gael sincerely hoped they wouldn’t have to leave the diving bell in armored suits just to storm into the lab. Preferably, they’d crash their bell right through and storm in without having to touch a single drop of water.

So as they neared the lab rapidly—with Jin and Vivi sharing a strong look and Fergal nodding at the other Saint’s Hands in the other bells to course-correct their descent path—there was nothing left for him to do but pull out a flask of ninety-one percent alcohol and start chugging.

A second later, their diving bells slammed into the laboratory. Metal screamed beneath them, but this time, nobody was thrown sideways as they smashed through the reinforced ceiling and into the ground, punching iron and glass inwards in a storm of debris. Seawater flooded in immediately after, but the mounted vials atop each of the bells shattered on impact exactly as intended, causing pale alchemical foam to erupt upwards and expand across the holes. The foam sealed the openings in thick hardened masses within seconds, leaving only sheets of water running down the walls as Gael unlocked the hatch.

Wet, metallic air rushed in, and he stepped out first with his boots splashing in shallow water. The Wraithpier lab was a far cry from the elaborate grandiosity of the Bleakhearth lab. Rusted pipes crowded the ceiling and walls with valves hissing and dim red lights flickering along the ribbed metal supports. It was dark, gloomy, and empty for the most part, prioritizing functionality over aesthetics—but who was Gael to judge someone else’s beauty standards for a lab? The Saintess knew how shoddy his own Heartcord Clinic was just a little under three years ago. If a lab wanted to look like an abandoned ruin, then who was he to say no to that?

But he could judge a lab by its cleanliness, and unfortunately, this lab failed the test, because the hallway before them was completely packed with Myrmurs.

They were all humanoid crustaceans that filled the passage from wall to wall—crab-like men with heavy shells layered across their torsos, lobster-bodied women with long segmented arms, broad-clawed children crouched low in the water, among other Blight-Class Myrmurs all enveloping their Hosts in their living armor. Their carapaces glistened beneath the red emergency lights, and their limbs scraped against the floor. Several opened their claws wide with sharp cracking sounds while others lowered themselves into charging stances.

They didn’t particularly scare Gael, though. And not his Heartcord Clinic behind him either. Maeve stepped out of the bell and stood at his side. Jin and Vivi emerged just behind them, and then Fergal and the rest of the Saint’s Hands stormed out of their bells, cracking their necks and sharpening their blades with their chitin-armored palms.

“... Fast and hard. We’ll rescue all the artificial Myrmur Hosts, ravage the lab, and beat the shit out of the Breeder,” Gael said cheerily. “Let’s be back home for dinner tonight, yeah?”
