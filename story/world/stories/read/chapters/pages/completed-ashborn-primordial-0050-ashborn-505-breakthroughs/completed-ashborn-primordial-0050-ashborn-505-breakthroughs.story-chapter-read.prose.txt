Days marched on as Vir threw the full weight of his knowledge and experience into solving the riddle. Yet now, the combat exams were upon them, and this universe’s secret remained tightly shut. He’d made solid progress, to be sure, and under any normal circumstance his findings would have been remarkable.

But now? He feared his final gamble would fail.

While the book he’d been so generously loaned rambled at length on the nature of prana, most of it was either wrong or misguided. At this point, Vir was all but certain the Altani had stumbled upon the truth of things by mistake and even now didn’t fully understand how the rules had shifted.

Even still, by forming his own theories based on the content, Vir was able to piece together the core essence of the mechanism for prana generation.

It all began with the burst of inspiration he’d had during Professor Vadin’s lecture. That prana could somehow be generated out of thin air never sat right with him. Where did the energy come from?

If it could simply be generated at will, wouldn’t the universe grow more and more full of prana, eventually becoming like the Ash itself? No doubt that was likely the Fateweavers’ intent, to flood the realm with so much prana it became toxic to intelligent life.

He doubted, however, that this place would last long enough to get there. But something still didn’t fit right. The energy had to be coming from somewhere, and the fact that it had eluded him nearly drove him crazy.

The answer, as was often the case, came to him when he activated Prana Vision. Having rationed his prana for days, he used it all in one burst while Maiya cultivated at the glacial pace they’d been forced to live with.

In the brief moment he had, he saw into her unlike ever before. Saw her pranic pathways and the magic coursing through her. Saw the mass of accumulated prana she could no longer use.

And he saw where the prana seemingly sprung into existence from nothing.

Except it wasn’t nothing. It was so simple, having been there all along.

Simple… and horrifying.

He was right that the energy was coming from somewhere. It wasn’t created out of thin air.

Like in his universe, prana was conserved. Just that this energy came from something far more sinister. Far more precious.

Instead of the vitality of life slowly emanating prana, it came from one’s very essence.

It came from their soul.

“You’re telling me,” Maiya muttered conspiratorially, leaning in even though they sat alone on the floor of their rather empty fourth-floor home, “That everyone who uses magic here is taking years off their lives?”

“Not just that,” Vir replied somberly. “They are eating away their very souls. Eventually, I imagine the damage will crack whatever is left.”

“Like what happened to you.”

Vir nodded. “It is an insidious thing. The moment I tapped into my soul and drew from the energy there, my cultivation speed increased tenfold. And I am sure this is but the beginning. With practice, I am confident I can make the process more efficient.”

“That’s… horrifying,” Maiya said. “With every improvement you make, the more you carve out your soul. Vir…”

Maiya trailed off, searching his expression.

He grasped her hand and squeezed it, giving her what he hoped was a reassuring smile. “Relax. I have no intention of having my soul ruptured again. I very much doubt I would survive a second time.”

Maiya’s shoulders relaxed a bit and she let out a sigh. “All those students… They have no idea.”

“No wonder the Altani are reluctant to divulge the secret. I’m guessing their introductory classes are there to indoctrinate students rather than teach them anything meaningful.”

“Convince them that it’s a necessary sacrifice. That the only way to get strong is to slowly kill yourself,” Maiya said darkly. “Ironically, they’re not wrong.”

“No,” Vir admitted. “And that’s the terrifying part.”

“Stay weak and die in a year’s time, or grow strong and at least try to fight for the future of the universe, only to live with a crippled soul if you do succeed. What a twisted universe.”

“Or perfectly designed, depending on your perspective,” Vir said. “Under these new rules, there really is no hope for this place.”

Slowly, despite every fiber of his being protesting it, Vir was coming to the realization that if there was ever a chance of saving this universe, it was probably millennia ago.

He only hoped it wasn’t already too late for their own.

This went far beyond the encroachment of the Ash. Far beyond Ash Tears and monstrous beasts.

This was the corruption of intelligent life itself. On the very concept of intelligence, as though its existence grated upon those who sought to control Fate.

“Please tell me you have another way?” Maiya asked. “I’ll do whatever it takes to save our people, of course. But…”

“There is,” Vir said. “At least, there should be. But it’ll be somewhat convoluted.”

“I’m listening,” Maiya said softly, leaning in closer.

“Well, I think it actually relates to my other problem of affinity conversion. The issue right now is that, despite the vast reserves of prana within our bodies, we can’t access any of it.”

“None too happy about the fact that we can’t use that, mind you,” Maiya said. “Put in a lot of sweat and precious time building that up.”

Vir nodded. “But I think this is actually a blessing in disguise. Because if we can manage to harness that, it means we won’t have to tap into our souls at all.”

“That sounds good, but what does this have to do with prana exactly?”

“Well,” Vir explained, feeling very much like the old days when he’d explored prana together with Maiya as kids, “the reason we can’t use that prana is because it didn’t come from our soul.”

“Right… and?” Maiya said.

“And it’s also not purely environmental. Because we’ve cycled it, because it’s lived within us for so long, it has a certain amount of us in it. I think that’s why it hasn’t dissipated, despite the lack of ambient prana. It’s still bound to us.”

“And you’re hoping to exploit that to be able to use it somehow?”

“Not directly,” Vir said. “I tried that already. It doesn’t work. But what if we could make it ours?”

He pointed to a piece of parchment he had written an inscription down on. “Just an idea, of course.”

“Sorry, Vir, but you know I’m nowhere near as proficient when it comes to inscriptions. Care to explain what this is?”

Vir didn’t especially consider himself an expert, either, but working with Saunak over the years on his various contraptions—often as a guinea pig—had imparted some measure of the method behind the madness that was Thaumaturgy.

“It’s a small portion of Aspect of the Inferno,” Vir explained. “Specifically, the part that focused on generating fire. What it does doesn’t really matter—I think this will just create a puff of fire in front of us. But rather than channeling it to an external effect, I’ve inverted it.”

“So, you can harness the energy somehow?” Maiya asked with a frown.

“Right. In order for tattoos to function, they consume prana to create an effect on the universe. But the arts that fire off elemental magic necessarily convert the wielder’s prana into the prana of that specific element. I’m simply intercepting the process. Instead of unleashing a physical effect on the world, I’m bottling that prana and returning it into the body. And because it’s been channeled through a tattoo that’s on the body…”

“It might be enough to make it our own!” Maiya cried. “Vir, that’s brilliant!”

“I dunno about brilliant, but as a byproduct, it should no longer be Ash Affinity prana.”

“Well, that’s great for you, but I’ll just be happy if I can use my prana again.”

“Oh?” he asked, cocking a brow. “You might not need it, but you certainly want it.”

Maiya opened her mouth to deny it, but then the meaning of his words settled over her. “You’re saying that based on the tattoo, we can convert prana from one affinity to another? Any other affinity?”

Vir grinned. “You’ve always wanted to be a fire mejai, haven’t you?” Vir said. “If this works, you can finally make that happen.”

Maiya’s eyes lit up. “That’s… I don’t even have words to describe how much I’ve wished for this, Vir!”

She threw her hands around his neck, and he wrapped her up, enjoying her familiar warmth. He hadn’t realized how much stress he’d been under here, in this strange universe full of danger.

Maiya finally pulled away to look him in the eyes. “What you’ve come up with here… I don’t see anything that would stop it from working in ours. Do you?”

Vir smiled. “Not especially,” he said. “I think the solution was there all along. We just never knew to look for it.”

“Incredible,” Maiya breathed. “That’s truly incredible! Humans have always been reluctant to use demonic tattoos, but with this? Those tattoos will let mejai choose their affinities from now on! It’s… this will change everything, Vir!”

“Well, temper your expectations. I still need to test this out.”

“But wait,” Maiya said, pulling away. “Won’t this be a problem for you? The reason you haven’t inscribed any other tattoos is because they’ll conflict with your Ultimates, right?”

“If I make it permanent, sure,” Vir said. “But I see no reason to do that. It’ll be horribly inefficient, and there will be a rather strict limit as to how much prana I can channel. But do you recall before when Aida inscribed a temporary tattoo upon my body to mask my Ash Prana signature so I could participate in the Bairan Tournament?”

“Right,” Maiya nodded. “As I recall, it burned off during your fight with Cirayus, yeah?”

“It did,” Vir said, scratching the back of his neck in embarrassment. “I had to use everything I had to win against him back then. Regardless, you’re right that I’ll have to pick one to ink over. I’ll only be able to use that once the temporary inscription is removed.”

“So I guess that means that I’ll be the only one who can switch affinities on the fly once we get back to our universe,” Maiya said smugly.

“Be my guest. Not like I’ve ever once needed Affinity prana back there.”

“True,” she said. “Well, are you ready to test this out?”

Vir nodded. “I think I am. But as you know, it’s nearly impossible to inscribe a tattoo on one’s own body. Especially when said tattoo needs to go on my back.”

Maiya took a moment to digest what he was implying.

“Oh no. Nonono. You’re not seriously asking me to do this, are you?” Maiya said, suddenly looking very unsure of herself.

“You have to,” Vir said. “Ashani would be the best option, but I’m afraid none of this will bring her back online. It’s all you, Mai,” Vir said.

“What… what about inscribing it into a crystal instead?” Maiya asked, eyes full of hope.

“I’ve already thought of that,” Vir said. “Because prana exits the body in that case, it will no longer be ours. It will not work.”

“I see,” Maiya said, deflating.

“But hey, good news is, if you mess up, we can just start again,” Vir said, hoping to cheer her up.

“Oh, right!” Maiya said, visibly relaxing. “That’s good. I’d forgotten about that.”

“Right, so no pressure. But, er, do try not to mess up? It’ll hurt a lot if you do. And we only have enough ink for about six tries, assuming I can do yours in one.”

“Great,” she said, rolling her eyes. “Just great. So much for ‘no pressure’.”
