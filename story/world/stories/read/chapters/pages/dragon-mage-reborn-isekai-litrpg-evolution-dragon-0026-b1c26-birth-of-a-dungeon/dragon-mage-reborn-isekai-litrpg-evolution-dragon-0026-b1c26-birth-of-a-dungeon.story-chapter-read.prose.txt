As she started to work on his beard, I looked over at Nolan, his eye closed as he trusted Mae with the razor. Apparently, Mae had been a guild leader for a couple years now. As she continued to work her magic on Nolan, she told me about her championship win at the tournament, and how she wanted to continue working for the people, hence, her little side gig.

The bandit and mercenary route she took was something I bet Nolan had a part in, because I couldn’t see sweet cowgirl Mae doing it all on her own!

She told me about a few of her missions, making her seem even more badass. Thea left the office to meet up with their next client, and that was when Mable mentioned that Thea was her cousin.

“She ain’t like my sister, Glama, thank goodness.” She scoffed. “Thea is a little bit on the quiet side, but she’s good company.”

I couldn’t help but notice that she was also a cat, and Mable was a cow. She must have read that question off my face because she suddenly started giggling to herself before she confessed, “Beastkin love to crossbreed.”

I wondered how creative mix-matching those interesting animal features got!

When his shape up was done, Mae raked her fingers lovingly through Nolan’s dark locks, then brushed them down his shaped beard. Nolan looked like a brand new man, my eyes glued on Mae’s masterpiece.

From a bum to a chad—Mae had special magic fingers.

She spun Nolan around, and he reached for the hand mirror on the desk as Mae stripped off her barber cape. By the expression on his face, I’d say that he was more than satisfied with the new look.

In what seemed like a blink of an eye, it was time to leave the café, but not before seeing an interesting exchange between Mae and Nolan. Nolan had been adamant about Mae keeping the eye-patch around his eye while she was cutting his hair. It made it harder for her to style it, but Nolan didn’t care. And neither did Mae. Not until we were about to walk out from the back of the building, where Mae took it upon herself to snatch the cloth off of him when his back was turned. I sat there swinging my tail around the ground, creating clouds of dust while those two bickered. It was like watching parents fight, except it was light-hearted and silly.

Nolan swiped at the empty air where his eye-patch once was, whipping around to face Mae empty-handed. She was obviously taller than him, which made it even funnier. This was the first time I saw Nolan let loose a bit, and I was enjoying every minute of it.

“Who did this to you?!” she cried. “Just answer the question, and I’ll give it back!”

“Quit being childish!” Nolan hissed.

“I told you, once you start losin’ body parts, I’m pullin’ you out!”

It took a lot of convincing from Nolan to stay in the guild. Mae was just worried about his well-being, and wanted to rip the faces of those who hurt her sweetums. She was very protective of Nolan, and was ready to bench him forever, but Nolan brought up a few good points about his record that made Mae reconsider. Nolan was her best kill-for-hire, and their future clients needed him. He wasn’t retiring anytime soon.

We bid our farewells and hit the ground running on our trusty horse. Nolan reassured me that there were plenty of dungeons to spare, and that we were sure to encounter one in no time. He even knew the area well enough to confidently tell me exactly how long it would take. Even so, I bet all of this horseback riding would be cut in half if I could evolve and fly. It was only a matter of time before I got my hands on my next evolution stone, and I couldn’t wait!

‘Soooo, how long have you and Mable been a couple?’ I asked, purposely shifting the topic.

“We aren’t,” he said bluntly.

‘She sure thinks that you two are.’

“She’s delusional.”

‘She’s sweet. Eccentric, and fun. I like her.’

“I feel like you like that filthy witch too, so your opinion doesn't matter.”

I flat-eyed him.

“She’s just my guild leader and a friend. Nothing more, nothing less.”

‘How did you two meet?’

“On a covert mission. Hell, she was the one who got me exposed and was real defensive about it, too.”

I giggled.

“A woman hired me to find her daughter that went missing a week ago. At that time, I was working alone. I had very few jobs and was only recommended to those who knew me. My name went around pretty fast. That being said, I had helped an old couple recover their farm, that was my first ever mission of service. And then one thing led to another. I was doing jobs every other day, retiring from my life of leisure and traveling for something meaningful.”

‘So you met her at this covert job?’

“At an underground bunker, to be more exact. Assholes were kidnapping refugees and harvesting their organs. Melda, I’d never forget her name. She swore that her daughter was still alive. I told her that the likelihood of her daughter breathing was slim—too much time had passed. These harvesters worked quickly, especially when it came to products on demand. The younger they were, the faster they sold. Though, she didn’t care. I also told her that I wasn’t cheap, and she didn’t care about that either. The only thing that mattered to her was bringing Sernes back, and that was what I planned to do. Little did I know, Sernes’ father had hired someone too, and Melda was completely unaware about it.”

‘So Mom and Dad both hired out, and didn’t tell each other?’

He smirked. “Hiring a mercenary isn’t something most people are proud about nowadays, especially religious folk like them. So I guess it was easier for them to keep it to themselves. They both wanted the same thing but were too proud to admit how desperate they were.”

‘And that’s when you met Mae?’

“She was already in the bunker when I got there. I guess Sernes’ father had hired her first, so she had a bit of a head start.”

‘So what happened?’

Nolan chuckled, “We tried to kill each other, that’s what happened. We were both in disguise, and we didn’t know who the other was and assumed we were enemies. It wasn’t until we’d nearly beaten each other senseless that she mentioned her client’s name. We realized we were on the same side, and I guess you could say the rest is history.”

‘The start of a blooming career path. Proud beginnings for sure.’

“I ended up not charging Melda. There was something about her determination and headstrongness that reminded me of myself, so I did the job for free.”

‘That was sweet of you, Nolan.’

“I don’t pass those around frivolously.”

‘Oh yeah, I can tell. So does Mae go out there and do these missions, too?’

“No, she doesn’t. In fact, she refuses to.”

‘Why?’

“As you already know, I’m not the easiest person to be around. I’m stubborn, pigheaded and prone to impatience. Mae is the total opposite, which is why she is sitting behind a desk and not out there running missions herself. She’s afraid that her impatience will end up with her making mistakes. Like getting someone innocent killed. So, she strategizes to the point of exhaustion. Wasting time is something I refuse to do, and we could never see eye to eye with that. That being said, I get the job done. Blood will spill, but the mission will be complete.”

‘She seems pretty serious about strategizing given the conversation you two were having while she was shaping you up. There is a difference between planning, and planning to take action, and from what you’re telling me, she seems to be lacking in the latter. Though, the only way she could think this way is if her approach had backfired before.’

Nolan gave me a look, answering my question without words.

Oh no—did Mae really cost an innocent person their life?

“It was an honest accident, but she never forgave herself for it.”

For a while, our conversation evaporated into pure silence as I mulled over what Nolan told me. He said to keep that secret between us, and that it was a topic Mable was sensitive about. The last thing I wanted to do was bring back bad memories, so I wouldn’t think of reminiscing about the past.

On our way to the nearest dungeon, Nolan spotted a cluster of tall stones on our right. Along the horizon, I saw them too, but there was nothing special about them, besides their strange formation. The way they were lined up made them appear to be barricading something behind them. Nolan pulled the horse to a stop, giving me a stern look that suggested he wanted to inspect the stones.

“Those were never there before,” he said.

I turned to him confused. ‘What do you mean never been there before? How do tons of stone rocks suddenly appear in a field?’

“Good question. Stay here,” he cautioned as he swung down and walked toward the monoliths. In the eerie silence of the looming stones, I noticed a faint humming sound. It was ominous and it made me nervous, so I shouted at Nolan to come back.

More like roared.

Either way, he ignored me. What was that saying? Curiosity killed the cat? I was getting bad juju from this place. What was supposed to be a quick examination turned into three minutes of Nolan inspecting every square inch of those strange stones.

Impatience was a shared trait among the both of us. At some point, I couldn’t stand it anymore, and I hopped down from the wagon to join him.

The monoliths were much bigger up close, and they had strange glyphs that were etched across their surfaces. It was in a language I couldn’t understand, probably ancient, cracked with age and exposure to the environment around them.

Nolan was still engrossed in examining the runes when I approached, his forehead furrowed in deep concentration. I couldn’t help but feel a strange pull toward these towering stones—an irresistible attraction that didn’t make sense to me. I wondered then if Nolan felt that same pull? Was that why he wanted to inspect them?

‘Nolan, you don’t feel that?’ I asked, referring to the noise and the subtle vibration of the ground.

“I thought I told you to stay back?” he retorted.

‘I’m not a kid, Nolan. You’re not babysitting me,’ I hissed. ‘Now, do you feel that vibration or not?’

“I don’t feel any vibrations.”

‘And there’s some humming, too.’

“All of that dry heat must be going to your head,” he teased.

‘I’m a dragon. That doesn’t even make any sense.’

“What doesn’t make sense is the fact that these stones are here. I can’t wrap my head around how they just appeared like this. It doesn’t seem to be some type of illusion…” Nolan said, and then he touched the surface of the stone structure for the first time.

When he stroked it, nothing happened. I didn’t know why I was expecting some type of shocking revelation or calamitous event. Nolan’s hand simply glided over the weathered surface, tracing the alien lines of the etched symbols. I tried to understand them, but the longer I focused on it, the more my head felt dizzy. The lines began to dance in my vision, their constant swaying making me lightheaded.

I shook the weird sensation off and batted my eyes a couple times to regain my composure. First, the vibrations, then, the humming, and now, the awkward dizziness from trying to read foreign glyphs.

Why wasn’t Nolan experiencing the same side effects?

The stones had an inexplicable aura which, combined with their sudden appearance, made me suspicious about them.

Maybe this was a trap? Set up to detour us?!

Could be my paranoia talking….

Nolan carried on studying them, absolutely engrossed in his task. This must have been what adventurers did, obsessing over strange mystical objects. So I joined him and touched the surface too, but the reaction wasn’t as uneventful for me as it was for Nolan. When I touched it, the glyphs began to glow an eerier blue, and all of a sudden, the ground beneath us shook with a violent tremor that sent both of us running for cover.

“What the...” Nolan began, his eye wide as he watched the glow intensify on the stone monolith I’d touched.

I wasn’t sure why I was the secret sauce to the puzzle. The humming transformed into a deafening roar that filled the air and made my ears ring. The vibrations increased tenfold, pulsating through the ground beneath us.

Even as the ground opened beneath us, the stones remained intact. As a matter of fact, they began transforming. One by one they fused into each other, creating a concave shape. The glowing glyphs on them began to drip off of the surface of the stones like liquid light. They mixed into each other and poured into the crater that gradually grew deeper. Eventually, after all of the dust had settled and the rocking was over, the fusion of the glyphs formed an entrance-way to what looked like a dungeon.

I went in for a closer look, Nolan following not too far behind. Looking down into the gaping abyss, I noticed that the glowing substance had hardened, forming an ethereal flight of steps that descended into the depths of the unknown. The glyphs on the steps pulsated gently, providing just enough illumination to make out the walls.
