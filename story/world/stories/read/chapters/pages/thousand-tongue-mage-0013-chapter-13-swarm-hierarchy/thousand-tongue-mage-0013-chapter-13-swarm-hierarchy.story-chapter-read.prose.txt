Sharp at eight in the morning, Zora started knocking on doors to wake all the children up, and then he sent them down to the common room where Cecilia was already sizzling eggs and toast for breakfast. The eggs wouldn’t last a full week in the pantry, anyways, so they might as well eat those first before digging into the rations.

… At least they’re energetic, he thought, yawning as he hurried the last group of rowdy boys down the stairs to the common room. There was just one room remaining at the very end of the second floor hallway that he had to check on, and then he could go downstairs for breakfast himself. I can’t believe most of them look like they got good sleep last night, though.

Obviously, he and Cecilia had stayed up most of the night to take turns watching the foyer gates—just in case any survivors came knocking—but it still surprised him that most of the kids seemed cheery and optimistic about welcoming a new day. Maybe it helped that he’d snuck into all of their rooms last night to pull down the window blinds one by one, stopping them from peering out at the destruction wrought about the academy, but still… he couldn’t really remember the last time he’d gotten a good night of rest.

Maybe if the Headmistress paid him more, he’d at least be able to drown himself in a bed of silvers.

“Emilia?” he called out, clearing his voice as he knocked on the little girl’s door at the end of the hallway. “Breakfast’s ready. Go downstairs and at least grab a couple of bites.”

No response.

He didn’t wait. He turned the doorknob and pushed the door open, finding a simple furnished room with no personalised items, decorations, or any belongings to mark it as a specific someone’s room—it was just a closet, a desk behind a window, and a single bed with the blankets folded neatly before the pillow.

The little girl herself was nowhere to be seen.

Breaths hitching, he stepped into the room and looked left, right, up, and down… and then up again.

He sighed a breath of relief when he saw her sleeping in the corner of the room, curled up in a ball with a web of glowing red threads wrapping her in a cocoon. It wasn’t exactly the first time he’d had to knock on her door to wake her up for morning class, but whenever he walked in and saw her bed neatly made, his first thought would always be one of panic and worry. She didn’t seem to like beds. She’d rather be sleeping somewhere else—the strangest position he’d found her in was a week ago when he caught her sleeping upside-down once, legs stuck to the ceiling.

“... The early bird gets the worm, Emilia,” he said, flicking his wand up and turning it into a sword to slice through her threads, catching her as she fell into his arms. She jolted awake, looking left and right with her milky human eye before looking up at his face. “You have pyjamas, don’t you? Don’t sleep in your uniform. You’ll crumple it and make it harder on the washerwomen to clean it for you.”

She mumbled something incoherent—a sleepy apology, maybe—so he let her off gently and had her walk on two feet, stumbling around her barebones room as she searched for her blazer. Sighing, he yanked it off a hook on the wall and draped it around her shoulders before nudging her out the door.

“Go down and get breakfast from Miss Sarius,” he said. “It’s simple eggs and toast, but you’ve had Miss Sarius’ toasts before, right? I know she sometimes comes by the dorm to make everyone breakfast, so you can ask her for extra crunch on the crust if you want—”

“I’m… not hungry, Mister Zora,” she muttered, rubbing her eyes groggily with all four fists, “and I don’t want… toast.”

He held her hand as he walked her down the stairs, anyways, not wanting her to see his lips pursing. She wasn’t a picky eater—she’d eat just about everything the cafeteria had to offer—so he couldn’t help but wonder if her lack of craving for food right now was yet another symptom of her onset of mutations. He knew a bit about what eating bug meat would do to a human’s biology, and one of those things was a lack of appetite for ‘normal’ human food. Without a system matching her exact class to keep her mutations in check, she’d only grow more and more voracious for either insect or human flesh.

But feeding her what she wanted to eat would only make her mutation develop even stronger and faster, so she had to eat eggs and toast.

He wouldn’t allow her to forget what it meant to be human.

“Just a few bites, then,” he said, patting her head softly as they stepped down into the common room where all the children were already lining up along the kitchen countertop, ceramic plates in hand. She immediately zipped behind him and cowered from the rest of the kids. “You have to line up at the very back, though, since you were the last to wake up. Go grab a plate and fork. I’ll make sure Miss Sarius gives you a smaller portion.”

She looked hesitant to leave him, her hands shaking as they gripped his cloak, but eventually she mustered up the courage to at least stand behind everyone else. It wasn’t like she actually had to talk to anyone or try to make friends with them, after all. Just being close to her fellow students for now was a good enough start for rehabilitation into human society.

Sighing again, he trudged behind the countertop to see how Cecilia was holding. She was manning six frying pans on three stoves with two hands and burning all the eggs at once—he forgot to stifle a laugh as she scowled at him, throwing a towel at him and telling him to help out with the bundle of katydid legs sitting off to the side, at least.

“Everyone’s still here?” she asked, her voice a bit dry and flaky as she flipped an egg, scolding a few rowdy kids to wait a few more moments over the countertop. In turn, he spoke "fry an egg" at two of her pans, and the spell activated this time. The eggs were already on the pan, and the stove was already turned on. He could easily imagine himself frying two eggs at once, so Cecilia took a step back out of surprise as the pans started moving on their own, sliding the eggs back and forth.

She was terrible at cooking, so there was no way she could cast the same spell.

“All forty-two kids of class 2-A and 2-B are accounted for,” he muttered, tying an apron around his waist and getting to work cracking the tough katydid legs open with his sword. “You should be a cooking teacher instead, by the way. You’ve got a real talent for it. Those eggs are looking every bit as crunchy as your toasts—”

“Quiet,” she muttered back, serving her ‘eggs’ and toast to the first three children in line. They immediately ran off with their plates, jumping onto the sofas with giddy laughs. “Don’t you try to pull the wool over my eyes, either. I thought about it long and hard last night while we were taking turns watching the gates—”

“—you mean while I was watching the gates, since you slept an extra two hours past the agreed upon switching time—”

“I wasn’t going to ask because I saw your face back then, but you know who ‘Nona’ is, don’t you?”

He split the first katydid leg open and scrunched his nose, setting a pot of water to boil on the stove to his right.

When he threw a glance at Cecilia to see if she was still staring at him, he sighed reluctantly.

“… It’s harder to not know who she is, no?” he said, lowering his voice as he dropped the first leg into the pot, working on carving the second. “Generally speaking, the majority of the Swarm is composed of three classes of bugs: Critter-Class, Giant-Class, and Mutant-Class, ranging from F-Rank to S-Rank. You see Critter-Class bugs everywhere—they’re the tiny ants we squash, the bees that sting our faces, and the spiders spinning webs in the corner of your room. They’re typically harmless, but they function as the spies of the Swarm. If you don’t take care of them, Critter-Class bugs will eventually grow into Giant-Class bugs, which are the big ones we fought last night.”

“Uh-huh.”

“The bugs can evolve and rise through the grades by eating,” he continued. “An S-Rank Critter-Class can evolve into an F-Rank Giant-Class bug after consuming enough bioarcanic essence, and then they can move up the ranks again until they reach S-Rank Giant-Class. Afterwards, they can evolve into an F-Rank Mutant-Class, which… have you seen one before?”

Cecilia threw another towel at him. “You know I haven’t.”

“A Mutant-Class is a bug that stands on two legs, grabs with four arms, and tries to behave like a human,” he said, dumping the second, third, and fourth katydid leg into the pot. “Their physical attributes and mutations are much more powerful. They gain the ability to use Arts. Their intelligence becomes eerily infantile. A Glasbrenner researcher once wrote, in an article on the Swarm hierarchy, that Mutant-Classes are like the ‘generals’ of the Swarm. Since they’re much stronger than your average Giant-Class, they’re each capable of leading a small brood as an infestation force, like the bugs in this academy right now.”

“And this ‘Nona’ bug is a Mutant-Class?”

He shook his head slowly. “We don’t give names to Mutant-Class bugs, but when a S-Rank Mutant-Class eats enough humans, they evolve into what’s called an ‘Insect God’—and these are the bugs humanity have individual names for. They’re much stronger and rarer than your average Mutant-Class. Their intelligence matches ours. They can speak our human tongues. Their Arts are a hundred times more powerful. They can lie and deceive and play on our words, and their amplified physical attributes border on ‘godlike’. The last report I read from the Attini Empire stated there are around thirty quintillion bugs and counting in the Swarm, and of those thirty quintillion, there are only about a hundred Insect Gods.”

Cecilia narrowed her eyes as she flipped an egg onto a kid’s plate straight from the pan. “And… are there ranks for these Insect Gods as well?”

“From F-Rank to S-Rank as usual. In fact, they're so infamous you’ve probably heard of a few particularly powerful Insect Gods,” he mumbled, flinching as he started stirring the deep pot with his wand. The foul, putrid stench was quite unbearable. “The Nine Sun Moths of the Hellfire Caldera Front. The Four Leviathans of the Deepwater Legion Front. The Seven Spider Spinners, the Beast of Ka’lan, and the Lysmata Chitosan, just to name the most infamous ones. They all have peculiar magic and mutations so they’re incredibly hard to put down even with an entire army barraging them with blades and explosives.”

“... ‘Nona’ is an Insect God, then.”

He nodded slowly. “Of the hundred or so Insect Gods, the three Magicicada Gods known as the ‘Magicicada Witches’ are… human-like cicadas with the Essence Art ‘God Tongue'. They're the original users of our magic, and the Magicicada Mages were the ones who picked up scraps of their wounded bodies from the battlefield and forged our magicicada systems with them. That’s part of the reason why there haven’t been any Magicicada Mages in the past few decades. To make new magicicada systems, you need parts harvested from a magicicada, and the three of them—the only magicicadas in the world—haven’t been injured in decades.”

“They’re that strong?

“They're some of the stronger Insect Gods out there, but they’re less well-known because they don’t attack very often, and they’re almost never seen together,” he explained. “Physically speaking, they’re considered the weakest of all the Insect Gods in the world, but that’s because they all have a nasty habit of stealing people’s voices to bolster their own spells. Nona is the youngest sister, who steals the voices of children. Decima is the middle sister, who steals the voices of adults, and Morta is the eldest sister, who steals the voices of elders. The spells they’re able to cast are vastly different from each other as a result, so they never stick together, but make no mistake. They’re physically weak only because they can afford to be physically weak.”

Cecilia was quiet as she continued serving breakfast, and he couldn’t really blame her. It was a fair amount of information to absorb, and the truth was, he’d half a mind to give her only a fraction of the details so she wouldn’t have to confront the reality of the situation… but the thing was, if that katydid hadn’t been lying, there was an Insect God walking around the academy right now.

What he wasn’t going to tell her was the fact that it usually took a small army to slay a Mutant-Class, and that there was no record of an Insect God being slayed by any army other than humanity’s six strongest militaries—none of which were even remotely close to Amadeus Academy.

Nona is what humanity would call an ‘Insect God'.

We face an Insect God in our academy.
