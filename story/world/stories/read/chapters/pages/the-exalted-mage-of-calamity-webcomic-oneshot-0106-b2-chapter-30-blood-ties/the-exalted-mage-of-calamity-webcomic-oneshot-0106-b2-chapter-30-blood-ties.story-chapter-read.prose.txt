The building stood at the edge of a narrower street, tucked between a closed appraisal shop and a game parlor.

It was large, but not extravagant. A middling business. Not too shabby, but not too regal.

Nasha walked through the front doors, passing beneath a large sign that read: “Viper’s Nail.”

Inside, several seats were arranged in neat rows, and three women were already sitting with attendants working on their nails. Nasha didn’t spare them more than a glance before walking directly to the counter.

The clerk was a man in his mid-twenties, clean-shaven, with rough black hair. He smiled the moment he saw her approach.

“Welcome to Viper’s Nail. Did you have an appointment?” he asked, wearing the usual merchant’s front.

“No,” Nasha said, smiling back. “I was actually recommended this place. I was wondering if you had a premium service available?”

Nasha placed the red string atop the counter, keeping the motion discreet enough to avoid any prying eyes in the lobby.

He looked down at the string, but his eyes didn’t so much as blink. He reached out and took it, closing it inside his palm. “Of course! We have a lot of services available. Please, follow me to the luxury rooms in the back.”

Nasha nodded and followed without another word. They passed several rooms before reaching a door deeper in the building. The man lifted the red string toward it. Something clicked, and the door opened by itself. Cold air rushed out from within.

“Come,” the man said, taking the first step down the descending staircase.

A minute later, Nasha stepped into a wider chamber, all of it hidden beneath Maftem’s city and far away from prying eyes.

It was a lobby of sorts, similar to an adventurer’s guild, but with far fewer people. There were maybe six others inside. Some waited against the wall, their glaring eyes hidden beneath hoods as they watched Nasha’s every move. Another pair sat at a table, conversing and laughing with each other. One of them twirled a knife around their hand before stabbing it down into the wood with another laugh.

Psychopathic killers. The most unyielding and dangerous kind.

Around the room, large pillars supported the roof, along with the several tons of dirt and stone above. Each pillar was etched with glowing runes. Scry-protection, silence magic, and defensive barriers had all been set in place to protect the room.

The man who led her down stopped at the bottom of the steps. He turned to Nasha and placed the red string back into her hand.

Nasha accepted it.

With a single nod, he left and headed back up the stairs.

Nasha crossed the room, making her way toward the front counter. She felt some of the watchful eyes following her, but she ignored them. No one would cause her trouble in a place like this.

A woman sat behind the counter, facing slightly to the side, one leg crossed over the other as she filed her nails in slow, almost theatrical strokes.

She was dressed far too lightly for someone who worked in a regular underground office. A loose black garment hung from one shoulder, barely held in place by thin straps. A slit along the side of her dress revealed most of her thigh. Her hair was short, dark, and cut unevenly around her jaw. Two necklaces hung from her neck, one flashy, the other a dull black bead.

She looked bored.

Painfully bored.

Nasha approached and placed the red string on the counter. The woman didn’t turn her head, but her eyes slid down toward the string, then back toward Nasha. Her shoulders shifted with a faint chuckle.

“Well,” she said, dragging the nail file across one finger. “Got a pretty one today, huh?”

Nasha stared at her with a neutral expression.

The woman’s smile didn’t falter. “What’s a thing like you doing as an assassin, hm?”

Again, Nasha said nothing.

A few seconds passed in complete silence, broken only by the low murmur of conversation from the others in the lobby.

“Tch.” The woman set the file down and turned to face her. “You pretty no-talk types aren’t as cool as you think, you know. A little banter makes the workday bearable.”

“I’m not here to make your workday better.”

The woman’s face brightened slightly despite the remark. “Oh, pretty girl talks. Rude, but talks.”

Nasha’s eyes flicked briefly to the red string.

The woman followed her glance and gave another exaggerated sigh. “Fine, fine. Straight to business with you. How dull. Just like the rest.”

She lifted one finger and pointed it down at the string. With a swift flick, she swiped her finger across the air, and the string was cut in half.

There was no damage to the table, nor any visible sign of what she had done. Whether it was aura or magic, even Nasha wasn’t sure.

As expected of someone in one of the major cities, she was not ordinary.

The woman tapped the counter once. “Summons confirmed. Vania Arlington.”

Nasha narrowed her eyes, hearing that name again. “Who called for me?” she asked.

The woman smirked. “If they wanted me to tell you, I would’ve told you.” She leaned forward slightly. “Or maybe I am supposed to tell you. But silent types. You know? Heard they’re in fashion nowadays.”

Nasha held the woman’s gaze for a second longer before turning away.

“Toodles,” the woman called, waving with her fingers.

The corridor was narrow, lit by red mana lamps fixed along the walls. Each door had a number carved into the wood. The markings themselves were plain, but each was embedded with several deeper runes, none weaker than the ones covering the lobby. Everything was arranged to keep conversations discreet and untraceable.

Nasha stopped in front of the second room. For a moment, her hand rested against the handle. Then she opened the door and stepped inside.

The room was small. Not cramped, but certainly plain. Two couches faced each other from opposite sides of a low table. A tea and coffee set had already been prepared, steam rising gently from silver pots. There were no windows, decorations, or guards inside. There weren’t even any lights, and yet, the room was still illuminated.

The only other thing in the room was the woman sitting on one of the couches, one leg crossed over the other.

She was already smiling when Nasha entered.

Nasha recognized her immediately. Her posture changed on instinct. Her shoulders went still, the weight on her legs shifted, and her hands drifted close to the daggers hanging at her thighs. Her entire body became a bowstring pulled to its limit.

The woman’s smile widened as she watched Nasha’s reaction.

“Hello, Vania.”

Nasha’s eyes sharpened.

The woman leaned forward, resting her chin on her fist. “Or should I say… Nasha?”

Nasha sat with her body tense, facing the woman in front of her.

She was thirty-one years old, with long black hair spilling forward over one shoulder and down her back on the other side. Her face was carefully maintained, stunningly beautiful and youthful, matching both a seductress and a renowned singer on stage.

She was slim, but the way she sat made it clear she was anything but delicate. Black leather armor covered her from neck to toe, with several slitted plates of black metal fitted between the layers.

Dark bracers wrapped around both of her wrists. They resembled cuffs more than armor, but Nasha knew better. Both were high-tier artifacts capable of boosting her striking power by nearly five times.

“You were the one who called me?” Nasha said slowly. Her words carried no warmth, only bitter ice.

“Still so cold, Vania. And here I thought you would be happy to see an old friend.”

“We were many things, Charlotte. But friends were not one of them,” Nasha said plainly.

“No?” Charlotte tilted her head, a finger resting along her cheek. “Lovers, then?”

Nasha said nothing, but her glare sharpened into a weapon that could decapitate dragons.

Charlotte’s amusement deepened. Her grin was like that of a sly snake, playing with prey already caught in its tightening coil.

“I missed that look of yours. So sharp, it almost makes my eyes bleed.”

“Enough with the pleasantries. Why did you call me? You know I don’t work for Vermillion anymore.”

“Is that so?” Charlotte said, leaning back. “Last time I checked, you never quit. No. No one ever quits. They become decommissioned.”

Nasha’s jaw tightened at the word.

“The only ones who get decommissioned are those who betray Vermillion. And you know what happens to those who are decommissioned.”

“Are you threatening me?”

“Never!” Charlotte said, clapping her hands together. “I don’t want you decommissioned. After your latest escapade, some of us thought you might have gone rogue and abandoned us. But I advocated for you. I told them, ‘There’s no way! Vania is our little devil. She’d never quit!’” She pitched her voice higher as she recounted it, as if she were a young, innocent child. “I told everyone you were simply taking a break. A small pause from work. And that you’d return to taking missions soon enough.”

“How kind of you,” Nasha said.

Charlotte smiled. “Wasn’t it? What would you do without me?”

“It sounds like you expect me to thank you.”

“I wouldn’t mind.”

“You’ll be disappointed, then.”

Charlotte laughed, soft and pleased, as if Nasha’s hostility was something nostalgic and warm rather than insulting and distant. She reached down, took her cup of coffee, and sipped from it.

“Oh, I do miss this persona of yours. The mask for the public performance, and the one meant to hide the sins crushed beneath rose-petal wheels. A poetic masked jester.” She frowned with a pouted lip. “The sad,” she said, then changed it to a smile, “and the gleeful.”

“Get to the point, Charlotte. You know as well as I do why I quit. Now, why have you called me here after I’ve already made my position in Vermillion clear?”

A sigh escaped Charlotte’s lips. “A particular thing happened a couple of months ago, you know?” she said, using her coffee cup to gesture through the air. “A certain business funded by Vermillion in Ronswick. High Crates. Suddenly collapsed. Several routes, all silent. A storehouse full of indebted slaves, exposed. All our members there, imprisoned or executed.”

Nasha did her best to keep her fingers from tensing.

She understood now.

“You know, we were using that base as an important source of funds. But suddenly, that entire alley of revenue disappeared. And in the middle of this mess, your name kept coming up. Not Vania, but the singer. Nasha. All while coupled with a mysterious dark elf. A mage who became the centerpiece of everything.”

Charlotte looked at Nasha more closely. “Tell me, Nasha… Lowell. What do you know of Veronica Everwells?”
