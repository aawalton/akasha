Alex, in the empty valley…

The tree we rested in kept us above the swampy floor of the valley. Brackish water that stank worse than the marshes in the Sanctuary covered everything once we had descended to the valley floor. Yet, the canopy stretched wide in all directions. Most branches were able to take our weight, which meant we could stay above the swamp.

We rested near the trunk of a massive tree, and I searched for a better path through the darkness. The heat had barely retreated when the sun finally went down.

Yet, the songs of insects and small animals that I had expected never arrived as the air cooled.

Nothing.

No buzzing flying things, so crickets. No birds, or even bat-like creatures.

Just an oppressing silence.

Here in the forest, the mana flickered strangely, almost visibly even without my special sight.

Lenna reacted to it without knowing why. She stepped away from the dense areas, and stayed in the gaps between them.

“I don’t like this place,” she said, right next to my ear.

I shook my head. “Me neither. It feels dead.”

If we hadn’t seen those beings in the distance, I’d think the entire planet was dead.

By the time morning hit, I almost doubted what we’d seen the day before. As soon as light trickled in, I picked up the pace. We sprinted across trees, both of us trying to hear or see anything living, besides the trees.

I slowed down when we reached the far end of the valley. We had to be coming up on the area where we saw the chase. That meant something was alive. At least, I hoped it did.

Lenna tapped my shoulder and pointed at a cut in one of the massive trees.

We both leaped closer.

A platform hung in the distance, empty.

But, the cut and bound boards screamed that living people had been here. With the humidity and heat, wood wouldn’t survive long here without being tended. That argued that whoever had made this was likely still here.

I slowed down even more before stepping into any brighter lit area. The shadows still held me, and Lenna carried her bow. I didn’t want trouble, but I knew better than to expect things to be easy.

After another hour, a noise caused both of us to freeze before I peeked around a tree.

Laughter.

“Let me go!” someone yelled.

More laughter. “We need something to bring back to the head,” said a deep, gruff voice.

“That doesn't mean me!”

The smallest one of them yelled at the other two from within a net.

[Byn, child.]

The information caused me to jerk back behind the tree trunk.

Lenna peeked out, then pulled back and shook her head. She mouthed a word, but the translator didn’t work on it.

“Children,” she whispered, close to my ear.

I nodded.

We weren’t going to hurt any kids.

“Did you hear that?” said one of them.

Lenna froze.

“There isn’t anything out here,” said Byn, sounding concerned. “You guys chased away the only swamp bug. The head is going to be buzzing. We were supposed to watch and see if it laid eggs, not try and attack it.”

“That isn’t what happened,” answered another one.

“Hush.”

Again silence.

“We should leave,” said Byn. “Get home before nightfall.”

“Let’s get out of here,” added the second one. “It might be the monster.”

“The Head said this area was clear,” whispered the third.

A splash echoed through the swamp, coming from a different direction from the talking children.

“Oh, no,” whispered Byn. “We gotta go.”

Buzzing came from the platform as another splash rose from the water, closer than before.

I peeked around to see all three kids take to the sky as fast as they could, heading toward the other end of the valley. Ripples traveled across the surface of the water, coming from our right.

A red dot appeared on the edges of my radar, but didn’t move closer. Instead, it traveled after the children. First, whatever it was swam closer to the tree with the platform. Once it was clear they weren’t there anymore, it continued on swimming in the same direction, heading off to our left and following the path of the flying children.

The creature never broke the surface of the water. Ripples were the only sign it was there at all.

As soon as it crossed off my radar, I relaxed.

Lenna did not. She stared off into the distance, her hands clenched around her bow, with her shoulders tense.

“You okay?” I asked, taking a step closer.

It took a moment for her to respond.

“You didn’t feel it…” she whispered, as she suddenly blinked. “The dread…”

I shook my head.

“Those children don’t–” her voice cut off, as a scream echoed through the air.

I moved before I really thought about it. My feet found the next branch several trees over, as I launched myself into the air. I didn’t dare use my wings. The high energy requirement would destroy what was left of my stock of meat, and this felt like a fight. I’d need that energy.

Still, I skipped along the canopy on the massive tree branches, catching up to the screams in moments.

Two of the kids were wrapped around the upper branches of a tall tree, while the third hung from a lower branch.

None of them had their wings out.

Hissing rose from a massive creature that had twisted up the trunk of the massive tree.

[Snapper, Barometric Siphon, Sedge-Stranger, Level 225, Moss Bright, Predator, Unknown.]

The creature twisted up the tree, revealing a massive, long body covered in scales that matched the bark on the tree. It rose another foot, faster than I thought possible, getting closer to one of the kids.

[Hal, child.]

The child swung up onto the branch and started climbing, reaching the other two quickly. All of them climbed to the highest branches of the tree.

I held off attacking as Snapper suddenly moved slower, almost giving them enough time to get away.

Wings formed behind Byn, and she took off heading in one direction. The other two took longer to pull out their wings. The two of them flew off together in another direction as the snake surged up another couple of feet.

It suddenly dropped into the water, then reared up. Its scales flared, and something happened that I couldn’t quite follow. It was too fast, even for me.

The two kids were caught by something, and plunged to the trees again.

I leaped to the next tree, staying within the shadows.

The snake crashed back into the water, sending it out in all directions, but it didn’t move after the kids.

Instead, it hesitated.

They both jumped back into the air, much quicker this time, and took off.

I didn’t get it.

“You don’t need to stare,” whispered Snapper, as its head poked up out of the water. “They are gone from the swamp.”

I blinked as it stared directly at me within the shadows.

“I would think one such as you would understand punishing trespassing by those destroyers,” it added.

“Destroyers?” I asked.

“Killers of the balance, vow breakers, whatever you want to call them,” he grumbled, shaking his head. “They do not get to bother those that have survived the destruction of the balance against all odds.”

Bright green eyes stared at me. “Or, are you here to fix that?”

“I’m here to retrieve a friend that was taken by Clan Vielether,” I explained, as I tracked Lenna inching closer to the two of us.

“Good luck dealing with the destroyers. Unless the great ones take care of this soon, no one will survive…” Snapper turned, and started swimming away.

“Wait, what do you mean?” I asked, moving out of the shadows.

“The mana fluctuations grow more severe, and they closed off access to all of the portals, except for their kind. Can’t leave, and can barely live…” The snake shook its tail. “They will move soon. The great ones. I can feel it in the air.”

The snake continued on away from us, without stopping again.

Lenna arrived at my side, her eyes on the snake leaving. “It let them go…”

I opened my mouth, then closed it, frowning before letting out a sigh. “Yeah, I think something weird is going on here.”

“You mean, beside the weird air, and some sort of pressure that comes and goes?” she asked, shaking her head. “The heaviness feels like I shouldn’t be here, but not like Kabi’s world. Like, the world itself is upset.”

She shivered before I could respond. “I don’t like it.”

“Well, let's get Strange…” My voice trailed off, as I thought about the mountain peak. The connection to Strange stretched farther away from us, in the direction that the kids had gone. But the mountain wasn’t on the same path.

Lenna nudged me. “You got this look for a moment, are you with me?”

“That other stop I need to make… I’m just trying to figure out the best way to do this,” I replied. Strange was my priority. “Let’s keep heading after those kids.”

Lenna pressed both her lips together and stared up into the sky. The light increased as well as the temperature. The stickiness of the air had increased, too.

I nodded, and then leaped to the next tree, keeping my pace slow enough for Lenna to stay with me. Time passed quickly as we traveled through the dense trees above the swamp.

No other creatures popped out. Nothing even buzzed in the distance.

It was so freaking creepy.

The far reaches of the valley ended in a narrow canyon, with a slow moving river that connected to the next valley.

Life picked up almost immediately at the valley edge. Everything from small buzzing insects, to fish in the water, and small birds in the trees.

They might have more wings than I was used to, and be covered with scales, but they were still birds. They flew, chirped and…

Did that bird just breathe green gas?

I paused along with Lenna, as a small flock of the scaly birds with four wings collectively attacked a different kind of flying creature. One of them opened its mouth and a thick green gas shot out.

The feathered bird didn’t have a chance, before the others dove in with claws.

I pointed in a different direction, and Lenna nodded.

That's when it attacked.

A mess of vines dropped from the very top of the canopy and landed on Lenna, completely covering her.

[Mass of Tangles, Suffocation, Unknown.]

I triggered Sovereign's Call, but the mass of blues, greens and browns didn’t react. But then, Lenna phased through the literal mass of the creature. She jumped up and away, but once she landed, she shivered.

“Let’s not do that again,” she whispered, staring at it.

It still hadn’t moved.

We left it behind, then I dropped my skill.

We had too much ground to cover.

Even though I wanted to hop into a fight with the thing, it didn’t feel worth it.

Then another one dropped from the trees and landed on Lenna again.

[Mass of Tangles, Suffocation, Unknown.]

Lenna phased through it before I could do anything. This time, though, she didn’t leap away, and instead nudged it with her toe.

“Are these traps?” she asked, in a careful voice.

I immediately stepped closer, and knelt next to it.

The thing didn’t react at all. That's when I realized it wasn’t on my radar.

I touched it. Again, nothing happened. The texture reminded me of rubber tubing. Switching to my second sight, mana swirled along inside the tubes, but had already started leaking out. Bit by bit, the mana left the thing completely.

“It’s not a creature,” I said, before trying to pick it up.

Each of the vines just dangled from my hands. I let it drop, and it fell off the branch and into the water below.

If it wasn’t a creature, then it totally had to be a trap. The fact that it immediately tried to suffocate anything that it caught bothered me.

Looking up around the canopy with my second sight made the next one easier to spot. It blended right in with the bottom of a branch. Someone had stuck it to the tree with mana. The area under it lacked as much mana as the surrounding areas, creating what Lenna would call a ‘low-pressure’ zone.

“It’s targeting anything that crosses the lower level mana areas,” I said, searching for any more of them.

“Show me,” said Lenna.

We inched closer to the target.

This time I passed my hand through the space, but nothing happened.

As soon as Lenna leaned closer, it trembled. She snorted. “So, it isn’t looking for anything Rank 3…”

Lenna’s hand shot out and pointed at something a bit away. “Is that one triggered?”

I leaped closer to the bundle on the branches. A mass of tangles wrapped around a small, 4 limbed creature without wings. It reminded me of a monkey.

The creature had died at least a day ago. Bugs flew around the thing, but fled as I approached. The smell of rotting meat was thick near the corpse.

“Why would someone set traps and not check them?” I asked, studying our surroundings, trying to figure out an answer.
