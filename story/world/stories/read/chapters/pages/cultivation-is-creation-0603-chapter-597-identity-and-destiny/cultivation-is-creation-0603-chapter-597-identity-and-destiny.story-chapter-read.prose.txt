There was no point arguing with the others as there was no way of getting through to them.

Despite this not being his life, Tian’s hands still trembled at his sides.

It was something that he had known since the Hall of Shattered Reflections. It was there that he had learned the truth that he wasn’t Tian but was actually Wu Kangming in an inner world created for the tournament final.

Yet why…why did it hurt so much?

The logical part of his mind, the part that had absorbed Wu Kangming's memories and understood the nature of cultivation tournaments, knew that nothing mattered except defeating Ke Yin to win the finals.

But the other part of him, the part that had lived as Tian for years, that had experienced five years of memories traveling with Hou and Master Jian; that part was screaming at him to do something, to get revenge for his best friend.

Even now, he remembered the day Hou approached him in Moonhaven City after Tian had just failed Master Jian's sword test and was in the middle of walking away with his head down, convinced once again that he would never amount to anything.

"You move different," Hou had said, looking at him with those pale blue eyes. "Like you're remembering something instead of learning it."

And that was how they began a journey together that lasted five years, where they had eaten together, hunted together, trained together, and had those conversations beside the campfire where they'd admitted their fears and insecurities in the darkness of the night.

"Do you ever feel like you're supposed to be someone else?" Tian had asked one night.

"Every day," Hou had replied quietly. "It’s like I'm living a life that should have belonged to someone who actually deserved it."

"Me too..."

The feeling of displacement, of being strangers in their own body, was something that brought them together. Also, both of them couldn’t cultivate dream qi in a world where everyone else was able to. And they both felt destined for something more but couldn't understand what or why. It was all of this that created a bond unlike any other.

And then Hou had died.

Tian's chest tightened as he remembered the demon's tail strike, watching his brother go over the cliff edge. The hollow emptiness that followed. The grief that had finally unlocked his dream cultivation, as if losing Hou had broken something fundamental inside him.

Reuniting in the Sunken Dream Palace had brought a relief that had been overwhelming. This was his friend, his brother, the person who'd given him a purpose when he thought he had none. Now he was dead once again, killed by these people right in front of his own eyes.

They were people that he had trusted, Shen Bolin and Li Na, had seemed honorable. That was why he had agreed to join their expedition. Li Na had been kind, and Shen Bolin had shown genuine care for his team members. But that was all an act. A lie. What kind of monster would use their own team member as a sacrifice? And was this the reason they had recruited Tian so readily in the first place? If Hou hadn’t been there, would he have been the expendable person they required for Li Na's bloodline ability?

Throughout his life, Wu Kangming had rarely felt true anger. The only one who had been able to get under his skin had been his fiancé, Wu Lihua, but other than that, everything else could be shrugged off. And after having met his master, he had learned to channel emotion into technique, to let feelings flow through his sword instead of letting them cloud his judgment.

However, at this moment, as his brother's blood was still wet on the ground, Wu Kangming felt angry, angrier than he had ever felt before. Turning slowly, he saw black tendrils of dream-stone emerge from the floor to wrap around the corpse and pull it down. It was absorbing it; adding it to the palace's collection of the dead.

There was a part of Tian that hoped some miracle would occur and Hou would return to life. After all, since Wu Kangming had exited the Hall of Shattered Reflections, he had realised that Hou was more than he appeared. The way he fought, the techniques he used, the energies that didn't match any normal cultivation method. Tian had suspected that maybe, just maybe, Hou was actually Ke Yin using a false identity.

If that were true, then Ke Yin would resurrect. The tournament's safety measures would activate. His opponent would return, and the final battle would continue as designed. But Hou's face disappeared beneath the black stone without any resurrection, without any intervention from the Dream World's protective mechanisms.

It was clear that Hou might have been hiding something, but it wasn’t a secret identity. Hou wasn't Ke Yin. Hou was Hou. Which meant he wasn't coming back, which meant his sworn brother was really dead.

Without any hesitation, Tian's hand drifted to the sword at his waist, and he drew it slowly. Spiritual energy began gathering along its length. This was something other than dream qi. This was something that had nothing to do with the energy systems of any world. This was Sword Intent.

It transcended individual energy systems, as it was pure will made manifest through countless hours of training and an understanding of how the weapon moved through space.

"Tian." Shen Bolin's voice cut through his focus. The Lucid Lawbearer had backed up several steps, his spiritual pressure flaring defensively. "I understand you're upset, but you need to think clearly. Hou knew what he was getting into. We all did. This is a dangerous expedition. Sometimes—"

"Sometimes what?" Tian's voice came out flat. "Sometimes you sacrifice your teammates when it's convenient?"

"It wasn't convenient." Li Na's voice shook slightly. She'd moved to stand beside Shen Bolin, her spiritual energy still stabilized from the transferred injuries. "It was necessary. I was dying. The expedition would have failed if—"

"If you died? So, you killed him instead?"

"He was going to die anyway!" Li Na's composure cracked. "The palace would have killed all of us if we didn't work together. This gave us a chance to complete the trial. To get the Celestial Dream Lotus. That's what we came here for!"

Tian took a step forward, letting the sword intent around his blade intensify and distort the air itself.

"Tian, stop." Shen Bolin's hand moved to his weapon. "You're an Oneiric Sovereign. I'm a Lucid Lawbearer. You know how this ends."

"So was he." Tian gestured with his sword toward where Hou had died, and where there was no sign of his existence left. "And you killed him anyway."

"We didn't kill him!" Li Na's voice rose higher. "My bloodline ability transferred the injuries, yes, but that's not the same as—"

The attempt at justifying her actions made Wu Kangming feel sick.

"You took his life to save your own. What else would you call it?" Tian's grip tightened on his sword, which was screaming at him to cut and end the people responsible for his brother's death.

"I'm sorry," Shen Bolin sighed, sounding like he meant it. "But this changes nothing. We still need to complete the trial. If we fight amongst ourselves, we will accomplish nothing except killing each other."

"Then we’ll all die."

Tian crossed the distance to Shen Bolin in two impossibly fast steps, the blade already cutting upward in a diagonal arc aimed at the Lucid Lawbearer's neck.

Shen Bolin's spiritual pressure exploded outward as dream qi condensed into a defensive technique to protect him from the incoming strike.

"Dreams Made Real", he cried out, trying to solidify his defense into something absolute.

But the Law was too slow to activate, and even if it did, Tian's sword didn't care about Laws.

The blade cut through the golden layers like they were paper.

Most techniques cared about overwhelming an opponent's defenses through superior energy, but Sword Intent was different, it was all about understanding exactly how and where to strike so that defenses became meaningless.

The golden barriers of Shen Bolin’s technique broke apart in front of his face, and his eyes widened as he watched helplessly at the sword descending toward his neck, silver light leaving an afterimage in the air.

Then something shifted.

Reality bent slightly, just enough that the blade's trajectory altered by a few critical centimeters. Instead of cleanly severing Shen Bolin's head, the sword passed through empty space half an inch from his throat.

Shen Bolin didn't waste a second, he activated a movement technique to retreat fifty meters in an instant, his hand touching his neck where the strike was meant to have landed.

Tian looked down at his sword in confusion. The technique had been perfect, his form had been flawless, but somehow, impossibly, it had missed.

"That was me."

Song Wei's voice carried across the distance. The other Lucid Lawbearer had straightened from his casual posture, silver energy crackling around his body.

"Probability Collapses To Certainty." Song Wei's Law activated fully, spiritual pressure flooding the area. "I made it certain that your strike would miss. But I have to admit your sword intent is impressive enough that I couldn't completely negate it. Though a few centimeters was enough."

Tian's eyes narrowed. So, this Lucid Lawbearer wanted to get involved.

"As much as I'd like to see Shen Bolin die," Song Wei continued with a sigh, "I can't let you kill him, at least not yet. I still need him to reach the Chamber of the Sleeper King. After that..." He shrugged. "We'll see."

“Fine.” Tian's grip tightened on his sword. The silver light around the blade intensified, sword intent gathering for another strike. "Then I'll kill you both."

Xie Ling pressed herself even firmly against the wall, clearly signaling that she wanted no part of this confrontation.

Song Wei studied Tian for a long moment.

"Tian, you're an Oneiric Sovereign, which is impressive for your age, certainly, but still just an Oneiric Sovereign. I'm a Peak Lucid Lawbearer. Shen Bolin is a Peak Lucid Lawbearer. Even if your sword techniques are exceptional, you can't defeat two of us. The cultivation gap is too wide."

"I don't care."

"Then you're a fool." Song Wei's silver energy pulsed slightly. "But even fools should understand basic mathematics. Work with us to complete the trial and reach the lotus. Afterward, if you still want revenge, we can settle this like civilized cultivators. But throwing your life away now accomplishes nothing."

Tian opened his mouth to reply, to tell Song Wei exactly what he could do with his reasonable suggestions and civilized compromises. But before he could speak, another voice cut through the tension.

"Tian might not be able to kill you all by himself." The voice came from behind him. "But he has me."

Everyone turned.

A figure materialized on the step, emerging from nothing as if walking through an invisible door. Copper-brown hair, pale blue eyes, and completely free of any injury.

"Hou." The word came out before Tian could stop it, before Wu Kangming could reassert control over his reactions. "You're alive..."

Inside his mind, Wu Kangming struggled with the impossible reality. This double identity situation was seriously messing with his ability to think clearly. Part of him, the part that had lived as Tian, felt overwhelming relief and joy that his friend, his brother, was alive and well.

But another part, the part that knew the truth about this world and this tournament, recognized what Hou's survival meant. If Hou could resurrect after dying, if the palace's absorption hadn't been permanent, then there was only one explanation. All this time, the person he'd thought was his sworn brother had actually been Ke Yin, his opponent, the one he was supposed to kill in this final trial.
