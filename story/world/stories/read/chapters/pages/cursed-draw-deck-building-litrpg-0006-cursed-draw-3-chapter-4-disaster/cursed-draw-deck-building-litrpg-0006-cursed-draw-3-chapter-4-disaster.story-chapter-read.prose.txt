Soren couldn't quite believe what he was seeing as he soared over the deep orange of the Dune Country desert. The sand-covered land below him was strewn with shattered and burning wreckage that covered both the road he was above and the titular dunes that it cut through.

The Country of Dunes was less a nation and more a collection of disparate tribes that migrated between the various tiny lakes that dotted their wasteland of a country.

Despite their lack of unified government, infrastructure, or culture of any value at all, Soren had been forced to admit the Dunerunners did a marvellous job building and maintaining the ornate stone road that ran both the length and breadth of their country.

The wandering desert dwellers might have surprising skill in stonework, but they lacked a standing army or militia; there should have been no way they could stand up to the expeditionary force Soren had sent south to begin bringing the pseudo-nation to heel.  And yet the wreckage below him could only belong to the supply train that had followed that same force.

While it was possible that warriors from one of the dune tribes had snuck past his main force and deployed powerful enough Cards to overwhelm the rearguard that travelled with the wagons and Resonance-powered machines, Soren knew that simply was not the case.

If the main force that he had deployed still existed in any meaningful capacity, it would be here now, salvaging what water and other supplies that they could. The utter lack of anyone’s presence could only mean that the army he’d sent south had suffered a devastating defeat, and the Dunerunners had broken through to shatter the supply train behind them.

That simple reality begged an equally simple question. How? Even if the various tribes of this desolate land had somehow banded together, they shouldn’t have had the capacity to challenge Soren’s far better-trained and equipped warriors, let alone deliver them a crushing defeat.

The mystery and the event itself left the flame-winged god utterly incensed. Soren had never actually tasted defeat before, and while he hadn’t been with the expedition at the time of this clash, they were still his soldiers, doing his bidding and belonging to his nation. In every way that truly mattered, this was a defeat of Soren and a thwarting of his will. It was a new sensation to The Lord of Creeds, and he hated it with a depth he would struggle to put into words.

With a flap of wings, Soren pushed further south, following the path of destruction that inevitably led him to the site of an enormous battle. The Lord of Creeds was hardly a tracker or expert in warfare; he had people to do those sorts of things for him. However, from up high, even he could get a rough idea of what had taken place amidst the dunes.

His finely armored warriors had formed up in their thousands, forming loose ranks where they could both support each other and leave room enough for their Deck wielders to Manifest creatures and spells. That was no surprise; that was what they had drilled to do when met with resistance of any sort.

Still, it seemed to Soren that his army wouldn’t have done so unless their advance had been met by a large coalition of the tribesfolk. He didn’t really need to employ logic to tell, as the highway and dunes below him were littered with bodies by the hundred, both dressed in the shining bronze of his own warrior’s armor and the robes and gembesons of the Dunerunners.

“They advanced on the tribesfolk in a tide of steel and Cards, and it was going well.” He said, giving voice to his thoughts so he could try to walk through what had taken place. “ The resistance was light, but determined, and then…something happened.”

The blonde-haired god swopped low several times, criss-crossing the blood and body-covered slaughter that the orange dunes had become. He could tell that his own forces had been outnumbered in terms of pure manpower, but it hadn’t mattered until some sort of relief force had come from the west and driven deep into the ranks of his advancing warriors, breaking their lines and driving them to the west in a grand route.

Soren had sent vast swathes of soldiers wielding Decks and led by the most military-minded of his own siblings; it was simply impossible that the folk of the Dune Country had defeated them like this. Even making use of the towering waves of sand to cover their advance, the tribesfolk lacked the strength of arms and Cards to truly threaten a force led by multiple Creed children and working together with military precision.

So who had done this? The question answered itself after around twenty minutes of observing the remnants of the battle. The god caught sight of sky-blue cloth that the combined efforts of the wind and sand had not yet fully covered. Frowning, Soren dove downwards and landed so he could more closely inspect what he had spotted. In his frustration, the god failed to slow his descent properly and slammed into the hard sand with enough force to shatter both of his ankles and shins.

The pain was brief as his body immediately consumed Life Essence from the sea that was his pool of it, and knitted torn flesh and broken bone back together within seconds.

“Tch,” He said, annoyed at himself. The injury was irrelevant, but his blood had spattered out from his legs as his shinbones pierced the skin and covered the lower portion of his pale robes in red droplets that would stain. Now that he was no longer on a pilgrimage, he had promised himself that he would stop ruining so many sets of robes.

With an irritated yank he pulled the half-buried blue fabric from the sands. It tore a little as his god-rank strength was applied without care, but soon Soren was holding an abandoned banner bearing the icon of a huge brown feathered bird with its wingspan blocking out an image of the sun behind it. Soren’s eyes had been narrowed in frustration and against the brightness of the real sun high above the desert, but now they widened in surprise.

The bird in question was the Shimurg, and the banner was that of the Shimurg Nation military. Whilst far from the powerhouse that the Final Peaks were, Shimurg country was an almost equally ancient nation to the south of the orange dunes. They were certainly close enough to have assisted the Dunerunner tribes, but given the contentious relationship between the roadbuilding desert dwellers and their southern neighbours, it should never have taken place. They had been skirmishing and raiding each other for hundreds of years, with the Dunrunners stealing water and supplies from the Shimurgians, and the Shimurgians striking north seeking treasures and ancient Cards buried beneath the sands from when the land here had first become chaos-locked, and ceased to warp and shift.

While his fury at the defeat of his forces had only grown, Soren had to admit he was less frustrated with his own people. They could never have predicted that a military force from the south would come to the aid of the Dune-dwelling nomads.

Dropping the blue fabric, Soren curled one of his wings downward to incinerate the remains of the torn banner before leaping into the air once more. Even caught off guard and shattered by their opposition, there had to be something left of the expeditionary force Soren had sent south, and he intended to find it.

A little over two hours later, the Lord of Creeds landed once more, this time containing his irritation enough that he didn’t break any bones in the process. Before him were the remnants of the army he had sent south, which he had spotted following the western coast back north. His forces looked exhausted and filthy. The water they were next to was salt-filled and couldn’t sustain them, and with their supplies destroyed almost a hundred miles to the south, they had obviously been keeping themselves alive with what they could carry, along with some clever Card usage to supply themselves.

The force that should have been approximately twenty-five thousand warriors strong was around ten thousand now; a horrifically high casualty ratio for a single lost battle, but Soren realised they had likely been harried by the tribesfolk as they made their way north, not to mention the toll taken on the force by simply marching through the desert without sufficient support.

It had been a wise move by the trio of commanders to bring their army near to the coastline. Yes, the water here was no use to anyone, but the dunes gave way to beach that was far flatter and thus easier to traverse. As the god of the Final Peaks had flown into view, the remaining army had seen him and began to genuflect at his presence. It had flowed through the army like a wave, so that by the time Soren had landed, the entire force was down a single knee before him.

They might be dehydrated and starving, but the armed force was still wise enough to bow before their god when he arrived. That was all except a single figure who came striding in between the rows of kneeling warriors. He was dressed in an even more ornate version of the bronze colored armor that the soldiers wore, with amber skin turned dark by the desert sun, short-cropped blonde hair, and eyes that shone the same flaming orange as Soren’s own.

The frustrated god would have to be blind not to recognise one of his own ‘siblings’. While the other Creed children lacked Soren’s own power and did not have a hand on the pillar of divinity as he did, they were each extremely formidable when compared to a regular person. This one, Savat as he was known, wielded a Deck based around Might and Wind. He was, in theory, the best general in Soren’s arsenal. He had also grown whilst Soren had been on pilgrimage; it had surprised the Lord of Creeds when he had called the Children together at his return, and it surprised him now. The eighteen-year-old Savat was taller than his master by almost a full head, though that ceased to matter when he dropped to one knee like the other soldiers, but directly before Soren.

“My Lord of Creeds.” He said to his older ‘brother’ without meeting the flame-winged god’s eyes.

Soren’s face was plastered with contempt at this man who had failed him, but it wasn’t like he would snap Savat’s neck for a single disaster that no one could have predicted. He did however, have questions and demands.

“Ellie and Fyera.” He said, naming the other two Creeds that had been in charge of this army. “ Where are they?”

Still not looking up, Savat cringed a little at the question, but managed to answer it without allowing his obvious fear to infect his voice.

“Fyera is dead, slain by a team of five Shimurgian officers who attacked her in unison. She panicked and Challenged one in an effort to stymie their multi-directional assault,but the man simply refused her…” Savat trailed off, not needing to finish his explanation. Once someone declined a Challenge both parties were locked out of being able to use their decks for several minutes. At that point, the Shimugians would have easily torn her apart four against one.

“Ellie is too injured to walk, and so is at the front of the column where our wounded are being carried and tended.”

That almost made Soren smirk. The classic Final Peaks doctrine of keeping the wounded at the front of a marching formation had been largely abandoned as it forced the army to move at the speed of the slowest members, but it seemed Savat had chosen to revive it.

“ So all of this has left you with sole command.”

“No, my lord.” Replied the darker-skinned man who was still looking at the sand in front of where he kneeled. “ I have promoted several of the non-Creeds into positions of power for now,as even one of us cannot lead such a force alone.” Soren had to admit that was true, but he still didn’t like it. The Creed children were the closest thing the god of the peaks had to contemporaries, and he didn’t enjoy the concept of regular humans being treated as their equals.

“You will be returning north alone.” Soren declared suddenly. “ I will Manifest a Card that will fly you to my escort force, and then you will make all efforts to catch up with Gwenia on her quest to a newly arisen world. I will be the one who takes this army and conquers the Dunerunners.”

“My Lord, the warriors need fresh supplies, water, medicine, and rest. They cannot be expected to continue waging a campaign after such a devastating defeat, not without time.”

At that, Soren really did smirk. The contemptuous little smile leaking its way across his features.

“That will be my concern, not yours. You will tell me the details of this disaster you allowed to take place, and then you will return north with all haste, as I have already stated.” Soren did not like repeating himself and his irritation at the events that had transpired, and the man before him was obvious in the tone of his voice.

“As my god demands,” Savat said, before finally meeting Soren’s eyes. “There is one development I believe will please you, however.”

As he spoke, the former commander of the Final Peak invasion force’s eyes glowed blue, a sure sign he was accessing his collection. A few seconds later, they returned to normal, and a Card had been summoned forth onto the palm of his hand. Even before Savat spoke again, Soren’s eyes  widened, and he leaned down to take it.

“A False God.” Murmured Soren, his mood instantly lifting. “ Where and how did you come across this?”
