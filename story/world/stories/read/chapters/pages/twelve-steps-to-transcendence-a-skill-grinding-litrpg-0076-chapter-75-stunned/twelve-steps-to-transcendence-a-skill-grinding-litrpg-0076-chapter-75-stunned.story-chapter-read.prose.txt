Which was perfectly fair, really. Trash shouldn't be rewarded for killing trash.

But a real kill, something Tier 2, was worth a good deal more.

And there were hardly any of those in a Tier 1 rift. You hunted every single day for every single point.

So your final score was the plainest measure in existence of how hard you worked. You got a few extra points at the end for performance metrics, but killing was the only real method.

Knowing how hard it was to get points, she was immensely proud of herself. Because even with her [Mana Beam] vaporizing things left and right, it was still a grind.

Aiming actually took a toll on her wrists. And she had to constantly endure the awful smell of burning flesh.

Hunting from the very first minute they entered, she and Thomas had bled for this.

Well, Thomas had physically bled. But she suffered immense mental fatigue managing him. Leading a meat shield required exceptional tactical brilliance on her part.

She essentially carried their team's strategic burden entirely on her own shoulders.

And it came easy to her, especially when men like Thomas threw themselves at her, pretending their interest was hidden. But she could see their drooling from miles away, and she made sure to use them as the fools they were.

They made for good meat shields. Laughable if they thought they even had a chance. It wasn't her fault they threw themselves in harm's way for her, she never asked.

She thought back to the giant frog at the pond. That beast should have been the biggest kill of their early rift. It rightfully belonged to her the exact second she decided she wanted it.

But a filthy commoner stole it right out from under them and took her bag with it. He robbed her.

Everything after that had been a pain. Facing monkeys that teleported right behind you, monkeys that turned completely invisible, and that massive monster tide at the monolith.

She had done everything she could.

Reaching the top of that awful stairway had left Thomas beaten half to death and her entirely unable to breathe.

But every single kill there was theirs and theirs alone. Consolidating their efforts, they reached exactly four thousand six hundred and twenty-two points.

Comparing numbers with the other nobles at the top of the monolith, she saw most of them preening over a measly few hundred to a thousand at most.

As far as she knew, hers was the best hunting score in the whole rift. Well, assuming she didn't count Theodore or Ethan. She was a prodigy. Earning every one of those points herself, she absolutely did not care who knew it.

She just cared about the bet. She cared about putting a peasant back in his rightful place beneath her boots.

She had to win. She had to win!

That was why she demanded the count in front of everyone.

"Seven thousand four hundred and twelve."
