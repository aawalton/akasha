“They’re never going to stop.”

The whisper was the only sound in the tiny room where Ryada stood, one hand splayed against the face of a monitoring cabinet and the other cupped against her ear. The room was dim, and flickers of gray writhed above the plate she’d slid out of the cabinet. She didn’t look away from them as the door behind her opened, but when another’s self brushed against her, she shoved the plate hastily back into its slot.

“You didn’t wake up Seth for her turn?” Tilliver stood in the doorway. “You’re too good to us. Were you even going to wake me?”

“I’m just restless.”

“Take something for it. We’ve been pushing ourselves harder, so we should all rest when we can.”

She smiled at him as she slid off a heavy bracelet and tucked it into its own slot in the cabinet.

“What were you looking at just now?” Tilliver asked.

Ryada turned away.

“Sit down and let me redo your braids,” she said, pulling down a seat that had been folded against the monitoring room’s wall. “You look like you were plucked at by angry panpi’is on your way here from your bed.”

“You’re not someone who can talk about other peoples’ hair.” He flicked at one of the purple curls spiraling near her temple before taking the seat. “So what were you looking at? Did you sense something we should worry about? Any messages?”

“Nothing like that.” She tugged the ties out of his long hair. She tapped a light on the wall, and it glowed like it was full of embers. The pale strands in her hands shone. “I was…curious about how things are progressing behind us.”

“The nests we cleared today? Everything is well?”

Her knuckles rested for a long moment against the back of his neck.

“Ryada?”

“Everything is as expected,” she said lightly, her hands moving again. “And we are making more progress now.”

“If we keep this up, we might even request an additional squad to keep up with the work behind us,” Tilliver said in a satisfied voice. “You were right about spreading your skill wider. It works, we’re moving twice as fast, and we’re still leaving everything clean enough for the restorers to finish up without trouble. Of course the mission takes as long as it takes, but it feels so much better to be striding forward instead of slogging. Postor and Emban are in a much better mood today. We’re going to be one of those squads that others are envious of. I don’t mind that.”

“I’m…everyone being happy makes working out here less of a trial, doesn’t it?”

“I know I’ve been finding it easier.” He held himself close to her for a while, then withdrew. “What are we going to do about Emban leaning too much your way too soon?”

“We’re supposed to have these conversations all together.”

“I know, but since she’s the only one with such a singular preference this early in our time together, and that singular preference is our most widely compatible member…it will lead to resentment. It already is somewhat. I don’t want that. I want us all to find someone and remain friends.”

“I want that too.”

“I think when you feel drawn to one of us more than the others—if you do—you should say so. Even if it’s early. If not, it can’t be helped. Don’t put pressure on yourself. But if you did happen to know already…”

“I don’t,” said Ryada. “Being as comfortable with the closeness of others as I am has its own drawbacks.”

“I’m sorry for bringing it up then. I’m not sure what the right thing to do is, so maybe I should have kept my words to myself.”

“You’re fine, Till.”

“I’m a bit useless to the rest of you actually. The sculptures are so pitiful. Of course it’s based on complex magic, and I knew the efficacy would be unimpressive at first. But I’d be more useful out here with my wizardry. That bothers me more than I expected.”

“None of us would win the prize for strongest new knight if they gave one out.”

“I know. And given the type of squad this is, I wouldn’t even be with you if I were much more powerful. That would be a loss. I’ll just have to content myself with watching you kill everything for now. When they ask me what the most impressive thing I did on Goldbush was, I will point to you and say, ‘I assisted Hn’tyon Ryada-bess.’”

“I pictured Life to Water being different.”

“In what way?” Tilliver asked.

“I don’t know. You’re right. I should have let Seth take her turn in here.”

“It’s cramped and corrupt. Not the best place for clear thoughts.”

“No.”

She finished his hair in silence then patted him playfully on top of the head. “All done.”

“Thank you, Ryada. What were you saying when I came in here? I didn’t quite hear you. Something never stops?”

“Oh, I don’t even remember,” she said. “Too sleepy. See you when I wake.”

“Bithe’s in charge of first meal. It should be good.”

After she’d left, Tilliver sat for a while, checking over everything in the room. Then he reached for the plate she’d shut away and the bracelet she’d been wearing.

The flickers of gray light appeared.

“Just the area we did today,” he said. “She worries too much. Her skill will finish those off soon.”

He put on the bracelet and cupped his hand to one ear.

Nothing out of the ordinary. Just the sounds of things that once lived here dying.

In the past, Victor had gifted Alden with some mice, a rat, roaches, and a disturbing clump of fur—all deceased, which was a quality he hadn’t appreciated enough. Before she completely disappeared to attend to her own assignment, the griveck killed several of the problems he would have encountered without her help. He saw the evidence in wrecked mimics and splashes of vibrant red.

But she’d also bullied a few of the fake demons, riling them up so that they’d be better sport for an Avowed on his first adventure in the Rapport III training fields.

Alden assumed her thoughts were along those lines. He squatted on a hill, looking down into a valley where demon goats roamed. Using his lenses, he’d been able to spot five. He could hear them screeching. They were charging threateningly at anything that moved. Mostly, that meant running at each other, and then stopping suddenly before they collided.

Maybe they’re friendly to other members of their species, and being demons is only halfway screwing up their ability to recognize their own kind.

They were hazard mimics, but he was seeing chaos-afflicted mountain goat creatures. He’d been studying them for a while. They weren’t as big as bokabvs, but they likely outweighed him. They seemed to be able to detect movement from a significant distance away despite the darkness. And the speed they moved at looked wrong on them.

They had forward-pointing horns that were perfect for spearing Alden in the chest, and each one he’d been able to check had a slash on its back right hip that had to be the griveck’s version of a signature.

Could be her way of weakening them for the human cub to practice on. But it seems more like she was infuriating them for me. Alien nurture sure is different sometimes.

Sorry, Ms. Griveck, but I’m not going to approach this with the type of battle plan you must’ve imagined.

The whole goat valley was stained redder than any area he’d run through since landing. The fastest route to Bithe was right through the center of it.

Going around would take time, getting killed by a goat would be stupid, and wasting his skill or his supplies would make him useless when he reached the others.

The griveck’s assumption that the squad was in a rough spot seemed true. They weren’t answering any of Alden’s attempts to make contact, and the lenses were telling him his comm dots were still working fine. They also weren’t moving toward him like they were supposed to be. At least, Bithe wasn’t.

It had taken Alden a while to be sure. His sense of his entruster’s distance wasn’t well trained enough. Not for faraway locations. If he and Bithe were both in the gym at Celena North, Alden would be able to picture exactly what spot in the room the Artonan occupied with little effort. Out here, he could say with absolute certainty that Bithe was across the valley, but was it that next hill? Two more?

He’d thought he would have found them by now, though, considering how much ground he’d covered. They were either stuck somewhere or backtracking.

The hills were higher here. Running straight down this one was going to be a little dicey even with his wordchains. There were no doubt things he couldn’t see in addition to the goats. Traveling through this much red would likely result in some damage.

Dozens of ways to make mistakes all piled together. Nobody to tell me what’s best. Consequences for others if I get it wrong.

Not my first time.

He extended his left hand and made the kelsithe into a shape that resembled a snow shovel. Instead of preserving it, he tried to hold it in that position purely with mental focus.

Okay.

He thrust it toward the ground. The kelsithe entered the dirt easily, but it took more than one attempt to make it behave exactly like he wanted. It was a minute before he managed to make the shovel head expand to an even larger size, deeper and wider into the ground, and then encapsulate a hunk of soil and grass. He preserved it that way and stood with something like one of the gym’s hundred kilo bags on a handle.

Familiar.

He waved his arm through the air a few times, getting a feel for the weapon physically and with his skill.

Yeah. Familiar.

He couldn’t twirl this and flick it like his Winsterminator, but at least one of the goats was less intelligent than Winston. He’d seen it fly into a fit of rage against a bush that had done nothing wrong, and now it was ramming hooves and horns into the splintered remains.

That’s the one whose territory I trespass through. Maybe it’ll come at me. Maybe it won’t. No more time to waste.

Get to Bithe.

One morning, the squad found a dying golden fenyan near their campsite. Emban spotted it struggling in the red mud and went to end its suffering with a spell.

“Not even a demon,” she said as she let her auriad wrap around her bow again. “It looks like it barely got caught at the edge of your range Ryada.”

Ryada had dropped to her knees in the mud beside the animal. Its fur was almost completely gone. Its tail was only an oozing stump. “They’re supposed to live in trees!”

“Are you both all right?” Seth called, heading toward them.

“They’re supposed to live in trees, Emban! There aren’t any trees near here.”

“Well, it’s…things like this are going to happen sometimes with the way we’re clearing the patch. You know it’s not your fault.” Emban looked to the others for help.

“It’s not at all,” Postor said at once. “I’m the one who suggested you finish your skill off for the night right before we made camp. The fenyan must have been struck by it then.”

“I’m the one who said we could put off range testing last week,” said Seth. “We’ve all grown more powerful.”

“We could take the morning and do that now,” said Tilliver. “And I should have mentioned last night that we’re close to the edge of our patch. Close enough to hit things we shouldn’t with some of our magic.”

“If I’d checked the area more carefully yesterday, maybe I could have spotted the fenyan,” Emban suggested. She motioned at Bithe.

“I…it looks like an elderly fenyan that was nearing the end of its natural lifespan. I’m sure it has many offspring that will thrive in this patch we’ve been healing.”

There was a pause before Ryada looked away from the corpse toward Bithe. “Are you pretending to be able to tell the age of a random Goldbush animal to make me feel better?”

“May it nourish the land that feeds its young,” said Bithe. “I’m sorry. Everyone else had already said the more obvious things.”

“I thought Life to Water would be nourishing like that.”

“It is,” Emban said. “You are.”

“Corrupted life becomes water that brings life,” said Bithe.

“I know,” said Ryada. “That’s how it’s supposed to be.”

She stood. Her authority greeting rang out—cheerful and familiar to all of them. “It’s all right. I’m being absurd today for some reason. You’re right that these things happen. It’s been a long trip, hasn’t it? A little more, and we can all go home.”

“Nothing unsettles you for long, does it?” Emban said admiringly.

“Ryada’s the one who keeps the rest of us sane out here,” Seth agreed. “We’re all learning. We’ll learn from this too. No more golden fenyans…of any age.”

A chain for speed and one for gracefulness.

A few foundation points.

A body that was taller and stronger. Night vision through enchanted lenses that cost more money than he’d ever imagined having last year. Specialized shoes that let him feel the ground without hurting his feet.

All of that practice with Bobby and superheroes and all by himself and with Stuart.

The kelsithe, light despite its current bulk, was barely awkward. He swept it through the air as he ran. He heard the air rush around it. Once, he felt something he hadn’t seen strike his skill. Far more impact than anything the size of an insect should have had.

He imagined he’d killed a fake demon, one no larger than a Thegundese grasshopper, and he didn’t slow down.

Flat out. Downhill.

Ground element everywhere his feet could go. His trait sent him over it, the feeling turning almost lightweight on some steps.

Nothing on Anesidora was ever like the ground on Thegund. The way he could run across it and look back to see that he’d been running on something that was barely there. Pits and trenches in his wake as it collapsed.

This wasn’t like that either, but it was closer. He hit a stony place, and gravel tumbled away from his feet and rolled.

Should he have fallen there?

He was already gone.

The more he ran, the more he remembered what he wanted to remember. How, when you made your final choice, there was nothing left but action.

Once the lab was gone, you ran or you died. You ran until you died.

For Kibby. For his entruster.

The mindset wasn’t quite that one, but it was closer to it than he could get normally.

Bithe became more and more like a weight in front of him. A sun he was turned toward. Pulling him forward as the slope gentled, almost leveling out.

He skimmed nearer to the stupid demon than any of the others.

It didn’t come at him.

It didn’t come.

He’d passed it.

Good. He wanted to save as much power as he could to help Bithe.

He heard a screech from a different goat. To his left. Up ahead. Well over a hundred yards away. He’d thought that one wouldn’t notice him from so far. It had. It was charging at him.

All right.

Brutes and spells and weapons have come at me faster.

He let the demon slam once into his preserved kelsithe, because that was what he’d decided he’d do before he started running.

It’s heavy. Dammit. Bithe needs me at full strength.

One of the goat’s horns snapped. The animal rolled. Not quite like Winston or the bokabv in his dreams or anything else.

Close enough.

Alden brought the kelsithe down like a hammer. He heard his own breathing. No battlecries down here. He’d decided that before he started running, too.

He ended preservation inches from the struggling demon, and the misty weapon returned to his arm almost simultaneously. The dirt wasn’t perfectly contained like it was in a sandbag, but it was at least a couple hundred pounds of ground moving as fast as he could make it move.

It slammed into the goat.

His lenses informed him that his dust blockers were filtering out particulates.

He wasn’t sure the demon was dead. He could still sense magic from the hazard mimic. Was that leg sticking out from under the soil twitching in its death throes, or was it planning to get back up?

Alden paused for the first time. A few precious seconds were sacrificed to thought.

Shovel.

His kelsithe flowed forth obediently.
