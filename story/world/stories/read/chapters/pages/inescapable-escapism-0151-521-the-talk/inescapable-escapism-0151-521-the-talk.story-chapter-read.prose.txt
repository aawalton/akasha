We reached the end of the aisle, and Dad immediately turned left into the next. His eyes scanned the shelves, seemingly looking for something, but I had no idea what. We’d already loaded a couple of tubs of the paint we’d found into the trolley, and he hadn’t mentioned needing to pick up anything else, but we’d been wandering around the hardware store for a while.

Dad seemed intent on going down every single aisle, and I was beginning to suspect it was intentional, that there wasn’t actually anything else he wanted to buy. Instead, I was pretty sure he was just trying to drag our shopping trip out because he didn’t want to go home, and that was why I hadn’t said anything or asked him what we were looking for.

I didn’t mind, obviously. I was absolutely happy to walk aimlessly around the store for hours if it meant we could stay out of the house for longer. It was fun to just spend time with Dad, and I had nothing planned for the rest of the day. Well, apart from reading, but that didn’t really count. I could do that whenever.

Which book did I want to start next? The covers floated through my mind, trying to tempt me, but I was torn. I really wanted to finish that series I started a while back, the weird fantasy one about space and past lives, but it was going to be sad. I’d somehow managed to miss all spoilers about it online, but I could just tell.

A silent sigh escaped my lips. I wasn’t ready for that. I’d just finished a book that had torn my heart apart, and I didn’t want to go through it again. Not so soon, anyway. I needed something more lighthearted, something that wouldn’t make me cry…

Mentally, I crossed a few of the books Dad had bought me off my list. Anything that seemed like it might break my heart was gone, but there were still a few remaining that I could read. It was difficult, though. There was no way of knowing which would have unexpectedly tragic twists without Googling them, and I didn’t want to risk seeing spoilers.

Maybe a romance then, I thought as Dad stopped to examine something. I was pretty sure I had a couple of those, and they weren’t generally too sad. I mean, sometimes the characters broke up or got into a fight during the book, but at least they generally had happy endings. That made it slightly easier to deal with the sad bits.

Yeah, that’s a good idea, I decided. I’d read one of the romance books when I got home, and then, maybe it would help me feel more prepared for my date with Duncan. It might show me what to expect or what could happen, and then I’d be ready.

Or perhaps it would show me how unprepared I was. It could point out just how lacking my knowledge of dates and dating was. I mean, I’d never been on a date before, if the thing with Duncan even counted as a date. I still wasn’t quite sure, and I didn’t want to let myself believe it was, just in case.

Duncan wasn’t as inexperienced as I was, though. He’d dated people before, and that realisation did nothing to soothe the growing anxiety within me. If anything, it just made me feel even more nervous.

He was popular and good-looking, so girls always had a crush on him. Plus, he was funny and so kind, which definitely helped. Even if someone was just into him because of how he looked, they couldn’t help but get sucked in by his personality too.

Whenever he did start dating someone, though, it never seemed to last long. They’d be all over each other for a few weeks, and then it would just… die out. I had no idea why. I couldn’t exactly ask him; we never talked about that kind of thing, and there were never really any rumours.

Not that I’d been listening out for them. I hadn’t. It didn’t matter if or who he dated, and I never paid that much attention to his romantic life. We were just friends, so that didn’t matter if he was with someone or not. It was his choice. He could date a hundred people for all I cared. I hoped he wouldn’t, but…

I let out a shaky breath as Dad stopped again, crouching to examine something on a low shelf. Maybe it would be better if I just told Duncan I was nervous and why, I thought. That might help, right?

But before that thought had even finished forming, I was already dismissing it. I couldn’t think of anything worse than whipping out my phone and confessing to Duncan that I was such a loser that I’d never been on a date before and was overthinking something he’d most likely said as a joke.

It would be mortifying, and he would probably be so embarrassed if he knew I’d misread the situation like that. It would ruin our friendship, and I couldn’t bear the thought of that. He was one of my best friends, one of my only friends.

No. I’d just… play it by ear. I could wait and see how it went when I got to his house, and go from there. For all I knew, he could just be planning to have a super normal afternoon together playing video games, like we’d done hundreds of times before.

“This one’ll do,” Dad said as he straightened up again, a tube of something clutched in his hand.

“What is it?” I asked curiously.

“Filler. It’s used for stuff like covering up cracks in walls and that kind of thing,” he explained. “It’s pretty good, but unfortunately, I’ve learn it dries out fast if you don’t put the lid on properly. Really should have figured that one out sooner.”

He let out a laugh, and I joined in, but my mind was distracted again. I knew he must have bought it to fill the holes in the wall Mom made sometimes, even though he hadn’t mentioned that. He wouldn’t. He never really spoke about it, especially not to me, and I didn’t bring it up to him.

It felt weird to talk about. Wrong, almost. I knew it wasn’t right for Mom to act like that, and it scared me. I was terrified of her hurting Dad badly, but there was nothing I could do about it. I just had to keep doing my best not to annoy her or set her off.

“Does it work?” I blurted out, trying to distract myself from my racing thoughts. Dad had already said it was good, but that wasn’t what I meant. “Like… does it fix the crack or does it just, like… cover it up?”

Dad looked thoughtful as he placed the tube in the trolley and started walking slowly again.

“Huh, you know what, I’m not sure. I’ve never really thought about that before. I assume it holds the crack together a little bit, like superglue, but I don’t know how much that actually helps with the structural stability of the house, you know?”

“Yeah,” I replied.

Part of me was tempted to Google it, but I didn’t really care that much. Even if I read the answer, I’d probably never have to use the information, and the question had done its job. It had stopped my mind from spiralling any further.

We continued to walk in a comfortable silence until we reached the end of the aisle, and Dad looked around. His eyes scanned the signs above each of the aisles before he met my gaze again.

“Well, I think we’ve got everything we need from in here. Want to check out the garden centre?”

A smile tugged at my lips.

“Sure!” I said happily.

I’d always loved the garden centre section of the store. It was usually my favourite part of any hardware store. They didn’t always have them, but when they did, it made me happy. There was no real reason for that, though. The only time I’d really done any gardening was that summer when I tried to keep the flower bed Dad planted alive, but that didn’t count.

Still, there was something so calm about the area normally. Even when the main part of the store was filled with people, there were always fewer people outside, and the scent of freshly watered plants soothed me.

But as we approached the automatic doors that time, another emotion began to pull at me. Panic. Sheer terror started to grow in my chest, and I could feel my breathing speeding up as my heart thumped in my ears.

Nothing was wrong, I tried to tell myself, but that didn’t help. Something felt inherently dangerous, and I had no clue what it could possibly be because I was in reality. I was meant to be safe there. Nothing bad could happen to me, like in the other worlds.

Oh. A sudden realisation slammed into me, making my steps falter for just a moment. That was what it was. The other worlds. They were the reason I was panicking so much about something completely normal, something that I’d done so many times before.

It was the humidity. It seemed to hang heavily in the air, and the mugginess, mixed with the rich scent of wet earth, reminded me of another place. Somewhere unsafe that I’d been before. I’d died there a hundred times over.

The swamp world. The weird time loop one. That was what I was thinking of, I realised. It smelled worryingly close to the way the garden centre did, but there was no scent of decay in the air. Just plants.

I was okay, I told myself. My body was just freaking out because it thought I was back in that world where I was going to die and then be forced to relive my last few minutes over and over, but that wasn’t the case. I was just in a B&Q with my dad, and nothing bad was going to happen.

Still, panic thrashed in my chest, making it ache, as I continued to trail after my dad. My eyes scanned the plants, barely seeing them as I forced myself to take slow, deep breaths.

“Huh,” Dad said as he came to a stop, looking down at a weedy-looking orange flower.

“What?” I asked, my eyebrows drawing together.

Dad glanced back at me, a slight smirk on his lips.

“Do you think it’s meant to look like that?”

I couldn’t help but snort.

“Dad!”

“It was a genuine question!” he shot back, holding up one of his hands in surrender. “I was just wondering because… I mean… surely not, right?”

Pressing my lips together to hold back my smile, I looked at the offending plant. Dad was right; it was pretty ugly. Each one had a thick, dark stalk that rose high out of the plant pot, sporadically dotted with tiny leaves, but it was the flower itself that was the worst part.

At the top of the stalk, bright red tubular… things seemed to erupt from it. I wasn’t sure if they were petals or individual flowers, but there were so many stacked on top of each other.

Only the top few layers were red, though. After that, they quickly became a pale shade of green, and beneath that was a tuft of what seemed to be dying or dead, shrivelled flowers. Either that, or they were buds and had yet to actually turn green or red.

“They’re not… that bad,” I lied.

Dad burst into laughter.

“Really?” he demanded, still chuckling slightly. “You don’t think they’re that bad? Look at them. They look like something that would be found on Mars!”

A flash of dizziness washed over me, almost making me stumble. Not Mars, actually, but I had seen a concerningly similar flower somewhere else. It was in another world, on another planet. I hadn’t actually been there, but a version of me had, and the flower had definitely been an alien species.

I stepped back, moving away from it instinctively.

“I mean… you’re not wrong,” I admitted, making Dad laugh again.

The one in front of me definitely wasn’t a plant from a different planet. Logically, I knew that, but I didn’t want to risk it, just in case. The memories of it snapping around and shooting some kind of acid or venom at me were too fresh in my mind.

“This one’s pretty, though,” Dad said as we moved on.

I didn’t want to look away from the spuo, but Dad had stopped walking again. He was waiting for me to say something, and I could feel his eyes on my face. It would have been weird for me to not even glance at the thing he was talking about.

My body felt tense as I looked away, though. It was almost as though it was waiting for the plant to take advantage of my distraction and attack, but nothing happened. It was just a completely normal flower.

“Oh, that is lovely,” I remarked.

It was. Each blossom had bright blue, papery leaves that seemed no thicker than tissue paper. They were delicate and beautiful, and I reached out before I could stop myself, gently stroking one.

That was stupid, a voice in the back of my head warned me, prompting me to snatch my hand away and wash it immediately. I should have known better. The brightly coloured ones were always the most dangerous. If they didn’t evolve to hide themselves from predators, that meant they were the predators, and I should be scared.

I shook my head, trying to push that thought aside. In another world, it had kept me safe on countless occasions, but I didn’t need it in my reality. I didn’t come face to face with predators anywhere near as often as I did in the world that was pulling at my consciousness, trying to suck me in.

There were no sentient plants in my reality, so I didn’t need to worry about the brightly coloured ones, I told myself firmly. I was completely safe, and nothing bad would happen to me if I touched them. They didn’t have any neurotoxins, nor would they release a puff of gas that would knock me unconscious, so it could begin to digest my body. It was fine.

“Do you want to get anything?” Dad asked, interrupting my thoughts once more.

“Mmmm… I think I’m good,” I replied.

“You sure?”

I hesitated. I was conflicted. Part of me did want to buy some of the beautiful plants and try to make another flower bed. For the short period of time that I’d had the last one, I’d loved it, and there was something strangely satisfying about working in the garden, but I didn’t think I could handle it. Not again.

The guilt that would consume me as I watched yet another load of plants that Dad poured his love and time into die would be too much. It would hurt too much, and I couldn’t cope with that. It would tear me apart, and I already wanted to cry whenever I thought about the last time it had happened.

I had to swallow before I could speak again. A lump had lodged itself in my throat, making it hard to get the words out.

“No, it’s okay,” I said, keeping my eyes fixed on the ground so that Dad wouldn’t see the tears that threatened to form there. “I mean, I loved the flowers you planted for us last year, but… I don’t know if I’ll have enough time to look after them once school starts again.”

That was a lie. I hadn’t even considered it until I said that, but it was a good point. I mean, all I needed to really do was water them once a day, if that, but sometimes after school, I was so exhausted that all I could do was flop down on my bed and stay there all evening.

“Ah, okay,” Dad said, thankfully accepting my excuse. “So, what are your plans for the rest of the holidays? You’ve still got a couple of weeks until you’re back to school, right?”

“Yeah,” I said, grateful that he’d changed the subject. “I’m not really sure, actually. Probably just read a bunch.”

Apart from my date with Duncan, that was.

“That sounds nice. Is Phoebe still in Italy?”

“France,” I corrected him. “Yeah. She’s back next week, though.”

Dad beamed at me.

“Oh, great. I bet it’ll be nice to see her again. What have you two got planned?”

My hand twitched towards my phone as the urge to text her flared within me.

“It will! Mmmm… not much, really. I think we’re just going to have a few movie nights and stuff. A bunch came out whilst she’d been away, and we’ve been waiting to watch them together,” I told him.

“Aww, lovely. And what about Duncan? Are you going to see him at all?”

My heart seemed to skip a beat at the mention of Duncan. I shouldn’t have been surprised that Dad asked about him. I’d known Duncan since I was a kid. He used to have sleepovers at my house when we were younger, and Dad had met many times, but I was still taken aback.

The urge to lie flared within me, but I hesitated. I didn’t really want to. Not to Dad, anyway. If it were Mom asking me about him, I never would have told her the truth, but I could tell Dad.

“Yeah, I’m actually going over to his tomorrow,” I said, trying to keep my tone casual.

“Oh really? What are you kids going to get up to?”

I could hear my pulse racing in my ears as Dad looked down at me.

“Not sure really. I think we’re just going to play some video games.”

“That should be nice?” Dad replied.

“Yeah.”

There was a pause, and out of the corner of my eye, I saw Dad glance at me again. My anxiety spiked, and I felt the need to say something else. I had to distract him. I had to change the subject or ask him something, but I couldn’t think of anything.

“You don’t sound too sure about that,” Dad said, concern edging into his voice.

I could barely keep from hyperventilating. I wanted to run, to get away from that conversation, but I knew that wouldn’t help. Dad would just assume something was wrong, and then he’d worry even more.

“No, no. It should be good,” I told him, trying to sound as reassuring as possible, but I don’t think I was particularly successful.

Dad’s eyes remained fixed on my face, and I silently begged him to look away. I was used to hiding things from Mom, but it was so much harder with him, and I just didn’t want to. I wanted to be able to tell him the truth.

“Yeah?”

“Yeah,” I started, trying to gather the courage to say more. “It’s just…”

I trailed off, my words deserting me. Maybe I should just lie. I could say there was something else going on, that I was worried about something unrelated to him, and that would be fine. I could probably think of another thing to talk to Dad about. I mean, I was anxious about pretty much everything, so I had a lot of options.

School, I thought, grasping at ideas. I could pretend I was just panicking about school next year. He’d understand that. I mean, all of my teachers had said it would be tougher. We had our exams at the end of the year, and if we did badly in them, it would be bad.

We wouldn’t be able to stay on at the school for sixth form, but that could be the least of our worries. A lot of universities demanded certain GCSE grades, and if we didn’t achieve them, there was no way we’d be accepted when we applied in a few years.

I was sure there were some jobs that didn’t require a degree, but my school never really talked about them when they taught us about careers. They made it seem like if we couldn’t go to university, that was it. We’d be unemployable, and that couldn’t be right. Lots of people didn’t have degrees.

“Yes?” Dad asked, his tone becoming increasingly worried.

“We kind of…” I started before trailing off again. “I think it might be a date.”

“Oh!” he said. “Wait, you think it might be?”

My face was burning, and I wanted to see his reaction, but I couldn’t bring myself to meet his gaze. I kept my eyes fixed on the concrete floor as we turned back into the main building.

“Yeah,” I muttered. “I’m not really sure… but I think it might be. Don’t tell Mom, please!”

The panic was enough to force me to look at him again. It came on so quickly, and I knew if he even mentioned it to her, she’d stop me from going. I wasn’t sure what she’d do, but she’d find a way to make it impossible for me to leave the house.

If I managed to get away from her, she’d find a way to get me back, even if it meant calling the police on us. She’d threatened that before, and I knew she’d play it off as a concerned mother, but it would be mortifying.

“Don’t worry, I won’t,” he reassured me, holding his hand up. “I know your mother is a little… touchy about some things, and there’s no need to worry her. You’re a smart kid, and Duncan is a good lad. I know you’ll be safe.”

“Thanks, Dad,” I told him with a smile.

“That’s alright, sweetheart,” he said before hesitating. “Your mom has given you… The Talk, hasn’t she?”

“What?”

The word slipped out of my mouth before I could stop it, and I stared up at my dad in horror. He seemed just as uncomfortable as I was, though.

“You know… the birds and the bees?”

We were in public. There were people around us, and he was talking so loudly.

“Yes!” I cried, my voice high-pitched.

“Okay, good,” Dad said, sounding relieved that he wouldn’t have to be the one to do it.

“Oh, god,” I muttered, peering around at the other customers walking past us.

Any one of them could have heard what Dad said, and the thought of that made me wish lightning would strike and put me out of my misery.

“Well… if you do need anything,” Dad started.

“Please stop.”

“Any protection or anything,” he continued, pretending he hadn’t heard my request. “Just let me know. No questions asked.”

“I don’t!” I said, but it was more of a squeak.

“I know it’s embarrassing, but I’d rather you be safe than sorry,” he finished. “And now I’ll stop.”

“Thank you,” I muttered.

My face continued to burn as we made our way towards the checkout. I wasn’t sure it would ever return to a normal colour, and don’t get me wrong, I was glad Dad offered, but the thought of going to him and asking for… anything like that, made me want to die. It would be horrible.

“So…” Dad said as he loaded the tubs of paint onto the conveyor belt.

I glanced at him, my anxiety skyrocketing again.

“What?”

“How long have you two been an item?”
