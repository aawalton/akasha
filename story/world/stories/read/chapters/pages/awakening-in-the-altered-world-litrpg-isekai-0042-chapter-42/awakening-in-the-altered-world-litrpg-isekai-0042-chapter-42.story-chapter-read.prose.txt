As Alex crossed swords with the first of the swords-wielding minions, the one wielding two blades at once, all he could think was, Ice is ridiculous.

The minion’s attacks felt at least as heavy as an Ice Warg’s, despite being significantly slighter and lighter. He was certain a reason for this was peculiar icy magic which cycled through its body, brightening at the instant of impact; it was very visible in the minion’s translucent, ice-sculpture-like physique.

Alex absorbed the slash. The Force mana in his own body brightened, helping take the sting off the slash. He ducked a second, very telegraphed slash, and sliced for the minion’s neck.

Which only reinforced his opinions on Ice mana.

PING!

His slash left a nice inch-deep cut running down its neck, zero bleeding.

If that peculiar ice enhanced the minion’s physical capacities, it enhanced the minion’s defenses twice as much. It did make some sense to Alex that hardening would be a speciality of an ice-based buff.

He wasn’t even certain slicing off its neck would kill it, necessarily. He rammed a Mana Spear into its face, which hardly slowed it; he didn’t really expect it to.

Beside him, Beckett used a little body lightning infusion to quickly slip between the two minions chasing him, and struck them with a quick, forking arc of lightning.

The lightning might’ve induced a split-second’s freezing, but only a sliver of it was able to penetrate the minions’ bodies. That peculiar mana seemed even more resilient to mana than it was to physical attacks; it drained mana much like the way the Frigid Knight’s slashes drained mana, Alex noticed, except obviously on a much smaller scale.

It did kind of make a tiny part of him wonder, in the back of his mind—should I have focused more on getting an Ice skill?

Except he also did instantly think of two pretty decent reasons why his choices, given his circumstances, were probably fine. The first, obvious reason was Ice talent was slight/fair, which likely meant it’d take weeks to get a single proficiency level.

There was also the fact that there was a little bias going on, probably? He was seeing relatively high-level—well, high-level for a low-leveled rift—applications of Ice; it was entirely possible if this was full of, say, Water-based creatures, he’d be equally as astonished by all Water was potentially capable of.

He did take a few exchanges to appreciate what, exactly, this minion combo was accomplishing via Ice—and also feel it with his soul senses, the way this peculiar mana was integrating into their bodies, as easily as a raindrop in a pond; it felt utterly fluid, despite being an ostensibly foreign mana.

Alex was also very much able to appreciate the effects with his body.

CLANG!

He caught two blades at once with his sword before shucking it off, and dancing out of range of the shield-bearing-minion’s thrusts.

His teeth gritted. “They’re getting faster!” he shouted.

“It’s the melting ice!” said Beckett a few seconds later. “Are you feeling this? It’s helping them, it’s feeding this freaking buff—”

He stabbed a shield-bearing minion in the chest, rolled out of the way, and locked eyes with Alex. Beckett grinned, breathless. “We’ll have to—”

The Frigid Knight slashed, and Beckett cried out; he managed to get out of the way of a hellacious arc of ice. An arc which was likely a half-second away from catching him.

Beckett’s eyes flickered back toward the icy arc; his grin had left his face.

“It’s also the Elementalist,” called Alex, still feeling the buff more acutely with his soul senses as he exchanged once with the shield-bearing minion. “It’s starting to pour its mana into its buffs, it’s helping sustain them!”

The minions themselves didn’t fight very intelligently. One, the dual-wielder, was speedy and very aggressive; Alex was forced to parry it repeatedly to keep it off him. The other, the sword-and-shield Guardian, was slower and quite defensive. The strategy, as far as Alex could tell, was to try to pen him in, and separate him from Beckett—making them fight multiple two-on-one duels.

Actually, that wasn’t the strategy.

The actual strategy, in a wider sense, was likely to wear him and Beckett out. Which would also likely succeed if things went as they were currently, since neither he nor Beckett seemed to have the firepower to kill these buffed Level 5 minions right now, and the Boss was still standing there, those giant blocks on its legs slowly melting.

And once they did—that thing will make its minions look like Ice Slimes.

Alex had realized, entering this fight, that there’d be plenty about it he wouldn’t fully appreciate until he was deep in it. Still, he didn’t need to get deep in to realize this Boss had barely scratched the surface of its abilities.

Which all led to a pretty obvious conclusion.

“We need to focus the Elementalist, now!” said Alex. “Retreat if you have to, just shell that thing!”

And in the next twenty seconds, the two did exactly that.

Two waves of projectiles; Alex focused his on clearing the way for Beckett’s. The first wave were countered pretty effectively between the Elementalist’s rectangular ice shields and the Frigid Knight’s ice arcs. In the second Alex managed to get time a Mana Shield well, breaking up an ice arc that was meant for a lance of fire and lightning.

Beckett’s fire-lightning lances were pretty variable right now; the variability had improved as Beckett practiced, but it was still pretty high. At times there was more interference between the lightning and fire manas, making the lance scarcely more effective, if not less effective, than a typical fire lance. The lance Beckett managed to land on the Elementalist at the very start of this fight was one such lance.

And then there were lances where the fire and lightning worked near perfectly, enhancing each other beautifully, such that the damage was comparable to multiple normal fire lances. Lances such as the one that struck the Elementalist right now.

It was lucky, and yet after seeing the results, Alex really wasn’t in the mood to celebrate.

How many of those will it take to kill it? Like, ten? Twenty?

There was a part of Alex’s mind that said he was an idiot at this precise moment.

Forget the Boss—he and Beckett were getting walled by freaking minions. He’d overestimated himself in hilarious fashion, as had Beckett—at least, severely underestimated the enemy—and they were in a race against time and melting ice, a race that was likely about to become a humiliation.

Which was all probably true, to an extent.

It was a voice of pure panic, and also, following it, pure despair; it did have a certain allure in present circumstances.

It was a voice Alex was happy to entertain once the fight was finished.

Right now he was utterly uninterested in that kind of thinking.

If this thing actually wins—an eventuality he’d realized was a possibility from the start, hence the emergency crystals—I’ll at least force it to beat me.

What he was deeply uninterested in was defeating himself.

Alex focused.

His mind, in that instant, felt very clear, and very cold.

We’re not going to be able to break it with projectiles.

If we can get in close, isolate it, and get it melee, we might be able to stack damage faster.

At least, Beckett will, he’s much better at blitzing offense. Except he’s being chased by two minions, one of which is at least matching him for speed, the second of which is trying its very best to herd him away. And he can’t abuse lightning like before because he doesn’t have ice in him.

He wasn’t going to stack Beckett full of ice simply so Beckett could try abusing lightning again, obviously, but a few ideas did enter his mind.

“I’ll break you out, if you fuck the Elementalist up?” was what he said to Beckett.

“I’ll have to get—free, first!” said Beckett, teeth clenched.

“Yeah, I have an idea for that,” said Alex.

“What!”

“Shield abuse.” Alex felt just a twinge of shame as he said it. He had to admit, he was maybe relying on these things a bit too much; they also felt far more versatile than any of his other abilities, to be fair. He suspected that wasn’t a coincidence; actually having the Skill, rather than creating a shield Mana Manipulation, opened up a lot in terms of versatility and ease of use.

Could be a bit more creative with the Skill selection, but I’m still trying something new.

Beckett grinned, and—“I like it, but these things are fast, and they can use shields too, and plus there’s that—freaking slash—”

He stepped off a Guardian minion’s shield and somersaulted backward; one such slash passed a few feet away.

“Are you sure it’ll get me there?”

Alex gave a smile. “I didn’t say shield use, I said shield abuse.” Beckett clearly hadn’t seen the depths to which one could abuse a Mana Shield. And he told the guy what he wanted.

Beckett burst out laughing, before adding a suggestion. “Please slap some force on that thing?"

After which it was time to execute.

Two Mana Shields briefly walled off the minions in front of Beckett, who burst through the gap; he had a little airspace. The issue was there was about fifty feet between him and his target, the Elementalist, standing ten feet away from the Frigid Knight itself. And its giant sword was already beginning to brighten.

And at this point, a flat Mana Shield appeared five feet in front of Beckett, a few feet up. A lily pad, one might call it.

As Beckett stepped off of it, he infused a little lightning into his legs.

At the same time, the Frigid Knight slashed.

Alex very much wanted, in that instant, to place a Mana Shield where he knew its arm was going to go, and deflect its aim a little—therefore also deflecting the arc. Unfortunately, fifty feet was a little beyond Alex’s casting range.

Luckily Beckett’s burst of lightning-infused speed seemed sufficient to get past it; and besides, it seemed to have misjudged where Beckett was trying to get to.

A second Shield appeared about fifteen feet in the air. It was angled downward, and the change of direction was largely what Alex was banking on to get past both the Slash and the rectangle ice shield.

Beckett turned mid-air, planted his feet against the shield like a swimmer performing a turn.

Alex deflected a slash off his bracer, and was forced to simply take a second slash across his ribs; it was a price he was forced to pay, and which he was fine with.

He gave Beckett a mental salute. Godspeed, you beautiful bastard.

Alex rammed that Shield with Force, overcharging it as much as he could, just as he’d done with the Mana Spears that killed his first Gelid.

At the same instant, Beckett infused his legs with so much lightning Alex was pretty sure it was borderline dangerous.

Finally, Beckett pointed his spear straight at the Elementalist; a spear whose tip exploded with lightning as the Lightning Spear Skill activated.

A lightning-infused, super-charged Beckett carried the momentum of his charge, and was flung directly at the Elementalist, his spear aiming for its heart.

The Elementalist’s staff also exploded with light as a shield appeared in front of it.

This shield, like most shields, took a fraction of a second to summon, and by the time it materialized, Beckett was already beyond it.

What followed was the unmistakable sound of steel shattering ice.
