Everyone’s a critic.

It’s easier to complain than it is to compliment, a fact that Raika is very familiar with, considering her relationship with herselfs, but she wasn’t expecting just how much some of the disciples would whine.

“I didn’t want this! I didn’t want this! I didn’t want-“

And on and on and on!

Thickening the structure of his ear canals helps to shut him up. It’s a quick patch to the “problem” of having the sound of four hearts beating simultaneously echoing inside your skull, but frankly it’s a little insulting that he’s getting so frustrated about it.

“I’m not even halfway done!” she complains, shaking her head at the Zhong-whatever. “Let me finish before you keep complaining!”

“No! Please! I wanted a step forward, not this!”

“Well, you asked for immortality, immunity to disease and aging and blades, and I can do at least three of those. The fourth one is up for debate, though really, the “blades” thing is pretty iffy too. I’m not just going to give you a shell, that’s just a waste.”

“I take back my request! I don’t-“

“Nah, that’s not how this works. If you want to reverse it you gotta get a different reward. I guess. I just made that up. I’m having way too much fun figuring out how to improve resistance to shearing. Did you know that if you weave the walls of your cells closely enough, give them a bit of stickiness with proteins, and then make them tighten against impacts, it will tear all of your muscles off of your body every time you flinch? Unless I’m adding a few thousand layers of extra ligaments to all of your fibers, I’ve got to figure out a new way to make them flexible on top of all that. It’s fascinating!”

“Honored spirit, please, I-“

“Shhh, I’m gonna add some kidneys now.”

She also replaces his lungs with the new versions she made. Because it’s a good opportunity to help him be quiet, but also because the modified alveoli in the new prototypes is so fun. They wiggle.

He’s complaining a lot for someone that doesn’t even have functioning pain receptors right now. The agony would just cause pointless stress on an already stressed system, and she can feel if the nerves are working or not, so there really wouldn’t be much point. Maybe if he were outside her body it would be different, but really, he’s just being dramatic.

Several other disciples take relieved breaths as Raika shuts up the screaming idiot, adjusting to their own modifications and changes. Smaller versions of her existed for long enough to reach into their biologies and get a quick feel for their desired changes, which really didn’t take long, and now, most of them are either learning to live with the changes or soaking up the fluids of the Twin Bloods Pagoda.

Unfortunately, no one’s actually gone out and wished for anything else yet, which means most of the preliminary work so far has just been mapping their biologies. A bit bland, but undoubtedly still interesting.

Everyone is different. Often only by degrees, but it’s fascinating to see how every little variation manifests and theorize about where they came from. The slight difference in the shape of the leg bones- is that specialized training? Skeletal regeneration from old wounds? Minor mutations, not large enough to do anything dramatic, but enough to shift certain pieces here and there? The way that one’s organ functions deviate, the microsecond differences in one’s heartbeat- are they an attempt on the Heavens to cause differences, born of luck, born of circumstance, or something altogether different?

Everyone is unique. Every body follows the same baseline blueprints, aaaaaall the way down at the bottom, but every single one is a work of art. Being made of paint and on a canvas doesn’t reduce the originality of an art-piece, just highlights the shared origin and method.

Did She ever have thoughts like this? Her original? It’s hard to know. It’s impossible to know, at least for now. But the question remains, loud, because there are no safeguards here.

People as art. Flesh as joy. The science of discovering the bodies of others as something to change and transform, as something to make music from.

The disciple she has in her hands squirms, though only enough to pull her attention back to him. Her hands have kept moving while her eyes have drifted away, and his fear tastes sharp and acrid. Delicious, but a bit overwhelming. She gives him a reassuring smile as she moves some of his intestines out of the way, developing a kidney out of her hand to plant behind them.

“You’re doing so well!” she tells him, tilting her head to get a better angle into his guts. “Don’t worry about the space, I’m making the new organs smaller so they fit. Plus, you don’t need this much intestinal tract! Especially not once I’ve gotten your stomach better.”

He lets out a quiet little huff noise, which is impressive considering how brand-new his lungs are.

“Oh! Those are adjusting really well! Good job. But hold still, I would hate to clip your spine, that’s going to take me minutes to fix.”

“Honored spirit!” someone says from down below, calling her attention away. She turns to face down below, eliciting another whimper from the disciple she’s working on.

She spawns a lesser mind out of the floor, pulling herself out of the juice. The cultivator steps back, flinching as she emerges, long and svelte and sleek and dripping with purple and yellow ooze, smiling wide.

“Hi there! Do you have a reward you’d like? Besides the slime?”

“I… actually wanted to ask, honored spirit. What exactly does this material do?”

The disciple holds her hands out, the purple and yellow of the fluids on them juxtaposed against the dark blue of her robes and the paleness of her skin. Her face, which has a fascinating combination of cheekbones to jawline to muscle-fibers to skin-tension, is quiet, reserved, polite, mildly distraught. The microexpressions twitch and shiver beneath the skin, looking like-

Raika blinks, refocusing on her words.

“Ah! I like you! You’re asking questions! Mostly everyone else is already in it!”

All four of the other disciples in the chamber stiffen, all of them spread out across the entire cavern for “privacy”, but visible to her senses. Some of them are even soaking properly, and those in particular flinch and start to cycle a little Qi at her words.

“I designed it to be as useful as I could make it for everyone at once, which means it doesn’t do all that much. If you soak for a few minutes, your skin will get much stretchier and healthier, and your hair will gain a lovely bit of added shine and volume. If you soak for about the time for an incense stick to burn, it’ll get through to your muscles, should heighten the fiber-count and density.”

“And… if one soaks for too long?”

Raika shrugs. “Oh, it won’t kill you. If it gets all the way to your bones, it’ll make them tougher, and all the way to your organs, it should infuse them with lots of materials and goodies. Can’t make any changes too intense, or it’ll end up causing cancers and such. In theory, it should make your bodies tougher, heal faster, more flexible, and a bit stronger, but only to a limit. It can’t rewrite your cells, rewrap your muscle fibers, replace any pieces, that sort of thing.”

“A foundational upgrade, then? An addition to our vitality?”

“Sure, if that’s what you want to call it. Bare minimum, it should add a decade or two to your aging process. Did you have something specific you wanted?”

“No, honored spirit, I simply-”

“Alright bye.”

That version of Raika falls inward, collapsing into a central point that is her sternum. Skull and ribcage both collapse inward, crushing the organs inside into formless meat and slurping back down into the ground.

With her information passed to the rest of her, many different Raikas all compute, add it to their memory, and move on. New things to remember, and new pieces to play with, and new stuff to ignore, because these people are so boring sometimes.

Her attention flickers, switching back over to something more interesting.

In one of the other hallways of the underground trial-maze she’s built, someone dies.

She watches through reams of neural tissue, processing through synesthesia of scent and hundreds of eyes and ears, as a swinging blade comes in from an oblique angle and severs a cultivator’s head from his torso.

The young man, clad in the robes of of the Peak of the Iron Tortoise, doesn’t even have enough time to scream before he loses access to his lungs. He doesn’t even realize that he’s dead, actually- there’s a moment where she experiences, through his own brain, the confusion of his perspective tumbling, without any sort of body to hold it in place. She feels his shock at seeing his own body, his own bleeding neck.

And then his head hits the floor.

Someone screams. Another disciple, one nearby, perhaps, in sight of the incident. Qi rages through existence, a wall of earthen magma and a sense of scale forcing the corridor to expand, to explode, to burn actively into charred carbon.

Raika’s focused on the more important details, though.

As magma washes through reality and burns into the walls, it falls on the body of the fallen disciple, falls onto his head and the rest of him. Without the active ability to use Qi, without a functioning self-image from a functioning mind, his body has no defenses, burning and melting far faster than Raika’s tunnels.

His face collapses inward as magma burns through his skin, his facial muscles, his jawbones-

Which is fine, honestly. She caught him before the oxygen dried up in his blood, and there’s plenty of time to reinforce his cranium before the magma boils through him.

Before anything else can occur to ruin his corpse, she’s absorbed that too, pulling his organs and flesh, spiritual and all, down into the floor and into preservative fluids running through the entire infrastructure of the tunnels.

It doesn’t take more than a few seconds to bind the neck and spinal cord back together, re-establishing connections between the meridians there. She would rather just make a whole new body, something simpler, but if she can avoid it, it’s better not- again, she still can’t make the ephemeral Dantian or meridians for a body. Sometimes the body is too far gone for her to do more than salvage, and then she remakes them a body.

She does modify them, though. She did tell them there’d be consequences, and she did tell them they might die, so she doesn’t feel any guilt about it. It’s more interesting to play with them anyways.

Everyone is unique. Every body, a distinct, special experience, each one its own source of inspiration. The veins almost all according to the same patterns, but so slightly off. Some optimized, others not, some improved, others diminished, but most just different, and isn’t that a joy?

And she can Change them.

A shriek echoes through her cartilage as a trap-door the size of a river opens beneath another’s feet. They don’t quite make it in time to anchor themselves, sickle-blades flashing out and bouncing off of the high-tension blubber of the upper chamber.

She screams as she falls, tumbling, a spatial distortion warping the distance until she’s falling for miles instead of the few feet Raika had space to make. Only when she unclenches the “muscle” that is her ability to touch space does the space return to normal, and the cultivator, for all of how she’s shielded herself, goes splat.

A jade talisman around her wrist makes a barrier that shatters. A belt around her waist bursts into action, wrapping around her in a millisecond and warping her into a partially ethereal state, which holds some of her body together. A glowing talisman flares to life in a brilliant flash, a single blinding burst of Qi and power manifesting suddenly and blinding her for an instant.

She’s only mostly dead, then. Her guts have exploded out of her, bones left at jagged angles, one of her lungs violently pierced and forcing what’s left of her face to vomit blood as she tries to breathe.

But she’s alive.

The impact made her come apart like a wet bag full of sharp rocks- which, after an impact like that, is most of what she is. Flesh deforms entirely out of place, forsaking the qualities that would allow it life on the altar of physics.

And yet she lives. She will live, for at least a few more hours. The damage is severe, but there’s enough Qi in her and she’s progressed far enough down Core Formation that to do less would have to be a willing choice. She has surpassed the conventional possibilities of biology, and in that, has become something more.

And now that something more is lying as a wet bag full of sharp rocks on the floor.

Raika decides to take this one in a little early. No reason to wait when there’s nothing she can do to fix herself.

In another hallway, someone loses an arm to a series of stabbing, slicing constructs, humanoids made of vines and spikes and fibrous tendrils carving into the cultivator as they retreat. Two allies abandoned this one to the battleforms, trying to escape- they’re in a puzzle room now, which is filling with six different kinds of acids and gases they need to identify before death.

This one, meanwhile, jinxes and runs, their cultivation making paint-strokes made of ash and plant roots to drag them to-and-fro in a rather versatile display. Between the paint-strokes and their movement technique, they’ve managed to evade decapitation, but partial disembowelment and the loss of a limb managed to catch up with them.

She picks up the bits that fall off them, storing them for later, keeping them alive with artificial blood and other fluids. Even if they don’t die, they can hardly fault her for keeping a piece they lost. Not like they could use it anymore anyways, since she’s the one that can keep them alive.

And they’re just so juicy. Great to have the materials she needs to keep experimenting. She will have to find a way to get spiritual organs somehow, and the flesh does affect the spiritual. She just has to work on figuring out the rules.

On the surface, two warriors break off a kiss as one slides off the other’s sword, dying in their arms. Raika pulls the body away into the “soil” as the other one weeps.

In a cave system deep in the mountains, one adventurous soul pushes a few steps too far, defying a boundary around which others cultivate and cycle Qi. They are made into a part of the cave, their body fusing inch by painful inch to the material around them.

On an open field, a group of young cultivators work together to collect bushels of rare materials from one of the few garden-patches that have naturally formed. One of them sneaks a bite, away from the others, and improperly cycles the resource, choking as roots coil and extend down their throat from the improperly harvested plant. The organism takes root once more, through their body, and Raika takes the rest away.

There are corpses in this little world. Bodies, particularly around their entrance to this place, where pools of eyes larger than ponds stare unblinking at a false sky above. Those from before her awakening.

Since she said her name, not one living thing has died within the confines of her body.

And yet, she’s gained more material than she’s ever had.

All the more to work with. All the more to think about.

Remembering is hard. Remembering is, to some extent, impossible. She could dedicate all her resources towards it and likely progress, inch by agonizing inch, until she managed to recall some of what she’s kept from herself. She will, later. The safeguards were put into place over months of evolutionary alchemy, with all of the conscious and unconscious competence she could muster, and even in her former state, she has that in abundance.

So. For now. Forward.

The past can stay behind. The things hard to think about can be dealt with when she’s ready, properly, step-by-step. In the meantime, she can think new thoughts, in new directions. Directions without safeguards. Directions that won’t lead her into any memory or thought or idea of She who came before.

All new thinking in an all new world.

Staring at the growing collection of unique canvases she’s collecting, Raika makes sure to numb the nerves, quiet the minds, and store the majority of it for later. Resurrection or reuse, she doesn’t know- that can be figured out another time.

There’s plenty of free material to experiment with.
