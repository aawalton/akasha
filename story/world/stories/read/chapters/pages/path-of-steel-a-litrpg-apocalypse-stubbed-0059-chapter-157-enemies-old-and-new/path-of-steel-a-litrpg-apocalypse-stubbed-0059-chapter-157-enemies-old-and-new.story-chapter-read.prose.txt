“Why do you think the tower is like this, Marla?” Gary asked, resting on a tree stub.

“What do you mean?” she replied.

“It’s so needlessly cruel. People killing people for no reason, people killing people with reason, people killing monsters, monsters killing people, monsters killing monsters… They didn’t run out of cruelty, I ran out of categories. Why? Why are they making us do all this?”

She gave a sad smile. “I don’t know, Gary.”

“And then there are floors like the gymnasium, which are just silly. Beef boys in yoga pants? Who does this? Why create them alongside tentacle horrors which suck your brain out through your nose?”

“I don’t know.”

“I thought it was just the Floor of Trials because Quirk had designed it and he’s crazy, but he had no power here. The fourth floor has existed since forever. It was always silly. Why, why would someone create a vast cosmic tower and sprinkle it with yoga pants?” He sighed. “I just don’t know anymore, Marla… Every time I think I know the tower, it throws something new at me. What am I supposed to do?”

She rested a hand on his shoulder. “Your best,” she replied. “I don’t know how much you’d experienced on Earth, Gary, but our planet was equally dumb and chaotic. There were billions of people, each doing their own thing. Trust me, it made very little sense.”

“No, it didn’t. I had my job as a programmer. I knew how things worked.”

“And how long had you taken to figure everything out?”

His lips tightened.

“We’ve only been here six months, Gary. Of course everything is new and confusing. It’s a whole new world—a bigger world compared to our planet. But, in a way, it’s also simpler. All we have to do is become stronger. Survive and climb—that’s all.”

“It’s not that simple. Should I kill enemy climbers just because they’re on the enemy team? I have nothing against them.”

“Should you kill the enemy soldiers in war?”

“Marla, those are completely different things. A war is serious. This is a game.”

“How is it a game if people die?”

“Okay, it resembles a game.”

“It’s silly, yes. But it’s war. Everyone who chose to come here knew what they were getting into. They came to war, Gary.”

“I don’t believe that. Everyone was living together on the Clan Floor. They were drinking together. How can we go from that to killing each other?”

“You don’t need to hate someone to kill them,” Marla said, taking a step back. “This is the tower, Gary. It’s brutal. The war had already started on the second floor, and it will never end, even after we reach the top. This is just one battle. Of all the climbers currently here, how many do you think will become toppers? One person? Zero? To everyone except us, killing each other is a natural part of the climb. The person you drink with today might become your enemy tomorrow. They’ve made peace with that. On their way to the top, they will have to fight everybody. That’s why they can come here and attack people just for being on opposite sides of a game—because it’s something they’ve resolved to do since they first became climbers.”

Gary crossed his arms. “So, I should kill them too?”

“You have to. Climbing is already hard, and giving yourself massive handicaps will kill you. We’ve joined a death game, Gary. Killing has nothing to do with morality anymore. The weak die, the strong proceed. That’s all.”

“I don’t believe that,” Gary said, but he couldn’t find more words. He just leaned back, staring at the sky. The fake sky.

“Maybe this is why they did what they did to Earth…” Marla whispered. “It’s all a game to them. They’re so deep in their death game that they assume everyone is playing.”

“I wasn’t playing.”

“Neither was I, but we’re in it now.”

“I’m still not playing. I’m fighting a war.”

“You joined the invasion quest, Gary. Whatever your reasons, you’re participating.”

“Okay. I can kill them. I don’t give a fuck. But why must the beef boys suffer? They’re alive. Maybe not fully, but they have feelings, they have reason. They aren’t just monsters in a videogame, and they never chose this war. Why must they participate?”

“Since when does the invaded side get to choose a war?” Marla replied wistfully. “If you care about the monsters of the gymnasium, win the war for them.”

Gary fell silent.

“War is a simple thing. People kill each other. Weak or strong, willing or unwilling, it doesn’t matter. You kill who stands before you. When you become so strong that you can choose to stop the war, then you should consider more things. But not now. Now, you fight, and you pray that it’s you slaughtering others instead of the opposite.”

Marla eventually walked away, leaving Gary alone in the forest. He looked at the sky. His soul pulsed heavy.

“What do you think, Goldie?” he said.

“She made perfect sense. That’s how it’s always worked, right? The strong kill, the weak die.”

“That’s unfair.”

“It is what it is. If you spare them, you won’t grow, so then you’ll be the one to die eventually.”

“These are the same people who doomed Earth. The people who decided not to save all the incompatible population even though they clearly could. That’s who I want to kill, not random monsters and people. I don’t want to play their game, Goldie…”

“You can’t win a game you don’t play, silly. It’s either this or being a farmer on the Clan Floor.”

Gary thought for a long time. Finally, he stood and walked all the way back to Bayonet Gym.

“Gary!” Cog said, training with Alicia on the grass field.

“Hey guys,” he said. “Wanna join my team?”

“What?”

“I’m a field boss now. If you join my team, I can see where you are and your status at all times, but you give me 10% of your merit points.”

“So if I earn a thousand merit points, I’ll only get nine hundred?” Cog asked.

“You won’t earn a thousand, but yeah.”

“Count me in!”

“My grandmother always told me to be safe first,” Alicia said. “I’m in, too.”

“Perfect. Uh, I don’t really know how to finalize this. Send team invite to Cog and Alicia?”

They looked up as if receiving some screen, and soon, Gary’s event screen changed.

Gary Steel, Level 25

Rank: Field Boss

Team: Sean Cog, Alicia Everglade

Merit Points: 0

“I’m going to ask more people, and we’ll catch up later,” Gary promised them.

“Okay!” Alicia replied, and she and Cog resumed their sparring. Gary watched for a while. They weren’t bad. They really must have been training.

Idly, he wondered how close they were to a breakthrough.

It didn’t take him long to locate the next person he was looking for. Asim stood over a small cauldron which he’d placed on a bonfire, mixing all sorts of ingredients he pulled from his space ring. His beautiful friend, Delilah, was by his side.

Gary approached and explained the field boss situation.

“Sure,” Asim said.

“Can I join too?” Delilah asked.

“Why would you want to join?” Gary asked.

“Because you’re strong, and I want to be safe,” she replied, smiling with her wide, playful eyes.

“She’s good,” Asim said.

“Alright then,” Gary said, inviting them both. When she accepted, he saw that her full name was Delilah Estrazet.

Mini-map, he thought. A small, transparent circle appeared in the corner of his vision, showing a green dot in the middle and two blue ones nearby. Two blue arrows indicated the position of Cog and Alicia, presumably, while a cluster of pink arrows must have been pointing at the beef boy mercenaries. There was no indication of enemies or the terrain, just the relative positions of his team members.

“Nifty,” Gary said. He’d been about to close the interface when he noticed something weird. “Uh, Asim? There’s a white dot right next to yours.”

The young man grinned, lifting a corner of his robes. An imp glared at Gary from under it. “Edgar hangs on to me all the time. Just in case,” Asim said.

“Sure,” Gary said.

“Isn’t it cute?” Delilah said, petting the devil under its long chin. Edgar seemed so done with this shit.

“Yeah. So cute,” Gary said dryly.

He left them to work on whatever they were brewing, but only moments later, a new screen popped up in his face. Gary looked around, realizing he was the only one who’d received it.

New Missions!

The next round of missions will be announced in ten minutes. Field bosses have until then to pick one, or they and their teams will be automatically assigned:

Strike First: We have secured the three bridges! The next step is using them as a foothold to expand our territory. The scope of the expansion will be determined by the reigning sector boss.

Scorched Earth: The waste collectors of Satan Gym are full of radioactive shake waste. Spread this around the conquered Kamowaga Gym so the horrors cannot pass.

Reclaim the Muscle Orchard: The horrors won control of the orchard, but they haven’t had time to reap all the sap. Thirty percent remains. Conquer it, and earn us at least some of the big bucks!

Gary read through the missions. At first glance, they all seemed less important than in the first round. The war’s original chaos was settling. However, the missions being less crucial for the war didn’t mean they were less crucial for Gary.

“Reclaim the Muscle Orchard,” he commanded, and the screen went away.

According to the description, there was some sap remaining. Gary didn’t plan on restarting the muscle sap operation, but since they were already familiar with that field, who knows what they might learn. It sure beat spreading trash or spearheading enemy territory.

The ten minutes passed, and a pro boxer left the gym to yell that the new missions were here. While everyone ran over, Gary calmly walked to his mission’s meeting point, where the Core Formation witch leader, who’d failed to occupy the orchard the first time, waited. She nodded at him, and he nodded back, deep in thought.

I must go all-out, he thought. The brothers are against me, as is the puppeteer… Marla is right. I can’t be hesitant. If I don’t give it everything I got, I will never reach the top.

He saw the band of beef boys stomping his way with glee. “Boss!” they yelled, waving from a distance. Gary gave a small smile. He would protect these boys, and they would earn him merit points. It was a pragmatic but necessary exchange of value. He only wished he could recruit more of them.

Unfortunately, he was all out of aura stones, but thinking to this point reminded him of something else. Something he’d had for a while, and which he intended to never use, but now, it may come in handy…

Gary reached deep into his space ring.

Ball Reader, Rare

Boss Balls are a resource mostly found in the Floor of Trials, floor quests, or other special circumstances. This device can channel the residual aura inside the ball to summon an avatar of the slain boss.

The eggshell-like device appeared in his hands. He stared at it for a long moment. This had been one of the rewards from the Floor of Trials quest, but he’d stashed it away under his bed and never looked at it again. It was only on a whim that he packed it before leaving the Clan Floor.

He’d given away most of his boss balls to turn into Muscle Sap, but he’d kept two due to their sentimental value.

Should I really… he thought, gripping it hard. Every little bit will count…

Gary: Hey, Quirk. If I use the Ball Reader with level 10 bosses, won’t they be useless?

Quirk: They adapt to your level. In this case, they’ll be Level 25 field bosses, and they will listen to your commands like hired mercenaries.

Gary: Damn it. I really didn’t want to hear that.

Quirk: Always happy to help.

A level 25 field boss was too good to pass. Gary acted before he had time to reconsider. He stuck a ball into the reader and let the device pull at his aura. It absorbed a surprising amount. Finally, it glowed, and a ray of light shot from the ball to land on the grass, forming into the shape of the slain boss.

Evil Shrek, Level 23 (Field Boss)

Woohoo! My creations are back! I hated when you gave them all away, you psycho!

Anyway, Evil Shrek. Remember how, at the start of the movie, a bunch of villagers grab pitchforks and come to deal with the evil ogre who lives in the swamp? He turns out not evil at all, but I’m a sucker for a good redemption arc. This version is exactly what the villagers expected: a nasty, baby-eating, rape-donkey-rearing ogre ready to slaughter anything that disturbs his waxy peace, with a couple extra touches by yours truly. After killing all climbers in his zone, he was going to travel to the Kingdom of Very Near to Here and conquer it. He’s also a novice cultivator. Fun, right?

The donkey, which was also part of the boss, looked around curiously. Evil Shrek cocked his shotgun, looking at the perplexed cultivators around him. “This is not my swamp,” he said.
