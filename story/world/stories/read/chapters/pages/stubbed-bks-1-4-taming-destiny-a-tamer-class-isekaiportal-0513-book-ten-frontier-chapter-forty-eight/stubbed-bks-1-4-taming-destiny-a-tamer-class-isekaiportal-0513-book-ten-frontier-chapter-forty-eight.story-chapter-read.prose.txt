Lizil strides towards the square space, pulling a diagram out of her pouch. I follow her curiously. Looking over her shoulder, I recognise it as the same array we looked at together, but it has undergone some significant changes.

Where before it was simply a circle of runes around an empty centre, now it also includes a spiral of runes from the central edge of the runic circle, all the way to the very centre. There, I see a dense collection of several runes, none of which I recognise.

I think wryly about my Rune-Carving Skill – still stuck at Beginner. It’s just that there are so many other things that are more worth my time. I should probably think about removing it entirely – I’m hardly using the Skill and, now I know that there is a limit to the number of Skills I can have, I should probably free up the space for something more useful. There are probably a couple of other Skills that are worth removing, even if they create only Skill fragments.

Shaking my head, I return my attention to what Lizil and the other enchanters are doing. The Master Enchanter is pulling extremely thin metal pieces from her Inventory and handing them out to the others. I recognise them as blackiron from their signature colour, but I can tell from the enchanters’ careful movements that they’re incredibly fragile. Maybe that’s why the array is in pieces – to put something into the Inventory, it’s necessary to lift it up with one or two hands, and with an item this big and fragile, that probably wouldn’t have been possible without breaking or bending it.

“You seem to have been working hard,” I comment to Lizil, impressed at just how fine they’ve got the metal. “Is that Happy’s work?”

She nods, gently passing another piece out.

“I was impressed by just how delicate her touch is with metal.” She nods at the settlement – and the two figures I see approaching us. “There she is now – she wanted to be the one to weld the pieces together.”

“Understandable,” I agree, then, after observing how the Enchanters and their apprentices carefully lay the pieces of metal out in a perfect match to the diagram Lizil showed me, I head towards the two Pathwalkers to greet them. “Hello strangers,” I joke a little, rubbing cheeks with Happy and Hunter when they lean in, bright blues in their spikes signalling their happiness.

We have not had much time recently, it is true, Happy replies with a touch of sadness. I have been greatly engaged with the beauty of metal-shaping – with magic, and without. Blacksmith Thomir and the others would be overwhelmed by the demand without my help.

“Hey, I’m not throwing stones here,” I hurry to assure her as we walk towards Lizil’s array. “I keep meaning to come and visit you too, but there’s never the time. You’ve been busy, then?”

Indeed. Repairing equipment, making anew what is broken beyond repair, creating weapons and armour for the army and tools for the crafters…though our biggest demand is for nails.

“Nails?” I blink. “I thought that the mages were the ones building the wooden parts of the houses?”

It is not for huts, but for their contents. As I have noticed many times since coming to this world, humans seem to require far more objects in their living spaces than samurans.

“Oh – furniture. Of course.” I should have thought about that. As for humans having more of it than samurans, I’m not surprised. I was the one to introduce chairs to the samurans – which they still weren’t entirely sold on by the time I left. “So, you’ve become an expert nail-maker.” I feel a grin pulling at the corner of my mouth. “In fact, we could even say you’ve…nailed it.”

Hunter lets out a hiss of amusement even while Happy groans.

That was a good one, sister, Hunter clicks.

No it wasn’t, Happy grumps, but I can tell it’s all for show.

“No it wasn’t,” I agree. “But I couldn’t resist. Anyway, you’re still enjoying your work?”

Very much so, Happy agrees seriously. I just wish we had more materials. I can see the worry in Thomir’s eyes every time we have an order for new parts. We can repair what we have well enough, especially with my magic meaning that all metal that returns to us can be repurposed, but there is increased demand for new items.

“You can tell him that I’m doing my best to open up new lines of supply,” I tell her firmly, thinking of the ore that is in Tilathis’ mountain.

I will, Happy agrees, sounding relieved. We need iron the most, but other metals would not go amiss. Her eyes gain a faraway look. There were so many beautiful – and functional – objects in Senior Tamer’s great huts. I long to one day turn my claw to making some of those.

I smile at her, remembering well how Happy had inspected things like the window fastenings in Nicholas’ house.

“I’ll do my best to make sure that day comes as soon as possible.”

For a moment, we gaze at Lizil and her group working on the array in silence. There’s excitement in their movements – though they did large-scale enchanting when we created the settlement, this is probably the first experimental project some of them have worked on. I’m a little surprised that Hunter doesn’t go to join them, but perhaps she feels the same way I do – grateful to have a little time together that isn’t in the middle of a crisis.

“How are you Hunter? I’ve seen you more than Happy, but only just. Last I heard, you were working on more rune bombs. Are you still doing that? Have you seen the clay balls the farmers have been using to absorb the plant network’s mana? I thought of you when I threw a full one against a tree and it exploded.”

I cut myself off, realising that I’m asking questions, but not actually giving her the chance to answer them. I send her an apologetic smile, then wait.

She returns my smile with an amused flash of colours in her spikes.

I have been busy with Master Lizil’s project, she admits. We all have. However, the process has given me a good number of ideas to try to affect the impact of my bombs.

“Like?”

Like effects. Her eyes flash passionately, and her claws come up, describing movements in the air even as she speaks. A bomb can explode, yes, but can it not implode? What if it could be thrown into the middle of a wind-blade and suck the mana out of it before it can slice? Or perhaps the bomb could explode, but with healing potion that could help those suffering? Or with a poison specific to the creature it’s targeting? I feel like pure explosions, no matter how fun, only scratch the surface of what my runic bombs are capable of.

I gaze at her with my eyebrows high on my forehead. She’s definitely taking this bomb development far further than I had considered. And in directions I hadn’t even thought about. Whoever would have imagined a healing bomb?

Then again, thinking about it, wasn’t there something of the sort in a game I played years ago? I clearly just haven’t played enough of them if it hadn’t occurred to me that bombs, weapons of mass destruction, couldn’t be used for healing, I think wryly to myself. Though it does raise a question – how do we ensure only those we want are healed? It’s not much good if we’re healing our enemies along with our allies. I should raise it with River – perhaps there’s something in alchemy that could offer the solution.

For now, though, I congratulate Hunter on her creative ideas and suggest a few of my own. In this dangerous land and with those blood bastard elves probably coming here, landmines seem like a good idea. And maybe figuring out how to make proper grenades – the clay bombs do a decent job, but if we can enhance them with metal fragments….

“Happy!”

We’re interrupted by a call from Lizil. I note in approval that the pieces for the outer circle have all been put in place. No wonder Happy is needed to weld them together. The large, muscular Pathwalker nods at me and then steps surprisingly lightly over the earth to Lizil.

My attention back on the array, I realise that I have someone here who might be able to answer the questions I have about it – and who isn’t so busy she’ll begrudge taking the time to do so.

“Can you explain what’s happening with the array?” I ask Hunter.

Certainly, she agrees, then proceeds to detail what each of the runes are and why they’re present. I nod as she explains about the various rune clusters, fascinated by how complex the array is. The more she explains, the more I’m reminded of computer programming, as little as I know about that. Or perhaps how a body works – that, I’m far more familiar with.

Some of the rune clusters are sensors, identifying the mana types and their concentration both inside and outside the design. These are attached to memory storage clusters that will take a snapshot when the array is first activated. They won’t regulate the concentration of mana already within the circle, but they will ensure that nothing else is allowed in.

Working with these are other rune clusters that are attractive, drawing all non-permitted mana towards them.

“So even if there’s another manastorm and a mana bolt hits this land, it will be quickly drained,” I muse. That could be both a good and a bad thing – the manastorm’s results are unpredictable, but can lead to positive consequences. Still, I suppose that when we’re talking about the food that keeps the settlement alive, consistency is what is most important.

Exactly, Hunter replies with satisfaction. Though it may take a little bit of time depending on how much mana is added – what is absorbed must be redirected.

Since the spiral is heavily involved in the redirection, we step closer to see that better. By this point, the outside circle is complete, the circular design encompassing approximately thirty square metres.

It seems small in comparison to the field of approximately four hundred square metres that the farmers and Plant Mages are tending a short distance away, but we are limited in the amount of blackiron we have, and we don’t have all the materials to forge more. It’s already a feat that the few ingots we had have been forged into a complex circular border of runes measuring more than twenty metres in length and a spiral covering three times that. It has been achievable purely thanks to Happy and her delicate magical touch.

Anyway, when magic means that we’re able to speed up the growth of crops to the point that it’s possible to get multiple harvests in one day – if we push it – even as little as thirty metres squared is enough to feed a good number of people. Besides, this is mostly a test to see if the design works in practice as well as in concept. If it works as well as I hope, then we’ll have to create bigger ones – even if it means buying some of the needed resources from the merchants.

I do my best to listen as Hunter continues explaining how the spiral draws the collected mana inwards and condenses it in the centre, building up pressure – though I’m amused by the interest in her voice as she mentions the possibility of a massive explosion if the containment runes aren’t powerful enough. However, I’m distracted by watching Happy.

The way she Shapes metal is incredibly impressive, and I actually have to ask Hunter, a little apologetically, to pause her explanation so I can focus more on Happy’s work. Fortunately, Hunter doesn’t take offence.

Closing my eyes I watch what Happy’s doing with her mana. I frown. She’s not actually Shaping the metal directly, I muse. Instead, she’s acting like…a magnet?

Not a magnet I’ve ever seen, but the comparison seems to be the closest I can find. Instead of the mana suffusing the whole area like I do, she’s using her mana to attract the metal and reshape it that way. It’s a far more efficient method than I’ve ever tried.

Would you like to watch from closer? she offers.

“If you don’t mind,” I accept eagerly.

Of course.

As I step forward and touch the metal near where Happy’s working, I muse wryly that the student has become the teacher.

As I watch Happy connect the pieces of metal together seamlessly, so that even through my magical senses I can’t identify where the separation was, I feel inspired to spend some time on Metal-Shaping. It’s been ages since I did anything proper with it. I definitely need to go on that trip to Tilathis’ mountain – I’m sure I can find a good excuse for why I shouldn’t delegate it.

Finally, the design is finished. Oddly, the final two pieces are steel sticks, each with a thin line of blackiron embedded in them. They reach a bit above my head, one at the edge of the circle, the other at the centre of the spiral. The one at the centre of the spiral has a box about the size of my shoebox on the top and it’s absolutely covered in runes. I recognise it as the mana condenser, but why is it so high above the ground?

“My lord, would you be so kind as to sink the design into the ground? The height of a door should be sufficient,” Lizil requests firmly, and my question is answered.

“Sure,” I agree, then press mana into the earth. It takes a minute or so for my mana to saturate the ground enough for me to manipulate it, and then burying the design without disturbing it is just a question of concentration.

“My lord Elarion,” Lizil calls next. “Would you kindly bring your mages and potions over – we will activate the formation once the field has been prepared.”

His expression faintly curious, Elarion organises his people, and they quickly prepare the ground. Since it’s a small area, it doesn’t take long to force out the unwanted mana and replace it with what is desired. The Plant Mages are a little hesitant when Elarion orders them to tip in all the potions – they’re clearly used to the potions being applied in stages – but they do it nonetheless. They also ensure that their own mana is present, even if only in small quantities.

Finally, we’re ready. Lizil stands near the small hand-sized controller that sticks up from the edge of the circle.

With electric anticipation running through all of us, she presses mana through her hand, and into the circle.

With an almost audible hum, it comes to life.
