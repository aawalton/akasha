Oskar, as always, felt the familiar urge to climb higher into the sky and go as fast as he could. He did that sometimes, but only on the darkest nights, and he always stayed within range of Erik’s PUB in case they needed him.

They had yet to test the full range of the PUBs, and Oskar wondered if there would be a difference between the global network and their usual local network, but that was a theory to be tested later, when he wasn’t in the sky hoping he wouldn’t have to fight the pirates below.

Curiosity Hydra is alive and well.

The wind, full of Sora, some of it living Sora thanks to the nearby Tree, swept by in a steady swirl of color. If he went higher, he could see the red sun over the dunes, but his focus was below.

Fox and a small team of capable Kobolds, including the light gray twins she’d fought beside in Bene’s compound, closed in on the Clinkers. From Oskar’s viewpoint, Fox’s daggers and the twins’ hand-axes were nowhere to be found, but he knew better than to think them unarmed. They didn’t sneak or hide, which was probably difficult for the naturally stealthy Kobolds, and they stopped to wait for the pirates to approach as soon as the pirates saw them.

Waving at the Kobolds in greeting, an equal number spoke among themselves and then made their way over to meet Fox and her group, small wooden and metal trophies gently clacking in the wind as they walked. They held their weapons out to their sides at arm’s length in what Oskar figured was a sign of peace. Slowing to put their weapons away, the pirates took the final few steps and smiled as they greeted the Kobolds.With all eyes on the Kobolds, Oskar stopped hovering and gently landed on the other side of the sunward dune, looking down at the gathered mix of people below.

He tried, but couldn’t make out what was being said. Oskar relaxed some, though, when Fox’s hands went from casually near her daggers to casually at her side. It was likely more a signal for his sake than genuine relaxation, especially since she could have a dagger in hand just as fast from her new position.

Come to think of it, that might not be just because she’s fast, she could just have a lot of daggers.

His PUB flashed a blue flickering light around the outside edge of his vision in lieu of a chuckle.

The familiar crisp outlines of his PUB’s battle mode slowly faded as, after a few scans, it became clear the Clinkers were no threat to Fox. All low green threats except a Croc that hung out with his head down, trying not to look directly at anyone like he was trying to hide. He was still a low threat, but a brighter green than the others. Squinting, Oskar tried to figure out why that Croc had so quickly caught his eye, other than how cagey he was acting.

Yooo, is that Kang? Our old buddy who tried to mug us outside the Gramm Collective?

// It appears to be. I’m guessing he’s better than most Crocs at recognizing Kobolds, because it sure seems he remembers Fox, doesn’t he? //

Oh yeah, he’s squirming. Hope his mugging days are behind him, or he’s not coming anywhere near the others.

After a few moments, Fox turned and made her way back toward the Tree. Oskar stayed back for a few minutes to make sure they didn’t try anything silly, but they simply talked excitedly among themselves.

Oskar made it back to the Oasis himself just as the red sun poked over the distant dunes and landed near Fox, ready to ask a dozen questions. Erik walked up, acting like he was gonna wet willy Oskar with his skeleton hand, and Oskar laughed, stepping around so that Fox was between them. She gave Erik a stern glare.

“Boys. Behave please.”

“But mom-“

“Tsst,” she hushed them. “We have actual information to discuss. Do you wish to hear what they said or not?”

Oskar put his hands up in surrender and grew more serious.

“Okay, okay, what info?”

Nearby, Touwon’s tinkering in his tent quieted, and Oskar watched his ears perk up when she spoke.

“Do you remember the Drakon that disappeared as soon as the fighting started at the Gramm Collective?”

“Yep. They’ve been nagging at the back of my mind since. We’ve had more pressing matters, and no time or info to do anything about it, anyway. So, what did they say?”

Fox sighed and answered. “The Drakon took off east, toward their home, as soon as it became clear the Collective was imploding.”

Both looked at Erik when he spoke up.

“They rarely did anything other than eat and whisper to Gramm. When Gramm started getting all aggressive when… when Valla snuck back in with her tail tucked, they stopped even doing that. I think they knew they’d lost what little influence they’d gained and dipped out.”

“Great,” Oskar added. “More missing enemies. With Benedicto at least, we can be reasonably sure he won’t come after us.”

Nodding, Fox said, “Yes, though capable, he knows this place is death for him. He will move on and make fools of someone else.”

Fox all but spat out that last bit, and Oskar felt bad for even mentioning the name, but they all knew they couldn’t avoid talking about it, especially when they were running through threat assessments.

Speaking of…

“You saw Kang the Clinker, right?”

Fox brightened up a little at that.

“Yes, the bone-dry fool. He is ashamed, and he should be. Not a threat, but like all those hovering for a place near the Tree, he will bear watching.”

Erik was deep in thought staring at his bone hand again, but Oskar had more questions.

“How did the rest of the talk go?”

“As planned, I told them they could set up camp after midday, a hundred meters down the valley for now, and should stay in sight when they are not hunting. They pushed to camp closer, of course, but I told them that must be earned with trust, and when I added that we would fill one waterskin per person a day, they eagerly accepted.”

“Perfect. We’ll start letting some of the other groups closer once we build up trust with the Clinkers, but for now, everyone seems to know this is a tense situation and is behaving. I expect the others will send someone to talk once they see we’ve let the pirates camp nearby.”

Counting them off with his fingers, Oskar said. “As far as threats, we’ve got missing Drakon, missing Bene and his followers, and the growing number of people coming from God knows where because of the Tree. And the typical stuff, wildlife and heat.”

Beside him, Erik blinked and looked up to add, “And not so much threats, but still problems are, not knowing what’s going on with the global network and Lavern, Oskar and his Seed Forge, and me trying to figure out this stupid arm.”

Mentally, Oskar added the Insight Gem he’d gotten from Valla.

Fox looked down at the arm for a moment and shook her head.

“You will figure it out soon, and we will be the safer for it.”

“Thanks, mom.”

“I will stab your face.”

“I already have plenty of scars. No thanks.”

Fox and Oskar laughed, and Oskar glimpsed Touwon in his tent facing the other way, but shaking his head at them, obviously still listening.

Gotta love dark humor.

Around midday, the Clinkers moved their camp to a bend in the dunes that gave them a view in both directions and then sent people to watch over the top of the dunes as well. Sitting against the Tree, Fox and Oskar watched them busying themselves getting settled. They sent a Hyenaman and two female Crocs over with empty waterskins, and the Kobold cubs took it upon themselves to help by running the filled waterskins back over to the Sand Pirates a few at a time.

Of course, the cub’s real goal was to see the Clinkers and their collection of trophies that clacked with each step they took. One cub, the same one that had gotten hurt the other day doing the stupid Froyo jump thing, stopped to talk to the Clinkers every time he brought a waterskin over, and had to be dragged away by his friends to go get the rest of the waterskins that were now all sitting, full, at the top of the sand hill that led down into the Oasis proper. For their part, the Clinkers were good-natured about the curious cub and laughed at something he said when they finally pulled him away.

Oskar chuckled and leaned in toward Fox to talk.

“The cubs will probably be wearing trophies this time tomorrow.”

Fox smiled, nodding slowly.

“I think you are right.”

“Are the Clinkers going to be alright camped out there? I feel bad, but we can’t take any chances. This is the test group.”

“Many of their classes are scavenging or gathering related,” Fox said. “But they are survivors; that is a truth. If anything happens, they can take care of themselves.”

“And now they have us as backup. I really hope they behave; we’ve got enough going on.”

Fox stretched her arms overhead, groaning. “Do not worry, just observe. Worry is not a tool; it is a weight.”

“Fair enough. I’m gonna go train and try to get used to the way this foot feels.”

With a grunt, Oskar pulled to standing with the help of his spear, and after staring at the grass growing around the tree for a moment, walked down the decline into the glass cavern.

As his vision quickly adjusted thanks to the PUB, the explosion of life just in the past day became evident. Life was everywhere. He could feel it in every part of him, and it called out to him like… like home.

“Holy crap.”

// Just beautiful… your poetic words, not the glowing vines and magical moss growing up and around the walls. It pales in comparison to your reaction. //

Smirking, Oskar leaned down and ran his hands through the thick grass somehow growing atop the glass along the floor of the long cave. The entire cavern was crafted from a colossal Sandwyrm, which had died from extreme, rapid heat, resulting in its natural sandy layers of armor melting into glass. The cavern was larger than the cave portion and was a ten-minute walk to the entrance of what had been the Gryphus Vulture’s lair.

I just hope whatever killed the Sandwyrm is dead, too.

He walked over to the Oasis and gave Bastet a smile before leaning his spear against the outside wall to scoop up a cold drink of Wayspring water, sending chills down his arms. He dried his hands on his raggedy shirt and grabbed his spear, making his way out of the cavern and into the slowly climbing cave. Shortly, he found the self-appointed guardian of the cave, Sara, stretched out on her back, looking at him upside down. She purred as she saw him, guilting Oskar into stopping to pet her.

“Hey lady. Anyone creeping through trying to sneak up on us?”

She batted at his hand as if to show what would happen to anyone who tried. They hadn’t worried about the cave much, especially during the day, considering how hot, stuffy, and dark the cave was when the suns were up. No point in being dumb, though—they still made it a point to have someone, or some-cat, down here at all times to keep an eye out.

They didn’t quite have the numbers yet to put people at the entrance of the Gryphus Lair, and no way to communicate with them if they did, so keeping watch in the cooler part of the cave was the best they could do for now.

For now, it’s still too hot to worry about the Crocs or Clinkers trying to sneak in.

The problem was that the cooler part of the cave was quickly expanding, and the small, cool breeze from the Great Tree above ground was a cool wind down here.

Sara stood when he did and followed him up the cave as he explored the new plants and growth.

“The Tree must have been setting up a foundation or something before, because this is crazy,” he told Sara.

// 74% increase in expansive growth from yesterday. If this rate remains mostly stable, the cave will be a full-on greenhouse in three days. //

That means we need to have more people down here keeping an eye on things. And do you think it’s stable? Why would it be?

// I think you were right about foundational growth. Rather than increasing in thickness and expanding slowly, it’s spreading now. I left some room for error, though. Meh, we’ll reassess tonight when you come back down here. //

Maybe I should sleep down here?

// You spend half the time in the air at night now. No point. //

I’m a peacock. You gotta let me fly!

A minute later, the glow from moss and growth finally dimmed, and he could feel the temperature would be unbearable if he went much further, but the area was flat, isolated, and perfect for his purpose. Sara made her way to the thickest grass and settled in as Oskar began working through his spear forms.

I haven’t been in one place this long since earth, and it’s got me itching. Worse, I can’t shake the feeling that fun time is almost over. But like Fox said…observe. Worry is not a tool, it’s a weight.
