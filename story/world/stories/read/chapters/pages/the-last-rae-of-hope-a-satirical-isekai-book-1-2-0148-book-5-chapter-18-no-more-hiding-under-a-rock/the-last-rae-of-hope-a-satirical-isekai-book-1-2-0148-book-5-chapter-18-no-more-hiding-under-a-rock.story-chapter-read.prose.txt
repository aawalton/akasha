Tetora didn’t wait for us to gather ourselves. With his arms up and staff resting across his taut shoulders, he turned and started walking.

“Well?” he barked without looking back. “Are you coming or what?”

We followed, quietly at first. It was clear he didn’t want my explanation here and now. Olethros lagged on purpose, giving Tetora the most space.

“You need to eat more, Rachel,” Tetora said flatly over his shoulder. “You’re scrawny and weak again. How’re you supposed to stay on your feet if you don’t have a proper core?”

“Well—”

“And stop stressing Nora out,” he added, his ear flicking. “She’s too young to look that frazzled!”

Nora muttered something, her hand going to her white lock of hair.

I tried to change the subject. “So… How long did you know we were here?”

“Most of the day,” Tetora replied with a snort. “You’d think for a royal, he could afford better cologne!”

I glanced back, noting Olethros looked faintly offended.

“Citrus!” Tetora went on. “And sage. It’s too cloying. I can’t smell anything else.”

That was probably the whole point of it, since demons didn’t have an innate smell.

“I don’t mind it so much…” I admitted.

Tetora stopped walking. “What was that?”

“I said I like it.”

“What do you even see in him?” Tetora scoffed, storming forward again.

“He makes me laugh,” I answered carefully. “On the surface, he’s aloof and arrogant and even a bit tricksy… but underneath, he’s just as ridiculous as everyone else. He pretends he doesn’t really care for most things, but he does.”

Tetora froze. With a full-body shudder, he replied, “Forget I ever asked.”

***

Food waited for us outside beneath a crude temple awning: roasted potatoes split cleanly down the middle and salted fish wrapped in broad, green leaves. There was also rice mixed with bamboo shoots, and I couldn’t help but notice that the stoneware was of high quality, all things considered.

“Sit and eat,” Tetora said as he flopped down.

Though he sounded casual, I could sense the tension under it. The temple itself was quiet and shadowed, half-hidden beneath a swath of vines, but there was no mistaking that it wasn’t empty. Something was here that he wanted to protect.

Nora picked at her rice, eating carefully around the bamboo shoots.

“They’re the best part,” I remarked. “Everything else is just simple carbs.”

“Hmm…” she said, pulling out a piece much smaller than the others to nibble on.

Tetora ate swiftly, scooping rice with his chopsticks. “Well. Why aren’t you bothering to hide the fact that you’re a demon, demon?” He shot Olethros a look. “Aren’t your siblings still trying to kill you?”

As Olethros shrugged, I paused mid-bite and shared an uneasy glance with Nora.

“Have you been hiding under a rock this whole time?” she exclaimed on our behalf, setting down her bowl.

Tetora returned the shrug, failing to hide a flinch. “I left Chairo a while back.”

“What’s the last thing you heard, then?” I asked.

He rubbed his cheek, brushing away a few errant grains of rice. “The Orders were preparing to rescue you from the demons in the Land of Dragons,” he said slowly. “Sounded like they knew what they were doing, so…”

“So you left?” I asked. “What about Aleph?”

At the mention of his name, Tetora’s eyes clouded. “Wouldn’t do the future councilman any good to associate with Tigers. Besides.” He sniffed. “I had more important things to do.”

“Did you two have a fight?”

“Of course not!” He puffed his cheeks and looked away. “I would have won, if we did.” Then his gaze dropped to his bowl. “Where’s Relias, anyway?”

Talk about being out of touch.

While I hesitated, Nora took point once more. “He’s floating in the sky on that other giant rock you’re hiding under!”

Olethros finally spoke up. “You did notice it, right?”

Tetora scowled openly. “It’s none of my concern. As I said, I’ve been busy.”

“Yeah, busy sulking!” a clear voice rang out.

I dropped my chopsticks.

“Kiko!”

“We’re supposed to be quiet and listen,” a young boy admonished from somewhere.

“Oh, right. Then you shush, too, Talib,” she whispered back as loudly as possible.

“Kiiiko!” Talib, the former child archivist of Chairo, exclaimed from above. “Why’d you have to get me in trouble, too!”

Kiko the Ninja hopped down from the awning, landing lightly with a grin already in place. “He can only punish so much. Better to split it across the guilty rather than sacrifice myself!”

Talib jumped down, making a bit more noise as he landed. “Sorry, Grandmaster Tetora…”

Tetora sighed. “Have a seat then, you two—Kiko?!”

She had marched her way to Olethros, her monkey tail twitching with mischief. “You’re the demon king?”

“Yes.”

Kiko folded her arms and tilted her head. “Where’s all the fire and the shrieking?”

“I took the day off.”

She squinted. “Can you really hear my thoughts?”

“Yes.”

“Then what am I thinking right now?”

“You are unimpressed,” he said calmly. “But that is because you have only given me a cursory inspection. As I speak further, you realize that your bravery is in constant flux with growing fear, knowing that should I wish to harm you for your insolence, the only thing preventing that is the societal convention I ascribe to with the others at this table.”

Kiko’s grin wavered.

“Do not be concerned. Your actions have elevated you in your companions’ eyes. The boy over there would walk through fire if you asked. You should exercise restraint; his survival instincts, like yours, are insufficient.”

Kiko stared at Talib for a moment, calculating. “So in the end… It was worth it!”

“Enough!” Tetora shouted as he stood up, slamming his hands on the table. “I demand to know what’s going on—and you kids aren’t helping!”

“He’s not going to hurt them, I assure you,” I said. “But are you sure they should hear this too?”

“You want me to do something,” Tetora said bluntly. “I’m not an idiot. If you’re going to ask me to walk away from here, it better be important!” He glanced uneasily up at the darkening sky. “I don’t… want to keep abandoning people.”

And so, we told them. We made no mention of CUP, or of the mechanical nature of NAUGHT. We also didn’t mince words over Relias’s betrayal, or what had made Epiales… Epiales. Nora took over when my voice cracked or wavered, but Olethros remained silent the entire time, likely aware that any testimony of his own would sound suspicious.

Tetora was not one to hide his emotions, and watching him cycle between outright disbelief and boiling rage was both painful and vindicating.

“I can… show you the scars, if you wish to—”

“No.” He turned, staring straight at Olethros. “Is what they said true?”

“I believe it is far worse than we perceive, but their summarization is accurate.”

The two children stared openly, holding their breath, fearful of making a single sound.

“Even their description of an angel?” Tetora pressed.

Olethros nodded.

“It’s too dangerous for the children. And I can’t leave them alone here.”

“Do you believe they will be safe?” Olethros countered. “Do you think he will spare them simply because they are children? You are ignoring the fact that once he finishes hunting demons, he will turn his attention to the tigers’ legacy next. Waiting here for your sister will be fruitless at best.”

Tetora shook himself as if he had been struck. “What do you expect me to do then, Olethros?”

He shrugged. “Bring the children. Leave a note. Fight back.”

“I can’t just—”

Talib started to cry.

“I’m sorry, Talib,” I began, assuming he was mourning yet another lost home. “But it seems this place is no longer safe—”

“I don’t want to be Talib anymore!” he howled. “That bad man gave me that name! I want a different name!”

“You can give him a new name, right?” Kiko asked as she hugged him tightly. “Something brave and strong!”

“Of course,” I agreed. “Brave and strong…”

“And smart,” he added, trying to regain his composure. “Brave and strong and smart.”

Kiko nodded her head. “And rich.”

“Um…”

“Edric,” Olethros said slowly, “is not a name to be taken carelessly. Do you think you can carry it?”

The boy looked up. “Edric?”

“Edric…” Kiko folded her arms as she considered it.

“I once considered the name Edric,” Olethros continued smoothly. “It means prosperous king. To bear it, I would need to embody every quality you named. But if you want it instead…” His eyes flashed. “You’re going to have to convince that tiger to join us.”

Both children immediately rounded on Tetora.

“Stop sulking in this sweaty jungle and go fight the villain!” Kiko screeched.

Talib stomped his foot and leveled a pointed finger at Teora. “Edric, your future king, commands you!”

Tetora refused to look at any of us. Then he exhaled, long and slow.

“Go tell the others to get their things,” he said. “We’re leaving.”
