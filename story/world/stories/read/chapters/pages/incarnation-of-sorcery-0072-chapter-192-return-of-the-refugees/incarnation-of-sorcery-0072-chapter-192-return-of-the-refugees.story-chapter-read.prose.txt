Xyke was right. Ryland should have probably paid more attention to her when she had mentioned that they would be targeted. Of course, Ryland had anticipated it, which was why his fortification spells were active already.

But it seemed things were a degree or two worse than he had assumed, according to the Oracle.

“A danger worse than any Calamity that has come before will befall us all,” she said. “If we fail to act.”

Ryland raised an eyebrow.

The Oracle and her blindfolded gaze didn’t look away from him.

“Uh.” Mage Knight Ophelia was trying to draw their attention back to the other matter on hand. “What do we do with him?”

They all turned to survey the man with the innumerable body parts floating around them. In much the way Ryland had dealt with the gangsters in the Arcoryx undercity and how he had treated Devas, the infiltrator was now all around thanks to the rifts separating different sections of his body.

“Must we do anything with him?” Ryland asked. “He seems dealt with for the time being.”

“But will reinforcements arrive and allow him to be a pain once again?” Ophelia insisted.

That was a fair consideration. No doubt, the next infiltrator’s additional objective would be to find whatever had happened to their predecessor and act accordingly. That would likely include freeing the other insurgent, if that was possible, and then Ophelia’s prediction would turn out to be true.

“I will simply delete him then,” Ryland said.

“Why not do it now, then?”

“Now, we have an opportunity to extract some information. Perhaps find out about potential future incursions by his associates.”

Ophelia momentarily looked like she wanted to argue that it would be more prudent to kill the infiltrator now, but she stopped herself. Perhaps she recognized that arguing with someone like Ryland, not the undefeatable mage but the veteran of the Realmbreaker Wars, wasn’t the brightest idea.

The captive’s eyes briefly widened at the casualness with which they were discussing matters. Maybe he feared being interrogated more than just being outright killed.

Whatever the case, Ryland decided to leave the interrogation to the Oracle. Of course, her presence meant some of the interrogation wasn’t necessary to begin with. Her readings of the threads of Fate already predicted future infiltrations in ways Ryland couldn’t simply prepare for with overwhelming magic, even if she couldn’t determine the exact specifics.

“Now,” Ryland said. He wanted to set some things in order to deal with the Oracle here and now instead of leaving her as an unknown quantity in what was to come. “Have you seen my intent, Your Eminence?”

She was silent for a moment, considering both their infiltrator who could hear everything, and then Ryland himself. “I’m aware that all this is an ironic tragedy.”

He blinked at the answer. Admittedly, he wasn’t expecting that sort of perspective. “A tragedy, is it?”

“Indeed. No one knows that you do this out of the greatest emotion that influences us all—love.”

Ophelia gawked. “Love? Who’s he in love with?”

The Mage Knight’s tone made Ryland raise his eyebrows at her instead of the Oracle. “Are you suggesting I’m incapable of displaying affection?”

“I’m not suggesting it. I’m positive that’s true.”

Ryland took a deep breath to extensively refute that statement, but he was prevented from doing so when the Oracle decided to interrupt.

“Not all love is romantic,” she said.

Ophelia fell silent at that, and Ryland also found his defence of his romantic capabilities dying in his throat. Obviously, the Oracle had meant Acrypha. And it was true. Ryland cared for his pupils, enough that he would go to great lengths to ensure their safety.

Including, but not limited to, using a broken Realm as the site for a ritual to breach the Overrealm.

And of course, the Oracle had to appear with a warning about breaching the Plane of the Great Gods leading to unintended disastrous consequences.

“The missing students,” Ophelia said. Her eyes darted this way and that, as though she’d find the children hidden somewhere nearby. The incredulity from before had turned grim. “The rumours. They really are gone, aren’t they? And you’re trying to get them back.”

“Indeed,” Ryland said. “This is all for Acrypha.”

Ophelia froze. “But not Viren?”

“No! I’m here already.”

To prove it, Viren himself appeared. Ryland had informed him and Devas that there was a slight disturbance before rifting away to take care of it. Now, Viren had finally arrived, panting a little as though he had run all this way. Devas was not far behind.

“Who is he?” Ophelia asked after exchanging warm greetings with Viren. The two of them had met each other a few times in Arcoryx, and their mutual respect had grown a great deal, despite the great difference in ages and stations. Ophelia squinted. “Wait, is that a Turnalian?”

Devas bowed. “I am Devas, Eleventh Disciple of the Great Marionys Ryland, the Realmbreaker, the Half-step Celestial, and—”

Ryland cleared his throat. Devas shut up immediately.

“Eleventh?” Viren asked. “What in all the Realms happened to the other ten?”

“The other sixteen! I have counted nineteen of us who have taken tutelage from the Great Master.”

Viren began counting off his fingers. “Nineteen… sixteen…” He blinked up. “Wait, are you counting me and Acrypha among the disciples?”

“Of course! How could I not, especially when Master goes to such lengths to free you from the grasp of those bastards, those villainous Great Gods who—”

Ryland cleared his throat yet again. Most people looked uncomfortably between the Oracle and Devas, mostly because Devas had enough power to say whatever he wished without fear of reprisal. He was Empyrean-ranked as well, just as the Oracle was.

But it seemed the Oracle didn’t care to defend the Great Gods. Not in present company.

“Please confirm one thing, Marionys Ryland,” the Oracle said. “If achieving success in your endeavour means that you must destroy the world, would you do it?”

Ryland considered it, but for a moment only. He waved his hand around to showcase the current state of Rhel. “Have I not done so already?”

There was a certain truth to his statement that none of them could deny. Every single person present looked uncomfortably at the remains of a world that had once been a refuge for those who had fled the ravages of the Realmbreaker Wars. Now, it looked as though all the devastation of that very conflict had been condensed into the single world of Rhel.

No escape. The Realmbreaker Wars let no one leave its clutches. Even if it took centuries and generations to reach them.

But the real point wasn’t even just the fact that Ryland was intent on doing what was necessary. More importantly, he was not about to be stopped even by the Oracle herself. He had no intention of causing any unnecessary problems, but he also was not about to heed anyone else’s judgment over his own until he agreed with their view.

“Then it seems I must remain here,” the Oracle said. “If only to stem the tide of what is to come.”

“Should I get rid of her, Master?” Devas asked bluntly.

Ryland sighed. As if the tension wasn’t thick enough in the air already. “Devas, you will not harm a single hair on anyone present.”

“Understood, master.”

Too easy. Ryland deliberated for a moment. “You will also not insult or denigrate or manipulate anyone present. Nor will you cause them any mental stress in any way. And you certainly will not allow them to come to harm.”

“…drats.”

Viren stared, then had to stifle laughter. Even the Oracle had arched her eyebrows.

Ryland turned back to the Oracle. “I’m glad you’ve chosen to remain here, Oracle Vertha.” He flicked a hand and the disembodied parts of the infiltrator floated over to the Oracle, who valiantly suppressed any flinches she might have felt. “I’ve heard the Churches have significantly improved their… interrogation techniques. Please, have at it.”

“What are you going to do, Ryland?” Ophelia asked. “In fact, what am I doing here, Your Eminence?”

“Guarding,” said the Oracle. “Against whatever may come.”

That somehow satisfied the Mage Knight, which Ryland found a little odd since the directive was so vague. He had received the impression that Ophelia was a lot more practical. But then again, the Oracle commanded the kind of reverence Ryland had rarely seen elsewhere.

With all that settled, they parted for now. Ryland was done gathering the ingredients needed for the Inscription. It was now merely taking time to charge up the required amount of mana, plus a few adjustments and fixes that Ryland was taking care of with the help of Xyke. She had taken such a great interest that she hadn’t bothered accompanying Ryland to the disturbances.

There was one last important addition left for the Inscription to perform its job, but that wouldn’t take too long. It just needed to be added at the exact right moment.

They didn’t learn much from their prisoner. The man refused to give up anything. All they got was that he was from the Flourinus Order—big surprise, that—and he wasn’t the last of them.

“Don’t worry,” the Oracle said. “Allow me some more time and I will make him spill everything.”

Ryland left her to it.

The Oracle’s warnings turned prophetic in only a day. For that was when the new issue arrived. An issue that what little they had gleaned from their captive wouldn’t help with.

A group of Rhellians had returned to their homeland. They were refugees who had left for Jure, and a few Juren mages had accompanied them back to Rhel, for the refugees sought to reclaim the possessions they had left behind in their haste to escape the dangerous state of their home.

Unfortunately, it turned out their possessions and belongings were long gone. When Ryland accompanied the Juren and the Rhellians, he found only devastation awaiting them. Their homes had been located at the foot of a mountain which was part of the range that was above the Crypts.

Ryland’s battle against Mavar had simply destroyed the mountain. And everything else around it too.

“Gone,” a dragonkin Rhellian said. He stumbled forward, before falling to his knees in despair. “I was trying not to believe it but this… it’s all gone.”

The hurt in his voice made Ryland’s heart feel like it was clenching in his chest. Although, he wouldn’t lie to himself that perhaps his sympathy wasn’t as strong as it could be. Ryland had never been one to feel strongly inclined towards material possessions. As an itinerant mage, he had never properly understood the idea of being tied to stationary things.

But that didn’t mean he didn’t understand the Rhellians’ sadness. They had lost something precious. How would Ryland feel if he failed to retrieve Acrypha safe and sound?

“I’m sorry,” Ryland said. He wasn’t apologizing for the battle. That had been unavoidable. But he was sorry for the experiences the Rhellian was going through. “If there’s any way I can help, please let me know.”

“How could you help now?” the Juren mage asked. The pale man’s expression was contemptuous. “After you’ve already cost them everything?”

“I have a great deal of possessions I can spare,” Ryland said.

“Alms? You’re offering them alms?”

There was something there that Ryland couldn’t place. “I’m offering them recompense in a way that should work. By selling what I can part with, the refugees can obtain funds to purchase what they need on Jure, to establish a new home for them and their families. What’s wrong with that?”

“Don’t you see you’re insulting them? You think your offering is enough to replace the years of effort and time they’ve put in?”

It wasn’t. Ryland knew it. But he was granting what he could, which was certainly much better than nothing at all. But the sense of wrongness came at him again. There was something there. The Juren mage’s intent wasn’t as benevolently aggrieved as he made it appear.

Ryland addressed the Rhellians themselves. The Juren mage didn’t matter. Or shouldn’t, ideally. In truth, he likely did, but Ryland would deal with him in due time.

“Again,” Ryland said. “I apologize for all this. But I cannot turn back time, only provide what help I can. I understand you’re devastated, but please tell me what you think you need.”

While the first dragonkin still seemed too sunk in despair to even hear what Ryland had said, the others did. They looked just as devastated, but they had martialled their despondence and turned to Ryland with resolve in their eyes.

“We want our home back,” a younger dragonkin said. “We want Rhel.”

“You… want the entire Realm?” Ryland asked.

“This is our home. Our birthplace. The only Realm we’ve ever known. Return it to us. Please.”

Ah. There it was. The catch. Exactly what the Juren mage was hoping for, going by his reaction. Or lack thereof, rather.

Before this meeting had started, the Oracle had given Ryland a solid warning. The refugees—and more importantly, the ones accompanying them—were duplicitous to some extent. Ryland had agreed that they likely had hidden intentions, though he had decided to reserve judgment until he had seen it for himself.

And now he had. More or less.

“I’m afraid I can’t simply give up Rhel just yet,” Ryland said. “But once I’m done, I will be sure to return it to you. In fact, just as I created Rhel, I shall recreate it to a habitable state when the time comes. But until then, I ask that you be patient.”

The moment he finished speaking, Ryland sensed something off in the distance. His Glyph of Detection was picking up more magical signatures.

He was distracted from whatever it was by the refugees.

“Patient?” the first dragonkin said. He had risen back to his feet, looking at Ryland now that he had wrestled some level of control over his anguish. “How long do you expect us to be patient? What guarantee do we have that you won’t leave our home in a worse state than it already is?”

“I won’t need tremendously long to deal with my current predicament. Perhaps a week. A month at most. And we can seal the issue with a magical Inscription where I can swear that once my business is done, I will ensure that Rhel returns to its original condition, if not even better than before.”

“Not good enough,” the Juren mage said. The Rhellians had started looking just a mite resigned to agree with Ryland after his insistence, but the mage from Jure dashed those hopes. “Are you intent on standing against the wishes of those who have a rightful claim over these lands?”

Now that the man was speaking, the Rhellian refugees regained their earlier resolve. It seemed they were determined to go along with his wishes.

Ryland didn’t detect any spells or anything of the sort affecting. They had just been plain old manipulated by the Juren mage. Which probably hadn’t been that difficult. Living as a refugee was tremendously hard, and no doubt, the path offered by the Juren mage was attractive.

“I am intent on completing my business, yes,” Ryland said. His voice hardened. “I will not brook any interference. From anyone.”

That left nothing to doubt. Ryland had made it very clear that he wasn’t going to accede to any wish that went against his intentions. No matter who they came from. The Rhellian refugees were clearly spooked and were surreptitiously looking at the Juren mage for guidance.

Right. They needed to be separated. This whole schtick was that man’s fault.

“Then it seems Jure itself will need to act,” the mage said.

Ryland frowned. “What do you mean?”

“We’ve been suspecting this for a while, and your choices and actions have turned those suspicions into near certainty. Vyrd has laid claim to Rhel. Jure opposes such an unsanctioned takeover of a foreign Realm, and therefore, Jure will act, while ensuring that all other Realms are aware of Vyrd’s incursion into Rhel.”

“That’s…” Honestly, Ryland was stupefied enough that he didn’t have the best response ready to go. “Quite the reach.”

“In fact,” the Juren mage went on. “I have already sent a signal to other mages from Jure regarding this incident. They are coming to investigate the issue even as we speak, if they haven’t already arrived, that is.”

Lo and behold, the other Juren mages arrived. Ryland sensed their approach before they were present. He could tell there were several more entities capable of magic present on Rhel now.

At least they didn’t arrive alone. Devas and Ophelia were accompanying them like guards taking suspects to their interrogation chamber.

“Alright then,” Ryland said as all the Juren mages gathered together to stand against him. It was time he acted. “I am willing to talk, negotiate, and resolve whatever misunderstandings we have.”

“Good,” the first Juren mage said, a satisfied smirk crossing his face. “Now—”

“But only with the Rhellians who have a true claim to this land.”

All the Juren mages stiffened. The first one frowned. “What—”

“[Void Seal].”

Ryland’s spell snapped closed around everyone from Jure save for the Rhellians. He turned to their wide-eyed expressions with his own face as placid as he could keep it, despite how the Juren mage had acted.

“Now, then,” he said. “Let’s discuss, shall we?”
