We made good pace that day. Arjun had told us about the monsters they’d encountered in the woodland-hills biome, so we felt more confident walking straight to Kuriolis instead of circling around.

From what I understood, Arjun’s group had gone out of their way to fight pretty much everything they saw, which meant most of those monsters probably wouldn't bother us if we just gave them some space.

With that in mind, it seemed possible we could reach Kuriolis before dark.

“Should we stop to rest here?” I asked, when we reached a nice grassy clearing.

“No chance those guys are catching up, right?” Juliette asked.

“No way!” I told her. “They’re probably just waking up now—heads pounding, completely confused. It'll take them another hour before they can even walk in a straight line. They won’t make any real progress today, and even tomorrow they’ll probably just get lost again. We could get to Kuriolis a full day before them. Maybe even two.”

“Great,” said Samar. “Dubadilu is gonna want a long grass meal.”

We laid the sleeping Big Bird down gently and pulled out some food of our own. I wasn’t exactly sure how smoke roses affected her species—I hoped it wasn’t dangerous.

Luckily, not long after, our sleeping beauty began to stir.

“Cum… Cumkettle…” she mumbled, “Cumkettle-snortpiss!”

“And good morning to you too,” Juliette smirked.

Big Bird opened her eyes and noticed she wasn’t tied up anymore. She gave us a suspicious glare, stood up, walked a few steps away, and then stopped.

“We’re not with the group that attacked you,” I explained. “You’re not a prisoner anymore. You’re free to go.”

Of course, I didn’t really want her to just walk away, but it was important to make it clear we weren’t holding her captive.

She looked at us, then at the trees, then back at us.

Then she spoke in her high-pitched, squeaky voice full of curses.

“You were friends with them, Pisssock-dinglebucket. Eating. Talking. FRIENDS.”

“Not really,” I said. “We were pretending. We wanted to rescue you.”

“Yeah,” added Samar, “they were… how you say… Garbage-ladlewank or something.”

“Chuuu,” she squeaked. “You are a Naked-Nula. Am I to believe you would help a pretty princess like me?”

…Uh?

“We’re humans,” Samar clarified. “You’re the cursing Big Bird. Not a princess.”

She squealed, offended. “Insolent Naked-Nula fool!”

I turned to the bird. “You call us Naked-Nula. Do we… look like animals from your planet called that?”

“Naked-Nula are from stories,” she explained. “You look very alike, but you’re shorter, have more fingers, and you have this ugly thing—” she poked my nose “—in the middle of your face. Chuuu! What does this even do? Is it a small horn?”

“That’s a nose!” I said, offended. “For smelling!”

“Whatever,” she huffed. “And Naked-Nula are known for kidnapping and… depraved acts on pretty princesses like me! Since you rescued me, you want a reward? I’m not doing anything sexy for you! Not with my tail! No way! No belly rubs. Maybe… belly rub. But that’s it!”

We all stared at her, unsure what to say.

“Why is everyone so damn weird around here?” Samar whispered.

“Can we put her back?” Juliette deadpanned.

“Do you have a name?” I asked.

She puffed up proudly. “Lady Scheelha Pam Fizzlesteck, Duchess of Dingleberry Downs, the Third!”

“Okay… Can I call you Pam?”

“Call me Duchess!”

“Okay, Duchess,” I began, “we are, as you already know, Geshem, Juliette, and Samar. We come from another planet, Earth. We just appeared here one day, probably like you, and we’re just trying to survive in this weird world. We’re not really Naked-Nula, and we’re not expecting any reward. I’d just be happy to hear about your world and how you got here.”

She looked thoughtful. “As long as you don’t expect sexy things.” She snorted. “Well, I can tell you.”

And she did. She was very talkative when given the chance.

For half an hour she ranted—about her home planet, endless squabbles between pompous noble houses, betrayals, petty wars, and people she hated (which was basically everyone). Honestly, it was exhausting to listen to.

Still, I learned a few things: her world was weirdly similar to Earth, like 17th-century Europe mixed with early 20th-century technology. No digital age, no nukes, but not far off. They were constantly at war—apparently even more than us humans. It didn’t sound like a very pleasant civilization.

She also claimed she was five years old, which was supposed to be the late-teen equivalent to us. But I wasn’t sure if the system translated years correctly—after all, who knew how long it took her planet to circle its star? Either way, she didn’t know much about history or geography, so I took some of what she said with a grain of salt.

The second interesting part was how she ended up here.Her people had arrived months before us, on a different continent up north. She was part of a group of about fifty birdfolk that had settled in a city, living peacefully, getting jobs… and not leveling up.

Then they were attacked by a different faction of bird people—ones who had leveled up. There weren’t many of them, but they were strong, and they were apparently continuing some old-world race-based war.

Her group had fled the city by boat, but hers became separated from the rest and came ashore not far north of here. She was traveling through the forest with two other survivors when Arjun’s group found them. One was killed, the other escaped, and she was taken captive.

The last interesting bit was when I described the local monsters. I wanted to know which ones she recognized.

She knew the care-bear armadillo and called them Veegath. Apparently they were common in their myths.

The furry horn snake, the suddenly-snake, and the gorilla birds were also part of her world’s lore.

But she said she'd never heard of scorpion suricatas or zebra antlions—or a few others I listed. That was intriguing. It suggested there are at least three species of player-originated monsters. I was getting more curious by the minute.

“I don’t get it,” Juliette said. “Fighting people just because they’re from an enemy country? That’s so stupid.”

“But some are Vruumstickish-blood,” Duchess calmly explained. “They look like us, but their fur is a bit greenish, and they don’t have souls.”

Yeah, this big bird was also very racist.

“Dubadilu’s finished his lunch,” announced Samar. “We need to move fast.”

Once Dubadilu finished a large meal, we had a short time window before he passed out like a snoring rock. And then we'd be stuck with a snoring rock.

“Look, it was nice meeting you,” Juliette said to Duchess. “We’re heading to a large city called Kuriolis. We’ll probably stay there for a few weeks. Take care. Bye.”

Duchess just stood there, staring at us, clearly contemplating something.

“Bye,” she said finally.

We started walking. After a few minutes, I glanced back. Duchess was following us.

I wasn’t even remotely surprised.

We walked for hours, made great pace, and nothing slowed us down.

“Pissfunnel-hobknocker! Cruststool-shitwhapper!” Duchess suddenly shouted.

I turned. “What?”

“It’s just…” She flapped her wings dramatically, “it’s HOT!”

We kept walking a while longer and then—finally—we saw it.

A mighty city. Easily ten times larger than Kamatouu. With beautiful white towers rising high.

The tallest was the keep—a regal, elegant structure that looked like it belonged in a fairytale. Wide, arching windows, gleaming spires, and flags waving high. Probably breathtaking inside, too. It annoyed me just thinking about those idiots using it as a hideout.

We left Dubadilu in a patch of delicious-looking mossy growth, zipped Kim into a bag, and headed for the city gate.

We passed two armored guards who didn’t even glance at us.

As I expected, Duchess didn’t cause any reaction either. The guards saw her as just another player.

We wandered into the city, amazed by the streets, stores, and buildings. It was dazzling—far more alive than Kamatouu.

Soon I spotted a district with more magical-themed shops. It looked older, dustier, with cracked walls and crooked rooftops. But exactly what we were looking for.

We went through a cool-looking alley. And then—

“Hello, friend!”

Someone jumped out from around a corner and scared the crap out of me.

“I’m so happy to see more humans!”

Oh no. That tone. That friendliness. Not again.

“Hi… friend,” I said weakly. “Did you, uh, see us coming into the city?”

“Of course! I’m part of a large group of players. We have regular patrols. We see everyone who enters.”

That made sense. Damn it—we were stupid again. Now Farhad's group knew we were here, and in a day or two, when Arjun’s men returned, things could get messy.

“And I see you have a tamed monster!” he said, pointing at Duchess. “That’s so exciting!”

“Oh boy,” Juliette muttered.

“But you look so tired from your journey! Please, you must come to the keep—to rest and eat. We have much to talk about.”

“Hmmmmpf,” squeaked Duchess.

Well… they did have a wizard and a scientist. And they didn’t hate us—yet. We could still learn a lot. All we need to do is bolt tomorrow, around afternoon, and we’ll be fine.

I gave Juliette a pleading look. She rolled her eyes and made a “fine” face.

“Sure!” I said to the player. “After you.”
