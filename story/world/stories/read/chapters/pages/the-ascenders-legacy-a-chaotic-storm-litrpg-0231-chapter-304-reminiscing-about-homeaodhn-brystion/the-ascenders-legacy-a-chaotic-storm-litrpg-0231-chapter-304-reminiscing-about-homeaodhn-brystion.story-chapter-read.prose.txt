While Zarxan and the other High Elders deliberated the imminent fate of the sect, Aodhán was being led to the visitor's quarters by an outer disciple called Rhetta Kael.

Rhetta was an advanced-class storm awakened—or cultivator, as she called herself—at the 31st tier. Her core glowed to his senses, completely free of impurities, yet lacking the blinding radiance he expected from someone so advanced.

She was weak for her tier, but with a core that pure, Aodhán suspected her only limitation was motivation. He would have said understanding as well, perhaps, but with thousands of storm cultivators gathered in one location, he doubted knowledge was as jealously guarded here as it had been in Lutia.

The girl skipped ahead of him, no more than a year older than he was, her raven-dark hair swishing with each bounce. She bore no bloodline manifestation, like most of the disciples he'd seen so far, but that was only one peculiarity among many he'd noticed about the outer sect since his arrival.

Everything here was different from what he was used to—the cultivators, the architecture, the culture, and even the greetings.

Aodhán remembered his first run-in with another disciple a few minutes ago. The young man had been at the 37th tier, and when they'd locked gazes, he'd made the mistake of greeting them with a simple hello.

He'd earned himself a scathing glare for his troubles, and Rhetta had laughed for a full minute straight before correcting him. "It's so obvious that you're not from here. In the Storm Sect, we don't say hello to our equals. That's a greeting for the mundane."

"What do we say then?" Aodhán had asked.

Rhetta raised a single finger to her forehead in demonstration. "We greet with a blessing. Storm bless you."

"Storm bless you." Aodhán repeated, mimicking the gesture to make sure he got it right.

"Better." Rhetta laughed, but her smile died the moment he asked his next question.

"What about those who aren't our equals? How do we greet them?"

"Don't worry about that." Rhetta replied dismissively, a grimace etching itself onto her features. "Only those of the inner and core sect are considered our elders, and they barely come down here. I've been here for six months now, and I haven't seen anyone beyond the core sect." Her expression deepened into something sad, and she smiled at him. "But then you come recommended by High Elder Al'ruhn himself. You must be special."

Aodhán shrugged but made no comment to confirm or deny her statement. This was a new chapter of his life, and he'd rather not begin it with lies and deception.

That had been two minutes ago.

Now, as they walked through the outer sect in silence, and Aodhán took in the stretch of pagodas and cultivation halls dotting the mountainside, he couldn't help but smile in admiration.

The Sect was beautiful in a rugged, almost violent way. No flowers or ornaments softened the landscape, and no precious stones glittered like Sunstone's flamboyant excess. Yet something about the brooding mountains, the rain-slicked pagodas, the perpetual storm, and the ambience just felt perfect.

And that beauty hammered home a single truth: he was no longer in Lutia. He was on an entirely different continent now, and the sooner he became like them, the less trouble he was likely to encounter.

They soon came across another group of outer-sect disciples, and Aodhán raised a finger to his head in greeting. "Storm bless you."

"Storm bless you, brother. Storm bless you, sister." They replied, and Aodhán let out a small smile of amusement.

Rhetta must have misread his expression, because her brow furrowed. "I'll admit their gear was fancy, but they're still outer-sect disciples." She shot him a sideways glance. "If you're impressed now, what'll you do when you see the core disciples?"

Aodhán didn't bother correcting her assumption. "Are they fancier?"

"Deadlier is more like it." Rhetta chuckled, shaking her head. "Those beasts are on a whole other level entirely. Most of them are inheritors. They could literally flatten us with a stray breath."

"Is that so?" Aodhán asked, his amusement deepening. "They must be very dangerous then."

"They are." Rhetta nodded, her voice lowering to a whisper as she slowed her pace so they walked in sync. "But even they are nothing compared to the special disciples. There are barely a dozen of them, and those guys are literally gods, I tell you. They run the entire sect under the elders."

Aodhán's interest was piqued. He lowered his voice to match hers. "Are they transmigrants?"

"They are. And they live up there." Rhetta pointed to the top of a distant mountain, just beneath the roiling clouds. "The elders and High Elders live above them."

Aodhán glanced at the mountain, squinting against the pouring rain to see clearly, but aside from the distant glow of golden lamps, he couldn't make out anything else. He frowned. "Does the rain ever stop?"

"Sometimes." Rhetta shrugged. "The clouds never do, though."

"I don't mind the clouds."

She grinned. "Good. Because you'll be seeing a lot of them." She gestured ahead. "Come on, we're almost there."

Aodhán looked away from the Special Sect and followed Rhetta. They'd reached the visitor's quarters, and as they walked into one of the large stone buildings, Aodhán sighed in relief.

He hadn't exactly doubted Zarxan, but hearing that transmigrants were as respected as the Calamity had claimed set his mind at ease. They reached the receptionist desk a moment later, and five minutes after that, Aodhán was saying his goodbyes to the only other person he knew aside from Zarxan within the sect.

"If you need anything, find the receptionist." Rhetta jerked her thumb over her shoulder. "And if you end up in the outer sect after initiation, come find me. I'm usually at the arena." She paused, then added with a crooked grin, "Fair warning—I'm terrible at sparring, but I love watching other people get their asses kicked."

Aodhán smiled. "I'll keep that in mind."

"You do that." She gave him a casual salute and left.

For the first time since his arrival in Gordon, Aodhán was finally alone. He shut the door behind him, then turned to take in the room.

It was surprisingly large, obviously spatially expanded, and absolutely covered in runes. Aodhán recognized a few at first glance—water resistance, reinforcement, wind, and space. Though he peered at them curiously, he didn't spend more than a few minutes studying them before turning to take in the rest of the room.

There were no windows or openings within the room, yet air streamed in seamlessly, blowing coolly against his skin. A bed sat at the center of the room, a huge mirror hung on the opposite wall, and in a corner, a wooden table stood, containing a single brown parchment, a quill, and a jar of ink.

Aodhán smirked at the primitive writing materials and, with a faint smile, walked toward the table, tracking water across the floor without care. But it didn't matter. Heat runes flared to life beneath his feet, and an instant later, the wet tracks vanished.

When Aodhán reached the table, he picked up the parchment to read its contents.

Welcome to the Tempestuous Bastion, dear visitor. May your stay be tempestuous indeed.

Storm bless you.

Aodhán chuckled at the contents and shook his head. He understood the need to stick with the whole storm theme, but he doubted this would read as welcoming or encouraging to anyone else.

A tempestuous stay wasn't really a good thing, now, was it?

Still chuckling, Aodhán dropped the parchment and glanced back at the stone walls of the room. A memory of his room back at the 5th academy flashed across his mind in that moment, and his emotions slowly soured as an intense longing for his home and family rose within him.

Shuddering, he closed his eyes, and in the darkness, he could almost see Synové's ecstatic expression as she rushed to hug him and Daruk. Yue's face came next, and then Unrid's. Then Andrew's, Yurin's…

Like water from a dam, the images didn't stop. People, places, and events—hundreds of memories, both unnecessary and important, all flashed across his mind, each one adding to the intense feeling of nostalgia and longing brewing within him until his legs began to shudder—suddenly too weak to hold him up.

Eyes still closed, he lowered himself to the floor, leaned his head against the soft bed, and let himself cry. The tears came hot and unbidden against his cheeks. His lips trembled, and a sob tore itself from his throat—raw, broken. Then another. And another.

He'd been holding it together, and he'd been succeeding, but now that he was alone, he could feel the pain pressing down against his chest like a mountain. Nostalgia and longing curled within him so tightly it felt like grief.

And perhaps it was.

Grief for the ones he'd lost. For the ones he’d never see again. For the comfort of family—the warmth of love and the steady assurance that he was never alone.

It didn't matter what had happened in Lutia; he'd never had to face it alone. He'd always had Daruk or the champions looking over him.

But he was alone now, and despite the comfort of storm essence roiling around him, Aodhán still felt cold.

He might have taken a step forward, but it felt as if he'd just buried a dozen family members. His heart ached, and he wondered how he was supposed to survive the next few months or years without Daruk to keep him in check, without Aldric to be Aldric, without Synové's wise words and matchmaking attempts, and without Unrid's love and pride.

They were his first ever family, and now he couldn't even celebrate his freedom with them. He was free, but the freedom was bitter.

Minutes passed in silence and tears. Maybe hours. Time felt meaningless in the darkness behind his closed eyes.

But eventually, the sobs subsided into quiet tremors. His breathing steadied, and the tears slowed before stopping entirely, leaving his face wet and eyes swollen. The pain pressing down on his chest eased, and with a shuddering breath, Aodhán finally opened his eyes.

His gaze landed on the stone ceiling, and after staring at it for a while, Aodhán pulled himself back together. This is a phase, he thought to himself. In a few years at most, I'll reach the Mythic class, and I'll finally be able to return to Lutia without fear or consequences.

The sooner he achieved that, the sooner he could see his family and friends again.

And he couldn't wait.

Filled with a budding determination, he grasped the spatial pendant on his neck and began pulling its contents out—glad he'd managed to keep it during his time in Abyssos.

There wasn't much left within it after absorbing all the cores he'd stored, but every single thing that remained, Aodhán considered valuable.

The first was a pouch of Lutian credits, and though he couldn't spend it here, he was certain he could exchange it for whatever Gordon considered currency. Setting the pouch aside, he pulled out the next item and swallowed down another spike of nostalgia.

It was Geneva's rune book, and nestled within it was his own practice note, glowing with the last rune he'd inscribed.

SKORVAL—the rune of fragrance.

The sight of it caused another wave of nostalgia as he remembered Professor Caldwell's teachings as well as her enthusiasm to help him create his weapon. She had been his favorite professor back at the academy, and the very idea that he might not see her again caused another wave of sorrow.

He studied the rune for a moment before placing it aside to pull out the next item, and then the next.

One by one, he studied each item closely, memories flashing and emotions bubbling within him. With a chuckle, he pulled out the pile of clothes Kaelith had sewn for him, wondering where he could ever wear them now that he was in Gordon.

Shaking his head in amusement, he set the clothes aside, pulled out the last item from his spatial space, and froze.

It was the holographic projection array Geneva had given him—whole and intact. With only a bit of energy and willpower, he could activate it and speak to her. It probably wasn't even dangerous. He was in Gordon now, far, far away from the greedy fingers of Lutia's Mythics.

Nothing bad could happen from a single conversation with the woman who had been there for him on more than one occasion. She'd taken him on as her assistant, given him her practice notes, and even helped him bail Aldric out from the hands of the awakened council.

She was on his side. There was no doubt about that.

And yet, Aodhán hesitated.

Not for any clear reason, but a raw, almost instinctual feeling that told him it would be a mistake. Aodhán tried to rationalize it, but before he could come to a decision, a voice broke through his thoughts.

"That is not a very good idea, Aodhán."

Aodhán lurched to his feet immediately, black lightning crackling across his fist as he aimed it at the entrance. His fear, however, stuttered to a halt the moment his gaze landed on a familiar figure.

"Sorry." Aodhán panted, pulling his hands down to give a small bow. "You scared me, High Elder Zarxan."

"Al'ruhn." Zarxan corrected, lightning flowing fluidly around him as he floated into the room. Gone was the sense of mundaneness and restriction he'd emanated in Lutia. Here, right now, he looked larger than life. Regal.

Runic irises shone from his eyes as bright as suns, and a subtle weight pressed down on Aodhán, heavy yet sparing, as if the weight could crush him to powder if Zarxan so wished.

Aodhán didn't doubt it.

"High Elder Al'ruhn." Aodhán corrected himself and looked the Calamity in the eye. "Thank you for bringing me here, but I have so many questions—things I need to understand."

"Of course." High Elder Al'ruhn rumbled, and in a move so eerily familiar, he created a chair construct for himself, plopped down on it, and pulled out a tray of steaming tea from nowhere.

The heady scent of tea Aodhán had smelled so many times before in visions of Az'marthon quickly filled the room, and Aodhán shuddered faintly.

Zarxan smiled at him and gestured to the bed. "Sit down, Aodhán. There is much you need to know. But first—" He raised a cup to Aodhán.

Aodhán accepted it carefully, and Zarxan's smile widened. "Let us begin this conversation with our mutual friend, shall we? My master, your patron. Az'marthon Ranok."

Aodhán's gaze sharpened, and he nodded. "I think that's a good place to begin."

Zarxan chuckled, but in that moment, as he watched a Calamity down a whole jug of steaming tea at once, Aodhán suspected he was in for a surprise.

And not the good kind.
