Sally stared blankly at the new figure in front of her.

In one word, the man was clearly a wizard. Almost stereotypically so. Matching robes and a pointed hat. Flowing gray beard. Small spectacles at the tip of his nose. Eyes still burning a slightly unnatural emerald color.

“A-Archie?” she ventured.

“Ah, I’m…” The wizard looked both concerned and confused. “The short answer is yes.”

“So the artefact was…”

“A fragment of my soul.” Archie raised a hand to look at it. “No wonder I was so drawn to it.”

Sally pulled a face and glanced around the chamber. Aside from unpacking this revelation, they also needed to find a way out of the round room. From what she could see, there were no doors, windows, or other defining features. As if they weren’t meant to escape. With the Radochs here any second… things weren’t looking great.

She looked back at the wizard. “Will you be okay to move? It looks like this evolution took some toll on you.”

“Not as such.” He grimaced at her. “I’m just… trying to decide which memory is worse. That I can still recall every time I groomed myself as a cat, or…”

Sally raised her eyebrows.

“…or that I was the one who created this System universe.”

Theo peered over the rocky edge to the splashing waves below. “Nice cliff,” he murmured.

He turned back around and dragged his bloody sledgehammer through the sparse grass. Chuck needed a clear space to drop in the scanning device, and this brief field right by the coast seemed far enough away from any civilization to not cause any collateral damage. Apparently, the steep change in elevation by the cliff made it easier to gauge and not drop the fresh cargo into the water.

The vampire had just needed to clear the surrounding monsters from the site. A simple matter that hadn’t been stressful at all. Out of all the worlds he had traveled to, there was something quaint and comforting about this one. Perhaps it just reminded him of Sanctuary.

After sending confirmation that he was in place, it took very little time for a rod of dark metal to burst from the sky. With a crack of thunder, it burst into the ground a dozen feet away from Theo.

He approached it with his hands now in his pockets. The device was encased in a familiar material. It was the same metal as his coffin. Nigh indestructible, and it had few issues traveling the Sea. This case was about a foot long and two inches in diameter. The vampire crouched beside it and unscrewed the top part of it.

Within was the scanning device itself. A thinner rod of various different materials, coated with a layer or two of spells. The System was rooted by a mix of technology and magic, often interchangeable or indistinguishable from each other.

Setting up the device was a simple matter of pulling it from the casing and then planting it in the ground like a flag. Theo understood it would take something like an X-ray or brain scan of the world, reporting important details back to Sanctuary. How it worked beyond that was none of his concern.

After pushing it a couple of inches into the dirt, an orange light blinked on and off.

“Won’t take long to connect,” he told himself, before looking back out at the ocean and the distant horizon.

What a lovely day.

Sally clenched her hand into a fist and ground her sharp teeth together. “You are the cause of all this?”

The wizard furrowed his brow, looking down at the ground as he tried to recall his memories. “Figuratively. The real me who created the System died almost immediately.”

Some of her ire simmered away, but she crossed her arms. “The real me died when I was kidnapped to Sanctuary as well, but I’m still that Sally. Tell me what you remember.”

Archie fidgeted with edges of his sleeves and gave her a nod. “Everything is… hazy. I was once a powerful archmage on a world… Othea. Very powerful. I can’t remember my actual name. Something went wrong with a spell. All I was trying to do was create a pocket dimension for item storage.”

“But you actually created a pocket universe. Only not so pocket.”

He shook his head as if to remove cobwebs from inside his skull. “I accidentally opened the door to becoming a funnel for all the magical power in the atmosphere, turning into a living cannon. The beam of pure arcane energy would have destroyed the planet. Folding myself into the imperfect dimension was the only way to end it.”

Sally nodded and tapped her foot on the floor.

“My memories end at that decision. I don’t know how part of my System soul ended up here, or how all of these worlds came into being… or why they steal real people from the limited multiverse. My gut tells me I am only half of that puzzle.”

For Earth to get wrapped up in this mess, perhaps there was something on one of the three versions that somehow met Archie’s uncontrollable energy in the middle. Adding his magic to some technological marvel, like the simulation theory or a quantum computer. Speculation at this point.

Sally shook her head and sighed. “Damn it, Arch. Part of me wants to tear you apart for being the cause of all this, but I have neither the strength nor the hands to choke you out. It’s just a leaning tower of accidental fuck-ups, huh?”

“For what it is worth, I am truly sorry.” He managed a weak smile. “I can’t even shirk the responsibility off on the old me, or a fully formed version of me. It’s only right that I atone for my recklessness.”

The zombie pointed up to the small opening three dozen feet up. “How about you help fix your mess instead? That nugget of real Archie also gives you some power-ups?”

He followed her gaze. “Hmm. You’re right. We do seem to have a pest problem.”

Before she could respond, a flash of blue engulfed her. She was now standing back up at the lectern in the small temple. Archie was beside her, and twenty gathered Radochs stopped their clamoring to glance up at the pair.

“I was never too proficient with combat magic,” the wizard griped as he held a hand out.

A shimmering portal stretched across the eastern wall of the room. Just as the cockroaches gathered their wits to act, a large wave of water surged out, knocking several bugs over as it crashed against the western wall. No sooner had it crossed the width of the chamber, Archie clicked his fingers and the water immediately turned to ice.

The portal vanished, leaving the temple partly cemented in a rough ice cube. Many Radochs were buried fully, while others struggled, only partially stuck in the sudden attack.

“Summon Tide and Flash Freeze,” Archie stated idly. “I’m a little rusty, but it’s slowly coming back.”

“How did you do that?” Sally stood in shock. “Say the names of your skills like that, I mean. That’s different.”

“Ah, I have… a different System to you.” The wizard shot her a sheepish grin. “Before you go thinking I’m now an all-powerful god—or what you might call an ‘administrator’—I’m more like an… Aspect.”

“Well, sign me up if I’m supposed to be this powerful.” She drew her Skeleton Key into her right hand. “What are you the Aspect of, then?”

“Isn’t it obvious?” Archie held out his hand and moved his fingers, trailing light behind them. A purple portal opened above the trapped Radochs, and a large polar bear dropped down onto the ice. “I’m the Aspect of Creation.”

The shadowy figure put his hand back down at his side, as the dusty remains of three bandits blew away in the wind.

“W-what did you just do?” The woman cowering behind him whispered.

He turned his head back to her, his rounded glasses catching the sunlight, gleaming bright white. “Kept you safe, Nancy. I told you it’d be dangerous to follow.”

“You just seemed so lost and out of place. I didn’t want anything bad to happen to you.”

The figure grunted and adjusted his hat. “I may be lost and nameless, but defenseless I am not.”

That much was clear to Nancy. It hadn’t been that long since the stranger had appeared, and he was already causing trouble. Normally a shadow-clad man suddenly showing up in your home was cause for concern, but she could tell he held no ill intent toward her.

He just seemed simple and misguided. Wanted to travel and find some mystery person, despite the memory loss. It wouldn’t be right to let him wander off and get himself into trouble. In fact, taking him to the local doctor or guard station had been her idea - not that the stranger listened to her suggestion to head into town.

When the bandits had hopped out of the bushes to shake them down for money, the amnesiac had just held his hand out, and the trio of brigands had all but decayed to dust right in front of her eyes. It had happened so quickly that she wasn’t sure if she’d actually imagined it.

“Just… what are you?” she asked.

“A jumbled mix of half-memories guided by a clear and singular purpose. I just need a lead. Maybe literally. A guiding rope that I can take hold of, even if it drags me beneath the bow first.”

Nancy glanced around nervously. “Didn’t you say this was the wrong world? How would you… is it even possible to leave?”

“If you stare long enough at the stars, eventually they don’t seem so far away.” The shadow doffed his hat and started walking. “That said, it would help if I were a little closer to the heavens, huh?”

She followed after him, realizing the direction they were heading. “You’re not… but the evil wizard lives in that tower.”

“Evil? Hmm. He doesn’t happen to be a giant bug, does he?”

“Er, n-no?”

Beneath the rounded glasses, the stranger grinned. “Shame.”
