The doors of Large Format Patron Access closed behind Bart. They sealed with a heavy thud.KADDAK!

For the first time since the Archive had begun regular public service, he was off-site while the institution remained open without him. He immediately looked at the small display hovering near his wrist.

PORTAL CONNECTION: CLOSED

KEEPER STATUS: OFF-SITE

REMOTE LIAISON CHANNEL: ACTIVE

Gregor stood on the reinforced concourse, staring up at the vaulted glass ceiling and the sprawling network of commuter tracks stretching across the Cawnee transit terminal.

Bart did not look at the tracks but was distracted by his wrist display. Bernard stood beside him, clutching his case. The dragon watched him repeatedly check the display.

“You have checked that at least four times in the last few minutes” Bernard noted.

Before Bart could argue, the Cawnee municipal terminal subjected them to civic procedure. A ticket kiosk adjacent to the platform scanned them automatically. It immediately recognised the retired auditor.

PASSENGER CLASS: LARGE FORMAT

REGISTERED MASS: 8.94 TONNES

Bernard frowned deeply at the screen. “Still incorrect.”

“Still unverified,” Bart murmured.

The machine swept a blue light over Gregor as he stood very still. The kiosk chugged briefly before producing a crisp white ticket.

VISITING NON-RESIDENT - MUNICIPAL TRANSIT

TEMPORARY CIVIC FARE CATEGORY: GENERAL

SPECIES ACCOMMODATION: NOT REQUIRED

REALM OF RESIDENCE: UNREGISTERED

Gregor took the ticket trying to read the printed text carefully.

“It says I don't live anywhere,” Gregor pointed out.

“It says you are not registered as living here,” Bart corrected, retrieving his own ticket.

“That I guess is slightly less insulting,”

They moved out onto the main concourse. Bart looked at Cawnee through the lens of his own intense administrative interests. The city was remarkably functional in every aspect. Cawnee was simply operated well.

Bart noted the clear, multilingual and species neutral signage suspended overhead. He saw wide, reinforced transit lanes designated for large format commuters, running parallel to ordinary passenger walkways. Load ratings were visibly posted on every elevated bridge. precise, regularly updated maps stood at every intersection. Librarians, clerks, doctors, workers, business people in the form of minotaurs, dwarves, humans, and goblins hurried past them without a second glance.

Gregor noticed an entirely different set of  things to Bart.He pointed out a brightly lit stall selling hot pastries wrapped in greaseproof paper. He watched people carrying no visible weapons. He stared as a sleek tram slid silently along an elevated track without any dramatic bursts of magic. And he also noticed that in a terminal containing thousands of people, absolutely no one seemed alarmed that an ancient, dark scaled dragon was walking among them.

“The signage is excellent,” Bart said, stopping to admire a particularly well structured directory at an intersection in the hall. Gregor looked out through the massive glass windows at an entire sprawling foreign city.

“That is what you noticed?” Gregor asked.

“Yes of course immediately,” Bart confirmed.

They did not make it far before Bernard’s past caught up with him.

“Auditor Bernard,” a raspy voice called out.

An elderly person seemingly like a clerk in a faded blue uniform waved from a glass fronted booth.

Bernard sighed, his heavy shoulders dropping slightly. “Retired please David.”

“The west platform drainage report was finally implemented,”  David yelled over the terminal noise.

Bernard stopped in his tracks. “Which revision?”

Bart knew immediately that they were going to miss the next tram.

“You know everyone here?” Gregor asked quietly as the dragon engaged in a highly technical discussion about water runoff capacity.

“No,of course not everyone” Bernard rumbled without turning around.

Another clerk passing on the opposite concourse waved. Bernard reluctantly raised a claw in acknowledgement.

“Unfortunately, the municipal government is persistent,” Bernard muttered.

Ten minutes later, they finally boarded a municipal tram. The vehicle was a triumph of variable format design. It did not merely consist of a large empty space; it was divided into structurally distinct load zones.

STANDARD PASSENGER

HEAVY FORMAT

EXTENDED BODY / TAIL CLEARANCE

Bernard stepped into the reinforced heavy format section, settling onto a wide, lowered platform designed specifically to support extreme weight without obstructing the aisle.

As the tram prepared to depart, a recorded voice sounded overhead.

“Please retain your ticket until completion of your journey.”

Gregor carefully folded his ticket and put it inside his coat. “I was already going to,” he muttered.

He looked at the designated load zone. “They have a place specifically for dragons?”

“For large format passengers,” Bernard corrected.

“How many dragons use this route?”

“That is not the point of accessibility,” Bernard stated simply.

The journey across the city was smooth, efficient, and almost entirely uneventful, until they encountered a purely administrative obstacle. According to the impeccably designed transit map that Bart had memorised at the terminal, they needed to transfer at the central interchange.

“Platform Seven,” Bart announced as the doors opened.

Bernard turned left. “Platform Four.”

Bart stopped. “The map says Platform Seven.”

“The map is wrong,” Bernard said without breaking stride.

Bart frowned. “It was wrong?”

“It was rerouted 17 days ago,” the auditor explained.

“Then why has the map not been updated?” Bart demanded, genuinely horrified.

Bernard stopped and looked at him. The dragon appeared equally annoyed. “An excellent question my dear Keeper”

Bart stared at the offending map also put up nearby again and noticed a small, temporary yellow sign bolted beneath the main directory.

PERMANENT ROUTE MAP REPLACEMENT SCHEDULED

CURRENT STATUS: DELAYED (SUPPLY PROCUREMENT)

REFER TO TEMPORARY TRANSIT UPDATES (PLATFORM 4)

Bart visibly relaxed,  the error was at least documented. Gregor watched the entire exchange. “You were more worried about the map being wrong than the platform being missing.”

“The platform was clearly still located somewhere,” Bart explained reasonably.

Halfway through the second leg of the journey, Bart’s wrist display chimed softly.

He immediately checked the registered channel, bracing for an emergency alert.

ARCHIVE STATUS: NORMAL

KEEPER ACTION REQUIRED: NO

Directly beneath the system output was a brief, manually appended message.

NOTE FROM PATRON SERVICES:

Stop checking.

Bart looked around the tram car sheepishly.

“Is something wrong?” Gregor asked.

“Ah, no ....its nothing”

“Then why do you look like a pig on a paved road?”

“I do not,” Bart said quickly, closing the display.

He checked it considerably less often after that incident.

They reached the Cawnee Municipal Records Office shortly before noon.

It was nothing like the Infinite Archive. It was an organised civic building constructed of grey stone.

Clear signs directed them through the entrance hall.

CAWNEE MUNICIPAL RECORDS OFFICE

HISTORICAL SURVEY & BOUNDARY ADMINISTRATION - 3rd FLOOR

LARGE-FORMAT ACCESS - WEST LIFT

Gregor glanced at Bernard. “Why not the east entrance?”

“Stairs.” Bernard replied with just one word.

They took the reinforced west lift to the third floor, emerging into a quiet, climate controlled reading room filled with numbered wooden desks and glass preservation cases.Bart approached the main reference desk. The clerk did not look up from her ledger immediately. When she finally did, she presented an expression of absolute boredom.

“Institution?” the clerk asked.

“The Infinite Archive,” Bart replied.

The clerk simply waited and held out a hand for the required paperwork.

Bart realised instantly that prestige was entirely irrelevant here. He provided his registered identification and the formal external liaison request. At that moment he loved Cawnee even more.

Gregor watched the exchange with mild confusion. “You didn't even tell her who you are.”

“I told her my institutional affiliation,” Bart said.

“You're the Keeper.”

“She asked for an institution.”

The clerk processed the request, checking the identification against a register.

“Holding verified,” she announced, her voice flat and professional. “Boundary-Relative Survey Standards, Revised Compilation. Compilation status is complete. Preservation status requires restricted handling.”

She looked up. “Request approved for supervised consultation.”

A records specialist appeared a few minutes later, carefully pushing a padded preservation cart toward one of the reading desks.The cart contained a canvas binder with brass corner guards.The specialist placed it on the desk and carefully opened the cover.

Bart & co  leaned forward.

The table of contents was impeccably structured.

SECTION V  - POSITIONAL FRACTIONS

SECTION VI -  RELATIONAL VALIDITY QUALIFIERS
