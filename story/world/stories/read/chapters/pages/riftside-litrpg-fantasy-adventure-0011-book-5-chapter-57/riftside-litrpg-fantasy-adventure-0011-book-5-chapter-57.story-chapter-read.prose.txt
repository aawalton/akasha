“The paired half of every gem-gram outside Kingsworth is kept in Kingsworth. Messages go there first, then the operators decide what gets copied and sent onward.”

That meant any message sent here, even to the gem marked “Dawnwatch,” went to Kingsworth, not back home at all.

“Why? No. Never mind. It’s all to have some sort of control, isn’t it?”

“We are at war, Ash. Against an enemy far stronger than we are. We cannot afford panic.”

“Sounds sensible if monsters are coming.”

“And the neighboring counties had to support them.”

Her fingers paused before continuing to tap on the gem.

“So Central Command took control of the reports. We decide when news spreads and how far.”

“You nudge events.”

“We stop people from fleeing an area because the report of a rift breach. And yes, sometimes that means it will only be reported after the enemy is defeated.”

“So, just how often are rifts breached?”

She sighed. “More often than we want. But keep in mind that most often we are talking a few winged monsters making their way through during an attack. Central Command and the King do not want those tending the land to empty for miles and miles around a rift simply because a flier or two gets through.”

“But they are the ones most likely to be killed by stray monsters.”

“But with caravans and travelers, how come I didn’t know this? You can’t control what they say.”

“People are slow, prone to exaggerate, and love to gossip. Gem-grams are instantaneous and ‘official.’ Which one do you believe if someone says a cousin of theirs was killed three towns over by a flying monster? Drunk story or covered-up rift breach?”

I frowned, thinking back to how even rumors of Quarris were doubted.

“It also stops contradictory information from spreading,” Mara said.

“It takes decisions away from those closest to the problems, that’s what it is,” I said. “It also means a town can go dark and nobody knows why. The people here didn’t even know Riverdam’s rift was breached!”

“What good would it have done them? Would they have retreated behind their walls any sooner?” She scoffed. “It would have made it harder for them to control the town, and that’s it.”

“No, it—”

“Ash. A Hive Mind. Imagine how fast information must spread through it. One mind, controlling the empire, that’s what we’re facing.”

They’d realized it meant the town had fallen, but there was no army coming to rescue them because the army was gathered at Kingsworth.

Every rift across the kingdom was under attack, and another two had been breached, one north and one south of Kingsworth. Like Hillock, Calamour was a walled settlement. Mara told me that if the monsters attacked, the current defenders would be expected to hold. If the monsters marched past, then reinforcements would have been a waste.

I didn’t like the logic one bit, but I was starting to appreciate not having to be the one to make those decisions.

Dunedin, Cageheart’s fourth territory to the north, still stood. The northern breach was somewhere else.

But there was some good news in all of it too.

“Thank the bells,” I muttered.
