“Understanding ourselves is only one part of the equation. If we never want to fear battle again, we must understand the enemy.”

Scarlet glanced at him as he munched away. “Which means,” she said, “we are going to have to see what everyone else is doing.”

Maus stilled, some part of Scarlet’s feelings must have bled through the bond.

That would take some getting used to.

She gave Maus a reassuring scritch as she slid into her computer chair. For all the good it did, the internet had been a bit of a buffet of awful before Integration. She sincerely doubted recent events had improved things.

As she prepared to delve into a field both familiar and daunting, she reminded herself why she was online in the first place.

Information.

Scarlet settled Maus on his pillow and booted up her monitors. Time to see what was going on in the rest of the world.

She almost wished she hadn't.

The first post she saw was a looping short video that was quickly racking up views. The teenager who posted the clip had captioned it ‘Night and Day’. Outside the window, she filmed the sunrise. Then she turned to show the inside of her home, where seven people, from children to the elderly, were frozen in place, unmoving. She turned to look out the window again, then the camera flipped to show her face.

In the background was a dark room full of still people, while rays of morning sun illuminated her face. The tear tracks glistened in the light.

Outside, something roared, and the girl began to sob. Then the video cut off, then started again.

Scarlet hadn't even seen the creature that made that noise and her handgun already felt inadequate. She added 'Upgrade Firearms' to a rapidly growing list.

She briefly considered getting a silencer for her handgun as well, though that would need to be sourced. She knew a guy. Hopefully, he hadn’t been mauled to death by some mutated System horror.

Unfortunately, contacting him meant braving some of the less regulated forums. The pre-Integration internet had already been a cesspit for the vilest humans to act without conscience. Now every POS with a power fantasy had been given the magical means to see them out.

They wouldn’t all gain power, but enough of them would.

The thought made her shudder.

That’s when something flickered across one of the feeds so quickly she almost missed it.

She clicked on the video before it was buried by uploads.

The clip was short, grainy, and filmed from a terrible angle. The contents themselves were disturbing, but that wasn’t what had caught her attention. For a moment, the knife in the man’s hand was glowing.

Were her eyes playing tricks on her, or was that magic?

For some reason, the roidcoons using sonic attacks were strange enough that her mind had simply accepted it. Monsters doing impossible things felt almost expected. A human wielding magic, however, was just…

Scarlet shook her head.

There had to be another angle of the video. A security camera, a higher resolution upload, something.

She leaned forward in her chair as the bunker filled with shifting light and overlapping noise. Eight monitors (she'd picked up two more) were arranged in two clean rows. Their screens were packed with livestreams, message boards, newsfeeds, shaky phone footage, and panicked speculation from across the globe.

The internet had exploded.

The world was in chaos and so many people’s first response was to document it in real time.

What a mess. What a wealth of information.

In one corner of her screen, the video replayed. A man chasing a woman through a park. The video shook violently. It was clear the person filming was hiding. A knife caught the light. No, it was the light. The knife was glowing. The camera jolted again as the woman ran past, then the video abruptly ended.

She split the feed onto another monitor and began searching.

China. Nairobi. Brazil. London. Australia. Social media posts had fractured into thousands of competing narratives. Message boards were overflowing with speculation, and live streams – both personal and official. The sheer volume was staggering.

Emergency services, the ones that were even running, were overwhelmed. In major cities response times were spiking, communication between citizens and the-people-in-charge was breaking down. Not surprising considering a lot of them were still unconscious.

A lot of people, especially in dense population centres, seemed to be adopting a ‘wait and see’ approach.

Then there were the opportunists. Violent crime had gone up exponentially, and some people had chosen to film it. For what, entertainment?

Scarlet kept scrolling.

Official broadcasts had cut away or gone silent. News channels had gone dark or shifted to pre-recorded messaging. Radio hosts were replaced with looping statements and vague reassurances.

What was unnerving was just how many people looked to still be unconscious, or in some sort of stasis. Completely unresponsive despite the chaos around them.

It looked like the System was at least protecting these individuals as whenever someone tried to interact with them, strange blue barriers would blossom into place.

Of those who were conscious, few people seemed truly present. So many people were just fumbling around in a daze; weeping, injured, spaced out. On one feed the person holding the camera kept flipping between their face and the world around them. They were clearly in distress, and yet they continued to film.

The sheer volume strained Scarlet’s focus as she filtered through.

Then she found it. Another angle.

Scarlet watched as one stream cut from a frantic monologue into CCTV footage of a man attempting to stab a woman on a park bench, only for her to wake up. It looked like the barrier stayed with her for a minute after she woke up.

The scene was stitched together from surveillance footage. In the video the woman ran from the park to the parking lot. The knife the man was holding glowed with an ethereal sheen that made it seem unreal as he chased her.

She’d made it into her car before the shielding disappeared, however that’s where the scene went from confusing and horrifying, to genuinely interesting as the man managed to stab his glowing knife into her car.

The knife, which looked to be a plain kitchen utensil before Integration, somehow slid into the car. It looked more like it was stabbing into cake than layers of metal, hard plastics and other sturdy components. As the woman drove off the knife was dragged along the car’s flank, leaving an ugly line that went from the middle of the driver’s side door to the taillight.

There was no resistance, no drag, nothing that pulled him off his feet as he held onto a knife embedded in a moving vehicle. The cut it left behind was impossible. There were no jagged edges to the metal. The man may as well have taken a laser to the side of that car with how easily it cut through.

What was that, and how had he done it? There was no way he was the only one.

Scarlet needed more information.

She switched from screen to screen, trying to find more examples of phenomena, corroborating information, first-person accounts with evidence. Anything.

So many people were posting, commenting, sharing. For the volume of data she was wading through, it was absurd how difficult it was to find meaningful information. She just needed to filter out the treasure. She focused her attention on patterns, repetition, overlaps, and consistencies buried beneath the chaos.

She opened a separate window on the lower right corner of one screen, then another, then another as she began pulling data in from multiple sources at once.

She did not try to watch everything. Instead she prioritised posts that shared people’s System Interfaces (turned out you could share those), and sent a silent thank you to the people daft enough to do it. She also skimmed for impossible feats, supernatural phenomena, and anything that resembled corroborated information rather than just speculation.

The fans on her computers kicked into high gear, the hum deepening as her machines pushed to keep up with the load she was placing on them. Honestly, Scarlet felt the same. Her attention was in too many places at once, trying to do too many things at once. Yet she persisted. She needed more information, more data, more, more…

She leaned back slightly, not disengaging, but allowing herself just enough distance to try and piece together the whole picture.

It seemed like most people were still unconscious. Those who weren’t were fumbling. There was no unified terminology, no consistent understanding of what the System was, how it worked, or what it meant, and no central authority to help people get a handle on all the strange things they were experiencing. Right now, people were guessing, arguing, desperately trying to find their footing.

Most of all people were desperate for community.

Ideas were spreading but accuracy was not, and people were scared. They wanted to know they weren’t alone, that someone, somewhere had their act together. That things wouldn’t be terrifying and chaotic forever.

Scarlet was quiet for a moment, her fingers still as an idea settled into place.

“If it’s not there yet, it’s only a matter of time before someone fills the gap,” she said. “Why shouldn’t it be us?”

Maus stirred a little at that.

“Exactly,” she said softly. “We will strike first, and strike hard.”

Her fingers flexed. “Hello World,” she said. She cleared one of her screens and began to build.

Once the skeleton was in place she worked on security.

The structure was coming together quickly, but not fast enough.

Her fingers flew across the keyboards, her mind moving faster than her hands.

Patterns, systems, information. This was her domain. She pushed.

Something shifted.

It wasn’t a dramatic thing, but the change was noticeable. One moment she was struggling to handle the mental load of processing so much at once, and the next her thoughts felt so clear. It was as though her mind had all this extra capacity. Disparate threads began to pull together to form more cohesive theories. It was simpler for her to spot and categorise certain patterns. It was like her mind was adjusting to the volume of information she was handling.

She exhaled softly and opened her Status.

Perception: 11 → 12

Intelligence: 10 → 11

She looked through the Stats as she processed what she was seeing. Literally. She didn’t blink as the meaning of those numbers increasing settled into place alongside the sensation.

However, splitting her attention so many ways was like juggling. With a cup of water on her head. While standing on a yoga ball. It was taking all her effort just to keep up. Too much effort if she was honest. It seemed the System agreed because there was another shift.

The change was smaller but still noticeable.

Dexterity: 7 → 8

Scarlet’s mouth curved faintly. “Processing speed,” she said. “So, it’s not just a physical Stat.” It made sense when she thought about it. It didn’t matter how fast or well you could theoretically move, you would be capped at the speed your mind could send those signals.

She increased the number of layers and false leads. Routed access through long-established systems. Her signal bounced through blind corridors and dead endpoints that ensured nothing led back to her. It wouldn’t be the fastest website, but by the time it went live there would be nothing to trace. She was just one ‘ping’ of many.

Accurate, discreet and effective. The same things that made her an excellent PI.

“STAR,” she said quietly as she set the name. “Surviving the Apocalypse Resource.”

It would do. It took her moments to register the domain, and even less time to copy over a premade front-end web design.

It didn’t need to be ‘original’ right now. In fact, familiarity was a benefit.

Clean categories, clear structure, with no unnecessary noise or clutter.

Then the highlight: the forum.

It took her more than twice as long to create the forum as it did the rest of the website. For now, it was user-friendly and functional. This was the foundation that would expand into something more.

She had bots populate it before she launched it.

Threads appeared. Questions, answers, and arguments began to pop up. Some with theories she was willing to share, some with deliberate misinformation. All of it meant to give the impression of activity. Life.

Then she seeded in her own questions. One read.

@ Crit-Hit (OP): Got FS from a kill. Put 1 into PER. Now it says 4/11. Anyone know what that means?

Some responses followed, the bots doing their job.

@Crit-Hit (OP): Bug maybe?

@LanceisLot: LOL. Five is average, no wonder you’re confused.

@NirVananas: Mine looks like that too. Stats are higher though 😛

She seeded more. Different questions, different variations, different stats, and ‘potential’ caps. Each one was designed to pull something useful from whoever responded.

She set up a program on the back end to consolidate and organize information. Set some parameters for it. Pulled out several external hard drives and allocated it some space. Eventually, she would have glossaries compiled from multiple data points. People would be feeding her the answers she desperately needed. From there she’d be able to curate an FAQ.

She set an auto mod, one to delete, one to obscure.

Then she released it.
