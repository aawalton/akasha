All of the options apart from keeping the Skills separate are risky, but the third is particularly so. Merging Skills is at least familiar – setting them into an array is not. I should reject that option immediately just based on my lack of knowledge.

But when have I ever allowed not knowing something to hold me back?

I consider the option with the same kind of speculation that I applied to the other two possibilities, pulling up the message again to review the message that I’ve all but memorised.

An array…. Like…a runic array? I suppose it’s possible. The Enchanters created runes from thin strands of metal; why shouldn’t the System create runes from my internal matrix?

After mulling over that thought for a few moments, I shake my head. I can’t see it. As far as I understand them, runes are a human construction based on trial and error and magical theory that direct mana to create a certain effect. A little like irrigation channels if certain shapes of irrigation channels invariably produced the same effects, warming the water, cooling the water, filling the water with nutrients, turning the water blue, that kind of thing. But they’re not System-made.

I’ve never seen anything in anyone’s internal matrix that resembled runes. The arrangement of Energy channels tends to be more fluid, complex in a different way. And given the stress an internal matrix is put under during Evolution or level-up, I doubt that the sharp angles of runes would even be suitable.

Which suggests that the array is different from what Enchanters do. I’m tempted to take the Skill just for that – for all I know, the System-made array could be the foundation of a new field of study for Enchanters.

But I hold back. My Shaping Skills are the backbone of not only my combat capabilities, but what I can offer the settlement as a whole. If I mess them up, all I’ll have will be my Skills as a Tamer and my knowledge and experience built over three worlds. Not bad, sure, but not nearly as much as I can offer now. And I know that both World-Shaping and Omni-Shaping would allow me to at least offer as much as I can now – once I’ve regained the ranks that will be averaged out, that is.

Linking them is far riskier. It even warns about it in the description – gaining the wrong Shaping Skill at the wrong time might even destabilise the array, potentially causing ‘catastrophic damage’. That alone should eliminate this as an option.

My jaw tightens and I look back at the first two possibilities, deciding that I should pick one of them – and of the two Omni-Shaping seems like the standout.

However, I find my eyes drifting back down to the option I should have already discounted entirely.

On the face of it, the stated advantages don’t outweigh the possible risks. ‘Elements may inform each other or strengthen each other, and multi-elemental wielding will become easier, though not automatic’ doesn’t seem like much when placed next to ‘considerably more flexibility and efficiency’ and ‘enabling you to Shape elements that you have not specifically learned how to shape’. The array option wouldn’t even allow me to Shape elements that have their roots in the array, though it does make learning them easier. And yet….

Inspect is a perfectly serviceable Skill. Indeed, in most cases, it’s vastly superior to the original three, because it allows me to gain whatever information I need about my environment and the living beings within it in seconds, as long as I don’t mind my targets potentially feeling my touch. I’m even hoping to add Inspect Item to it when they end up at the same rank – that will create an all-rounder Skill that will probably meet all of my needs.

But there is a drawback to combining all the Skills: depth. I learned this from a random conversation struck up with one of the Farmers. His Inspect Environment is at Journeyman nine and he has used it almost exclusively to learn about the state of his fields – the soil and the plants in it. In that narrow arena it leaves my own combined Skill – not much lower level, at Journeyman 4 – far in the dust.

His Skill is capable of informing him about the soil’s fertility, its hydration, its mineral content, its composition, the plants that have grown well in it and those that have withered before fruiting. It’s also capable of giving him specialised knowledge about the plants he’s growing, informing him of their maturity, their state of health, whether they have parasites hiding within curls of almost-closed leaves, if they are diseased.

He can’t look at a beast and tell whether the beast is dangerous to him, or what its weaknesses and strengths are. He can’t find ore deposits, or buried treasure. But he knows everything about the state of his crop farm.

That’s what I’ve lost. And that’s not an issue – I don't need such specialised knowledge for Inspect. I have Flesh-Shaping to tell me about my companion’s health, Earth-Shaping to tell me about the soil, and now Plant-Shaping to tell me about plants both domesticated and wild.

But that’s the point. I compensate for a generalised Inspect Skill with specialised Shaping Skills. The message doesn’t warn about loss of functionality in the Skill descriptions as it did when I combined the three Inspects, but how could combining the Skill result in anything but loss of depth? If not of the present, then of possibilities in the future. I’ve noticed that each of the elements seems to have a sort of personality – how could combining them preserve that?

The most likely answer: it wouldn’t.

There’s also something else that I hadn’t noticed at first. The third option explicitly indicates that learning new elements would be easier and that new elements would be added to the array. The other two options say nothing about learning new elements.

Of course, that might just mean that I could learn new elements, and they just wouldn’t be added to the combined Skill. I suspect that would be the case for World-Shaping. But Omni-Shaping? If I could just use the element without learning it, what would be the motivation to actually learn it properly?

Reduce the mana cost? Sure. But I could also get around that by adding more points to Intelligence or Wisdom. Since I know how to do it manually, I wouldn’t even need to level up – just find a way of getting sufficient Energy. I’ll probably have to do that anyway.

I know enough about myself and the demands on my time to know that if I can use Ice-Shaping, for example, without dedicating the time to learn it properly, I’ll be willing to pay the mana cost.

The more I think about it, the more I wonder whether Omni-Shaping isn’t in fact a trap. It offers what sounds like awesome power in the short term, but in the long term? I think it would end up stifling the creativity and determination to progress that has brought me this far.

World-Shaping isn’t quite as bad in that sense, but I still fear that I would lose depth in each of the elements that compose the Skill, as well as losing functionality in the short term as the combination pulls the average down.

Linking the Skills together seems to hold the advantages of keeping the Skills separate, while also offering something more.

I consider my logic one more time, looking for any holes in it, any place where I am forcing through an idea just because I’m now insanely curious as to what an array in my internal matrix would look like.

There is one thing I haven’t yet considered – that by combining some or all of my Shaping Skills I’d end up with a lot more Skill slots available. Then I dismiss that as a factor – if I need Skill slots, I might as well remove things like Alchemy, Rune-Carving, and Enchanting. It’s not like I’m likely to have time to spend on those things, and I have close companions who are far better than I am in all of them.

Delegation, I remind myself.

Then, realising I’m getting side-tracked, I pull my thoughts back to the decision I have to make. The decision I recognise that, in my heart, I’ve already made.

Ultimately, if I had always chosen the safest option, I wouldn’t be here. I wouldn’t be capable of wielding any of these elements. I wouldn’t have all my friends and family. I’d still be stuck in a lonely apartment – or dead. And now? I decide to choose the option I believe holds the most future potential.

A moment after I select Link, I have the fleeting thought that I probably should have checked with Sarran and Lizil if they’ve ever heard of Skill arrays.

Too late, I sigh wryly to myself as I sink my awareness into my Core space, eagerly anticipating whatever the System is going to do to me. I’ll ask later.

For a moment, nothing happens, but I don’t fear that something’s gone wrong – there’s a humming tension in the air, like in the orchestra when the conductor has just lifted their hands but hasn’t yet given the cue to start the first note.

And then, with a crash of Energy through my System, the symphony begins.

Each of my Shaping Skills lights up, their illumination quickly reaching blinding levels. Shortly after, they begin humming and vibrating, shifting more and more in place. And then they move.

My internal matrix seems to be undergoing significant rearrangement. The smooth curves of the mandala are twisting and pulling, like threads in a loosely-constructed ball of wool, shifting to allow the Skills passage through without detaching from anything they’re attached to, or truly losing their overall shape.

The Skills all collect in the space below my Core, the threads of my internal matrix cradling them loosely. The Skills shift around a bit and then settle into place. Though I’m not certain in my identification of which Skill patterns match which Shaping Skill, I can make some educated guesses.

I know Fire-Shaping, Flesh-Shaping, and Earth-Shaping simply because those have been in my Core space since I first discovered the mass of golden threads. Lightning-Shaping seems pretty obvious – there are even lightning bolts within the Skill pattern. Air-Shaping has a loose, airy quality to its patterns, and Water-Shaping has a fluid movement to its lines that is also fairly indicative. Plant-Shaping is obvious due to being completely new. Metal- and Lava-Shaping are the ones I’m least confident about, but one seems far more ordered than the other, and the other has hints of fire’s pattern to it, so I can make some guesses.

The seven ‘basic’ elements have all arranged themselves in a circle that would resemble one of Saturn’s rings around my Core, except for the fact that it’s significantly lower down. Instead, I think with amusement that it almost looks like my Core is a basketball, about to make a perfect shot into the hoop.

Since there are seven of them, none is directly opposite the other, but Fire and Earth are as far away from each other as they can get. Moving to the right from Fire is Flesh, then Water, then Plant, then Earth, then Metal, then Air, then back to Fire.

Lightning and Lava are both within the circle. Lava is perfectly central between Fire and Earth, and Lightning is equidistant between Fire, Air, and Earth. Each of the Skills is connected with threads of gold in a way that they definitely weren’t before, though they’re also still independently connected to the greater body of my internal matrix. I do notice that there’s plenty of space – clearly the System is expecting me to gain more Skills and doesn’t want to have to rearrange my entire matrix every time I do.

Looked at it this way, I see what the notification said about balance. Currently, the seven elements are perfectly balanced, but both of the elements that are…second tier? Advanced? Combined? Sub? I decide to use ‘combined’ for the want of anything better. Both of the combined elements I have are connected to Fire and Earth, with only one also being connected to Air. Currently, that means the circle is roughly balanced.

But what if I gained a whole load of Fire elements without any Earth ones? Or only Earth and Fire with no Water or Flesh or Plant? It would tip the balance in the wrong direction.

Maybe I should ask Elarion if I could watch him Shaping ice, I muse. That would immediately help balance the ‘plate’ a little more.

Pulling further back from the arrangement, I see that the Shaping Skills weren’t the only ones to move. My Class Skills, though not exactly in an array, have shifted to be opposite my Core from the new Shaping array. Dominate and Tame are still separate, though Companion Bond has grown to almost envelop Tame in the same way it does Dominate, and Alliance is nestled against the opposite side of Tame from Dominate – part of its patterns, yet still somewhat separate.

My other Class Skills aren’t pressing so closely, but surround those four. Finally, the other non-Class, non-Shaping Skills have shifted further from my Core, littering the outer parts of my internal matrix, though most are closer to my Class Skills than my new Shaping array.

The matrix itself doesn’t seem to have changed much – the mandala still curves lazily, threads of gold interweaving casually, connecting my Core with my Skills and with the outside of my Core space entirely. It’s grown a bit bigger, but that’s almost to be expected – it’s enveloping more space with the new, more spacious form of the Shaping Skills.

Pulling out of my Core space, I’m surprised to feel the nagging of a notification.

“Another one?” I ask out loud in surprise.
