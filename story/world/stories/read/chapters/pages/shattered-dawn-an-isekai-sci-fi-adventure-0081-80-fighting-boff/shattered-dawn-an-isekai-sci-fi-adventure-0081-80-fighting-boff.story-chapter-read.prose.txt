“What did you do with Keyla’s Bondhold?” Elion demanded, righting himself.

“Venya has it,” Boff said, swinging his shotgun around and pointing it at Elion’s chest. He fired. Elion took the blast head on. It hit like a sledgehammer blow, lifting him off his feet and slaming him into the far wall.

Boff racked the shotgun and aimed again. Elion staggered, diving for cover behind the fallen table. He slipped on gemstones rolling under his feet. This shot boomed, splintering a gaping hole in the tabletop.

Yaret dashed in, his rifle popping. Wood paneling at the back of the room splintered.

“Tough one, ain’t ya?” Boff yelled. “Guards! Help!” He ducked behind the massive safe, and shot around the corner, forcing Yaret to back off.

Boff yelled loud enough for the owned in the next room to hear him. “All you sleeping morons, wake up and run down here. Come kill this thief!”

Elion vaulted over the table, his soggy clothing squishing beneath his breastplate. He fired a half dozen times, trying to keep Boff pinned down. The magazine automatically dropped out of Elion’s weapon when he fired the last bullet. He didn’t have any more shots.

Boff leaned around the corner and fired again. This shot clipped the side of Elion’s breast plate, dozens of pellets bouncing off the curved metal and stabbing into his arm. He felt a wave of Ascendency fatigue, but surged forward.

Elion grabbed Boff as he racked his gun again. Footsteps clattered down the hall from the room where the owned slept. Grappling, Elion caught hold of several bondholds wrapped around Boff’s arm, tearing them free.

Boff brought the butt of the gun around and slammed it up into Elion’s chin, knocking his head back and snapping his teeth. Elion tasted blood, and rolled away.

Boff scrambled to his feet and raised the gun. Elion slapped at the weapon, striking the barrel of the gun aside as Boff fired. The blast stabbed into Elion’s ears, making them ring. Elion gripped Boff by the collar with both hands and rolled backwards, pulling the man with him.

Yaret shouted in the hall, and bodies impacted, thudding against the wooden walls.

Owned men and women stumbled into the room. Elion slammed Boff’s head down into the ground and knelt on top of him. Boff lay limply on the ground.

Hol charged across the room at Elion. The intention in the big man’s eyes was clear. He would obey Boff’s orders to come ‘kill the thief.’

“Wait!” Elion shouted. He tore bondholds from Boff’s neck and wrists, hurtling them at the oncoming man. Trinkets and baubles bounced off of Hol’s chest.

“You’re free!” Elion yelled. “Take these, I order you to stop!”

Hol caught a key tied to a cord, and held it in his hand, looking at it in wonder. Remy brushed the Bondholds aside, and slammed into Elion, wrapping her thick hands around Elion’s throat.

People poured into the room. Elion punched at Remy as she squeezed his throat. More owned piled on top of his arms and legs, pinning him down. He felt the blood flow to his brain weakening. *Not again,* he thought, his blows losing strength. *Strangled twice in one night.*

Yaret shouted.

Hol tucked the key that was his bondhold into his pocket. He walked over to Boff’s unconscious form and plucked a small, laminated photograph from Boff’s pocket.

“Get off him, Remy,” the man grumbled, and shoved woman off of Elion with his foot.

Remy rolled, obeying the order, then her eyes widened as she caught sight of the photograph.

“My picture, Hol,” she gasped. “That’s my picture.”

Hol started tossing other owned off of Elion.

“We’re free now,” Remy said, gripping Hol’s shoulders. “We’re free.”

Tears welled up in the big man’s eyes.

One of the other owned picked up Boff’s gun and aimed it at Elion.

“Help me!” Elion gasped.

Hol and Remy sprang into action, restraining owned and matching Bondholds with their owners. In a few short minutes, everyone in the room cradled their precious mementos.

Elion rubbed at his throat, grateful for the protection buffs of his ascended state. Everyone looked at him.

“What should we do?” Remy asked.

“You’re free now,” Elion said. “Do whatever you want. But you should know that there’s an army attacking New Kairn Tol.”

The former owned looked around uncomfortably at one another. Elion deactivated his Ascended state. *I’m going to have to dry out that armor. It’s so squishy and uncomfortable. Is it not going to dry while it’s… off?*

Elion stood, kicking purple gemstones aside. “And,” he said, gesturing at the cends and coins lying on the ground, “This is pay for your labor,” he declared. “Collect it.”

The owned started picking things off the floor, like Elion possessed their bondholds and had ordered them to.

Elion stooped and picked up a few stones. Something was strange about some of the purple ones; the Zelian Splinters.

He asked Praxis about one of them, but received no response. Normally the system would tell him to offer the Ascendency Stone on an altar. He picked up a different one, and tried again, this time getting the message he expected.

> << Place your Ascendency Stone on the Altar >>

“Some of these are fake,” he said. Yaret walked over to him, and looked at the stones in his hand. Elion quickly sorted out a small pile of real and fake cends. Only purple gemstones lay in the pile of fakes.

“The warlocks are ripping us off,” Yaret said. “They’ve been counterfeiting cends!”

“Prator’s going to be mad when he hears about this,” Elion mused.

“He’s not going to find out,” Hol growled. “I’m going to kill him!”

The others murmured agreement.

“Wait,” Elion said. “I can use your help.” He looked at a small pile of bondholds gathered in the middle of the room.

“This isn’t everyone,” he said. “There are still more Bondholds here.” He pointed to the pile. “If you’re willing to help me tonight, deliver these bondholds to their owners. Find a weapon, then come with me. We still need to get Keyla’s bondhold back.”

“Where is it?” Hol asked.

“Venya has it,” Elion said. “I won’t blame you if you don’t want to go charging into the warlock’s camp with me. But I will say this. After reclaiming our belongings from Venya, we’re going to go rescue Selna from the hole where Bortim has her locked up. And then we’ll have to confront Prator.”

“I’m with you,” Hol said. “We all are.”

Hol started organizing the former slaves, sending them out to free and bring in the others. Elion scooped up a few of the real Ascendency stones and slipped them in his pocket. He was going to need to get to Prator’s altar and use these if he was going to stand a chance of recovering his own bondhold.

How was he going to level up and beat Prator without the man stopping him? All he’d have to do is give him an order.

Elion hoped that Yaret was right about using a stronger bond to break a bondhold. He was going to need to be able to do that. Tonight.

*Then what? Reclaim our bondholds, free Selna, get my knife back… Then what do we do? Where will we go? Something has happened in Aterfel. Will the army have our backs? Are they our friends, or our enemies?*

Elion decided to cross that bridge if they ever got to it.

A short time later Elion stood in the refinery, in front of a group of strong men and women, newly in possession of their bondholds. Most of the owned were here. Hol had forced a few of the sicker or older ones to stay behind, but everyone wanted to strike back.

They all held makeshift weapons; large wrenches, long poles, hammers, and other tools found around the refinery. Some of them had managed to scrounge up guns. Yaret had found a replacement magazine for Elion’s weapon, which now rested in the crook of his arm. Soon, Elion wouldn’t need the rifle. He’d have his knife back.

Sure, he’d keep the rifle too, he supposed. But his soul yearned to be reunited with his blade.

Around 50 people worked here in the refinery, and they were all ready to fight Prator. Elion felt bad for the man for a moment. He seemed to be sincere, even if he’d ended up enslaving hundreds of people. Tonight was going to be a bad night for Prator.

Hol divided the refinery workers into four groups. He moved from group to group, giving them instructions. Three other buildings around the acid pit housed owned laborers, and Hol assigned three of the groups to go break into each of them, kill anyone who resists, overpower the overseer, and then free the owned.

“Why did you make four groups?” Elion asked, as Hol dismissed the third group.

Hol turned to face Elion. Behind him, Remy, Yaret, Ryfer, and a half dozen others stood.

“We’re going with you,” Hol said.

Elion’s gaze immediately turned to Ryfer, the man who had attacked him in the pit.

Ryfer scowled. “If you’re going to fight Prator, I’ll help,” he grumbled.

A sense of relief washed over Elion. He didn’t have to do all of this alone.

“Why do the warlocks have Keyla’s bondhold?” Hol asked.

“They’re trying to get to me through her,” Elion said. “And I don’t intend to let them. They have also stolen my weapon from me. Step one, reclaim those. Then we’ll rescue Selna from Bortim, and we’ll give Prator a kick in the teeth!” This drew a cheer from the squad, and Elion lead them out of the refinery.

As they ran across the dark road outside, a massive explosion shook the ground.
