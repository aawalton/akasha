Her pride warred with fear, but the stakes were too high to give in to either. She couldn’t afford a misstep. Not now. "If I am to swear a soul pact," she said, each word deliberate, "then I will not agree to one that forces me to harm myself or those who have pledged to me. Not by command, consequence, or clever phrasing."

Arthur raised an eyebrow, amused and slightly intrigued. But before he could speak, Drogan's voice surfaced in his skull, low and familiar.

"Gods above. This is why I never bother with these fancy leash-riddles. You want loyalty? Win it in blood. Crush your enemy, stand over what's left, and take your prize. You waste more breath on promises than most do in a war."

Not every problem can be solved with a hammer, Arthur thought.

Then you're not swinging hard enough, Drogan replied, smug as ever.

Arthur exhaled through his nose, gaze still fixed on Ysara. Behind his calm exterior, anticipation simmered. Was she stalling for time, or simply playing her last card? He remained watchful, letting the silence stretch just enough to test her nerves. "Fine. Keep your little clause."

Ysara nodded once, slowly, and stepped forward.

"By will and witness, I swear," she intoned. "To speak no falsehood to the bearer of my pact. To obey his commands, so long as they do not cause harm. Neither directly nor indirectly. Not to myself, nor to those who have pledged themselves to me. To remain loyal until the pact is broken."

The air snapped tight. A flash of invisible force passed between them, gone in a blink, but leaving the air charged and still.

Ysara stiffened. Her hand went to her sternum, fingers brushing against fabric as if trying to locate something beneath the skin. She didn’t speak, but her eyes told the truth: she’d felt it too. It took a few heartbeats for her breathing to steady.

Only then did the glowing notification bloom across her vision, confirming the pact and revealing the enforcer’s name. Her eyes widened as the word settled. Her jaw dropped.

Ysara snapped her mouth shut, face flushing with sudden awareness. She hadn't meant to react like that, especially not in front of the MONSTER SLAYER. The one who had subdued Yudursh, the twin-blade. The one who conjured and vanished him like it was nothing.

She forced herself to stand straighter, collecting the royal composure that had momentarily slipped. Her hands trembled slightly at her sides, and she took a deep breath to steady the shaking in her chest. The fear still clung to her spine, but she wore her mask anyway.

"Never mind," she said, shaking her head. "Arthur… now that I’ve taken the pact, please allow me to heal the warrior under your custody."

"Not so fast," Arthur said. "First, you need to answer a few of my questions."

Ysara's brow furrowed. "But you've wounded the warrior badly. He might bleed to death if I don't heal him soon."

He’s in stasis now, so you don’t need to worry about him bleeding out while we talk. Now answer my questions. Only then will you be allowed to heal him."

Ysara fell silent, but her thoughts churned. He’s definitely E-rank. The system wouldn’t let anyone higher participate in the hunt. But first he makes Yudursh appear and disappear, now he says he has him in stasis?

It tugged at something she knew, something impossible. It felt like a storage ring, but no storage ring she knew of could store a living being.

She narrowed her eyes. "Did you just put the warrior in your storage ring?"

Arthur didn’t react. Not a flicker of muscle moved on his face. But inside, he was already nudging Drogan. "Isn’t storing monsters and people in a storage ring normal?"

Drogan snorted back in his mind. "Sure. If your ring was forged at the heart of a collapsed nexus and etched with elder runes. Otherwise, no. Not normal at all."

Arthur mentally made a note to avoid using the storage ring in front of others from now on. Keeping his face serious, he said, "You will not speak of this to anyone. That's an order. Do you understand?"

But he wouldn't question a tactical advantage when it landed in his lap. Just hours ago, she was a commanding presence, leading her guards into a monster-infested forest with steel in her voice. Now she folded like paper. The contrast was amusing, and useful.

He needed answers, and if posturing helped get them, so be it. He let the title slide.

He started with questions he already knew the answers to. A straightforward technique from his previous life, using baseline truths to test for honesty. As she responded, he carefully watched her face, her posture, her breath.

"What’s your name? What are you doing in this forest?"

Ysara had planned to offer the bare minimum, to fulfill the pact and nothing more. But now? Now she wasn’t so sure. If Arthur truly was the heir of some A-grade faction walking the frontier in disguise, then even a hint of disrespect could be fatal.

So she answered clearly, voice low but steady. "My name is Ysara. I belong to House Valen. My father rules this E-grade planet. I, along with other young scions under the age of twenty, are here to participate in a system event."

Arthur gave a slight nod. She was doing better than expected since seeing the ring. Might as well press the advantage.

I didn’t expect him to go this far, though. I should’ve known he'd take the insult personally."

Arthur watched her a moment longer, then asked, "Do you think there are others who might be after you?"

Ysara shook her head. "No, I don't think so."

Then Arthur posed the question he'd been waiting to ask all along. "How far is the nearest city, and in which direction? How do I get into the city?"

She replied, "If your lordship wishes, I can take you to the nearest city."

Arthur quickly shook his head. "No need. I like doing things solo. Just point me in the right direction for now."

Ysara gave a small nod. "The nearest city is Copperpine. It’s approximately a two-day hike north from where the ambush took place. Here," she reached into her garments and pulled out a small silver emblem, engraved with the crest of House Valen. "Show this sigil at the city gates. It will grant you safe passage under my house’s authority."

Arthur accepted the emblem without a word and slipped it into his storage ring.

He then asked, "What is your relationship with the warrior?"

Ysara replied, "His name is Yudursh. He is sworn to protect and obey me, and if he fails in that duty, he forfeits his life. He was chosen for this role when he was only six years old, and he’s been with me ever since. Officially, he's my bodyguard. But to me, he's more like an elder brother."

Arthur studied her closely. Her answer rang with truth, but more than that, it carried weight. The kind that came from lived experience, not rehearsed lines. The flicker of sincerity in her eyes told him she wasn't putting on a show, at least not this part.

He gave a small nod, the tension in his shoulders easing just a fraction. "That’ll do. For now."

He didn’t summon Yudursh, not yet. Let the silence stretch, let her wait. Trust had not yet been earned, and in this game, every edge mattered.

The pact was made, the questions asked. And now, a new thread had been woven into Arthur’s plans, one that could either unravel everything or become the key to surviving what came next.
