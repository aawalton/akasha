Eola stared at the wall behind Patrice, determinedly not looking at the redhead as she glared back. She slowly started sketching her first mark, the one for Personal. It was a simple mark, and she sketched the Forsooth Script quickly and easily, then added Echoing next to it.

“You’re sure about this?” Patrice asked, a hint of anxiety in her voice. “We could just not do this and try a different idea. There’s got to be something.”

“Do you want magic or not?” Eola asked through gritted teeth. Casting a spell-chain for the first time took more effort than she liked.

“Yes. But it’s complicated. What if…”

“What?”

Eola finished her marks before Patrice responded. “What if it’s incurable?”

“That’s not what you’re worried about. You’re worried it is curable, and you’ve been afraid for nothing.” Eola finished writing the central rune and poured Mana into the spell before Patrice could argue more. “Oh. Oh, I see.”

“What do you see?” Patrice asked, nerves coming fully undone.

Eola didn’t respond. She hadn’t seen anything in particular yet; the spell hadn’t revealed any critical secrets. But for the first time, she at least understood why so many people used Mana lines as a theoretical framework. It was just so…clear. The way Mana flowed through her friend’s body, from her soul and out through her hands and feet…if she’d seen this as a child, she’d have absolutely believed it.

Then she dug a little deeper into just what she was seeing.

Patrice’s soul was in the right spot—right above that place that ached whenever Eola got too hungry. It was, as far as she could tell, the right size. Not that she had any idea what the right size of soul was, but it didn’t look strange or out of place to her. And all her Mana lines connected correctly. But where her conduit should have been was…

Well, she had a conduit. But it wasn’t connected to her soul. Not properly, at least. No Mana lines ran to it, and it seemed to float away from her body, up near the back of her head, right where her skull met her vertebrae.

“Patrice, do you remember the Phantasm? Can you show me exactly where on our head it wanted to touch?” Eola asked.

Patrice pointed. “Right here.”

“Then you have homework.”

“I won’t do it,” Patrice said quickly. “I never do homework. I’m the worst student at Varin’s Academy.”

“You will do it. You’ll do it, and you won’t complain. You’re going to learn everything there is to know about Phantasms, and everything there is to know about Mana lines and conduits. And then you’re going to tell me all about them.”

“Oh really? And what will you be doing?” Patrice asked.

“My schoolwork.”

Two days later, Eola still hadn’t exactly decided what to do about Colin, Roth, or Patrice.

The redhead was studying. She was trying to learn. But she was just so bad at it. It was like she’d never cracked a book on her own and tried to learn without a tutor. Not for the first time—and probably not for the last—Eola remembered what Colin had said last term about her being an actual toddler.

But at least she was studying. At least she was trying. Roth wasn’t. All he did was act furious about High Cultist Andrese being at Varin’s Academy. Eola was pretty sure he wouldn’t plot anything without telling them…but not all the way sure.

As she suited up for Spellsword Tactics, though, her mind was mostly on Colin.

She had absolutely no idea what to do about him. He’d all but disappeared into the library in the wake of whatever conversation he’d had with Instructor Tarik, and when he wasn’t there, he was asleep, the tell-tale signs of Mana deprivation on him. Either that or he was in the hospital, casting rituals. And he wouldn’t explain just what he was working toward. Eola would have helped him if he’d given her half a chance—of course she would have—but for Y’aer’s sake he had to give her that chance. And then there was the kiss. She hadn’t gotten a good opportunity to return it since he’d gotten up off the couch and…

“Eww. Absolutely not,” she muttered as she pulled her practice armor on. There were some memories too fresh, too horrifying, to ever want more than to sit close to someone on that couch. Last year’s suitemates had been…and done…a lot.

Today’s lesson had been announced as an extra day of practical fencing. Apparently, Lieutenant Librarian Fringe was out, killing some monster he couldn’t trust the Patrol Duty students to handle, so it fell to Maestro Yarrowbloom to teach Spellsword Tactics. As Eola walked out onto the gymnasium floor, though, her heart dropped.

They had a guest. And that guest was High Cultist Andrese.

Today’s lesson was on the mirror.

Alone against a Battle-Mage, a spellsword’s only hope was to get in close and flank, never to engage head-on. The Battle-Mage was a line-breaker, heavily armored and focused on both melee weapon attacks and heavy, repeatable spells, but their armor could be a weakness if the spellsword could stay out of their vision.

Alone against a Siege Mage, however, the situation was reversed. A spellsword would easily kill a lone Siege Mage if they could survive the gigantic ritual magic being thrown at them and break through their barriers and shields, so the game became about drawing out that big spell, finding the weaknesses inherent in every barrier spell from Safe Shield all the way up to the runes the Great Mage Tomlish had used over Catrine and Eola’s duel last term, and killing them quickly.

But against a spellsword, the Taron-Li took over again—at least in part.

“Lady Andrese and Miss Lemiene will demonstrate,” Maestro Yarrowbloom said. “Remember, cantrips only. No First Order spells. And no aiming to kill, maim, or seriously injure. You fight to first blood, yield, or until either High Cultist Andrese or I call the fight.”

Eola swallowed. Her practice sword felt a touch off-balance, and she adjusted her grip. Better. Still not as good as her mother’s smallsword, but better. She walked out onto the pitch and pulled her helmet on. In the moment before Catrine did the same thing, Eola saw something in the other woman’s eyes. Something she couldn’t place, something so foreign to what she thought about Catrine that it didn’t make sense.

Fear.

“Begin,” Maestro Yarrowbloom said, and Eola and Catrine both sprang forward. Eola feinted, then ducked below Catrine’s blade even as the noblewoman pulled her blow and danced backward. A Safe Shield appeared mid-air, then a second, as they blocked each other’s attacks. Steel rang on steel as they parried and lunged. And the whole time, Yarrowbloom kept talking.

“As you can see, Miss Lemiene and Lady Andrese are evenly matched, with very similar skill sets. I’ve taken away the weight Lady Andrese would usually use to break the Taron-Li and overpower her opponent by acting more like a Battle Mage. That gives her opponent an advantage if she can discover it.”

An advantage? Eola didn’t see an advantage here. She’d beaten Catrine with her own magic last time, but the same opposed magic wouldn’t work twice. The scent mage would have a workaround. And for this fight, it all came down to skill.

Catrine lashed out, a sweeping blow at Eola’s chest. Eola caught it on her breastplate, turning with the blow; in a real fight, it would have gouged the steel. But here, it only drove her back a step. She quickly backpedaled and cast a Spark Shot, then another Safe Shield to block her opponent’s counterattack.

They traded blows as Maestro Yarrowbloom narrated the fight, pointing out errors for the other spellswords-in-training, and Eola found herself slowly getting used to the fight. Catrine was good. Very good. But she wasn’t Eddarn.

She didn’t see it in Catrine’s eyes when the fight shifted—their masks obscured their faces too well.

But High Cultist Andrese’s posture changed. She leaned forward suddenly as Eola slipped her sword through, jamming it into the gap in Catrine’s practice armor, right below her knee. Catrine yelped as her leg crumpled. She hit the ground. Eola’s sword touched her neck, right below her helmet.

“That’s enough,” the High Cultist said softly.

Eola waited a beat, practice sword still held out. Then she nodded, flipped the blade back, and tucked it under her arm. Catrine stayed down, shaking slightly, and for a moment, Eola thought it was rage or fury, or any of the other emotions the noble had shot her way. Then she closed her eyes, reached down with one hand, and offered it to Catrine.

The other girl took it, and Eola pulled her to her feet. Her hand didn’t linger in Eola’s, and as her helmet came off, her mouth opened to say…something. But then she glanced at her mother, and that same fear flickered across her face. “Well fought, for a toddler,” she said.

But her voice didn’t bite like it had last term.

“Right. Well fought, both of you. Now, each of you has had a chance to see the mirror. Break into groups of two—Miss Lemiene and Lady Andrese will both require different partners—and continue practicing it. Remember that while the Taron-Li is the core of the mirror, breaking it by shifting the tempo of your spells or having the correct opposed magic prepared is powerful, as is simply out-fighting your opponent,” Maestro Yarrowbloom said.

Eola looked around. Catrine had already found a partner, thank Y’aer, and Jeanette Ferrin waved from across the room. She swallowed—Jeanette was good—and walked over to join her.

Extinct Tongues was brutal.

The lectures had increased in difficulty. Instructor Tarik had left the safe fields of Old Alemic and dove straight into the wild, untamed woods of Emerald Cursive. The language was just as difficult as it had ever been, with meanings changing every time a branching, curving path intersected another, and half of the class looked like they’d rather stab their eyeballs out than translate another page of children’s stories.

Eola certainly felt that way—and with Varin’s notebook sitting, a third or so translated, on her desk, she couldn’t even look forward to the class ending. More Emerald Cursive would only be waiting in her room, along with Atta.

Worse, Instructor Tarik was speaking Emerald Cursive. The language made more sense as a spoken tongue, but that wasn’t saying much. And the half-dozen elves in the classroom seemed to be almost smug about being able to speak back. The rest of the class was hopelessly lost from the moment they walked into the room after lunch until the professor made rude, dismissive hand gestures and the elves left, with their classmates following, despairing looks plastered on their faces.

In any case, Eola had plenty on her mind.

For example, um, Colin.

The smug little jerk. He thought he could hide from her? Well, she’d show him. She’d track him down, sit him down—but absolutely not on the couch—and tell him that she’d enjoyed his kiss, and that she wanted to do it again sometime, but less…rushed. Yes, that’s exactly what she’d do. Y’aer’s blessing, it’d be that simple. He’d say yes, they’d kiss, and then she’d get back to work.

Not that him disappearing had been all bad. In the last two days, she’d made some real progress on the journal, and her wand’s design was coming together—along with all the materials but silver and yew. If she could just get those, she’d be ready to start assembling the device, along with the layers of ensorcellment it’d take to make it a functional tool for her.

And yet…

Colin didn’t show up for their apprenticeship, either. Eola and Patrice spent the time studying Mana lines—much to Instructor Tarik’s amusement. Their understanding of just what Patrice’s conduit was doing way outside of her physical body hadn’t improved, but if nothing else, their theoretical knowledge was much better than it had been a few days earlier. And, even better, Patrice had mostly stopped whining about how hard it all was.

When class ended, she returned to the suite, Patrice and Roth behind her. Then she pressed her hands against each other, weaved her fingers, and walked straight to Colin’s door before she could stop herself. “Are you in there, Colin?”

Nothing.

“We need to talk.”

“Ooh, no, you can’t say that to your boyfriend. Never, ever say that,” Roth said.

Eola rolled her eyes and knocked. “Colin?”

Still nothing.

Then, just as she’d made up her mind to force the door open with magic, the suite’s main door opened, and Colin stepped in, looking like a strange combination of pleased with himself, sick to his stomach, and terrified. “Well, tomorrow’s the day,” he said quietly.

“The day?” Eola asked. “What day?”

Colin nodded. “Well, yes. The day. Or…did I not tell you? Y’aer, I’m a terrible friend. I’m sorry. I’ve been so busy.”

“You sure have,” Patrice said. She threw an arm around Colin’s shoulder and dragged him to the couch, sat him down, and plopped down beside him. Eola pursed her lips as the redhead continued. “Your beloved has been worried sick.”

Y’aer curse her. Y’aer curse her a thousand times. Eola’s face burned.

“Um, sorry. I’m taking the Journeymage’s trial tomorrow. Instructor Tarik’s suggestion, but I just got registered. Proctors are Tarik, d’Leonide, and Clearance. The standard exam is five First Order spells, and I’ve got mine all picked out. So, tomorrow, when Patrol Duty would’ve been, I’ll take the exam, the professors will talk it over, and if everything goes well, I’ll have my fancy new badge and hat.”

Eola swallowed. She didn’t want to kiss him anymore. Journeymage. Y’aer, Journeymage. He’d be the first of her friends to make it. To be recognized as a proper mage of any type. It was such a big step, and one that Eola had been working as tirelessly toward as she could for a long, long time. And now Colin was about to reach that point.

She needed it. Her parents were waiting, and while she knew they were okay because her armor kept resizing to fit her every time she put it on, that pressure was still at the back of her mind. Everything else she did—the ensorcellment, the journal, and even her spar with Catrine this morning—was in service to that. She crossed her arms over her chest, trying to contain herself. Y’aer, it should have been her.

No. No, it would be her. Soon. She was getting stronger. Her soul was still growing. Her casting against Catrine hadn’t even strained her, and she was getting better at using the right amount of Mana for a cantrip. And it wasn’t Colin’s fault. Instructor Tarik had a reason for pushing her friends the way he pushed them. Colin was no different. She forced herself to relax, then sat on the couch’s arm, next to him, and threaded her hand through his.

And she tried to force what Headmaster Tomlish had said at the end of the last term, about how she’d never reach Truemage, out of her mind—tried to be happy for Colin instead. “Good job, you,” she said. And she meant it.

“Thanks, Eola.” Colin smiled up at her. “I’m pretty nervous. It’s a big step, and I’m not sure my soul can handle it. I’ve been doing it in practice, but there’s no room for error. I’ve never had a soul like yours.”

“Right. Um, tomorrow, after the exam, can you help us out? Patrice and me, I mean? We’ve been doing some studying on Mana lines, and—“

“You want me to look at yours? Of course. As soon as I have the Mana for it.”

Patrice interrupted. “Don’t be stupid, Eola. If Colin succeeds, we’re going to Varin’s Town to celebrate!”

Eola was about to say something, but Colin got there first. “And if I fail?”

“Then we’re going to Varin’s Town to drink off the agony of defeat!”

Colin laughed, and so did Eola. So did Patrice, and even Roth’s near-permanent scowl lessened a little. Patrice was right. They could wait a day—especially if Colin succeeded. It’d be a huge victory for all of them to have one of their weird little group make it to Journeymage.

She still didn’t want to kiss him, though. Not yet. She had a perfect moment planned for that.
