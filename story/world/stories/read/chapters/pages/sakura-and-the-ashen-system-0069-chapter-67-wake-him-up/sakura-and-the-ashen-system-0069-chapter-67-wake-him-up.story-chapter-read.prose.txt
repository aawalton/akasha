"He caught up with us!"

As soon as Gilbert shouted it, something struck the wall of the station again. The stone shuddered, and dust spilled from the crack already running through it. One of the jars on the floor jumped and shattered. Sakura instinctively covered her head with her arms, then immediately got up, still barely understanding how long they had spent inside that memory or what had happened here while they were unconscious.

Lir was only beginning to recover from the brew. Gilbert was still supporting him under the arms, but the boy was already trying to stand on his own and looking around. Ater was nowhere to be seen.

"Where’s the old man?" Sakura asked.

"No idea. He disappeared when I woke up."

A familiar dry crunch came from the crack in the wall beside the door that had been blown open. Sakura turned and saw black spikes pushing through the stonework. Obsidian forced the stones apart, widening the opening. It was beautiful and deadly at the same time. Gilbert swore.

"Outside. He’s about to bury us along with the building."

Lir pulled away from him and braced a hand against the wall. He swayed immediately, but stayed upright.

"I can walk."

They moved toward the back exit, away from the spikes, but only managed a few steps. The wall ahead exploded with such a roar that stone fragments showered over Sakura. Gilbert shielded Lir from the larger pieces, and cold air rushed into the room along with smoke.

Faerd stood beyond the ruined wall. His clothes were covered in road grime, dust from the blasts, and dried blood. One sleeve was torn, and fresh cuts marked his face, but Faerd himself stood perfectly straight. His gaze found Sakura immediately and did not shift to anyone else. Black veins slowly spread across the ground from beneath his feet.

"Obsidian Hierophant," he said.

His calm voice made Sakura’s blood run cold. She had already met people who wanted to kill her, but none of them had ever made her feel the same way Faerd did.

"Faerd," she said.

He did not react to his name. Meanwhile, Gilbert let go of Lir.

"Get back against the wall," the mage ordered.

"But I can help."

"Right now, the best way you can help is by not making us pull you out from under a pile of obsidian too."

Lir clenched his jaw but did not argue. As he backed away, he bent beside the overturned table, grabbed its edge for balance for a second, then picked up a short utility knife that had fallen out with the rest of Ater’s things. At least he wanted some kind of weapon in his hand when they were facing an enemy this dangerous.

Sakura stepped forward.

"You came all this way for nothing, Faerd. I’m sorry."

He raised one hand.

"The threat must be eliminated," the man said.

Sakura recognized the wording. He had said it before with the same expression, as if repeating a memorized order.

An obsidian spear shot forward almost as soon as he finished speaking. Sakura threw herself aside, and it passed close to her shoulder before punching straight through what remained of the table. Almost at the same moment, Gilbert raised his hand, and lightning struck Faerd directly in the chest, throwing him backward.

For one brief second, Sakura actually thought that might be enough. But that would have been too easy. Faerd got up. The fabric over his chest was burned through, and the skin beneath it was damaged. He glanced at the burn without much reaction, then looked back at Sakura.

"This is seriously not normal," Lir said with a nervous laugh.

"Yes. Get used to it if you want to keep traveling with us," Gilbert replied.

"If we survive today," Sakura added quietly.

Faerd spread his arms, and black spikes began rising from the floor in several places at once. They looked like solid plants growing out of the ground.

"Run!" Sakura shouted.

One spike punched through a bench, another split the stove, and a third forced Gilbert to jump over the remains of the table. Lir pushed himself away from the wall and made it to the opening, still gripping the knife. They could not stay inside the station. Sakura got outside first. Almost immediately, Pampli burst from the bushes. The beast saw Faerd, roared, and charged.

"Pampli, stop!"

Too late. Faerd turned his palm, and a black wall rose in front of him. Pampli slammed into it with his full weight. Cracks instantly spread across the obsidian, but the wall held. The beast was thrown aside, rolled across the grass, and almost immediately jumped back to his feet.

"Does anyone here ever listen to me?" Sakura breathed.

Then she fell silent at once. This was no time for jokes.

Faerd was already moving more heavily than he had at the beginning. One side of his body moved noticeably slower after Gilbert’s lightning strike, but he clearly had no intention of stopping.

Sakura pulled one of the remaining combat scrolls from [Inventory].

[DAGGER SWARM]

The daggers shot forward at the same time as another bolt of lightning from Gilbert. Faerd managed to raise a slanted obsidian slab in front of himself. The lightning struck it first, blue branches of electricity spreading over the black surface, and most of the daggers hit a moment later. Two still made it around the edges of the defense. One tore across Faerd’s shoulder, while the other somehow buried itself in his side. The man staggered but kept coming.

"Does he even feel pain?" Sakura asked.

"He should. Probably."

"Doesn’t look like it."

While Faerd pulled the blade from his side, Sakura used the opening.

[CHOKING PETALS]

Scarlet petals wrapped around his arms and chest, tightening around his neck. Faerd stopped, and Sakura immediately tightened their grip. But short obsidian needles began growing along his arms. They cut through the petals, tearing the deadly improvised cocoon apart from the inside.

"Of course..."

Faerd thrust a hand forward. A heavy obsidian sphere began forming in the air in front of him. Sakura recognized the attack immediately.

"Move!"

Gilbert lunged to the right. Lir threw himself toward a stone outcropping beside the station. Sakura managed only a few steps before the sphere slammed into the ground between them. The shockwave knocked her off her feet and dragged her across the wet grass. Her shoulder hit the ground painfully, and the air was knocked from her lungs.

She raised her head and immediately searched for the others. Gilbert was already trying to get up, and Lir was looking out from behind the stone. Pampli, meanwhile, had circled Faerd in a wide arc and attacked from the side while the man was watching Sakura.

His claws raked across Faerd’s back. Faerd dropped to one knee, blood quickly spreading across his torn clothes. Pampli was already about to jump away when an obsidian spear formed in front of Faerd’s hand.

He struck the beast from almost point-blank range. Pampli twisted aside at the last second. The spear scraped along his flank, only clipping his armor, then buried itself deep in the ground. The beast jumped back toward the trees and immediately turned around again.

"Pampli!"

Sakura jumped to her feet. Faerd was already rising too. Blood ran down his back and side, his breathing had grown heavier, and more obsidian spears were forming in front of him.

Sakura had already tried to use Devourer against him once before, but Faerd had managed to break free of its grip. Now he was wounded and had stopped in place for several seconds to form new obsidian spears. Sakura did not wait for him to finish the attack. She stretched out her hand.

[DEVOURER]

The skill activated, but Sakura almost immediately realized something was wrong. Instead of catching Faerd himself, Devourer latched onto the obsidian he was creating. The spears trembled, cracks spread through them, and dark power began flowing toward her palm.

Sakura clenched her teeth. Pain quickly climbed from her hand to her shoulder, but with it came that familiar sweet sensation of power. She kept pulling, and the unfinished spears began crumbling in the air. Then the black veins beneath Faerd’s feet cracked, followed by the nearest obsidian wall.

Faerd himself remained where he was. So Devourer still had not reached him.

Faerd looked at the disappearing magic, then at Sakura.

"Devourer," he said.

Sakura remembered Crowlis’s words:

You need to master the class before the class masters you.

She broke off [DEVOURER] while the skill was still draining power from the obsidian Faerd had created.

Almost immediately, Gilbert shouted:

"Sakura!"

She turned too late. Faerd was already beside her. He struck her in the chest with his bare hand, without magic, but the blow was strong enough to throw Sakura backward. She hit the ground, and the metallic taste of blood immediately filled her mouth.

Faerd came closer, and a black blade began forming along his forearm.

Sakura raised her palm.

[DEVOURER]

A System message immediately flashed before her eyes.

[DEVOURER] UNAVAILABLE.REPEATED USE IS IMPOSSIBLE IN THE IMMEDIATE FUTURE.

"Damn it..."

Lightning struck Faerd in the side before he could bring the blade down. He was thrown aside, and Gilbert stepped between him and Sakura.

"You need to be faster."

"Great time for a lecture."

Faerd got up again. This time, the damage was finally showing more clearly. He straightened more slowly, held one arm slightly lower, and blood had soaked through the clothes over his side and back. Even so, the killer started toward them again.

Gilbert was breathing heavily, and his hand was visibly shaking after the latest discharge. Sakura looked at Faerd’s burned clothes, the cut from the scroll, and the deep claw marks left by Pampli. Any normal person would already have been unable to stand.

"We can’t beat him," she said quietly.

Gilbert did not answer. Faerd raised both hands. Dozens of thin obsidian needles appeared behind him.

"Lir!"

The boy had already ducked behind the stone outcropping. The needles shot forward, and Pampli instantly threw himself between Lir and the attack. Some shattered against the beast’s obsidian hide, but the rest flew past. Sakura managed to cover her face with her arms. One needle slashed across her thigh, while another punched through Gilbert’s shoulder before he destroyed the rest with lightning.

"Gilbert!"

"Not fatal..."

Faerd kept coming. Not as quickly as before, but just as inevitably. And only then did Sakura understand why they were losing. He was not trying to end the fight with one blow. He did not need to. They were getting tired, losing strength, and afraid of taking another wound, while Faerd treated his own body as if it meant nothing. He could keep fighting much longer than they could. At one point, Sakura thought he reminded her of a kamikaze. He did not care whether he survived this fight as long as she was dead by the end of it.

"Faerd!" Sakura shouted.

He stopped.

"Do you even understand what you’re doing?"

"The threat must be eliminated."

"That’s not an answer."

"The Obsidian Hierophant is a threat."

"To whom?"

For several seconds, everything went quiet. Even Gilbert turned his head toward Sakura.

Faerd froze too.

"Who am I a threat to?" she repeated.

"The threat must be eliminated."

Word for word. Sakura slowly exhaled.

"You don’t know."

Faerd took a step.

"You don’t even know why you’re hunting me."

The black blade along his arm grew longer, but Faerd himself was moving more slowly now, and not because of his wounds.

"Faerd, who ordered you to kill me?"

His face twitched.

"Stop," he said.

Sakura froze. For the first time in the entire conversation, it did not sound like another part of an order.

"Sakura," Gilbert said quietly.

"I heard him."

She stepped forward.

"Who gave the order?"

The obsidian around Faerd’s feet shot upward instantly. Gilbert grabbed Sakura by the shoulder and yanked her back. A spike tore through the place where her chest had been a moment earlier.

"Not that close!"

Faerd grabbed his head for a second, then forced himself upright again.

"Eliminate the threat."

His voice had grown rougher.

"Faerd," Sakura said. "Remember who you are."

He flinched. A wave of magic passed through the ground around him. Obsidian erupted in several places at once, ripping through the grass and splitting one of the nearby trees. It looked like a small, unpredictable earthquake. Sakura and Gilbert both backed away.

"It’s working," she muttered.

"Or you’re making him angry enough to kill us faster."

"Also possible."

Lir had already come out from behind cover. He still held the knife, though he clearly had no intention of getting close to Faerd.

"He said ‘stop.’ He can hear you," the fisherman’s son said. "You hit something real."

Gilbert looked at Faerd.

"But something keeps pulling him back."

Sakura remembered their earlier encounters with him. The same strange pauses, the same repeated phrases, the same complete disregard for his own wounds. He was like a programmed machine.

"They didn’t just convince him," she said.

"Looks like deep magical compulsion," the mage replied.

"Can it be removed?"

"If we know what was used to put it there."

Faerd started forward again. Gilbert raised his hand.

"We don’t have time to figure that out right now."

Something shattered against the ground almost at Faerd’s feet. A small clay vessel broke open and released a cloud of pale gray powder. Faerd breathed it in before he could step away, coughed sharply, and staggered.

Sakura turned toward the station. Ater was nowhere in sight, but a figure flashed for a second in one of the dark windows.

"Old man!" Gilbert shouted.

There was no answer. Faerd dropped to one knee. The obsidian around him trembled and began crumbling into fine black grit.

"What is that?" Lir asked.

"No idea."

Faerd tried to rise, but his arm gave out, and he braced himself against the ground with one hand. Sakura cautiously moved toward him.

"Don’t go closer," Gilbert warned.

She stopped. Faerd was breathing hard, his fingers digging into the grass. The dark veins on his neck became more visible, then slowly began to fade. The blankness in his eyes began to disappear. His gaze moved over the station, the trees, and the people around him as if he were only now beginning to understand where he was.

"Faerd?"

He jerked his head up. For one brief moment, Sakura saw real horror on his face. But he was not looking at her.

"Where..." Faerd rasped, and his body jerked again. "The threat..."

He clenched his teeth.

"The threat must..."

"No," Sakura said. "It doesn’t have to be. That’s an order. Not your will."

Faerd squeezed his eyes shut. Ater’s voice finally came from the station window.

"Don’t let him slip under again."

Gilbert spun toward it.

"What did you do to him?"

"Nothing that will last long."

"Then come out and explain!"

"Later."

Sakura could almost imagine how badly Gilbert wanted to strangle the old man right now.

Faerd moved again. A short obsidian spike appeared beside his palm, grew only a few inches, and stopped.

"His magic is coming back," Gilbert said.

"Then what do we do?"

Ater answered from the darkness:

"Don’t try to defeat him."

Sakura looked up.

"What?"

"You’ve already figured out that it’s pointless."

Faerd groaned and pressed a hand to his forehead. Now there was pain, rage, and complete confusion on his face. A few minutes ago, he had gotten up after every blow with almost no reaction. Now, for the first time, he looked like an ordinary person.

Ater’s voice grew quieter.

"You need to wake him up."

Sakura looked back at Faerd. He opened his eyes, and for one brief moment his gaze became clear. His lips trembled.

"Maev..."

Sakura froze. Faerd flinched as if the name had frightened even him.

Then the obsidian beneath his palm began to grow again...
