The reason Tenor hadn’t been able to locate Stacy’s partner was because they were the same person.

In confirmation of my theory, Stacy’s shoulder squared, her posture straightened, and when she spoke it was like talking to someone else completely.

“You think you’ve figured it out, don’t you?”

Stacy’s voice was still feminine, but it felt deeper, like she was speaking from somewhere within her chest.

“You and Zortana being the same person?” I shrugged. “What’s there to figure out?”

Stacy, or, I guess Zortana as she called her other personality, stepped towards me.

“We are not the same.” She spoke slowly, articulating her words.

Tranquility fell into my hand, glowing faintly blue. My pity for the woman in front of me hadn’t faded. If anything, her revealing that she was crazy only solidified in my mind that she needed to be put down.

“Well, I guess we’ll just have to wait and see.” The familiar stream of energy I’d come to recognize as the mana proceeding a judgement, tugged at my chest. “If you survive, when Stacy dies, we’ll know you weren’t the same.”

Was it possible that my judgements of Stacy might be a little bias, on account of her actions against me?

Absolutely.

In fact, before I met Tenor I was still waffling on what I might do if I met her again. I didn’t have those same trepidations anymore. It was one thing to fight and kill another faction, but the way she went about it just felt so...wrong.

Badlan’s bald head lying in the mud at my feet was plenty proof of that.

There was no need for more talking.

“Did you think it would be so easy, Healer?”

I ignored Stacy.

My judgement was determined, and Tranquility was ready to enact it.

The hinges of the temple door creaked.

Huh?

I hesitated.

BOOMMM!

The doors erupted in a shower of splinters and metal shards.

Stacy was making her move.

Tenor was still between me and the temple. I covered him in a barrier long ago. Debris bounced off the invisible shield.

He dove to his right vacating the space between the temple and myself.

I didn’t panic. I figured this was coming.

Time crawled.

No more waiting, time for Stacy to die.

Tranquility blurred. I waited for the familiar sensation of blade meeting flesh.

It didn’t come.

Stacy’s body fuzzed, and instead of cutting through flesh and bone, all Tranquility cut was the collar of Stacy’s pink button up shirt, and the black cloth covering her head.

Her clothes fell to the mud, black bag and all. Pale white skin stretched over the bones of a hideously emaciated frame, looking more skeleton than living human.

She’d become incorporeal.

Behind her was a gaping hole in the front of the temple. Naked hairless creatures that vaguely resembled Tenor and the other Gabordians, rushed through the jagged opening.

I launched myself forward to meet them, summoning my four phantom blades.

It wasn’t clear what was happening to Stacy, but I’d be crazy to wait around and let it happen.

A dark blue bolt of lightning beat me to the punch.

Daevon’s lightning didn’t flash like normal lightning should. The light was muted, and dull. I still felt the heat on my cheeks as it lashed into the temple wall.

CRACK!

Wood splinters and stone joined the drizzling rain, but the corrupted Gabordian’s remained unharmed.

Did Daevon just miss?

I wondered, trying my own skill, sweeping blade.

Just like it had with Stacy, my attack passed through harmlessly through the bodies of my targets.

“Are they invulnerable?!” My dad shouted coming to my side.

I wasn’t sure if they were or not. Probably not though. They did blow the door off the temple after all. They would have had to be solid to accomplish that.

Whatever their current condition was now, I didn’t think it was going to last. Not only that, but I suspected that although I couldn’t hit them, they also couldn’t attack me.

Whether I was right or wrong, the ruined Gabordians completely ignored me, passing to each side.

I turned around in cofusion, in time to see the lead Gabordian literally jump head first directly into Stacy’s body disappearing in a flash.

“What’s happening?” My dad asked me. I could hear the concern in his voice.

“I’m not sure....”

“I knew right away when first I saw you, Healer.”

Stacy’s voice sounded like it was coming from the opposite side of a long tunnel. As she spoke, dozens of Gabordian’s streaked by each diving into her incorporeal body.

I knew that there was no point trying to fight Stacy in this state, so I figured at the very least I would get some answers.

“What did you know?”

“I knew you had to die.”

I shook my head.

“Why?”

But Stacy didn’t answer. Or, more accurately, she couldn’t answer.

It was easy to miss at first, but Stacy appeared to be absorbing the Gabordians. And the more that barreled past me, merging into her body, the more she changed.

And it didn’t look painless.

Stacy’s face was locked in a soundless scream. Her veins wriggled and squirmed under paper-thin skin, and muscles bulged in random places like her body was a cheap balloon.

Tranquilities phantom blades oscillated in my peripherals like pendulums offering their subtle assurance.

Seconds passed and all anyone could do was watch.

Her body didn’t stop growing. One of the bulges popped, releasing blood and pus that seeped down her bicep.

I forced myself to watch until the last of the Garbordians from the temple had all been absorbed.

But the change was far from over. Stacy’s body stretched like chords had strapped her feet to the ground, and some other invisible force tried pulling her into the sky. Skin ripped and reknit as her five-foot nothing body became over seven feet in seconds.

It was hard to know how much time passed, but once it was all said and done, the Stacy we’d all known was completely gone. She’d been replaced by something that I never would have expected.

The face and body were that of a man in his early twenties, with broad powerful shoulder, bronze skin only achieved by hours in the sun, wavy shoulder length brown hair and square jaw.

And I would’ve assumed right away that he was a man, if not for the simple fact that whatever it was, was still naked.

There was nothing. Nothing at all that could identify this being as man or woman definitively.

Like a ken doll.

From thin air, robes materialized and draped themselves over Stacy’s chiseled frame, followed by a dark silver two-handed war hammer that it held with ease at its hip.

“Healer.” Its voice was deep, with a strange accent reminiscent of Italian.

“I....am Zortana.”

And I didn’t care.

Because now that the transformation was complete, “Zortana” was no longer incorporeal.

I whipped Tranquility forward, to Zortana’s credit it moved with impressive dexterity stepping backwards and deflecting my attack away with the metal shaft of his war hammer.

It even maintained its balance and adjusted its stance to meet my incoming flurry. If I was alone in this fight, I could tell Zortana might have been one of the most dangerous enemies that I’d encountered barring the three Chaos Spawn champions.

The only problem for Zortana was that I wasn’t alone.

Which is why it didn’t surprise me when a quarter size pitch black hole sprouted in front of his chest appeared, nearly at the same time as a blue bolt sprouted at my right ear.

An arrow zipped through the black hole, sinking into where its heart should be, up to the feathers on the shaft.

Then Zortana’s head exploded.

Daevon must’ve been charging up his spell while Zortana was undergoing its transformation.

Blood teeth and bone blossomed out. My barrier did its job against the pieces that could cause harm, but the blood splashed against my face some even getting into my mouth.

My gag reflex activated and I wretched bending forward to spit the blood from my mouth. This was far from the first time I’d ingested blood splatter, but I was definitely not prepared.

Red spittle dripped from my mouth into the grass, and I closed my eyes trying not to swallow.

It happened anyway.

And whether it was my imagination or not, I felt the thick red liquid coated against the back of my throat.

I grimaced while offering a silent prayer that I hadn’t contracted some kind of freaky disease. But if I didn’t get anything from the gallons of Chaos Spawn blood I swallowed, I figured I would probably be ok.

I pulled the soft fabric of my robe over my hand and rubbed it on my lips to wipe away the blood that didn’t make it into my mouth.

Then I stood up, took a breath, and opened my eyes.

“What on earth...?” I barely breathed the words.

The temple, woods, town, rain, people, and my dad were all gone.

Now, somehow, I stood facing a wall that seemed built of some sort of burnt orange stone that reminded me of sandstone. At the center of the wall was a door with two intricately crafted wood stands on either side holding deep blue vases with winding silver vine stenciling.

Footsteps approached and I tried to move and hide myself against the wall of the room, but my body was totally locked into place except for my head.

The footsteps grew closer.

I strained against the invisible bonds, but it was no use. Whoever was coming, I would be entirely helpless against them.

That didn’t stop me from trying though. I rolled my head around like a maniac trying to break free, even trying to cast healing spells but I couldn’t feel my man flowing.

Knock, knock, knock

Three sharp raps at the door.

I braced myself, which is to say, I scrunched up my face and prayed whoever came through the door was friendly.

“Enter.”

The voice came from behind me. It sounded very familiar. The door slid open soundlessly.

A tall slender woman with light brown skin, dark brown eyes and brown nearly black hair pulled tight into a ponytail entered the room.

She wore a crisp black uniform that hugged her hips and shoulders, suggesting an athletic build beneath.

In short, she was stunningly beautiful and pretty much exactly my type.

I opened my mouth ready to try and talk my way out of whatever was happening.

“Zortana.” The woman said, with a soft Hispanic accent. “Father is looking for you.”

The woman couldn’t be more than three feet directly in front of me and yet it was as though I didn’t even exist.

Did she just say Zortana? Wasn’t that the dude who just got his head blown up?

“Can it wait?”

“Do what you want.” The woman rested a hand on her hip. “I thought you’d be more excited though....”

The beautiful newcomer let the bait dangle.

And Zortana snatched it right up.

“Why should I be excited?”

“They found a willing vessel in a newly inducted world.” She purred.

I heard the sound of a chair rubbing against carpet. The next moment, Zortana appeared beside me. But this wasn’t the same Zortana I met at all.

This Zortana was somehow even shorter than I was, with thin shoulders that didn’t fill out his male version of the black military attire the woman wore. He had a wiry face, wispy blonde hair and a scraggly goatee.

Walking quickly, this new Zortana shoved his way past the woman in the doorway disappearing.

Leaving me alone with who was apparently his sister.

“Thanks Diale for coming to find me...” She muttered under her breath, then she turned to follow, leaving me alone in the room.

I felt a pang of disappointment. What did she just say her name was?

Diale?

Then my head whipped back, as I was dragged unwittingly out of the room into a stunning open-air atrium.

From my limited vantage, it appeared as though I was in some kind of tower. I knew that we weren’t on the first floor. I would have loved to peer over the railings to take the architecture in all its glory but sadly my body seemed tethered to either Zortana’s or Diale’s.

What I could see though was still incredible.

The center was enormous.

So wide that it could probably fit several baseball diamonds. The air smelled sweet like pineapple.

Vast garden pavilions levitated within the opening spilling waterfalls that glittered like emeralds before vanishing into nothing.

Within the gardens people walked and laughed, eating and enjoying their day. In between the pavilions were saucers that varied in size depending on the group of people that it carried through the open air.

If I’d seen such a place before the world was inducted, I would’ve assumed that I’d died and gone to heaven.

I wanted to stay and explore, talk with people, learn.

Anything but keep moving.

But soon the Atrium was left behind, and I was being pulled through a sleek blue hallway lit by dim moving luminescent lights.

We were moving fast but upon closer inspection, I realized what I was really looking at. We were traveling through an aquarium, and the lights were actually fish. The blue seemed to go on forever making the fish seem like different colors of stars in the night sky.

Then the tunnel was behind us as well, and I was immersed in brilliant white light.

Zortana and Diale exited the tower and I was right.

We were far from the ground floor. So far from the ground in fact that when I looked down, I was literally able to see the curvature of whatever planet I’d been taken to.

I already knew I couldn’t be on earth, but if there were ever any sliver doubts, they would’ve been completely washed away.

Some towers extended all the way from the ground, way, way down below, all the way up to where I now floated many rising even higher. Most of the structures were built with the same burnt orange sandstone of the tower we just left behind.

In front of me Diale and Zortana floated together on one of the bronze saucers, however this one was a bit different. There were two highbacked chairs upholstered in sleek black that were being used to lounge comfortably.

As for me, I just floated through the air trailing the two of them while we weaved through the world defying towers.

We were far from being alone. Hundreds of thousands of people hovered on their own bronze saucers all around, while other people didn’t need them and used their own abilities to navigate the air space without the help of saucers.

Some moved so quickly I second guessed if I’d really seen them at all. That was until one blur came to a screeching halt just next to Diale’s and Zortana’s saucer.

A shirtless man made of chiseled muscle waved at them, they waved back and he landed gracefully on the saucer. Which seemed like no small feat considering we must be moving hundreds of miles per hour.

The three appeared to engage in casual conversation, but I was too far away to hear what was said.

However, I wasn’t too far away to see man tilt Diale’s head back and kiss her on the lips.

So she was into tall hansdome men with muscle? Typical.

Suddenly my interest in what was happening just in front of flatlined and I tiled my head towards space, which from how high we were, felt very, very close.

I gasped.

Suddenly realizing just how small I really was.

The stars blanketed the heavens in hues of green, purple, yellow and white. They were so close I felt like I could reach out my hand and pluck one out of the sky. In all my life, I’d never seen anything like it.

Space had always fascinated me. I’d lay out with my friends on the trampoline in the backyard and wonder what was out there.

Now I knew, and the answer was more than I ever could’ve imagined.

Then that too was ripped away and I found myself staring at a mosaic mural hundreds of feet above me.

The mosaic portrayed a man with thick red hair and a red beard on the back of a giant serpent.

The man wore a simple crown with a single pure white diamond at its center.

“Zortana!”

A man boomed.

I tore my eyes away from the mosaic to see that I was in the most extravagant throne room I’d ever seen.

Fifty-foot-long gold and white banners waved from high archways leading to a throne built for a giant.

The seat alone was taller than I was.

It wasn’t empty.

The red-haired man from mosaic lounged with one leg draped over an armrest leaning casually against the backrest.

In one hand he held a book, in the other a golden white orb that hovered just above his hand.

“I have a gift for you son.”

The man flashed a gleaming smile, and to my surprise, I realized I was smiling also.

“How would you like your own world?”
