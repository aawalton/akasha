Silence reigned.

The trees were still. The only sound was the heart-clenching drip… drip… of Royce’s blood.

Durst wouldn’t knock. And the townsfolk… it wouldn’t make sense for any of them to come all the way out here, in the midst of whatever chaos besieged Fellbrook, and just politely tap on our front door.

I held my breath, eyes transfixed on the door. I couldn’t seem to move.

The knock came again. Once, twice, three times… soft but insistent.

Perhaps it would think no one was home. Perhaps it would leave. Perhaps Durst would come and kill it, and help me, and know how to help Royce, and perhaps everything would be alright.

Drip. Drip. Drip.

I found myself pleading silently up at my friend. Please wake up. Please open your eyes. I’m so scared. I don’t know what to do. I don’t know how to save you.

My mother’s letter had said that I would be beautiful, clever, and brave. The thought twisted through my mind like a distant dream; here, trembling in a pool of my best friend’s blood, I felt quite the opposite of all those things.

“Excuse me.” The voice was muffled, deep, and horrifyingly unfamiliar.

The front door creaked open.

And there, framed by distant firelight, was a monster. But not the one I expected.

Every mortal knew that Fae were ghastly, inhuman creatures. Monstrosities formed of darkness and dread; the most wicked nightmares given shape.

This one… was not.

He didn’t tower in the entryway, blotting out every scrap of light. He didn’t spread great, leathery wings, didn’t bare dripping fangs beneath a face formed of gore-matted fur or razor-sharp scales. He wasn’t covered in the blood of innocents.

He was, by far, the most striking man I’d ever seen.

The Fae stood tall and broad-shouldered, robed in dark fabric that shimmered like a river in the night. A curtain of shoulder-length, raven-black hair framed moonlight-pale skin and eyes the color of starlight. Sharp cheekbones sliced down into a graceful jawline and full lips pursed with all the arrogant beauty of a lush rose.

And yet, despite looking so very human, there was no mistaking this creature for anything but a monster. He was… big. Too big. Not in size or shape, exactly, but in the sheer presence of him. The palpable weight that braced each stone wall as he stepped inside. It felt as if a great fist had closed around the very house.

I buried my face against the soaked crimson rags. I was going to die, right here in my kitchen. Slaughtered like a helpless animal. Not beautiful or clever, and certainly not brave.

I’m so sorry, Royce. I couldn’t save you. Mom, I’m sorry I wasn’t what you thought I’d be. Durst… please be okay. You have to be okay.

The monster spent a long while looking at me. And then, in a voice as smooth and rich as velvet, “I don’t mean to intrude, but I seem to have lost my dog.”

Silence crawled through the house. The phrase, such a shockingly mundane thing to say, made me turn in disbelief. “W-w-what?”

The Fae stepped further inside, examining every facet of the cramped kitchen. “I don’t mean to interrupt this… moment,” he waved a hand towards Royce, sculpted nose wrinkling, “you’re clearly having. It’s very touching, I’m sure. But I’m looking for my pet.”

I didn’t respond, other than to stifle another sob.

After some time he gave an irritated-sounding sigh. “He’s about this tall, likes to step in and out of the shadows, and… mm…” The Fae’s nostrils flared, his attention drifting towards the hallway. He pondered for a moment, then looked at Royce and added wryly, “Might be covered in blood.”

This was it, then. An otherworldly monster was going to execute me because I’d been party to the death of his pet. The wildness of the situation made me bold enough to stammer, “I’m… I’m sorry, y-your… it attacked us. W-we… it tried to kill us.” Every word spilled through numb lips and chattering teeth. My breath came in choppy gasps. “The… the trees… they… he’s outside.”

Perhaps I could run past him, to the safety of the witchwood. Perhaps they could skewer him just like the hound. But… that would mean leaving Royce.

I couldn’t leave Royce.

Two languid steps brought the Fae beside me. A floral smell filled my nose as he knelt. “He attacked you? Mortal, I don’t have time for half-truths. Try again.”

Blood roared through my ears. He was too close, too real, I couldn’t breathe… I closed my eyes. Turned my face away. Babbled. “That’s… that’s wh-what happened. He came into my room, the shadow, it was terrible and…. I’m sorry, we didn’t… m-mean for him to die. Please don’t… please don’t hurt us.”

Silence. I counted my final heartbeats.

“Hurt you?” Warm breath ruffled my hair as he chuckled. “How dour. I have a better idea. This… poor man is clearly very dear to you.”

I dared to look up. His voice was gentle. Mesmerizing in its richness, curling around me like smoke. And that perfect face, so close to mine...

I should recoil, I knew. I should be terrified. And deep down I was, but… he was so beautiful…

“Would you like me to save him?”

My heart stopped. “What?”

The rose-red mouth bloomed into an enticing smile. The teeth behind it were milk-white, straight, and perhaps just a bit sharp.

“Would you like me to save him?”

Those eyes were just a breath from mine. This close, I could see that they were not silver but rather an endless, depthless black, scattered with pinpricks of pale light. It was like gazing into a clear night sky adorned with stars.

Fathomless, beyond beautiful, and I was falling into them…

“You just need to help me find something,” he purred.

“Help… you?” My head swayed. The smell of roses filled my nose. My hands slid off of Royce and glass tore at my palms. Heat blossomed in my chest and I jerked backwards, feeling like I’d woken from a deep sleep.

“S-stay back!” I staggered back, raising my hands as if to ward him away. Blood dripped down my wrists.

He stood, mouth twisting down at the corners. “Curious. Is it a binding,” he spat the word like a curse, “That bolsters you?”

My back hit our pantry door. My head spun with the image of an endless night sky. “I… I don’t know what you’re t-talking about.”

He stepped towards me and for a moment I saw, as if out of the corner of one eye, shadows looming behind him, above him… shadows that swallowed up the moonlight, that stretched away forever and ever...

I gasped and they disappeared.

Behind him, Royce slumped fully out of the chair. He hit the floor with a heavy thud; a hollow sound that echoed off thick stone walls and rattled my very bones.

The Fae’s sculpted face grew thoughtful. “Perhaps my language is… inadequate. What term does your kind use? Pact? Covenant?”

I gave my head a wordless shake, unable to look away from Royce.

“Use words, human. Does that gesture mean you don’t know the term, or that it’s not your condition?”

“I… I don’t…” I sucked in a deep breath. He had said he could save Royce. Perhaps there was a way to help my friend, after all. “I’m sorry, I’m… p-please, you said you could help him… I’ll answer your questions if you… s-save him.”

My voice cracked. Hot tears dripped down my cheeks but I managed to meet the monster’s gaze, even managed to lift my chin a bit.

He seemed to consider my words.

“Please,” I whispered. “He’s my best friend.”

He took another step forward, and this time I held my ground. Full, red lips parted in something between a smile and a sneer; predatory and dangerous and very, very pleased.

“Aren’t you bold, human? Very well, we have a deal.”

Ice coiled through my insides. Snaked itself around my chest, squeezing… I fell to my knees with a cry.

The Fae crouched over Royce and rested both hands atop ragged, blood-weeping flesh. Moonlight spilled in through the windows, through the open door, bathing the kitchen in silver and washing over both figures.

The horrible, cold sensation stopped just as quickly as it had started. The monster stood and, with dizzy relief managing to swell past dread, I staggered to kneel beside my fallen friend.

He was fine.

Perfectly fine.

His trousers and shirt were flooded with crimson, but beneath the ruined fabric there was only the puckered white of scarred skin. It looked as if the awful wounds had healed years ago. His eyes remained closed, but his face was peaceful and his broad chest rose and fell in a steady rhythm.

“There,” a deep, vaguely amused voice rumbled over me. “He’s good as new. And now, to my questions.”

I rose and stepped away, swallowing the sudden lump in my throat. I hadn’t thought this far ahead. What if I simply didn’t have the answers to whatever it was he wanted to know? Would my life, and Royce’s, then be forfeit?

“I might not… know everything.”

He scoffed. “I hardly expect you to.”

“That wasn’t… I m-mean that I-”

“What Fae have you bargained with?”

My brows knotted together. I gnawed my lower lip, wondering if the question was somehow a trick or something I’d misunderstood, then offered uncertainly, “Well… um, you?”

He blinked. Then his expression darkened. “I am not amused, human.”

“I’m not trying to… I’ve never met a Fae before. At least, not before your… pet.”

Dark hair swayed down, dipping past one shoulder as the Fae tilted his head. “That’s not… hm. Mortal, you’re either astoundingly oblivious…” His eyes narrowed to glittering slits. “Or you’re a very good liar.”

I shivered under the weight of that look and the clear hostility lurking behind it. “I’m n-not lying. There… there haven’t been Fae here for years. Decades. M-my whole life. I’ve never m-met one of… of you.” The claim was met with frigid, unyielding quiet and a little snarl curling one corner of his mouth. My heart slammed against my chest. “I’m… I’m actually a very bad liar. R-really, I… I promise. You can ask… a-anyone. I’ve always b-been terrible at-”

“Be silent.” He tilted his head again, examining me much in the manner of a bird regarding a worm. Then, tone dripping disdain, he muttered, “Well, it can’t be generosity. Just look at you.”

I was vaguely aware that perhaps I should have been offended by the statement, but all I could manage was a squeak.

Brittle quiet snaked through the kitchen as he continued to stare. I started to twist my fingers together, a nervous habit I’d had since childhood, then flinched at the immediate flush of pain.

Finally he slipped one hand into a pocket and pulled out something sharp, golden-hued, and luminous with warm light. Something I’d seen countless times before.

I could even see chips of polished white rock still clinging to its edges. Had he ripped it from the statue in town? I shuddered at the thought of what terrible strength it would take to tear away part of the stone.

He examined the glowing weapon fragment, twisting it this way and that, making its edges catch the moonlight. Those otherworldly eyes glittered with a thousand pinpricks of gold. “This… relic of your quaint town’s history. I trust you know the story behind it?”

“Y-yes.”

“Good. Then the gravity of my words should not be lost on you when I say that history is about to repeat itself.”

I sucked in a sharp breath. Ashes, what did that mean?!

He prowled forward, attention fixed on my face. Curious and intent, searching for… something. “Why did my pet attack the other human?”

“Why… what does that have to do with history-?” I drew back, hands wringing at my nightdress. Another surge of pain. I whimpered.

“Explain the circumstances,” he rumbled, now only a breath away, “Around why my dutiful hound enacted such violence upon your friend.” His voice was low. Commanding. Steel beneath the velvet; the voice of someone who expected, and was perhaps always given, utter deference. “The truth, this time.”

I cringed back. “I… I already told you the truth. Your d-dog came into the house. Into my bedroom. My friend was trying to s-save me. And then the trees…” I shuddered at the memory.

He seemed to consider this, lips pursed. “He went into your bedchamber?”

“Yes.”

“Ah. And do you happen to have something… hidden away in your bedchamber? Something magical, perhaps?”

I blanched, immediately thinking of the runebook. My gaze fell to the sun-bright steel in his hand. Part of something that had killed a powerful Fae.

Was that it, then; why he was asking me strange questions instead of just slaughtering me? He’d said he was trying to find something. Something that might pose a threat? Was this horrible, blood-soaked night, just a… a scouting mission, of sorts? It made a terrifying kind of sense.

“No. N-no, there’s nothing in my room.” I lowered my lashes, heart hammering, and shook my head in earnest denial.

Silence settled like a frost. Then warm air ruffled my hair as the monster let out a little huff of laughter. “You were right, mortal. You are a poor liar.”

Too-sharp teeth flashed in a triumphant smile as he spun away. He took two steps towards the hallway and a lustrous silver arrow sank into his back.
