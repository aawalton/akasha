Despite the fact our visitors outnumbered us five to one, I could tell from their postures, they knew they were outmatched.

Nervous glances shifted between Nick, Daevon, Samson and some even landing on my dad who was looking exceptionally threatening in his new Armament.

As usual, I passed relatively unnoticed even though I stood at the head of the group. Maybe if I wore my helmet and extended my wings, I might draw more attention, but it didn’t bother me sliding in under the radar.

There was plenty of intimidation already taking place without my help.

Habitually, I made sure each member of my faction was covered in barriers, and we closed the distance to the rock where the monkey people waited. They were lean and tall but not quite the height of the dark elves unless you counted the extra few inches from their curved horns that wrapped behind their heads.

Now that we were closer, it was easy to see they were haggard.

Weary shoulders drooped like they were carrying heavy loads, eyes bloodshot, and fur matted with grime and filth.

“That's far enough.” One of the men cautioned revealing sharp canines. His tone wasn’t hostile, but it carried some force to it.

I inclined my head respectfully towards the one who spoke. He wasn’t especially tall compared to his comrades, and there was a heavy looking crossbow slung over his shoulder. His armor was ragged and in poor repair with large gashes that revealed whole flesh beneath, however the matted fur spoke or past injury.

“Interesting you should say that, considering you're the ones who’ve come to our borders.” I said, opening my arms to gesture towards the invisible lines separating the two territories.

“Fair.” The monkey man said with a polite bow. “But we mean no harm to you or your faction, we merely come to speak with your faction leader.”

“Here I am. What is it you’d like to talk with me about?”

I felt a warm buzzing in my chest, and I couldn’t help but feel a stitch of excitement. For the first time ever since the world changed, a faction had actually come to us without immediately trying to kill us.

The man's eyes narrowed ever so slightly at my revelation. I tried not to roll my eyes. Why was me being a faction leader always so hard to believe?

“Expecting someone else?”

“No, no.” He said, shaking his head, apparently surprised that I noticed his suspicion. “I had no expectations, just admiration for all your great faction has accomplished.”

“And what is it exactly that we’ve accomplished?”

I was pretty sure I already knew the answer, but I asked anyway just to confirm my theory.

“Surely you are aware of the region's leaderboards? You must watch the numbers same as us.”

Yep, it’s exactly what I expected.

I could only imagine what the factions in our region must be thinking as our numbers continued to skyrocket while theirs remained essentially stagnant in comparison. If I were in their shoes, I’d probably assume it was due to a massive influx in population. Which, to be fair, population increase was a part of our rapid climb.

But it wouldn’t be all that hard to piece things together somewhat. Numbers only increase when two things happen. Either the power level of your current faction members increases, or the number of faction members increases.

In our case, we had both.

Either way, that still begged the question. Why were they here?

If they’d done any scouting whatsoever, they would have to know ours was the only human faction in the region. Maybe they figured out that we'd allied ourselves with other species?

“I’m aware of the numbers. But what does have to do with why you're here now? Aren’t you afraid we may kill you?”

The monkey man's lip twitched and his men shifted uneasily.

“I believe if you were going to kill us, we’d already be dead.”

He had a point. If there was anything I’d learned about this world, it was that you killed first, talk later.

Or not at all.

“If you really feel that way, then it was quite a gamble to come here. You must have a pretty good reason.”

My hand twitched as the monkey took a step forward. But it wasn’t an attack.

In a single movement, the monkey man bowed his head low and knelt onto one knee.

“We’ve come to offer ourselves up as your slaves, in hopes that, in your mercy, you might spare our lives and the lives of our loved ones.”

At his words the others followed suit, all kneeling and bowing low.

My jaw dropped, and I looked dumfounded at my dad who I somehow knew was equally as baffled as I was despite only being able to see his eyes through his helmet.

Right on cue, a system message appeared in my field of vision.

A faction leader has submitted himself and his people as slaves to your faction. Should you accept their supplication, their people will become slaves within your faction until they die, are relinquished of the title of slave, or traded to another faction.

Will you accept their submission?

“What?! Are you kidding me? No. Absolutely not.” The message vanished from sight.

The monkey men bowed even lower.

“Great lord, we beg you to accept us!”

Another message appeared with the same words, and I refused it just as quickly.

“I already told you, the answer is no.” I looked up at the sky. A part of me knew it was silly to assume that the system was up there somewhere listening, but old habits die hard.

“We don’t have slaves!” I shouted, hoping the system got the message.

Whether the system did or not I wasn’t sure, but the horned monkey man definitely did not. He was to the point of groveling, prostrating himself until he was nearly flat against the dirt of the forest floor.

“Are we not fit?” His voice sounded muffled by the dirt and fallen pine needles.

I strode over.

“That’s enough of that.” With a firm but not aggressive grip I pulled the man from the dirt. “I just told you we don’t have slaves.”

He clambered to his feet and stumbled back, looking at the men behind me. The way he looked at them seemed like something wasn’t computing in his head. His mouth worked but nothing coherent came out at first.

Eventually, he managed words.

“You...but....you’re army?”

I looked back at my gathered soldiers. What about my army?

“Huh?” I asked, feeling more than a little confused.

Daevon’s deep voice shattered my confusion like breaking glass.

“He thinks that we are your slaves.”

My attention shifted back to the monkey man. He looked back at me. And I could see in his scrunched-up eyes Daevon was right. And the more I thought about it the more it made sense how the man had come to this conclusion.

If he was familiar with the system, how could the composition of people behind me be anything else?

“They are not my slaves. They are free members of Faction Layton Mischief.”

Behind the man, the other soldiers were stirring, removing themselves from their prone positions.

“And even if they weren’t, who offers themselves as slaves anyway?”

The man was only a few steps away and his head tilted up to look at something behind me, grass shifted and I sensed someone's presence.

“A beast with no other options.” Samson’s voice came out more like a low rumbling growl.

As usual, I hadn’t heard the large wolf man approaching. For being tall enough to be a starting NBA center and weighing at least three hundred pounds he moved as quiet as a mouse.

My head turned slightly towards him, but I kept my eyes forward.

“Is that true?”

The man's shoulders sagged.

“It is.”

“A rival faction?” I guessed.

The man studied me, then slowly, he shook his head.

“Mana Spawn?”

Again, he shook his head.

“What else is there?”

My heart sank. It wasn’t one of our people, was it? That was always possible. The scourge had created some seriously powerful individual members of our faction. It wouldn’t be all that unlikely one decided to stretch their legs and try their new power against a neighbor.

“She calls herself Stacy.”

Stacy?

That was a human name. My eyes narrowed. Did I know someone named Stacy? My mind raced trying to recall if there is someone in my faction with that name capable of terrorizing an entire faction on her own.

“Stacy?” I asked, keeping my voice steady while faces of faction members scrolled through my brain.

“We call her the one who smiles.” The man spoke it in almost a whisper.

My mind froze.

“A witch.”

I didn’t need to hear more. My hand moved reflexively to my chest and I’m taken back. Back to that day.

That day of pain.

That day I thought I’d never see my dad again.

That day I thought I was going to die.

My chest tightened, but I forced myself to breathe normally. There was still a chance that “Stacy”, might be different from the woman who cursed me in my fight with the cyclops. And yet, somehow, I knew it was her.

The woman who I remembered as the smiling soccer mom, was their witch. Fear coiled within my gut, conjuring fantom pains from my chest being crushed, of urine running down my leg, of searing agony while attempting to use my spells.

With practiced effort, I defied that fear and breathed it out in a calm, steady breath. I’d already come to grips with that day. I was alone then. Today, I had over twenty of my powerful friends and my dad standing at my side.

“This Witch, does she look like us?”

A nod was all it took to confirm what I’d already surmised.

“Will you tell me more?”

As if merely waiting for the invitation, the man began his story.

The monkey man's name was Tenor. His faction filled in the space where Cassie’s once started after the dungeons opened. It was the territory that was abandoned by Jordan’s faction and then again by us.

Their faction was led by a man named Badlan Gaborda, Tenor’s brother-in-law. For the past several months, their faction enjoyed a period of relative prosperity.

They were smart.

Capturing the unoccupied land allowed them to establish a base, where they brought through builders and crafters. While others fought, they prepared.

Once an outpost was established, they lured other factions to attack them at their fortifications, stacking victories and levels.

Everything was going according to their leader, Badlan’s plan. They were growing and even became a regional power. Their town flourished and so did their strength.

But that is where their good fortune ended. Badlan became greedy. He set his sights on our faction. He assumed that we’d fled to LM because our power wasn’t on par with their own. He assumed that it was our population that inflated our numbers as the regional leader.

His victories made him complacent.

“By the time we arrived at your borders we found your territories surrounded by an invisible system barrier blocking us from invading.”

By now, most everyone was gathered near enough to hear Tenor's story.

“Right, that would’ve been the scourge trial.” I muttered off hand, confirming the timeline.

Tenor gasped and the other men who heard muttered nervously. It was easy to tell from his gaping mouth that he wanted to question me, but perhaps he knew it was not his turn to ask questions, and he hesitantly moved on with his story.

“That was when, Badlan remembered that one of our scouting parties recently captured a woman while she was feasting on the corpse of a mangled mana spawn.”

I cringed at the image of the soccer mom hunched over some poor mangled corpse, blood dripping down her chin. The grotesque contradiction painted an unnatural image.

“Our scouts nearly killed her. If only they had.” He shook his head. “Instead, she was captured. Of course, the woman was questioned. That’s how we learned both her name and that her class was witch.”

His eyes took on a far-off look. As if he was watching the past play out on a screen.

“We didn’t learn much else though. She said little besides speaking of some unknown ally she called Zortana.”

“Zortana?” I asked.

Tenor shrugged. “We searched our territory but found no such person.”

I scratched my chin. “What else did the woman say?”

“She said she had to be freed, she had to find a boy, a boy who would join her. We assumed it was another human like herself.” He shrugged again and pointed at me. “Like you.”

“Layton, what if Stacy is the Soccer Mom?” Nick asked excitedly.

I shot him a stern look. “Why don’t we let Tenor finish his story?”

Tenor observed the exchange, thoughtfully. For a moment, he seemed on the verge of questioning me but chose instead to forge ahead with his story.

According to Tenor, when Badlan encountered the barrier enclosing our faction, he became impatient. He’d heard stories of the power of witches on their home world and believed it was possible they could use Stacy to force their way in.

Tenor was more reluctant. He’d also heard the stories.

He warned Badlan against using the witch, but the man insisted. Convinced that they could manipulate her by telling her the boy she was searching for would join her if she lowered the wall.

So, Tenor reluctantly returned to the hole they kept the woman in. A place with no windows or doors. A cave covered with a large boulder that took three of their men to move, that allowed just enough space for sufficient air to whistle through.

“So that’s what I told her. I said that if she helped us, we would give her the boy she spoke about while she slept.”

“And she believed you?” I asked.

“Whether she believed it or not, she agreed to come.”

They led Stacy to the wall, where Badlan and his army waited.

“Badlan asked the witch if she could use her strange magics to destroy the barrier.”

Tenor shivered.

“We should’ve known, you never give a witch that can see you time to speak. Badlan knew this, but his victories made him bold.”

“What happens when you let a witch speak?” Samson asked.

“For a witch to use her mana she needs two things. Visibility to her target, and time to speak the incantations. Like fools, we gave her both.”

If I wasn’t sure before, I was deadly sure now. The soccer mom, Stacy, must have been watching my fight with the cyclops from the safety of the trees. From there, she would’ve had everything she needed to use whatever curse she pleased.

“After two hours of chanting, Badlan got his reward.”

“But it wasn’t the barrier coming down, was it?” I asked.

Tenor shook his head. “It was not.”

“Then what?”

“Without warning, Badlan and several of his generals fell to the earth and began to scream and writhe.”

Some of the soldiers looked away, as though hearing the stories forced them to relive the painful memories.

“Fur and flesh sloughed from their bodies, revealing bubbling sinew. Their bones crackled and stretched, muscle bubbled, new flesh forming pink and steaming.”

I covered my mouth.

“By the time the transformation was complete, Badlan was gone. Replaced by an abomination.”

Tenor’s jaw clenched and he spoke the next words through clamped teeth.

“Then, the monsters attacked.”
