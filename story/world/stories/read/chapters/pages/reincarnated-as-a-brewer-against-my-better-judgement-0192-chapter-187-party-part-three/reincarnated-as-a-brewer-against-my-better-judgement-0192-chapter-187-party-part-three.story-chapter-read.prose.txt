By mid-afternoon, Alric’s stein had three lines. He had been here since mid-morning and was trying to pace himself. He did respect why Stromni had been so serious about breakfast first.

Rein had shown up earlier; Alric handed him the stein Seren had gotten for the headquarters. Rein turned it over, nodded, and thanked him. He’d been nursing that same beer ever since.

They stood at the edge of the muster yard, watching eight soldiers lose hopelessly in a tug of war. “Pull!” someone shouted.

The soldiers pulled; on the other side, a mixed group of adventurers braced; they lowered themselves.

“Come on!” Rein shouted. He lifted his tankard.

Rein’s men did their best, but they were pulling against a mountain; the soldier third in line slipped first. When his line dipped, the man behind him tripped over him. There was no recovery after that. Those behind the fallen men ended up in a heap; those in front took a short slide in the mud.

Alric looked away, chuckling, before he glanced at Rein. Rein was too busy watching his men execute a synchronized collapse into the mud to notice. The crowd, ever appreciative of other people’s suffering, responded with a cheer. Rein let out a long sigh, glancing down at the heap of his defeated men.

This group of adventurers had been undefeated since they started the tug of war. Currently, the adventurers were celebrating by doing slow, circular victory dances, approaching one another before doing big belly dongs with a hop.

“My boys are going to need extra training after this,” Rein said, closing his eyes and shaking his head.

Alric eyed the adventurers' group; each sported big bellies and big arms. “You might be a bit unfair, Rein,” Alric said, pointing. Rein followed his gaze and shrugged, “You are enjoying yourself though?”

“I am. This is a welcome surprise.” Rein said, pointing to the whole muster yard and then gesturing to his own soldiers, “Good food, good beer, and no one shouting at them. Well, except each other, but that doesn’t count.”

Alric grinned and looked across the muster yard. The party was going along nicely now. People danced in big groups scattered across the muster yard, and musicians followed, playing various instruments. People gathered around fires; some games were still going, but they were winding down.

Alric was watching one couple busy spinning each other around and laughing when he heard horses.

Four Crown guards rode in slow formation, flanking Calvern like particularly well-armed bookends. Calvern had already spotted the field; even from a distance, Alric could see a wide grin.

People began clearing the way for the horses when Calvern stopped them, shaking his head and waving a hand before stopping. He spoke to one of his guards briefly, who nodded. Three of the guards turned around, heading to the approach. Calvern dismounted and handed his reins to the last guard before approaching alone on foot and unguarded.

Calvern was still grinning and taking it in when he approached. Alric lifted his stein as he approached, “You came!” Alric called with a genuine smile.

“I did say I would,” Calvern said, raising both hands. He looked at Alric’s stein and the field behind him. “Not sure I would have missed it.”

Alric laughed; he was about to reply when Rein stepped forward and gave a deep bow.

“Lord Calvern, it would be my honor to assign some guards to you,” Rein said, still bowing.

Calvern's shoulders dropped. “Please, now, none of that.” Rein was about to protest before Calvern gestured to the field. “Captain, I suspect I’m safer here than I am in the palace.”

Rein opened his mouth, then closed it again.

“He’s not wrong,” Alric said with a shrug. Alric turned to his own guards, “Please get food to Calvern’s guards, then I just need two of you.” Alric’s guards nodded and began making arrangements.

“Surely not,” Rein said with a frown.

Alric shrugged. “Rein, there are seventy of your men within shouting distance, not to mention adventurers have been keeping people from bothering me all day.”

Rein just glanced over at the muster yard and shrugged.

Calvern nodded and pointed at another group. “Alric’s guards, your guards, Captain, and how many adventurers, Alric?” He paused as an adventurer wandered past, balancing a halberd on one shoulder, a pie in the other hand, and a tankard dangling from a spike. The man was clearly prepared for any emergency.

“Don’t think anyone’s counting, but it’s about two hundred adventurers, seventy soldiers and my own staff,” Alric said, looking around.

“So, call it three-hundred-armed people,” Calvern said, taking off a glove and glancing over at Alric. “You could probably invade a minor barony if you leave now.”

One adventurer slept under a table; two soldiers argued and pointed at different portions of food on a wooden board. Someone near a fire had apparently lost a boot, and someone was calling out for its owner.

“Would be a very slow invasion, much stumbling,” Alric commented, pointing to the adventurer who now had the boot on his head.

Calvern grinned and nodded. “So normal then?” Rein and Alric both laughed.

Another loud cheer rang out from the tug of war; Calvern laughed as the belly dong procession began their victory dance.

Rein took a long drink, then shrugged, “Fine.”

Calvern still chuckled at the belly dongs, then pointed to the center, “Is that where the beer is?”

“It is, but first I have something for you,” Alric said. He pulled out the last stein from his item box and handed it over.

Calvern accepted it. He turned it over, tested the lid with his thumb, and nodded. “I wasn’t expecting a souvenir, but thank you. What are those?” Calvern said, pointing to Alric’s chalk marks.

Alric rolled his eyes as he explained whilst walking to the beer wagon. Calvern smiled and glanced around. Alric could see the smile all the way into his eyes; it was genuine.

As they reached the beer wagon, Calvern spotted Pavise. “Pavise!” Calvern called.

“Calvern! You made it?” Pavise said, approaching and shaking hands.

“None of you have any faith. I did say I would. Now. I have a question.” Calvern said, standing more upright. Pavise took Calvern’s stein, passed it to a beer pourer over the heads, and got it filled.

Calvern took a small sip first, before he eyed out the stein. “Oh, that’s bitter, but good. I know everything you make is dangerous though.” Calvern said, glancing at Alric.

Pavise nodded enthusiastically and pointed at Alric, “His cider is the most dangerous.”

Alric just shrugged. “Not my fault you two can't handle the booze,” Alric said and quickly realized his mistake as their faces shrank.

“You do not make a claim like that unchallenged,” Calvern said, turning serious. He turned to the nearby people, “Does anyone here think they can out down someone of such great lineage as myself?” Calvern called out to the crowd.

Many hands shot up. “Oh? That many?” They quickly arranged the drinking competition in a small circle, Alric and Pavise having to finish their current beers before getting refills.

“On three!” Came the nearby voice from one of the beer pourers. “One, two… and… three!”

Alric had already resigned himself to only drinking half, which he accomplished with the efficiency of a man who knows his limits. Calvern, meanwhile, was still valiantly struggling through his, while adventurers, soldiers, and two carters held up empty tankards in silent, gloating victory.

Calvern lowered his half-drunk stein, breathing heavily. Some of the beer ran down his coat. “Well. That was humiliating!”

That got a laugh and roar of approval from Calvern, who went on to finish the other half of his beer; he handed it to Pavise, who had barely tried for a refill.

When Alric glanced around, he noticed the crowd had loosened up. Calvern stepped away from the wagon and got pulled into a conversation with nearby adventurers. Someone patted him on the back. It had taken a year for Alric to establish himself among the people here; it had taken Calvern just one failed drinking contest. He couldn’t help the pang of jealousy.

“So, what do you do then?” one of the adventurers asked Calvern.

Calvern leaned back slightly and considered the sky for a moment. “All the paperwork the king doesn’t want to do.” Which got another laugh.

Alric grinned; he suspected the truth was more complicated, but he hadn’t lied. The conversation drifted to Calvern’s origins, only to be derailed by a story about a farmer losing a wagon of potatoes in the river. Calvern listened carefully and nodded at all the right times. He countered the story with one of a large noblewoman whose carriage had fallen into a cold stream. The noblewoman’s guards had to lift her out of the carriage by her underwear, which got a loud laugh from the listeners.

One of Alric’s guards retrieved a full tankard, now with a second mark on it for Calvern. He glanced around for Pavise, who had apparently been called away.

By the time Alric finished his beer, Calvern was standing near a fire, someone had handed him a skewer, and people were laughing again.

“How does he do it?” Rein asked, watching. Alric hadn’t realized Rein had followed.

“I don’t know, and if I’m honest, I’m a bit jealous,” Alric said, gesturing at Calvern, who was now holding court by the fire. Rein nodded. “Think he’s enjoying himself?”

“No doubt.” Rein said with a shrug, “I need a refill.” He said, nodding to Alric.

“Alric!” Calvern called, gesturing him over. Alric could only shake his head and approach. “There is a thing of great importance. Apparently, nearly all the inns have roast pork, and I am deeply partial to it.”

Alric dropped his head; his own guards nearby started laughing.

“That wasn’t that funny?” Calvern said, eyeing them out as if they were being mean.

Alric shook his head; he hadn’t eaten in a while. “We had this same argument about sausages this morning.” Alric gestured to the inn wagons. “Let's go then. It looks like our table is still fine.”

Everywhere they went, Calvern had people laughing. He refused to be treated differently and got a small slice of roast pork from each of the stalls. Alric made a beeline for peppered beef stew and some roast pork from Berrin.

Alric had just arrived back at the table when Calvern joined him.

“Right, tell me about this sausage competition. I need the details. Might be a matter of royal concern.” Calvern said, rubbing his hands together and grinning at the many slices of roast pork.

Alric glanced back at the inns. “The eventual result was that Denner of the three barrels had the best sausage. It was, however, heavily contested whether his use of pepper and herbs was cheating.” Alric said, using a slightly snootier air.

That earned a proper belly laugh from Calvern, who seemed to appreciate the gravity of sausage politics.

“How many of the pigs are you planning to try?” Alric asked as Calvern took a slice on a wooden fork.

“All of them. This is a serious matter. As the host, you should take this matter seriously.” Calvern said after swallowing and nodding down at his board.

Alric scoffed and took a sip of beer. “Pavise is the host. I just brought the beer.” He said, gesturing vaguely to the field.

“Oh, dear. Alric, it pains me, but I shall have to report to his majesty that you failed to consider culinary matters when throwing a party.” He said with a mock sigh, and he speared another piece of roast pork.

That comment got a proper laugh out of one of Alric’s guards, who had to retreat before he choked on his own amusement. Calvern grinned.

Alric paused, watching Calvern closely. “How do you do this? I never knew you had such a side.”

Calvern smirked. “It's not every day I can come to something like this and nothing I say matters.” He said, gesturing to a group of adventurers nearby who were having piggyback races. “I regret not coming earlier.”

Alric took another sip of beer. He glanced across the field and locked eyes with Tarrow; the pair exchanged waves. Tarrow seemed to be going to different groups, drinking with them.

“Come on, Alric, get serious about this,” Calvern said, pushing his board between them.

Alric rolled his eyes. After a day of eating heavily and snacking irresponsibly, he found himself debating human nature over the merits of roast pork with the King’s spymaster.

After discussing the ins and outs of their third piece of roast pork, Calvern glanced around. “There is something I wanted to talk to you about. Do you mind?” Calvern asked, glancing at Alric’s guards.

Alric nodded, glancing at his guards, who also nodded and moved away a bit. “What’s the issue?” Alric asked, intrigued.

“Do you know that you are apparently a lich?” Calvern asked, keeping a small grin, “Or you are the king's secret nephew. No one is quite sure.” He took a small sip of beer.

“The king's nephew?” Alric asked with a wince.

Calvern nearly spat out the beer, holding in his laugh, “That’s what you have an exception with?”

Alric just shrugged, “What’s this about?”

“Rumors, the entire noble’s quarter is abuzz with rumors about you,” Calvern explained. He held up a hand and started counting, “You are an ancient lich using forgotten beer brewing techniques. It’s how you have so much mana.” He held up one finger. “You are the king's secret nephew.” Second finger, “You have bewitched the king.” And third finger, “Oh, and you are a dragon. Even I think that one is weird.” He said, raising a fourth finger and waving it away.

Alric rolled his eyes. “All absurd. I’d never claim relation to the king. Nephew is weak too. I’d go for cousin.” Alric said, using his stein to gesture toward the palace.

Calvern was about to speak before the caber, turning end over end, caught their attention as it flew, and they paused.

“Still, I need to ask a favor. It’s not something you need to worry about, but I do want you to avoid court or the noble’s quarter for a while.” Calvern said, watching Alric carefully.

Alric frowned, then shrugged. “I think you have misjudged me on that, Calvern. I want as little to do with nobles as I can get away with.”

Calvern blinked rapidly. “You wound me, Alric. What do you have against Nobles?” Calvern asked.

Alric took a sip of beer to buy time. “No offense, but you guys complicate everything. Nothing is easier because a noble is involved. You guys make more obstacles than you solve, all because someone’s grandfather was impressive.” Alric explained as he pulled his lips to the side, looking at Calvern.

“I see. I can see where you're coming from. It’s true.” Calvern said, glancing back at the party; he watched for a while. “Most of us aren’t important, though, just so you know.”

Alric stayed silent, giving Calvern room to speak.

“You say you envy me, but I also envy you. I am disposable. It’s right in my job, in fact.” Calvern said, tapping his beer stein. “Unless you are the count, the head of the house, you are not important. Even then you are subject to the whims of those above you.” He drew a line with his finger in the air in front of him with a frown.

Alric nodded, “Doesn’t make me want to get involved, you know?”

“I know, but still, you have far more fun. This would be such a dull affair if it were a noble's event.” Calvern said, gesturing to the party, as if on cue, a huge cheer rang out somewhere.

Alric grinned; he wasn’t sure how much credit to take. “Should I be worried about your warning about court? Be honest.”

Calvern shook his head. “No, well probably not. I can’t say much, but the King, Queen, and I are testing something.”

Alric glanced at the walls. “So, nobles’ intrigue and plotting then. You don’t sell the idea of getting involved with nobles, you know?”

Calvern followed his gaze and frowned; even from this distance, it was clear the walls around the poorer parts of the city were not well maintained.

“Can you at least consider me a non-noble?” Calvern asked, tilting his head.

“Not a chance,” Alric replied quickly; he hadn’t quite forgiven Calvern for eating his grilled cheese sandwich in front of him.

Calvern pouted for a moment before he laughed, remembering the queen’s pout. Calvern quickly launched into the story, and they both chuckled.

The sun began its slow descent toward the horizon; somehow, everyone knew the drinking would soon turn more serious. Alric glanced at his four lines.
