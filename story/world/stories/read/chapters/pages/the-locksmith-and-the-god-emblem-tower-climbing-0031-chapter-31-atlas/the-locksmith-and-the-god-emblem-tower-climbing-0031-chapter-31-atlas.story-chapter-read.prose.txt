After a few seconds as he gave paid Petrikov a tribute, Harry decided to come back, judging maybe a few minutes before the mountain closes in. It seemed to fall slowly, but the young thief knew that it had already traversed miles.

He picked up Vela's candelabrum. Harry knew he'd go back with it anyway at the start of the loop, just like Harry and his previous journal. Just by his mind's mention of it he grimaced.

A thief who steals from a thief gets 100 years of forgiveness... He chuckled softly as he considered the absurdity. Harry never considered himself the best of people; he abandoned those he loved and actively worked as a cheat in pursuit of fame and knowledge. Although he didn't do the latter solely out of necessity; his goal was always to be thrown into the Logos Tower. In any case, Prism truly earned a place in his heart. One very cold and distant.

There's a certain moral code, a certain law of men. Common sense, one might say. What separated a good person from a horrible one wasn't the absence of it, but rather fragility. At the first moment of weakness, many would jump ship without a second thought. Prism was one of those.

Talking to himself, Harry jumped quickly as he approached the top, the place where the exit was supposedly located. He looked around, eagerly searching for any sign of Vela, Madeline, and the others. Maybe they've all already left, or maybe... Before he could reach another conclusion, everything shook.

"What the hell...!" Harry shouted as he lost his balance even in mid-air. If it was an earthquake, something originating from the mountain, simply staying on top of one of its doors in the air would prevent him from falling. It was the floor... He then suddenly looked to the side, almost like a sixth sense. He squinted and soon saw a crack, albeit tiny, rapidly expanding on the horizon.

The Locksmith looked up, curious to see the state of the mountain, and almost felt his heart stop. Something had made the inverted mountain unstuck. It plummeted from the sky at an absurd speed, almost as if it were no longer dragging itself along the walls of the floor. Stones the size of a tavern were falling from the sky, a torrent of death raining down on Harry.

He spotted the top and ran towards it, leaping through the space and dodging as much as he could. No matter how fast he went, everything seemed to get further away. No, not now... His mind was spinning. Was it the effects of the blow Prism had given him, or something else? They say that strong enough emotional damage has effects on the body. Harry felt both his head and his heart tighten, an invisible force preventing him from acting quickly.

From the outside, Harry looked like a defeated fly, flying erratically through the sky. Shit, shit, shit... He looked at the flag and then noticed the colossal object approaching the young thief. He almost gave up right there.

Before he could be overwhelmed by pain and confusion, he locked eyes with a man with blond hair and steel plates around his body. The man, who until then had a somber expression, lit up when he saw Harry and his doors. He shouted something Harry couldn't hear, but it didn't matter; The man's determination was contagious.

He remembered his mother and brother. He thought of Devon and how much he blamed himself for the librarian's death. He looked at Vela's candelabrum and was also reminded of Petrikov. Holy shit that hurts... His face contorted as he held back vomit.

"...Harry!" He could hear Goldman's cries. "By the gods! I thought you were dead. What are you doing with Vela's candelabrum? He's gone, you can throw it away. He gave it to..."

The realization was clear on Goldman's expression as he connected the dots. He looked around for Petrikov, but there was no sign of the professor. He remained silent for a few moments and looked up, the mountain almost colliding with him. Harry, despite jumping the gap between the summit and himself, wouldn't make it in time.

"...Fuck it." Goldman said, cracking his knuckles.

Realizing that he didn't have enough time for the Locksmith to reach the hatch, Goldman created a polar field on the soles of his metal boots and another on the uneven mountain ground. As a result, he threw himself with terrifying speed, positioning himself precisely between the two mountains, his body becoming a bridge. His eyes met Harry's once more, and upon seeing the pirate, the young thief became even more confused.

What is he doing? He squinted, struggling to create his doors and cover the path. He made them in just a few meters, however, and because of this, in addition to his crooked trajectory, he was taking longer.

Plummeting as if representing a stake of worldly proportions, the mountain reached its point of contact. Goldman then created a polar field exactly at the tip of the inverted mountain and another on the mountain below it. As if caught in a hydraulic press, Goldman immediately began writhing and screaming in pain.

He was pulled as the two mountains drew closer. At the same moment, a gust of snow covered Harry's entire vision. The wind, like the kick of a horse, pushed Harry several meters back, almost making him fall to the ground again as he lost Vela's candelabrum. As when they reached the floor, he was surrounded by snow that got in his way and obstructed his vision. He felt a sudden relief, as if the gust of wind had freed him from something. The nausea quickly subsided, and Harry remained steady. It hadn't been the wind, however; Harry was simply experiencing the famous calm before the storm. When the pain and confusion returned, they would return twofold.

After a few seconds wandering through the snow like a vagabond, without the slightest idea where he was going, out of the corner of his eye, he saw a light contaminating the area. As if he were seeing a mythological creature, Harry was mesmerized by the scene.

Between the two mountains, a hole remained. A hole the size of a man. Goldman, now smiling, stood between the two mountains, preventing them from colliding. The Locksmith, seeing this, lost his breath for a second. Around him, as if in procession, the snow swirled around Goldman.

Instinctively he wondered how he was doing it, but seeing the movement of his hands, he soon understood. "Goldman!" he cried from the bottom of his heart, tears streaming down his face. Not only for Petrikov, nor for his mistakes, but for this moment. He felt like he was in a dream. The wind swirled around him like a small hurricane whose eye represented Goldman.

When their eyes met for a third time, the gentle giant chuckled. A smile spread across both cheeks as he yelled for some reason. Harry, caught up in the emotion of the moment, started yelling at the snow and lost himself in laughter.

"Fuckin' hell!" Harry lost his glasses, but didn't even blink as they disappeared into the snow. "I didn't know your Emblem could do that! Can't you just throw yourself on the ground and do it remotely?"

He chuckled again before replying. "Me neither!" He looked up and down. "But I knew that the shorter the distance, the greater the force. I believe I could, but I fear that this small human determination is what's preventing them from touching!"

As soon as he said this, the floor swayed once more, almost causing Goldman to lose his concentration; the pain in his shoulder was still there, throbbing. Despite this, the smile on his face didn't disappear. Harry, feeling this, now no longer as disgusted as before, quickly realized that the floor was about to collapse in on itself. He remembered the cracks and imagined that his time was almost up.

Near the flag, a small hatch hid among the snow. Almost forgotten, it had been closed, possibly due to the wind. Before immediately descending, he used one of its doors to get close to Goldman.

"No!" He then shouted without raising a hand, and Harry stopped. "Don't come any closer! I don't know how much longer I can hold out... I don't want you to get hurt!"

"But the hatch is directly below you!" Harry shouted, pointing downwards.

"...Damn it, that's right." He let out another laugh as he turned to the side and noticed the wooden hatch. "I guess I didn't think that far into my plan. I just saw that you wouldn't make it in time and tried to buy some time. Argh..."

His teeth still showing, he held on to avoid collapsing under the pressure of the mountain.

"By doing that," Harry began. "You killed yourself, Goldman. It didn't have to be like this! Petrikov—"

"He's dead." The legendary pirate continued for Harry, a weary tone in his voice. "I know. As soon as I saw Vela's chandelier and not Petrikov himself, I immediately knew. We've already lost Petrikov. If we lost you, I..."

"...Did you know he was inspired by you?" Harry said with a smile. "When Prism left me to die, he was the one who came back, almost dead, to rescue me. If it weren't for Petrikov, I wouldn't even be here. Conversely, you could have already gone up to the next floor... Or down, in that case." He chuckled slightly.

"But of course not!" Goldman returned to his determined manner. "Until I was sure you were dead, I wouldn't do anything differently... Wait, you said Prism left you to die?"

"Long story." Harry said, changing the subject. "The important thing is that—"

Then an idea appeared in Harry's mind. One that would drain all his strength. Perhaps an idea even crazier than Goldman's. Seeing the man, who using his Emblem managed to hold up two mountains, he became curious about what he could do with a door large enough. It wasn't a competition, of course; it was genuine curiosity. Besides, if he did nothing, they would both die here.

"I have an idea," he said, cracking his neck and adjusting his tie, which, miraculously, remained intact. "But I need you to trust me."

Seeing Harry's face, Goldman looked around almost as if that were answer enough. "Do you think I wouldn't do all this if I didn't trust you?"

Harry, in response, only nodded and began to mentally prepare himself. Holy shit. Holy shit. I'm screwed. I've always dreamed of this kind of situation, but now that I'm in it, I can say it's truly terrifying. Okay. I can do it. I can do it.

"I believe I can hold the mountain long enough before the door breaks. I don't know what will happen if the pressure is greater than my Emblem's capacity. Perhaps, if someone were there, they could be cut in half."

"By the gods, Harry! That's morbid!"

"I know, I know..." He laughed at himself. "Here's the deal; I want you to, at my signal, close your two camps and use one of them to forcefully throw yourself down. Can you do that? I imagine that, where you are, if you do it backwards, you'll only land in a handful of snow."

"Of course I can, Locksmith..." Goldman then said worriedly. "But are you sure about this? If it goes wrong, we'll both be buried before we reach the next floor. The mountains will collide almost instantly."

"That's why I have to be quick." Harry replied, rubbing his hands together. "You'll land in the snow, I'll do what I have to do, and then we'll both go straight in there, okay? It would be kind of you to leave it open for me..."

"Right." Goldman then chuckled again before sighing deeply, the weight of the mountain clearly reaching him. "I'm ready."

Okay, here we go. I can do it. I can do it.

He imagined the size and structure of the door he would make, and which direction he would place the other one. He had to make the passage almost instantaneous, and so he had already created another gigantic door right behind him. The Locksmith wasn't afraid it would open the wrong way, since the directions and mechanisms of where the door would open or close were instinctive. He couldn't make the door open inwards, since it would hit the mountain, so he defined that for the other door.

Now, more than ever, he hoped his physical capacity would be enough to withstand the power of his own Emblem. It would be a shame to be exhausted before reaching the fourth floor. Goldman seemed to be holding up, but Harry believed it was just adrenaline, and as soon as they reached the next floor, they would be forced to rest.

The young thief closed his eyes for a second. He felt the wind on his cheeks once more and the snow brushing against his long hair. His glasses were gone, but for the first moment in his life, it seemed like Harry was seeing everything.

If it goes wrong, it goes wrong. Life is absurd, isn't it? He laughed to himself. I can do it. I can do it.

"Now!" He shouted with all the fervor held in his heart. Goldman, following his order, threw himself to the ground with all his might, leaving everything in Harry's hands. At that moment, the two mountains came closer by a few centimeters. During that time, however, almost like a force of nature, a door that had already appeared opening was placed between the two.

"SHIT. I CAN'T DO IT!" He shouted as soon as he created the door, giving Goldman the biggest fright of his life.

Holding on to avoid being thrown again, Harry felt a third jolt, fearing it would be the last. It was the combination of the near collision of the two mountains and the near total collapse of the floor, which was always on the verge of breaking. When he opened his eyes to see the result, he looked back and saw a small piece of earth desperately trying to escape from the other door. It was about to crack, however, and before he could throw himself towards the hatch, he heard his own door creaking.

I guess that means the weight of a mountain is the limit...

He lunged at the hatch Goldman had left open and fell. A second later, the hatch slammed shut and Harry heard a bang of planetary proportions. Perhaps it was the adrenaline that was keeping him alive, but as soon as he realized he had put, even if only a tiny piece, of a mountain through one of its doors, his body and mind collapsed.

Is that what mountain climbers have to do on a daily basis? He thought as he fell.
