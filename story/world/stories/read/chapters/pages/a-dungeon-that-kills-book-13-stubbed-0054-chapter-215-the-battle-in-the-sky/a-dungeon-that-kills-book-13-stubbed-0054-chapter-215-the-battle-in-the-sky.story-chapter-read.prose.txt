Hundreds of platforms hung in the air above the cathedral, great slabs of stone torn from the structure’s own walls and roofs and towers, kept aloft by the Tengu’s magic, undoubtedly the same trick they had once used to drop that fake building on top of Dagnar. Galatea probably wouldn’t thank them for treating her sacred house with such indignity, but it couldn’t be helped. War didn’t much care whose temple it borrowed, and the place was going to suffer heavy damage in the upcoming fight anyway. So they might as well get some use from the wreckage first.

Viktor had picked one of the larger blocks to conceal himself. The rock was hollow within, and it was here that he settled himself down, letting his back rest against the uneven surface. He sat alone, accompanied by the one thing that was so desperately scarce in this Domain of Light.

Shade, of course.

Yes, he needed it. He needed enough of it to gather round him, to coil close and form his dark veil when the time came.

Closing his eyes, he watched the platform where the stairs came down from the third floor. The Apostle could arrive at any moment.

And sure enough, here he came.

The gray-haired man descended from above, wings spread wide enough to swallow the blinding white sky. He dove like a falcon, plunging straight toward the cathedral’s great doors.

The Tengu rose to meet him.

They came from every direction, a black-feathered storm converging on the diving Apostle. The metallic rings on their staffs sang as they closed in, strike after strike aimed at his wings, his neck, his knees. The aerial duel was a blur of motion, the bird-men darting in and out with the grace of creatures born to the sky, while the Apostle twisted and spun, using his wings to pivot, his hands to block and parry.

Until he put a hand to his back and pulled his own spine out.

Vertebra by vertebra, the gray-haired man tore it free. They kept coming out, as if he had an endless supply of them, and by the time he was done, he now had at hand a monstrous whip much longer than anyone’s back had any right to hold. Bone cracked through the air as he swung his macabre weapon, impossibly long, impossibly fast, impossibly precise. The Tengu were now on the defensive, frantically trying to evade that horrifying serpent.

It caught one of them anyway. In an instant, the whip of bone coiled around the torso of the luckless Tengu in a tightening spiral. The bird-man let out a sharp, startled cry as the vertebrae cinched shut, pinning his arms to his sides, snapping his wings flat against his body.

Then the flesh bloomed.

From inside the whip, pink tissue seeped out between the bone segments in glistening threads. It spread across the Tengu’s body, his stomach, his chest, his face. The bird-man thrashed, battering helplessly against the cocoon of new-grown meat, his beak opening to shriek, but the flesh poured into his mouth, muffling the sound, filling the throat. His talons scraped at the encroaching mass, tearing strips of it away, but more grew back in an instant, thicker and hungrier. His feathers dissolved into the pink tide, one by one, and then all at once. His legs kicked a while longer, before his entire form was swallowed, absorbed into a pulsing, featureless lump that throbbed once, twice, and then went still.

The other Tengu did not stay idle, of course. Some went for their trapped kin, trying to claw him free. Others went for the Apostle himself, hoping that they could force him to release his prey. But the whip never slowed. The gray-haired man kept it moving in long, easy arcs, all the while continuing to eat the one he had already caught.

Suddenly, a large gash tore open across the Apostle’s chest, and at the same time, a handful of arrows hissed past his face for good measure. The ground forces had joined the battle, a tide of silver and scale surging up from the cathedral below to meet their opponent in the open sky.

Well, yes.

Sebekton and the Templars were jumping from one floating platform to another to get close to the Apostle, which was exactly why Viktor had set things up this way in the first place. The Crocodilian swung his axe in great sweeping arcs, sending crescent after crescent of invisible projectile out ahead of him, his legs never once slowing as they drove him forward in great bounding strides. Behind him, the silver-armored soldiers loosed volleys of arrows from their great bows, which probably wouldn’t achieve much on their own, but damage is damage, and enough of it added up eventually.

As the wound on his chest sealed over within seconds, the Apostle raised his left arm. It twisted and, in the span of a heartbeat, transformed into a crossbow of bone and sinew. He fired rapidly as the string moved on its own instead of requiring him to pull it, bone arrows growing from inside the limb before being launched away in quick succession.

Sebekton spun and dodged under the barrage, his axe smashing aside what he couldn’t evade. The Apostle then turned his attention to the Templars instead, and this time, his attacks were far more effective. Each time a projectile found its mark, a silver-armored soldier was knocked off his platform, tumbling back down to the cathedral below. Clearly, these bone arrows had a great deal more weight behind them than their size suggested.

The Apostle switched back and forth, between shooting Sebekton to suppress him before he could launch his own attack, and shooting the Templars to blast them out of the sky, never settling on one target long enough to let the others press their advantage. All the while, the whip in his right hand never stopped its work, coiling and feeding on any Tengu who made the mistake of getting too close.

It was time.

With the Apostle kept busy by his soldiers, there was no better moment to strike. Viktor hurled himself from the hollow block, the dark veil already wrapped around him. It would not last long up here, though, not in a place where blinding light poured in from every direction like this. In other words, he couldn’t afford a prolonged engagement.

He flew toward the Apostle from behind, closing the distance within a heartbeat. His fingers found the hilt of his rapier, and the blade flared to life as he drew it clear of its sheath. He brought it down, an arc of radiance, aimed for the gray-haired man’s neck. And—

A third arm grew from his back, right between the bases of his wings, and in its grip was that same bone sword the man had wielded earlier at the Chamber of the Dead.

The two blades clashed, and the sword of bone gave ground. Viktor didn’t waste the moment feeling pleased with himself. He followed through with a second strike that cut off both the third arm and the right wing with one clean arc.

The Apostle fell.

As he turned to gaze up at Viktor, for the briefest of moments, there was a flicker of genuine surprise in his eyes. Didn’t see that coming, did you, you bastard? Viktor allowed himself a grin. Good thing he had never used this rapier in front of the masked men, keeping it a secret until now, hoarding this one advantage precisely for a moment like this.

But of course, that was not enough to win. The Apostle had already regenerated his wing, and the whip in his right hand now came at Viktor in a relentless series of strikes that demanded his full attention to parry. He swung his blade to fend off the attacks as he flew backward to put distance between them. Meanwhile, Sebekton and the Tengu pressed their assault on the Apostle to cover his retreat.

Viktor made for a different hollow block and quickly got himself inside, out of the light. The shadow here would help his dark veil last a bit longer.

The first engagement had gone about as well as he could have hoped. He had dealt some damage, yes, but more importantly, he had learned how much his rapier could hurt that man.

In theory, the blade of light could cut through everything, but in practice, there were things tough enough to withstand it. Other Reliquaries, for example. Those artifacts were extremely hard to destroy, after all. However, a Reliquary’s actual durability depended greatly on the material from which it had originally been created. Sebekton’s steel axe had proven that much. The blade of light couldn’t damage it, at least not with one single blow. On the other hand, against a Reliquary made from cloth or wood, it was possible for the blade to destroy it outright.

And the earlier little skirmish had shown that even though Viktor couldn’t cut through the bone sword and the bone whip, he could take an arm and a wing off the man without much difficulty. In other words, the blade of light could damage the less protected parts of the Apostle’s body, while struggling to do so with the parts he chose to reinforce.

Valuable information, indeed, but it came with a cost. The bastard had learned something too. Now that he knew about the rapier, the next time he would be better prepared. But it was fine. Viktor had already accounted for that when he made this plan.

He then waited for the next opportunity to strike, which wasn’t long in coming, since his soldiers were doing everything they could to keep the Apostle occupied. Two more Tengu had fallen in the process, but losses were to be expected against an opponent of this caliber. Viktor would not let their sacrifice go to waste. And so, he launched the second attack.

Only this time, he was not the one who went out. At least, not yet.

As it happened, he wasn’t actually alone inside this rock. Instead, he had a friend who sat with him the entire time. The guy was simply not very talkative. Well, corpses tended not to.

Yes, it was a dead body Celeste had brought here from the first floor, a golden ringed cross already planted within its torso. Now Viktor cast upon it a modified version of “Dark Veil,” one that allowed him to cloak someone else in the shroud of darkness instead of himself. Thus, when the corpse flew out, no one would be able to tell it was not him.

Closing his eyes, he woke the dead man. He made it leave the rock, then approach Apostle from behind just as before. As soon as he drew near, the gray-haired man immediately reacted. This time, ten arms burst out instead of one, long and reaching. Webbing stretched between them, forming a great sheet of flesh wide enough to swallow a man whole. And it did exactly that.

Viktor chuckled as he switched back to his normal vision. The Apostle must have believed he had caught him. Best of all, he didn’t tell his subordinates about this particular part of the plan, so now they panicked, throwing themselves at the enemy in a desperate attempt to free him, which made the act even more convincing.

Despite their effort, the cocoon of flesh retracted into the gray-haired man’s body all the same.

And, a second later, he exploded.

Viktor had detonated the bomb inside the corpse, of course.

The Apostle came apart in a rather spectacular fashion. Head and limbs and organs were flung away in every direction all at once.

Or they should have been.

Unfortunately, reality was every bit as horrifying as it was disappointing. Scraps of the gray-haired man—countless of them—had been caught, dangling suspended in a web of flesh strung between the wreckage, and that web was now hauling the pieces back to reassemble the body.

Sebekton must have been informed by Celeste about what had happened by now, for he was already swinging his axe, crescent after crescent tearing at the ravaged body that was trying to knit itself back together. Viktor doubted that would be enough to kill the Apostle, but at least it would slow the regeneration down.

Let’s go, then.

He drew his rapier from its sheath, and then he let the scabbard fall. Because he no longer needed it.

Time to use my new technique.
