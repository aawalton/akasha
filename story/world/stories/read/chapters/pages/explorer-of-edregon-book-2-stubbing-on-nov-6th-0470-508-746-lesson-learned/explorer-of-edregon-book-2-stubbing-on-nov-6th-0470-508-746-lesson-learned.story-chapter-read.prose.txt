Unfortunately, while Harmstead proved to be a bustling town filled with all sorts of incredible people, to Vin’s surprise, they were lacking in mages almost entirely. The sole exception was a small handful of Healers who utilized life magic in conjunction with their skills to patch warriors up and keep everyone healthy, but other than that, there wasn’t anyone for Vin to learn magic from.

Still, he would happily take whatever he could get.

“And there you go,” the Healer said. The kind, older minotaur smiled as he finished sketching out the runic formation for Invigorate in the air between them. “I appreciate all the help you were with collecting the materials we needed for these potions. Not a lot of people are willing to venture so far north above the town. Even our strongest of warriors fear fighting against the frost bringers.”

“Always happy to help,” Vin said, returning the smile while he committed the spell to memory. In exchange for the tier-2 spell, the Healer had asked if Vin wouldn’t mind collecting some much-needed herbs for him. Alka had already headed up there the night before, and she’d been more than happy to show him the way. While the frost bringers were undoubtedly tough opponents for any martial warrior, as they looked like ghouls that had died from frostbite and would freeze anything they touched, Vin was able to handle the weird undead with ease. Fireball made short work of them, blowing undead limbs apart and turning a harrowing combat into little more than a trivial encounter. Honestly it had been the trek up to the location of the herbs that had taken most of the day. Even he had been forced to stop once or twice to catch his breath, the incredibly high elevation making the oxygen quite thin.

With the new spell firmly tucked within his Iron Mind, Vin left the hospital behind, taking in a deep breath of fresh, mountain air as he looked down at the rest of town.

Shame the others couldn’t be here for this one. We’ll have to come back later.

Once he had finished helping Lumel live through one of her older fantasies, the two of them had cuddled in bed for a while, content to just lay in one another’s arms while they chatted. She’d happily listened to his descriptions of Diveral and Harmstead, asking him all sorts of questions that he didn’t have the answers to. Despite knowing how interested Lumel was in other cultures and societies, she always managed to surprise him with her questions. Trying to predict what she’d want to know and seeking the answers out ahead of time for her was all but impossible.

No, he didn’t know if the minotaurs had any unique courtship ritual.

No, he wasn’t sure if a petian was capable of becoming a Battle Mage or if their mana pool was smaller than anyone else’s.

And no, he hadn’t thought to ask if the minotaurs knew how to swim. Though he had a hunch they didn’t, seeing as they didn’t really have any bodies of water conveniently located on the side of a mountain.

Walking back into the Vanguard house they were staying in, Vin blinked when he entered the front door to discover Alka and Drintus locked in an intense arm-wrestling match. At least, it was intense for Alka, who was currently trying to use what looked like the full weight and strength in her entire metal-clad golem body to force Drintus’ hand down.

Drintus may as well have been cast from steel from how still his arm remained.

“Ah, Vin the human!” Drintus grinned, turning his attention to him as Alka continued trying to find leverage to shift his arm. “How was your day?”

“Pretty good. Helped out one of your Healers gather some potion ingredients in exchange for a spell,” he said, raising an eyebrow as he watched Alka struggle. If she’d actually had lungs, he was pretty certain she’d be huffing and puffing right about now. “…Need some help?”

“Like you could do better,” she snapped. Finally giving up, she motioned for him to take her seat. “Go nuts. This should be good.”

“Uh… maybe we do left hands?” Vin asked, smiling sheepishly as he put his golem arm onto the table.

“Whatever makes you more comfortable,” Drintus smiled, switching over to his left hand and placing his arm down on the table hard enough to shake it. His hand was easily twice the size of Vin’s own, and Vin’s face paled as his golem hand was all but engulfed in Drintus’ grip. “Ready?”

“…I’m starting to have second thoughts about this,” Vin admitted. But before he could change his mind, Alka shouted from behind him.

“Go!”

Vin focused, his golem arm flaring a brilliant blue as mana coursed through the limb and he yanked with all his might. He’d sent warriors flying through the air with a single punch. Shattered stone and ripped metal apart with the strength contained within his limb. In response to this terrifying power…

Drintus’ grin widened when his arm was forced about two inches down toward the table.

And then Vin’s arm shut down as it expended all its mana.

The world flipped upside down as all the strength Drintus had been using to fight back against his arm suddenly found itself without an opposing force, and Vin smashed down into the table hard enough to shatter the wood. A brilliant blast of golden light erupted from his arm, and Vin gasped as Drintus was knocked away, the light forcing his grip to come loose.

“Vin!” Drintus shouted, his smile turning into a look of concern as he ran back to his side and looked over him. “Are you injured?”

“Only my pride,” Vin sighed. Poking at the connecting point between his flesh-and-blood bicep and the golem arm that began at his elbow, he shook his head. “I’m pretty certain you would have just ripped my prosthetic arm off at the elbow if my divine boon hadn’t kicked in. Well. Let’s chalk that one up to hubris and never tell a soul what just happened.”

“I can’t wait for Shia and Alice to hear about this,” Alka chuckled darkly, taking far too much pleasure in his misfortune in his opinion.

“I’d almost forgotten about your strange power,” Drintus admitted, looking relieved he was okay as he extracted him from the splintered remains of the large table. “That was what saved us from falling from the floating library, correct?”

“Yeah. It’s pretty useful, but it’s a one-a-day thing,” Vin explained, brushing splinters off his clothing. “And because of that, I will humbly accept defeat and turn down any offers of a best two out of three.”

“I would not feel bad about losing,” Drintus said, his grin coming back in full force as he clapped Vin on the shoulder. “I am the strongest warrior across my entire people, and you managed to overpower me for a moment there!”

“Right up until my arm ran out of mana,” Vin muttered, poking at the dead limb that now hung limp at his side. “Guess that’s as good a lesson as any. I knew the arm was powerful, but even at max output it can only match a warrior in the third prestige for a single moment.”

As Alka challenged a ‘weakened’ Drintus to a rematch, Vin excused himself to his room. Once inside, he shook his head at his own idiocy. He didn’t know what he’d been thinking, challenging someone who almost certainly had a strength attribute higher than 100. Hell, knowing Drintus and how much he prided himself on his power, there was a good chance his strength attribute was closer to 150.

“And that’s the last time I agree to arm wrestle someone who weighs five times more than I do,” Vin muttered, crossing his legs as he slipped back into Introspection. Ovord was sitting off to the side, having already used up his three minutes staring at Alka that morning yet again. Vin could tell Alka was starting to grow genuinely annoyed with the fact that Ovord was doing nothing more than just looking at her each morning. Vin was doing everything he could to keep her calm and remind her he’d said it was going to be a long process, but he didn’t know how much his words were getting to her. He knew better than anything that Alka was a tad impatient, and for something as important as this, it was no wonder she wanted to feel like actual progress was being made. If the staring went on for too much longer, he was planning to tell Ovord to at least pretend to put a finger on her head or something and act like he was starting to experiment.

Focusing on his own magic, Vin pulled up the tier-2 structure for Invigorate he’d just gotten from the Healer. While he wanted to prioritize finishing up the spell he’d designed with Lumel’s fragment in mind, now that he’d wasted his divine boon for the day, he wasn’t willing to take the risk. Creating his own tier-3 spell was far more dangerous than copying the runic formation of an already completed spell, especially one of a lower tier, and the last thing he needed was accidentally ripping himself apart in a runic backlash while trying to rush things.

Vin got to work methodically copying over the many runes making up the spell as he recreated the structure from memory. He’d gotten to the point where copying a tier-2 spell only took him a couple of hours, and he finished it up without a single runic backlash. Smiling to himself, he allowed his mana to flood through the spell, his head snapping up when it activated.

Unlike Renewal, which was the creation spell linked to life mana and did nothing more than blast said mana at an injury and encourage the flesh to heal, Invigorate was a bit more specialized. The spell was like dousing oneself in a giant vat of coffee, giving a person a second wind as a wave of adrenaline was shot through their veins. The Healer had warned him that repeated casts of the spell in a short time frame could be quite dangerous as the heart might give out if it wasn’t given a chance to rest, but for waking someone up in a pinch or helping them find a second wind, it would be invaluable.

“Woah!” he laughed, snapping out of Introspection as his blood pumped and his fingers twitched. Despite the fact that he was just sitting in bed, Vin felt as though he’d just been jumped by a pack of epic monsters, his body rearing to summon his staff and leap straight into the fight. Forcing himself to stay seated, he took a few slow breaths, counting in his head to see just how long the base spell would last. He’d be able to alter it with Runic Recalibration somewhat, but he was planning to be careful with this one. The last thing he needed was to give someone so much energy their heart just failed the moment he cast the spell.

And he hadn’t asked, but the Healer had warned him that the spell could only be cast on a willing participant. Vin didn’t know what that said about him that the Healer felt like he had to provide that warning in the first place, as though he feared Vin would immediately try and turn the healing spell into one of instant death, but he decided to just chalk it up to the Healer covering his bases.

New spell learned! Tier-2 Life spell (Invigorate). 10,000 exp gained.

With another new spell under his belt and his divine boon already spent for the day, Vin decided to turn in early. Flopping down onto his mattress, he wove together Dream Walk and searched for a different target than usual. As much as he preferred spending his dreams with Lumel, he was trying to be better about keeping Spur and the rest of Terra’s council updated on what he was doing. Spur’s dreams tended to be rather strange, often filled with old battlefields or mountains of paperwork spilling out for miles in every direction, but the Commander always realized what was going on when Vin appeared.

Only a few days left before the festival, and then not long after that before the battle for wave eight, Vin thought, frowning slightly as he wondered what the future held for them. I sure hope those neilans are as good as they say they are. Otherwise, Terra is going to need something much larger than a festival to keep people’s spirits high.
