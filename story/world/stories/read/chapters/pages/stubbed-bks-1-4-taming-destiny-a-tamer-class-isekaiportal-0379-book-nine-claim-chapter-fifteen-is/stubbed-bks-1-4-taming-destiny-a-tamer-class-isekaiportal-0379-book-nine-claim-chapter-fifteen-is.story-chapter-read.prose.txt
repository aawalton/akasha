From my vantage point on deck, I watch as Kyrian, his manservant, and his captain load themselves into a boat and sail towards House Siren’s ship. I’m not surprised they don’t go to House Bladestrike’s ship, now that I know how few resources they have available. I can imagine that’s a sorry prospect to someone like Kyrian. I’m sure he doesn’t feel any obligation to help make up for the damage his actions have caused. If anything, he probably regards it as their own fault for losing the battle for him.

“You should eat,” Sarran remonstrates when I go over to ask whether he’s already checked through the items that Kyrian and his retinue left behind.

He has a point. I haven’t eaten since the battle, and as far as I know, everyone else has been fed. I head over to the canteen line on one side of the deck. The servers are surprised when they see me, but they quickly ladle out a generous portion of food when I ask politely for a meal.

I thank them and find a place to perch on a pile of ropes near the railing. I know it’s not very lordly of me, but to be honest, I’m too tired to care.

The meal certainly hits the spot. It’s some sort of fish stew – tasty and filling. I devour it hungrily, wiping the last of the sauce away with a hunk of bread. Only then do I surface enough to look around me.

Activity on deck has reduced significantly since the last time I was here and all the debris from the battle has been cleared away, as have the blood stains. I spot a pile of raptorcats curled up in a heap with Artemis and Orion with them. The two deris are keeping one eye open, evidently still a little keyed up from the battle, even if they didn't play much of a part.

On the other deck, on Kyrian’s former ship, people have already started repairs. I spot Master Mirena, and Alyna working on the enchantments. Sarran mentioned it would probably take them a few candle marks, but hopefully we’ll be able to set sail before dark.

A brief flash of surprise goes through me as I see Hunter is there. Elarion must have already arrived for the meeting of allies that I’ve organised – either that or Hunter returned to the ship by herself. I glance around but I can’t see him. Odd. I’ll have to ask Sarran whether he’s sent the other Great House heir below decks already.

I know it’s a little risky to call for all the lords to come to the same ship when House Siren and House Bladestrike are still in close range, but I strongly doubt that they’ll start anything now. Both ships have already pulled further away from us as it is – after Sarran received and confirmed that the tribute was present and correct, or that the promissory note was bonafide in Heir Bladestrike’s case.

I think the only reason they’ve stuck around this long was that they were waiting to see what happened with Kyrian. I doubt that either of them are keen to have him on their ships, but they won’t want to have to face Torrent senior and explain to him why they left his heir on an enemy’s ship in the middle of the ocean either, especially now they know he’s unable to use his magic.

As it is, now Kyrian isn’t on the ship, I’d like to take advantage of our pause to evaluate how things went in the battle and rejuggle strategies ahead of the next crisis. And get an explanation for Elarion’s actions during the battle.

But that will have to wait for after the meeting – already I see several boats sailing quickly towards me from my allies’ ships.

It’s not long before we have united in my cabin. Unlike any other cabin I’ve been in, this one has two rooms. The bedroom is comfortable albeit small, but the adjourning room is far more stately – clearly designed for moments like this. It’s dominated by a large table – ideal for maps and spreading out documents. At present, I just have a list of inventory that Sarran has completed listing what has been seized from the other ships.

My Steward is present, as is Loran. As for the other lords, Layton, Josiah, and Valence have brought their own stewards or manservants with them to take notes. Elarion is alone, curiously. Since it’s already rather crowded, I was forced to say no to my Bonded who wanted to join me, even Bastet. I miss having her pressing against my leg – having this many Classers in such close quarters has an oddly electrifying presence.

“Thank you for joining me here, everyone,” I start, the quiet murmurs of conversation dying down as all eyes turn to me. “I thought it would be a good idea to meet together in person while we can, and use the time to quickly go over key aspects of the fight and its consequences. First of all – a damage report. Valence, I noticed your ship took significant damage. Are you seaworthy still?”

“We did, but we should be, as long as we don’t encounter a storm in the next couple of days,” Valence confirms.

I nod. Good.

“And do you have the people and materials to repair the damage?”

This time, he hesitates for a moment.

“I should do,” he replies slowly. “Though it would go faster if I had either more hands or better material.”

“You took tribute from Heir Torrent and his entourage, did you not?” Elarion asks smoothly. “Perhaps you might be willing to spare some of it for Heir Fell here. And with the rest of us who took damage from the battle.”

Something about his tone puts my back up, despite the surface reasonability of the question.

“We’ll discuss tribute later,” I reply firmly before focusing back on Valence. “Speak to Sarran afterwards. He’ll have a better idea of how to help you.” I cast a look over the rest of the group. “What about all of you? What damages or losses have you taken? And are any of them significant?” I don’t like the thought of ranking deaths based on how useful the people were in life, but for our purposes here, a ship’s carpenter being killed is more significant than a common fighter.

Everyone chimes in with their damage report. Elarion seems to be the only one who didn’t take any real damage – the shield over his ship remained in place for the whole time, though its energy storage became rather depleted and some components will need replacing.

Sor’az was able to avoid losing anyone with his speed, but his ship still took some superficial damage. Josiah’s ship’s shield went down early so his ship is second only to Valence’s in the amount of damage. He was aboard Layton’s ship at the time and sounds troubled at the fact that he was unable to help protect it and his people. Layton’s, meanwhile, came off pretty well – his shield failed, but only near the end of the fight.

“In summary, our enemies came off a lot worse than we did,” I conclude with a curl of satisfaction.

Sor’az snorts.

“Fool nobles,” he judges with prejudice. “Water mages they might’a been, but tacticians they sure ain’t.”

The comment earns him several cool glances from the other nobles in the room. He notices the change in atmosphere and then clears his throat uncomfortably. “No offence meant te present companions, o’ course.”

“Actually, on that note, let’s consider our tactics,” I cut in, redirecting the conversation onto a more productive topic. “Let’s hope that we don’t have to deal with another fleet of ships any time soon, but this is the first time we’ve fought all together – we may be able to identify areas where our team work functioned beautifully – and others where it failed.” I can’t help flashing a brief glance at Elarion. He’s gazing at his nails, seeming to inspect them for dirt – vanity, or avoidance?

I return my gaze to the more general audience.

“Layton, Josiah, I was impressed with the strength of the hailstorm you caused,” I start with warmth in my voice. “It definitely helped – without the hailstones, the shields would have taken longer to go down.”

Layton pinks while Josiah appears more than a little pleased.

“It did work pretty well,” Josiah agrees with satisfaction. He darts a look at Layton. “I don’t think we’ve ever managed to call something quite as powerful as that before.”

Layton shrugs elegantly.

“We have never tried to call a storm over the sea before. I suspect the conditions were simply eminently suitable.”

“Well, let’s hope that happens again in the future – I can imagine something like that being useful against airborne enemies, or even those who must surface to pose a threat to us.”

“It won’t be much use against a leviathan,” Valence points out. “Hail would hurt us more than it.”

“Of course, it’s situational,” I agree. “Layton, Josiah, I’d like you to continue practising together when you get the opportunity, please. We never know when a weather effect like that will come in useful, and if we all know what you’re capable of, it will help us make better strategies.”

This is met with rather less enthusiasm, but I understand why – concealing trump cards is something coded into the nobles here from birth. But if we’re going to succeed in the challenges facing us, we’ll need to learn to work together.

We continue by dissecting each of the battles. Valence struggled so much because of the voice magic House Siren is known for. Since there are some sea creatures who are known to employ it as well, we decide that having a supply of enchanted ear plugs would be a good idea.

I compliment Elarion on his use of ice and he accepts it as his due, but doesn’t really get involved in the discussion of how else it could be used to target a variety of potential threats on our journey. Nor does he comment when I mention rather pointedly that my part of the battle would have gone rather smoother if I’d had more support.

But his lack of participation is the only downer. All in all, we have a good discussion and I feel far more confident about leading this group of disparate Classers in battle than when we saw Kyrian’s sails approach.

Then, of course, comes the question that has been on everyone’s minds.

“Now, tribute.” I see everyone’s attention sharpen. “I have seized Kyrian’s ship, all of its contents, and demanded tribute from House Siren and House Bladestrike. Most of this has been supplied in resources; some in gold. I recognise that you all played a part in the battle and that you should share in its fruits – to a certain degree.”

“Why not equally?” Elarion challenges, his voice full of nothing but benign curiosity. I don’t trust it in the slightest. “Is this not a coalition of equals? Or did I misunderstand your intentions?”

“Equality in humanity does not necessarily equal apportioning of something like tribute. Need before greed,” I answer, my voice hard, something about his demeanour getting on my nerves.

“How convenient,” Elarion replies. He doesn’t sneer, but there’s a contempt in his eyes that makes such an expression unnecessary. “I suppose the Great House of Titanbend has far greater needs than anyone else.”

I could cut the sudden tension in the room with a knife. I narrow my eyes at Elarion. I wasn’t going to do this in front of everyone else, but if he’s going to throw down the gauntlet, I’m not ignoring it either.

“My family’s name has no bearing on the situation,” I answer calmly, coldly. “I recognise that everyone in this room is a knowledgeable, experienced, and powerful individual. I value your contributions, and your perspectives. But you cannot say you joined the expedition under false pretenses.” My eyes bore into Elarion. “You chose to come, even when I offered you the option not to. Therefore, you have agreed that I am the expedition leader. Even if Kyrian had not targeted me directly, I would have handled the negotiations of surrender with the opposing leader. Everyone contributed to this battle so I feel it’s only fair to share some of its bounty, but Sarran will be the one determining who should get what.

“And since we’re on the subject, I had a question that didn’t quite get answered earlier, Elarion. You were supposed to work with me to take down Kyrian. We knew that that ship was the most powerful of the three of them. If you had fought with me, I doubt I’d have sustained as much damage – and the nasty surprise of the secondary ward wouldn’t have been so impactful if there had been two of us wearing it down.”

Elarion is very still, appearing more like a marble statue than something real. The expression on his face is completely unreadable. Finally, I am reminded that he is actually made of flesh when his mouth moves to speak.

“I saw our ally’s ship in difficulty. You seemed to have the situation under control.” He makes an off-hand gesture. “With all your words of allies and not servants, I thought I had the autonomy to choose the path I believed better for our whole alliance.”

I glance at Josiah.

“How dire was your ship’s state when I was moving to confront Kyrian?”

The lord hesitates.

“Heir Bladestrike clearly noticed that my ship was the weakest of the pair targeting it. He decided that if he could sink my ship early on, he’d be in a better tactical position and focused his fire on it. If Lord Elarion hadn’t intervened, I’m not sure my ship would have survived long into the battle.”

I nod slowly. So, at least Elarion did have some motivation for his absence.

“Then well done for noticing Josiah’s ship's difficulty and moving to support,” I start, my compliment earning a startled flicker in Elarion’s expression. “That said, even if your deviation was justified, why did you not inform me of the change of plan? We might have decided on a different approach if we knew we had no one supporting us.” Honestly, I’m not sure I would have done anything different, not with what we knew at the time, but that may not always be the case in the future.

Elarion shrugs fluidly.

“There was little time.”

Bullshit, I call, letting my expression flatten. From the way Layton’s expression goes carefully blank and Valence drops his eyes to study the table, I suspect I’m not the only one questioning his response.

“You had Hunter with you specifically so you could communicate with the rest of us quickly and easily,” I point out.

“Yes. Hunter.” I see a faint curl to his lip that I dislike. “I apologise. In the heat of battle, it must have…slipped my mind.”

Again, I highly doubt that. I think I have a good idea of what’s going on here.

“Elarion,” I start, my tone warning, my mind racing as I find a way to word this that both does not overtly accuse, but at the same time makes my meaning very clear. “Just so you know, I do not appreciate allies who test me. I especially don’t appreciate allies who, in order to test me, disregard and put those dear to me in danger. I do not, in fact, consider those kinds of people allies at all.”

My eyes bore into his until he gives a fractional nod.

“Noted,” he murmurs tonelessly.

I give it a moment of silence, and then nod just as fractionally myself and then turn back to the rest who have been watching the confrontation in tense silence.

“Speak to Sarran about tribute. It will be assigned on the basis of who needs it and who will gain most benefit out of it rather than who wants it the most. Otherwise, any more questions?”

I give everyone a moment. No one seems willing to break the silence. No one but Sor’az who, in his complete disregard of noble hierarchy, is often a breath of fresh air.

“What o’ t’ ship? Got no crew far as ah know.”

I give him a smile, thinking of the close to a hundred people currently in my crew’s quarters.

“Not yet.”
