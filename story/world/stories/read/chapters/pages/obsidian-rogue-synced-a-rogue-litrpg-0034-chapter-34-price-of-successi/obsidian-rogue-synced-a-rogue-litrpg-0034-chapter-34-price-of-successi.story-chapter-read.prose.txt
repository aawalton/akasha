“You're welcome,” while he wasn’t particularly interested in the village chief's gratitude, Levi knew it wouldn’t do him any good to be rude to the man in front of his people. “Where is my friend?”

“Your friend is okay, she’ll recover to a reasonable degree by the morning,” Meron said. “In the meantime, you look worn. Do you want to see the healer or have some food?”

“No, not really, I just need a bit of sleep.”

“As you wish. Gazun, show the man to the room where the girl is being kept. Make sure that they’re comfortable, “ Meron said, then turned to Levi, “You can leave the head of the krupui here.“

Levi dropped the head of the krupi where he was told to and followed the lead of Gazun, who had begun to move away from the Chief’s building.

Gazun walked briskly, and so did Levi, both men doing their best not to agitate the other.

The walk was a silent one, and Levi couldn't believe how mellow the guard was now compared to the first time they’d met.

A respectful grunt was the only sound that Gazun made when they finally came to a stop in front of the building assigned to him. The guard simply pointed to the door and turned his back on Levi, as he followed the path they’d just come from.

Levi hesitated for a moment before opening the door and stepping into the sparsely furnished room. His eyes scanned the room for anything suspicious before landing on the figure tucked in the bed—the mage.

She seemed tired-–exhausted even—but color had returned to her face, and he could tell from the way her breathing escaped her lips evenly that she was going to return to full health soon, if everything went well with the treatment that she was receiving.

It was easy to tell from a glance that the healing that she had gotten was subpar to what she would’ve received by a synced healer, but it was all they were going to get, so she was going to have to manage it.

“How’re you feeling?” He asked as he sat by the edge of the bed.

“Like I almost died,” she chuckled, a soft sound to his ears.

Levi couldn’t help but let out a chuckle of his own at her words, before nodding to her and getting off the bed in a bid to find a little bit of space on the floor to sleep. There was no way that he was going to share the bed with the mage in her condition, and he highly doubted that they’d have shared it even if she was healed up.

It was a wordless gesture on his part, but they both understood the reasoning behind it, so neither argued nor pushed the matter.

Levi lay on the floor, his mind considering the implications of what was happening.

The Chief had put them together in a room that seemed to have some personal effects of a couple of the tribe members, and Levi could understand why those things were there.

These people were so detached from the outside world that it made a bit of sense why they didn’t have a guest room that he and Xara could stay in, or maybe they did, and this was just their ease of being able to keep track of them.

He might’ve saved the village by taking down the krupi, but it didn’t mean for a second that the villagers trusted them. It was something that Levi himself could understand, seeing the way that he himself often contemplated whether he could or couldn't trust the mage.

Levi gripped his dagger tightly at the thought and then curled into a fetal position as he attempted to sleep. There was no telling exactly how most people would take the fact that he had survived slaying the krupi.

He had seen many villagers who’d celebrated his success, but there was no doubt in his mind that there’d be people amongst the villagers who’d frown upon his feat. They’d consider him a threat, and they’d attempt to take their lives sometime tonight.

Levi believed that his fears were well-founded, considering the type of people these were.

The villagers were brought up to believe that people like him were unwelcomed here, and while the general populace of the village weren’t as hostile as he’d expected, there were still a few who’d bared their hatred at him openly, such as Gazun and those people wouldn’t hesitate to kill him if the chance presented itself and what better a chance than the at night when Levi was exhausted from his battles with the Krupi.

The place and the opportunity were obvious even to a blind person, and so in reality Levi was simply curled up in a fetal position, with his health below what he would've liked, waiting for an attack that might never come.

But one that probably will. It was simply a matter of when, not if, the attack would come.

It was up to Levi to decide if he was capable of handling multiple foes as he was or if he was just risking his and the mage’s lives because he was against sleeping in the forest.

Maybe I can void step all the way to Krupi’s cave. It’d take a couple of void steps to do that, but Levi was sure that the points he had would be enough to boost the current mana he had till it was enough to cross the distance between the village and the house without having to walk that much.

It seemed like a reasonable plan, but he hesitated on making that move because of how much blood coated the Krupi’s cave. There was no telling just how many monsters would be attracted to that much blood.

At the same time, staying in this room, which had been offered to them by the village chief, was basically the same, but the monsters would be replaced with dissatisfied villagers, because Levi and Xara were targets of those who considered the Chief's decision to let them leave unsavoury.

He was well within his rights to spend the night in the room offered to them, but he had a feeling that Chief Meron would deny them the right to leave if the dissatisfied villagers attacked and Levi killed them.

There was no two ways about it. No matter how badly the village chief wanted his people to sync, he wouldn’t do it at the risk of fracturing the village. Meron would be forced to kill them by the time morning came, because they’d spilled the blood of villagers, and then Levi would either have to take Xara and run away, or kill almost everyone in the village and still run.

It seemed like a better idea for him to abandon the village now, while they were still on good terms and avoid spilling blood that wouldn’t earn him anything but guilt.

Killing them was unnecessary. He could leave right now, and that’d be the end of everything. Levi had paid his dues by slaying the krupi, and now he could run away with Xara, and they wouldn’t have to fear being chased by the villagers. By all accounts, it was a win-win situation, and all he needed to do was leave.

I guess now’s as good a time to check out my skill rewards from slaying the krupi. Maybe I’ll get a skill that’ll make things a lot easier for me. According to how many levels he’d gained from killing the Krupi, it was reasonable for him to expect anywhere from two to three new skills.
