Bog ran straight through ever-thickening stalks of ice, breaking into passages far too small for his form and relying on his great weight and tough hide to see him through.

Caleb followed helplessly behind, constantly looking out for signs they’d been detected. Despite the dragon having to clear his own path, the wreckage he left behind was difficult for Caleb to traverse and the gap between them grew as Bog ran. He only caught up when he found Bog digging into a wall of ice, clawing at it frantically and making quick progress.

“You’re going to get us killed!” Caleb yelled at the dragon, not concerned anyone would hear his voice who hadn’t already heard the commotion they made coming here.

Bog ignore Caleb, only sending the sense that he was after something delicious.

And then Bog broke through his wall, making a small hole he quickly widened as he forced his steaming body through it. Reluctantly Caleb followed.

The ground dropped immediately beyond the wall, and Caleb found himself sliding down into what looked like the night sky. The chamber was dark, but throughout, tiny pinpricks of light shone, illuminating nothing more than the air just around them. That, however, was only what his eyes told him. His metal sense told him he was sliding down a sea of metal with the weight of a mountain.

The lights! It’s magical ore.

Once he’d made the connection, he knew what it was. These specks of light were the ore they’d found in that first vein and throughout all the ice in the caverns. Unlike all that, however, this wasn’t ore. It was refined metal. He called a cloud of dust to his hand as he fell, and the flecks illuminated it as they flew around him.

Eventually Caleb’s descent came to a stop as the ice below him leveled out. Bog stood waiting, his head ridges raised as he stood alert. To Caleb’s surprise, he wasn’t gorging himself on the metal around them—though the sparkling around his mouth showed he’d not completely abstained from the bounty.

“What is it?” Caleb asked, but then the darkness moved.

They stood at the bottom of a massive dome illuminated by specks of the magical metal. Throughout, rocks and other debris free of metal flecks blocked the view of the surrounding walls, but as soon as Caleb spoke, the largest black area moved.

Slowly the shape came into focus as Caleb’s enhanced eyes became accustomed to the dim light. A pyramid of darkness elongated upward, and the outline of a snakelike head as large as him shot towards them.

Caleb jumped back, using his Imperium Ventorum to propel himself faster out of instinct. The giant snake head snapped its jaws shut just inches in front of Caleb, but he was flying back too fast to get the upper hand. Up close, he saw that thick stone scales covered the snake like armor and spines extended from its face.

“Basilisk!” Caleb yelled to Bog, not sure why he felt the dragon needed the information, but as he sailed backward through the air, it was all he could do. “Don’t look at its eyes!”

Caleb had been lucky that the creature had closed its eyes to lunge, else he’d already be dead.

The basilisk had lunged over Bog in its attack on Caleb, and the dragon took advantage of being overlooked. He jumped up at the underbelly of the stone-plated beast and sunk his teeth into the flesh.

Or at least tried to. The stone plates covered the body all around, and he only succeeded in ripping one free. Caleb sensed what Bog had done through the bond in surprising detail, though he had received no image of the feat.

Ding!

He ignored the notification, thinking fast—he needed to stop himself from becoming petrified. Basilisks weren’t completely unknown to his village, and he knew that a high constitution or willpower would allow him to fight the effect, but he had no idea how his stats compared to this creature’s.

With no better plan, Caleb closed his eyes and turned to his wind sense to track the movement of the giant snake. Through this, he watched as the creature whirled around on Bog, letting out a hiss of pain. It brought its tail around to slam down on him, but the dragon saw the attack coming and narrowly jumped out of the way.

While the head was facing away, Caleb risked opening his eyes to get a better picture. His wind sense gave him a sort of afterimage of where the monster had been and a vague idea of where it was now—not enough to fight by, but more than enough to give him some confidence he wasn’t committing suicide by taking a look.

The basilisk’s tail had wrapped around Bog, who’d failed to escape this time. While the dragon was being crushed, Caleb ran back to cover the distance, spear in hand. While it was a poor weapon against a plated foe, he hoped the earth aspect of it would give him an edge. Leading with his weapon, Caleb ran toward the giant snake. He lunged at the last instant, and putting all his body weight behind the thrust, he activated Power Strike.

Stamina flooded into his arm, propelling the shot forward. It struck a plate dead center and glanced off, taking stone with it but not harming the creature beneath.

The basilisk spun its head around and whipped it at Caleb, who closed his eyes, holding his spear in front of him as a bar to block the blow. The stone head struck the shaft, and once more he went flying across the chamber, this time less willingly. As he flew, Caleb brought the wind around to slow his descent, turning a bone-crushing impact into only a painful one. He rolled across the stone, and watched from afar as their foe turned back to regard Bog, who was now glowing with heat.

Either the creature was immune to the heat or the plates served as an insulator, but both options meant Bog’s defense was less than useful. The spiral around Bog tightened as the basilisk crushed its captive. As it constricted, the stone plates that covered it crumbled beneath the force required to kill the iron dragon and Caleb heard a series of cracks with each tightening.

Gathering himself, he ran again at the basilisk, propelling himself forward with the wind. The foe turned to him just as he approached, forcing him to close his eyes and attack blindly. This time, Caleb forwent Power Strike, and instead used his Imperium Metallorum to enhance and guide his thrust. Once more his tip met plate, and once more he cut a gouge into it, but despite the horrid sound the attack made, the creature was unharmed, and Caleb was once more sent flying as the nuisance he was.

The snake head hit him in the chest, and the spikes dug deep into his padded leather, though they failed to pierce it. Had he not been so dressed, the spikes on its head would have pierced his lungs instead of only knocking the wind from them.

The landing was equally unpleasant, Caleb taken by surprise by the blow and failing to slow his descent. He landed on his back and his head came down hard, only his helmet saving him from being knocked out cold.

Bog send pleas for help through the bond even as his internal flames grew hot enough that he appeared to be on fire. Flames licked up from the shrinking cone of flesh and stone that was the basilisk.

Desperate but without hope, Caleb charged at the basilisk once more. If he could see, he’d be able to land a strike between plates, but the snakelike creature knew that as well, and ensured he had to close his eyes with each attack.

Caleb pushed harder with his Imperium, propelling himself forward with each step, and reached a speed he knew impossible for the uninitiated. The basilisk spun around to meet him again, head up high out of reach, leaving its plated belly to take the weak blows.

Eyes closed, Caleb focused fully on his skills. He let his control of the wind drop as he stepped into his thrust, then switched over to his Imperium Metallorum, using the honed instinct he’d developed in forging the very spear he held to further empower his magically enhanced strength.

“Power Strike!” he shouted, uncertain he’d even be able to activate the skill with his mind so occupied with his magical control. The spear flew forward in his arms, and met plate once more, only this time he felt some give. At first, that give was only the tip of his spear as it dented under the power of his attack, but that was followed an instant later by the crushing of stone as he pierced through the plate and into the basilisk’s chest.

The monster reared up, letting out an outraged hiss. It uncoiled from Bog, leaving the dragon behind as it retreated from Caleb, who’d suddenly become a threat. As it retreated, it took the spear with it.

Caleb didn’t want to give the creature the opportunity to lunge at him. In his current state, he didn’t think he’d be able to dodge. He pulled his hammer from his belt and ran after the shifting snake. When he sensed the head pointing away from himself, he opened his eyes and threw his hammer with the combined might of his Power Strike and Imperium. The tool flew through the air in a whirl so fast it shone in Caleb’s wind sense and struck a plate, shattering it even without an earth aspect.

A deep fatigue set in within him as he felt his stamina drop to a low he’d never before experienced, but still he ran after the creature. Realizing it wouldn’t get the space it needed for a lunge, the basilisk stopped retreating, bringing its tail around to swipe at Caleb. By then he was within range of his spear and he pulled on the tip, trying to bring it toward him and out of his foe. Instead, his body flew toward the spear, the force of his pull bringing himself to the lodged weapon.

The tail swiped ineffectually underneath Caleb as he flew up the two meters to where his spear stuck out of the neck. He crashed into the neck, a meter below the head, and grappled it with his arms and his legs. His tenuous hold was tested as the basilisk began to shake, but using his spearhead as an anchor for his magic, Caleb kept himself in place. It tried to bite at him, but he was too close to the head, so it resorted to crushing as it lowered itself down onto him. The spear shaft broke under the weight of the basilisk, and Caleb jumped free just short of being crushed himself.

He rolled in his landing, and came up with his sack of nails in hand. Bracing himself for death, he looked up at the head now at eye level less than a meter away. He looked deep into the eyes of his enemy, and felt it as his soul was crushed under the magical weight of that petrifying gaze.

The weight, he found, that he could resist. He pushed against it with a part of his mind he’d not been aware he had any control of and felt the weight lessen.

Ding!

With a moment of reprieve, Caleb shifted his focus to his hand, and pushed with every last bit of mana he had, sending the bag of nails flying at the basilisk’s face.

The nails burst through the sack as if it weren’t even there. Many bounced harmlessly off the stone scales, but enough found the soft flesh of the basilisk’s eyes, pierced through them, and continued on into the creature’s skull.

Juvenile Stone Basilisk (rare) has been killed.

Caleb felt the rush of essence flow into him, but he was out of both mana and stamina, and in the last moment before he passed out, he realized that was not a good place to be.
