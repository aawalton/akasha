...still Chapter 31Gork

Lei-rei and Craft hopped out. The gork tended to a campfire by the roadside, poking it with his machine gun’s barrel.

Lei-rei stepped forward. Craft blocked her with an arm. “I’ll do it,” he said. He still had faith in Enthusia, but with things exploding and gorks being a racial class, he might be wavering a little bit. Talking to the guy up ahead was the only way he could prove to himself that Amatoria was still just as advertised.

He had faced down armies of cold steel and demons alike. Asking some hunk with a funny accent for directions should be easy.

He approached him. “Hey, man—”

The gork turned around with a predatory grin, his gun’s barrel cherry red. “Oi, yer propa lookin’ fer a foite?!”

Jesus Certified Christ. Negotiate? Flee? Fight? All of life had just flash-vaporized into three choices. The gork had a full man’s height over him, and that autocannon looked like it’d turn him into mist in one shot. The heck do I do here? His specialty was in making egotistical leaders feel so self-important that they’d hand him the keys to the kingdom over a drink—not whatever this was!

Just then, he caught something off about the autocannon…and it was too painful to overlook.

“Craft,” Lei-rei called out of concern, but again, he raised his hand as a point man would—and pointed at the autocannon.

“Advice,” he said.

“Oi.” The gork knelt down, and they saw eye-to-eye. “Waddaya sayin’ ’bout me grokbusta?”

“That thing jams every fifteen rounds, doesn’t it?”

The grok narrowed his eyes. “ ‘Loiten me.”

Craft pointed at a nearby shell casing. “The dents and scratches on the shell are telling me the bolt’s pushing forward faster than the gun can eject the shell, which means either the shells are underpressured, the extractor’s too flimsy, the bolt spring’s too stiff, or the bolt itself is too light.”

“You’ze…” The gork stood and loomed over him. “You’ze a Gunwizerd o’ somethin’ ?!”

Lei-rei’s gaze flicked between them. “Craft, are you possibly from the same world as him?”

He jerked his head and squinted at her, opening his mouth—but closing it. He couldn’t outright deny it. He faced the gork. “Uprising of 2128?”

“Wot?”

He looked at Lei-rei. “That’s a no. It’s just—a gun is a gun, you know? You have to take care of it or it won’t take care of you.”

“Wot—but yea, sounds roite,” the gork added.

“Here I thought that technology was peculiar to them.”

If Craft’s ears could flick, they would have.

Lei-rei shook her head. “Wait, no, that wasn’t the question.” She faced the gork. “Do you know another way to Monstera?”

“Oe, dat all? Follow da gorge, an’ ya’ll find yerself in da dead forest, yeah! Watch yerself, tho, place is propa weird, even fer gorks!”

The gork pointed at a trail behind a thicket on the left side of the bridge.

“That way’s...” Lei-rei muttered. Craft barely heard her as the gork continued, loud as ever.

“Me’ll lead ya—not! Krump dat! Me’ll get lost an’ never even make it ta da forest! Me’s a bit of a grot’ead, yeah! So good luck ta ya! Me’ll probably end up stuck in da mud or somethin’ !”

All things considered, Craft could appreciate someone self-aware enough not to get others caught up in their own weaknesses. “Appreciate it,” he said.

“Hol’ it.” The gork threw a satchel at him, and when he looked inside...

“What—grenades? Are you sure? No, what’s in the forest—”

“If me’s tell da otha gorks me’s met a gunwizerd an’ me’s forgor ta give ‘em a sack o’ bombas, they’ll krump me at spawn!”

O-oh, so it was a cultural parting gift. No, he didn’t fully understand; regardless, he felt safer having a familiar weapon on him. Well, a handgun would’ve been less terrifying to carry around than a bunch of spicy sardine cans with exposed blasting caps, but gorks probably didn’t make their guns for small hands.

He faced Lei-rei, expecting equal surprise, but she had bowed her head and covered her mouth, deep in thought instead.

“What is it?” he asked.

Her head shot up, meeting his gaze, then looked down at his newly acquired satchel of explosives. She nodded in approval, pointing the same way the gork had. “That’s the Petrifying Forest.”
