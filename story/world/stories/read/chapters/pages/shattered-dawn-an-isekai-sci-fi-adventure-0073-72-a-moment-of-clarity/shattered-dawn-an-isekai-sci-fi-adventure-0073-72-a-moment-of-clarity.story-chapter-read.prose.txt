"Elion."

Elion stretched, rolling out his stiff shoulders. His bones ached, pressing into the cold, hard stone floor that had served as his bed for the night.

"Elion. Your name is Elion, isn't it?"

Elion sat up, wiping sleep from his eyes. The darkness of the cell surrounded him.

"Yes, it is," he said. "What?"

Across the room, Selna looked at him, her eyes still black with the infection. Her voice though, sounded clear, alert and aware.

"I’m sorry," she said. “It’s hard to tell what’s real and what’s imagined. It all seems so real. I go in and out of the—” she gestured vaguely with her hand. “I’m glad you’re real.”

“I’m real,” Elion said. He still sat across the room from her, unsure how long her sanity might last.

"You said that my daughter was here. Looking for me?"

"Yes," Elion said. "We came from Aterfel. We destroyed the crystal shard that caused your infection.”

"You destroyed it," Selna said. "I wondered. Something happened, and I felt it in my mind. It freed me for a time, but it's trying to regain control. I can fight it, I have moments of clarity. But it's hard."

“It’s the power of a false Sentinel,” Elion said.

“A false Sentinel,” Selna said, nodding slowly. “That makes sense.”

"How did you end up here?" Elion asked. "How did you get infected? The last time Keyla saw you, were were coming to New Kairn Tol with Prator.

"That turned out to be a terrible decision," Selna said. "Prator was not the man I thought he was.” She lifted her arms, chains clinking as she rubbed her brow. “He is only interested in his own wealth and success. He talked about rebuilding New Kairn Tol, the beautiful, pleasant, peaceful place where we used to live. I wanted to do it for Keyla; to show her the life that I loved with her father.”

“Prator lied to you,” Elion suggested.

“He did. He was only interested in Thrandicite. Mining. Mapping the tunnels. Refining acid from the Pits. His charming optimism changed into a desperate obsession. Then people started disappearing. He started using Bortim’s abilities to bind us. We had a plan to take him down, to stop him, but it moved too slow. I escaped; I abandoned them before he could get me.”

“He was already working for Venya?” Elion asked. “Selling the Thrandicite to her?”

“Not at first, I don’t think so,” Selna said. “He almost seemed more interested in digging, than in what was dug up.”

“When you left, where did you go?”

“I tried to return to Aterfel. I was on the plains when a scavenger tribe ambushed me and dragged me to their crystal. They forced it upon me. I—” Selna screwed her eyes closed, pinching her mouth. “I do not wish to recall it.”

“No need,” Elion said. “Don’t think about it.”

“It gripped me. It took my mind. I fell entirely under its sway, its control."

"Is it the same as the Bondhold?" Elion asked. "Or is it different?"

"It's different," Selna said. "It's unlike anything I have ever experienced. It's a kind of madness, but it directed us, it guided us, it sent us to do its bidding. It just wanted to kill or infect everyone." Selna wrung her hands. “It does have a sense of Sentinel power about it, though no true Sentinel could do that to a person. It makes sense that it would be the power of a false Sentinel. How did it form? How do you know it’s source?”

“It was Gorman,” Elion said. “He was tampering with Festrin powers.”

Selna wrapped her arms around herself, clutching her shoulders. “You can’t trust anyone,” she muttered. “I placed my daughter in his care.”

“He tried to control her,” Elion said. “But we killed him.”

“He’s dead?” Selna looked shocked. “How?”

“We had to. We had to stop him. There wasn’t any other choice.”

“I still hear his voice in my mind,” she whispered.

“I shot him in the chest. I saw his body fall to the ground.”

Selna fell quiet.

“I didn’t want to shoot him,” Elion said.

“I shot my daughter. I killed her.”

“No. Kelya is alive.”

“I never miss a shot,” Selna said. “I leveled my rifle. Placed a bullet right between her eyes. I see it, now in my mind. In my dreams. She falls, dead to the ground. Dying. Bleeding. I have her blood on my hands, on my face.”

“No!” Elion said, blood rising. “You didn’t kill her. You could have shot her, but you didn't. You missed on purpose!"

“She’s dead and I killed her!” Selna screamed. “I murdered my baby!”

“Stop it right now!” Elion shouted, raising his voice, trying to drown out Selna’s cries. “Keyla is alive and we’re going to help her. We’ll get her out of this place!”

Selna fell into a mess of strange mutterings, raving and ranting nonsensical phrases. Elion couldn’t always decipher the words and phrases, but even when he thought he could understand her words, they meant nothing to him.

“There’s ice on my toes! Ice—ice on my toes!”

“Buttery gnomes, buttery gnomes, buttery gnomes.”

“Rat tat tat, fish fry the bat, rat tat tat.”

Elion watched the ray of sun through the window move across the wall of the room. Senla continued to mutter. She would occasionally rise, yanking against her chains, snarling and spitting. Elion wondered how Keyla would react to seeing her mother like this.

When the sunlight had nearly completed its arc across the wall, Bortim pushed a tray of food through the flap in the door. Selna did not react.

“Two bowls for you today!” Bortim called. “If she doesn’t eat hers, I wouldn’t blame you for eating it yourself!”

Elion picked up one of the bowls, sniffing at the grey mush inside. Bortim had not provided silverware, so Elion raised it to his mouth. The stiff gruel slumped into his mouth.

Despite the texture, the flavor was not unpleasant. Elion was very hungry, having hardly eaten in the last 24 hours. He gobbled the food down, then eyed Selna’s portion.

But he did not eat it. Instead he brought the bowl over. Selna’s eyes danced sightlessly across the room.

He placed the bowl on the bench beside Selna. She did not react to his presence. Snickers nuzzled his head against the woman, purring. Elion wasn’t sure if the cat’s efforts were helping calm the woman.

Elion stalked back over to his side of the room, sitting back against the wall. Snickers padded along the bench and began eating from Selna’s bowl.

Wondering what he was going to do next, Elion turned everything over in his head. New Kairn Tol offered so many of the things Elion needed, but held them just out of his grasp. Ascendency Stones, being freely traded. If he could get his hands on some of them, he might be able to get down into Prator’s excavation, and offer them at the altar.

His knife. It was here, close. He could sense it’s location. But he had no way to get into the warlock’s camp without being captured by them. No way to free his blade from the machine without Keyla to glitch it out.

If Elion could get to Boff’s, perhaps he could free Keyla, and the other owned. He could seize their Bondholds from Boff, and take the man’s stash of Ascendency Stones. Then he and Keyla could free his sword, make their way down to the pit to offer the Stones. They could bring Selna back to Aterfel, where Kasm might be able to heal her…

But Prator still held Elion’s Bondhold. He needed to be free of those chains, before he could move forward with any potential plan.

Prator had spoken about working together; about becoming partners. But if Elion examined Prator’s actions, what had the man done besides lock him up?

Elion pulled the ball of green earplug putty from his pocket, kneading it with his fingers. *This could be useful. But when would be the best time to use it? It wouldn’t be useful if I’m already being compelled by an order.* He slipped it back into his pants.

The rays of sun beaming into the room faded, gold, orange, and red sunset light through the window. Someone worked the lock on the door.

Elion rose, standing to face his captor. Snickers dipped into a dark corner of the room.

Bortim entered. “Come with me,” he said. “We’re going to see Prator.”

Elion followed Bortim out of the room and down the hall. Bortim handed him a dark cloak with a scarlet lining, similar to the one Bortim wore but without the elaborate designs.

“Wear this,” Bortim said. “Pull up the hood. We don’t want anyone in the street to identify you because of your hair.”

“I don’t have to obey you,” Elion said, holding the cloak. “Prator has my Bondhold, and he never ordered me to obey you.”

Bortim shrugged. “It wouldn’t work even if he had. You’re only bound to the Bondhold, and only must obey the orders of the one who holds it. That authority cannot be transferred, without a physical transfer of the Bondhold. ”

“You’ll just let me leave?”

“I would try to stop you,” Bortim said. “I have some experience in physical bindings, as you know. Are you going to try? Personally, I’d love to turn you over to Venya and be done with this. I don’t know why Prator dragging this out.”

“We made an agreement,” Elion said.

“So you’ll come then, you won’t try to run? Keep your word like a good Aurelian?”

Elion sighed. “I won’t try to run.”

“Good,” Bortim said. “I don’t want to fight. Come, Prator requested your presence.”

Bortim opened the door and led Elion into the street. Arachnatronics moved along the road, transporting their cargo of acid-filled barrels. People moved in the shadows of dusk. The open road called to Elion, urging him to flee, before Prator could use the Bondhold to further restrict him.

But there were people here who needed him. He’d led Keyla into this mess. He wouldn’t leave her enslaved to Kendry and Boff. Selna needed their help, to get some form of healing.

What about Liora? Didn’t she need his help too? Elion needed to find Zev, rescue his sister. But he couldn’t just leave, not yet.

Down the road toward the Acid Pits he felt the pull of his knife, calling to him. He needed to recover that too, or else he’d never be able to hide from Venya. It was only a matter of time before they got their machine working better.

They crossed the street to Prator’s mansion. Marble columns lined the portico, and the stained glass windows blazed with color, lit from within.

A half dozen Arachnatronics stood before the steps. These were different models, not cargo transporters. Arrayed with rockets, machine guns, and armored plating over their legs and bodies, these mechs were ready for a fight.

Working their way around these vehicles, Elion and Bortim proceeded up the steps into Prator’s mansion.
