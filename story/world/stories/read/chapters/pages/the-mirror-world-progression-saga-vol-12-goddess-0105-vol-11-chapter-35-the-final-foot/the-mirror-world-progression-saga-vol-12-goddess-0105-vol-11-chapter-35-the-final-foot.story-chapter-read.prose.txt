Samantha was extremely fast. In addition to being double-hasted, she was even faster when enclosed in the light sheath. Only a few could compare: Odysseus, Six Moons Falling… possibly that was it, and she probably topped them.

To Samantha’s equally quick senses, Glae was very much in trouble and had been grievously injured, almost certainly enough to interrupt the ritual. He was straining at the edge of his willpower to keep it together and continue. Likely, a botched ritual near the final stages would be catastrophic on a scale best not imagined, much less seen in the world. A new era might still be caused, just not one anyone would like.

In fact, botching this particular ritual might error the system out and reset, wasting all efforts, wiping memory, and perhaps dooming everyone to an endless, slowly degrading series of cycles once more.

So, though she was loath to do what she needed to do next, she was capable and nonetheless had to.

The decoys parted for her, giving Samantha an angle and line of sight to Glae through the cage, to which she stretched her hand and splayed her palm, activating [Spellfyr: Cleanse], which fired a beam of white, purifying energy. It engulfed him, healed him, and attempted to clear away any negative status effects.

Samantha senses a comparison, so she utilized [Alter Fate], in an attempt to force failure on the part of the status. The demon’s disruption once again prevented this… but she still succeeded, wiping out some sort of paralysis effect that would’ve absolutely ruined the ritual, bar none.

The coughed-out chant of before regained its oomph as a fully healthy Glae had a sudden reinvigoration and resurgence, continuing the ritual with only the slightest of hiccups.

A sphere of Hard Light made of checkered patterns suddenly enshrouded one of the assassin demons, ripping into the dirt as it scooped him up and carried him away, courtesy of the jester, Ozra.

Dragon jaws chomped down on the other courtesy of Athos, who thrashed it around a few times like a crocodile and then quickly slung it away. The dragon then shapeshifted into the smaller form of a troll, as the nasty spines that were previously embedded in the dragon’s mouth fell free.

All around, pressure continued, as the spike-demons pushed and pressed against defenses, and the eyes slid forward, going for the ritual stones, taking advantage of the focus shift to cause damage on the periphery.

Samantha used several single-target converted force beams to blow away demons, left with the necessity to trust others to handle the ‘lesser’ threats, unable to be everywhere at once.

Above, more Servitors had shifted to practically dogpile on the colossal demon’s feet, keeping it from making more signs. It tried to shake them off, oddly with little annoyance or expectation of success, somehow. Indeed, it didn’t work, though damaging efforts were of mixed success. Only Deathsting seemed to have really managed destruction, severing two fingers and shredding up the rest of the demon’s hand that he had been working on tediously the whole time.

Beware the wyvern saw.

The demon called, “And so we come to the final foot of our feud.” Bodily, it began to move, flying down at an angle, headed for the ritual area. “It’s distasteful to be so crude, but we have no further options.”

“That could be bad,” Azure said in the gestalt. “Pretty sure he’s going to try to stomp our shit, and that’s a really big foot. Can probably smush and pulverize half the damn ritual area. Aside from that, I doubt Glae can do his thing embedded in the dirt.”

“We can’t attack its feet and stop the ‘toe signs’ at the same time,” Michael said. “Aside from that, the other fronts haven’t stopped, and we’ve suffered more casualties from those incapacitation effects.”

Briefly, Samantha tried to use her raw telekinetic force to slow the demon. She might as well have tried to move the sun. Sighing, she quickly abandoned the tactic.

“Create a framework,” Samantha offered in the gestalt. “A blocking lattice rising higher than the stones. I can contribute, but cleromages will need to add more support if it’s to help at all.”

“I can help,” a minotaur added. “I made the additional support around the standing stones with my gauntlet artifact. I can raise it higher, with effort.”

“Perfect. Get to work!”

Samantha first checked over injuries for critical members. Revemort and the shaman, Flugurt, had been paralyzed, and it looked like the toxin had a duration of minutes.

Her [Alter Fate] ability had already been recharged by ‘back-mind’ processes in coordination with Jeeves, a long-standing effort to analyze the efforts around the world for acceptable ‘curses’ to recharge through. After what happened at Matrazere, it was pretty much sacrosanct that her realm would never leave those stones unturned.

Sensing she needed the extra oomph, Samantha used [Hoping for Daylight], and one more standard [Alter Fate] use, to clear up those two important members.

Fortunately, dealt-out casualties to the spine demons and the mostly contained ring demons allowed enough people free to hold back the eye demons, despite the focal gap. Once Revemort jumped back into the mix, that allowed more to begin constructing a framework.

It was a fairly simple affair: scaffolding-like structures of many different kinds of material, from physical matter, to force energy, to druidic tree growth, all grown up to clear the tops of the standing stones. The minotaur with the gauntlet was directed to mostly reinforce existing structures, especially by anchoring underground, to prevent their construction from simply being driven into the earth and made irrelevant. They needed it braced into the bedrock, essentially. Fortunately, the bedrock was not far down in the mountainous valley.

All the while, the demon came. Deathsting traded off the hand so he could attack a foot, as otherwise efforts were made to double up on damaging the demon’s feet. Sadly, he seemed able to redirect his regeneration and focus it, sacrificing his hands and body to pour more into his feet, and he didn’t need any acute control to stomp.

It perhaps doesn’t matter, anyway. We can’t destroy his whole mass.

The colossal demon was either very slow naturally or was choosing a deliberate moderate pace. Still, his sheer scale covered a lot of area more quickly than things seemed, and it wasn’t long before its literal shadow was casting over the ritual.

It loomed. By then, all the spike demons were torn apart, and the rings, though impossible to completely destroy due to regeneration, were a sorry thing, more like mere clusters of floating flesh islands.

Samantha made layer after layer of scaffolding herself and reinforced it multiple times with divine enchantments. She used divine spells as well as [Conjure] constructs in multiple overlapping layers. One layer she used as further anchor reinforcement, which was a rarer capability, mostly confined to the druids using roots and the minotaur with the gauntlet.

It was a chaotic, many-colored, madhouse jungle-gym looking mutant construct grown up all around. One side benefit was that they protected the borders of the standing stones, too. A few times, one of the eyes managed to test it and practically bounced off, unable to penetrate the new, fortified zone. Cracks formed, but were quickly repaired.

Perhaps never before in history had such a strong scaffolding been made as this thing, and she felt a certain pride. It should’ve been technically invulnerable to being overcome, but she well understood how untrue such words were.

One other oddity existed: a minotaur holding a ridiculously long, bronze pole, the top rising even higher than the constructs. He stood on a firm stone foundation, apparently ready and expectant of this actually mattering. Samantha believed it, even as her eyes found it rather ridiculous. Trusting minotaurs usually turned out well, in her experience.

Finally, the great foot came down, and the last effort Samantha saved for right before contact. She, together with the greatest of masses and strengths among her Servitors still free, namely the Faerie Dragons, such as Porthos, flew up hard and rammed the bottom of the demon’s foot, driving up as hard as they could.

They might’ve slowed it. Slightly.

The foot continued coming down unstoppable, and the ‘effort-givers’ slipped away before they were crushed on the flat ceiling tops of the scaffolding.

The last thing before that was the minotaur’s pole. As soon as the foot was caught on it, the whole momentum was arrested, the foot stopped…

For one second. And then the pole gave way and bent, and the foot hit the tops of the supports.

With the sound of much groaning, the initial pressure utterly held, and the demon above was basically standing on it, legitimately supported.

The pressure began to increase, and the pillars began to shake. The demon’s muscles began to strain above; his full mass began to bear down as he lowered himself and pushed.

Meanwhile, the ritual neared its end — Glae held the hammer up as an unthinkable concentration of energy networks collapsed into it, making it hum louder and louder. As Samantha extended her senses to feel for the tablet, it felt changed and transmuted. It was like an effigy ready to burn, like a voodoo doll ready to be detonated, representing the contrast to the hammer, its opposite: endless networking structures of terrible, terrible spellcraft. Not just ‘Forgetfulness’, no. Multiple spells, screaming the dominance of the sages and of entropy itself. Empowerments. Dominance itself… the very meaning of ‘World Spell,’ perhaps, made into a small and fragile thing, now under the hammer of commonality.

‘All concords can be broken. Who, in hubris, assumes otherwise?’

“Get your back up into it, everyone that can find a spot!” Samantha called as she flew upward, slamming into the tough, leathery flesh of the demon’s foot with her shoulder and then bringing her hands up, pushing with all she had, gritting her teeth. Others — her allies, her compatriots — did the same, all to add one tiny extra bit of delay, as their constructs below strained and squeezed into the bedrock.

Whatever happens, whatever I think of him, I know this is needed; I know this is right.
