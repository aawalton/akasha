Sally frowned for a moment before clicking her fingers at the shadowy figure. “Oh, it’s you again. Where have you been hiding?”

The man gave her a brief shrug as he joined her and the wizard. Even with the large plumes of ash and dust darkening the world, the shadow’s eyes still shone bright white. “Took a little vacation on a different world. Couldn’t help but overhear the racket you were making and made it back here.”

“Interplanetary travel.” Archie frowned and adjusted his hat. “In such a short time as well.”

“Ain’t a big deal. Here, there, either way I’m still no closer to my goal.”

Sally crossed her arms. “Got yourself a human body, or at least a form, though. Is that the real you?”

The shadow shook his head. “Just a strong memory. What I am is a fragment of something bigger. At least I have a better idea of what exactly I’m after now. A human woman.”

“That doesn’t really narrow it down,” Sally said. “I’m going to assume you need to either save or kill them?”

He shrugged. “All I have is the drive to meet them. Things will shake out as necessary.”

Sally exchanged a look with Archie, before raising an eyebrow. “Sanctuary has a lot of humans. Even if you don’t find your gal there, we might be able to help you. Your chances might be better with some help rather than jumping around the cosmos randomly.”

Archie nodded. “You helped us; allow us to help you. Do you have a name?”

“No.” The shadow tilted his head from side to side. “You can call me Hunter, though. Seems fitting.”

“Hunter.” Sally grinned. “Welcome to the team. Now, where do we start?”

Theo looked over the edge. “It just ends.”

Max nodded, but had nothing to add. The partial destruction of their world was something that happened before he had even arrived on the planet. Seeing it for the first time had been a terrifying surprise, even amidst the horrors he had endured to get to this point. The vampire, however, looked rather nonplussed.

It was like a video game, in a way. The map was incomplete, ending sharply and abruptly. A ledge that stretched across the entirety of this side of the continent. Past that? A dark void.

“I’ve seen this sort of thing before.” Theo stood back up from his crouched position. “Less static, however. One of the first worlds we went to was slowly decaying. Being absorbed by this void. It’s essentially the Sea, or you could compare it to blank data.”

The magician lowered his top hat to tidy his messy brown hair. “So the questions we need to answer revolve around why this happened and why it stopped.”

“These… Guardians you had here are rather interesting.” The vampire put his hands in his pockets and turned to address Max. “I wonder what happened to those who fell to the void.”

“Deleted? Like everything else.”

Theo shrugged. “Power sometimes lingers. Especially if the nature of said power is an underlying rule of the world. It could be that the System prevented deletion of the world once one of the Guardians vanished. Like removing the battery from a clock.”

“We’ve already scouted the remaining world for anything out of the ordinary.” The magician returned his hat. “Even Hell. Other than this, things are… as normal as one of these System worlds could be.”

“It takes a while to get used to living like this, huh? Even after so many years, I catch myself in the middle of something and question where I am.” Theo smiled, revealing his fangs. “But just like the missing Guardians, things seem to fall into place, exactly how I imagine them to.”

“Didn’t you say you died once?”

“A canon event.” With a shrug, he turned back to the endless void. “Our world scan determined there is a control room, similar to where the Architect works back in Sanctuary. My best guess is that it is out there.”

The magician grimaced and narrowed his eyes out at the empty expanse. “Then it is lost? Even if it still existed out there somehow… you surely can’t expect to find it, nor exist out there.”

“I fell into the void, once. Became one with the Sea.” Theo’s smile faded. “My entire being was reduced to atoms and spread across an unthinkable distance. Somehow, my consciousness remained unbroken. I survived total obliteration through willpower alone and became whole again thanks to Sally. Maybe it was because I was the Aspect of Death that allowed me to persist, but… I am not afraid of the void, even though it is rightfully feared.”

“Be that as it may, Theo, I wouldn’t count on threading the needle twice.” Max rolled his eyes and shrugged. “And that’s saying something coming from me.”

The vampire turned in place and grinned. “Luck has nothing to do with it. This is all calculated risk based on known factors.”

“Ugh, now you’re speaking my language. Just what are you suggesting, so we’re on the same page.”

“I’ll simply go out into the void and find this room.” Theo gestured to the darkness. “Then, if required, bring you and Ren over.”

Max sighed. “I’m all for pulling off a good trick, but this just sounds like a bad joke.”

“But the punchline of ‘pancakes’ is more of an anti-joke, which often has no relevance to the setup.” Sally crossed her arms and nodded.

Archie exchanged a dull look with the shadowy man. “Yes, well. It is probably best we stick to the more important parts of our backstory.”

“You were a cat last time I saw you,” Hunter agreed. “Things have changed for us both. I appreciate the information, though, even if it doesn’t help me. You seem like decent folk, and I’ll stick by you until the thread of my destiny takes me away.”

Sally nodded before looking up in her STAR windows. “Jackie says the remaining survivors are gathering. We might have to fight again, which is bad. I’m pretty spent.”

“As am I.” The wizard sighed and glared out at the crashed vessels. “I am loath to stretch our capabilities without knowing if we have safe passage to either the Omen or Sanctuary directly. Death here could be permanent.”

“Some actual stakes? Woe is me.” Sally rolled her eyes. “Here’s what we’re going to do, Arch. Teleport us up to the mountain with Bully and the dwarves. Then find a way for us to get out of here. I’ll work on breaking this new System you’ve labored me with, and Hunter can… well, what can you do, bud?”

“I can…” Hunter furrowed his brow a moment before pushing his jacket out to the side. From his hip he drew a shadowy revolver. “I guess I have a gun now.”

Archie tilted his head. “A firearm? Curious, and it seems it is also a surprise to you.”

“Memories,” came the murmured reply.

Sally eyed the odd man a second, glancing at the wizard as well. She had known her share of oddballs over the years, so Hunter was no different in that respect. Maybe she was reading into things with an exhausted mind, but there was a good chance that having him around would be important.

Chuck would certainly like to meet him, and maybe they could help him find the woman he was after. Either way, they needed all hands on deck to leave this world safely. The last dregs of the Radoch forces were also stuck here, and while they were just a minor threat compared to the whole… what remained of the Outsiders wasn’t much better.

“Best ‘port us, Arch.” She scrunched up her nose as she tried to make out shapes amid the smoldering wreck. “I can’t see how many survived, but if they want a last stand, then they’ll have to walk for it.”

“As you wish.” The wizard held out a hand.

With a flash of blue light, the trio vanished from atop the building. A second later, they reappeared on the mountain. To their surprise, the skeletal dwarves were already outside, standing in rough regiments. Bully stood nearby with his arms crossed.

On seeing the rest of the Outsiders his expression changed from one of boredom to something more worried. The frogman stepped over and raised a questioning eyebrow at the two men accompanying Sally.

“This, would you believe it, is actually Archie,” she explained. “And this is Hunter. He’s the one who helped Edward take down the first spaceship.”

“Ah.” Bully nodded slowly. “I take it you took down the others? The explosion gave me a headache.”

“Take a potion for it.” Sally gestured toward the dwarves. “What’s going on here? They’re preparing for war?”

“Being immortal on an empty rock turned out to be a terrible prospect. Apparently, one last fight for their honor is a more fitting end.”

The zombie grunted in understanding before turning in place to face Archie. “I don’t suppose you can conjure me a book? Specifically, a blank tome?”

He held out a hand. “That does happen to be one of the spells I know. Might I ask why?”

“You’ll see. Bully, I need you to dump all of your crafting items on the floor. Hopefully we have enough to work with.”

A swirl of white light spiraled over Archie’s hand, slowly forming a thick book. Once fully formed, he handed it over to Sally. The cover was black, with a twisting inner border of purple and green lines. She received it gleefully and sat down on the rocky ground while the alchemist dropped everything in his possession around her.

Archie glanced over to the distant massive plume of gray cloud from the wreck, before turning back to the zombie. “Trying your hand at spellcraft?”

“Taking advantage of the vague power before the new System slots me into the right box. Look.” She waved a distracted hand as she flipped open the book to a blank page.

[Sally Danger][Human, Zombie, Aspect][Level: Pending…][Class: Pending…, Aspect of Life][Affinity: Stamina+, Power, Vitality]

“It’s likely to be some mix of necromancer and rogue, I guess,” she continued. “I’ve always bounced between a caster and a sneaky melee class. I’m going to take advantage of the ambiguity to write some rules myself.”

“I’ll leave you to it. Please let me know if you need any help.” The wizard turned to Hunter. “While we are on the topic, what are your skills? I can’t read any sort of System on you.”

He grunted and pulled out the shadowy revolver. “Not too sure what a System is, but I guess this is more aesthetic than using my hand.”

After spinning the gun in his hand three times, Hunter pulled back the hammer and aimed out at the nearest tree. With a click of the trigger, a dark bullet burst through the air. Bark blasted from the trunk before the entire tree decayed away, turning to ash within a couple of short seconds.

Archie stood in disbelief, his eyebrows raised. “That is… I’ve never felt such dread. How draining is that? Or, more importantly, how high can you scale that amount of damage?”

Sally paid the pair no heed as she sat, tongue sticking out of her mouth as she concentrated. While Bully hadn’t been stocked with tons of useful materials… she also wasn’t sure what she had been looking for. With a quill in hand, she had put several lines of swirly letters on the first page of her tome.

Mostly going by intuition and random gut feelings, several other items made their way into the mix. A pressed paste, red string, lines of charcoal drawn with her fingertips. It looked like an art project gone wrong more than anything else, but gradually the zombie became content with her work.

Eventually she sighed and snapped the book shut. Her red eyes glared at the blank cover, and after a few awkward moments, the book burst into green flames. As the fire simmered and shrank away in seconds, it left something behind.

In bright green, the letter O for Outsiders blazed in the center of the cover. Within the oval shape was a single eye in the shape of a five-sided star over a mouth of sharp teeth.

“Hah!” Sally jumped to her feet and held the book aloft.

[Deathforged][Basic Tome of Necromancy][1/5 Abilities Stored][Abilities cast using this focus have greater Efficiency (+15%)]

With a smug grin on her face, she let go of the tome. Rather than drop to the ground, it hovered in the air beside her.

Sally turned to the treeline and held out her hand. The book flipped open, pages flickering to where she had written in her spell. A skull enveloped in bright fire rose from the tome as she cast Mortis Bomb, before she flicked it down to explode amid the forest below.

“I must admit, I’m rather proud.” Archie stood beside her, admiring her craft. “Once you upgrade it, you should be able to put most of your spell-like abilities in there.”

She gave him a warm smile before turning her eyes to the regiments of dwarves lined up. “Oh, I’m going to do much better than that, Arch.”
