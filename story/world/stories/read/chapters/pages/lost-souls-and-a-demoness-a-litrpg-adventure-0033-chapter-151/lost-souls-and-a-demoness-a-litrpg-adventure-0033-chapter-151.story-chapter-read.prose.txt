Vylith had known before setting out that tracking down her target would not be a simple task. The Labyrinth was vast, even just considering the region connected to the newly integrated human world. Searching the first few floors for a single person was futile, especially when the person in question was a shape changer. Instead, the wraith demon focused her attention on the apertures leading to this "Earth". There were far too many of them for her to lie in wait, hoping her quarry would pass by, but fortunately, she wasn't entirely without resources. Zertrolk had stationed multiple contingents of demons on the outer reaches of the first floor, where they waited to aid in acquiring the human devices needed for the grand ritual. Vylith wasted no time in putting them to work.

"Lady Vylith." One of the demons, a red-skinned Lugren whose name she hadn't bothered to learn, spoke up hesitantly, stepping to the forefront of his contingent. "We've been ordered by the commander to stay hidden until signaled. The weapons are under heavy guard, and if alerted the humans may secure them further. I don't think we-"

Vylith casually took a step closer to the demon, reached out, and snapped his neck with a quick twist of her wrist. The audible crack arrested the attention of the other dozen or so demons in the hideout, all of whom radiated fear and uncertainty. The wraith demon remained expressionless as she slowly turned to face the rest of them, brushing a strand of her long, white hair out of her face.

"Are there any other objections?"

Unsurprisingly, there weren't. The demons chosen for the Earth operation specialized in disguise magic, subterfuge, and assassination, although none were her equal in any of those categories. Still, Vylith could only be one place at a time, and she had a lot of ground to cover if she wanted to find the demon Wayfarer in a reasonable amount of time. The commander wanted her dealt with before the ritual commenced, so she didn't have time to play around.

She began by sending some of the stealthier demons out to act as scouts. Several groups of humans had set up fortified encampments around the portals leading to their home world, and many of those had Wayfarers passing through frequently. Vylith had Jade's name and a description of her succubus body, which wasn't much to go on for a person who could shift her form with a simple thought. Fortunately, the information she'd gleaned from some of the survivors of that disastrous first corespace expedition included descriptions of some of her companions, as well. That, more than anything else, was what she leaned on.

The first two groups of demons she co-opted into her efforts yielded disappointing results. Not only did they not discover anything about the demon Wayfarer, but one of them was discovered spying on the human encampments. The following increase in security became such a headache that they were forced to relocate, abandoning their cavern hideout for a fallback location in a woodland quadrant. Vylith left that mess to them, making for the third of the four forces they had lying in wait near various Earth apertures. There, finally, she met with some good fortune.

"You found something?"

Vylith stared down the much smaller imp, slowly sharpening one of her daggers. The tiny, winged demon bobbed his head rapidly, fluttering up to eye level as he gave his report.

"One of our infiltrators in the human organization in charge of their Wayfarers recognized the name I brought to him." The imp said in a raspy voice. He spoke quickly, as if worried that, if he took too long, she would simply reach inside him and tear the information free with her bare hands. It was a well-founded concern. "She is with one of the groups operating out of one of the nearby portals. People have seen her come and go multiple times, although she seems to be inside the Labyrinth now."

After a couple weeks of fruitless searching, Vylith was relieved to finally make some headway. Her expression remained impassive, however, and she crossed her arms.

"Where in the Labyrinth is this group she travels with?"

"We, uh… don't know, Lady Vylith."

The imp swallowed nervously, looking around. None of the other demons in the meeting tent spoke up, though. Vylith assumed they were trying to avoid drawing her ire. They seemed to think that if they stood quietly in the background, she wouldn't notice them. What, did they think she was an animal? Only able to focus on the prey directly in front of her? She found the notion irksome. Perhaps she would kill a few of them just to show that one couldn't simply fade into the background around her.

But no, that would place the mission in jeopardy. Seizing the Earth artifacts would be challenging enough at full strength, and Zertrolk would come down on her like a hammer if she tore through too many of his men. Instead, she simply gestured for the imp to continue.

"Go on."

"Uh… well… they're supposed to be some of the highest-level Wayfarers from that world. So, they're probably up on the third floor, working on finding the Cores."

L-ReF Compound, Medical Ward, Washington DC

Once you'd been in enough of them, all hospital rooms started looking the same. There were the adjustable beds, uncomfortable side chairs, and a privacy curtain that did absolutely nothing to mute the constant beeping of various machines or hallway conversations. Nurses came and went at unpredictable intervals, always seeming to arrive just as you were finally drifting off to sleep. Alan was used to it all and had long since kept a travel bag full of various supplies to make his depressingly regular visits a little more comfortable. What he wasn't used to was the pretty, dark-haired girl sitting at his bedside desk, keeping him company.

"You don't have to keep coming, Maria." Alan said, pushing himself into a sitting position, back against what passed for a headboard in a hospital bed. "I feel bad, keeping you cooped up."

"I'm not leaving you all alone in here." Maria said, crossing her arms and adopting a stubborn expression. "Like Mama said, you and Jade are like family to us. Your sister and big brother are taking care of each other on their adventures, so we'll take care of each other here."

Alan suppressed a sigh. He was always such a burden to everyone around him. His poor health had broken his family apart, made things difficult for his sports friends, and kept making his teachers devise custom lesson plans he could complete at home. Now, he was another obligation to a family already under immense stress. The guilt must have shown on his face, because Maria scowled.

"Don't give me that look, mister." She said, jabbing a finger into his side. "I already have you figured out."

"Do you?" Alan smiled despite himself. He was used to people treating him like a fragile object liable to shatter at any moment. Not Maria, though. It was refreshing.

"I do. You feel like you're imposing on everyone, forcing us to visit you."

"Yeah…" He turned away. "I guess I do."

"Well, too bad." She jabbed him again, harder this time.

"Ow… what was that for?"

"Family watches out for each other. That's just how it works. If I were in the hospital, would you visit me?"

"Of course. If you wanted me to."

"Great! So, do you like it when I'm here?"

"Yeah." Alan admitted. "I do."

"Then it's settled." Maria leaned back in her chair, grinning triumphantly. "Besides, I need someone to give me tips on how to get past level six of Seven Blades. That last boss cheats."

"I knew it." Alan laughed "You do have an ulterior motive."

"Of course I do. None of my sisters play, and big brother went off to another Universe." Maria paused, then shook her head. "I still can't believe that I'm saying that with a straight face."

"I know what you mean. But don't worry, they'll come back safe. Jade--" A sudden wave of nausea cut him off. Alan grunted, bending over and squeezing his eyes shut.

"Are you okay?"

He felt Maria's hand on his shoulder, warm and reassuring. He leaned into it, taking long, deep breaths. The nausea slowly faded, leaving a sheen of sweat on his brow.

"I'm fine," he said, hoping his voice didn't sound as weak as he felt. "It's just side effects from the medicine."

Alan's condition had been better than normal for the past few months, but the reprieve hadn't lasted. He'd known it wouldn’t and accepted his situation with a certain robust stoicism that only others with chronic illnesses could understand. Shortly after he and his father relocated to the L-ReF compound he'd needed to be checked in to their hospital. The facility was surprisingly nice, considering how quickly it had been thrown together. But a nice coat of paint only did so much to improve the experience.

"Will your dad be back soon?"

"Tomorrow morning. He's taking care of the house and getting everything we need moved here."

A nurse entered to take Alan's blood pressure and give him his afternoon medication. Alan downed the nausea meds immediately but set the pain pills aside.

"Not taking those?" Maria asked once they were alone again.

"They'll knock me out. I don't want to sleep through the day. Not if you're here."
