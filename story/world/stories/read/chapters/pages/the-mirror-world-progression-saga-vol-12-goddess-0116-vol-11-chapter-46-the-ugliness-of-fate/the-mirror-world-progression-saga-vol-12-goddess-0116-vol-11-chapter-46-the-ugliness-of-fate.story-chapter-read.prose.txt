Stunned and utterly disoriented for a period, Ashawein found herself free of the vulture's beak as she stumbled up to her feet. Her skin had been penetrated at multiple points, her clothing ripped, and her body was bruised all over. Her lip had been busted, and her right eye felt tender.

Her blood felt hot in her veins, now. A growl began in her throat.

Samantha mostly gave in to the fury surging up in Ashawein’s blood — a rampage was coming, and she knew it was best to capitalize on the full ‘motivation’ of a generally gentle giant.

Ashawein saw the bloodied vulture head woozily lifting its head from the stone, and instantly, Ashawein let out a warcry and brought her club down in a terrible arc, smashing the skull in and cracking the beak. She did it again, for good measure, causing gore to spray in all directions. The vulture's head was still.

She felt a painful acid spray her back, and spun around to see the dragon-cobra arced up above the downed and bloodied chimera body. Ashawein charged, tossing her club aside and leaping for the serpentine head, pulsing with adrenaline and a giant’s slow-rising, but potent anger. The cobra head was ill-prepared, still drawing back. Its snapping dodge backward left Ashawein to land on the ribs of the chimera, and she immediately followed up to hop onto the dragon-cobra’s neck.

With her prodigious, hypercharged strength, she gripped and swung the beastly neck into the ground, its head slamming down hard from being made into the end of a lever. Ashawein once more followed up on the stunned foe, leaping up closer to the head and wrapping her arms around the neck.

She couldn’t quite get her arms around, so she squeezed as hard as she could to try to get there.

The snake head, unable to bite or spray her, now thrashed, slamming and dragging Ashawein through the dirt. With clenched teeth, Ashawein held on. This particular head was not as physically strong as the rest of the body, and she was not knocked off, with the damage dealt nothing to be concerned about.

With a final tremendous effort, her muscles straining, letting out a guttural cry, Ashawein crushed the beast’s throat inward, and her hand wrapped around her wrist on the other side, locking in.

Choked-off sounds came from the beast, acrid venom sprayed randomly from its mouth, and it thrashed worse than ever… briefly. Then it slowed, and finally went still, as Ashawein crushed its throat in.

Rising eventually, Ashawein found the body still breathing shallowly, twitching, and trying to move. Retrieving her club, she walked over to the near-blinded, profusely bleeding lion head and raised it up high for a final, catastrophic coup de grace. She felt the lion's head turn to her subtly, felt the ruin of one eye still with enough of a gaze to peer up at its executor.

It seemed so pitiful, suddenly. It almost seemed to beg… but whether that was to be spared, or ended as a ‘mercy’ after so much damage and agony, she couldn’t say.

Her bloodlust, quite unhelpfully, had passed.

Her eyes met Othenia’s across the body, who was watching from behind a tree, her face unreadable.

‘To take the heart of the world, there can be no restraint.’ The haunting voice once more, from outside of time.

Is it for me? For Ashawein? Both?

With a fierce cry, Ashawein brought the club down as hard as she could, caving the chimera's skull in with a fountain of blood. With the sheer size of the beast, the blood soon became like a small pond. Panting, blood-splattered, Ashawein stared at it, both she and her ‘rider’ feeling uncannily disturbed, even though the execution was technically life-saving. The chimera was responsible for hundreds of dead gnolls, including children.

And then she saw a new kind of twitch in the body, from the torso, now apparent that it was rather bloated. Twitches in its fat belly.

The realization made them both feel immediately sick: it was pregnant.

‘To take the soul of Fate, one must have a ruthless hand.’

Ashawein, fully taking over from Samantha, threw her club aside and flopped down in the dirt, weeping and shaking her head, overcome with a sense of regret. “Why? Why must we do these things? I don’t know that I’m cut out for this, after all…”

Othenia slowly walked over, proceeding to the beast’s belly, touching it with a hand in multiple places, with the feel of delving magic. Finally, she dropped her hand and declared, “Three babies; they live. They can be extracted. I know an interested party that could raise them to be guardians. Sometimes they tame, sometimes they grow up too feral whatever is tried and have to be put down. One out of three, perhaps, will be almost like pets. A spoiled life, even.”

Ashawein listened soberly, her weeping slowly drying out. “A life for a life…”

“And maybe more, hmm? Would you care to arrange it?”

“What’s the catch? Your kind so often seek bargains.”

Othenia turned to her and grinned. “Only the delay this will make on your victorious return to my mother.” After a pause, she added, “A gift, as thanks for slaying it, when it dared to attack me.”

“Would you have killed it, if I didn’t?”

Othenia didn’t answer, just continued peering back at her, maintaining her mystique, effortlessly appearing royal and beautiful — as ever, self-possessed and not caring to answer when she didn’t feel like it, without explanation. It was frustrating and appealing at once, and certainly alien to the ways of giants, who tended to express themselves openly.

Ashawein sighed and asked instead, “Would you think me weak, to agree?”

Othenia had another faint smile. “Would it stop you?”

“No.”

“What if I told you I wanted to put them out of their misery, too?”

“I’d refuse — and protest. But will you help them or not?”

“As I said, I will, as a gift. Enough prattle, then. I think I have my answer, however shy you are to speak it.” She gestured to the belly. “Come, then. Help me with this extraction.”

Ashawein rose with a groan of effort and carried herself over. She put a hand to the belly, feeling movement.

‘There is no other way to win when your foe never relents to destroy you, in turn. We destroy each other, do we not? And therefore, always ourselves. Such is The World, such is the trap I made of it.’

And suddenly, Samantha had her answer about the voice, because Ashawein replied, within her mind and soul. “Weary though we are, we must not stop trying, Creator. The world is where we are, and sleep is an illusion of escape. All of it is worth it to awaken, but I will show you what is worth fighting for… just wait.”

Othenia began precision cuts to get the babies out of the chimera mother, and Ashawein helped with the tremendously gross act, mostly to tank the baby aggro, who each came into the world screaming, snarling, clawing, and biting.

The little monsters did no harm to her tough hide, of course. Actually, they were kind of cute.

Euck! You’re crazy, girl. These things are uglier than unwashed sin…

In the void of a scene transition between ‘tests,’ Samantha pondered the oddity of Ashawein. She knew the Director had ‘turned away’ long before the incident that took the Iterations to exile on Earth. Apparently, Ashawein at least wanted to wake her up. If she succeeded, perhaps it was the trigger to enact a new plan?

Which then resulted in Sammy. Er, me, that is.

Her thoughts faded as she absorbed the new flashing memories of the giantess’s life that ensued after the first test.

Ashawein went on to pass test after grueling test after nigh impossible test, each one crazier and more outlandish than the next. She captured the ‘Teleporting Hare’ over weeks of effort, greatly entertaining her ‘host’ in the process, who watched the ‘festivities’ on a floating little golden princess throne. She’d at least helped with finding the right trap, a magical sphere that blocked even supernatural ability teleportation.

A rather fun time was had with the Mountain Man of Jittur, who would give up the Crown of Bears only after a Duel of Creativity, continuously solving each other’s puzzles. Ashawein finally won with a trick: adapting and innovating the magical sphere, she wove in the trigger of a ‘power stasis’ effect to her trap. When the Mountain Man performed his solution, which needed his powers, it simply failed.

Stumped, he had to surrender the duel, though he asked how it was done. Ashawein, a bit ashamed, revealed the secret cheat she wove, ready for a new battle after such deception. But the old man with a beard touching the ground fell over laughing, and from the ground, presented the Crown of Bears as a prize.

She stole the Knot of Garenades, hidden away in a pyramid full of cultists, traps, and extremely stabby, toughness-ignoring guardian spirits. Othenia declined to go within, giving only a few choice pieces of helpful information as well as dubious suggestions, one of which — that involved enlisting the aid of a spirit — almost got her killed when it literally stabbed her in the back. Fortunately, it had been in a non-essential organ, by chance.

After presenting this priceless wonder to the princess, who took it to her queen, she was charged with cleaning out the Catacombs of Dobesh, which were chock full of giant oozing grub-like demons that could regrow from even a tiny piece of their corpse, and when attacked, liked to ‘spit’ a part of themselves somewhere, to make sure they would.

She was given a wand that continually spewed annihilating flames for a small mana charge, but the task was still impossible with it, fit to go on for eternity without making headway.

Samantha was once more given control to solve the problem and began work on trap design construction. She fashioned mana-drawing circles to pull ambient energy to power trap after trap, covering zones for automatic destruction as grubs populated. She designed a long-term summon of ‘mini-phoenixes’ like fiery hawks, who roamed around and ‘firebreathed’ grubs to oblivion.

Sadly, when grubs were in large numbers, they gained additional self-defensive mechanisms, and when enough of them were present, one thing they loved to do was de-summon the birds. She couldn’t gain the best benefits of the roaming firebirds if they were confined to a circle, either…

Though she knew it would take a long, long time — and even would if Samantha tried such craziness on Calrenazzod — she decided she needed to crack the rune Othenia had taught her. When she told Othenia her intentions, the half-dragon princess laughed like one indulging a fool, telling her she’d traded one eternal punishment for another. That only fueled her determination more.

Over the course of many days, she reverse-engineered an ancient rune, likely what took a lifetime to cheat into reality. But the ‘one behind the wheel’ was the ultimate cheater, and in her world, she was the Goddess of Magic.

She would not be denied innovation.
