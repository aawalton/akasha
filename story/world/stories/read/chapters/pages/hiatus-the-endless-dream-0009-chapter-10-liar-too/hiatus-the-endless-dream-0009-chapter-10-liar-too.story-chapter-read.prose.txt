Standing a little taller, I left my room. I had twenty-two hours to kill in this world. While I didn't doubt I could wake up and wait out the time, I also thought it a waste. The biggest issue was a general lack of knowledge. What was an Instance, an Instance Holder? Were there consistencies? In short, what were the rules of this place?

Asking the System was little help.

[I will not tell you. You have to figure it out yourself.]

Which only frustrated me more. The thing rarely answered any questions to a degree that made sense and seemed entirely reluctant to be here, but despite that, it was never 'I can't tell you'. No, instead it was 'I won't tell you'.

They had the answers. They just weren't handing them over.

Considering the options, there really was only one answer. The System had said there weren't any friendly Instance Holders I could reach for now. That was fine by me. Whether I got information from a friendly party or a lurking monster didn't matter.

With that in mind and equipped with a crowbar, I stepped out of the facsimile of my parents' house into another world.

It was the same stitched cosmos I had seen when meeting The Spinner. Endless lines of stars given form. There was no panic in me this time. I did not have a monster staring me down or a System pelting me with information I had no clue how to process.

So, I spent my first thirty minutes sketching the sky. It was a kind of satisfying drawing that real life never offered. There, everything was, for lack of a better word, same-y. All the houses looked the same, and all the apples had the same shade. If you saw one snowflake, you saw them all.

This. This was different. Beautiful. The variance was stunning. So stunning that I was disappointed in my inability to capture the scene with my meager talent. Having no colored pencils or paint didn't help either.

"Hey, System. Did you know my brother well?" I eventually asked.

[... Yes. I did.]

"What was he like?"

[The two of you are similar in more ways than you could imagine.]

After hearing that, I snorted. I stood and began walking down the street, not knowing what else to do besides marvel that even the houses were varied.

"I think that's the only description I've ever heard from anyone." I thought that the System was done talking to me—had taken that to be the end of the conversation and moved on to do whatever it did. But then it continued.

[He was not as kind as you may think. Not cruel, but harsh.]

Out of all the responses I expected, this wasn't one of them. My walk briefly turned into a shuffle before I stopped completely. A million questions tinged my tongue, but I fell for the biggest one.

"... How did he die? Or become unable to … come back."

[I will not say.]

The response left me feeling empty, like something had been stolen from me. Maybe that's why I didn't stop myself.

"I think I hate you."

[A natural response.]

"You drag me from my home, burden me with all these rules, and drown me in riddles, say that I have to experience endless horrors, and you can't- won't answer any of my important questions."

[Correct.]

"Fuck you. You're fucked in the head. If it weren't for you, I- I wouldn't be…"

My voice shook at the end, and I stopped speaking. I didn't trust my own words.

[You, too, are a liar.]

[You would come back here over and over, regardless.]

"How would you know? Last I checked, you can't read my mind." I hissed out. A moment passed where I considered my words and hoped that I wasn't making another bad assumption.

[Because you are the same as Rowan.]

I stared at nothing for a long second. Chest tight. Throat dry. I just continued walking, looking for an instance, uncaring what the System might've meant.

For not the first or even third time that day, I was surprised.

"I didn't think Instances would be so direct."

There was no response back, but I continued out loud, just in case the System would chime in with helpful information.

"The ones in the real world. I just get this vague feeling something is off before the overlap becomes apparent, but this? There's a boundary line."

I had to stop myself from scuffing the line with my shoes. If I got dragged into an Instance because I was making a point, that'd be the last straw for today.

[It signals to those stronger that the Instance will not actively expand.]

That meant this was a relatively weak Instance. Hopefully, more willing to share information under threat of violence. There was a lot that I wanted to find out, but I didn't even have the base of knowledge to start asking the right questions. I considered what I truly wanted to learn, and that had to do with my brother. The chances that this Instance knew anything about him, though, were slim, so I'd settle for information about the nature of The Endless Dream. Checking the time, I nodded to myself. Twenty hours and five minutes – plenty of time.
