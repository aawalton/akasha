Jianyi stood on the same field at the bottom of the mountain that he had six months ago.

Some things were different, though.

For one, he was confident in his physical condition. Instead of dreading what was to come, he looked forward to exerting himself—to explore how far he had progressed.

Second, he stood beside a friend. Miyao had been in his group all these months ago, but she had been just one of many others he had no desire to get to know. Now, he was glad to have somebody by his side, though they both were comfortable in their silence.

They even had the same inner disciple, Liu Guanming, overseeing their group. Though neither Lu Shan, Hong, nor—for obvious reasons—Jun were part of their group this time.

“Listen, you have done this before. The challenge is still called Everlasting Ascent. You need to run to the nearest plateau, where you will be handed a cloth. You need to return here, and once you hand over the cloth, your time will be stopped. During the test on arrival day, you had ninety minutes. That is not the case today—there is no time limit.”

There was some whispering among the thirty or so disciples in their group, though Jianyi ignored it—the reason was obvious, and Guangming confirmed this right away.

“You have had the benefit of six months of training and, hopefully, a couple of stages in the Qi Gathering Realm; the sect expects you to be faster, a lot faster, than before…” The young man looked at them, conveying the weight of his words, then nodded.

“Step to the line.”

Jianyi looked at Miyao, who smiled with a raised eyebrow.

He responded by nodding towards the mountain.

Without hesitation, she moved past a few others to the front of the group, and Jianyi followed.

“Go!” Guanming shouted.

The first time Jianyi had to do this had been torture. Even if nothing else had happened, his damaged lungs had ruined his endurance, and running up the mountain had been the worst kind of challenge for him. Then, Lu Shan attacked Miyao to rob her of the cloth she had received as the frontrunner, and the ensuing altercations led to Jianyi missing the deadline.

The only positive had been a minor breakthrough with his soul-strengthened will, which had helped him complete the race while he was consumed by pain.

Six months ago, multiple groups had formed, with a significant number staying together to get through the race at a decent time. Jianyi had tried to keep up with them.

Miyao and Jianyi set out at a speed that just a few months ago would have seemed suicidal.

Reasonable only on the final stretch, downhill, maybe. Not at the very beginning, up the mountain.

A few disciples followed, but most were very quickly distanced.

Jianyi marveled at the ease with which his body dealt with the challenge: his legs ate the ground up, his arms moved smoothly, and his lungs… his breath barely changed, and no hint of pain could be felt.

It was only now that he noticed how much those weeks of pain had ingrained a decent amount of anxiety in him. He had feared exerting himself to the fullest extent.

Every breath he took with his tempered lungs expelled that fear into the crisp, cold mountain air.

The first league flew by.

He had noticed this a few times before, especially when he had struggled running laps around the training ground: Running in sync with one or more others was helpful, and Miyao’s constant pace beside him helped him keep to a sustainable rhythm.

There were just two other disciples who kept pace with them. Given that he had never noticed them in Amber or Lapis, they were likely both in Jade. He must have seen them during the initial assessment, but a lot had happened since then, and, if he was honest with himself, he had not been that interested in other disciples at the time.

Not that he was eager to engage with others necessarily, but his opinion that any interaction with others his age was a waste had softened quite a bit. Elder Gao’s advice to look for potential members for their circle was also very much on his mind.

His interest in opening up to new people was limited. He hadn’t thought about it much so far, but when it had come up, he somehow always associated his group of friends with his circle. He almost faltered in his step when that thought led to the memory of Jun and the fact that they were only four these days.

Maybe Shuhua had ideas on how to solve this problem.

Deeply focused, league after league flew by, and Jianyi was shocked when they arrived on the plateau where disciples handed out the yellow cloth they needed to return to their starting position. It had felt as if hardly any time had passed.

His calves were the first part of his body that he noticed at all when they transitioned to running downhill, but it was only a slight burn, nothing more.

He had defeated spirit beasts on his own and done some spectacular things to make that happen, but never in the nine months since the Heavenly Trial had he felt what the true difference between a mortal and a cultivator was. Of course, he had flown through the countryside with Elder Sun, he had seen the man fight with speed and strength that he himself would not be able to match for decades, maybe centuries to come—but this simple mountain run, the ease of every breath, the wind in his hair as he traversed the path, made it more real than anything else before.

Seeing and feeling what it meant to be a cultivator was one thing; seeing how big the differences were amongst them was another thing. As they ran down the mountain, they passed by all those still on their way, and he sympathized with those at the end of the small field. Six months ago, that had been him. He didn’t know whether they were hurt, lacked physical talent, or were lazy, but he did know that none of them wanted to be last. None of them wanted to face whatever punishment would be meted out to those not having made sufficient progress.

Would they be forced into the body cultivation battalion?

He had barely enough time to parse all these thoughts before they were at the final league before the finish line.

The strangest thing was, he felt like he could accelerate if he wanted to, if he needed to. There were prizes on the line.

But… Elder Sun had sent him off to the wilderness so as not to raise undue attention to his progress. There was also no reason to warn his fellow disciples ahead of the sparring that he exceeded their fitness by a lot. And lastly, Miyao was still doing fine, but he had the impression that she would struggle to accelerate a lot, and it was not worth leaving his friend behind.

He settled into the final paces and enjoyed the feeling of painless training. He would inflict enough pain on himself when cultivating in the evening.

In the end, they completed the run in thirty-four minutes—a world faster than his failed attempt to complete the run in ninety minutes during the test.

Guangming, who seemingly remembered him well, congratulated him with a raised eyebrow and an impressed look on his face.

Miyao pulled him to the side, where they could sit and observe the remaining disciples. “That was quite the change compared to six months ago.”

Jianyi first chuckled, then laughed out loud—the relief of a lot of suppressed emotions breaking free. “It really was. It was genuinely fun. Not even Lu Shan around to sour the mood.”

His friend snorted. “Tell me, how much of your progress is due to your body cultivation?”

He scratched his chin, thinking. “I believe that without it, I might have been able to follow, maybe with some distance—but it would have cost me everything. Especially the second stage, tempering my organs, made this a pleasant experience.”

Miyao, whose brow was still wet with sweat, nodded. He had noticed that she displayed the same intensity that she brought to spear fighting to everything related to body cultivation. “I really think that going through Body Tempering is the right move for you. After that, you can decide which path to pursue—and you can tell your grandmother that my teacher said that it was very common in the past to pursue both,” he closed with a smile.

She nodded intensely. “I will!”

The final member of their group arrived almost fifteen minutes later than them. On the one hand, that was almost half again as much time as he, Miyao, and the other two disciples had needed; on the other hand, it was still a massive improvement versus the run six months ago.

Jianyi didn’t know what the expectations were, but the result underlined what Minghao had said many times to him: none of the other disciples stood still. Every one of them progressed. Maybe not all of them with the same discipline he did, but the best certainly did.

Good. It needed all of them to fight in the Celestial Confrontation.

***

Shuhua was in the next group scheduled for the Everlasting Ascent and stopped by while they were still waiting to be brought to the next challenge.

“How did it go?” she asked curiously, but clearly not worried.

“I was fortunate that they didn’t have a time limit this time around, so I didn’t fail,” Jianyi replied.

Both Miyao and Shuhua looked at him, eyes wide.

“Jianyi! Was that a joke? Did you just joke?” Shuhua asked excitedly, barely restraining herself from jumping up and down.

Jianyi sniffed, faking outrage. “How dare you make light of my plight!”

“Will you call me Shushu now?” she asked, grinning at him, while Miyao just shook her head.

He cleared his throat to win time. His attempt at levity might have taken things too far. Pointing to Guanming and turning to Miyao, “I think we need to go.”

The girls laughed at him as he walked away, trying not to let a moment of weakness impugn his dignity.

Shortly after, the group moved to a partitioned part of the field. Before they could enter, another group exited, Lu Shan being part of them.

In truth, Jianyi hadn’t seen or interacted with the boy in any meaningful way since their last duel. He still thought that his fellow disciple was crazy, but his emotions, anger, and outrage at his behavior had cooled off.

To his surprise, Shan nodded his head towards him, face impassive, as they passed.

Jianyi just shrugged and focused his attention on what the next challenge would be.
