Fifteen minutes before James disappeared…

“Hey, Christian!”

The Time Master greeted the Assassin King with a simple nod. Christian could not help but notice that the other man was slightly breathless. He had clearly been rushing around for the entire short duration of the melee, looking for the Time Master.

I could have saved him the time, Christian thought. But he really didn’t want to bother. Working with the Assassin King was going to be a drag. At least he pays on time.

“Good to see you,” the Assassin King said in an entreating tone of voice.

“Is it?” the Time Master asked. “You can skip the pleasantries, really. I know you need something.”

“Another little glimpse of the future, huh?”

You already know the answer to that, Christian thought. He said nothing.

“Well, I know your time is money,” the Assassin King said.

The Time Master nodded and checked one of his watches. How long has this damned conversation lasted already? Oh, only thirty seconds. It felt like four times that, at least.

“You’re right,” Christian said.

“So, how much for… well, I guess you know what I’m going to ask for already?”

“I have visions of the future in fits and starts, Assassin King,” the Time Master said. “I have no idea what you’re specifically going to request, except that knowing you, it’s going to be a pain in the ass. I also have a pretty good idea of who your request pertains to. You shouldn’t go after him, but you can’t help yourself. So, whatever.”

“Okay, so how much do you want for your time?” the Assassin King finally asked, looking slightly peeved at the way the conversation was going.

“I charge differently based on what you’re asking for. Last time you needed my help, you had me buff your speed. That is my cheapest service. This time, I’ll let you know right up front that if you want me to get involved directly and fight visibly, that would probably cost you more than whatever paltry sum you agreed to take for this suicidal assignment. I’m not interested in being on the Fisher Emperor’s shit list. If I help you, he doesn’t see me, you understand?”

“Sure, sure,” the Assassin King said, as if what the Time Master had just demanded had been expected.

But Christian was fairly certain the Assassin King had not been expecting such a sharp negotiation.

“He’s destroying souls,” the Time Master added. “Playing for keeps.”

“Really?” the Assassin King asked. He looked as if he was not surprised in the slightest.

I guess that’s what you would do, too. Probably what you’re planning to do.

“As for my other services, the more expensive stuff is where I get involved more directly or I do something more demanding or that does more violence to the timestream. If I fuck with time too much in the wrong direction, I’ll just get deleted by the System itself.”

Christian had explained this to people before. One of the ironclad rules of the System, the first thing he’d learned upon acquiring his class, was that one could not go back and change the past. It was both impossible and actually prohibited. If you tried to do it too earnestly, and you had actual time-related powers, the System would find you and erase you from existence, be you human or divine.

Gods who wanted to screw with the timestream had to use incredibly convoluted methods to do so, like predicting the future rather than waiting for it to pass, or using other universes to evade the future waiting for them in their own. On rare occasions, one managed to do something sneaky that actually altered the past, though Cronos, Christian’s patron, did not explain how precisely that had worked.

Everything he had said about that was just calculated to keep Christian from trying it.

For the moment, the Time Master was content not to test that limit.

“Yes, I know you can’t change the past, Christian,” the Assassin King said. “I do remember you told me that much.”

“Well, stopping time in a fixed area is pretty darned close,” Christian said. “It violates the laws of physics more than most of the shit you do with your powers. So, even if I get to stay completely out of view, that’s going to be at the upper end of my price range.”

“Great, anything else I need to know?”

“I have another Skill I can offer to use. Temporal Drift. That’s also, you guessed it, expensive. And you’re going to pay me for my time.”

“I thought that’s what we were already talking about.”

“No, we were talking about you paying me to use my Skills. Paying me for my time is very different. I mean you’re going to pay me by the minute, starting the moment we finish our negotiations.”

“Why? Fucking why?!”

“Because you need me. You’ve bitten off more than you can chew, and you know that. And also because I don’t really want to do this.”

“You’re that scared of the Fisher Emperor? You?”

“It’s not that, although yes, he is scary, you shouldn’t have taken on this assignment, et cetera. No, I just wanted to come here and have a good time, man. Test my capabilities. Not do some job for a bunch of System Credits. Even an outrageous amount of System Credits. It’s extra money, because you’re interrupting what should have been fun time with your work crap.”

“And how does that Temporal Drift thing work again?”

“I explained it before…” He explained again.

“Okay. How much, if I want you to follow me around and just use that Skill if I’m clearly in trouble?”

“You will get into trouble, almost certainly. For thirty minutes of my time, I’ll charge you…”

The Time Master quoted an exorbitant figure.

“That’s more than half of my fee!”

“Yeah, and if you don’t pay it, you’ll see zero percent of your fee.”

Dumbass, Christian thought. He has to know he won’t succeed without me. That’s the only reason to come crawling to me now, pretending to be nice, trying to negotiate a deal, wasting both of our time…

“For that, are you guaranteeing success?” the Assassin King demanded.

“Fuck, no,” the Time Master replied bluntly. “I’m going to follow you to where the fight is going to be, I’m going to hunker down in one spot, and I’m going to watch you get your ass kicked. When you need me, which you will, I will unleash my Skill and send the Fisher Emperor drifting through time. You can signal me, or you can rely on my judgment. But that’s the only thing I’m going to do. I’m not even going to follow you and the Fisher Emperor if the fight takes you somewhere else. I can sit in a single place, and my Static Stealth Skill will hide me from him, like I’m part of the background, but if I have to pursue you guys, all bets are off. I’m not going beyond what I’ve just outlined in any way. So, yeah, if you can’t beat him after he returns from Temporal Drift, that’s on you, not me.”

“If you can’t guarantee results, it’s ridiculous for me to hand over more than half of what I’m hoping to make.”

“Fine.” Christian quoted him a slightly reduced fee. “That’s to follow you for just twenty minutes and do the same thing.”

“There’s no more wiggle room?”

The Time Master checked his watch again. “We’d better get going if we’re doing this. If you don’t want to try it after all, it’s no skin off my nose. The Constellation King seems like he’d be a cool opponent, and I’ve been wanting to ask the Virgin Queen how her arrows work—”

“Fine, fine,” the Assassin King interrupted. “I’ll pay you. Let’s get out of here before my brain catches up with my tongue. One last thing: if the fight is going inconclusively, and your time is about to run out, just use the Skill, all right?”

Christian nodded and concealed a smile. That’s a wise request.

From there, he simply followed the Assassin King until they had reached near where the Fisher Emperor was. They were surprised to witness him destroying one of the Holy King’s souls directly, right in the open, and then even more surprised when Robard got into it with a dragon.

The Assassin King did not allow that fight to continue for very long before he inserted himself, though.

After all, he couldn’t let Christian’s entire purchased allotment of time elapse without using him. The Time Master almost wished the Assassin King had just decided to run away instead. As funny as it was, watching the dragon and Robard beat up on him, Christian really hadn’t wanted to do something that was virtually guaranteed to alienate one of the more dangerous Rulers out there.

If he finds out, and if he survives, and if he has any bandwidth after dealing with the Holy Kings, Christian reminded himself. Those were a lot of ifs. The Time Master would probably be fine.

As he had that thought, the timer on his watch went off.

Well, shit, Christian thought. It’s time for me to use the Skill.

He took another minute preparing. The Mana was already in place, and the Assassin King had done well in keeping the fight locked to a single location, but Temporal Drift took immense focus to get right.

It was also a positive in Christian’s mind if the fight became really desperate for the Assassin King before his paid ally rescued him. That should substantially decrease the odds of complaints about the price for next time.

Finally, a cloud of smoke obscured the space around the Assassin King, but the Time Master sensed a web of magical silk appearing behind him. It was time for Christian to make his move.

He concentrated on Temporal Drift and felt the latent Time Mana he had already seeded in the area manifest in the space he’d been focused on, around the individual he had locked onto. James Robard.

Send him to where he’ll lose the fight, the Time Master thought. Send him to where he’ll get his ass kicked.

That was the plan. Transport the Fisher Emperor through time, to wherever he was most likely to suffer a nonlethal but brutal defeat at the hands of someone else he was destined to fight. After that, his body would snap back to the present, complete with the existing injuries from that fight.

Please let there be a place in the future where he gets his ass kicked, Christian silently thought as he focused his power on Robard. Please don’t let him be just completely undefeated until he attains godhood or something. And above all else, please don’t let him have some Skill that would let him escape this…

The Time Master’s eyes were closed as he unleashed his Skill, so he wouldn’t necessarily even know if Robard had suddenly escaped. He might not know until he felt Robard’s blade inside his skull, perhaps even destroying his soul.

Ah, no, I feel it! Yes! Yes, I’ve done it…

Robard’s presence in the present was completely gone, the Mana had pulled him away.

Christian’s eyes remained closed, though. He had discovered, the last couple of times he’d used this Skill, that if he was very subdued and didn’t disturb the mystic portal Robard’s body was moving through, that Christian’s own consciousness could piggyback with that of his victim. And he was very interested in watching this.

Who could defeat the Fisher Emperor in the most devastating way?

Perhaps the Time Master would find his next client here.

As Temporal Drift finally dropped Robard off in the new location, Christian looked around at the new surroundings that James blinked and frowned at.

They found themselves in a land of snow and ice.

Robard stood across from a warrior in green armor.

For some reason, the Fisher Emperor looked more annoyed than surprised.

“Well, fuck me,” Robard said aloud. “If it isn’t the Ghost of Christmas Future.”

He already understands what happened? Christian questioned.
