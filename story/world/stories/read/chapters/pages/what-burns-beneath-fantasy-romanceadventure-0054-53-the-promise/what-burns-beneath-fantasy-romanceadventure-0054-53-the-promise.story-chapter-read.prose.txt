The Fae stood waiting on a white balcony. Bathed in pale, rosy light from a sunless sky clouded dove gray and blushing pink. The balcony, and the cracked glass door, were chillingly familiar. A once-ornate golden door handle lay twisted on smooth ivory. Spattered drops of my own blood stained the stones.

I crept through the grand room on numb legs. My boots made silent impressions on a thick, brocaded red rug. Once I reached polished marble, the chamber echoed with the soft tap of every step.

He turned.

I could see the sheen of sweat along his brow, make out the heavy rise and fall of his chest beneath ethereal blue silk.

He’s hurt. Or tired. The realization snaked through my belly with grim satisfaction. It was a reminder I desperately needed, at the moment. He wasn’t unshakeable. He wasn’t invincible. Is it from fighting the hag, or from something else?

He straightened. Swept dark eyes across me and smiled.

I shivered. He’s won. He knows it. One hand slipped into my pocket, gripping the little piece of metal. It felt oddly cold again.

Renner’s suggestion echoed through my mind.

I could try. I could. He’s hurt. Weakened. Still a thousand times stronger than me, but… I’ll never get another chance. It’s too small to pierce his chest, but if I aim high… I pulled the shard out, fingers iron-tight. Cool, fresh air ruffled my hair as I reached the door.

He stretched one palm out. “Finally.”

My arms trembled. My exhausted muscles tensed, ready for one last burst of strength.

“Ah. I know that look.” He held my gaze, eyes narrowing. “Don’t try, mortal. I’d rather not hurt you.”

I hesitated, heart pounding madly. Is he afraid, even the slightest bit? Can this even really hurt him? What if that was a lie, too?

“You’re entitled to your anger. I was… less gentle, perhaps, than I could have been.”

Heat surged through my veins. “Less gentle? Less gentle?! What does that even mean?!” I didn’t give him a chance to answer. If I was to die here, I’d at least do it with a shred of valiance. “You awful, wretched, cruel, vicious-”

Black eyebrows rose. “Well-”

“Horrible, salted monster! You actually made me think-! I was happy to see you! I was relieved! Can you believe that?! I thought you’d come to save me! I thought… I can’t believe I thought…”

“I did save you.” He chuckled again, head cocked to one side, looking suddenly thoughtful. “I quite enjoyed saving you, in fact.”

I shook with anger. “Because you needed something from me!”

“Not… entirely.”

I waited for more, but he offered nothing. “Why, then? Just so you could torment me afterwards? Watch me beg? Laugh at my dying friends?”

One broad shoulder lifted in a shrug. The gesture was so flippant that it made my blood boil. “I was angry. Impatient. Trying to frighten you into compliance."

A bitter laugh burst from my lips. “Frighten me? Frighten me? Well, good job. You did it. You… you frightened me. Last night was… I was…” I dug my nails into my palms. Stared up at him with loathing. “Last night a man c-climbed… climbed on t-top of me. Held me down and beat me.”

He went very still.

Iron coiled through my insides. I didn’t believe for a moment that my words had any impact, but I would say them for my own sake. “Someone held a knife to my throat. I was tied up. Gagged. Spat on. Locked in a cage. Listened while an innocent man was torn apart. Watched as my friend was nearly killed. I was nearly strangled to death!”

A muscle in his jaw twitched.

Warmth spilled down my cheeks as I forged on, voice drenched in fury. “And guess what? You. You scared me more than any of that. After all of that. Because for a moment I thought it was all over. I thought I was safe, I thought everything was alright, I was happy, and then you…”

He watched, silent and unreadable, as I wept against my open palm.

When my breathing finally slowed I raised my eyes, glaring at him. “Here.” I threw the shard. It hit his chest, then fell to the ivory with a dull ring. Just as in the cave, a rush of warmth pooled through my belly. Relief. Release. “You win.”

The Fae retrieved it slowly, gaze never straying from mine. The golden steel, absent of its usual luster, vanished into a silver-gilded pocket.

I stalked past him, to the banister. Sagged against it. Blinked through endless tears up at an impossibly beautiful imitation of dawn.

There. It’s done. And now… do I even dare hope for freedom? Will he just… let me go? Leave this awful place forever, run back to Fellbrook, to Royce and Durst and Marion and-

“Twenty years.”

I swallowed. Didn’t turn. Didn’t answer.

Strong arms braced nearby. His voice was very soft. “That’s how long the garden took.”

I turned my face away.

“I’d never thought to grow something. I’d never tried. This realm… horror was the fate of all things, here. Every desire, twisted and denied. Love. Family. Children. Our nightmare,” the word came out drenched in vitriol, like it was the vilest of curses. Then, much gentler, “The sky changed, first. Endless black, every moment of my existence, and then… one star. Just one. A little over two decades ago. It was beautiful. I watched all night, certain it would fade.”

Something in his voice almost made me turn. He sounded mournful. Raw.

“At times, I think of that night as my beginning. Many here do. Centuries of mindless survival. Endless blood. Then the fog lifted, and… life. Thoughts and hopes beyond that red haze.”

He’s a monster. Cruel. Whatever new trick this is… I won’t fall for it.

“I started the roses not long after. They wept blood for years, and still grow teeth if I’m not careful. I haven’t quite mastered… well. There are quite a few things I haven’t mastered.” A wry note crept into his tone. “It’s the soil. It drank horror for centuries, and hasn’t yet learned to dream.”

I shook my head. “Why are you telling me this?”

“Because I upset you. Frightened you.” The words were low but clear. “I would prefer you understand why, rather than fall to hysterics. Cruelty is instinct for my kind, Brin. Bloodshed comes as easily as breathing.” He chuckled. “And, like all things in this realm, I am slow to alter my course. Restraint comes with effort, and in the heat of the moment… in that heat of those moments, I was angry. Furious.”

“At me?” I snarled, keeping my back turned. “As if that justifies-”

“Ripped from my home. Disoriented. Trapped beneath dead earth with you and that wretched… I haven’t felt so feral in years.” His voice was solemn. “I only hurt the hag. You have no idea how much effort that took.”

“Thanks,” I spat. “How thoughtful of you.”

“And you seem to have no idea,” he murmured, sounding almost regretful, “of the threat you now pose.”

“What… why-?”

“Where did you learn my name?”

My heart stuttered. His… what?

I blinked down at lush green grass and midnight-blue flowers, eyes growing wide. A voice rang through my head. One of the Fae’s comments from last night. ‘Vel’s been holding out.’

Vel. Vel. No, but… that can’t… he can’t mean… “Velidan?” I whirled, mind spinning. “That’s not… that doesn’t make any… that’s your name?!”

The look on his face was answer enough.

“It can’t be! That doesn’t make any… It’s not a name, it’s… it’s a rune!” I shook my head. Nothing made sense. It’s impossible. I don’t understand. Why…

Why did my mom write his name down?

“A rune,” he growled, expression dark. “And I’m very eager to hear how you came upon it.”

I lifted my chin and, despite my cataclysmic confusion, bit out, “I’m not going to tell you.”

Narrowed eyes glinted. “Mm. And whatever strength dragged me across the realms… I don’t suppose you’ll be forthcoming about where that came from?”

I clenched my teeth. “I already told you, I’m a-”

“Runekeeper,” Velidan said the word slowly, tasting every syllable. “A nearly extinct breed. One that’s managed nothing more than parlor tricks for years. Not real power. Not for two decades. And yet, just last night, you,” he stepped closer, searching my face intently, “did something impossible.”

I wrapped my cloak around myself, shivering beneath the intensity of his stare. “I… I don’t know anything about all that. Runekeepers have always been-”

“Always,” he laughed, but there was no humor in it. “What a blessing for humanity. It’s different for us. We can’t peer through the cracks, watch your kind from beyond the veil.” He scoffed. “Not that we’d want to. Humans are such pitiful things.”

“I… what?” I shook my head, mind spinning. “What are you talking about?”

Sharp teeth flashed in a mirthless smile. “It took months to even find Fellbrook. Grasping across darkness on the word of an eyeless loghair who spoke in riddles. As for the portal itself… mm. They used to be much easier. Back when the sanctimonious bled magic through, let cracks ripple for their own ends. Blessed you. But now? A hefty blood price, indeed.” He inhaled deeply, hunger brimming in his gaze. “I nearly forgot myself.”

The words were confusing, but the malice they dripped was not. I backed away, breath coming too fast.

He followed slowly, mouth twisting into a sneer. “But I was careful. And kind. Commanded each hound to only hunt for Fae relics. I forbade even a drop of human blood spilt, unless a mortal tried to interfere.”

My lower back hit the banister. “Royce-”

“Interfered. And still, I’m left wondering. Mystified. I saw him. Smelled him. He was no one. Just a human who stank of blood and despair. What did he do? Did he take something from the hound? Hide something in your home?”

I shook my head through every question. “No. No. I’ve told you all this. He just… he just tried to keep us safe. Your dog made a mistake.”

“Or it didn’t.” His eyes narrowed to slits. “What did you have in your room, Brin?”

My pulse fluttered, voice turning shrill. “Nothing! Nothing from a Fae! The dog made a mistake. Or it was hungry and disobeyed you. Or… or…” My shoulders slumped. I dragged my gaze up, meeting his wearily. “You know what? I don’t care. I don’t. I’m done. I’m done talking. I don’t know anything, anyways. You have what you wanted. I don’t have anything to tell you. I don’t have any answers. I won’t give you any answers. Please. Please say we’re done.”

It was my turn to search his face. Dark brows furrowed, carved planes of fair skin tinted dusky red by the light.

His answer made my heart sink. “You’re far too dangerous to let leave.”

“I’m not!” I insisted, fighting back tears. “I’m not. I’m not dangerous at all, I just want to go home!”

“Always so earnest.” The Fae gave an incredulous shake of his head, scoffing in obvious disdain. “And to think I’d been so certain of your guile. But… beaten half to death. Left bloody and bound, strangled within an inch of your life… and no one intervened. Well. No one else.” A wicked smile flashed across his features. “Every shred of your fear, your confusion… this whole time, it’s actually been genuine. You’re just… fragile.” The smile grew, and he gave a mocking little laugh. “You haven’t been pretending to be helpless, you’re actually helpless. Why does someone think you’re important?”

It felt foolish to be so stung by the declarations, but my heart throbbed nonetheless. “I don’t know! I don’t understand what you’re asking!”

“You really don’t. You have no idea. You’re baffling, mortal. And yet, somehow… you’re a threat.”

“I’m not! I don’t know anything, I don’t have anything!”

“You have my name. You used it, with magic you truly seem to have no understanding of, to tear me into another realm.”

“I’m sorry! I won’t do it again! Saints, as if I would-”

“You could kill me, you know.” His tone was almost casual.

My lips parted. I stared up, stunned into silence by the proclamation.

“Mm. I didn’t think you’d quite realized that, yet.”

“I… how can… how would I-”

He laughed. It was a mirthless, malicious sound that slid around my throat as sure as a noose. “Use your imagination, Brin. You could summon me to your side beneath sunlight. Climb Thalessa herself and-”

“I won’t,” I breathed, growing numb with horror. He was right. He was right. I hadn’t even thought about such things, not for a moment, but… If using that rune could rip him into the mortal realm, then… I could kill him.

Every muscle quivered as I shrank back against the unyielding stone. Stared in true, visceral terror at the cruel visage above me. “I w-won’t. Oh, saints, I won’t. I swear. I won’t do any of that. Please-”

“We’re going to make another deal.”

And with that, all hope of freedom died like shattering glass. “No! No, I don’t want to make another… Please, please just believe me!” I clutched at him, close to screaming. “I haven’t lied to you! I don’t have anything from a Fae, I haven’t made any deals, I was telling you the truth about my magic! Saints, just believe me!”

Velidan’s carved face remained impassive. Cold.

“I won’t use it again, I promise. I promise. I just want to go home, I want all this to be over, I swear I won’t-”

“And I want you to drop the proverbial knife at my throat,” he scoffed, clearly unmoved by my pleas. “If you truly don’t intend to use it, this shouldn’t be so upsetting.”

“B-but then… we’ll still be… if we m-make a deal… I’ll still c-come here, won’t I?” There was no answer, and for a moment all I could hear was the wild pounding of my own heart. “I… I don’t w-want to keep coming here.”

A thousand pinpricks of silver glittered down. “Is it really so terrible? I’ve been a gracious host. You very nearly enjoyed yourself at times.” One hand caught my chin and his tone lilted, threaded with laughter. “Don’t think I didn’t notice, Brin.”

I turned away, clinging to the cold banister with shaking palms. Shuddering at the unwanted closeness. “B-because you l-lied, you told m-me you… I don’t want to.” The words tumbled out too fast. I slammed my eyelids shut, trying to rally some semblance of composure. “I don’t want to be here. Please. Please. I want to be free.”

He was quiet for a while. Then, low and measured, “Freedom is not a choice. You’re leaving bound, or dead.”

“But why?” Below me, vivid blue and green swam through a haze of tears. “Saints, why… why bother? Why torment me? Why not just kill me? If I’m so dangerous, if you’re so worr-”

“I could. Truth be told, I should. But you’re so… entertaining. You’re fun.” Pale hands braced beside my hips and warm breath chased the curve of my throat, making something dark and shameful pool beneath my belly even as I pressed against the stone in a desperate, futile bid for distance. His voice lowered to that hypnotic purr. “You weren’t the only one enjoying yourself, Brin.”

I nearly choked. “S-saints, don’t… h-how can you s-say something l-like… you’re threatening to murder me!” He offered no answer and I cringed away, fighting down helpless, wracking sobs. “H-how can I agree to this? I can’t! Ashes, I won’t! I won’t l-live my life afraid, h-hiding this from everyone, c-coming back here every night to s-someone who s-said he’d kill me!”

He went very still. Then, with a slowness that made me go cold, “Eventually.”

Every muscle froze. My heart slammed out a frenzied, terrified rhythm.

“It doesn’t need to be quick.” Soft lips brushed the shell of my ear, narrowing my world to the press of his body against mine. Bringing a fear I’d never felt before, never for a moment in my life, but felt now. “I imagine you’ll be here for a little while. Long enough that if you say no to this very simple request… well, I’ll have time to ask again.” Cold fingers curled into my hair. “And again. And again. And I will be less gentle,” he gave a sharp tug, sending a spike of terror straight down my spine, “every time.”

Dread crushed the air from my lungs as my mind flooded with a thousand visions of all the terrible things this monster could do to me. Trapped between flesh and stone until every shred of defiance just broke-

Renner. The thought rose on a wave of desperation. If he hurts me, Renner’s with my body. He’ll see. He’ll wake me up. He’ll save me.

The image flashed crystal-clear. Renner, shaking me awake and calling my name across the realms. The stone balcony would shiver, the sky would swirl, and safety would lay moments away.

Moments. Enough time for him to exchange torment with death. Enough time to tear out my tongue or break my neck.

A muted crack snapped me back to the present. One pale hand had tightened along the banister, creating sharp black lines in the ivory beneath. “Answer.”

I did.

One breath. That’s all it took to twist around, mouth open in a fierce cry, and reach for the silver-lined pocket. My fingers closed over familiar steel, my only hope of defense against this nightmare-

Bone-grinding pressure closed over my wrist, making my fingers spasm loose, and he yanked hard enough to send me off-balance. Metal tinged against the stones.

I tried to tear away, squealing in fresh panic, but the Fae held fast.

“Really, mortal?” He looked almost baffled, and entirely unconcerned, by my attempt to fight back.

I clawed and twisted, to no avail. Threw myself sideways, towards the fallen weapon, and was wrenched back. Tried again, with the same result.

“Brin.” A frustrated, grating sound ripped from the back of his throat on my third attempt. “Be reasonable. I’d rather not snap your neck.”

If he’d meant that as a method of dissuading me, it was a spectacular failure. I raged and railed, thrashed and screamed, my world a pinpoint of frenzied panic. Maddening terror of the nights awaiting me here in this awful prison, of a life spent bound to this monster until he decided I was no longer entertaining and decided to-

I hope Durst gets my letter.

The thought hit like a hammer. I went still. So suddenly that my snarling captor balked, clearly surprised, but I barely even saw him. I pictured something far more frightening than him.

A bed set with a large depression and rumpled blankets on one side. And on the other… smooth, untouched fabric. A silent monument to grief.

Would he weep? Tear at his hair, throw his head back, scream until his throat was raw? Or would he just be silent? Stare into my room every time he trudged down our little hallway, eyes shadowed and broad hands shaking?

Would my bed remain smooth and untouched?

“No. No.” The fight drained from my limbs. I sagged, almost against the Fae, then flinched back. “No. That won’t happen.”

Velidan blinked, seemingly taken aback by my sudden calm. “I’d rather it not, but you’re not leaving me much choice. And you’re trying to kill me.”

My chest swelled. Iron settled in my stomach. “That’s not what I… meant.” I lifted my chin, meeting those endless eyes boldly. He seemed to be waiting for more of an explanation, but all I whispered was, “What... what do I have to say?”

The Fae regarded me for a long moment, then said evenly, “In exchange for your safety right now, you will never summon me again. You will not say my name, nor write or share or indicate it in any way.”

I repeated it all unflinchingly. Tears dripped down my cheeks with every word. With every new chain.

The moment I finished, he stepped back with a heavy exhale. “We have a deal.”

I sank with a scream, ice bursting like knives in my belly. It hurt much more than the other times. Hard pressure dug into my knees as I panted against the stones. Pulled my cloak tight. Counted my roaring heartbeats as quivering muscles fought for calm. “I’ll find a different way to kill you.” The vow crawled out before I even realized it.

The Fae gave an incredulous little laugh. He stepped forwards, stretching a hand down as if to help me up, then frowned when I struck it away. After a pause he stooped to retrieve the shard. “Let me know if you do. It’s exactly what I’ve been looking for.”

The words echoed through my mind with every ragged breath. “What?”

Velidan leaned against the banister, holding the dull gold at eye-level between one thumb and forefinger. “All this effort for just one, after the loghair swore your town held two. I was thorough, yet found no trace. Miserable creature.”

I shook my head, hair swishing along the stones, and said again, “What?”

“Eloquent, as always. All these conversations, and you never once asked me what I planned to do with it.” His eyes glittered. Endless stars, fathomless black.

The air at my neck and arms grew warm. The back of my hair, my shoulders… it felt like a sun was rising behind me. I half-turned, face tilting up, but the heat had no source. The sky remained a haze of dusky rose and iron. The stones beneath me remained frosty.

Then they rippled. Shivered. My breath rushed out in relief and I curled my fingers against the ivory. Finally. Finally. Thank you.

Velidan chuckled. “I would’ve lied before, of course. I was so certain you’d run off to tell one of the others. But I still find it amusing. You’re one of the most talkative creatures I’ve ever met, yet you never even asked.”

I glowered up at him as the clouds swirled. As familiar, distant whispers and rustling velvet breathed against my skin. “Fine. I’ll bite. What do you plan to do with it?”

Full, arrogant lips parted in another smile. Endless silver flickered gold. But not from within; their sudden light came reflected from glinting, jagged steel.

He didn’t seem to notice. Just gave a soft laugh as the world slipped away and promised, as gently as a shadow, “Why, Brin… I’m going to save you.”
