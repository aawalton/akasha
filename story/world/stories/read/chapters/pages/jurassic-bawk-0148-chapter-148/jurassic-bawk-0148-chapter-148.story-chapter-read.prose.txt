Charlemagne looked at the yellow bar that indicated his vessel’s damage with displeasure.  During the battle, he had learned from the Squiggles that green was good, yellow was all right, and that red was bad, at least when it came to the remaining integrity of his ship.  If Phatagin hadn’t gotten himself killed, they would have come out of the fight in the green.  The rooster hoped that his Squire’s failing wouldn’t affect his final score, especially since he was going to be penalized by the Squiggles for the pangolin’s demise in the first place.

“Well, that could have gone better,” Phasima huffed as she landed next to Charlemagne on deck, breathing heavily but appearing to be uninjured.  “It’s a good thing that death isn’t permanent in the Delves, but that couldn’t have been pleasant for Phatagin.”

“Bawk,” the rooster responded.

“That’s…that’s actually rather horrible, have to say,” the Vessarian responded.

“Charlemagne doesn’t actually possess a noble bone in his body, sugar,” Josephine complained as she emerged from below decks.  “If it wasn’t for the penalty, he’d probably have abandoned Squire Phatagin long ago.”

“Bawk,” Charlemagne agreed.

“You’re supposed to deny it at the very least!” the Attendant yelled.

The rooster ignored the two women and refocused on the task at hand.  But, before he could enter Bluebeard’s Chamber, there was one thing he needed to do.

“Bawk,” he ordered Phasima.

“I am not diving to the bottom of the sea to get Phatagin’s body so you can eat it!  Or his Items!”

“Bawk,” Charlemagne demanded again, his anger rising.  His dreadful aura began to press down on Phasima and Josephine.  The former stood defiant, but the latter collapsed to her knees under the incredible pressure.

“Just get them so we can move on,” the Attendant begged the former Empress of the Forest.

“I can’t…he’s not actually dead, so he and his items are probably back in your chambers.  But it’s the principle of the matter, here.  Eating your enemies is one thing, eating your allies….”

“Ser Charlemagne, I need you to confirm that you understand that eating Phasima or myself would be considered a horrific act under the rules of chivalry, and thus you should never do that under any circumstances,” Josephine demanded.

But the rooster had already tuned the ladies out after Phasima had reminded him that he would see Phatagin again later.  Sufficiently mollified by that knowledge, Charlemagne ordered the captain to make full speed toward the tower’s entrance and wait there for his return.

“Bawk!” he commanded the two ladies as he took flight.  Phasima sighed as she grabbed Josephine before following the rooster toward the cavernous entrance that lay at the base of the tower.  She watched as Charlemagne disappeared through an open gate that seemed more suited for a city than a tower before breathing a sigh of relief that the Floor was almost over.  Although she found the rooster’s Physique Skill, Core, and mana channels endlessly fascinating to study, his continuous demands, bad temper, and laziness made prolonged close association with Charlemagne a trying experience, to say the least.  And that was with Charlemagne having spent much of his time off scouting.  Lost as she was in her thoughts, the Vessarian almost failed to spot the Knights that were tailing her.  With a smile, she swung around to face the incoming threat.  She had yet to pit herself in direct combat with a Knight that wasn’t Charlemagne, and despite the handicap of having to fight while protecting Josephine, she felt good about her odds.

Charlemagne, not realizing that he was missing out on all the fun behind him, swooped through the opening at the base of the tower and landed with a thump that echoed through the barren room beyond.  A quartet of burning torches failed to adequately illuminate the room, but Charlemagne’s eyes adjusted almost immediately, showing him the nondescript wooden door that he had already noticed due to its incredibly powerful and complex mana signature.

He approached the door without hesitation, ready to put this entire Floor behind him.  There had been entirely too few creatures that didn’t require an underwater excursion to fight, although the Knights he’d consumed at least made up somewhat in quality what they lacked in quantity.  Still, the rooster was growing tired of the endless waves and longed to feel soil under his claws again.  He also needed to berate the pangolin for his uselessness.

Charlemagne was caught by surprise when the Legendary cudgel lodged in his abdomen began to react as Charlemagne drew close.  Like a magnet, it began to pull the rooster toward the door, and the attraction was so strong that the rooster was pulled off his feet.  He sailed through the air and slammed into the door.  But it was not wood that he encountered but cold metal.  The cudgel inside him shifted slightly as it sought a corresponding groove in the door, snapping several of the rooster’s ribs in the process.  Charlemagne’s flesh was stressed to its limit as the weapon aligned itself and sank into the door, trapping him in a net of his own flesh.

The rooster’s excellent field of vision came in handy once again as he noted the changes to the door that he was now technically embedded in.  There were grooves on the exterior of the door that corresponded with different weapons, which explained how the door was meant to be opened.  Above the door there were three green circles, which Charlemagne determined meant that the door could be opened three more times.  Even more intriguing were the complex patterns of mana that swirled through the metal.  But when he reached out in an attempt to examine them more closely, his efforts were completely rebuffed by a mysterious force that must have come directly from the Squiggles.  He tried several different ways to examine the mana in more detail, reluctant to abandon such an amazing example of magical craftsmanship without better understanding its secrets.  After all his attempts to examine the underlying principles in more detail were rebuffed, however, he quickly grew bored and abandoned the project.

Having no reason to remain stuck to the door, Charlemagne pulled away, accepting as a natural consequence the twinges of pain that accompanied ripping the skin on his abdomen and chest away from his body.  The wounds were already mostly healed by the time he grasped a convenient metal bar with his beak and pulled the door open.  As he did, one of the circles at the top of the door changed from green to yellow.  Forgetting entirely that he probably should have waited for Phasima and Josephine, the rooster passed through the door and into the room beyond, catching only a glimpse of what it contained before swinging back around at the sound of a familiar voice.

“Catch that door for me, would you?”

Before he could stop to think about what he was doing, Charlemagne moved to block the door.  Labrissa, the Knight who had piggybacked off his victory in the Delve’s Second Floor, slithered into view, a smirk of victory on her infuriatingly peckable face.

“Thank you,” she said with a smile as she approached.

Charlemagne stood frozen, his body ignoring his brain’s commands to slam the door shut and finish the Floor without her.  The mutiny of his flesh ignited the rooster’s rage to heights that he had never before experienced: his Ignition Core began to thump wildly in his chest along with his physical heart, his internal temperature and blood pressure skyrocketed, his adrenaline and cortisol levels surged, and the rooster literally saw red as capillaries within his eyes burst under the strain.  And still, his muscles refused to obey him.  Well, that wasn’t entirely true…he could tell that his body was responding, but it would take forever for it to complete the sequence of movements required to move out of the way and close the door.

Ignoring the incapacitated rooster for a moment, Labrissa looked behind her as three new figures appeared in the round antechamber.  She spoke a few soft words to them, words that Charlemagne couldn’t make out due to the roar of his own blood.  Then the other Knight turned back toward Charlemagne, smiled, and began to slither over.

Bawk!

The rooster demanded to his body.  Labrissa said something else that was unintelligible as she drew near before stopping and frowning.  Evidently, she expected Charlemagne to do something else now.  The rooster felt a glimmer of hope at the other Knight’s displeasure…if she couldn’t reinforce her commands, he could beat her, if only his body would….

Bawk!

He demanded again, screaming on the inside for his body to move.  As he strained, he could feel something building up in his brain, something that felt like a distant cousin to the concept of Pyro that he had incorporated long ago into his Core.  But the energy seemed to be confined to his head.  Perhaps that was the problem: maybe the energy was supposed to be travelling all through his body, just like his blood and his mana did.  In the scant seconds that Charlemagne had left before someone physically removed him from the doorway, the rooster pushed even harder as he studied the strange energy.

Straining his internal senses to their limits, Charlemagne noticed that his original assessment had been wrong.  A tiny fraction of the energy was travelling down microscopic pathways that his Skills told him were called nerves, which explained why his body was slowly moving in the direction he wanted.  Furthermore, there was some sort of war going on within the energy itself, which caused most of it to dissipate uselessly inside his brain.  Redoubling his efforts to move, the rooster was rewarded with seeing an increase in the energy passing through his nerves to the rest of his body, but it was still nowhere near the amount needed to slam the door in Labrissa’s face.

Bawk!

He demanded one more time as he pressed against the mental compulsion with all his might.  Despair began to set in as, despite his efforts, he simply couldn’t muster any additional mental energy: everything that he had was already being put to use.  That feeling of despair intensified as Labrissa crooked a finger, causing another familiar figure to approach the doorway.  It was Phasima.  Slowly, but without hesitation, she reached out to grab the door handle as she floated closer.

The rooster’s rage and despair commingled to produce a last-ditch burst of violence aimed at eliminating the source of Charlemagne’s inability to act.  But, rather than send a white-hot ball of mana toward his antagonist, Charlemagne directed the burst of mana inwards, bursting countless blood vessels in his head despite his incredible control.

Using the mana, Charlemagne targeted each and every cell in his brain that was producing the bad energy, creating tiny scalpel-like blades with Mana Surgery and using them to isolate the cells producing the “bad” energy.  The memory of overcooked baguettes assailed him as the mental block lifted, allowing him to stumble back out of the doorway.  Although clearly shocked by the turn of events, Phasima reacted almost instantly, forming a block of solid ice in a bid to keep the door open.  Charlemagne’s vision began to fade as his consciousness retreated to protect itself both from the incredible damage that the rooster had done to his brain as well as the backlash from manipulating that many microscopic mana constructs at once.

Labrissa’s mouth was moving again, but Charlemagne’s hearing was gone completely, along with his sense of balance.  It was difficult for him to tell which way was up, not that it would matter for long.  The rooster had mere moments before he collapsed entirely.

Bawk!

He demanded his failing limbs one more time as the Vessarian put her hand on the door and began to pull it open.  And this time, his body responded.  With the blockage gone, the energy flowed through his body to his muscles as it was supposed to, prompting an immediate response.  He lurched forward, blood dripping from his ears, eyes, nostrils, and mouth.  The rooster’s sudden approach, combined with his crazed appearance, caused Phasima to freeze in panic.  Charlemagne reached out one last time with his mana and pulled the door shut before the Vessarian could recover, snapping the block of ice like a twig.

Alone in Bluebeard’s Chamber, Charlemagne received a message from the Squiggles.  He didn’t see it, because he was already unconscious.
