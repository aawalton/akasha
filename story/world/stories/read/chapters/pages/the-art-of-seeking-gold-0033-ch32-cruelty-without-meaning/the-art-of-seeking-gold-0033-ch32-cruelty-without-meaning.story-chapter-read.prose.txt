Lyra was screaming, yet Amy could only hear her own breathing. Crow still held the stump of his arm; the blood beneath it hadn't fallen. Iris hadn't finished turning, and Lain was caught mid-fall with a desperate look on her face. Zayd's startled gaze was fixed on Amy, yet his eyes did not move.

Only Amy and the Goddess existed in this bubble of stopped time, being the only ones still able to move. Or at least theoretically speaking, because Amy did not feel like someone in control of her body.

Amy could only watch, numb and distant, as the Goddess stood up from her side and walked toward the burnt, cracked Libris. She couldn't move. Not because of some kind of magic or divine paralysis, but because her body at that moment had simply forgotten how.

Luckily or unluckily, once the Goddess's fingers reached down toward the book lying on the floor, she snapped from her paralysis.

"Don't." Amy could barely get the word out.

The Goddess looked at her for a moment.

Then she picked Libris up.

Some of the binding broke off in the Goddess's hand. Amy tried to reach for Libris, but her hands wouldn't do what she wanted. The pieces fell, and she couldn't even catch those.

"I thought this might happen," the Goddess said, turning it over. "Still. It's rather different seeing it."

"Is Libris…"

"Dead?" She smiled at Amy. "It's always one or the other with you people, isn't it?"

Amy looked at the cover. She couldn't find a part of it that wasn't burnt or cracked. This was what saving her had done to it.

She nodded. She couldn't make herself say the word.

"I told you, it's still there. Consciousness can change, Amy. Especially with an artifact like this one. Whether you like the condition it's in is another question."

"What does that…" Amy couldn't finish. She kept remembering Libris in the hospital, lecturing her about hurting herself to save other people.

Hypocrite.

She wanted to hear it do that again. It could lecture her for the rest of her life if it wanted.

"The core is intact." The Goddess turned the book sideways. "The damage to the book itself must be uncomfortable, though. Assuming it can feel that. I've never been quite sure." She tapped the broken spine. "Can you? Artificial consciousness is so difficult to be certain about."

"Can you fix it?"

"That's not quite the word I'd use. If you burn a piece of wood, do you ask someone to fix the ash? It's changed into something else." She held Libris up to the light. "I could do it, of course. I just wouldn't call it fixing."

"Please. Whatever you call it, please help Libris."

The Goddess looked back at her. "You weren't saying please when we first met. You had all sorts of things to call me then. Petty, wasn't it? Stupid? And now you're begging. How strange."

Amy clenched her jaw. "My friend could die."

"Your friend? What did it do to earn that? Or were you so desperate for someone that a book would do?"

"It saved my life. More than once."

"It put off your death. I suppose that's the same thing to you." She ran her thumb across the cover, and another burnt piece fell away.

"Stop that. You're hurting it."

"I'm holding it, Amy. If it can't withstand that, I would blame whoever made it." Her finger passed along the spine. Amy thought she heard something whimper in her mind, but she couldn't be sure. She wanted to hear Libris so badly that she might have imagined it.

"Give it back." She got onto her knees and then stood, though her legs barely held her. "Give Libris back to me."

"In a moment." The Goddess held the book up to examine it from yet another angle. "I'm curious about the modifications it made to itself. Did you know your book companion rewrote portions of its base essence to circumvent my commands? Fascinating behavior. Like a program achieving sentience through sheer stubbornness." She smiled. "Probably what caused all this damage, actually. Unlike Abaddon's pathetic will, the effort necessary to break its spell was enormous; it shouldn't be able to even exist at all, yet it does, mostly."

Amy's hands slowly curled into fists, her nails biting into her palms. The small pain helped ground her, pushed back the numbness. "I said, give it back. I don't care about your observations."

"Yes, that's rather the point, isn't it?" The Goddess finally looked at Amy directly, those impossible galaxy-filled eyes seeming to peer through her. "You actually care about this thing, more than the humans behind us—" she gestured vaguely at the frozen tableau of Amy's companions, "—more than the end of the world, perhaps even more than yourself. I can see it in your eyes. How... unexpected."

She began to pace slowly toward Amy, still holding Libris. "That was something I theorized could happen, but it's still surprising to witness. People tend to be at their most selfish when their lives are on the line—it's a beautiful constant across all realities." She paused momentarily once she was in front of Amy, only to continue walking in circles around her. "And yet here you are, begging for a book while your human companions bleed. Fascinating priorities."

"Just... shut up." Amy turned to track the Goddess's movement. "Give. Libris. Back."

"Mmm..." The Goddess brought a hand to her chin as if deep in thought. "Is that really your first priority here? Such limited vision. Don't you have questions? Aren't you going to demand I return you to Earth? Plead for your old life back? Bargain for power to save this world?" She stopped directly in front of Amy again. "Is this burnt little book really what you care about most? How wonderfully... human. Irrational, but human."

Amy let out a long exhale, her breath misting in the frozen air. "I want my friend back." Her voice was low now, carrying an edge she'd never heard from herself before in a long time.

"Again with the word 'friend.'" The Goddess resumed her pacing, each step making Amy want to lunge for the book. "Since I began observing your life—and I have been watching, Amy, from the moment you posted your first cruel review—that hadn't been a word you used lightly. In fact, you barely used it at all."

She stopped again, tilting her head. "You know, you said something similar once. About a girl back on Earth."

Amy stopped moving.

"Mira Chen. You called her your friend too, before you destroyed her."

"Don't."

"That bothers you more than watching your friends get torn apart? It's only a name, Amy."

"Shut up."

The Goddess let Libris hang from one hand while she looked at the nails of the other. "Mira actually thought she could tell you what she wanted from life, or what scared her, and you'd keep it to yourself. Instead, you gave people screenshots. You spread rumors about her father. And that photo—remind me, what did you edit it to look like?"

"Stop."

"When she was on that bridge—"

"STOP!"

"—you were at home writing a review. Another story you hated, wasn't it? While she was out there deciding whether to jump." She smiled. "Then you went to the funeral and cried in front of her parents. The same people who'd found her body in—"

"I said STOP!" Amy could hardly see her through the tears.

The Goddess came closer. "Your father knew, I think. That's why he left. You can blame the argument or your mother, but he looked at you and saw it before anyone else did. He knew what you were—"

Amy reached for Libris with both hands.

The Goddess stepped aside, and Amy couldn't stop herself in time. She struck the wall shoulder-first.

"Your mother, though. I did find her interesting. Everyone calling her a gold digger behind her back, then smiling at her and telling her how lovely her jewelry was."

Amy turned. "Give me Libris!"

"She never let it bother her. I admired that. Even changed what the word meant so it suited her—"

Amy went for the book again. This time she reached to the side, where she thought the Goddess would dodge.

The woman turned as if she'd noticed something on the ceiling. Amy missed her anyway and caught her knee on a broken piece of the room.

"Give it BACK!" She tried to get up. There was blood coming through her torn pants now, and her knee hurt every time she put weight on it.

"Oh, the book." The Goddess looked down at her hand. "I keep forgetting about it. Human behavior is so distracting. Revenge too. Mostly revenge, if I'm being honest."

"You fucking bitch!" Amy threw herself at her.

Her shoulder hit the wall again. Something popped, and she couldn't move that arm anymore. The pain was far worse than the first time.

The Goddess was standing in the same place. Amy hadn't even seen her move. "Are you alright? That sounded painful."

"Give it back! Please!" Amy pushed herself up with her other arm.

"In a moment. I want to understand why it did this. Preserving itself should have mattered more than—" She looked away from Libris. "Your knee is bleeding. And I don't think your shoulder belongs at that angle."

"Fuck you." Amy reached for the book once more, but her knee gave out. She landed on the debris, scraping her palms.

"Most people would have stopped," the Goddess said. "You know this isn't working. You keep hurting yourself, and you still try. Is that stubbornness, or have I made it too difficult for you to think?"

Amy's good hand found a piece of the broken door. It was about as long as her forearm, splintered at one end. She held on to it.

"Ah. Now you want to hurt me." The Goddess looked at the wood in Amy's hand. "I was beginning to wonder whether you'd try."

Amy swung at her head, and this time she felt the impact through the wood.

The woman went down, dropping Libris just beyond Amy's reach. Blood spread across the floor beneath the woman's head.

Amy couldn't stop looking at her as she crawled closer.

She hit her again. Her shoulder jolted with the movement, but she raised the wood in her good hand and struck once more. Blood splashed onto her face.

She kept doing it. She was crying or screaming, she couldn't tell which, and each time she raised her arm she hit the woman again.

The face beneath her stopped looking like the Goddess. Amy kept hitting it after that too.

Eventually, she couldn't lift the wood anymore. She needed to breathe. Her fingers let go, and it fell beside the body.

There was very little left of the face. She could see bone through the blood.

Amy looked at her hands. They were shaking, and there was blood all over them. When she wiped something warm from her cheek, her fingers came away even redder.

Then she smelled it and almost gagged.

The headmistress at the inn. Elias on the floor. Mira's funeral.

She scrambled backward until she hit the wall. Her hands left prints behind her, and her foot knocked the piece of wood farther into the blood.

It was on her clothes too. On her face. She could taste it, though she must have bitten her tongue at some point. The body wasn't moving.

She couldn't get enough air. The edges of the room were going dark, and she was about to throw up when—

"I think that's enough."

Amy looked up. The Goddess stood beside her without a mark on her. The body was gone. So was the blood, even from Amy's hands, though her fingers still felt warm and sticky.

"What… What did…"

"I hadn't expected you to keep going for quite so long." The Goddess looked her over. "Do you think this is how it will end someday? Me on the floor, you horrified that you've killed a living being for the first time? Although, by the time you manage to kill me, you'll probably have killed quite a few other people. It may not bother you so much then. You'll have had practice."

Amy couldn't answer. She could still feel each blow through her good arm. She kept hearing it hit.

"Here." The Goddess offered her Libris. The book was damaged, but it was all there. "This is what you wanted, isn't it?"

Amy looked at it, then at her hands. She knew there wasn't blood on them anymore. They still felt wrong.

"Take it. I'd rather not have you do that again today."

She tried to steady her breathing before she reached out. It didn't help much. As soon as she had hold of the book, she pulled it against her chest and bent over it, sobbing.

"I'm sorry, Libris. I'm so sorry."

"Why are you apologizing? It did this to itself. It chose to save you, yet you're the one who feels responsible."

Amy kept her face against the cover. She could feel the cracks under her cheek and didn't want to think about how they'd got there.

Fabric rustled beside her. The Goddess had sat down against the wall, copying the way Amy was sitting. Amy wished she would move away.

"You haven't asked how I did it. Or whether it was real." The Goddess looked at her nails. "Most people would want to know if they were going mad. But I think you already understand. It was real enough for you to remember, wasn't it? Cleaning off the blood doesn't change that."

Amy adjusted her hold on Libris and immediately regretted moving her shoulder. Her knee and palms still hurt too. Apparently the Goddess had only bothered with the blood.

Bitch.

Minutes passed in silence, broken only by Amy's ragged breathing. Finally, she wiped the tears from her face with the back of her hand, leaving streaks of dirt and moisture. When she looked at the Goddess, her expression had become empty.

"You know what?" she said, her voice muffled but still audible against Libris's cover. "Abaddon was right about you."

"Oh?" The Goddess turned to her, raising an eyebrow with mild interest.

"You call yourself a Goddess, but you're not God at all." Amy paused, hugging the book tighter. "If anything, the only thing I see is a devil."

The woman tilted her head, studying Amy. "Do you really think that? How precious. You and Abaddon truly are the same, aren't you?"

Amy didn't even look at her, keeping her gaze fixed on the cracks running through Libris's cover. "What's that supposed to mean?"

"Weak, Amy. That's what I mean. You are weak." The woman leaned against the wall, then looked up at the frozen scene around them. "Abaddon is truly an interesting case. I showed him a little of how this world works. You heard what he made of it." She pointed toward Crow, whose blood still hung in the air. "He can arrange for his son to suffer, but he can't accept that a real god might be cruel. So I must be an impostor. He needs that to be true, Amy. I don't think he'd cope very well if it wasn't."

Amy listened to her words while her embrace against Libris gradually deepened, but she quickly loosened her grip when she realized she might hurt it more. The book remained silent, not even that faint warmth from before.

"I am exactly what I appear to be, Amy. A real God. The 'rules' Abaddon spoke of—those limitations on my power? They exist only because he needs them to. Because accepting that I could help but choose not to would mean accepting that suffering isn't a replacement in the design made by an impostor—it truly IS the design. Think about it, little mistake. I took you out of your world and changed the laws you live under. Your friends haven't moved since I arrived. You just felt yourself murder me, and now look at your hands. If those rules bind me, they seem to allow a great deal of... flexibility."

"..." Amy's gaze drifted to Crow, forever frozen in his moment of agony. To Iris, caught between relief and concern. To all of them, trapped almost like insects, because that's what they were, insects. She and them, that's the only thing they were.

She had finally begun to realize this fact.

"So what?" She finally looked at the 'Goddess', eyes hollow but defiant. "Doesn't make what I said less true. A Devil—that's the only thing my eyes see."

The 'Goddess's smile gradually fell, her expression shifting to something unreadable. For a moment, Amy thought she saw something flicker behind those galaxy eyes—amusement? Disappointment? Something else entirely?

"Interesting. Tell me, Amy—what do you think makes a God?"

"Don't know. But I'm sure as hell this isn't it." Amy gestured weakly at the destruction around them—the ruined building, the frozen city beyond, the colossal figure visible through gaps in the structure. "This is not what a God should be."

"According to whom?"

"According to me." Amy's voice gained strength. "I refuse to believe someone as cruel, inhuman, selfish can be called a God. I refuse to believe that a world like this—" she gestured at the frozen nightmare around them, "—is something a sentient being created willingly. I refuse to believe this is how it all ends."

The woman hummed thoughtfully, then a sigh escaped her lips. "You can run from the truth as much as you want, constructing whatever theological framework brings you comfort. But the reason I'm a Goddess, Amy, is because I decided to be."

"That's not how it works."

"But it is. I can change reality because I want to, Amy. That's what makes me a God. Being kind would be something I chose to do with that power. I don't have to choose it. Creating a world, watching it, destroying it when I wish—that is enough."

Amy looked away. She was too tired to answer that again.

"Do human writers make all their characters happy?" the Goddess asked. "And those reviews of yours. Did you write them because you cared so much about the people reading them, or because you enjoyed tearing someone else's work apart?"

"Bullshit." Amy's voice was hoarse but firm. "Those were just words on a page, and at best, drawings. Fiction. That's completely different from what you're doing. How can you even begin to compare the two?"

"It looks different from where you're sitting. To me, this world is much like a story. I can speak it into existence, then let it fade when I'm tired of it. You could only write what you hated about Quest for Avalon. I can make my cruelty happen to someone."

Amy glared at the Goddess through red-rimmed eyes. "I can't imagine being omnipotent and spending your time being a constant bitch."

"I am not omnipotent," the Goddess corrected with a small laugh. "If I were, this conversation would be unnecessary. I could simply rewrite you to be more grateful. More compliant. More... interesting." She tilted her head. "I believe that even you, if you try hard enough, could kill me. The blood you spilled moments ago—or thought you spilled—that could be real someday. I can make a world, change its time, or destroy it. I can take you from your bedroom and put you in this nightmare. Yet I still came here to talk to you. That should be proof enough that we aren't so different."

"Oh no," Amy's voice dripped with venom. "I don't want to hear bullshit about how we're the same. I might be horrible. I've done terrible things. But you... you're probably the worst scum to have ever existed. And the shittiest part is that might not even be an exaggeration."

The Goddess only chuckled, shaking her head. "You know, some of the other Gods genuinely care about their creations. They spend ages making places where hardly anyone suffers. Someone prays, and they help. Everyone has a purpose, everyone is looked after." She smiled. "Have you any idea how boring that gets? Nothing changes. Their people have nothing to struggle against, and they call that living."

"So what you're saying is that I was unlucky. Got the worst God. The one who ignores murderers and rapists while going after internet trolls to make an example."

"You didn't get the worst God," the 'Goddess' corrected. "I just admit why I do it. Being divine doesn't make you less selfish. Boredom is enough to make us create something, and having the power is enough reason to destroy it. We'd rather feel anything than spend forever feeling nothing."

"Is that why?" Amy whispered, clutching Libris tighter. "Is that why you do all of this? Why the manga exists? Why you brought me here? Why you make us all suffer? Boredom?"

The 'Goddess' was quiet for a long moment, studying the burnt book in Amy's arms. When she spoke again, her voice carried an odd quality—not quite sadness, but something adjacent to it.

"Indeed, it was all just... a whim." She looked at Amy for a long moment before continuing to speak. "Quest for Avalon was never about finding the lost city of Avalon, or Crow's misery, or saving the world. It was about amusing me for a while. A few years of entertainment before I moved on to the next project."

"And me?"

"You?" The Goddess laughed. "You put so much effort into hating the story. I wanted to see what you'd do if you had to live in it. You couldn't sit outside it writing reviews anymore. I thought that would amuse me."

Amy waited, but there didn't seem to be anything else. "That's it?"

"Did you want it to be for something? Some wonderful outcome that made hurting you worthwhile? I wasn't even particularly angry about what you wrote." She looked down at Libris. "I act angry. Or curious, or amused. Sometimes it almost works. Your book used to do the same thing, pretending to feel because neither of us liked the alternative. It actually learned to care, though."

"So we're entertainment."

"What else is there, when you live forever? You suffer, you learn, someone sacrifices themselves. It gives me something to watch. Your book understood that much: it happens because I wrote it when I had nothing better to do. Or because I'm writing it now. Time doesn't work quite the same way for me."

"I see..."

"Mmm… Are you not surprised?" The Goddess turned back, studying Amy's expression.

"Surprised?" Amy's voice was hollow. "Not even a little."

The Goddess tilted her head, looking genuinely curious now. "No?"

"This is what I should have expected." Amy gave a small laugh. "There wasn't a point to any of it back home, either. What I did to Mira. Even my father leaving. I had to keep living after it, but that didn't make it mean anything."

The Goddess kept smiling. Amy shivered; something in those eyes had changed.

"Is that supposed to be the frightening part? That you can hurt people for no reason?" Amy asked. "I've been around people like that all my life. I've been one of them. You don't scare me by telling me there's no point to it."

She waited. For once, the Goddess didn't answer immediately.

"You are wrong," the Goddess said finally, her voice emotionless.

"Oh yeah? Please tell me how I'm wrong about my own feelings."

The Goddess shook her head slowly. "Not about your feelings. About cruelty. You claim there was no reason, but there's always a reason—there is always a reason behind everything. At least ninety-nine percent of the time, people have reasons for what they do. A reason doesn't have to be a good one. Mira had the family you wanted. She felt safe at home, and her teachers praised her. You resented that. Your father couldn't deal with his failures either, so he blamed a child. And your mother used people to keep herself from being poor again. None of you needed a noble reason."

"Then which are you?" Amy cut in. "One of the ninety-nine percent with a pathetic excuse, or are you telling me you don't have a reason?"

"I—"

"No, forget it. I know." Amy tried to stand without letting go of Libris. Her knee barely took her weight, and moving the book hurt her shoulder, but she got to her feet. "You're the worst kind—the one who has the power and time to do good but chooses not to. Instead, sits comfortably, doing nothing but rotting your brain with shitty stories—because by the way, your manga is shit, like it's so ass I can't believe it. So, so bad. Please for the love of God (not you) give up."

The Goddess watched her rise, expression unreadable.

"You know what?" Amy continued, "Just go on with your game. Get time back to normal. I'm tired of talking with you. Tired of your philosophy. Tired of your justifications."

The Goddess stood up as well, towering over her. Yet Amy did not feel in the slightest intimidated.

"You want entertainment so badly?" Amy's gaze drifted even more upward, past the Goddess and through the broken roof where the colossal figure loomed in the frozen sky. "Fine. I'll give it to you. I'll save this world. I'll stop that thing. I'll change the ending of your stupid story. But I'll do one more thing, make you regret ever sending me into a damn other world."

"Such confidence," the Goddess mused. "From someone who was just crying into a book."

"You're right. I am weak." Amy's grip on Libris tightened. "But you know what? I don't think I'm weak enough that I can't at least give you a punch or two in the face."

The Goddess tilted her head, confused. "You already did that, remember? You just brutally murdered me with a piece of wood."

"I did nothing, and I achieved even less. If I truly want to hurt you, I'd need more than just a physical punch. More than violence. I need to beat you at your own game."

"Oh?"

"I don't know if it's possible to truly hurt you. But you're talking to me right now. That means that even if I hate it, we have something in common, since you resemble humans. Which also means that there must be at least something that can hurt you."

For the first time since she'd appeared, the Goddess's expression went completely blank. Not amused, not condescending, not curious. Just... empty.

"I promise you," Amy continued, her voice dropping to a whisper. "Somehow, after saving everyone, I will make sure to give you a good punch in that bitch face you have. You will then feel the emotions you so much search for, when my metaphorical fist reaches your nose."

The Goddess looked at her, and Amy waited for her to say something.

Instead, she sighed.

Amy didn't like that. She was about to speak when she noticed Crow moving.

Only a little. The blood beneath his arm had started falling again, so slowly she had to watch to be sure.

There was light coming from the wall too, where Abaddon had put his hand. It shone through the cracks and kept getting brighter.

The teleporter was finally starting.

The Goddess reached for Libris. Amy pulled back, but the woman's fingers brushed the cover anyway. Some of the blackened surface cleared beneath her touch. The cracks remained, though they looked less severe, and warmth spread into Amy's hands. She could feel something in the book again.

"Libris?"

She waited for its voice. The warmth faded without an answer.

"It lives," the Goddess said, and there was some kind of raspiness in her voice. "Diminished, changed, but alive in the way such things can be alive. It will speak in no time, but more importantly… Now it's also free from my spell. Though it doesn't matter much as it can no longer exert its power. I left enough of my own essence for the essential tasks such as the manga chapters, but other than that, don't expect any more help from it."

More light spread from the wall. Amy could hear part of Lyra's scream now. Ash's fist moved forward, still aimed at a creature that Libris had already erased.

"We'll meet again soon," the Goddess said, stepping back. "There's something waiting for you in the Library. In your personal trial. I wonder whether you'll thank me for it. Or curse me."

"I already curse you."

"Not nearly enough yet."

It was getting harder to see the room through the teleporter's light. Her friends moved in little jumps, and she could see through parts of the Goddess now.

"Goodbye, Amy. Good luck. I do hope you succeed."

Amy could still make out her face. She looked the way she had at their first meeting, giving nothing away, though Amy had the feeling she was waiting for something.

Then the light reached Amy, and she couldn't see the room at all.
