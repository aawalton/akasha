[Time Span]: 1987 — 1988[Location]: Caroshadel / White City / Yggdrasil Academy

The leather satchel sat at the dead center of the table.

Moonlight cut through the gap in the cheap linen curtains, landing square on the scuffed leather. The water damage in the plaster above had dried into the shape of a screaming skull.

Inside the satchel: five hundred thousand Solars.

Three months of simultaneous bounty contracts, lethal spatial dives, hazardous artifact disposal, and a sustained, methodologically sound campaign of grift. They needed one million for tuition. They were exactly halfway there.

Paddy woke.

She didn't move her head. She just opened her right eye, stared at the satchel, and didn't count it. She didn't need to.

The exact number, down to the last fleck of gold-dust, was etched into the architecture of her brain with a permanence that suggested it would survive decapitation.

Slowly, beneath the blanket, her left hand crossed her torso. Her fingers locked around her own right wrist. She pulled both hands down and tucked them flat against her stomach.

She closed her eyes.

She slept the way a predator locked in a cage with another predator sleeps. Proximity alarms at full sensitivity. Every creak of the floorboards logging an entry. Every thought that surfaced from the dark was the same thought, turning over and over, wearing a groove into the floor of her mind without ever becoming an action.

Her eyes snapped open.Across the room, Adeline's hand was pulling back from the edge of the satchel.

Neither of them said a word.

By morning, the money was still there, which meant they had an infinitely more complicated problem.

The Baroness in Valdrecht had standing orders on sight. The guild contact in the Hellreef was still looking for her signet ring.

They needed a recommendation letter. From someone with enough institutional weight to absorb the liability they represented. Someone who had, miraculously, never been scammed by either of them.

A name hovered in the space between them. It was heavy. It was golden.Irene.

They had met her exactly once. They both possessed the bizarre, groundless, unshakeable certainty that if they walked to her door and asked, she would write the letter. She would ask if they had eaten. She would mean it.

Adeline was cleaning her fingernails with a small, terrifyingly sharp dagger. The blade slipped. A thin line of red opened across her thumb. She didn't flinch. She watched the bead form.

"Not her," Paddy said, her voice flat."Never her," Adeline agreed, and pressed her thumb to her palm.

They sat in silence. Then Paddy unrolled a sheet of parchment."There is someone in the White City," she said. "The Mansion of Dragons."

Adeline's eyes lifted. "Lee Rodeline Lancaster."

The operational difficulty was architectural.

The Grand Luminary did not attend galas. She did not entertain petitioners. Her estate was a fortress of academic isolation; entry required a formal introduction from an Arch-Witch of equivalent standing.

Paddy and Adeline had no introduction. They had a loitering problem.

For three consecutive days, they paced the perimeter of the Mansion's wrought-iron gates. They analyzed the patrol rhythm of the warding constructs. They stared at the heavy oak doors until their eyes burned.

By the afternoon of the third day, the two estate guards had stopped pretending to look elsewhere. Hands rested on the hilts of their staffs. The decision to formally arrest the two vagrants was approximately sixty seconds from execution.

Paddy let raw mana bleed into her fingertips, preparing to deploy a smokescreen and run.

The oak doors clicked.The guards snapped to attention. Paddy aborted the spell.A maid stepped out onto the marble portico.

The uniform was immaculate: black-and-white, apron precisely starched, frills cascading with architectural exactness. Beneath the hem of the long skirt extended a slender, white-furred rat tail. Atop her head, white-tipped rodent ears framed a pair of eyes the color of a frozen lake.

She looked at Paddy. She looked at Adeline.

"If you are guests with matters to discuss," she said, her voice a hollow, crystal-edged chime that seemed to originate from somewhere inside the bones, "please follow me into the mansion."

She did not wait for a response. She simply turned and walked back into the dark interior of the hall.

The two of them froze.Then their eyes met.Lancaster.

They slipped through the oak doors before the guards could process the timeline.

The Mansion of Dragons was not a house. It was a continent folded into a hallway.

Floor-to-ceiling panels of spell-glass lined the corridor on both sides. Behind the glass: volcanic ranges choked with ash. Sub-zero tundras swept by blizzards. Acidic swamps bubbling with toxic gas. And in every biome, massive, unhurried shadows moved.

Adeline cleared her throat."I have always held Dragon Ecology in the highest esteem," she said.

Behind the polite tone, she was red-lining her own memory, aggressively stripping out fragmented texts and overheard academic debates.

She asked three highly specific questions about bio-magical conversion rates in the Core-Energy Order. The questions were technically brilliant and deliberately incomplete: the exact frequency required to trigger the pedagogical instincts of a master who enjoys explaining things.

Lancaster's blue eyes caught the light of a simulated magma vent as she walked, her voice drifting back down the corridor.

"To a Witch," she said, "dragons are raw material that breathes. Ecology as grammar. Three thousand, five hundred species housed in these folds. Nearly the entirety of the Dragon Kingdom classified. Perhaps one day, the Witch race would discover its own evolutionary path through them."

Paddy stepped into the gap Adeline had carved.

"Your classification methodology is exactly what the field needs," she said, her tone easy, conspiratorial. "It's staggering out there. Half the mercenaries I've worked with can't tell a Terra Dragon from a Prime Dragon at range. And don't get me started on the ones who use 'Dragon Kingdom' and 'Dragon Class' as synonyms. You come back. Your equipment doesn't."

Lancaster stopped walking.She turned fully to face them.

She held one particular belief that the rest of Witch civilization dismissed as mysticism: that raw, world-altering talent possessed a scent. Not a metaphor. A physical, olfactory fact.

Standing in this marble corridor, looking at these two exhausted, razor-alert vagrants, she breathed in.

It smelled like crushed ironweed, copper blood, and night-blooming belladonna. It was aggressive, suffocating, and breathtakingly potent. It was the scent of something that would strangle a garden to ensure it got enough sunlight.

"Do you wish to study at Yggdrasil Academy?" she asked."Yes," Paddy and Adeline said, in unison.

Adeline's eyes tracked Lancaster's posture, bracing for the condition. The debt. The decade of service. The impossible favor.

Lancaster walked to a writing desk beside the glass panel of a tundra enclosure. She withdrew a sheet of heavy, gold-embossed parchment, uncapped a fountain pen, and began to write.

The nib scratched against the paper.Thirty seconds later, she folded the letter, sealed it, and held it out.

Paddy took it. The sensation was precise: like stepping onto a stair that wasn't there.

Lancaster looked at the two of them."I took the Lucent sisters as my disciples," she said. "Nino and Heidi."

Paddy went very still. Adeline's gaze moved to Lancaster's face."In my eyes," Lancaster continued, "they did not possess the magnitude of talent that the two of you do."

Adeline inclined her head. "You're far too generous, Grand Luminary."

The Horned-Rat Witch looked at her. The white-tipped ears did not move. Her nose twitched, once, almost imperceptibly, like a compass confirming its bearing.

"I speak from the bottom of my heart," Lancaster said.

The letter was secured.They still needed five hundred thousand Solars.

They walked out of the Mansion of Dragons, the gold-embossed parchment heavy in Paddy's coat. Neither of them spoke of it. Neither suggested leveraging the letter to find a wealthy sponsor.

They returned to their rotting White City apartment, placed the recommendation at the very bottom of the leather satchel, and went back to work.

Three more months.

Ninety days of fractured sleep, blood-soaked bounties, and every legal form of hazardous employment the White City had to offer.

They never mentioned the letter. They just bled for the second half of the money, silently and methodically, until the count inside the satchel hit exactly one million.

Then, and only then, did they pack their bags.

The entrance duels were a formality. Designed to screen the ambitious from the capable, to determine whether practical application matched theoretical foundations.

They had not been designed for this.

Paddy's opponent, a seasoned upper-year, opened with a flawless, silent weave, a [Spatial Lock] designed to crush the arena's gravity in a single frame. The geometric array was architecturally perfect.

It didn't matter.

Before the spatial fracture could snap shut, Paddy flooded her own musculature with a violent Draconic surge, crossing thirty yards in a localized sonic boom.

She didn't counter the spell; she bypassed its axis entirely and drove a concentrated kinetic shockwave directly into the senior's sternum, folding three layers of passive defensive wards like wet paper and blowing her across the arena floor like a fired artillery shell.

In the adjacent ring, Adeline's opponent deployed a frame-perfect [Absolute Containment Sphere], a high-speed, multi-layered abjuration fortress that left zero tactical blind spots.

Adeline didn't attempt to shatter it. Her eyes tracked the microscopic, silent flow of the senior's mana weave.

In the fractional millisecond where the defensive array drew upon the ambient atmosphere to equalize its internal pressure, she slipped a concentrated, concept-level paralytic toxin directly into the spell's intake filter.

She stood motionless, watching with polite detachment as her opponent collapsed quietly within the safety of her own pristine, unbroken fortress.

The proctors wrote nothing on their clipboards. They handed over the dormitory keys.

Room 314. South Wing. Spider-silk sheets. Climate wards humming at an unchanging twenty-two degrees Celsius. A private alchemy station with silver-lined ventilation hoods.

Paddy checked the window latches and calculated the drop to the courtyard. Adeline swept the baseboards for acoustic surveillance arrays.

By unspoken agreement, they turned around and walked back out the door.

Yggdrasil Academy was not a place. It was a statement.Every gilded mansion they had ever infiltrated looked like cheap theater props from here.

Paddy held a cone of ice cream in her right hand. After tuition and materials, their combined net worth amounted to pocket lint. She had walked up to the cart and bought it anyway.

"The eastern patrol ward has a three-tenths-of-a-second blind spot when it cycles," Adeline murmured beside her, eyes tracking a cluster of upper-year students. "The heir to House Valerius just passed us with her spatial ring hanging loose on a chain. I could take it before she finishes her sentence."

"We're trying to graduate," Paddy said. "Not get deported.""I am merely cataloging opportunities," Adeline replied. "A Witch should know her environment."

They turned a corner and left the sunlit courtyard behind.

The corridor ahead was cooler. No windows. The walls were carved from seamless obsidian, the only illumination coming from the soft, permanent glow of names etched in solid gold.

Thousands of them. Architects of magical theory. Champions. High Councilors. Packed so tightly the columns blurred into solid bands of gold.

No portraits. No statues. Just names, dates, titles: the quiet, crushing arrogance of an institution that had produced so many world-altering Witches.

Paddy and Adeline walked in silence.Paddy's boots stopped.Adeline stopped beside her.

The name was there, halfway down the corridor, catching the ambient glow.

Irene Berenice Habsburg.1987 World Champion.1981 Yggdrasil Grand Prize.1980 Yggdrasil Grand Prize.

The text was small. The wall had too many names for it to be otherwise.

Paddy took a bite of the ice cream. The frost-mint bloomed against her tongue, cold and sharp and very good.

White City ice cream, she thought. Better than Caroshadel.

She kept walking.The sound of Adeline's boots resumed behind her.
