The world was bathed in silver light.

I stood on an ivory dais beneath the looming moon and endless constellations. Pearl-hued double doors and a white-walled building towered over me. Cool wind brushed my hair back, and with it came a faint chime.

I turned. The dais curved down into pristine steps and an expanse of lush, fragrant plants. Further away a familiar line of willow trees swayed in the breeze, their cascading leaves tinged silver by moonlight. A glass wind chime, carved into some indistinguishable shape and tinkling quietly, hung from one of the branches.

The sky’s the same. It’s still a dream, of course, but it’s… like the other ones.

I examined the building. Walls of marble set with great gleaming windows stretched far to either side. The roof arched upwards in elegant, gilded spires.

It’s not real. It’s a dream. Just a dream. A beautiful dream palace that’s definitely not real. I chewed my lower lip. Should I… knock?

The thought was so odd that I giggled aloud. Immediately the chime fell silent.

I turned back and started. There, perched on a bough above the glass, was an owl. It stared at me with round, golden eyes set deeply within sleek sable feathers. The leaves and branches around it were hued with cold light, but the bird’s shape seemed incongruously dark. Almost like the falling silver couldn’t quite touch him.

An owl. They have owls here? Not that I even know where here is, and of course I recognized a few things in the garden. And there’s willow trees. So why shouldn’t they have owls?

It was a very beautiful owl. I was starting to think that everything in this place was very beautiful. Almost like something out of a… well, out of a dream. I snorted.

“Uncivilized, aren’t you?” The owl said dryly.

I shrieked and jumped back, nearly tripping on the ivory. Wind rustled and the owl hooted out a chuckle.

It can talk! But… maybe I shouldn’t be surprised. Why shouldn’t owls talk here? It’s a dream, after all! It’s not as though it’s… real.

I glanced down at my smooth, unscarred hands. Dread coiled through my stomach.

It can’t be real.

The large bird eyed me critically.

I mirrored the look. Either this was a dream, in which case nothing was to be believed or feared… or it was something else entirely. And perhaps a talking owl wasn’t the best source of information, but I didn’t see any other options at the moment.

“You… can talk.” I walked towards his perch. Soft grass and pleasantly cool soil cushioned every step.

The owl clicked his short talons against the branch. “I can.”

I spent a moment debating what to say. Then, uncertainly, “Am I dreaming?”

“Are you? What a curious thing to be uncertain of!”

I crossed my arms and answered, somewhat indignantly, “Are you always sure when you’re dreaming?”

“I don’t sleep.”

“Oh. Well, you didn’t answer my question.”

“I did, though ‘twas apparently not to your satisfaction.”

I’m being chided by a bird. Although I suppose that’s one of the least strange things to happen over the past few days…

I peered up. If this was a dream, then perhaps a talking owl wasn’t so unusual. If it wasn’t a dream- like how my time in the garden had somehow not entirely been a dream- then… well, birds couldn’t talk. Which begged the question…

“What are you?”

“Why, I’m an owl, of course.” His feathers ruffled. “Ah, I do remember this habit. Humans and their endless, inane questions! ‘What do you want,’ ‘why are you doing this…’ Hmph. I eat because I’m hungry, of course.”

“You… eat…” I backed away. Never mind that he was only the size of a large barn cat; I felt it was only wise to be cautious of just about anything in this place.

“Oh-ho, don’t fret! He preened his already-glossy feathers. Then, with a sly curve at the corners of his beak, added, “It’s considered, ah, poor taste among our kind to hunt another’s prey.”

My blood went cold. “P-prey?”

The curve widened.

Heavy quiet settled between us. I curled my toes into the cool grass, arms wrapped around my middle, and whispered, “I… I’d like to wake up.”

He twittered a laugh. “Mortals always want. Here you are, witness to glory, and all you can do is want something else.” His sleek head tilted upwards, gaze fixed on the moon.

“It’s very beautiful,” I said carefully, not wanting to offend him. “But I don’t belong here.”

“The young lord seems to disagree. Fae want, too, little morsel.” His beak parted, then snapped shut with a sharp clack.

So that’s it, then. Saints, I’m being brought here. Intentionally. “The young lord?”

“Mm. He and I haven’t met. Well, not that he knows. But I do quite like what he’s done with this little portion of the Night Court. Just a few years ago it was dreadful. And now? Why, I’m enchanted!” He clicked his talons against the branch, making the chime tinkle, and inhaled deeply. “Flowers, food, and a female. The makings of a fine night for any Fae.”

I shivered and turned a slow circle, gaze rising to the enormous building.

“Is he in there?”

The owl chuckled.

“Well… maybe I should go that way, then.” I nodded towards the forest and its graceful, swaying treeline.

“Oh-hoh, aren’t you bold! Verily, mortal, that way holds your darkest nightmares. Go if you want, though. It’s been far too long since I’ve heard human screams.”

I felt my face drain of color. “B-but you said that I’m safe! That no one will-”

“I know the meaning of respect. Not all of the Court’s denizens are so considerate, and most won’t find you quite so… endearing.” He gave another soft laugh, head twisting.

Wind whispered at my skin and swept my hair aside. I wrung my hands. Finally I dared to whisper, “W-what is he going to do to me?”

Feathers rustled as the owl swept from his perch and landed in front of me.

Enormous eyes peered up. This close, I could see them more clearly, and upon looking down I staggered back. Something loomed within the little creature’s eyes. Or perhaps not within, but… behind. As if someone else gazed out through the glowing gold.

“I think you should go find out.”
