Chapter V.XLIX (5.49) - Mindful Experiments

Warm water enveloped Kizu as he leaned his head back and stared up at the wooden ceiling. His body still ached from the beating and the hot water stung his wounds. He welcomed the pain. Kizu couldn’t help but feel it had all been deserved. What had he been thinking these last few months? He’d been rationalizing his actions and putting it out of his mind. When he truly pondered on the nuances of his decisions, it sickened him. He wanted to get stronger and had told himself that it was in an effort to protect his niece. A lie hiding behind a thin truth. In reality, Kizu was using Anata like a stepping stone in his idiotic chase of her mother. A mother that disappeared and left Anata in the clutches of a Blood Lord menace. It was all in an effort to further his own selfish goals. He wanted so badly to save Anna that he damned Anata in the process. Someone innocent who loved him unconditionally.

Kizu ducked his head under the water and let out his breath. For nearly a minute, he stayed in that position with his lungs begging to be filled once again. Once the burning ache became too much to bear, he complied and broke through the surface.

Taroe was correct. What he’d done was unforgivable, even if Anata forgave him. She was a child and he’d treated her as an object. Children were meant to be the parasitical ones in the natural world, not the other way around.

At the moment though, he wasn’t certain what to do. Obviously, he would stop taking his niece’s blood. How did he make it up to Anata though? When he’d tried to explain the situation, she didn’t even understand what he’d been doing wrong.

Not to mention, he already felt the itch in the back of his eyes that hinted at withdrawal. He’d need to brew up a solid supply of the addiction stabilizing potion Roba had taught him. It was a temporary solution though. Even just from the listed ingredients, Kizu recognized the fact that his body wouldn’t be able to rely on this brew for long. He’d become immune to it in a matter of months.

Dropping the lower half of his face into the water, Kizu let out a breath that bubbled on the surface.

With the curtains pulled around him, he could almost pretend like he didn’t have a thousand different problems contending for his attention all at once. More than half of those problems were all tied to the single issue of his academy ranking.

While he’d unquestioningly made excellent progress, he’d now stagnated. There was simply only so much he could do with the time at his disposal. Anata’s blood had been the only thing keeping him functional up to this point. It not only refilled his own supply, but kept him active on only a few hours of sleep. Even this bath felt like a luxury he couldn’t afford.

Without fully leaving the bath, Kizu leaned over the edge and rolled his scrying orb on the floor in front of himself.

Sitting in the hospital wing bed for hours on end had gotten tedious, so he’d convinced Roba to let him take the paper tests under the supervision of one of the James golems. After he’d been discharged from the hospital wing, Roba had taken a sample of his blood for the ranking system. Since he’d just recently met with Professor Ignis about his musical ranking before the debacle with Taroe, his scrying orb should be fully up to date now with his current status on the academy’s leaderboard.

He pulled up the stats and scanned them. Finally, for the first time since Taroe’s beating, Kizu let himself smile.

Combat 100, Astronomy 2, Divination 60, History 271, Politics 61, Rejuvenation and Restoration 352, Conjuring 800, Brewing 1, Numerology 18, Music 74 (Piano), Enchanting 81, Illusion 28, Elemental 35.

Roba might have uncovered the secret to how he’d mysteriously advanced his magic, but now his new leaps and bounds in the paper tests would drive the woman insane.

With near full access to the depths of Zayne’s memories, Kizu had been able to pull out information otherwise buried in his soul companion’s consciousness. While the system of more advanced arithmetic and algebra was drastically different from Kizu’s, Zayne actually knew far more than Kizu on the topic. It was simply a matter of transplanting the formulas to match what was being asked of him in the tests. Different methods to obtain the same results.

The lower easy ranks out of the way, he’d basically plateaued hard in his medical studies. Especially since now he no longer had access to Taroe to ask questions to. Still, ascending 400 rankings in a single semester was nothing to scoff at. He’d surpassed half of the student body. Zayne’s memories might not have given him actual magic, but they did give him extensive knowledge on the human anatomy and how it functioned. That, in turn, helped speed up his practice with the flesh golems Taroe had let them experiment on in class.

Politics posed another strange challenge. While most of the specifics involving political moves and laws erected had to be studied on his own, Zayne did offer unique perspectives on how to run a nation and the pitfalls failed empires encountered. That political theory knowledge of understanding of how laws rippled outward and changed the populace put all the specific laws Kizu did study into a new context. Everything clicked together better when he pulled from Zayne’s memories.

The same could not be said for history. Unfortunately, all of those ranks had been earned simply through hours upon hours of book reading. Zayne knew a fair bit about ancient history, likely far more than Krimpit, but those sorts of questions barely ever made it onto the paper tests for rank evaluations. Honestly though, it might be for the best since Krimpit’s account on history might differ drastically from Zayne’s. Best to avoid repeating the events from his first History F class.

The rest of Kizu’s scores were simply a result of vigorous practice throughout this last semester. He’d sincerely pushed himself to the edge with his magic.

He’d obtained a decent mastery of scent illusions and his fire elemental spells were more powerful than ever. He recently had been practicing using flames to propel himself forward, a technique Aoi’s new bodyguard, Ueno, had volunteered to teach him the basics of. While it was a little redundant with his current level of spatial magic and borderline dangerous depending on where the flames went upon casting the spell, Kizu had to admit that blasting himself around with explosive flames was extremely fun.

“I… might actually be able to do it,” he said to himself, counting up the total number of ranks. Added all up, it equaled 1883. A bit less than nine-hundred away from his goal of sub one-thousand. Next semester, all of the fifth-years would leave the academy, knocking out a ton of the top ranking slots. Even with his conjuring rank remaining at the absolute bottom, he just might be able to slip under his goal. His heart quickened at the thought. An end to his quest to locate his sister. Or, maybe just the beginning.

A part of him wanted to spend the next several hours researching and mapping out each of the fifth-years’ ranks and how much he could expect their departure to shift the overall rankings. While that certainly appealed to him on a very deep academic level, Kizu resisted the urge. His time was better spent whittling away the ranks above him than dreaming and theorizing about possibilities.

After all, his first tournament bracket was just around the corner. Victory there would help him ascend those final combat ranks.

And, of course, there was a mission between the brackets he’d committed to achieving. Last delve into the World Dungeon, Kizu had found himself woefully unprepared. Now, after a year of brewing, he now had a decent variety of potions stored in his spatial ring. He felt far better equipped to handle the challenges under his feet. Still, he kept foreseeing more circumstances where he’d require something not yet prepared. It was an endless chase.

He drew himself out of his bathtub and activated his drainage system. He’d enchanted a sponge with spatial storage to absorb the bathwater to an unprecedented degree. It hadn’t been nearly as difficult as he’d first assumed, since the enchanting process worked alongside the sponge’s natural effects. He suspected that if he’d attempted the same spatial absorption with a plank of wood or a random stone, he’d have encountered far more hurdles in his process.

Kizu dried himself off with a towel and tossed on a plain white robe with matching gloves. It looked like garb a priest might wear. While he’d never dare wear it outside of his lab and nursery, he’d enchanted these clothes with acidic and fluid resistances. He’d chosen the color white because it helped him spot whenever part of his enchantments started to fail.

A few minutes later, Kizu stood over the small cauldron he’d left simmering while he bathed. It was almost ready to go. He’d needed to improvise and swap out a few ingredients from the normal recipe, so the liquid was thinner than he hoped for.

He dropped shredded pedals of a pale flower into the mixture and stirred them in. The brew’s contents shrank as the pedals were absorbed. That was fine. He didn’t require a ton of quantity so long as it was potent.

Closing his eyes, he felt on the verge of another vision from Zayne. Something happened after the imprisonment of the Genie. Something that had granted humanity magic. Kizu resisted the urge to slip away into the memory. That would take precious time from his current objectives. He never knew how long a memory would black him out for, so far better to seal it away as it arose and focus on his current brew.

With the memory now sealed away to be studied at a more convenient time, Kizu hummed to himself as he sprinkled a dry crushed stone into the cauldron.

He had never heard the tune he hummed. Not in his life, at least. It was a song that crossed thousands of years of the grand expanse of time to rest in his subconscious.
