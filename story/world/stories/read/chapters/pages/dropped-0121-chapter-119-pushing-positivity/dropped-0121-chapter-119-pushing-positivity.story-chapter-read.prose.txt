Chapter 119: Pushing Positivity

I was old enough, back on Earth, to remember the heyday of door to door salesmen. Born well into the last century, I remember watching through my window, filled with excitement as the vans rolled up, unloading slick entrepreneurs in well-fitted suits, pushing trolleys laden with everything from gardening tools to vacuum cleaners, Tupperware and self-help books and so much more besides. It was a simpler time, where the majority of shops had yet to recover and rebuild from the war, modern mega malls only existed on the fringes of society, and the convenient mass marketing channels like the television and the internet were still the stuff of science fiction.

Back when people were happy to see strangers at the door, before it was corrupted by fraudsters, debt collectors, and missionaries, forever tainting perceptions of an entire industry. A time where the working men and women had enough flex in their schedule to spend an hour chatting at their doorstep, without feeling stressed or running behind. Don’t get me wrong, the average quality of life in the 2020s was better by every measurable metric, so don’t take this as a case of rose-tinted glasses, just an honest nostalgia from a vestige of my childhood.

By the time I was old enough to make purchases on my own, telemarketers had already taken over, and by the time I entered the workforce, I was just in time to see the craze of the dotcom bubble, and well, it was all software, all the time after that. In my last decade on Earth, even the drugs had gone online, with sophisticated web portals promising service with a smile, and an average speed of delivery that beat most fast food joints and chain restaurants. Funny, then, that I’d end up doing a full circle in this new life, walking the streets with an open mind and powder in my pocket.

That, among other factors, also made my choice of level up an easy one. Some of my previous decisions had been agonising, choices made along the thinnest of margins, but not here. Of my three options, Edgerunner was powerful, but required far more defensive optionality than I currently possessed. I’d need at least one, preferably more lifesaving techniques before I’d be comfortable doubling all damage received, which at present just seemed like a quick way to die.

Marked For Death was better in that respect, as the vulnerability was bestowed entirely upon the target, but the lack of flexibility also limited it dramatically. I could only ever use it when a kill was guaranteed, lest I risk the target vanishing, and having no way to reset my skill, potentially forever. At the same time, if a kill was already a foregone conclusion, then why the hell did I need a trait like that? It was the very definition of a chicken and egg problem, one without a clear solution.

With that in mind, Stray Cat Strut was by far the most versatile skill, because becoming faster was pretty much never a bad thing, and I already travelled with one permanent cat companion, who was levelling up alongside me and likely to remain faster than my baseline speed in perpetuity. That wasn’t even taking into account some of the overpowered cats who made Heaven’s Reach their home; if I could get in contact with one of them, even briefly, that represented a potentially massive power up in times of need. Moreover, I’d already committed to the cat path with my previous choice, and all the synergies that entailed, which ultimately made the choice obvious in the end.

[Stray Cat Strut selected.]

I confirmed the choice while already on the move, prowling the streets after getting Joe’s tacit assent to operate in his neighbourhood. He wasn’t quite a kingpin-type character, didn’t have the levels to assert that kind of authority on the fractious underworld, but he was at least a gatekeeper for the surrounding neighbourhood, which contained most of the low-income population outside of the slums. They were the perfect target market for the first stage of our business, the kind of people with some money on hand to spend, but not enough of it to have the luxury of being picky with what they bought, while also having a poor enough lot in life to seek out distractions and protection in equal measure.

It was the old boots theory of poverty in motion, one that I intended to take full advantage of in these early weeks. For my first day out, I had no targets in particular, merely exploring the lay of the land. I walked past far too many beggars in the process, sorting them into two categories; the noisy and combative ones who genuinely needed the money they begged for, whether for food, drink or shelter, and the quiet ones that merely observed on the sidelines, who were far more likely to be informants of the local information peddlers, or lookouts for other criminal activity.

One of them was brazen enough to block my path, and attempt intimidation, verging on extortion. They weren’t truly threatening, by any means, and Pumpkin was perhaps a few seconds from tearing the man to shreds, so I decided to take mercy on him while testing the difference in speed. It was pretty big, I had to admit; before the level up, it would have taken me a full second to grab the beggar’s wrist, twist to snap it cleanly, and follow up with a fist to the head that laid him out flat. Now? I did it in less than half that time, in a display that was solidly superhuman, requiring no tricks with coins or knives, just raw physicality.

[10 XP gained for a decisive display.]

I didn’t get much for my efforts, but that was never the point to begin with.

”I could get used to this,” I muttered, leaving the stricken man where he fell; usually, I would have looted him in retaliation, but personal hygiene wasn’t the best for such folks, even in a world of magic, and I wasn’t too keen on lingering near him.

Moreover, I’d spotted my first potential marks for an easy payday; a trio of youths who were trying and failing to blend in, wearing rags just a cut too fine, clothes that fit just a little too well for the cast offs they were supposed to be. Middle class kids, I reckoned, slumming it while looking for excitement. Perfect.
