A deceptively heavy silver hand slammed down onto the surface of the table. The force behind it was enough to immediately splinter the wooden surface, not only sending shards of wood flying through the air, but also leaving a clear indentation behind.

But more importantly…

The sound of the impact was enough to silence the arguing in the room. Where once there had been dozens of raised, all shouting over themselves in fury to make their voice heard, now there was only silence. Instead, every single eye, and every single shocked gaping mouth…was aimed at her.

But she only had eyes for him.

“Commandant…” Sylvia said slowly, her silvery voice slicing through the quiet. “What did you just say?”

Sylvia, daughter of Greycton, Lieutenant of both the Order of the Eclipsed Dawn and the Herztalian expedition…

Was more worried than she thought she’d ever been in her life.

Perhaps naively, Sylvia had thought that things had been going astonishingly well. After long months of travel both on sea and on land, they had finally reached a place that was friendly to her and her people. There had been plenty of mishaps along the way, and dangers that had reared their head, but Sylvia frankly hadn’t minded it.

Yes, for much of the expedition, there had been an aura of grim determination and even fear among the troops, but that was to be expected. Their goal was to essentially save the world, after all, which Sylvia was admittedly important. But…that didn’t mean there hadn’t been joy to be found along the way.

The joy of adventure, the joy of finding new monsters and killing them, the joy of camaraderie she had found among the other Orders…

It was all so new to her. Things were rather different in the Eclipsed Dawn. Most of the time, they were silent, dutiful, and to the point. Sylvia had been raised in that environment. She had whet her blade among the Eclipsed Dawn, and been shaped by them. But lately, she had found that there was a great deal more to being a Classer than just the silent, grim duty of her Father’s Order. During her time acting as Captain Nyx’s Lieutenant, she had grown to know many of the other Orders.

The Flaming Drops, the Grallax Brigade, the Veiled Mist, and even…Nathan’s own Polaris Reach.

Nathan…

Really, she doubted Nathan even knew about many of them. There were so many individual members of the missing Orders among the expedition that it was dizzying. There weren’t many of each Order, as they were all the people who had been left behind from the separate monster hunting expedition, but collectively…they were many.

That wasn’t even counting all of the other factions among the expedition.

The Gnolls, the Herztalian Army, the Pirates, and even the later arrivals like the Dwarven mercenaries and the Elves.

Elves!

Sylvia had thought she understood the world fairly well, for her age. She had thought herself worldly and experienced.

And then that had gone out the window as she had met and broken bread with so many different people, from all over the world. Some, the prior Sylvia had met and even formed bonds with. She had done her best to forge some of those bonds anew, and she knew that…Nathan likely wouldn’t have approved of some of them. He wouldn’t have stopped her, of course.

But he was very testy when it came to Bella. He wouldn’t have known how to react to her striking her friendship back up with the Pirate Captain.

Truly, Sylvia didn’t care that he had copulated with the human after her previous self had been incapacitated. It didn’t matter to her. Nathan hadn’t been unfaithful to her.

Though…Sylvia did think that her previous incarnation might have cared and even been upset at the relationship.

But all that existed of that girl were memories. Cloudy, dream-like ones, with emotion almost entirely separated from them. Sylvia mentally referred to her as her previous incarnation, but she knew that wasn’t quite right.

That other Sylvia…she was like a ghost. Impressions upon her soul, instincts, knowledge, and experience burned into her. Just different enough from her current self to offer a usefully divergent perspective.

Not quite a mind…but more than a shadow.

Oftentimes, Sylvia thought that Sylvia-who-was existed as an almost self-illusion cast over her own mind.

But she was useful, and so Sylvia had never sought to excise her from her soul. She was almost like a second set of combat instincts, at times. Lady Honoka could most likely remove the impression from her, and would be happy to, but Sylvia had never asked. The fleshies didn’t understand this part of burgeoning Sculpted belief. Something her people were starting to realize about themselves and found they didn’t mind.

Continuance of consciousness did not always mean continuation of self.

Really, for how they were all constructed of such worldly, physical materials compared to flesh, a Sculpted’s metaphysical self was far more malleable.

But all of that was just dodging what was really bothering her.

Nathan.

They had taken him.

They had taken him from her.

That…couldn’t be allowed.

Nathan was hers, and hers alone. He was one of the great joys of her life. His presence had been a balm on her spirit when the joy of adventure threatened to fade and she grew overwhelmed. Their private moments together were extremely comforting both to her and her spiritual guest. The family she had been slowly forging with him prior to the Skyfall had been so perfect. Aveline was such a sweet, polite little human that Sylvia couldn’t help but be fond of her, and the lighthouse…

What a perfect metaphor for who her love had forged himself into.

The Dwarves couldn’t have him.

And if the expedition didn’t want him either…

Well.

For as much as she had grown fond of it, Sylvia had her priorities. Carefully, she let one of her Mithril hands drift down below the table, while the other remained embedded in the surface of it. Moments later, she felt the unmistakable feeling of a lupine nose nudge into the palm of it, insistent in its obvious worry.

Involuntarily, she flexed the hand still embedded in the wood of the table and hid a grimace. She still wasn’t used to these…muscles that her Mithril had been transformed into, following her second Breakthrough. Or at least, the approximation of fleshie muscle she now had.

They were so twitchy sometimes.

In the sudden silence of the room, Marcel, the Gnollish Captain of the Expedition, stood up from his own chair and met her eyes fearlessly. The stark yellow of his robes and turban stood out noticeably in the meeting room of the Ashen Bride, his grey eyes even against her own sapphire. “I said,” Marcel crossed his arms over his chest. “That Marshal Hart has become an outright hindrance to our mission.”

Sylvia didn’t care for the scattering of approving mutters around the room, nor even for the uncertain glances exchanged by those standing silently.

“Our goal is far more important than the fate of a single man, no matter who it is,” Marcel continued grimly. “We set out to put an end to the Skyfall. We did not sign up to be party to the internal politics of the Dwarves and their squabbles. Good people, I have long had reservations about interfering in this conflict. However, my protestations have been overruled time and again by the most prominent voice among the Captain’s circle.”

“Marshal Hart.”

Here, Marcel let his gaze linger around the room, breaking eye contact with Sylvia as he did. She had to squash the urge to immediately move to crush the insolent Gnoll’s throat, if only to keep him from speaking.

But…Nathan would probably object to such a move. Father too.

Possibly.

Neither of them had much love for the Gnoll.

“And what is your proposition then, Captain Marcel?” Nyx finally spoke up next to her. Sylvia didn’t miss the warning look her fellow Sculpted shot her then, likely having picked up on her idle thought. “That we simply leave the Marshal to the ‘tender’ mercies of the Dwarves and continue with our mission? You cannot possibly tell me that you believe the words of Anguis Savoy, when he says that the Skyfall will pass harmlessly in time.”

Here, Marcel leaned forward, placing his palm flat on the table. “Yes…and no. We should affirm Rhoscaran custody of the Marshal for the duration of the emergency, and resume our search for the truth of the Skyfall. I, of course, do not believe that Prince Anguis was telling the whole truth of the matter. The Dwarf is a known deceiver. He is no doubt obfuscating matters to an extent, to better present his side in the conflict. We should depart this city with all haste and investigate matters ourselves. We reverse course for the Barren Forest and seek a way through the barrier. Once through, we discover if Anguis is telling any modicum of truth of the matter, and act accordingly. A fact-finding mission, as opposed to an immediate all-out assault.”

That got more than one approving mutter from the watching expedition members.

“And the Marshal?” Gustave finally spoke up in a quiet voice. “And Alveron? It wasn’t just N-the Marshal that was taken. If we leave Alveron behind, the Elves will stay here with him.”

Marcel was unfazed. “It’s well known that the Marshal has ties to the House of Florens. I doubt that they shall judge him overly harshly, when he is close friends with their Prince. In addition, it would be better for the Elves to stay within Rhoscara in the first place. Need I remind everyone that over half of their number are non-combatants in the first place? Families and children? They have no place upon the battlefields we march for. No, my friends. We should excuse ourselves from this situation and leave this city behind.”

The sound of a match lighting cut through the following contemplative silence, and all eyes turned to the source.

Bella didn’t flinch under the weight of those eyes and unhurriedly lit her pipe, leaning back in his chair on two legs as she did. She puffed once…twice…and then spoke. “I ain’t leavin’ Nate behind,” She said, in an almost bored tone of voice. “You all can go if’n ye like, but me men and I will be stayin’ with the Elves.”

Her tone brooked no argument, and Sylvia loved her for that.

On the other hand, Marcel clenched his jaw tightly at the direct refusal to play along with his plans. “That’s…one nay, then,” He forced out between grit teeth. “And the rest of the Captains? All in favor of leaving Rhoscara and continuing our mission elsewhere?”

The rest of the Captains meaning Nyx and Gustave.

Olag wasn’t here, oddly having chosen to remain behind with the rest of the Rhoscaran forces. From everything she’d learned about the Dwarven mercenary, Sylvia would have expected him to continue his obligation with the expedition. Still, she supposed familial ties were more important to him right now.

And as for Bleddyn…he might not have been an official Captain of the expedition, but his voice often carried the weight of one. However, the leader of the Unchained had vanished after Nathan had been hauled away by the Corvid knights. Sylvia had no idea where he was, and none of his men had a tendency to hang around.

It came down to Nyx…and Gustave.

And Sylvia had a bad idea about where they stood on the matter.

The young Herztalian officer in charge of the Army forces shook his head. “I…unfortunately agree with Captain Marcel,” He said regretfully. “I think the Marshal should be fine remaining here, while the Dwarves negotiate for peace. In the meantime, we should be investigating the cause of the Skyfall away from all the fighting.”

All eyes turned to Nyx, then, as the onyx Sculpted woman crossed her arms in thought, visibly contemplating the issue with closed eyes.

Sylvia’s heart sank when they opened, and shot her briefest of regretful looks. “I concur. This matter with the Dwarves…it is beyond us. If the Rhoscarans are unable to render us aid within the Barren Forest, then we should depart. We have no business interfering in international conflicts if there is no tangible benefit to it,” She said frankly. “My vote…is to leave.”

Sylvia took a deep breath, and with a shake of her head…

Mentally divorced herself from the majority of those in the room.

Without a word, she removed her hand from the indentation in the table and turned her back on it. As she began to leave the meeting room, she heard more than one chair scrape back from the table and join her as she left. Chief among them being Bella.

But there was another voice that cut through the quiet. A familiar one.

Renauld’s.

“You treacherous old fuck…” Sylvia hear him say, disgust, fury, and most of all disappointment within his voice. “You just can’t help yourself, can you? Always jockeying for position. That’s the only thing you’ve ever gave a shit about, isn't it? To think I thought you might have changed even a little bit. Go die, for all I care. Go get pincushioned by a thousand Elven arrows, and never bother me again.”

As Sylvia reached the door, she found she wasn’t the only one standing at it. Renauld was with her, as was Maria, the former spy’s face blank of all emotion. As Bella and her first mate came to stand with them, Sylvia exchanged nods with everyone.

And then they left the room behind, caring nothing about the shouts from Marcel, demanding they return.

He didn’t matter anymore.

Instead…

“We must secure the stone,” Maria breathed in her ear, as they marched down the hall. Sylvia’s only response was an acknowledging flicker of her eyes.

She had already made the same realization. The ward stone of the Astray did not belong to the expedition.

It was Nathan’s. If the expedition intended to forsake her love and leave him behind...

Well, then they didn’t deserve it.

It turned out, though…that someone else had already had the same thought.

When Sylvia and her companions came to the room holding the ward stone, they found the two Gnollish guards outside of it knocked out cold, lying on the floor.

Inside, though…

Stood Meia Itzelan, her broad, Orcish palms held out in front of her as she flexed her skill in Abjuration. Her black robes fluttered in an ethereal wind from the force of the Mana she was exuding. The half-Orc woman didn’t even look at them as she spoke. “Took you all long enough,” She grunted. “Give me a moment, and I’ll have this down. Then let’s get the hells off this tub.”

Sylvia shook her head with a wry smile. The Sorceress must have left the meeting as soon as Marcel had begun his talk of leaving, to have already started this process.

What an oddly perceptive person she was.

The Sculpted woman shook the thought off and instead turned to face Maria, who had a distinctly satisfied smile on her formerly blank face. “Go and get who and what we need.”

“It’s time to break Nathan out.”
