The adventuring party was led through an additional two caverns, one of which had another choice of paths to go through before reaching a grotto several times bigger than the other rooms inside the dungeon. A dock had been constructed out of driftwood, leading to what almost amounted to a ship.

It was clear that the ghosts had tried to rebuild their vessel, the Calamitous. The boat had been put together in a way that mostly retained its original shape. Its Frankensteinian construction meant it would never be seaworthy again. Planks were completely missing, half of the railing looked like it was made from the mast, which was nowhere to be seen, and there were huge holes throughout.

Trevor couldn’t help but be impressed, though. With the mist from the sea and sickly green lights in the lanterns that were lit at even intervals around the deck, it certainly looked like it was a proper ghost ship.

They cautiously approached the Calamitous, wary of the driftwood dock. There were still ghosts behind them, but they had gone silent and stayed a fair distance behind the party. More had joined them from the other rooms. Despite their escape path being cut off, Trevor followed Adabelle’s lead and tried not to let it bother him.

The deck of the ship creaked as they stepped aboard. For a moment, the courier was worried that Clay was going to fall through the old boards. Their steps became more deliberate, and they thankfully didn’t break the rotten floor beneath them.

More ghosts flew up from the surrounding waters, surrounding the Calamitous. There had to be at least fifty of them, and they all began to chant. “Tahn-ahz-ee! Tahn-ahz-ee!”

“Ad-ah-bell, Ad-ah-bell,” Trevor said to himself, pulling a smile from his wife’s previously serious and stony demeanor.

“You can be a little louder if you want,” she told him, reaching up to scratch her cheek. “I enjoy hearing you cheer for me.”

The courier immediately put his hands around his mouth to create a makeshift megaphone. “Ad-ah-bell! Ad-ah-bell!” he chanted loudly, though he couldn’t match the sheer volume of the pirates.

She giggled at his antics, glad that he was feeling well enough to be goofy in this situation. It had been something Adabelle worried about, especially given his aversion to ghosts, but she could see that their particular brand of weirdness had alleviated most, if not all, of his trepidation.

“No, Tanazi!” a few of the ghosts called out.

“Nuh uh, Adabelle!” he returned.

“The captain’s not going to let some fresh meat beat him!”

Trevor scoffed. “Please. My wife’s the greatest adventurer on two planets. Your captain’s going to be chum.”

“Chum!?” a panicked voice asked. “FOR SHARKS!?”

One of the ghosts started crying and fled from the formation, heading down a small tunnel opposite of where they had come from. The others shook their heads, grumbled, and sighed.

“Great, it’s going to take us hours to remind Talwa that he can’t be eaten by sharks.”

“Ah, my bad. Didn’t mean to cause him any distress,” Trevor said, sharing a glance with Adabelle. “Was he, like, eaten… by a shark or something?”

“Pfft, no,” another ghost answered. “He’s just got an irrational fear. We don’t even have sharks here! It’s all a tale the captain told us about the horrors of the deep back in his world.”

“Like the Kraken.”

“And narwhals.”

Trevor leaned towards Adabelle. “We can never let Rashie come here,” he whispered. The demonkin stifled a laugh, silently agreeing.

“Enough!” Tanazi roared, his voice loud enough to shake the grotto. Droplets of water fell from the ceiling. “You all are constantly getting distracted. Normally, I’m all for it, but don’t get friendly with the guy who has the ability to destroy you with a single touch.”

“I can’t help it, I’m a people person,” Trevor said unapologetically.

“Why don’t you show yourself, Dread Pirate Tanazi,” Adabelle called. “I challenge you to a duel, just the two of us!”

A dark chuckle came from behind them, and the sound of heavy boots on the deck caused the party to turn around. The demonkin brandished her sword in the direction of the captain, who was much more than a vague shape of lights like his crew. He was a fully formed spirit.

The Dread Pirate Tanazi stood a head taller than Trevor with a posture that could only be described as ramrod straight. His barrel chest was decorated with medals on his dark coat, and a cutlass with a basket hilt hung from his belt. Both the semi-translucent blue skin and his eyes, which were a pair of floating green flames that matched those in the lanterns around the ship, marked him as no longer human.

He reached up and adjusted his wide-brimmed hat, staring down at the party. “I am the Dread Pirate Tanazi,” he declared, the ship rumbling as he spoke. “You have the gall to challenge me, girl? The scourge of the ocean?”

Adabelle took a few steps forward, standing tall as she looked up at him. “I’ve met the scourge of the ocean, captain. You’re a poor replacement for such a primal force of nature.”

Tanazi glared down at the demonkin, whose tail was swishing from anticipation, before throwing his head back and letting loose a booming laugh. “We’ve got a brave one, boys!” he exclaimed, and the pirates joined him in his revelry. “Very well. I accept your challenge!”

Without waiting for Adabelle to be ready, the captain raised his knee towards her stomach in an attempt to get the first blow. She twisted to the side, avoiding a hole in the floorboards, and lashed out with her blade. Tanazi was in the process of drawing his cutlass, but she had attacked his opposite side so he wouldn’t be able to block in time. The sleeve of his jacket was cut.

“First blood, as it were,” the noblewoman announced with a smug look.

“A lucky blow, nothing more,” Tanazi stated before going after the woman in earnest.

Their blades clashed, and Adabelle’s expression became more serious as she gauged her opponent’s strength. Unlike the other pirates, this one could actually push her back. Her style wasn’t one that often allowed itself to meet power with power even if she was strong enough to handle such moments, but it wasn’t often that she was lacking in stats.

Tanazi lashed out with a left hook while she was distracted fending off his cutlass. She ducked to the other side of his blade, letting it fall to the floor, and spun away as he stomped at her knee. Adabelle flowed as she jumped and kicked off of the ship's railing. Sticking close to the ghost like a dance partner to avoid his attack, she scored another strike as her sword scratched its way down his back by the time she landed.

Only to have to immediately fall to the ground in order to dodge a diagonal swing as he turned to face her. She quickly pushed herself upwards with enough force to propel her to her feet, and blocked the follow-up attack, another punch. The captain’s fist proved just as hard as his weapon; Adabelle’s blade didn’t cut it. She seemed momentarily surprised since it should have had an effect on incorporeal entities, but quickly accepted that it hadn’t and moved on.

Tanazi opened his hand to grab the sword, but she had already retreated. He followed with another wide strike, and the noblewoman parried the blade downwards towards the floorboards. Despite its cutting edge, there was enough force to send splinters everywhere. Adabelle winced as some of them went into her ankles like shrapnel, but the injury wasn’t debilitating.

Moving towards his sword arm, which was still out of position, the demonkin struck at his side in quick succession. Her attacks left three slices in Tanazi’s dark coat before she was forced to jump over a low slash. The act put her on the staircase to the quarterdeck. A triumphant grin emerged on his face as his hand shot out to grapple his airborne challenger.

Instead, she reached out and grabbed his wrist first. Adabelle was pushed farther up the stairs before swinging off of his arm, back onto the deck to land lightly on her feet. She was close to another hole in the floorboard, but the dancer seemed to glide over the imperfections as she thrust her sword through his chest.

This time, he grabbed it directly, holding it in place inside his ghostly body. Tanazi didn’t need to worry about the blade’s edge cutting his hand, and he raised his cutlass high. When he brought it down, Adabelle stepped into his personal space, shoulder checking the pirate. The maneuver would have knocked the wind out of any living opponent, but it put her inside of his guard and his arm came down on her shoulder.

Tanazi tried to throw her off by thrashing about, but Adabelle proved too strong for that. While one of her hands kept a firm grip on the hilt, the other pushed down on his sword arm. She was lifted off of the ground, but the captain couldn’t really do anything to her. His sword’s basket hilt made it tough for him to strike from this position, and if he let go of her blade then she would slash at him again.

What would have been stalemate in most other situations turned in the demonkin’s favor due to an extra addition to her anatomy.

Adabelle’s tail retrieved a shining knife from one of her belt pouches and securely wrapped around its hilt. The extra appendage flailed with all the speed of a whip, sending the much smaller blade up and down Tanazi’s coat and cutting through the only part of him that could be injured.

Recoiling, the captain took a few steps back. He let go of her sword to try and grab the tail that was suddenly dealing so much damage, but it moved too quickly. Adabelle put a foot against his chest and kicked off, bounding away before he could grab her. She landed on the staircase's railing, taking a bow before backing up onto the quarterdeck.

Tanazi’s expression looked vicious as he gave chase, but there was an undercurrent of entertainment in the glint of his eyes. At least, that was what Trevor thought he saw. He and Clay had moved to the side of the ship, and the courier was leaning against the railing while eating from a bucket of popcorn that he summoned. While he was concerned about Adabelle, he had faith in her abilities.

“That’s my wife,” he told one of the ghosts that had come to sit on the railing beside him.

“She’s pretty good to go toe to toe with the captain,” they remarked.

“Yeah, she’s awesome,” Trevor gloated with a grin. “We only got married a couple of days ago. She wanted to go adventuring as a part of our honeymoon.”

“Aww, that’s sweet that you two adventure like this,” the pirate replied, and it sounded like they were being sincere. “And congrats. May your marriage last until the sea runs dry.”

“Thanks! Is that some kind of pirate saying, or something else?”

“Nah, I’m just trying to sound profound.”

“Well, could have fooled me,” Trevor chuckled.

Adabelle’s straight sword clashed again with Tanazi’s cutlass and redirected it away from her. Not towards the deck of the ship this time, though. She had learned her lesson with the splinter shrapnel before. The captain’s blade struck it at an angle, the tip of it skipping off of the wood and throwing him off balance.

Capitalizing immediately, Adabelle’s twin blades flashed. A deep cut appeared through Tanazi’s dark coat, revealing the armored plates that had been stitched inside of it, and he fell to one knee. The captain held up his hand, causing the noblewoman to stop with her sword raised.

“You started this duel by attacking before I was ready, so I hope you think twice before ending it with a false surrender,” Adabelle warned coolly.

Tanazi grimaced before tossing away his sword. He sat back and held up his hands. “No tricks this time. I know when I’m beat. The victory is yours.”

“Captain, no!” one of the ghosts yelled.

“Pipe down back there,” he grumbled. “You saw me be outmatched just now. This woman’s the real deal. I admire your skill, miss…?”

“Lady Adabelle Ramansa Ka—” the noblewoman stopped herself before smiling. “Anderson-Katine gen Tosa.”

Trevor grinned proudly as he heard her give her new name. She just looked so cool as she flicked her blade and slid it into its scabbard. It was already clean thanks to Dory’s magic oil, so there was no need to worry about it.

“Your dungeon has been defeated today by myself, my husband, Trevor Anderson, and his familiar, the earth elemental, Clay.”

“That’s what it is!” one of the ghosts exclaimed.

“So it’s not a lighthouse golem?” another asked.

“No, she literally just said what it was. Get the seaweed out of your ears.”

“Kind of want to see an actual lighthouse golem now, but it’s pretty cool.”

“Clay’s the best, just like my wife,” Trevor announced, and he was pleased to see many of the pirates nodding as they agreed with him.

“I just want to know one thing,” Tanazi said as he stood up and brushed off his coat before jabbing a finger at Trevor. “You’re clearly some sort of swordswoman, but what’s he?”

“If I tell you, then we’ll need the armor of you and your men,” Adabelle bargained. “Preferably intact, though we are not opposed to obtaining them the hard way.”

“Hmm. I suppose you haven’t collected any yet since he keeps deleting them somehow,” the captain said, stroking his chin as he stared at Trevor. “That is very important to you adventurers.”

The courier didn’t say a word, letting Adabelle handle this one. They didn’t need to know what his Power was. Not yet, anyway.

“Alright, you’ve got yourself a deal,” Tanazi said after a few seconds of consideration.

“And I can trust the word of a pirate captain?” the demonkin challenged, arching an eyebrow.

“We’re really more privateers than pirates, but that doesn’t make nearly as good a story,” he laughed. Putting his hands on his hips, he held himself up proudly. “I may fight dirty, but my word’s good and a bargain is a bargain.”

“Very well, Dread Pirate Tanazi. I will hold you to that,” Adabelle said graciously before motioning for Trevor to join her up on the quarterdeck. He was quick to climb the stairs and offer her some popcorn, but she shook her head while looping her arm around his. Her sword was still in reach. “Honey, would you please inform the captain of your power and occupation?”

“Sure,” he agreed with a smile. Holding up his bucket of popcorn, he sent it away to his extradimensional space. Then, he retrieved one of the full pieces of armor he had snagged earlier minus the slime. “My Power is Inventory, and I’m an inventoryman. A courier. I wasn’t so much as deleting the parts of your crew that was bound to this world as I was just sending it away.”

“A courier?” Tanazi asked incredulously before he burst out laughing. “So you were scared stiff when you came in here! You’re no big shot adventurer mage, you’re just some guy.”

“He’s not some guy,” Adabelle disagreed, taking umbrage with his description. “He’s my husband.”

“Yeah, yeah,” the captain said as he stopped laughing, waving her off. “That’s a funny interaction of the system. Can’t say I ever saw that coming. Power of Disintegration, or maybe Void or something. That’s what I put my money on.”

“Sorry, I am but a humble courier,” Trevor replied with a shrug. “Honestly, I’m pretty happy with my chosen profession. Though, I am curious about you. I also came from Earth, though probably not the same one as you, and I enjoyed the references.”

“Ah, my old home,” Tanazi sighed wistfully as he thought about it. “It’s been a long, long time since then.”

“Yeah, especially because I understand the references but you came here five hundred years ago?” the courier asked.

Which netted him a shrug. “Different worlds, different timelines. Honestly, I don’t think I’ve ever met anyone from before… I want to say the 1920s? There was that one bloke from the Rumbling Twenties, but that was it.”

“That is strange,” Trevor stated, hands on his hips. “Also, I don’t know a lot about that era but don’t you mean the Roaring Twenties?”

Tanazi eyed him for a moment before grinning. “You’ll have to find out next time.”

“Huh?”

“You want to talk more? Come defeat us again,” he said, grinning. “Things’ll be different next time, but I’ll have answers for you. Just a little incentive to come back. We’ll be ready for you then. And, little miss, I expect you to bring my hat so I can win it back.”

Trevor shared a glance with Adabelle. “Well, that’s not really—”

Before he could finish his sentence, Tanazi looked at his crew. “Alright, you salty sea dogs! We’ve been defeated. Leave the shells, and prepare for the next wave!”

“Aye, aye, captain!” they responded in unison.

As one, the lights comprising the ghosts blinked away. Most of their armor fell into the water, as they had surrounded the ship, when they disappeared. The only set that fell neatly was Tanazi’s right in front of them.

Trevor and Adabelle stared at the armored jacket and hat in front of them. It was in pristine condition, aside from the cuts, and there wasn’t a trace of slime on it.

“I… guess the hat is mine now?” she asked, gingerly reaching down to take it.

He looked at the black and red wide-brimmed hat and nodded. “We’ll have to put some holes in it for your horns, but I think you could pull it off.”

“Maybe. We’ll see,” she said, clearly ambivalent. “I suppose we’ll eventually need to come back here.”

“Good thing we have a teleporter we can borrow,” Trevor chuckled before looking over the side of the ship. He grimaced at the thought of wading through the water.

Clay’s body shifted into a semblance of a crane and reached over the edge for the nearest set of armor. They fished it out with minimal problems, letting it plop onto the deck with a wet slap before getting the next.

“Thank you, Clay. Once again, you’re a life saver. I did not want to go down there,” he admitted.

The earth elemental was pleased with the praise, and continued their work. Soon, the dungeon would be complete.
