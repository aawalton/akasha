Confusion slowly mixed with my horror as I tried to work out what I was seeing, but my mind couldn’t process it. All I could do was stare at the door that had appeared, seemingly out of nowhere, trying to convince myself that it was really there. It was partially blocked by an almost empty metal shelving unit, but there was no mistaking what I was seeing.

Hope swelled in my chest, and I blinked hard. My head was still spinning from the impact of the door, and I was scared that it had given me a concussion or something, that it was making me see something that wasn’t real. I half-expected it to disappear the moment I opened my eyes again, but it didn’t. The door was still there.

Another bang sounded, but I barely heard it. My body tensed automatically, preparing for the pain that would follow, but I didn’t even really feel it. My attention had been consumed by the door, and I was too scared to look away from it. I didn’t know where it had come from, how it had just appeared, or if I had done something to make it happen, but it felt as though the moment my gaze left the door, it would disappear.

I couldn’t let that happen, though. Tears stung my eyes, but I continued staring at the door. If something happened and it did disappear, I’d be trapped again. I was too aware of that, and I had no idea how it had appeared, so I wouldn’t be able to make it happen again. I’d just be trapped. It was my only chance of escape, and if it faded from existence, I would have no way out of the room I’d locked myself in.

Someone kicked the door again, and I felt my eyebrows twitch together as confusion slowly dawned on me. Something was wrong, I realised as I stared at the door across the room. It was too far away from me, and it should not have been. The room I was in was small. It was basically just a closet, and the opposite wall was so close that my feet had been pressed against it, but suddenly, it was much bigger. There was a whole room, far too much space, between me and that door.

A shaky exhale escaped my lips, and I forced myself to glance back over my shoulder, my eyes searching for the door that I’d entered through. I knew it should have been there. It had been moments ago, but I did not find it. Instead, my gaze landed on a white wall, strewn with occasional scuff marks.

I stared at it, trying to will my eyes to focus and my mind to work out what was happening, but they refused. Everything remained strangely hazy, as if I were not entirely anchored in the world just yet. Still, I knew it was wrong. I should have been leaning against a metal door, not what was clearly a white wall. I could even see the faint outline of bricks through the paint.

Another muted crash came from in front of me, and my head snapped around. I watched, my eyes wide, as one of the last remaining bottles on the shelving unit toppled over. It rolled unsteadily across the thick plywood surface, the liquid inside sloshing as it moved, before plummeting the short distance to the floor, and I flinched.

The noise wasn’t particularly loud. In fact, it was practically inaudible, but it seemed to unleash a floodgate, and suddenly, I could hear everything. I pressed my hands to my ears, trying to block it all out. I didn’t want to hear the voices coming from outside the room, the many people excitedly talking over each other, or the seemingly endless banging, but it still found a way in.

I tangled my fingers in my hair around my ears, clenching as tightly as I could, but it did nothing to muffle the almost deafening cacophony happening around me. Even if I managed to block out the sound, it wouldn’t really help, I realised. I could feel it. The banging seemed to reverberate through the floor, walls, and ceiling, and even my own traitorous heartbeat seemed to be keeping the same rhythm.

My breathing was just as fast too, I couldn’t help but notice. I could feel that now, and the awareness that I was hyperventilating, that I was breathing so quickly I was making myself dizzy, crashed into me, but I could do nothing to soothe it. Each inhale was nothing more than a choked gasp. Fear had wrapped itself around my chest and throat, suffocating me. It was slowly tightening its grip, engulfing more and more of my body, and trapping me in the world, but there was no escape.

It was starting to feel more real too. The room was solidifying around me, and I could feel everything. My heart hammering in my chest, the chill radiating into my back from the wall behind me, and the blood drying on my bare arms and legs. I could feel it all.

My breathing caught in my chest, and I hesitated before lifting my head. I hadn’t realised that I’d shut my eyes, that I’d screwed them shut in an attempt to block everything out, but I opened them and looked down at my pale legs.

A soft cry escaped my lips, and I stared at the blood smeared across my skin, searching for the source. There didn’t seem to be one, though. I couldn’t see any cuts, bullet holes or open wounds on my legs, but I turned my legs slightly, examining them from all angles to make sure I hadn’t missed anything, but there was nothing. My legs were shaking. I was trembling, but I was pretty sure that was more from fear and panic than it was pain.

My eyes landed on my dark blue slippers that somehow still clung to my feet, and I felt my heart skip a beat. Immediately, I recognised them. I recognised the crest atop of each foot. It was on my uniform, on so many of the books and documents that I’d read since being taken to that place, and that realisation made horror rise within me, dragging another revelation with it: the blood was not my own.

I was in a different world, and I knew which.

A sob welled in my throat, and I began to move a hand to my mouth to stifle the sound before catching myself. My eyes flitted towards my fingers, and I stared at them. They were covered in the blood of my friends and fellow trainees, and the sight caused a wave of nausea to tear through me, gripping me so tightly that I almost vomited. I could feel it clawing at my throat, threatening to escape, and I pressed my lips together and clenched my hands into fists again as I fought to keep it at bay.

How much time had passed since I was at the Academy last? The question pulled at me, demanding my attention. It felt as though I’d lived an entire lifetime. Multiple, really. I’d been hunted and terrified over and over again, trapped in the worlds I’d inadvertently thrown myself into, unable to stop or turn back. Each had been horrible and exhausting, but nothing scared me as much as the people that I could hear outside the door.

Surely, it couldn’t still be the same people, though. Time had passed since I was there last. It must have. I’d gone to other worlds, and I hadn’t spent particularly long in any of them, but that didn’t matter. I’d still been to them, and that must have taken time. It must have been hours or days since Seth had turned on me, and those people stormed the building, slaughtering everyone.

Something must be different, I thought desperately. I must have been wrong. It must have been a different world, a different room, or a different Academy. That was the only explanation. The world had not just been standing still, paused, as it waited for me to return. Time didn’t work like that. It never had before.

I scanned the room, frantically searching for evidence that I was right, that I was in a different world. I just needed something that was out of place, one thing that hadn’t existed or looked different in the world where I’d spent so much time, but I couldn’t find anything. I hadn’t been in the storeroom long, hadn’t really had enough time to examine it fully, but everything looked exactly as I remembered.

My gaze landed on the bright yellow box not far from me. The empty box that I’d been hoping to find weapons in so long ago, or perhaps just minutes before, was right where I’d left it. The floor was littered with bottles of shampoo, conditioner, and other random toiletries that must have fallen from the shelving unit when I’d dragged it in front of the door or as the people outside tried to kick it down. I was back in the Academy, and that meant I was just as trapped as I’d been in the last world.

A scratching came from above me, and my head snapped up as goosebumps erupted on my arms and a chill slipped down my spine. Terror pulsed in my heart as I stared at the hatch on the ceiling. It was barely visible from where I was seated, my back pressed against the wall, but I knew it was there.

Seth was up there. I was sure of it too. I’d left him on the roof, run away from him there, and he’d waited, somehow knowing that I’d find my way to another room with a ladder to the roof.

It wasn’t locked. The hatch was unsealed, and he could have pulled it open the moment he knew I was in the room, but he hadn’t. He was waiting, biding his time, and toying with me. He enjoyed my fear, revelled in it, but he’d already waited so long. Surely he was bound to get bored soon. Then, he’d throw open the hatch and plunge down towards me, but I couldn’t escape. There was no way for me to get away from him, and I couldn’t fight him. I was in a bad position, I had no weapons, and I was exhausted. He would win.

There was a pause, a brief reprieve in the endless noise, before another bang came from the door. Fear exploded in me, burning through my chest, as the shelving unit skittered back, and the legs squealed loudly against the floor before it began to teeter. Instinctively, I threw myself back, diving away from the collapsing furniture.

My vision turned black. There was a fleeting burst of pain before dizziness engulfed me, leaving nothing in its wake. My body tensed, waiting for more noise or my sight to return, but it did not. My head just continued to swim.

I couldn’t help the sob that burst from me as I realised I was back in my void, but the sound was quickly whipped away from me as I fell. I tumbled, spinning so quickly I couldn’t catch my breath, until suddenly, something warm and scratchy caught against my cheek.

Fear thrashed in my heart, and I scrambled back. My eyes flew open, and my chest heaved as I met a pair of dark, intelligent eyes that were right in front of me. I flinched, recoiling even further away as I noticed just how close they were before the rest of the face came into focus, and I finally realised who I was looking at.

Rex sat directly in front of me on my bed, and for a few seconds, all I could do was stare at him. I was terrified that if I looked away, if I acknowledged the world around him, it would realise I was there and people would come after me, but I couldn’t ignore it for long. Eventually, I had to rip my eyes away from his face and glance around the room.

A laugh that was closer to a sob burst out of me, and I threw my arms around Rex, clinging to him as tears slipped from my eyes. I’d done it. Somehow, I’d finally done it. I recognised the room I was in, but there was more than just that. I knew the feel of the world I was in, and I couldn’t believe it. I’d managed to get back to my reality.

Eventually, I loosened my grip on Rex. He was so warm and reassuringly solid that I couldn’t bring myself to fully let go, but I pulled back enough to look him in the eye. There was something I wanted to say to him, something that I needed to tell him.

“Oh, Rex,” I started, my voice thick with emotion and tears. “I missed you so much. Thank you for coming to—”

Three bangs in quick succession came from downstairs, and I fell silent, my gaze darting to the door. My breathing felt shallow as I stared at it, unsure whether I was hearing things. Perhaps it was just the other world. I’d only just left, so maybe it still had its hooks in my brain. The sound could have come from there, seeped into my reality, but it wasn’t real. None of it was real.

A soft growl came from Rex, and I swallowed before looking back at him, a million questions racing through my mind. Was I wrong? Had I been mistaken, and I wasn’t actually back in my reality? Perhaps it was just a world that felt the same, but it wasn’t truly.

I wasn’t certain, and I didn’t know what the noise was, but I felt myself rise from the bed. It felt as though I was being dragged forward by an invisible string, compelled to place one foot in front of the other until I reached my door. My heart thumped in my ears, the noise deafening. I couldn’t breathe, couldn’t think, and couldn’t stop myself as I reached for the handle and slowly twisted it.

My movements were careful. I didn’t make any sound as I pulled the door open and peered out into the hall. It was painfully familiar. Everything in the hallway, from the carpet to the trinkets on the table at the end by the stairs, looked exactly how it did in my reality.

“I’m coming, I’m coming,” I heard my mom mutter from downstairs, her tone irritated.

The urge to rush out into the hall gripped me. I wanted to shout at my mom to stop, not to open the door. I didn’t know who it was, but I was terrified. It could have been no one. Perhaps it was just the postman, but something told me it wasn’t. It was more than just a coincidence. The timing was too good for that.

I had been hunted in every single world I’d been to, a small voice in the back of my head pointed out. I wasn’t sure what I’d done to deserve it or if it was even the same people, but I couldn’t help but wonder how long it would take them to start hunting me in my reality too. And what would they do once they found me?

Stepping forward, I opened my mouth to cry out, but fear had stolen my voice from me. I couldn’t speak, couldn’t even make a sound. Another soft growl came from Rex as I heard the scrape of the lock being pulled back before a voice rang out from downstairs.
