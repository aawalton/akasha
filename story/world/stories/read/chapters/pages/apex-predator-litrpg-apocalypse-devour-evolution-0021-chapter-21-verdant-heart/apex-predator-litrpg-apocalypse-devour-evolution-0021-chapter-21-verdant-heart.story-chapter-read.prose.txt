The dungeon, of course, made no sense.

Don wasn’t sure why he was expecting to see the inside of a tree, and some kind of a tower-dungeon.

What he saw was a dreamscape.

Don found himself in a large underground cavern. The air was heavy and thick here, reminiscent of an old forest. The ceiling was some kind of brown rock with thin stalactites jutting down. The walls were earthen and filled with vines and moss. Thick roots of blue and green trailed above and under the terrain. A stream trickled through the cavern, its banks glistening with tall grass and bright blue, glowing flowers.

There were these glowing blue flowers everywhere, which illuminated the cavern.

Everything looked peaceful and serene, but Don’s Survival Instincts was sending him a steady stream of warnings. Don was getting pretty good at listening to this evolution, and he figured his translation was good. This area was not safe.

Don looked around and he saw no enemies. No beasts, no monsters, no bloodthirsty goblins. But there were buildings.

Shacks designed with the same curvy geometry and carved from the same golden tree he had seen outside. The buildings were mostly along the walls suspended four or five feet in the air.

“Alright…” Don muttered to himself.

The cavern opened before him, some two hundred yards away across the creek. He’d go there soon. But he just had to check out one of the strange golden-wood shacks. Maybe it would give him a clue about what he was dealing with here.

Don figured it was safe to assume he would have to fight here. So gathering a bit more intel could help his chances of success.

Don moved across the branch and dropped down onto the railing of the golden-wood shack. It held his weight easily. He stepped over it, landing lightly on the threshold. The shack’s front door was hanging half-off its hinges, leaning like a drunk against the frame.

He pushed it open.

The smell hit him first. Not rot, but something milder and older. Dry bark, crushed herbs, a faint chemical stink underneath. Inside was a wooden room of medium size. The walls were curved like the inside of a gourd, smooth, organic. It was fine craftsmanship, really. And completely alien to Don’s human origins. But whatever elegance this place had once held was long gone.

Furniture had been tossed and shattered. A long table lay split down the middle like a chopped log. Shelves had collapsed inward. Bits of ceramic and wood crunched under Don’s feet. One whole wall was scored with nasty claw marks—deep ones. There had been a fight here.

Don crouched beside a weird green goop smeared across the floor. It had dried into a cracked layer but still glistened faintly. He didn’t touch it. It was better to be safe..

He stood up, eyes scanning. In the corner, half-buried under a split chair leg, he found what looked like an alchemy kit. Glass tubes, beakers, vials. Most of the equipment was broken.

This wasn’t some caveman’s potion table. These were professional’s tools, Don could tell that much. Whoever had lived here had skill. And tools. They were sophisticated.

And now they were gone.

A scimitar lay on the floor, snapped clean at the hilt. The blade was unbloodied. The break wasn’t jagged either—it looked melted. Don picked it up and turned it over. The blade was patterned like tree rings, gold and green worked into the metal. He dropped it with a clink. It was useless to him.

Near the back wall, a single potion vial sat upright on the floor, untouched. It was narrow, with a wooden cork, and the fluid inside shimmered faintly with a light blue glow.

Don picked it up and tilted it toward the light. Don used Identify on the vial.

[Potion of Alertness: +3 Mental for 60 minutes.]

“Nice,” Don muttered. He stashed it in one of his pouches. Never know when something like that could save your ass.

He wondered if he should use it instantly or save it. The dungeon wasn’t a safe place, that was for sure. But he had no idea if he was going to be here for an hour or a week.

I might not make it past the first hour if I don’t chug this. I’m underleveled to begin with.

He compromised and decided to use it the second shit got hairy. He turned back to the shack entrance, planning to check the next one. That’s when he froze.

Something had just moved by the door. Don didn’t know what it was, but it had been quiet and fast. His Survival Instincts flared hard. A sort of a red feeling for lack of better description.

Something was outside. And it was hostile.

He crouched instantly, body still, breath shallow. He moved one step back, pressed his shoulder against the inner wall. Slowly he extended his claws. There Don held his breath and listened.

No footsteps. No growl. Just a subtle shift in air pressure. A feeling of being watched.

Don didn’t move. He waited.

Then he heard a soft rustle. Something brushing leaves just outside the shack. Whatever it was, it knew Don was here, and it was being careful.

And then silence again.

“Alright…Let’s see what kind of bullshit this dungeon throws at me next.

If there was a time to drink the potion, it was now. Slowly, constantly watching the door, Don uncorked the vial and drank the liquid. It tasted bitter and spicy, like someone had spruced it up with black pepper and chili. Don suppressed a cough. Almost immediately the world became sharper.

[Temporary boost to Mental: +3]

Don realized he should have been putting points in Mental. He was just so much more acutely… aware. Of everything. His body’s condition. His surroundings. Everything was sharper and he suddenly had the ability to take in much more details. He noticed a footprint with three large toes in the dust of the shack. That had belonged to a reptile. He listened carefully. He could see an off pattern in the shadows the blue light in the cavern cast just before the door. That told him there was something to the right of the door, waiting not too far away.

Suddenly the scimitar’s blade seemed much more interesting. Don picked it up, wetted the blade with a squeeze from his water flask and polished it.

Then he angled himself against the doorframe and used the broken blade as a mirror to look outside.

It was really hard to make out anything concrete. The shack was next to the cavern wall, which was covered in roots, moss and vines that bulged inwards. However, as he traced the scimitar blade along the doorframe, he started seeing patterns. And when he traced back to the spot he had had heard the noise and seen the strange shadows, he understood.

There was something there, but it wasn’t visible. It was the size of a very large dog. Don could see it in the way it had shimmied between the vines and roots. The bulging vegetation was slightly off-kilter. But there was nothing there. Just vegetation.

Whatever it is, it has high Stealth skill and some kind of camouflage.

It was the worst kind of enemy. Not a natural enemy to Don’s tactics. Worse, something that was using Don’s playbook.

Ambushing was his thing. And now he couldn’t play to his strengths.

I’ll have to reverse-engineer it.

What sucked the most to fail at when Don faced an ambusher. Well, failing the initial attack of course and being revealed. Don had already won half the battle by knowing the ambush was coming. But if the creature was fast, which was a fair assumption, he couldn’t just waltz out of the shack.

Looks like I’ll have to get creative again…
