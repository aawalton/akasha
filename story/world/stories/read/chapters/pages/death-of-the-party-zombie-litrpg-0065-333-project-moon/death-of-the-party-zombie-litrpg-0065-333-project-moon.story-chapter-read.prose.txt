Sally stepped out of the changing room and adjusted her jacket in the mirror.

“Nice how they’re customized to be similar to our usual look,” Ren said as she joined her.

Both had white dress shirts and black ties. The elf had a similar jacket made of almost-black, deep-blue leather. Where they differed were the designs - Ren’s was a single-breasted jacket with tails at the back that matched the magician look. Sally’s was a crop jacket, and her diner skirt was now a dark blue instead of red.

On their back of the jackets was the Outsiders logo. A slightly faded O shape, with a central star eye and two rows of teeth at the bottom.

[Outsiders Jacket][Minor Damage Absorption][+10% Affinity Effectiveness][Waterproof]

“Suits me well,” Sally confirmed, hands on her hips. “I find there’s nothing quite like the freedom of a battle-skirt.”

“I’ve always found them to be a nuisance.” Ren smiled briefly and adjusted her collar in the mirror. “But, I grew up in the forest where loose clothing gets caught on everything. I’d prefer something more form-fitting than these slacks, but theme is important.”

“Creatures of habit.” Sally turned to eyed-up the logo on her back. “Speaking of which, we should see how the boys are looking.

Stepping into the main room once more, she noticed that Ren’s outfit also had a slight sparkle of blue to it as she moved through the light. Almost as if the leathery material had been coated in a subtle layer of glitter. That fact was made clear when she saw Max, as his uniform had a subtle sparkle of purple to match his prior outfit. Much like Ren, his was a tailed showman’s jacket, with matching slacks.

Theo had a cutaway jacket and wore a dark waistcoat over his white shirt. The trimmings of both were a dark burgundy. He grinned as she approached. “Now we look like a team, huh?”

Before she could answer, the arrival of their fifth member caused her to pause.

Sally whistled. “Talk about a glow-up, Arch.”

The graying wizard had ditched the robe and traditional hat. He wore a long, double-breasted jacket buttoned up. Instead of a robe, he had a hooded overcoat in deep gray that he wore like a shawl over his shoulders. Rather than a long staff, he had a cane. He’d even trimmed his long beard a little tidier. Other than looking a little self-conscious about his exposed, almost-bald head, it was quite the uplift.

“It’s giving Gandalf the Gray as a corpo-pimp,” Theo agreed.

Archie pulled a face. “I know at least three of those words, but I’ll take it as a compliment.”

“I—unfortunately—understood the references, and can confirm it is.” Max pulled a face briefly, but nodded.

"Regardless.” Archie waved for them to follow. “It is beneficial if we all match, and the robed look is rather antiquated.” He stepped over to a small cabinet mounted on the wall and opened it. From within, he withdrew a handful of slim objects and handed them out.

Sally took hers and held it up to inspect it. A rectangle of laminated card, with her mugshot on it. Sally Danger, it read. Outsider. She opened the clip attached at the top and affixed it to the pocket of her dress shirt.

“Rather useless at present,” the wizard continued. “In time, we will gain notoriety and… detractors.”

“What’s new there?” Theo murmured, as he attached it to his waistcoat.

Archie took them to a doorway leading out into a long corridor. With a wave of his hand, the next door, almost immediately to the right, opened up. Inside was a large, round table, similar to the one in the tower. Everything was the same marbled, light gray tone.

Sally stepped in, walking past the chairs to the back of the room - where there was a long, if thin, window. In the middle of pure darkness was Sanctuary. The globe turned ever so slightly. The zombie pressed her face up against the thick glass. “Neat, I think I can see my house from here.”

“We’re not… directly orbiting the planet. It’s more of a visual representation of what the mind expects to see.”

“Isn’t that usually how sight works?” Theo gave the wizard a grin as he walked over behind the zombie. “Puts things in perspective, doesn’t it?”

“Just like our eyes,” Sally agreed.

The next room in the station was a massive hangar. Sitting right in the middle was the Omen - although it looked slightly different. Longer and bulkier.

“I had it made to be something we could actually live on,” Archie explained. “Aside from the necessary spaceship parts, it’s also essentially a five-bedroom house.”

“It can expand, for the future, I assume?” Ren asked.

The wizard nodded. “Indeed. One benefit of you all being married couples is saving on space.”

After the group had a good look at the upgraded Omen, they went back into the corridor to check out the next room.

“Is there a place to hold captives?” Sally asked.

“No. I didn’t have the time to put in a torture dungeon.”

“Rats. There I was with a ‘nice rack’ line primed and ready.”

Archie rolled his eyes before opening the door on the left side. This room was almost as large as the ship hangar, but was devoid of any furniture or furnishings.

“Work in progress?” Max ventured.

“Emulated Training Arena. You could consider it a secondary System layer, where it reads your intention and mimics your abilities as you go to use them. Damage is simulated, but no real harm is caused to occupants nor the structure itself.” Archie adjusted his cloak. “I’m rather proud of this one.”

“Ooh, me first!” Sally hopped from one leg to the other. “Do we have time to give it a test run?”

“I guess it couldn’t hurt to have a brief trial.” The wizard gestured to the others. “There’s a small viewing gallery we may climb to, so we aren’t in the way.”

Max grinned and pulled white gloves from his pockets. “You caught my eye, Mrs. Danger, that means we must battle.”

“Hah! We’re playing by pocket-monster rules? You’re on.”

Theo pulled a face as he left with the others, displeased by the zombie’s reference, while Ren didn’t get it at all. Once the three had vanished from the arena and the doors closed, the duelists took up opposing sides in the wide-open space.

The magician bowed before standing up straight. “From the depths of Hell, I call forth Demon Hound!”

With a flash of his hand, a burning sigil bloomed into life on the white floor. Rising mist gave way to a large doberman-like dog of dark red burning fur.

Sally grinned and threw an empty potion bottle to the ground, shattering it. “Big Joe, I choose you!”

Up in the viewing booth behind a one-way window, Theo shook his head gently but smiled. “It’s good to see her more herself.”

“A lot of hard times lately. Can bring anyone down.” Ren watched the giant zombie rise up from the ground with a neutral expression. “Oh, I forgot to thank you for helping with my Aspect.”

“Don’t mention it.” He glanced at the elf and nodded. “Apologies for killing you so soon after.”

“A little more practice and I might have given you some competition. Glad you’re on our side, however.” She leaned forward slightly to look at the wizard. “Now I’m interested to see what Archie’s Aspect looks like.”

“I’m not sure I’ve even seen any of his Ultimates,” Theo agreed. “How are you comparing, boss?”

Archie paused briefly. “Don’t call me that. You are correct that since the STAR+, I have not used any of my Ultimates, nor my Aspect form.”

“Multiple?” Theo narrowed his eyes. “Something tells me you have all six available?”

The wizard turned his attention back to the duel, but a wry smile edged the side of his mouth. “There was the option of giving up all of my power during the reconstruction. I went for a more selfish take. Since the Sea is in part my creation, it bruised my ego to see it so dysfunctional and incomplete.”

“Ah.” The vampire nodded. “So we are glorified tech support.”

“It must take some weight off Chuck’s shoulders,” Ren offered. “It lets him focus solely on Sanctuary and not meddle in all sorts of minor worlds.”

“Naturally. But…” Archie sighed. “There will be others, like the Radoch. Monsters—both human and otherwise—who seek to destroy what we build. We’re not just going to solve problems, but remove troublemakers.”

The trio fell into silence as they watched the odd duel. Big Joe had every advantage against the hound, except speed. The two summons spent a while dancing around each other, neither able to land any damage.

“What are our chances, realistically?” Ren asked.

“Hmm? I know that saying you two are the most level-headed is quite a statement, but I'll be straight with you.” Archie turned from the window to look at them properly. “There are too many variables. What if the first ship is a decoy and we take the bait? What if they deploy viruses or antimatter bombs? What if they have ways to add us to their 'collection' without fighting?”

“All we can hope for is that their arrogance and underestimating us helps us pull ahead then.” Theo exhaled through his nose and closed his eyes.

Ren worked her jaw for a moment. “Two lives each and enemies numbering at least in the hundreds of thousands.”

“I’m working on a third revive, but don’t count on it.” Archie tapped a finger on the top of his cane. “Distribution of any scroll or item capable of resurrection is currently hotly contended. Chuck has pulled many strings, but being able to conjure up those rare items is beyond his scope.”

The elf gave him a curt nod.

“Let’s go rejoin the others.”

Sally stood with her arms crossed. “I guess we’ll call off the stalemate. My enemies usually stay still long enough for Big Joe to hit them. I’ll have to get some… action speed buff scrolls.”

After doffing his top-hat, Max sent away the hound with a wave of his hand. “Although I am sure we won’t just be fighting with a single summon without interfering ourselves. It would be a little unfair if I had brought out a fireball-toting imp.”

“Likewise, if I had risen forty zombies.” She grinned and looked over at the door as it opened up. The rest of the Outsiders walked in. “Is the tour ready to resume?”

“That’s actually all worth showing you at present,” Archie replied. “While we are still inhabitants of Sanctuary I didn’t see it necessary just yet to build living quarters. There are a couple of other projects that are waiting until after the war as well. As such, you now have free rein of the place, or you can return to the surface if you want to get back to training.”

Sally gestured to the empty space. “Gift horse right here, if you’re not too scared, old man.”

The wizard sighed, and the door shut behind them. He stretched his arm out, and with a buzz of magic, his cane extended and widened until it became a lance. Metal plating made of iridescent silver shimmered into reality and attached to Archie’s body until he had a full suit of armor on.

“It’s been a while since I’ve used Mage Knight,” he growled, his voice muffled. “Let’s train.”

The Outsiders drew their weapons, each with a smile on their face.
