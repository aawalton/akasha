The leaf-crown Alyssa had braided for herself blazed with verdant light, and she felt great.

Even if she didn't usually daily drive a Light-based class, she usually had kept at least one Light-based Skill on hand at all times. It was just a really useful form of magic, and it was proving its value ten times over as she was exploring the deep caves of The Jungle. She'd also armed herself with a hefty supply of various nuts and twigs, for additional illumination and mark where she'd already explored.

She didn't need to worry about getting lost, at least. [Rustlewind], particularly in its new Light-mode, was more than sufficient to help her find her way back to the surface no matter how deep she delved, which left the only real remaining concern whether she would get stuck somewhere. Which... well yes, that was a concern. But ⟨Leaf on the Wind⟩ and ⟨Springlaunch⟩ at least made it unlikely. They made her very good at getting out of sticky situations.

The caves themselves were just as impressive as when she'd first found them, nigh a year prior. The deep Magic still resonated through her, a thrum of power that she could feel in her bones. Dark, Water, Earth... they were the start, with lots more past the edges of what she could feel with Wood, Air, and Force. But now, instead of inspiring unease within her, it now was far more of a sense of awe and curiosity. Most of that just came with a solid twenty levels. Even if she was still at just over half her pre-Jump level, her stats were still pretty solid, which helped a lot.

Double-digit Dexterity in particular, with four of those points dedicated specifically for movement, meant that even without [Leafstep] it was basically impossible for her to lose her footing or to slip or anything like that on the frequently wet and slick cave floor.

Distorted reflections of her light cast all kinds of weird illusions and shadows on the stalactites and stalagmites around her... stalagmites and stalactites? Did it make a difference? Whatever.

Pillars of stone, many of which sparkled with crystalline reflections, surrounded her, and she picked her way through what almost felt like a stone Jungle of its own. Insects and small creatures were the only life down here, and they scuttled every which way, plenty fleeing the light she brought with her, while some of them seemed to almost seek it out. Whenever that latter group got too intense, Alyssa would ⟨Illuminate⟩ one of her sacrificial light sources, hide whatever she was using as her main light source, and toss the distraction behind her where it would take any creepy crawlies with it.

This was her... fourth or fifth, depending on what counted, delve into The Underground in the past couple of weeks. So far, she'd encountered a lot of very impressive-looking caves, an outgrowth of glowing mushrooms atop an unrecognizable but decomposing body of some kind, a brief look at a rushing river which came almost immediately out of one wall and went directly into another, a perfectly still pond in a deep cavern filled with all kinds of pure-white fish, and a cavern where the walls and ceiling were equally 'down' to the floor.

In other words, a lot of really cool tourist-like attractions... less on the useful or informative finds. Not that they'd necessarily expected there to be anything super awesome in most of the caves - plenty of caves were just caves - but the Magic she felt radiating from it had seemed like there should have been... well, something more than just what could be found in mostly-mundane caverns. The odds of there being something useful were high, whatever that might be.

She didn't have the divination tablet with her this time, because whenever she had it, she wanted to use it, and this time she just wanted to trust her gut. Nothing better than good old human intuition, taking in the raw and unfiltered sensations from nature and processing them in unparalleled means to grant truly spectacular information. She was just going to follow her instincts and let nature guide her, like the people of old.

And the compass Oliver had made for her that pointed towards increasing Mana densities. Because unlike the people of old, Alyssa quite preferred being alive and not needing to spend multiple lives or lifetimes learning things that a basic tool could help figure out in three seconds flat.

She consulted the compass again, studying it with both her eyes and her touch as the tiny charge of Arcane alignment directed her off to the left, which she then needed to cross-reference with how she could actually go left. Casting any magic... or using actively magical objects like the tablet... would interfere with its measurements. For some reason that didn't include certain types of existing magic, like her inkling clothing or her glowing crown of leaves, which was good because she didn't know what she would do if it did.

She carefully stowed her fragile bit of kit, ⟨Illuminate⟩-[Rustlewind]ed for "passages" while constraining the search area to her front, recast the skill while constraining it a bit more tightly, and followed the trail of light to the tiniest of fissures, way too small for her body to fit through.

She tried again, and this time her spell pointed her towards an opening near the ceiling, where a trickle of water was coming down and was creating an enormous waterfall-like buildup of colorful stone. She shimmied her way up the annoyingly wet and slick wall, scared a bunch of bugs out of their hiding place before she stuck her head in the hole in the ceiling, then slipped her way into the too-small passageway.

Alyssa needed to crawl on her hands and knees for a little while, heavily using ⟨Tumbling⟩ to prevent her from getting bruised, until it opened up into a new, larger cavern.

This one felt different, and practically all of it was a rich cobalt in color, hard stone of that color covering every inch. Her appearance startled a giant pile of lizard-like creatures, which skittered off into the still-dark parts of the cavern, and after a moment of stretching - Dexterity may have allowed her to fit through uncomfortably small spaces, but it didn't stop the desire to stretch out afterwards - she retrieved the mana-compass and kept moving.

Nobody ever liked to talk about the boring bits of exploration where you didn't find anything exciting, so why couldn't they have been the rare parts?

The caveclaw pounced at Alyssa again, the speedy little bugger - all hard, sharp scales that left a ton of nicks in the stone surroundings - being as territorial as it had been when it first tried driving Alyssa off. She had figured out via [Rustlewind] that she'd accidentally tread too close to its nest, and would need to get even closer to follow the mana-gradient. She was pretty sure she was getting close to something neat, but to get to this particular portion of the caves she'd needed to climb up a fairly substantial way, and really didn't want to have to repeat the experience. Which was unfortunate, because this entire cavern was apparently the claimed territory of the weird, way too pointy and way too bouncy creature.

What made it particularly annoying was how each time it pounced at her, it curled up, flinging itself at her face like if a cat were actually a porcupine made out of knives, and Alyssa's axe was really bad at hitting past its defenses. So, she'd spent the last couple minutes ducking frantically around the open cavern, trying to get a few moments of peace that she could do some kind of tracer-fire [Rustlewind] combo.

"Fire!" She finished her latest attempt, and a few sparks leaped off her hand... and immediately fizzled out.

She still managed to dodge it - a creature whose primary attack required flinging its entire body through the air wasn't hard to avoid, it was just really intimidating to face down. One slip and she'd be taking basically a ton of knives to the face, and Clark wasn't around to fix it.

Some loose rocks caught her eye, and as the caveclaw wound up for another leap, Alyssa bounded over to them, scooped one up in her hand, and just... threw it. Charging up a ⟨Springlaunch⟩ would take longer than she wanted, but swapping to a baseball throw was faster than moving her entire body out of the way. The rock collided with a solid thunk, and Alyssa moved to...

Oh.

That killed it.

Oops?

Well, she wasn't going to tear herself up over it. But a fist-sized rock, thrown by someone with four Strength, colliding with something not that much bigger than a housecat did make sense it would be lethal. She just... hadn't meant to kill it that quickly.

Alyssa decided to move on before looking at the limp body could make her feel weirder. It could have at least had the decency to be in the center of a slowly-spreading pool of blood or something.

Though, as she followed the bend in the cave to the next section, all of those thoughts just floated away.

Crystals, mostly a very blue-purple in color but shimmering in every color, filled her vision. Massive columns, wider than she was tall, stretched across this cavern, crisscrossing in every direction. Along the edges, smaller crystals only as thick as her arm and as tall as she was jutted out of the floor. The light from her crown lanced out dramatically, filling the area but almost lagging as it reached the edges, going far slower than instantaneously.

She hopped into the room, sliding slightly as she landed on one of the crystal pillars, and looked around. Magic filled the air, pressing in on her and rejuvenating personal mana. It felt a bit weirder than usual, but other than a brief hitch in her breathing and a renewed sense of power, it didn't really impact her. Besides, she already knew how awesome being in the Material Realms was, and she had better things to pay attention to right now.

It really did seem like the inside of an absolutely enormous geode, but somehow even better. Some crystals, the size of her hand or even smaller, were floating through the air, and needed to be moved out of the way or ducked around. She briefly tapped on ⟨Illuminate⟩d [Leafstep] to help her slide down the angled crystals like a ramp, looking for the bottom. She wasn't able to quite identify one, her way instead blocked by other crystals growing at odd angles.

Alyssa ran her hands along the crystals, feeling the magic within them. Crystal was obviously the most present element, but there was the same Shadow and Earth she was familiar with, alongside Light and Illusion, Force... trace amounts of what might have been Mud and... Life? Some Significance for sure, perhaps a bit of Primal.

"Definitely seems useful," she muttered.

She snagged a few of the loose crystals - some of the floating ones seemed fairly stuck in place, which was interesting, but she just grabbed ones which weren't - and stashed them in her bag, withdrawing a 'divination beacon' as she did so. The 'beacon' in question this time was actually just a dragon's skull that had been briefly dipped in iron, and was only a beacon in the sense that it was something very distinct both physically and magically, which they could later find on that basis.

Before she headed out, she had some food - smoked and dried meat, same as always, because nothing else really traveled well - wound up finishing her potable water, which wasn't great but was... fine, and took a brief nap on the most horizontal crystal pillar she could find. Once rested up, she asked [Rustlewind] to "lead her home," and... went up?

That was unexpected.

She dropped a trail of nuts as she followed the light-thread to make retracing her route easier, ⟨Springlaunch⟩ed past a couple of what would be quite annoying climbs, and unexpectedly found herself flashbanged by daylight reflecting off a pool of water as she landed on the top of one of the climbs. Once her eyes adjusted and she made it out of The Underground, she was a bit surprised to find herself already back in The Jungle proper. The entrance was a bit blocked by a few boulders, but they were easy enough to squeeze past and emerge out into a small break in the trees.

She left another divination beacon - this one a pottery shard with some of Clark's blood on it - at the entrance, and launched herself towards the treetops, dropping the ⟨Illuminate⟩d [Rustlewind] in favor of listening to The Jungle directly. Somewhat surprisingly, she found herself on Clifftop - the area that Clark and Jacob had first washed up on, the portion of The Jungle which was beyond the massive cliff Shelter had been tucked up next to. She hadn't thought about it that much when she'd actually been cave-delving, but she had been traveling up a lot...

Whatever. It was disorienting, but she could deal with it.

⟨Leaf on the Wind⟩ buoyed her as she launched herself off the cliff, and she soared across the sky.

And only needed to dodge a single fourgle.

And three dragons.

Once Alyssa got over a bit of the shock that came with the Jungle wastelands having grown a tiny little corridor zooping across the landscape, right up to First Tower, and then veering off to the side - courtesy of Warrior Jacob Veeran... man it was a shame his last name wasn't pronounced that way - she was more than happy to pass off her findings to the team and let them look over her bounty. It wasn't just the crystals, as she'd come across a couple new edible plants on her journey that Clark was really happy to try and work with, but the crystals were obviously the most interesting.

"That is some good resonance," Oliver muttered, two fingers resting on the crystal he was studying, his other hand twisting in some uncomfortable-looking ways. The crystal in his hand was glowing, and so was the one he was merely touching, though not quite the same color. "It would require a bit of an adjustment to the enchantments, and some various forms of refinement, but I do think we can probably replace the Light Crystals and Network Caches with these, materially speaking. And I think it'll be easier to make some of the spider-bot logic, once I get around to that, with these... You said that there was a whole cavern of this stuff?"

"Yeah," Alyssa answered, between sips of her soup. "Like a massive Geode the size of a building, at least."

"Definitely worth some kind of harvesting setup, then," Oliver nodded.

"Is it?" she had to ask. "It's not like it's that much work to make a bunch of glass, when we're all working together."

"Any work we can offload, the better. I've been waiting three days for you to get back, because we need to make more now. Some of my more recent attempts to get the Metalpress working have been... kind of destructive for the Caches, so I went through them way faster than expected."

"Ah."

"So now that you're back, that's going to be tomorrow's full-day activity. It's fine," he sighed. "I was so annoyed when I ran out of them, I really felt like I was getting somewhere."

Oliver tapped at the purplish crystal a couple more times. "I'm kind of doubtful that random inklings will be able to cut it for harvesting these... gahhh. Okay. How many of these smaller ones were present? I can probably use those to figure out what processing is needed to get them turned into something useful. But getting pieces of the bigger ones... maybe I can base some kind of harvesting tool based on the Metalpress, once that's working? Transmute a material into a smaller piece? That's... hmm."

"Well," Alyssa snapped her fingers, giving a bit of a wink. "I'm glad I don't need to figure it out."

"Yeah. You don't figure much out," Oliver absentmindedly responded.

"What's that supposed to mean, huh? Is that really a way to talk to a fellow mage?"

"I suppose you did figure out the instant-firing spell," he continued. "Which isn't nothing. But it's still not much."

"And how many skills have you figured out how to create, huh?"

"Enough. Hmm. Maybe that's the solution?" Oliver fully stopped paying attention to her. "Maybe I need to increase the power more, flash-forging it... take some reference material from the instant-firing kiln..."

"Don't you want to ask my opinion on what I think is wrong with the press, huh?" Alyssa asked. She had... absolutely no idea what was wrong. But she could probably get some kind of reaction out of Oliver by wording things just right where he couldn't say she was wrong about things.

"Huh?" She repeated a moment later, when Oliver seemed to be more determined to ignore her.

Oliver didn't respond, too busy with his own thoughts than to respond to her totally mature and definitely more-important-than-their-actual-mission teasing.

How dare he.
