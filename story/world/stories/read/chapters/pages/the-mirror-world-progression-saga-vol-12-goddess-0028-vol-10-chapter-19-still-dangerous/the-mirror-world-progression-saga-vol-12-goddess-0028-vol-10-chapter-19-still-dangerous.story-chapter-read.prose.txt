Samantha could tell immediately that she’d won by more than 5 Effective Levels. Reality twisted and contorted itself around the lich, pulling away and separating him completely from mana. His source for magic. She could already tell it wouldn’t unmake him or what animated him. That was too innate, in this case.

The effect on the lich — who’d finally climbed out of the acid to stand — was immediate. It seemed like he was struck suddenly in the head and stumbled as if stunned. The knowledge somehow contained within that decrepit noggin had suddenly vanished. The shadow aura flickered as his mana was taken away, but there was something in the staff that drained and kept it going. Barely.

Less to work with. Far less, and hopefully without recharge. We’ll see.

The lich screamed in rage, so loud it pierced even his reduced shadow armor, to scour the room. He thrust his staff at Samantha angrily, causing pages to flutter further toward the end. Ominously. A sickly green nimbus of energy surrounded the staff, and then an array of zipping beams began firing from the pages, one after the other. A great mass fired continuously within seconds, each deadly on its own.

Samantha, with her absolutely ludicrous levels of speed, dodged the first few handily, sliding away from the beams with mere fingers and hairs to spare each time, as the green energy passed her by to hit the walls and ceiling. One beam just shaved her armor hanging over her shoulder, disintegrating it. Another beam that she estimated she’d fail to dodge hit her floating shield, which her minotaur friend had gifted her, Ardioq, quickly disintegrating almost half of the big round protector, as the beam was absorbed and ate the material.

She dodged another, barely, swinging away in her flight. Every one she dodged got harder, though, as the unpredictable beams made her jerk around in chaotic zig-zag patterns. Their speed was frighteningly comparable to hers, likely due to Accuracy bonuses.

A beam was directed right where she was dodging, and she knew it would hit her — she moved the shield in the way again, but this time, she used its reflective surface for [Reflect Force].

The beam immediately reflected and hit the shadow armor of the lich, scouring it, causing it to smoke and dissipate, going even slightly transparent, making the furious countenance of the Consolidator just visible underneath.

But the beams still cascaded toward her. She scrambled to dodge. Another. Another — just barely, thanks to expending the Dodge boost from Three Small Fortunes. They were endless! How many more? Another beam, she missed a shield block exactly where the destroyed portion would’ve blocked it-

Shit.

It hit her nastily: dead-on to the heart.

No! Not there, not again!

Energy scoured her, destroying her remaining armor, which sloughed off like wax from the rest of her body. The rest bit deep, begging for some sort of alteration of chance. She had an Alter Fate use, and some measure of panic wanted her to blow it.

But she had something else. She forced herself to keep a cool head and utilized Battle Dress of the First Mage: Hair’s Breadth to change a Critical Wound to a Minor Wound.

You have suffered a Minor Wound to the heart. No penalties yet enforced due to Avatar's equivalent body toughness.

You have successfully avoided pain penalties due to resistance.

It hurt, like pins and needles, but it wasn’t that big of a deal. She could ignore it.

Right. Right. Right. It’s just a meatsack. I don’t even need a heart! It’s the same as every other part!

Well, it wasn’t entirely true, because it gave a wounding upgrade all the same.

Samantha was desperately trying to get her head around the fact that the staff’s attacks were capable of inflicting Critical Wounds on her. She’d taken his magic away, and yet he was still two or three hits away from destroying her. Possibly even one on a head strike. Boom! Light’s out.

After all I’ve done, he’s still fucking dangerous in the extreme. This is insane.

There were two beams left, and she was well on target to dodge them both in her current movements, not needing to account for running into other beams.

She dodged the second-to-last beam. And then the last suddenly changed course, angling back at her like it had turned into a super-fast snake made of green energy.

What the fuck?!

Samantha couldn’t afford to get hit by that thing. She had a Fate use left, but that was for last resort. Wasn’t she at the last resort? Almost. Ideally, they finished the bastard off after this, his last gasp. But what if they didn’t? Was this his ‘item backup’ standard? It was damned effective. He might use it again and wipe her out. Ultimately, if she were hedging bets, she’d just add some Wound Negations.

Or… Gabriel. The shield.

Samantha couldn’t avoid the snaking last beam. She soaked it and utilized [Alter Fate] to win their contest, avoiding all wounds.

Immediately after this, she felt the staff drain again and refuel the lich’s shadow armor, making his features hidden behind the opaque void once more.

We need lots of firepower. No problem, right?

Her few remaining segments of the wire mass moved into a reserve defensive position. They were no longer going to accomplish much offensively, so it was best that the lich just ignore them until they could move themselves into the way of attacks. If she had to endure another beam-volley, that could make the difference in survival.

First things first, Samantha sent a message in the immediate team mental gestalt. “Gabriel, I need you to use the shield when this bitch attacks again. If it comes to it. Be ready. All-out defense, don’t worry about getting wounds on your lizardman. Just keep him at bay. No one can do anything to us when the shield effect drops.”

“Roger, Madam!” Gabriel replied. “You can count on me!”

“I’m going to start attacking,” Azure added. To this, Samantha only sent a mental nod, even as she was already spellcasting herself.

Despite the need for firepower and despite Gabriel’s shield, Samantha felt way too naked in this fight without wound negations. She hedged, using some now precious mana to double-cast. The first was the level 12 general spell, Supreme Spellarmor, giving her 3 wound negations, restoring some physical armor (as glowing pink solid matter encasing her body), and even improving her current Magical Armor total by +1.

It was something of a downgrade from using [Sheath of Light], but that’s why it was a hedge job. A spell was a spell; an ability, an ability. With some notable exceptions like [Light Manipulation], she had to use something just shy of 5 seconds to use abilities, giving her more flexibility with magic, albeit at high mana expenditure.

For her second spell… she cursed herself for not having a custom, high-level ‘machinegun’ spell by now, like she had been planning. She’d just never gotten around to it. She needed the damn reflection effect more for this battle! She didn’t think she’d need to shred wound negations. She didn’t, technically. She briefly considered [Aura of Punishment] for a free attack round to round, but then she wouldn’t attack immediately, which was practically admitting they couldn’t take him out before he attacked again.

By the look of things in her own mid-casting, the lich was doing something with the staff again. What else? She could try and take the staff away, but it was likely a fool’s errand rather than clever. Her own staff was about as ‘take-awayable’ as her titles. She could summon it right back. And a priceless artifact was not going to go poof from a Shatter type effect, either. If she wanted to damage it, it would take time, same as getting through his stupid shadow armor — which was weaker, which couldn’t last forever.

No. She was going to attack the armor. Sadly, default spells weren’t really oriented around rapid fire. Magi liked big booms. That was just the reality of spellcraft.

But she did have an Array-type spell, low level but punching higher. [Mirror Array]. Despite being low-level, all damaging spells used the same contest for the most part, anyway. The bad thing was the maximum wound capacity — it was designed to shred wound negations, after all. She didn’t really know if wound severity mattered against the armor. Maybe just the contest mattered? Perhaps it would drain the armor before the actual reduction. Based on her memory, it was distinctly possible. That would make the spell even stronger against this than against wound negations.

And was the contest high? Technically, two blasts from her lasers could be used round to round, with a higher base total… but [Light Manipulation] did not benefit from her magic dress bonus from Sorcerous Onslaught, which was to spell wound severity. She’d tested this, and it didn’t count, even trying to pull bullshit aesthetics to make light beams through her staff. No dice. The bonus only applied to certain destruction effects like [Spellfyr: Unleash], or something involving a legit whack with the staff. It made sense, pretty much. Lasers normally shot from her finger, or bigger blasts from her palm.

But it did apply to [Mirror Array] because it was a spell. Furthermore, [Mirror Array] was low-level and cheap and could have [Alter Spell: Amp] applied to it. That made it not-so-cheap, but that was a total +6 bonus that her lasers couldn’t get. Technically, that shit reached a higher total! A comparison that was even worse for higher-level spells, though they couldn’t match the cost efficiency and convenience of her beloved ‘finger lasers.’

But there is still the Minor Wound maximum thing… ugh. It’s either really good for this or complete and utter trash.

It was a gamble. She tumbled it around in her head for the brief fraction of a moment she had, and by cognition and intuition, she decided to go for it. She cast [Mirror Array], using two SP to save 20 MP for the Amp modifier. She utilized the Alternate Effect, too, to make them fire physical burning beams, figuring that would be better against the shadow.

A small horde of tiny mirrors spawned in an arc over her head. Back-to-back, she fired two of them at the lich, who probably had about as much chance of dodging them as he did dodging his own stench of decay.
