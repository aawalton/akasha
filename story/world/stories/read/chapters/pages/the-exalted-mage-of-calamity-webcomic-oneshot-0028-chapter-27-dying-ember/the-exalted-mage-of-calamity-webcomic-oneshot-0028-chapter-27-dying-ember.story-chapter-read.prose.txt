“Go! Move! Move!” a guard shouted.

Two armored guardsmen—the ones stationed at the eastern gate—came rushing back toward town.

“I can’t believe the Captain is calling a town emergency alert,” the male guard huffed.

Their armor rattled as another pair of guards passed them, already briefed and moving to replace them at the gates.

“You think it’s that kid?” the female guard asked. “The one talking about demons?”

“Damn that idiot. I can’t believe he went to the Captain and convinced him,” the man growled.

All around them, people poured from their homes. Shouts overlapped, confusion spreading through the streets. Able-bodied townsfolk turned toward the barracks—all registered members of the militia, answering the call.

Then a bright flash sparked at the edge of their vision, far off.

Both guards stopped and looked toward the forest.

“Hey,” she exhaled, squinting, “was that the direction the kid came from?”

The man’s face scrunched beneath his helmet as he stared at the fading light.

“What the hell is going on?”

Finn had severely misjudged the speed of the crankbarrow. There was a reason for the warning not to overcrank it. The only light came from the moonbeams and the flames at the front of the crankbarrow, which Finn used to steer the death contraption through the dark forest.

He took the route he always used, one that had been beaten and worn down by his steps and those of the other kids who sometimes followed him. He knew the path even with his eyes closed. No stray branches or bushes would surprise him. The only problems were the occasional bumps and rocks in the way.

Although Greystone’s forest was mostly flat, there were a few hills just outside the town. That meant most of the crankbarrow’s speed was lost while climbing them. But once the downward slope came, he accelerated far faster than he wanted.

Finn zoomed through the forest, trying his best to remember each marker that told him where he was. A tree that looked like a fork. A dead, withered bush he had accidentally set on fire once.

But everything flew past him far too quickly.

Then something caught his attention. Finn leaned forward slightly and noticed a light ahead of him. A small spark. One growing so quickly that—

“Ah—!” Finn screamed just as the cart’s wheels rolled over a rock. The sudden disruption, combined with Finn leaning forward, tipped the crankbarrow over. Finn immediately sat down and curled himself into as tight a ball as he could just as the machine fell onto its side and skidded several meters before crashing into a tree.

“Augh…” Finn groaned. His fingers scratched against the dirt as he pulled himself out of the overturned crankbarrow. He pressed a hand against his side. It stung. A bruise, for sure.

Finn slowly stood, catching his breath between a few coughs.

He turned around to look at the crankbarrow and noticed that the remaining coals had spilled out. The fire inside had been completely snuffed, probably due to the sudden impact. The coals still looked hot, but he had landed mostly on bare dirt, so there was likely little danger of a spreading fire.

He turned toward the glow that had distracted him before. It was much brighter now. He limped toward it, one hand pressed against his side as he moved.

Ahead of him was a large scorch mark along the ground. Leaves had been burned away and bushes blown aside, as though someone had detonated dynamite in the middle of the forest.

As he drew closer, he noticed something even more important.

Someone was sitting against a tree with a fallen lantern at his side.

A man.

His hood had fallen back, and blood ran from a gash near his hairline, dripping over both eyes. He kept blinking, but it only smeared the red across his face. One hand twitched weakly at his side, while the other pressed against his ribs.

A cracked mask hung loosely around his neck.

Finn stopped with one hand against a tree, not daring to take another step forward.

Then he heard the man groan.

“Agh… Jon… is that you…?” the cultist rasped. “Jon… you there…?”

Finn froze for a moment, his fingers tense, but then he quickly considered the situation.

Who else would be out in the forest at this hour? That certainly didn’t sound like a demon. Nor did it sound like Claire or Veronica.

That left only one other possibility.

Thinking on his feet, Finn tried to deepen his voice. The bruise along his ribs might have even helped with the impersonation.

“Y-yeah…” He stuck to simple, short words that might fool the man.

“You sound different,” the man said, his words airy and strained.

“Hurt,” Finn said as deeply as he could.

Silence followed for a moment. Sweat dripped down Finn’s face as he waited.

Had the man caught on?

Then the cultist coughed. “Hit by the brat too?” he asked.

Finn let out a quiet sigh of relief. But… brat? What brat?

“Yeah…” Finn said.

Trying to get a better look, Finn slowly crept to the side.

The man was severely injured. Maybe he couldn’t even stand properly.

Finn could run if he needed to.

“Damn whelp. Thought the draught would keep her down. But that insane bastard actually gave her—” He coughed violently, raising his hand to his mouth. He looked at it for a brief moment, but the blood in his eyes obscured his vision. “—gave her a damn artifact.”

Claire.

This was one of the men who had kidnapped Claire. And whoever this Jon was. But from the sound of it… she had escaped?

“Where’s Marven?” the man asked.

Finn pursed his lips. “Dead,” he said, simply guessing.

The man leaned his head back. It began to sway from side to side, as though he were dizzy. “Figures.”

Finn had to take a risk. “Where are the others?” he asked, doing his best to mumble the words.

“Others?”

“Got hit hard,” Finn said, making something up. He didn’t dare utter anything else. Hopefully, it was vague but clear enough.

“They’re already in town. Unless you mean the others at the mines,” the man replied.

The town… the ones Veronica had warned were going to attack. But what about the mines? Wasn’t the ritual site supposed to be in the forest?

“Mines?”

For a moment, the man didn’t answer. Slowly, his head tilted forward again, though it continued to sway.

“You… who are you?” the man asked slowly. “You… look too small to be Jon.”

Finn’s body tensed again, but only for a moment.

He had gotten a clue.

Immediately, he turned and bolted, but not before lunging forward to grab the fallen lantern.

“H-hey!” the man shouted, reaching out to grab him before coughing violently. A spatter of blood painted the ground in front of him. He looked up and saw Finn running away with his lantern, leaving him in darkness.

“C-come back!”

Finn ran through the forest. The crankbarrow he had… borrowed was out of commission. Its wheels had broken off, and the coals were too hot for him to throw back inside. He also had a sneaking suspicion that the entire thing might explode.

Finn was now headed toward the mines. He had just visited the ritual site, and as expected, the stones there had all been smashed and destroyed. Everything stripped down, the surrounding ground all burnt and thrashed. Piles upon piles of that ashvein powder had been poured all over the stones.

Everything that had been set up was now gone.

If that dying cultist’s words held any truth, then perhaps he would find a clue at the mines.

As he ran, a thunderous boom split the night behind him. A bright flash tore across the sky—a lightning bolt in the middle of the clear darkness. Gone in a second.

Veronica! You’re alive!

Relief surged through him. Only she could make the sky burn like that. She was still fighting. Still alive.

Finn smiled and clenched his fists.

He crested the hill and ran down toward the old Greystone mountains. Jagged stone rose against the night sky, and there—beneath splintered beams—yawned a dark mouth in the rock: the entrance to the abandoned mine.

He slowed to a stop and looked around. No voices. No people. Only the distant rumble and the occasional flash far behind him.

The moment he stepped beneath the beams, darkness swallowed him. He slowed, making each step as quiet as possible. The silence pressed in, broken only by the faint scrape of his shoes against stone.

For a long moment, there was nothing. Just blackness and his own heartbeat.

Then he saw a flicker.

Light spilled from ahead. Torches burned in the dark.

There shouldn’t have been torches burning in an abandoned mine. That told Finn only one thing.

He pressed himself against the damp wall and crept deeper. The air was colder, heavy with dust and something sharper—copper and rot. Every step was careful. Even his breathing felt too loud.

The torchlight grew stronger. He reached a bend in the tunnel and crouched low, inching forward until he could peer around the corner.

Two robed figures moved in the chamber beyond, their shadows stretching long across the stone walls, warped by the dancing flames.

Finn didn’t know their faces, not really—but one of them made his stomach twist. He had seen that jaw, that hooked nose, on a poster nailed to an inn’s bulletin board months ago. A missing man. Farmer? Merchant? He couldn’t remember. He only remembered that the face had been stamped with a single word:

Wanted.

The two men worked in silence, bent over a ritual circle inscribed into the dirt. Dark streaks glistened along its lines.

Finn’s gut tightened.

Demon blood, he realized. It looked far too dark and vile to be human blood. And he had seen a dead deer carcass before, so it wasn’t animal blood either.

They adjusted vials, poured measured drops of liquid, and traced the edges with care. Unlike the one in the ruins, this circle was far more intricate. Fuller and more complex. As though the other one had been incomplete—an imitation—and this was the genuine article.

“They should’ve brought the girl by now.” The first man’s voice was impatient, fraying at the edges. He glanced at a pocket watch resting on a small table beside him.

The other—the one from the wanted poster—shifted impatiently, rubbing the back of his head in a nervous tic.

“I don’t know. Maybe they ran into a boar or something in the forest. You think they’re dead?”

The man scoffed and shook his head. “If they’re dead, they’re dead. We’ll move to Plan B. The viscount and the baron won’t lift a finger for commoners. They’ll be too busy protecting themselves.”

Finn’s heart thumped hard and fast. He dug his fingernails into the dirt to keep himself still, but the urge to run and shout clawed at him.

They really are going to do it. Even without Claire. They’ll slaughter anyone just to finish this.

That sealed it. If the ritual wasn’t stopped here, it wouldn’t matter what Veronica did to the demon. It wouldn’t matter how many guards Hadrian rallied. The cult would simply keep going until they had their sacrifice.

They were that confident they could lay siege to the town.

He pressed himself flatter against the corner as the two men turned, their backs to him now, crouching low as they resumed working over the glowing circle.

The man working on the circle was being oddly precise, as though he had to get every line exactly right. Even back at the ritual site, the circle had been pristine whenever it appeared during their chants.

Maybe if he could ruin it, the ritual could be stopped. Judging by the contents of the room, there didn’t seem to be any other jars of blood. Perhaps that was the last one they had. If he could just knock it over and stall them, then maybe he could prevent another demon from being summoned, at least for the time being.

It was risky… but no one else knew they were here. He just had to get in and out. He couldn’t wait and risk more lives. He could do something now.

He could become a hero.

His fingers dug into his pocket. He pulled out three small black-gray pellets. These were the smoke pellets he had snatched from the cultist who had knocked him out and kidnapped him. Instead of grabbing just one, he had taken the whole pouch. He had lost a few of them while running, but that didn’t matter.

He still had enough.

Finn crouched low and peered into the room once more. The cultists continued talking. Once their backs were turned, Finn tossed two pellets into the center of the chamber.

Gray smoke burst free, rapidly swelling and spreading across the small room. It swallowed the torchlight within seconds, dull orange light smearing across the walls through the wispy haze.

The man drawing the circle stood in a panic. “What the—”

The second cultist spun around, a knife flashing from his robes. His eyes widened at the sight within the smoke—a filthy boy, no older than twelve, standing near the tunnel entrance.

“What?!” he barked. “Who the hell are you? How did you get in here?”

Finn stood there shaking, his breaths ragged and his entire body trembling. He was scared, and he couldn’t stop it.

“…A brat?” The man sneered, swatting smoke away from his face. He stepped closer, his hand tightening around his knife. “You’re from the town, aren’t you? You’re going to regret this.”

Before anything else could happen, the mountain roared.

A deafening explosion thundered from outside, shaking the tunnel. The torches flickered as dust and stone rained from above. The mountain groaned, deep and angry, and rocks collapsed behind Finn, partially sealing the passage.

Finn’s eyes barely registered it, flicking behind him before returning to the cultists.

What was that? Was that the mountain? Why? Why was it collapsing now of all times?

The head of the cultist drawing the circle snapped upward, panic flashing across his face. “What—what’s going on?!”

The cultist with the knife gritted his teeth and lunged toward Finn, his weapon at the ready.

But Finn saw it coming. He threw his final pellet between himself and the charging cultist, immediately shrouding the cave in even more smoke.

The man swung, but his knife merely cut through the air.

He began to cough. “Damn it!” he shouted. “Where’d you go?!”

“No!” his partner’s voice cracked a moment later.

The man swatted more smoke out of the way. “What?! What happened?!”

He stepped closer, though the haze made it extremely difficult to see. But by pure chance, he noticed a shadow moving near the ground. It was only the sound of Finn’s footsteps that gave him away.

“Got you!” the man shouted.

Finn was suddenly hoisted into the air, a hand bursting through the smoke and clamping around his throat. His back slammed against the nearest chamber wall. The air vanished from his lungs.

“You stupid brat!” the other cultist yelled.

Beside him, the vial of blood had shattered and spilled. Demonic blood splattered across the seal like a bucket of spilled water.

“The seal’s ruined!” His glare snapped toward Finn, his bloodshot eyes livid with rage. “That’s going to take hours to fix! Not to mention us having to beg that demon for more blood! Do you know how many people he’s killed?!”

The cultist holding Finn leaned closer, his face twisted with rage. “Do you have any idea what you’ve done?”

Finn’s mouth opened soundlessly.

He knew exactly what he had done.

He had stopped it.

The mine groaned once more.

The sound was low and vast, like the mountain itself shifting in its sleep. Finn’s head jerked upward just as the first wooden support beam overhead split.

Finn’s legs hung limply as he was held in the air. Both of his hands clutched the cultist’s arm, but there was barely any strength left in them. The world grew hazy before his eyes.

His gaze focused on the panicked cultist for a moment. Then it drifted toward the ritual circle.

Nothing else mattered now.

He had done it. He had saved everyone. He could finally be recognized as the greatest hero of Greystone.

Just like his father.

Crack.

The sound echoed like a cannon firing. Splinters rained down as the timber sagged, bending beneath the weight above it and beginning to snap.

“Quick! Get out! It’s collapsing!” the man yelled, throwing Finn to the ground.

He landed with a crash. Through his blurred vision, he saw the cultists running, black shapes stomping across the stone.

A loud snap of wood echoed above him as pebbles began to fall.

“Damn it! The beam fell!” one of them shouted. “It’s blocked!”

“Move it!”

“Help me push it up!”

Their shouts blurred together, and soon everything became hazy. The sounds faded as the smoke continued to fill Finn’s lungs. The thud of rocks striking his head echoed through his skull.

His eyes squeezed shut one final time as a crash sounded overhead. A large slab of rock broke loose beside him, beginning a cascade of falling stone.

“Veronica…”

The beam above him broke, and everything came down in a storm of dust and falling debris. Once the first support shattered, the rest followed, caving in the small mineshaft as the mountain shuddered.

Finn’s voice never left the cave.

All sound became the rumble of collapsing rock, burying Finn, the cultists, and the demonic ritual circle that had been at the center of everything.
