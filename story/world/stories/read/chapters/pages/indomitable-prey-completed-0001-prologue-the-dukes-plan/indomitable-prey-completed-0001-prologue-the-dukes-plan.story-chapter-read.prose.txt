Duke Ryonic flicked blood from his phantom blades with a sigh. The spy had come far too close to escaping this time.

First the sultanate had sent their Al Alakir skulking around, then he’d found a sellsword hired by Escribar sniffing at his gates, followed by the Council of Talsyn instructing an inquisitorial agent to sidle through his lands, and then once he had finally dispatched them and dealt with the diplomatic blowback of that whole situation, the sultanate had tried sneaking around once more. His castle was beginning to resemble a diplomatic base for the various powers of the Sunset Kingdoms rather than the seat of power of one of their weakest.

He dismissed his weapons and pulled a cloth from his belt, wiping away the viscera that caked his forearms with weary detachment. His mind was elsewhere. If any of them learned what he had discovered here…

He bent down to grab the ankle of the unfortunate infiltrator and dragged him across the walkway. Dark crimson stains marked the wood of the scaffold, but the duke didn’t mind. It would be covered by morning by the ever-present black dust of the excavation. He looked out over the chasm below and the obsidian pyramid that hung beneath, inverted and hanging in the empty void like the dark of the moon on an even darker night.

The body fell through open air for a long moment, the duke hearing no impact. He strode back to the exit across the criss-crossed stairs that clung to the inverted pyramid of volcanic slabs. They were steady. Reliable. He had made sure to pay well the labourers he had employed and paid them even better to leave the Sunsets and speak of their task to no one once complete. He had considered killing them to ensure their silence, but he had a begrudging respect for their work and knew that to punish competence with death would only deter it in others.

That was something the aristocrats and nobles that ruled the varied principalities of the Sunset Kingdoms didn’t seem to understand. There were always those with ambitions in any territory, and he pitied the ruler who gave them no outlet for it except rebellion. Rebellion was a topic he knew well, after all, as was ambition. He hoped to teach the upper echelons of the Sunsets a lesson in both one day.

He knew that most thought him a brute, a soldier risen far above his station by the strength of his arm and a willingness to dispatch whoever stood in his way. And they were right in that assessment. But what they simply refused to see was that he was just like their forefathers, and the only difference between them was a few generations of iron rule to wash away the bloodstains and legitimatize his position.

Before he could leave the titanic cavern and return to Castle Ryonic, he was accosted by a streak of purple. He watched the light resolve itself into a burst of shadowed feathers, and the next moment saw a raven perched before him on the dusty handrail. It ruffled its feathers, peering at him with keen intelligence in its beady eyes.

It opened its beak, hacked out a cough, and then spoke with the eerily smooth voice of his spymaster.

“The Sage has sent word—another is arriving imminently.”

He nodded. “How soon?”

“Weeks,” Varice replied, channeling her voice through the shadowed raven. “She cannot know for certain, but she has the location almost exactly: an abandoned church at the foot of the Unclaimed Peaks. I have it marked.”

The duke frowned. “How is that she can predict to the mile the arrival point, but not the exact date?”

“The Sage is reluctant to discuss the nature of her class, but from what she has let slip I believe that the timing and location are in constant flux. The more specific the sense of one, the more vague the other.”

He hummed to himself. “It would match the pattern of the previous tips she has given us,” he said with a sigh. “No matter. I cannot spare the men—Decker and Harman are the only two I would trust for something like this, and both are recovering from their last ill-fated sojourn into Storm’s Harbour, and I cannot leave without risking the discovery of the vault. We’ll need to consider other options.”

“The Lions?” the raven asked, and he nodded gravely.

“Yes, loathe as I am to involve them. You have a contact in the Academy, do you not?”

It was her turn to nod, though she seemed hesitant. How a partially incorporeal raven made of swirling shadows could communicate that emotion through body language alone was a mystery to the duke, but one couldn’t argue with reality.

“Speak your mind,” he said with a wave.

“I am not the only one with such contacts. I suspect all of the major kingdoms within the Court have them, probably with deeper penetration than I, so it will be… difficult… to disguise our activities.”

The duke looked to the ceiling far above, tracing the criss-cross of the rickety-looking scaffold as it snaked its way to the top of the cavern. It didn’t matter that it was made of foot-thick wooden beams hewn from hundred-year oaks and reinforced with expert precision. Anything contrasted against the monolithic structure of volcanic glass that dominated the dim cavern would look feeble and weak.

“You told me many years ago that we could track the Butcher reliably,” he began, and waited for an answer from Varice.

“Yes, I recall.”

“Is that still true?”

“Yes,” she confirmed slowly, sounding perplexed. “But again, the problem is not with finding her. We could track her halfway across the continent if needed, but we have nobody capable of killing her. That is likely more the case now than it was years ago. Why?”

“We don’t need to disguise our actions for long. The excavation is only a few months from complete, and if we can obtain a God-Touched soon…”

“I don’t follow,” Varice said, and he took a selfish moment to relish the feeling of knowing something she didn’t. That wasn’t often the case when dealing with a spymaster, after all.

“We use the Butcher as cover. She and her companions are mercenaries, correct?” He let the hook dangle before her for a few more moments before confirming his intentions. “We hire them.”

The raven looked at him like he was crazy for several heartbeats, but then the brilliant mind of the woman behind it began to whir to life, and it cocked its head.

“Easy enough to create a fake contract near the Unclaimed Peaks. It doesn’t even have to be through us—I have plenty of intermediaries that I can burn some favours with.”

He could almost see her in his mind’s eye—pacing as the plan spooled out from her mind, her hands rising to bat the invisible thread of intrigue around like a cat as possibilities and problems simultaneously occurred to her and were solved.

“We lure the Butcher to the Unclaimed Peaks—it gives us a great pretext to send a team without arousing suspicion from the other kingdoms. They’ll have little knowledge of the God-Touched til we have him trussed and secured.

“The quest itself will have to be close by, and how will our team break away? But no, we send a full complement. The Butcher will deal with any that get too close to her, but we’ll have a small team—a loyal one - break off and collect our ‘key’. Yes, it could work. It will be expensive though.”

The duke smiled. “Our coffers are not empty, and if all goes well, this will be nothing more than a minor downpayment on our rise to power. Consider your budget functionally unlimited in this regard.”

A dangerous thing to give a subordinate, but Varice was a smart girl, and she also had a thorough understanding of what was within his means. They could not simply hire a 4th tier mercenary to take out the Butcher, as tempting as that was. The price would be astronomical, only able to be shouldered by the largest empires on Tsanderos. As a minor territory in the back end of the continent, they could simply not afford the extravagance. But a few teams of Crimson Lions? That was an expense his treasury could bear.

“Who do you want leading this mission?” Varice asked.

“It may be best to allow some of the other powers to jockey for position and assign their own assets within the Lions to lead the expedition as a whole, but I want Francis D’Sware to lead the team going for the God-Touched.”

There was a pause as his spymaster consulted her ledger of favours and debts, contacts, and assets. It was incorporeal too, of course, locked away behind one of her many skills. He’d only witnessed her conjure the thing a few times and couldn’t make heads or tails of the strange tome with its archaic branching sigils and disturbing glyphs. Still, he was content to trust her—she knew her work, and it was as much in her interest as his own for House Ryonic to thrive.

Varice tsked in annoyance a moment later. Quite how the raven managed the noise was again beyond his comprehension, but some things simply had to be accepted. Skills didn’t need to strictly obey the natural laws as everything else did, and puzzling over such mysteries could drive one to a lifetime of study where nothing of real consequence was ever achieved.

“He’s on assignment in Colchet. Closer than us but diverting him away will be obvious to any of our more astute rivals. The sultanate in particular has been sniffing around as of late, and I would not trust to the discretion of the Lions’ leadership in this matter, despite their nominal independence.”

“I know,” he said wryly. “I have just caught one of their rats down near the vault. I’ve dealt with problem, and will continue to do so, but I suspect the sultan knows something. That old bastard has always been a cut above the rest.”

He sighed, turning his slate-grey eyes back to the raven. “I leave the final decision up to you. If there is nothing else, then I will return to my duties.”

The raven nodded, gave a perfunctory bow, and then flew out into the empty cavern with a burst of purple feathers. They disappeared before they hit the ground, nothing left to disturb the thin layer of black dust coating the planks below.
