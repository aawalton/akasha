When Lira took her next day off, she made her way to the safe house where Maren was hiding. The small home sat tucked away in one of the city's better neighborhoods, making it a good spot to lie low, since guards were more likely to search the slums.

When she reached the door, just as she raised her hand to knock, she heard laughter drifting through the wood.

She paused, listening more carefully. There were two voices coming from inside. One belonged to Maren, but the other… was masculine.

Lira frowned.

Was Maren entertaining guests?

She knocked, making the conversation stop. A few footsteps approached before the door swung open.

"Pallan?" Lira said. "What are you doing here?"

"Lira." The crime boss smiled. "What a surprise."

"A surprise indeed." She stared at him. "I repeat, what are you doing here?"

"You asked me in your letter to keep an eye on your friend."

"By that I meant to make sure no one bothered her."

"That is exactly what I am doing." He gave her his most infuriating grin. Then he stepped aside with a flourishing gesture.

"Come join us," he said. "Your friend makes excellent tea."

Lira narrowed her eyes suspiciously before stepping inside.

The room looked far brighter than during her last visit. The curtains were open, allowing warm afternoon sunlight to spill across the wooden floor. Fresh bread rested beside a basket of fruit on the table, while Maren stood beside the kettle wearing a simple new blue dress instead of Lira's cast-offs.

Even her appearance had changed.

The ugly bruise covering her face had completely faded, leaving only healthy skin behind. Her new blond hair still looked strange to Lira after knowing her as a brunette for so long, but there was no denying that the girl looked good, happy even, as she was smiling again.

"Lira!"

Maren hurried across the room before wrapping both arms around her.

"You came!"

Lira returned the hug.

"Of course I came."

She stepped back to look the girl over.

"You look much better."

"I am. Thank you."

The three of them settled around the small table while Maren poured tea into three cups.

Lira accepted hers with both hands.

"So," she asked, "how are you really doing?"

Maren smiled.

"I'm fine."

She hesitated before correcting herself.

"No... better than fine."

She glanced between Lira and Pallan.

"I don't know how to thank both of you."

Lira looked toward the syndicate leader.

"What exactly did he do?"

Maren looked embarrassed.

"The first few days..." She lowered her eyes. "I was so scared, I couldn't gather the courage to leave the house."

Lira grimaced.

"Sorry I left you on your own, but if I had changed my habits just after you disappeared, people might have suspected that I was helping you."

"I don't blame you." Maren smiled at her. "I would have been caught without you."

"You could have at least left her with more food," said Pallan. "I found the girl starving."

"Oh," said Lira. "I am sorry."

"Don't apologize. I told you it was my fault for being too scared to leave the house."

"But yeah," she added. "Mister Pallan has been coming every couple of days to bring me food and make sure I am alright."

Lira nodded, then narrowed her eyes at Maren.

"Wait a minute, how did you know you could trust him?"

"He said he knew you."

"A lot of people know me; that doesn't mean he is trustworthy."

"He said he was your uncle," she insisted.

Lira closed her eyes.

"Did you ask for proof?"

"He had a key to this house," Maren said.

Lira relaxed a little.

"You had me worried for a bit. I thought you trusted the first stranger at your door."

"She kind of did," Pallan winced. "It didn't take much prodding for her to tell me everything that happened to her with the prince."

"You told a complete stranger you were hiding from the royal guards." Lira facepalmed.

"Well… he isn't a stranger; he is your uncle."

Still… Lira hoped that she hadn't told him about the magic. While she trusted Pallan, she wasn't sure she would ever confess openly to anyone that she could cast spells. She gave the crime syndicate leader an assessing look. He didn't seem to be treating her differently. Maybe Maren had only told him about her troubles.

Speaking of which, Lira's expression softened, and she gave Maren a warm smile.

"I've got good news," Lira said. "You don't have to worry about the guards finding you."

Maren looked up.

"What?"

"The palace isn't searching for you anymore."

The empty cup she was about to fill slipped from Maren's fingers onto the table with a soft clink, her mouth open in surprise.

"They... aren't?"

"No."

Lira grinned even more.

"Lady Celene managed to clear your name."

For several seconds, Maren simply stared. Then tears began filling her eyes.

"Really?"

"Really."

She laughed weakly while wiping at her face.

"I thought..."

"I know."

Lira remembered how terrified Maren had been when the whole ordeal started.

"Does that mean I can go back to the palace?"

"Technically..." Lira hesitated. "But I wouldn't recommend it. You know, Prince Kavard is still there."

The excitement faded from Maren's face.

"Right."

Eventually, she sighed.

"I guess I have to find something new to do with myself."

Lira nodded.

That sounded healthier than throwing herself back into the lion's den.

"It's just..." Maren looked down at her cup. "I used to send money home every month."

"To your mother?"

She nodded.

"And now I don't even have a job."

Before Lira could answer, Pallan spoke.

"I know a few establishments that could use a worker."

Both women looked toward him.

"You do?" asked Maren.

"I can probably arrange something." He nodded.

Relief immediately spread across Maren's face.

"Really?"

"It shouldn't be difficult."

"Thank you."

She bowed her head.

"Thank you so much, Mister Pallan."

"My pleasure."

Lira considered the offer for a moment before adding,

"Keep her in the kitchen."

Pallan looked at her.

"Not as a server. Or anything too customer-facing."

"Because of Kavard?"

She nodded.

"The guards may have stopped searching, but he might not have."

"I understand."

"I'll find somewhere she won't be seen by the wrong people."

Lira finally allowed herself to relax.

Perhaps involving Pallan hadn't been such a terrible idea after all. His organization reached into every corner of the city. Finding honest work for a former maid would be far easier for him than it ever would have been for her.

After another few minutes of conversation, Pallan rose from his chair.

"I should be going."

Lira stood as well.

"I'll walk you out."

The moment the front door closed behind them, and they were safely beyond Maren's hearing, Lira folded her arms.

"She's far too young for you."

Pallan blinked.

"I beg your pardon?"

"You heard me."

"I honestly don't know what you're talking about." He gave her a smirk.

Lira wasn't fooled for a second.

"Seriously." She pointed a finger at him. "Back off."

His playful smile disappeared, and he answered completely seriously.

"I would never make a move on a woman immediately after living through something like that."

Lira studied him.

"Good."

The smile slowly returned.

"Besides..." He adjusted his gloves. "I'm not a womanizer, as you seem to think."

"I lived with you, and I have seen the women visiting you. And yet you remain unwed."

"Lira, child, not all women want a husband." He smirked. "Some value their freedom far too much for that. I think you are old enough by now to understand that. Married life isn't for people like us."

Lira deflated at that comment. It was true. She doubted she would ever trust anyone enough to get married. A husband was someone she was supposed to share everything with, and she doubted she would ever tell anyone that she had killed Thadwick.

Lira reached into her satchel.

"Here."

She handed him a folded letter.

"Your reward for being a good uncle."

He opened it enough to glance at the first page.

His expression immediately sharpened.

"The Kelroth estate?"

"They've been quietly buying taverns, theaters, inns... hundreds of them."

He looked back up.

"You confirmed it?"

"Harwin did," she shrugged. "I just stole the information."

"They're turning them into gambling houses." She lowered her voice as she spoke.

Pallan glanced at her, then continued skimming through the documents.

"And half the kingdom's nobility now owes them money."

"I see..."

"So that's what you meant when you warned me to stay away from them."

"I don't know what they're planning," Lira nodded. "But it's probably something big."

"You think it's more than just a money scheme?" he asked.

"I don't know, but I would avoid getting involved with Lord Kelroth if I were you."

Pallan folded the letter.

"I appreciate the warning."

Then he gave her a small nod before leaving down the street.

Lira watched him leave before returning inside. She hadn't even closed the door when she noticed Maren watching her.

The former maid wore an unusually dreamy smile while absentmindedly twirling a strand of blond hair around one finger.

"Lira?"

"Yes?"

"Is your uncle married?"

Lira froze.

"What?"

"Your uncle." Maren smiled innocently.

"Is he married?"

Lira stared at her.

"He's far too old for you."

"He doesn't look that old."

"He's thirty-five."

"And I'm twenty-five."

Lira blinked.

"You're serious?"

"As a palace maid, I always assumed I'd never marry." Maren shrugged. "My future was comfortable, you know? I didn't really need a husband."

She looked down at her teacup.

"But now..." she added in a hesitant voice, "I'm not in the palace anymore."

She gave a small shrug.

"I suppose having someone to protect me doesn't sound so bad."

Lira pinched the bridge of her nose.

"Why him?"

Maren answered without hesitation.

"He's handsome, charming..." Maren smiled sheepishly. "And he has that dangerous, mysterious gentleman thing going on."

Lira slowly covered her face with both hands.

"What a disaster."
