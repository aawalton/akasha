In the early morning, at the back of a shop, a light came on. Amidst the dust, a circle had been formed, chairs and tables arranged around it. Shelves blocked the light from reaching the streets while a man and a young man stood in the middle. The man sat in one of the chairs around the circle, writing on a piece of paper. In front of him, the young man paced back and forth, talking to himself.

"Are you sure, Devon?" He turned and said, frowning. "I mean, we still don't know what or how it's going to happen. I—"

"Of course we know..." The man said with a smile. "I've done this a few times, although... Anyway, it'll work out. What is it now? Until last week you wouldn't shut up asking if we were finally going to do the ritual. Now that we're here, you want to back out?"

"That's because you didn't tell me the consequences!" He pointed his finger at Devon.

"Oh, stop it, Harry..." He chuckled softly and stood up. "The Symbol you chose is neither an element nor a force of nature; you'll be fine. I've never seen a human Symbol cause more than perhaps a broken bone or two."

"Broken bone!?" Harry now had a look of despair. "What do you mean by broken bone?! You told me I could at most twist a knee or something!"

"Twisted knee, cracked leg... In the end, it's all the same thing."

"It definitely isn't!" The young man walked faster.

"Anyway," Devon placed a hand on the boy's shoulder. "This will only happen in the hypothetical situation where you can't pass through and the power ends up leaking into the real world. Think of it this way: you're connecting with this concept. It's a bridge, nothing more. A bridge that connects your mind with... that. If it doesn't work, a part of it will leak here, but as I said, it won't be anything serious. Either way, you'll gain your power, even if you break a bone in the process. Except, of course, if your Emblem is already in use, but that's unlikely."

"That?" Harry asked, but before Devon could answer, he continued. "I'm not making a pact with any ancient god, am I? I don't want to wake up tomorrow with seven eyes and an egg in my stomach. At least I'll have a cool tattoo on my back, right?"

Shouldn't have told him to read The Beckon Souls... Devon added inwardly. He then turned to the young man. "No, Harry. This won't happen. You just need to be sure that this is what you want... The 'tattoo' only happens if you get two Emblems. And it isn't going to be today."

Devon walked over to a table and picked up a vial containing a white powder. Awaiting Harry's response, he turned to see the young man reconsidering his decisions.

"Is this really what you want?" Devon said, prompting a response from the young man.

"Yes!" he replied, his face quickly shifting from worried to determined. "I-I think so!"

"You can't think, Harry. You have to be sure." Devon removed the vial's cap and poured the powder onto a black disc.

Made of obsidian, the disc was extremely valuable to the nobles of the region. Devon knew this and therefore kept them stored in secret compartments. Since opening his shop, not a single disc had been found in any government inspections.

Although the existence of such an object wouldn't immediately determine the owner's nature as an Emblem user, it certainly implied otherwise. With the idea of ​​monopolizing the technology only for exceptional soldiers and members of the high society, they actively searched the entire continent for any Moon Rock and obsidian sales.

Besides discs, any obsidian objects representing one of the three forms were coveted.

Meticulously, Devon spread the contents of the vial over the disc to form an image in the center. He stopped halfway, leaving only a mound of white powder on the object. The rest was not his concern.

"Ready." Devon said, placing both hands on his hips and stepping back, a nostalgic smile on his face. "I never thought I'd do this again..."

"I still find it strange that you chose not to have an Emblem..." Harry asked. "If you were to have one, hypothetically speaking, which one would you choose?"

"We've had this conversation before, lad..." Devon said in a tired voice. "I wouldn't have one, and there hasn't been a day in my life that I've seriously considered it. Most of this is muscle memory... But, to satisfy your question, the Symbol I always imagined was that of the Door of Yurgenhower."

"Door of what?" Harry's eyes widened. "Are you kidding me? You always told me to pick something more dynamic and you would choose a door?"

"It's not just a door, Harry..." Devon replied, disappointed. "It represents the passage of life and death. In the story, the main character has the power to bring back those he lost through a door. In the end, he sacrifices his power to ensure they all live happily. It's silly, I know, but that's what I would choose. It would be a way to honor myself. Isn't that what we live for? To honor our past?"

"I..." As if he'd been kicked, Harry took a step back.

"Besides, didn't you tell me you'd read the whole trilogy? Damn it, Harry, I even told Mary you were getting into the classics! I just hope you don't talk about this at the Thursday club..."

"...Mary, huh?" Harry spoke, trying to forget what Devon had said before. "Was she the one who came asking for a book and you gave it without even charging? I thought we didn't do charity work..."

"Oh, don't give me that, Harry..." He said, his cheeks even redder. "It was a pretty old book already... Speaking of which, she invited us to her house the day after tomorrow."

"I don't want to spend my afternoon—"

"Her niece will be there. She's your age."

And with that, Harry shut up and didn't answer. As if thinking about a million things, his eyes darted from side to side, eventually falling on the disc in the center of the room.

"You were supposed to calm me down, not make me anxious!" He said, pointing at Devon.

"Ready, then?" Devon asked with a grin. "It's in courage that we make our best decisions, not in fear."

"But you need to be afraid first to be brave," he replied, crouching down and getting closer to the circular object.

"And in that case, which one will prevail?" Devon crouched down and took the boy's hand. It trembled.

Harry didn't want to say it out of pride, but he was terrified. Devon had an understanding of the young man's past, even if he didn't share much. He knew of his traumas and heard his cries when the night was silent. Despite this, the improvement from months ago until that moment was visible. Little by little, the young man was regaining his confidence and, for a moment, things had stabilized.

He didn't like the rituals and was totally against the use of Emblems. The world, however, didn't care about his opinion. The more he looked outside his shop, the more he appreciated what was inside. He imagined how the boy would survive after his time came. He also remembered the young man he once was. It was inevitable. He told himself. To survive among legends, one would also need to drink from the chalice.

"Look, no matter what happens," Devon said, his voice warm. "Know that I'm here, okay? I know I can't keep up with you most of the time, but know—"

"It's okay." Harry, still trembling, turned to Devon and said with a smile. "I won't fail. We'll do this ritual, and in the end, we'll still go to Mary's dinner tomorrow. That way you can get a candy cane and I get to see the outside for the first time in a week."

A little confused by the young man's reaction, Devon then looked to the side, adjusted his glasses, and chuckled. "You brat... It's not my fault the work keeps piling up."

"It's certainly your fault, even if it's for having too good a shop." Harry then stood up, cracked his knuckles, and sighed quickly.

"It'll be the Lock Emblem then, right?" Devon confirmed with Harry, who simply nodded and pretended everything was fine. "Make sure you make the Symbol exactly the way you imagine it. Remember the sketches we practiced all morning? If you forget, just look to the side and see the papers there, okay? Just don't take too long. When you're ready, leave the Symbol the way you want and hit the disc with all your might."

"...Are you sure I'll be able to break it?" Harry said after a moment. "I mean, it's made of obsidian. I don't think even a monk could break it."

"And monks are now known for their violence and strength? It becomes fragile after the Symbol is set," Devon said. "It'll have a shine when it's all done."

"Okay..." The young man sighed and once again bent down and rolled up his sleeves. Devon, consequently, moved away and leaned against a shelf.

Harry carefully spread the Moon Dust across the disc. To his surprise, it was much less dense than he had imagined. He smoothed out the white mound on top and began by making a hole in the center. When he realized, based on the Lock Symbol he had drawn, that he would have to create a uniform line volume if he left it hollow inside, he decided to leave it painted.

The Symbol itself represented the image one would have of a lock: a circle followed by a smaller square and a triangular shape below, piercing the other two shapes. As he worked on the mound to make everything perfect, he felt his trembling lessen. Devon said it was like working with sand, but Harry had never seen the ocean. He could now imagine the sensation.

The young man began to feel more confused as he continued drawing the symbol, which remained clear in his mind. It reinforced his thoughts and the powers he wished he had with it. In his naiveté, Harry imagined the ability to create blockable areas, using them as barriers against physical and even abstract things, whatever that might mean. Furthermore, he thought about how it could be used to observe things from afar, almost like binoculars, the way people see through keyholes. He saw himself as a great scout and general, using his skills to spot attackers and stop projectiles alongside his brother. He knew, however, that this was only a dream. The more contact he had with the nobles, the less he thought about protecting them. After all, that was what the law was for.

He could have thought of more useful things, but even if it wasn't what he wanted, he wouldn't do anything differently. It was his way of honoring his mother and moving forward.

Devon, watching from afar, squinted, searching for any sign of danger. The disc in front of Harry began to glow faintly. The young man, for some reason, didn't notice, or if he did, he didn't show it. He continued to precisely form his Symbol, his thoughts absorbed by the expectation of finally being someone in life.

This, Devon suspected, was also the work of the Emblem; it did this to people. It gave them a sense of power, of control. It sold them a future, a moment that was yet to change everything. He wasn't wrong about that last part, because in a few minutes, everything would change.

Increasingly sleepy, Harry then finished his Symbol and promptly fell backward, his eyes rising and leaving him on the ground. Before losing consciousness, he heard Devon's footsteps.

The disc, now with a white powder in the shape of a lock, glowed brightly enough to match the candles. As if it had its own center of gravity, a pressure pulled Devon towards the object. Refusing to do so, Devon held Harry in his arms and pretended to be calm. But the heart cannot lie.
