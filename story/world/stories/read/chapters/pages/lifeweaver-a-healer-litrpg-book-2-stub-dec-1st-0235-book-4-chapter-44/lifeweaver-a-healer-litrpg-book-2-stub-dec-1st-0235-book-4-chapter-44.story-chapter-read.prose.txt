The ease with which Luke drifted into the weave was a joy to experience. To think that a title could grant something like that. It made him consider what else might be in Guide’s power to give. That, however, brought thoughts to the levels they’d stripped from him. Whatever they gave, they could also take away. Annoyance was not a strong enough word to describe Luke’s thoughts on that. Neither was ‘anger’ nor even ‘fear.’ It was a mix of all those things. As he concentrated and deepened his meditation to allow for traversing the system weave, Luke considered the implications of bestowing power to and stealing it from an Integrated, and didn’t like how much it made him feel like a pawn in someone else’s game. His accomplishments were his own, along with the experience to go with them. In that moment, before reaching the site of the incursion, he made a vow to reach a height where no one was in a position to mess with his abilities. Never again.

Not unlike with the last tear in the weave, void creatures bearing a striking resemblance to The Deep Dweller were focusing their attention on a single end thread, doing their best to gnaw their way through the system protections. No other consciousnesses floated around, so Luke figured he was on his own with this one. He figured wrong.

Someone else popped into the metaphysical realm right beside the void creatures. Not just a consciousness either, but in the flesh!

Integrated Dimitrios. Level 42. Mythos.

Luke wasn’t sure what to expect, but he certainly hadn’t expected the young, brash, and far too hot-headed guy to bring out a pair of deep blue, glowing short swords and use them to cut through the end thread.

“What are you doing?!” Luke projected as he drifted closer to deal with the damage.

The end thread frayed as Dimitrios threw himself at the void creatures, and rather than help, Luke was forced to do everything in his power to keep the weave itself from unraveling. Perhaps it shouldn’t have come as a surprise at that point, but the blades cut into the void creatures and even seemed to hurt them, if not much. Their counterattacks were instant, and a single lash of a void tentacle sliced Dimitrios’s leg clean off. Light took the leg’s place, and the Mythos didn’t even seem to notice the wound as he kept up the attacks. Spells formed in the void, too many for Luke to follow what the younger man was doing, but some seemed to empower the blades while others cut into the three void beings, and even the void itself around them. It was an impressive display, but it was also a losing battle. Despite the many power-ups and the myriad of defensive spells, the void itself was feeding on the fighter, and it wouldn’t be long before Dimitrios stopped moving.

“Use your will to push them back!” Luke projected as he repaired the damage to the weave and reinforced it using the same method as last time. It was a much simpler task when he didn’t need to contend with the creatures at the same time. Ah, that was it. Dimitrios was buying Luke time. Quite the team player, that guy, going through all that.

Having finished the work except for the final thread, Luke sent out another message to Dimitrios to let him know it was time to return. “Get back in!”

Either Dimitrios didn’t ‘hear’ the words, or he was too busy staying alive to realize Luke had sounded the retreat, because he didn’t turn away from the fight. Instead, he went on the offensive again. Horns of light grew out of his head and he charged forward, sinking them into the nearest void creature, along with his two swords and a myriad of offensive spells focused around his shoulders, attacking on their own, like snakes lashing out again and again. Lightning crackled out of nowhere and struck the void creature as a finishing blow, and Luke goggled when the damn thing shied away and fled. Dimitrios had stood against three and made one turn away and retreat back into the void. Impressive, to say the least.

That the other two almost consumed him in the process, feeding on his mana and draining his will, didn’t detract from the accomplishment. Not much, at least. Dimitrios, nearing the end of the line, turned toward the weave and did his utmost to return to the system weave, but it was too late. Getting away from two of those creatures in the state he was in was futile. Good thing Luke was there to pick up the scraps.

Not wanting to leave the weave and enter the void himself, he extended a thread of mana. The moment that thread touched the void, the speck of void mana secured within Luke’s Meta-Heart trembled, as if excited. It took an effort of will to keep it contained, which meant it was with a divided focus that he grabbed hold of Dimitrios’s one limb that wasn’t just a remnant of shining light. His right arm. The two remaining void creatures would not let the Integrated go without putting up a fight, but now that Luke had practiced it a lot more and grown comfortable in wielding it, he put his will to good use.

Leave.

The command struck the void creatures. At first, they resisted using their own significant willpower. Clenching his jaw, Luke pushed.

Leave.

Release the Integrated.

He was unsure whether the monstrous beings understood the words or not, but knew the meaning behind them wasn’t lost on them. They understood, but they did not want to comply. It was a closer contest than Luke would have wished, but in the end his own iron will and solid intent were enough to push those bastards back into the void. Once clear of them, he pulled Dimitrios back into the weave and closed the tear behind the Mythos. Just as he was about to heal the impulsive guy, he disappeared.

Dimitrios: “I was just about to beat them.”

Luke was about to send a reply saying he very much disagreed when another message followed.

Dimitrios: “But thank you.”

Luke: “I can heal you.”

Dimitrios: “No need.”

Congratulations! You have reached Level 50!

There it was. With more than a little excitement, Luke repaired the weave and added his own layer of protection, removing it from the previous location, just to make sure the creatures didn’t return the moment he left. His concentration wavered at several points, since he couldn’t help but consider what this new level might mean and what kind of skills had become available now that he’d reached this milestone.

Once back in the physical realm, he didn’t waste any time before diving into his interface to check out what’d changed.

With one point remaining from a previous level up and one new point gained upon reaching Level 50, Luke could pick two new skills. All three of the new additions sounded like they’d be amazing. While pondering what the descriptions could mean, trying to read between the lines, Luke added the new attribute points so he wouldn’t forget them. Three points to Willpower meant he’d reached a whopping 75, and two points to Wisdom put that attribute at 50 alongside Vitality and Intelligence. The mental attributes were pulling away from the physical ones by a lot at this point, but that was okay. Luke wouldn’t use Strength much when fighting the void. In that vast nothingness, will was the greatest weapon, even if Dimitrios showed it wasn’t the only way to fight.

That left the skills. Weaver’s Dominion sounded to Luke like it would improve his perception, or maybe his ability to register information in an area. Weaver’s Perception served up everything in an avalanche of information which was sometimes too much to parse. This was true for both fighting and healing situations. While it would be a good skill, he doubted it would serve him better than the other choices. Adaptability could be useless or amazing. From the description, Luke figured it would pair well with Boon of Potential, allowing his attributes to grow faster. That wasn’t all, however, and in the best possible scenario, it also improved will-based workings and pretty much every other aspect of improvement. Or it could be a dud and not do much of anything. Even the smallest chance it would be as great as Luke hoped meant he couldn’t skip over it. A skill to better his meditation would be great all around, and with the added benefit of improving his focus, which was something he needed when traversing the weave, he couldn’t pass that up either.

To that end, Luke selected Adaptability and Greater Meditation. Their being passive meant he couldn’t just activate them to get a sense of the changes. This added to the difficulty of knowing if he’d made a good choice or not, but he settled in right there on the ground to form a proper opinion, heading into a meditative trance as he perused the ever-growing list of skills available to him. It was a lot, to be sure. Something about the skills called to him, and after looking at it a little longer, Luke knew what he needed to do.
