Katherine Dragomirov had always been a fan of the darker side of culture.

Whether it was the vampire tales of her youth, the endless pop culture obsession with witchcraft and gothic indulgence, or the very specific kind of fictional “evil” that was wicked, indulgent, and entirely consensual, she’d always felt a natural fascination with what mundane society considered “taboo.”

Now she had entered the most immersive dark-fantasy game ever created beside the boy who had been her best friend for most of her life—and had become the man she’d fallen helplessly in love with five years earlier. She should have been living her dream.

Darian, of course, being Darian, had the insight of a rock regarding her feelings for him.

She didn’t begrudge him that ignorance, naturally. His lack of indecent desire toward her was what had, ironically, made her fall for him in the first place. The idiot was about as conniving as a golden retriever and three times as loyal.

It was depressingly predictable that she’d fall for the one person who’d never wanted her that way at all.

Of course I’m thinking about this now, Katherine grumbled mentally from the narrow stone ledge outside an open upper-story window. Darian had sent her to deal with “Romeo and Juliet” before entering the chapel, and she had found them exactly where his blood-sight had promised: hurriedly pulling on chainmail and other accoutrements while arguing over the sounds of slaughter outside, with every sign that they had only just stopped fucking like rabbits.

The room still reeked of recent sex, because Eternus Online was offensively committed to realism, and Kate was trying very hard not to think about what that scent was bringing to her mind. Lilith’s decision to remake Darian into something akin to a Castlevania-style protagonist was doing no favors for her imagination, either.

“We have no idea what we’re up against, Jorry!” the woman said urgently. “It could be cultists, or rebellion, or—”

“It doesn’t matter, Lacy,” Jorry replied firmly. “We swore an oath to Hyperion. We’re Squires, for Light’s sake! We can’t just sit back and do nothing while people are dying!”

“Sir Roderick—”

“Would expect us to help, just like we were taught,” Jorry said firmly. “So let’s help.”

Man, this game makes these people way too relatable, Kate lamented while nocking an arrow and activating her [Dark Mana Channeling] to prepare a [Null Arrow]. Mortarius’ gratifyingly detailed lessons on Nightstalker tactics had been immensely helpful, and the System had rewarded her practice by unlocking [Night Shroud] and [Double Shot]. Her own Codex had given her numerous insights into her expected role and the normal tactical disposition of Nightstalkers—describing them as a mix between ranged mage-killers and close-quarters assassins, built specifically for stealth, intelligence work, and the elimination of dangerous magic-users.

She’d played enough Elder Scrolls XV to know she always gravitated to Stealth Archer.

Avalon Online had only reaffirmed that addiction after she’d gone on her first PK spree.

Slow is smooth, smooth is fast, Kate recounted, remembering her Uncle Sterling’s advice.

She waited until the pair turned to reach for their weapons and only then made her move, using her still-active [Dark Mana Channeling] to wrap herself in a [Night Shroud] and smoothly step into the window’s opening, firing her nocked [Null Arrow] at Jorry first, and following it up with a second toward Lacy without a second’s hesitation.

Nock. Draw. Anchor. Infuse. Loose.

The process had become a mantra for her already, and she felt only a distant sense of guilt when the arrows punched through the two lovers before they were ready for what was happening.

The front of Jorry’s throat burst outward in a spray of blood as the arrow punched through his neck a second later. Lacy went rigid at the sound, and the sudden hesitation killed her. The second arrow punched through the unfastened seam beneath her left arm, buried its barbed head deep within her chest, and dropped her hard to the floor.

Katherine grimaced and smoothly settled her bow into its harness on the back of her armor, drawing her long knives and dropping soundlessly into the room as her [Night Shroud] dissipated. Jorry spasmed on the floor, trying and failing to heal himself, by the looks of it, as the [Null Arrow] blocked his mana. Lacy, meanwhile, was trembling and reaching for Jorry, with neither of them having the wherewithal or lingering sensibility to see Kate approaching them.

“I know you guys are NPCs, but for what it’s worth, I really am sorry,” Kate said quietly, coming to a halt beside them and idly flipping her knives into a reverse grip. “I hope you get a better go of it when the world recycles you.”

Both Jorry and Lacy turned toward her with rapidly fading awareness, and Kate smiled apologetically before driving her knives through the bases of their skulls with [Void Strike]. The Dark Mana ability parted flesh and bone without resistance, and Kate saw both NPCs die at the same moment. Some people died with epic last stands; most died frightened, confused, and far too quickly for poetry.

“At least you were together, I suppose,” she said quietly, with a faint pang of envy, and then wiped her knives and sheathed them at her hips. A quick glance at her mana told Kate that the entire sequence had cost her more than she liked. Still, it was infinitely cheaper than allowing two Squires to reinforce a Paladin.

Now then, let’s go see what sort of stupidity Dee has found for himself.

* * * * *

Kate found her way to her perch less than a minute later, after several self-satisfying parkour moves, slipping through the shattered remains of Hyperion’s stained-glass likeness and settling into a crouch upon the narrow stone ledge above the altar, [Night Shroud] once more in place and active. Darian, already inside, was speaking to a man and woman whose identities Kate had already deduced to be Sara Lightspeaker and Roderick Vayne, thanks to Darian’s own words as much as the simple realities of gaming.

The tense discourse between her best friend and the pair of lightstick-worshippers happened while she observed in silence, and she mused over the exchange with quiet pride for Darian’s stubborn defiance. She kept an arrow nocked but undrawn, mostly because Darian was talking, which meant he wanted information. Revealing herself before she understood which of the two Hyperian fanatics mattered most would have been a noob move.

Roderick looked like the obvious threat, but Sara’s staff, positioning, and watchful stillness screamed support caster—precisely the sort of target Kate existed to handle.

The fact that Sara and Roderick had no idea they were speaking to Captain Righteousness himself only added to her amusement.

Right up until Roderick attacked Darian.

The Paladin moved with an alacrity that had no business belonging to a man wrapped in heavy armor and carrying a hammer almost as large as Kate herself. One moment, he was standing beside the Priestess and glaring at Darian with a mixture of hate and disgust, and Sara began a benediction. The next, his weapon was blazing with golden-white radiance and descending toward her best friend’s head.

Darian dodged, because he thankfully still retained survival instincts, and Roderick’s warhammer struck the chapel floor with a detonation of holy fire and shrapnel.

The impact shook the entire building and nearly dislodged Kate from her perch above the altar, while a dome of radiant fire expanded outward and turned the shadows beneath her into writhing darkness. Her [Night Shroud] shuddered beneath the wash of Light Mana, becoming briefly translucent as Darian was hurled backward by the edge of the radiant blast. His warplate shrieked along shattered stone, and smoke clung to the dark steel where the Paladin’s magic had burned it.

Kate lifted her bow and drew string by reflex, sighting on Roderick when he charged after Darian—but something else drew her attention before she could complete the infusion and loose.

Sara Lightspeaker, the Priestess of Hyperion, planted the base of her staff beside the altar and continued the benediction she’d started before Roderick’s charge while changing the cadence of her prayer, lifting her left arm in beseeching posture toward the heavens. The sight was momentarily baffling to Kate, until the moment that golden light ran outward from the butt of her staff to cover the floor in narrow veins of radiance. That same light, a moment later, climbed the Priestess’ white robes and gathered around the sunburst crowning her staff.

A filament of radiant light unspooled from its center, crossed the chapel like a beam of distilled sunlight, and embedded itself between Roderick’s shoulders as the Paladin pursued Darian through the doors.

Support caster, she thought sourly. I fucking called it.

Kate shifted her aim immediately and infused the drawn arrow before loosing it without hesitation.

The [Null Arrow] left her bowstring with a sharp twang and crossed the space between her and Sara in less than a second, cutting through the air like a void-wreathed missile, and hurtling toward the unprotected hollow beneath Sara’s raised left arm. The Priestess’ attention was entirely fixed on the retreating form of Roderick, and one for one beautiful moment, Kate thought the fight was already over.

That, of course, invoked the opposite result.

A radiant dome of pure light erupted around the altar as if in reaction to her attack, and the [Null Arrow] struck it with a crack of shattering antithesis, burying itself halfway through the gleaming barrier and eroding its cohesion with tendrils of black-purple virulence that shattered the dome. The arrow’s momentum continued after breaking the barrier, but its angle had been compromised, and it passed the Priestess’ ribs by inches to impale itself in the chapel floor.

Sara’s head snapped around immediately in surprise, and her hard blue eyes found the origin of the attack without spotting Kate herself.

“The chapel ward—Assassin!” she said, half in accusation, and half in raised-voice alarm.

Kate’s expression flattened while nocking a second arrow, only to be interrupted a second later as the side door from which Sara and Roderick had entered burst open a second later, and four people charged into the chapel with weapons in hand and half-fastened armor clattering around them.

There’s the other four Squires, then, Kate thought sourly. Of course they were already here.

A broad-shouldered man with brown hair led the group with a heater shield on his left forearm and a longsword in his right hand, followed by a dark-haired woman clutching a metal quarterstaff, a lanky redheaded male archer already reaching for his quiver, and a dark-skinned spearwoman with her mail coif hanging loose around her shoulders.

Kate was already calculating her first choice from among the new targets when Sara spoke.

“Gareth, shield me!” The Priestess called immediately, slamming her staff once against the stone and erecting a golden field to shield her from the ledge. The barrier protected Sara’s front and flank, while remaining open behind her seemingly to avoid impeding the golden light flickering out toward Roderick. “Mara, reveal the assassin! Tomas, suppress the ledge! Elin, engage the enemy when they’re revealed!”

Three of the Squires moved without hesitation: the shield-and-sword bearer moved immediately to Sara’s side in a guardian posture; the quarterstaff-wielding woman began chanting, and the archer drew his first arrow. That made the first one Gareth, which made the archer Tomas.

The spearwoman alone hesitated, her gaze flicking toward the living quarters behind them.

“Has anyone seen Jorry and Lacy?”

Kate’s stomach tightened involuntarily at the genuine concern in her voice, and she forced herself to bury it while Sara’s expression hardened with resolve.

Time to move.

“We will discern their fate later. Handle the threat before us, Elin!”

Elin locked her jaw but accepted the reprimand, and Tomas raised his bow.

That makes the last one Mara. I’ll have to test [Examine] when I get more breathing room.

Kate, mid-thought, was already moving. She poured her mana into [Shadowstride] and stepped sideways from the ledge, her boots finding purchase on the chapel wall as if the vertical stonework was flat ground. The shallow darkness between two of the stained-glass windows caught her Eidos and drew it forward in an abrupt rush, sending her skimming along the upper wall like a wraith while the bowman’s first arrow shattered against the ledge she had already abandoned in a shower of radiant sparks.

At the same time, Mara stepped forward and thrust her quarter-staff toward the ceiling, speaking in a beseeching voice as golden runes igniting along the white surface.

“By His Light, let no profane villain hide within the night!”

A sphere of golden-white radiance expanded from quarterstaff and rolled across the chapel a moment later. Kate saw no way to evade it as it rushed toward her, and gritted her teeth as the illumination blew apart her [Night Shroud] with a sound like tearing fabric. Her Spellform shattered against the revealing spell’s luminance, and the shadows distorting her peeled away from her half-plate in strips.

“There!” Sara called, and the redhead archer loosed a second arrow immediately.

Elin was already moving. The spearwoman vaulted the altar rail and sprinted beneath Kate’s path along the wall, angling her spear upward to try to catch the Nightstalker the moment she abandoned the ledge.

Kate cursed and deactivated her [Shadowstride] in response, launching from the wall toward the floor of the chapel and landing in a combat roll to dive behind the pews immediately as the arrow shattered on the wall above her, and Elin’s spear thrust wide. Another arrow was nocked in her own bow, and she came up firing, unleashing one shot at Tomas and then another toward Mara, where she stood holding the revealing spell.

The first arrow was dodged by the redhead, who rolled to avoid it, and the second was intercepted by Gareth, who thrust his shield out and stepped two paces away from Sara to block the attack on his fellow Squire.

I hate it when they’re fucking competent, Kate groused as she ducked back behind the pews. And they’re not chumps, either. Not with those reaction times. Rank 3 or 4 maybe, based on what Morty explained. Weaker than Wardens, at least.

“You cannot win, assassin!” Sara proclaimed while Tomas’s next arrow detonated in another shower of sparks near Kate’s trailing boot, forcing her deeper into the pews. “You are outnumbered five-to-one, and your profane master will soon fall to Sir Roderick’s righteous fury!”

“Oh fuck off, you bitch!” Kate shouted back and moved low and fast behind the pews while taking stock of her mana reserves. She’d already used almost a third of her total available mana in the first few seconds of the fight, which meant she needed to be both tactical and efficient, or she’d be screwed.

“Crass and uncultured, just as I would expect from a cultist!” Sara retorted.

“I’m not a cultist, you cunt!” Kate called back and came up with a combat roll, taking aim with two arrows nocked and use of her [Double Shot] Skill. “I’m a Nightstalker, and I’m about to send your souls to Lilith!”

Kate fired again, at both Tomas and Elin, and the fight began in earnest.
