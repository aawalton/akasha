“So, what is the plan?” Mandragora asked.

Viktor gave Ekon a glance, and the bald man nodded. “The plan is simple,” he said. “The Sovereign and I enter first. He ties me up, drags me in as a prisoner, and tells them he managed to capture me after tracking me down. After that, we get a look at the place from the inside. The moment we see an opening, we strike immediately. Meanwhile, you and Renee stay outside and wait for the signal. Once the fighting starts, you move in as well.”

“Will that actually work?” Mandragora asked, skepticism visible on her face. “Do you truly believe they will be fooled by something like that?”

Of course not.

For one thing, this dead man had never been sent to capture Ekon in the first place. His actual assignment was merely monitoring him from a distance. And even setting that aside, dozens of them had been killed during their last attempt to take Ekon, so the idea that a single man could succeed in the task was just absurd. Anyone with half a brain would immediately realize something was off the moment they walked into the Folded Chamber together.

Still, that didn’t mean the idea lacked value.

After all, those masked men were creatures of routine. They were rigid in thought and hopeless at improvisation. When the situation played out as predicted, they could handle it with speed and competence, but if something unexpected happened, they would stumble over themselves trying to squeeze it into frameworks they understood. Against enemies like that, you didn’t need to deceive them outright. Sometimes, just confusing them was more than enough.

“Maybe they buy it, maybe they don’t,” Ekon said with a shrug. “The problem is we don’t exactly have a better plan. But don’t worry. Even in the worst-case scenario, the Sovereign would have no trouble getting me out.”

Mandragora looked at the bald man. “You’re really going to gamble on this?”

“Yes.”

“How about you, then?” Mandragora then turned to the youngest member of the group. “If you’re having second thoughts, now would be the time.”

“Hey,” Renee said with a frown. “It’s a bit too late to throw me off the wagon, isn’t it? If you two are going, then of course I’m going too.”

“...Fine,” Mandragora muttered. “Let’s get this over with. Where is the place?”

“North of town,” Viktor said. “Come.”

Without a moment’s delay, he activated the gems strapped to the dead man’s legs. A gust of wind exploded under the feet, hurling the body upward into the sky. The sudden surge in speed jolted his borrowed vision, before it steadied as he adjusted his balance in midair.

Well, triggering the gems through a corpse was undeniably awkward compared to using his real body. Good thing he had spent long hours practicing in the dungeon, experimenting with different cadavers in the disposal pit. And now, he had finally gotten the hang of it.

As he ascended, he glanced back over his shoulder. Below, Renee had lifted both Mandragora and Ekon into the air with practiced ease. Watching that, he started to wonder maybe Ba’atar not joining them wasn’t such a tragedy after all.

And so, they flew through the night sky. For a stretch of silence, only the wind spoke. But then, voices reached Viktor from behind.

“Anything happened in town while I was hiding in the dungeon?” Ekon asked.

“Well,” Mandragora said, “the biggest thing—what everyone in Daelin is talking about now—is that an employee of the Adventurer’s Guild has been assaulted in broad daylight by one of those Iskora adventurers, right in the middle of the street.”

“Really?”

“Yes. In fact, it happened on the very same day you went into the dungeon.”

“Why... why would they do something like that?”

“Beats me,” Mandragora said. “Given everything they’ve done so far, and the connection they have with those masked men you mentioned... I can only assume that they are insane. Yes, that’s the only explanation that makes sense.”

“The Adventurer’s Guild in Daelin won’t let this go unanswered,” Ekon said.

“They won’t. I’ve heard that the Guildmaster has sent a formal petition directly to the Conclave, requesting the immediate arrest and trial of the man responsible. Yes, they’re taking this very seriously. After all, the poor girl was badly injured. She hasn’t regained consciousness even after weeks.”

“Is it really that bad?”

“It is,” Mandragora said. “Her brother came to me last week to ask for help. So I went to the Sanctuary, where she is staying under the supervision of the local Keeper, to check her condition. There wasn’t much I could do, though, other than preparing some sustaining potions. She can’t eat on her own, you know, so we have to feed her another way to keep the body from giving up. But when it comes to actually treating the root cause...”

“There’s no way to wake her up?”

“I could force it, but I can’t predict what would happen afterward. I have no idea what damage such an action might cause. It could restore her, or it could break her beyond repair. I don’t want to gamble with someone’s mind, so I won’t take that risk.”

“So we just wait.”

“Yes, we maintain the status quo.”

The status quo, huh? Viktor sighed. He didn’t like that word at all, but for the moment, it was all they could afford for Claire.

At the very least, her condition was stabilized now. Thanks to Mandragora’s potions, and the two Emerald Mages dispatched from the caravan station, the likelihood of her simply withering away into nothing—of her body giving up on life while her mind remained trapped elsewhere—had been drastically reduced.

He had also taken further steps to make sure this delicate balance would not depend entirely on the goodwill or availability of others. He had asked the Druidess for alchemical recipes that could help sustain Claire, ones that didn’t require the involvement of magic, ones that he could brew himself.

And that, more than anything else, had factored into his decision to initiate tonight’s assault on the masked men’s secret lair. Because, even though he would try his best to keep Mandragora alive, there was no guarantee that she would survive the battle, given how little he knew about what was inside the Folded Chamber. So he had waited until he was absolutely certain that Claire’s survival would not hinge upon the woman’s continued existence. Only then did he allow the operation to proceed.

Before long, they reached their destination.

To anyone else, it was yet another stretch of the endless woods of the Central Plains, unremarkable in every way. But Viktor was not just anyone. He had seen this place countless times through Galatea’s eyes, studying every little detail until the whole landscape had become thoroughly engraved in his memory. He had done it to make sure there would be no mistakes when he finally came here himself.

Well, not exactly himself. But himself enough.

Galatea had come back to the hideout’s entrance after he killed the masked man to resume surveillance over the location. She must have spotted them approaching, and that meant she was probably on her way to meet them now.

Viktor gradually slowed his flight before landing on the forest floor, boots touching the earth with a muted crunch of dead leaves and loose soil. Behind him, the other three followed suit.

“Where is the hideout?” Mandragora asked.

“Ahead,” Viktor said. “You two stay here.”

The Druidess frowned. “When your signal comes, how exactly are we supposed to find it? And what is the signal anyway?”

Rather than giving her an answer, Viktor turned around.

“You there? Show yourself.”

The words had barely left his mouth when a burst of radiant light erupted beside him. Ekon and Mandragora recoiled instantly, while Renee threw an arm over her eyes as the forest was drenched in brilliance. Then the glow softened, and a figure emerged, as if sculpted from the light itself.

Needless to say, the trio all froze in place, staring at the luminous newcomer with utter astonishment.

Viktor had pondered for a while whether he should show Galatea to them or not. Eventually, he had chosen to do so. Even though he preferred to keep as much information about himself and the dungeon hidden as possible, especially from that mysterious woman—the Eternal Flame—he ultimately concluded that revealing only this projected form was probably not much of a big deal. Not to mention, later on he could use her as a messenger, instead of having to deliver letters by himself.

“When the time comes,” Galatea said, “I’ll take you to the entrance.”

The Druidess slowly nodded.

“Let’s begin,” Viktor said. “You brought the ropes?”

“Yes,” Ekon replied, his eyes lingering on Galatea for several seconds before shifting to Viktor.

Oh, well. Probably the most normal reaction imaginable. When a man saw someone like Galatea, he stared. Hardly anything mysterious about that. As long as it did not interfere with the operation, Viktor just couldn’t care less.

Without another word, he stepped closer and began to bind Ekon’s wrists together. He looped the rope in a way that appeared convincingly secure, but in reality, the bald man could break free in an instant once the fighting started.

Then, leaving Mandragora and Renee behind, he and Ekon followed the luminous Guardian toward the hidden lair.

“Are you... Lady Galatea?”

Huh?

Viktor blinked at the sudden question from the bald man.

Galatea glanced back over one glowing shoulder, a faint smile appearing upon her lips as soft golden light shimmered around her. “You know me?” she asked.

“Haku has told me a great deal about you,” Ekon said.

Ah. The bird-man.

Viktor found himself wondering exactly what sort of stories that smug-looking Tengu had shared with this guy during their time together. Still, he was not overly concerned. Celeste had been closely monitoring the conversations, making sure Haku revealed nothing truly important about the dungeon. In fact, she had even instructed him to slip in bits of misinformation here and there to muddy the truth. So no, there was little reason to worry about it.

“Is that so?” Galatea said. “Now I’m curious. I wonder what exactly he told you about me.”

“He had nothing but praise for you,” Ekon said. “Even so, now that I’ve met you in person, I can tell even his words didn’t do you justice.”

And Galatea laughed softly at that.

Viktor nearly rolled the dead man’s eyes. First Kazyk, then Haku, then this guy. Apparently, every man he knew had mastered the art of flattering beautiful women. Small wonder she preferred walking around in this form, instead of showing people what she really looked like.

Then, he noticed Ekon was glancing sideways at him. “Now I understand,” the bald man murmured.

Understand what?

Before he could ask, however, Galatea slowed to a halt. They had reached the tree in question.

“Shall I speak the phrase?” she asked. “Or would you prefer to do it yourself, my lord?”

“You do it,” Viktor said. He wanted to know whether the rift responded to the phrase itself, or if it required the voice of one of the masked men specifically. If the latter proved true, then the plan would have to be revised.

Galatea inclined her head slightly before she spoke, “Glory to our Father.”

For a moment, nothing happened. Then, the bark rippled. A distortion spread across the trunk, twisting and warping until the center of the tree began to split apart. A rift emerged, widening gradually until it became just big enough for a person to pass through.

“Let’s go,” Viktor said.

Ekon nodded once before lowering his head and slumping his shoulders, allowing his posture to sag as he trudged forward with the heavy, exhausted gait of a prisoner being dragged toward captivity. Viktor followed two paces behind him.

Together, they stepped through the rift.

And a whole new world opened before their eyes.

A vast expanse of water surrounded them on every side. Directly ahead, a long wooden bridge extended toward a distant island rising from the center of the place, upon which stood a massive stone structure whose silhouette loomed against the horizon.

Though he had never been here before, Viktor immediately knew what it was.

This was a water realm.
