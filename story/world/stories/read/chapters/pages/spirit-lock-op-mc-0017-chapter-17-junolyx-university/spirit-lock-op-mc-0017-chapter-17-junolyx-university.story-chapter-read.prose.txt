Junolyx University was built in a large meadow along a river. It looked like a small city with luxury buildings, shops, and skyscraper hotels. There were two skyscrapers of note—the South Tower, which was a hotel for elites. Then there was the North Tower, which would soon house all the rifters from other schools and guilds. The North Tower was necessary because all individuals entering a rift had to coordinate to prevent collisions, and half of the coordinators—those in the rift—had no means of digital or analog communication. So, rifters chose one location as a base of operations and then coordinated on a time system.

Junolyx was the host, so it got prioritized access to the rift. That was a primary factor in Hayden's decision to choose Junolyx. Another major reason was that the rift opened near the school—and if he needed to enter it illegally, he would have to do so before the “opening ceremony”—the point at which individuals were allowed to enter the rift.

Loricks stood as the ship was landing.

“Now that we’re here, let’s go over the entrance exam. It’s in four parts: an interview, a written test, a combat proficiency test, and a spirit showcase. All four are notoriously lax at Junolyx, but you’re going to have to overperform the martial arts showcase if you can’t pass the spirit portion.”

Loricks looked at Lina.

Hayden furrowed his brow in a rare show of emotion. “Would Junolyx really deny her?” he asked.

Loricks pursed his lips as Lina folded her arms. “It’s complicated,” he said. “Her core is…”

“It’s none of your business,” Lina declared.

Hayden raised his hands in sarcastic surrender. The thought that anyone would turn down an augmentor appalled him—but he wouldn't mention it. People clearly didn't know what Lina's core did, and that was a boon. If Lina was rejected, he could save her to gain favor with her father; if she got in and he trained her, she could become a hidden ally. Either way, it wasn't a fortune he would squander. He would approach things strategically.

“Anyway…” Loricks said awkwardly, and then looked at Hayden. “Rosen will be taking a special test since he was a past student, so he’s coming with me. Lina’s also coming because Mayle wants to speak to her in person. Unfortunately, that means you’ll be alone. Do you think you can manage?”

“That’s fine.” Hayden said.

“Okay, then follow the path. You’ll see people checking in. Just give them your name and spirit, and they’ll prepare you for your interview. Do you have any questions?”

“No, I believe I’ll be fine,” Hayden said. “Is there anything else?”

Loricks grimaced. “Yeah. The tuition here’s three billion a year because the students either got in trouble with the law, or couldn’t make the cut for a good school. Everyone here has rich, influential parents, and they’re volatile, so keep your head down. I’m serious Hayden. I… I helped start this school. I left because I saw some pretty heinous things happen to scholarship students. So just… please. Take care.”

Lina looked at Hayden worriedly.

Hayden was unfazed. “Thank you for the warning.” He jumped out of the shuttle and waved before following the cobblestone path down the fresh, spring-like campus to the registration line. He quickly found it.

Applicants stood in line or chatted under shady pink trees, waiting for their interview.

As he walked through the area with his characteristically bored expression, a group of women sneered at him.

“Guess which student got a scholarship?”

“That’s crazy. He must be talented A.F. to get in with a face like that.”

“Right? It's disgusting. I'd like, totally kill myself if I looked like that.”

Hayden didn’t react to the mocking women. He was a rather vain individual, but the group was so petty that responding felt like a chore.

“Hey kid!”

Hayden ignored the group's leader, a dirty blonde he would eventually know as Sia Leeda. She was off-putting to look at because she curled a single lock of hair around her finger and held it still, maintaining it like the appendage was an ornament. It wasn't even a tick, just a pose to increase her sharp, rude body language at all times. Hayden couldn't think of a single situation where the look would be approved of, and that, strangely, annoyed him.

“Yoo~hoo,” Sia said. “I’m speaking to you, rat.”

Hayden turned to her with chilling eyes that made her lock up. Her friends grew defensive, but the line opened, exposing the female registrar’s line of sight.

“Chill,” Sia's friend said as she glanced at the registrar. “She’s watching.”

“Yeah, but… what the fuck? Did you see that look he gave me?”

“Yeah, it was so not cool.”

Hayden ignored them as he approached the registrar.

“Name?” she asked without looking up.

“Hayden Reskco.”

She scanned through her pad. Her face twitched with disgust when she saw his picture, but she still didn’t look up.

“Spirit?” she asked dryly.

“Winged Horix.”

“Type?”

“Beast.”

“Class?”

“Clear.”

She lowered her pad and looked up. “Did you just say…” Her face creased in artistic angles when she saw him, and she looked at her pad and back up.

A buzz of gossip broke out when students saw her face. It was clear they thought she was mocking his acne.

“Is there a problem?” Hayden asked.

“I’m just making sure you’re… the same person.”

Hayden maintained his dry expression. “I am. You can speak to Loricks Raskin. He’s on campus.”

“No… there’s no need.” She looked at her pad and recorded his spirit, but paused again. “Do you have a second spirit?”

“No.”

The registrar looked up again. “Is there one you can trade out before the test? Because—”

“It’s more than adequate.”

“Uh… okay.” She raised and dropped her eyebrows mockingly, rolling her eyes after. “Wait at the gates. We’ll call your name when your interview’s ready.”

“Thank you.” Hayden turned and walked to a tree. As he passed, Sia folded her arms.

“You got some guts for someone with a training spirit.”

Hayden turned to her. “Are you accustomed to badgering strangers?”

She stared at him intensely, but didn’t say anything.

Hayden took that as a positive sign and walked off.

Sia didn't find it positive at all. She watched the scrub walk away, feeling like someone just stabbed her with a syringe full of annoyance. “Am I going crazy? Or did he just… insult me?”

A nearby male turned to her. “Are you serious?”

Sia’s friend Vanna rolled her eyes. “Obviously. He just challenged her entire personality ‘cause she called him out.”

“Right?” Sia said, clicking her tongue. “Should I say something?”

“Yeah, obviously.” Sia's other friend—Huri—said. “That wasn’t cool.”

“Yeah, but… what should I say? I’m totally gonna sound like a bitch if I say the wrong thing.”

“Just walk up and flip ‘em off,” Vanna suggested. “If you don't say anything, everyone’ll assume the worst.”

“Nah, it won't work ‘cause he's a charity case,” Huri said. “You'll sound like a classist bigot. You need to say something like, Do that to my friend again, and I’ll break your legs! or something. That way, people know he did something, and you're not just classist or whatever.”

“Whoa, that’s good,” Vanna said.

“Right?”

The male from before snorted. “You bitches are crazy.”

“Was someone talking to you?” Sia asked. “Whatever. I'm going to do it.” She strode forward, capturing the attention of the nearby students. They whispered and pointed, thirsting for the drama.

Three seconds to impact.

Just as she was about to lift her hand, a familiar voice screamed, “There you are!”

Sia stared dumbly when she saw Kayline Iska, who was like, big famous, rush past her—and stop before the scrub she was striding up to.

“Fancy meeting you here,” Hayden said. “Do you work here, too?”

Kayline giggled. “I don’t work here. I’m a student. I was just at Galma as part of a work program.”

“Oh, well, welcome home.”

“Uh… yeah. So? How do you like it?”

“It’s…” He glanced at Sia. “Charming.”

Kayline turned to Sia with narrowed eyes, making Sia’s cheeks flush as she turned away. She was extremely grateful she hadn’t flipped the guy off.

“Do you need something?” Kayline asked.

“Uh… no. I was just gonna ask ‘em a question, but I forgot it.”

Sia rushed back to her friends before Kayline could twist her into a confession. As she neared them, Huri and Vanna acted like they didn’t know her. Two-faced clouts!

Just as she was about to leave, a nearby man said, “Why does she look so nervous?”

Sia turned and saw that Kayline was wiping her hands on her skirt as she forcefully giggled and fidgeted. Wait… is she going to ask him out? It was the most unbelievable thing, but that body language was unmistakable! There’s no way.

Sia's bracelet lit red.

Suddenly, a proctor at the gate yelled, “Hayden Reskco!”

“It seems it’s time to go,” Hayden said.

Kayline jumped forward to block him. “Wait!”
