Ryland felt like he was being unnecessarily distracted. It was inevitable that the Great Gods, the Flourinus Order, and other infiltrators and insurgents from various locations all across the Realms would come interfering. For one, he was creating a massive Inscription with a very momentous goal. For another, lots of Realms and entities wanted to claim an abandoned Rhel.

But all of that just felt as though he was having to deal with minor annoyances. Ryland was forced to clear the clutter from the room before he could enjoy its actual purpose.

The Avatar he had trapped under [Continental Step]—the others had been able to escape, but he had caught one—now exploded out of the foot of the magma giant. Whatever damage its form had suffered was quickly healing back up.

A bellowing cry of pain echoed out like endless thunder from the rift through which the gargantuan foot jutted out. Ryland felt a pang of sympathy for the magma giant before dispelling the rift. Then he cast [Animus Aegis] once more to ward off the near-suicidal charge of the escaped Avatar, repelling its burning, golden magic.

The others were throwing their spears from a distance. They fell like ballista bolts shot from the top of a mountain.

Ryland would have let his barrier deal with it as he had done before, but he switched things up this time.

“[Translocate],” he incanted.

The rifts he created went up both around him as well as close to the distant Avatars. Their flung spears shot into the tears, only to emerge from different ones and strike their own allies.

And yet again, the damage wasn’t permanent. The wounds they inflicted on themselves quickly healed up.

Ryland tutted. There was a certain way to deal with Avatars, and he wasn’t sticking to it, which was why he was facing trouble. To defeat them for good, one needed to defeat their source of power. That wasn’t necessarily the mages who had called them forth.

He figured that partially explained why Devas’s and Ophelia’s attempts at taking down the Juren mages hadn’t yielded positive results. Because, with at least a few of the Juren mages now dealt with, the Avatars should have at least been reduced in power by now. Not so. They were going as strong as they had since the battle had begun.

Which meant they were all wrong about just where the Avatars were drawing their strength from.

They attacked once more. This time, a few of them swooped in to keep Ryland busy, while the others prepared a devastating spell in yet another futile attempt to annihilate him.

Well, he called it a spell internally, but it really wasn’t typical magic when it came to Avatars. They didn’t cast. They had no need for it. Their intent was channelled straight from the will of the Great Gods, shaped by the sliver of their own power they were slipping into the cosmos from the Overrealm.

The ones attacking Ryland directly were using a combination of ranged and close-up attacks to keep him occupied. Ryland just shook his head.

Pummelling blasts of gold crashed in on him, each blast loaded with a great amount of what looked like dark lightning. Every impact led to furious explosions, bursts that thundered in the air and pressured his barrier hard enough to make it flicker.

But Ryland bore it because he could.

And also because the attacks were aimed at him specifically. So far, the Avatars had targeted him, whether it be their magic or their weapons crafted from the same divine energy that had created them. They were attacking Ryland directly, which he was thankful for. It meant he didn’t have to worry about his Inscription.

That could change soon, though. With that gigantic spell being prepared by the rest of the Avatars…

The ones assaulting him directly played their final card. Two of the Avatars quickly enmeshed him in nets of gold, as if to restrain his form. Ryland sensed a certain effect on them too. Like an Inscription that actively prevented him from evading, even with his rifts.

This let the rest smash in like half a dozen meteors of burning gold. Their weapons were aimed straight for him, and when they struck, the air itself shivered and the sky ripped apart with frightful bursts of shining gold and lightless black. Their power was so strong, it felt as though Rhel was going to suffer a second apocalypse after what Ryland and Mavar had wrought.

But Ryland weathered through it all. His multilayered barriers were enough to keep him safe, even as they flickered and cracked at certain spots.

He had thought he might need to pull out [Concentric Time] as a final barrier to fully protect himself, but he was glad to see that it hadn’t been necessary. It was hard to place just where the Avatars fell on the spectrum of power, but it certainly couldn’t have been higher than low-Empyrean.

More importantly, with their tantrum weathered, Ryland could turn his attention to the potential worse offender. For while he had remained secure that they had targeted him so far, the spell that the Avatars at the rear had created didn’t leave him reassured.

So, he ended it. “[Dragongod’s Spinespear].”

A colossal rift cracked open right next to them, with an even more colossal draconic tail spearing out at the same instant. The undead appendage was just as enormous as Ryland remembered it. Even his rift was struggling to accommodate the girth, the golden edges crackling as the gargantuan draconic tail punched through.

The Avatars it had targeted were smashed down, and the enormous sphere of golden and crimson energy they had condensed together now exploded.

That explosion was the worst of the bunch so far. The shockwave emanating from it actually pushed Ryland’s body back, despite the powerful presence of his barriers. He had been right to end it far above his Inscription.

To ensure the struck-down Avatars didn’t reach the ground, Ryland had the tail swiping through the air and up again in a loop to catch any of the summons it could.

But the real problem remained. The Avatars were still fine.

This was getting a tad annoying. He had now dealt with them in myriad ways, yet they continued to return and cause trouble.

Even now, despite their earlier failures, the Avatars were once again preparing for yet another attack. Ryland wasn’t sure what they were going to cast next, but he decided he had cared enough. His goal, as ever, was to protect his Inscription, so he got to work.

“[World Node],” Ryland incanted.

The surface of Rhel started being overtaken by a series of three-dimensional graphical coordinates. His goal was to cast a barrier much like he had done to himself, but around as much of Rhel as he could cover in the short time he had.

However, simply casting [Eternal Bastion] and any of his other spells powered by his Soul Glyph of Aegis wasn’t going to cut it. The planet was far too enormous.

As such, he’d perform a small trick. Ryland just needed to duplicate the cast as far and as wide as he could within a short period of time. By transferring the spell circles through the nodes of [World Node], he could do just that.

Unfortunately, just like when he had tried moving an entire star, [World Node] was taking a little too long to cover as much surface area as it could. It was true, he didn’t need to protect the entirety of Rhel. The total area he needed covered wasn’t anywhere even close to how much magic he had needed to move a star.

But in the midst of a battle, against Divine Avatars, every second was precious. Every span of a heartbeat was a time that went on too long for comfort.

They attacked again. Ryland was confident that his barriers could withstand whatever they might have thrown at him, but it seemed they were capable of learning some lessons. For even after their powerful blows from their weapons left no real mark, they maintained contact.

Because a spark of darkness leaked out of their weapons and assaulted Ryland’s form, eating away at his very being at a conceptual level.

Ryland actually blinked. This was the same kind of attack that the Rhellian students had suffered, where the Conceptual Glyphs that had been a part of them had been forcefully corrupted. In their case, it had been their very manasouls that had been targeted. Technically, Ryland’s was too, since he had manifested his manasoul in his Incarnation form.

“[Translocate],” he incanted, quickly disappearing from the area before the Avatars broke away too much of him. He could recover, but he didn’t want his cast of [World Node] to get interrupted.

They followed. Fast. It was almost as if they simply broke apart where they had been and instantly reformed at their new location, which was right next to Ryland.

That was teleportation of a sort, he supposed.

It wasn’t surprising. In fact, he should have figured they’d follow in that fashion. The last time he had tried using [Dimensional Lock] to stymie them, they had executed the exact same manoeuvre to get away. Wily things, these Avatars.

Ryland rifted away again, but he left a surprise for them this time. “[Singularity Trap].”

Another point grew to being where the Avatars were located, a point not dissimilar from the ones created by [World Node]. Except, this point drew in everything Ryland had targeted. Since he had branded the Avatars with a specific Subglyph in the spell circle, they were all immediately drawn to a single location.

The compressive force, not unlike gravity, was so powerful that there was no chance for the Avatars to escape its clutches. They were trapped. For good. Even when they escaped by deforming and reforming elsewhere, it wasn’t enough. [Singularity Trap] just pulled them back.

Which granted Ryland all the time in the world that he needed to finish casting [World Node]. At least as far as he needed it to go.

With that done, and with the Avatars reduced to merely attacking him from a distance, Ryland cast his barrier spells next. He could actually feel the strain on his manasoul as he cast [Eternal Bastion] and [Absolute Timefort] from a distance. As far as his eyes could see, Rhel was soon shaded by faintly translucent forcefields of frighteningly powerful magic.

Ryland didn’t really check what the others were doing. He trusted they’d handle the matter of the attacking infiltrators themselves soon enough. All he needed to do was deal with the Avatars and ensure a general level of safety.

By the time the barriers were in place, Ryland looked up to find the Avatars attempting a different approach to dealing with him. He had to commend their tenacity if nothing else.

They were massing themselves amorphously now. Since the still-active [Singularity Trap] was still constantly pulling them away from Ryland, they had chosen to create a mass of roiling divine energy close to their location. Ryland’s spell was pulling on it too, ripping it apart as his Subglyph ensured everything from the Avatars was targeted.

But in this case, they were acting all together. With so much energy being pushed into one location, even a Tier [27] spell like [Singularity Trap] wasn’t enough.

Not that the violent beam, burst, or whatever else would emanate from that gathered energy would actually leave a mark on Ryland. His barriers were absolute. And because of that absoluteness, Ryland didn’t allow the Avatars to finish casting and continue wasting his time.

“[Sun Summoning],” Ryland incanted, the voice of his Incarnation form echoing across the atmosphere of Rhel.

Rifts broke open high above them with a crackle like thunder, conjoining together to form one gigantic tear in the airspace of Rhel. Then the sun was indeed summoned. The star of the solar system they were currently in was called forth, bathing Ryland’s locality with burning wrath.

As expected, the brightness and power were unlike anything Rhel had suffered before, besides Ryland’s battle against Mavar, of course. Unimaginable heat and light that seared through space crushed down upon everything, hammering down with endless power.

Ryland was always fascinated by the spell’s noise. The way air itself burned, the way everything grew superheated and matter was converted into pure, untameable energy.

He shut off the spell soon, allowing Rhel to return to its regular atmosphere. Ryland had no intention of keeping up the spell any longer than necessary. Even with the barriers he had set up, he had no wish to cause lasting harm to Rhel, especially after what he had already done.

When it ended, the world slowly returned to normal. Air returned, light was no longer burning out his retinas, and no more horrific sizzling and shearing sounds invaded his ears.

And the Avatars were still alive. The sheer amount of energy he had just brought in had incinerated them over and over, but they were far from dead and gone, as he had preferred them. Their forms were constructed purely from energy, so it was impossible for Ryland to technically burn them away.

But his hope had been to convert the energy to such a highly-interfered and highly-condensed state that reforming them would be impossible. Sadly, that didn’t seem to be the case. Slowly but surely, they were recreating themselves.

At this point, Ryland’s annoyance was just giving way to resignation. Would he really need to—

He paused. A new presence was rapidly growing in strength not far from his location. A presence he faintly recognized. It seemed Xyke had succeeded in her plan, as Ryland had always known she would.

It took just a few more seconds before the Incarnation of Growth appeared before Ryland.

“You’re looking quite well,” Ryland said.

The Incarnation regarded him with eyes still forming within his skull. Like the Avatars, he was also constructed purely of energy for the moment. In his case, it was pure mana expressed in Conceptual Glyphs crowding his body.

“I am… well,” the Incarnation said. He looked away then, his slowly reforming eyes focusing on the Avatars who had nearly regained their forms too. “And it seems I’ve arrived at an opportune time.”

“Really? Do you believe you can do something about them? They’ve been vexing me for a while now. I suppose it’s my fault for not simply destroying their anchors, but I thought I’d prove a point.”

“…yes. I think I know what to do.”

A spell circle formed on the palm of the Incarnation’s green hand. Ryland blinked, needing a moment to recognize the spell.

“[Infinite],” the Incarnation incanted.

Time. That was what Ryland’s new ally had manipulated. It wasn’t quite a Soul Glyph of Time. Rather, he had used his Soul Glyph of Growth with a Subglyph of Time to create a localized region where time kept expanding eternally and endlessly. Growing on and on, faster and faster.

This didn’t mean time had sped up. That wasn’t how growth worked in this case. Rather, every moment represented by a unit of time was now growing. A second would last hours. A minute would become a month. If anything, time was slowing down for the Avatars.

Essentially, it trapped the Avatars in a bubble of time that seemed to stretch forever. A process that would have taken them a mere blink before now took ages.

“There,” the Incarnation said. “I believe they have been successfully stymied for the time being.”

“…indeed.” Ryland found himself feeling rather foolish, though he didn’t have a spell off the top of his head that allowed that sort of temporal manipulation. “Well, thank you for that elegant solution, temporary though it is.”

“Yes. It grants you time.” The Incarnation paused for a moment, then looked again at Ryland. He moved in an odd, stiff way, like he was an automaton of some sort. “It is time.”

“Time? For what?”

“For another of our kind to appear, I think.”

Ryland was about to ask what the Incarnation meant, when he spotted something quite worrying. The bits of the Avatar trapped in the domain of slowed-time were simply disappearing. At first, he thought they’d reform elsewhere until and unless the Incarnation cast [Infinite] everywhere.

But then, he noticed they were disappearing permanently. They were gone for good. And Ryland couldn’t see where.

Wonderful. Now he had not one but two different things to worry about.
