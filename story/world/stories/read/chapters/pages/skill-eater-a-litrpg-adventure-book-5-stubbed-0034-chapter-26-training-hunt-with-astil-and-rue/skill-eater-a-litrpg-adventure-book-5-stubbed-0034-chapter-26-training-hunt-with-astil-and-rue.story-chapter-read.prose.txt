Edge cast his gaze across the azure heavens, enjoying the late autumn breeze caressing his cheeks as he walked with his friends at his side.

Today, the crew was traveling to a high-threat biome bordering the Lakelands, which had only been recently discovered. Part of the region had been charted prior to the anomaly, though it hadn’t been on this side of the planet before. It was the safest biome within a hundred miles, an ideal location for Rue to take on stage-two prey while the team watched his back.

Two additions to the core crew had joined them on this hunt. Trapper had invited Astil to tag along, the first time the young woman had left World’s End since the disaster. Astil was working as a guide since she wasn’t strong enough to cycle up near the free city, and this was a perfect opportunity for the novice hunter to resume her journey to stage two. She was incredibly grateful, thanking them every few minutes while blinking back tears.

“You’ve already gone above and beyond.” Trapper pulled Astil in for a hug. “This is the least I can do to repay you. Just be careful and remember to stay in the middle of our formation. We don’t want to lose you… or you.” She reached down to scratch Rue’s ears, who had been angling for some recognition from the crew’s leader for the last few miles.

Stellia laughed at the fox’s antics. Edge had run into the veteran hunter the night before and invited her to participate in today’s outing. It wouldn’t yield much profit for the stage-three lightning wielder, but she seemed content to enjoy the weather while chatting with the team. He was pleased that everyone was getting along so well. He wanted Dialla to recruit Stellia and convince her to relocate to Puppet Town—maybe even join Trapper’s crew—and this was a fortuitous occasion for them to get to know each other better.

The Association hadn’t had time to survey the region and update the job board, which meant this wasn’t an official mission, although they would pay for any information the crew collected along the way. Instead of pursuing a predetermined target, the hunters were going to do this the old-fashioned way. They would scout the area while looking for beasts Astil and Rue could handle, helping the duo cycle up their cores and rank up their skills. Along the way, Edge would keep his eyes peeled for any promising powers he wanted to steal, along with any valuable resources he uncovered with Dragon’s Greed.

Three hours after they set out from World’s End, the crew arrived at the borderlands separating the Lakelands from the Patchwork Prairie. The prairie was unusual in that it lacked sub-biomes, and the region was covered in fragrant wildflowers for most of the year. Each species claimed its own swath of ground, which was where the name came from. The growth only came up to his knees. They didn’t need to blaze a trail, and it should be easy to locate suitable prey.

After they crossed into the prairie, Edge, Jumo, and Sasha split up from the main group. They surveyed the surrounding terrain, making sure nothing nasty was prowling within the verdant expanse and there weren’t any environmental hazards along the floating wagon’s path.

When he made it back, Stella was standing guard with Rue, and Trapper and Astil were in the middle of a conversation. “If you’re looking to join a crew, we have a spot open. We have seven members stationed in the Ivory Plains. Working out of Puppet Town would be a lot safer until you evolve and hit the middle of stage two.”

“Thanks. I deeply appreciate the offer. I’ll need to give it some thought before I decide, if that’s okay with you.”

“Of course. No pressure, but I think you would like living in Puppet Town. Everyone on our crew is a good person, and the settlement is a close-knit community working toward our mutual advancement without all the competing factions and shady characters that call the free city home. I have a bad feeling that the situation in World’s End is going to get worse before it gets any better.”

Edge smiled as Trapper continued to apply some subtle persuasion. He could tell Stellia was listening closely, and he knew the conversation was intended to tempt her as well. While they walked, he reviewed the plan for the rest of the hunt.

Since their goal was earning experience for Astil and Rue, the crew was looking for some stage-two beasts that weren’t likely to have any surprises in store. Warlord’s Mantle, Mirror Image, and Bulwark should shield the duo from incidental fire while the team intercepted any direct attacks.

When the creatures were isolated and contained, the pair would engage them under the crew’s watchful eye, maximizing the gains they obtained from the hunt. Rue had grumbled about not being given free rein until Edge took his Companion aside and told the fox he had been given the most important mission of all: staying by Astil’s side in case something slipped past and threatened the young guide.

It wasn’t complete bullshit, and he’d done his best to project sincerity through their bond. In the end, the beast bought it, in part because Rue had grown rather fond of Astil. He stopped complaining from that point on, looking remarkably diligent as he scanned his surroundings for danger.

They walked for another hour or so, basking in the biome’s natural beauty and the fragrant scent of flowers filling the air. The hunters parked the wagon when they spotted a pack of loombas in the distance. The fuzzy marsupials were the size of Earth donkeys and a perfect target for the stage-one pair. They were herbivores that prioritized agility instead of offense and didn’t have many offensive powers in their skillsets.

The crew spent the rest of the afternoon splitting the pack apart and engaging them a few at a time.

Stellia could have wiped out every loomba in sight without batting an eye. Luckily, the woman was enjoying the outing while feeling out Trapper’s crew, trying to decide if they were worth hitching her wagon to in Edge’s estimation. He had liked her from the start, but his appreciation only grew as the day wore on, and it was clear that the rest of his team thought well of her too.

When he looked at Trapper, she nodded to him, indicating that she thought Stellia would be a good fit for Puppet Town. He had a hunch that the hunter would take the bait and accept Dialla’s offer, which should be coming with whoever accompanied the Wave-Bound Warrior when Widow’s crew completed the return trip.

All in all, it was a thoroughly pleasant day. The Patchwork Prairie was gorgeous, and it had been quite a while since he’d been able to relax and enjoy Ord’s natural splendor. Stellia was a ray of sunshine, and by the time they wiped out the last loomba, Astil had picked up three cycles, and Rue had gained some valuable experience too.

They wanted to make it back before dark, so after butchering the bodies, Edge stored everything in his vault, and the hunters walked back the way they had come.

After crossing into the Lakelands, he split off from the rest of the group on the pretense of needing to get in some skill training before returning to the settlement. What he really wanted was to steal some powers without revealing Skill-Eater to Astil or Stellia, and he’d spotted a likely target a few miles back.

He bid them farewell and circled around in stealth mode, observing the peak stage-two stag before making his move. After confirming it couldn’t sense his presence in shadow form, he crept up behind it and thrust an iceblade into the beast’s neck, restricting the blood flow to its brain and rendering it vulnerable to Extraction.

He stole three skills and put their experience into Rejuvenation. To his delight, the final theft was sufficient to fill the avatar’s cup to the brim. At long last, it was time to pick a major upgrade for one of his most important powers. As a broad grin split his face, he stepped into his inner world to check out his options.

Edge praised the excited avatar, laughing as the little glass man did cartwheels and handsprings. It was glowing like a magelight from all the transformative energy waiting to be given a concrete form. While the rest of the gang celebrated Rejuvenation’s big moment, he called on his Guide and began to read.

The skill Rejuvenation has advanced to Rank 4.

Its mana efficiency has been enhanced.

Please choose between one of the following options:

Warning: If no option is selected, your skill will remain unchanged and gain a slight bonus to mana efficiency.

Every upgrade on the list was great, but Edge chose the third option without hesitation.

He had his Organ-Guard to protect his vitals, and Rejuvenation’s healing grew faster and more comprehensive every time he increased his Amplification. The second pick would be a nice way to supplement his resistance to poisons, venoms, and acids, along with any skills that penetrated his body, but there were other ways to acquire something similar after he evolved and had more skill slots to work with.

Even if that wasn’t the case, he would have gone with the third upgrade and had no regrets. It offered something he had desperately wanted to acquire for months—a way to heal his allies. The battle with the stage-four slime had been a terrifyingly close call, and he needed a skill that could treat critical injuries. Hunting prey above their stage was a regular occurrence by this point, not to mention the ever-present threat posed by the Ancients, the Wardens, and the Coalition.

At Rejuvenation’s current strength, five minutes was sufficient to heal most life-threatening wounds, even regrow organs and limbs over multiple applications. While the cooldown was a genuine limitation, it was a night-and-day difference from relying on medicinal consumables, which weren’t nearly as effective for emergency treatment.

It didn’t say anything about limiting the skill’s benefits, which means the aura will purge toxins and foreign mana from their systems on top of healing their wounds, plus any other powers Rejuvenation acquires as I continue to rank it up.

It would provide an invaluable safety net while hunting with Rue. Edge was worried that a high-stage predator would inflict a mortal wound on the golden fox if he let the beast join his excursions in the Lakelands. With a healing aura in his skillset, hunting prey above his stage wasn’t nearly as risky, which would let his Companion advance far faster than before.

He wondered if his Skill Bonds played a role in the options he was given. He had discussed the issue numerous times while hanging out with his avatars during his daily visits. He doubted he could take his powers in a direction that ran counter to their natures, but he decided to confer with his collection more often about how he hoped to use each skill going forward, in case it made a difference.

While he was mulling the matter over, Rejuvenation’s avatar continued to change. When it stopped shining, the glass figure and the circulatory system suspended in its body looked the same as before. What was new was the palpable aura of health and well-being the avatar exuded.

Edge was so thrilled that he picked the power up and carried it on his shoulders, running around his village while cheering like a fool. The rest of the gang thought this was great fun and followed hot on his heels, along with Rue, who was yipping like crazy.

He already knew most of the details from the vision that always accompanied a skill upgrade, but when everyone calmed down, he called up his Guide to read the description in full.

Rejuvenation <water, limited aura>: Rank 4. Paths: Unlimited Regeneration, Purification, Aura.

Rapidly heal wounds. Rejuvenation can restore destroyed organs at a significantly slower rate. Rejuvenation will actively purify foreign mana from your body along with a range of venoms and related poisons. The skill can be used as an aura for five minutes, with a cooldown of 24 hours.

Edge was overcome with relief as he dismissed the floating screen. Not only would his friends have some considerable insurance from this moment on, the aura would make a huge difference during large-scale conflicts. On top of healing anyone who needed it and dramatically reducing their downtime after major engagements, he could combine Rejuvenation with Overdrive, making his allies nigh unkillable for five minutes during a decisive battle.

If I had this earlier, I could have healed Earl’s limbs and saved a lot of people during the conflict with the kaiju. Maybe Momo would still be alive, although I can’t do anything if someone is already gone.

Any way he looked at it, this was one of the most important upgrades he had ever acquired. Edge was smiling the whole way as he made his way back to World’s End—excited to share the good news with the people who had become his second family.

***

When Edge returned to the free city, he made his way to the Lost Traveler.

His friends were thrilled to hear about Rejuvenation’s rank-four upgrade, which led to a spontaneous celebration over grilled meat skewers and frosty bottles of beer. There was another low-rung brawl scheduled in a little over an hour, so he walked with Melenia to the arena’s registration annex.

The group was smaller this time, which meant Edge and Mel were participating in the same fight. He would have had reservations about battling his friend, since the arena’s magic allowed the combatants to suffer significant wounds. However, now that he could restore her to perfect health the moment the match was over, he could give the brawl his all without holding back.

The System would penalize them if they tried to team up. Instead, he worked his way around the opposite side of the fighting floor so they wouldn’t run the risk of eliminating each other until closer to the end. They exchanged a few blows over the course of the match and did fairly well overall, but neither of them was fated to take first place.

They were caught off-guard by a gladiator who used a powerful combo to win the fight. One spell transformed the arena’s floor into mud, while the second caused a force field to descend from above. Everyone who was standing in the center of the ring sank into the muck. The barrier drove them deep into the mud and prevented them from surfacing.

Edge was getting ready to unleash a Disruption surge when the man turned the sludge back into stone, entombing him inside. He might have been able to force his way out using Overdrive, but he didn’t want to reveal his ultimate’s existence just yet. Even if that wasn’t an issue, he needed to save Overdrive in case their meeting with King took an unexpected turn for the worse. He tried shifting into shadow form, but the skill turned his body into a substance similar to malleable mist. It wouldn’t let him pass through solid stone unless there was a gap.

Being trapped by tons of rock while he slowly suffocated was one of the most terrifying experiences of his life, an eye-opening lesson that revealed why so many gladiators retired after just a few matches. His thoughts grew dim as carbon dioxide poisoned his body, repressing the urge to panic by reminding himself this would all be over soon.

Sure enough, before he had time to asphyxiate, the arena teleported him into the ready room, where Mel appeared by his side. She was wide-eyed as she gasped for breath, followed by a furious growl. “I should have placed a stone by the wall and Swapped out of the mud. I need to work out a better way to distribute them during a complex fight.”

“I have room for improvement too,” he replied. “I was overconfident after my last brawl. This is a timely reminder that there are tons of deadly combos out there, and I can’t counter them all. Even weak opponents can kill me if I lower my guard.”

“My near-death experience wasn’t any fun,” she agreed, “but going head-to-head against professional killers is even more valuable than I realized. There aren’t any guardrails when fighting outside the ring, and a single mistake can cost us everything.”

“There are a lot of skills out there that we haven’t seen before.” Edge nodded. “And many of these gladiators have been at this for years. Even the low-rung warriors are dangerous, but we won’t get caught by the same trick twice.”

Despite their loss, they placed in the top five. Their performance was sufficient to improve their rankings, but they needed to up their game during the final two brawls if they wanted to qualify for the Duos’ Clash before the cutoff. Edge and Mel put their heads together on their way back to the inn, coming up with counters and hashing out tactics as they crossed the mixing bowl.
