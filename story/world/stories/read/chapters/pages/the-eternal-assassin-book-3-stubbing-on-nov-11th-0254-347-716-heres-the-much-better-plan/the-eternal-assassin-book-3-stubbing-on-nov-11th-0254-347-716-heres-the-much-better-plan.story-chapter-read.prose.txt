Despite the wild series of events that resulted in losing one of their own, as soon as Asher returned them all back to the Eternal Mark he’d tossed down in the meeting room before Recalling away, they got right back into the swing of things.

“I know that was a little eventful, and I apologize for the theatrics, but I needed to make sure I got an actual confession out of him before dealing with the problem,” Donvath explained. “I’ve long since learned that I can’t use the excuse, ‘I saw them do it in an alternate timeline’ or ‘they do it in the future’. Never really works all that well.”

“No apologies needed. We have no space in our midst for one playing frivolous games when there is actual work to be done,” Groln stated. “Not that I disagree with your choice of handling things, but you don’t expect to receive any fallout from the Lambit Republic? If they were to declare war on the Noala Kingdom, that sounds as though it would make our job of finishing off the last six liches all the more difficult.”

“I’m not going to give them a reason to do so,” Donvath shrugged. “After all, we’re going to declare he died a hero. We made no effort to hide the fact that what we were doing was going to be beyond dangerous. If the Lambit Republic tries to use the fact that their own volunteer representative fell while helping us take down one of the liches as a means to invade, I have a feeling the Eight Crests would be more than happy to back us up and counter attack. Not to mention I’m on rather good terms with arguably the deadliest assassin in the entire world.”

“You’re serious?” Asher asked. “Didn’t you just mention how we had that talk about not going after leaders of countries?”

“We talked about not going after leaders unprovoked,” Donvath clarified. “A single assassination attempt is one thing. If they declare war, that’s quite different. I think once their representatives start dropping like flies one after another, they’ll come to their senses. And I think they know that’s how things would go in the first place, so I highly doubt they’d do anything as reckless as openly declaring war. Damn, it’s good to have greater powers on our side.”

“This talk of wars between countries depresses me,” Amanda admitted, sighing as she removed her helmet and placed it on the table before her. “Can we get back to the matter at hand? We’ve yet to go through the debriefing, much less pick our next targets.”

“Of course,” Donvath said, motioning toward Asher. “Asher, do you want to start us off? We’ve heard the gist about what happened to Lanra, but I’d like some more detail as well.”

“Flunx’ castle was even more heavily defended than expected,” Asher began, grimacing as he recalled the sheer number of near-death experiences they had. “The team from the Eight Crests remained outside to keep an anti-spatial field running around the castle and prevent Flunx from escaping. While they dealt with golems and the lich trying to punch her way out, we took the long way through the castle in search of her position. I can’t even tell you how many times we nearly died, but eventually, we managed to find her in the center and took her out. Then, while we were looting the battlefield, the entire castle detonated due to some form of deadman’s switch. The magical explosion was larger than anything I’ve ever seen. Almost like…”

Asher had to stop himself before comparing it to a nuclear blast. Obviously, none of the people sitting before him knew what that looked like, and he shook his head. “…Anyway, it was massive. Many times larger than the palace, or even the monster you guys just took down. We were a few miles away and the shockwaves of magical power swept over us in seconds. I was out of tritessence so Lanra borrowed a ton from her soul and shoved it into me in order to ensure we’d manage to escape with our lives, but she put herself deep into tritessence debt to do so.”

“By the realms,” Amanda muttered, her eyes going wide as she no doubt tried to picture the sheer size of such an explosion. “It’s a good thing you all managed to escape.”

“No kidding. It was a lot closer than I would have cared to admit,” Asher nodded.

“Our battle was far more standard,” Groln stated. “Richard got us close enough to the lich’s suspected location, and then we spent a few hours fighting our way through a mass of zombies so thick you could have walked along their heads. Thankfully, Falling Sun and her Order were able to wipe out entire swathes of zombies in moments, giving the rest of us the breathing room needed to keep fighting. Eventually, we fought our way deep enough in that the abomination you witnessed rose up before us and tried to destroy us. The mercenaries fell instantly, but my team and her Order managed to hold our own easily enough. There was a bit of a death aura we had to deal with, but again, Falling Sun has some form of aura to help counteract it, and combined with the artifacts we procured ahead of time, we were able to shrug it off well enough. Through our joint efforts we eventually wore the abomination down, and that was when we called you in.”

“Actually taking it down wasn’t easy,” Amanda admitted. “The abomination seemed to have the power to consume the surrounding horde of zombies in order to repair itself and continue fighting. It’s why the battle took us a good couple of hours. I probably killed more undead in the last twenty-four hours than I have in the last decade of fighting in the deadlands. And for Donvath’s sake, the finale was simple enough. Asher lent me his void dagger to carve a hole into a bunker we discovered in the core of the abomination, where he then dropped down and finished off the lich.”

“I’m pretty certain she thought her bunker was impossible to break into,” Asher added. “And without access to the void, it very well might have been. She looked about as shocked as a lich possibly can when we cut a hole in the roof and I dropped in. No doubt she was going to wait us out for as long as it took before running away.”

“Not a bad idea. Teleportation can be blocked easily enough, as you and Lanra have both shown,” Donvath admitted. “Creating an impregnable bunker within the core of an abomination so deadly it took two greater powers and their skilled teams hours to whittle down was probably viewed as a safer bet. In fact, I wonder if she’d actually sealed herself away in there ahead of time. There’s a chance she did so preemptively the moment she discovered that the Council was collapsing in the hopes of avoiding losing her own life as well.”

“I killed her before she could say anything, so we’ll never know,” Asher shrugged. “Though I think that’s pretty likely. I didn’t see any sort of door or window in or out of there.”

“Well, it’s a good thing we managed to take them both down, because I’ve got some bad news,” Donvath admitted, growing serious all of a sudden as he looked between them. “Technically it’s also good news, but I think the bad probably outweighs the good. The liches are almost certainly going to launch an attack on the Noala Kingdom in retaliation for what we’ve done.”

“How in the world is that good news?” Asher asked, his heart sinking as he thought back to the screams and dead villagers as Horntho Village burned around him.

“It’s good news because it gives us an opening,” Donvath clarified. Getting up and moving over to the chalkboard, he circled the six remaining journals he’d hung there the other day. “We’ve got six liches left, and unfortunately, we only know the approximate locations for two of them. Rather than having to scour the continent for the final four liches…”

“They’ll come to us,” Amanda said, her gaze hard as she looked at him. “However, while that makes our job easier, your kingdom will no doubt suffer in exchange.”

“Hence me labeling it as bad news,” Donvath said.

“You’re sure this attack will come?” Groln asked.

“The future is never set in stone, and wizards are notoriously difficult to read… but yes, the critical point is all but certain. What is still up in the air is precisely how much damage they are going to manage to do to the kingdom before we stop them. And with that in mind, I would like to propose our next target.”

Plucking one of the journals from the chalkboard, Donvath slapped it onto the table. Groln immediately recognized it, and he perked up.

“You believe we should go after the lich hiding within the Hojon Empire? Not that I disagree, but why now?”

“From what little we know, it sounds like this is arguably the most dangerous lich we have left in terms of sheer destructive potential,” Donvath explained. “With nine minions who were once in the Stage of Power and three more who were once greater powers, we can’t allow a force like that to reach our soil. Not to mention whatever the lich itself is capable of. The Hojon Empire has already done the hard part of creating a quarantine zone, which means we have an area to fight within where we don’t have to worry about innocent casualties. If we can take out this lich before their coordinated attack, the damage the Noala Kingdom will suffer will be greatly reduced.”

“How long do we even have before they come for us?” Asher asked, thinking back to the horrific state Lanra was currently in. “Do we have time to prepare?”

“While I am all but certain they are coming, the when is still up in the air,” Donvath admitted. “I told you one wizard was tricky to follow through time. Six is nearly impossible. It’s only due to the sheer level of devastation they are going to wreak that I can see the attack coming at all. At best, we have a month or two. At worst… maybe a week. Mind you, for a bunch of liches used to working at plans over the scales of centuries, that’s moving lightning quick on their part. I’ve already begun notifying the various noble families around the kingdom of what is to come. We can only hope that my truthseekers and the nobles are enough to fight back against whatever fresh horrors the Council brings our way.”

“My Order will provide as much assistance and manpower as we are able,” Amanda declared, pressing a fist against her chest in salute. “We are allied with the Reaper and the coalition alike. No undead shall set foot in this land without crossing through our steel.”

“The Hojon Empire might be able to spare one or two more greater powers to help out,” Groln said. “...For the right price. Apologies, Immortal, but my Queen is ever the opportunist, and leaving our own land undefended is always dangerous. We’re not the only superpower on the continent, after all.”

“Don’t worry, I’m happy to pay whatever she wants if it means my people might be spared even a little suffering,” Donvath smiled. “If you could set up a meeting through your sending stone, I’d appreciate it. And maybe warn your Queen that our little coalition is going to be paying your country a visit in the next couple of days. We need to wait for Asher’s soul to fully recover, and ideally, we’ll have Lanra back in action before we go after the Council’s heaviest hitter.”

“I can’t believe it’s all coming down to one final battle,” Asher said, wondering what it would be like to go up against four or five liches all at the same time. Their power sets were so varied and different from one another that there really wasn’t any way of knowing what such a battle would even look like. The only thing that was all but guaranteed was wanton death and destruction wherever they went.

“And we’re going to be ready for when it does,” Donvath declared as he got to his feet. “Now, no more meetings. Go take a nap and finish recovering from your soul damage. I need you in peak form before we launch our preemptive strike. Even if they’re undead minions, we’re still going to be going up against three greater powers. That’s not a fight you willingly take at anything other than one-hundred-percent!”
