“Kalanthia?” I call hoarsely – I must have been screaming; my throat is painful.

No response.

I reach through the Bond and find the quiet placidity of sleep. Unconscious, then. A mixture of relief and concern goes through me – she’s not dead, but unconscious isn’t a good sign either.

Forcing myself to my feet, I scramble into the next-door circle, almost slamming my shaking hands onto the closest bit of Kalanthia I can reach. I close my eyes, sending my mind into her body, already cataloguing the injuries.

There are many. None life-threatening, but all painful. Unusually so – like someone has been doing their best to inflict pain without risking permanent injury or death.

I pull my mind out of her body and frown. I had been assuming that the injuries had come from the rough pull of Bond Teleport. I know it normally pulls my companions to me without injury, but I felt just how much the Skill was straining to pull Kalanthia through something and I feared the consequences of that. But this is far too deliberate.

Hopefully Kalanthia will be able to shed some light – when she wakes up.

I’m about to dive into healing when I notice just how little mana I have. I should have expected it – Bond Teleport took everything I had and then some. I grimace as I realise my health is down to less than ten percent of its total too. No wonder I’m feeling so bad.

Willpower Leverage is helping my mana to replenish more quickly than it would without the boost to Wisdom, but I’m going to need a lot more than I can recover on my own. Pulling a potion out, I tip it back and see my pool jump up by a little under half within a few seconds. Another potion sees my regeneration rate doubling. Finally, I reach out to my companions.

It’s worked – Kalanthia’s here and nothing has exploded, I tell them. A wave of relief laps at me from multiple directions at once. However, she’s injured. The relief is joined with concern. I may need mana to help heal her.

Take what you need, River tells me immediately, the others chiming in almost immediately after, seconding her offer.

Thank you, I tell them gratefully, then focus back on the nunda, diving back into her body.

Healing the injuries takes more effort than I was expecting. There seems to be a sort of malignant mana in the wounds that impedes my healing magic. It reminds me a little of the fight I had with Nicholas soon after arriving in this world – the way the sword he used to cut my foot off made it difficult to heal. Unlike that one, this also limits blood loss.

I don’t like the picture the pieces are currently making. There aren’t many reasons why something – or someone – might want to inflict painful wounds that don’t lose too much blood.

Thanks to my experience with Nicholas, I know what to do here. Isolating the malignant mana in each wound, I cut that section of Kalanthia’s flesh away from it and then regrow it. Wet splats meet my ears as bloody pieces of flesh begin to rain upon the stone floor.

Little by little, Kalanthia’s body begins knitting together again, fur regrowing and hiding that there had ever been a wound.

I’m more worried about her regeneration, though – a Tier three’s health regeneration might not be that of an Immortal, but it’s still impressive enough. Yet here I’m having to do practically all the work myself. If I’d just cut the malignant mana out and left the wounds open, I suspect she’d have bled to death, despite the relatively shallow nature of most of the cuts.

Has she been given some sort of potion? I wonder, though I can’t find any traces of it in her stomach.

I started with the most serious wounds so it takes me a while to reach some minor wounds that are even more tell-tale than the nature of the malignant mana. Fury ignites in me as I see the rubbed-raw flesh around her ankles and around her throat. It intensifies when I see ragged rope around her back legs and then even more when I look further and see just what she has wrapped around her head.

“A muzzle?” I demand, absolutely seething. My mana burns within my veins – it’s an almost impossible struggle to keep it contained and not set something on fire. But since the perpetrators aren’t here and burning the offending article would just cause Kalanthia to be further injured, I manage to hold it back.

There are so many implications to my newest discoveries, but I force my mind to stay in the present. Healing Kalanthia comes first. Figuring out who did this to her is next and would be easier with her cooperation.

I heal the burn from the bindings, examining the broken remnants of a cord made of unknown materials around two of her paws. The bindings must have broken when I summoned her – perhaps that’s why it was such a challenge to do so. Something tells me that isn’t the whole story, though – I remember that bindings were part of the impression I got from my Skill, but so was a veil and a net.

The two frayed bindings resist my attempts to remove them – even my knife is unable to cut through them. For now, I have to leave them there, though I heal the rubbed-raw skin beneath them.

Finally, I’ve healed all her injuries and I turn my attention to the muzzle. Pulling out of my healing trance completely, I’m briefly distracted by the sounds of movement. Glancing around, I see that everyone has clearly left the bunkers – because most of them are now in the square, gawking at me like this is prime entertainment.

Right at the front are the lords. Elarion and Josiah are watching in fascination. Valence, at least, has the grace to look a little embarrassed, though is clearly still curious. Tiria and Keshaka are behind them, curious too. And what looks like half the settlement is filling the square, most eyes on me or Kalanthia, but some studying the blackened traces of the ritual with interest.

I feel an almost overwhelming urge to snap at everyone – Kalanthia isn’t a beast in a circus and I’m more than a little uncomfortable performing under so many eyes – but I’ve spent too long building up a reputation of a calm and thoughtful settlement leader to lose it in one fit of pique. However, I do have a good excuse for clearing out the majority of people.

She’s been through something serious. I don’t know what state she’ll be in when she wakes up, I warn. Waking up to so many people is unlikely to help.

Do you think she could be a danger? Josiah asks, alarmed.

Possibly, I confirm levelly.

Couldn’t you control her? Elarion asks, his mental tone politely blank. I send him a long look. I thought we were past these kinds of tests.

Given what she’s clearly gone through, I’m not going to continue the nightmare by forcing her to do anything unless she’s a danger to herself or others, I reply flatly. Which is why I wish to reduce the chance of either by limiting the number of people around. Targets, or possibly threats depending on what state of mind she wakes up in.

We’ll clear the area, Valence agrees quickly, his embarrassment turning to resolve. It’s not as if we don’t have plenty of other things to do, he continues pointedly.

The other lords mutter reluctant agreement and I feel a sudden wave of appreciation for Valence's support. Out of all the nobles here, he probably understands best what it is to be brought low in front of a crowd.

Even as I turn back to Kalanthia, I hear orders being barked out – and the aggrieved murmur of a crowd being told to disperse when they want to remain.

I inspect the muzzle visually, then use Inspect Item on it.

Tamer’s muzzle (rare)

This muzzle is an enchanted piece of equipment that is commonly used by tamers to subdue beasts while the tamer works to break their spirits. Enchantments imbued within this muzzle are effective while it is attached to a beast’s head, and can be controlled by the tamer. The muzzle can only be removed by the tamer who is blood-bound to it.

Enchantments: Health Regeneration Control; Indestructability; Mana-Freeze; Blood-bound; Will-Sap; Shock.

When I read the description my Skill gives me, I glare at the muzzle in disgust. I can’t be certain of what all those enchantments do, but I can draw enough conclusions to harshly judge the tamer who put it on Kalanthia.

Break their spirits…no wonder Kalanthia hated me when we first met if this is the kind of taming she encountered before, I mutter darkly to myself.

Markus? Lathani is the one to reach out, though I sense the concern and curiosity of the others too. Given how much I feel her yearning to see her mother, I appreciate her remaining with the Tier four all the more. Is mother…well? Her voice is full of fear.

She’s out of danger, I assure the young nunda. She’ll be fine.

Father is agitated. I think he realises that something has happened, Lathani warns me.

He can’t come here! I tell her urgently. It will be a disaster if Kalanthia’s mate finds her unconscious with a muzzle this awful on her face. He already suspects me of abusing her – this sight will hardly change his mind. Keep him away – do anything you need to.

I’ll do my best, Lathani agrees, though she sounds a little forlorn. My heart aches for her, but I can’t say she can come to see her mother with her own eyes – her job is just too important.

I glance at Bastet.

Lathani might need help, I tell her and see her nod.

We’ll keep him out, she promises and I see her rallying our companions. The Tier ones begin chivvying the samuran hatchlings away while the others depart with Bastet. She sends me a final look over her shoulder. You help Kalanthia.

I nod back at her – hopefully a Tier three and his own cub will be enough to keep the Tier four quiet for now – and then focus on the muzzle. There’s no catch, and when I try to use my knife against it, my blade makes no mark. Not that I was expecting it to work – indestructibility is rather self-explanatory. But I had to try.

I feel the pressure settle back on my shoulders as I survey the muzzle. The lack of any enchantment linked to Kalanthia’s state of consciousness indicates that it’s something natural. For all I know, that’s the only real consequence of Bond Teleport and she’ll wake up soon. As if I wasn’t already on a timer – if Kalanthia wakes up with that muzzle on her face, I don’t want to know what she’ll do.

But how am I going to get it off?

Hunter, is Lizil anywhere near?

I know where she is – I can get her if you want.

Yes, please. I then switch channels, speaking into the Alliance Bond. Do any of you know anything about blood-bound enchantments?

There’s silence for a long moment.

They aren’t very common in Moriax, Layton ventures after a moment. I encountered two when fighting the Nethyar. Devices that only responded to the binder or his direct relatives.

Were you able to overcome the binding? I ask impatiently.

No, Layton replies, disappointing me. We just destroyed them so they couldn’t be used against us.

I breathe out a controlled exhale. Not helpful when the muzzle is ‘indestructable’.

The others don’t seem to know anything – which only supports Layton’s statement about how rare this kind of enchantment is – so I continue inspecting the muzzle with my physical and magical senses while I wait for Lizil to come.

I can sense that the mana within the muzzle is exceedingly well-contained and it utterly disregards my attempts to touch it. It’s almost like I’m trying to grasp a bar of soap in the bath – every time my grip tightens, it slips away.

My stomach drops as I feel Kalanthia begin to stir.

No! Not now!
