~ * ~

Cara picked at her food, doing her best to avoid making even the slightest sound. Any other night she wouldn't have worried, everyone made some degree of sound when they ate. But making sound would draw attention and something told her that attention would be dangerous.

Gwen was seated beside her, eating almost as quietly. Morgan separated her from Bern and Keldren took up a third of the table on his own. None of this was unusual or merited the caution. Lord Bern and Lady Phila's presence was the weight that turned a casual affair into something serious.

Cara's eyes started to drift towards Seb and Anya's table, towards the light sounds of laughter and friendship that kept drifting over. None of them were overly loud or drawing attention to themselves, but every reminder of their presence led to a deepening of Lady Phila's frown.

"Is the food usually like this?" Lord Bern asked, breaking the silence.

"Slightly more plain," Bern said. "It has never been bad but they apparently recognize that there are visitors of importance present."

"I had this exact dish last week," Gwen said. "And I had your meal a few days later."

"You did not."

"I did. The food looked and tasted exactly the same."

"Are you trying to say they did not improve their meals for the visitors?" Bern snapped.

"I am saying the meals are identical to what they always offer. You simply never bother to look at what you're getting and take the first thing available."

"At least I still eat with my family."

"I am eating with my parents, and since we are the only direct family members present you cannot be eating with your family if we are eating separately."

"Morgan-"

"Enough!" Lady Phila said icily. "You are both adults. You are siblings. You will be civil to one another and work together."

Cara felt Gwen tense next to her, the signs subtle enough she doubted anyone at the table other than perhaps Morgan would notice.

"Mother," Gwen said. "I have already agreed to attempt to Dive with Bern. With Morgan's coordinating we should be capable of working together."

"You refuse to stay in our dorm." Bern answered. "You spend all your time with her and that arrogant human."

"I spend my time with friends who respect both me and my Lifesworn. With people I trust."

"If you will not live with us then you are not-"

"Bern," Morgan said gently. "Do not draw lines at this time."

"Listen to your coordinator," Lord Bern said. "He comes from a line known for attention to detail and wisdom."

"Now you side with her?" Bern snapped. "I thought we had agreed-"

"You stated your opinion," Morgan said sharply. "I neither agreed nor disagreed."

Cara realized her eyes had met Anya's. She'd glanced away, tried to pull back from the conversation and had caught Anya looking at her. She could see that she was smiling, enjoying herself, and yet was looking at her rather than at her family.

She forced herself to look away, to focus on the table. Gwen was safe, or rather there was nothing she could do in this situation to make her safer. Dinner was not a disaster, at least not entirely. It was not pleasant, thinking back she wasn't sure it had ever truly been pleasant, merely less obviously unpleasant.

"Do you expect difficulty taking the top rankings for the year?" Lady Phila asked, her eyes focusing on her son.

"The Seer dislikes us for not being human. The Depths instructor has done nothing but lecture so there is no way of knowing."

Cara could hear the sneer in his voice as he continued.

"Dueling I expect I will win all three categories, especially if they allow full effort and use proper protection rather than force us to limit ourselves. Keldren will come second of course. Sadly I doubt my sister and her guard will do nearly so well except as part of my team."

Cara choked before she could stop herself. Technically Bern was right, neither she nor Gwen would outperform him or Keldren, alone or in pairs. But to assume they would be victorious in every category without any chance of being wrong? Last she'd heard his record was at best a draw against Anya and Seb, and that was without considering how much more dangerous they'd become.

Without restraint he was a true menace, but to be so certain? She coughed a second time, feeling every eye other than Keldren's focus on her. She had wanted to avoid attention, not become the center of it.

"Do you wish to say something?" Lady Phila asked. "Add to my son's statement perhaps?"

"No my lady, I do not wish to add anything to Bern's statement."

"Do you disagree with him?"

Cara froze. She had to answer, but if she answered she'd draw even more attention and have to deal with the fallout. But the geas would force her to answer if she hesitated too long. She could feel the compulsion building, the tension that would turn into pain if she fought it for much longer.

"I feel he underestimates Holding Syl as a pair and individually," she said carefully, the words relieving the pressure.

"Holding Syl..?"

"You met them earlier mother," Gwen said. "Anya and Seb."

"Are they skilled?" Lord Bern asked.

"Nothing Keldren or I couldn't handle if we weren't forced to hold back," Bern said. "Unlike them we are more than capable of dealing with threats."

"Of course," Lady Phila said. "They don't have the benefit of your training, nor your lineage."

Cara dug her fingers into her legs. She would not react, not show any hint of her true opinion. She kept smiling, the pain and forced pleasantness enough to prevent her hair from betraying her.

"I'll make sure to remind him of that next time Seb holds back from punching him in the nose," Gwen said as she stood. "Cara, I'm done with dinner and wish to exercise."

"Yes Mistress," Cara said as she nearly jumped to her feet.

It took everything she had not to laugh, to keep her face properly neutral as they left the table. If she smiled she would start to relax, if she relaxed her hair would show her true feelings, and that would make things worse.

~ * ~

Anya didn't turn to watch Gwen and Cara leave. She didn't need to, she'd been paying partial attention to them all night through Seb's gift and knew he had been paying even closer attention to the entire table. They hadn't eavesdropped, they weren't able to, but the tension had been obvious from the start.

Think one of us should?

No, Seb sent back. They could have walked past us to get our attention if they'd felt the need to.

"You still can be obvious you know," Sandra said.

"What?"

"You two, you're still obvious. You get caught up with one another and we might as well not even be in the room."

"We are not that-"

A trio of disagreement cut her off. Maybe they had been distracted, maybe they had been paying attention to another family squabble rather than devoting all their attention to their family. She hadn't seen her sister or Briana in a year, she hadn't said much to Fay in the last six months, now was the time for family.

"I thought we had gotten better."

"Compared to when I met you?" Fay said. "Absolutely. Compared to people who aren't having a second private conversation in public? You still slip, especially if something distracts you both at the same time."

"It's never stopped us from catching Sandra trying to steal bacon," Anya said.

"I got away with it all the time. Now that you're not cooking it's harder."

"You mean the bacon that was always hanging off the edge of the plate closest to you?" Seb asked. "That the bacon you got away with stealing?"

"The- You- You LET me?"

"He decided it was easier than constantly trying to keep you away from it." Anya clarified. "Just made sure you could steal your actual portion."

"You mean I was stealing my own bacon?"

"Sandra," Briana said. "What do you think would happen if you stole my bacon more than once?"

Anya saw her sister hesitate then flinch.

"No good coffee?"

"I don't make bad coffee."

"No coffee at all?"

"Or pastries."

"No PASTRIES?!"

"Sandra," Anya said. "You're shouting. We are in public."

She glanced over at the fae table and caught Bern glaring at them. She smiled, letting him see a glint of her teeth before adjusting her chair so there was no doubt she had her back to him.

"Who is he?" Sandra asked.

"Who?"

"The man with the bright blue eyes who looks like Gwen."

"Ignore him," Seb said. "Gwen's brother doesn't like us."

"Why?"

"Remember how mother's court felt about me?" Anya said.

"You mean he's an idiot?"

Anya coughed, not bothering to cover her smile.

"Exactly."

"Do you two like him?"

"I'd rather drink Anya's coffee," Seb answered.

Hey! she sent as the entire table, herself included, reacted in disgust. So would I, but how dare you.

I dare because I know you agree.

"One of these days I'll get it right," she grumbled.

"Or we can just all make coffee instead?" Fay suggested.

"I can't," Sandra said. "The guest quarters don't have a machine and mother refuses to let me have one."

"She's worried you'll burn the Holding down," Anya said.

"I'm not the one who burned a pot of water."

"Because no one let you near one."

Anya chuckled as Sandra settled for sticking her tongue out in response. It wasn't breakfast with coffee and bacon, but they could do that tomorrow. It wasn't dinner in a restaurant where no one knew them, but the food was good and the company better. What it was, was family.
