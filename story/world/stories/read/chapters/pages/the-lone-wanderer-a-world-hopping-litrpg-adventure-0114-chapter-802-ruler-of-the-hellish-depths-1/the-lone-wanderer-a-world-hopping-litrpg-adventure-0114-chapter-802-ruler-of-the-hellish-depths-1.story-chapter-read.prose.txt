Reinforcing his host’s soul, Percy rapidly channelled his friend’s entire supply of spiritual blood through the enriched cord, noting that the arriving mana now sported the midnight blue colour of Micky’s blessing.

The fact that the resource was even more potent didn’t really help much in this instance, because any variant of Green mana would be equally useless against a quasi-divine beast. If anything, its augmented nature only made it harder for Percy’s fragile host to withstand the flow, though he still managed to transfer the Huehuan’s reserves dozens of times faster than he had on Tenefyre.

A few hours later, his borrowed body was surrounded by fifty-five sentries: fifty fist-sized constructs created solely for reconnaissance, and five human-sized minions that possessed a modicum of fighting strength.

Percy didn’t expect any of his constructs to fight against the giant eel directly, but his plan did require a handful of decoys. He would have made them even bigger if he could have, but this was the largest size he could produce without consuming Micky’s entire stash on a single sentry.

Arranging the constructs in a spherical formation with a radius of over forty-three kilometres, he began advancing towards the depths of the alien ocean. His trip was mostly uneventful, because very few creatures on this planet were even capable of harming him. The water was full of ruby-coloured shrapnel and schools of ravenous beasts, but Percy spotted those threats long before he approached them.

Even if he allowed his sentries to get eaten, he could easily drill a hole through the marine creatures’ souls to escape. Lacking the Soul Predator mutation or a domain, the Green beasts had no way to effectively fight against any variant of soul mana – let alone one as deadly as Micky’s augmented soul-freezing ice.

The only vulnerability was Percy’s host, whose fragile body was decaying more rapidly than he would have liked. Thankfully, he was able to shield the creature from external dangers with his domain.

A few minutes later, Percy finally spotted the whirlpool that violently captured and delivered the fattened Green beasts into his target’s cavernous maw. The crimson ribbons of concentrated ruby dust illuminated the outlines of the spiralling currents, painting a stark contrast against the ink-coloured ocean of willpower permeating the water.

It was arguably a hellish scene, but he wasn’t concerned. Even if everything went horribly wrong, he’d merely lose a clone. Maybe a piece of his spatial seal too, but only if he somehow failed to send it back to Remior in time. He’d also spotted his prey long before it saw him. He was still several kilometres away from the whirlpool, so he wouldn’t be in danger until he was ready to engage.

Before attacking, there was something he had to check.

Carefully circling around the spiralling domain, he phased a handful of sentries through the seabed, scanning the area beneath the eel’s coiled body. The fact that there were three of these creatures on Ruby Heart meant that Percy wasn’t dealing with some lone mutant, but an entire species. The eels obviously had to reproduce, somehow, yet Percy hadn’t seen any others elsewhere. That only left a single spot where the younger beasts could be hiding, and it was right under their progenitor.

Sure enough, Percy spotted a vast cavern hidden beneath the sand, teeming with Orange, Yellow, and Green eels that looked extremely similar to their oversized ancestor. Their exterior was covered in dimmer but smoother scales, yet there was no question that the creatures were related.

They also appeared strangely docile. They didn’t attack one another, nor did they seem like they needed – or were allowed to – hunt their own food. The giant eel probably used its domain to redirect some of its prey into small vents scattered around its body to feed its spawn. Percy hadn’t noticed those holes before, partly because his previous visits had been short, and partly because visibility in the area had been too poor without the full power of his sensory domain.

He still had no idea whether the quasi-divine beasts reproduced asexually, mated with each other, or bred with their lesser kin, but it was quite clear why they kept the latter under constant supervision.

A generous observer might credit the benevolent eels for protecting their relatives from external threats, but someone as callous and experienced as Percy could easily see through their sinister motives: they couldn’t afford to let the smaller eels escape, lest they grew into powerful rivals.

Even with the entire ecosystem working for the quasi-divine beasts, the ocean could only support three. Letting a fourth emerge would quickly result in one’s death – either from starvation due to limited resources, or from another’s jaws.

‘Green must be the highest grade they are allowed to reach,’ Percy speculated.

The strongest among them would only have a chance to fight for their ancestor’s place when it died of old age. If they were any stronger than Green, they would eat up too much of their parent’s food. If they were any weaker, they wouldn’t be able to protect themselves against other Green creatures in the area when their protector perished.

As for what the succession would involve?

Perhaps, the victor would slaughter its male siblings and only keep its sisters around to mate with, or it might kill all of them regardless of gender and reproduce with the other giant eels or by itself. Either way, Percy was more concerned with the mechanism behind their unique mutation.

Unlike their progenitor, the smaller eels didn’t seem to secrete ruby powder of their own.

They either only unlocked the superior version of the mutation at a higher grade, or snatched it from the carcass of their deceased sire. The first powder-generating eels had obviously developed the ability without relying on others, but Percy had studied enough ecosystems to understand a thing or two about how animals tended to evolve.

It was more efficient to steal rare and valuable resources from other lifeforms than produce them from scratch. It made more sense for a newly-crowned overlord to devour and recycle the most abundant source of nutrients in its surroundings – namely, the enormous treasure trove that was the former demigod’s body.

After countless generations, the eels might have adapted to the almost-guaranteed process of inheritance, growing more reliant on building blocks they could harvest from their predecessors than wasting energy to recreate them.

At least, that was Percy’s hope.

He knew that he might be wrong, or that his familiar might fail to assimilate the eel’s gland regardless, but it wouldn’t be the end of the world even if he ended up empty-handed. The local ecosystem should be resilient enough to recover eventually, even if it took a little longer. If Percy failed to pass the mutation to Nephthys, he’d just have to look for another way to strengthen her.

‘If you’re right, should we try to upgrade our own mutations as well?’ Micky asked after listening to Percy’s explanation. ‘I’m not saying that we should kill all three of the giant eels at the same time, but we could wait for them to be replaced.’

‘I don’t think that this mutation would be very useful for the two of us,’ Percy replied, shaking his borrowed head.

As a sapient, his body would never be nearly as large as a beast’s, so the ability to generate ruby powder would be wasted on him even after he became a demigod or true deity.

Micky had a different problem. His mutation allowed him to replicate any biological function via his malleable ice mana, but that only worked within his own body. They’d already tried taking advantage of his extreme regeneration to chop a few limbs off and pass his Grey Panoply to others, but the powder harvested from his crystalline bones didn’t work like the original ruby dust.

Nephthys was closer to a regular beast than either of them.

She grew larger with each grade, and her exoskeleton contained the standard version of the exotic resource. Generating more was currently quite difficult and ill-advised, but that might change if Percy’s plan worked and she became able of secreting large quantities of the powder.

It was admittedly a bit of a longshot, but Percy would give Remior its own permanent source of ruby powder if he was right, in addition to pushing his familiar’s other mutations to fuse. This would save him the hassle of constantly sending clones to Ruby Heart, and would also secure his ability to procure the exotic resource in case the planet was discovered by a powerful faction or the eels ever went extinct. Finally, it would give him the option of selling or trading the place for something equally valuable.

He did feel a little guilty that he was planning to kill a creature no less intelligent than a person, but the giant eels weren’t exactly innocent. They had practically enslaved every other animal in the ocean and devoured tens of thousands of them per day to remain alive.

Percy wasn’t hypocritical enough to pretend that he was doing this out of kindness toward the local wildlife or that he hadn’t slaughtered countless beasts himself, but it was a fact that killing the monster would offer a morsel of relief to millions of other creatures for centuries to come.

Steeling his resolve, he retrieved his sentries from the cavern and arranged them in a loose formation around his host once more. His temporary vessel wouldn’t last much longer, and the Great Houses might attack any day now, so there was no time for hesitation or misplaced pity. The eel only did what it did to survive, but the same could be said about Percy.

It was time for him to challenge his most powerful opponent to date – a being at the very peak of the Colour realm!
