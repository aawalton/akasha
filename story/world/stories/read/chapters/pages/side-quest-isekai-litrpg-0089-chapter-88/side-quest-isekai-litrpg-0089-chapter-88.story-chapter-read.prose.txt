Logan gripped one rung, then advanced to the next. His fingers buzzed with numbing discomfort, but he bit his lip and reached for another.

This isn’t so bad.

On reaching the third rung and stretching for the fourth, his grip gave out, and he fell flat on his back.

“Oof!” His grip should have held, given his Climbing perk. Rubbing the back of his head, he double-checked the description on it and sighed at himself. It only improved his grip while he was stationary, which was impossible if he was climbing. He frowned at Nugget. “Well, this might be a little more difficult.”

On his next attempt, he went only two rungs up. When his grip started to slip, he just stopped moving. His hands still tingled, but his grip held.

Even with the pause, though, when he moved to ascend, he still couldn’t get past the fourth rung. And there were probably at least 100 rungs on this thing.

Nugget whirled lazily around the ladder, chirping. A feeling of encouragement warmed Logan’s chest. If Nugget wasn’t ready to give up, neither was he.

Logan folded one arm across his chest and tapped his chin with the other hand. The Vicelock perk wasn’t enough on its own… and trying to Null Pulse the entire distance would do more damage than good if he smashed into the ceiling… but what if he used the two in combination?

If he used Null Pulse to cover short distances of only a few rungs at a time, he could catch himself with the ladder and just stay in place.

He got back to the third rung, then tested it.

Null Pulse!

He barely engaged the skill, but it still propelled him up from where his feet connected to the lower rung. As he advanced a distance of five rungs up, his feet slapped against each rung they passed like the ticker on the Wheel of Fortune wheel until he came to a stop at eye level with Nugget.

“Ow.”

It actually hadn’t hurt, thanks to Pain Resistance. The bigger concern was the noise echoing through the empty temple. Nugget didn’t seem bothered, though, and gently bumped noses with Logan through the open space between rungs before flitting up another small distance.

With every blast up, Logan covered four or five rungs, and Nugget chirped happily before surging upward and waiting for Logan at the next spot.

Even though Logan was only using Null Pulse intermittently, it was still a heavy drain on his mana. He had to pause at the three-quarter mark and meditate to let it recover. Falling from this height was not an idea he cared much for, no matter what his pain resistance was.

As he neared the top, if Null Pulse acted like an accelerator, he just barely tapped on it to cover the last two rungs. Then, with his hands firmly gripping the upper platforms of the ceiling grid, and notably not on the slick ladder rungs, Logan hoisted himself up.

He peeked down at the covered distance and swallowed. He was a lot higher up than he thought. But there was nothing to do about that.

Alrighty then. From up here, he had a clear view of everything above the paneled ceilings.

Once upon a time, Logan had worked a construction gig. Surprisingly, he had really liked that job. There was something beautiful about looking out over building skeletons with fresh wood and seeing the sunlight drifting in on sawdust above the rafters.

The problem was, he got so excited to get up there and see the view that he’d inevitably leave crucial tools down on the ground. And then when he’d go down to grab them, he’d leave something else on the ground too. At the end of the day, he’d have the same problem, but with leaving things up above.

His boss at that job didn’t find it amusing, and thought Logan was trying to eat up time on the clock to run back and forth. Even though Logan had offered to knock off an hour for each shift, his boss figured Logan was scheming something and so had fired him.

Well, that was all before Logan had a handy inventory system. He checked his storage, just to confirm he did, in fact, have the replacement mechanism. Good thing he did, because now that he saw how high up he actually was, he didn’t think he’d want to make another trip up here on the ladder.

Unlike any kind of modern Earth building, there was no ductwork or sprinkler systems or electrical wiring.

There were, however, a surprising amount of pipes and ropes. The pipes all interconnected and spread from the ceiling, then diverted down and out to the far ends of the otherwise open attic space. He had seen gargoyle-like statues outside the cathedral, and he suspected these were drainage systems leading to those. As for the ropes, that was an easy one to figure out because he had already seen Brother Timmons tug the rope to open the panel he currently stood above.

He admired the gothic vaulting for a moment and smiled as Nugget walked along the wooden rafters as if he were a tightrope walker.

“Okay, Nugget. Shall we get this over with?”

Just as Lambert had shown him, there was a latch where the triangular assembly fit into a frame. Logan lifted the latch and pulled the old one out. Part of it started to unfold prematurely, and he had to scramble to grab it from the center rather than the outside so it didn’t unfold and drop through the ceiling. That would have been a headache.

He then took out the replacement, wiggled it into place, and closed the latch.

With the assembly secured, he took a deep breath and admired his work. Aside from the whole ladder-of-death thing, it had actually been pretty simple after all.

As he examined his handiwork, though, something caught his eye. Some of the wood around the latch looked like it had been eaten through. It didn’t look rotted or anything, but as if someone had intentionally drilled a thin channel along the edge. He followed the path with his eyes, and it wrapped all around the entire assembly, plus a rafter that extended further back.

Lambert had asked him to look out for anything strange that might be responsible for the continued breakdowns of the assembly.

Unrelenting Hunt.

His sight honed in on the wormhole-like channel. It disappeared behind a network of piping.

He shared a look with Nugget. “Guess it’s my turn to try tightrope walking. I hope it’s as easy as you made it look.”

Carefully, he rose to his feet and began treading along the widest rafter. Mindful to plant each step with care, he kept his arms spread out for better balance. Nugget hopped parallel to him, two rafters over.

The trail of half-eaten wood continued from one panel to another, zigzagging as it jumped from one edge, rounded a corner, then shifted to a different panel. Occasionally Logan passed service openings in the ceiling, where more of the light-blue ladders led up. The trail led him past all these, though.

As Logan walked through a sunbeam, the combination of bright light and thick layers of sawdust drifting in the air finally got to him, and he sneezed. He waved his arms wildly to each side to keep himself upright.

Nugget paused and cocked his head at Logan, ears low and ready to jump if Logan needed support. “I’m fine, bud. It’s just dusty is all.”

As he neared the last panel where the damaged wood led, the ceiling once again opened up. But when he reached it and peeked down, he realized this wasn’t just a service opening like with all the ladders. No, he thought he recognized this spot, but it took him a second to make sense of what he was looking at from this angle.

He glanced back up at the far wall, and then behind him to where the centerpoint of the star-shaped temple was. Sure enough, he was about halfway between the two spots. When he looked back down, it clicked. This was where the glass casing displayed the three fancy-looking casket boxes of rare books, one of which was allegedly titled The Shrine of the Living Path.

The runnel worn into the wood that Logan was tracking took a sharp turn, descending into the display case’s enclosure.

Logan hesitated at the scaffolding above the open space.

It was a pretty thin space to wedge into. He patted his belly. He had trimmed down since arriving in this place. Had it been his first few days here, he definitely wouldn’t have fit. Now, though… he might actually be able to slip in there.

Nugget leaped over and chirped before landing on the rafter and dipping his head below the ceiling level, pointing with his snout down.

Logan followed where the dragon pointed and then chuckled wryly. “Of course.”

The golden glow from his bond with Nugget shone from somewhere below. At his birdseye-view, whatever this top book’s casket was obstructed his view from what was down there, but if he had to guess, it was none other than the Casket Box of The Shrine of the Living Path.

Brother Timmons had said that the actual books were kept in a separate vault. Perhaps, though, this casket box held a clue that might lead Logan to it?

He lowered himself until he sat on the scaffold, and then dipped his legs down into the display area. His thighs pressed as much against the glass front of the display as they did against the ribbed wooden backing. At least the patterning on the wood gave him better handholds than the ladder did.

Nugget waited above, although it looked like he wanted to join Logan. The dragon sent an apologetic phantom feeling of cramped wings to Logan.

“You’re fine, Nugget. It’s hard enough for me to maneuver. I’ll be in and out, so don’t worry about it.”

Descending involved more pushing himself down than it did climbing. He knew spelunkers sometimes did that when they were in tight spaces, but that thought made him feel claustrophobic, so he ignored it. He couldn’t really maneuver enough to track the channel carved into the wood—heck, he couldn’t even look over his shoulder—but he figured it was no coincidence that the golden bond glow was getting stronger the lower he went. So, he just judged his closeness to whatever he needed to get to based on the intensity of the golden light.

Unsurprisingly, he finally reached eye level with none other than the Casket Box of The Shrine of the Living Path. Equally expected, the glow was strongest at the casket’s latch. What was slightly unexpected was that the damage to the wood stopped right where that clasp met the mount suspending it on the wall. As Logan looked closer, part of the clasp even appeared to have a little worn channel in it.

Logan grew a little uneasy. Perhaps he should have considered that there might something caustic sealed away down here before getting himself into a position where he couldn’t get away quicky.

Then again, the box was only as big as a book. A big book, sure, but nothing larger than Logan’s torso. Besides, there was nothing actively leaking out from the casket box. And clearly this was important.

He kept going back and forth in his mind. Part of him itched with curiosity and wanted to pry the box open. That would involve him finagling himself a little bit lower so he could twist his wrist within the tight confines of the glass display case.

But the other part of him kept replaying the definition of luck as it applied in this world. Even if the System was nudging him towards opportunity, that didn’t mean it was promising him safety. He could always work his way back up to the ceiling and figure out a better way to open up that box.

As he teetered between what to do, the loud boom of a bell tolling caught him by surprise. He struck his head on the back of the glass casing. He craned his neck as best he could, but it was barely enough for him to glimpse his surroundings.

What he did see were two things. First, that the door of the temple had just opened and a crowd of about thirty monks were processing into the temple. Second, that when his head jerked back, his ear caught the clasp, and revealed exactly what mystery the casket box held.
