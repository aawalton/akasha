Henry had to keep the parasite busy, and he had to do it while keeping his distance. That ruled out a significant number of skills that worked best when he made contact. But it also opened up a few avenues. Instead of latching on to it—or to its puppet, which he wasn’t forgetting about—Henry had to make the most out of his clones and toxins. Starting with his own personal brew.

With the B-rank upgrade of Bite, Henry had gotten his own magical venom. It’d allow him some sustain, sapping the parasite out of its mana and life-force, while simultaneously weakening it so it’d be more susceptible to whatever else he’d throw its way. At least, that’s how it technically should work. But the difference in levels might make things difficult. And the venom was the most expensive in his arsenal to produce, so he didn’t want to waste it on Toxic Rays that would be countered with a shield.

“[Maurice, we’re sticking to range battle. I really don’t want our real bodies to get close to this thing.]”

“[I agree. It feels awful. Like feeling a breeze in your ear after a stinky dog licks the inside of it. But in your soul,]” Maurice grumbled, his face cringing in disgust as he continued peppering the parasite and its pearly shell with mundane bubbles, most likely to keep it busy. Unlike Henry, it seemed that Maurice was in no hurry to shed his human appearance. As for what he said, Henry had no words. He had questions, but he also had bigger fish to fry.

“[Let’s put a pin in that. First, I’m going to inject some venom, both on this guy and the big one below. I’m expecting the latter to be much easier, but if that bastard sees me doing it, it might not let me give it its own dose, so I’ll work on that first. I’m thinking… keep it busy while I sneak a couple of Octominds from behind.]”

Henry didn’t expect his simple clones to be capable of administering a good Bite on either A-ranks. But he couldn’t get there himself either, so the best option would be two of the summoned, empowered Octominds and once he tagged the parasite, he’d have time to do the same to the starfish though hopefully, the latter wouldn’t require multiple Bites. ]

At least it’d be impossible to miss, Henry thought, grimly. It was a lot larger than the much smaller and much more dangerous parasite, and there was no way the creature floating in front of them could keep it fully protected.

Cloaking two of his Octominds with Absolute Camouflage along with a good dose of Thaumaturgy to empower their existence, he sent them forward. He considered making a gestalt, but he didn’t want to risk them getting destroyed and lose their manifestation. Losing one Octomind was one thing. Lost a gestalt of three or four was another.

In any case, these two should be able to score a hit, so long as he created a good opening for them. Each were readying a powerful Bite, which he’d also empower with Predation for maximum efficiency, and as his minions moved forward through the exploding geysers of water, Henry spoke to Maurice while at the same time, a fifth of a mile away, his clones discretely pulled the sailors who had passed out up toward those who hadn’t, so the latter could get them away from here.

“[Maurice, if we’re out of here depth here, we’re leaving. Let’s say… if it manages to take control of either of us or our limbs for even half a second, we book it. Deal?]”

Henry wasn’t making the same mistake he’d done with the seahorse, especially not against something as dangerous as this, and thankfully, it didn’t seem like the parasite could outright take over their bodies. It needed to latch on to them, and it needed to bypass their resistance. It’d do that sooner or later if they stuck around, but neither of them were going to give it a chance. But, plans go awry, so if they slipped up, they were leaving.

No more suicidal last ditch efforts. Henry wasn’t walking away with debilitating injuries or worse, losing control of himself to this thing. He’d live to fight another day and all that.

“[Deal! But I think we’re probably okay. If it could have taken us over, it would have already! Right?]”

Henry had reached the same conclusion, but he still winced at Maurice’s words. If the universe was listening, this might be the time it’d slap some well-timed misfortune to punish that over-confidence, which was why Henry decided to peer through his A-rank-retrieving Octomind to get an update, only for alarms to blast his ears as his envoy approached the half-demolished elevator shaft. A moment later, he noted the eerie presence that wafted off the dark depths. One he was growing familiar with.

Oh, he managed, horror crawling up his throat. Oh no…

Henry snapped his attention away, his guts squirming, and with a nudge of Telepathic Orchestration, both of himself and Maurice activated their dispelling skill, empowering them with an Aspect each to burn the pale puppeteer thread slithering their way.

“[Velistraine and Attal might be dealing with another problem. We might be on our own for a minute,]” Henry managed, still trying to piece together what might be happening down there. He did not like the presence he had felt. Had they freed the thing down there to run some test and it overpowered them… or did it manage to get reinforcements, somehow?

That felt too much for a B-rank, Henry thought, his stomach gnawing at him.

With his clone surrounding the area, Henry peppered the parasite with Toxic Rays, shooting them from multiple floating and submerged clones. Some of rays had an extra punch to them, thanks to them being cast by his remaining Octominds, but most were countered by yet another ability the parasite pulled out of its behind. The pearly, damaged shield was still around but now, a glowing, starfish-shaped apparition flashed around it, much larger than it was, like some sort of blue arcane body. The form shuddered and broke as the rays hit it, and while it was not as resilient as the pearly barrier had been, it was regenerating much faster, which was bad news for his approaching Octominds.

Henry had to figure out a way of getting through it.

No, Attal and Velistraine couldn’t have been overpowered by that thing… something else happened, Henry thought, his mind still partially digesting what he’d felt through his clone. He was doing his best at keeping the parasite busy to buy time, but then, he recalled the handful of instances where he had felt himself being watched.

Henry felt cold for a moment, even though the sun was bearing on all of them. Had something beyond the System’s barrier managed to peer through because of their meddling with its spawn? Had that creature in Attal’s laboratory been really isolated, or had it been an act? Or had they somehow weakened the barrier by experimenting with it?

Henry sent his Octomind down the shaft and focused on the enemy at hand. His two Bite-ready Octominds were in position, but he had to give them some sort of opening or they’d waste their attack on the ship-sized arcane body that encased the parasite.

So after hesitating for only a brief moment, Henry began dismissing the edges of his network—so the clones in Thalis—and re-summoned them here. Using all of his present clones and Octominds, he pulled more mana to him though Siphoning Touch as faster and faster, the network to Thalis was subsumed to inflate his numbers here. It felt slow, but with the multiple minds aiding in his efforts, it only took seconds for the number of his surrounding clones to double once, then to double once more a few seconds later at which point Henry was forced to down a Lifeblood Orb to recover some mana.

But it was worth it. Because right now, there were nearly three hundreds and fifty clones around, watching the parasite, filling the water and the air around the creature like a swarm.

“Whoah,” Maurice exclaimed, but Henry was too busy coordinating all of his clones. It was… surprisingly easy to orchestrate all of their action. But it did take all of his attention. Less than a second after they all appeared, the world lit up with a rainbow of colors, most of which were purple rays of corrosive toxins, and from a distance, it looked as if the large arcane body was suddenly pin-cushioned with nearly three hundred pale needles.

It had been an expensive offense, and Henry was forced to down another Lifeblood Orb, cutting even more into his emergency reserves, but it bore fruit. The massive arcane body trembled and shuddered before popping like a balloon, and even its inner shell had been damaged. The quality of the rays might not have been Henry’s best, but the quantity made up for it. The parasite let out a keening, angered wail as its small tendrils shook with fury. But a beat later it stopped when out of thin air, two Octominds, glowing with Predator red and Thaumaturgy blue appeared above it, arms spread and black beaks gleaming dangerously in the sunlight for a brief instant before their limbs whipped out and wrapped around the parasite.

Henry felt it when the parasite’s thread sank through his Octominds. He felt his control over their summoned form slip. But that happened after they’d bit into the surprisingly tough flesh and injected Henry’s toxin in its body. Tough or not, the combination of Predation and his extremely sharp beak were hard to resist. Especially considering that this thing didn’t seem to be specialized in taking hits. Still, it was a good thing he’d opted to keep his distance.

At least it doesn’t seem to be fast. And please work. Please, he prayed. With a flex, Henry undid the two captured Octominds’ manifestation, and had the pleasure of seeing them dissolve away into motes. They only left an octopus-shaped bundle of parasitic tissue that slowly receded back into the creature’s limb as it turned its deadly attention back to him.

Letting out a slow breath, Henry swallowed. He couldn’t relax. Even when he felt the rush of power and mana slip out of the creature and into him.

This fight was just getting started. But step one was done.
