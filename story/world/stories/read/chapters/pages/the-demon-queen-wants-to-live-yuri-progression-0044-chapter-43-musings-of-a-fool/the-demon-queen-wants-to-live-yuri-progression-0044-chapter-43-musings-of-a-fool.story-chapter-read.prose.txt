The room was still empty when I returned. There was no Hero waiting on the other end of the door. That was fine. She could do what she wished with her time. I stepped inside and set the satchel on the bed. I checked the egg. It was whole, just as it had been the last time I checked it. I set it down on the pillow where it belonged.

“It took longer than it should have,” I told it, or her, or him. I did not know what the thing inside was. “I apologize.”

The book I had brought back with me was nestled in the crook of my arm.  It had been surprisingly easy to stow it away. I placed it on the table between the one bed and the remains of the other and stepped back. The cover glowed in the light of the lantern. Zarvok's sigil was a simple one. Even the horns barely looked like horns. He had always preferred to chisel them himself.

The room was empty and quiet. There was no second heartbeat, no second sound of breathing. It was just me, the egg, and the book. A Queen did not require company. Especially not the company of a Hero who could not so much as find her way back to her own room after a simple measurement ceremony. Where was the fool?

I sat on the bed and looked at the far wall. I looked at the book on the table. I looked at the far wall again. I had to force myself to look there.

This should not have bothered me so. The book I had recovered was the collected writings of a general I had trusted with nothing that required subtlety or deeper thinking. A man whom I had to explain everything to twice, and then a third time just to be sure. A man who could turn words into useless sentences, and useless sentences into needless paragraphs. A man whose tactical assessments I had overruled nine times out of ten, who had once gotten his own warhammer stuck in a crevasse while attempting to tame a Hellstag. These would be the musings of a fool. There was no reason to read them, and every reason to sleep instead.

An aching came, and for the first time today, it came from somewhere besides my core. I reached towards the egg and adjusted the cloth around it, making sure it was secure. It was. I had already checked. It pulsed once. I pulled my hand back and looked back at the book.

"This is…merely to confirm its worthlessness. That’s all," I said to the empty room. I moved towards the book and opened it. The entries were exactly what I had expected.

There were only two words to describe Zarvok's handwriting. Massive and excessive, just as the demon himself had been. Each letter took up space enough for three, with curving letters that served no purpose at all save for making his writing difficult to read.  Even with the book restored, I almost could not read it at all. To attempt to do it anyway would be nothing short of a waste of time. I tried anyway.

The border skirmish at Zel'shar was a feat they will sing songs about. The enemy -a pitifull and meager host of perhaps three hundred- broke upon our lines as waves upon the Endless clifs. I, Zarvok, The Red Giant of the Kraghul, led the charge myself. The sound my hammer made was, I venture to say, the final cord of a great symfony, if that symfony were composed intirely of screaming. Her Royal Darkness would be proud.

What I read was almost enough for me to close the book and never open it again. Zel’shar had been the location of a small border skirmish against the elves. One of many. It was the first mission I had assigned solely to Zarvok. Zarvok had asked for the chance to distinguish himself, and distinguish himself he had. He had been tame in his boasting to me. He had not been tame on the page. If I had known, then we would have had words. Strict words.

I read more entries. It was almost impressive how many things my General had gotten wrong at the start. He misspelled "reconnaissance" on the next page. Then again two pages later, differently. Then a third time, more differently still. It was almost an achievement.

Was this how all of these entries were going to be? Knowing what I knew of Zarvok, the answer was obvious. I should not have wasted the specks of Essence it had taken to recover the journal. I sighed and read on, and even I did not know why I did. At some point, the bluster faded. Zarvok spoke of his achievements more seldomly, and when he did, they were described almost clinically.

Then, he began to name the dead. The soldiers who fell in every skirmish, great or small. He wrote small details about them. One had carved his lover’s name into his shield. Another had hummed a tune before every battle -Zarvok described this tune poorly, and so I could only guess. Another had a great penchant for lighting himself on fire with magic. Apparently, this was supposed to be some type of trick to entertain the others. Zarvok wrote of these and more, often in far more detail than he used to describe the battles themselves.

Twelve fell today. I will list them here, for they fought well, and it costs me nothing but ink to honnor their memmory, and what is ink to a man who has so much of it?

He had turned this journal into a record of the dead that nobody had asked for. One I had never asked him to make. I turned the page. The entries changed again. These were all about the other Generals. Zarvok spoke of them with more warmth than I had thought him capable of feeling.

I hesitated. I did not know why I hesitated. These were just the entries of my Generals, all of whom I knew well. I stilled my hands and read the first entry. It was about Malrath, the one we all called the Great Boaster. The one whose voice had always been the loudest in any hall.

Malrath spoke to an empty hall today as though a thousand knelt before him. I watched him from the doorway. His voice was loud, such that it carried outside of the hall. I watched from the doorway. He did not see me, or if he did, he did not care. He gave that same speech again, later, to a large gathering of watchful imps. He looked relieved when the speech ended, and when they applauded him.

The next was about Orzathiel. I only noticed now, but the letters here were scrawled more carefully, in smaller sizes. Ones that almost fit on the page they were meant for. I did not know the care it would take for a Demon of his size to write so.

Orzathiel tends the garden still. I asked him once who it was for. He did not answer. The flowers are lethal to all who touch them. Even to me. Even to Zera. Even to him. All save one. I do not think she knows. I do not think he would want her to.

I read the passage thrice. The second time was slower than the first. By the third, the page was clenched in my fingers.

He made the garden after the stone halls fell. Before them, he had not been that kind of demon. I do not think anyone told him to start. I do not know why. He does not answer when I ask.

Orzathiel had started his garden after the Dwarves, then. I knew this, of course. Had always known this. I turned more pages and my hands were steady. They had no reason not to be.

Then, he spoke of Zera, his sister. The letters here were more careful still. They did not even have the curving flourishes they did before.

My sister found another one today and this time it was a creature so wretched and strange that it defies the language I have at my disposal, and I have much of it. I do not know where she finds these beasts. Perhaps even the System does not know. She placed it in front of the Queen’s chambers as she always does now. The Queen pretended not to see it and my sister pretended the Queen had not pretended.

My jaw tightened. Zera’s small rebellions had grown more and more bold, the more years we had spent together. Always I had considered tightening her leash. Always there had been a reason not to. I read the section again, and understood it no more than I had the first time. Or I pretended not to, at least.

There were other entries to Generals I had lost before the others. The ones I should not have lost. I did not read theirs, though I knew not why. The lantern flickered. I had been reading for some time.

The entries changed again. These were shorter. Some were barely a line. They were scattered between campaign notes and complaints about food, or the weather or any other mild inconvenience my General had gone through. They were about me.

The Queen gave the order today. Her voice did not shake. Her hands did afterwards. I do not think she knows they do this.

The words were simple and easy to understand, even in Zarvok's writing. I still stared at them. I still reread them, again, and then again. My General was either a fool, or a liar. He was probably both. My hands did not shake, nor had they ever shaken. I had never- I was the Demon Queen, and my hands did not-

Something past my chest ached. A sharp pain radiated out from me, stealing the breath from me. I pressed a hand against my chest and forced myself to breathe, slowly. It took some time for the pain to finally pass. I turned the page again when it did.

She stood at the glass again tonight. The battle ended at the fourth bell. She did not move from the window until the ninth.

The next entry was about a campaign. The one after that was about a siege. Both times, Zarvok spent most of his words on naming the dead. I read them all with more care than any of them warranted. I turned the page again. There was another entry about me. This one was longer than the last two had been, but only just.

The Queen has not eaten since the executions. When I press, she only says the new food displeases her. That we need to find more chefs.  I do not understand. The food has not changed.

Then, much later:

I saw the Queen in the eastern corridor today. She paused at the passage that leads to Orzathiel's garden. She stood there. She did not notice me, though she always does. Then she turned and left. She still did not notice me.

“You…bastard,” I whispered. I had always thought that I saw and heard everything that had ever happened in my fortress. That I knew the lives of all those who walked within it. That I kept an eye on them even as the Gods kept an eye on me. I was wrong.

I turned the pages again, and now he talked about himself. He had stood guard outside my chambers on nights he was not assigned. Not that anyone ever was, as I had never needed protection.

I could not sleep. The corridor outside the Queen's chambers is quieter than most. I stood there until dawn. The Queen did not emerge. I left before she could see.

Then, he spoke of the nights where I could not sleep. The nights where I traveled the length of my fortress.

I followed the Queen tonight. She always walks, on the days after a great battle. After the losses, great or small. On these nights she does not notice me, though she knows most things. Her path is always the same. She stops outside the doors of all of her Generals. Those who live. And those who do not. She lingers outside theirs longest. Mine she passes third, and this I consider a great honor. She always ends at Zera's beast hall. I have not found the courage to follow her inside.

I set the book down entirely. It was some time before I picked it back up, and even then, I hesitated to flip the page. The next entry was about the Dwarven campaign. The final Dwarven campaign, the one that had brought an end to their civilization. Every other campaign had warranted my General's full poetic judgement. This one was only a few lines. Amidst these lines, he wrote about me.

The Queen has not eaten in three days. She says it is because the food displeases her. The food has not changed.

The entries that followed were fragments scattered between other things. I almost missed them. Perhaps I should have missed them.

Orzathiel was in his garden when I passed this morning. I passed by him and he did not look up. He asked me if I had looked in on her. I told him I had. He nodded and returned to his black flowers.

Malrath entered my chambers not long after the campaign briefing. It is one of the rare times he has spoken to me without a crowd to watch. His usual bluster was there across his face. But when he spoke, his voice had none of it. He asked me if she had eaten today. She has not.

The pain came again. Once again, it came from a place beneath my chest and spread outward. It spread until it reached my throat. It stayed there. I just read. I knew what I was reading, though I wished I did not.

It seemed they had known. All of them had known more than I had ever imagined they could have. My Generals had spoken to each other in half-sentences that never quite said what they meant. This was a service I had never asked of any of them. My hands were trembling. My vision was blurring. I had to force myself to read further.

The next entry was written most carefully of all. More than any other I had seen in this journal. It was written with letters that did not needlessly connect to one another. It was the first time I found his writing adequate.

Malcis fell to the void today. Zera had to slay him herself. She struck clean. It was a mercy to him. It was not a mercy to her. My sister's face was stone. The Queen entered the chambers afterward. She ordered everyone out, everyone but Zera. Her voice was iron, as it always was, and nobody questioned it. Then the Queen left as well, leaving Zera alone.

I was standing in the corridor outside, though I should not have been. She walked past me without seeing, or without caring. She pressed her back against the wall. I looked back through the closing door. Zera was alone. Her face had an expression I had never seen on her before. She wept. I had never seen that before either.

The Queen's hands shook for the rest of that day. I have seen the Queen do many things I cannot explain. This is the first I do not wish to.

I closed my eyes. The pain in my throat had only grown. Before, I might not have been able to speak to it. Now, even breathing through it proved a challenge. I breathed anyway and slowly opened my eyes. The next entry was about the reports. It was an entry I had expected to see near the very beginning, but I found it here near the end. General Zarvok had many roles in my forces. One of them, the one I had forced onto him, had been the reports.

The Queen demands another account, this time of our daily mana crystal expenses. I do not know what purpose these serve anymore. We would not run out of mana crystals even if we fired them from our cannons. Perhaps she feeds these reports to her hearth. It does not matter. The writing is the task, and I will give them all the effort they deserve, even if no eyes but mine ever see them.

He had thought them punishment for a general who was too poetic. An order given by a Demon Queen to her General was not an order he could refuse, chain or otherwise. It was an order that forced him to use the words he so loved. This journal existed because I had given him the excuse to write. I had not thought he would stretch my permission this far. If he was still alive, I would berate him for it. Perhaps…perhaps I would not.

I flipped the page, though there were few of those left. I had almost read through the entirety of the journal. It must have been hours. It felt longer. Ash had still not returned from wherever she had gone to. I kept reading. I kept reading until the only light that came was from the dim lantern. There were more entries about me.

Tonight, my walk took me down the halls. I found the Queen in Echidna's chambers. She was sitting on the stone floor.

The Hydra was curled around her. The Queen was speaking to it. Her hands traced its many scales. I could not hear what she said. I felt no spell. I did not know she could speak so softly. I did not know she could touch something so softly either.

I left before she could know I was there. I should not have seen what I saw tonight. I will not write of this again. Why did I write of this at all?

My face was very warm, though the room was not. It seemed I saw less in my fortress than I would have liked. It seemed those below me always had a way of wandering where they were not wanted. If the man had still been alive, I would have executed him on the spot. I tried to close the book again. My hand rebelled. I tried to quash the rebellion, and failed.

I turned the page again. It was dated to the day before the Hero's party breached my fortress.

The others do not see it. Perhaps they only choose not to. The Queen has been preparing for something, and I have watched her too long to miss it. She has been preparing for a long time. I do not know what comes tomorrow, only that it comes.

Orzathiel tends the garden. Zera has gathered her creatures close. Malrath practiced his lines today, for the last time, I think. Even Seryth has mingled with us freely today, though she never has before. Yes, perhaps they do only choose not to.

I have served the greatest Queen this world has known. She will not hear this from my lips and I think it would only burden her if it could. She carries too much already. She has never set it down. But I wish her to know, if these words should ever find her, that we-.

I turned the page. It was blank. I turned another, and it was blank too. I turned every remaining page in the journal, one after another, and they were all blank, and they would always be blank. The man who filled them had died soon after. I stared at the last entry for a long time. Until the words blurred into each other.

"You stupid fool." The words came out quieter than I had intended. I barely heard them over the sound of something I did not recognize at first. It was coming from me. "All of you were such fools."

By the end, I did not recognize the sound of my own voice. My core hummed with the same pain that had been there since I had made a third thing inside me. The humming became a grinding inside me that would not stop.

A spot fell onto the page. Then another. Then another. They covered some of the letters. I stared at them for a moment before I understood where they had come from. I wiped my eyes. It did not help. I wiped them again. I tried to read the unfinished sentence one more time, because surely I had missed something, surely there were more words if I only looked hard enough-

There was a sound. The door, perhaps, or footsteps on the stone. It came from very far away. I did not look up. Warmth settled behind me. I might have mistaken it for the hum, had it not been so familiar. Arms wrapped around me from behind. I stiffened. The arms did not let go.

"Are you okay?" Ash's voice was quiet, from somewhere just behind my shoulder.

I did not answer. I could not. Ash was very warm. I did not care that she was late. Only that she was now here.
