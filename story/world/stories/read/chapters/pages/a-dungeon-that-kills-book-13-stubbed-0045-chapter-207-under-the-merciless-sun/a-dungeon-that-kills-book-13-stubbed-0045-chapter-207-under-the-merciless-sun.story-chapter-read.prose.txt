The five remaining members of the Iskora team had trudged their way to the edge of the desert.

After the initial strike, Viktor did not launch any further attacks. After all, surprise was a coin he could only spend once. His enemies now knew what he was capable of, which meant repeating the same trick would accomplish little beyond wasting resources he would rather not squander. So he held his forces back and let the party stroll through the rest of the first floor without so much as a skeleton to cross their path.

Yet, that very absence of conflict carried a cruelty of its own, one that required no effort on his part. For after an opening exchange so brutal it had killed one of their own outright, the sudden and total calm that followed proved to unnerve the survivors far more than any second explosion ever could. They grew restless. They flinched at any faint sound. They imagined enemies lurking in every shadow. The psychological toll mounted steadily, and Viktor found it almost amusing that he didn’t need to lift a finger to inflict it. But of course, this only worked on the three normal adventurers. Neither of the two monsters wearing human skin seemed troubled by any of it.

When the party reached the second floor, they found the chamber under the staircase standing empty, its resident Cyclops long gone. Viktor had ordered the creature to withdraw, obviously, as he didn’t want to waste a perfectly good monster for no meaningful return. And so, after a trek through a cave flat and stretched thin, the Iskora team had at last arrived at the entrance to Khenemhotep’s kingdom of sand.

“Why does it have to be a bloody desert?” Valeska muttered, wiping at the sweat that had already begun streaming freely down her face.

“Stop complaining,” Xander said, glancing at the great tomb looming in the distance. “That’s our destination. Let’s go.”

The oppressive heat of this domain, thick enough to make the very air shimmer, appeared to have no effect whatsoever on the bastard. Another perk of being a ghost full-time, Viktor supposed.

Valeska turned to Rowan. “How’s your hand?” she asked, a flicker of concern crossing her hardened features.

The former soldier let out a long breath. “...I don’t know. It feels strange, like it doesn’t quite belong to me, but...” He drew his sword with his dead hand, testing its grip with a few tentative slashes through empty air. “I think I’ll do alright.”

Still, all three of them, him and the two women both, nonetheless spared a wary glance for the hooded figure standing behind them, the way one might eye a snake that hadn’t bitten anyone yet.

“If you have concerns about the aesthetic aspect of that hand,” the Apostle said, his voice carrying that same unsettling nonchalance as before, “then I can see to that later, once we are done here. I can make it match your other one close enough that no one would ever notice. But of course, that will have to wait until after we come back out of the dungeon.”

Come back, huh? Viktor chuckled. Funny thing to promise, considering the fate that had already been decided for these people the moment their usefulness finally ran out.

At the head of the formation, Xander threw back an impatient glance.

“Are you lot done?”

The three adventurers traded glances and then, with resigned shrugs and heavy sighs, they forced their legs to step forward once more. And so, the party resumed their march, pressing into the scorched sand ahead.

[What should we do next, Master?]

“What else?” Viktor said with a chuckle. “Have Khenemhotep’s minions attack them relentlessly the entire way to the great tomb, of course.”

He had no desire to waste his valuable resources on meaningless attacks, yes, but the ancient priest’s raised undead had cost him practically nothing, so there was little reason to let them sit idle. Besides, the deployment for the interior of the tomb had already been assigned to someone else, which meant failing to make use of those bones out here would amount to a waste in and of itself. And then, there was also the added benefit of forcing his enemies to fight and fight again under a merciless sun, wearing them down bit by bit before they could ever reach whatever waited for them at the tomb.

And sure enough, the moment the adventurers reached the first cluster of sun-bleached ruins, they immediately found themselves swarmed by skeleton warriors, who came pouring out from behind toppled columns and half-buried walls.

“Bloody hell!” Rowan spat out a curse. “These things again?”

“Be careful,” Valeska said. “There might be an exploding one mixed in among them, just like the last time. Cynthia, if you see anything at all suspicious, do not hesitate. Blast it away immediately with your wind. Do not let it come near any one of us.”

“Got it,” the aeromancer replied.

The two warriors crashed into the skeletons’ ranks. Rowan slammed the full weight of his tower shield into the leading skeleton, staggering it backward, before following through with a clean swing of his sword that cut down another in one fluid motion. Valeska, meanwhile, let out a fierce roar as she brought her great axe around in a wide, sweeping arc, cleaving through three skeletons simultaneously and sending fragments of bone flying in a glittering spray.

Cynthia hung back, her ashwood staff raised and ready, her eyes darting from one position to the next across the battlefield as she searched for any sign of the danger her companion had warned her of. And then, those eyes went wide with shock as she caught sight of the things that rose from the sand itself.

What was joining the fray wasn’t more of the ordinary skeletal warriors they had been fighting, but two towering tomb guards, one hefting a massive halberd, the other brandishing a pair of axes each larger than the one Valeska carried. They were not merely some oversized skeletons, but creations born from death and earth magic working in tandem, one animating them, the other granting a mass and resilience no ordinary bone could ever hope to possess. They came up out of the ground the way a swimmer comes up out of water, the sand around them rippling outward as it parted to let them through.

“What the hell are those things?” Rowan’s voice cracked as he beheld the sheer scale of the two constructs now bearing down upon him.

“Here they come!” Valeska shouted, already tightening her grip on her axe and shifting her weight into a more defensive stance. “Brace yourself!”

The tomb guard wielding the halberd barreled forward, raising its massive weapon high overhead and bringing it down in a single, devastating strike aimed squarely at Rowan. The former soldier threw his tower shield up just in time to intercept the blow, and the result was a titanic clash, sparks bursting in every direction like a shower of dying stars. The sheer force of the impact sent Rowan staggering backward several unsteady steps until at last his balance abandoned him entirely and he crashed down hard on his bottom. And, while he scrambled back onto his feet, the next strike was already on its way.

But before that deadly blade could complete its descent and cleave into the fallen adventurer, it was abruptly stopped mid-swing, arrested in the air as though it had struck some invisible, unyielding surface. Behind Rowan, Cynthia stood with her ashwood staff extended toward the towering construct, her lips moving rapidly through the words of an incantation.

She must have summoned another one of those walls of compressed air to shield him, Viktor thought. Convenient trick, that.

As Valeska rushed to help her companion, the second tomb guard, the one who wielded the twin axes, thundered across the battlefield, ignoring the three adventurers altogether and charging directly toward the Apostle instead. It swung an axe in a brutal arc, a blow meant to take his head clean off his shoulders.

And then—

The man caught the blade’s edge between his two fingers.

As the strike halted dead in its tracks, the Apostle took a step forward. With a casual jab, he sent his colossal opponent flying across the ruins, mowing down several smaller skeletons in its path before crashing into a wall in an explosive spray of bone fragments.

Damn it, Viktor cursed. In terms of raw physical strength, this guy stood above even Sebekton.

The battle against the other tomb guard, by contrast, proved to be a far more grueling and protracted affair. Rowan, who had finally regained his footing, circled his towering adversary in a coordinated dance with Valeska. They struck whenever an opening presented itself and retreated at the first sign of a counterattack, all the while Cynthia lent her support from a distance with her spells.

When she at last spotted an opportunity, the aeromancer unleashed a concentrated blast of wind at the massive skeleton’s legs, knocking it off balance and sending it toppling forward. As the construct planted one heavy hand against the ground to arrest its fall, Valeska seized the moment and lunged in close, delivering a crushing blow that shattered the crown of its skull.

But of course, that was not enough to finish off the undead, and it retaliated with a sweeping backhand that caught the warrior woman squarely across the chest. The blow sent her staggering backward, coughing up a mouthful of blood in the process. Still, she did not fall. She steadied herself after a few stumbling steps, wiped the blood from her lips with the back of her hand, and charged straight back into the fray without a moment’s delay.

The battle raged on in this fashion, grinding through several more exhausting bouts of strike and retreat before Valeska and Rowan finally managed to bring the tomb guard down for good, though not without taking a fair share of beating themselves along the way.

And, naturally, from the very first clash to the very last, Xander hovered at the same spot, watching the struggle of his “friends” without so much as lifting a single finger to help.

As the two warriors leaned heavily on their respective weapons, chests heaving from exertion as they stared down at the shattered remains of the powerful opponent they had dispatched, the Apostle approached them at a leisurely pace.

“Anyone need healing?” he asked. Simple as asking about the weather.

Valeska and Rowan both pulled the same face, the sort someone made when they had bitten into something thoroughly rotten.

“No... thank you. We have herbs...”

“Make it quick,” Xander said. “We’re not stopping here all day.”

Viktor opened his eyes, leaving those adventurers behind. In an instant, the scene of the battle under that scorching, merciless sun vanished entirely, replaced by the familiar surroundings of the Core Room.

“Continue to have the skeletons harass them as they make their way to the great tomb,” he told his Dungeon Core.

[Yes, Master.]

He rose from his chair and walked toward the door. The rest of it was just mundane things Celeste could handle perfectly well without any further input from him. So he would leave the matter in her hands and turn his attention instead to the next phase of the plan.

Crossing the threshold, he stepped out into the wider expanse of the fifth floor. To his left was the colosseum, an absurdly costly project he still could not, for the life of him, understand why he had ever approved in the first place.

And, to his right, stood the trebuchet.
