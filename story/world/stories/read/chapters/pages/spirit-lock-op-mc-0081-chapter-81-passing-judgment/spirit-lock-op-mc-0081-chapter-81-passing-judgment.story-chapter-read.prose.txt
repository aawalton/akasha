Calid readied his strike, prepared to kill everyone—even the teammates that couldn’t escape. It was cruel, but it was clear that they weren't going to win. They were up against six top-tier As, and the ambushers were shockingly powerful as well. It had been a blood bath—and it was hopeless. If he wanted to save the Crimson Sickles, he needed to sacrifice everyone, including himself. So he released that hurricane with conviction, but just as it was expanding, a mouse the size of a rabbit flew into the sky with angelic wings and erupted in blinding light.

The most violent torrent of cora that Calid had ever experienced suddenly ripped through the air, nearly stopping his heart. His hurricane disappeared; the same was true of Carian’s shadow cat and Hayden’s team’s Bubble shield. Everyone, without distinction, fell to the ground except Hayden himself.

“I planned to spare everyone left,” Hayden said, standing slowly. “But anyone who is willing to kill their party without warning… like a coward… deserves punishment.”

Calid looked up and saw Hayden glowering at him, his eyes chilling. On his shoulder, there was the winged horix. It had changed. It was still a mouse, but it had tiny black antlers. It had evolved—

And it was emanating a sinister pressure.

Hayden glanced at Tibbi. “Get him,” he ordered.

A purple tentacle shot a hundred meters out of her back like a squid, grabbing Calid. His body lost gravity, and then the tentacle pulled him to Hayden.

Calid’s heart welled with dread when he got close. Hayden had evolved. His aura was like a calm, yet shearing force that cut like razors. That power matched his eyes, which were like merciless mirrors, allowing one to reflect upon their actions. Hayden lifted his hand, and hundreds of thin, clear line threads formed into a sword. The horix glowed, making the sword shine with brilliant light.

Calid felt icy shivers crawl down his spine. “W-Wait. If you kill me, you’ll be implicated,” he reasoned. “They’re going to use a truth spirit to pull out the truth.”

“What truth?” Hayden asked. “That you tried to ambush me, only to awaken a colossus? That you tried to kill me and a trade commissioner’s daughters?”

Calid gritted his teeth. “You’ll still be condemned as a murderer.”

“Oh, I rarely punish my enemies with death,” Hayden said with a kingly gaze. “I reduce them to mortals and let them wither.”

His eyes widened. “What?”

Hayden pulled back his sword and thrust it into Calid’s chest.

To Calid’s surprise, the sword didn’t pierce his flesh. It stabbed his core, injecting it with an erratic pattern of cora that rattled his core like a kicked can—before it exploded. His vision blurred in sharp wobbles, and he hit the ground face-first.

Hayden took a deep breath, glad to be relieved of all the pent-up tora in his core. He finally felt free. His core was still locked without his main gate open, but it had reached a level of purity that he doubted anyone in this universe had accomplished. It was incomplete, but it was enough to subjugate the rest of his prisoners.

Hayden turned to “Mackerel,” as he had heard him called. “Do you have words of repentance?” Hayden asked.

“We were just doing what we were told,” Mackerel said.

“By who?”

“Karva. She didn’t trust your team could handle your security, so she ordered us to take over your security. So that’s what we tried to do.”

“I see. Is there anything else?”

“No.”

“Honorable.” He stabbed Mackerel in the core, but he didn’t destroy it. He just injected a pattern.

Mackerel’s lips quivered. “W-What’d you just do?”

“I locked your core’s exhaust mechanism. It’ll recover, but if you try to use your core before it unlocks, your core will balloon—and rupture. So unless you want to join this man as a mortal, you’ll stay still for the next week. I’ll let the authorities deal with you.”

“Thank you…” Mackerel said.

“Don’t thank me,” Hayden said icily. “Thank your circumstances.” He left, giving the man nothing before walking up to Carian without the slightest hint of a smile. “Hello, Ms. Raina.”

“Don’t you dare,” Carian said.

“Oh, you have no right to object. You’ve made your actions—and you’ll pay the price.”

“I’ll pay the price—fine. But you won’t call me Ms. Raina.”

“Oh, I suppose that is unfitting—given that you tried to kill your students.”

Carian pursed her lips and looked at the ground, tears welling in her eyes.

“Pathetic,” Hayden said. “You betray my trust, deny our relationship—and then play upon my sympathies? A true harpy to the end.”

-

Carian’s eyes flashed open, and she opened her mouth, but lost words when the sword pierced through her core. She felt its icy sheen, and for a moment, it was unclear what he would do. She thought he’d lock her core, but she felt it rattle, crack, and explode, sending pain signals to her brain. She then blacked out.

Hayden glanced at the ambush team’s healer, Miki. She was an A-rank healer, and she managed to save all the injured—going so far as to reattach limbs in some cases. Still, she was struggling. Hayden glanced at Tibbi, their team’s healer. She was drenched in sweat like the others.

Hayden sent Gluttony into her arms.

She looked up. “What is this?”

“He’ll fill your cora reserves to let you heal people. Heal our people and then the enemy.” He turned to Lina. “Help anyone else that’s useful.”

Lina shot up and saluted, and ran off. Hayden then turned back to his enemies with a cool gaze. It would be a long day.

Kayline watched Hayden seal the other cores before building a safe camp for them. Hayden’s team hunted and brought back wild game, and then he personally cooked it for everyone. When Hart confronted Hayden over why he healed and fed the enemies that had killed half their team, Hayden said, “If you forgo your humanity for revenge, your enemy harms you twice.” He looked him in the eye. “Choose your punishments wisely.”

No one questioned Hayden after that confrontation. After dinner, everyone listened to his victory speech before shackling the Sickles with cuffs that he had the ambush team bring. Only then did he rest by the fire with her and Lina.

“Good job, you two,” Hayden said. “Especially you.” He glanced at Lina. “I hear you saved the day.”

Lina blushed. “Well… a little.”

Kayline smiled wryly. She had been the one to be praised her entire life. Having Lina be the centerpiece was bittersweet.

“And you,” Hayden said. “Sometimes it’s not what people do, it’s what they don’t. If you would’ve used your spirit, it would’ve killed everyone. So good job.”

She blushed slightly as well.

“Ew,” Tibbi said as she sat down. “You know he’s an old man, right?”

“Yeah, but he’s super hot,” Kayline said, tucking a lock of hair behind her ear playfully.

“Well… there’s that,” Tibbi said. “Glad you two know, though. Kinda wigs me out to see people who look his age.”

“You have age dysphoria?” Lina asked.

“Eh...” Tibbi pinched her fingers. “A little. But it’s mostly just this guy. Guy freaks me out.”
