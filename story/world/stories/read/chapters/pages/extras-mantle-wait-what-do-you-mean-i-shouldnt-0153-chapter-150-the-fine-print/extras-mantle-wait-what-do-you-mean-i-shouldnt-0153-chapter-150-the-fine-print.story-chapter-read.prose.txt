The bathwater had gone cold about an hour ago.

Jin hadn't noticed. That was the part worth worrying about.

For days now his body had been a failing system he checked hourly—annoying Angel ran scans every few hours, and it had gotten to the point where even Angel started cussing at him—so now he just sat in a tub of cooling water long enough for his panicking part of his mind to calm down.

Still, all the time away from being in the center of the battle had given Jin the very much needed time for introspection and self-reflection. He chuckled to himself at the thought.

He had read as many science textbooks as he could get his hands on. It may sound really stupid, and it even felt stupider reading the books initially, but as he persisted, he felt really dumb that he did not really know how the laws of energy worked and how he was using his thermokinetic engine.

And that’s not all; he also learned firearms drill and postures from Veric, who surprisingly was a crazy shot.

Angel. Am I dying?

« You are sitting in cold water. Thinking about life and the choices that led to so far. »

That's not a no.

« It's a very emphatic no. It is also the third-stupidest question you have asked me today, and it is not yet noon. »

What were the first two?

« I've started a list. It's getting long enough that I may need to categorise. »

The screen bloomed before he could get a word in.

THIRD ASSIMILATION MILESTONE REACHED.

“Finally,” Jin exhaled. “Show me just the changes, Angel.”

« Please?” »

“Oh, c’mo—” Jin sighed. “Please, Angel. Help your buddy out.”

« Updates coming right up. »

•••

RANK: D3 → D1

•••

« All stats ranked up across the board now averaging D1-D2,» Angel added. « You can see a detailed one later, but most skills are now at elite. »

•••

❂ RACIAL ABILITIES

[Elite] Mystic Eyes of Sight Beyond the Veil (124) » RANK UP!

❂ MARKED SKILLS [4/5]

[Elite] Inevitable Doom (105)  » RANK UP!

[Elite] Thermokinetic Engine (GROWTH TYPE) (101)  » RANK UP!

[Elite] Spellshot Synthesis (102)  » RANK UP!

[Elite] Spell Weaver’s Matrix (101)  » RANK UP!

❂ ACQUIRED SKILLS

[Elite] Essence Honing (109) » RANK UP!

[Elite] Essence Reinforcement (108) » RANK UP!

[Elite] Essence Construct (112) » RANK UP!

[Elite] Path of Fleshcrafting (109) » RANK UP!

❂ STATUS EFFECTS

[Essence Pathway Disruption] » CLEARED

[Soul Identity Degradation] — SEVERE » MINOR (residual, permanent)

Containment Seals — External] » STABLE. Degradation halted.

•••

Jin read it once. Then again, slower, because the first pass had mostly been his own pulse in his ears.

“Well, I put a lot of effort into pushing the skill to elite now once I’m fully cleared of the bullshit status effects; I’ll push them up to master!” Jin mumbled excitedly.

« Yeah, that would boost our power levels. Also, it says cleared for the pathway, but still says minor, residual, and permanent for your soul, and you skipped that line so fast you left a wake. »

I read it.

« You just looked at it. »

Permanent's fine. He let his head fall back against the tub, which was colder than the water by a decent margin. Permanent means it stopped moving. I can work with things that stopped moving.

There was a pause.

« ...That's a healthier framing than I was braced for. »

Don't sound disappointed.

Fine. Time for an experiment.

He lifted one hand clear of the water and turned it over, watching it drip. Then he reached inward—past the mind star, down through the channels, to the coiled place where the mantle sat waiting like something with opinions—and pulled. Gently. The way you'd test a rope you'd personally watched fray.

A single chain manifested around his palm.

Silver, thin as a bootlace, no thicker than his little finger. It hung over the bathwater and turned slowly, throwing a pale wobble of light across the tiles.

He waited for the flinch.

For days there had been a flinch.

Nothing.

He held it for a five-count. Then ten. Then he thickened the chain until it could have moored a boat, which was well past the point where his soul used to start screaming, and the only thing that happened was that the water sloshed.

Angel. Numbers.

« Soul architecture at ninety-one percent structural integrity and holding. No degradation over the last four hours. Essence heart stable under load. Your channels are wider than they were before the possession, which is frankly an insult to everyone who earned theirs the slow way. »

So the plan works.

« The plan works on you. »

That's what I needed. He let the chain sink back into his palm. No mark, no burn, no cold. That's the whole gate. If it works on me, it works on—

« Jin. Ask me why it healed. »

Jin closed his eyes.

It's the harvest.

« It's her harvest. Kiyon's power went into your channels. Trish's went into your soul, and it did not repair the damage so much as move in, put its feet up, and take over the load-bearing sections. » A beat. « Your race still reads three question marks. Your pact obligation still reads unresolved. Whatever is currently holding you together has a name, and one day it's going to knock and ask what it's owed. »

Water dripped off the tap. Jin counted four drops before he answered.

“I know.”

« You sound calm about it. »

“I'm not calm about it. I've got what… fifteen days before this city gets turned into a battery and nine before I throw everything I own at a cathedral, so I've had to start a queue.” He opened his eyes. “She's third. Maybe fourth.”

« ...Who's second? »

The thing that's still looking at me.

« Ah. » Angel sounded almost proud. « At least the triage is correct. »

Jin hauled himself upright, sending water over the rim in a sheet, and pulsed the Engine—barely a flicker, less than a charge—until the moisture leapt off his skin in a mist that drifted toward the drain.

« I will never not find that obnoxious. »

"It works." He was already reaching for a shirt. Messages.

« Ready. »

“Veric, Vera, Jack, Basta, Bram. Joe. Rudy. Reyana.” Jin listed the names as the shirt went over his head. “Get them to the core training floors, ASAP. Don't say why.”

« They will ask why. »

“Eh,” Jin scoffed. “They'll come anyway.”

« That is not the comfort you appear to think it is… Sent. Joe's already flagged read. He's typing. He has stopped typing. He is typing again. »

“Add a line to his. Tell him to bring Reyana if she's with him.”

« Why? »

“Because if I don't, he'll come alone, she'll hear about it secondhand, and then I'll be dead, and none of this will matter.”

« Sent. »

« For the record. It works on you. We have tested it on precisely one person, and that person is a documented lunatic with a soul held together by cosmic leftovers.»

“Then it's a good thing I'm persuasive.”

Floor twenty-eight still smelled of sealant.

Illiana's repair crews had spent the last few days pouring structure back into the hole Jin had punched through three floors of bastion, and the repairs showed. Someone had chalked an arrow on the wall beside it with YOU ARE HERE underneath in Jack's cramped handwriting. Someone else, almost certainly Rudy, had added (BLAME JIN) below that in letters twice the size.

Jin looked at it for a second and decided morale was holding.

The room past it had been a mess hall once. Now it held a round table and eight people in various stages of not having slept.

Veric had the chair by the door with an unlit cigarette between two fingers, which meant Vera had gotten there first and laid down the law. Jack sat with one boot up on the table's edge, flipping a wooden peg from hand to hand. Bram—the most recent addition to the team, but the man was a good soldier and now held the 1st Mage Company together—took up a chair and about a third of the next one. Beside him sat Basta—one of Veric’s old team members—and Joe held up the far wall. Rudy had the chair beside him and was eating something.

Reyana sat directly opposite the door and tracked Jin from the moment he came through it.

"Boss," Veric said.

"Veric," Jin pulled out a chair and sat. "So before we move on to the main topic, I wanna know what’s happening. Go, what’s the status of the recruitment?"

Veric turned the cigarette over. "Better than we deserve. We put the C2 footage on the network as one of the promos for your guides."

"And?"

"Well, the survivors are loving it and almost caused a riot trying to be part of Mage companies. " He tipped the cigarette at Jin. "Second and third companies are locked. Fully. Two hundred people, screened and cleared. Soft commitments on ten more… give me two days and I'll make them hard."

"Ten companies. Two days." Jin nodded approvingly. “Good work.”

"People will do a lot for the chance to stop feeling useless, and the promise of power makes it only more enticing," Veric said. "You handed them a picture of a hundred nobodies erasing an outpost without losing anyone. That's worth more than any speech you could've given them, no offence."

"None taken. My speeches are terrible."

"They're not good," Jack agreed.

"Also—" Veric jerked his chin at Bram. "Your 1st Company has a squadron leader now.”

“I knew that,” Jin said.

“Officially. As of this morning."

“Ohh.” Jin nodded. “Good.”

Bram cleared his throat like a man shifting furniture.

Jin looked at him. "You have a lot of responsibility. Keep your mind focused.”

"Aye," Bram found something fascinating in the table's scoring.

"Good." Jin meant it, and let it show. Bram had folded his arms in a hall full of frightened people and asked, with respect, how one clever little sorcery was supposed to do a damn thing against monsters. Then he'd gone into the booth first because Jin had shoved him, and walked out casting. "You're the right pick. You were the last man in that room who wanted to believe me. Everyone who followed you in followed a sceptic. That's worth more than a believer."

Bram's ears went red to the tips. He grunted at the table.

"Anyone else?"

"Gear," Joe said. "Rudy's kit is stripped to components. I’ll have to talk to you about your stuff. Illiana is handling others’ gear. “

"Hey!" Rudy said around a mouthful. "Not my fault enemies target me first!"

Jin let the room run itself down. Then he put both hands flat on the blast door.

"Right. Why you're here."

The room quietened down.

"I can push you all to Order III." He went around the table, unhurried, one face at a time. "Today. In this room. In about as long as it takes to boil a kettle."

For one whole second, nobody made a sound.

Then everybody did.

Bram came out of his chair hard enough to shove it back a foot. Jack said something that was mostly grunts. Basta's fist hit the table. Vera's voice cut through the top of it, “How, that isn't—”

And Veric didn't move at all.

« See, I had told you this would happen. »

Yeah. yeah.

Jin let his aura up.

He didn't push, just let his presence unspool off him and fill the room.

Jin drew it back in just as quickly.

"Questions are fine," he said. "But we are civilized people. So, one at a time. Veric, you've been sitting on one since I opened my mouth."

Veric tucked the cigarette behind his ear. "How fast is the procedure?"

"Same as the sorcery imprint. I take the subject inside a booth; we would need some hardcore incense this time cuz it’ll sting this time."

"Define your version of sting," Veric asked dryly.

"Well… it'll hurt. A lot. For about ninety seconds."

"Right," Veric nodded slowly.

"Everyone can get there?" Bram was still half standing. "Everyone here?"

"Yes. For now, limited to people in this room."

"Obviously, except those of us already at or past Order III," Joe said, smiling with about a third of his face. "Some of us intend to sit here and enjoy the show."

"Jeez, at least you could make some refreshments," Jack said, with no heat in it at all.

“Sure,” Joe nodded.

"What are the side effects?" Vera said. Every head came round to her, then swung back to Jin.

There it was.

Jin had rehearsed a smoother version in the tub. He threw it out, because Veric would smell it and Bram deserved better than a sales pitch.

"They're severe. So I'll say it plainly, and then you can sit with it."

Nobody moved.

"Any expectation you've got of future progression—of ranking past where I put you." He grimaced. "Give it up."

Jack swore under his breath.

"Is that a fact?" Veric asked.

"No," Jin shook his head, and let some of the smile back in. "It's not a fact. There's a chance. It's just minuscule. You'd need to accumulate a monumental amount of latent aura just to push for gains, and as for Order IV." He spread his hands. "Well. You get it."

"I never expected Order IV in my life anyway," Jack said. He'd gone quiet and level. "Not before this. Not after. But Order III across our whole line?" He tapped the peg against his thigh. "I’d say it's a gift. With an ugly receipt stapled to it but a gift nonetheless."

"Aye. And I'm assuming—" Bram lowered himself back down, frowning "—you'll not be stopping with us. You'll be doing this for the Mage Company."

"I'll be pushing every member of every Mage Company that will be formed to Order III," Jin said. “So yeah, I’ve a lot of work to do.”

Bram had spent enough hours around him by now. He watched the smile go from pleasant to pleasant—the exact expression it had worn in the corridor outside the imprinting booths, thirty seconds before the worst minute and a half of his life—and the colour went out of his face.

"We get a say," Bram said. "Right?"

Jin kept smiling.

"Right?"

"How long per person?" Vera asked. “And would you be able to do this for hundreds of people?”

"Thanks to Joe and his contribution, I have enough reserves to do it,” Jin said. “And around 4-5 minutes for a full procedure.”

"Four minutes." She wrote it. "A company's a hundred. That's six hours forty a company, assuming you don't stop, don't eat, and don't fall over. Veric says thirteen companies inside a week — thirteen hundred people." The pencil kept going. "Eighty-seven hours. You've got nine days, you're the only one who can do it, and you're also running a war."

She looked up.

"So which is it? The companies or the war?"

The room turned as one thing and looked at Jin.

« I like her. »

Everybody likes her. She just cost me my afternoon.

"That's a genuinely excellent question," Jin said. "Once I’ve got ample experience, I can do the boosting part in under one minute and hand off the subject to stay in the stabilization array nearby. Something I’ve had Illiana work on.”

"Aura," Veric said. "You've told us what we give up. You haven't told us exactly what we get. A boosted Order III and a natural one aren't the same thing, I’m assuming.”

Jin winced. "Yes. There's another small problematic problem."

"Problematic problem," Veric repeated. "Go on."

"Your aura won't match a ranker who climbed to the ranks on ORDER III on thier own." He tapped two fingers on the table. "It'll be a general aura, no speciality. Give or take twenty-five, thirty percent weaker than the real thing, and obviously the chances of getting a racial ability are even lower. All in all, if you ever end up in a straight contest of aura against someone who earned theirs, you’d lose."

Heads went down around the table.

"Aura matters," Vera said slowly. "I'd argue it's the most important thing about Order III. But none of us can reach it naturally inside the next ten—"

"Eight," Rudy said. "Nine at the outside."

Vera turned and looked at him for exactly as long as it took him to lose two inches of height.

"—inside the next nine days," she went on. "So it's an acceptable trade. And it can be strengthened afterward. With work?"

"Yes, you can," Joe said, and looked at Jin.

Jin crossed his arms. "You'd have to put in far more effort than someone who came by it honestly. But it grows if you feed it. Ranking past it is nigh impossible." He paused. "Not impossible. Which brings me to the last thing."

He waited until he had all of them.

"If any of you are serious about the path, I can stop early."

Vera's eyes narrowed. "Meaning."

"I take you to the peak of Order II and set you against the threshold. No further. I let you feel what aura is—one touch, just enough that you know the shape of the door—and then I take my hands off." He held her gaze. "You'd form your own. Attuned to your soul instead of mine. A real one."

"And don't," Joe said into the gap, "mistake forming an aura for something easy. It might take you seconds. It might take days. I've known men sit at that door for a year and die still knocking." He looked around the table, and every trace of the showman was gone. "Take that option if you mean to give the rest of your life to this. Not because you like the sound of it."

"And if the shooting starts before I've formed it," Basta said, "I'm a peak Order II standing in a company of Order IIIs."

"You are already dead before the nine-day mark, the moment you set foot outside," Reyana said coldly.

Everyone in the room sucked in a cold breath.

"In nine days, I will walk everyone in this room and in the bastion at a cathedral full of things that eat people. If we lose, then it’s only a matter of time before every last soul of Vienna under this roof dies." He let a breath out. "It's a bad deal. It's just better than the other one on the table."

Reyana held his eyes a long moment.

"Good," she said. "I wanted to hear you say it instead of sell it."

"Noted."

"I still hate it."

"Also noted."

"For what it's worth," Veric said, turning the cigarette over, "I'm forty-three. I've been an Order II for twenty-two of those years, and my future consists of a war I probably don't survive and a pension from an empire that isn't coming." He shrugged. "Forgive me if the ceiling doesn't keep me up nights."

"Aye," Bram muttered. "That."

Jin clapped once, and the table jumped.

"Okay. Everyone." He stood and put on his most reassuring face. "There's no risk to your life, and the procedure is one hundred percent failproof—"

« LIAR. We haven't tested it! »

Nobody needs to know that, buddy.

« One of them is writing this down. »

Let em.

"—so unless there's anything else," Jin finished.

Jack thumped a fist on the table. "Then what are we waiting for? Gleeward's been unbearable since his rank-up."

Veric knocked twice on the table. The sound carried. “Anything you're forgetting, boss?"

Jin was already at the door.

"Bram," he said cheerfully and turned and pointed with both hands. "You're up first, buddy. Non-negotiable."

Bram's chair went over backward.

"What?"

"Fine print of the contract." Jin was grinning now, wide and entirely without mercy, and Bram had gone the particular grey of a man recalling an operating table and a rhythm of knuckles on wood. "You and everyone of the Mage company signed. There was a whole clause."

"There was no contract!"

"There was absolutely a contract."

Jin held the door and gestured through it. "Let's go."

Behind him, Veric clicked his tongue and said to nobody in particular: "He never answered the question."
