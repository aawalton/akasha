A minute of practice wasn’t all that much, but Trevor tested the latency of the controller, what to expect with the attacks, and when he would be able to strike or move once the fighter was done performing an action. Every blow felt heavy, not just from the controller’s feedback but from the sound design, too.

Still, this was only testing. He had to gather information on this game since he had only played pieces of before.

He still had to find out how a fighter reacted to being struck on the ground or in the air, when he could get back into a neutral state, and what this comeback mechanic looked like in action. Since the person who was doing more damage became easier to combo without gaining super armor, to prevent flinching, it gave him a lot of chances to lean hard into trying something new.

Trevor never intended to win his first match. He had picked what the manual had described as the jack of all trades to discover what the game considered its standard. Red was the average fighter. With four more matches and only needing to win half of them, he felt confident.

So when Jedfry’s fighter tossed his own off of the stage, the courier just smiled as his opponent celebrated. Out of habit, he offered the kid a fist bump, which was granted.

“I got you good!” Jedfry said as he stood up and struck a pose.

“You sure did. Good work, little man,” Trevor praised.

“Are you sure this guy’s played games before, dad?” Hedry whispered suspiciously from behind them.

“Don’t be rude,” Stammy chastised.

Trevor glanced at his familiar, asking a mental question. It was met with an affirmative and the man nodded at Jedfry. “Hey, since Adabelle’s still reading the manual, will you hold Clay for me?”

The kid immediately stopped his pose, his eyes getting wide. “Can I?”

“Yeah, so long as you don’t drop them. They’re a little heavy.”

“And you have to take the Equalizer off, first,” Dee warned. “Hedry’s up next.”

“This is going to be too easy,” the older boy muttered under his breath, though not so quietly that Trevor couldn’t hear him.

His words were ignored as Jedfry threw the headband onto the couch and reached up for Clay. The courier took the stone stoat off of his shoulders. “Now, remember to be careful,” he said before passing them off.

The demon boy struggled a little bit before pulling the earth elemental to his chest like a child would to a cat. “Clay is smooth smooth,” he remarked with a wondrous smile.

“They sure are,” Trevor chuckled before Hedry shoved the Equalizer towards him.

“You lost fast, but you still have to renew it,” the older boy said.

“Roger that.” The courier took it, received another message, and handed it back.

Hedry put it on and grabbed the controller before falling back onto the couch. With practiced ease, he flipped to teal. Instead of going back to red, Trevor went to green, just like Jedfry had played against him.

“I’m guessing teal is your main?” he asked.

“Yup. Best one in the house, and I know all the counters to green so this is going to go by even quicker than your last match,” Hedry replied, unimpressed.

Trevor smirked. “Just because you prefer to hold forward doesn’t mean you’re going to win faster than your brother.”

“Hold forward?” the boy asked, frowning.

“I’m just saying, overconfidence is not a good look,” the courier said with a little laugh. He offered his hand. “Good luck, have fun.”

“Yeah, whatever,” Hedry said, shaking it despite his attitude.

Trevor leaned forward when the match started, watching the fighters carefully. Just as he predicted, Hedry went straight into a blitz, and he reacted appropriately.

Green ducked under teal’s first strike and performed a grab out of their crouch. They suplexed the other fighter backwards into the ground, resulting in the same dodge ball hit sound as any regular attack would make. Trevor’s robot was faster to return to their feet and he blocked the get-up attack Hedry threw out.

Another crouch and another grab, performing the same action as before. This time, teal was thrown a little farther thanks to the mechanics of the game. Trevor’s fighter jumped into the air, closing the distance while covering his opponent’s options. The only good one for Hedry was to roll away, resetting neutral.

That was not the one they picked, instead opting to roll towards green to get closer to the middle of the stage. It was the best option after the one Trevor expected, but his fighter threw out an airborne kick that caught teal out of their standing animation.

The main takeaway the Summoned had gotten from playing Jedfry was that green had great throws, but their striking game was weak. This allowed them to combo slightly longer than the others with certain moves. While teal was still stunned by the hit, Trevor’s fighter landed before sweeping low twice, rising into an uppercut that pushed the opponent backwards, and finally connected with a high kick, sending them into the air.

Flicking the control stick twice, green responded by dashing forward, canceling to a stop, and then repeating the process. The act put them almost inside of teal before they blocked high. Hedry, as expected, threw out an airborne attack. Their fighter’s knee extended, crackling with energy, and dealt some chip damage though not enough to turn the tides.

The moment the attack wasn’t active anymore, Trevor held forward. Green’s physical presence pushed teal towards the edge. No damage, no attack, just taking advantage of the fact that the game was completely physical. Hedry leaned forward, mashing one of the buttons, but his fighter was stuck in its move a little longer.

Inches before teal touched the ground, Trevor had his character slide to a stop, canceling its dash with a crouch. Once the fighter was standing back up again, he initiated a grab. Green took its opponent by the arm and began spinning around.

“No, no!” Hedry yelled, standing up as he mashed the buttons, as if that would help him.

Green threw teal at a forty-five degree angle towards the end of the stage, which they had been pushed towards since that first roll. Hedry’s fingers clashed against his controller, trying to alter the trajectory as best he could.

It was no use. Most of teal’s body disappeared behind the curtain. That was apparently enough because green overtook the back screen to indicate that Trevor was the victor.

There was a moment of silence through the room as everyone processed what had just happened. The courier took great joy in the shock on Hedry’s face as he stared at the fighters returning to their starting position without input.

The boy whispered something, and Stammy chuckled lightly.

“Sorry, I didn’t catch that,” Trevor said.

“Best two out of three,” Hedry croaked. “You won’t be able to spam grabs next time.”

“Sorry, I think your sister's next,” he replied, trying not to be cheeky.

“Good work, honey,” Adabelle praised as she leaned down and kissed the top of her husband’s head. “I’ll admit I’m not quite sure what happened there, but it was a very convincing victory.”

“It was a complete blowout is what it was,” Lilsian said as she patted her brother on the back. Then, she took the controller and headband from him. “Why don’t you watch a pro at work.”

“Stuff it,” the boy replied, crossing his arms.

“Jedfry, why don’t you let your brother hold Clay for a bit,” Trevor offered. “They don’t mind getting passed around if everyone’s being gentle.”

The earth elemental reached for Hedry even as Jedfry pouted, but the older boy turned away, clearly upset.

“Looks like you’re going to miss your chance,” Dee warned, though in a light tone. When that didn’t work, she shrugged. “Let’s get ready for round three.”

“So you know, I’m in a league above my brothers,” Lilsian said conversationally as she offered Trevor the Equalizer. She sat down after he touched it. “I know what you’ve been up to. I’m in your head.”

“Oh, mind games, nice,” the courier said before looking up at his wife. “Would you like to play? I’m a little scared of her.”

“Are you really?” Adabelle asked, arching an eyebrow.

“Nah, but I’ve already played two games and I don’t want to hog it.”

She smiled at him before running her fingers through his hair. “You can win one more time. I’ll join in once the pressure is off of us for the quest.”

“Sure thing, lady,” Trevor replied, taking her hand in his and kissing it. “Alright, I’m ready when you are, Lilsian.”

“We’ll see how true that is in a moment,” she returned smugly before putting on the Equalizer. Once it was firmly around her head, she started flicking through colors. Instead of yellow like Lilsian had played when they first saw the game, she picked pink, the stance specialist.

“Interesting,” Trevor remarked, tapping his chin. That would give her a good level of unpredictability, allowing her to play into whatever feints and ruses she liked. Against a more seasoned player, that would have been a great tactic.

But against Trevor, who was still learning the ropes, it just meant she had a few extra moves she could throw out depending on stance. His inexperience was going to be the same regardless of what she picked. So, he chose the color that seemed to be the safest to him.

Lilsian immediately groaned.

“Something the matter?” he asked casually.

“You’re seriously playing silver?” she asked in return, making a disappointed face.

“Problem?”

“No one likes playing against silver,” the teenager complained.

“That’s awesome, because it means I made the right choice,” Trevor said with a smile. He offered his hand. “Good luck—”

“Have fun, yes, I heard you the first two times,” Lilsian sighed, shaking his hand. She pulled her feet up onto the couch to sit cross-legged as he locked in his color. The fighters moved their arms, counting down to three when the match started.

Pink rushed forward to close the distance, but silver retreated back with another flick of the control stick. They crouched, causing Lilsian to jump to avoid a low kick. Trevor let up on the controls, standing to produce a large, sweeping attack upwards. His fighter’s forearm grew by half a foot, extending its reach and knocking the other one back.

“In my head, indeed,” he chuckled as he dashed forward.

“How does that one have extra reach?” Adabelle asked, talking more to herself, and she flicked through the manual.

Trevor smiled; that was exactly why he picked this color. Silver used a dash attack, his fighter going into a straight leg slide to catch Lilsian’s landing. She countered by throwing out an attack, and pink shifted higher as they performed a falling kick. His attack ended first, missing her fighter by inches, before standing up into hers and getting knocked back.

Lilsian attempted to follow up with a long range punch, but Trevor came out of hitstun before it could connect. Silver blocked, getting pushed back before crouching and striking. Pink blocked in return, going low, but the gap between them became wider. Both fighters stood up at the same time, with silver performing an uppercut.

Pink ducked under it, not quite crouching but still minimizing the fighter’s physical presence. This was the boxing stance, which traded reach for faster attacks. Trevor nodded as Lilsian started a combo on him. He was patient, trying to block when his fighter wasn't stunned as he waited for the finisher. It came with a brutal downwards punch that knocked silver down.

Instead of standing, rolling, or attacking, Trevor stayed on the ground for a moment. The pink fighter moved back and forth, waiting for him to do something, but he was still. Beside him, Lilsian released a disgruntled noise before going for a low punch.

That was when he performed the get-up attack. His fighter knocked her attack aside as they stood, sending pink flying backwards. With neutral reset, silver went on the offensive.

Changing stances seemed to take a second or two, which Trevor specifically did not give her. Silver’s natural reach was much greater than Pink’s in this form, and he used it well. As the strikes came, chipping away at the advantage Lilsian had already gained, her fighter barely moved. The courier could admit to mashing buttons until he wasn’t.

Instead of going for another attack, silver reached out with both hands. Grabs beat blocks, and pink was lifted up, slammed down, and then tossed into the air. Trevor had noticed this combo almost immediately when he saw that this color was built for zoning, creating a safe space around themselves.

There was a subtle shift as, instead of attacking, Lilsian thought it was wiser to change stances mid-air. That turned Trevor’s string into a true combo. The opponent was met with an anti-air punch from a still-grounded silver. His fighter dashed forward, repeating the attack, and then did it again.

There was no air dodge, and pink was getting juggled across the stage. The fighter landed on one of the platforms, but Trevor simply stood still, waiting for her reaction. She did the same, though he could hear Lilsian’s finger tapping on the controller.

Trevor made his fighter hop. Not a full jump, certainly not something that would get them onto the platform. Silver left the ground for half a second before falling again. This was to bait her into action.

It was then that pink initiated the get-up attack, but it hit nothing but air. Trevor had silver jump for real this time to strike midair, pushing the opponent off of the platform. They went into free fall, and silver tumbled down after them, throwing out another aerial. It connected at the edge of the range, sending the other fighter flying towards the end of the stage.

Pink landed on the ground, prone once more, as silver slowly walked up. Lilsian froze, thinking of her options. Rolling away would put her fighter off stage, and she still had the nasty taste of a whiffed get-up attack in her mind. For her, the only options were to roll towards her opponent or to stand up. Neither looked good.

That was Trevor’s assessment, at least. The fifty-fifty didn’t matter to him because he had an option for both. Silver continued their slow approach. He made a guess and turned around, showing Lilsian his back. His fighter wound up, throwing their leg forward.

His opponent stood up and cringed. The attack missed, but only the first half.

Silver’s leg swept backwards, striking pink and sending it off stage. Trevor’s color overtook Lilsian’s on the background as Adabelle shook him in excitement.

“What was that!?” his opponent asked incredulously, pointing her controller at the stage.

“I just walked up, slowly, and down smashed,” he answered with a light laugh.

Lilsian frowned. “What’s a down smash?”

“Earth term. Don’t worry about it,” Trevor chuckled. “Good game, though. I’m sure you’ll learn to counter me as we play more. I’m just very used to mind games. I played a much more annoying zoner back home, so that was pretty refreshing.”

“Yeah, good game,” the teenager replied, trying not to grumble as she took off the headband. “Guess that means you won the quest. Congratulations.”
