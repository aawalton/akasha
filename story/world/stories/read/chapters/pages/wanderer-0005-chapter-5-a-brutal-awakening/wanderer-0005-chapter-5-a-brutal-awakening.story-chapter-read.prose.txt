I wake to the sounds of hundreds of birds chirping in the trees, and Torsten moving about to pack up and ready a few horses. It's a few hours after dawn.

My body is stiff from sleeping on the ground, and I groan as I roll to my side to sit up. So I'm still in this world. It wasn't a dream. I'll admit, part of me thought it might be. But the deep blue and purple bruises all over my legs and arms, with accompanying scrapes and scratches prove otherwise. My ankle is still stiff as well.

"We'll be' leaven in a few min'ets Lass. Take care o' yer needs now," Torsten calls over his shoulder when he notices I'm awake.

I nod and head behind one of those gnarly, prickly yellow flowered bushes to awkwardly relieve myself with bound hands. Fuck this pencil skirt. I manage to wiggle it up enough one side at a time, and use the same method with my underwear, occasionally looking over my shoulder to make sure Torsten is not watching, but his back is turned away as he's still packing the horses. I'm going to have to find more suitable clothes in Tír Tairngire.

After we break our fast on some more salted meat and hard tack, we head out at a steady pace, taking a few extra horses with us. We ride for a couple hours without incident, and I try to think of a plan for when we reach Tír Tairngire. The next advantage I might have to escape would be at night when there are less people awake and on guard, but who knows what my sleeping situation will look like and if I even make it that far. I shudder at the thought.

I see the smoke well before we reach the village, but it's not enough to prepare me for what we see as we ride into Tír Tairngire. The smell of burnt flesh and smoke burns my nostrils as we ride through the remains of a cluster of homes with completely destroyed thatch roofs and blackened walls. Livestock run amok and turned over carts have spilled produce soaking in the mud. Dozens of bodies litter the ground in a way that indicates frantic attempts to flee. Some have spears and blades jutting out of their backs and chests. Others have slit throats, pools of blood forming under their face down corpses. Blood stains the mud and dirt where our horses’ hooves tread, smearing it more and more the further we head through town.

Near a doorway of a burning home, I spot a man that's still alive. Lying on his back, he clutches the intestines spilling out of his stomach with shaking hands and whimpers. His eyes lock on to mine for a few moments, wide with fear, before a gurgling sound comes from his throat and he stills, eyes open and no longer seeing. I shudder and tear my eyes away from the sight, gripping the pommel with my bound hands and breathing hard.

Breathe, just breathe. You will get through this.

A blood curdling scream sounds from my right, and whip my head up in time to see a woman fleeing from the doorway of a home still standing. Her dress is torn from her shoulder and one breast is exposed, her dark hair unbound and flying in the wind. She manages to get a foot out of the door before a man grabs her hair and yanks her back into his arms. It's the bulky, brown haired man from Froðe's clan who was brandishing his axe before the raid. She thrashes violently and claws his skin trying to escape, but he's got at least 100 pounds on her and is unmovable. He easily picks her up off the ground and takes her back into the darkened doorway before slamming the door shut, her screams becoming more muted. I think they will haunt me forever.

I'm going to be sick...

But my horse trudges on, the reins attached to a rope on Torsten's hip, tugging me along, forcing me to witness the rest of this atrocity unfold.

A gust of wind temporarily clears some of the smoke billowing across our path, and I can see that on the hill ahead that a monastery is burning. A group of Froðe’s warriors are descending the hill, carrying large sacks filled with what I imagine are spoils from the raid.

Suddenly a child runs across our path, soot smeared on her tear stained face. She barely dodges my horse's hooves, causing her to whinny and stumble. The girl looks to be about 6 years old, and wears a tartan overdress and a white blouse underneath, her chestnut hair lovingly braided down her back. She clutches a handmade doll in her arms, looking frantically around her. “Mamaidh? Mamaidh?!” she wails. Only the crackling of dying flames answer her, and she begins to cry, clutching her doll tighter to herself. The sight is heartbreaking.

This is horrific.

Is this what raids were really like? What will happen to this girl?

“It’ll be best for yeh not to look, Lass,” Torsten says, and I realize that I said the horrific thing out loud.

As if that's even possible. Everywhere I look, there is carnage and devastation. This village has become a place of pure suffering.

"What will happen to the survivors of this village, Torsten?"

He hesitates, and I start to dread the answer. "If yeh want to know, she’ll pro'lly become a thrall.”

My heart drops to my stomach. I don't like the sound of that word.

"What is a thrall?"

"She'll beh forc'd to work. Trad'd with oth'rs like her in oth'r nations." A slave.

Rage boils up inside me at his nonchalant answer, and I grip the pommel with such force that my hands turn red.

“All this…for gold? Spoils?" I ask, my voice becoming a snarl I've never used before. "That you claim are yours?” I glare at Torsten’s back as he rides in front of me.

“Dinnae assume yeh know our ways, Lass,” he replies without looking back. Fuck him. He fucking joined them after they did the same to his village.

Be careful! You are also in a dangerous situation!  My analyst brain alerts me. I don't quite know what these people have in store for me. I might very well become a thrall myself.

The little girl wails again as we pass, the sounds of roaring flames on nearby thatched roofs accompanying her, and I just can't do it. I can't do nothing. Not this time.

Something overcomes me, and I act without thinking. Bracing my bound hands on the pommel, I swing my right leg behind me and dismount, feet sinking into the mud with a squelch. My horse continues on without me, and I turn to the girl.

“What are yeh doin’ Lass?! Get yer arse back here!” Torsten stops the horses and dismounts angrily. His large frame sends mud flying as he lands on the ground and stomps towards me.

"Just give me a minute. It's not like I can really go anywhere, Torsten," I bite back, displaying my bound hands for emphasis. He growls in frustration, but thankfully does not pursue me further.

“Hey, it’s ok, come here,” I motion to the girl. She looks over at me with watery eyes. “Cò thu?” she asked, her brows knitting together in confusion. “Càidh a..” that sharp pain slices through my brain again and the strange echoing sounds in my ears...ow! “my mama? Where's my mama?" Alright, a new language learned I guess. This one sounds more like Gaelic.

"I'm not sure, but I will help you try to find her," I say gently in her native language, offering my hand to her as best I can with it bound to my other. "What's your name?"

"Sorcha," the little girl says, rubbing her eyes. "Why are yer arms tied up?"

"Don't worry about that, I will sort it out later." I try to smile. "My name is Beatrix. Would you like me to help you find your mother?" I don't say parents...because I doubt her father is alive at this point. I couldn't bear to get her hopes up.

Sorcha nods, and slowly wraps her small fingers around my bound hand. The other holds the doll close to her chest. I lead her back to the horses, the reins held by an increasingly aggravated Torsten.

"And what d' hell yeh plan to do with her aye?" he says as he eyes Sorcha.

"She can ride with me until we spot her mother," I say and lift my chin. Torsten raises a fuzzy red eyebrow. "Are yeh sure yeh want to risk his wrath, Lass?" Froðe. Fuck him too.

I stand my ground, keeping Torsten's glare with my own. Moments pass, maybe a minute as we stare each other down.

"Argh! Difficult wo'man!" he grumbles as he stomps forward, unceremoniously lifts and throws me in the saddle once again, but this time I manage to grip the pommel and not fall over. Sorcha squeals and looks like she's about to flee.

"It's alright Sorcha, you can ride up front." I make a pointed look at Torsten once I'm settled.

He rolls his eyes, but then picks up Sorcha and hands her to me in the saddle.

"Now, if yer done causin' me problems Lass, we bes' be on our way."

"Thank you, Torsten," I say earnestly. In all honesty I didn't think he would let me take her, there must be a soft spot still there left in him despite turning against his own people and becoming a viking clan member.

He says nothing, and returns to his own horse. Soon after we continue riding through the wreckage and havoc, and I look around for anyone who might resemble Sorcha's mother, but all I see are bodies and smoke.

At last we reach what appears to be a tavern, with several of Froðe's clansmen moving around and about. They eye me with recognition as we approach, one of them offering a feral grin which I pointedly ignore. From this vantage point, I can see that the town is near the bank of a large river to our left, likely why the people of Tír Tairngire decided to settle here. Out of the corner of my eye, I spot a giant raven, Huginn (or is it Muninn this time?) flying west. Probably communicating the success of this raid to Skuld and the other raiding party, I imagine.

"What's the meaning of this?" My heart jolts and I whip my head forward to see Froðe standing in front of us. His ice blue eyes are already narrowed in on me of course. His sandy blond hair is wind swept and there is a generous amount of blood coating his armor. He has a wooden tankard of what appears to be mead in his hand.

I hold his glare. I've been doing a lot of that lately. "I'm helping her find her mother."

Froðe blinks slowly tilting his head as he studies me, and I can't read his expression. Even though I'm on horseback and technically above him, he still has a way of looking down at me somehow.

"Hmmm...then I suppose you should have a look at the new thralls we have acquired," he finally responds.

With the hand holding the tankard, Froðe gestures lazily with his pointer finger to his left, away from the river. "Good luck," he smirks, gives Torsten a look that elicits an exasperated sigh from him.

"Oh...and by the way, Beatrix," Froðe says, as Torsten helps us dismount.  "I thought I told you not to cause trouble."

I freeze. Sorcha sees my response and freezes too, looking up at Froðe in fear. But his eyes are only on me, dark with a promise of something sinister.

"We'll address it later when you return." Great. Froðe then turns and heads back into the tavern.

After dismounting the horses, Torsten hands the reins off to another clansmen and we head in the direction Froðe pointed, Sorcha holding on the hem of my pencil skirt. Soon we approach a fenced off field in front of a block house, the thatched roof still in tact. A group of about 20 people, including men, women and children stand in front of the house, weary, wounded and clearly frightened. Like me, their hands are bound, and another one of Froðe's clansmen are securing chains around their necks. They share the field with a few highland cattle that graze the field lazily despite their new co-occupants. A female and male member of Froðe's clan stand watch. The male looks bored and like he'd rather be inside the Tavern drinking mead. The female watches stoically, arms crossed in front of her chest and a battle axe strapped to her hip.
