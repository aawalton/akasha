Ashton tiredly looked up at the figure looming above him.

"Arkis, masta furig lo izta ek–" they spoke with a sharp tongue. Clearly they were angry at him, or questioning what he was doing here. Of course, that made sense. It wasn't like he was particularly disguised right now. He didn't care to be disguised all the time, either. He was strong enough that he didn't have to worry about people messing with him just because he wasn't a hapi.

But... why was this person so upset about that? They clearly weren't one, either.

Actually... "What are you?" Ashton asked. The Summoner had placed a spell on him that would let him communicate with all the people in this world, but this person wasn't speaking a language that this spell could translate.

They weren't a hapi, either. They looked completely different to the ways hapis did. More... human, somehow.

Hapis gave Ashton an uncanny feeling; they didn't quite look the way that he was used to. But this person didn't. Their features, despite the colour of their skin and their enormous size, was more like that of a person from his home.

However, Ashton couldn't enjoy that nostalgic feeling in any way, considering that they were digging the edge of their blade into his skin right now.

Not that it was able to cut him all that deep. His skin was too tough for the amount of pressure they were applying.

"Suma lo astura isg fori!" The person's yell was so loud that it startled even them. How awkward. They clearly weren't used to fighting, or even threatening, other people.

With a slight sigh, Ashton assessed the situation. He was laying flat on the ground, with this person squatting over them, pressing down on his torso.

So, Ashton raised up his legs and twisted them around their loose-hanging cloak until it was a tight enough grip, before rapidly pulling down his legs again. The stranger was startled and could feel the pull on their neck, trying to turn to see if they missed some companion Ashton was travelling with.

But no, of course there was nobody else. The moment they got distracted enough, Ashton grabbed their arm, twisted it away, and then spun his body in a way where he was soon freed while the stranger was laying on the ground instead. Ashton's old, ill-fitting boot pushed down on their neck.

"I'll ask again, what are you?" Ashton asked, staring down at them. "What do you want from me?"

The stranger glared up at Ashton, grabbing at his leg to try and tear him away. They were strong, but he still didn't budge.

Ashton rolled his eyes. Should he just go? He might as well continue the journey. He didn't have to bother with stuff like this. They were probably just some kind of vagrant that was trying to rob him.

But... for some reason, Ashton didn't just want to go. This was the first time that he had met someone here that wasn't a hapi. Well, beside the other summons and the summoner themself, of course.

With a sigh, Ashton took his foot of the stranger's throat. He approached the bag that he had been using as a pillow and pulled out some smoked, dried meat he bought from a town he passed through, then threw some at the stranger.

He dropped back down onto the ground and started eating. Clearly, this person wasn't able to hurt Ashton if they wanted to, at least not the way they looked right now. Their cheeks were sunken and they had dark circles under their eyes. If they were similar to humans, then that was clearly a sign that they were struggling. And maybe, that they didn't belong here, either.

"... eat?" they asked, holding the food and pointing at themselves.

Ashton nodded. So they did know how to speak one of the local languages, at least a little. They didn't seem particularly comfortable with it, though.

The stranger looked at Ashton confused. Of course, anyone would be in this scenario.

But after seeing Ashton be so relaxed, they slowly started to eat, wolfing down the meat and finishing it in a few bites before looking at Ashton expectingly. Well, they were just a lot larger than Ashton was, so it must not have been particularly filling to them. He threw them another piece, and watched as they quickly devoured that as well. They were just eating it so happily that Ashton almost forgot they held a knife to his throat just a moment ago.

Ashton sighed, dropping his head into his knees. "What am I even doing?" he whispered to himself.

"What... you?" the stranger finally asked. "Not hapi."

This did confirm to Ashton that they had just barely picked up parts of the language. He figured he should be simply with it, then.

"Not hapi. Human. Not from Lumia. What are you?"

The stranger thought about it for a few moments, trying to make sure they understood Ashton right. "Not hapi... Ganta. Not... uhm... lumia."

So they really weren't from this world, either? But how were they here then? Were they the victim of another summoner, or were there other ways to travel between the worlds?

Ashton actually got pretty excited at the idea. Maybe if he was able to learn about that, then he could escape from the summoner. Get back home. Or, at the very least, he could maybe figure out how to sever part of the connection between them.

He looked at the knife that he had snatched from the stranger, and slowly held it against his throat. "Why?" he asked, purposefully overplaying a puzzled expression. Hapi had different natural facial expressions to humans, but maybe this person was more similar.

The stranger thought about the answer for a few moments again, though the finally just replied with what Ashton already figured. "Not hapi."

That made sense. If you suddenly saw someone that belonged to a clearly different species to the people of this world, when you didn't belong to this world, either, a lot of thoughts ran through your mind. Usually, Ashton wouldn't be that aggressive, but it wasn't like he didn't get it.

The people of this world were... rather ruthless. They were cold in certain ways, and overly emotional in others. They had extremely low empathy. They could still love others, and they formed tribes and towns and lived alongside each other, but always focused on themselves at the end of the day. Sure, there were humans like that as well, but even the 'kind' hapis were distrustful, mean, and deeply bitter when you were someone new. That was why most hapi communities were actually fairly small and tight-knit.

In a world like this, looking the way this stranger did, not even really knowing the language, it must have been really hard to get around. Being extra careful just made sense in that sort of scenario.

But it was clear that the stranger wasn't like that inherently. They were actually shockingly trusting, and just started happily eating and looking at Ashton with wide, open eyes.

He gave them some more to eat a few times, and then gave them some water from his flask. There was a stream nearby, so that wasn't an issue. It was actually kind of nice to be around someone this trusting. It had been a while.

But still, this was an issue. Ashton couldn't just get distracted with something like this. He had to get a bunch of things for the summoner from across the continent, and then periodically bring them back.

Since he was one of the few summons that could speak to others, Ashton also was the one that had to go and find information from across the continent as well. He had been given a long list, and had to make sure he hit his 'quota'.

He was ahead of the list for now, but trying to learn information about how to escape from the summoner would probably slow him down considerably.

Plus... there was the language-barrier. Would it be worth it to spend the time to either teach this person a local language or, which was more likely, learn theirs?

Ashton didn't even know if this was really someone that could help him figure something out. It might just be a waste of complete time. For all he knew, this was just a hapi that fell victim to a spell that changed their body and turned their head to mush, and their 'language' was nothing but gibberish.

But... seeing them kneel there, on the forest ground, was just weirdly comforting. This was the first person he had met, outside of the other summons, that he didn't feel on edge around.

Ashton sighed and scratched his scruffy, unkempt beard. And then, he stood up and grabbed the staff from next to him. It wasn't anything special, just a simple staff that he picked up somewhere. He should really get something better, but with how often the staffs he used broke, it felt like a waste to spend the little money he had access to on something like this.

As Ashton grabbed the staff, the stranger flinched. They jumped up off the ground as well, and Ashton just rolled his eyes.

He threw the knife on the ground in front of them. They weren't a threat to Ashton right now anyway, so he might as well give it back if it made them feel more at ease.

"Follow," Ashton said, waving them toward himself. He couldn't go back to sleep right now anyway. He could still get tired when he wanted to, but whenever Ashton was met with even the slightest bit of adrenaline, he fully woke up like he had just had the best sleep in his life. It was convenient at times, but usually pretty annoying. This magically-created body was just... interesting.

The stranger followed after Ashton as he walked. It was clear that they were still hungry, but he couldn't just give them all of his food.

So, instead, he climbed up one of the nearby trees, and threw down some of the fruit growing off it. They were a little similar to oversized coconuts in the way that they had a hard outer shell, though they were filled with soft, somewhat banana-like fruit. The shell had some medicinal properties, too, so Ashton wanted to grab some to make potions out of if needed.

The stranger looked at him, confused, but reacted excitedly when Ashton hit one against a tree and then pulled it open. To make sure they knew it was safe to eat, he grabbed some himself first and placed it into his mouth.

Almost instantly, the stranger tore into the soft fruit and filled their mouth completely.

Whenever they were done eating, Ashton cleaned away the rest of the leftover fruit and then cracked the useful parts of the shell up a bit to stash away for potion-making.

But as he put his bag back on his back, the stranger looked at him. They smiled broadly, and pointed at themselves.

"Oka."

Ashton raised his eyebrow. "Hm? Oka?"

They nodded a few times, excitedly. "Oka. Me."

"Oh, you're Oka. Oka of the ganta people. Got it," Ashton nodded, not thinking about it much.

And then, the stranger pointed at Ashton with a curious smile.

"... My name?" he felt weirdly nervous. He hadn't said his own name in a while. He cleared his throat, and pointed at himself the same way Oka had. "Ashton."

Oka smiled excitedly. "Ashton. Ashton so la-re human."
