Anomalous realms could be quite bizarre.

Among the strangest things about them was their tendency to conjure false human beings. The Fortress Dungeon was one such realm, and countless others were scattered throughout the empire.

There were numerous theories and explanations for why dungeons worked this way, but ultimately, it didn’t really matter. Nothing but the reward was actually real.

Still… There were some dungeons that pushed the boundaries. Real or not…

It was quite disturbing to play the role of the villain.

Both in the underground, and in the surroundings of Starhold, there were countless passages. Many had been claimed by different factions. The rules were different from how they were in the Northern Belt, and private ownership was far more common.

Some factions preferred to keep things for themselves, only giving access to their own members, but a good number of them publicly sold access to whatever dungeons they got their hands on.

Freddy had underestimated just how much of Starhold was underground. Most of the underground complexes were private, but there were public tunnels that allowed one to travel between destinations.

The dungeon they were headed to was owned by some large conglomerate Freddy had never heard of, and located three steps into the interspace. They pulled up, Vladimir handed over a ticket, and moments later, they were walking through a large pair of steel doors; the entrance to the dungeon.

In short—this realm was almost exactly like the Fortress Dungeon.

Just… Well.

The roles were swapped.

Vladimir walked into a snowy pass between two steep mountains, a frigid snowstorm howling. He was clad in nearly pitch-black synthetic gear, carrying a gnarly wooden stick in his hand. Compared to the shovel that broke during the fight with Nahar… well, the scepter was better than nothing.

To his side stood Freddy, wearing similar armor, albeit a little bulkier. In his right hand, he carried a massive, gold-tinged sword, and in the other, a heavy scutum shield. The piece of defensive gear covered him nearly head to knee. He didn’t really need it for himself, but since he was playing the role of a tank, he needed something to keep Vladimir safe.

“If you pass that rock over there, the doors will close!” a man shouted from outside the dungeon. “You can have up to a maximum of five members with you. If you’re waiting for someone, make sure nobody steps over that threshold!”

Freddy nodded. “Its just the two of us.”

The man gave them an odd look, and then shrugged. “Suit yourselves.” He turned around and then whispered, “It’s your money and your life.”

Freddy waved forward, and walked towards the aforementioned rock. As soon as he passed it, the doors slammed shut with a loud bang.

Before them, about a hundred meters ahead, was a tall wall of stone. The pass through the mountains was tight, and the wall completely closed it off. Atop the wall, he spotted a number of armed guards—down below, a line of soldiers stood guard, weapons raised.

The scenario of the Mountain Pass Dungeon was simple. There were ten walls, each better defended than the last. They had to make it to the other side of at least five for the iron doors behind them to unlock.

Freddy raised his shield, and Vladimir stepped behind him to take cover.

The gear Freddy was using was just some cheap stuff he picked up on the way there. Well, nobody sane would call a 100-million dollar set of gear “cheap,” but compared to what he was used to, it was definitely on the lower end.

They started the delve, and it went about as expected.

Every wall had a number of troops, with a small number of them being the elites, and one being a commander. It was uncannily similar to the Fortress Dungeon, but that wasn’t a huge surprise.

Freddy moved in on the infantry while Vlad shot down the rangers.

The wall had several ballistas, with the rest of the defenders using bows and crossbows. Unfortunately for them, their helmets left their faces exposed.

Vladimir fired tiny shards of crystallized miasma, and as long as they as much as touched the skin of the target, their heads rotted away immediately. Meanwhile, Freddy dutifully fulfilled his role as a bulldozer, bowling the vanguard over and slashing them apart with the massive sword.

In minutes, there were no more defenders, allowing Freddy to simply pull the massive gate off the hinges.

“That strength of yours is utterly unnatural,” Vladimir commented.

“Of course it is,” Freddy scoffed. “I’ve taken more steroids and dubious concoctions than probably anyone else in history.”

Vlad raised an eyebrow. “That’s… well, on-brand for you, I guess, but aren’t you afraid of the side effects?”

Freddy smirked. “What side effects?” He broadly gestured at himself. “Remember?”

“I mean, sure, you can heal from the obvious damage, but you do realize that a lot of that stuff affects your neural pathways, right? And as far as I recall, not even supreme-quality healing can do much about shifts in brain chemistry.”

Freddy paused. “Huh… Shit. That’s actually a really good point.”

Vlad laughed. “Well, I’m sure it’s not a big deal.” Then, he whispered, “For strength like yours, I’d take that trade-off in a heartbeat.”

They continued.

The fights quickly ramped up, and it didn’t take long for them to become challenging. In fact, the second wall was already a massive jump in difficulty. And the reason why was simple—the last wall’s rangers had parts of their faces exposed. These ones barely had an eye slit.

Miasma was fantastic at damaging organic tissue, but against practically anything else? It was a bit lacking, to say the least. The pieces of crystallized miasma weighed practically nothing, so there was little to no force behind them, and while exposure to miasma could wither inorganic matter, the effect was unnoticeable in an actual fight.

“Shit!” Vlad cussed as he kept taking cover behind Freddy’s shield. There was a downpour of bolts and arrows headed their way. He fired small crystals again, but hitting the small slits in the helmet was easier said than done.

“Have you tried aiming?” Freddy goaded as he dutifully pushed the incoming soldiers away and blocked as many of the incoming ballista bolts as he could.

Vlad was too busy trying to survive to banter.

Freddy could probably conjure some crude blood metal constructs with raw essence manipulation and help out, but he’d have to put either the shield or the sword down to do that, and he was already barely holding the attackers back.

After an agonizing half an hour or slowly whittling the defenders down, they finally killed the last archer.

Vlad, gasping for breath, slowly sat down on a stone.

“You’re already tired!?” Freddy reeled in disbelief.

“You…” Vladimir paused to take a deep breath. “I’m getting the feeling you’re not exactly taking this seriously, are you?”

“I mean… it wasn’t really—”

“You’re not about to suggest that this fight wasn’t tough enough for you to give it your all, are you?”

Freddy opened his mouth, but Vlad interrupted him as he brought his arm forward and pointed at a spot on his biceps. “Do you see this?” There was a small scratch there. “A ballista bolt grazed me. The armor deflected the blade, but that’s still a massive piece of iron that hit me at high speed. There’s a bitch of a bruise under there right now. And I got lucky. An inch to the side, and I’d have taken a lot more of the force.

“It could have dislocated my shoulder. Could have torn muscle. Broke an arm. Hit a nerve. Or any combination of those things. I don’t have your talent, kid. If I get hurt, it's going to suck.”

“I know that.”

“Do you? Do you really? Tell me, what were you doing during the fight?”

“You saw very well what I did.”

“Did you?”

Freddy scowled. “I was busy trying to stop you from earning yourself an improvised porcupine cosplay.”

Vladimir stood back up. “Not busy enough to stop you from bantering.”

Rolling his eyes, Freddy groaned. “I made one little joke.”

“There’s no room for jokes in a fight. None. And what’s up with the way you fought? All you did was camp right in front of me while half-heartedly swinging those things around.”

“What exactly do you expect me to do?”

“I expect you to do everything you can, and you didn’t do that. You didn’t track the cycles of the ballistas or crossbowmen. Had you been paying better attention, you’d have spotted openings where you could let me do my thing while you focus on pushing the infantry back. And look at all those spears lying around—you could have easily thrown a few to help out.”

“I just… I didn’t expect I’d have to do any of that before at least the third wall…”

“Why?”

Freddy halted at that. “I mean… don’t take this the wrong way, but I… I guess I expected you to be a bit…”

“Stronger? Well I’m not.” He spread his arms out. “This is the peak of what I achieved the first time I was a two-star. Not very impressive, is it?” He sighed. “Look. I get that you’re used to being surrounded by freaks, but I’m going to need you to adjust here. Also, please, for the love of god, treat every fight you’re in seriously, whether you’re alone or not.

“You’re tough, but you’re not immortal. There is always a non-zero chance you’ll stumble upon a deviant with a freak talent that counters you somehow, and you only have to be caught with your pants down once. If you can do something, then do it!”

Freddy slowly nodded. “Sorry. You’re right. I don’t know what got into me.”

“Also, where’s that thing you used to empower your weapons in Repentawa?”

“Why do you ask?”

“I’m just curious if there’s a good reason you’re not using it.”

“There is… sort of.”

“Is the reason good enough to risk our lives over it?”

Freddy winced. “Not really.”

“Then fuckin’ use it, for god’s sake. The fights are only gonna get harder from here on out, and we have to win at least three more to be able to leave.”

Freddy remained silent for a long moment. Then, he stretched his arm out.

The Cycle Scythe coiled across his skin, seeping through his armor like a black sludge as it enveloped the sword. As soon as it did, the lips parted and laughed in mockery.

“Gasp!”

“A father figure!”

“Watch out, Freddy!”

“Don’t let this one die!”

The mouths cackled.

Freddy gritted his teeth.

Vladimir scowled as he looked between Freddy and the wicked thing in his grasp. “What the…?”

“Hey Vlad—” the Cycle Scythe started, but Freddy shouted in anger, slamming the sword into the ground. “SHUT UP!” He took a ragged breath. “Just shut up. Keep yapping, and I’m putting you away.”

“We know!”

“Do it!”

“After all—”

“—the harder the fight—”

“—the better the reward!”

“Ain’t that right, Vlad!?”

“Weak!”

“You’re so weak!”

“Struggle a bit!”

“You must fight like hell!”

“Like hell!”

“Toil!”

“Weak!”

“Work harder!”

“Weak!”

“You can’t be weak!”

“Those who are weak—”

“—should stay away from Freddy!”

The mouths suddenly went silent, and then snickered.

Vlad turned to face him. “Freddy… what the hell is that thing?”

Freddy remained quiet for a long second. Then, he tore the sword out of the ground. “Evil.” He walked forward. “It’s fucking evil, that’s what it is.”

After a long moment of hesitation, Vladimir followed. They walked to the next wall in silence, only followed by the giggling of the Cycle Scythe, and the wail of wind.

“Vladimir,” Freddy called.

“Mmh?”

“You want to run these dungeons just to earn money, right?”

“Well… Not just, obviously. But money is the biggest reason I’m here today.”

Freddy nodded. “Do you mind it if I take over for the rest of this dungeon?”

“All by yourself?”

“Yes.”

Vlad went silent for a long moment. “I’m guessing you think things would go faster if you didn’t have to protect me?”

Freddy reluctantly nodded.

The man glanced at the snickering construct of black coiled around the sword, and then slowly nodded. “I’m not going to earn much from my reward in that case, but I think it would be for the best, nonetheless. Go on, then. Let’s see what you can do.”

Freddy handed Vladimir the massive shield, and then, without pause, sprinted forward, rushing into the fray of the next wall.

***

Vladimir held the massive shield up while peeking around it. He scoffed.

It had been a long time since he felt like this.

Back when he had made his way up the ranks the first time around, he had experienced this emotion a million times. Staying back, cowering in safety, as his contemporaries rushed forward, shining brighter than the sun.

It was only as he watched those contemporaries drop dead over and over that his insecurities finally faded. Oh, sure, a couple of them made something of themselves before their recklessness could catch up to them, but in the end, he always made it further, and always achieved greater things.

And the reason why was simple—it was basic math.

Those who gambled repeatedly were bound to lose.

Or that was how it would be if the world made sense.

There were also people like Freddy Stern. Those who weren’t bound by common sense. Those who repeatedly took astronomical risks, and yet, still managed to make it out on top every single time. Those who transcended the boundaries everyone else was bound by.

Vladimir would never be such a person.

But even he could admit that he had played it too safe the first time around.

He was off to an admittedly shit start, though, having lost a gut-wrenching sum of money to bad luck.

Thankfully, his golden goose was still around to lay a different kind of egg.

Freddy ran towards the line of soldiers, sword ready to slash horizontally, doing little to nothing to actually defend himself. He suddenly slowed down, almost as if he were moving through water and not air, and then, a few seconds later, he sped up.

“What a bullshit spirit ability,” Vladimir scoffed. He wondered where the hell Freddy stumbled upon something like that. It was among the best spirit abilities Vladimir had ever heard of. And that wasn’t even mentioning the one that could summon the skeleton.

For a long moment, Vladimir wondered what Freddy was doing. He seemed to be about to swing the sword right at the fully armored vanguard. He cringed at the sight. That sword had been costly, and the kid was treating it like it was one of his single-use blood weapons.

Vladimir expected the sword to suffer heavy damage.

Instead, Freddy proceeded to cleave right through four soldiers, armor and all, as if he were cutting tofu apart. “Right. I wonder why I’m even surprised.”

That black thing wrapped around Freddy’s sword sent shivers down Vlad’s spine. It was eerie as hell, and it was yet another thing Vlad had never even heard of. He wasn’t sure what exactly it did, but obviously, it made whatever weapon it was applied to a lot more powerful.

Freddy chopped the soldiers on the ground down like was preparing the ingredients for a stew, and then leaped up the side of the mountain until he scaled the wall, where he proceeded to clear the top of the wall as well.

It was a bit depressing to see him complete the wall by himself in minutes. It made Vladimir wonder whether he could even be of any use to the boy in a fight.

Once he was done, Freddy carved up the door, and they repeated the same thing on the fourth wall.

Vlad had been hoping that he would be at least a little bit slower this time, but alas. Again, they were done in minutes, and they were off to wall five.

It took him a bit longer this time, but not much.

“He can do maybe two more walls by himself,” Vladimir judged. The difficulty would spike heavily after wall five. Still, he was more than happy with that. That would land them a hefty reward at the end.

Not that Vladimir would take any of it. He truly hadn’t deserved it this time around, as he had been of zero use. But that wouldn’t be the case in every dungeon. He would make sure to work hard so he could be of proper help in a fight, so that he at least wasn’t dead weight Freddy dragged around.

Wall six took Freddy about twenty minutes, and wall seven around half an hour, and there, the strength of the defenders was enough to land him some pretty serious injuries. The boss was a powerful halberdier in heavy armor, whose casual attack could send Freddy flying back dozens of meters, while his armor repelled all of Freddy’s attacks.

Frankly, Vladimir hadn’t expected Freddy to win the fight at all.

However, that bizarre thing around Freddy’s sword wasn’t done with its surprises.

The scratches Freddy left on the boss’s armor made the armor… bleed. As if that construct wasn’t eerie enough already. The bleeding effect wasn’t just for show, either, it appeared to gradually drain the armor’s durability, and eventually, it was enough for Freddy to break through, stabbing the boss in the chest and ending the fight.

I have to keep him happy by any means necessary, Vladimir decreed.

If he could do this much at the second star without even using his abilities, there was no doubt he would make it to the fifth star one day. Hell, the threat of the triple-block was already completely out of Vladimir’s mind. If anyone could break through that boundary, it would be him.

“Well done!” he shouted as he approached. “Honestly, I had doubted you would—”

“Blood Sacrifice.”

Vladimir’s steps halted.

He watched in fascination as a torrent of blood gathered above Freddy.

Why is he using that now?

From what he gathered, to conjure the massive skeleton, Freddy needed a vast amount of blood. But it seemed like he could activate it regardless of how much blood he gathered, it would just be smaller. He smiled. Maybe the next wall was still on the table, he thought—

And then, the skeleton finished summoning.

Far from giant, it was only about two meters tall.

Seriously…? That seems too tiny, even for the amount of blood it consumed.

“Let’s go, Bloodshed,” Freddy called the thing, and they proceeded to the next wall together. He seemed confident, so Vlad kept his mouth shut and followed, curious.

What happened next shook him to the core.

Freddy ran towards the vanguard while the skeleton stayed back.

Vladimir was curious about what it would do.

Bloodshed proceeded to raise a bony hand, palm out—and fired a projectile of blood. Before Vlad could even express his shock, the projectile struck a soldier on the wall—and then exploded with enough power to blow a dozen defenders into smithereens.

Vladimir stood back, jaw slack as the defenders scattered in terror.

The attacks didn’t end there. The skeleton fired several more shots, and then absorbed the blood from the corpses to seemingly replenish its power.

That is not normal in the slightest.

Freddy hadn’t even used a fraction of the blood he had used to summon it in Repentawa, and yet, the skeleton was displaying firepower easily on the level of a competent three-star caster.

What the flying fuck is that thing? Is that really the product of a spirit ability? One without a permanent cost, at that? How is that possible?

The ability to conjure a titanic skeleton was already something the imperial army would be highly interested in. This was on another level.

The duo of Freddy and Bloodshed tore through the eighth wall with ease, and before long, they marched to the ninth.

There, things weren’t so simple. The power of the footmen was reaching an extreme level, and even a full party of experienced two-stars would struggle here.

Bloodshed focused on disabling the balistae this time, and after a few shots, it stopped firing. Then, shockingly, it conjured a massive cleaver, and joined Freddy in the melee. If that wasn’t enough, it then used rubbery tendrils to restrain soldiers.

If it were just using brute force, that would be one thing, But this creature fought with a level of expertise that left Vladimir shocked to the core. Unless it had something to do with their core concept, sentient ether constructs were rarely skilled at combat. But it was as if this skeleton had been trained in combat for decades.

The ninth wall took the duo a while to finish, but alas, they managed to complete it.

And then, they proceeded to go to the tenth wall.

Usually, he would have stopped Freddy. The final wall wasn’t something to be taken lightly. But his curiosity was set ablaze. He needed to see how they fared.

The fight started cautiously. Again, Bloodshed focused on taking out the biggest ranged threats, but it struggled. Freddy held his ground somehow, but was being forced back. Each soldier was easily as strong as an ordinary peak two-star, and yet, somehow, Freddy still managed to fight them off with that cursed weapon of his.

The defenders fought using complex tactics, shieldwalls, formations, and the rangers were well-protected. Freddy suffered devastating blows over and over, but even when he did, Bloodshed used an explosive attack on a couple of bunched up defenders, and that provided enough healing to keep Freddy together.

For hours, the two whittled the defenses down. The soldiers rarely showed any kind of emotion, but by the end, their despair was palpable. The boss was powerful enough to make three-stars think twice, and yet, Freddy casually chipped away at its armor, and then its body, each tiny cut leaving a hemorrhaging wound that bled like a faucet.

He was untiring and relentless, more akin to a natural disaster than a living man.

Throughout the whole thing, Vladimir sat back, watching, an anxiety building inside him.

He had known Freddy was strong. But this… This was a problem.

It was one thing for Freddy to be fated to become an Overlord. And it was another thing altogether for him to have potential rivaling—no…

Far surpassing the empress.

Eventually, with the numerous soldiers standing on their last legs, the skeleton marched forward, leaping at the boss and using all it had left to restrain it, giving Freddy just enough of an opening to slice the warrior’s wrist open. Added to all the other wounds, and with the empowered bleeding, it was enough to sap their boss of the last of its strength, and a few moments later, it fell, its life abandoning it.

The fight ended with Freddy’s inevitable victory over the worn-out soldiers, and they headed back towards the steel doors.

“Hey, Freddy,” Vladimir called. “About that deal… I’m sorry for putting you on the spot like that. I think I’ve changed my mind.”

“What? About what?”

He smiled tightly. “I don’t think we should party up. The difference between us is just too big. Even if we split the profit ninety-nine to one, I don’t think I can justify taking any part of what you earn. I’d just be exploiting you if I did that.”

Freddy evenly gazed at him. “I see. It’s a shame you feel that way.”

“You’re not denying it, though.” Vladimir chuckled. “But this might be for the best. Besides, I’m happy enough if we just stay friends.”

Freddy nodded and offered the man a fistbump.

Vladimir hesitated for a long moment before accepting the gesture.

Once they made it to the steel doors, a light lit up before both of them.

Vladimir received a small pouch of coins, and Freddy received some kind of bracelet. He checked it over. “I’ll have to see what this thing does, but I’m sure it has a nice effect. I need some cursed items, anyway.”

Vladimir chuckled as he bounced the pitiful bundle of silver coins in his hand. “I’m sure it’s great.”

For the rest of that day, Vladimir helped guide Freddy as the young man bought himself a luxury apartment close to the center of the city. He had considered going for one of the floating properties, but they were brutally expensive, enough to put them outside even Freddy’s budget.

Not that the apartment had been cheap. It was absolutely stunning, and heavily spatially expanded, with a private gathering chamber, a training room, and an assortment of other luxuries, but still, the 11-billion dollar price tag was no joke. A similar apartment in Pittersville would cost a fraction of the price.

That evening, they went out for drinks, just casually resting and chatting about random topics while trying to take their minds off the whole organ sale disaster. Eventually, they parted ways.

“Hey Vlad. I had a fun time today. Thanks for taking me to that dungeon. “

“No problem. If you ever need help getting access to dungeons, you know where to find me.”

Freddy grinned, offering him an enthusiastic handshake. “I really appreciate everything you’ve done for me. I mean it.”

Vladimir smiled tightly. “No problem, Freddy.”

Freddy’s grip tightened. “I’ll make sure to pay it back one day. I promise.”

“Damn right you will, you bastard.”

They laughed.

Freddy turned to leave, but Vladimir called out, “And hey, Freddy…”

“Hmm?”

He chewed on his words for a long moment. “Being talented is good, but just be careful, all right? There are many people out there who don’t like it when someone outshines them.”

Freddy looked at him for a long moment, before returning a slow nod. “I’ll keep that in mind. Have a good night, man. I’ll see you around.”

The two of them parted ways, leaving Vladimir alone with his thoughts.

“When she comes for you…” he muttered, “ I hope you win.”
