"The test is usually fairly simple,” the dean said as he stood and circled the desk to lean against the side of the table. “In a nutshell, I cast the spell, and you fall into something of a trance. “How come you do not see what I see?” Arielle asked.

“Because if I did, fewer people would agree to the test,” he said. "After all, people value their secrets more than they value their lives sometimes. Also, that would turn it into a memory reading spell, which, as you know, makes things a little more complicated than they should be.”

Arielle was a little relieved at that. She had a lot of secrets, though most of them, the dean already seemed to know about. It was a wonder he couldn't actually read minds, because he seemed to be able to deduce things fairly quickly and accurately.

"But as I told you before, your situation is a lot more complicated," he said. "Simple illusions don't work on you. Neither do complex ones. So I had to get creative."

"Well, a combination of things I have been working on. You know, I used to work at the Luxarium, doing research. Do you know what my field was?”

She shook her head.

“Evanescent magic,” he said. “It deals with things that can never be truly known, and things whose existence depends on being unknown. Have you ever heard of anything like that?”

Arielle was about to shake her head, but then she remembered the Unmaker. The mystery of its presence and the fact that no one knew much about it, almost by design. It seemed related to the fact that Vacu existed in dead space, that she could only find the Drift by venturing into the abyss.

All those things were hidden by default.

Luckily, the dean wasn't awaiting her response as he forged on.

“It’s a paradox that some magical entities occupy,” he said. “We can never know what they are or how they operate. Sometimes we don't even know if they exist because they remove themselves from memory, leaving only the faintest traces behind. My job was helping to develop a way to uncover them.” He chuckled. “It was hellish research that felt like twisting in the wind pointlessly for hours, but eventually I got something out of it. It helped me develop the illusion spell I'm going to use on you. It’s a spell that makes you forget that there is a spell, which should help you believe that everything you see is real. I've tried it on a couple of people who were not as impervious to illusions as you are, but who had some resistance to it, and it worked on them. Now, as this is a memory-tampering spell, at least a temporary one within the enclosed margins of the illusion, I will need your permission to cast it. Know that I'm a skilled memory mage, though not a truth seeker, and there will be no damage to your mind. But the entire test relies on you being under the thrall for as long as possible.”

Arielle nodded. She was also curious as to how the memory magic worked and wanted to copy it.

“We can try,” she remarked. “But what if it doesn’t work?”

“If it doesn’t work, then we'll likely schedule some kind of follow-up. Alright?"

"Alright."

“Good. First I’m going to cast the analysis spell,” he said as the orbs in the room began to gather, glittering. Luxa stabilized with Massa, and a bit of Vacu, circling her. He murmured foreign words, and she paid attention to the spell form, though she tried to pretend she wasn’t looking. It was a similar composition to Elric’s [Cathedral of the Living Light] spell but with a different structure and extremely complex.

It seemed that he had combined the spell forms, so she couldn't tell which part affected her memory and which part was simply for the illusion. Or was all this just for the analysis?

She was so busy trying to memorize and understand the spell structure that she almost didn’t notice when it began to work.

It was a strange feeling. The dean began to count, and with each word his voice moved farther and farther away. Arielle's grip on reality began to loosen until she was no longer sure where she was or what time period she was in.

All she knew was that she was falling.

There were still orbs in her vision, but she didn't know what they were for. She knew that they affected her in some way. She would only have to reach out and break them.

But she didn't.

She let the spell take her deep into the depths of her own mind.

It was the first day of school for Arielle.
