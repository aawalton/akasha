When I woke again, the world was quiet.

No thunder-steps, no robe-shadows. Just earth hugging me, light trickling down, and the ache.

Oh god, the ache.

I felt my broken stalk, a jagged, sensitive wound halfway up. Pain wasn’t abstract—it was me. Every fiber screaming: you’re cracked. You’re fragile. You’re breakable.

And yet… I was alive. And still a grass.

That was the terrifying part. That the pain was so real. That this wasn’t a dream I could shrug off.

My strange sight stretched, sharp for a meter around but then becoming increasingly blurry and strange as distance opened up.

I was surrounded by the same giant forest of leaf blades. Dozens, hundreds of tall green spears shooting up around me. Some were bent, crushed flat against the soil. Others stood tall but weary, their edges browned, yellowed, their luster gone.

And I felt… sad.

I didn’t know if grass could feel sadness, but staring at their brittle, dried bodies—neighbors who once grew beside me—it hit with the weight of a tiny funeral. Some of them had died. Just gone, their blades curled in defeat, their roots fading.

Others clung on, like me. I could feel them. The faint tug of water and nutrients still flowing, though a few had snapped halfway, jagged scars mirroring my own. My blade was cleanly cut, but I wasn’t alone in that brokenness. A whole patch of us stood like wounded soldiers in a battlefield of sandals and shadows.

Grass. Yes. But skyscraper-tall compared to my pathetic little form. I wasn’t even the short kid in class—I was the pebble under their shoes.

And farther off, through the haze, I glimpsed something else. A darker bulk. Brown, rising tall, with patches of green trembling on its arms. For a second, my mind whispered: tree. My human brain wanted to scream forest. But it was all distorted, as if I were peering through foggy glasses I didn’t own.

Thankfully, no humans. The people who stepped on me earlier were weirdly dressed, but my vision was so bad everything looked like a bad cosplay blur. Could’ve been Comic-Con for all I knew.

At least not in my corner. No trampling sandals, no robes swishing overhead. Small mercies.

Hours trickled by. At one point, my roots dragged in water from the soil, and let me just say: drinking through your toes? Humiliating. Like using a bendy straw you can’t control. Refreshing, yes. Dignified, no. I was left to stew in my own thoughts.

Everything beyond was blurry. Vision—or whatever plant-vision is—was terrible. Shapes smeared together, green into brown, light into shadow.

Nobody warned me newborn vision was this bad. In all the novels I used to read, reincarnators popped into their new lives with eagle eyes and zen-like calm, narrating their grand destinies like they were reading off a movie script.

Yeah. Right. What a steaming load of bull–

Reality check: waking up as a plant is messy.

I wanted to laugh and cry and panic all at once. So forgive me if I was not exuding grace. I was grass. Grass that could snap. Grass that could die.

My senses sucked, my so-called “limbs” twitched like damp spaghetti, and my first interaction with humanity had been getting stomped on.

The loneliness followed close behind. Watching the grass sway was pretty for the first five minutes, but the novelty faded fast.

Too quiet. No familiar voices. No background hum of cars or phone notifications. No home, no family. And worst—no distractions. Back then, whenever anxiety wrapped around me like barbed wire, I’d grab a romance paperback, or scroll aimlessly on my phone, or even binge-watch a k-drama and pretend my problems weren’t real. Now? No Wi-Fi. No books. No comfort. Just… dirt.

I would’ve happily bartered away a root—heck, two roots—for my favorite creased paperback. Or a bowl of cookie-dough ice cream with rainbow sprinkles. And marshmallows. And maybe those tiny chocolate chips.

Instead, the ache grew, heavy and insistent, and panic coiled tight in me.

“Okay, okay, deep breath.” Except—yeah. No lungs. So what, deep light? Deep water? Fine. Improvised breathing exercises, it is.

“Green box?” I called out in my head, desperate for any new distraction. “Hey, glowing interface thing? Still there?”

Nothing. Just silence.

For a moment there I was thinking that I might have hallucinated it in my deliriousness.

I twitched my blade, trying to force that Flutter skill to activate. The snap of pain across my cracked stalk made me stop instantly. Okay. Bad idea. Abort mission.

Totally normal to panic, right? Not dramatic. Perfectly rational meltdown.

I focused on the simple things. Soil pressing against me. The faint taste of minerals sliding into my roots. The lazy warmth of sun pooling across my blade. Not breathing, exactly, but I turned it into a rhythm anyway. Drink in. Glow out. Drink in. Glow out.

The panic didn’t vanish, but it dimmed to a low buzz, like background static. I clung to it, rhythm steady as a lifeline.

And then—

Another pale little box flickered in my mind’s eye.

You Learned a new Skill!

Serenity [G] Lvl. 1

[ Sacrifice Evolution Credits for Complete System Connection: 2 / 50 ]

Heck yeah!

I just… stared. Or, whatever counts as “staring” when your entire face is a blade of grass. Relief flooded me—it wasn’t just my imagination. Something out there was responding.

The window hung for a moment before fading, leaving me oddly bereft. Then another one opened.

Serenity: By steadying yourself against overwhelming sensations, you can restore clarity and calm.

[ Sacrifice Evolution Credits for Complete System Connection: 2 / 50 ]

It shouldn’t have been comforting, but it was. Maybe it was the familiar shape of a screen. Or maybe it was because it was in plain, clear English. A small slice of home, coded in glowing letters.

Maybe it was the increase in my Evolution Credits. I don't know how it increased, but I was a little excited by the increase.

Still, I was worried it was all a hallucination, so I tried again. “System! Menu! Status! Skills! Stats! …Inventory?” I cycled through every keyword I could remember from every RPG, isekai, or gacha game I’d ever played. Nothing.

Just me shouting digital gibberish into the void.

Frustration bubbled. I knew I hadn’t imagined that screen. It lingered too long, felt too sharp.

Wait. Maybe that was the problem. Maybe I wasn’t on Earth anymore. No smartphones, no internet, no game menus. This wasn’t a VR headset. What would be the simplest, most natural way to… check yourself?

I paused, focused inward, and thought, Show me… me.

The air—or maybe my brain—shimmered, and then a faint screen flickered into being.

Name: ( ??? )Race: [G] Wild GrassVitality: 4/10 (Damaged)

Evolution Credits: (Locked)

Skills ( 2/3 ):

[ Sacrifice Evolution Credits for Complete System Connection: 2 / 50 ]

…That was it.

But it worked.

It was so obvious in hindsight. If I wanted to see my “status,” all I had to do was really focus on myself. Not screaming internet keywords into the void. Not bellowing “STATUS MENU OPEN SESAME” like an idiot. Just… intention. A quiet nudge on myself.

And seeing it? Equal parts terrifying and thrilling. For the first time since waking up in this grassy nightmare, I had proof: I wasn’t hallucinating.

Kind of underwhelming. But also… kind of thrilling?

I wasn’t shocked by the numbers. Vitality was low—I mean, duh, I’d been stepped on. Flattened by a sandal like the world’s saddest daisy chain. But the number itself… 4/10 was worrying.

That hit differently.

Did that mean I was technically less than half-dead? Like, walking around (well, rooting around) at 40% life support? That was a terrifying thought. And the way it glowed there, so clinical, so blunt—it felt like my very existence was being reduced to a battery percentage. Warning: low life, please plug in sunlight.

My stalk shivered.

Okay, note to self: top priority is to get that number up. Because being one unlucky stomp away from game over is not how I want my reincarnation arc to end. No tragic headlines, please. “Girl Dies Twice, This Time As Lawn.”

Ideas began bubbling up already. Photosynthesis marathons. Root juicing. Meditation spam. Maybe I could even find a way to leech nutrients off the grass next door (was that cannibalism? Plantibalism?). Desperate times, desperate roots.

And then my skills… Nothing fancy there. Wiggling and calming down. Not exactly legendary, but hey, better than nothing.

What threw me was the whole race thing. I wasn’t a baby this time, not really. I wasn’t lying around in diapers—I was planted.

Which begged the question: if this was the starter stage, what was next? Sprout? Shrub? Eventually… tree? Did I get to choose? Could I evolve into something awesome, like a mandrake that screams at people? Or was I doomed to forever be the background lawn in someone else’s heroic story?

That thought made my stalk itch.

And of course, my brain (or soul, or whatever powered me) wouldn’t let it go. I kept circling the idea. Stats. Skills. Interface. It was like every RPG I’d ever wasted a weekend on—but without the fun part where you get to pick your hair color, choose your charisma stat, and declare yourself destined for greatness. Nope. I had been handed the “Grass Girl Starter Pack” and told: good luck, have fun.

…Which got me spiraling.

So… does this mean my soul came along for the ride? It had to, right? Otherwise I wouldn’t be me. My brain was technically new—plant-brain 1.0—but my memories, sarcasm, and questionable life choices had carried over. So maybe it wasn’t brainpower keeping me coherent, maybe it was soul-power. Which… if true…

Wait. When my stalk grows bigger, do I get smarter? Will it increase my soul-power??

The thought made me genuinely giddy. Who hasn’t wished they could respawn better? Smarter, prettier, maybe actually good at math this time? Maybe this time around I wouldn’t peak in high school. Maybe I’d evolve into some dazzlingly brilliant Tree-Queen, wise as an oak, graceful as a willow, with leaves that fluttered like a crown in the sunlight.

Or maybe I’d get chewed on by a passing goat before I hit level two. Honestly, 50/50 odds.

The system itself wasn’t much help. “Serenity” sounded nice, but was it just “don’t panic 101,” or could I zen my way into enlightenment?

“Flutter” was even vaguer. Was it literally just leaf-wiggling? Could I weaponize it? Upgrade it into a slap attack. Smack an enemy in the face with a dramatic swoosh? The description didn’t say. Rude.

I pulled the screen back again just to test if it would reappear. It shimmered obediently, steady and smug. In plain English. Which was weirdly comforting. Like the universe threw me a bone—or in this case, a glowing green sticky note—to remind me I wasn’t completely lost.

But then my eyes landed on my Race. Maybe my focus

Race: Wild Grass.

Wild. Grass. It glared at me in bold green. I don’t know why, but it felt insulting, like the screen itself was laughing. Wild Grass. Not Noble Grass. Not Majestic Bamboo. Not even “Pretty Weed.” Just… wild. The kind of word you slap on weeds to make them sound cooler before you rip them out of your garden. It sat there in glowing green letters, practically mocking me.

Skills ( 2 / 3 )

The skills, too, were clearly limited. Two out of… three? That had to mean something. Three slots. Which begged the question: what happened when I learned more? Did I get to juggle them around? Trade “wiggle leaf” for “root sense”? Prioritize “serenity” over “sunbathing”? It felt like a cruel parody of a skill tree.

My inner gamer squealed. My inner human panicked. Both were equally loud.

But what really got under my blade was what wasn’t there.

No body stats. No strength, no intelligence, no charisma. Nothing. Just vitality. Was I so insignificant they didn’t even bother giving me numbers? And worse: no class. No profession.

Did that mean I could never get one? No cultivator? No sorceress? No demon-vine overlord? Please, please don’t let it mean “unemployed plant.” If this was going to be a destiny system, it better not pigeonhole me into decorative.

I imagined for a moment: me, the great and terrible Tree Witch, roots snaring enemies, leaves glowing in holy sunlight, blossoms raining down destruction… okay, maybe not destruction, but at least pollen-induced allergies. A girl can dream.

For now, though, all I had was mystery. And pain. And a status screen that mocked me with its simplicity.

Still… it was something.

Answers existed. Skills could grow. A class might be waiting. And if I had to, I’d root myself here until I found out.

“Step one,” I whispered in my head, “learn the rules of this weird little world.”

And maybe—just maybe—step two would be: take over the lawn.

My blade twitched, and if anyone had been around to hear, they might have mistaken it for an evil laugh.
