Chapter 48: Crag

As tired and cranky as she was, Jessica had a passion for learning and experiencing new things. Back before the days of the Academy, she’d often go traveling with her mother and grandfather to the outer provinces, where both of them would fill her head with oodles of new knowledge and drill her on it later. It had always been something she cherished, before pressure and expectations got to her.

Now, surrounded by a bunch of incredibly enthusiastic molekin with poor senses of boundaries, some of that old joy returned.

“Lookit you, you’re just as short as us!” said one older mole with graying fur and spectacles as she took Jessica by the arms and examined her. “And you got such lovely hair! My Mallah would be so jealous.”

They weren’t even all the way into the town proper, but all of them were accosted, poked, prodded, and gushed over. Raeleq didn’t seem to mind, but Raeva swatted at claws that poked her sides, and Ambrose looked like a trapped animal about to start snapping. Even the prisoners got the same treatment, like they were heroes too. It took Mulch several seconds of pulling his fellow moles off of the group before he could get in a word.

“Folks, folks, please. Our new Imperial friends here just took care of that big mama spider off the bend of Trail 431. Burned ‘em all, and got me out of a pickle, lemme tell you. They’re tired, sore, hungry, and need help. If the Empire takes care of us, we gotta take care of the Empire, right?” Mulch slapped the back of a larger mole with especially big front teeth.

Jessica wasn’t sure how she felt about how close they all were, nor their distinctive, earthy aroma. The molekin didn’t smell bad so much as…well, a bunch of sweaty miners, just a touch more musky than the laborers she’d met back on Amaria on family trips. They were friendly though, and only a couple of them hesitated when Mulch mentioned the Empire.

“What were you doing out there, Mulch?” A rickety, croaky voice demanded. The crowd parted, and an old, stooped mole leaning on a cane limped forward. “Why weren’t you at your post?”

Everyone turned to look at the man of the hour. He shrunk sheepishly. “Look, don’t be mad Elder Marphy.”

The collected moles groaned. Elder Marphy took a deep breath. “C’mon boy, just spit it out. Or else I’ll bring your ma in.”

Mulch shifted uncomfortably. “Well, I know we’re not supposed to drink or nothing anymore…But I had a stash. And that damned spider got me and wrapped me up like one of mama’s pastries. She was gonna eat me before they showed up and fought her off. They’re offworld heroes, sent by the Empire to help us with a task or two, and for us to help them out in return.”

Jessica wasn’t particularly adept at lying, and was even less comfortable with it. She knew they wouldn’t be able to just blab their true purpose to a bunch of strangers, but pretending they were agents of the Empire…Well, it had almost worked before. It might have, if petty personal grievances hadn’t gotten in the way. It could happen again, for all they knew. She feared that the number one thing to do would be to keep her mouth shut for the moment.

Luckily, it turned out that having a hoard of alcohol was far more interesting to the residents of Crag.

“Dammit Mulch,” Elder Marphy grumbled, “what have we talked about? You’re gonna get the rest of us in trouble if you’re not careful. Do you need a reminder of why the Empire doesn’t allow us alcohol?”

Mulch shook his head vigorously. “Nossir. I know the reasons, and I’ve learned my lesson this time, I promise. I just want to welcome our new friends in the right way and make sure we toast their heroism. After that, I’ll accept whatever punishment you wanna send my way.”

He even sounded genuine. Jessica half expected him to kick the ground while looking down and saying ‘shucks’. They didn’t know much about their new…friend? Co-conspirator? Whatever, they didn’t know much about them, but the more he talked, the more she trusted that he wasn’t out to get them, at the very least. After the last twenty-four hours, it was something of a relief.

The crowd muttered, but Elder Marphy let out a resigned sigh. “Ain’t no arguing with you. We’ll take care of our new friends, but you’re gonna be pulling overtime in the mines for your inability to stay dry.”

“What’s the big deal about alcohol?” Raeleq blurted out. “Why is it worth punishing? Doesn’t everyone drink?”

Dead silence.

“It’s the law,” said the Elder, puzzled. “That the Empire imposed just ten years ago, after the last rebellion. Is this some kind of a test?”

Ambrose stepped in smoothly. “We’re traveling agents and don’t have time to learn every local law and custom. Mulch has been a decent guide to us and we may require him further, so as of now, you are not to punish him. Am I clear?”

He had the perfect tone and body language for it, despite his shredded clothes and wounds everywhere. Jessica had been raised in luxury and power on the rise, while Ambrose’s family had been powerful once and were now on the decline. She was almost jealous of his ability to project scorn and importance into everything he did. Almost.

“Clear as crystal, sir,” Marphy said, a little colder than before.

Luthor cleared his throat and said, “We greatly appreciate your hospitality and understanding. We’re on an important mission, and we can’t afford to be slowed down too much. More than that…”

The Elder grimaced, as if appalled at his own manners. “Then without any further hoopla, let’s get you folks looked at. The spiders here aren’t as venomous as the ones deeper down, but I can’t imagine you’re feeling too great. Relax and take a load off, friends. Haley, Mitch, why don’t you show our guests to the good doctor? Mulch won’t be punished yet, but he still has to answer to his mama.”

The mole groaned but nodded. Two more pushed their way through the crowd. A larger one with bulging arm muscles and thick whiskers motioned with his claw. “Alright men, come with me, doctor and tailor first, I think, then maybe a nap and a good soak.”

“Ladies with me,” Haley, she assumed, squeaked. She was smaller than the rest, with longer hair pulled into a thick fishtail braid. She offered her claw to Raeva, who took it with an enthusiastic grin.

Just like that, the congregation of Crag dispersed, making room for their new guests to be guided deeper into town. Their chatter filled the long passage as they returned to their previous tasks. Jessica’s face burned with snippets of gossip she tried not to listen to. She focused on following Haley the adorable mole girl as they broke apart into a group of four and three.

“I always appreciated you molekin,” Dexi’Lynn said. “Hard workers, real welcoming, and easy to beat at cards without even needing to cheat. We’ll be safe here, ladies.”

“I’m not worried,” said Raeva, and Jessica believed her. The Bedamin Princess wasn’t afraid of much, and she’d always been a bit jealous of that confidence too. “I’ve got a good feeling about these folks. I know salt of the earth people when I meet them.”

“Why, thank you,” Haley squeaked, a little hop entering her steps. “We’re eager to be of any assistance to the Empire as we can. Ma and Pa say that your people saved us all those years ago, and help keep us from making the same mistakes!”

“What mistakes were those?” Jessica asked, hastily adding, “the more we know about the world while we’re here, the easier our mission will be.”

Raeva looked over her shoulders and nodded appreciatively her way. “Agreed, Haley. After we get patched up, we wanna hear everything.”

It was Dexi’Lynn who answered with a snort. “Drinking, gambling, anything fun. That’s the only problem with molekin. Kind people, love to eat, but no other real vices we share.”

Haley tensed, but said, “The doctor’s this way. He’ll have y’all right as rain in no time.”

She opened the door and led them down a stone-lined tunnel, into a comfortable place with a few open slabs. One mole with a crushed leg was passed out, but another in a white coat turned their way. “Oof, what in the deepmother’s name happened to you?”

“Spiders!” Haley answered brightly. “They saved Mulch, so Elder Marphy wants them taken care of.”

The doctor wiped his brow with the back of his claw. “That damned fool didn’t get hisself killed? Huh. Awright then, come in, and let’s get you taken care of. I’ve got the best medical cards in town.”

“What about the others?” Jessica asked, suddenly concerned. “Will they get good treatment too?”

“Relax,” said Dexi’Lynn, leaning against one of the slabs. “He’s just talking himself up. Healers are too important to the Empire to ignore. So each town has at least three stocked healers and doctors to take care of the population as needed. Ain’t that right, Doc?”

The doctor grunted and rolled up his sleeves. “Sure is. And we’ve had an easy week, so I’ve got plenty of charges left. Who’s hurt the worst?”

The act of healing them reminded Jessica of just how much the envenomed bites stung, when all of a sudden relief flooded her. It took two spells for each of them; one to ensure the venom had no lingering effects, and then an uncommon card that sped up their bodies’ natural rejuvenation to return to a more neutral state. The problem was, that kind of healing stung and left one both exhausted and hungry.

“Don’t you worry about that,” Haley said once they left. “We’ve been fixing to have a party of some kind for a while. Ain’t had nothing to celebrate though, so you folks really came at a great time.”

The next stop was a seamstress, which surprised her. Jessica had some of the least damage to her clothes due to her slippery nature, but Raeva and Ambrose especially needed something better. They were measured and asked about preferences about fabric. No one asked about what to make them, but she assumed it would be something they could use.

Each stop along the way, Raeva, Dexi’Lynn, and Haley chatted, while she found herself dragging worse and worse. It got bad enough that Raeva stopped them from going to their next stop.

“Hey, where can we get some rest?” she asked, looking over her shoulder at her friend. “I don’t think Jessica’s got much longer in her.”

“I do too,” she grumbled lifelessly, before flashing a rueful smile. “But I wouldn’t be opposed to a nap.”

“Not a problem at all,” said Haley, changing directions. “When we have you Imperial folks in town, there’s a couple of houses made up for you. They should’ve been done with cleaning it by now.”

Even through her fatigue, Jessica tried to pay attention to the way they’d gone, but it seemed like Crag was one long circle, with homes built along the outside walls and businesses in tunnels bored through the remaining stone on the inside. There was nowhere to go but around. That wasn’t to say it was homogenous, though. She greatly appreciated the rock sculptures they came across, and the glowing moss grown artfully on the wall that formed a picture of miner moles going to work.

She was so invested in this one moss mural that she jumped when Dexi’Lynn tugged on her shirt. “We’re here, Jess. C’mon, let’s get you to a bed before you pass out!” The kobold acted like they were old friends instead of her captors. It was kind of funny.

The house inside was better than she expected. While the walls may have been made of stone, the furniture was made of fine, polished wood and plush cushions. If the Empire was mostly humans and humanoid races, as SNAC had indicated, then it stood to reason that all Imperial safehouses would probably be similar. Another fact she tucked away for later.

Haley hopped forward and led them down a narrow hallway, with four doors leading to bedrooms along the way, and a toilet at the end of the hall. “Here you fine folks are. Grab a nap, take a bath, and clothes will be ready for you by the time the feast starts!”

She bowed and left them behind in the dim hallway.

Raeva stretched. “I guess we don’t have much choice, but I’m not complaining about the chance for a rest. I think we’ll be safe here, and we can regroup with the others in a bit. Mostly, I’m just glad to not be on the run and fighting for a change. That was starting to get a little old. Are you going to be a problem, Dexi?”

The kobold shook her head vigorously. “No no no, not at all! I’m one of you guys now, right? Team….What’s our team name?” She grinned, showing sharp canines in her odd, lizard-goatlike mouth.

“I don’t think we have one yet,” said Jessica. “Or that we need one.”

“Of course you need a team name!”

“Team Ransa,” said Raeva with a grin of her own. “Well, I’m going to pass out. Don’t make us regret trusting you. You doing okay, Jess?”

Was she? “I think so,” Jessica responded with a yawn that had her seeing spots. “Like you said, we’ll regroup and figure things out with the ship later. Good…night? Day? It’s impossible to tell here.”

They parted, and Jessica went inside the room she’d picked at random. It was slightly bigger than the cabin on the ship, but a lot more homey and earthy. A fire burned in the fireplace, keeping the place toasty. She trudged across the small room and fell onto the bed, laying on the slightly scratchy crocheted blanket.

They made it out alive. That was enough for now. There was still the spare Wildcard she hadn’t added to her deck, and getting the ship out of the Forlorn Pit without being torn apart by ghouls.

And, of course, her grandfather, still pissed and no doubt coming for them even now.

She could resist no longer, and fell asleep, thinking of Charles Vanderborn’s stern, disapproving gaze.
