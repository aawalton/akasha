Elarion observes the battle with cool calculation. Fell’s initial strike has failed to break through to the tunnel, despite the carnage that his blade creates with every slash. A shame, but not unexpected. More concerningly, his thrust has dug deeply enough that his retinue risks being surrounded.

Heatwave and Softrain were too heavily engaged on the left flank to disengage, while Markus and his companions had already vanished into the chaos on the right. They are not within easy reach of Fell.

Elarion nods. He will defend his ally. Fell has earned his aid over the last few months.

“Shieldwalls, defend,” he orders calmly, but loudly enough that every member of the retinue around him will hear. “Mages and archers, to me.”

His retinue is far more mage-heavy than any of the others, and they have the second highest proportion of archers, next to Softrain and Heatwave’s forces. In comparison, they have very few melee fighters, and all that they have are specialised in defence. For good reason.

The fighters lock shields and form their namesake battle formation, settling into their positions between the beasts and the rest of Elarion’s retinue.

“Mages, archers, fire at will on the flanks,” Elarion orders. “Do not let our ally be engulfed.”

After taking just a moment to ensure his orders are being followed and are having the desired effect, Elarion closes his eyes. The Skill he intends to use to control the battlefield takes both concentration and power – it’s not something he uses frequently.

Yet, despite how much he has to dedicate to it, Elarion feels more than a little satisfied that he has a good excuse. It is the most powerful single Skill in his arsenal, after all – and the centre of his Class.

Leaning down, he presses his palm to the earth, silently intoning the Skill’s activation: Frozen Forestheart.

Immediately, his mana is drained out of his pool. Elarion, like his father, has dedicated as many of his points into Wisdom and Intelligence as he dared – always resenting the Titanbends and his grandfather’s legacy that led him to need to at times prioritise Willpower instead – and therefore has a mana pool and regeneration rate that dwarfs even other nobles. Yet the drain of this Skill is such that it consumes half of his mana pool in the first two flickers of its activation, and the other half shortly afterwards.

He could stop here – the Skill will not take more from him than he willingly allows it – but no. Not yet. He has other options.

“Mana regeneration potion,” Elarion demands, not able to remove his hands from the earth or risk disrupting the Skill. The mage beside him tips one of the strongest mana potions they have into his mouth. Elarion gulps it back, then shivers as he feels his body briefly replenish with that vital energy – a moment before that too is drained away.

“Mana replenishment,” he orders next, and the process repeats. This time, the feeling of replenishment is stronger since it’s directly filling his mana storage, but even that isn’t enough for the greedy Skill.

“Another,” he commands. There is a hesitation from the mage next to him.

“My lord…”

“Another,” he commands firmly, lifting his eyes to glare at his father’s mage. Already, he can feel the last dregs of his renewed mana draining away. If he doesn’t get the potion in the next few minutes, the Skill will enter its next phase. “I know my limits,” he hisses.

The mage hesitates, no doubt weighing up her orders from his father to keep him safe and his orders to her.

Then, reluctantly nodding, Vianna pulls another potion out of her storage, quickly uncorking it and pressing it to Elarion’s lips.

The infusion of mana comes just in time – the last dregs of the previous potion and everything he’s regenerated since then are whipped out just as he feels the energy suffuse him.

Once the mana from his third and final potion is pulled from him, Elarion concentrates on the seed that has formed below the ground. Filled with a massive amount of mana, it pulses with increasing speed even as he presses it to reduce in size. Where others might think that it’s losing mana, the Ice Druid knows that it’s simply condensing it.

When the pulsing increases to a speed where it is no longer visible except for a very faint flickering, Elarion knows it’s time. He presses his intentions into the seed, using the small amount of mana that has regenerated in his core to trigger its growth. He then scrambles backwards.

Just in time. Below where his hands had been pressing, the earth bulges and then breaks as a thick shoot strikes upwards. He watches, rapt, as it rapidly grows in height, within flickers reaching the height of four men standing on each other’s shoulders. Its trunk is stocky, its branches deceptively slim. As for its construction, it’s a meld of wood and ice twisted together into something that offers both strength and flexibility. A wondrous combination – like his Class.

Stepping forwards as soon as the tree finishes growing, Elarion places his hand on the trunk, connecting with the spirit inside it. Not sapient exactly, it is nonetheless identifiable as something other. As its creator, however, he finds it easier than others would to make it obey.

He feels a thrill as the connection snaps into place – this is the strongest and most headstrong Frozen Forestheart he’s ever made. Perhaps adding those points to Willpower wasn’t a waste, he muses for a moment, then shakes away the thought and turns his attention back to the battle.

In the time it’s taken him to create his guardian, Fell’s forces have ground to a halt and been forced onto the defensive as a large Tier three reminiscent of a farm creature has begun attacking their lines like a living siege weapon. Its wide and sweeping horns act as deadly weapons; its heavy body and armoured hide seem to deflect even Fell’s enhanced blade.

Elarion concentrates on his Forestheart and sends it a command.

Invisible to perhaps any eyes but his own, a ripple of ice-magic is carried through the ground by roots of wood, and emerges just ahead of Fell’s front line as a forest of sharp ice spikes. They spear through a large number of beasts, killing many in an instant and injuring at least double that number. More satisfyingly, the sudden field of sharp spears deters even the Tier three from crashing into the shieldwall as it was obviously intending to do.

Was that you, Elarion? Fell asks through their Alliance Bond, his mental tone astonished.

It was, Elarion replies simply, but allows himself a little smugness as he sees the chaos – for the enemy – his action has caused.

Impressive, Valence compliments him. Can you do it again? Or anything else?

Of course, Elarion replies – as if he would have put so much mana into a creation that could generate only a single attack.

Quickly, they work out the best way of using the Forestheart’s capabilities, and Valence starts making steady progress towards the tunnel with Elarion offering defenses to the sides of his forces as well as covering fire – in between ripples of ice magic sent through its roots, Elarion directs it to volley clouds of ice-shards from its branches at the areas thick with beasts.

The shards do not individually create much damage, but they are fully capable of slaying a Tier one beast, and when they pierce the hide of a Tier two, they spread a cold aura that slows the beasts down and makes them easy prey for the fighters in Valence’s retinue. Meanwhile, the other mages and archers in his retinue continue steadily picking off beasts, pacing themselves, but nonetheless working their way methodically through the forces on either side of Valence’s own unit.

It’s working well.

Too well.

Elarion is unsurprised when the enemy changes the battlefield – though he wasn’t expecting two Tier threes to be sent his way. He clearly is more of a threat to the Tier four than he flattered himself to be.

“Brace for impact!” he warns as a beast that towers over even the other Tier threes raises its nose tentacle and trumpets loudly, its large ears waving in the air. At its side, the Tier three that had been attacking Valence’s forces changes its target, its loud bellows filling the air and driving the beasts around it into a frenzy.

Pressing a command into his Forestheart, Elarion triggers a ripple of ice that creates a forest of icy spears between his retinue and the Tier threes.

At least, that is what should have happened.

Instead, even as the two Tier threes break into a charge, covering the ground between their starting points and his forces with alarming speed, Elarion feels the ripple of ice is disrupted by some unseen enemy. Some of the intended icy spears do emerge from the ground, but it is far from the comprehensive field that he intended.

More worrying are the way sections of the Forestheart’s root network have become unreachable – as if they’ve been destroyed or cut off.

As the earth mounds up and a flat and black creature emerges from the earth only to dive back down into it a moment later, Elarion’s heart sinks.

Make that three Tier threes.

The pikeheads bury themselves in the Tier three’s fur, but go no further – this beast’s skin defenses are just too good. All that most of these pikes have done is contribute to halting the Tier three in its tracks, and two of the pikes have broken in pursuit of that temporary goal. It doesn’t help that they can’t dedicate all their fighters to the task – they would be overrun by the Tier twos and Tier ones that follow in this beast’s wake if they did.

Taking advantage of its brief halt, Josiah, Layton, and the other mages do their best to repeat the strategy that worked so well with previous Tier threes – using vines and ice to slow and bind them in place.

It sweeps its head one way and then the other, the multiple tines of its rack tearing at the plant vines with ease even as its hide sheds the water that covers it before the liquid has the chance to freeze.

At the same time, it lowers its head, pikes snapping or slipping away with its movement. Josiah winces as, before anyone has any time to brace, it slams straight into the shieldwall.

The shieldwall is collectively forced back a step, but the Tier three is briefly stunned as it triggers a defensive enchantment on the shields, strengthened by just how many are interlocked.

Taking their chance, everyone nearby piles in with whatever Skills or weapons they have available to them. Josiah doesn’t have any Skills that are able to damage this Tier three directly, not with its armour, but he presses more magic into the clouds above, having them dump more water upon the beast so other mages have more material to work with.

Let’s just kill it, he suggests to Layton. Trying to keep it alive is just going to lead to our own forces dying.

That’s just as much a challenge as trying to keep it alive. If only we had Earth Mages here – they could drop it into a pit or something, Layton suggests, strain in his mental voice. Josiah can see how he’s trying to reduce the temperature of the water as fast as possible to once more try to freeze the beast in place. Nonetheless, Josiah sees Layton’s point. Apart from its ability to grow its antlers, the beast hasn’t proven to have any other active magic. The pit wouldn’t even need to be that big – a bog that prevented it from using its legs would be sufficient. Meanwhile, its hide is frustratingly thick and resistant to both physical weapons and magic.

Then maybe Markus can come and help out, Josiah points out. His eyes flicker over to where Markus’ forces are, on the opposite side of the battlefield. His brief hopes flicker as he sees that Markus himself is currently in battle with a large, reptilian predator and his Tier three Bonded is facing another beast very similar to her in the air. Of course he’s occupied, he thinks bitterly.

Layton, Elarion’s in trouble.

We need to help him, Layton replies with determination.

But how? Josiah demands. This Tier three is bad enough.
