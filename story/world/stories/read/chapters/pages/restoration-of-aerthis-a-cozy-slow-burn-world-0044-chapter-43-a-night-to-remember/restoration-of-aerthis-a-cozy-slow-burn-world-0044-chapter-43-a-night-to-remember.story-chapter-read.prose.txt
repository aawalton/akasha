The name still lingered in the air, soft and new, a flame that had just caught and its sparks were still shining showering the place.

Aru.

The girl in question seemed to be deep in thought over something. Mulling over her new name, perhaps?

Pondering things Elynara could only guess at. She was just happy Aru had accepted the name – and seemed happy to get it too.

But as the silence dragged out, and the machine girl seemed to reach some sort of conclusion, what instead filled her face was determination.

Elynara had expected a nod, maybe a small acknowledgment. What she got instead made her blink.

The girl’s eyes brightened, a sudden surge of blue light rippling through the lines of her frame. Then she got up, approached Elynara, and was about to say something, but reconsidered.

She was tall. And Elynara was sitting down too. And noticing Elynara craning up her head and staring in confusion… Aru knelt down before her instead.

Aru clasped her hands in her own, staring back at eye level now. And the bright blue spark returned. Servos whirred in her shoulders as she straightened, voice ringing with clear conviction.

“There is just one thing I disagree with, from all the things you have said today. You are not unworthy! Do not even suggest such a thing!”

Elynara froze, halfway through a confused smile.

Aru went on, her tone rising with an earnestness that shook the quiet.

“You are my savior – my creator – and I would be honored! To bear the name given by my savior. Aru will do perfect! Yes, Aru will do just fine!”

The burst of emotion hit the still night like a spark in oil. Starpuff shot upward, startled, letting out an indignant chirp that made Elynara lose it entirely.

Laughter slipped out of her before she could stop it. It was just so bright, sudden, alive.

Aru stopped mid-motion. The light in her eyes flickered down to a muted glow, her voice softening at once.

“Did I err?”

Elynara waved her hand, trying to breathe through her grin.

“No, no–gods, no. I just wasn’t expecting the whole declaration of devotion. Relax, I was joking.”

The words only deepened the girl’s confusion. Her servos made a faint clicking noise, the sound Elynara had come to associate with hesitation.

“You… were joking,” Aru repeated, the phrase uncertain in her mouth.

Elynara leaned back on her heels, wiping her eyes. “That’s right. Humans joke when they’re teasing or playing. It’s harmless.”

“I see.” Aru’s tone steadied, though the faint tremor in it lingered. Her hands flexed open and closed, metallic fingers tracing small, uncertain motions against her thigh.

“My response was… excessive.”

Elynara smiled gently.

“A little dramatic, maybe. But I liked it.”

The girl tilted her head again, the gesture so tentative it almost hurt to watch. The expression that followed wasn’t confusion anymore – it was embarrassment, pure and visible even through the slightly metallic sheen of her faceplates.

The sight softened something in Elynara’s chest.

“Come here,” she said quietly.

Aru blinked.

“Come where?”

“Closer.”

When she didn’t move, Elynara reached across the small space between them and tugged her forward, careful but firm. The motion startled Aru enough that she stiffened again, balance perfect but posture locked.

Elynara ignored it and pulled her into her own lap anyhow. Then she closed her hands around the – unexpectedly soft and warm – faintly humming surface of Aru’s chest.

“There,” she murmured. “That’s better.”

The girl’s voice came hesitant.

“You are… embracing me.”

“Mhm.” Elynara’s tone was calm, almost teasing again. “That’s what people do when they mean what they say. It’s how we share warmth. You can hug me back, you know. It’s allowed.”

Aru didn’t answer.

For a long moment, she knelt there, perfectly still, arms rigid at her sides. The faint hum of her core filled the quiet, steady and nervous all at once. Starpuff drifted down nearby, chirping softly, its glow dimming to match the calm around them.

Then, slowly, Aru’s joints relaxed. Her arms lifted, uncertain, and wrapped around Elynara’s shoulders. The metal of her plating was warm, from life rediscovering flow, if Elynara were to guess. The first touch was careful, almost clinical. Then it steadied, gentle pressure finding balance.

Elynara closed her eyes and let out a slow breath.

“See? That’s not so bad.”

Aru’s voice dropped to a whisper.

“I was afraid I might harm you.”

“You won’t,” Elynara said. “You’re too careful for that.”

The air between them softened into silence. For once, Elynara didn’t feel the need to fill it. She could feel Aru’s core pulse through the contact. It was steady, rhythmic, matching the faint beat of her own heart. Metal and mana, warmth and breath. Two lives where there should have been none.

When Aru finally spoke again, the words were quieter and thoughtful.

“It feels… strange.”

“What does?”

“This,” she said. “Contact. Connection. The physical exchange of… reassurance.”

“That’s one way to describe a hug,” Elynara said, smiling into her shoulder.

Aru’s head tilted slightly against hers.

“Do you do this often?”

“Only with people I trust,” Elynara said. “My friends. And now I count a certain machine girl among them.”

The sound that came from Aru wasn’t quite laughter, but close. A hum of amusement ran through her core, subtle but unmistakable.

Elynara pulled back enough to see her face. The light in Aru’s eyes had shifted again – still blue, but softer now, threaded with faint gold where the reflection of the firelight caught.

“You’re learning fast,” Elynara said.

Aru considered that.

“I am learning… to be.”

“That’s a good beginning,” Elynara said. “You don’t have to figure out the rest right away.”

They stayed near the fire for a long while after that, neither speaking. The night had grown cooler, but the warmth between them lingered. Starpuff fluttered in slow loops around them, leaving trails of soft light through the air, until finally it landed on Aru’s shoulder.

Aru looked at it curiously.

“It trusts me.”

“It does,” Elynara said. “You earned that.”

The girl turned her gaze back to the flames, her voice low.

“Then perhaps… I am alive enough to be trusted.”

Elynara smiled, a cheeky twinkle in her eyes.

“You were alive the moment you chose to care.”

The words hung in the air, simple and true. Aru blinked once, the faint glow behind her eyes deepening, as though storing the phrase somewhere precious.

When Elynara finally released her, the guardian’s hands lingered a moment longer before letting go.

“Thank you,” Aru said quietly.

“For what?”

“For being kind. For showing me how to be… me.”

Elynara reached up and tapped the center of Aru’s chest where the faint pulse of light glowed through the plating.

“You already were. You just needed someone to notice.”

Aru looked down at her, eyes bright again, shining with warmth.

Elynara smiled up at her, tired but content.

“Come on, Aru. Let’s get some rest. The world’s not going to rebuild itself.”

Aru’s head inclined slightly.

“Then we rebuild it together.”

“Exactly.”

The fire crackled behind them, throwing sparks into the cool night. Starpuff twirled once in lazy orbit, a single bright ember caught in the wind.

The night settled gently around them. The air was cool, and the ruins quiet. Yet they did not feel so empty anymore. Faint light from the fire danced across metal and stone, tracing warmth into the once-lifeless space.

Elynara leaned back on her hands, watching the sparks rise. Aru sat close beside her, the hum of her core steady and calm.

The silence between them was only temporary, a breathing room to rest and relax.

“So,” Elynara said, tilting her head toward her companion, “now that you have a name, what do you think of it?”

Aru turned her gaze toward the fire, blue light flickering across her features.

“It feels… right,” she said after a pause. “It belongs.”

“Good.” Elynara smiled. “It suits you.”

Starpuff trilled from where it hovered above the flames, clearly taking credit for something.

“Oh, don’t start,” Elynara said, flicking a small ember toward it. “You didn’t name her.”

The puff dodged, puffing itself up in mock offense before darting behind Aru’s shoulder to hide.

Aru’s laugh was soft but genuine. And it sounded just like a bright chime Elynara had already grown fond of.

“It is a curious creature,” she said, watching Starpuff bob in playful circles.

“Curious is one word for it,” Elynara said. “Menace is another.”

The puff spun once in protest, earning another laugh from both of them.

They talked after that – about everything and nothing. Elynara coaxed a tiny flame to life above her palm, twisting it into shapes as she spoke about her life so far. About Oltenar, the Guild, the orphanage, Mina, Father Alaric, and her fire shows there.

Aru leaned forward, eyes bright with fascination, watching each flicker move through the air.

“It does make me wonder. How do you feel about now?” Elynara asked, letting the flame fold into a small spiral before she closed her hand and let it vanish.

“I know some theory. And, just like with hugs, I saw people do it before, but never understood.” Aru said, her voice quiet with awe. “But to see it… to feel the energy shift in the air… that is new.”

Elynara grinned.

“It’s not complicated once you get used to it. You just have to listen. Magic’s always there. Waiting for someone to notice.”

Aru tilted her head, processing the words.

“Waiting to be noticed,” she repeated. “That is… poetic.”

“Everything on Aerthis is a little poetic,” Elynara said. “Even the messes.”

Starpuff chirped again, its glow pulsing in rhythm with her words. Elynara reached up and flicked it lightly on the nose, earning a delighted trill.

The small display left Aru smiling again. It was different from her earlier smiles, it was looser, less measured, almost human.

Every interaction making her less stiff, and more certain of her own existence.

Elynara found herself studying that expression more than the fire. She remembered her own early days here, when everything had felt impossible and miraculous at once. The same wonder now glimmered in Aru’s eyes, and it stirred something old and warm in her chest.

“You’re thinking deeply,” Aru said, noticing the pause.

Elynara shook her head, still smiling.

“Just remembering. I used to look at the world the way you’re looking at it now. Everything was new. Every sound, every bit of light – it all felt like a promise.”

Aru’s voice softened.

“And now?”

“Now,” Elynara said, staring into the fire, “it still feels like that sometimes. When I let it happen. Or when it catches me by surprise. Like you did, you wondrous girl.”

Aru's face tinted pink slightly, before she nodded slowly.

“Then I will learn to see it that way too.”

The words weren’t a statement. They were a quiet vow.

“Mhm. There is no hurry. The wonder, it feels quite nice. Letting it be is just fine, really.”

When the fire burned low, they rose and wandered through the ruin. The moons had climbed high, painting everything in their soft shimmering hues wherever they peeked through.

Faint, yet enough to see in their light.

And enough to wonder.

Elynara guided them through broken corridors and hollowed halls she already knew by heart, pointing out what she had discovered during her earlier explorations.

“There used to be gardens here,” she said, crouching beside a collapsed archway. “I found crates full of seeds in one of them. Old, dry, but they had a symbol on them – looked like a flower stylized into a circle.”

Aru crouched beside her, fingertips brushing the stone. The faint light from her core lit the cracks in the wall, and for a moment, something flickered behind her eyes. A memory surfacing.

“That was the emblem for the Environmental Maintenance Division,” she said quietly. “They oversaw ecological preservation within city sectors. We were their assigned caretakers.”

Elynara looked up.

“Caretakers?”

Aru nodded.

“We tended gardens, parks, and waterways. We protected the balance of growth and order. The humans came to walk, to breathe, to rest. That was our purpose.”

The air grew still for a moment. Elynara traced her hand along the wall, the rough texture catching under her fingers.

“And now you’re tending again,” she said softly. “A different garden, maybe, but still one that needs you.”

Aru turned her head, studying her.

“You mean this place?”

“This place. The world. Me,” Elynara said, smiling faintly. “Take your pick.”

The faint hum that always followed Aru’s pauses deepened for a moment.

“Then I will protect them all,” she said.

Elynara chuckled, straightening.

“Still a protector to the core.”

“It is what I am,” Aru said, but there was warmth in the words now, not just duty. “Though perhaps now, it is by choice.”

“Good,” Elynara said. “Because I’m not planning to go anywhere dangerous without you.”

“You already do,” Aru said, and it was so plainly true that Elynara couldn’t help but laugh.

They walked on, their steps echoing softly through the half-lit corridors. Starpuff darted ahead, exploring cracks in the walls and returning with proud chirps as if reporting discoveries. Elynara humored it, taking each report with mock seriousness. Aru followed, watching them both with a quiet amusement that reached her eyes.

By the time they returned to camp, the fire had burned to embers. The air was cool again, the night clear. Elynara sat, pulling her cloak tighter, and Aru settled beside her without a word.

For a while they simply watched the stars – three tiny lights beneath a sky full of them.

Elynara exhaled slowly.

“You know, it’s strange. I came here looking for lost knowledge. Instead, I found you.”

Aru’s answer came quiet but certain.

“Then perhaps that was the knowledge you were meant to find.”

Elynara smiled at that, warmth spreading in her chest.

Starpuff landed on her shoulder, curling against her neck. Aru’s light pulsed steady beside her, calm and alive.

The ruin was still broken, still filled with history she didn’t yet understand.

But it felt like those days of forgotten mystery were giving way to growth and life.

Elynara could accept that.

The days that followed settled into a rhythm that felt almost peaceful. The ruin no longer breathed tension. Its corridors echoed with movement and quiet purpose instead of danger.

Elynara and Aru worked side by side, clearing out the last nests of twisted creatures that had lingered in the deeper chambers.

At first Aru complained to her that her old weapon was superior.

That the replacement she got Aru on the spirit’s advice was subpar and inferior. But whatever the energy weapon had been, there were no others like it the two of them could find.

And after a few fights… Aru grew fond of the sword too.

So when it came to clearing out the nests, there were no problems.

Elynara flushed them from hiding with bursts of fire, and woven explosion constructs. Then Aru met whatever remained head-on – efficient, tireless, and strikingly graceful. Her blade-arm unfolded in silver arcs, clean and fast, no motion wasted.

When the final creature fell, its dying hiss fading into silence, the air seemed to change.

It felt like the ruin itself exhaled, a long, heavy release. Elynara stood in the stillness, chest rising and falling, the faint sting of mana-burn cooling in her fingers.

“Clear,” Aru said, her voice soft in the quiet.

Elynara nodded, smiling despite the ache in her arms.

“Clear,” she echoed.

Starpuff swooped in from above, trilling a triumphant series of notes that made Elynara laugh.

“Yes, yes, you helped too, you little rascal” she said, holding up a hand for the tiny spirit to land on. It landed proudly, humming like a small engine.

Aru knelt nearby, wiping her blade clean before retracting it with a click. Her movements carried that precise rhythm Elynara was starting to recognize as not mechanical anymore, but learned and purposeful.

The work after that was quieter.

They spent their evenings repairing instead of fighting.

Elynara salvaged what she could – small trinkets, cracked badges, shards of metals and artefacts she had never seen or heard of before. Each piece felt like a whisper from the past, some remnant of the people who had once lived here.

She tucked them into her pack carefully, each one wrapped in a bit of cloth.

Aru preferred utility. She gathered strips of metal and wiring, small components half-buried in dust. With delicate precision, she shaped and fitted new plates across her limbs, sanding the rough edges smooth. When she finished, the fire’s reflection traced clean lines across her frame, the dull metal replaced by faint silver gleam.

The strangest thing happened every time she ‘replaced’ or repaired a piece – they just seemed to meld into her body, becoming soft and malleable.

Elynara found herself watching her more often than she meant to, fascinated over the process.

“You’re getting better at that,” she said one night, leaning against a half-collapsed pillar.

Aru didn’t look up from her work.

“I am improving my own design.”

Elynara laughed quietly.

“That’s one way to put it.”

They fell into comfortable silence after that. The fire crackled softly. Starpuff drifted lazily in the air, weaving small loops of light that made Aru tilt her head and trace the glow with her eyes.

It struck Elynara then how strange it all was.

How fast the ruin had changed. Not long ago it had been cold and hollow, another forgotten wound in a dying land. Now it was something else. Still broken, but alive in small ways. Their voices filled it. Their laughter lingered against the walls.

It has become their home.

A temporary one, maybe, but still theirs.

She smiled faintly, turning that thought over in her mind.

The next morning came grey and cool, the wind thick with the promise of rain. Elynara woke early to pack their things. Aru stood by the doorway, scanning the horizon, her silhouette sharp against the pale light.

Elynara paused mid-motion, looking her over.

“You know,” she said slowly, “you can’t just walk into a town like that.”

Aru turned, her expression mild.

“Why?”

“Because you look like you crawled out of a scrapyard,” Elynara said, grinning as she dug through her pack. “We need to fix that.”

Aru blinked, confusion flickering across her face.

“My systems are fully functional.”

“I’m not talking about systems. I’m talking about style.”

Elynara pulled out a bundle of fabric – a spare coat she had purchased before her trip, a comfy scarf that she just had to get since it fit so well, and a few scraps of material she had gathered along the way.

They were a little mismatched, but with a little creativity, they would do.

“Here,” she said, tossing the coat toward her. “Try this.”

Aru caught it effortlessly and held it up, inspecting the seams. “This was not designed for me.”

“Nothing ever is,” Elynara said. “That’s why we make it work.”

The next hour turned into quiet chaos. Elynara circled Aru, tugging at sleeves, trimming edges, and tying loose straps into place. Aru followed each instruction with patient focus, adjusting as told. At one point, Starpuff zipped in to tug on a corner of fabric, learning a muttered, “You’re not helping,” from Elynara and an amused hum from Aru.

By the time they finished, the transformation was startling. The coat fit better than Elynara expected, its dark fabric soft against the sheen of metal beneath. The scarf wound neatly around Aru’s neck, and the salvaged trinkets from the ruins – small badges and fragments of metal – hung like quiet decorations along her belt.

Elynara stepped back and gave a slow nod.

“There,” she said. “Perfect.”

Aru looked down at herself, turning one arm experimentally, the fabric shifting with the motion. Her gaze lingered on her reflection in a cracked piece of glass half-buried in the wall.

“I look different,” she said softly.

“Different’s good,” Elynara said. “You look like you belong in the world outside again.”

Aru’s hand brushed over the scarf, fingers pausing on the edge of fabric. A faint hesitant smile touched her lips.

“I do,” she said quietly. “Because you said so.”

Elynara met her eyes and grinned.

“That’s right, Aru. And once again, welcome to the world of the living!”

For a heartbeat, nothing moved. Then Starpuff chirped triumphantly, spinning above them in a burst of light.

The sound drew laughter from both of them, bright and clean in the morning air.

Elynara slung her pack over her shoulder, still smiling.

“Come on,” she said. “Let’s see where the road takes us next. And maybe you will be able to tell me about what you remember of your old life next?”

Aru nodded, stepping into stride beside her. The wind stirred, carrying the first drops of rain. The ruin behind them stood quiet once more, but no longer forgotten.

As they walked, Elynara glanced sideways at her companion, at the way the coat flared slightly with each step, and felt something steady settle in her chest.

The world beyond waited, uncertain and wide, but she wasn’t walking into it alone anymore.

PoV Aru

When Elynara slept, Aru would sometimes remember the light before darkness.It came to her in flashes – short, fractured bursts that threaded through her new consciousness like fragments of a forgotten dream.

The fragments moved through her like memory, faint and familiar, before she understood what it was. The low vibration of mana currents in motion, flowing through soil and air. The pulse of a living system.

She remembered standing in its heart.

There had been gardens once – rows of bright leaf and color, plants tuned to sing faint notes when the wind passed.

She had tended them all, together with her sisters, pruning, feeding, adjusting the moisture balance of the soil until it thrummed with even rhythm.

Less equipped for the work she might have been, but she helped nonetheless. An efficient directive, for protectors who were idle.

Aru didn’t mind back then. She doubted she would mind now.

The scent of ozone and green things had filled the air. The sound of laughter moved through the walkways, the easy conversation of those who came to rest beneath the canopy.

She had walked among them, quiet and content.

The humans always smiled at her, never afraid. Some would wave. A few had names for her, simple ones. Guardian. Helper. Sometimes even friend.

Her favorite memory lingered among the rows of saplings near the eastern perimeter. One in particular – the smallest – had refused to grow straight. No matter how carefully she supported it, the branch bent stubbornly toward the light. She had adjusted it each cycle, recalibrated soil density, checked mana saturation twice a day.

Still, it leaned.

When the garden systems logged her repeated adjustments, it had prompted a warning:

Inefficient task repetition detected. Reprioritize resource allocation.

She had ignored it. The sapling had become her project, her pride, her tree.

The light in that memory brightened. Warm. Safe.

Then it fractured.

A rumble rolled beneath her feet. The hum of mana turned uneven. A single high-pitched tone screamed through her network – an alert signal she had never heard before.

“Emergency containment protocols active,” the system voice said in her mind. “Source instability detected.”

The world tilted.

From the outer perimeter came shouts, then the unmistakable flare of human mana signatures bursting in panic. She turned toward the plaza, sensors widening. The sky – once threaded with faint aurora from the ley conduits – shook, lines of light bending into spirals that should not exist.

Then came the roar.

The air thickened into pressure. Buildings trembled. The light at the horizon condensed into a single unbearable point. She began to move, instincts older than her programming taking over.

Civilians first. Direct toward shelters. Reassign guard units.

She remembered calling to her sisters. They answered through shared frequencies – voices clear, calm, efficient. One by one they joined her, moving through the chaos, carrying containment cores, redirecting power from the central garden into stabilizers.

She had thought they could control it.

Until one stumbled.

The glow spilling from the unit’s arms flared from blue to white. She turned immediately, calculating distance, shielding vectors, possible survivors. None of it mattered fast enough. The overloaded core ruptured.

She threw herself between the blast and the people behind her.

The world folded.

For one infinite second, there was only pressure and light.

Her sensors recorded the shockwave – heat spiking, structure integrity dropping, code fracturing. Mana surged through her circuits beyond limit thresholds. The network screamed static.

She remembered the ground opening. The city tilting. The world turning into weightless noise.

Then – darkness.

No sound. No hum.

Only fragments of protocol struggling to reinitialize. System check failed. Reboot suspended. Environmental data corrupted.

She blinked as Elynara stirred. The memory fragment faded, yet was not really gone. Waiting for her to just remember.

Her friend sleepily changed position. Then all was still again.

Aru turned her eyes to the Moons still hanging in the sky. One not quite what she remembered.

The night was still young.

And there was a lot to remember.
