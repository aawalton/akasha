“Stay here,” I told Burr, patting her side.

There was a chance Hinlaw was leading me to a trap, but if there was one, he wouldn’t have much time to set it. Clearly, he had been expecting to escape. If he set a trap, he’d only be able to use the time I was giving him right now.

I couldn’t wait any longer.

Anchor, Sage, and Brawl took off after Hinlaw’s son, and I charged after Hinlaw, rushing through the gates of his fortress. A three-storey-tall archway yawned, leading into a chandelier-lit hall beyond.

Raising my shield and spear, I rushed into the hall.

My Presence Skill would help me identify Hinlaw, sure, but it also cost Presence. I couldn’t use too much, especially with how much I’d used my Art so far. Using my Art to empower my spear strikes made it even more costly. I would only use it if it was an absolute necessity.

For now, staying away from enclosed hallways was my best bet. If I was out in the open, I could use my physical senses—which didn’t cost Presence to use—to identify Hinlaw.

The hall’s interior was a grand audience chamber with arched ceilings and pillars coating the walls. A few men with brooms scurried about in the wings, just trying to escape the commotion they’d heard outside.

“Get out of here!” I called to them. “Go! No, not deeper inside the fortress! Outside! The farther you get away, the safer you’ll be!”

I didn’t know what a fight between Golds would look like, and I didn’t want to catch any innocents in the crossfire. Sure, Hinlaw was a wicca, and he probably had a weapon that doubled as a siphon, just like Hinfic had. But aside from that, I had no idea what he was capable of.

There was no sign of him in the hall. He knew this place better than I did, and if I let him get too far away, he’d have another chance to escape. After all, that was what he wanted more than anything.

Wincing, I used my Perception Skill. A Gold-tier weight burgeoned in the wings of the hall, and behind a set of pillars, I saw a faint outline of Hinlaw running up a staircase.

I sprinted as quickly as I could, pushing my new attributes to the limit. The polished marble floor cracked under my footfalls, and the air bent around me. I tucked my arms forward and lowered my head, clipping a pillar—I couldn’t turn fast enough.

It crumbled and made me stagger, but it didn’t hurt me.

I ran through an archway at the edge of the hall, eyes locked on Hinlaw’s outline until the Perception Skill wore off. He kept climbing, so I followed. I jumped up the stairway one flight at a time, winding back and forth up the side of the hall. Judging by how much larger the outline had been getting, I was gaining ground.

The stairway emerged in a short promenade, which led past a set of tower bases. Tacked-on wooden walkways stretched over to newly-built structures, which reminded me far more of the regular city on the ground. Half-timbered walls, thatched roofs.

Hinlaw’s cloak blurred into a tower at the end of the hallway, so I followed him. I caught the corner of the tower’s entryway with my shoulder, this time using it to help me change directions faster. With the agility and training from the obstacle course, I gained ground again. Hinlaw’s stats may have been higher than mine if we could’ve examined him through the System, but he hadn’t spent the last year training on obstacle courses and in the Labyrinth, testing his agility.

As soon as I entered the tower, I raised my shield and pointed my spear. The interior was cramped.

I used my Perception Skill again, looking out for ambushes, and sure enough, Hinlaw had stopped just around the corner. He used the high ground of the staircase and the curve to give himself an advantage. This one was a spiral staircase, and they were always designed to work in the favour of the defenders.

Pretending as though I didn’t see him, I shouted, “Hinlaw, there’s nowhere to run. Face me!”

My voice echoed back to me, but the echoes were notably muted by something soft standing around the corner. Still, I pretended not to notice.

“You talk about honour and loyalty and all that,” I continued. “So why run from a fight?”

As soon as I finished, but before I rounded the corner, I envisioned my Art triggering on the stairway ahead, dragging down on Hinlaw. The air thwumped around the corner, Hinlaw gasped, and as he staggered, I rushed in.

It wasn’t enough to knock him to the ground. It was barely enough to stun him for a few seconds. The stairs below him, however, cracked, and dust fell from the roof.

I seized my advantage, attacking as he reeled, and rushed in spear-first. He took a defensive stance with his Great Warbrand, and he wielded it with two hands. He had no shield, but he swished his blade side to side, blocking my jabs.

As he retreated up the stairway, I cut off my Art so I could pass through undisturbed, then used another one in combination with my spear jab and a resonance Skill. It sliced through the air in the blink of an eye, then collided with the cutting edge of Hinlaw’s blade, taking off a chip as the man redirected it into the wall.

My spear pierced the marble, and I fell forward, not meeting the resistance I expected. The wall just broke before my speartip.

I tried to wrench it free, but the shaft was still wood. If I shifted side-to-side any harder, I’d shatter it.

As I struggled, Hinlaw slammed his sword down toward me. I raised my shield, even as the cutting edge of his blade glimmered with an Art. I couldn’t exactly see what kind of Art it was, but it seemed to catch the air in a high wind around the blade, creating a web of friction around it and heating it up until it glowed white-hot.

Had this been one of my old shields, the blade would’ve sliced through like it was butter, charring the wood with its heat and slicing it with its sharpness.

It bounced off my shield. A curtain of sparks fell over me, but nothing else.

It bought me time to back out, pulling my spear out of the wall without breaking it, then keep attacking. My Imperial Steel spearhead could handle that blade, but the shaft couldn’t. I couldn’t use my spear to block.

We traded blows all the way up the spiral stairway. I kept my jabs close to me, containing them, drawing on the smallest moves baked into my main spear Skill. I’d sorted all of them out in my Memory Palace, and I could call on them all with ease whenever I needed, switching effortlessly between styles. When I used an Art, I used it to help me imagine my next attack, flowing between moves far more efficiently.

Hinlaw didn’t restrict himself nearly as much. He didn’t have to, being a proper Gold-tier Path-walker. He used broad swipes, slashing through the ceiling. His blade melted the roof as he used heavy overhead swipes, and it sliced clean through the central pillar, leaving glowing red marks.

As we ascended the staircase, it began to crumble beneath our feet. Chunks of marble brick fell away, and a segment of the wall crumbled, falling onto the fortress below. It landed with a boom, creating a plume of smoke.

His blade left scrapes in my armour, but like my shield, it was strong enough to deflect a glancing blow. The heat did more damage to the scales, but the Labyrinth Keepers swarmed over the impact point, repairing it instantly.

When he did land a strike on the back of my hand and cut through my glove, slicing skin, he tightened his grip on his blade’s hilt. I couldn’t see what happened, but just like during the joust, something drew on my Presence. It took a fraction of my Presence, Siphoning it through the hilt.

He had a Siphon embedded in his weapon. I couldn’t let that happen again.

Hinlaw stumbled when he stepped on a segment of stair that he’d slashed from below a half minute earlier fell away beneath him, now that he’d circled around to stand on it. After that, he retreated, sprinting up the rest of the spiral stairway. I followed, keeping my senses aware.

When I reached the next doorway in the side of the stairwell, I used my Perception Skill to track Hinlaw. Sure enough, he’d darted out to the side, passing down a hallway and racing toward a…balcony?

No, he was going for the room beside it.

Was he trying to set up another ambush? Or did he really think he could get away? He was slowing down. His chest was heaving. He cut off the Art enveloping his sword, probably for lack of Presence.

I sprinted down the hall, keeping him in my sight the whole time. As he reached for the door, I pulled my crossbow off my back and fired the bolt enhanced with a resonance Skill. It struck his hand, cleaving off a finger. He pulled back, hissing in pain, as my bolt continued on into the room beyond and clattered across the floor.

Before he could try reaching for the door again, I closed the distance, switching back to my spear. I pulled myself along with an Art, then swept my spear to the side. It crashed through a potted plant, then smashed Hinlaw in the shoulder, sending him tumbling out onto a balcony at the end of the hallway.

I hadn’t realized how high we’d climbed until I looked down over the city. We were at the highest point in the city. A sea of houses spread out over the cracked land, smeared to the very edge of my sight. The mountains loomed, snow turning pink in the sunset. Seagulls whirled in the rushing, salty winds.

Hinlaw skidded over to the railing. He pushed himself back to his feet, panting, holding on to the railing to support himself. “Sir Levi, let’s think about this. I can—”

“You had your chance,” I snarled, lunging forward. He deflected my spear with a sloppy swipe, then backed up to the side, moving toward the balcony’s far corner. An image of the rabid cultists ripping through the civilians rippled through my mind. All the cultists before, who had done unspeakable things to the people of this entire domain. “You’ll pay for what you did. Someone has to bring you to justice.”

I lunged again. Hinlaw desperately deflected it, but he didn’t push hard enough. I used my Art to empower the strike, aligning the gravity well with my arm, and my spearhead ripped through his armour with ease. It tore deep into his shoulder. His arm flopped down to the side, loose.

“The cultists?” Hinlaw gasped. “You think I control them? I simply gave way to them. I let them spread. Sure, some of them listened to me, as a reward for my good behaviour, but I don’t get any say in the bigger picture. I was just following orders! The Subtle Men told me what to do, and I did it. I could follow your orders—”

“I don’t want men like you following my orders,” I said. I slammed my shield into his head, knocking off his helmet and sending it tumbling to the fortress below. It mashed his nose, turning it into a bloody mess. He stumbled back, falling into the corner.

I set my sights on his forehead, pulling my spear back, aiming between his eyes and preparing my Art.

Before I could attack, something boomed in the distance. I hesitated for a moment, extending my senses. A coldness seeped down my spine, and a faint pressure popped in my ears.

Hinlaw laughed. “Fool. It’s too late now. Neither of us will win this fight. Neither of us are going to see the end of the day.”

“What’s that? Is there a trap?”

“He’s here. You finally caused a big enough stir, and you don’t have any protection from his wrath. He’s going to swat you like a fly.”

“Who?” I demanded. “Your handler? Someone from the Subtle Men? Who?”

My stomach dropped.

Had I pushed too hard and too fast? I used my Perception Skill, but it didn’t work. Overwhelming pressure popped in my ears, and my arcane senses went haywire. Everything lit up in golden light, like I’d stared at the sun.

Hinlaw’s sword racing toward my chest was the only stressor strong enough to shake me out of the haze. I deflected it with my shield, then drove my spear into his skull with all my might. His body collapsed, slumping into the corner, arms splaying out to the side as a pool of blood grew around him.

For a brief moment, I let relief flood through me. Hinlaw was gone. I’d defeated a Gold, and all on my own. I’d barely taken any damage, and the Labyrinth Keepers were already fixing it.

But the source of that pressure was still here. Could I face it? Was it too strong?

I had to get back inside. I had to reassess my options.
