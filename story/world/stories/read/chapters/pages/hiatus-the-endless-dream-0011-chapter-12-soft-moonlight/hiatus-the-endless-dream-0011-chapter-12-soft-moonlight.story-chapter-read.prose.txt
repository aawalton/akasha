Moonlight

Scorching moonlight revealed only hazy shapes in the cornfield. Occasionally, a gust of wind rolled through, sending the otherwise silent night into a torrent of temporary noise before everything returned to silence.

No crickets. No bugs. Not a sound.

And then, cutting through the quiet … a noise. The sound was choked and smothered, forced out between gasping breaths of pain. Soft soil cradled a man's neck and back, damp and warm beneath him.

The man was tall and muscular with ochre skin that paled with every passing minute. A creature pressed against his chest, studying the man intently. From gnarled offshoots hung a network of thin, lithe roots, the tips of which curled weakly—exploring the air like worms on a hook.

Those curious things. The ones that touched skin tunneled underneath. They pierced veins, caressed nerve endings, and mapped the fibers of muscle. Occasionally, understanding would ripple through the creature's body, prompting it to gently manipulate the roots. A press here, a pull there, and the man's arm contracted, obedient despite his paralyzed state.

With every success, the creature would vibrate, forgetting itself for a moment and looking away from the man, allowing his pained screams to echo as its roots violently tore flesh.

Time caused the creature's confidence to rise. It wasn't satisfied with causing the man to twitch. It desired to make him dance, but was limited in how far it could go. The instructions were encompassing and restrictive.

It tried to move the left arm, and the skin tore open like plastic – stretching, bulging, and thinning before rupture. More warmth coated the ground, seeping out of the man. The creature grew angry.

How dare this man ruin its progress. It was on the cusp of discovery, yet the paralyzed man was still sabotaging it. It wanted to kill him, but it couldn't, so it settled for torture instead. The roots underneath the man's skin grew rigid and began to grasp onto everything near it.

Dorian stood wide-eyed, not five paces away from the scene. His heart hammered in his chest, and he fought shaking legs and the instinct to run. He had thought that cutting his arm off was the worst it could get. That nothing could disturb him after that. No matter how horrific of an experience it was, that process was still a human horror. This. This was a nightmare.

The creature continued to act as a puppeteer, causing the man to twitch as it tore through flesh. Dorian grit his teeth. He had to move, do something. He forced himself to think, to detach himself, and think about the situation. About what he could do. One of Dorian's hands crept down to the bottom of his shirt, which he slowly lifted.

A gust of wind shook the fields, and in that commotion, Dorian bounded forward the two steps toward the monster. Using his sudden momentum, he swung wildly toward the creature's head. The cloth wrapped around, allowing Dorian to grab the other end and quickly knot it together before diving to the side.

The creature slid off the man as it slowly vibrated in rage. It swung wildly, not fully understanding its lack of sight as it lashed out in scything arcs that cut through the surrounding stalks with whip-like cracks.

By the time it finally shook the shirt loose from its head, neither the man nor Dorian was anywhere to be seen.

But the creature still felt the warmth of blood trailing on the ground and the call of the power that bound it.

Dorian

My feet dragged against the dirt as I hoisted the man along. Saving the half-dead man had never been in question, especially after seeing what that monster was doing to him. I accepted the consequences that would surely come. Even still, my heart was racing, and I found most of my thoughts dedicated to strings of panicked cursing.

Regret, shame at that regret, and then anger at the feeling of shame roiled simultaneously inside me as I guided the man along.

Slowly, he was recovering from his shock, but his breath was shallow, and there wasn't a shortage of blood.

The steady, almost careful rustling of stalks followed behind us in the distance, leaving no doubt that the monster had given chase.

The same trick wouldn't work twice for multiple reasons. I didn't have a shirt anymore, I would no longer be able to see the creature, and I lacked the element of surprise.

It was gaining, but we couldn't hide because we hadn't made enough distance. But we couldn't gain distance because it was faster than me while I was carrying the other Visitor.

I needed a plan. The same trick wouldn't work, but a new one might. If I needed to, I could use the stranger's shirt and temporarily use the man as bait to regain the element of surprise. What else? Think! There was nothing I could do about not seeing the creature, though. If I had a blanket or sheet, something large I could cover myself with or cover it with, maybe I could do something better. I scowled.

Was the best I could come up with really to throw dirt in its eyes?

"Two minutes," the man spoke, voice dry and pained.

"What?" I bit out, confused and more than a little frustrated.

"That's all I need. Please. I can run on my own then." The man's voice was hoarse, begging, but I could also hear the resignation.

My face twisted further. I was someone prone to anger, to violence a little, and sometimes people really pissed me off, but I wasn't a coward. Did the man think so little of me after I saved him to assume I'd condemn him to death just as easily?

I kept moving, but I was growing winded. Besides buying time, did anything change with me moving? It was corn stalks in all directions. There was the road, but that left us wide open to being paralyzed. I had my Boon, but like hell, I'd use that here.

God. It really was just going to be throwing dirt in its eyes, wasn't it?

Control. What could I control? I needed to control where its head was. I could use the man as bait to accomplish that, but how specifically?

Inspiration hit me, and I threw the man to the ground, pivoted on my feet, and reached out to the nearest plants. They were thick here, so thick it was hard to see through them.

Things naturally followed the path of least resistance.

I started bending stalks near their base to create a natural path. I didn't have much time before the creature would be on us, so I couldn't afford to make the path too big. I then reached for the further stalks and bent them towards each other, starting from a taller position and getting lower and lower. Hopefully, the creature would crawl low, but even if it didn't, I could gauge where its head was.

The man pointed in a direction and opened his mouth to say something, but I cut him off. I didn't have time for someone else to contribute ideas.

"Give me your shirt," I hissed at the stunned man watching me in what I could only assume was pure confusion. Thankfully, he didn't ask questions and unbuttoned his Hawaiian shirt. I was hardly done with my work when the rustling told me I was out of time.

I snatched the shirt, slid onto the ground, and began crawling parallel to where I heard the monster approaching. Its movements came to a slow pace as it caught a glimpse of the other Visitor. From a scramble to a stroll, if the quieting of movement was anything to go by.

I felt my movements grow sluggish, and my heart dropped as I realized the creature had seen me. I prepared to run when I realized the thing was moving towards the Visitor alone and not me.

I almost laughed. I was in its vision, but that didn't necessarily mean it knew it saw me. Perfect.

Soon, the movement of corn stalks passed me, and I began trailing the movement behind.

I moved slowly, carefully choosing where my feet went and only making larger movements with the wind. The wind itself was a double-edged sword. It let me move quickly, but I couldn't tell at all where the creature was. The rustling caused by it and the air were identical to my eyes.

Strangely enough, the creature left no footprints or indentations on the ground.

Another gust. The creature was nearing my passage. Panic swelled inside of me as I didn't dare blink. Where was it? Had it already gone through? Or maybe it was smarter than I gave it credit for, and it went around.

The Visitor let out a yell. I saw him begin to slowly thrash and convulse. I saw a thin trail of blood floating in thin air, solid red at the skin, which was slowly enveloped by translucent air, not even a foot away.

Shit. Shit shit shit.

I tumbled through the field towards the spot where I had left the man. I had briefly seen the creature before. That thin red line looked kind of like the creature's hands. I'd have to estimate. I reached the edge of the miniature clearing, raised my foot, and slammed it down onto where I hoped the creature's head would be.

I don't know what I hit, but I hit something.

The fingers sticking into the other man retracted. I threw myself over my knee, hoping to get onto its back. My body hit the upper back, and my head scraped against its own. I could feel both of us recoil in unison, and I thanked gods that it didn't catch me in its vision.

My left knee slid down onto the dirt, and I pressed against it to gain as much leverage as possible.

The creature writhed underneath me in waves of motion, and I barely managed to keep control. Not because it was strong—the inverse in fact. The thing was too light for something that could take down a grown man. It was weak.

My teeth pressed painfully together, and I felt a sharp pain lash against my back.

I hissed and followed my instincts. I raised my hand, scrambling for the back of this thing's head. I found purchase after another painful lash -- my scream echoing far and wide. With all the force I could muster, I pushed down, punching at the thing's neck with my free hand, hoping that would help.

I could see the indent of its face in the dirt. Which meant I could estimate where its eyes were. Another flash of pain against my bare skin. I hissed and pressed my fingers into the dirt on either side of its invisible head. I could see my hands working closer to where their eyes should be. Its skin was soft but scratchy. A prickly mush which made it hard to distinguish vital organs from skin.

Eventually, I found its two bulb-like eyes with my pointer and middle finger. I curled them so my nails were perpendicular to the eyes and then gouged as hard as possible. It reminded me of mashed potatoes.

The thrashing redoubled, which combined with a new vibrating from the creature. The strength in my arms almost gave way as lash after lash opened the skin on my back, but even still, I continued to dig. I pushed deeper and deeper and, to my surprise, found no resistance of bone. Just the somewhat stiff mush.

My entire body felt the strength of the vibrations now. It dug down to my bones, causing surges of pain as my internals rattled inside of me. With one last push and yell of pain, I grabbed a chunk of whatever I was holding and ripped.

I finally lost purchase of the creature, falling backwards.

The invisible monster stood, thrashing as it crashed into nearby stalks sporadically, lashing out at anything and everything in a maelstrom of invisible limbs.

My breathing came in short gasps, and as the adrenaline left my system, I felt the full brunt of my brief altercation.

It was blinded, but a dead enemy was better than a blind one.

Struggling to my feet, I tracked the path of breaking stalks that slowly expanded the clearing. If I could get it on the ground again. I stepped forward, but the man spoke in a hissing whisper. "Don't kill it! We don't have time."

At some point, he had stood back up and grabbed my wrist, and before I could really process what was going on, we broke out into another run.

We ran until we couldn't breathe. And somewhere in the wheezing and the aching of my chest, I realized I wasn't afraid. I should be more frightened. I wanted to be more afraid, but all I could think about was the growing stinging sensation that littered my body every time I moved.

Now that the chaos was done, I got a better look at the man. Light skin, long twisted locks, and coffee-brown eyes. The man was the first to speak, "Thought that was it for a second there. Appreciate the help. Name's Nate." Nate got to giving me a nod before grimacing as he noticed something. "I don't suppose you have a healing Boon?"

Ah. How bad was it, really? Absent-mindedly, I stared down at my body. I looked like a training post covered in open wounds and long gashes.

I couldn't help but let out a small, hopeless laugh as I shook my head. I opened my mouth and hoped that just this basic information wouldn't cause the System to rip my memories out again.

"Ha. I don't even know what a boon is really." I wanted to curl up. I wanted to sleep. I was so tired. My heart was still pounding in my chest, almost agonizingly so.

The man frowned before understanding dawned on him, "No way. You're a newbie then? Did your fellow Visitors not explain anything?"

I shook my head, taking a knee to give myself more time to breathe.

"You're the first Visitor I've seen. I think my brother was one … but" I didn't finish the sentence; the words just hung in the air, and Nate nodded solemnly.

"Sorry. That's tough, man." He looked around the cornfield, shivering slightly before continuing. "Let's find somewhere safe. I can give you the rundown then, yeah?"

I shakily stood to my feet and nodded. "You have a safe place in mind?"

Nate's face contorted slightly as he did a so-so motion. "Eh. It's alright. As long as we keep cool we should be safe. My partner is there."

I wasn't sure how I felt about being outnumbered, but the worst that could happen was I'd die and be ejected from the dream.

Hmm. I paused. That thought disturbed me just a little. Just dying? It hurt like hell, but I didn't feel like it affected me as much as it should. I mean. I grew up in suburbia, for god's sake. It wasn't that it hadn't caught up with me yet, either. Thinking back to cutting my arm off … I didn't feel anything about it. Was that true? I thought back on it. I imagined the just sharp enough edge of the key on my flesh. I remember how [Fragile] made the tearing easier. It all came back in a flood I couldn't stop. I thought about–

"Uhh. Hey," Nate's voice broke through to me in a whisper, "you alright?

My vision, which I hadn't realized had been full of static, cleared and returned to focus. "Sorry, I was just thinking."

Nate stared at me with a strange expression that I couldn't place. I stared back. He broke eye contact first and then continued, "So, you cool with that?"

"Sure," I nodded. He was saying something about a safe place before I got sidetracked. I focused back on the feeling I had. I was afraid when I thought about my arm. Afraid and angry. Spiteful enough to want to tear that bastard's Instance apart, but not much else. Something wasn't right. Despite that, I felt giddy. Anxious too. Excited even. What could these people kno—

"I never ended up getting your name." Nate spoke again.

I scowled as my thoughts kept getting interrupted. I was trying hard to grasp onto something, but it kept slipping through my fingers. A shiver went down my back, and I almost stopped to look around the field of corn.

"Dorian. My name is Dorian," and I genuinely smiled at the man.

I was about to get answers.
