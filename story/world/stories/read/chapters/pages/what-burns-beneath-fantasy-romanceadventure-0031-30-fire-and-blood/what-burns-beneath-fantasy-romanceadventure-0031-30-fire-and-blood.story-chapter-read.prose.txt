“You’re lying.” I bit it out through numb lips. Blood drained from my face in equal parts horror and sudden, startling anger.

Gil’s eyes held mine. Wide, streaked with the red of lost sleep and too bright after his tears. And so very, very frightened.

My hands curled into fists. “You’re lying! You know, you have to know!”

Because if he doesn’t know, then what can we do? How will we save her? She’s alone and cold and scared and-

“I don’t know. I swear.”

Renner leaned in, eyes narrowed to slits. “Oh, he’s definitely lying. You heard him earlier; he left you. Means he was planning to take you somewhere, and I’ll bet his worthless hide that it was straight to whatever monster has its claws in this damned place.”

I stared at the stablemaster, thoughts churning. Renner had voiced the same awful idea I’d chased away earlier, the one I could scarcely wrap my mind around; that this frightened, haggard man had intended to harm me last night.

His tears were real. That awful look on his eyes when he saw my magic… that was real. He ran away last night and drank himself into a stupor and now he’s telling me to run. And his hand… ashes, whatever’s going on, I don’t think he wants to be a part of it.

I didn’t have much evidence, beyond the man’s behavior, to reinforce the thought. An ugly voice in the bowels of my mind whispered that, for all I knew, his grief and fear were a trick. Perhaps every tear was a well-crafted lie; perhaps his pleas for us to run were just an attempt to throw us off the trail.

Gil’s voice was strained as he ground out, “Not a lie. I can’t… I won’t-”

Renner balled his empty hand into a fist and, too fast for me to do anything but cry out, rammed it into the man’s stomach.

He doubled over with a heavy wheeze. Glass shattered as a bottle behind him was knocked to the floor. Sweet-smelling liquor pooled around Gil’s scuffed boots. He swung his good hand out, as if to ward the younger man away.

Renner swiped his knife-hand against the blow. His fist curled again-

“Stop!” I screamed, grabbing his arm and throwing myself backwards. It was enough to pull him off balance, and the two of us tumbled away.

Renner steadied himself, half-turned, and pushed me back. Not hard enough to hurt, though the expression on his face certainly did. It was the same look he’d worn outside, during our walk up. Cold. Ruthless. “We tried this your way. We asked nicely, and it’s getting us nowhere.”

Gil slumped against the counter again, mangled hand clutching at his ribs. Every breath was a thick gasp. He eyed Renner with the tense, wary stillness of a wounded animal.

“It’s not!” I cried, practically throwing myself between the two men. Or at least, I tried to; Renner’s free hand shot out, catching and shoving me back again. His glare remained fixed on Gil. Still with that coiled readiness to him, as if he expected the stablemaster to produce a blade or stretch out his hands and erupt in wicked magic.

But Gil just stood, shoulders shaking with every gasp.

“Saints, Renner, he might be just as much of a victim as Teela! We don’t know the full-”

“I’m not a victim.”

The words died in my throat.

The stablemaster straightened, squaring his shoulders. His face became so solemn that, once again, for a moment I could’ve sworn it was Durst standing in this dingy little home. “I’m a… a wretch and a liar; everything your friend says, lady.” His lips quivered, and once again his eyes were too bright. “And gods’ help me, I’m a coward to my core. But I won’t stand here and let you call me a victim.” He swore the word like a curse.

I wasn’t sure how to answer. “I… I’m sorry, I didn’t mean to-”

“I’ve made my own choices. Not proud of them. Not… not gonna make excuses for them.” He slashed his hurt hand through the air with a little growl, eyes darting to Renner. “And I’m a liar, it’s true. But I’m not telling you any more. Your friend’s dead, and there’s no… no helping it. No changing things.”

Renner grew very still beside me. The knife glinted in his hand.

Gil bowed his head. “One dead’s a sorrow. It is. But all three of you? Senseless. Leave. Leave, and tell folks to stay far away from this damned place.”

Silence hung like a fog. I opened my mouth several times, head shaking back and forth in furious denial, but I couldn’t seem to form any words. The quiet crept between all three of us, choking my breath away as I fought to make sense of this nightmare.

Renner finally broke it. His tone was flat. Dull. “Maybe she is dead. Salt, it’s been two hours since sunrise. She probably is. But, as far as I’m concerned, that makes your life forfeit.”

I moved forwards immediately. “No,” I ground out, pulsing racing, as once again he shoved me back. I pushed futilely at his arm. “No. We are not giving up hope, and you are not killing this man.”

“Not sure that term applies.”

I opened my mouth to argue, but Gil spoke first. “Won’t help.” He coughed out a breathy, harsh laugh. “Won’t end with me. She has others. Salt, I’m the one who…” He dipped his head, passed one hand across his eyes. “The Watch won’t find anything. I’ve tried.”

Renner snorted. “Wasn’t planning to involve them.”

The older man’s eyes flashed. “I’m not… fine. Damn you, fine. It won’t bring your friend back, but if that’s the justice you want, I won’t fight it. I surely deserve it.” His gaze swung towards me. “Maybe the gods will have mercy on my soul, sent before your eyes.”

I staggered beneath the weight of his proclamation, one hand covering my mouth. “Sir, you… you can’t mean that. I… there should be justice for whatever crimes you’ve done, but I’m not the one to decide that.” I seized Renner’s shoulder again and added, somewhat wildly, “And neither are you!”

He scowled at me, jerking his arm free. “No? Because I don’t see anyone else offering.”

Gil slammed his hand onto the counter. The sound made me jump. “Kill me, or don’t. And then go. Don’t stay another night.”

Renner continued to stare down at me, mouth twisted into a hard slash and jaw tight. Then his eyes glinted with a sinister light. “It’s her, isn’t it? Why you’re so concerned. Because she’s a runekeeper.” Every word dripped menace.

I flinched back, wondering at the sudden venom.

Gil flinched, too, and his face clouded with shame.

“Didn’t warn us ‘til you saw the magic. If she hadn’t done that, were you just gonna… what? Keep playing dumb?”

Gil paled. Turned his face away. “I-”

Renner bared his teeth in a vicious leer. “He values your life, ladyship. Didn’t before, but he does now. Isn’t that nice? He was ready to drag you off to die last night, but now that he knows you’re blessed…”

I swallowed the sudden lump in my throat.

The knife remained still in Renner’s hand, but he may as well have stabbed me with it as he added, almost casually, “Too bad Teela wasn’t special.”

I could have hit him. I almost did.

He moved forwards, backing me away from Gil. “How do you think she felt? I bet she was scared. Probably wanted her mom. Do you think-”

“Stop!” I shoved him, hard, but he didn’t budge. “I know what you’re t-trying to do! I won’t… we can turn him into the W-Watch, but-”

“How many?”

“What?”

He turned away, ignoring me. “How many? The tears, the smashed bottles… she wasn’t the first, was she?”

Gil’s weathered face was the color of bone. Tears dripped into his matted beard.

Renner continued mercilessly, “Nah, the state of this place, the state of you. This is years of guilt. How many people are dead because of you? And how many more,” he shot a harsh glare towards me, eyes simmering, “If we just leave him?”

“That doesn’t make it right,” I choked out. The words nearly caught in my throat.

Is he right? Is he? It’s not our place to decide what his fate should be, but… doesn’t it make sense? If killing him saves lives…

‘What if they were just… normal people who messed up?’

Teela had said that. She’d looked so thoughtful and sad, red eyes dull and rainwater sliding down the sharp planes of her face. Wondering at the actions of criminals, at whether they were truly monsters. Showing empathy towards a world full of people who despised her for no reason besides… no. For no reason that mattered. For no reason at all.

“He left,” I whispered, managing to meet the stablemaster’s gaze. “You… you left. Last night. Before you knew I was blessed. You chose to leave. And I saw… I saw you weeping. I saw how upset you were. Sir, I won’t call you a victim. But I don’t think you’re a coward. I think… I think you just need help to be brave. Please. Please, be brave. Tell us where it took her. There might still be time…” my voice caught, too thick to continue.

Beside me, Renner gave a little growl.

The pallor on Gil’s face didn’t wane. “I’m sorry. I’m sorry. Truly. But I’m not… it’s too late.” His hoarse voice cracked. “I’ll not send you to your death. I have no more answers.”

I covered my face, tears spilling out. Cloth and leather rustled beside me and a footstep fell just ahead, fast and near-silent. I lunged, grabbing Renner’s arm with a cry.

I wasn’t fast enough.

Gil staggered back as silver flickered, sinking into his stained tunic. Cracked lips parted in an awful, wet gasp-

“Veth!”

I did it without thinking. Foolishly. Scorching fire surged out around us. I saw white and the flames dwindled almost immediately, dying at my fingertips as spots danced across my vision. But it was enough. Too much. The countertop lit with blinding heat, and the spilled liquor was ablaze in a heartbeat.

Gil collapsed beside the burning pool.

It’s too close, oh no saints no he’s going to catch-

“Alar! Alar, I didn’t mean to, Alar!”

The world spun and my vision went black and my chest roared with heat and fire was scorching my throat, Too much too much I’m burning oh salt what’s-

The cold came like a shock. I gasped as the world came back into focus, first as a pinprick of white light and then, slowly, the dim expanse of the kitchen. There was water everywhere. It hadn’t been a little rainshower, it had been a torrent. I was soaked. The flames were doused.

Renner was white-faced on the other side of the room, stamping out the last smolders of his clothing. Smoke trailed from his boots and trousers. He was shaking.

I gasped for air, praying I hadn’t hurt him.

His head snapped up, eyes narrowed towards me. He looked furious. Murderous. One hand closed around his sword hilt.

I didn’t have time to dwell on my sudden surge of fear. The world tilted sideways, edges blurring, and I crashed to the floor. Gil moaned beside me and I twisted my head to the side, trying to see if he was okay…

The fire was no longer a danger, and it didn’t look like he’d been burned. But the knife… its black hilt was embedded in his side. The cloth beneath was dark. He stared at me, ashen-faced and slack-jawed, nostrils flaring with each heavy breath.

I stretched numb fingers out, pressed my palms against the damp wooden floor and tried to push myself upright. Both arms buckled. Spots danced in front of my eyes again and I felt, very distantly, the impact as my forehead hit the floorboards.

As if from far away, Gil said something. A question. Breathless. Concerned. Asking if I was alright? My head swam. I wasn’t sure. I didn’t feel alright. I felt like I was falling.

And then another voice, thick with rage and much too close. Rough leather scraped against the back of my neck.

The world fell away.
