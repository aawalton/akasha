I wanted to say that this was nostalgic…but I would be lying. This was not even remotely what I enjoyed about traveling.

The underground tunnels of the Long Road were the farthest thing from a fun journey.

We had officially set off from the Unchained’s settlement in the caldera that lay beneath Addersfield only this morning. The chosen combined forces of the Herztalian expedition, the Unchained, and the Elves had mobilized quickly once we had gotten assent to utilize the Chamel herds. With trained precision, the strange, horse-sized lizards had been bridled and bound to the newly finished Final Line, and with a frenzy of preparation, we had launched our new expedition.

To the heart of the Dwarven Mountain Holds.

All five hundred of us.

The leadership of the combined forces had, upon further review, decided to cut down on the number of soldiers we’d be taking north. It was quite a reduction, but the decision had been made in the interest of expediency.

After all, it was public knowledge, now, that we were essentially in a race against time. I wasn’t spreading the knowledge of the impending collapse of global wards far and wide, but if I was asked, I wasn’t shy in sharing that fact. There wasn’t a point, anyway. The expedition knew, considering that tidbit of information was one of the things Anguis had tricked me into sharing, upon our first meeting at the Rhoscaran docks. It had quietly spread through the ranks, and to my eternal fondness, it hadn’t demoralized the forces of the expedition.

It had galvanized them.

Maybe the ethos of the Polaris Reach was spreading.

And so, with that decided, five hundred turned out to be the absolute limit of what could be stuffed into the Final Line. With just the reconstructed and repurposed ship, we would be able to move faster, borne along by the strong, sinuous backs of the galloping Chamels. The Unchained had opened the barred path that led up and out of the caldera, on the opposite side from where we had entered it, and off we’d set, wasting not a moment more.

Because there wasn’t a moment more to waste. It may have just been an estimate, though from several different sources at this point, but we had a time limit.

Three weeks, give or take. The corruption being shed from Fynneas’s skybound Mantle of Divinity would reach its peak around that time, and that’s when the wards would fail. That knowledge hung upon the shoulders of everyone who had been chosen to join the journey north.

The trip across the breadth of Velanica undertaken by the original expedition had been downright jovial, in comparison to the mood on the Final Line.

Everyone understood what was at stake, and nobody wanted to be the reason we failed.

Focused would be a kind word to describe the morale among the new expedition.

Grim and determined would be a better description.

So, no. Nostalgic wasn’t quite the word I’d call this new adventure we’d set off on. There was none of the even mild enjoyment I’d found on the trek across Velancia, just from visiting new places, seeing new vistas.

Even under the grey light of the Skyfall, Vereden was still a beautiful place.

Instead, the only thing around us was the impeccably carved walls of the Long Road, lit only by the light given off by the lanterns on our deck. They zoomed past us with startling speed, driven by a startling amount of saurian might. I’d never seen a team of Chamels that were really being let off the leash, so to speak, and allowed to let loose.

They were far, far stronger than the mightiest horse. No wonder the Elves preferred them.

Not…really the smoothest gait, though. I’d give this to the long-burned Ashen Bride and her team of horses.

The trip had been way smoother.

Still, the Chamels came with their own benefits over the horses, even beyond the speed they could travel at.

It hadn’t taken us long to leave the brief section of the ancient Dwarven highway that the Unchained had personally cleared. That stretch of the Long Road had been downright pristine in comparison to what we were racing down now. Bleddyn’s people had cleaned their section out well, actually removing the stone they had broken down.

However, once we had moved past that section and merged into a larger, main trunk of the Long Road, we had discovered something a bit alarming.

Azarus may have, somehow, been able to clear a path through the collapsed parts of the Long Road…but it’s not like he had cleared it. The path was littered with shards of stone, rubble, boulders, and even a few crumbled sections of the walls or ceiling. There was a visible road through it, but not something to the extent of what the Unchained had done.

Thank God that the Chamels could navigate their way through it fairly easily. By far the main benefit of the Chamels was just how nimble they were. They could bound right over the rubble and even some boulders that lay strewn about with relative ease. When the Elves had domesticated these lizards, they had done so in a way that they were able to function in what were essentially packs, as if they were mammals. It really was astounding…if not extremely uncomfortable.

The Chamels were, after all, more than able to drag the Final Line through the air with them, as they bound through the rubble. The way my heart had skipped a beat the first time the entire ship had been launched into the air of the enormously tall roadway of the Long Road…

Well, I’ll just say I didn’t enjoy it and leave it at that.

The state of the Long Road that Azarus had left behind additionally made me thankful for an addition the Unchained had insisted upon when reconstructing the ship. The engineers had called it a wedge.

I called it a huge fuck off wedge bolted on to the bottom of the ship. It was meant to protect the wheels of the Final Line as it raced toward the Holds, and it was certainly doing that. The first time I had seen it, I’d almost felt a strange sense of déjà vu. It didn’t remind me of anything I had seen on a wagon before, much less a converted ship.

It looked like something I would have seen on a train from Terra. I swear I felt every single impact against the addition. The combination of rubble smashing against the steel reinforced wedge that our engineers laughably called a ram, the jumps of the Chamels as the Elven herders maneuvered them through the rubble, the impacts once the Final Line touched back down onto the Long Road…

Lets just say it made for a bumpy and fraught ride as we barreled toward the Holds. If this entire hunk of wood and iron hadn’t been magically treated and enhanced for durability, it probably would have fallen to pieces after the first jump.

Still, if you twisted matters in your head just right…it almost felt like the Final Line was out on the sea, from all the rocking.

Maybe.

If you squinted.

Bah, whatever.

And yet, for all that I could have told the drivers to slow down, I didn’t want to. At the rate we were going, we had estimated we might reach the Holds within three days, if we pushed the Chamels hard. A grim-faced Ragnvald had translated the driver's words for me, and they could do it. We’d need to rest for at least five hours after two days of travel before getting back underway, just to let them rest, but they could do it. The lizards would be near death by the time we reached the Holds and would need several days to recuperate, but it was still possible.

Three days to get there. Three days back to the caldera, where our forces were preparing for the campaign on Smaragd. That would take at least four days of breakneck, incautious, overland travel down the highways of Velancia to reach the western half of the Barren Forest and the specific chunk of it that the Elven capital lay within. And I couldn’t exactly order the rest of our waiting forces to preemptively make for the borders of Smaragd, not with the overworld still under the effect of the Skyfall. I was the one with the mobile ward stone, safely installed onto the Final Line with the help of Meia.

Which meant I needed to factor in the time that it would take to actually fight our way to the seat of Fynneas’s power, where Anguis was no doubt crouching. That would be a battle through thousands of near-feral elves and the combined forces of the Principality that bowed to the Savoy.

We wouldn’t be able to take things slow. We just didn’t have the time to engage in a protracted siege.

There would be blood because of that. Oceans of it, on both sides of the conflict. The inevitable conflict that was coming might just become one of the bloodiest in Veredenese history since the time of the War in Heaven through sheer desperation.

But for all of that…

It needed to be done.

I sighed, staring down at the surface of my barebones desk as I contemplated matters. With how frantic our journey was, I wasn’t really needed for minute to minute campaign. Our drivers knew what they were doing, and I sure as Hell had no idea what to do with driving Chamels. I had to trust the Elves with that.

Which meant I needed to keep myself busy somehow. So, here I was…

Playing with rocks. Crystals, in fact. Two of them.

Things that I was…incredibly wary of even touching, but this needed to be done. Especially after all I had learned about the nature of the Skyfall, and Fynneas in particular.

Sitting on my desk were two of the violently corrupted hunks of magenta crystal that Anguis handed out to his Magi. With these madness-tainted apparent fractions of a fraction of Divinity, the same kind that littered the sky of Vereden as enormous bars of frozen lightning, they could both protect his people from the degradation of the Skyfall and wield it as a focus.

It invariably drove them mad…but that didn’t stop more of these ‘crystalmancers’ from popping up.

Both of them were roughly the size of my outstretched palm, jagged chunks of evil-looking crystal that almost seemed to pulse where they lay.

One of them was a ‘gift’ from Olag, back when he had first joined up with us. He had taken the thing from an envoy of Anguis’s by force and been using it to protect his forces from the Skyfall.

And, you know. Ambush us.

Water, and bodies, under the bridge.

The other…was from the Savoy spy I had captured within Rhoscara, before everything went to shit. The Rangers-

(I ignored the pang I felt at the thought of them, and one in particular.)

-had given it to us for safekeeping, while they interrogated that madman.

We, the forces of the expedition, had made a brief study of our first chunk of crystal, back before I learned it was literally a chunk of Divinity.

Allegedly.

At the time, our thoughts had ended up in the direction of ‘it’s too dangerous to handle, store it in the hull and forget it’. I’d been surprised that both of these things had made the journey to the caldera and then to the Final Line. I owed that to, once again, Meia Itzelan.

I don’t know why the Half-Orc Sorceress had thought to preserve these damn things, and she had gotten shifty-eyed when I questioned her on it.

I dropped the subject, and had accepted them back.

Which led me to now, where I was going to try…well, experimenting on them.

My entire purpose as a Precursor was to return the Divinity that the gods had stolen from the System and the Administrator. Well.

Here were two fragments of Divinity. Nothing was stopping me from trying to ‘return’ this.

Carefully, I picked up the eating knife I had laid off to the side for just this purpose. It was a bit embarrassing, but…I’d had so much Oninite back in Blutstein as part of my contract with Kawamara that I had kind of forged myself a set of personal eating utensils from it. Incredibly wasteful, sure.

But damn was it satisfying to cut up a steak with a metal that only I could easily and reliably forge.

As far as sharpness went, I hadn’t bothered much with that. It was at the level of ‘it’ll cut a hunk of meat nice and cleanly’, but it wasn’t chef levels of sharpened.

Just…an eating utensil, really.

But it was a blade, and that’s what mattered.

I triggered what was likely my most foundational Skill.

The Scintillant Blade.

The eating knife burst into a rainbow flame, clinging to the surface of the Oninite, writhing across it. I considered the familiar sight thoughtfully for a moment, as the light played out across the small room I sat within.

Then, carefully…

I laid the blade down edge-first on the rightmost stone, bracing myself as I did so.

…nothing happened. The flame licked at the magenta surface of the crystal, doing nothing. Which was…about what I had expected. The Scintillant Blade wasn’t actually a cutting Skill. No, what it did was pierce mystical defenses.

And this was a rock, in the end.

Test number two.

Carefully, I reached out with my leather-gloved Primordium hand and gingerly grasped the crystal, holding it in place.

In the past, whenever I had successfully retrieved a spark of Divinity and sent it back to the System, it had been because a blade with my fire on it had actually pierced the subject.

So, I reared back with the knife and drove it down at the crystal.

It was just a rock, really…and Oninite was far stronger than a rock.

The blade slid cleanly into the stone with a small shriek of tested metal, and the metal won.

This time, there was a reaction.

An…honestly incredibly odd one to witness, in truth.

Under my befuddled gaze, the magenta colored crystal…stopped being magenta. From the point that the steak knife had pierced the rock, the color inside kind of flowed into the point that the scintillating fire of my Skill was pouring into. It was deeply odd to watch, almost like water that was being let out of a drain. What was left behind was something similar looking to quartz, but I could tell it was more mystically active than that.

Just…not a hunk of Divinity, anymore.

I tensed up as I felt the lightest, almost feather-weight touch upon my Status as a long-dormant Skill within it roused.

Dream of the Infinite, one of my three racials I had eventually pegged as the primary engine for ‘stealing’ Skills. Primarily, it was what had allowed me to accrue all seven of the Professions and then acquire Aetherial Melding.

The more important aspect, I believed, was that it was what was transferring the spark of Divinity back to the System.

But…it failed to activate. The sensation was decidedly odd. It was like the Skill ‘woke up’ for the lack of a better term, cast a bleary eye about, and then promptly went back to sleep.

For whatever reason, it had drunk of the Divinity within this crystal, but it hadn’t been enough to matter. I’m not sure if it had done anything with it. I’m…pretty sure it hadn’t transferred the spark back to the System.

That sensation was unmistakable to me.

If this had been a small fragment of Divinity, it wasn’t enough to activate the process where I would receive the ‘gift’ of a new Skill.

Carefully, to confirm matters, I repeated the process with the other tainted piece of crystal.

Nothing.

Once again, the magenta was drained from the crystal, Dream of the Infinite turned over irritably like a misfiring motorcycle, and nothing happened.

I stared down at the two cleansed pieces of quartz-like crystal, utterly confused in a way I hadn’t experienced in quite a while.

Just what he Hell did this mean?

The only answer I received was a particularly hard jolt from the Final Line as the Chamels flung the ship around.
