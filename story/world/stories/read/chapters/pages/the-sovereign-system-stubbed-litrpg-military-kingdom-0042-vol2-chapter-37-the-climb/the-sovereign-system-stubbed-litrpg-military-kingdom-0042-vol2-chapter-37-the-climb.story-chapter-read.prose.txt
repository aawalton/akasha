Luke and Tristan stood in the valley, their necks craned as they gazed up at the sheer cliff face that stretched endlessly into the sky.

Neither spoke, but the tension between them made their hesitation palpable. The silence wasn’t peaceful—it was heavy with unspoken dread about the climb ahead.

After a moment, Luke reached out and placed a hand on Tristan’s shoulder, a small gesture of reassurance.

But Tristan pulled away wordlessly and stepped forward. Reaching up, he grabbed a narrow handhold and attempted to haul himself up.

He lasted only a second before slipping, the rock crumbling beneath his grip. He dropped back to the ground with a thud.

Luke’s eyes widened. His heartbeat quickened. They hadn’t even left the ground yet—and already Tristan couldn’t support his own weight, let alone Luke's.

And he’s supposed to carry me? Luke thought, his chest tightening with unease. He has S-grade strength… there’s no reason why he shouldn’t be able to hold on.

“The handhold is too narrow,” Tristan said, offering a wry smile. “If I use too much strength, I’ll crush it.”

“Ah…”

Understanding dawned on Luke. It wasn’t that Tristan was too weak—it was that he was too strong. The stone couldn’t withstand his grip. One handhold might hold, but not all of them would.

Then, a spark lit in Luke’s eyes. “Why don’t you make your own handholds?”

He quickly explained his idea, drawing inspiration from old anime back on Earth. If Tristan’s hands were tough enough, there was no reason it wouldn’t work.

Tristan said nothing, merely examining his hands—hands more used to turning pages and drafting reports than climbing. They were smooth, unmarred, save for a faint blister from holding the reins over the past week.

Then, without a word, he stretched out his fingers and thrust them into the cliff face.

The rock cracked and crumbled as his hand sank into it. Dust and small stones rained down, but when he withdrew, his fingers were firmly lodged in the stone. He pulled himself up effortlessly.

Not yet satisfied, Tristan repeated the motion with his other hand. Same result.

Bit by bit, he began his ascent, carving out new handholds in tandem with the natural ones. His movements were methodical but powerful—each thrust and pull showcasing the raw physicality he rarely displayed.

Luke stood below, awestruck. The quiet, bookish man was scaling the cliff like a one-man siege engine. He moved with surprising fluidity, brute-forcing his way upward without hesitation.

Just as it seemed Tristan might disappear over a ridge and leave him behind, the older man paused and began descending.

“It seems viable,” Tristan said as he reached the ground, his eyes settling on the harness Luke had fashioned from hemp ropes tied to the saddle.

“Will that hold your weight?”

Luke nodded. “Don’t worry about me—just focus on keeping us from falling to our deaths,” he said with a nervous chuckle.

Though their improvised plan had worked, there were risks.

Every time Tristan punched into the cliff, debris rained down, shaving away layers of stone. If he struck a weak section, the entire segment could give way—taking them both with it.

“Alright, I’m feeling more confident. Climb onto my back,” Tristan said, turning around and crouching slightly.

Luke obeyed, carefully positioning himself against the older man’s back. Tristan tied the ropes together across his chest, pulling them tight to ensure Luke wouldn’t slip.

His body pressed flush against Tristan’s broad back. It wasn’t comfortable—far from it—but Luke knew comfort wasn’t something either of them could afford right now.

“Ready?” Tristan asked.

“As ready as I’ll ever be…” Luke replied, his voice tinged with anxiety.

With no further warning, Tristan lunged upward, jamming his hand into one of the holds he’d carved earlier. The sudden motion caused Luke to yelp in surprise, his stomach lurching from the abrupt lift.

Then the climb began.

Despite the added weight, Tristan moved swiftly, each step deliberate and full of power. He scaled the cliff face with the ease of someone traversing a staircase rather than an uneven, vertical wall.

Though the initial jolt had startled him, Luke began to relax. Every one of Tristan’s movements was confident, measured. It was enough to settle the storm in his chest—if only for a moment.

All he had to do was hold on and wait until they reached the summit.

But just as that thought took shape, a deep rumble echoed through the valley.

Luke’s body shifted violently as Tristan’s left hand slipped. They lurched sideways, a sudden drop tugging at Luke’s stomach as a massive chunk of rock broke free and tumbled down—crashing onto the very spot where they had been standing minutes earlier.

His heart pounded as adrenaline surged through him. He couldn’t breathe. Couldn’t think.

But just as quickly, Tristan recovered. He drove his hand upward again, securing a new hold.

Instead of pushing forward, he paused and asked, “You alright?”

“Y-yeah… I’m fine,” Luke managed, though his voice was thin, and his mouth had gone dry.

“Good. Because if I feel anything warm running down my back, I’m cutting you loose,” Tristan quipped, chuckling.

Under normal circumstances, Luke would’ve appreciated the joke. But hanging off a cliff with death looming below, he was hardly in the mood. Not now. It felt like they were dancing on the edge of a knife.

Tristan resumed his climb, his movements as precise as clockwork. His limbs moved with practiced rhythm, his muscles showing no sign of strain. Clearly, the boost to his strength and endurance had paid off.

Luke wanted to shut his eyes, to escape the terrifying height—but his body refused.

Can I use my Steady Heart skill? he wondered, hope flickering in his mind.

The moment he activated it, calm washed over him. The wind felt gentler, and the constant, gnawing fear of plummeting disappeared. His breathing evened out. His limbs relaxed.

Then, suddenly, Tristan stopped.

“Young Luke!?”

“What’s wrong?” Luke asked, his tone now calm, almost detached.

“Oh… you went limp for a second. Thought you passed out,” Tristan replied.

“Focus on conserving your strength,” Luke said. “We’ve still got a long way to go.”

Tristan grunted in acknowledgment and continued upward, carving new handholds into the cliff face.

At this pace, they’d reach the jagged ridge—marking the midpoint of their ascent in around an hour.

Despite the danger, Tristan never wavered. He moved like a man possessed, unshaken and steady, scaling the rock like a nimble monkey. If fear lingered in his heart, he never let it show.

And that, more than anything, helped keep Luke’s own fear at bay.

If he hadn’t activated his Steady Heart skill—which essentially muted his emotions—Luke might’ve felt genuinely touched by the man’s quiet dedication.

“The tough part’s coming up. How are your arms holding up?” he asked.

“They’re fine. A little tense, that’s all.”

“Once we pass this ridge, there should be a spot to rest.”

Luke hadn’t been idle during the climb. With his emotions stabilized, he’d been using his Eagle Eye skill to scout ahead. High above, nestled just past the jagged rock face, he’d spotted a narrow shelf.

It wasn’t wide enough for both of them to sit, but it would be enough for Tristan to rest his arms before tackling the final stretch of the climb.

By now, the sun hovered directly overhead. Its rays, however, were blocked by the towering cliffs, leaving them cloaked in cool shadow. The wind that whipped through the valley carried a biting chill, adding another layer of discomfort.

“That’s good to know,” Tristan said. “Hang on tight. This next part will be tricky.”

As he began to scale the rock face, Luke felt the strain intensify. The incline steepened, shifting more of his weight against the harness. Every move Tristan made caused subtle tremors to pass through his frame.

If it weren’t for the man’s immense grip strength, they would’ve fallen long ago.

Luke remained silent, not wanting to distract him. He steadied his breathing and scanned the path ahead with Eagle Eye, analyzing every crevice and protrusion for danger.

Then he saw it.

A small, jagged crack traced its way from the edge of the shelf upward. At first, it seemed harmless. Just another flaw in the stone. But as Luke followed its path with sharpened vision, his chest tightened.

The crack wasn’t small at all—it ran all the way up the mountainside.

Even with Steady Heart suppressing his fear, he felt a cold weight settle in his gut.

“From here on, you’ll need to use only natural handholds, Tristan,” he said, his tone low and urgent.

Tristan froze, then gave a curt nod. His fingers moved to grip a jutting stone a foot away from the crack. The rock let out a low, ominous groan beneath his weight.

Luke’s gaze snapped back to the bird’s-eye perspective granted by Eagle Eye—and what he saw made his blood run cold.

The crack was widening, creeping higher up the mountainside with terrifying speed.

“Quick—climb!” he shouted.

To his relief, Tristan didn’t hesitate. The older man launched into motion, scaling the cliff like a gecko, his limbs moving with a speed and desperation Luke had never seen before.

Within moments, they reached the top of the ridge. Tristan heaved himself over the lip, his boots scraping against the stone.

That’s when they both saw it.

The ground beneath them rumbled violently, the vibration echoing through their bones like the tremors of an earthquake. But the true horror came a second later—when the entire rock shelf below them began to collapse.

Massive chunks of stone tumbled away from the cliff face, thundering down the mountainside. Dust and debris filled the air. Luke’s breath caught in his throat as he stared at the sight, frozen in place. It felt like watching the final scene of his life.

“Shit!” Tristan swore loudly, springing to his feet. Without hesitation, he jumped with everything he had, the full strength of his legs sending them airborne.

The sudden force jerked at the hemp ropes that bound them together, stretching them taut to the point of snapping.

The leap sent them soaring nearly twenty feet into the air before gravity reclaimed them.

Luke’s heart pounded in his throat as the world seemed to slow around him.

Below, the rocky shelf they’d stood on moments earlier was gone—crumbling into the void, tumbling toward the valley floor like a cascade of debris.

But there was no time to marvel at the destruction.

They were seconds away from joining it.

“Hold on tight!” Tristan shouted through gritted teeth.

Luke obeyed without hesitation, wrapping his arms and legs tightly around the man’s torso just as they crashed back toward the cliff.

The impact rattled his bones. Unlike Tristan—whose body had been strengthened by the system—Luke was still as fragile as any untrained teenager.

The jolt nearly ripped him free from the harness.

A sharp grinding noise echoed through the air. Luke glanced upward and saw Tristan’s fingers buried deep into the rockface, carving gouges as he fought to slow their descent.

Their bodies skidded down the cliffside, leaving a trail of disturbed stone in their wake.

Then, finally, they stopped—just above another narrow ledge.

Without a word, Tristan stepped down and collapsed onto the rocky shelf, breathing hard. He gently lowered Luke beside him.

Neither of them spoke for several minutes.

They simply sat there—shell-shocked, lungs burning, hearts pounding—allowing the weight of what had just happened to settle.

Now above the shadowed valley, the midday sun poured down on them, its rays warming their cold, battered bodies.

It was a small comfort, but one they both needed.

Tristan broke the silence first.

“You owe me—big time,” he said, still catching his breath.

Luke nodded, not even trying to argue.

He couldn’t. He didn’t want to.

They may have survived the worst part of the climb, but the summit was still far above them.

“Rest as long as you need. We won’t move until you’re ready,” Luke said, pulling his bag around and digging into it. He produced some rations, breaking off a piece of jerky.

“Want me to feed you? You can rest your arms.” He held out the strip, his tone somewhere between genuine concern and a teasing smirk.

Tristan grumbled and snatched the jerky from his hand, tearing into it with his teeth.

The way he chewed—loudly, with exaggerated annoyance—only made it funnier.

Luke chuckled.

Then he laughed.

It started small, but quickly grew—rolling into uncontrollable, slightly childish laughter that echoed off the cliffs. The kind of laugh only a brush with death could summon.

Tristan tried to resist, holding onto his grumpy façade, but it cracked.

A smirk tugged at his lips. Then he let out a low chuckle, and soon, he was laughing too.

Side by side on a sun-warmed ledge, with the wind whistling above and the abyss below, the two men laughed—bonded by terror, relief, and survival.

The mountain had nearly claimed them. But in return, it had only brought them closer together.
