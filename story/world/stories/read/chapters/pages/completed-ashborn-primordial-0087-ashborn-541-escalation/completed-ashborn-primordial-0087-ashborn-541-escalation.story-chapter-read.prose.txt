Vir left Siya on the Sarvaak, cursing every time the name came up. He would never live that down.

As ever, Siya didn’t argue at all. Despite clearly wanting nothing more than to join him, she understood his reasoning, and why he’d never allow her to no matter how much she begged.

While Vir might survive a direct hit from the Vimana’s arsenal, he’d never risk putting Siya in that sort of danger. Not even if she swore she could take a blast head on. He was quite certain she couldn’t, anyway.

“Well then,” he said to Maiya, Siya, Cirayus, and everyone else assembled on the deck. “I suppose I’ll go and test their defenses.”

“Don’t do anything rash, lad,” Cirayus said, frowning. “All we wish to know is whether they are amenable to negotiations and if they’ve activated the Vimana’s weapons systems.”

“Cirayuc, believe me. I’ve likely nearly died as many times as you have in your long life, and I have no intention of going through another such experience.”

He kicked off the deck and let Balancer of Scales take over, reducing his weight until he was sailing toward the Vimana in the distance.

He’d grown quite proficient with the ability over the years, and while he still couldn’t quite fly, by observing the Wind affinity prana in the air, he could very accurately ride thermal updrafts the same way Saunak’s gliders did, gaining altitude rather than simply falling slowly.

And now, with Wind orbs at his disposal, course corrections and even acceleration were possible without having to touch the ground first.

It was only now that Vir truly appreciated just what handy things orbs were. Far more one dimensional than tattoos though they might be, the value of altering one’s arsenal by simply picking up a different orb could not be overstated.

Still, while the orb did allow him to travel in a way he couldn’t before, with his featherlike weight, it was an odd way to approach something. He didn’t fly so much as he failed to drop, which meant he was buffeted far more than a skyship ever was.

As such, instead of attempting to fly directly at the Vimana, he positioned himself in its path and waited for it to come to him. It filled the sky as it drew closer, looming over him like the Mahakurma had when Vir first entered the Ash. Far larger than it ever looked from the ground, its golden hull blotted out a significant portion of the horizon.

This should be close enough, I think?

Aspect of the Demon God had been active the moment he left the skyship, and after he’d received the prana core, Clarity had been active almost continuously. He turned it off when interacting with Asura and especially Maiya and Siya, of course. The ability to predict someone’s answer was far more disconcerting than anyone could believe, all but ensuring every conversation became insufferable and frustrating. To say nothing of self-sovereignty.

There were no such concerns here, however, and with luck, it might even allow him to dodge whatever attack came his way.

Steeling himself, he channeled Wind Affinity prana into his voice amplification orb.

“Hear me! I am Sarvaak, Raja of Garga and the Akh Nara to demonkind.” His voice thundered across the distance between them, loud enough that anyone inside the Vimana would have no difficulty hearing it through their sensors. “I come to you not just as the foremost authority in the demon realm, but with the blessing of your supreme leader, Maiya, as well. You are ordered to cease this advance immediately and exit the Vimana.”

He waited a moment before adding, “Whatever your grievance, you will accomplish nothing here but senseless death. Your own king has ordered you to stand down. If you will not accede to us, I ask you to honor your oath to your king. If you do not, I will be forced to bring the full might of my powers to bear against you.”

For effect, Vir flared his infinite reserve of prana, forming a column of visible black Ash prana so dense it became completely opaque, spearing from the ground all the way to the clouds high above.

It was an impressive display, even for Vir. No being alive could pull off anything even remotely close. If this didn’t strike terror into their hearts, Vir knew nothing would.

Vir waited for a response, but the Vimana continued at its lumbering pace, entirely unbothered by the display.

He waited another minute, giving them the benefit of the doubt on communications, but as time passed and the Vimana drew ever closer, Vir’s own disbelief grew.

When was the last time he had been so thoroughly ignored? He couldn’t remember. Perhaps never.

Rather than anger, it was frustration and disbelief that roiled within his chest.

Why would they take such a risk? Did they presume themselves dead either way?

That actually seemed rather likely, and if so, it spelled terrible news.

There was nothing quite as terrifying as a desperate soul who had lost their will to live.

Vir pushed as much prana as he could channel through Aspect of the Demon God without burning out the tattoo, readied Yuma’s Embrace, and opened every Chakra before the hull of the Vimana slammed into him.

When he had entered the Vimana in the past, he’d simply passed through its open bay doors.

Now, he was stuck to its outer shell, the metal beneath his feet humming with power and prana alike. As with all Imperium machinations, massive inscriptions decorated every inch of the surface, powering some magic while also standing on their own as beautiful pieces of art.

As for the experience itself, awkward best described it. This Vimana, like so many others, was shaped something like two elongated pyramids connected at their base. They were mostly vertical structures, and as such, its hull ran at a steep angle to its summit.

Vir was attached to its side, facing sideways, and the only reason he hadn’t fallen was thanks to Balancer of Scales pressing him into the hull.

He plodded awkwardly along the smooth golden surface until he found a seam that looked promising.

Summoning the tiniest trickle of prana from his prana core, he fired it all in a concentrated blast so thin, it was almost invisible, magnifying its penetrating power a hundredfold.

Not only did his attack have no effect, the spear of prana reflected off the hull, shooting off into the distance, and Vir could only thank the gods that nothing had been in its path.

He tried again, this time with so much prana that his arm warped and melted from the effort, drowning him in pain.

He didn’t relent, firing as much raw, concentrated prana as possible at the hull.

This time, he thought he saw the hull glow slightly red, but the instant he stopped, it returned to its normal gold, leaving Vir gnashing his teeth in agony as Yuma’s Embrace regrew his tattered limb and Aspect of the Demon God restored his armor.

Giving up on the idea, Vir instead plodded his way to where he thought  the Vimana’s prana receptors were. The ones that opened the hangar bay doors.

While unlikely that this Vimana required the same sequence as the Vimana above the Altani capital, it was all that Vir had left.

He fired the bursts of prana into what he thought were the right locations, though his warped perspective made the task far more challenging than it ought to be, forcing him to actually leave the hull and fly some distance away.

Unfortunately, even that bore no fruit.

Cursing, Vir was about to head for the summit where he knew the Vimana’s command center would be when his communications orb pulsed with prana. Pulling it out, he found Siya’s face on the other end.

“The main hull won’t yield to any weapons we can muster,” she said straight away. “The shielding was designed to withstand anything short of another Vimana, but several of its weapon emplacements are vulnerable. They’re mounted to the hull, but aren’t protected by the shield because the dampening stabilizer’s pranic field cannot…” Siya coughed, seeing Vir’s eyes glaze over. “Suffice it to say, they are not nearly as well shielded. I suggest you target those.”

“Thanks, Siya,” Vir said with an embarrassed smile. To think he had to rely on a child to this degree was humbling, no matter how special she might be. “Can you describe them to me?”

“They resemble long tubes connected to an ovoid casing covered in inscriptions. You will know them when you see them.”

He soon found the objects she spoke of. They were unmistakable once he was looking for them, and nothing like any Imperium weapon he’d encountered before. Just simple tubes jutting from recessed housings along the Vimana’s flanks. He’d missed them because they were so small next to greater structure. Each was connected to a distinctive ovoid casing with inscriptions a hundred times more dense than the rest of the hull.

He fired a jet of prana at the first one, beginning with a level that didn’t destroy his arm.

To his pleasant surprise, the casing flared a hot red, then melted as its inscriptions broke. The vast amount of prana contained within the tube dispersed harmlessly into the air.

Vir worked his way methodically along the Vimana, taking out emplacement after emplacement, growing faster as he learned where the casings were weakest.

By the time he’d completed a full circuit of the hull, he’d dismantled every external emplacement he could locate.Thankfully, not one of them had fired their load.

Figuring he had done as much as he could, he pushed off the hull and let Balancer of Scales carry him back to the Sarvaak’s deck.

— — —

The Sarvaak’s command room had filled up by the time Vir arrived, and he felt like half the crew had crammed into the space, eager to hear what came next.

“Well, we confirmed some things at least,” Vir said, moving to the map table, which was far more filled in than it had been just hours prior. “They’ve managed to lock the Vimana against external entry, but thankfully, it seems they have no control over its weapons. Every external emplacement I found is gone.” Turning to Siya, he asked, “don’t Vimana have self-regenerative capabilities?”

“Yes,” Siya said from beside him. “However, this is also a system that must be activated. If they fail to regenerate, we will know that any damage dealt to the Vimana is permanent. Although, even if they do not, the weapons you destroyed are only a portion of its full armament.”

Siya looked away, as though embarrassed that she didn’t have a way to fully disable the Imperium creation, and Vir had to resist from rubbing her head.

“Are you kidding?” Maiya said, beating Vir to the punch. “Just that knowledge alone is invaluable. You might just have saved a lot of lives, Siya.”

Janak’s daughter turned away to hide her blushing cheeks.

“We need to get Saunak on this,” Vir said, looking at Cirayus, then, somewhat more reluctantly, at Siya. “He needs to find us a way in. As much as I detest asking this, would you be able to work with him to see if you can find a way? You can say no. There’s no obligation at all, and if you get bored or tired, you can leave at any time.”

Siya, of course, nodded immediately.

“He’s a little… off,” Vir said carefully. “But he’s the best mind we have for this kind of problem, and you’re the only one who knows more about Imperium systems than anyone alive. He’ll never be out of sight of either myself, Cirayus, or an Asura while you work, so you should be quite safe.”

“I said yes,” Siya said, puffing out her cheeks.

It was only then that Vir remembered Siya had shown quite an interest in the Mad Thaumaturge during their tour of Samar Patag earlier.

He glanced at Cirayus. “Can I ask you to bring Saunak to us? We should do everything we can to solve this problem before it turns into a crisis. After that, I’d like you to look after her. As much as I want to do that, there will be times when I am needed elsewhere.”

“I will protect her as my own daughter,” Cirayus said, without any of his usual levity. “You have my word.”

Vir caught the old demon glancing at Siya with a warm smile. Despite his stature and terrifying reputation, Cirayus had always been exceptional with children. Something that always came as a surprise to anyone who didn’t know him well.

Siya would warm up to him in no time, Vir was sure.

“And in the meantime?” Maiya asked.

“We have a crew of humans locked inside a superweapon from the Age of Gods, and nothing in our arsenal can touch the hull,” Vir said, looking around the room. “It’s simple then, if our weapons don’t work against a Vimana, then we just need something that does.

“We need a Vimana of our own.”
