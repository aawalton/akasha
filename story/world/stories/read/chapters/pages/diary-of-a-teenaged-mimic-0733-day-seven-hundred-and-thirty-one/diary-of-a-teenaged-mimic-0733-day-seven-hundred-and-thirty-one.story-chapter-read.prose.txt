"A decision that is made,When you have all the power?Will be judged accordingly."- Tabitha Diaz, Doctrine of Tabitha, Book of Egalitarianism

Here we have the second of the intended pair of Verses, Verse eight of the Book of Egalitarianism. Much like Verse seven, while the full impact of the Verses is only obvious when taken as a pair, Verse eight can enlighten us on its own.While Verse seven tells us that decisions made under duress are not true Consent, verse eight talks about the opposite situation, where the decision maker has the power to fully employ their Agency. In this type of situation, there is no excuse for harming others, no excuse for a lack of mindfulness. Every consequence of an action taken, of Agency employed in such a situation will be, eventually, visited on the decision maker in full.When taken together, the Verses speak eloquently to the idea of Equity of Judgement. Where not only the action taken, the decision made, the Agency employed, is judged, but the circumstances surrounding those choices, those decisions, those actions. She shows us with this how we can seek Equity in the Mortal Realm, rather than waiting for intercession by the Divine or a balancing of the scales in the Afterlife. Having been shown, having been given this knowledge, this power, it is now our duty to, as best we are able, do our part to seek Equity, both for ourselves and others.- Priestess Most High Above All Others, Archmage Imperator Saffron Aetos-Diaz, Commentary on the Doctrine of Tabitha

While she went back to doing Archmage things, I Inspected her. Specifically I poked at the word 'Strength'.

Strength is a measure of a subject's ability to exert physical force. The most common untrained use for Strength is lifting and moving objects, although it also plays a part in moving the subject's own body, such as when they jump, and can both improve a subject's ability to cause damage to physical objects and absorb physical impacts.

As some of those interactions can be difficult to objectively quantify, Inspect now measures Strength entirely by the subject's ability to lift heavy objects using their core. A value of 'one' in Strength indicates that the subject can lift one hundred fifty pounds using those core muscles in a 'squat', 'dead lift', or similar use of large, central muscles.

That seemed kinda vague, especially with how precise Personality had been. "Hey, Kitten?"
