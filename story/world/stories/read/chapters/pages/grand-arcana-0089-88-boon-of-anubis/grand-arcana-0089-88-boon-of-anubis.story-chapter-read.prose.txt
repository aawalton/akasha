Avon had once asked me what I thought to be the most dangerous opponent. He was in one of his philosophical moods shortly after Pup had died. It was the first time I had seen him so affected. I also missed the kid, but it was common for participants to perish. It was all part of the Grand Arcana.

At the time, I had said dragons or hydras, mostly just to get him off my back. He had shaken his head and told me that humans were the worst. To this day, I remember his exact words: “They are always nearby and grow with your level.” Now I saw how right he was.

Carl’s brother had a small set of cards, but he used them to their maximum. The combination of sand and stone in the chamber let him do pretty much anything he wanted. The construct was proof of that: one creature yet composed of endless components. Even if my blades had the ability to cut through rock, they wouldn’t have caused a dent. More alarming, my Gatekeeper card had been rendered completely ineffective.

“Freeze!” I ordered, using the Emperor card.

The construct instantly froze. Losing consistency, it poured down to the floor in the form of sand, only to re-emerge whole again.

“I really hate that card,” Max said, shaking off the sensation. Whatever benefits his pact granted, they were enough to considerably diminish the period of awe. “Always makes things messier.”

One of the statues left its position and charged right at me.

Restrict! I evaded its punch, landing a series of hits on its arm.

Not waiting to see the result, I cast a new spell circle on the floor.

Fire!

Flames rose up, only to have the sand construct shield its creator.

Smirking at me, he reached into it. His hands sank into the marble monster, pulling out a series of daggers he threw at me.

I leaped to the side, avoiding three of them. The fourth I blocked using the Five of Chains. That proved to be a mistake. The dagger burst into a cloud of sand, quickly reforming into dozens of stone needles, all of which flew at my arm.

Ten of Chalice!

I reacted just in time to cover it with a layer of ice before the projectiles could strike.

That was too close. All it would take was for one to enter my body for the fight to be over. The Stoneweaver could change its form and direct it up my bloodstream, earning victory through a fatal heart attack on my part.

Eleven of Chalice!

I covered Serp in acid, following up with a series of circular slashes. From what I remembered from school, marble was weak against acid, though that didn’t seem to hold true here. Droplets landed on both the sand and the normal statue, causing no damage whatsoever.

“Harlequin!” I shouted.

Not for a second did I imagine the monster would appear to help me. Rather, I was hoping for the instinctive fear that uttering the name would cause. If there was one thing that every participant—even betrayers—feared, it was the rule-masters.

Max flinched. For a split second, his attention was diverted as he glanced to the side.

Got you! I dashed across the chamber.

The Anubis statue struck at me in an attempt to squash me before I could reach him. A spell circle appeared next to my foot, erecting a stone pillar. At my level of magic, it wasn’t enough to stop the blow, just slow it down enough for it to miss me.

Go! I threw Serp at the man, then drew my divine blades and followed up with a double thread strike.

Walls rose up from the floor, as the tiles themselves were pulled up to create a new construct.

Stoneweaver, I thought. It stood to logic that he could control any stone, though why was he playing a trump card so early? More importantly, why wasn’t he using it to attack?

Despite all my achievements this run, I tended to be more reactive than proactive. Experience granted me a huge advantage, but it wasn’t foolproof.

Serp was also blocked, caught by the sand composite. Max was careful enough to leap away despite the apparent lack of danger. Clearly, he was familiar with living weapons and—by what it seemed—afraid of them.

I unsummoned the weapon and cast another mega spell, sealing off the entrance with a wall of earth. Then, I cast another, flooding the entire space with water.

To my surprise, that caught Max slightly off guard. With both statues a fair distance away, he had no protection against the initial blast. Within seconds he was sent flying towards the ceiling. Naturally, that didn’t cause any harm. Before he could make contact, the ceiling itself transformed, cushioning the blow.

However, that hadn’t been my main goal. Thanks to my experience in the well, I had seen that stone statues were useless underwater. While they couldn’t die, they were slow to move and didn’t appear to see very well. Additionally, Max himself was at a huge disadvantage. Usually, a person could manage underwater for about a minute before their efficiency dropped. In this case, I gave Max a quarter of that.

The segments of the sand construct extended upwards in an attempt to provide some assistance, but the action was slow.

No, you don’t! I combined the Nine of Chalice and the Archer cards.

A thread strike flew forward. Unaffected by the water, it cut through two elements of the statue, causing its arm to fall off. Interestingly enough, no reconstruction followed.

Of course, I told myself. Divine blades had a special effect, slicing whatever invisible force held the entity together. If Serp hadn’t consumed the last booster sphere, I would have leveled up my Observer card and potentially been able to see them. Now, I was effectively fighting blind.

More slashes followed as I used a multi-attack.

More segments of the futuristic statue were sliced off. Like perfectly formed chunks, they floated off the main body, slowly making their way to the floor. It was telling that no part had transformed into sand.

Knowing I’d hardly get an opportunity such as this, I raced through my options. My divine blades still had an electric gem, allowing me to shock everything in the water. I would also be affected, but there was a chance that Max would get it worse. Additionally, there was one other risky move I could try with no guarantee of success.

One option held the risk I might harm myself through inactivity, while the other might waste the window of opportunity I had fought so hard for.

The fuck with it! “Obey!” I used the Emperor card.

My voice ceased where the water began. Just because I could breathe underwater didn’t extend to talking. Thankfully, the card didn’t require it either. I felt the power of the order. All statues ended their clunky movements, freezing completely. What was more, Max also seemed to remain still. With my water spell still active, he remained pressed against the ceiling of the room with no air left to breathe.

Die. I thought.

All I needed was for the card to affect him for half a minute, maybe less. If his lungs got partially filled with water, his brain would panic, going into overdrive in an attempt to save him.

After five seconds, the man’s entire body shook. Bubbles blasted out of his mouth, then abruptly stopped.

“Obey!” I ordered again.

Could it be that I had won? The battle had been more intense than many I had experienced so far, and yet victory felt too easy to be true. Hyena hadn’t died from the first attack, and neither had any of the other Grand Arcana holders.

MAXIMILLIAN SWANSEN has died

A black message appeared.

I stared at it in disbelief. I had actually managed to kill a Grand Arcana participant with this? Maybe I was stronger than I expected.

ANUBIS boon activated.

MAXIMILLIAN SWANSEN is now undead.

Or maybe not…

The new message filled me simultaneously with confusion and dread. I had heard of participants turning undead, though I had never seen one with my own eyes. Several veterans in the past had claimed to have had friends go through that. If they were to be believed, certain cards granted that ability, similar to the Phoenix, transforming a person into something different.

I had asked Sparta about it, but to little surprise, she had been highly vague in her answer.

“There are many Grand Arcana cards,” she had told me with a smile. “There’s no way of knowing what all of them do.”

That was the truth, but I remember thinking that there was more to the story. Now, I was certain of it. In a single instant, the tables had turned, and my entire plan had been reduced to nothing.

Specials: Dark Vision, Sand Walking, Undead, Poison Breath

Two new specials appeared in his status box among the rest.

Shit! I instantly dissolved both my spell circles. What was more, I cast a new one, filling the chamber with a massive burst of air.

All the water was shoved out into the lower dungeon area, taking a statue and several chunks of marble with it. I myself was almost caught in the current, but thanks to my boots and a few auxiliary spells, I remained where I was.

Max really had an answer for everything. Even if I wasn’t breathing, poison breath in water would have quickly poisoned the entire chamber, causing me to die without him lifting a finger. Being a chosen of Anubis, he didn’t care much. There was a good chance that he might be brought back to his standard existence, if he even bothered. I, on the other hand, would have just ended my run, this time forever.

Max fell to the ground with a thump. There was no coughing, no shouting, or cursing. It was like watching a crash test dummy return to the ground. No longer constrained, three Anubis statues rushed to surround him, while the marble construct reclaimed some of its fragments.

“Thought you might get this far,” Max said in a calm tone, his voice obtaining a slight echo. “Congrats on doing it so quickly. The last anomaly needed an hour. Then again, they were a lot smarter than you.”

Gesturing the statues away, as if they were common thugs, the man started walking towards me. I kept using Harlequin’s Sight to check for any new developments. There didn’t seem to be any new skills or weapons present. Other than the specials, nothing had changed. What concerned me now, most of all, was the “undead.”

“I’ll give you a choice.” Bones grew out of the man’s body, ripping his clothes in the process.

Neither weapon nor armor, they could be used as both in combat. Why didn’t I have a fully developed Paladin card? Things would have been so different if I had.

“Answer me a simple question and you get to die quickly.” Purple liquid oozed over the bones emerging from his arms.

Judging by the way it dissolved what fabric remained on him, I could tell it was corrosive and likely poisonous.

“Which faction is backing you?”

I took a step back, entering a defensive stance. For whatever reason, this seemed to surprise him. The man stopped and looked at me for several seconds, as if he had just realized he had bought the wrong thing from a merchant.

“You don’t know?” he laughed. “You’ve done all this and you still don’t know?”

“Know what?” I asked, using the Lover card. “If I’m about to die, why not tell me?”

“How cute.” Max smirked. “Why not? Everyone else knows.” He crossed his arms. All his clothes were pretty much gone, as his body was covered with a natural exo-skeleton. “The pact is a lie. The more trials you complete, the more trials you get. The only way out is to break out on your own. You’re just too stupid to realize that.”

Am I? “You’re stuck here as well,” I said.

“Keep telling yourself that.” Max extended his arms to the sides. Part of the bones covering them extended to form blades. “Time’s up!”

The stone construct dashed towards me.

I had been keeping an eye on all the statues, knowing that they would come into play again. Based on the cards he had, there was no other way. The poison and the exo-skeleton were only there for dramatic effect. Even while undead, the greatest strength in his arsenal remained the Grand Arcana card.

Get ready, Serp! I thought.

Suddenly, a volley of force blasts struck the construct and one of the Anubis statues.

Holes the size of bowling balls covered the stone entities. Confronted by this sudden change, the construct leaped back out of harm’s way. The Anubis statue was not so lucky. Losing a leg, it collapsed to the ground. The damage dealt to it was so severe that although technically not destroyed, there was nothing it could do.

A new figure entered the chamber.

Eleonor? I wondered.

She had the same clothes and appearance, only this time her entire being was surrounded by a faint layer of yellow light. More importantly, her eyes had turned gold.
