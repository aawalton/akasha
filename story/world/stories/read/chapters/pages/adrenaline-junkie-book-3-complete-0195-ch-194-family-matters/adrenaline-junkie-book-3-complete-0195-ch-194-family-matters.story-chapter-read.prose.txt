The ritual circle behind Noor shimmered one final time before the energy held within it mere moments ago faded away from her senses, the last of its pale grey energy curling away like mist in the breeze.

As she stepped away from the faded marking along the brick flooring, a deafening hush fell over the teleportation room, causing her to blink slowly as her newly gained glowing purple eyes adjusted themselves to the sheer amount of mana around her that was now visible to her newly gained ocular abilities.

And just like that, she was home.

Her boots, worn leather worked through with silver thread, clicked against the dark marble floor of the manor's foyer – her manor, given to her by her recently gained Father when he’d located her shortly after she’d teleported onto an undead planet after having purchased her valuables from the System Bazaar.

A massive chandelier hung overhead, made of polished bone and obsidian, glowing faintly with various embedded gems. The air carried a dry chill, the kind she’d become accustomed to after having become an undead and accepting herself as one.

As she stepped into the hall, the undead attending staff all turned in synchrony, the undead sparks within their cores, sensitive to her, flared with recognition as their master had finally returned from the duties assigned to her. From tailored butlers in silk suits to maids with fine lace collars, all stilled their duties the moment she, along with her skeletal guards that emerged one by one from the locket that hung across her neck, came into view.

“Lady Noor,” they said in unison, hurriedly bowing.

A skeletal hand holding a feather duster clacked against the floor as its owner dipped too fast in deference. Another, a ghoul with half an opera mask stitched into its decayed face, swept off its top hat with a flourish.

Noor gave them a slight wave and a bright, toothy smile. “Hello, darlings. Don't let me interrupt. I’m just walking through. Thank you so much for your hard work!”

“Of course, Lady Noor,” came the immediate, breathless reply. “Your words are far too much for beings such as us.”

She rushed down the manor corridor without a care in her mind, with her tattered cloak dragging soundlessly behind her, along with her skeletal guards that kept up pace with her easily, who were now joined by various skeletal snakes and scorpions that were being carried by said skeletal guards. The candles along the walls lit themselves as she passed, flames turning a pale purple in her presence, an effect of her newly gained Affinity.

“Good evening, Grennet,” she nodded to a hunchbacked butler polishing the ribcage of a harp.

“Glory to you, Lady Noor,” he rasped, eyes not leaving his work but spine bent low enough to where his face nearly touched the floor. “Is there anything for this lowly servant to be of service to you?”

“I’ll be in Quilnera’s room, so if you could bring us some drinks and some snacks that would be delightful,” Noor smiled as she made a quick twirl before continuing her rush down the hall. “Thank you!”

Noor finally reached the tall double doors in the east wing, ones carved from the ribbed wood of a tree long extinct.

Without hesitation, she pushed them open.

“QUILNERAAAA!” she sang out, launching herself through the doorway.

Inside, lounging atop a plush violet sofa chair, was a mannequin, one that looked to be made out of charcoaled wood, faceless save for two black crystal eyes that shimmered as they were now disconnected from her own Aetherglass, Quilnera remained perfectly still until the last moment.

Then, with a sound similar to bones softly clicking into place, Quilnera’s arms moved to wrap around Noor’s form as she barreled into her lap, before pulling her close into a hug.

“I missed you,” Noor mumbled into its collar, nuzzling into the smooth curve where a neck might have been. “So much…”

Quilnera’s head tilted slightly, the gesture as expressive as a smile. Her voice manifesting her own mind, “You’ve returned with joy in your steps and victory in your flame. Tell me, how do you feel?”

Noor pulled back just enough to meet the glinting obsidian gaze of the mannequin, her glowing purple eyes alight with giddy energy. Her skeletal guards had paused just outside the door, instinctively knowing this room was not for them, while those of even lesser intellect, the more bestial of skeletons, moved about in Quilnera’s room.

“I feel amazing,” she said with a wide grin, one hand playing idly with one of Quilnera’s finger joints. “The domination of Plut’uls is nearly finished. And the rest of the undead generals’ forces had already taken the southern hemisphere by the time I returned, and the two cities I was asked to raze?” She gave an exaggerated gasp. “Reduced to foundations and ash! General Tarth was truly an amazing help, she helped me so much with the D-Grades that were in hiding within the cities.”

Quilnera’s fingers flexed, stroking her shoulder once in approval.

“You razed them well, then. I can feel the soot in your aura still.”

“I know!” Noor giggled, delighted. “But! But, but, but I remembered not to touch the Omniversal Solution’s Branch Tavern! I remembered how the generals were very, very adamant on me not doing it as it could be a problem for Father.”

“An act of wisdom, those generals were correct,” Quilnera noted approvingly. “There is a truce between the Undead Queen and The Voyager. And as one of her majesty’s Crowns, if the daughter of Ossarion the Crowned intentionally harmed the property of The Voyager, problems might have occurred. You’ve shown quite a lot of growth since the last time I’ve seen you.”

She sat up slightly straighter, still nestled in its arms, her expression shifting to mock seriousness. “You bet I have. I even went into this old crypt I found on the outskirts of the second city, some weird rumor said it connected to one of their sacred underground tombs. And guess what? It did.”

“And what treasure did you find in the grave of the forgotten?”

“A trapped guardian dwelf priestess,” Noor said, grinning like a cat. “She screamed for two full hours after I bound her soul, but after I finished razing the first city down, she calmed down enough to talk. So now she’s sealed in a bone lantern I’ve got in my pocket, she gives terrible tea recommendations, but I think I’mma make her my first soul sentient undead follower.”

Quilnera chuckled, though the sound wasn’t audible so much as it was felt in the marrow of the air. It tightened the hug briefly, then let one arm uncoil to gently tuck a strand of Noor’s hair behind her ear.

“Two months apart… and you’ve accomplished much and completed the task given to you by Ossarion the Crowned. Just as expected.”

Noor leaned her head back on its shoulder, the ambient purple flames of the room dimming slightly in sympathy with the calm warmth flooding the space.

“And you?” she asked. “What did you do while I was gone? Please don’t say ‘sat in this room and did nothing,’ or I’ll get mad.”

“Of course not,” Quilnera replied. “I visited the Dreaming Spine to speak with the oracles. I also helped reorganize the left wing of the manor. The taxidermy library now has alphabetical sorting, and the lich portraits have been silenced… mostly.”

“Ah, so that’s why it was quiet when I passed by,” Noor hummed, visibly pleased. “You’re the best.”

“You’re biased,” Quilnera said.

Noor grinned again. “You’re right. But also right.”

A knock echoed gently against the chamber doors.

“Come in~!” Noor called sing-song.

Grennet entered, a silver tray balanced expertly atop one crooked hand. On it were two fluted glasses filled with ghostfruit extract, alongside a plate of spiced bone-dust crackers and sliced spiritvine cheese.

“Lady Noor, honored Quilnera,” he rasped, bowing deeply. “As requested.”

“Thank you, Grennet!” Noor beamed, accepting the tray and setting it on the low table beside the sofa.

Once the butler had left, she poured a glass for Quilnera

“To us,” Noor toasted.

Quilnera raised her glass with her, nodding regally.

“To us.”

Their glasses clinked.

After a while of pleasant conversation between the two, shared laughter, and the faint crackle of pale-purple firelight, Noor finally stood, brushing the folds of her repaired cloak, eyes lingering onto the remaining snacks before shaking her head.

“I should go check in with Father’s steward, Peelus, and get my report officially filed,” Noor said, stretching with a groan. “If I delay again, I’ll have to hear that awful lecture of his on how I shouldn’t ignore the ‘chain of command’ for another three hours.”

Quilnera offered no protest, merely extended a hand, which Noor took and squeezed.

“Don’t miss me too much,” Noor grinned, walking through the tall doors that her skeletal guards opened for her from the outside, skeletal scorpions and snakes trailing behind her like a drifting tide.

The doors clicked shut behind her.

The moment the echo of her leather boots vanished down the corridor, Quilnera’s posture changed. The gentle curve of relaxation vanished into one of regal stature.

The walls shimmered as fresh layers of warding magic reactivated, one after another, glyphs stitched from shadow, silence, and sightlessness weaving invisibly across the room’s borders.

With her wards reactivated to ensure that no one would be able to disrupt her, she turned to the back of the room.

From a hidden alcove behind a carved bookcase, she retrieved a small, sealed red glass bottle, its contents thick and viscous.  Along with the sealed red glass bottle, she carefully took out the silver-rimmed chalice beside it.

Quilnera held it with both hands, reverently.

The seal on the bottle flared dimly as she broke it with a twist of her fingers. Slowly and carefully, she poured the red, thick, and viscous liquid into a silver-rimmed chalice, to which the runes along the chalice began to activate.

The moment the last drop touched the rune at the bottom of the cup, the liquid began to gently stir, increasing in speed with every second that passed until a whirlpool-like stir was formed.

Quilnera bowed low before it. Her knees pressed into the stone floor, her back a graceful arc of humility. She folded her hands and lowered her head.

The chalice floated.

And the air shifted.

Quilnera’s voice, quiet and unwavering, cut through the silence. “Ossarion the Crowned, my liege.”

She paused.

“Yes, my liege, by your orders.”

There was no audible reply, but something spoke to her nonetheless. The flickering of the flame dimmed as if the room itself were listening. The whirlpool in the chalice deepened, spinning faster.

Quilnera’s brow furrowed. She hesitated.

“I am unsure if,” she admitted slowly, “the seal that… the blessed daughter Noor requested upon her mind will still be in place once she evolves–”

The whirlpool twisted violently, and the flames of the chandeliers rose.

Quilnera silenced immediately, spine straightening under unseen weight.

“…Of course, my liege,” she said softly, eyes never rising from the floor.

Another pause. Then the liquid in the chalice stilled.

Quilnera remained bowed.
