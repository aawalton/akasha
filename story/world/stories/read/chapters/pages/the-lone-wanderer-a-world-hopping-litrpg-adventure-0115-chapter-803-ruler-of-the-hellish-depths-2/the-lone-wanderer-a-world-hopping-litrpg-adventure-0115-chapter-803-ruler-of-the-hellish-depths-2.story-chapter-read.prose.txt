The quasi-divine beast was nearly a kilometre long from head to tail tip, its exterior consisting of rough, glowing scales that resembled mineral deposits. Percy had found the creature intimidating the first time he had laid eyes upon it – and it was still, objectively speaking, one hell of a freak – but Percy was no longer the same person.

For one, he’d caught up to the monster’s strength much sooner than he had expected. For another, it barely registered as a tiny worm compared to the Spectral Broodmother and the other threats waiting for him out there, across the stars.

Shrouding his host in willpower, Percy had one of his person-sized sentries wrap itself around the domain and shift to the colour of the surrounding seawater. Combining the optical illusion with the seals scattered within his friend’s spiritual blood, Percy concealed his physical appearance, mana signature, and even his soul, making himself completely invisible.

The eel was still technically capable of sensing him with its domain, but he wasn’t planning to get very close, and the fish’s tiny body would barely register as a speck of dust within the monster’s willpower even if he did.

He spread the remaining person-sized sentries in four directions, transforming each into a copy of Kassorith. Percy didn’t expect anyone to interrogate the quasi-divine beast, since it would hopefully be dead soon and no outsiders seemed to even be aware of this planet’s existence, but there was no reason to assume his human appearance when he could just as easily choose the form of his favourite scapegoat.

Using the concealment runes in reverse this time, he made each construct look like a White-cored mage, complete with a fake lump of high-grade mana embedded in its sternum.

‘Do you think it’ll buy it?’ Micky asked.

‘Not at first,’ Percy admitted with a sigh. Ironically, the eel’s ignorance of cosmic matters would work in its favour. It had no idea that sapients existed, so it might not believe that a creature with a White core could possibly be that small. ‘But it’ll have no choice but to acknowledge the threat when it feels the power of our domain.’

He positioned his host within two kilometres of the eel, resisting the powerful current with his willpower. Percy’s greatest disadvantage in this fight was that the range of his actual domain was much shorter than that of his sensory domains, so he wouldn’t be able to avoid the battle entirely. He’d have to carefully linger at the edge of the battlefield and keep harassing his opponent from afar.

He just had to make sure that the eel never realized where the attacks were coming from.

With everything in place, he finally unfolded his domain, though he didn’t aim for the quasi-divine beast directly. Instead, he unleashed his willpower on the Green beasts trapped within the whirlpool, crushing their bodies, shattering their cores and allowing their mana to dissipate in the water.

The eel’s glowing eyes widened in shock. It didn’t attack immediately, as it clearly struggled to understand what exactly had happened. It wasn’t every day another creature dared to challenge it, and this was probably the first time it had ever encountered a powerful opponent. Not to mention that it still didn’t know where the intruder was.

This worked to Percy’s advantage, so he didn’t rush to unveil his decoys either. He pulled away from the eel and simply waited as it scanned its surroundings with its domain.

As Percy had deduced in previous trips, the whirlpools were natural phenomena that the quasi-divine beasts had merely hijacked for their own feeding purposes, so the powerful currents didn’t stop spiralling around the eel. They continued to drag unsuspecting Green creatures in from the surrounding seawater, though that happened more slowly without the monster actively participating.

A couple of minutes later, it probably decided that the interruption wasn’t important enough to delay its meal, because it resumed mixing its willpower into the water to gather its food faster.

That was when Percy struck again, massacring hundreds of Green beasts and denying his target a second batch.

The eel opened its maw wide and let out a furious screech that caused every droplet of seawater in the area to vibrate.

Percy’s domain protected his host from the tremor, and his magical sentries resisted it without much difficulty, but the few surviving beasts within three kilometres of the eel suffered serious internal damage. Their organs ruptured, their wounds widened, and blood poured out of their already-mangled bodies even faster.

Worse still, the quasi-divine beast finally decided to stop lazing around and start actively searching for its opponent. It lunged towards Percy’s general direction – where the brunt of his domain had struck from – with the speed of a meteor, methodically sending wave after wave of willpower outwards and trying to detect any anomalies in its path.

Percy swam away as fast as he could, though it was difficult to match the eel’s speed with his willpower alone. His body was countless times smaller and lighter, but also incomparably weaker, and the Orange creature’s injuries made it harder to move without cutting its remaining time short. Worse still, the more Percy relied on his domain, the easier it would be for his opponent to feel the disturbance within its own.

Realizing that he was seconds away from being caught, Percy decided to bring the decoys in.

The eel slowed down briefly when four fake Thess’kalans surrounded it, probably trying to determine whether they were truly the culprits behind the attacks. It hadn’t met sapients before, but it obviously understood the threat that a group of White-cored creatures represented, having passed through that stage itself.

Thankfully, it didn’t hesitate for long before steering its massive body and swimming towards the nearest sentry.

The constructs were too far away for Percy to shield with his domain. He had deliberately kept them several kilometres apart to avoid drawing the eel’s attention to his host. Unfortunately, this meant that he had to keep the puppets completely outside the monster’s range, lest it captured them with its willpower.

The good news was that he could have them phase through the water to easily outspeed the mountain-sized creature, allowing him to keep them at a fixed distance from its body. The eel’s movements were straightforward and easy to predict, so Percy had no trouble reacting to its every attack before it even started.

Suffice to say, Percy would never kill the monster with just a domain and a handful of powerless illusions, but he didn’t have to.

A quasi-divine beast’s greatest enemy was its own body. Less than twenty minutes after the start of the battle, Percy could already sense his opponent’s growing frustration and its movements getting progressively more sluggish. Its enormous body burned through its limited stamina at an unsustainable pace, bringing it closer to death with every wasted movement and each second it spent unable to eat.

The eel tried to supplement its waning reserves by funnelling any Green beasts unlucky enough to end up in its path into its enormous maw. The monster’s sporadic snacks produced far less energy than its usual setup at the bottom of the whirlpool, but they did serve to prolong the battle – much to Percy’s dismay.

He tried to interrupt his opponent’s meals whenever he could, though he found it difficult to keep up with the quasi-divine beast and the sentries without betraying his location. At least, the other fifty constructs helped by constantly scanning the surrounding regions and allowing Percy to guide the monster along the route with the lowest concentration of food.

About an hour into the battle, the eel seemed to realize that it would never catch its nimble opponents, but also that they were unable to harm it directly. Weighing the genuine risk of starving to death against the minimal threat the illusions posed, it finally decided to return to its whirlpool.

Percy couldn’t allow the creature to recover.

Grabbing a few hundred slabs of midnight blue ice that his main body had packed into his seal, he charged them with as much soul-freezing ice as he could transfer through the cord, turned them invisible and arranged them into a three-layered formation around one of his sentries. Next, he activated the enchantment while ramming his minion into the eel’s side, freezing a small chunk of its soul solid.

This was the most powerful attack that Percy was able to unleash under the circumstances, and he wouldn’t be able to replicate it for some time. The damage it had caused was minimal, but it had done its job.

The monster screeched in pain for a handful of seconds before locating the “perpetrator”. Percy had the sentry pretend to escape, yet ultimately allowed the eel to pulverize it with its domain and devour its remains. He didn’t know whether the creature found the small amount of mana within the sentry’s core suspicious, but it probably blamed it on the construct’s small size.

The suicide attack had barely scratched the monster, but it had achieved two important purposes: it had made the sentries appear far more dangerous than they were, and given the beast hope of defeating them.

Aborting its retreat, the quasi-divine creature resumed its chase, stubbornly trying to catch its three remaining opponents.

Percy moved them more slowly than before, to make it look like they were starting to grow tired as well. He had them nearly fall into the eel’s grasp a couple of times and eventually sacrificed a second construct to reinforce the illusion that the creature was winning.

Three hours later, the eel was swimming at a third of its original speed, and its uneven scales had grown visibly dimmer.

Whether it had finally seen through Percy’s ruse, or decided that destroying the remaining sentries wasn’t worth it, it began swimming back to its feeding spot once more. This time, there wasn’t much that Percy could do to stop it. However, the monster was now weak and slow enough for him to harass directly with relative impunity.

Percy’s host wasn’t exactly in great shape either, but his ability to wield his domain and sentries luckily didn’t depend on the creature’s physical condition in the slightest. Revealing his position at last, he began to openly destroy the Green beasts once more, not allowing even a single intact core into the eel’s mouth. It tried to chase him away a couple more times, but it was no longer strong enough to offer any serious resistance.

About six hours after the start of the battle, the eel’s eyes finally glazed over and the tension left its body as it collapsed onto the seabed with a muted thud.

It was dead.
