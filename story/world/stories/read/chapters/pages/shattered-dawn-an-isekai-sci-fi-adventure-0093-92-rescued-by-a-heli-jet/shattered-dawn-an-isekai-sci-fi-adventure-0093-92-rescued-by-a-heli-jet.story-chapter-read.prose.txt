Tuft transformed back into Snickers and prowled along beside Zev. Keyla, Selna, Elion, and Yaret fell in as Zev passed them.

"Tuft is Snickers?" Elion said, astonished. "Like... all along? Or is he just pretending to be right now? Is he mimicking my cat?"

"Tuft _is_ Snickers," Keyla confirmed. "You haven’t seen him shifting back and forth?"

Elion shook his head. "I guess I’ve been distracted by all the fighting going on."

He followed Zev down the hall. "Speaking of the fighting," Elion said, "what about… Tael and Kasm? What did Gorman do to them?"

"It’s the mind-control chip," Keyla said. "He must have figured out whatever he was trying to do with the crystal, and now he controls everyone in Aterfel—along with all the infected scavengers."

"We have to help them," Elion said.

"How?" Keyla asked.

"I killed Gorman once," Elion replied. "I’m not afraid to do it again."

They reached the back of the manor and exited into a cleared area. Zev’s helicopter rested there, its sleek form illuminated by faint moonlight. A second, smaller vehicle, like a skyskimmer, rested nearby.

Rennick, the Aurelian who had come with the Skyskimmer to pick up Elion earlier, waved as Zev exited the mansion.

"There’s an army closing in," Rennick said. "We gotta get out of here!”

Zev directed the injured and unconscious Prator to Rennick, who loaded the man in the smaller vehicle.

“Take him back to the healers,” Zev said, inspecting the small space as he turned to look at Elion’s friends. “Come with me, Elion!”

Zev led Elion to the helicopter as its powerful rotors started up, kicking a cloud of dust into the night sky.

Rennick’s small craft took off, darting away into the night sky.

Keyla, Selna, Yaret, and Tuft followed, but as they tried to board the craft, Zev stopped them.

“You can’t come!” Zev said. “We’re going into a war zone!”

“We can’t leave them!” Elion protested. “This is a warzone!”

“We’re short on time,” Zev yelled back, voice straining over the whirring rotors.

“They’re my friends. They saved my life!”

Zev shook his head, but gestured for the others to board. “We can put them down on the outskirts of Erod,” he said. “But that’s probably not any safer than here!”

As everyone climbed into the craft, Zev moved and sat in the co-pilot seat beside Isalyn. Elion grabbed Zev’s arm.

“Wait,” Elion said as the helicopter began to lift off of the ground. “We can’t leave yet. There are more good people back there. Friends. We can’t let Gorman take over!”

Zev scowled. "What do you mean?”

"We can’t abandon Kasm, Tael, and the others!" Elion said. "Gorman is doing something terrible to them."

“Has Dorian detected us?” Zev asked Isalyn.

Isalyn tapped a screen displaying squiggly lines and shifting shapes. "We’ve been detected," he said. "Looks like they’re sending a few fast interceptors."

“Zelian’s halls,” Zev muttered. “How long?”

“Under ten minutes,” Isalyn said.

“That’s fast,” Zev noted.

“Not as fast as us,” Isalyn said. “If we hit the transition.”

“Sorry Elion,” Zev said. “But we have more important things to deal with right now. Your friends will be fine.”

The helicopter lifted off and flew low over the city. A few poorly aimed laser shots zipped past the chopper, but soon they were free, soaring into the night.

Isalyn called out. “Someone take the gunner’s chair and return fire!“

Selna moved to obey.

"No don’t shoot! They’re mind-controlled," Elion said urgently. "Most of those people don’t want to fight us. Don’t shoot them!"

“We’ll have to fly low then,” Isalyn said, dropping the chopper down closer to the wreckage of Kairn Tol. “It’ll take longer before we can hit the transition.

“We can’t just leave them!”

“Elion,” Zev said, grabbing him by the arm. "We don’t have time to rescue anyone else right now, Elion. Getting you out of there was the priority. If we’re still here when the interceptors arrive, nobody will get rescued."

Elion clenched his jaw.

Zev exhaled sharply. "It’s a noble impulse, Elion. I praise your loyalty to your friends. We will come back for them."

The helicopter cleared the wreckage of Kairn Tol. In the distance behind them, lights flashed and dark shapes moved along the walls of New Kairn Tol.

*What is going to happen to them?*

Four bright white lights caught Elion’s attention. They shone like stars, but moved underneath the canopy of Celestial Sphere shards. They didn’t appear to be moving, but grew brighter as he watched.

“Are those—”

He was cut off as Isalyn yelled.

“Brace yourselves!”

The aircraft surged into the air. Elion’s legs groaned under the increased weight of his body.

“Stowing the blades!” Isalyn reported.

The sound of the chopper’s blades stopped, and the craft became strangely silent. Elion grabbed hold of a strap, becoming weightless as the craft fell back toward the ground.

“Wings!” Isalyn said.

A groan from the fuselage as wide wings extended out on either side of them, catching the rushing air and propelling them forward. Elion’s weight returned.

“Jet!”

A deep rumble, then an overwhelming rush of noise signaled the powerful jet engine at the back of the craft starting up. The craft sprang forward, slicing through the night sky.

The sudden acceleration pushed Elion back, and he stumbled into one of the chairs in the cargo hold. The lights of New Kairn Tol fell away rapidly in the distance.

*I’ll come back for you,* Elion promised. *I won’t leave you in Gorman’s grasp, my friends.*

“That was a rough transition!” Zev yelled.

“She’s doing her best,” Isalyn replied.

Elion remained seated, deciding it would be better in case the craft engaged in any other abrupt movements.

Yaret grunted, seated across the craft from him. Blood spattered across his shirt and pants; he’d been injured at some point in the fighting and Elion hadn’t even noticed. Beside him, Tuft sat, still working his healing magic.

Elion watched Tuft silently for a moment before speaking. "I still can’t believe you were disguised as my cat all that time," he said.

Tuft glanced up from his work, a smile spreading across his remarkably cat-like features. "It’s an old Heranan skill," he said with a shrug. "Didn’t you ever wonder why your cat lived longer than all the others?"

Elion shook his head. "It never crossed my mind," he admitted. "Did my parents know? Did Zev know?"

Tuft shook his head. "None of them knew," he said. "I am of the Secret Order of Protectors of House Starhold. In my old age, I was assigned to watch over your mother by Queen Loreign."

"As a healer, it’s better for enemies not to know of my existence," Tuft continued. "If they did, I could become a target and bring more danger to my warden. Besides, in this old body, moving around has become... challenging. My other form—the one you called Snickers—is far more agile."

Elion rubbed his brow, his expression caught between disbelief and exasperation. "I still can’t believe I never realized it," he said.

Tuft chuckled softly, his eyes twinkling with amusement before returning to his work.

Nearby, Keyla sat across from her mother, staring at her. Selna leaned her head back against the bulkhead, eyes closed.

The cold, considering look in Keyla’s eye saddened Elion. She no longer knew or recognized her mother. The terrible wound inflicted on her by the Desmian power had robbed her of her most cherished memories.

He wanted to go to her and say something to make things right. But he didn’t know what he could say.

Zev came back from the cockpit and sat down beside Elion. As the craft reached cruising speed and altitude, the noise quieted somewhat. Keyla and Selna appeared to be sleeping, while Tuft continued checking on Yaret's health.

"Elion," Zev said, "what happened to you? Why didn’t you go with Rennick when I sent the Skyskimmer to pick you up?"

Elion took a deep breath, remembering how he’d felt in that moment—faced with the promise of safety and comfort aboard the Skyskimmer, but unable to abandon the people of Aterfel to fight the infected alone.

"It’s kind of a long story," Elion said.

"I’ve got time. We’ll be in the air for several hours," Zev replied.

So Elion began his tale. He spoke of how nervous he’d been when Zev’s promised return was delayed, and of finding the scrap of Warlock’s cloak, the strange whispers, and his unusual transportation to what he now knew was an Elion Altar. He described being locked up in the Walkers’ Clubhouse, seeing the Warlocks in the woods, and his leap into the portal, worried that the burden of rescuing Liora might now fall solely on his shoulders.

He talked about Kasm, Domas, and the courageous people of Aterfel. He described the crystal shard that oppressed them, Gorman’s lies and betrayal, and their plan to destroy the shard. He confessed his sense of obligation and how he felt he couldn’t leave the people to fight without him.

"And that’s why I didn’t take the Skyskimmer," Elion finished.

"What about your sister?" Zev asked.

"I love my sister," Elion said, "and I know we have to help her. But once I found out you were still alive..." He trailed off.

Zev shrugged. "And what about her?" he asked, nodding toward Keyla.

"I didn’t know you were still alive," Elion admitted. "I didn’t want her to be in trouble or captured by Gorman. I couldn’t leave her behind, knowing he might hurt her somehow."

Zev smiled. "You’ll make a great Knight one day, Elion. Tell me the rest of your story. How did you end up in New Kairn Tol?"

Elion continued his story. When he finished, Zev smiled again.

"You know," Zev said, "many in our Order—those who follow the Path of Dawn—believe that training in the knightly virtues at an Aurelian school from a young age is essential to becoming the kind of Knight who can keep his oaths. But I don’t have any doubts about you."

Elion slumped, overwhelmed. "I don’t know that I’ve been that good at it," he said. "I don’t think I even understood what I was saying. I was just desperate for the power to help Kasm. I wish I’d understood better what I was getting into."

Zev laughed. "None of us really understand what we’re getting into. Years of training with the knights might help prepare you mentally, but until you’ve tried and failed to keep those oaths, none of us truly understand what they mean."

"But I have failed," Elion said. "I killed Tharnen. I nearly abandoned Aterfel. I gave away Ascendancy Stones to Kasm when maybe I should have kept them to save Liora. Isn’t stopping Dorian more important than a few injuries in Aterfel?"

"Maybe it is," Zev said. "Maybe it’s not. Grappling with the contradictions of the oaths is part of being a knight."

"But," Elion pressed, "doesn’t that make me unworthy? I heard Dorian broke his oaths."

"Yes," Zev said, his expression grave. "But you are nothing like Dorian. You are brave, kind, strong, and thoughtful. He is none of those things.

"Dorian was stripped of his powers by a council of the Path of Dawn—not because he did his best to keep the oaths and made mistakes, or because he tried and failed, but because he willfully rejected them."

“And Prator?”

Zev’s gaze shifted to the floor. "Perhaps it will be the same for that man. We will see."

Elion closed his eyes, drowning in the emotions of the moment. His senses were muted by the steady rumble of the jet engine vibrating through the craft’s frame. The smell of oil, metal, and recycled air grounded him in the space.

For the first time in a very long time, he felt like he could relax. Tension slid from his shoulders, his arms loosened, and his breathing steadied.

His mind wandered to Liora—to the look of panic on her face as an arachnatronic dragged her from the house.

And he felt powerless to help her.

"Where are we going?" Elion muttered, hoping to take his mind off of his failings.

"To Erod," Zev said. "Your sister needs our help."
