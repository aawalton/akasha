Miraculously, Cyros manages to catch up to us before we’ve left the Market. He’s trying to control his breathing so he doesn’t look as winded as he is, but I’m pretty sure he had to pull off some impressive gymnastics to keep up with Piria’s casual air-walk.

“Where are we going?” I ask as Cyros joins me, trailing her through a maintenance hatch that leads into a dusty alleyway back in the main portion of the city.

“Sorry, didn’t I say?” Piria glances back at me apologetically. “The cidery! At least, I hope so. If it’s still there.”

“Oh.” I’d just figured we weren’t still going to do that after the encounter with Widengra’s champion. “What is there to talk about? I mean, the ice champion is here. Now I know to keep an eye out. That’s it, right?”

Piria hesitates, wringing her hands. “Well, you know. We’re both Shirasil’s champions, aren’t we? I thought it would be nice to get to know each other.”

“Wait,” Cyros says, catching his breath. “You’re both what?”

“Nothing,” I snap. “It’s just a misunderstanding.”

“Oh, no.” Piria looks apologetic. “I assumed he knew, since you two seemed so close.”

“What?” Cyros repeats, looking at me. Then he glances down at my Noxious Gauntlet. “You said it was a blessing from a god. It’s from Shirasil, isn’t it? And it’s not just any blessing.”

[Noxious Gauntlet ended.]

I shake the ghostly green light away from my hand, which I stuff into my pocket. “It’s just temporary,” I grumble. “Not like he gave me any choice in the matter.”

Cyros just stares at me, eyes wide. But there’s something else in his look.

He’s hurt. He doesn’t have to say it for me to read it on his face: Why didn’t you tell me?

I let out an annoyed sigh, glancing away. “Whatever. Let’s get this over with.”

Piria manages to look even more concerned, but she nervously bobs her head and then skitters ahead of us back into the main streets of Moonfall. Cyros and I have to hurry to keep up.

Neither of us say anything as we climb the city, but Cyros’s presence begins to feel heavy at my side. My stomach churns, and my mind keeps returning to that look he gave me. I haven’t done anything wrong. I didn’t ask for this. And if I’d manage to get Shirasil to release me from being his champion already, it never would have mattered that I hadn’t told Cyros. I never lied to him. I just didn’t tell him the whole story.

None of these justifications settle the queasiness in my chest.

“Aha!” Piria abruptly cries, practically shivering in excitement—I’m abruptly reminded of a chihuahua. “It’s still here! Oh, I’m so glad.”

Sure enough, Piria leads us to a storefront with a sign displaying a golden apple with a bite taken out of it, dripping a single drop of juice. Her tail wags in excitement as she pulls the curtain aside and ushers us in.

I haven’t been to a tavern before. At least, not aside from working in Gugora and Iski’s. I pinched a bit of Gugora’s mead when I was staying there and quickly spit it back into the mug before I could be sick. One time, on Earth, one of my friends even snuck some wine into the hospital in a water bottle. I was underage, and I definitely shouldn’t have had anything to drink with all the medicine in my system, but hey, I was going to die anyway, so why not?

My experience with the wine was about the same as it had been with Gugora’s mead, though I hadn’t managed to spit it back into the water bottle, and the nurse had nearly had a heart attack when she saw the red marks sprayed all over my blankets.

Even though I’m legal age now—well, by Earth’s standards, anyway—I really don’t understand the appeal. So it’s with these very low expectations that I follow Piria over to a stoney alcove in the corner of the room and take a seat on one of the pillows arrayed around the low circular table there. Curtains are drawn back around our alcove, which Piria leaves open so she can gesture for the barkeep.

An orc lumbers over, and my heart feels like it’s pinching in my chest, until I notice it’s a woman, and the pain gradually fades.

“What can I do for you?” the barkeep asks.

Piria nods excitedly. “I’d like a glass of your cactus blossom cider, please, if you have it!” She looks excitedly to me and Cyros.

I shrug. “Sure. I’ll have that, too.”

Piria brightens at this, and I immediately regret my choice. “A pitcher for the table, then!”

The orc nods, glancing at Cyros next.

“Do you have any nettle cider, actually?” he surprises me by asking.

“We’ve got thornwood, if that works,” the barkeep says.

“Thank you kindly,” he says as the orc steps away.

I snort. “The politest assassin I’ve ever seen.”

Cyros just rolls his eyes. Piria makes a quick gesture, and the ties of our alcove’s curtains come undone, falling down to give us some privacy. I try to Check whatever spell she just did to untie those ropes, but the effect is already over, and all I’m able to find is a basic Sound Dampening spell woven into the curtains themselves. It won’t stop a Blackcloak from eavesdropping, but I doubt this establishment was designed for such precautions.

“Is this okay?” Piria asks, brows wrinkled in worry. “I thought you might like the privacy, but I don’t want you to feel uncomfortable.”

All I can do is stare at her. How is someone like this a champion? She moves like she’s about ready to fall apart at the slightest breeze. And yet, what she did with Widengra’s champion is not something I can dismiss.

“It’s fine,” I say shortly. “What is it you wanted to talk about?”

Piria seems to be oblivious to the shortness in my tone, as she actually looks a little happier at this. “Right! Well, as I said, I was hoping we could just get to know each other a bit? I mean, now you’re aware of Widengra’s champions. And if you wanted help with that, I’d love to assist you.”

I raise an eyebrow. “What, like, help kill them?”

Piria’s eyes go as round as Lusio’s moons. “Kill? Oh, no! I just mean, if you wanted to leave for a different city…”

“I’m not going to run,” I interrupt her. Besides, there’s still that basilisk fang I need to get my hands on.

Piria nods hurriedly. “Yes, of course. Completely understandable! I’m sure you’re all settled in here and wouldn’t want to uproot your life.”

Well, that isn’t even remotely it, but I’m not about to clarify my motivation. “What can you tell me about them?”

Piria looks a little relieved by the question. “Oh course! Um, well, you’re probably aware that both of Widengra’s champions were chosen two months ago.”

“Keenly,” I say flatly.

“Yes, yes, of course,” she says quickly. “Well, their names are Grace and Oullus. Grace was an ice wielder, and Oullus was an enhancer with Storm affinities.”

“Was?” Cyros repeats. “You mean they aren’t any longer.”

Piria wobbles her head uncertainly. “Not entirely. When a god takes on a new champion, their natural abilities can be colored by the god’s domain. For Lorata’s champions, for instance, some of their standard abilities gained light-based attributes after becoming her champions.” Piria turns to look back at me. “In the case of Widengra’s champions, their abilities have taken on a Blood aspect.”

Discomfort squirms through me at this revelation. “Does that happen with all champions?”

“Most of them,” Piria says. A beat later, her eyes widen. “Oh! You mean, you’re wondering about yourself—”

The curtain is pulled back as our barkeep returns, setting our drinks and mugs out on the table before us. No one speaks until she’s left once more—well, aside from Piria, who trips over her own words to express her thanks.

The dracid pours out a glass for me and herself while Cyros grabs his own cup. I suspiciously sniff the amber liquid; it smells fresh and sweet, but so had the wine.

“I can’t really say if Lisari’s aspect has affected your magic at all,” Piria continues as I take a sip. Sweet and sour citrus sparkles across my tongue.

I raise my eyebrows, then take another sip. Hey, this stuff isn’t half bad. I fish a vial out of my pocket and pop the cork off to dump the contents into my glass. After tucking the bottle away, I go back to drinking the cider.

[Minor Poisoned status effect self inflicted.]

Piria stares at me. “Did. Did you just poison yourself?”

Cyros shrugs. “She does that a lot.”

“What?” I ask, glancing defensively between the two of them. “It doesn’t affect the taste. And it helps level up my Poison resistance. It’s not like it’s a Major Poisoned status effect.”

Piria blinks. “I think I understand why Lisari chose you to be her champion.”

I scowl. “What’s that supposed to mean?”

Cyros snorts, and I turn my scowl on him.

“Lisari likes potions,” Piria explains. “And experimenting on oneself. And also being rash. And—”

“Okay I get it,” I snap. Piria flinches, and I sigh out my annoyance. I just don’t like being compared to him. “Why do you call him Lisari, anyway?” I wonder. “Isn’t that his fake identity?”

Piria blinks, looking genuinely confused. “Fake identity?”

“You know.” I gesture vaguely. “The human disguise. Where he pretends to be a girl alchemist.”

Piria tilts her head. “It’s not a disguise. Okay, well—she does use that form when she doesn’t want to be recognized as Shirasil. But it’s just as true as her godly form.”

I skeptically glance between Piria and Cyros, but he actually seems to be considering this.

“I’ve heard that Shirasil sometimes appears as a woman,” Cyros admits. “I’d just assumed she would have used the same name.”

“They can be a bit fickle,” Piria says with an apologetic smile. “Some days are Shirasil days, and other ones are Lisari.”

I shake my head. Fine—whatever. “But her pretending to be an alchemist?”

“She is an alchemist,” Piria says, and Cyros nods along with a helpless shrug. “It’s one of her favorite passtimes, actually.”

I open and close my mouth. I guess she had given me alchemy books to study from, and noted spells that would be compatible with my magic. In hindsight, I had just assumed that she’d used some sort of godly knowledge or other to point me to the right spells. It didn’t occur to me that she’d been familiar with the subject out of genuine interest.

In fact, now that I’m thinking about it, her class is Archaic Alchemy. Hmm.

“Okay so maybe not everything she said was a lie,” I reluctantly admit.

“Lisari doesn’t lie,” Piria objects.

Okay now that is definitely nonsense. I just raise an eyebrow at her.

“Not all the time,” she relents. “Only when—” She stops herself, abruptly taking a drink from her mug.

I wait until she’s done. “Only when what?”

Piria glances guiltily around the room, avoiding my gaze. She takes another sip. Then she stares down into her glass and whispers, “Only when she thinks it would be funny.”

At that, Cyros laughs. “That does sound like Shirasil.”

Traitor. Although I suppose that excuse is something I could believe—given, I suspect, there’s a very wide range of things the god might find funny.

“I’m surprised, though,” Piria continues, watching me with a faint frown. “Has she not told you all this yourself?”

Now it’s my turn to take a drink of my cider and pointedly avoid the question. Unfortunately, Piria has more patience than I do.

“We haven’t spoken much,” I say.

“Oh.” Piria frowns thoughtfully. “I’m sorry. I was under the impression that Shirasil visited you recently.”

I wrinkle my nose. “He did.”

Cryos looks surprised by this revelation. “He did? When?”

I don’t respond, and Piria just looks confused. Finally I sigh. “Look, I don’t exactly ask him many questions when he shows up.” I glare down at my drink, flicking the side and watching the ripples bounce across the surface. “I mostly just… told him to go away.”

“Oh.” Piria appears genuinely perplexed. “Well now I see why he sent me to talk to you. But why would you do that? It would make it very difficult to learn about being his champion, I imagine.”

“Because I don’t want to be his champion,” I snap. “I don’t want to work for any god. I didn’t ask for this. I just want to be left alone.”

“I see. I’m sorry.” Piria’s look turns sympathetic. “I didn’t ask to be his champion, either, you know.”

Cyros sits up in surprise. “Really?”

I’m also pretty surprised. “You didn’t?”

Piria chuckles lightly. “I suppose it makes sense. I don’t think any of his champions ever volunteered for it. But it saved my life.”

Her eyes go distant. “A long time ago, I was a simple healer in a small village. Or, I was training to be. This was in a very different age, you see, when dragons still hunted the skies and elementals commonly stalked the land. So common, in fact, that one day a great stone elemental crashed through our valley’s wall. It was made of boulders and moss, and magma bled from a gash across its back. We had no time to evacuate—but the creature wasn’t coming for us. It was fleeing in fact, and its hunter showed up moments later.”

Piria pauses to take another sip of her drink, but she still doesn’t entirely seem to be with us. “Rinviu had been hunting the elemental for miles, and our valley was where it made its final stand. The god eventually slew the beast, but I'm told it put up an incredible fight. I didn’t get to see most of it after it crashed upon our village, and I became buried in the rubble. That I survived at all is a miracle unto itself.”

Both Cyros and I have stopped drinking, unmoving as we stare at her and listen to the story unfold. I can almost see it playing out in my mind as if I were there.

“I don’t know how long I was buried," Piria continues. “But I’m sure I was close to death when Shirasil found me. I remember the darkness turning to light, and the pain turning to relief. His magic washed over me, and with it, my body was able to stitch itself back together.” She smiles sadly. “It was far too broken for it to be completely repaired. I should have lost limbs—I should have been left paralyzed. But even this weakened form I wear today should have been impossible to recover. He saved my life. And then he gave me hundreds more years than I ever would have lived as a mortal, and I’ve made sure not to waste them, returning his gift by healing as many others as I could find.”

A silence descends as she finishes. Piria blinks a few times, looking between the two of us, then goes back to self-consciously sipping at her drink.

“And you sure you’re the same Piria who’s called the Withering Witch?” Cryos asks.

Piria giggles. “I did say some of my achievements have been exaggerated."

It’s not Piria’s titles I’m wondering about, however. Shirasil saved her life? That doesn’t sound like him. I mean, from what I know, at least. I guess it does explain why he didn’t ask her if she wanted to be a champion, first; she would have been too hurt to reply.

But I was lucid, and he still didn’t give me the choice. He just foisted it upon me. If I’d had a choice, I would have said no!

…And then I would have died. I would have gone outside without the Noxious Gauntlet, without Shirasil’s protection, and if that ice candidate hadn’t killed me, Widengra would have. And if I’d been dead, what would have happened to Cyros? To Iski and Gugora?

I glare at my drink before taking another sip. Then I take a gulp. Then I down the whole rest of the glass. Cryos and Piria have stopped talking to watch me with wide eyes.

I set the empty glass down hard. He still should have let me have the choice. Even if it would have killed me. I should have been allowed to choose.

“You okay?” Cyros asks quietly.

“Fine.” I want to snap, but it comes out as a sigh. The next time Shirasil shows up again unannounced, I’ll at least have some pointed questions for him. I gesture to Piria. “Well, let’s have it, then.”

The dracid blanks, uncomprehending. “Have what?”

“Whatever you’re here to tell me,” I say. “You said Shirasil sent you to tell me about being a champion.” I can’t hold in my grimace when I say the next words. “So tell me what I need to know about being a champion.”
