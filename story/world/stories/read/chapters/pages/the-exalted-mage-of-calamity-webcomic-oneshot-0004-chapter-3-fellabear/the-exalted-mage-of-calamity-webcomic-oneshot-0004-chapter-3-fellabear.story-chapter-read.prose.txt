“Shit.”

Veronica pressed her back against a tree, breath ragged from jogging. She peered around its edge.

A wagon lay on its side, split wood and shattered wheels scattered across the clearing. Supplies had spilled everywhere. Sacks of grain, cracked pottery, and a few copper trinkets half-buried in the dirt that looked pitiful.

The bodies were worse. One man, one woman. A third that barely resembled a person at all; the body had been crushed, pressed deep into the dirt, with large claw marks along their back.

She was too late. Without flight magic, she mistimed how long it would take her to arrive. That, and she was still horrified at the notion of running. Even with Sage’s reassurance of the absence of Medusa’s curse, immediate physical exertion was too high of a risk. What if her body fell apart from running? That fear was deeply ingrained in her bones.

Veronica’s eyes wandered over.

The thing that had killed them lumbered over the ruined cart. She recognized it immediately.

A fellabear.

They were dangerous, monster-like bears that ordinary people would have trouble dealing with. Towering bulk, armored backs bristling with sharp spines, and extremely territorial behavior. The bear in front of her, however, seemed much more vicious. Its fur was mottled with blackened patches that crawled like ink across its skin. Its eyes burned red, and saliva thick as tar dripped from its mouth.

Demonic corruption.

It took root when a demon lingered too long, its presence poisoning soil and beast alike—or when blood was swallowed and fused into flesh. Demons would sometimes appear in the world; animals and monsters being corrupted by them wasn’t rare.

The fellabear was no longer just an animal. It was an infection on four legs. She couldn’t let it live.

Veronica peered around the tree a little closer. The beast was munching on something inside the wagon. It didn’t take long for it to sense her. Its head snapped up, nostrils flaring with a huff. It turned, red eyes locked onto her.

It had her scent.

Veronica exhaled. Her fingers flexed.

The growl that followed rolled through the clearing like thunder, rattling branches overhead. The fellabear heaved forward, claws gouging the dirt as it charged.

She stepped from behind the tree, naked, unarmed, but not defenseless.

Not even close.

Even with low mana reserves, she could pull and refine mana from the air. It was extremely inefficient, but it would do for the time being. She gathered enough to cast a particularly powerful Tier-2 Path of Ruin spell.

[Mana: 200+141/200 MU]

Light kindled in her palm, swelling into a blazing knot. Two bright wings of mana flashed behind her hand. She pointed the palm of her hand at the beast. The fellabear barreled closer, saliva slinging from its jaws, each paw strike shaking the ground.

A piercing hum emanated from her spell, equally sharp and powerful. With two mana cores, her spells were exceedingly destructive, each one designed to use an exorbitant amount of mana compared to regular spells. Instead of relying on fancy, complicated effects like invisibility or golemancy, her spells took the pure, simplistic route: destruction incarnate with overwhelming efficacy.

Veronica unleashed the spell.

A torrent of violet-white annihilation tore from her hand in a sweeping cone. It stopped the fellabear mid-charge. Her magic violently dug into the beast; the air filled with the metallic smell of blood as the spell burned through its hide. For an instant, its roar split the clearing, embellished with fury, before turning into a pained shrill as it collapsed.

When the wave subsided, silence reigned.

The fellabear’s body lay ruined, flesh charred and sloughed away, ribs gleaming through its burned fur. Smoking meat.

Veronica looked down at her arm. It looked the same. There was no tingling sensation. No curse creeping up her skin.

She allowed herself a small, genuine smile. “I could get used to this.”

Veronica tugged at the collar of her new brown tunic, frowning at how it clung to her shoulders. The fabric smelled of blood and sweat, but it was better than walking around naked.

She had checked the woman first—though the truth had been plain even from a distance. Crushed throat, broken bones, partially missing, chomped limbs. The others had similar injuries. All of them had died before she arrived. Judging by the scene—she wouldn’t have made it either way, even if she had rushed over.

Veronica took the woman’s tunic and pants. It would serve her better than being worn on that of a corpse. She made sure to check the rest of them. There wasn’t anything in their pockets. Whoever they were, they had died nameless and empty.

The wagon, however, told a different story.

Daggers lined the back in neat rows, far too many for simple travelers. Small vials rattled in a chest, each corked and labeled with crude sigils. All poisons, just by the smell alone. Beneath them: sacks of dried meat, wheels of cheese, and bundles of hard bread.

At the front, the magitech power-crystal was also shattered into pieces. The wagon wouldn’t be able to move with mana anymore. Aside from that, there wasn’t anything else mechanical or mana-based. Not even a simple shield generator.

She really was in the sticks.

There was also drool dripping everywhere. Considering the beast was corrupted by demonic blood, almost everything was now spoiled and infected.

She couldn't really say if it was a good thing or bad thing that the small stains of blood on her tunic were from the woman and not the bear. Demon blood was much darker.

“Bandits,” she muttered, staring at the scene. Disguised as harmless merchants—until they’d picked the wrong route and the wrong predator had found them first.

Being this far out in the middle of nowhere told her one thing. That they tried to be discreet. Taking a path where they could be hidden. There wasn’t a road in sight. Instead, it seemed they took their cart between the largest opening of trees, far off the beaten path. The cart itself was probably stolen, too. No way bandits could afford such a thing.

Veronica smoothed the hem of her plain, partially torn trousers, glanced once more at the ruined corpses, and exhaled through her nose.

She dragged the bodies into a nearby brush and hid them. It seemed odd to just leave bodies out in the open to rot.

As for the cart filled with supplies—there was no reason to leave a source of demonic corruption like that out either. She set the cart on fire with a spell and waited until it all burned before putting it out.

Veronica thought about doing it to the bodies—but the smell of flesh burning wasn’t exactly pleasant to be around.

With the situation settled, she stared back in the direction the wagon might have come from.

At the very least, she had clothes to wear now. But it was only a temporary solution. Especially since she hadn’t taken the undergarments. Reusing another person’s underwear wasn’t an idea she was fond of.

Even in the apocalypse, there were some lines she didn’t want to cross.

For now, she could wander around and explore the forest without being labeled as a forest-dweller or deviant. As for the rest of the supplies—there was no need. A wagon here meant it was traveling somewhere. Civilization couldn't have been far.

She could handle being on an empty stomach for a few hours. If necessary, she could scavenge for berries or hunt a wild animal and cook it later. Her assistant Viya had made many dishes and meals for her—how hard could it be to cook a wild deer?

“Think,” she muttered to herself, trudging forward. “Forests like this always lead somewhere, right? Water, trails... something.”

She paused beside a tree, looking up at the leaves. Her breathing steadied as she tried to recall what lay outside the city she had left behind.

“Hey, Sage. Where the hell did you send me? Was there always a forest behind Annesheim?”

[Martin tried to calculate precise spatial reversion coordinates, but the calculations were much more complex than he anticipated. As time rewinds, the spatial coordinates of the planet have changed due to its rotation and position in space. He instead focused on sending you to the safest coordinate that wouldn’t place you inside the ground or a wall.]

She narrowed her eyes, disturbed by that thought. How would being teleported inside a wall feel? There were horror stories of mages using teleportation magic and landing half-buried in the ground. Space couldn’t contain two types of matter at once.

“What about the year? How far did you send me back?”

[I do not know.]

“Tch. Figures.”

With nothing else to go on, she chose a direction and walked where the trees grew thinner.

The forest itself was quite flat with several open clearings where sunlight peeked through. The trees were sparse, and she could see perfectly in any direction. Although the ground was filled with brush and other wild flora, she could walk easily without much resistance.

“Hey. Although Medusa’s Curse is gone, and my magic has been lost…” She turned her consciousness inward, checking her mana cores. She could feel it—both of them. But she needed to be certain. “…do I still have twin mana cores? They were the only reason I was able to reach the 10th-tier after all.”

[Yes. Your current body still has twin mana cores inside of it. Your maximum MU would have been 100 with a single core, but it is instead 200 MU.]

“Then how come I’m not being affected by Medusa’s Curse?”

This was why she wasn’t sure if she still had them, despite sensing them within her body. Perhaps she was just… feeling something that wasn’t there. A phantom pain, like the condition people felt when they lost a limb. It was something common among soldiers.

[I do not have sufficient information to come up with an answer. A plausible hypothesis may be that the temporal rift has changed your body’s historic biology, keeping some attributes while removing others.]

She nodded and continued moving. She didn’t want to ask. This realm of temporal hypotheses was just not her thing. For now, she still had twin mana cores, all benefits, no downside. At least, none that were obvious to her.

Hours seemed to pass as she continued, the forest idly shifting by. Her stomach rumbled with hunger, but there wasn’t anything in sight to eat. She killed an aggressive boar an hour ago, but gave up on trying to cook it. There was too much blood. Burning it hadn’t helped either. She especially couldn’t stand the smell of burned fur.

During the war, most animals had died or become infected through demonic corruption. She never had a chance to learn how to cook, and instead relied on food caches stored all across Vitian. Rimeboxes, powered by mana or mana crystals, could keep food and other perishables chilled and frosted for years on end.

The Elven forests especially had enough nutrient-rich fruits and vegetables to sustain her. Until finally the demons found the forests and destroyed that, too.

Sage did provide some instructions on how to cook the boar, but they proved difficult to follow, so she gave up. In the end, Veronica just ate wild fruit or berries she found lying around in bushes or trees. Luckily, Sage could determine if some of them were poisonous.

Veronica did also consider stopping and reforging her mana cores—but it would require her to be vulnerable for a duration. For now, she just focused on finding civilization. A town or city meant a safe place to rest and plan.

Eventually, she smelled a faint scent of smoke drifting through the air.

[Veronica. Multiple life-form signatures have been detected northeast of your position. Within 300 meters.]

She stopped in her tracks, eyes narrowing as she strained to listen. Slowly, she moved forward until finally the voices were carried along faintly on the breeze, low, gruff, and accompanied by the sound of clinking metal. She crept closer to the source, careful to keep her footsteps light. As she drew near, she spotted the source: a group of mercenaries setting up a small camp in a clearing.

There were five of them, all clad in mismatched armor that had seen better days. Two were tending to the fire, which crackled with the scent of roasting meat. A third was brushing down the horses, all tethered to a tree, while the remaining two inspected weapons and gear.

Veronica crouched behind a tree, observing them. Her gaze flicked over their camp, cataloging details. The meat roasting over the fire looked fresh, likely something they had recently caught. Their weapons were well-used but functional, and their horses, though rugged, were in decent condition.

Judging by the fact that they were using horses, they weren’t aura users. Perhaps they could be low-tier mages that hadn’t learned flight magic yet—but she doubted any mage trained their muscles that thoroughly. But it was also obvious they weren’t rich enough to afford transport like a magitech cart or a mount. They were just regular people.

A sliver of doubt crept into her mind.

Approaching them was a risk. Her low mana reserves made her vulnerable, and she didn’t trust her younger body to fight as effectively as her older self could have, even with Medusa’s curse. She was a mage through-and-through. Swordsmanship and fist-fighting were not her forte. Would this group be friendly? Or hidden bandits in disguise, like before?

She’d rather avoid killing people; human lives were a luxury, and she knew that.

She needed information—where she was, when she was, and how far she might be from the nearest city. Worst-case scenario, she reminded herself, she could gather ambient mana from the surroundings to use a few low-tier spells. It wouldn’t be anywhere near her full power, but it might be enough to wound them and escape.

[Caution is recommended. Individuals may be dangerous.]

Veronica took a deep breath and stepped out from behind the tree, her hands raised slightly in a gesture of peace. The moment her foot crushed a branch on the forest floor, the mercenaries all snapped towards her like wolves.

“Who’s there?” barked one of the men by the fire.

He was stocky. An unkempt beard and a dented breastplate made it clear that the man had years of experience in fighting. His hand was already on the hilt of his sword when the words left his mouth.

Veronica kept her expression calm, her voice loud but clear. “I mean no harm. I’m just passing through.”
