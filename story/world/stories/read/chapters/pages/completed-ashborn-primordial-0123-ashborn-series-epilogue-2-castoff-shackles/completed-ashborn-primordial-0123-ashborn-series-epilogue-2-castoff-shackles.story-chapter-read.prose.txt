In the audience chamber of the royal castle of Kartara to the north of Sai, Riyan Savar turned to address the assembled throng of Sawai lords and their families.

“With this action,” he announced, “I hereby relinquish the throne of the Saian Kingdom. I return the crown back to its rightful ruler.”

Riyan removed the jewel-studded crown of Sai from his head and placed it upon the head of the woman who kneeled before him.

Horns blared, and the chamber filled with applause.

The Queen of Sai rose to face him, a pensive look upon her face. “You are sure of this?” she asked. “You could have had your dynasty. The realm would remember you as the king who ushered Sai into this new Age.”

“Your throne was never mine to take,” Riyan said as the court band began to play and dancers took to the hall. “At the time, I felt that it had to be done. To prevent the realm from falling into the hands of the Kin’jal. I do not regret my decisions back then. But now my time has come to an end. Giving back the throne will not right the wrongs I have wrought upon your family and your kingdom. But I know that you and your daughters will lead this country better than I ever could.”

“You are correct,” she said. “I shall never forgive you or our late Saint for murdering my husband, king Dilber. She has paid with her life, and as for you… I never wish to see your face again.”

“You needn’t worry about that,” Riyan said with a wry chuckle.

“What is it you plan to do now?” the queen asked.

“For all my life, I have lived with many shackles,” Riyan replied. “The Kin’jal Empire took my wife and daughter from me long ago. Not one day has gone by where that loss has not plagued me. Now I have other shackles. The loss of Kaia. The death of two great beings I once raised and trained.”

“And so you believe that by relinquishing the throne, you can shed those shackles?”

“Not just that, no,” Riyan replied. “I intend to go on a journey. To the uncharted lands. To discover what lies in store for me.”

“And to rediscover yourself, perhaps?” she asked.

“If Fate allows.”

“Go, then,” Queen Sai said. “With my blessing. May you come to terms with your many burdens.”

Riyan bowed before the queen, turning his back on her, on the throne, and on his past. Leaving without another word.

He hadn’t much in the way of provisions. Only a rucksack and a walking pole. Plenty to begin this journey.

He’d just collected his things from a store closet and left the castle by way of a secret passage when a hooded stranger accosted him.

Riyan’s hand went to his talwar, freezing upon its hilt when the stranger threw back her robe.

“You,” he snarled. “Last I saw, you’d lost your mind.”

“I’d lost a few limbs as well,” Princess Mina Hiranya replied with a light laugh. “And yet it seems whatever miracle knitted the realms saw fit to restore my mind and both.”

“Incredible…” Riyan whispered under his breath. “The boy…”

“Rumor has it you wish to depart on a journey,” Mina Hiranya asked, and it was only then that Riyan spotted she carried a rucksack of her own.

“You wouldn’t happen to have space for two, would you? Because I, too, have shackles I would very much like to cast off.”
