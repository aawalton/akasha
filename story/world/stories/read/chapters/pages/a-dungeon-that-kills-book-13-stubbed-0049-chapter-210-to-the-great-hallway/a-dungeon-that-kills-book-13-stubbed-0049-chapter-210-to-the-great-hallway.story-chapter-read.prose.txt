“What a troublesome enemy,” Viktor muttered.

[Indeed. I am not entirely sure we have anything at our disposal capable of dealing with someone like this man.]

Viktor let out a sigh. “If it comes to the worst, I’m going to use Rekindled Ember.”

[Master, surely you don’t mean to—?]

“Yes. I’ll allow myself to be struck by a lethal blow. That’s the only way to trigger it.”

The Supreme Thauma, after all, was meant to work as a purely defensive ability. He could cast it once per day, and once he did, it granted him a protective effect that would persist for the following twenty-four hours. Which meant, in theory at least, he could keep it running indefinitely. As a result, if he ever sustained damage severe enough to kill him, the effect would activate automatically, giving him extraordinary resilience against all forms of damage, unparalleled regenerative powers, and a surge of strength far beyond the limits of the human body. Of course, it could only trigger a single time, and once it had been used, he would need to wait until the next day before he could recast the Supreme Thauma and restore the safety net once more.

Well, that was the intended way to use it. But he could also turn the Thauma into an offensive ability by deliberately taking a fatal injury. In doing so, he would gain the raw power he needed to stand toe-to-toe with the Apostle.

The question was: would that be enough?

Going by what he had seen of this enemy of his so far, he believed he had a good chance of winning. However, that assessment was based strictly on what the Apostle had chosen to reveal rather than on any understanding of the man’s actual limits. So in reality, it remained uncertain whether he would be able to defeat his foe. And even if it was possible, there was no guarantee he could do so within ten minutes.

Yes, ten minutes.

That was the full span of time the effect would grant him before it faded, the entirety of the window he would have to work with. “Rekindled Ember” was his trump card, and a card he could only play once, so he needed to weigh every possibility carefully before he could afford to let it leave his hand.

And that meant the wisest course of action right now was to throw everything else he had at the Apostle, to exhaust whatever reserves of strength the man possessed, to force the man to reveal more of his abilities. All the while, he would remove himself from the fighting. He would watch from a distance. He would observe, and he would analyze. Only when victory was assured, or when he was completely out of other options, would he finally step forward and enter the fray in person.

[Are you going to join the battle in the Great Hallway, Master?]

“No. We stick to the original plan. Sebekton and Galatea take command of the Templars and the tomb guards for the pincer attack. Undead mages provide support. Wisps weave themselves into the net of light. And so on and so forth, exactly as we laid it out before.”

[But that is the preparation for Xander. We are making no changes on account of the Apostle?]

Viktor slowly leaned back in his chair. “If I were the Apostle, I would simply blast straight through whatever stood in my way without a moment’s hesitation. There’s nothing in the hallway capable of stopping a man like him, after all. And Xander, along with that, well, creature, would follow right behind, breaking through the gap the Apostle just tears open. Yes, that would be how all three of them get out of the trap.”

[That does sound like a reasonable plan.]

“So what we will do is simply let the Apostle pass through alone. There’s no point in wasting our strength trying to stop something that is unstoppable. Just leave him be. We will settle our business with him later, on the lower floors. Meanwhile, we do everything in our power to prevent Xander and the abomination from following in his wake. The trap remains sealed against them, specifically. It was designed from the very beginning to kill Xander, and I’m sure it should be able to put that cobbled-together beast out of its misery as well.”

[It would certainly be convenient if events unfolded as you describe, but what if the Apostle, upon looking back and seeing his companions still trapped behind him, chooses to turn around to help them instead of continuing forward?]

“From what we’ve seen of him so far, it seems more likely he would abandon them without a second thought and press on alone. But you’re right. There’s indeed a chance he would stay and fight in the Great Hallway. Should that happen, then we will have no choice but to oblige him. We still have reserves nearby, so we’ll pour them into the fight as needed, replacing our losses and maintaining the pressure.”

It was going to be a bloody slog, though. A slow, grinding, and ugly battle of attrition that would leave behind unimaginable casualties. But there was no way around it. Against an opponent of this caliber, losses were to be expected, and sacrifices were probably unavoidable.

With his mind made up, Viktor closed his eyes and sent his vision forward once more, toward the current location of the three remaining members of the Iskora team—one of the sloped corridors that wound their way through the great tomb’s interior.

Xander was drifting at the head of the formation, his eyes glowing now, an unmistakable sign he had activated the Reliquary that allowed him to see the unseen, which also had the useful side effect of lighting the way for his companions, saving everyone the trouble of torches.

Behind him trailed the former trio of Valeska, Rowan, and Cynthia, now merged into a single grotesque body that lurched forward with a stuttering gait. The three faces were frozen in expressions of twisted anguish, and Viktor found himself wondering whether their suffering came solely from the torment of the flesh, or from the mind as it struggled to comprehend the horror of what they had become. Well, it hardly mattered. Regardless of what turmoil churned beneath those three visages, they had no choice but to keep moving forward, to keep obeying every command the Apostle had given.

And last of all, strolling along at the rear as though out for an evening walk, was the gray-haired man himself, with that same faint smile on his face, the one that suggested he already knew how this would end and found it mildly amusing.

Soon enough, they would arrive at the Great Hallway, where the decisive confrontation was meant to unfold. Still, Viktor had one more move he wanted to try first. Not because he expected it to accomplish much, but because it cost him almost nothing, and because there was a certain pleasure in making the enemy dance a step they hadn’t planned for. If nothing else, it might rattle them into showing a little more of whatever they were hiding up their sleeves.

He moved his vision ahead, toward the junction further down the corridor, and there he found the spider-women waiting in position.

To anyone who didn’t know better, they might have passed for noble ladies from some distant, exotic land—black hair falling thick and glossy past their shoulders, robes of heavy silk hiding every last contour of the bodies beneath. The only detail that betrayed their true nature was the presence of their eight eyes, which was generally considered six too many in most polite circles.

Four of them were waiting here, at this end of the corridor, while five more stood at the junction on the opposite side, poised to spring a coordinated pincer attack the moment the order came.

“Have them begin,” Viktor told his Dungeon Core.

At Celeste’s command, the spider-women began to disrobe, though any fool who hoped for a glimpse of anything enticing would be in for a rude awakening. Because what came out from under the silk was not skin and curves, but chitinous masses that swelled and unfurled with wet, clicking sounds as segment after segment of arachnid abdomen pushed into view. As it turned out, the “woman” half of “spider-woman” began and ended with the head, while the “spider” part was, well, practically everything else.

And, as if that weren’t enough on its own, that bloated and glistening thorax came with company of its own—a seething carpet of ants, beetles, scorpions, and other things with too many legs, crawling in a churning tide that never ceased, never settled.

As the spider-women raised their “hands” in unison and issued the command, the insects poured forth from both ends of the corridor like two dark and swollen rivers breaking free of their banks, a cascade of chitin and legs and mandibles that flooded across the floor, up the walls, over the ceiling, leaving no surface uncovered.

This is so gross. Viktor felt his stomach turn, even though he was the one causing it and not the one on the receiving end of it.

“What the hell?” Xander yelled, voice cracking with a sentiment that mirrored Viktor’s own disgust almost exactly.

Being in his spectral state, the bastard could, of course, have just stood his ground and let the entire swarm pass harmlessly through. Nevertheless, he chose instead to drift into the solid stone of the wall, removing himself from the corridor entirely.

And that meant Valeska-Rowan-Cynthia was going to be left standing alone to catch the full weight of this particular nightmare. The three heads screamed as the creature frantically tried to brace itself against the onslaught. Cynthia’s hand, the one that had been fused permanently to her staff, snapped forward and unleashed a powerful gust of wind that tore through the first wave, which bought perhaps two seconds of satisfaction before the next one arrived to fill the gap.

The aeromancer kept blasting and blasting, but despite her best efforts, the swarm kept coming, closing the distance to the body she shared with her companions. The four remaining hands, gripping axe and sword and shield, swung wildly at the air, while the two legs stamped at anything that got underfoot. Still, against a tide made up of countless tiny opponents, such resistance amounted to little more than a formality. It was only a matter of time before the insects finally reached the creature’s flesh and began climbing across it in earnest. At that point, the hands abandoned their weapons altogether and resorted to swatting at the crawling mass with bare palms.

Interesting as that was, the creature’s reaction was not what Viktor had come to see. He left it behind and turned toward the Apostle, curious about how the man would handle the bugs on his side of the corridor, since obviously, strength alone was not the right tool for a problem like this one. You couldn’t punch a swarm to death, after all. What you needed was something like Ekon’s fire breath, a technique the bald man had once demonstrated with devastating efficiency.

So Viktor watched, waiting to see what card the Apostle would play. Well, perhaps the guy would simply choose to run through the swarm, leaving the others behind to fend for themselves.

To his surprise, the gray-haired man didn’t take even one step from where he stood. Still wearing that same smile, he simply raised a hand toward the onrushing wall of chitin and legs.

And then—

The hand enlarged. Or rather, the skin of that hand stretched outward, spreading itself across the width of the corridor until it formed a translucent lid of flesh, one large enough to seal his half of the passage away from the half where the insects were advancing.

Viktor frowned. Was such a fragile-looking barrier really capable of blocking the entire swarm? And even if it could, what then? Was the man planning to stand there indefinitely, holding back the bugs until the end of days?

He had his answer soon enough.

The instant an insect brushed that sheet of skin, it vanished—sucked straight through and swallowed whole. And the swarm, having no capacity for hesitation or self-preservation, kept coming, wave after wave of them marching cheerfully into oblivion, into whatever bottomless void lay on the other side of that thin membrane.

Did the guy just “eat” the bugs with his skin? Viktor felt something unpleasant turn over in his gut.

[What kind of power is that, I wonder?]

“My guess is that he’s absorbing them into his body through physical contact. And I highly doubt he could only do that to insects. No, I’m sure he could ‘eat’ any living thing. Actually, probably corpses too, while we’re at it.”

Viktor let out a long, weary sigh. On the one hand, this little experiment had proven extremely valuable, as it had revealed yet another aspect of the Apostle’s power. On the other hand, the more he learned about the guy, the more his mood soured.

“That’s enough,” he told Celeste. “Tell the Jorogomu to retreat. And have our Guardians stand ready. The battle in the Great Hallway is starting soon.”

[Yes, Master.]
