Mia could see the massive change in cora fluctuations—but she chose not to say anything. It wasn’t because she wanted to cheat Hayden. It was because she was hypnotized by his body. When he benched the heavier weights, his muscles pulsed with cora, as if they were a white-hot sword that a blacksmith was striking with a hammer.

This is insane… she thought, heart pulsing, adrenaline flowing through her veins. If I call him out… I’ll never know…

Mia desperately wanted to see what his body was like when it was strained. She wanted to see if the whole thing would glow. She needed to know, so she didn’t say anything.

Unfortunately—

“He’s cheating!” a woman screamed.

Aysha? Mia turned, and her pupils constricted to pinpricks. It was indeed Aysha, one of the few people she respected, and she was dressed like…

Mia couldn’t put it into words. Objectively speaking, Aysha looked normal. Women often wore skimpy workout outfits, and there was nothing wrong with them. But there was a problem with Aysha wearing one. Aysha was austere and stoic—someone who lived like a true monk with absolute self-control. Now she was without her blindfold, plastered with makeup—revealing a substantial amount of her body. That contrast told a story that Mia really, really, really-really-really didn’t like.

Time slowed down for her—but continued on around her.

“Aren’t you here to seduce Hayden or something?” Alico asked, eyes locked onto Aysha.

Aysha’s body trembled with rage, and she looked to Mia. “You can see it, right? No, you can see it. If I can see it, you can definitely see it.”

Mia didn’t like what Aysha was doing, and she really wanted to see Hayden bench the stacks. That’s why she replied, “First off, what you’re doing is hideous. Stop it. Second off, there’s no change. So stop lying.”

“You bitch!” Aysha screamed, pushing through the crowd. Mia welcomed it. The Pressure Point club was vastly superior to the Blindfold Boxing club; if Aysha wanted to let Mia prove it, she’d accept.

The crowd responded. “Fight! Fight! Fi… ght.”

Suddenly, the tense crowd fell silent.

Mia looked around. Why did… She turned and witnessed something truly incredible. Hayden had lifted the bar and was benching a thousand pounds. It wasn’t as effortless as it was before; she could see his arms straining and his veins popping out. Cora was striking in his veins like sparks off a welding rig. Yet he was doing it—and he wasn’t even using aura. He just took deep breaths between reps, like normal people did with benching plates.

“Six…” A nervous man counted, looking around for others to join in.

Another followed. “Seven.”

“Eight.”

“Nine!”

“Ten!” The whole audience was now screaming their chants.

“Oh my God, he’s—eleven!”

“Twelve!”

“Thirteen!”

Hayden was trembling now. “Fourteen!”

“Fifteen!”

“That’s nine hundred pounds at least! There’s no… I can’t believe it… Sixteen!”

“Seven… teen.”

“Eight… teen.”

“Nine… teen!”

“There’s no way! Is he really… twenty!”

The gym rioted. Men ripped off their shirts and flexed as they screamed, oddly reminiscent of silverback gorillas. The women giggled and shut their eyes, but they peeked a bit.

Hayden then sat up and looked at Alico, whose lips were trembling. “Since there’s been a disagreement over whether you’re cheating, why don’t you sit at my press?” he suggested.

Aysha’s lips curved as she looked at Mia.

Mia turned to her. “I wanted to see if Hayden could do it.” She looked Aysha up and down. “What’s your excuse?”

Aysha grinned. “I’m trying to be more human. You should try it.”

As the two were quarreling, Alico was trying to make it out of his predicament in one piece.

“I forfeit,” he said. “I was wrong about you.” He got up and acted like he was going to give a handshake, but at the last second, he reached for Hayden’s gravity stack controls.

“Look!” A woman said. “He’s trying to get rid of the evidence!” She was standing beside him, red light flashing. “Look at his face! He didn’t think we knew he was cheating!”

Alico looked and saw that he was encircled by people. Many people knew he was cheating—and just didn’t say anything. They let it happen… he thought.

It was lost from the start.

Those who couldn’t see rushed in to see.

“Oh my God, he really set it at five hundred. That’s so cringe.”

“Right? But it’s badass that he repped it.”

“Yeah… I think he should thank that loser for letting him show off.”

Alico had never empathized with people who wanted to bash their skull into a wall to end the suffering, but now he did—and knew by the way that bracelets switched from red to green that people were posting it on socials.

Classes tomorrow were going to suck.

Suddenly, all the recorders turned their attention to Mia and Aysha, who were standing off against one another.

“You really think you can fight me?” Mia asked. “I’m in a martial club. I fight people like him without equalizers.” She nudged her head at Alico.

“Using Divine Eyes,” Aysha said. “And Divine Eyes suffers most under illusions.”

Mia sneered. “Do you think I need a seeing spirit to strike channel points?”

Channel points referred to the areas where cora entered channels. Unlike normal pressure points, touching coran channels could overload spirits or drain someone of energy.

“I guess we’ll see,” Aysha said.

A man jumped onto a bench and yelled, “Fight! Fight! Fight!” again. It was official—no one cared about Alico. They’d care tomorrow. For now, they were focused on the catfight that was about to go down.

As Alico made his escape, Hayden stared at the two women in surprise. It seems natural fortune won again, he thought. He abruptly walked between the two, silencing the crowd.

He turned to Mia. “Do you have the Divine Eyes spirit? Or another spirit?”

Mia swallowed. “Divine Eyes.”

“And you’re skilled at working with coran channels?”

She chuckled viciously, clearly indignant that he was asking. “Yes. As a matter of fact, I am. The best among the third years, in fact.”

“Even better.” Hayden looked between them. “Will you two help me with something?”

Aysha went home that night feeling conflicted. She was publicly shamed, but she accomplished her goal—and she didn’t have to sleep with Hayden. It seemed surreal to think it, but she really accomplished it. Or rather, Hayden knew what she wanted and gave it to her.

Aysha replayed the scene in her mind. Hayden had led the two to a private study room. Mia spoke first.

What do you want?

I want your help in healing an acquaintance’s core, he said.

First off—unless you’re a spirit healer, you shouldn’t even get near a core. Second off, why would I help you?

Because I am a healer, and if you join me, I’ll teach you about augmentor cores. Once people learn of their existence, you’ll be the most sought-after expert in the universe.

What’s an augmentor core?

I can’t explain. So watch this instead.

Aysha couldn’t see what Mia saw, but Mia whispered, No way… and stumbled backward. It must have been incredible.

Aysha turned to Hayden, panicked. She still had to secure her place. What about me? she asked. Why do you need me?

I need a second pair of eyes and hands, and finding help that will remain silent is difficult to come by. That said, you do have a favor to ask, yes? Something you’d exchange for your aid.

Aysha looked at her clothing and back with a bitter smile. Yes. Sairan wants to offer you protection and rifting opportunities if you join the team. It’d… also benefit me. Immensely.

I’ll hear him out next month—if you help me.

Aysha’s eyes lit up. You have my help!

Aysha crashed onto her bed, rolling onto her side in embarrassment. He saw through me instantly… she thought. And he didn’t even look… Is that good or terrible?

She groaned and rolled to the other side. She wished she could die and then instantly be reborn because she didn’t actually want to die, but she certainly didn’t want to be alive. She needed sleep. Sleep was, after all, time travel. Yet Aysha was far from sleep. Between the late-night embarrassment and burning questions, she couldn’t stop thinking.

Who does he want to help? she wondered.

Hayden refused to say. He just said he would call upon them to show up on a certain date and time. That was it.

It was so mysterious. Hayden Reskco, the man who captivated countless women but never spoke to them, the scholarship student who outshone all the clubs but never participated in them, suddenly showed concern for someone—and asked students he had never met to help them. Not teachers—not experts—students. It was so out of the blue it felt like a dream. He felt like a dream, a supernatural force—and her curiosity was eating her alive.
