Grick shoveled down tasty fishy after tasty fishy, while Lily picked clean her third Puffstork leg that morning.

They had just completed their third mission for the empire, and each time they returned, they were met with a cheering crowd at the gate. Grick couldn’t remember most humans but he did remember the group of three girls who always waved and blew kisses at him. It made Grick feel happy as he’d blush and wave back, but for some reason the last time this happened, Lily walked faster. It was like she wanted to get through the city gate and away from them as quickly as possible. Grick figured she was just excited to get back to the palace and eat more Puffstork.

When Lily finished her third Puffstork leg, she stared at the fourth but didn’t go right for it. “Grick?” she asked through Pack Bond, looking up at him.

Grick finished slurping down a fishy, then licked the grease from his fingers as he looked across the table at Lily.

Lily’s ears folded back. “Why do Humans like it when we hunt?”

“Because Grick and Lily keep nice humans safe from nasty bandits!” Grick explained.

“But…” Lily narrowed her eyes slightly as she looked at the plate of Puffstork legs. She wasn’t sure what the problem was, she wasn’t sure how to explain it. She just felt something seemed wrong.

“What wrong Lily?” Grick asked.

“It’s just hunting!” Lily said, not sure how else to explain.

Then, a door on the far side of the room swung open, and Fox came stumbling out. She looked pale, eyes droopy, and her hair and clothing were disheveled. She flopped down at the table with a groan, rubbing her temple. “Oh, hey. You guys are back…” she said groggily. She put a hand over her mouth and let out a long yawn. “I swear, nobody tells me anything around here.”

“Grick and Lily kill nasty humans,” Grick said as he reached for his next fish.

“Mmmm, looks like you each gained another level too.” Fox smirked through a wince as she rubbed her temple a little harder. “Nice going, kids.” As she eyed the table full of food her grimace deepened and she looked away, as if the elegant buffet of delicacies was some disgusting thing she’d rather not have to set her eyes on.

“How is everything this morning, Lady Fox?” a serving girl asked as she came over with a pitcher and poured some red liquid into a cup.

“Oh, uh, everything's just peachy,” she said, flashing a fang. She picked up the glass, but then sniffed it and set it down. “Hey, do you think you can get me some tea instead?” She smiled humorously despite the obvious pain she was in. “I think I’ve had it with the wine for a while.”

“We have a saying in the verdant empire,” the serving girl said, bowing her head. “It’s ‘take a hair from the Vulpyr that bit you.’”

“Oh, how convenient. I don’t remember biting anyone, though. Mmmm, at least not too hard.” She flashed her fangs again.

The girl chuckled, hiding her mouth behind her draping sleeves as she turned and blushed. “What it means, Lady Fox, is that when one has had too much to drink the night before, a little drink in the morning is the perfect thing to stave off the sickness and make you feel better.”

“Hmmm, right, I know the expression. Down south though, it's a Drakewolf’s tail.”

“Then I’ll be sure your glass remains full,” the girl said. “Is there anything else I can get?”

Fox chuckled. “No, really though. Can you swap this out with some tea? I understand the logic, but since the kids are back, I should probably work on sobering up. You know, gotta get down to business in the war room. I am their advisor, after all.” She winked over to Grick and Lily. Grick was too enamored with his meal, while Lily turned her head curiously. She did not understand human speech very well, but she was getting better and better at it every day.

“Very well, if that is what you desire, Lady Fox,” the serving girl said, bowing beneath her sleeves once more, though she peeked past them mischievously. “But I did pick that brand of wine specifically to help with your hangover. If you might make a compromise and take only a few sips while I fetch your tea, I’m sure you’ll find it helps ease the pain and won’t be too much to inhibit you from your work.”

“Hmm, sounds like you came prepared…” Fox forced a smile. “By the way, I don’t think I’ve seen you around. What’s your name?”

“Yakimo,” she said sweetly. “I was recently transferred to this wing. I very much look forward to serving the infamous Lady Fox.”

She smiled. “I suppose my reputation precedes me. I hope the rumors haven’t gotten too out of hand.”

“I was hoping just the opposite, if I might be so bold as to say,” the girl answered, blushing.

Fox chuckled despite herself, a very pleased look coming over her. “Well, I’ll do my best to live up to it,” she said, her fluffy orange tail wagging behind her.

“As do I.” Yakimo bowed once more and then floated off to retrieve Fox’s tea.

Fox picked up the wine cup, twirled the liquid around the rim a few times, sniffed it and then took a sip. “Hmm. Not bad.”

Lily looked at Grick. “Ask Fox when sister returns.”

“How many more sleeps until Puppy back?” Grick asked between mouthfuls of fish.

“Uh lets see… Well, it’s been what ten days? Yeah about ten. I’d guess at least forty or fifty more sleeps, Grick.”

Grick relayed this information to Lily, whose ears folded down upon hearing the news. It felt like so long already, and they were not even halfway into the time it would take for sister to return. Lily was beginning to miss her more and more.

“So how many bandits did you take out on this trip?” Fox asked, sipping more of the wine, then looking at the cup as she smacked her lips, ears perking up. She took a bigger drink, obviously finding she liked it very much.

Grick asked Lily, because she was better with numbers, then he answered Fox, “Five nasty humans.”

“Hm, yeah they seem to be just keeping you guys on single day quests lately. Probably a good idea.”

Fox drank a little more wine and by the time half the glass was gone and her tea was still yet to arrive, she was feeling so much better that the food appeared a bit more appetizing and she began making herself a plate.

When her glass was nearly empty another, different serving girl floated over and refilled it before Fox could stop her.

“Oh hang on—ahhh…” She winced at the full glass.

“I apologize, have I done something wrong, Lady Fox?” the serving girl asked, her posture stiffening immediately.

“Oh, no, no, you’re perfect,” Fox chuckled to reassure her. “It's just I told that other girl I’d like to switch to tea. Her name was Yakimo?”

The new girl bowed below her sleeves. “My apologies, Lady Fox. Yakimo had been pulled away to attend other duties and the request must have gotten lost. Is it tea you asked for? I will be sure to correct this error at once!”

“Oh, hey, no harm done,” Fox chuckled, waving a hand to reassure the panicked girl. “Seriously, it’s no trouble.”

“You are too kind, Lady Fox,” she said, seeming to relax a little. “I’ll be sure to return shortly!” Then she floated off just as Yakimo had.

“Oh hey, you can—” Fox tried to call after her but she was already gone. Fox looked at the full wine glass that was still in front of her, shrugged, and took another sip.

Despite the assurances that she would return with the tea right away, the girl did not return. Fox continued to sip the wine, and while her headache was eased just as promised, her face was beginning to flush with warmth. Then, when the glass was half empty, another pitcher appeared, refilling it before she even noticed.

“Ahhh… hey, I ah…” Fox looked up to see Yakimo had returned. She straightened as she finished pouring the drink and smiled.

“Is everything satisfactory, Lady Fox?”

“Well, it’s just that, I asked for tea…” Fox said, looking at the glass.

“Did the tea never come out?” Yakimo’s eyes widened. “Oh! My apologies! I was called into another room and had asked one of the other girls to bring it to you. I suppose that hadn’t ended up happening for some reason. Then, when I returned and saw you still drinking wine, I had assumed you’d already finished your tea and decided to go back to wine.” She bowed deeply below her sleeves. “I assure you, this will be corrected at once! And I’ll get to the bottom of—”

“Hey, hey! It’s no biggie! It’s just a drink!” Fox laughed, once again waving it off. “It’s a simple mistake, right? I know you girls are busy. And you’ve been very accommodating.”

“Yes… but surely that’s no excuse.” She smiled as she rose above her sleeves once more, blushing. “Perhaps I can make it up to you later?”

“Oh? Well, it's not necessary.”

“But it’s the least I can do, Lady Fox. Maybe once my shift is over, I can come by your room in my off time and see how I might atone?”

“Heh! Ahhh… wellll, if you insist.” Fox chuckled stupidly, her face filling with even more color.

“I look forward to it, Lady Fox,” she said, brushing Fox’s shoulder before floating away.

“Heheh…” Fox laughed and shook her head, but as her gaze drifted back to the table she realized something and quickly turned. “Oh! Hey, and the tea… Ah… never mind.” She blinked at the full cup of wine, then shrugged her shoulder and took another sip.

Lily looked from Fox, to the doorway Yakimo had disappeared to, to Grick still completely enamored with his fish, and then to the now very red-faced Fox who was drinking with not-so-slow sips anymore.

Lily’s ears pinned back and she involuntarily let out a very soft whine as she looked at the plate of Puffstork.

By the time breakfast was over, Fox had given up on asking for tea and happily drank the next cup of wine that had mistakenly been brought to her table.

“Ah, you know, this place is pretty great…” Fox slurred, leaning over the table. “Everyone's just sooo frikken nice.”

Grick nodded in agreement. “Grick likes nice empire humans. Humans cheer for Lily and Grick when we kill nasty humans.”

“Yeah? Well good! You deserve some credit, man. Wanna know something? You’re pretty great for a goblin. Sorry I was kinda ah… you know, when we first met I was all like ‘Hey! What’s this goblin up to exactly?’ But you know, I had you all wrong heheheh…”

“That okay! Grick likes nice Fox too!”

“Yeah man, you just—hick—you just keep being you, Grick!”

“Grick just want to help puppies!” Grick said.

“Yeah, exactly! Puppies first.” Fox pointed at him with her cup, nearly sloshing wine over the rim. “That’s what I’m talking about. You’re always looking out for each other. You’re a real—hick—a real cool pack!” She took another drink. “I can’t wait for the other one to get back. She can be a reaaaal pain in the ass sometimes, but…” Fox chuckled, shaking her head. “Kinda miss having her around.”

Then someone at the end of the table cleared her throat, and they looked up to see it was Aiko. “High Regent Rin Zaoh is prepared for your audience.”

“Waaaah, really!?” Fox exclaimed in drunken surprise.

Aiko let out a heavy breath, placing her hands on her hips. “Honestly, Lady Fox. It’s not even noon. Are you drunk already?”

“Hey, hey! It’s not like that…” She burped into her hand, her whole body lurching. But as she recovered from that, she staggered up to her feet. “I’m good, I’m good. Let’s go get this meeting over with.”

Aiko exasperated, shaking her head. “Lady Fox, I’m afraid you are in no condition. I think it better you get back to bed.”

“Hey, but these kids neeed me. I’m their adviiisor, you know?” she said, swaying on her feet as she pointed a thumb at herself.

Aiko smiled, her eyes narrowing. “Oh, but it is just a simple mission report. They should be absolutely fine without you in this case. Yakimo!”

Yakimo floated up alongside Fox, bowing beneath her sleeves. “Yes, Senior Attendant Aiko?”

“Please help Fox to her room and make sure she has everything she needs. It seems she has had a little too much to drink.”

“Nooo I said I barely even… drink… drank ah thang.”

Yakimo grabbed her arm with both hands and softly said, “Please, right this way, Lady Fox. I’ll help you with your morning wash.”

“Oh? Ah…. Heheh okay then…” Fox said, allowing herself to be dragged away as Yakimo pointedly kept very close as she guided her.

Grick tilted his head curiously at all of this, one ear lifting up from his temple. Lily’s ears however remained pinned back on her head.

“Please, right this way, Grick, Lily,” Aiko said. “We don’t want to keep High Regent Rin Zaoh waiting.”

They followed Aiko out of the guest wing and down the hall, just as would always be normal after one of their quests was completed. Only this time, Fox wasn’t with them. Fox would always join them, even for the simplest of meetings.

Then, as Aiko slid the war room door open and stepped to the side, Grick and Lily were met with the sound of a room full of chatter. A chatter that hushed the moment they entered.

The war room typically only had Rin herself sitting at the far end, and maybe another military official or two, but this time every single seat at the long table was occupied.

Every single person turned to look at them, but then stood from their seats and bowed, chiming together, “Hero Grick!” Every single person, that is, except for Rin herself and General Hakimo who sat beside her.

“Please, take your seat, Hero Grick,” one of the men invited, pulling out the only empty chair. It conveniently had a block of wood strapped to it so Grick would be able to sit up higher without having to stand on the chair.

As he climbed up and took his seat, Rin said, “At ease, gentlemen.” They all bowed and returned to their seats.

Though even as they quieted, there were a few lingering excited whispers about Hero Grick joining them.

“Apologies for the abrupt nature of this meeting, Hero Grick. I’m sure you’d only planned for a usual mission report, but there’s been a few developments since you’ve been away,” Rin said, her voice carrying easily across the room as she sat at the far end of the table, metal fingers interlaced beneath her chin. “We’ll do our best to get you up to speed, and then we would love to hear your expertise on the matter.”

“Matter? What matter?” Grick asked, turning his head, his long green ears flopping.

“General Hakimo, please fill in Hero Grick as best as you can,” Rin ordered.

“Of course, High Regent Rin Zaoh,” Hakimo said as he stood from his chair. He cleared his throat, then continued. “There has been increased goblin activity in the northwestern region. The Iron March has taken advantage of the disturbance this has caused and has been organizing strikes in our vulnerable sectors.”

“And… Grick can help?” he said uncertainly, looking amongst the table.

“Of course,” Hakimo assured, bowing. “And that’s just a very brief rundown. Why don’t we begin this meeting in earnest with your usual mission report on your last outing? I’m sure that will demonstrate your skill and competence to the room, then we can go from there.”

Grick wasn’t sure what competence meant, but he figured he should just try his best. He reached down and patted Lily’s head reassuringly beside him as she let out another anxious whine. Then, as the room of decorated military personnel watched him from along the massive table, each intently hanging on his words, Grick began to explain.

“Grick and Lily left empire two sleeps ago, and Lily began sniffing for nasty humans…”
