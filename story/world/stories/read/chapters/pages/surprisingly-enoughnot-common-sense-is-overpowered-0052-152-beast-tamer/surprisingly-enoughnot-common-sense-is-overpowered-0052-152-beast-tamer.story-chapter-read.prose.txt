Ning crouched beneath the shade of a twisted pine, carefully brushing aside the surrounding leaves. At the center of the small clearing grew a pale-blue plant with thin, translucent petals.

Illusory Mistflower.

It was a first-tier spiritual plant commonly used in illusory formations, low-grade pills, and illusion-based techniques.

Ning harvested it with practiced precision, sealing the roots with a wisp of qi before placing it inside a jade box.

During missions, Ning often chose tasks related to gathering spiritual plants. They weren't the most exciting, but they steadily enriched his small garden and expanded his options for experimentation. Compared to monster hunting or escort missions, this path was also considerably safer.

Of course, despite finding several useful plants over the years, he had never stumbled across something like Dragon Vein Grass again, the kind of spiritual herb that could directly improve one's cultivation simply by eating it.

Repeatedly encountering treasures like that was the privilege of a protagonist, so it didn't particularly bother him. Luck was unreliable, while accumulation was something he could actually control.

Ning straightened and brushed the dirt from his hands, only to freeze as a piercing roar suddenly tore through the distant forest.

Almost immediately, the undergrowth erupted.

Branches snapped, birds burst from the canopy in panicked flocks, and the ground trembled faintly as something massive tore through the trees. Ning could even hear cultivators shouting in the distance, panic clear in their voices.

His expression changed.

Commotion in the forest wasn't unusual. Beasts fought and cultivators clashed all the time, but something on this scale was rare.

Ning didn't hesitate. Qi surged into his legs as the High-Grade Wind-Stepping Boots hummed softly, the runes covering them flaring to life.

The boots possessed two modes: Flowing Wind Step, which provided a sustainable increase in speed, and Swift Wind Step, a burst mode that consumed qi rapidly in exchange for far greater acceleration.

In all his years of frequenting this forest, Ning had never seen an uproar of this scale.

This wasn't normal.

Act first. Think later.

In situations like this, speed mattered more than curiosity. After all, among the thirty-six stratagems, retreat ranked rather highly.

Ning switched to Swift Wind Step.

His speed spiked as his qi reserves began draining rapidly, and he shot through the forest, weaving between trees while the distant shouts grew louder.

"Run!"

"It's coming this way!"

Ning didn't look back. Anyone who had watched even a single zombie movie knew better than that.

By the time he reached the outskirts of Blackthorn City, his breathing had quickened, but his stride remained steady. He wasn't alone either. Other cultivators were fleeing in the same direction, their faces pale and strained.

Then a shadow swept across the ground.

Ning looked up just as something enormous burst from the treeline.

A black boar, massive as a siege beast, thundered forward with earth-shaking force. Its bristles stood on end like iron spikes, and every step cracked the ground beneath its hooves as violent spiritual energy surged around its body.

A Foundation Establishment Black Boar.

Even from this distance, Ning could feel the pressure radiating from it. Unlike human cultivators, wild beasts rarely restrained their auras, especially creatures as naturally violent as black boars.

Several cultivators fled ahead of it, hunters judging by their gear, scattered in complete disarray. Blood stained their robes; one was missing an arm, while another limped badly and was barely keeping ahead of the beast's charge.

"We're almost at the city! Everyone, run!" one of the men shouted hoarsely.

Ning watched the boar plow through trees, its glowing tusks smashing through everything in its path.

A city established in this wilderness naturally possessed defensive formations, especially one so close to the Pure Qi Sect. Normally, even powerful beasts knew better than to approach such a place.

This one wasn't slowing down.

It was charging straight toward the city.

Ning's expression hardened.

The outer defenses were more than sufficient to handle ordinary beasts and Qi Condensation cultivators, but a Foundation Establishment beast rampaging at full speed was an entirely different matter. A single charge from something that large could smash through the gates and kill hundreds before the city's stronger cultivators managed to stop it.

Before the boar could get much closer, however, another massive shadow swept across the ground.

Ning looked up.

An enormous avian beast descended from above with a piercing screech, its wings spanning dozens of meters. Ning recognized it as a Red-Beaked Spearow, and the pressure radiating from its body immediately revealed that it was also at Foundation Establishment.

What caught Ning's attention, however, was the man standing calmly on its back.

He wore the robes of the Pure Qi Sect, pristine and unruffled, his posture relaxed as though he were on a leisurely patrol rather than approaching a rampaging monster.

The man stepped off the spearow in midair.

"How bold," he said, his voice carrying easily across the surroundings. "A mere beast dares charge a city under my watch?"

He paused before adding, "Fu Zheng's watch."

Ning stared.

Did he really just introduce himself like that?

Fu Zheng descended slowly as spiritual energy gathered beneath his feet, supporting him in midair.

The black boar sensed him immediately and skidded to a halt, its hooves tearing deep furrows into the earth. Its eyes burned red as it roared, and the violent qi around its body suddenly deepened into a visible crimson hue.

Some kind of innate ability?

"As expected of a black boar," Fu Zheng sighed as he drew his sword. "No thinking at all. Just attacks anything that moves."

The boar answered by charging.

Its powerful hind legs propelled its enormous body forward with terrifying speed, turning it into a living battering ram. Fu Zheng made no attempt to meet it head-on, instead slipping aside with a movement technique and sending a blade of sword qi across its flank.

The attack struck cleanly, but the boar merely staggered before roaring and charging again.

"Tsk. Its innate defense really did skyrocket after reaching Foundation Establishment."

Fu Zheng continued moving around the beast, avoiding its charges while leaving more wounds across its body. None of them seemed particularly serious.

By now, more cultivators had gathered at a safe distance to watch. Most were low-level cultivators who rarely had the opportunity to witness a battle at this level, and the sight of a Foundation Establishment cultivator fighting at full strength was enough to keep them rooted in place despite the danger.

"I didn't expect a black boar to become this strong after reaching Foundation Establishment."

"Black boars have always been known for their strength and defense. It makes sense."

"It almost looks like Senior Fu Zheng is having trouble."

Someone nearby immediately scoffed.

"You lot clearly don't know Senior Fu Zheng. He's been in charge of Blackthorn City for years. His sword arts aren't even his specialty."

The man gestured toward the Red-Beaked Spearow circling overhead.

"He's a beast tamer."

At this point, Ning had long since accepted the existence of cultivators who mysteriously appeared whenever convenient information needed to be explained, so he ignored the man and returned his attention to the fight.

Beast taming was one of the more popular professions within the Pure Qi Sect, and considering that Fu Zheng had arrived riding a Foundation Establishment flying beast, the explanation made perfect sense.

The real question was why he hadn't used it yet.

Perhaps he was testing the boar.

Or maybe...

He's aura farming.

Soon enough, Fu Zheng leapt backward and looked over the battered but still very much alive black boar.

"It seems I still need to use that move."

He raised one arm.

Light flickered around him for an instant.

[Falling Impact]

High above, the Red-Beaked Spearow folded its wings and dove.

Qi surged violently through its body as its feathers took on a faint metallic sheen. Its speed increased with every passing moment until the enormous beast became little more than a streak descending from the sky.

The impact shook the earth.

A massive shockwave erupted outward, and even from hundreds of meters away, Ning felt the force tear through the air as wind and dust blasted past him.

When everything finally settled, a deep crater had appeared where the boar had been standing.

At its center lay the black boar, motionless.

"Still alive?" Fu Zheng muttered, sweeping it with his spiritual sense.

Apparently satisfied, he glanced around at the crater and the surrounding destruction before sighing.

"...Heavens. I'll need to call the earth-repair cultivators again."

That was apparently the worst part of using his ultimate move. It looked impressive and hit extremely hard, but someone still had to fix the ground afterward.

The surrounding cultivators finally relaxed, excited voices quickly spreading through the crowd as they discussed what they had just witnessed.

Ning remained where he was, quietly reconsidering his understanding of Foundation Establishment.

The difference was larger than he had expected.

Fu Zheng's sword attacks had already been far beyond anything Ning could currently produce, yet the Red-Beaked Spearow had finished the same beast with a single coordinated strike. More importantly, Fu Zheng hadn't even needed to fight alone. That was perhaps the greatest advantage of a beast tamer.

As a Qi Condensation cultivator, Ning roughly classified his own maximum destructive potential somewhere around boulder-to-building level, depending on what technique he used and how much preparation he had.

Foundation Establishment was clearly another matter entirely.

A sufficiently powerful cultivator at that realm could destroy an entire street with frightening ease, and someone considerably stronger could likely cause devastation across a large section of a city.

Clearly, cultivators didn't merely surpass ordinary humans in lifespan. The difference in destructive power between realms was enormous as well.

While Ning was still considering what he had witnessed, conversations from the surrounding crowd gradually revealed what had caused the entire incident.

"I heard those hunters were chasing something rare."

"Some kind of spiritual beast, apparently. Supposedly worth a fortune."

"They must've gone too deep and provoked the boar."

Ning listened for a while and quickly pieced together the likely sequence of events. A hunting party had gone deep into Black Boar Forest while pursuing a rare spiritual beast, only to somehow provoke a newly advanced Foundation Establishment Black Boar and send the entire area into chaos.

"Seems classic enough," Ning muttered.

With the danger over and nothing else to gain from standing around, he slipped away from the crowd.

Someone else did the same.

Ning's gaze instinctively followed the figure, only for their eyes to meet.

Xiao Fan.

For a moment, Xiao Fan froze, and that tiny hesitation was enough for Ning to notice the storage bag hanging from his waist.

It was bulging unnaturally.

More importantly, something inside it moved.

Ning looked at the bag, then at Xiao Fan, before recalling the conversation he'd just overheard.

A rare spiritual beast had disappeared.

The hunters had returned empty-handed.

And Xiao Fan was quietly trying to leave the scene.

Cause and effect.

Clearly, the protagonist was at work.

"Ning... didn't expect to find you here," Xiao Fan said awkwardly, looking far too shifty to be convincing.

Ning raised a hand before he could continue. "Not here. Let's go to my place first. I have something to give you anyway."

With that, Ning turned and left, leaving Xiao Fan to swallow whatever excuse he'd been preparing and follow after him.

...

Ning's hut was small but orderly.

"Sit," Ning said as he moved toward the table. "You look like you've been involved in something strange again."

Xiao Fan merely shrugged and took a sip of the milk tea Ning had brewed.

"What are you talking about? I don't understand."

"Alright," Ning replied mildly. "You can let it out now. Otherwise, I don't think your bag will hold much longer."

Xiao Fan froze before suddenly spraying out his tea.

"How did you know?!"

Ning had already stepped aside in anticipation, safely avoiding the classic startled spit-take.

"Even if I didn't know before, you just confirmed it," he said blandly. "And don't spit next time. It's disgusting."

Xiao Fan hesitated for a moment before finally loosening the seal on his storage bag.

A flash of white spilled out, and something small landed silently on the table.

It was a fox.

Its fur was pure white, without a trace of the red coloration Ning would have expected from the fire foxes native to Black Boar Forest. Its bright eyes moved around the room with an intelligence far beyond that of an ordinary beast, and after taking in its surroundings, the fox lifted its chin and flicked its tail as though it had already found the accommodations lacking.

Ning blinked.

"A fox?"

He knew Black Boar Forest well. Deep within it lived a small population of fire foxes, red-furred, timid creatures with a natural affinity for fire.

This one was clearly a shiny.

In one particular world, that might have meant little more than a color variation. Here, however, a drastic change in appearance often indicated some form of bloodline mutation.

And unlike the normally timid fire foxes, this one radiated pure arrogance.

The fox turned toward Xiao Fan and snorted.

"It seems quite dramatic," Xiao Fan muttered, somehow sounding offended by the judgment of his own contracted beast.

Hearing that low-EQ remark, Ning couldn't help retorting, "Well, you did stuff the little guy into a pouch and run all the way here. I think a bit of drama is justified."

The fox looked toward Ning, nodded once, and then snorted at Xiao Fan again.

"It's very intelligent," Ning said. "Even among foxes, which are already smarter than most beasts, this one feels exceptional. How did you even run into something like this?"

Its cultivation was only around the middle stages of Qi Condensation. Normally, a spiritual beast displaying intelligence at this level should have been considerably stronger.

Xiao Fan proceeded to explain what had happened.

The story itself was remarkably simple.

The hunters had discovered the rare fox and attempted to contract it. Just as their ritual was underway, the black boar appeared and threw everything into chaos. Xiao Fan, who just happened to be nearby, somehow ended up forming the contract instead.

Ning wasn't even surprised.

Finding a rare mutated beast and contracting it for free?

That checked out.

He reached out to pet the fox, only for its tail to immediately brush his hand aside.

"It's quite arrogant," Xiao Fan snickered, finally seeing Ning encounter some resistance.

Ning said nothing. He simply took out part of his lunch, a piece of candied bacon, and placed it on the table.

The fox glanced at the food, then at Ning.

"If you let me pet you, this is yours."

The fox hesitated.

Apparently, pride had limits.

It barked once before lowering its head to eat, allowing Ning to run his hand across its soft fur. Ning immediately glanced at Xiao Fan with a smug smile.

"This beast has no stubbornness," Xiao Fan remarked.

A chilly breath immediately washed over him.

"Hey, don't get mad. I'm just telling the truth."

Ning continued petting the fox as he examined it. "Judging by its appearance and attributes, its bloodline has clearly mutated. Normal fire foxes usually cap out around the middle stages of Qi Condensation, so this may actually be very good for you."

"I guess that is good news," Xiao Fan admitted.

"As expected, you're quite lucky." Ning paused before remembering something. "Which brings me to a request."

"What is it?"

"I want you to help me look for a wild beast egg," Ning said directly. "I want to try raising a spiritual beast myself. Eggs with good bloodlines are ridiculously expensive, so I'd rather find one naturally."

"That makes sense." Xiao Fan nodded. "But are you sure I can find something with a sufficiently strong bloodline?"

"I just need you to keep an eye out. There's no need to force it, and I'm not asking you to help for free."

Ning took out two small books and handed them over.

Xiao Fan flipped through the first one, and his eyes widened.

"This is..."

"The technique from the jade talisman slip," Ning explained. "The Pure Eyes Technique. Like I said before, I'm sharing it with you. The later portions are missing, but as an ocular technique, its value is still quite high."

"You're really giving this to me?" Xiao Fan asked seriously. "Just like that?"

Techniques of this level were usually guarded carefully, so even though Ning had mentioned sharing it before, Xiao Fan hadn't entirely expected him to follow through so casually.

Ning shrugged. "I'm keeping my word. If you ever happen to find the later portions, I hope you'll let me buy them."

He was laying the groundwork for the future.

Xiao Fan nodded. "I probably won't find them. It'd be like searching for a needle in a haystack, but I'll keep an eye out."

That was enough for Ning. At the very least, he now had another possible path toward finding the rest of the technique.

"As for the second book, that's your reward for helping me look for the egg."

Xiao Fan opened it and began reading, only for his expression to change again.

"This is... an explanation of how to reach the third layer of the Pure Qi Sutra? And it's incredibly detailed."

"Yes. That's my understanding of it. If you haven't reached the third layer yet, it should be useful. Of course, if you don't want it, I can change the reward."

"I don't need another reward," Xiao Fan said immediately. "This is exactly what I need."

Even from a quick glance, Xiao Fan could tell that Ning's notes approached the Pure Qi Sutra differently from the usual explanations. The sections concerning meridian circulation and the appropriate amount of spiritual qi to guide through each route were particularly precise.

Ning smiled.

"Then you've accepted my quest."

As everyone knew, no matter how difficult or strange a task was, once it was handed to the protagonist, its chances of success increased dramatically.

Ning finally understood why every household in an RPG seemed to have some random quest waiting for the protagonist. Once you enlisted their help, you could simply sit back and wait for fate to do its thing.

Honestly, Ning rather liked being an NPC quest giver.

...

Fireball.

The spell every beginner mage learned first, whether in tabletop games, novels, or cheap fantasy media. If magic had a universal starting point, this was probably it.

And now, after all these years, it was finally Ning's turn.

It was the spell he had chosen after listening to the senior sister's suggestion. At first glance, the choice almost felt underwhelming. Compared to the esoteric farming spells he had learned before, Fireball seemed almost painfully crude, but the more Ning thought about it, the more sense it made.

First, it was cheap.

Compared to most elemental techniques, Fireball consumed relatively little spiritual energy for the damage it produced. It was also brutally straightforward, with no complicated effects or special conditions to worry about.

Most importantly, its effective range scaled heavily with proficiency.

In fact, Ning was more surprised that he had learned it this late. Thinking back, however, it made sense. For a long time, his priority had been finding ways to earn enough spirit stones to support his cultivation, and Fireball simply hadn't fit into that plan.

Until now.

Standing alone in a clearing outside the city, Ning took a slow breath and steadied himself.

Fireball required structure, and as a transmigrator, Ning had a slight advantage when it came to understanding the basic idea behind it.

In games and stories, Fireball was rarely just throw fire. There was usually some form of compression, ignition, stabilization, and release involved. Energy gathered, took shape, remained contained for just long enough, and was then violently expelled toward the target.

The exact mechanics here were obviously different, but the general logic translated surprisingly well.

Ning raised one hand and began with visualization.

He imagined a sphere of heat forming above his palm, spiritual qi spiraling inward rather than leaking uselessly into the surroundings. He focused first on maintaining the shape, then gradually increased the amount of qi flowing into it.

Clear mental imagery had always been one of Ning's strengths, and this time was no different.

Spiritual qi flowed slowly through his palm. The air above it shimmered as the temperature rose, and a dull red glow appeared for several seconds before flickering out.

"Too loose," Ning muttered, jotting down the result in his notes.

He tried again, tightening the circulation and guiding excess qi back into his meridians instead of continuously feeding it into the spell. The glow gradually became more stable, its vague edges condensing into a recognizable shape.

By the forty-sixth attempt, a small sphere of flame finally hovered steadily above his palm.

[Fireball: (Starting: 1/200)]

"Good." Ning smiled. "Baseline achieved."

He flicked his wrist, sending the Fireball toward a rock he had prepared earlier.

Boom.

The explosion was small, closer to a violent burst of flame than a true detonation. Fire splashed across the surface of the rock and left behind a scorched patch, but the actual damage was limited.

Ning examined the result before writing several observations in his notes.

Damage radius: limited.

Heat dispersion: inefficient.

Qi consumption: moderate.

"As expected, its power is quite low at the start."

That wasn't a problem. Now that he could reliably cast the spell, he finally had something to improve.

Ning repeated the process, changing only one variable with each attempt. He adjusted the compression speed, experimented with the direction of the qi's rotation, altered the release timing, and recorded how each change affected the resulting Fireball.

During one attempt, however, the flame in his palm began condensing much faster than expected.

Ning immediately stopped feeding it qi.

"This is the dangerous part."

Overcharging Fireball was practically the classic beginner-mage mistake. Put too much energy into the spell, lose control of the containment, and suddenly the Fireball meant for the enemy was exploding in your own hand.

Ning had no intention of personally testing that particular trope.

He carefully dispersed the unstable sphere and allowed the remaining heat to bleed into the air before stretching his fingers.

In D&D terms, he supposed this was somewhat similar to the difference between using a first-level spell slot and upcasting the same spell.

Here, however, there were no spell slots.

There was only proficiency.

And proficiency was something Ning could grind.

He looked down at his palm before forming another Fireball, already considering what variable to test next.

Perhaps learning such a basic spell this late wasn't a bad thing after all. There were no complicated mechanics to distract him, which meant he could experiment with almost every part of it himself.

Compression, temperature, rotation, size, release speed, range...

Ning smiled.

There was a lot to work with.
