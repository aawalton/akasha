"Oh, you apologize for the early hour?" I asked, perhaps a touch too sarcastically, and settled into the chair at the head of the table without returning either of their bows. I leaned to one side, rested my chin on a fist, and couldn't help but smirk at the elder woman. "I have been told the two of you waited here since before dawn. I do not imagine the early hour was anything but intentional."

Neither Cardinal flinched at the accusation, implied as it was.

Snow only inclined her head. "I do not intend to hide the fact that we have been quite eager to speak with the famed Duchess of Alistar."

I stayed silent, studying them. Dralos had positioned himself near the hall doors, while Roxanne stood two paces behind my left shoulder. Hannah was... somewhere.

The Cardinals did not feel hostile, so I chose to accept the woman's reasoning for waking the Spire so early.

"The Church of Balance," I repeated, turning the name over aloud and in my mind. "I cannot say I have heard of your church before. I must admit, I know rather little about the distinctions between the Holy Kingdom's various religions. Its internal politics have never risen to the level of being my concern."

The unspoken “not yet” hung in the air for a moment before Tundra broke the silence.

"They may need to become your concern," he said. His voice was quieter than I'd anticipated. Slower. More precise. I'd expected something that quavered. But no. I quickly revised my opinion of the man. Cautious, not fearful. "That is, in part, why we are here."

"In part?" I raised an eyebrow and motioned for them both to sit. "What is the other part, if not to win my aid in your politicking?"

"You have a... guest in your household," Tundra said. "A woman who arrived in Elyndor some months past. She goes by the name Mirabelle. Or Phoenix."

My expression remained perfectly neutral. "That is rather vague, Cardinal."

"You would know this one," Tundra replied gruffly, his eyes sweeping the room again as if he could pull Mirabelle from the shadows. "She has hair the color of fire and wields elemental heart energy the way one might breathe or think."

I let the silence after his words stretch. "What is your interest in this woman?"

"She is one of ours," Snow said simply. "Or she was. Mirabelle disappeared from the Holy Kingdom nearly eight months ago. The last we knew of her, she was in Sealrite. Then, Elyndor. Then, nothing."

Her tone was sincere, but her words fell short of the truth. I knew Mirabelle had been a Cardinal of the Church of Light, not the Church of Balance. Still, I absorbed the information.

"This woman, Mirabelle," I said. "She belongs to your Church?"

Tundra grunted as if trying not to laugh. Snow glared at him, but he only shrugged.

"She was raised as one of ours," Snow answered. "Lately, she had been... experimenting with other beliefs. Before she disappeared, she was conducting sensitive work on our behalf. Regardless, it was brought to my attention that someone with similar skills has been seen working alongside the Alistar military. I would simply like to meet her and see whether they are the same woman."

I hummed, dragging a fingernail along the smooth wooden edge of the table. "I will consider your request, Cardinal. Now, is that all you came for? A woman and a warning?"

Tundra shook his head. "No, Duchess. Our warning about the Holy Kingdom's political climate is only context for our request."

Snow folded her hands beneath the table. When she spoke again, something in her bearing shifted. Before, she had been merely formal and composed. Now she was deliberate. Her voice firmed, and her eyes filled with true belief. "Are you familiar with the structure of religious authority in the Holy Kingdom, Your Grace?"

"Assume I am not."

"The Holy Kingdom is governed spiritually by the Pope, who sits the High Seat and holds authority over every church within our borders: the Church of Balance, the Church of Light, the Church of the Veil, and several smaller orders. He belongs to none of them. His role is to hold the balance between them." She paused. "The Church of Light, for its part, names its own high priest 'Pope' as well. Pope Betrant, at present. A vanity the rest of us tolerate. Betrant would very much like the High Seat to go with the title." She smiled softly. "For most of recorded history, that balance has held."

"And now it doesn't?" I guessed.

Tundra's mouth pressed into a thin line that might have been frustration. "The Church of Light has grown considerably in influence over the past two decades. They have... external support." He ground his teeth, forcing the next words out. "They are heavily influenced by the Pandorian Empire."

A low growl rose from somewhere deep in my chest as memories of my dream encounter with Niro flashed through my mind. For a moment, the aura of my heart and mana cores rampaged in anger and slipped from my control. Both Cardinals grunted, their bodies folding onto the table, pinned where they sat. Roxanne yelped behind me, and a moment later I heard the soft thud of her hitting the floor. Dralos left his post by the door, snatched up a whimpering Hannah from where she'd fallen, and swiftly carried her out of the room.

Fuck.

I forced thoughts of Niro from my mind and reined in my aura. The pressure drowning the room vanished as quickly as it had come.

"I apologize for that," I said, watching the Cardinals gasp for air, their eyes wide as saucers. "I do not have a good relationship with the Pandorian Empire."

"Well," Snow breathed, struggling to straighten in her seat. Her breaths came short and ragged, but after a moment she collected herself enough to continue. "The current Pope visited Pandoria some thirty years ago. When he returned, he returned changed. It wasn’t immediately obvious, but became ever more clear with every passing year that something… affected him. Those of us who watched closely have had concerns for a long time. Now he has fallen ill, and we do not know from what, despite the continent's greatest healers working without sleep. Worse, the Church of Light has been positioning itself for the succession. If the High Seat passes to one of theirs, the balance our order exists to maintain will be gone. The balance between every religion and belief in the Holy Kingdom will crumble."

I looked at them both steadily. "And you want my help stopping that."

"We want your presence," Snow said. "A majority of the Holy Kingdom's Cardinals have called a formal gathering of representatives from every major power. A convocation, to address the question of succession openly before it becomes a crisis too large to address at all. Pandoria will send a representative. Lysoria has, of course, been invited. As have Velora and Tenebris. Every nation has been asked to send someone who can speak on behalf of those who rule it."

"And you want House Alistar's duchess at this convocation," I said. "Representing Lysoria's interests, instead of the King or some more stable house?"

Tundra shook his head. He too had regained his composure and, like Snow, acted as though I hadn't nearly crushed him with my aura. "We want you there representing your own interests, Your Grace. You are a power in your own right. The aura you just revealed speaks for itself." He swallowed hard. "What happens to the High Seat determines what becomes of the Church of Light's ambitions. And what the Church of Light does with the backing of the Pandorian Empire should concern anyone who shares a border with Pandoria or its allies. You, I am told, have already seen the lengths the Church of Light is willing to go to."

It was true, at least, that Lysoria had cause for concern. To the east lay Cael, an enemy still licking its wounds. To the northwest sprawled the Pandorian Empire, and beyond the southwestern border, the Holy Kingdom. Noctaron, pressed against Pandoria's flank to the north, would likely be the first to fall.

I was quiet for a moment. Behind the Cardinals, the fire in the hearth burned low, untended.

I turned the proposition over, examining it from every angle. They were asking me to walk into a room full of people who had reason to want me dead, as the representative of interests they had decided I held, in a kingdom I had never visited, in support of a church I had no loyalty to. Against, presumably, the envoy of the most powerful empire on the continent.

It was, objectively, a great deal to ask.

On the other hand, a gathering of nations like this was likely to provide me an opportunity to fan the flames of war against the Empire. And that, I wanted.

With that war, I could burn everything Niro had built to the ground. Again.

"I will consider it," I said.

Snow's expression didn't change, but something in her posture eased, and she released a long breath. "That is all we ask."

"Don't mistake consideration for agreement," I added sharply. "I have questions that need answers before I commit to anything. About your church. About this convocation. About what happened to Mirabelle." I let that last one settle. "We will speak again tomorrow, once I've had time to think."

The Cardinals exchanged a glance, then nodded. "Of course, Your Grace," Snow said. "We are in no immediate rush."

I suspected that was a lie and that they were in a very great rush. But they were patient enough not to show it, and I found I could respect that.

I rose, ending the meeting whether they were ready or not. The two Cardinals stood with me, their bows deeper than before. Roxanne was already moving past me toward the door. Dralos had returned at some point and was fixing me with a rather disapproving stare.

I paused at the threshold, ignoring the draconian's judgment.

"One more thing," I said, without turning back toward the Cardinals. "The Catalyst of Destruction in Sealrite. The one that leveled the Duke's compound and nearly buried me with it. Benedict swears Cael only bought it." I let the silence sit. "Who made it?"

The pause before Snow answered was a fraction too long.

"That," she said carefully, "is one of the things we would very much like to discuss. But yes. The Church of Light built it."

I nodded once and left the room.
