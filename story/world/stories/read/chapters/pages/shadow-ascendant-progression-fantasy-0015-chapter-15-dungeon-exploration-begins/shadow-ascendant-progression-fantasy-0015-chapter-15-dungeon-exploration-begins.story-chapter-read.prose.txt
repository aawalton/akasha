With my training partially complete, I went looking for instructor Mirayel. Searching for him wasn’t really hard—he had a habit of drifting to the library whenever he got a chance.

I told Instructor Mirayel everything, from the dungeon exploration I had planned out, and how the training is taxing on my body. He gave me a thoughtful nod, perhaps understanding my plea, and then let me off early.

An hour earlier than everyone else!

With that said and done, I quickly went back to the Cryz dorm, slipping in through the same gate I’d left before and caught the faint smell of food. Wait, already?

A glance into the hall confirmed it. Lunch was already made, probably because the seniors were busy with their exam preparation—lucky me.

I headed to my room, dropped my stuff, and then marched to the mess hall. Loading my plate with an ample amount of food… I even took two more eggs!

After which, I moved back to my room, not wanting to disturb my seniors. And then quietly devoured it… and no, I don’t mean eating by devouring. I literally ate like a hungry animal; was I even chewing properly?

As I finished my food, I placed the plate on the table, my body on the bed, the blanket over me and the light?

“Luminous,” I murmured, turning it off.

Time to sleep—or meditate. Technically, meditation worked better with absorbing mana, and that was totally what I was gonna do. I mean, hey, it’s not like I am a kid who requires ten hours of uninterrupted sleep.

Still, since I had a mild headache, I decided to meditate lying down. Sadly, I passed away.

No, no, I didn’t die. But I fell asleep, though I didn’t have a headache now, so I think it all worked out.

“OI ARNOSSS!!!” someone yelled from the outside. It was probably Mabel; I could tell by the voice.

She’s here to collect my soul…

“Coming, give me a second!” I yelled back as I sat up. My eyes adjusted to the darkness of my room. I threw the blanket to the side and walked to the door.

Before opening the door, I glanced at myself once, adjusting my shirt a little.

“Hey!” I smiled, opening the door. Mabel stood out there with her arms crossed, and mouth pouty. Did I make her wait long? I wondered.

“I have been patiently waiting for two minutes, and what are you doing? Sleeping on the bed!”

Two minutes, huh? “I mean, bed is… where we sleep, right?”

She paused, her lips parted, then closed again as she nodded. “Right,” she murmured, then with a sharp glare, “get dressed!” She commanded.

“Yes, ma’am!” I yelled back, doing a mock salute. I mean, hey, the girl was helping me out, right? It’s only fair to show her some additional respect.

Mabel’s facial expressions finally softened. Maybe the salute worked? not wasting any time. I shut the door and walked towards the cupboard.

Wouldn’t want to waste her time, right?

Needless to say, I got an earful of how ‘it isn’t polite to shut the door loudly in someone’s face,’ and I am pretty sure I was at fault, so I apologised like any responsible person.

With that out of the way, Mabel and I began our journey to the dungeon.

We had no time to spare. Mabel believed getting out of the fourth floor before night was a necessity, so we used multiple Quick Step spells on the way to the city. After which, we walked to the dungeon, which probably took around thirty minutes, since it was a shorter distance compared to Duxelle and Cellera.

Not sure about the exact distance though.

By the time we started descending to the third floor, the curved staircase looked almost as if it had never been destroyed by that overgrown bull. The Dungeon of Death sure is an engineering marvel.

“Mhmm, hmm, hmhm” Mabel hummed as we walked, then she stopped. “Oh…”

“Yeah?” I asked. The gate’s opening was now in sight when we stopped, four steps from it.

“So… you know how numbats are a pain in the ass?”

I nodded, remembering how crouching to kill them was literally a pain in the ass.

Mabel slipped the humongous brown backpack from her shoulder. “We could… could!” She repeated as her hand searched frantically in the bag, as she finally pulled something out.

In her hand, she held a see-through bottle with green liquid.

A glass bottle of perfume? Why’d she pull it out? Wait, am I smelling bad!? I am quite certain I bathed properly… yesterday, fuck. I should not have delayed my bath until tonight!

“We could put Ereceus extract on ourselves and make them stay away.”

“Oh, it’s that?” I relaxed.

“What’s that?”

I shook my head. “Nothing,” I murmured, grabbing the bottle from her.

As soon as I opened the cap, that familiar crushed basil-like smell burned through my nose, only this time it was two hundred times stronger.

“It’s made by removing the impurities!” Mabel beamed.

To me, it felt like it was the impurities that were collected instead, but whatever, I sprayed it twice on both of my armpits and then glanced at Mabel.

Narrowing her eyes and pursing her lips, she murmured, “Hmm… I think it’s enough,” and took the bottle from my hand.

Following that, she spritzed herself, and then spritzed herself again. She glanced at me one final time, then sprayed my chest once more.

“And what was that for?” I asked, my brows raised.

“Precaution,” she murmured. “I am sure about the amount I have to spray on myself, but not about you… everyone has different body odour, right?”

To which I nodded; it made sense.

And just as Mabel had said, we both were able to move through the third floor with no problems. I wondered how the numbats would have reacted to seeing us. Walking plants, which they despised, must have been terrifying. I mean, I’d probably be terrified if one day sculptures began walking by themselves.

With the gate to the fourth floor now a minute away, I glanced at Mabel, noticing how she liked to look at the illusory sky. Maybe she secretly liked numbats? I mean a few were still flying—

“I really want to improve my Water Bullet spell, but because of you we can't.” She grumbled.

“What do you mean? I am always up for a numbat hunt!”

I mean it was peak afternoon; they were practically blind; hunting them would be as easy as breathing.

Mabel rolled her eyes. “And that’s your mistake. They’re not even credit-dense.” She tilted her head, neck cracking faintly.

“Hmm. I mean, we could make it up by sheer numbers, right?”

She shot me a look. “Have you ever been so exhausted after a numbat hunt you’d actually fall asleep on pointy rocks? With your butt exposed?”

I squinted at her. “No. And for the record, even if I were that tired, I still wouldn’t sleep on pointy rocks—with my BUTT exposed.” Pretty sure I put too much emphasis on the butt part.

“Get it? There’s a limit to the monsters you’d find in a day, can’t expect to gain 40 credits a day by killing numbats.” She added, looking at me.

“40 credits, you are saying as if you…”

Mabel didn’t let me finish. With a smirk, she flicked the Celoris band she wore on her right hand.

A small blue hologram flickered into existence before her eyes.

[Name: Mable Weeble]

[Class: Magician]

[Credits: 475]

Note: Displayed credits reflect this month’s accumulation. To view lifetime totals, shake the band again while focusing on it.

“You…” I gasped, my eyes widened. Here I was collecting pocket change, and there she was with 475 credits. Yeah, she can talk all the shit she wants; she has my permission.

“It’s not totally your fault though,” Mabel murmured, her smile vanishing; “I mean, you did not have a team; it’s only logical not to go deeper. Knowing you, you might have gotten 200-300 credits if you had one.”

“You are trying to console me, aren’t you?”

To which she shook her head. “I am judging you…” she paused, “wait, that doesn’t sound, ahhh, well whatever.” She shrugged. Entering the gate first.

I paused for a second before moving inside. It was my first time going deeper than the third floor.

It’s said floors deeper than the third, except for the boss floors, don’t have any illusory sky or the sun. I wasn’t sure how it would feel without them. Would it be completely dark? Like a cave, or something else, I didn’t know.
