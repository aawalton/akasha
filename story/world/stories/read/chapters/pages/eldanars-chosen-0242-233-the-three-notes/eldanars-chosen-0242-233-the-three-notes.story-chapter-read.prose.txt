The hollow, scraping emptiness of a completely dry mana core was a physical agony unlike any other. It did not burn like a blade or ache like a bruised bone. It felt as though a vital, internal organ had simply been scooped out of Brett's chest, leaving a freezing, vibrating vacuum in its place.

Standing on the central wooden platform, the elemental mage dropped his trembling hands. The massive, roaring pillar of liquid white plasma he had just unleashed upon the plains finally winked out, leaving the world completely blind for a fraction of a second.

Brett could not even draw a breath to command his lungs. His vision swam with dark, pulsating spots. His ears rang with a high-pitched, deafening whine that completely drowned out the chaotic noise of the siege. His knees buckled instantly, his long legs no longer able to support the weight of his own body. He collapsed heavily onto the blood-slicked floorboards of the rampart, his crimson Ember-silk cloak pooling around him like a shroud.

"Brett!" Carcan cried out, her voice barely breaking through the ringing in his ears.

The elven healer rushed to his side, dropping to her knees in the freezing grime and blood covering the platform. She pressed her glowing hands firmly against his chest, her amber staff clattering to the floorboards beside her.

But there was very little she could do. Healing magic repaired torn flesh, sealed ruptured arteries, and knitted shattered bone. It could not artificially refill a magically depleted core. Brett simply had to endure the crippling, nauseating hollow until his body naturally regenerated the arcane energy. He felt her warm, golden magic washing over him, easing the physical trembling in his limbs, but the crushing emptiness inside his chest remained.

He lay on his side, his cheek pressed against the rough, splintered wood. His chest heaved with shallow, ragged gasps. Every single muscle in his body felt as though it had been beaten with heavy iron hammers.

Slowly, the blinding white spots cleared from his vision. Through his half-closed, soot-stained eyelids, he peered through the wooden railing of the parapet. He looked down into the massive, smoking crater his plasma had carved into the earth directly in front of the gates.

He expected to see nothing but glowing white ash. He had poured every single remaining drop of his power, drawing on the absolute dregs of his life force, into that strike. He had done it to save Josh and Bhel. He had given everything.

Instead, stepping silently and effortlessly out of the swirling, superheated steam, came the nightmare.

They were completely untouched by the apocalyptic inferno. The Wight elite guard marched in perfect, terrifying unison. Their pristine, blackened plate armour actually seemed to absorb the residual heat of the plasma strike, projecting a spectral, freezing aura that chilled Brett to his very marrow even from thirty feet above.

Brett let out a weak, desperate groan, his pale eyes squeezing shut as hot tears of pure frustration and despair leaked onto the wooden floorboards. He had given absolutely everything he had to give, and it had not been enough.

Down in the freezing, glassed mud of the crater, with their backs pressed firmly against the heavy iron portcullis, Josh and Bhel watched the terrifying phalanx advance.

The heat radiating from the baked earth was intense, but it was actively warring against the unnatural, necrotic cold pouring off the approaching undead knights. The clash of temperatures created a thick, swirling grey fog that curled around the heavy steel boots of the monsters.

The Wights did not charge blindly like the lesser ghouls. They halted ten yards away, forming a perfect, impenetrable semi-circle of dark steel, heavy tower shields, and raised greatswords. The bright, piercing pinpricks of icy blue soul-fire burning within their dark visors locked entirely onto the exhausted human and the bleeding dwarf.

Then, the impenetrable wall of dark steel parted slightly.

Only two of the skeletal knights stepped forward. They were massive, towering a full head above Josh. Their heavy plate armour was adorned with rusted, silver crowns, marking them as champions of whatever forgotten kingdom they had once served. They were designated to execute the isolated prey.

They raised their massive greatswords. The dark, rusted metal hummed with a freezing, necrotic energy that caused the moisture in the air to crystallise into falling snow around the blades.

"Two at a time, is it?" Bhel grunted, spitting a mouthful of blood onto the cobblestones. The dwarf tightened his grip on his iron axes, his chest heaving beneath his blood-soaked bandages. "Right polite of them. I was worried we were going to have to share."

"Do not underestimate them, Bhel," Josh warned, his voice a low, heavy rumble.

Josh raised his dark heater shield, ignoring the screaming agony in his left shoulder. He could feel the heavy, sluggish fatigue dragging at his Adamantite-infused bones. He was running on pure adrenaline and stubborn refusal. "They are not mindless beasts. Watch their footwork."

The two Wights closed the distance with an explosive speed. They moved with the trained, lethal grace of master swordsmen, completely unhindered by the heavy steel encasing their bones.

They did not swing wildly. The Wight on the left thrust its heavy greatsword forward in a precise, punishing lunge aimed directly at Josh's throat.

Josh brought his shield up, angling the dark steel to deflect the heavy blade. The impact sounded like a ringing church bell. It was not just physical kinetic force. A wave of spectral, biting cold instantly travelled up Josh's arm, bypassing the steel and the thick leather padding entirely, momentarily numbing his fingers beneath his gauntlet.

Josh gritted his teeth against the freezing pain. He pivoted, using the deflected momentum to sweep his electrified longsword in a brutal counter-attack. The glowing steel struck the Wight's blackened breastplate. Sparks rained down, and the blue lightning flared wildly, but the blade did not cleave cleanly through. The ancient, runic armour held, the dark magic absorbing the worst of the electrical shock.

To his right, Bhel was fighting a desperate, spinning duel.

The dwarf ducked beneath a sweeping horizontal strike that would have cut a normal human completely in half. The heavy blade whistled inches above his helmet. Bhel used his low centre of gravity, driving his left iron axe heavily into the side of the Wight's knee joint. The iron bit deep into the bone, but the monster did not even flinch. It did not feel pain. It simply dropped its heavy steel boot, aiming to crush the dwarf into the mud.

"They are too thick!" Bhel bellowed, rolling frantically away from the crushing stomp. He brought his right axe around in a tight arc to parry a descending blow that sent a shower of sparks into the fog.

Josh stepped in, using his massive bulk to bash his dark shield directly into the side of the Wight attacking Bhel. He threw his entire, steel-clad weight into the blow, throwing the creature slightly off balance and giving the dwarf a fraction of a second to breathe.

The Wight fighting Josh immediately exploited the opening. The creature brought its greatsword down in a punishing overhead cleave. Josh raised his longsword to block, crossing the blade horizontally.

The heavy impact forced Josh to one knee. The freezing cold radiated from the crossed blades, creeping down Josh's arms and chilling his sweat. He felt his muscles locking up, the necrotic energy actively trying to freeze his heart.

"Now, Bhel!" Josh roared, holding the monster locked in the brutal bind.

Bhel did not waste the hard-won opening. The dwarf launched himself off the cobblestones. He ignored the screaming pain in his stitched chest, bringing both of his heavy iron axes down in a brutal, crushing overhead strike directly onto the back of the distracted knight's neck.

The iron blades physically severed the spine. The heavy helmet rolled into the mud, and the glowing blue soul-fire within the armour instantly extinguished. The Wight collapsed into a heap of blackened steel and golden dust.

Josh immediately surged upward, using his freed blade to trigger Adamantine Breach. He dropped his shoulder and slammed his electrified sword directly into the exposed gap beneath the armpit of the remaining Wight.

The raw voltage exploded inside the monster's chest cavity, violently shattering the glowing core hidden deep within the plate armour. The second champion crumbled into the smoking mud.

They stood panting heavily, having successfully put the two champions down. The physical cost had been immense. Josh’s sword arm was trembling violently, and his shield felt as though it were forged from solid lead. Bhel was leaning heavily on his axes, his face pale and drawn. They were completely drained, running on empty fumes.

Then, a sound echoed across the cratered clearing that made the blood freeze entirely in Josh's veins.

It was laughter.

It was a collective, hollow, metallic grating sound echoing from the remaining dozens of Wights standing in the unbroken formation. It was the sound of rusted iron scraping against stone, completely devoid of warmth, empathy, or humanity.

The terrifying realisation crashed over the two fighters. The undead had not sent two champions out of a twisted sense of martial honour. They had sent them simply to toy with their prey. The general commanding them wanted to watch the living struggle, to let them exhaust their final reserves of hope before the final, crushing blow.

Then the metallic laughter ceased instantly.

The entire phalanx stepped forward in perfect, terrifying unison. It was an advancing, solid wall of blackened steel, heavy tower shields, and raised greatswords. There were no more gaps, no weaknesses, and no more duels. They were simply going to march forward and grind the exhausted human and the bleeding dwarf flat against the iron gates.

Perched incredibly high in the swaying, dead branches of the ancient oak tree, Perberos watched the horrific scene unfold through his enhanced vision.

The elven scout’s hands were perfectly steady as he held the heavy elven longbow, but his heart was hammering a frantic, desperate, and terrifying rhythm against his ribs. The psychological torture he was enduring was absolute agony.

He had watched Brett’s apocalyptic pillar of fire illuminate the valley, feeling a massive surge of hope. He had seen the Wights emerge from the smoke entirely unharmed, the hope shattering instantly. He had watched Josh and Bhel fight a desperate, brutal duel in the mud, bleeding and exhausting themselves against impossible odds.

And now, he was currently watching the impenetrable wall of dark steel closing the final few yards to execute his best friends.

Furthermore, he could clearly see the tiny, fragile figure of his twin sister standing on the wooden parapet just thirty feet above that slaughter. Carcan was completely vulnerable, leaning over the railing, helpless to stop the heavy vanguard from crushing her protectors.

Every single protective instinct in his elven body was screaming at him to act. The primal urge to loose his arrow, to fire blindly into the Wight phalanx in a desperate, foolish bid to save them, was almost overwhelming. His fingers twitched against the bowstring. He wanted to rain steel down upon the monsters threatening his family.

But he forced his breathing to slow. He forced his jaw to clench, locking his turbulent emotions away in a cold, dark iron box in his mind.

He remembered the harsh, necessary logic of Ronald's warning. If he fired now and struck a Wight, the towering Bone General stationed deep in the rear lines would instantly react. The boss monster would raise its runic shielding. The assassination would fail, the city would fall, and everyone he loved would die anyway.

He had to trust Josh to hold the line for just a few more seconds. He had to trust his friends to survive the impossible. He had to wait for the signal.

He forced his luminous silver eyes to remain locked entirely on the massive, runic-clad figure of the General.

The wind howled mournfully across the corrupted valley, rattling the dead branches of the oak tree. The tension was a physical, vibrating wire connecting the assassin to his target.

Chirp-kee-ee.

The sound was sharp, incredibly high-pitched, and completely out of place in the rotting wasteland. It was the first note of the mountain osprey call, drifting clearly from the dense brush near the valley floor where Ronald was hiding.

Perberos reacted instantly. The cold, detached persona of the elite sniper completely consumed him.

He drew the thick bowstring smoothly back to his cheek. The heavy, solid-steel armour-piercing bodkin rested perfectly against the riser. He did not simply rely on his immense physical aim. He activated Piercing Sight. His silver eyes began to glow with a faint, magical luminescence that entirely stripped away the visual interference of the swirling smoke, the falling ash, and the driving snow.

He then layered Shadow-Strike onto the arrow. A tight, spinning vortex of dark mana wrapped around the heavy steel shaft, designed to violently bypass and shatter magical barriers upon impact.

He locked his enhanced vision directly onto the narrow, dark slit of the Bone General's massive helmet, completely tuning out the slaughter happening at the city gates.

Chirp-kee-ee.

The second note sounded, slightly higher in pitch and significantly more urgent.

The tension in Perberos's shoulders was absolute. He was a coiled spring, holding enough raw kinetic force in his arms to punch a hole through solid oak. He adjusted his aim slightly, calculating the severe, freezing crosswind sweeping down from the northern hills. He adjusted for the drop, the distance, and the slight sway of the skeletal warhorse the monster rode upon.

Down in the valley, the Bone General suddenly paused.

The massive, towering monstrosity seemed to sense a shift in the ambient magical pressure. It stopped watching the execution unfolding at the gates. It casually, slowly turned its massive, horned skull away from the city walls, looking back towards the eastern ridge where the assassins were hidden.

For a single, breathless fraction of a second, the dark, narrow slit of the creature's visor aligned perfectly with Perberos's line of sight.

Through the smog of swirling smoke and freezing air, the elven scout found himself looking directly into the burning, sickly green vortex of the boss monster's soul-fire.

Then they made eye contact.

Perberos saw the cold, ancient malice burning within the creature. But crucially, he also saw that the General's runic armour was not flared. The magical shielding was entirely dormant, relaxed as the creature watched its army march. The monster did not expect an attack from the rear, and it certainly did not expect a lethal strike from two miles away.

It was the absolute perfect, flawless opening. It was a shot that defied the laws of probability.

Chirp-kee-ee.

The third and final note echoed sharply through the valley.

Perberos did not hesitate for a single microsecond. He relaxed his fingers, letting the bowstring slip smoothly away from his cheek.

The heavy, steel-tipped bodkin vanished from the bow. It did not fly through the air like a normal arrow. It tore through the sky like a streak of dark, silent lightning. It was propelled by incredible physical strength and wrapped in deadly, concentrated shadow magic, racing flawlessly across the corrupted valley towards the glowing green eye of the apocalypse.
