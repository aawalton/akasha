“How... how is she?” Rhea asked.

Lloyd did not answer right away. Instead, he lingered in silence, his gaze drifting back toward the woman lying motionless under the thin blanket. Blonde hair matted dark with sweat, eyes shut as if she were trapped in some unreachable dream, chest rising and falling so faintly she could have been mistaken for lifeless.

“Well...” he finally said, “her spine is still intact, and that’s the good news. She has several broken ribs, but fortunately none of them have punctured any vital organs. I’ve stabilized those injuries, and given time, they should heal naturally without further complications. There were a few bruises here and there, but again, nothing too serious. I’ve treated those as well. Now... the real concern lies with her head. The force of the impact it endured was... severe.”

“No way...” Rhea whispered under her breath.

“Let’s not circle around it,” Alycia said. “Just say it plainly, Lloyd. What exactly is her condition right now? And... when is she going to wake up?”

The man let out a slow exhale. “If you want my honest assessment,” he said, “then the truth is... I don’t know.”

“You don’t know? You are the healer here! If you don’t know, then who possibly could?”

“I am a mage, not a miracle worker!” Lloyd snapped. “Tobias and I have done everything we could. The rest... it’s no longer in our hands.”

“Please... you two...” Rhea murmured as she cast a glance toward the far corner of the room, toward the one who had remained silent the whole time.

Him.

Alycia followed her gaze, and the moment her eyes settled on him, she immediately faltered. “I’m... sorry, Quinn,” she said quietly. “I didn’t mean to—”

“It’s fine,” Viktor said.

Calm. Steady. Untroubled.

“I am alright.”

Of course he was. After all, the woman who was lying unconscious on that bed over there was not his sister. There was no blood tying them together, no childhood memories shared between them. They were, in every practical sense, little more than two strangers who happened to share the same roof for a handful of months.

No matter what had happened to her, there was absolutely no reason for him to be upset.

“I’ll send word to the other Sanctuaries,” Tobias said. “Just because Lloyd and I are out of answers doesn’t mean no one else can offer further help. There are others far more skilled than we are. Yes, a high-ranking member of our Order should be able to treat her injuries.”

But why should they?

The sort of mage who could fix this kind of damage would not waste their talents in places like this. They lived in big cities where wealthy patrons lined up with heavy purses and generous donations. There was no reason for them to answer a call from some backwater town. Tobias, with all his stubborn adherence to the teachings of his Order, was an exception rather than the rule. Most others were not nearly so idealistic.

Of course, there was still the sacred obligation of an Emerald Mage to help anyone in need. But that rule only applied to patients who were physically in front of them. If it was someone far away, well, distance gave a man plenty of convenient excuses.

Rhea, though, grabbed hold of that thin scrap of hope like a drowning woman. “Yes... please,” she said. “We have to save her.”

Suddenly, a knock came at the door.

“Who’s that?” Tobias asked, turning his head.

“It’s Isadora,” a composed voice replied from the other side.

“Come in, then.”

The door opened with a soft creak, admitting two figures into the room. The first was the Guildmaster of Daelin herself, and close behind her followed Calyssa, the Chief Secretary.

Isadora’s gaze went straight to the bed, settling on Claire’s unmoving body, and though her expression did not betray the thoughts she harbored, there was a faint tightening around her eyes that preceded her words.

“How is she?” the woman asked, and Lloyd answered her, repeating everything he had said earlier. Once he finished, she let out a heavy sigh. “I’ll talk with the Emerald Mages within the Guild. And I’ll send a message to Rennald as well, to see if he could dispatch someone.”

She opened her mouth again to speak, but her words died before they could be voiced. Her lips pressed together as she glanced at Viktor, with a complicated look on her face.

“If you have something to ask, or something to discuss, then please, go ahead,” he said. “There’s no need to concern yourself with me. I’m fine.”

Another sigh.

“Very well.” Isadora nodded. “So, what happened?”

“This afternoon,” Tobias began, “I was here, attending to my patients, same as any other day. Then, without warning, that red-haired girl—Jeanne—burst through the door. She was crying, barely able to form coherent words, all the while carrying her friend in her arms.” He glanced at Claire. “We put her on the bed immediately. And, while I examined and treated her injuries, Jeanne rushed out again to fetch other people.”

She had gone to Lloyd’s place first, before making her way to the Guild to spread the news. Rhea, upon hearing it, had come to Viktor, arriving at the house just as he returned from his dungeon. By the time they reached the Sanctuary together, Alycia was already there as well.

“Did Jeanne manage to tell you what exactly happened to Claire?” Calyssa asked.

“What she said was... fragmented, at best,” Alycia said. “But from what I could piece together, she and Claire were simply walking along the street when they were stopped by one of Iskora’s adventurers. He tried to take Jeanne with him. By force, from the sound of it. They resisted, of course, and... this is the result.”

“This is outrageous,” Calyssa said, her face suffused with anger. “How could they possibly think they’d get away with something like this?”

“Calm yourself,” Isadora said. “Where is Jeanne now?”

“She’s in another room,” Rhea replied. “As far as we can tell, she’s not hurt, but... She told us to leave her alone for the time being.”

Isadora nodded. “We will speak with her later, once she’s collected herself.”

“I think there should’ve been other witnesses,” Lloyd said. “This took place in the middle of the street, in broad daylight. Someone must have seen what happened.”

“I’ll send people immediately,” Calyssa said, adjusting her glasses, “to locate anyone who might have been present and gather their testimonies.”

“And then what?” Alycia snorted. “You file a complaint with Iskora and politely ask them to take responsibility? Do you really think that will accomplish anything?”

“No,” Isadora said at once. “We won’t be taking this matter to them. We will submit a petition directly to the Guildmaster’s Conclave.”

Her gaze swept over the others’ confused looks.

“You have to understand that everything they’ve done up to this point has existed in a carefully maintained gray area, on that thin line between what is lawful and what is not. The monster attack? Just a coincidence. Their takeover of the west side? They had the mayor’s approval. Their brutality against the local gangs? Necessary measures to maintain order. But this... this leaves no room for interpretation. An adventurer openly assaulting an employee of the Guild in the middle of the street? There’s no defense for that. And the Conclave is exceptionally strict when it comes to matters like these. At the very least, the individual responsible for the attack will be apprehended and held accountable. And if he resists, the Conclave will place a bounty on his head.”

Well, yes. That was how it worked.

After all, each high-ranking adventurer was basically a one-man army, so if they were free to act without restraint against others, especially against civilians who had no means of defending themselves, it would lead to complete chaos, and the entire system would immediately collapse. So of course, the Conclave had to be uncompromising, and unforgiving, about such violations. That was the very reason it had been founded in the first place.

“Is that so?” Alycia asked, still skeptical.

“It is,” Isadora said. “By the way, fighting between adventurers is also strictly forbidden. For example, an adventurer attempting to incinerate someone with her flying constructs would qualify as a serious offense, and warrant the most severe punishment the Guild could impose.”

“Is... that so?” the blonde asked with a nervous laugh.

“Well, in cases where no significant harm is done and both parties agree not to escalate the matter, the Guild will not intervene.” Isadora took a breath. “But this is not such a case. The damage is real, and we won’t stop until the offender receives the consequences he deserves.”

“What if... they resist?” Rhea asked. “They have at least two Mithril-ranked adventurers with them. If it turns into a confrontation...”

“This is not a matter of ‘them versus us.’ It’s ‘them versus the Conclave.’ The more they fight back, the worse it becomes for them. Mithril-ranked adventurers are powerful, but they’re not the top. Above them, there are Adamantite. And then... there’s the Judge.”

Rhea blinked. “The Judge?”

“I’ve heard of him,” Lloyd said. “A myth. A legend. He’s said to be the secret enforcer of the Conclave, and the reason why no bounty on a rogue adventurer has ever remained active for more than a year. That’s the time limit he allows for other hunters to collect their bounty, and for the condemned to surrender themselves voluntarily. But if they continue to evade capture beyond that period, then exactly one year after the bounty is issued, he appears before them, and carries out the sentence personally. He is the judge, and the executioner. That’s the story.” He turned to Isadora. “Don’t tell me he is real.”

The Guildmaster nodded. “He is.”

So there’s someone like that, huh?

It made sense, though. The system could not function alone on good intentions and politely phrased reprimands. Rules were only as effective as the consequences that followed. So there had to be something behind the Conclave’s decrees, an ultimate mechanism of correction.

Three hundred years ago, Viktor himself served as that final resort. After all, he was the one who had created the organization. Defying it was defying him, and that fact by itself was enough to deter most misconduct. Now, however, the Conclave no longer had the backing of the Emperor, so someone else had to fill the gap.

Is this “Judge” also an Ascendant? he wondered.

“Anyway,” Isadora said, “that’s our course of action.” She then walked over to Viktor, placing a hand on his shoulder. “Don’t worry. We’ll make sure your sister gets her justice.”

To that, he gave a disinterested nod. “Thank you.”
