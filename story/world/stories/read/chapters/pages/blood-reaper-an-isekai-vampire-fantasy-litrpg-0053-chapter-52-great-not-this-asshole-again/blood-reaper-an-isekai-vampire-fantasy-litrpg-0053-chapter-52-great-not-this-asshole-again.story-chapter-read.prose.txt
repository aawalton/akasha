The twilight cast long shadows across the mouth of their new cave, painting the rough-hewn walls in shifting patterns of purple and black. The Highlands had a slew of caves littered across the landscape, which worked out in their favor.

Ace sat with his back against the cool stone and his arms crossed over his chest. Not far off, Olivia stood at the cave's entrance. Her silhouette was sharp against the fading light as she kept watch. She glanced back at him, her crimson eyes lingering longer than necessary, and the corner of her mouth tilted upward into a sultry smile.

Ever since he had killed Victor, she seemed more interested in him than ever.

“Perimeter's clear,” she said, her voice carrying a melodic quality to it. “We should be safe here until morning.”

“Thanks,” Ace said, massaging his shoulder. “Wake me in a few hours, will you?”

Her smile faltered, and though she watched him for a moment more, she eventually turned her back on him and studied the land beyond their cave.

He took that as a “yes.”

Ace blinked, his eyelids heavy with exhaustion—but when he opened them again, he wasn't alone.

A familiar man sat against the opposite wall of the cave, mirroring his posture perfectly—one knee bent, his arms crossed over his chest. He wore dark leather armor that absorbed what little light reached this place, making it difficult to gauge where the fabric ended and the shadows began.

Darkmoor.

Ace instinctively summoned a Duskblade, and his eyes narrowed with unspoken warning.

“She can't see me,” Darkmoor said, nodding toward Olivia before Ace could speak. “None of them can. Only you.”

“Lucky me,” Ace muttered under his breath.

He blinked again—and this time, the world around him shifted.

The cave was gone.

The twilight was gone.

Olivia was gone.

He and Darkmoor sat in identical positions, but their backs now rested against the walls of what appeared to be a temple ruin. Crumbling pillars rose toward a ceiling that no longer existed, opening directly to a starless sky with no moon. The air felt thicker here, heavy with potential, and shadows wriggled around them—not simply in the absence of light, but as something alive and aware.

The Shadow Realm.

“What do you want, Darkmoor?” Ace asked flatly. “Another challenge? Another stupid test?”

The echo of a legendary man grinned. “Hello to you as well.”

Smart ass.

Darkmoor gestured to the space around them. “We have much to discuss, and little time to do it.”

“Do we?” Ace asked dubiously. He didn’t move, nor did his eyes leave the spectral figure who had yet again drawn him into this world between worlds. “You've been watching me every time I enter the Shadow Realm, so what do you want now?”

“I have a proposition for you,” the man said casually.

Ace frowned, not entirely sure he liked the direction this conversation was headed. He had enough to deal with each time the System popped into existence to terrorize him and his team. The last thing he needed was another supernatural powerhouse trying to turn him into a puppet.

Darkmoor pushed himself to his feet and clapped his hands together to rid them of the dusty ground. “What you did back there, with the Behemoth and that man Victor—it impressed me. It proved to me that you have what it takes to survive this place.”

“And that is?”

“Brutality.” Darkmoor’s crimson glare shifted toward Ace, and the man smirked. “You have it in abundance.”

Ace didn’t reply.

“And,” Darkmoor added, his voice dropping to something just above a whisper. “She's finally stopped watching you long enough for us to talk.”

“How can you tell?” Ace probed.

“It’s a remnant of our connection.” Darkmoor slowly paced what was left of the wide temple corridor. He brushed his hand across the worn stone, pausing briefly to inspect the dust that gathered on his finger. “The System and I are linked.”

“Why?”

“Because she killed me,” the man said flatly. “And because I cursed her.”

“Everyone here is insane,” Ace muttered under his breath.

To his surprise, Darkmoor laughed.

“You get used to it,” the man said.

Ace stood, not bothering to mask his annoyance. “As much fun as our little chats are, I have things to do. Let’s cut to the chase. What do you want from me?”

“Answers.”

“Join the club,” Ace said dubiously.

“You've been Marked by someone in the Shadow Realm,” Darkmoor continued, as though Ace hadn’t said anything. “Someone powerful.”

Ace’s brows furrowed in confusion for a moment before he pieced it together—Darkmoor was talking about the entity that had driven its hand clear through Ace’s chest the last time he was in the Shadow Realm.

In those moments before he’d killed Victor.

“Ah.” Darkmoor studied his face, seeing something in his features that he hadn’t meant to give away. “You know what I’m talking about. What do you know about him?”

“Nothing.”

“Don’t lie,” Darkmoor said, his voice lowering an octave.

“I’m not,” Ace said flatly. “And in the mood for this. I’m sick of your games and cryptic bullshit. You want answers from me?”

Darkmoor nodded.

“Then you’ve got to give me some first.”

The man frowned, his eyes narrowing in an unspoken warning. He stared at Ace for a moment, the shadows rippling around him like agitated snakes.

“You soldiers are so stubborn,” Darkmoor finally said. “I forgot how tedious military men can be.”

“Sorry my survival instincts are an inconvenience to your agenda.”

A begrudging smile crossed Darkmoor's face. “I suppose I wouldn't respect you if you were easily manipulated. Very well. How about an exchange, then? Information for information. Like the civilized monsters we are.”

“You first,” Ace replied with a sarcastic tilt of his head. “I insist.”

“How gracious.” Darkmoor feigned a bow with a theatrical flourish. “Ask away.”

“You said I’m Marked,” Ace started. “Who Marked me? You?”

“No,” Darkmoor answered. “But that answers one of my questions. You don’t know who it is.”

The man sighed with relief, which only made Ace more curious.

“The Mark comes from something else,” Darkmoor continued. “Something more powerful than me. Something—or rather, someone—that exists only in the Shadow Realm.”

“You can do better than that,” Ace pressed. “Name? Rank? Interdimensional serial number?”

Darkmoor frowned. “That's not something you need to know yet.”

Ace just raised one skeptical eyebrow.

“You’re not ready,” Darkmoor insisted. “Connecting with this entity before you’re strong enough to resist him will get you killed, or worse—converted by the shadows he controls.”

Ace crossed his arms over his chest and stood taller. “I've had enough of you mystery tour guides in this nightmare world. If you’re not going to give me real answers, then we’re done.”

Darkmoor stiffened.

“Suit yourself.” Ace shrugged and turned his back on the man. “Next time, don’t interrupt my nap.”

“Wait.”

With his back still to Darkmoor, Ace paused mid-stride and smirked in victory.

“Yes?” he asked, tilting his head until he could see his opponent in his periphery.

“I’ll make you a deal,” Darkmoor offered.

Ace didn’t move, nor did he reply. He simply stood there, watching the man through the corner of his eye as he waited for whatever came next.

“I will tell you,” Darkmoor promised. “But only if you obtain something for me when you go into the Crucible. Something important.”

“Are you serious?” Ace asked incredulously. “Didn’t you design the Crucible? Why would you need me to get you something from your own labyrinth?”

“Because I can no longer enter it,” Darkmoor said with a broad gesture to his own body. “I’m an echo, not a man. There are places I cannot go.”

“Sounds like you need me more than I need you.”

Darkmoor scoffed. “You’ve only gotten a taste of this world, Blackwell. You have no idea what’s out there, ready to eat you. You have no idea what’s at play beyond what you can see. You have no idea what sort of powerful entities are watching you, ready to make you dance for them.”

“I’m no one’s puppet,” Ace said flatly.

“Then they’ll kill you,” Darkmoor said with an apathetic shrug. “There are no friends in this world, Blackwell. Only enemies or allies—and, often, your allies will turn on you when you’re no longer useful.”

Ace’s gaze drifted to the ground as he thought of Tara.

“You’re wrong,” he said softly.

“We’ll see.”

“Does that mean you’re going to turn on me?” Ace asked in a gravelly voice. “When I’m no longer ‘useful’ to you?”

Darkmoor went still, and a muscle twitched in his jaw. “No.”

Ace laughed mirthlessly. “Right. Of course.”

“I can’t hurt you, Blackwell,” Darkmoor said. “I’m an echo. A ghost. All I can do is guide you. All I can do is help.”

Ace wasn’t so sure, but this time, he kept his thoughts to himself. He was tempted to leave, but he wanted more information. To get that, he needed to bluff his way through this conversation.

“Fine,” Ace continued with a casual shrug, doing his best to play the part of an apathetic observer. “Let’s say I am wrong. Let’s say I need you to give me all the juicy details about this place. Why approach me specifically? I’m sure it’s not because of my charming personality.”

“Hardly,” Darkmoor said under his breath. “You’re being a pain in the ass, but your affinity for shadow manipulation is impressive—and unusual—for your level. Your instincts mirror my own when I was first starting out. And,” his voice softened slightly, “I sense something in you that resists her corruption. Something I haven't seen in any other summoned hero.”

Ace stiffened. “Is she the one who Marked me, then?”

“No,” Darkmoor said with a dismissive wave of his hand. “But I know she can sense that something’s different about you.”

“Lovely,” Ace said dryly.

“That’s why I need you to get something for me once you go into the Crucible,” Darkmoor continued. “It’s something I hid there to keep it out of her clutches, and I need someone who can resist her… charms, for lack of a better word.”

“What is it?”

Darkmoor sighed in resignation and rubbed his eyes. “An amulet with incredible power. Something that she’s been hunting for centuries, and something she would murder everyone on Floor 1 to acquire. The only reason she’s not telling you to fetch it for her is because she doesn’t know where I hid it.”

Ace narrowed his eyes in suspicion. “You don’t even know me. Why would you trust me with something that valuable?”

For a moment, Darkmoor didn’t reply. He simply studied Ace, glaring at him over the bridge of his nose.

“I don’t,” he eventually confessed.

“Sounds like you’re wasting my time, then,” Ace pointed out. “That, or you’re desperate.”

At that, Darkmoor abruptly looked away, and his hands balled into fists at his side.

Ace’s eyebrows shot up his forehead, and he paused for a moment as he pieced it together. “You really are desperate, aren’t you?”

Darkmoor growled under his breath, and for a moment, he didn’t reply.

“We’re running out of time,” the man eventually said, his voice dark and quiet. “It’s been decades. Decades of waiting. Decades of wondering if the one who can kill her already came and went. Decades of debating with myself each time a new batch of heroes arrived, trying to decide if one of them was strong enough to face her. And all this time, I’ve been wrong. Everyone I chose, everyone I reached out to—they all died horribly.”

“You’re not really selling this whole alliance thing of yours,” Ace confessed.

“I don’t have a choice,” Darkmoor ran his hand through his hair and paced once more, walking the full length of the corridor as the words spilled out of him. “I’ve tried tricks. I’ve tried cons. I’ve tried tests. Being honest is the only thing left to try, and at this point, it might be too late. The one I summoned might be dead, or worse—corrupted.”

“Wait, wait.” Ace raised one hand to get the man to slow down. “Summoned? Why would you summon someone to this hellhole?”

It was a dick move, after all.

“It was my only choice,” Darkmoor answered, still pacing as he spoke. “It was the only way to kill her.”

“Who?”

“The System.”

At that, Ace balked. “Didn’t she build this place? How can you kill the creator?”

“Because she might not be the creator,” Darkmoor answered.

For a moment, Ace couldn’t speak. The idea seemed too far-fetched, too impossible, given all the power she had at her command. His thoughts raced backward through every interaction, every cruel game, every offhand remark she had made. All those moments when she'd presented herself as the master of this twisted realm.

“How is that possible?” Ace finally asked.

“I didn’t believe it at first, either,” Darkmoor confessed. “But something about her was always… I don’t know, off. To figure out what it was, I played her game. It wasn’t like I had much of a choice, after all, so I leveled as high as I could. It took centuries of fighting, killing, evolving, but I got strong. And, in my travels, I discovered her ultimate goal.”

“And that is?”

Darkmoor paused, looking out over the ruined landscape of the Shadow Realm. “She's been absorbing worlds into her domain. There’s something special about your homeworld that’s going to complete something she started a long time ago. I don’t know why, but Earth is next—and she’s going to destroy it.”

The words hit Ace like a physical blow to the chest.

“How?” Ace finally managed to say. “Why Earth?”

“I don’t know,” Darkmoor confessed.

“But…” Ace trailed off, trying to process everything as it hit him. “How would she absorb worlds? What happens to the people?”

“The people become her playthings. Her summoned heroes. Her monsters.” Darkmoor's voice dropped lower. “Her food.”

A cold weight settled in the pit of Ace's stomach. All those people back on Earth. His parents. His fellow Marines. Everyone he'd ever known—they were all livestock awaiting slaughter if Darkmoor was telling the truth.

But that was a very big if.
