Chapter 142: The Grand Reveal

The young woman winced and appeared mortified at Highfive’s loud proclamation. Nonetheless, she waved. “H-hey, Fiver.”

Naturally, Highfive tried to run up and hug her, but Permafrost threw her hands up and backed away, loudly calling, “No, Fiver, no! Stop! No jossling! I’ve got a headache!”

Highfive was stopped short by the last, though he laughed again with his hands out, a happy, amazed grin still painted on his face. He practically danced in place. “We’re gonna be on a mission together!”

“Yeah, so it seems…”

Jack stood up too, as her name was dropped, feeling a bit stunned. He walked over. Smiling a bit sheepishly, he said, “So it is you. Welcome aboard.”

Permafrost straightened once she convinced herself Highfive wouldn’t glomp her. She turned to Jack and raised an eyebrow. “Not going to bother denying you didn’t recognize me, eh?”

“Well, it’s hardly your twin when you’re running around as the ice sculpture.”

She snorted as she nodded sarcastically, then rubbed a hand on her forehead, finally looking away and sighing. “I couldn’t use my powers if I wanted to right now. I strained them a bit recently.” There was a long pause as she dropped her hand and glanced at the two in front of her, smiling faintly. “But if I hadn’t, I wouldn’t have been recalled and been here to join this with you guys!” She rocked up on her toes briefly and shrugged. “Silver linings. At least I know I’m not gonna get sidelined for a long time.”

Jack nodded, even as he saw the cracks in her countenance, suggesting something harrowing had happened. “I suppose that means we aren’t leaving immediately, regardless.”

“Perhaps not. Anyway! I’ll be alright. Go take your seat. No need to get up on my account. Trust me, I’m right behind you in parking my rear. Though maybe that’s a bad idea because I’m tired? Oh well.”

Jack shrugged and opened his mouth, then caught sight of Lindsay coming in. “Huh.”

“Nice! Jack’s other girlfriend is here,” Highfive declared, winking at Jack.

“Shut up, Blondy,” Jack replied distractedly.

Permafrost snickered. “Worried, are we? That one isn’t coming physically, Jack, believe me. She operates remotely, remember? Way more remotely than me. Usually as an emergency ass-saver. She comes to almost all of these away mission briefings. A busy, busy girl.”

They all exchanged waves, though Lindsay almost immediately bee-lined to speak with Bermuda.

“So, who’s your girlfriend, Jack?” Permafrost asked. “I bet she’s super pretty, like you.”

Jack just glowered at her rather than replying, which made her grin impishly.

Eh, well, anything to improve a friend’s morale, I guess.

Bermuda called, “Please start taking your seats if you haven’t. Some people are taking longer, and we don’t want a mad dash right as the buzzer sounds.”

Almost everyone took their seats at that point, including Jack. Jetset was lingering next to Ira, chatting her up, as Jack caught them mid-conversation.

Ira nodded up to Jetset at something he said. “I hope our journey is a smoother one than that, in any case.”

Jetset grinned. “Ah, so you’re actually going! Interesting. I look forward to hearing your role for this.”

“Let’s just say you’ll be quite surprised!”

Jetset noted Jack’s arrival and got out of his way, even as he chuckled and rubbed his chin at Ira’s comment. “Color me intrigued. We’ve got a live one here, Ferro. A true mystery lady on the scene.”

Jack smiled knowingly at Ira before shrugging at Jetset, just as he sat down. “Not to me, she’s not.”

Ira looked upward, in playful, false surreptitiousness.

Jetset grinned as he looked between them, doing a slow nod. “Ah, I see, I see, okay, context exists, nice. The intrigue deepens. Guess I’ll sit down and wait” — he pointed his finger and flicked it at both of them — “with anticipation!” He finished with a wink before sauntering back to his seat.

Jack exchanged a glance with Ira, both of them slightly amused by the exchange.

Through their mental bond, Ira said, “I think he might’ve been flirting, but I’m not sure.”

“Oh, I’m sure,” Jack replied. “It might not be obvious in his words, but it was in his demeanor, and I’m pretty sure that’s his nature.”

“Ah! Noted. Perhaps we should declare that we are dating? I have not gleaned how this is done among Skyhumans, however. It seems haphazard.”

“Just declaring it out of nowhere would be odd. Usually, it just comes up naturally.”

“Yes. Haphazard.”

Jack grinned to himself. “And how would it be done among your people?”

The question seemed to paralyze her. He saw her eyes widen slightly. “Er, well… you do need to know some things! But perhaps, ah, not here, not now — later!”

Jack was exceptionally amused as well as intrigued, now. He raised his eyebrows at her smugly, to which she slumped her shoulders slightly and gave him an almost glum glower.

“You two are sickeningly adorable,” InSite offered, shaking his head helplessly.

Just then, Highfive called out loudly from his seat, “Booooo!” while throwing up his hands in celebration.

Indeed, Bo rolled in, looking rather sharp and professional.

“Oh, thank the stars,” Jack muttered in relief, even as he found the look a bit off-putting.

Bo looked between them in puzzlement. “Did you guys think I hung myself or something?”

“They were worried you wouldn’t make it on the mission team,” Lighthouse replied.

“And you?”

Lighthouse smiled politically. “I had every confidence in you.”

“I bet.” She didn’t seem displeased, regardless, as she moved directly to take a seat next to her friend.

A few more came through the doors in a rush — with a minute left to go on the clock — including a veteran ANP with blue-grey skin.

An oceanic specialist, maybe? The color works.

Bermuda finally moved to her chair at this point, though still standing, declaring, “That’s everyone, then. Get settled, and-”

“I’m sorry,” Conflux called as she rose out of her chair, face confused and stricken, “but there must be some mistake, because the rest of our team isn’t here. We-”

“There’s no mistake, Agent Flux. You and Snapper have specialized abilities and skills needed for a mission with tight membership in a time where we’re stretched thin, and they don’t. You’ll understand once you hear the mission specs and other key member abilities. Now sit down and shut up — that’s an order.”

Conflux, eyes wide, face white, promptly fell back in her chair, nodding, even as she swallowed a lump in her throat. “Yes, ma’am.”

Bermuda swept cold eyes that brooked no argument over the rest of them. “Many of you are new to the jacket. Let me reinforce that you are not cadets anymore, and there are no kiddie gloves we can afford to wear for you from now on. You could die on this mission. Don’t frag around, and heed commands like your lives depend on it, because they do. Tell me you understand, Agentus.”

A chorus, vaguely irregular in timing, rang out: “We understand you, ma’am!”

“Perfect.” She glanced at the time, now ticking down to nothing, and finally sat down.

Once it struck zero, Memoria herself came through a door, flickering her hands and smiling. “Well, look at this handsome gathering!”

The majority of people stood immediately from their seats and saluted. Jack and Ira were the slowest of those who did rise. A handful didn’t rise at all, including Bermuda, Lindsay, and a couple of other Central Processing types. Lindsay seemed a bit amused at the reaction.

Memoria put her hands on her hips and gave the collective a playful look of admonishment. “Here, now, children — we’re not so formal at a gathering like this, are we?! At ease, and sit down, please, everyone.” Once everyone re-settled, Memoria nodded and put her hands behind her back. “I’m here to lend the stamp of authority on the gravity, importance, and sensitivity of this mission. Everyone was chosen rather carefully for overlapping versatility in a dangerous and unknown area; mostly unknown, anyway. Now, the mission could be smooth sailing from beginning to end, so let's hope for that.”

She sighed and walked over to an empty spot at the table next to Bermuda, dribbling her fingertip on the polished surface. “There are just data points suggesting we’ll need extensive capability, if not for the primary mission, then for the nebulous follow-up.”

Bermuda nodded and gestured toward the center of the table. “Let’s get into it.” The hologram blipped into resolution, showing a high aerial view of a mostly oceanic landscape that Jack’s geographically-saturated brain recognized even before Bermuda called it out. “The Calm, aka the Weddell Sea, framed by mainland Antarctica and Palmer Island, which is just beyond the Archontic borders of our civilization. The sea is an essential resource, the only coastline not battered by hellish storms and saturated with nasty oceanic monsters. It almost seems heaven-sent in its oddity, an oddity that has puzzled us more with each passing decade of use, observation, and exploration.”

The scene suddenly shifted to underneath the water, showing a few flashes of wildlife like fish, dolphins… and octopi.

Jack met Memoria’s eyes and raised a brow, getting a faint grin and a shrug in response.

Foreshadowing, perhaps?

“We’ve always known there was something strange lurking in the depths,” Bermuda continued, “but even our rare water specialists had trouble with their senses past a certain depth, to say nothing of technological failures. Light dies in the abyss, to put it plainly. All investigations were doomed to mishap and inevitably aborted. We never even identified the true borders of the null zone, as they shifted like the wind.

“For this reason, among others, we doubted this was actually an oceanic Archon. There was some debate due to various strange anomalies occurring here and there. A few had theories about an entity that specialized in stealth, but the lack of aggression remained puzzling, even with evidence that ocean and land Archons are entirely specialized, unable to seize each other’s territory, and territorial boundaries even able to overlap.

“We’ve dealt with plenty of direct aggression from strange beings elsewhere, and even seen them fighting each other. Meanwhile, the Calm remains like a seemingly miraculous sanctuary — one we’d likely not have decent harbors or exploratory ships without. It is our ‘shallow side of the pool,’ easing us back into the wild, blue yonder once conquered by our distant predecessors.”

Ira shifted a bit at all of this, grinning faintly. Jack could feel a bit of pride — even a bit of bubbly excitement — through the bond.

I bet before coming here, she didn’t really know how much the Calm helped us out. She’s proud that her people and Archon were facilitators of this.

Bermuda paused here, eyes shifting over the agents present before continuing soberly, “We now know absolutely that there is an Archon down there, and there is a sapient people, a PM, and a civilization carved out of the abyss, with structures in various rocky seabeds.”

Gasps resounded around the table, along with a gaggle of amazed exclamations and blurted-out questions.

Somewhere in the mix, one of the veterans, a tough-looking woman with something of a mohawk braid and wearing shades, asked calmly, “How do we know this?”

Bermuda held up her hand for quiet, but it was Memoria who turned to the woman and said, “You’re familiar with the Station Argus Waste Center Incident, Lattice? And the Eden Extraction Incident?”

Lattice slowly turned to Memoria and took off her shades, which revealed eyes that looked as if full of glowing blue crystal. Her mouth hung open briefly before she muttered, “Holy unholy shit…”

Ah, she has good taste in curses, I see.

Memoria grinned at her and turned to the table at large. “We have contact, my children, and let me assure you, in the ensuing days since, the verification of this contact being one not of an enemy, but of a friend and potential ally, is complete and total.”

Total shock was on the faces of many gathered at the table.

“Holy unholy shit,” Lattice repeated.

Jetset whistled, winced, and shook his head. “Maaaan, I dunno, this is damned suspicious timing with the Dreamer’s bullshit…”

Bermuda met his eyes and replied, “We believe the Dreamer is pushing its timetable specifically due to sensing the existence of this entity here. In any case, that’s irrelevant, and I can corroborate Central’s consensus in support of Memoria’s assessment. It is, in fact, Central’s assessment as a whole.”

“I corroborate and agree personally,” Lindsay added, “as does every specialist involved or consulted. Some exercise cautious optimism, mind you. This included the PM.”

A silence persisted for a moment. Another veteran, perhaps the age of Jetset with the aura of a fatherly doctor or scientist, clapped his hands together and exclaimed with cheerfulness, “This is momentous news, Agentus! Let it sink in: we are not alone, we are not without friends out there.” His voice and accent were all high education — Origin-born for sure. “I’m dying to know more, Mother, even a little hurt that I didn’t already know. Am I not your favorite optimist?”

Memoria gave him an apologetic smile. “Sorry about that, Solvent. Your project was too important to distract you from, and I needed it done as well as you here, for this, where you’re even more indispensable. It was a practical call.”

Solvent smiled and bowed his head in rather saintly understanding. “Of course, Mother. But now? Spill the bloody beans already!”

Memoria chuckled and shrugged. “Very well, son — just for you, of course. To put it plainly, the aforementioned incidents involved their members coming to us for help, but not properly understanding how violently we react to such surprises as aliens showing up on our doorstep — especially when the greeters are gungho quasi-independents with admittedly too much firepower. Only one foreigner survived and was taken outside of my knowledge, to be interrogated by those seeking high-value answers and intel, with the intent to sell. Fortunately, those touched by the Dreamer within these groups didn’t catch on properly. With the second incident, the agent made contact in a quite dramatic fashion.”

Awe continued to persist around the table.

“Incredible,” Permafrost muttered, rapt with attention, even as she leaned over the table with a hand on her forehead.

“You can say that again,” Snapper said, shaking his head. “I can tell I’m missing a lot of information to get briefed on before I can understand it.”

“Yes,” Bermuda replied, “some of you are a bit behind, but we decided to get this whole reveal out of the way first.”

Jack shifted a bit uncomfortably, and Ira was wincing and wringing a hand, obviously nervous about what was to follow.

Lighthouse was frowning between Memoria, Jack, Ira, and the smug-looking InSite, whose expression made it clear without speaking that he knew everything. Her eyes suddenly widened as something clicked. “Wait-”

Sato slapped the table with a hand and pointed at Jack with a wry ghost of a smile. “You sonuvabitch! You’re at the damn center of all of this! Powers ten years late! Damn. Knew it was fishy. It’s all making sense now, but I find myself believing something too good to be true.”

Jack shrugged at him helplessly, glancing at Memoria and saying nothing.

“I don’t get it,” Highfive said. “I hope someone is going to explain it.”

Bo, frowning, shook her head. “For once, I’m about as clueless as you, Fiver.”

Lighthouse had a stricken, blanched expression as she staaaared at Ira. Suddenly, she shot up on her feet and pointed. “You’re her! You’re-”

“Sit down, Agent Light!” Bermuda commanded firmly, almost shouting. “And don’t continue that train of thought, either. Nice work, but let us handle it from here, please? The same goes for you, Agent Sato.”

“Roger that, ma’am,” Sato said automatically.

Lighthouse gulped and plopped back down, nodding to Bermuda.

Meanwhile, Ira was staring at the table, looking pensive.

Memoria held up her hands. “Calm yourselves, everyone. Ira? Go ahead, dear. It’s time. Finally time, hmm? It’s a good thing.” She smiled and gestured with a hand supportively.

Ira’s eyes shot over to Memoria, and she gulped much like Lighthouse did, while nodding deliberately. She also glanced at Jack.

He nodded in confident support and sent the same through their bond, while also sending mentally, “You got this, Octobeauty.”

Ira grinned briefly, then took a deep breath and rose from her chair, turning to the table at large with a now solemn expression. Her poise got stronger as she spoke. “The Archon of which Memoria speaks is called Quallakuloth, and desires nothing more than cordial relations with your people and your Archon, and even something more, which I must translate somewhat inaccurately, but to you all, could be denoted as ‘friendship’ and ‘alliance.’ Another word would denote its desire between the peoples of New Babylon and the people of Deucalia under the sea: bonding.”

She smiled suddenly, brightly, as her gaze went to Bo, and then to Highfive. “I’ve forged many myself, with those I’d like to keep calling friends into a long and fruitful future together, if they’ll still have me.” Her eyes went to Jack, and her smile deepened. “And I’ve forged even stronger things, knowing the brilliance that it has alchemized deep in my heart, as sure as any truth I’ve ever known.”

Jack marveled, not only at her publicly personal words, thick with that bountiful love she had within her, but also at her amplifying grace as she spoke.

She really is a princess. Am I lucky, or what?

‘Ira’ turned back to the table. “My faith is supreme that these bonds are more than mere potential mutual benefits, but essential for our growth and the restoration of our mutual home, this, our beloved Earth. Today, tomorrow, we walk as microcosms of the coming fate of our world, and we owe the future all that we have. Daunting as that is, we can do it because we have each other. Because we can do it together.”

Smiling briefly, she took another deep breath. “Now then… the practical revelation… I must confess that it is through me, and my bond, that Jack now possesses powers, though I assure you it is entirely routed back through Memoria, as it was functionally the repair of a bond between them. Her words to me: ‘Thank you for giving me back my son’s wings, so that he can finally fly as he was meant to.’ I am narrowly avoiding echoed hysterics about that, right at this moment.” Indeed, her lip quivered briefly as she continued blinking.

Memoria smiled warmly, nodding in further encouragement.

Ira looked down at the table. “Here is the final admittance, which I will show you unequivocally." Her body then changed, going from human to her beautiful blue self, her hair revealing the greatest lie as the false locks became tentacles that wriggled and undulated showily.

They’re such show-offs.

There were gasps and hands held to mouths, but mostly, there was shocked silence and stares.

Proudly, the ‘octogirl’ lifted her head and declared, “I am Neexolei, and I am a Deucalian. I greet the Agentus and all the Skyhumans of New Babylon in peace and in love. You may call me Neex, as most of your kind prefer and even seem to find fun.”
