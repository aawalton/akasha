Calr

Calr sat a little apart from the others, turning one of the clam shells over in his hands.

It was smooth and faintly warm from the sun, its surface banded with soft colors that caught the light when he tilted it. He rubbed his thumb along the edge, testing its weight, pretending to evaluate it like a professional.

Was it worth bringing back?

Probably not. A few copper, maybe a full bronze if he found the right buyer. Not worth the trouble of carrying it, not worth the time it would take to sell. Definitely not worth bothering people like Raik or Ja’a about it. Even Alice would not care.

He exhaled and stared harder at the shell.

He was lying to himself. He knew exactly why he had turned his back on the others. With an effort of will, he refused to look over his shoulder again, to let his eyes drift on their own, just like they had before. He still could not believe she had caught him staring.

He was usually subtler than that.

But the way the water clung to her, darkening the silk under-armor, outlining every curve as she moved, had short-circuited something in his brain. His eyes had betrayed him before his good sense could intervene.

Having a perfectly rational mind and a hormonal body was deeply unfair.

Sometimes it felt like thinking with two heads.

And they wanted very different things.

Calr shook his head sharply, enough to make his vision blur for a moment.

Focus.

The shell. Think about the shell.

If he had a bag of holding, he would not even be hesitating. He would have scooped up everything on instinct. Skeleton femurs, troll teeth, wood saturated with death mana, black lilies, ghost ectoplasm residue, and now clam shells. They had left at least a gold’s worth of salvage uncollected since they started traveling together.

He could not carry everything, and only the rich carried bags of holding.

People like Raik, Ja’a, and Alice.

People who did not need to care about copper or bronze.

He rolled the shell between his palms, sighing quietly. Even if he did take it, who would buy it? The time investment alone made it pointless.

He considered asking Shingo to carry it in his larger pack. Shingo would not complain. Shingo never complained.

Was he being greedy? They had found pearls worth at least two gold coins. His share alone was enough to pay back Raik’s initial financial investment and still garner some profit.

Still, he needed more money, and fast. His sister Sara was in her prime marrying age, and it was his duty as her only living relative to provide her with a decent bride price. She had started moving in noble circles, and she needed the money to avoid being beholden to any noble husband. Now, if only he could make a hundred gold coins in a year.

That silly thought was interrupted by the sound of giggles behind him.

Soft, warm, and familiar in an uncanny way.

“Oh, Calr,” said Alice’s voice. “You’ve been very naughty today, staring at my chest.”

He knew he was caught, but he never expected her to confront him about it.

He turned.

Alice stood behind him, dry and clean, and her hair perfect. Her expression was playful in a way that made his pulse jump. Ja’a was beside her, eyes half-lidded, smiling with open amusement.

“Calr, you were so smart today,” Ja’a said, her voice low and approving. “A good boy deserves a reward.”

“I saw him first,” Alice said lightly.

“No, I saw him first,” Ja’a shot back.

Dream Alice laughed. “Fine. I wouldn’t mind sharing.”

Calr swallowed. He could tell he was dreaming. Of course he was.

He stared at them, his mind lagging behind his body for a heartbeat too long. Alice’s figure was… off. Subtly exaggerated. Her hips were fuller than Kuru’s, her chest more pronounced than Vena’s, and her posture was far too deliberately inviting.

His first instinct was guilt.

Guilt for thinking like this, for imagining his friends this way, and for letting his mind wander so far. Even if he could not control his dreams, they still made him feel guilty.

Then the second thought landed.

This was not a dream.

Not his dream, at least.

He froze.

Dreams were messy, emotional, and impressionistic. His mind might conjure a sexier Alice after seeing her in wet silk, sure. But it would not do this.

It would not be sloppy.

His eyes dropped to the ground.

The shadows were wrong.

The angle did not match the sun’s position. The length was incorrect for the latitude, for the time of day, and for the way the blue sun moved across the sky. The edges were blurred where they should have been sharp.

An amateur mistake. That was how a shadow would look somewhere farther north.

His analytical mind recoiled in offense. No matter how distracted, no matter how compromised by hormones, his brain would never fabricate a shadow that bad.

Understanding snapped into place.

This was a Dreamer attack. Someone was using the Sleepless Father's gift on him.

Putting strange thoughts inside his head. Projecting, manipulating, and probing for weakness.

Somehow, they had chosen temptation as their tool… how rude.

Calr’s expression went slack.

If this thing wanted him pliable, he would give it pliable.

He let his shoulders sag, his breathing slow, his gaze unfocused as if the dream was working. He placed himself in a sleeping position, making sure to completely relax before he bit his tongue.

He anchored himself in sensation. The grit of sand against his skin. The ache in his calves. The faint salt sting on his lips. Reality, sharp and unyielding.

The voices cut off mid-breath. The world collapsed inward with a soundless snap as he rejected the projection.

The dream fractured.

Calr made sure not to jerk awake, keeping his face pressed into real sand, his heart hammering so hard it hurt.

He forced himself to breathe slowly, shallow enough not to give himself away. Each inhale sounded as loud as a hammer strike to him, in his cautious state. His body wanted to move, to flinch, to roll over and draw a weapon, but he kept it still through sheer will.

He cracked one eye open just enough to see.

The angle was bad. He had collapsed facing away from the others, his shoulder blocking most of his view. He could not see his team. He could only see the reeds and the lake water, as well as some loose brown feathers catching his peripheral vision.

So he listened.

“You need to work fast,” said a feminine voice, breathless and strained. “I had to fly too high to avoid their soul-seer detecting me. I am exhausted, and I don’t think I would be useful in a fight.”

Calr felt a cold thread settle in his mind.

Flight, feathers, forced slumber, and a Dreamer. A Valkeren Dreamer.

“Don’t worry about it,” said another woman, her voice louder and more confident. “Usually, it takes people at least an hour to wake from your gift.”

“That one is special,” said a third voice, quieter and younger. “She noticed me. She knew I was reading her mind.”

Calr’s thoughts accelerated.

Alice.

Of course, they were here for Alice.

“You are the one who insisted we follow them into a dead zone, Kitame,” the confident woman snapped. “What are you waiting for? Start going through her head.”

Calr activated his Analyze Power, letting it burn.

Memories compressed, reordered, stripped of emotion, and reassembled into usable data in less than half a second. Conversations, warnings, and offhand comments Alice had made days ago.

Connections formed between ideas where none were before.

A girl in Ectamel. She had red hair. She reacted to Alice’s inner thoughts.

A telepath. Or at least a descendant of one, just like him.

“O-okay,” said the soft-spoken girl, Kitame, uncertainty bleeding into her voice. “I’m on it.”

Calr’s fingers twitched against the sand before he forced them still.

No.

Not yet.

“Hey, Obora,” the assertive woman said. “What do you think we should do after she is done reading her mind?”

“With my Dreamer magic, we could leave them, and they would not suspect a thing,” said the tired voice. Valkeren, no doubt. “I barely have the energy to keep myself airborne.”

“They have an Agame with them,” the confident woman replied. “We should slit their throats on principle.”

“That’s not the mission,” said the Dreamer flatly. “Donas ordered us to lie low and gather information. I would not recommend it.”

“But if they know about Kitame being a telepath, aren’t they already bad news?”

“We report to Donas,” the Dreamer insisted. “He decides.”

Calr’s jaw clenched.

She was debating murder like it was nothing. That girl was bad news.

He suppressed his emotional response and considered things logically. He could stay down, let them finish, then leave. If they were not planning to kill them immediately, playing dead might work.

Then his mind turned inward.

They were trying to get inside Alice’s head.

Information about the mythic rift gate was hidden in her mind. Not only that, this was Alice after all. He shuddered to think what kind of knowledge was stored there. Knowledge that could reshape nations, break economies, and collapse belief systems.

Or something even worse.

They were not just rifling through a simple teleporter girl.

They were poking into a loaded weapon.

No.

He could not allow that.

He needed to do something.

Could he attack them?

The thought barely survived contact with reality. Katar had called him a weakling more than once, and not without reason. He could hear three of them, and even then, the second girl was too confident. She talked like she had killed before. He would not stand a chance, especially without his crossbow.

Which left only one option.

Wake the others.

Slowly, carefully, Calr shifted his weight by a fraction, dragging his fingertips through the sand as if in a dream. He let his breathing hitch, then settle again.

Raik was the closest.

Raik was the strongest.

Raik would understand what to do immediately.

Calr tensed his body, ready to sprint.

If he was fast enough, if he was quiet enough, he might get Raik up and moving before Alice’s mind was torn open.

And if not…

Then at least he would die trying to stop it.

That was how scared he was of what they might find inside Alice’s head if they were allowed to dig long enough.

Calr moved.

He propelled himself forward on all fours, sand biting into his palms as he crawled, then smoothly transitioned into a low run. He kept his body close to the ground, using the reeds and uneven shoreline for cover, his movements quick but controlled.

He could still hear the girls talking behind him.

They had not noticed yet.

“Did you find anything interesting in her mind?” asked the assertive girl.

Calr clenched his teeth and ran harder.

“She is dreaming about a swordsman who keeps repeating his name whenever he fights,” said the mind reader, her tone puzzled. “Do you know any legendary warrior called Inigo Montoya?”

“Nope. Weird name. Probably mythic,” said the Dreamer dismissively.

Calr almost laughed despite himself. Alice might have the secret to ending the world, but good luck navigating that labyrinth.

The sound that followed wiped the mirth from his face entirely.

A soft woosh of air.

Too close.

Instinct took over.

He twisted and cartwheeled left, sand spraying as an arrow sliced past where his neck had been a heartbeat earlier. He rolled, came up on one knee, and finally looked back.

There was a fourth girl.

She stood farther back, half-hidden among the reeds, bow already rising again. Her blue-cyan hair fluttered in the quiet wind.

Because she had not spoken, he had not accounted for her.

Rookie mistake.

Unlike the other three, who were clustered around Alice, distracted by her, this one had been watching the perimeter.

Now all of them were looking at him in shock.

A Valkeren in a blue sundress with brown owl wings.

A girl in leather armor standing straight, with magenta hair like the night moon.

And a third girl his age with red hair that looked far too similar to his older sister for comfort.

“How did he wake up so fast?” the Dreamer snapped.

“Suu, shoot him again before he reaches the Agame,” shouted the loudmouth with the moon hair.

Calr heard the second arrow before he saw it.

This time, instead of dodging, he did something reckless.

He turned toward it.

He activated every scrap of mental power he had, forcing his focus into a razor edge. Time stretched just enough for him to see the arrow’s path, to feel the air split around it.

He slapped it out of the air, his palm hitting the wooden shaft.

The impact jolted his arm to the shoulder, pain flaring white-hot through his hand. The arrow spun, stripped of most of its momentum but not all of it, veering off in a new direction.

It struck Raik square in the midsection.

“Shit,” Calr muttered. “I was aiming for his shoulder.”

Luck favored them.

Raik’s spider silk under-armor absorbed the blow, the arrowhead failing to pierce fully. It felt more like a punch than a penetrating wound.

Raik’s eyes snapped open.

“Bingo,” Calr ground out through clenched teeth.

Raik surged upright in a single explosive motion, muscles releasing like a slingshot. The sudden movement sent Kan flying, the girl tumbling off his shoulder with a startled cry as she hit the sand and rolled.

Both of them were awake now.

Confused, yes, but awake.

The battlefield shifted instantly.

Now it was no longer one weak scout against four enemies.

Now it was three fighters, one of them an Agame, against a scattered enemy group that had just lost the element of surprise.

Raik took in the scene in a heartbeat. The arrow at his chest. The hostile figures standing over Alice and Vena. Calr standing between them, breathing hard.

Raik smiled.

A sharp, dangerous smile.

Relief flooded Calr so hard his knees almost buckled.

Raik could lead. All he had to do was give him all the information he had.

“They work for the cult, directly under Donas,” Calr started shouting information. “The Valkeren’s name is Obora. She put us to sleep. The moon-haired girl seems to be their leader. I don’t know her name. The red-haired one is a telepath, and her name is Kitame. The archer is called Suu. They have researched our team. At the very least, they know about you and that Ja’a is a soul-seer.”

Raik reacted strongly to the name Donas. Calr knew the fiery boy had a vendetta against the cult, so he gave him the names he had heard, just in case Raik knew them by reputation.

“Who is that clown?” asked the moon-haired girl. “And why don’t we know anything about him if he is that competent?”

“I don’t know,” said the owl-winged girl. “The initial report just said he is a street rat they took in just to pad the numbers.”

Calr smiled at that. He loved being underestimated. It meant he did not have a target on his back.

“Kitame, stop reading the sleeping girl and focus on him and what he can do. I don’t like getting surprised.”

Ah shit, thought Calr, as his eyes met the red-haired girl’s and the world went white.

Raik.

Raik did not move right away.

Waking to danger, his training and instincts suggested stillness. He took a steady breath, muscles coiled and ready for action, letting the world explain itself before acting.

He kept his hands loose at his sides as he faced the moon-haired girl, eyes sharp and posture relaxed enough to look confident rather than aggressive.

Beside him, Kan shifted almost imperceptibly.

Her foot touched her chains. Using a clever manipulation of mana, her weapon slid along her legs like a serpent, finally coiling along her arms as she adjusted her stance. Raik noticed and approved. Kan knew better than to rush. And here she needed her chain weapons to be effective in combat. Sadly, they did not seem to have time to don their armor.

He scanned the field.

Katar and Shingo were farther away, collapsed mid-patrol, still asleep. Too far to help immediately. Ja’a was closer, behind the boulder he was using as a backrest, sprawled on the sand with pearls pooled beneath her chin, frozen mid-assessment when the dream took her… typical. At least, she is safe from arrows.

Vena and Alice were the real problem.

They lay behind the hostile line, unconscious and undefended, with three enemies between them and safety.

And then there was Calr.

He looked like he had been running just a second ago. He probably was the one who had awakened him.

But now he stood rigid a few steps away, eyes locked on the red-haired girl. Completely unresponsive. His posture was wrong, too still yet unfocused.

Calr was the only one who had resisted the Dreamer magic.

That alone was strange.

Now he looked lost to something else.

He had said that the girl was a telepath.

Raik frowned. She strangely looked like him… no, not like him, Raik had met Calr’s sister, and the resemblance was uncanny.

If she were a telepath, did that mean Calr was too? Or something close enough to matter? More questions for later, if they survived.

Raik drew in a slow breath and spoke.

“Why are you here?” he asked evenly, “and what do you want?”

The moon-haired girl lifted her chin.

“We are called Donas’s Daughters,” she shouted, her voice ringing with conviction. “And we are here to fix the evil that you bloodline nobles have done to this world.”

Raik snorted.

“And you do that by kidnapping people and sacrificing them,” he said, mockery sharp and deliberate. “Very noble of you.”

“No cost is too great,” she shouted back.

Figures.

Behind her, the owl-winged girl suddenly stiffened.

“Hey, Kitame, what’s wrong with you?” she said, grabbing the red-haired girl by the shoulder and shaking her. There was real concern in her voice now. “Hey, NoName, something is wrong with Kitame.”

The red-haired girl did not respond.

Her eyes were open and unfocused, but she kept maintaining eye contact with Calr.

Kan moved slowly.

She approached Calr cautiously, one step at a time, chains still slack but ready. She reached him and shook his shoulder.

Nothing.

Calr did not blink. He did not even breathe differently.

They were staring at each other now, Calr and the telepath, frozen like statues locked in some private hell.

Raik felt a prickle of unease crawl up his spine.

“What did she do to him?” he demanded.

“What did he do to her?” the moon-haired girl snapped back.

They spoke at the same time.

That was not good.

Raik exhaled through his nose.

This was probably not intended to happen, their powers interacting in ways their owners did not fully understand. Some sort of feedback loop.

He might have been able to do something about it if he had any clue how Calr’s power worked.

This was exactly why he hated secrets.

Alice, Calr, and even Vena.

He knew nothing about them despite traveling with them for a couple of weeks. He did not know Vena was a princess of the Veil Kingdom. He did not know Calr was related to the telepath. And Alice, he did not even want to get started on Alice.

This made trust fragile, conditional, and dangerous for a team.

This was why he liked to work with Ja’a and Katar. They were an open book. With them, what you saw was what you got. Simple, honest, and reliable.

Raik tightened his fists.

Whatever had gone wrong, it had gone wrong now.

And until he understood it, nobody was touching them. They might not trust him with their stories, but they were still his team, his people, and no one was hurting them under his watch.
