Xavier was stuck in a room full of memory-wiped Denizens who’d just had their contracts completely erased but didn’t know it yet. He remembered the last time something like this had happened, the day he’d killed The Collector. Palini had been by his side. The man had gone with him, one-by-one, to each of the Denizens in the room and helped convert them to his side.

Palini was far better at talking such things through than Xavier was.

Except now Palini didn’t even know who he was, and back in The Collector’s throne room, the Denizens had just seen Xavier take down their former master. They’d known they were no match for him, and they’d been thankful to be released from servitude—and they still had their memories.

This was different.

If he didn’t play it right, he’d have a fight on his hands. One he wasn’t sure he could win.

Sixty-three Denizens stood in this room. Sixty-four including the barkeep—a B Grade in his own right. All frozen outside his time dilation field.

One at a time.

Xavier stood in front of Palini and extended his time dilation around the man.

Palini’s eyes snapped wide. He scanned the room before his gaze rested on Xavier.

“What is this?”

“I freed you.”

Xavier gripped Palini’s forehead and rapidly inputted every memory they shared. Their first meeting at the portal station. Palini taking him into custody. Their fight inside the Roving Seed Base. And everything that followed to turn a momentary enemy into a loyal follower.

It wouldn’t be enough for Palini to remember who he was. It was a glimpse, from Xavier’s perspective not his own, but it was a start.

Then he showed the memory of Palini’s contracts being erased.

Xavier pulled his hand back.

Palini’s forehead was dotted in sweat, chest rising and falling with rapid breaths. He gripped his forehead, eyes bulging in his skull. Then his breathing steadied, his arms came to rest at his sides, and he gave the room another look—a calculated one.

“We know each other. You came to save me.” Palini’s voice was flat.

Xavier gave a sharp nod. “And you’re not the only one I’m going to save.”

“My memories?”

“We’ll find them.” If they exist.

Palini stared into the middle distance. “What will you do to the people who run this place?”

Xavier rested his hand on Bones’s hilt. “That depends.”

“I hope you kill them.”

Xavier narrowed his eyes. “The Palini I know would want justice, not revenge.”

“Well, I don’t remember being him.” Palini nodded at the others in the room. “And them?”

“I show them what I’ve done, then we give them a chance to help.”

“If they don’t help?” Palini’s hand rested on the hilt of his own sword, a hard look in his eyes.

“Let’s just see if they will, first.”

Xavier wasn’t confident about this next step. Unlike Palini, he didn’t have memories to share with them. He didn’t know them. And he’d already killed one of their own to get here.

But if he wanted to find Palini’s memories and tear this base down in the process, the best way to do that was turn their own people against them.

He shut his eyes.

One at a time.

Then got to work.

The A Grades had been the hardest to deal with. He couldn’t slip memories straight into their minds. He’d dealt with them last. From weakest to stronger, Xavier and Palini had showed them their contracts had been erased.

The initial reactions had been mixed.

Fear. Elation. Some of them had even been oddly calm.

The hardest were the ones who hadn’t wanted to be freed. They’d grown used to the life they were leading. They didn’t remember who they’d been, and they’d been serving in this life for so long they’d stopped wondering about the memories they’d lost. Stopped caring about getting back to them.

But no matter how afraid or complacent they’d grown, none of them wanted to be slaves again.

When Xavier had made his way through all the B Grades—except for two he was intentionally leaving until last—and was finally ready to free the fifteen A Grades in the room, he’d hesitated. When he’d faced down the World Destroyer, he’d been as strong as an A Grade, and any doubts about his victory against it hadn’t lingered long. He remembered holding all that power—and he knew how big the gap was between that Xavier and the one he was now.

He was still incredibly powerful, and he knew in some ways he was as strong as an A Grade, and some ways even stronger, and could maybe even take one down—a weak one, especially since every one of them in the room was clearly susceptible to his time manipulation abilities—but it wasn’t something he wanted test if he didn’t have to.

So Xavier used every single tool he had to his greatest advantage, locking down the A Grades in separate time dilation fields with his drones—fields that were roughly half as fast as the one he was currently inside.

That wasn’t all he did.

He manifested spell patterns that would cause them paralysis, and other spell patterns that would lock their spell down so they couldn’t cast. He used about a dozen different spells on each of the A Grades, knowing that some might not even take, and others would last no more than a fraction of a second.

It would have to be enough.

As he brought the A Grades—one-by-one, just like with the others—into the slower time dilation field, he ended up losing several drones. The A Grades reaction to a perceived threat was faster than Xavier’s ability to help them understand what was happening.

But once they understood their contracts had been erased, all aggression seeped out of them—at least, all aggression toward Xavier.

Now he had a room full of Denizens, half of them seething with anger, the other half afraid but not willing to show it.

And there were still two people left outside the time dilation field. Two people he hadn’t yet brought into the fold.

Dybal and Lydad.

I killed Alica, their squadmate, and they think they killed me…

With an entire room of agents ready to move on the base, Xavier walked over to the twin brothers, the two mages who’d been more than ready to kill him. They were sitting at a table, both facing the door. He calmly sat across from them and extended the time dilation field until it encompassed them both.

The reaction was almost comical.

Shock. Fear. Realisation. They looked at Xavier, then around the room at the Denizens standing at his back—none of them trying to kill him.

“What the hell is going on?” Dybal said, summoning his staff into his hand, a prepared spell making it glow. Xavier could have paralysed the two men easily enough, but he didn’t think such a thing would be necessary.

He still didn’t.

Lydad put a hand on his brother’s arm. Their expressions shifted, and it was clear mental communication was being exchanged. Dybal’s fingers, tense around his staff, relaxed a fraction. The staff’s glow dimmed, though it didn’t disappear. He sat back in his seat. Took in things anew.

“I’ve erased your contracts.” Xavier raised his chin. “All of them.”

“How the f—” Dybal shut his eyes. “How?”

Xavier tilted his head to one side and peered into the man’s eyes. “You were sent to kill me, but you really know nothing about me, do you?”

Dybal opened his mouth, closed it. Shook his head. Something in his demeanour cracked. “I don’t even know where we are.”

Xavier stood, looked at the twins, then at the other Denizens in the room. “This isn’t what any of you expected to happen today. None of you knew erasing contracts was even possible, but now it’s happened.” He looked up at the observation crystals. “It’s only a matter of time until Management finds out what happened in this room, even with the simulation I’m feeding into their observation crystals. I’m not asking you to follow me,” he said, thinking, not yet, “but I am asking you to help me get your memories back, and help me get you all the hell out of this place. After that, well, you can each choose your own path.”

He looked to the door leading out of the Social Rooms, then at the other doors leading to the other bars and lounges of this place. He let out a sigh. He supposed if he was going to do this, he should do it right. “How many more agents are in this place?”

An A Grade stepped forward, his hands folded behind his back. He stood straight as though addressing a superior officer. “A thousand, sir. Maybe half on deployment.”

Xavier winced. “I’m not your commander. Relax. Call me Xavier.” He looked down, tapping his foot on the tile floor. “Roughly five hundred on base. We can’t wait for the other agents to return.”

“What’s the plan, s—” The A Grade cleared his throat. “Xavier?”

“What’s your name?”

The man’s clothing shifted as he quick-equipped a set of what looked like martial arts robes. The robes weren’t made from any material Xavier recognised—it was like someone had somehow woven steel into soft thread. He then summoned a long wooden staff into his left hand. “Arrack.”

“How well do you know this base?”

The man’s shoulders drooped and his head lowered a fraction. “Just about as well as anyone else in this room—which means not that well at all.” Arrack cracked his neck. “Despite having been here for ten thousand years.”

Xavier felt a vast weight push down at him. “Ten thousand years?”

That number barely computed.

Yes, he’d met people that were billions, trillions, more years old. Those who’d seen the births and deaths of universes. His own fate would see him live straight up to the end of this universe and—if he saved it—well beyond.

Ten thousand years would be a blink of an eye.

But right now, even after all the years he’d passed inside time dilation fields, to even contemplate ten thousand years of servitude without a single memory of who you really were…

He composed himself even as the weight of what these people had been through continued to pull down at him.

“Well, you’re not going to spend another day in this place.” Xavier swallowed. “I can promise you that.”

“That’s a hell of a promise to make,” Dybal muttered. “You sure you can live up to it? You’re what, C Grade? You have no idea how powerful Management are, do you?”

“No. I’ve no idea what we’re up against.” Xavier grinned. “But they have no idea what they’re up against either. They think I’m dead. Look at what I’ve already done in this room alone. Time to move.”

With The All-Seeing Eye active, Xavier couldn’t help but notice the way Dybal and Lydad exchanged a glance after searching the room and not finding Alica.

Their gazes ended up landing on him, and they were hard ones. But they didn’t ask the question. They didn’t need to—they’d already been suspicious of Alica turning up late.

They could do the math.

I’ll have to keep an eye on them. The All-Seeing Eye took in the rest of the newly freed Denizens in the room. In fact, I’ll have to keep an eye on all of them.

As often happened, Xavier was making things up as he went along, and he was fully aware that he was relying on everyone in that room following him through the next door, and the next.

There was a plan that had him doing this without them. A plan that had him taking only Palini and tricking every observation crystal in the entire base as they searched for wherever the man’s memories had been kept—if they’d been kept at all.

In a way, that would have been the safest plan.

But Xavier tended toward the more ambitious.

There was a part of him wondering if he really wanted to piss off whoever had the power to hold sway over A Grades in this way, but he was thoroughly ignoring that part. He’d already sent out what basically amounted as a threat to all the strongest Denizens in his universe, and whoever did run this place? Well, they’d kidnapped Palini, held the man’s family hostage, and tried to kill him.

He had every right to piss them off.
