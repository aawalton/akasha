At the bell, everyone started packing up. Chairs scraped back as students got out of their seats.

Amy stayed where she was, staring at the self-proclaimed Goddess.

The woman put her notebook in her bag, fixed her collar, then stretched. Apparently she was going to keep pretending to be a student. When she noticed Amy watching, she smiled at her.

"Yes, can I help you?"

Amy kept staring at the Goddess for a few more seconds, then ultimately let out a long sigh before standing up from her chair.

The woman was clearly starving for attention, and she showed it by coming all the way here and coincidentally sitting next to her…

Amy had nothing to talk about with her; everything that needed to be said was already done. If a conversation occurred, it wouldn't be her who started it.

"Amy!" Mira's clone came over, all but bouncing now that the quiz was over. "How did you even do that? I thought she was going to fail us for sure, and—" She glanced at the students still around them and lowered her voice. "Thank you. Really, you saved my life back there."

Amy's jaw tightened, and a whisper in the back of her mind reminded her of the past. She considered for a second just telling her otherwise, that nothing she had done had helped her in any way, but she forced herself to nod.

This is an illusion, not the real Mira, she reminded herself.

"I owe you so much. Mom said another detention would mean a month grounded." The clone reached for her bag, only to remember she didn't have one. "And of course my lunch is in there, too. I'll have to buy something. Have you seen what the cafeteria charges for—"

"Excuse me?"

They both turned. The Goddess was smiling at them as though she'd done nothing wrong in her life.

Great. Here we go…

"Sorry, I heard you mention the cafeteria. Are you going there now?"

The clone blinked, adjusting her glasses as she studied the grown-ass woman cosplaying as a high schooler. "Oh! Um, yes? I mean, I am. I usually bring lunch from home, but this time I forgot my bag. Maybe Amy..." She glanced at Amy uncertainly.

"I didn't bring anything."

"Right." Mira nodded at her. "So yeah, we are going to the cafeteria."

"Perfect!" The Goddess's smile widened— Christ, what an annoying smile. "I'm new here, and I haven't really made any friends yet. Would it be okay if I joined—"

"No," Amy immediately said.

Both the clone and the Goddess turned to look at her. The Goddess stared at her with amusement while the clone's eyes shot up in surprise and fixed on her with a mix of shock and disbelief.

Amy did not care.

"How about you go annoy somebody else. Seriously, nobody wants—"

"Amy!" The clone said, seemingly finally able to come out from the shock. "That's not a nice thing to say!"

Amy felt a bit of irritation at the interruption. She turned her head, fully ready to tell the clone to mind her own business; however, upon seeing the face of the person she had known since little, she had trouble even getting the words out.

In the end, she just bit her lip and sat back down, then slumped into her seat with a frown.

"I'm so sorry about my friend," Mira said after some hesitation, turning back to the Goddess with a mortified expression. "She's not usually like this. I think she might be coming down with something—she's been acting weird all morning."

Amy felt her eye twitch at the sight of Mira—no, the clone—apologizing to that woman.

"It's perfectly alright," the Goddess said with a clearly fake hurt expression. "Everyone has bad days."

"That doesn't make it okay." The clone insisted, then turned towards Amy, looking at her with disapproval. "Amy, I understand that you may be having a bad day, but that doesn't excuse you from acting like that…like a bully."

It took a little more than a second for Amy's brain to catch up with the words her friend just uttered. But once she did, her entire body went rigid, and for a moment, air was taken out of her body.

Her hands began to shake slightly where they rested on her lap. She could feel her chest tightening, that familiar sensation of something cold and heavy settling in her stomach.

Mira continued talking, oblivious to the effect her words had. "—being tired doesn't mean you can talk to her like that. She only asked to have lunch with us, Amy—"

Amy had lost track of what she was saying. Her eyes stayed on the desk. The trembling in her hands spread up her arms.

In the corner of her eye, she could see the Goddess staring at her with a completely blank expression. There was no amusement or satisfaction on her face, just... observation.

Amy imagined herself being judged, and hated it. Hated the scrutiny. Hated that this woman could see her reaction. Hated that she reacted at all.

The Goddess studied Amy's form for a long, silent moment. Then, as if nothing had happened, she patted Mira's shoulder and redirected her attention towards her.

"So…about the cafeteria..."

Mira broke off and turned to her. "Oh, right. Lunch." She pushed her glasses up and tried to smile. "They start serving at twelve. It's nothing special, but it's pretty good. Mom went here too, and she says we have it much better than she did."

"That sounds perfect." The Goddess smiled warmly, as if the previous scene had never happened. "Would it be alright if I joined you both? I won't be any trouble. And your friend..." She looked at Amy. "Perhaps she just needs to eat. Low blood sugar does make people irritable."

"Oh." Mira turned back to her. "Amy, did you eat anything today?"

Amy kept looking at the desk. She'd managed to stop her hands shaking by making fists, and she wasn't ready to let go yet.

"See? She probably hasn't eaten all day." Mira made a decision, nodding firmly. "Okay, yes. You should definitely come with us. I'm Mira, by the way. And this is Amy, though she's my best friend and is always super nice... normally."

"How lovely to meet you both." The Goddess's smile remained perfectly pleasant. "My name is—" she paused, as if considering, "—you can call me Saklas."

"That's a pretty name! Are you perhaps a foreigner?"

"Oh, no. I'm from here. It's just a nickname I like to use since it fits me somewhat."

"Oh, I see. Then nice to meet you, Saklas." Mira said with a smile. "Okay, let's head to the cafeteria before the lunch rush starts. The line gets really long if you wait too long, and—Amy?"

Mira stopped talking when she saw Amy still sitting there, stiff and staring at nothing.

"Amy? Hey." She leaned down. "Are you okay?"

Amy slowly opened her hands and looked up. Could she please just leave it alone?

Mira was watching her too closely for that. Behind her, the Goddess had her bag over her shoulder and was waiting for them.

"I'm fine," Amy said, her voice rough.

"Amy, look at you." Mira glanced around the mostly empty classroom before bending closer. "Is it... your period?" she whispered.

Amy stared at her, then felt herself blush. Of all the things Mira could have asked, she hadn't expected that.

"No, Mira. It's not that…" She said while trying to avert her eyes. "It's just… nothing."

Mira's gaze stayed on her. Amy could see her bite her lips and a visible frown appear on her face. "Whatever it is, please keep it under control… I truly just don't like seeing you act mean to people… It's not like you."

Amy's jaw tightened. And the urge to defend herself rose from her throat, but she quickly shut it down.

It did not matter. Nothing about this was worth it. Convincing Mira—the clone—that the woman beside her was the incarnation of Satan itself gave her no benefits. Moreover, it would be just too time-consuming, and with a low chance of succeeding. So really, it did not matter…

"Come on." Mira suddenly said, reached out, and gently took Amy's hand, tugging her up from her seat. Her grip was warm and soft. "Let's get you some food. You'll feel better, I promise. And..." she leaned in even closer, her whisper barely audible, "please try to be nicer to Saklas, okay? She seems really sweet, and she's new, and I know you're having a bad day, but you really hurt her feelings earlier."

Amy stared at the girl in silence, wondering if she should just go with the flow or run and get the hell away from here. In the end, she decided neither. Only silence, that was her response.

Mira seemed clearly dissatisfied with her answer, but did not push anymore; instead, she just tightened her grip on her hand and turned towards the Goddess, forcing a smile.

"Alright, we're taking way too much time here. Let's just go before all tables get taken."

The Goddess nodded with a smile of her own.

Soon, the three of them were on their way towards the cafeteria. Amy was on Mira's right side, and the Goddess on her left.

The cafeteria, just like the rest of the city, was a yassified version of the one Amy remembered. Long tables arranged in neat rows, large windows letting in natural light, and the smell of freshly cooked food mixing with the chatter of students. Basically, heaven compared to the actual one.

They'd managed to get their food and find a table near one of the windows. Mira sat across from Amy, with the Goddess—Saklas, she liked to call herself—positioned between them.

Amy used her spoon to pick at the chicken with rice, which Mira paid for, but did not really eat; she was just moving the grains around her plate. Mira and Saklas had apparently found plenty to talk about without her.

"I'm telling you, Madam Chen is a primordial vampire," Mira said, waving her hands as she explained. "The way she looks at you. Like she can see your soul."

"But she goes out in daylight," Saklas said, sounding as though she was seriously considering it. "Wouldn't that be a problem?"

"Sunscreen." Mira pushed her glasses up. "Really good sunscreen. Vampires can learn to use modern stuff, too."

Saklas laughed, and Amy tightened her hold on the spoon. "I hadn't thought of that. You have quite an imagination."

"Amy says I read too much fantasy." Mira smiled across the table at her. "I don't think there's such a thing as too many books, though. Right, Amy?"

Amy watched the steam coming off her rice.

"Amy. At least eat while you're ignoring us. You haven't touched it."

She rolled her eyes and took a bite. Even the rice was made exactly how she liked it. Apparently this place couldn't leave a single thing alone.

Satisfied that Amy was eating, Mira went back to her questions. "So where was your old school? It's a weird time to transfer. We're halfway through the term."

"In the city. We had to move because of my family's work," Saklas said.

"Oh! Which city? Amy's family is originally from the city, too! Well, her mom is. Her dad grew up here, but her mom was a city person, which is why Amy has that—" Mira stopped herself, glancing at Amy with sudden uncertainty. "Sorry, is it okay if I talk about that?"

Amy shrugged, not looking up from her plate.

Mira took that as permission and continued. "Anyway, Amy's mom gave up her fancy city life to move here. Super romantic, right? Like something out of a storybook. She fell in love with her dad and just decided to stay."

Amy's chopsticks stopped moving.

"How lovely," Saklas said, and Amy could hear the smile in her voice without looking up. "True love conquers all obstacles."

"Right?" Mira beamed. "And they're still so in love even after all these years. Actually—" She suddenly perked up, looking at Amy with excitement. "I'm going to Amy's place for dinner tonight. It's her parents' anniversary."

Amy finally looked up, confusion flickering across her face as she tilted her head. She understood the words coming out of Mira's mouth, yet couldn't process them.

Mira's smile faltered slightly as she looked at Amy. "Don't tell me you forgot?"

Parents' anniversary. My parents. Together. Having an anniversary dinner…?

Amy opened her mouth, closed it, then opened it again. No words came out.

Mira frowned, then scratched her neck uncomfortably. "Do you not want me to come…? I already told your mom I'd be there. It would be rude to cancel now... Plus, I also told Mrs. Henderson I was going, she would get confused if she didn't see me there."

Mrs. Henderson. Going to dinner. At Amy's house. For her parents' anniversary.

None of this made sense. No, that wasn't quite right. It made perfect sense. That was the problem.

"I..." Amy's voice came out barely above a whisper. Her mind raced, wondering what to say. Eventually, she just decided to go with the flow. "I just forgot."

"Oh, thank goodness!" Mira said, relaxing. "I thought you didn't want me there! Your mom said eight, by the way. Mrs. Henderson's bringing a painting for them. She's spent weeks on it. Oh, but don't tell your parents. They don't know about it yet."

Amy nodded mechanically, but inside, her mind was racing.

Her parents' anniversary. Her mother and father, married, celebrate together. The last time that had happened was... was...

The manga chapter retelling her backstory flashed through her memory. It had been heavily dramatized but still mostly accurate. That had been the last time.

After that, they would never get close to each other within a few feet.

But here, in this place, in this perfect fantasy—

An epiphany reached Amy, and suddenly, everything started making sense. She believed she had just discovered how to pass the trial.

It was just a theory, so she needed to check if it was true.

She stared at her satchel and then at Mira and all the people in the cafeteria.

Better do it in a more private place.

Amy stood up abruptly, her chair scraping loudly against the floor. Several nearby students looked over at the noise.

"Amy?" Mira asked, startled. "Where are you going?"

"I remembered I needed to do something..."

"Wait, you haven't eaten—"

"I'll be back." Amy was already leaving the table.

Then her stomach growled, loudly enough that there was no pretending it hadn't.

She stopped.

A few people looked over. Amy could feel her face getting hot, and behind her, Mira was trying not to giggle.

Really? She could barely feel anything about this whole nightmare, but this was embarrassing enough to get through?

She sighed and went back. Mira covered her mouth, still smiling. The Goddess didn't even bother doing that.

"Forgetting something?" she asked.

Amy picked up her plate without answering. Chicken and rice in one hand, she used the other to pull her satchel into place.

"Amy, you can't take that around the school—" Mira called after her.

She was through the cafeteria doors before she could hear the rest.

There were students all along the hallway. Amy ate as she walked past them, looking for somewhere she could be alone. The food was good, which annoyed her for much the same reason that everything else here did.

She took a turn she remembered. Fewer people went this way, and it should lead to the courtyard—

"Amy?"

For fuck's sake. She stopped and looked back.

It was the baker's son again. She recognized him just as his face started to turn pink.

"Hi. I saw you earlier, but... well, I wanted to talk to you. About something. I was going to—"

Amy took another bite while she waited for him to get to it.

"I've been meaning to tell you for a while, actually—"

"Sorry. I'm busy right now." She gestured with the plate.

She left before he could try again. His quiet "Oh. Okay" followed her down the hall.

She felt a little bad. Not enough to go back, though. Her companions were still outside this trial, probably fighting for their lives, and she needed to work out how to get out of here.

Finally, the courtyard.

There was a couple making out, but nobody else. They probably wouldn't bother her.

It was a small space between two wings of the school, with benches around a large tree. Amy remembered coming here whenever she wanted to avoid the other students.

She picked the bench partly hidden by the tree and sat down, setting her plate beside her.

She reached for her satchel, and she carefully pulled out Libris. The book was still damaged, causing a dark feeling to rise from Amy's stomach, and she quickly pushed it down.

"Libris?" she whispered. "I think I've worked something out."

It was still asleep, of course. She knew that. She kept talking anyway.

"I need your help to check, so... thank you."

She opened it carefully on her lap and reached for their connection. Shared Fate, the status screen had called it. It let her look further ahead without using mana, so she could still do it even like this. The limit was how many times she could use it. After that, she'd have to wait, though she didn't know for how long.

Amy closed her eyes and used it.

The courtyard disappeared.

She was looking at the road to her childhood home. There was no school around her anymore.

Above it, cracks spread across the sky. There was nothing on the other side of them, and they were getting wider. The whole thing was coming apart.

Then she saw the house.

It was on fire.

Flames were eating through it, sending smoke up toward the broken sky. People were screaming inside, and she knew their voices.

Her mother. Her father. Mrs. Henderson and Mira. She could hear a dog, too.

The Amy in the vision stood on the road, watching them burn. She made no move to help. There was nothing on her face, even when they screamed for someone to come.

Then it ended.

Amy opened her eyes. The courtyard was back, with Libris warm on her lap and the half-eaten rice beside her.

She stayed where she was, thinking about the house.

Then she heard herself laugh.

"Heh."

Barely a sound. She thought that would be it, but no.

"Heh. Heheh."

She could feel her shoulders shaking as she tried to breathe, only to laugh again.

She was too tired for anything hysterical. The horrible part was that she actually found it funny.

So that was what she had to do.

It wasn't enough to refuse to stay in this place. She had to destroy it herself. Burn everything she'd wanted, everyone in it, to prove she meant it about saving the people waiting for her outside.

Well, yes. Obviously she'd choose them. She couldn't live here knowing none of it was real, however much she wanted it to be.

But a fire at her parents' anniversary dinner? Killing everyone she'd cared about, after the trial had gone to so much trouble to give her perfect versions of them? That was... she couldn't help finding that funny.

"Haha... of course. What did I expect..."

She held Libris to her chest for a moment, still laughing, then lowered it to her lap. It hurt somewhere she didn't want to think about.

"Amy?"

She looked up, unable to quite stop chuckling.

The Goddess stood a few feet from the bench. She'd come alone. No Mira this time, just those ridiculous galaxy eyes looking at her.

"What's funny?" She came closer.

Amy tried to tell her, but couldn't get it out without laughing. "Heh... you already... know..."

The Goddess tilted her head, then shook it with a smile. "Actually, I made sure not to look into the future for this one. I wanted to see how it ends without spoilers."

She took another step forward, her gaze shifting from Amy's face down to the book in her lap. Her eyes shone for a second, then widened slightly.

"Oh," the Goddess said softly. And then, inexplicably, a small sound escaped her too. "Pfft."

Amy stopped and stared at her. Had she just heard that?

The Goddess was trying not to laugh.

This bitch, the origin of all my misfortune, is laughing.

Amy put a hand over her mouth. Her shoulders were already shaking again.

"Pfft—"

That did it. She couldn't even pretend to hold it in anymore, and the next laugh was loud enough for anyone in the courtyard to hear.

The Goddess lasted about three seconds longer. She had a hand over her mouth too, eyes squeezed shut, as though that was going to help either of them.

Then she started laughing properly. She stumbled toward the bench and landed in Amy's lap, knocking against the book and nearly tipping the plate off the seat.

Amy caught Libris with one hand and grabbed the bench with the other. She was halfway out of her seat, trying to keep them both from falling, and her stomach hurt too much from laughing to straighten up.

She couldn't save the food as well. The plate fell, spilling chicken and rice across the ground.

Neither of them stopped laughing. The couple had stopped kissing to stare at them as though they were crazy.

If only she actually were.
