“Luma is a great spirit.” Valdir answers.

Valdir sighs. “Well let's start with the calamity part first. To put it simply, a calamity is a being that will drastically alter the path of an individual. One person’s calamity could very well be another’s mother. Though there tends to be some sort of power gap in between the two.”

“So are you one of my calamities?” Valdir definitely meets the power requirements. But he doesn't seem to feel calamity-like, whatever that even means.

“I don’t think so. It was likely always a part of your path to meet me. Be it via interference or dumb luck. Plus, I ain’t exactly sharing any critical information with you, nor trying to maim you or whatever. Calamities aren’t just any random powerful being, they are ones that will directly affect you to a significant degree in often strange ways.”

Hmm, so Claire was counted as a calamity for me because of her effect on me? She definitely changed quite a lot about how I think about things. Mostly on the crafting side, but I think I have also been a bit more motivated to get stronger since I met her. Plus, if she didn’t exist, I am not sure I would’ve survived that incursion.

The implication of calamities in general is a bit worrying. Apparently even without that focus or whatever, I was bound to run into lots of them. Whether that be because of my heroness, or just me being me, I do not know. Still, to think my path is destined to be shaken up frequently is both exciting and terrifying.

At least I have those immunities and whatnot to help me. Though thus far they have come in more handy against things that aren’t even calamities. Perhaps I misunderstood the focus? Maybe it isn't to help me survive the calamities themselves, but to survive other events that would otherwise lead to me not meeting more of them? Only time can tell for certain, I suppose.

“Honestly, got no fucking clue. I am not all knowing, ya know. I only know what I know, and nothing more. All I can guess is that she will become important in the future. Likely in some grand way that not even an oracle could get a hint of. So perhaps just stay on her good side?”

“Perhaps. Great spirits are not normal spirits, as you have likely guessed. For one, they are absurdly rare, and this is the first I have ever heard of one forming a bond. Let alone with one so weak. Part of their power comes from the fact that they were never alive. Remember how I said spirits are remnants of animals and whatnot? Well great spirits aren’t. “Hmm, so a random spirit chose to bond with me and is harboring some unknown power. That sounds just lovely.”

Valdir guffaws. “Well, good luck with whatever it is. As for what else you can expect from Luma? I have only met two great spirits in my time. But they were both intelligent. Not to say the average spirit is a dumb animal, but they tend to lack a full personality, if you catch my drift.” My mind goes to Dovey and his eccentricness, I guess that makes sense if one thinks about it. “Suffice to say, Luma will likely become more of a member of your party, rather than a spirit companion. Okay then, so I really should not piss Luma off. I shall have to be very careful about when I accept quest rewards from now on. Lest we have a repeat of that incident. I sigh and lean back in my chair, popping another of the delicious pastries into my mouth. “Okay, so I only have one question left I think.”

So naturally, the most likely way that one becomes a spirit mage is to acquire Spirit Sight before they select their classes, or figure out how to change their class.”

“Well, what’s so hard about acquiring Spirit Sight?”

“You remember what I said about stars representing a fundamental restructuring of the corestone?” The old man says, and I nod. “Well acquiring the Spirit Sight skill is one step below that. It is extremely difficult for even a very powerful being with decades of experience to do. Let alone a child that has yet to develop a class.”

“That makes sense, Spirit Sight does say something about removing my perception stat? I can’t imagine that is a simple thing to do. I was lucky, I suppose, as I got summoned here and already had access to it.”

“Extraordinarly lucky. It took me nearly three centuries to acquire it, and then another millennia to figure out the class change. Though I did have a mentor guiding me at that point, and I was quite fixated on becoming a spirit mage, for reasons.” He says the last bit with a bit of distance, as if he is recalling some memories.

“I have the spirit magic. The problem is that there aren’t exactly scrolls of spells just lying about. I’ve only acquired one spirit magic spell so far, and that was from one of those universal choice scrolls.” I explain.

“That is because spirit magic is unique to the individual.” Valdir says, “Each person’s spirit is different. How they choose to manipulate it is up to them, ultimately. You are unlikely to find any spirit magic scrolls or tomes lying about. Your best bet at learning more is to figure out magic and invent your own spells. Do be careful though, spirits are quite dangerous to tamper with.”

I sigh, sounds like I am going to have to get risky if I want to learn spirit magic. Still, I want to try to learn it. Even if it turns out to be not that useful, it will definitely improve my understanding of magic in general.

“Growing stronger and stuff is obviously the goal. But don’t forget to live your life. The power isn’t going anywhere, and it really isn’t a race. As you grow stronger, you age slower. This guy has two extremely rare magics? I didn’t even know time was possible to control. I bet it's super powerful, not that I have any intention of chasing it myself. With a groan, I stand up and stretch a bit. “Fair enough, now where is this so-called bed?”
