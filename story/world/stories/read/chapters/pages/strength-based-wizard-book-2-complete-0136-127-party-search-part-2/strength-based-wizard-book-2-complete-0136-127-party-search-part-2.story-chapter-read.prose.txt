Chapter 127

Party Search, Part 2

The Harvest Guild training room is huge, rectangular, and reinforced in every direction. It reminds me a lot of the AEA’s training hangar that the agency had set up in preparation for the Joint Guild Operation. Of course, Labonte spared no expense with his. The floor is dark composite tile, each panel etched with tiny runes that absorb impact and make my boots land silently, even with the heaviest of steps and landings. The walls are layered metal and enchanted glass, scarred from previous use, but clearly showing that they’ve been battle-tested by powerful System Users. There are scorch marks near the far corner, a patch of vines crawling up one support pillar, and one suspiciously slime-shaped stain nobody has mentioned but everybody knows is Jelly Boy’s fault. Labonte had wanted to see what the little guy was capable of. He did not leave disappointed that day.

At the far end of the room stand two robotic training dummies. Calling them dummies feels unfair. They’re both humanoid in shape. Seven feet tall, with smooth faceless heads. Broad torsos made of dark Gate-produced metals with silver essence lines glowing through the joints. Their hands are blunt, articulated weapons, wide enough to palm my head with ease. Labonte swears they’re top of the line, and I don’t doubt him. But I did just eviscerate a high-leveled shark-lord in a single hit, so it’s worth asking.

“Level Sixty attack resilience, kiddo!” he had said ten minutes ago, with the enthusiasm of a man trying to sell me a timeshare in a faraway war-torn country. “These beauties can take a pounding, trust me!”

I had looked at the robots. Then at him. Then at Jerome, who was standing at his bosses side, arms casually crossed.

Jerome had only said, “The invoice was considerable” with a shrug. Which is Jerome speak for please don’t break them, but if you do, we have a good warranty anyway.

Labonte and Jerome watch from the observation deck above, behind a thick wall of enchanted glass. Labonte has both hands pressed to the railing, practically vibrating in his shoes. He loves these displays of power. Jerome stands beside him with a tablet tucked under one arm, expression neutral, posture immaculate. He looks bored, but I know his mind is busy taking in everything he’s observing.

I roll my shoulders.

I’m testing [Cast Fist], first. The name still sounds silly as hell. Like something a middle schooler would write on his binder next to a drawing of a flaming skull.

[Cast Fist] (Grandmaster)

Description: Sometimes spellcasting isn’t enough! You are no stranger to getting up close and personal. When your spellcasting fails you, you can solve problems instead with violence and stubbornness. Tackle all obstacles head-on and fist-first! You may combine [No Pain, No Gain] and [Muscle Mastery] into a single, all-out punch. This Ability may be applied once per day.

Note: Per the rules of [No Pain, No Gain], use of this Ability comes with a high cost of 75% of your Maximum Stamina and 60% of your Maximum Health.

Note: You are incapable of consuming any Health or Stamina potion for 15 minutes after applying this Ability.

Note: [Cast Fist] may be stored within your body for up to twenty-four hours after applying this Ability.

I open my status in the corner of my HUD.

Health full. Stamina full. For now.

“Ready?” I call out.

The air quivers and the two automatons come to life, cautiously approaching me with practiced movements.

“Ready!” shouts Labonte over an intercom.

I activate [Cast Fist].

The cost hits me instantly. My Health bar drops. My Stamina bar drops. Both plummeting in the blink of an eye. It feels like someone opens two drains inside me at once. The deleted Health and Stamina is replaced by an entirely new sensation. Power surges through my right arm with brutal speed, racing from my chest into my shoulder, then down through biceps, forearm, wrist, knuckles. My veins bulge like blue-black cables under the skin. They swell from shoulder to hand, thickening until the whole arm looks overinflated.

Steam bursts from my pores. Little white jets hiss from my skin, curling upward into the training room air. My arm flushes pinker, hot and tight, blood flow spiking so hard I can feel every beat of my heart in my fingertips. For a second, I think it’s going to explode. It doesn’t.

I focus on storing this new power. The Skill completes. The drain stops and I’m left standing there breathing hard, staring down at my right arm. It’s hyper-pumped. That’s the only way to describe it. It looks like I just did two hundred curls, drank a gallon of pre-workout, and got bitten by a radioactive bodybuilder. The muscles are swollen and dense, veins still standing out in thick cords beneath the skin. Near my right shoulder, when I move, there is the faintest sparkle of blue.

“This is not what I expected,” I call up.

Labonte cups a hand around his ear. “What?”

“I said this is not what I expected!”

Jerome’s voice comes through the room’s speaker. “Explain.”

I flex my fingers. The arm responds normally. No stiffness, or heaviness. But I can sense something there nonetheless: a horrific amount of stored potential sitting under my skin like a bomb with knuckles.

“It’s charged,” I say. “But it doesn’t feel like I’m actively using a Skill.”

A thought strikes me. I look down at my arm again, and specifically my sparkling shoulder.

“Oh my god,” I mutter. “I’m Donkey Kong.”

“What?” Labonte asks through the speaker.

“Nothing.” I blurt. But I can’t help but be reminded of my childhood. Countless bouts against neighborhood kids. I always selected the giant ape as my fighter, winding up a giant punch and then walking around with it stored. One super-charged haymaker waiting to ruin somebody’s day.

I look up toward the observation deck.

“Tell the dummies to give me everything they’ve got!”

Jerome just taps something on his tablet and nods.

The robots spring into action. They go from their cautious step-slides to murderous sprints without transition. The first launches forward, feet hammering the floor. The second circles wide, its torso rotating slightly as it calculates angles. Their movements are fast. Too fast for things that big. Metal limbs move in blurs of motion as the essence lines in their joints flare bright white.

The first dummy throws a straight punch at my head. I slip it, pivoting left. The fist passes close enough to stir my hair. The second comes in low with a sweeping kick. I hop over it, twisting midair, and land between them. I duck under another strike, plant my left hand on the first dummy’s forearm, and shove. My right hand comes up instinctively, but I don’t trigger the stored Skill.

There’s no difference in my Strength or ability to use the arm until I actually unleash the punch. That is horrifyingly useful. I can have this Skill locked and loaded at the start of each day. Though there’s the visual tell of the blue sparkles surrounding my arm.

The second dummy rushes me from behind. My [Perception] flashes. I drop, roll, and come up with both arms inside its guard. I clinch, shove my shoulder into its chest, and pivot, using its momentum to throw it into the first.

They collide with a massive clang.

Labonte whoops from above.

Jerome just makes a note.

The first dummy recovers quickly, stepping in with a high guard. The second shifts behind me, forcing me into a narrowing angle. The first dummy jabs. I slap it away with my left. It feints with the left, then pulls back. I see an opening. Its right side is exposed. It’s perfect.

My brain catches the trap at the exact same moment my body is already moving. I throw the hook with my right arm. The dummy’s guard snaps into place. It’s fast and I fell for its trap. The opening had been bait. A clean little tactical trap set by a faceless robot.

It doesn’t matter. It’s too late.

I activate [Cast Fist].

The stored power detonates through my arm. My fist hits the guard. For one microscopic instant, everything freezes. Then the world becomes the impact. Then, my fist drives through its guard, through its arms, through its chest, through whatever enchanted core counts as its expensive little soul. Metal folds inward around the punch, then explodes outward in a bloom of white sparks, black fragments, and screaming essence lines that explode in clouds of light and thunder. The entire upper half of the dummy vaporizes into shrapnel.

The shockwave keeps going. It hits the second dummy point blank. The second dummy is six feet away, moving to flank me. It gets erased sideways. Its torso implodes first, then the head snaps off, then both legs fling apart like the thing got disassembled by an angry god. Pieces slam into the far wall hard enough to crater the reinforcement panels.

Then the shockwave hits the observation deck. The enchanted glass shatters. The entire viewing window bursts inward in a glittering explosion of reinforced shards and pulverized runes. The sound is enormous, like a cathedral made of champagne flutes falling down stairs.

Dust rolls across the training room. Fragments rain from above.

For a second, all I can hear is the high ringing in my ears.

My right arm hangs at my side, no longer swollen. The pump is gone. The veins have settled. The blue sparkle has vanished. My arm is back to normal, and entire unharmed by the release of energy.

I stare at the empty space where two top-of-the-line Level 60-plus training dummies used to be.

“Oh,” is all I can say.

The dust begins to clear.

Above me, where the observation deck window used to be, a pink bubblegum bubble shield floats in the open space. Labonte and Jerome stand inside it, untouched. Labonte’s hands are pressed dramatically to the inner surface. Jerome has one hand resting on his tablet, expression still composed, though his tie is slightly crooked.

Summoned Sugar Plum Fairies flutter around the bubble, tiny wings buzzing, peach-pink dresses glittering. They grab the shield with little glowing hands and begin lowering it through the shattered observation window like a rescue crew moving an extremely expensive soap bubble.

The bubble descends to the training floor. It pops with a soft, sugary sound. Labonte steps out first, eyes wide, mouth open in a bright grin. Jerome follows, brushing one microscopic speck of dust from his sleeve.

Labonte looks at the destroyed dummies. Then at the shattered glass. Then at me.

“Holy shit, kid,” he says, voice full of awe. “You’ve got a daily nuke in your forearm.”

I look down at my hand.

My knuckles are smoking.

“Yeah,” I say.

Somewhere behind us, another piece of robot falls from the ceiling and clangs against the floor.

Labonte’s grin widens.

Jerome sighs.

I bet he’s already preparing the warranty claim paperwork in the back of his mind.

“Sorry about the dummies,” I say.

“Don’t sweat it, Joe,” Labonte says, waving one hand like I knocked over a stapler instead of detonating a mortgage worth of enchanted robotics. “You’ve earned us as many dummies as you’d like to smash.”

The summoned Sugar Plum Fairies hovering around him cheer and toss tiny handfuls of glitter into the air. The glitter disappears before it hits the floor.

“Good to know,” I say. [Cast Fist] is officially terrifying, even if it’s a once-daily Skill. “Now I just need to test that magical siphoning aura I told you guys about,” I say, flexing my hand a few times to shake out the last of the impact I made with the training dummy’s armored body. “Do we have robots that can cast spells, or will you be testing your magic on me?”

Labonte laughs. “Not my style, kid.”

“Your style is definitely making other people test magic on me.”

“Correct,” he says, pointing at me. “And I actually have a perfect idea.”

The short old man turns toward one of the Sugar Plum Fairies still bobbing in the air beside him. This one has lavender wings, a little peach-colored tunic, and the intense posture of someone who takes messenger duty extremely seriously.

“Sprinkles,” Labonte says, “can you be a doll and go fetch our visitor?”

The fairy snaps into a salute so crisp it makes the tiny bells on her shoes jingle. Then she zips off through the broken observation window and out of the room like a glitter-powered bullet.

I look at Labonte. “Who’s our visitor?”

“An internal recruit from within the Guild we’d like you to consider for your Party,” Jerome says.

“You guys already have internal recruits?”

Jerome raises one eyebrow. “Harvest Guild is an organization, Joseph. We’ve considered our other members very closely. Most of them local, too.”

Labonte beams.

I glance at the wreckage again. “Right,” I say. “Internal recruit. Cool.” I’m lying if I’m not interested in the kind of System Users Labonte recruited other than myself.

It’s not that I don’t trust Harvest. Labonte has supported me. Jerome has saved my life through the use of his Skills during the fight against Chernobog’s avatar. The Guild is paying for my Dad’s care. That means something. But the stakes of Stage Two of the God Game are so much higher. Anyone joining my Party isn’t just a coworker. They’re someone I might need to trust when a god-backed lunatic tries to turn my organs into soup. They’re someone who might die because I made a bad call. Or worse, someone whose bad call gets everyone else killed.

I rub the back of my neck. No pressure.

A few moments later, Sprinkles reenters the room. She is flying backward, both tiny hands extended in a dramatic “this way, please” gesture. Behind her walks a woman.

At first glance, she does not look like a System User. That’s my first thought, and it’s probably unfair, but my brain has developed certain expectations. Most higher-level System Users look enhanced. Even spellcasters are harder looking. It’s hard to describe the subtle ways the System’s enhancements effect those with access. Like reality has been sanding them into weapons. Even the ones who don’t look conventionally athletic usually carry themselves with the weird balance and density that comes from having stats poured into their bones and muscles.

This woman looks… soft. She’s in her thirties, with pale skin and a face covered in a couple splashes of freckles, like someone flicked cinnamon across her nose and cheeks. Her eyes are green and observant behind no-nonsense lashes. She has a large nose that gives her face character instead of prettiness, and reddish-brown hair pulled back in a loose ponytail that is already losing the war against gravity. She’s tall, probably just shy of six feet, but she doesn’t move like someone whose body has been boosted into a weapon by the System. In fact, I’d describe her gait as unathletic. She’s wearing casual street clothes. Jeans, sneakers, and a gray zip-up hoodie over a green shirt. She looks like someone’s aunt there to pick someone up from the airport.

Sprinkles lands on her shoulder, points at her like a game show hostess presenting a prize, then takes a tiny bow.

Labonte steps forward with theatrical warmth.

“Joe, this is Charlotte Greenbauer,” he says. “Charlotte, this is Joseph Sullivan.”

The woman’s eyes flick over me. They widen just a touch—the little hitch of recognition. I suppose who doesn’t know me… At least in Cleveland.

“She joined Harvest not long before you accepted your own invitation,” Labonte continues. “And I think she’ll be a good fit for your Party.”

Charlotte starts walking toward me.

Her steps are steady. Whatever awe she feels, she’s got it leashed. That gets my attention more than the rest.

She closes the distance and holds out her hand. “Hi,” she says. “Charlotte Greenbauer.” Her voice is calm, lightly Midwestern.

I take her hand.

Her grip is firm.

“Joseph Sullivan,” I say.

“I know,” she says, then immediately winces. “Sorry. I mean, obviously I know. Everyone knows. Not everyone. But a lot of people. The news.” She clears her throat. “I’m doing great already.”

Labonte lets out a delighted little chuckle.

I smile despite myself.

“Don’t worry,” I say. “I once introduced myself to a Gateborne cephalopod alien by buying him thirty rotisserie chickens. Social grace is not why I’m famous.”

Charlotte’s mouth twitches. “Rotisserie chickens?”

Then her gaze drops briefly to my right hand.

“To be fair,” she says, “I think you’re famous because you saved our city by punching a giant demon with a meteor-sized magical fist.”

She releases my hand and looks around the room at the ruined dummies, the shattered observation window, the scorch marks, the fairies, the pink shield residue, and Jerome standing there like the adult supervision nobody listens to.

Then she looks back at me.

There’s still a little awe in her expression.

But underneath it, there’s confidence. It’s a quiet sort of confidence that makes me a little jealous. The kind that doesn’t need to announce itself because it already knows where it stands.

“So,” she says, “I hear you need someone to throw spells at you.”

I glance at Labonte.

He’s still grinning like a fool.

Jerome says nothing, as he’s busy with something on his tablet.

I look back at Charlotte.

“What kind of spells are we talking?”

Charlotte smiles. “Depends on what kind of spells you need,” she says.

My [Perception] prickles.

Interesting.

Labonte claps his hands together.

“Wonderful!” he says. “Introductions now out of the way, let’s get to work!”

“What are you able to do exactly?” I ask Charlotte. “Sounds like you’re a spellcaster.”

Charlotte’s smile deepens. Like she’s been waiting for the question and already decided how much trouble she wants to cause with the answer.

Before she can respond, I reach out with [Aura Sense]. Not aggressively. Just a little brush to see if I can glean anything from my sixth sense. I can’t sense anything. She almost feels like a non-System User, or someone who is very weak. Which can’t be true. Labonte wouldn’t waste my time with someone too under-leveled.

I prod a little harder. My senses hit a wall. It’s smooth and firm and featureless, like pressing my mind against polished stone. My eyebrows lift and eyes widen slightly. She is intentionally suppressing her aura, I realize.

Charlotte’s smile shifts again. Did she sense me reaching out to her?

A faint reflection of blue-white light appears in her eyes. I recognize it as the faint tell of someone reading over an invisible System window.

A second later, I feel the familiar pulse in my brain.

Ping!

Participant Charlotte Greenbauer would like to share her Participant Profile with you.

Accept?

[Yes] / [No]

I glance at her.

She gives a tiny shrug.

“Figured that would be faster than me doing the whole mysterious recruit monologue,” she says.

“Appreciated,” I say.

I mentally accept.

Her profile unfolds in front of me.

I scan the basics first.

I blink. Gourmand. The Class is an odd one, but I immediately see how Labonte saw the value in it, as it functions fairly similarly to his own Class. Frankly, it’s a bit absurd. Charlotte is a Swiss Army knife spellcaster. As in this woman can meal-prep herself into different builds. That’s incredible, I think.

“Holy shit,” I say.

Charlotte tucks her hands into the pockets of her hoodie, looking pleased but trying not to show it too much.

I let out a small whistle. “That’s… okay, that’s extremely flexible.”

“It can be,” she says.

“How broad is the pool of Spells you get in each Build?” I ask. “Like, are we talking a handful of buffs, or can you basically turn a charcuterie board into a spell list?”

Labonte makes a delighted sound. “Oh, I like that.”

Charlotte laughs once, soft and quick.

“It depends,” she says. “Some ingredients are straightforward. This bite of cheese will get me the same spell each time. The trick is when I’m building the meal. Some combinations work well together. Others don’t. If I mix the wrong things, the results get muddy.”

“Muddy how?”

“Weaker spells. Higher mana costs for the same spells a single ingredient typically grants. Strange side effects.” She tilts her head. “One time I combined eel, ghost pepper, and a Gate-grown citrus fruit called a Mourning Lime. That entire day I was…” She catches herself, facing turning slightly red. “Actually… Never mind. Now that I think about it, TMI!”

From the side, Jerome speaks. “Charlotte has established ‘meals’ she’s built, which give her the Builds she can swap between.” He looks up from his tablet. “Specific consumption sets based on the expected role,” Jerome continues. “She has a very good Healer Build. Utility Caster as well. Offensive and Defensive packages, of course.”

Charlotte nods. “The pre-set meals are cleaner. If I know what the team needs, I can eat toward that.”

“Eat toward that,” I repeat. “That is… an interesting way of framing things.”

She smiles. “You’re one to talk. You flex for your magic, right?”

“Fair.”

I skim the Participant Profile again. Level 32 is a lot lower than I ideally want to accept into my Party. But her Class allows her to potentially instantly fill several roles at once. And the flexibility could be a massive boon. After all, I have no idea what we’ll be facing in this second phase of the Game.

I close the shared window.

“So, what are you on right now?” I ask.

“My Offensive Build,” Charlotte says.

“What Level of Spells do you have access to?”

She rocks gently on her heels. “Level Eight, but currently only enough Mana to cast Level Seven Spells. Sorry, most Level Seven Spells.”

Labonte’s eyes light up.

She looks back to me. “I understand you have a new ability you need testing. Some sort of shield against spells?”

“Not exactly a shield,” I say. “Aura. [Magic Sapper]. It’s supposed to weaken spellcasting within range. And I can counterspell certain spells within my aura too.”

Charlotte’s expression sharpens with genuine interest.

“That’s very useful.”

“Yeah,” I say. “It should work.”

“Love the confidence,” Charlotte snorts.

“I should’ve said whether it will work enough.”

“There it is!” Labonte chimes in.

Charlotte steps backward, putting distance between us. Her posture changes as she moves. Not dramatically. Just a subtle settling of weight, a shift through her hips and shoulders, like the casual woman who walked in has stepped aside and let the System User take the room. My [Perception] catches tiny sparks along her fingertips. Red-gold and thin as hair. They coil around her knuckles and vanish.

Charlotte rolls her neck once.

“Alrighty, Joseph Sullivan,” she says, “are you ready to have this aura of yours pushed to its limit?”

I roll my shoulders.

My Health and Stamina have both recovered enough for another bad idea. I grin despite myself.

“Sure,” I say. “Hit me.”
