She Who Stills The Waters left her writings to Li Shu. In those writings, there came a description of the different steps of power progression for Witches.

The first step: Apprenticeship. A learning step, very literally, undertaken as a sort of preliminary exploration. Both more and less fundamental than the Qi-Gathering realm, focused entirely on gaining new instincts and information relating to alkemy, physical properties, plants, biology, and more. As a cultivator tends to prepare for the first steps on their journey by improving their Qi channels and body, a Witch, or Craft wielder, prepares their mind.

The second step has a much fancier name.

“Crafting Of Self”.

Here, part of one’s being is severed, unmade, rewritten from being a concept connected directly to a person to something connected indirectly, through will and Qi alone. A Sacrifice for power.

Li Shu is rapidly approaching a further step, has been researching, in her own time, what it might entail. “Blessed Union”. The energies, saturating and infused into the Sacrifice over time, is more deeply embedded, a more foundational change. Like She Who Stills The Water’s hair, which grew out of shadows and could act as portals through them. Raika’s pretty sure she can guess what concepts Li Shu will be embracing, but frankly, it’s not really her business.

She’s been more focused on other shit anyways.

Crafting Of Self. A profound sacrifice, transforming a person forever. A Sacrifice of something intrinsic, forever alienated and made inhuman in turn, never again to be touched.

She considered a lot of options. Her remaining arm, the one that hasn’t been replaced by Blacksteel. Or her sight, maybe, removing even the possibility of conventional vision. Her ability to form arrays, even.

But if there is one thing Raika has remained quite dedicated to, it’s a fairly simple ideal.

“Go big or go home”.

The closest thing she ever got to a home, a real home, was a little log cabin with people who trusted her. Who healed her. Who helped her learn to be.

She found a way to take it with her, and even still, someone took it from her. Killed it from her.

So. All that’s available is to go big.

The ritual does not perform a particularly specific phenomena. Interwoven with the arrays of the chamber she’s in, intermixed with that of the collapsing formation orbiting in the islands that remain, is a simple and broad form of magic. It does not create a specific effect.

It opens the door to almost any.

As the pillar of Obliteration burns the world, as a sky made of stabbing Death cuts apart reality, as a million guns annihilate everything they can perceive and fire upon, the magics that have been painstakingly carved into Godfall’s advancing army activate at last.

To gain power is to create reality. For cultivators, they craft a world and a demigod within and of themselves. For necromancers, they find the gaps left behind in existence and magnify them, altering all things to match the echo they resonate with.

For a Craft user… they tell reality a Lie, and from it, a story.

Reality turns to LOOK.

Infinity turns inward. A recursion of all that is, bright and loud and quiet and dark, the lowest atoms to the highest phenomena. Attention is drawn from things which are best avoided, and the world, briefly, fills with an impossible DISGUST.

Existence looks upon itself, and finds it, and everything within, DISGUSTING. VILE. REPUGNANT.

A dream turned rotten, because it is not enough, because it is not right, because it is not good.

The Heavens and that which lies beyond them, in some infinitesimal fraction, turn their gaze upon the wretched world they have made.

In that single instant of attention, Raika Lies.

Lu Karai feels it when the gold cracks.

Lu Karai is dead. He cannot feel anything. Even the emotions, at this point, are distant things, echoed more than experienced. His love, his greed, his desires, all remain too anchored to be lost entirely, save to Stillness- and it has come closer than he would like to admit, more than once. But actual sensation? An alien thing. It has been centuries since he abandoned even the memory of flesh for bone, and longer still since he embraced the cold brilliance of gold.

He feels it when the gold cracks.

A fine fragmentation, delicate and lustrous, running up through the gilding that is his bones, that is his being.

In theory, that shouldn’t be possible here. Not here in the heart of the Fallen Kingdom, where Death is universal and the power that he exists within and grows from is ubiquitous. In a place of perfect, absolute and cancerous growth, perhaps, in a seal designed perfectly by one of the false gods of what they call the Overgrowth, but not here.

For a moment, Death vanishes from the world.

He feels a pull from reserves untouched in… as long as he can remember, really, reinforcing his existence upon the world. Near-perfect efficiency drives his Death into Existence, and his Comprehension reinforces his place in reality. He remains, as he always will, until everything Ends.

But for a moment…

The foundations slipped.

He turns to his wife, and feels his internal self roil in surprise.

She is standing.

Since he met her, a few millennia past, she has lain in repose, golden and gilded, within her sarcophagal phylactery.

She rarely even moves- to rise from her magical hovering, to float upright alongside her pet Witch, is nearly unheard of.

He transmits to her, through the Echoes they share, the post-mortal post-lungs equivalent of a sigh.

“Oh love of mine, what a mess you’ve made.”

She turns her head to look to him- another sign of just how out of sorts she is.

“I hardly see us getting our hands on the little Blessed now, dear.”

She doesn’t say anything, even through the flow of Echoes which bind them. Instead, she turns, facing her skull at the all-consuming radiance blasting a pillar into the ground and sky.

And the thing writhing within it.

To visual senses, it is already blinding. A million colors, and colors that exist beyond visible spectrums, flood the earth, turning the ashen wastelands of the Fallen Kingdom into a kaleidoscope. In several spots where the different forces shine brightest, the world actively changes, Qi forming and being consumed, strange new mineral formations and life spawning beneath the glow. In one spot, a plant made of something like crystals gives birth to a spasming spider of hooves, which vomits out a swarm of floating liquids and then collapses- and it’s only one of hundreds of such sights, all over the space around them.

To those who Comprehend the layers of real-versus-unreal, it’s the least interesting part.

At the center of the pillar is a void.

Striations and pulses of energy are thrown about, only to inevitably orbit back and collapse back towards the center of the mass. Like a sea, struggling and swarming away from a far-off shore, only to be forced back to the core over and over again, the process organic and mechanical at once.

Some of the ways that the Death at the center of the radiance moves look like hands, desperately reaching to escape.

Only to be unmade and returned, digested again and again.

It looks like it might collapse at any moment.

It looks like it might birth something out of that collapse.

Lu Karai’s wife slowly returns to her previous stillness, her phylactery returning to a gentler repose.

“We’ll have to move quickly,” she says.

“More foolishness, then.”

She sends no annoyance through the connection between them. In spite of his words, he knows that she knows that he is already putting his pieces in place.

Such is love.

Kai Valdir is not a particularly proud specimen.

It is not easy to tell by looking at him, because by the Church he knows how to dress, but he really, truly is not. He lounges, yes, and prefers finer vintages, it’s true, and prefers things a certain way, quite true. But proud? A silly idea. What a joke! A farcical comment to even make about such a figure as he!

No, Kai Valdir, a man of particularly common name and particularly bland talents, with little to his name except most of a city, a plot to overthrow a nation, and an eye for the most powerful upset on which to bet since the Corpse Martyr, is not particularly proud.

He is appropriately proud.

It is important, after all, to humbly and generously accept praise when it is properly earned.

Looking out at the massive orb of spasming, roiling impossibility in the distance, impaled on a stick-thin pillar of world-spanning scale, he thinks to himself- if anyone has earned praise for this turn of events, it is he.

A thrall emerges from out of one of their little alcoves, summoned by the barest hint of a change on the current face of the Valdir clan. They’re all well-trained; if they guessed wrong, or hesitated in their guess, they’d find themselves on the more unfortunate end of their retirement plan. This one, a veteran of nearly ten years of service, who has yet to guess wrong, has once again proven their value in their current position, and brought him the pen and paper he desired.

Quickly enough that non-enchanted paper would catch flame, Kai Valdir pens a series of letters and announcements, the quill flying across the pages as fast as the thrall can keep up with. The moment they’re done, he lets the quill fall, trusting that the thrall will catch it or suffer the consequence.

“Take that to the other houses and to the worriers among the elders,” he says dismissively. His eyes have never left the window he is watching from.

The fungal farms need batteries, after all, and rot does nicely for feeding the food. Not the worst fate, but there are better destinies than to be buried, squirming and limbless, beneath the soil, just undead enough to be good fertilizer.
