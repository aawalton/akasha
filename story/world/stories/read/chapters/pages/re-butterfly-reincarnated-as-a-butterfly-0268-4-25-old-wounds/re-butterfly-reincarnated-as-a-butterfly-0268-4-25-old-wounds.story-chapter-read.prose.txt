With the sun setting, the party stepped out of the trees and onto the path that led up to the hamlet.

William and Frederick attempted to disguise their weakness more now that they were about to be among strangers, but that effort quickly faltered. They found themselves forced to lean on their walking sticks or other party members to move forward, and there was simply no hiding that.

Despite her apparent confidence in her countrymen’s honor, Rosslyn moved slowly, carefully, trying to limit how visible the group was from each cottage by keeping their profiles lined up  against those of the nearby trees. The low lighting from sunset probably helped with keeping too many of the residents of the place from seeing the fugitive royals and their comrades.

After a minute of slowly advancing, the party finally approached close enough to one of the houses for Rosslyn to knock softly at the door.

There was a loud creak from within, and the sound of heavy, oddly uneven footsteps approached the door. One foot sounded distinctly different from the other, as if the mover wore two different pairs of shoes. Then there was a sound of someone leaning on the wood of the door as a small wooden slat slid aside and an eye looked out at them. It was a primitive peephole that didn’t require glass.

Finally, a knob turned, and a grizzled old man looked out at them from the doorway with an expression of disbelief.

“Is it truly you again?” he said quietly. “Your Royal Highness?”

He looked at Rosslyn with the air of one who knew her.

“Myself and the whole Royal Family, plus guests,” the Queen said with a slightly anxious smile. “Would you please let us come in?”

The owner of the cottage looked nervously into her eyes and bowed low.

Then Adon was able to read in both the Queen’s and the old man’s minds how they had met before.

When she joined the military, he was one of her enlisted. A Sergeant.

He glimpsed flashes of memory from both of them, mostly snippets from a single incident around which they each had mixed emotions.

They were near one of Claustria’s borders. Not the contentious border with the Demon Empire. A region that had been peaceful for years. Their guards were down.

Rosslyn’s squad had been patrolling and almost ready to return to their fortification, a few miles back, when the Sergeant realized they were being followed. As soon as he made Rosslyn aware, she enhanced her senses with magic. She was quickly able to detect the rough position of the potential enemy. She ordered the squad to conceal themselves, as the enemy were approaching so quickly that they would soon be within sight.

Sergeant Angus questioned that decision. Something seemed off to him. He wanted to confront their pursuers with steel in hand.

He did not so much argue with his commanding officer as simply not rush to obey. He stood there, posing questions, what-ifs about her plan.

For a few additional seconds, he and two other men who agreed with him remained standing while Rosslyn and the rest of the squad dropped out of the line of sight. In the middle of one of the standing soldiers asking a question, the pursuers stepped into view.

The enemy commander immediately sensed that something was wrong—the number of men they could see was far fewer than the number of tracks they had been following—and he and his men loosed dozens of arrows within thirty seconds of moving close enough to be observed. A few archers aimed at the men they could see, but most of them fired their arrows into the bushes and through the trees where they guessed people might be hiding. The encounter had turned to violence before enough time had elapsed for Rosslyn or the Sergeant to communicate with them or even recognize who they were dealing with.

The result was that the Sergeant and the other two men with him were struck with arrows, though miraculously, no others were.

Rosslyn’s squad responded with a flurry of arrows of their own.

The chaos and fighting continued for a few minutes until Rosslyn exploded a fireball over the pursuers’ heads and shouted a command to drop their weapons and surrender.

The enemy dropped to their knees, immediately aware that they could not take on a magic user coupled with the number of men required to shoot so many arrows.

The future Queen approached the opponents and confirmed that they had thrown their weapons at their feet, then stood watch over the group while a few scouts confirmed there were no other enemies lying in wait for the squad to lower their guard again.

She had a quick conversation with the leader of their side and learned that this was just a group of groundskeepers and some hired muscle patrolling a Galton noble’s land, looking for poachers. They had gotten a bit turned around and crossed the border into Claustria, still thinking they were on the trail of a band of criminals.

This was all confirmed by the embarrassed noble employer later, and the man paid reparations for the damages and inconvenience.

In the moment, having secured the scene, Rosslyn and her men could finally begin to account for the damage that had been done.

They discovered that two of their men—the men who had been standing with Sergeant Angus—were near death, while the Sergeant was bleeding heavily from a wound that looked to be nonlethal.

Rosslyn successfully healed the other two soldiers’ life-threatening injuries with magic. But she did not make it to the Sergeant before the arrow wound he’d suffered, coupled with the emergency first aid administered—a badly applied tourniquet—cost him the leg.

As the grizzled former Sergeant stepped backward into his home, his body language inviting the Royal Family and their entourage inside, Angus’s two steps back made distinctly different sounds. One was the ordinary tap of shoe on floor. The other was wood on wood, a peg leg.

Rosslyn led the group into the cottage. Adon’s eyes took in the whole main room of the place with a single look. It was humble but comfortable, in his assessment.

There was a large sword mounted on the wall, as if the retired soldier was prepared to take on all comers. In reality, Adon could see the man was very out of shape compared with a couple of years back, when the events from the memories took place.

“I heard a little about the events happening at the capital,” Sergeant Angus said awkwardly as the door shut behind them. “I would have been there, in the fight, if not for—” He tapped his leg.

“You have already served your country more than enough, Sergeant,” Rosslyn said. “I only regret the abrupt end to your career.”

He bowed his head in an acknowledgment of what she’d said, then said, “Given that you are here, I assume matters in Wayn have not gone well. You have only to give me the order, and anything I can attempt to be of assistance, I will do. If you are looking for support, there are many loyal men and women in the area…”

“I appreciate your hospitality,” the Queen said quietly.

“It is mere duty, Your Highness,” Angus replied. “What any loyal citizen ought to do.”

Adon could feel the uneasiness of the shared memory below the surface. Both the Queen and the Sergeant blamed themselves for what had happened, both to him and to the other soldiers, whose injuries had healed better but still not perfectly.

“Nothing ‘mere’ about it, Sergeant,” Rosslyn said. “We are in some danger. You may be in danger as well, by harboring us. Is it safe here?”

“You will be safe as long as I have breath in my body, Highness,” he said passionately. “The Empire will not have you or your guests except by walking over my corpse.”

“Sacrifice is not what I want,” the Queen replied in a gentle tone. “You have given enough, as I said before, and discretion is the order of the day, not valor. I only want to know if the Empire has reached this place yet. Is it a security issue we must be concerned with?”

Angus shook his head. Adon detected a trace of relief within him. The man was willing to sacrifice himself for Rosslyn and the others, but he was also glad to be reassured he would not actually be called upon to do it.

“They have actually taken the capital, then?” he asked.

“They killed my father and felled the city walls while my friends and I were putting down a local dungeon,” Rosslyn replied. “We had to flee in order to keep them from capturing us and legitimizing their rule over the territory by either extinguishing the royal line or forcing one of us into a sham marriage.”

“My condolences, Your Majesty,” the Sergeant said carefully. “Your father was a great man and well loved here.”

“I know it, and he knew it.” They shared a small, sad smile.

“Well, I have some stew simmering on the fireplace, there,” he said, gesturing at a large pot. “It should be edible, though I cannot promise quality. What is mine is yours, so please help yourselves. Your sick friends there can take my bed or the couch. I assume those two are the famous Dessian brothers who showed up to court you?”

“Guilty,” Frederick managed. He looked pale, and Adon could sense he felt worse than he looked.

“They seem to have taken ill—” the Sergeant began.

“Our friends were attacked by assassins and defended my family honorably,” Rosslyn said, cutting him off. “Demon Empire assassins wield poisoned weapons, because honor is not important to them. So, although the surface injuries could be healed, the poison has persisted. It may yet cause lasting consequences.”

“I see,” Angus replied with a sympathetic look to the brothers. He turned back to Rosslyn and asked, “Would Your Majesty be comfortable with speaking with a trusted neighbor of mine? With a brief conversation, we could arrange another couple of beds without having to alert the whole community that royalty is sleeping under our roofs.”

The Queen assented, and she went out with Angus, leaving the others to make themselves as at home as they could in the former soldier’s cottage.

Frederick and William staggered to the bed Angus had gestured at a couple of minutes before and practically collapsed into it. They didn’t even bother undressing, simply falling into what Adon sensed was a feverish sleep nearly instantly.

The butterfly turned his attention to Goldie now that they were settled and tried to talk to her, but the spider was understandably still withdrawn and put him off gently but firmly.

We will talk tomorrow, Adon, the spider sent. I just want to rest for now. I have done little physically, but I am tired.

All right, he replied. I’ll be here. Just let me know.

Then Rosslyn was back, bearing good news and a small smile. As Angus had promised, she had received the promise of a bed for Carolien and herself and one for the children.

“We have much to be grateful for as well as much to mourn today,” she said in a contemplative tone of voice. Adon fluttered over to her, ready for the inevitable crash once her adrenaline stopped moving her and she felt her mission was accomplished.

But the expected breakdown did not come yet. Within the Queen, there was a taut string of discipline that she still refused to relax.

In her mind, they were not safe enough yet for her to let go. Not until they were across the border and into Dessia—or Galton, or Parmonia, or any friendly country that was not currently being taken over by the Empire.

Baltazar opted to bunk on the couch in the Sergeant’s cottage, so that the Dessians would not wake up alone and his younger siblings could have more room to themselves.

We’ll be all right, Rosslyn, Adon quietly reassured her.

She simply gave him a weary nod in response. Everyone seemed near their limit of physical and mental endurance.

The group settled in for the night, uneasy and exhausted but apparently safe.

Hopefully sunrise would find them safe and provide a chance to begin healing some of the wounds of the day.
