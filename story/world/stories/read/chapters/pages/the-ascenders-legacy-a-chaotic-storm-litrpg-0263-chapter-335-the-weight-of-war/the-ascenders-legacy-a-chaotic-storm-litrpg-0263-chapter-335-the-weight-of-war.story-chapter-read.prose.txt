Elder Vasmos's apartment was crowded when Aodhán and Dravion arrived, cheers echoing through the space as elders laughed and celebrated her breakthrough. It shouldn't have been such a big deal. Mythics gained familiars all the time, but for someone so close to the Calamity class not to have gained one, Elder Vasmos had practically been written off.

"One would think she's won the lottery," Dravion muttered arrogantly as they approached the house. "She didn't even gain the familiar herself. She had external help."

Aodhán glanced at him with a slight frown. "It doesn't matter how she got it."

"Of course it does." Dravion glared at him. "She's a Mythic. If you and I can do it on our own, then she should have been able to."

Aodhán’s frown deepened. "We are transmigrants, Dravion. We are not the yardstick."

Dravion snorted in disagreement, but before he could respond, an elder spotted them and promptly shouted, "Hey, Ashscar is here!"

Immediately, people turned, smiles widening as their gazes landed on him. Dravion muttered something under his breath before plastering a smile on his face. The elders gathered around him, asking when he'd returned and how his time in the world rift had been.

Aodhán watched him for a moment before turning to search the crowd for a familiar face. It didn't take him long to find Elder Luseph's sardonic glare, and he promptly made his way toward him, bowing as he pushed through the throng of people.

Elder Luseph saw him before he arrived and let out an exasperated sigh, muttering something to his neighbors, whom Aodhán now identified as Elder Kobi and Sedgwick.

He bowed deeply when he finally arrived. "Good afternoon, elders. It's so nice to see you here."

"We called you," Luseph muttered. "Surely, you can't be surprised to see us."

"Lay off the kid, Luseph." Elder Sedgwick chuckled and gestured for Aodhán to sit beside them. "We're celebrating the success of the pendant. And you were as much a contributor to its creation as we all are. You deserve to celebrate with us."

Aodhán smiled and sat with them, accepting a bottle of bubbling wine from Elder Kobi. He took a sip of the liquid and groaned as the wine burned its way down his throat.

Aodhán beat his chest, gasping. "What is that drink?"

Elder Kobi chuckled. "Hot, eh?"

"Scalding," Aodhán managed, his voice hoarse. “What the hell is this?”

Elder Luseph's mouth twitched. "Leviathan piss. High-grade sect brew. You're supposed to take it like a veteran."

Aodhán shook his head and conjured a jug of water for himself to ease the burn. After three huge gulps, he placed the jug down.

“I’m never drinking that again.” He glanced around the house. “Where is Elder Vasmos? I want to see her familiar.”

Elder Sedgwick grinned. "She's out back. Beautiful creature she's gained, though it's not any I've ever seen before."

Elder Luseph nodded. "She calls it a Gryphon."

Aodhán's eyes widened. "A Gryphon? Are you serious?"

“It looks like a chimera to me.” Elder Luseph muttered, and Aodhán jumped to his feet, excitement racing through him as he rushed out back to see it. He burst out of the house a moment later, and his steps immediately faltered at the sight of the massive beast dominating the backyard.

It stood nearly eight feet tall, all storm and lightning—lion-bodied, eagle-headed, wings spread wide enough to blot out the sky. Lightning crawled across its feathers like living veins, and the ground beneath its talons smoked faintly where power bled through.

It was no different from the pictures Aodhán had seen back in the monastery, and yet they couldn't be termed the same. This creature wasn't one born of flesh, but of lightning and chaos. And from its size, it was obvious it was no cub.

To his senses, the gryphon shone as bright as a sun, blazing with the same intensity with which Elder Vasmos blazed, her smile wide as she stroked the gryphon's fur—which wasn't really fur at all, but a mantle of storm-silked strands that shimmered between silver and deep cobalt.

It was beautiful. It was powerful. And as Aodhán gazed at it, he realized just how instrumental he had been to its formation. This familiar would not have existed if not for him.

I GAIN STRENGTH TO GIVE IT.

His ideal hummed within him at the thought, and he shuddered, a wide smile blooming on his face. Elder Vasmos saw him then and waved him over.

Aodhán hesitated a moment before moving toward her, his heart thudding in his chest as he approached the regal creature, his gaze flicking between its powerful beak and gleaming claws—claws so long Aodhán was certain they could cleave him in two before he could even blink.

The thought sent a spike of fear through him, and he paused, unable to bring himself to go any further.

Elder Vasmos laughed. "Oh, don't be scared. Thryx knows you're a friend, and she's a good girl."

She?

Aodhán glanced back at the gryphon, his gaze locking with the two brilliant, unblinking orbs of molten gold, and for a moment, he saw past the danger, monstrosity, and regality.

He saw confusion, not the dull confusion of a beast lacking reason, but the sharp aching uncertainty of something newly born into a world it did not yet understand. There was no malice in that gaze. No predatory hunger. Only awareness—vast, overwhelming awareness—and the instinctive struggle to adapt.

A creature torn from one form of existence and thrust into another.

In those molten eyes, Aodhán saw a reflection of himself—that unmistakable sense of displacement, that quiet knowledge that something fundamental had been left behind.

Aodhán remembered the feeling well. He had worn the same expression once. Had felt the same discordance. The same happiness to explore a new world, yet aching with the same knowledge that this was not the world he had been born into.

He realized then that familiars weren't just partners or gifts from the origin plane. They were transmigrants too.

They were kin.

The thought stirred an inspiration within him, only a flutter, but that flutter filled his heart with courage, and with confident steps, he walked forward, his gaze fixed on that of the gryphon.

When he got within a few feet, Elder Vasmos chuckled nervously. "You might want to stop there, Aodhán. She hasn't quite let anyone else touch her."

"She'll let me," Aodhán whispered, his gaze straying from the gryphon for a moment. "If that's okay with you, of course."

Elder Vasmos hesitated, and that was when Aodhán realized how silent the backyard had become. The elders were all watching to see if the gryphon would treat him differently. Even Elder Luseph and Kobi had come to the backyard, their gazes alert despite their loose stances.

Elder Vasmos stuttered. "I— It's okay with me. Go ahead."

Aodhán glanced back at the gryphon, took a few more steps toward it, and very slowly placed his hand against its fur. Lightning prickled his fingers, far more powerful than what Varéc produced, but still harmless.

He stroked the fur once, twice, eliciting a chorus of whispers from the gathered audience.

The gryphon made no sound; it barely even acknowledged his touch except for a faint shudder every time his fingers ran through its fur. Aodhán smiled. However, just as he was about to pull back, the gryphon turned to glance at him and growled—a low, resonant sound that seemed to ask, "Who are you?"

Elder Vasmos gasped in shock at the sound and turned to glance at Aodhán. "She's asking if—"

“I know what she’s asking." Aodhán’s smile widened as he locked gazes with the gryphon. “I am Aodhán Brystion. A friend of your bond. She’s going to take very good care of you. You don’t have to worry.”

The gryphon stared at him for a moment, then at Elder Vasmos, whose marble-white skin brightened from whatever it said to her in that moment.

She smiled and glanced at Aodhán. "Thank you. For everything."

"You're welcome."

Elder Vasmos nodded, and with a burst of willpower, the gryphon vanished into her spirit space. She turned to the gathered elders. "Thryx needs rest. I was too excited to show her off that I didn't see that. Thank you all for coming."

The elders murmured their understanding, and though a few lingered, their gazes flicking between Aodhán and Elder Vasmos with barely concealed curiosity, many began leaving, saying their goodbyes now that the gryphon was gone.

Elder Vasmos watched them leave with a slightly pained expression, and after a while she whispered, "She wanted to rest after a night of rift delving, but I was too excited to show her off that I didn't listen." She glanced at Aodhán with a sad smile. "What does that say about me?"

Aodhán offered a reassuring smile. "That you're very proud of your familiar." He understood how she felt completely. When he'd gained Varéc, he'd wanted nothing more than to show him off. And in Elder Vasmos's case, that urge had probably been overwhelming.

Elder Vasmos didn't seem very reassured by his response, though, so he continued. "Our familiars tend to take on our personalities and reflect them back at us. When they're young, I think maybe it's easier to manage, but Thryx isn't young, and though I'm not sure about all of this, it's possible she's developed a personality of her own before you came along and is now dealing with a discordance of personalities."

"So, you're saying I should be patient."

Aodhán nodded. "Yeah. Give her time to become more like you. To know you. And maybe don't place her in front of a crowd for a while. Gryphons are proud, intelligent, and socially reserved creatures, after all."

Elder Vasmos smiled. "And you know all this how?"

Aodhán shrugged. "I am a transmigrant, Elder Vasmos. I know many strange things."

She chuckled, and they settled into a comfortable silence, simply watching as the other elders laughed and partied among themselves. The silence lasted for a few seconds before Aodhán asked, "What was the experience like?"

Elder Vasmos fingered the pendant on her neck and smiled. "It was new." Her smile widened, and she shook her head. "All this time, the whispers of chaos never made sense, and when I evolved to the Mythic class, I gained the ability to block them out completely. The pendant changed that."

She frowned in remembrance. "When I entered the Origin Plane this time, it was like standing in a crowded market with thousands of people shouting all at once. Buyers, sellers, advertisers—everyone was speaking, all of them distant and inaudible, until the pendant warmed against my chest."

"It filtered all that noise in an instant, and suddenly I could only hear the voice of a single merchant selling what I wanted to buy."

"A familiar."

She nodded. "The pendant knew exactly what I wanted, and it isolated the chaos whisper that would provide my solution."

Aodhán frowned as he processed her words. His own experience with the pendant hadn't been so clear, but then again, he wasn't a Mythic, nor had he known the true function of the pendant at the time.

Nevertheless, the implications of Elder Vasmos's words were insane, and Aodhán suddenly realized a point of concern he hadn't thought about before.

"Have you tried using the pendant again after gaining Thryx?"

Elder Vasmos chuckled, understanding his worry. "You're wondering if I can simply hear whatever whispers of chaos I choose now? Simply create anything I want?"

Aodhán's heart thundered. "Can you? Because if you can, then that is terrifying."

Elder Vasmos laughed. "Relax. I doubt the system would ever allow the creation of such an item. And yes, I tried again. How could I not?"

Aodhán stared at her for a moment, his expression falling. "It didn't work?"

"It did, but it was different. The chaos whispers weren't as clear. The pendant never warmed, and the whispers remained inaudible."

"Perhaps it has a cooldown."

"Or perhaps it was because I had no aching desire for anything. Think of the first time you used the pendant this way—you said you had a desire for a partner to get through the tower thing."

Aodhán nodded.

She continued. "I believe that's the common thread here. Desire. The stronger the desire for something, the clearer the whispers."

Aodhán's frown deepened. "That's still dangerous."

Elder Vasmos nodded slowly. "It is, but I doubt the system allowed you to create this item without enforcing some kind of restrictions or limitations. We'll never know until we test it well."

Aodhán nodded. The next time he accessed the Origin Plane, he would definitely be testing all these theories, but until then, they had over five hundred pendants waiting to be distributed.

No doubt the High Elders had been informed of Elder Vasmos's breakthrough by now. They would want to begin distribution immediately. And that meant they would have to create more pendants.

The war was in two days, and if they could somehow double the number of familiars within the sect, it would help greatly.

Elder Gumi would need his help, and though he would gain more for himself if he spent the next two days meditating and working on his own strength, Aodhán didn't intend to do that at all.

He may have started this war, but it wasn't about him. Not anymore.

It was about the sect as a whole. It was about his new family. And Aodhán was determined to give them every advantage he could. After all, he gained strength to give it.
