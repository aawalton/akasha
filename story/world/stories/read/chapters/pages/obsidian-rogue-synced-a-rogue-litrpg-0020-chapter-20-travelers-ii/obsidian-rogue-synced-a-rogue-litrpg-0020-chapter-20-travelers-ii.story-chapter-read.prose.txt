Elite Stables

Levi stared at the sign overhead the stable that Xara had stopped at. The mage had led him to the edge of the city and, more specifically, to a stable. The mage gestured for him to follow her into the stable, and so he did. Upon entering the stable, he was pleasantly surprised by the lack of odor. He’d expected the place to smell terrible since it was a stable, but there seemed to be a shocking lack of odor. In fact, he could pick out hints of jasmine in the air. The place smelled more like a perfume store than a stable.

He used his eyes to scan every inch of the stables, ignoring the tens of matas occupying the stables. He looked around for runes, but he couldn’t spot any. To the rogue, his inability to easily spot any rune did not mean that there weren't runes in the stables. Conceding defeat in his search, he gazed at the beasts. They looked well fed, perhaps even more than he. Matas were four-legged and beasts of burden. Their coats came in three different colors: black, brown, and white matas—according to what he heard. Regardless of their colours, the steeds were all majestic and of fur were perfect to say the least.

“Good day, how can I help you?”

“We want to get steeds.”

“Okay, but I think I should inform you that the matas we sell here are high-end and as such their prices might be a bit too much,” The stable owner said after sizing them up.

“You shouldn’t bother yourself with how deep our pockets are. “ Xara replied. “ Just let us have a look at your best steeds.”

Levi stayed quiet, observing the interaction between the two. His eyes, hidden beneath his hood, were drifting towards them. The exchange had seemed polite on the surface, but even a moron could see the silent pokes and jabs Xara and the matas dealer had thrown about in just a few short words. It was fascinating to say the least. From where he was, people were a lot more … upfront with their feelings. Some might have seen it as them being crass, but Levi saw it in another light. He saw it as being honest. These people were fake, even to themselves.

“Alright, follow me,” the dealer said.

The pair walked through the various steeds to the back of the stable, where three matas were grazing. The three were of different colors: black, brown, and white. The dealer walked right up to the black matas, which were tied to a pole. He gestured for them to come closer before he started speaking. “If you want strong and athletic matas, then you should pick this one. It’s best for travelling and riding, but I would warn you to beware that black matas are known as the hot bloods.”

Allowing them to digest the information he’d just given them, he moved on to the next matas, the white matas. “This mata here is cold-blooded. Not really much in the way of athleticism or strength, but it is a beauty to behold. It’s very calm and can be used for light riding and breeding. I’m afraid it isn't good for much other than that. Of course, all that can be improved upon with further training, but this is not just the type of beast that can handle much.”

Then he moved on to the last mata. “This one is a mixed breed, gotten from white and black matas. It is warm-blooded, which means its temper is right in the middle, and while it's not comparable to the black matas in terms of hard riding, it is a reasonable alternative. And it is the preferred steed for pulling carts, because of its lukewarm and okay athleticism. It all depends on what you want.”

“We’d be needing the steeds for travelling.”

“If that's the case, then I’d advise you to take the black matas. It's faster and has better endurance than the other two.”

“What do you think, Levi?” Xara asked.

“It’s best to take the black matas, like he said,” Levi replied.

He wasn't too stressed about which Matas they’d end up getting. Levi hadn’t ever thought about riding one of these; in fact, he didn’t know how to. The matas were creatures he’d only heard about by word of mouth. Seeing them in the flesh was a whole other thing from having them described to him. In short, the description he’d gotten had done the matas a big disservice.

“Okay, “ She said to him before turning to the dealer. “We’ll take one black and one white matas.”

“Fantastic choice,” The dealer said enthusiastically.

“White?” Levi asked incredulously.

“I’m still a noble,” she whispered.

Grakking hell?

For the love of everything sane, he couldn’t understand why the mage had chosen a white mata; a steed like that was bound to stand out and make them more noticeable at a time when he was trying to be invisible. It seemed counterintuitive to him, but she was the one paying, and if she couldn’t deal with the kind of attention it could potentially bring later on, then he’d have to part ways with her.

They weren’t running away to some fairy tale land. They were running for their lives… Well, his life. Then again, if Ghost eventually found them, he supposed that it wouldn't matter to the rogue who Xara was. At least that was the vibe he’d gotten when he’d had that discussion about Ghost with the mage who’d given him the stone.

“It seems like there's a bit of a disagreement between you two, I could—”

“No, there's no disagreement.” Xara and Levi shouted in unison.

The pair had shouted because the dealer had delivered his words in such a way that it made it seem like he thought that they were a couple.

“Good to hear. The Mata’s will cost you a hundred gold each, and that brings your total to two hundred gold.” The dealer said before abruptly clearing his throat. “ I’d also like to inform you both that we do not haggle in the Elite stable. Our prices are final, and if that does not sit well with you, then I’m sure that you can find your way out.”

“We weren’t planning to haggle, in fact, dear I say, your prices were too cheap. Xara retorted. “I suppose that was to be expected, considering the quality of your steed. Regardless of their pitiful condition, we'll take them.”

“ Okay, Cash or transfer,” The dealer said with a thin smile.

“Transfer,” Xara replied, and then paused as her eyes glossed over. “I’ve sent it.

Huh?... She must’ve analyzed the dealer to know his name.

“Received,” The dealer said.” Let me escort you so you can pick from any of the steeds outside.”

“That wouldn’t be necessary. We'll take the white and black matas in front of us.”

“I-I…As you wish.”

Levi was glad that his hood had covered his face for the entire interaction because it seemed like the mage and the dealer were having a word fight disguised as a negotiation. They’d both thrown word jabs at each other, with neither showing any facial reaction to the other’s words. He had to admit that the lifestyle of the rich was odd. Their mannerisms had him confused and, at the same time, amused.

The dealer had gone about untying the matas they'd bought from the poles and handing the reins over to Xara and Levi, and within a short time, they were both outside the stable, with the mage and rogue both holding their steeds by the reins.

“Do you know how to ride a steed ?” she asked, when they'd walked a reasonable distance away from the stable.

“No.”

“As expected," She sighed.

“Yeah,” he said. Even though she hadn’t said it in a way that belittled his background, her words still stung.

“If you'd known that I wouldn’t be able to ride, why did you spend that much gold to buy me one?”

“You're the... You know,” she whispered. “You need to learn these kinds of things, and you sure as grak weren’t going to ride with me.”

“Is that so?” Levi chuckled. He couldn’t help but notice how her face had turned a light shade of pink when she’d said the part about them riding together.

“Yes. It’s not just about the position you hold at the higher levels. It’s the way you comport yourself… It’s about the way you move. It's very distasteful if someone like you can’t ride a mata.”

“Someone like me?”

“Yes. Someone who belongs on the council.”

“That may be, but I don’t care about what anybody else thinks.”

“Evidently.”

A tense silence enveloped the pair even as they moved through the noisy streets, getting closer and closer to the edge of the city. They both preferred the silence at the moment; neither was willing to continue with the conversation that had seemed harmless at the beginning. The rogue had refused to respond because he’d had nothing nice to say, and he could tell that he wasn’t the only one. For all their posturing, neither he nor the mage was eager to end their new partnership, and so until something less insulting came to either of their minds, the silence would reign.

“Where are we headed?”

“The northern gate.” She replied dryly. “ We'll use that to get on the main road and begin our journey to Plantrith. It’d take us a few days by road, but we should be fine.”

The rogue nodded in understanding, and the silence returned, sticking to the pair like a wet blanket, a blanket that was lifted once they reached the northern gate. Two guards with dragon insignia on their armor stood at opposite sides of the gate. They were silent, and neither of them reacted to either his or Xaras' presence, which suited the rogue. Levi couldn’t help but stare at the massive gate attached to equally massive walls. It was now that he realized that the city was actually walled; he hadn’t been able to notice that since he’d come through a safe room within the city. His eyes widened as he focused on the details of the gate; it seemed to be made out of some kind of metal, and dozens of runes were etched on the surface. He couldn't help but wonder what they did.

“It's mainly to prevent monster attacks.” The mage said. Even though his hood had covered his face, she’d picked up on his interest in the gate.

“You guys get those here?”

“We do,” she nodded. “But it's not as bad as it is now; the attacks seemed to happen more frequently over the last five years.”

“That’s crazy.”

The same could be said for Gantus; his hometown might not have suffered many attacks, but it was no secret that the forest surrounding the single dungeon close to their town was now infested with monsters. The dungeon seemed to be leaking out monsters, and from what the mage had said, he had to believe that it was a general issue. Which meant something was up.

Levi tightened his grip on the reins of his mata as he led it through the gate. An ethereal feeling seemed to pass through him as he crossed to the other side. A slight shiver went up his spine at the weird sensation. Out of the corner of his eyes, he glanced at the mage, and he caught her staring full on at him.

“The sensation you just felt was just the scry rune doing its job. Each and everything that leaves and enters this town is scried… My great-grandfather added that rune,” She said proudly.“Impressive.”

“It isn't it,” she said sadly, before switching up to a cheery tone. ”Come on, now that we’re out of the city, I'll show you how to ride a mata.”

“You will?”

“Yeah, and we can talk about what your plans are when you’ve got the hang of riding.”

“Alright.”
