They followed the trail down. The tunnel sloped at a steady angle, wide enough that Clive could walk easily even while carrying Jill. Maleus led the party in the front while Azura's guarded the rear.

"How is this so wide?” Clive looked back at Azura who filled the passage behind him. Her wings stayed folded tight against her sides, wingtips occasionally dragging across the walls with a dry rasp. It may have looked uncomfortable, but Clive found it amazing that a dragon could even fit in a cave.

Every cave Clive had visited on Earth—Carlsbad Caverns, Mammoth Cave, that tourist trap in Kentucky with the gift shop—had required him to duck, to squeeze through narrow gaps, to turn sideways past jutting rock formations. Caves were cramped by definition. Tight spaces carved by water over millennia.

This tunnel could've accommodated several trucks.

"We moved all manner of creatures down this mountain in times gone past. Wyverns. Chimeras. Once, a hydra that required three wagons just for its heads." He glanced back, his eyes catching what little light filtered down from above. "A mere dragon is nothing in comparison."

Azura growled, releasing a rumble that vibrated through the stone. I am not a mere dragon.

I am Azura of the Sky Peaks. Daughter of Torax the Eternal. My lineage traces back seven generations to the First Flight. Her head lowered, bringing her massive eyes level with Maleus's face. Even in the dim light, her teeth shone. And I just raised claws against the Dragon King himself. So watch. Your. Tongue.

"Azura." Clive intervened. "He can’t hear you. And he didn't mean it."

Maleus stumbled, catching himself against the wall. "I meant no offense—"

She held Maleus's gaze for another moment, then pulled back. Fine.

Maleus exhaled slowly, his back still pressed against the wall. "I... apologize. To the dragon. If she's offended." He pushed off the wall, eyes staying on Azura for another moment before he turned back to the tunnel ahead.

They continued down.

The tunnel's character changed as they descended. Water trickled along channels carved into the floor. Apparently, caves had drainage systems as well. The air grew warmer with each turn. Not hot, but no longer the biting cold of the mountain heights.

Clive's shirt had mostly dried by the time the tunnel opened into a chamber.

"Here." Maleus stopped at the threshold, waving them forward with his good arm. "We can rest."

The chamber was natural. The ceiling arched fifteen feet overhead, lost in shadows. Stone formations jutted from the floor, and in the center, a pool of water reflected the faint light filtering down from cracks above.

Clive carried Jill to the far wall and lowered her carefully. She was still unconscious. Her breathing was shallow but steady. The bleeding had slowed, at least. He pulled off his jacket and folded it beneath her head.

Azura folded herself into the largest open space.

The remaining cultists collapsed where they stood. One woman—her left leg wrapped in makeshift bandages—leaned back against a stalagmite and closed her eyes immediately. Another sat with his head between his knees. He was clearly exhausted.

Maleus lowered himself more carefully. He propped himself against the wall. "We should be safe here. The tunnel splits twice more before this chamber. They won't know which path we took."

"How long until we reach the bottom?" Clive asked.

"Three hours. Maybe four." Maleus closed his eyes. "Depends how many breaks we need."

Clive settled against the wall next to Jill.

Everything still felt so surreal. Like he'd fallen asleep in one reality and woken in another.

Days ago, he'd stood beside Prince Sion as allies. They were going to stop Vandiel, and save San Dioral from their corruption. The mission had been clear.

Now he was hiding in a cave with those same enemies, running from the prince who'd called him friend.

The cultists had saved him. The same people he'd been fighting hours ago.

Why?

For Jill, obviously. She was one of them. Their Moon Mother. Whatever that meant.

Enemy of my enemy is my friend. The logic was simple enough. Sion wanted Jill dead, therefore anyone protecting Jill was now Clive's ally.

Except it wasn't that simple, was it?

Clive stared at Jill's unconscious face. Why did Jill tried to kill him, he still hadn’t figure that out. Come to think of it, why was he fighting Vandiel in the first place?

Because they were monsters.

That's what he'd been told. What he'd believed. They looked like monsters. Corrupted flesh, wrong proportions, bodies twisted by whatever dark magic they practiced. And they'd attacked him. First contact had been violence. Monsters attack people. That's what monsters do.

So they had to be monsters.

The logic had seemed solid then.

But if they were monsters, why was Jill with them? So much so that she would risk her own life than abandon them.

Maybe they weren't monsters. Maybe he had been wrong.

The thought sat in his stomach like a stone.

Clive took a look at Jill. She was still unconscious; he wouldn’t get any answers from her for a while. And given her silence, he wasn’t sure he would get anything from her even if she were awake.

But there were others weren’t there?

Clive looked at Maleus, who was burned, battered, and with one arm broken. The man had still come back, calling in reinforcements to save them. What kind of monster did that?

"Maleus." Clive spoke. "Please. Tell me everything. What is Vandiel?"

Maleus looked up. “What do you think it is?”

"I don't know anymore. I was told it was the source of the corruption. That the Demon King spreads it across Euchronia. That we were fighting to save humanity from being corrupted."

“Lies!” Maleus snapped. “ Complete lies. The Demon King didn’t cause the corruption. He stopped it. He is our saviour who saved us from the corruption of this world.

The vehemence in his voice made one of the other cultists stir in their sleep.

Clive held up a hand. "I'm listening. Tell me."

Maleus's good hand clenched into a fist against his lap. "Hundreds of years back, there was a plague. A vile plague that came from the sky. A comet crashed into the northern wastes. Where it hit, everything died. Plants. Animals. People. The land itself turned gray and lifeless."

A comet. Azura had mentioned this before. Perhaps there was truth to the old tale.

"The plague spread," Maleus continued. "Slow at first, then faster. Entire villages gone in weeks. The kingdoms did nothing. Euchronia, the Holy Church, all of them—they watched it happen and called it divine judgment. Said the afflicted must've sinned to deserve it."

Clive said nothing.

"The Demon King came from the east. Showed up with knowledge nobody else had. How to slow the spread. How to treat the symptoms. How to survive it." Maleus met Clive's eyes. "He saved us. Not the Church. Not your precious royalties."

"Then why does everyone call him a demon?"

Maleus shrugged with his good shoulder. "Because saving the 'wrong' people makes you a monster in San Dioral’s eyes."

Clive frowned, unsure who to believe. The world was a lot simpler when monsters were monsters.  Heroes fought monsters. It was comforting to believe he was on the right side.

But if Maleus was telling the truth, if they weren’t just monsters… then everything he’s done so far…

His chest felt tight but Clive shook his head, determined not to think too much about this. Politics had always made his head hurt. All he wanted was to be with Jill. That was all that mattered.

“What about Jill? Why is she with you?” He asked

“Jill?” Maleus paused. "You refer to the Moon Mother?”

Clive nodded.

Maleus studied him for a moment before replying. “ Before I answer that, tell me, what’re you to the Moon Mother?"

"I—" He looked down at Jill. "We were together. Before. We were in love. Sworn to always be together."

Maleus's eyebrows rose. "Huh. So you’re the one." He shifted, grimacing. "Six years she's been with us. Talked about someone from before, but never gave a name. Figured he was dead."

Six years… The number didn't land right. It didn’t make sense.

"Wait." Clive’s voice came out sharp. "Six years? Say that again."

Maleus blinked, taken aback at the sudden intensity. "Six years. Give or take a few months. She showed up in Vandiel territory back when the Demon King was still consolidating power. Blessed as a Chosen, moon marks on her skin and everything. Helped us fight off raiders. Healed the wounded. Earned the Demon King's attention real quick. Now she's the Moon Mother, and we'd die for her."

That’s not possible. Clive had only been here for one year max. How had Jill been here for six? The accident happened—he died first. Whatever happened to her, it should have been after. She should have arrived after him.

Clive looked down at Jill's face again, searching for proof that Maleus was wrong.

She looked exactly like he remembered. Same face. Same features. No lines around her eyes, no age from another six years of life. She could've fallen asleep in their apartment yesterday and woken up here.

Clive's head hurt.

Around them, the cultists had gone quiet. Even the dripping water seemed muted. Maleus watched him with curiosity at his silence.

The silence stretched. Clive didn’t know what to say.

Then Jill's fingers twitched.

Her eyelids fluttered. A soft sound escaped her throat. Her head moved slightly against his folded jacket.

Maleus straightened, suddenly alert. "Moon Mother?"
