Doctor Cecilia's Office.

Most days in her office were much the same. While the doctor's many nurses helped to lessen the workload, there was never a shortage of people who were more than eager to see her.

At the moment, she was taking care of a male patient. His clothes, once beige perhaps, were now a blotchy patchwork of grime and sweat-stiffened creases. A deep stench had settled into the folds like mold, invading the air and lodging in the back of her throat.

Doctor Cecilia stood over him, a damp cloth pinched between ink-stained fingers. Threads dangled from the edges of the fabric. She'd used this cloth too many times. Just like she'd treated this same affliction too many times.

Her patience, like the linen, was unraveling. They always wait until the rot sets in. Then they drag themselves to my door, expecting me to fix what they couldn’t be bothered to prevent.

She pressed the cloth to his forehead with a calculated touch. It wasn’t meant to comfort, just a step in the process. Nothing more.

The man let out a raspy groan. His eyes cracked open, rolling like a moth too stupid to find the flame. His lips parted, dry and flaking, moving with effort that barely formed words.

She didn’t care what he was trying to say. Cecilia turned to the tray beside her. The cup resting there was chipped, its rim stained by the same dark mixture she poured for half her patients: bitter, thick, and foul enough to shock the body into cooperating. The scent alone was enough to make some gag. She filled a spoon and brought it to his mouth.

“Drink,” she ordered.

The man opened his mouth. The spoon touched his tongue, and he sputtered. A line of the dark liquid slid down his chin. Of course. Can’t even manage that.

She stepped back immediately, resisting the urge to wipe her hands on her coat. The scent had already sunk into the fibers of her sleeves. With a flick of her wrist, she tossed the cloth into the basin. Water splashed as it landed, a single droplet hitting the side of her boot.

Cecilia turned away from the patient, and towards her most trusted assistant. “Lily, see him out. Slowly. Try not to let him fall over.”

“Yes, Doctor,” Lily replied.

Cecilia turned away from the fading shape of the patient. At her desk, she collapsed into the chair without elegance. Her spine curled forward, shoulder blades sagging as they sank into the worn backrest.

Her eyes drifted over the mess laid out before her: ink-streaked charts, a crooked quill resting in a dried puddle, discarded gauze curled like dead petals, and the bitter tang of herbs she no longer remembered the names of. She didn’t reach for any of it.

They come in droves. Coughing, moaning, whining for salvation as if I haven’t given enough already. As if there’s anything left to give. Perhaps I should never have come here.

“Doctor, shall I prepare some tea?” Lily asked.

Cecilia’s jaw clenched. The words grated against her skull, unnecessary noise in a mind already too full. “No, I’m not in the mood for tea."

Then Lily stepped forward. “Would you prefer nectar, Doctor?”

Cecilia exhaled, long and low, the sound catching slightly in her throat. She lifted her head just enough to regard Lily from under her lashes. “I'd like that very much, actually.”

“Right away,” Lily replied, already turning toward the back of the office.

She crossed the room with purpose, stopping before a tall bookshelf lined with worn tomes and empty ledgers. Her fingers found a thin, colorless spine, an obvious decoy to anyone who knew what to look for, and pulled. The mechanism gave with a dull click, and a narrow panel popped free, revealing a hidden compartment.

From inside, Lily withdrew a dark glass bottle. The surface bore no label, only a faint greasy sheen. Beneath it sat a polished crystal glass. She retrieved both and returned to the desk.

The moment the cork came loose, an unmistakable scent filled the room. The scent of blood.

The nurse poured with care. The liquid slid out thick and slow, clinging to the sides of the glass before settling at the bottom in a heavy, dark pool.

Cecilia accepted the glass, finger wrapping around the stem like it always belonged there. The crystal was cool to the touch, but the warmth of the liquid inside bled through faintly, pulsing with a quiet vitality.

She lifted it to her lips, and drank. The warmth hit her tongue first, but then the taste followed, and her nose wrinkled before she could stop it.

There was life in it, yes, fresh and rich, but it carried with it a bitterness that clung to the back of her throat. An earthy foulness, like soil soaked into flesh. Ugh. Peasant stock. I should have known.

If she hadn’t been so worn down, she might have spat it back into the glass. But the day had taken too much from her already.

Cecilia lowered the glass with slow disdain and clicked her tongue against the roof of her mouth. “Lily, where was this sourced?”

“A young male in his mid-twenties, worked as a farmstead for several families here in Volstarn. He had a strong build, and was surprisingly cooperative too."

“Oh please, I can taste the labor. It leaves a foul texture in the blood, like sucking on a fistful of soil.”

Lily let out a chuckle. “If you dislike it that much, Doctor, I’ll drink it myself.”

Cecilia’s eyes flicked up at her, not amused but not entirely annoyed either. She sighed and gestured toward the bottle with a lazy flick of her fingers. “By all means, pour yourself a glass and see what you’re defending.”

Lily stepped over to the shelf again, collected a second glass, and poured herself a modest portion of the thick, dark nectar. The blood sloshed against the glass with a slow weight, clinging to the crystal like sap.

She returned to the desk and settled into the chair across from Doctor Cecilia. The cushion gave slightly beneath her, the only sound in the room for a brief moment the faint clink of glass against lip.

Lily took a sip, holding the glass with the same careful poise she used for surgical tools. At first, her expression gave nothing away. Then the corners of her mouth tightened, and she swallowed reluctantly.

She set the glass down on the desk, the crystal clinking faintly against the wood.

“I admit…” she said, wiping her mouth lightly with the back of her hand, “it is rather unpleasant. How should I put it, it has that… barnyard edge to it. Like drinking iron from the inside of a boot left in the sun.”

Cecilia’s eyebrow lifted, a retort already forming on her tongue. “Oh, what a surpris—”

A sharp knock shattered the moment. Cecilia’s lips snapped shut. Her shoulders drew taut beneath her coat.

She turned her head toward the door. The interruption scraped against her nerves like bone dragged across slate. “We are not accepting patients at this time."

Then, a voice on the other side answered. “This isn’t about treatment. It’s about your brother.”

Time stalled. Lantern light caught on the rim of her glass, casting a red shadow that bled across the desk. The temperature hadn’t changed, yet the room felt colder. Silas.

“Lily, let them in.”

“Yes, Doctor.”

Lily stood immediately and quickly crossed to the door. The lock gave a small click under her fingers. Her hand paused on the handle for only a second before she pulled it open with the kind of measured care one used when expecting trouble.

The woman who stepped inside wore a deep, ash-gray cloak, pulled low over her shoulders, its hood already pushed back. Beneath it, her brown hair pulled into a tight braid that had started to fray around the edges. Her features were narrow and plain, save for the sharpness of her eyes.

But Cecilia recognized her immediately. “Marla."

The woman nodded once in acknowledgment and stepped fully into the room. She bowed her head. “Doctor."

Lily shut the door behind her and turned the lock with a quiet click. She then moved back to stand near the edge of the desk.

Cecilia’s fingers tightened around the stem of her glass. “Report.”

Marla lifted her chin. “Silas’s hideout has been destroyed.”

Cecilia said nothing at first. But her knuckles whitened against the crystal. The contents of her glass trembled ever so slightly, rippling across the surface. What did that idiot do this time?

She set the glass down slowly. "I want an explanation, now."

“Of course, Doctor. When I arrived, the cavern entrance was completely collapsed in. Rocks were split down to their roots. There were no signs of life or any sounds from inside." Marla said.

Lily folded her arms across her chest. “If that’s true, then it's entirely possible that Silas was forced to use his last resort.”

Cecilia exhaled through her nose. “Yes, and he would not have used it lightly. Say what you will about my brother, and I will say plenty, but he is not someone who acts on impulse.”

Marla stepped closer to the desk. “There is one more detail, Doctor. While going to the hideout, I observed a lone boy moving through the forest. His hood was drawn, and his face partially obscured, but I was close enough to study him clearly as he passed near the treeline. I cannot confirm a connection to the destruction, but the timing was... notable.”

“Describe him.” Cecilia demanded.

“Short black hair, tattered cloak. Looked like he’d been traveling. But what stood out most was his eye. Just one of them.”

“One of his eyes?”

“Yes, Doctor. The left was normal, but the right glowed bright red… though unfortunately I wasn't close enough to see any more details beyond that."

“And you are certain it was only one eye?”

“I watched him until the trees covered him again, only the right one glowed.”

Lily glanced at Cecilia, then back to Marla. “Doctor... are you thinking what I’m thinking?”

Cecilia didn’t answer right away. She closed her eyes for a brief moment, then opened them again. “Assuming he is not a vampire, as we would have easily recognized one of our own, then glowing eyes typically signify an Animus. However, it is unusual for only one eye to manifest. True Animus users can suppress their eyes entirely, which makes them especially dangerous."

She tapped her fingers lightly against the glass she’d set aside. “For only one eye to glow suggests either a lack of control… or something else entirely.”

“It’s common for the Wretched Host to use such cowardly methods. Disguising themselves. Hiding among us like rats.” Lily said.

Cecilia sighed, long and low. Her gaze drifted toward the darkened window as though expecting something to be watching from beyond the glass.“Their methods don’t matter, only our response.”

"If he’s truly linked to Silas’s disappearance… then I suggest we kill the boy. Immediately.” Marla said.

Cecilia raised an eyebrow. “Then tell me, Marla, why didn’t you try to kill him when you saw him in the forest?”

The question struck like a blade between the ribs. Marla’s shoulders stiffened. The confidence she had arrived with faltered under Cecilia’s gaze. “I— I was afraid.”

Lily snapped her head toward her. “Afraid? You’re not a child lost in the woods, are you?"

Marla turned to her. “I know what I am, Lily. But the moment I saw his eye… I felt it. Something that made every instinct in me scream to move away. Not because I doubted my skills, but because I didn’t want to be near him. Not even close.”

Cecilia watched the exchange without speaking. Her fingers tapped slowly against the desk. That kind of fear doesn’t lie. Not in someone like her. “I appreciate your honesty, Marla.”

Lily drew in a breath as if to protest, but Cecilia raised a hand, silencing her without effort. “Calm yourself, Lily. Emotion clouds judgment. Marla’s reaction may be unpleasant to hear, but it’s useful.”

Lily lowered her gaze. “Yes, Doctor. My apologies.”

“Thank you, Doctor,” Marla added.

Cecilia leaned back slightly in her chair, her mind working beneath the veil of her expression. “If your reaction was genuine, and I believe it was, then this boy must be very powerful indeed. Doubly so since he's very likely to also possess an Animus."

“Actually… Doctor, I’ve heard something.” Lily said.

"Go on."

“The boy. He came to Volstarn with Selene Whiteheart.”

Marla blinked, then nodded. “That’s true. I saw her briefly near the gates. She left the boy at one of the inns before heading out again, didn't stay long.”

Cecilia let out a bitter, dry laugh. “Of course. Of course it was her.”

“If she finds out what we’re doing…” Lily started.

“Selene is not omnipresent. Do any of you know if she is still in Volstarn?” Cecilia asked.

“No, Doctor, she left the boy in front of an inn and departed the same day. Didn’t speak to anyone after.” Marla said.

“Good. That simplifies matters,” Cecilia said.

She leaned forward, the faint creak of her chair lost beneath the distant ticking of a wall-mounted clock. Her elbows pressed into the surface of the desk, scattering a few stray ink-stained papers.

“We have a brief window before Selene returns. You should all start making preparations to leave Volstarn as soon as possible. I’ll handle the boy. And if Selene decides to do anything intrusive before you’re all gone… I’ll handle her too.”

“Are… you sure about this, Doctor?” Marla asked.

Cecilia didn’t answer at first. Her fingers curled around the edge of the desk, knuckles tightening. Of course I’m not sure. But someone has to be.

“I am, it’s fine if I die. As long as the rest of you make it out.”

The air turned heavy. Marla’s eyes darkened with protest. “No. We can fight, Doctor. Me, the others, together we're not helpless. We are not going to abandon you.”

Lily nodded beside her, arms crossed tightly across her chest. “Leaving it all to you isn’t an option. We’re staying.”

Cecilia exhaled, slow and long, as if trying to breathe out the weight crushing her ribs. Her head dipped slightly as she rubbed at the bridge of her nose. She was too tired to argue, but she could already feel the heat rising in her chest. Stubborn girls… They’re going to get themselves killed.

The thought trailed off, unfinished. She couldn't bear to continue imagining the worst case scenario.

Yet, just then, she remembered something. Wait… the full moon… wasn’t it close?

Cecilia didn’t speak. Not right away. The silence stretched, settling into the corners of the room like dust. She closed her eyes, letting her mind drift through numbers and dates. “Lily, show me the calendar.”

Without hesitation, the red-haired nurse crossed the room. She knelt beside a small cabinet near the windowsill, fingers sliding open the top drawer. A rustle of parchment followed. Lily returned, arms outstretched, the chart held delicately between her fingertips.

Cecilia took it slowly. The paper felt warm from Lily’s touch. She unfolded it with care, the edges curling slightly from repeated use. Her eyes scanned the markings, line by line, circling a date already etched in her thoughts.

“There will be a full moon tonight, yes?”

“Yes, Doctor, I’ve been monitoring the lunar cycle. The full moon is approaching, either tonight or tomorrow.” Lily said.

Marla stepped closer. “Then we’ll be stronger! The full moon amplifies all of our abilities, enough that it may be enough to tip things in our favor."

Cecilia’s gaze remained on the parchment, though her jaw tensed. “It’s true. The full moon will bolster you. But I wouldn’t place my faith in that alone.”

She looked up, tired eyes locking with Marla’s. “We still don’t know how powerful the boy is. And underestimating someone with an Animus is a pathway to humiliation, or worse."

Lily gave a light cough, a subtle signal that she wished to speak. “Then, if I may, Doctor… you and I could focus on the boy. The others would hold—”

“No.” Cecilia’s reply was immediate, harder than she’d intended. She looked between the two women. “Even with the full moon, it won’t be enough.

A small smile tugged at the corner of Marla’s lips. “We’re not planning to win, Doctor.”

Cecilia froze. The muscles along her spine locked into place, a chill blooming at the base of her neck and crawling downward. Her fingers twitched against the edge of the desk, then stilled. Her eyes blinked once. No. Don’t you dare.

Her gaze snapped to Marla, sharp enough to cut. The air in the room tightened around her, pulled taut like wire. “You think throwing your life away is a plan? You think that’s noble? That dying pointlessly buys someone else time?”

Despite the doctor's anger, Marla didn't flinch. There was a softness in her eyes, but it wasn't weakness, it was resolve. "Buying time is the plan, Doctor. And if we can take down Selene Whiteheart while doing it… then that’s a victory.”

No. You don’t understand. You think this is some heroic end. You think it’ll mean something. Cecilia wanted to shake her. To yell. To drag her away from that stupid acceptance.

But before she could say anything, Lily stepped forward. “It gives you a chance to survive, Doctor. You should be stronger than the boy at least, right? You have to be.”

Cecilia looked at both of her faithful nurses. Lily’s determined eyes, Marla’s quiet smile. Brave, stupid girls. Loyal to a fault.

“The boy with the eyepatch doesn’t match any of the known Animus profiles,” Marla added.

Lily gave a measured nod. “That likely means he’s newly awakened, perhaps that’s why Selene has taken him under her wing. In either case, it suggests he’s still far from being a master… which means there’s a chance.”

After hearing all that, Cecilia drew in a long breath, then let it out slowly. Her hands rose, pressing against her face as if she could rub the weariness out of her bones. Silence soon returned, deeper than before.
