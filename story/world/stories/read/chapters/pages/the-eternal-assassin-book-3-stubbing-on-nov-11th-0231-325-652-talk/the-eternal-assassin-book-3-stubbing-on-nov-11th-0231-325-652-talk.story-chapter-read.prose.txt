“Gran?” Asher said, more than a little startled to suddenly find himself in the presence of such a powerful figure once again. Just like the last time they’d spoken he was practically blinded by her brilliant aura of soft, white light that seemed to caress everything it touched. Gran was the one and only person he’d ever encountered who seemed as though she’d never even thought about doing anything bad, let alone actually done any bad deeds. She wore the same lengthy, brilliant gown of glittering diamonds that she had before, and her smile was just as gentle and grandmotherly as always. “What are you doing here?”

“Weren’t you listening? I asked her to come help me explain why I can’t explain certain things,” Donvath snorted, giving Gran a small wave. “Hey, Gran, thanks for making it. I think you just saved me another broken nose.”

“It is good to see you again as well, Donvath,” Gran said. Looking him up and down, she raised an eyebrow knowingly as she took in the High Prince with blood running down the front of his shirt. “Getting into the usual trouble, I assume?”

“Who, me? Never!”

“What is going on?” Asher demanded, his anger toward Donvath thoroughly quenched now that he was in the presence of Gran once more. The woman seemed to physically radiate calm and serenity, as if the mere thought of violence in her presence was unthinkable. “Don’t get me wrong, it’s nice to see you again, Gran. But Donvath was just about to answer a hell of a lot of questions I have for him.”

“Unfortunately, there are a couple of questions he is not allowed to answer,” Gran admitted, motioning toward one of the empty seats as she gave him a small smile. “May I sit?”

“Of course,” Asher found himself answering without thinking, shaking his head as Gran smoothed down her dress and joined them at the table. “What do you mean there are things he’s not ‘allowed’ to answer? He’s the High Prince of the entire kingdom!”

“I’m sorry to say, but that is one of the very questions he’s not allowed to answer in full,” Gran said, reaching out and gently patting Asher’s hand that still held Crescent in silent threat. “You have been through an incredible series of ordeals since we last spoke, my young reaper. Your lifeline tells of immense struggle and hardship unlike anything I have ever seen, even in my lengthy time on this world. I’m sorry for all the pain you have been forced to suffer through.”

Asher’s first reaction was to snap at her for changing the subject, but something about the woman’s genuine look of sorrow as she stared not just at him, but his twisted lifeline that only she was able to see, made him hesitate. While he wouldn’t put it past someone as powerful as Gran to have a way of bypassing the Honest Ears skill he was currently borrowing, according to it, she meant every single word she said.

“Thank you… Honestly, it’s been a lot.”

“Hey now, I get punched in the face and she gets a ‘thank you?’ That doesn’t seem very fair,” Donvath grumbled, crossing his arms like a petulant child.

“Gran didn’t kill me to push her own mysterious agenda,” Asher drawled, shooting the High Prince another glare.

“Truthfully… it was my idea to push you in this way,” Gran admitted, causing Asher to blink in shock at her admittance. “While I have been kept plenty busy across our fair land, I have paid careful attention to your exploits, listening as word of your accomplishments and growth carried from one kingdom to the next. I knew your Eternal Regeneration had to be nearing its third-tier evolution, and I believed it to be in both your and the world’s best interest to guide you toward the best possible outcome.”

“There, see?” Donvath said, grinning at him as he tilted his head toward the old healer. “Wanna punch her in the face too?”

“I don’t understand,” Asher said, his mind whirling at the revelation that at least a part of Gran had been focused on him all this time. “Does this have something to do with the horrifying monsters you told me about last we spoke? When I asked why you’d have Leighann lie for me and keep my skills a secret, you went on some terrifying tirade about ancient, unstoppable monsters so powerful they would cause the Council of Death to flee in terror.”

Donvath’s eyes widened in shock and his jaw fell open as he turned to stare at Gran like he’d just been informed she enjoyed drowning puppies in her free time. “What?! You told him about the—”

Gran lifted a single finger and Donvath froze, his words dying instantly as if he’d been transformed into a living statue. Turning toward the High Prince, she gave him a slight frown.

“I alluded to them. I did not call them by name, nor go into detail. Not even I would dare to go against the pact. If Asher is to be our answer, I felt it only fair that he have some semblance of an idea as to what the future holds in store for him.”

Having said her part, Gran lowered her finger, and Donvath collapsed back into his chair again, breathing heavily as he glared at Gran’s stoic face.

“God damn, Grandkeepers… Rules for thee but not for me, huh?”

“Grandkeeper?” Asher asked, seeing Donvath wince. Without warning, a pulse of energy seemed to implode within Donvath’s body, and the High Prince gasped as he slumped forward, panting as he leaned heavily against the table.

“My apologies, Donvath, but I request that you do not utilize your Timeline skill during this conversation,” Gran said, reaching out and laying a tender hand on his head. “We have much to discuss. I was planning to explain the pact to him regardless.”

“Did… Did you just cancel Donvath’s attempt at rewinding time?” Asher asked, staring at Gran in awe. The woman hadn’t so much as twitched, and yet here Donvath was, panting like he’d just run a marathon.

“Yeah… it’s a really annoying trick she’s capable of,” Donvath muttered, sucking in a deep breath as he sat back up. “Fine. I guess I’ll just keep my mouth shut rather than risk saying anything I’m not supposed to. Looks like all our usual rules don’t matter anyway, am I right?”

Asher didn’t even know what to think anymore. Not only did he still have all his original questions he’d wanted Donvath to answer for him, but with Gran’s appearance and the revelations of what she could do and the power she seemed to hold over Donvath, he had what felt like hundreds more. Rather than struggle to pick what to ask first, he sat there, deciding to wait patiently for Gran to get started.

“As Donvath has already mentioned, there are rules that must be followed,” Gran said, smiling at Asher as she spoke. “There is a pact in place. A grand working of power unlike anything this world has ever seen. While there are a small handful of people who know of the pact, primarily the leaders of the various kingdoms making up our small continent, I am one of the Grandkeepers. My days are spent roaming the land, ensuring that the rules are upheld and things are going smoothly. The pact is the reason why Donvath is unable to answer many of your questions. Until you reach the Stage of Power and agree to join our mission, there are certain things you cannot know.”

“Your mission? The killing of these horrifying monsters, right?” Asher asked, noting how Donvath winced yet again.

“Donvath is correct in that technically, I should not have revealed even that much to you,” Gran admitted, giving him a sorrowful smile. “But as I said before, we believe you will be a critical asset toward our mission. It was for that reason I encouraged Donvath to push your Eternal Regeneration toward the evolution it received.”

“If I hadn’t done anything, your skill would have reached its third evolution during your battle with Jormon,” Donvath grumbled. “The monster guarding his fort bites you in half and you sort of dissolve in acid. Your original evolution would have granted you the ability to regenerate your entire body from even the smallest piece, like a finger or a severed toe. Sort of similar to how nobles handle resurrection. The only problem is, that wasn’t good enough.”

“We needed to ensure that you would return to fight again even if you were completely destroyed,” Gran explained. “Naturally, there are still threats to you that you must watch out for. Anything that can harm the soul directly, or anything capable of harnessing the power of the void, could wipe you from existence, for example. But those forces are nearly impossible to counter regardless. This was as good as we were going to get.”

“Okay… I understand why you decided you had to meddle in my skill evolution,” Asher said, slowly taking this all in. Honest Ears still hadn’t buzzed a single time, which led him to believe they were telling the truth. “You want me to eventually help you fight these terrifying monsters that threaten the world, and they are so big and bad there’s a good chance they’ll destroy me completely the first go around. And you’re both part of some secret organization that keeps these monsters a secret from everyone? For some reason you can’t tell me?”

“That is all mostly correct,” Gran nodded. “As soon as you reach the Stage of Power, so long as you agree to join our mission and swear by the pact we all follow, I will personally reveal everything to you and answer all your questions. Until then, I’m sorry to say what information we can give you is limited.”

Asher sat there, drumming his fingers along the table as he looked between two of the most powerful people he’d ever met. Despite having had far more interactions with Donvath, he had to admit he trusted Gran more for some unexplainable reason. He didn’t know if it was a skill she had or just the purity of her aura, but either way, he felt inclined to trust the woman. Although she looked like she was in her mid-twenties, the fact that she was older than some kingdoms spoke a lot for her.

“Fine,” he eventually decided. “In that case, how did Donvath manage to prevent my Complete Resilience purge from working, and what the hell is in the damn basement under the palace that you don’t want me to see? Or is that conveniently related to your special little pact as well?”

Donvath looked hesitant to answer, but when Gran motioned for him to do so, the High Prince sighed. “My elements… are Knowledge, Time, and Mind. I have a skill that allows me to completely block someone from using one of their own skills, provided I know exactly what it is that I’m blocking. It’s a subtle mental attack.”

“You’re serious? You, the High Prince of the entire kingdom, has one of the elements you’ve labeled as forbidden for everyone else?” Asher asked, barely able to believe the hypocrisy. “…Have you been reading our minds this entire time?”

“No, I can’t read anyone’s mind,” Donvath snapped, looking like he was entirely done with this conversation and just wanted to go pass out in bed. “And yes, I know it’s not fair. My only defense is that the elements were already labeled as forbidden long before I took the throne. Only… I needed the Mind element for a very specific reason. A plan that I’d been putting together for years, ever since I was a little kid. The element is still incredibly dangerous so I couldn’t just remove it from the forbidden elements list, but I needed it if my plan was to have any chance of success.”

“What plan?” Asher demanded. “And don’t think I’ve forgotten about the basement.”

“The two are connected,” Donvath frowned, hesitating as if he wasn’t sure he really wanted to make this leap. The High Prince warred with himself for a moment, before letting out a weary sigh as he leaned back in his chair. “…I hope you can accept this as my apology for killing you, Asher. Beyond the pact I’ve sworn to follow alongside the other leaders of nations on our continent, this is my greatest secret. Have you ever wondered why my title is only that of the High Prince rather than the King?”

“The thought’s crossed my mind,” Asher admitted, wondering where he was going with this.

“The reason is simple enough,” Donvath said, giving him a tired smile. “My father… he’s not exactly dead.”
