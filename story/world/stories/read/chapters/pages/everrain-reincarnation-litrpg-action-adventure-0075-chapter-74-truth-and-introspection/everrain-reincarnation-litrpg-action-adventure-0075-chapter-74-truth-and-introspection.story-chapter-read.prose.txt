Day 357, 9:10 AM

Questions swarm my mind as we head down the tunnel. Why did I allow the incident with Lucy to happen? There were plenty of escape routes from the scenario in which I find myself now, but I took none of them. Deep down, I think I wanted a child.

One child.

Not what I had as a king, twenty-three of the adorable little buggers raised by nannies. Children who barely saw me as a father and viewed me as an intimidating wall looming over them from the moment they were born. I loved them, even Luck, despite his irresponsible drinking and whoring; but with work, the formal events we had to preside over, and with so damn many of them, it was difficult to give each child as much attention as they needed and deserved.

Maybe I wanted to have someone I could make it up to, and now I’m going on a suicide mission to save the world. No. I shouldn’t accept my vision at face value. Maybe we can survive. I’m an archmage, whatever is down there, it stands to reason that I have the power to defeat it with magic without losing my life. And I have Redo. For the sake of my child, I should give it my all to fight and survive. No, to win and survive. Losing and surviving is not on the table, not when the battle is for the world’s continued survival.

I look around the tunnel, Gila’s creating the light for us, treants rustle further back on their tank treads. The entire passage is paved with large blocks of black volcanic rock. No magic involved, nothing but countless hours and countless crafters built this place. The floor is scratched by the passage of monsters, but from what I can tell, there was little traffic down here.

“You really think some monsters from beyond wish to eat the world?” Edna obviously doubts my words, but that’s fine. I’ve always sounded like a crazy man.

“The scant few gods I’ve met didn’t exactly fill me with confidence as far as their selfless benevolence goes.” Light flickers as Gila loses her focus. “I am positive they would devour or abuse human, or well, mortal souls, to further their goals. They might even do it to alleviate boredom.”

“You’ve seen gods? Multiple?” Gila finally finds her words.

“I live and die and remember what was before. I’ve been to hell, and I can tell you all of it is real. Possibly not the way the church is serving it, never heard what they actually believe in, but there is something beyond the life you are living now. You’ve probably lived countless lives before, but you don’t remember them.”

“And you?”

“I don’t get born the regular way. I possess the bodies of those about to die, possibly those who had just died. This body suffered a heart attack before I got it.”

Even without magic, I can tell Gila’s heart is racing.

“I see another Guide, the one which the Guide’s creators mimicked. It has more complete information than what you see, that’s how we know which level up conditions exist and why you could waltz through your classes while Edna, our teacher, struggled through them and never reached the heights we did.”

I switch my attention to Edna, leaving Gila to deal with the planet-sized revelations.

“Now, I promised I would explain certain things about leveling up archmage. First, how many grand spells do you know?”

Edna is naturally stuck at level one, since she doesn’t know a single grand spell and developing five would have taken her decades, possibly centuries. I assure her I would teach her, should we survive, and proceed to explain other level up conditions and skill choices presented to archmages.

It would have been nice if we could have trusted each other like this from the start. In truth I’m the bastard there. Edna trusted me, or at least gave me the benefit of the doubt. She allowed me to go to the dungeon, to awaken the Guide, and pretty much gave me the freedom to do whatever. That’s the prerogative of the strong.

I was weak, so I had to hide my knowledge to keep myself safe, to dangle the carrot of useful information until I grew strong enough to share what I know freely. And a good thing I did that, given how erratic Edna had grown after becoming an archmage. But there’s a downside, too.

Had I been candid from the start, and had everything worked out well, both Edna and myself would have been much more powerful now. But who would dare take such risks? To gamble on the upright nature of man.

A parallel thought considers whether I would change something if I could start over, while another explains things to Edna, including what I have learned about the nature of magic. She is shocked when I tell her the real nature of black mana is matter, not object. It’s quite possible they never labeled any of them properly, save for green mana’s life magic.

So, if I started over, would I trust Edna from the start? No. And I damn straight shouldn’t trust her. She had molded her brain in such a way that she was suffering from mental conditions. Even now, I don’t dare tell her I can hop back through time. Who knows how she would react to that.

Gila is going half-mad just ruffling through the more innocent amongst my bag of secrets. In fact she’s slowly making her way towards the other half.

“Wormlords were mages once,” I drop another bomb while I’m at it. “Similar to path of life archmages, they need to meld two bugs together and they will receive their own branch of magic focused on bugs. My guess is that at some point, they did the same as you, Edna. They tried to optimize their bodies and minds and just broke.”

I catch her shudder in the corner of my eye.

“Bit by bit, they turned themselves into beasts. That is my guess, based on what you told me of your history. And their error and their conceit comes from the flawed way in which you understand magic and nature. All the books I read in Hadriuse’s abode discussed how the body reacts to a stimulus, sometimes the authors discussed the why behind a process, but for some reason you people never wondered about what exactly happens. A chemical is released, and in doing so it makes you afraid.”

I chuckle without mirth. “The solution is simple right? Disable the production of the chemical. But what does it do in small doses? What happens when it’s no longer present in the body? You never asked such questions, nobody did, despite them being fundamental. The trace amounts of those substances make us human. Without them, we turn into monsters.”

A silence descends.

“That’s not to say using magic to enhance the human body is impossible. But you need carefully controlled environments, well documented experiments, subjects tracked across years and decades, any irregularities noted and then critically examined. I know you lacked the conditions for that before, but progress needs to be stable. If you focus on your own body right now, you will sense countless cells deteriorating, dying, and death is a natural part of our body’s cycle. Stopping all cells from dying will have horrible consequences.”

A score of vats from Hadriuse’s secret lab come to mind. The crazy old psychopath had experimented on the subject, both on animals and on humans, and the results are best left in formaldehyde. The monstrosities are still potentially alive on a cellular level, despite the passage of centuries.

In the ensuing silence, I catch the scuttle and scraping of chitin against basalt.

“They are coming.” The girls shudder and strain their eyes against the tunnel’s darkness. “You two should support the tree soldiers to eliminate all of them. I’m distilling my mana, and the more I purify it, the more potent my magic will be when we need it.”

The level nine mage skill makes Edna jealous, but it really is a double-edged blade. While distilling mana, a mage can’t expand their reservoir. And the benefit of having more mana is permanent, while distilled mana is gone once you spend it, the time you spent distilling it wasted.

The three of us stop, letting ten treants advance, with ninety more behind us. Head-sized rocks whistle through the air, thrown with as much force as a cannon could offer. Dull squishes and screeches come from the distance as rocks smash against armored bodies and tunnel walls and floor.

Another barrage follows, each tree soldier armed with ten to twelve missiles. The scuttle grows louder, and a wave of minor abominations slams against our fodder before they fire their third volley.

The battle is a slaughter. While slow and lumbering, the tree soldiers are durable, and the few blows they land break the mutated insects into gory pieces and ichor. The battle is over in three minutes, the broken abominations scattered across the floor.

Two tree soldiers are damaged, but Edna mends them, and we continue down the tunnel. All is quiet, but above the treants’ light rustle, I can pick up another wave of distant scuttling. This is going to be a long and difficult trek.
