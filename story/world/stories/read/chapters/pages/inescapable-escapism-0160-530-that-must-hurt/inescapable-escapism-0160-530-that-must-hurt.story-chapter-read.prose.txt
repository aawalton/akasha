His hand slid slowly up my rib cage, moving higher and higher until…

“Mmmm… I don’t think that will happen,” I said, the words coming out more rushed than I wanted them to.

Duncan cocked his head to the side, his eyes narrowing ever so slightly.

“Oh, really?” he asked. “Why not?”

I shrugged, glancing away from him at the screen before responding.

“I mean… I did just defeat you twice in a row, so things aren’t looking great for you.”

Duncan snorted softly, causing me to look back at him.

“Maybe I was going easy on you before,” he said, his tone blasé.

“Were you?”

He was just teasing me, and I knew that, but I couldn’t stop myself from asking. It wouldn’t have surprised me if he were taking it easy on me and letting me win. A lot of the game was based on luck, but some skill was needed too, and he was better at gaming than I was.

Plus, Duncan had more experience than I did. He’d played a lot of the mini games before, and it was my first time, but even if I had as well, he would probably still win most of them. The only reason I’d won the last two games was because I’d had lucky rolls. I’d gotten good items, and they’d allowed me to steal stars. Without them, I probably would have lost almost everything.

“Not even slightly,” Duncan told me with a grin that immediately made me feel better.

I knew it didn’t matter. Winning a video game against Duncan didn’t really mean anything, but I was strangely proud of myself for managing to beat him, even if it was mostly luck. I had managed to win a couple of the mini games, so maybe I wasn’t that bad.

“I thought not,” I lied before a wicked realisation suddenly slammed into me, and I found myself smirking. “You know, maybe you should have a couple of drinks now to make up for losing the last two games…”

Mock indignation appeared on Duncan’s face as he turned to me.

“Fine,” he decided. “But then you need to drink for all of the mini games that you lost. It’s only fair.”

I pressed my lips together to hide my smile as I pretended to consider his proposal for a few seconds, but my mind was already made up. I knew what I was going to say.

“Fine, but so do you. It’s only fair, after all,” I said, throwing his words back at him.

Duncan’s mouth dropped open, and I had to bite the inside of my lip to hold in the giggle that threatened to escape. He closed his mouth, opening it again, but no words came out. It seemed as though he was trying to think of an argument so he didn’t need to drink as much, but, based on his silence, he wasn’t having much luck.

Had he expected me to refuse? That would have explained his shock, but I hadn’t even considered it. Not really, anyway. I wanted to drink more. It normally helped stop my mind from racing quite as much. I generally felt more relaxed when I’d had some alcohol, and it would make it easier to flirt with Duncan, which would have been good.

I wasn’t doing too badly, though. Since I’d left the bathroom, I felt like I was doing a pretty good job of flirting with him. I hadn’t said much, but it was definitely more playful than I’d been before.

That had to be a good thing. Duncan must have realised I was into him, and if he hadn’t, he probably would soon if I continued, which I planned on doing. It was getting easier to do it. The words were just spilling out of my mouth, and I had no idea where they were coming from. Even though I hadn’t said anything overtly risque, I normally wouldn’t have had the confidence to say half of the things I’d said, but I was glad I had.
