It was as I was slowly sipping at the soup Renauld had brought that I decided to go for it.

Finally, after literally weeks of waiting, I was going to update my Status. I hadn’t done so in the entire time since the battle had been lost at Rhoscara. Which, to be fair, I thought I had a pretty good reason for it.

I had literally been injured to the point of death, after all. I had thought that it really wasn’t a good idea for me to be doing anything that might change my body while it was laid up with some missing bones. That, and almost immediately, I knew I was going to need to undergo a bit of spiritual surgery to graft new bones into my Aetheric system. It had struck me as a bad decision to alter my spiritual makeup at all if I was going to need to carefully graft new bones onto both my physical and spiritual self.

And then Rafael had appeared out of nowhere and rendered all of that moot by virtue of being far more skilled in both areas.

But I was going to be honest. That was just an excuse.

I was still mulling over the sense of wariness I’d realized I still possessed toward the System back before the battle on the ramparts in Rhoscara. That had only grown more in the hours since Hermus’s revelations.

The fact that the System was not quite as automatic, that there had, at one point, even if not now, been an actual controlling mind behind it…

The power that this Administrator had at one point over the system screamed warning bells to me. It implied that if the System gave out all of this power, and if you crossed the Administrator in some way, they could take it away.

That was, of course, assuming that the Administrator wasn’t dead or something. I may have realized the broad strokes of how the gods had gone about overcoming whatever the Administrator was, but I knew Hermus hadn’t known how they had left it. It was entirely possible that my purpose as a Precursor wasn’t to actually return the stolen Divinity to the System itself.

No, I could be a catspaw so that the Administrator could recover its Divinity through me.

Did I want to restore the Administrator, in that case?

I don’t know.

Did I have a choice?

Decidedly not.

The fact of the matter was that although I was wary about some apparent Overgod of the System, like I was starting to think of the Administrator as, I still had to help them.

If that’s what I was doing.

The System was still slowly bleeding out, and I still needed to return that Divinity if I wanted to possibly staunch the bleeding. It was either that, or accept the long, slow death of Vereden and presumably all of the other planets that had once existed within the portal network.

First, though, I would have to overcome the gods, those Netherim who I wasn’t quite as willing to cast as storybook villains anymore, even if they had to die. Starting with Fynneas.

And to do that, we’d need to overcome the Savoy.

And to do that, we’d need to get past the War Wards of Smaragd.

And to do that, we’d need to get north and find Nehushtan.

All so we could end the Skyfall, before that catastrophe killed everyone on the damn planet.

What a shitshow this all was.

The first step in that plan, though…was accepting what power the System would dole out to me, no matter my hesitancy.

As I picked up my bowl of soup and drained the last drop, I triggered Hidden Amidst the Spheres and observed my Status for the first time in weeks.

I…really shouldn’t have been surprised at just how much I had grown.

But then again, I was one of the only people who received level Aether from actually killing other people, and not just monsters. Wordlessly, I cycled through the dialogue boxes, allocating my points as I went until I viewed my final Status sheet.

Ten whole levels. Only eight more until I hit the vaunted ceiling of two hundred and fifty. There had been a lot of fighting in Rhoscara, and intellectually, I realized I must have personally killed…possibly upwards of over a hundred different Savoy soldiers, during the Battle of Rhoscara.

But ten levels? God, I hadn’t gotten that many levels in one go since…maybe before I was even level one-hundred? Back in the good old simple days, when I was intimidated by confronting even a single squad of Loyalist soldiers.

I slowly set my bowl down, suddenly aware that Sylvia seemed to have immediately noticed the slight change in my mood. My love was staring at me out of the corner of her eye with a concerned look in it, even as she tended her own bowl. I subtly shook my head at her.

It was…fine. This was something I was used to and didn’t need to be comforted on. I had long since come to terms with the peculiarities of my existence. Even if I still felt like some kind of ghoulish vampire from how I gained power from literal murder.

I hated even the implication that I was similar to Nerexxa.

I shook it off and pushed back from the table. Rafael was right. A decent meal and a bit of time were all I needed to feel recovered from the procedure, even if it had only been about half an hour. I probably only felt like I was at roughly sixty percent or so, but that was enough.

It was time to stop pussy-footing around and take my place at the big boys' table once more.

I was ready to be a Marshal again.

………………………..

At a twitch of my hand, the door of the church slammed open…

And I strode out of it, decked out in my full regalia for the first time in weeks.

I hadn’t realized just how much I had missed this, so much in the wake of not only my personal defeat in Rhoscara, but the collective defeat of the expedition, as well as the de facto defeat of the Unchained as well. I had felt low; I was comfortable enough to admit that. I had dragged my feet, I had immersed myself in something familiar and comforting when it came to my crafting and Enchantment work. I had dodged my responsibilities under the excuse that I had only been a clone, a fragment of the collective might that was Nathaniel Hart.

No longer. My body may still feel a slight sluggishness, but the sluggishness in my spirit was gone.

In its place was a desire to act.

I bared my teeth at the world, as I felt my crimson and blue long coat snap behind me from the force of my stride, repaired from its ripped state in the aftermath of the battle. Not only was I back in the most recognizable facet of my public face as the head of a Martial Order, but I had decided it was time to stop pretending. I’d had more to my name than just the coat…

I’d had my stealth armor Azarus had forged for me as well. The Oninite chain and leather armor had served me well during my hunt for the Elves who had enslaved me.

It would do as an additional layer of defense as I shifted us all into a war footing.

Because that’s what I was intending to do. No more of this uncertainty about what our goals were. No more uncertainty about what our ultimate enemy was, here within this world dragged into the hell of the Skyfall.

Fynneas had to die, and for that to happen?

Anguis of Savoy would lie broken at my feet before this was done.

I may not have my extendable spears for that task any longer, but it didn’t matter.

Terractus on my left hip, pistol on my right, and staff in hand…that would be enough. I’d make it enough.

I would be enough, or I would die trying.

For Vereden. For my comrades. For Sylvia and Fade

For my daughter.

God, it felt good to be back.

Even in the new sense of purpose that had seemingly rolled over me in a furious, unending wave the moment my coat had touched my shoulders, I still retained enough sense of mind to be grateful for those who followed me. Both Sylvia and Renauld had seemed to sense the determination that had settled onto me with the mantle of responsibility, and reciprocated. With a kiss from the former and a cheeky wave from the latter, they had scattered out into the settlement of the Unchained.

They had very specific instructions.

Fade, meanwhile, had demanded his bandages off. With them gone, he might not command the same sense of majesty that a large rack of missing antlers would command, but he was still a very tense, ready-to-act wolf the size of a pony.

He commanded respect all of his own.

Trailing in our wake as I drove crowds out of my way through sheer presence alone…was Rafael. The Lich was quiet as he followed us, drawing no attention, and definitely not seeking it.

I think, as was usual for him, he was content to wait and see just where this was going.

That was fine with me. He was only incidental to what was about to happen.

Though the crowds of refugees had apparently all been settled by now, that didn’t mean that the streets of this place were any less busy. On the contrary, they were all the more filled for the new thousands that had come to now call this place a haven. I’m not sure if I was literally projecting any kind of aura. It was entirely possible, from how worked I felt, that my Mana was leaking, that the very air around me was near boiling with intent. It certainly felt agitated enough in the core of my soul.

But the result was that there was a distinct gap all around me, as I stalked toward the center of this settlement. People kept their distance rather than trail in their wake, almost shying away from my inhuman frame. With the odd scale patches that traced the little visible skin I had, my dagger-like ears, and slit, glowing emerald eyes, I didn’t even blame them.

They weren’t who this was for, anyway.

It was for those their gossiping would reach.

After, my people knew my description by Hart.

Heh.

I had learned from Josha before we’d left him behind that this unnamed town had a large, central square that surrounded the transport pillar up to the surface. Normally, it was actually quite empty, most of the time, considering the lack of a desire to travel to the poisoned surface. There apparently were only a token selection of guards, anyway.

It suited my purposes just fine.

I broke through the crowds, and there it was. The pillar loomed over the caldera in the first place, but this close to it, it was certainly monolithic. Even a bit intimidating to stand in the shadow of such a massive structure.

But I was more interested in the near square mile of empty space that surrounded the pillar in a circle around it.

And the tiered steps that descended from it.

As I stepped into the square, the first stages of my very impromptu plan were already starting to bear fruit. From all around the edges of the square, as far as I could see, people had started to stream in from the surrounding settlement. They came in all shapes and sizes, of different races and sexes…but all of one creed.

For they all bore one thing in common.

Draped over their left arms were short half-cloaks of varying shades of white. Some were clean, some were ripped and dirty from months of fighting.

But all of them bore the blue and red compass star of the Polaris Reach upon them.

Sylvia and Renauld’s task had been to spread the word. I was going to speak, and I expected the members of the expedition to make an appearance.

Those of the Polaris were just the first to answer the call, and I was pleased to see them. We may have lost a full third of the expedition in the bloody streets of Rhoscara, but we were far from beaten.

It was as I was climbing the steps that several people drifted my way. Sylvia and Renauld were back, and they had brought those who I considered my most loyal.

Maria at the side of Sylvia, and Walter with Renauld.

My head Steward merely smiled at the sight of me and whispered a short ‘welcome back’ before falling in line, but Walter…

My apprentice deserved more from me, after the uncertainty that had been driven between us. A large part of which was most definitely my fault. But he was here, now, armed and armored with a sword nearly as tall as he was on his back…

He was here, and that mattered.

I reached out and grasped the startled young man’s forearm and gripped it tight. Tight enough to cause the steel of his vambrace to creak in my grip.

I wanted him to understand me when I met his eyes.

“Never let them beat the empathy out of you, Walter,” I said ferociously, and Walter’s mouth parted almost instinctively at my words.

He recognized those words as well as I did.

I had spoken something very similar to him, after all, that very first night when we had met each other. That night under the stars of Vereden, outside of the slave barracks of Addersfield where Walter had been the man to set everything in motion that was to come.

If it hadn't been for him, I would have never learned Aetherial Melding, and the madness of Magnus would have eventually killed us both.

“The world is going to try and make something out of you that is less than human, and I have failed that test at times. But you haven’t. The Anguis’s and the Fynneas’s of the world want you to be nothing more than an unthinking, unfeeling machine that bows down to their will. But that is not our purpose. Ours is the lot of the chain-breaker, he who stands before the slave-catcher with blade bared and snarl on his lips, ready to spill blood in the defense of liberty. The times and the situation we find ourselves in are harsh ones, but I believe that the both of us can stand to meet it. Will you stand with me, Walter Meyers?”

Though the slight sheen of tears that filled his eyes was easy to see, the slight glint of a golden light within them was much subtler.

I caught it, nonetheless.

As well as the form of the young man as he leaned forward, and clasped me in a tight embrace, slinging his arms around me. I returned it just as strongly, a new smile crawling over my lips.

If I had felt ready to challenge the world before, now I was ready to conquer it.

After a moment to affirm our bond, Walter pulled back and returned my smile. “Till the end, Sir Hart.”

I clapped him on the shoulder and nodded sharply. “Good. Then let’s go, Walter.” I said, finishing our climb to the top step of the pillar and turning around to face the square…

Which was no longer deserted.

While it was far from filled to the brim, I would estimate nearly the sum total of the remaining forces of the expedition had found themselves here. One person sought out and was told to gather became two, and then two became four, and so on, increasing exponentially, each of them sprinting through the streets of the Unchained, spreading the word.

I smiled sharply, surrounded by those I trusted most in the world.

“It’s time to raise the flag.”
