Day 357, 3:30 AM

The trees silently infest the city. Three hundred surround the temple, hundreds of others encircling the other buildings based solely on their size. Then, without a word, hell breaks loose.

Twenty-foot-tall giants tear away chunks of masonry and hurl the rocks at the buildings they had surrounded, destroying them in moments. Hundreds of guards and residents run for the streets as the world crumbles around them, but a mere handful survive the first volley of man-sized boulders, which turn the rest into gory smears.

The temple collapses, but from the heap of white rock dozens of abominations surge into the streets. I throw myself from roof to treant and from treant to ruined roof until I reach the scene of carnage where abominations and tree soldiers tear at each other.

I touch a giant, slug-like mass, and from the place I touched rot spreads, undoing the magical evolution which had created the thing. Jumping for the giant praying mantis, I see that the other tree soldiers are still committed to their original task of razing the inner city. The mantis rips a tree in two with a single swipe of its blades, unleashing a spray of acid, which hisses and smokes in the rain.

Another tree soldier attacks it, but Edna had designed her army to attack people and structures, not monsters. Their movements are jerky and clumsy, their defenses nonexistent apart from their supernatural toughness, which impossibly strong abominations simply ignore.

The mantis alone shreds five tree soldiers before I reach it, and with how fast the creature is moving, I don’t dare touch it to undo its evolution. Instead, an arc of lightning electrocutes it. The lightning doesn’t kill the damn thing, but several tons of rock smash straight into the paralyzed monster, achieving the same effect as a mundane stone thrown at the creature from which the abomination has evolved from.

I jump from place to place, removing the biggest pockets of resistance, but it’s already clear the night is ours. I wonder how I would have acted if the ones fighting weren’t automatons, but people. Back in my previous life, I tried to end all sieges with minimal loss of life, usually by assassinating the leadership. Would that have worked tonight? Or would the abominations have flooded the city and started a carnage regardless?

Another wave of abominations rushes out of the ruined temple and into the violent night. This time it’s around fifty minor ones, and the tree soldiers can handle them, albeit with some losses. I run around like mad, finishing off enemies powerful enough to stand up to the treants, when I see a ring of nine men protecting four women and seven children.

Dressed in nightgowns, they wield the inquisitor’s poleaxes, hacking at the two treants attacking them.

“Desist!” I shout, but the tree soldiers don’t react. I touch one, and it devolves back into a tree, its roots digging into the ruined pavement. The other smashes a man into a bloody pulp before I turn it into a tree, and suddenly, there’s a moment of awkward tension.

The inquisitors glare at me, and I know they are inquisitors, scanner doesn’t see them at all, unlike the women and the children.

“You have betrayed humanity and consorted with the abominations and the wormlords.” My voice is surprisingly calm as I voice the accusations. “Surrender and face trial.”

Two of the men look shocked, but the rest of the survivors’ faces twist into grimaces of rage.

“The heralds are a trial sent onto humanity by the Holy One!” a zealot snarls at me. “They will purge sinners and heretics from our ranks!”

With that he lunges, as do the rest, with only one hesitating and standing there dazed.

My first instinct is to smash them with Batsy, but I’m an archmage. The stone around me turns to quicksand, swallowing the inquisitors. The men scream and throw righteous curses at me until they sink beneath the stone, which once more turns solid.

“You don’t wish to join them?” I ask the pale man, who had fallen onto his knees.

He shakes his head. “My father was a messenger. He died in an abomination attack when I was five years old. I didn’t know.”

He stares at his hands as if they were bloody, hyperventilating, but I can’t spare any more attention for him. Other tree soldiers are advancing towards me and the civilians.

“Edna,” my voice spreads throughout the city, empowered by magic. “Command the tree soldiers to attack abominations only. We can put the rest of the survivors to trial.”

I wait, but the tree soldiers don’t stop, and I’m forced to root another one. As minutes trickle by, I realize Edna won’t show mercy. The inquisition has slaughtered anyone related to mages, and now she plans to return the favor.

“What now?” I mutter instead of cursing. Fight Edna? I’m supposed to do it to protect the children of murderers and madmen trying to doom humanity to extinction? With parallel thinking, I ask myself hundreds of questions on morality, on whether the world would be a better place without humans, but all of that is sophistry. Seven kids, five and younger, stand before me, and I’ll be damned if I let them die. Edna can hang them once they reach adulthood for all I care, but I’m not letting her kill children on my watch.

I sink the women and children beneath the ground, making a bunker with a roofed chimney to give them access to enough air. With no targets in sight, the treants disperse, searching for other threats. My action is hypocritical. Heavens know how many children died in the razing of the city, but I now run with another mission, to save as many as I can.

Dawn arrives, and time has proven my efforts sorely insufficient. I don’t know how many children there were in the central district last night, but only thirty-four survived to see the dawn.

I unleash a wider, two-dimensional, ring-shaped burst of scanner, counting four hundred and thirty-nine remaining tree-soldiers, and seventeen trees I rooted in various places while saving the children.

I’m not entirely sure what I expected to happen here. A boss fight? A sudden revelation that the pope was a wormlord? Something. Running around, saving children and infants from Edna’s wrath never touched the edges of my wildest speculation.

And now, it’s all over. I walk over to the tunnel leading into the temple’s depths from which the abominations came. It’s huge, large enough for a pair of tree soldiers to march down, and it probably leads to some sort of spawning ground or something. But I guess that’s worry for another day.

I bring up the anarchist screen, and read the level up condition, disappointed I haven’t advanced already.

To level up, start a large-scale riot or a nation-scale public disturbance.

I thought staging a coup matched the second option pretty damn well, but I guess BSD disagrees with me. I clear my throat and cast my voice all over the outer city.

“Residents of Holy City, the church and its inquisition have fallen, guilty for conspiring against all humans and joining hands with the wormlords. A handful of surviving inquisitors will testify about what they know and about their involvement in this entire incident, but a larger investigation will root out all traitors and those who collaborated with monsters. The church’s tyranny has ended, and a new age is upon us.”

[You have leveled up.

Select a defining feature within sixty seconds or a random one will be assigned to you.

Visionary - You stand a small chance of recognizing the spark of greatness in those you encounter.

Derivative - You stand a small chance of mimicking the talents of great people you encounter.]

BSD hits, revealing that our definitions of nation-wide public disturbance differ. I let go of semantics, instead focusing on the options I’m given. Visionary seems like a good way to find students, apprentices, and perhaps dark horses even before they are dark horses. Derivative, on the other hand, allows me to grow stronger by meeting others who are great.

What does encounter mean in BSD’s context? What if I view a parade of champions from afar? Have I encountered them? With how restrictive BSD is, my guess is no. The only way I will encounter those I can copy is if I am already a great person, and at that point, copying others is pointless.

I make the choice, and I don’t think I’ll regret it, when another blue screen appears.

[The abyss stares back. Your brilliance is no longer concealed from the eldritch.]

What? The blue screen message disappears on its own, or I possibly removed it in my moment of shock, when the Guide’s golden screen appears before my eyes.

[There you are, little time-hopper.]
