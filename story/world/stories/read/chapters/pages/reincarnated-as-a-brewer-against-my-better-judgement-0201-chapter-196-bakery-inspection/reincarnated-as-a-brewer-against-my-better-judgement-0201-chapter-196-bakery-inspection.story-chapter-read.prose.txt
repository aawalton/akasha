Alric waited outside the bakery. Two of his guards had gone in to check that it was safe, while four others stood outside with him. He had tried to argue that it was a bakery, but all he’d gotten was the kind of looks that said he was being reckless.

Alric sighed. Clearly, butter knives were the new menace to his safety.

One guard eventually appeared at the doorway; he gave a big nod, then everyone headed inside. The first thing Alric noticed was the smell.

Fresh bread, smoke, and a slight sweet scent. Alric took a deep breath through his nose and smiled.

“This smells amazing,” Alric said, glancing around the space.

The owner stood nearby and smiled nervously. Vale was standing by one of the worktables with paper. Seren stood next to him, but she was watching the bakers. All the bakers were standing on the far side, bowing.

Alric glanced at the guards in the room with a frown. “Please, I’m not a noble. You don’t need to bow.” Alric called out to the room at large. The bakers straightened and glanced around, clearly avoiding looking at him.

And that’s the issue with sending guards ahead.

Alric turned to the owner. “They won’t get in your way.” He said, gesturing to his guards, “They're just being careful.” It wasn’t much of an apology, he knew, but he wasn’t sure how it would be taken in the context.

The owner nodded quickly and smiled again. “Of course, Master Alric.”

Alric looked around again; he caught himself from sighing because it might be taken the wrong way. Everyone seemed uncomfortable, but he wasn’t surprised by that.

The best thing to do then is get it done.

The bakery was smaller than he expected after he had heard Seren and Vale talk about it. It extended quite far back from the street, but the three large ovens took up nearly the entire rear wall. Worktables then filled most of the open middle space. Sacks of what he assumed was flour were stacked under the worktables, pushed into any available space, and were generally just everywhere.

It’s not tiny, but it sure isn’t much when everyone is moving around.

Alric approached the ovens first. All three had fires lit, and the doors were left open. Heat was coming off them in waves; Alric loosened his cloak slightly. Black soot marks covered the stone around the openings, and the insides were nearly entirely black. Looking down, he noticed how much ash covered the floor around the ovens.

If I was doing this or getting serious about it, I’d use heating stones.

He glanced over at Seren and then remembered he didn’t have a mage on hand for that. Battery was already too busy with the brewery; he was managing the distillery, and if Lessa was going to be a duellist or guard for him, then she needed to avoid mana exhaustion.

Bah, I wouldn’t need firewood then. Then all of this gets real cheap, and I probably sink another guild and people lose jobs.

Alric sighed, which seemed to be the signal for the bakers to look anywhere but at him. Possibly they feared he might start critiquing their technique next.

“Something wrong?” Vale asked, approaching with papers.

“No. You can ignore me. I’m just understanding a few things, that's all. I’d do this differently.” Alric said with a shrug and gestured to the ovens.

“Oh? How so?” Vale asked, leaning forward.

Alric’s expression turned flat as he faced him. “I’d use magic, and no, you cannot borrow a mage. So, it’s not happening.”

Vale chuckled; he made a note. Which made Alric frown.

“I said ignore me,” Alric said, giving Vale a bored expression.

“I heard you,” Vale said, nodding.

Alric narrowed his eyes but decided to leave that alone for now. Several cooling racks stood near the one wall. Seeing the bread like this made Alric both salivate and grin. They looked like real loaves of bread rather than his experiments with Seren. As he approached, his excitement dwindled.

“These are the test batches?” Alric asked out loud. He expected the owner or one of the bakers to answer, but instead it was Seren.

She stepped closer. “Most of it is from this morning.”

Alric picked up one of the loaves; he gave it a gentle squeeze. It was soft and gave under his fingers before going back to its original shape.

“See, this one is good.” He said, putting it on the worktable, “But these have issues.” Alric picked up another and put it beside the other loaf. “This one is no good.”

Alric continued going through the racks, placing different loaves next to the first. Eventually there were six.

“Uh, probably best if you all gather around so I can show you all?” Alric said, glancing at the bakers. Everyone shuffled forward till they could see.

“Alright, so this one is great. It's soft if I break it. See how it's crumbed inside and the crust isn’t too hard?” Alric said, breaking open the best loaf. He glanced around at the bakers; they nodded.

“Now this one? This one hasn’t risen. It needed more time to ‘proof’” Alric explained, pointing at the shorter loaf that hadn’t risen as far. “I don’t even need to break it to tell you it’s too dense inside, but I will anyway.” He said, breaking it apart and showing the bakers how dense it was in the middle.

It’s still a lot better than most of the bread in the city, though.

Which was not much of an achievement to Alric; he considered the ordinary bread in the city to double as building material or a reliable weapon in an emergency.

“It’s the same recipe, though?” One of the bakers said, comparing the two loaves.

Alric frowned at that, then he glanced around the room. “Ahh… My guess then is this loaf was too close to the door when you proofed it?” Alric said, pointing. When the bakers didn’t answer, Alric explained further. “This wants to be warm, not cold or hot, when it’s rising or proofing.”

The bakers glanced around the space and nodded, which didn’t really tell him anything. The owner cleared his throat. “There wasn’t enough space. They all rose, though?”

Alric smiled at him. “They aren’t rising the same. It just needs to be warmer, is all. Maybe close the door a little?” Alric suggested, and more people nodded.

“Can’t we just move them all closer to the ovens?” Vale asked, glancing up from his notes.

“That will probably be too hot. I don’t know your solution. You just want somewhere reasonably warm and consistent. You can take the cooling loaves and use them?” Alric said with a shrug. Vale nodded and wrote down more.

“Anyway, so this loaf. It’s too dark.” Alric said, lifting the next one. He tapped it; the crust was too hard. Eventually he managed to break it, and the inside was doughy. “So this one was in the oven when it was too hot, and then not long enough. See how it's burnt outside but wet inside?” Alric passed the loaf halves to the bakers, who inspected them and frowned.

Alric lifted another and noticed the ash underneath. He did the same with the others. They all had ash underneath.

Not much they can do about that.

“Do you know which ovens these two come from?” Alric asked, lifting two loaves. The bakers shook their heads. “Well, the thing to do is take the time to learn the ovens. One might need more time to cool; one may hold heat better.” Alric said with a shrug.

He showed how another loaf had a crust that looked right but was raw inside. He then began tasting and noted the differences.

“Are you using different flours?” Alric asked, looking at the owner.

The owner nodded. “Some. We get from three different mills.”

Ooof. So, another consistency issue, then.

“Can you show me the flours?” Alric asked. The owner nodded.

They walked through different sacks; some were wheat, some were wheat-rye blends, and one was finer flour, but not always. Another mill apparently changed differently depending on what grain they had.

“This is another issue,” Alric said, standing upright. He was rubbing some of the flour between his fingers.

Vale let out a sigh. “How?”

Alric shrugged. “Not all flour acts the same. Seren knows this.” Alric pointed to her.

Seren nodded. “Each flour we tried was different. The carters loaf we should have tried to make by the river, with how much water it needed.” That got a chuckle from some of the bakers.

They spent another quarter bell discussing bread, which mostly involved Alric talking and the bakers nodding with the enthusiasm of people hoping the conversation would end soon. The problems, as always, came down to heat, ovens, and flour. Once they dealt with those, they could address the recipe.

Just as he was about to leave, Alric realized he’d spent the entire visit criticizing everything except the wallpaper. He paused and turned to the owner. “Honestly, it sounds worse than it is. The bread’s good. It’s just inconsistent.” He grabbed two loaves for headquarters, because nothing says ‘constructive feedback’ like walking off with the product. “Fix the heat, mix the flours, and figure out which oven is your problem child.” He said with a smile.

The owner let out a deep breath and nodded.

“That sounds like a lot,” Vale said from nearby.

Alric just shrugged at him. “You weren’t there to see the brewery start. It was far worse than this.”

The owner and Vale followed Alric outside. Alric glanced back in at Seren, but she was talking to the younger female baker. That was when Alric went round to look at the front street counter. He sucked in air through his teeth at it.

“Another problem?” Vale asked, glancing at it.

Alric pointed at the slope in the street. “This isn’t going to drain right. Snow is going to pile up and well.” Alric approached the counter and tested it. It was very wobbly. “You don’t have an overhang, so water will drip down onto anything.”

“Uh, we don’t normally open during the winter, Mister Alric. If it’s raining, we sell through the door.” The owner explained, pointing.

“I’m guessing snow gathers at the door too?” Alric asked, looking at the space. The owner nodded. “Simple enough thing. It may take some people to clear the snow.” Alric said with a shrug; he glanced at Vale, who sighed.

“Anything else, Mister Alric?” Vale asked, glancing down at his notes.

“I think that’s all. Seems pretty strong.” Alric said with a shrug. He then said his goodbyes and headed back to headquarters.

Once Alric was out of earshot, Vale turned to the owner. “There is so much stuff you’ll need to fix.”

“Me?” The owner asked, his eyes going wide.

Vale just shrugged. “We are renting from you. We can’t fix your building.”

“But it’s been fine for years?” the owner protested.

Vale pointed to the counter and the lack of an overhang. “We hired your bakery to work through the winter. This tells me you need to fix the bakery to do that.”

“But—”

“We also need to fix the tables inside, the one oven, because I think that’s the one that burnt the bread.”

The owner glared and put both hands on his hips. “Mister Vale, do you expect me to rebuild the whole bakery?”

“No?” Vale said, tilting his head; he looked genuinely confused. “Just fix the problems.”

Inside the bakery, Seren was talking to the only female baker. She was much younger than the other bakers, who were at least close to the owner's age. The other bakers were clearing the test loaves Alric had used to demonstrate.

“How old did you say you were?” Seren asked,

“Uh… twenty-six summers, Mistress.” The baker replied.

“And you’ve been here over a year?” Seren asked, tilting her head.

“Yes, Miss. Last spring.”

“And your name?”

“Tibi, Miss.”

Seren nodded. She glanced at the other bakers with a frown.
