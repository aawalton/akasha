“The point of no return?” The words echoed with an aura of doom that made Lexie even more anxious than she already was. “What does that mean? Who are you?”

“It doesn’t matter who I am, only that you should heed my warning.”

“How am I supposed to heed the warning of someone I don’t know?”

He let out a frustrated human-sounding breath and then told her, “I guess you can refer to me as the system.”

“You’re the system?”

“Of sorts.”

“And the system talks?”

“When it wants to.”

“And the system also has the power to freeze me in space?” Since when did it control space and time? Or was she really frozen? Was she merely trapped in her own head? It freaked her out that she didn’t know the answer.

“You’re not the system,” she responded. "The system wouldn’t sound so human.”

“Says who?"

"Says me." She felt the grip on her body slacken, but she knew it wasn't intentional. He was losing his hold on her. She could wiggle her fingers and toes just a bit. If she strained, she could probably break the bond, but she found that she didn’t want to quite yet. First, she needed to understand what was going on.

“What do you mean by the point of no return?” she asked.

“I mean exactly that. You will not be able to undo things from this point.”

“Does that mean I could have undone things before?”

“I cannot answer that question.”

“What exactly is it that you don’t want me to see?”

"I can't answer that either."

“Okay, well then, you see the dilemma you’ve placed me in, right? I’m supposed to just take your word for it and not find the answers I’m looking for because you say so."

“You can find your answers without this. This will answer more than you should be asking, and it will change everything. I’m warning you, Lexie Sparrowfoot. Go back.”

“Tell me then,” she said. “Tell me how else to find my answers, or better yet, tell me the answers to my questions. Where is Saint-0? What does the Emperor want to do with him? How do we stop him?”

There was a second of silence that held like a tight breath. It felt like a struggle. Like someone was wiggling against invisible bonds that had bound them.

Then he said, “I cannot tell you any of that.”

She sighed. “Well then, I have no other choice.”

“You do. But you'll make the wrong one. I should have known.”

Just like that, the bonds were gone.

She appeared in...well, she didn’t know where she was. It resembled a spaceship, overlooking the galaxy. An old spaceship that she was pretty sure she had seen in Star Wars.

Overhead was harsh fluorescent lighting that showed off the sleek curves of the walls and the touch pads on them. Exposed pipes came out of the partitions. Light flashed on the buttons on a control board, and an intercom beeped from nowhere.

"Welcome, Lexie Sparrowfoot."

She stared out at the glassy sky, and just then the stars glowed too brightly, temporarily blinding her.

Then, the gongs started.

As they beat, Lexie turned around, her heart racing. She felt a presence surrounding her, but she didn’t know what it was or where it was coming from.

All she knew was the heat spreading across her soul.

It was like a reaction bubbling within her, and it boiled her insides until she screamed and fell to her knees.

The hot sizzle of a brand zinged up her spine, melted her bones, and she cried out for help, for reprieve, for anything.

Then, it stopped as randomly as it had started.

The bright light retreated, and everything went back to how it was, the weird spaceship-looking room becoming normal once more.

“You shouldn’t have come,” a voice said behind her.

Lexie got to her feet and turned around to meet…a man.

He looked just like a regular man, too. He was wearing cargo pants and a Hawaiian t-shirt underneath a dirty lab coat. Brown sandals on his feet. He had an average build with average brown hair and eyes and a very average face. There was something familiar yet foreign about it. Or maybe it was the fact that it was so indistinct.

She felt as if she looked away from him for one instant, she would forget what he looked like.

He pushed up his glasses as he pulled up his computer chair, sitting on it. “You should have gone when you had the chance. But I suppose now it’s too late.”

It took a second to realize that he was the voice in her head.

“Who are you?”

“The Engineer.”

Lexie cocked her head. “As in the old one? One of the original creators of the ISTS.”

“Yes.”

“How...you should be dead.”

He adjusted his glasses again before he responded. “In a way, I suppose I am.”

“You don’t look dead.”

He didn’t answer that question. “Why don’t you finish finding what you’re looking for and leave me to it?”

Lexie assessed him and the space and pieced a few things together in her mind. “You don’t want me here.”

“No. I don’t.”

“But you can’t kick me out either, otherwise you would have already.” Lexie brushed her chin in thought as she paced her feet, making zero noise on the floor. “Why can’t you kick me out?”

“I’m sure you’ll find that out on your own.”

“The faster you answer my questions, the faster I can leave.”

He stared at her closely, but she couldn’t tell if it was dislike that highlighted itself in his gaze or simply resignation. She didn’t understand. What had she done that was so bad?

“There was very little chance you would do this,” he muttered, “We weren’t expecting it.”

“You and who?”

He didn’t answer that either.

“Is it because I’m a Chosen? Is that why you’re letting me stay?”

“No,” he smirked. “Although that played into it in an unexpected way.”

“How long can I stay here?”

“A long time, but that time passes more slowly here than outside. So you should probably hurry back soon, or your friend will be worried.”

“You’re in my head, watching everything I do.”

“Yes.”

“Are you in all the other Chosen’s heads as well?”

“Is that truly the question you want to ask?”

Yes. She had so many questions she wanted to ask, about the Chosen Program, the ISTS, all of it.

But she would settle for the more life-threatening question right now.

“This space belongs to Janus, right? The Emperor…he wants something from here. Doesn’t he?”

The man sighed. "You know I can't help you. Anything you need to discover, do it on your own. And quickly.”

Hmm.

Now that Lexie was here, she wasn’t sure what to do. She visually assessed the room, the buttons, the drawers, and the shelves with books and all manner of things on them. Were those CD's? Floppy disks? Why did he have that there?

She decided that she would keep asking questions. She might not get answers, but she might be able to figure out what was going on based on his responses. “Did you intentionally make this place look like the inside of a spaceship? Or did it come that way? Was it a stylistic choice or does it serve a function?”

“You should leave. Before it’s too late.”

“I thought it was already too late.”

“There’s a chance that it isn’t. There’s a chance that you might still make the right choice, as small as it is.”

Lexie exhaled roughly. “You know the one thing I don't understand? This tug of war you guys are playing between free will and determinism. I thought the whole point of being a Chosen was making me choose my own path, but now you'retelling me that there is a right choice and I’m not making it? Has this all played out before? Is the story going off the rails because of me?”

“You were never supposed to be here in the first place.”

“So why not get rid of me?”

His eyes turned flat. “You think we didn’t try?”

Her heart went cold.

“There’s only so much you can push fate before it pushes back, and there’s only so much chaos that it can tolerate in its system.”

“Fate isn’t a system."

"No, but it can work like one."

"So what did I do wrong again?" She was hoping to catch him off guard so that he would admit it.

His eyes glowed menacingly. "This fight is much bigger than you, Lexie Sparrowfoot."

"That doesn't answer my question." She trailed over to the CD's, sliding them out of their compartment. The CD'sappeared to be old video games she'd never heard of before. She skimmed through the spines of the comics and fantasy books that lined the shelf. This place was a nerd’s paradise. "Do the Fae know about the Chosen Program?”

“I can't answer–"

“Yeah, I know." She sighed and rubbed her temple. Now that she was here, she was pulled in two different directions. A part of her wanted to keep asking questions about the Chosen program and eventually get to the bottom of it, but the other half knew that the Emperor was a priority.

She was worried about Torin and how much time was passing, but she wouldn't leave without the answers. “I mean, it would make sense for them not to know, right? Because if they knew, they would either steal it or shut it down. Which only begs the question, how in the hell are you guys managing to keep it from the Fae?”

He didn’t answer.

“On the other hand, maybe they do know. Maybe part of the reason they’re so desperate to take over Earth is to get their hands on this program to use it to run their own thing. It’s made almost entirely of alchemy and engineering, so it’s not something the Fae understand. It’s a wholly human machine, and it must bother them on some level that humans have and know something they don’t.” Lexie chuckled as she thought of the ambassador. “It's truly the pinnacle of human invention. But then why would the Alchemist ask the Emperor to bomb this place? I’m assuming the Emperor didn’t do it of his own volition.”

The man didn't answer, so Lexie filled in.

“Or maybe he did.”

She saw something then. A familiar symbol on a floppy disk. She slid it out and held it up.

Where had she seen that before?

It hit her. In one of Vulcan's visions. He'd had something like this in hand at one point.

"Did you send this to Vulcan?" she asked.

No answer.

“Can I take it?”

No answer.

She took it as a yes, although he didn’t look happy about it. In fact, he gritted his teeth while she tucked it in her pocket. Which meant that once more, he couldn’t stop her.

Good. She would take more things. There had to be a clue in these items they could use. Lexie began picking up everything odd she could see in the space as she talked. “Back to my original question, why would the Alchemist ask the Emperor to bomb a place that he helped create? A place that was the pinnacle of human imagination. It was either that he was trying to get rid of something in it, which seems overkill, or there was another plot." She looked at the Engineer's face as he tried hard to control his features.

"Unless the Alchemist didn't tell the Emperor to go on a bombing spree. How could he, after all, when he was locked up? Maybe the Emperor did it on his own? But why? Why go against the Alchemist? Is it just revenge? Was he insane? But he didn’t sound insane in those messages. As a matter of fact, he sounded like someone else was calling the shots. And this someone was Mikhail. He must have something here that he doesn’t want to be found. Isn’t that right?”

He seemed even more annoyed by her word salad.

"You really should go."

Lexie knew he was probably right, but there was still so much she wanted to know. A separate theory was forming in her mind, and she wanted to pepper him with questions, but he wouldn't answer anyway, and she thought she had enough clues to at least make some educated guesses. Also, she could probably come back if she needed to, if he didn't find a way to keep her out.

But just in case, she had to ask about the pain she'd felt.

"What had happened to me when I first arrived in this spaceship? After the gongs," she asked.

He brushed his hair back. "As with everything, you'll find out soon enough."

Lexie sighed. "I guess I'll talk to you later."

He didn't respond.

Lexie teleported out of the space and instantly felt like death.

It was the hardest teleport she'd ever done, agony ripping her veins, pain splitting through her skull. Shit. How was leaving harder than arriving?

It had the biggest strain on her pathways she'd ever felt, and she was pretty sure a few things had ruptured in her body.

It was almost as bad as exhausting herself in Indigo. She almost blacked out, and it didn't make sense how this was somehow harder fighting hordes of orcs.

She understood why after a second.

That space, in the ISTS, was the farthest she'd ever travelled in time or space, and it hurt like hell coming back, so much so that the second she appeared on Indigo, she collapsed on the ground, coughing blood.

It was nighttime. And Torin was gone.

The streets were empty.

"Torin?" she called out, and when she'd recovered a little, she took out her phone to call him.

He answered instantly. "Lexie! Where are you?"

"I'm at Indigo. Where are you?"

"At Indigo? Have you been there for the last two days?"

"Two days?”

She checked the time on the phone, and it showed that it was indeed two days later. Oh my God. "I thought only a few minutes had passed."

"No." He exhaled roughly. "I freaked out. I thought something had happened to you, but your father seemed to know you were safe. He said he had a chip in your head telling him that you were alive and unharmed, and he thought you'dprobably just gone back to the dungeon to create something."

Yeah, let's go with that. "I'm fine. Anyway, I have a theory I want to run by you."

"Us too. We found Mikhail."

"You did?"

"Yes. Tate did. I'm not exactly sure how, but he said he knows a cafe he frequently visits, although he didn't want us to go there until you got back."

Good call, Tate. "Okay. I'm on my way."

Lexie really didn't want to teleport herself there. Luckily, she had her father's teleportation orb in her inventory, and she used it to get home.

Where both Tate, Torin, and Aiden were waiting for her.

And Torin was somehow in pajamas that he was clearly too big for.

"Did you sleep here?" Lexie asked.

"Yes," His cheeks pinkened. "I didn't want to go home."

Lexie understood. She glanced at her father. "So, what you just made is an extra room?"

"Why not?" Aiden said.

"Fair." She addressed Tate. "You found Mikhail."

"Yes."

"Good. Very good." She took a deep breath. "But first of all, I want to run a theory by you all, something that just came to me on my little excursion." She cleared her throat. "What if Mikhail and the Alchemist are two different people?"

Tate raised an eyebrow. "I thought we already established that they were."

"Yes, but they're not just different. They’re foils. Their plans are similar but in opposition to one another."

"What are you saying?" Torin said.

She took a deep breath and considered the crazy thing she was about to say, which was less crazy after the trip she had taken today and the clues she'd picked up.

"I think Mikhail is the First Mage."
