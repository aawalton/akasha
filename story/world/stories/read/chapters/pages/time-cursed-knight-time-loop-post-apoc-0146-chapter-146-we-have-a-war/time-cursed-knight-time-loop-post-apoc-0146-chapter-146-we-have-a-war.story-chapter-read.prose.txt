Vaelith was beginning to suspect he would die.

Of boredom.

He woke before dawn because decades of campaign routine did not vanish merely because his new command had no campaign to offer. He trained until the first light touched the upper towers, ate alone, and walked the forward line twice.

The first inspection took him west.

The second brought him back east.

He observed the morning barrage from the central ridge. Four direct-casters crossed the trench at the designated intervals, raised their hands, and shot fire over the human fortifications. The first impact struck an empty communication trench. The second collapsed a surface shelter that had been rebuilt three times that month. The remaining shots disappeared against bunker wards.

The humans would repair the damage before nightfall.

Afterward, Vaelith returned to his quarters and waited for the day to end.

He maintained his magical practice. He reviewed readiness reports. He inspected the supply allocation for observation posts that had not faced a shortage in seventy-six years. Once a week, he met the officers responsible for rotating bombardment teams, and they informed him that the bombardment teams continued to rotate.

Beyond that, there was nothing to do.

The Northern Theater offered no politics worth navigating. Vaelith held the highest command in the territory, which placed every officer under him and made every external commander reluctant to acknowledge him. Those advancing elsewhere had no reason to build an alliance with the keeper of a dying front. Those already established had every reason to let him disappear here.

Reports still arrived.

That made the idleness worse.

A southern expedition had taken three mountain passes from the Vireth. A shaping force on another continent had broken a Durak-Kel surface bastion after nine months of excavation. Two colonies on another world had entered open conflict over access to a stable crossing.

Even the failures described movement. Armies advanced. Officers made decisions that mattered. Campaigns ruined careers or forged them.

Vaelith sat in a chair and watched humans repair trenches.

On the ninth morning of his command, he placed a transfer petition on his desk.

The document had taken less than an hour to draft. It asked for reassignment to any active theater where his experience against distributed fortifications could be applied. He had listed the Vireth frontier first, then the eastern continental colonies, then three off-world campaigns whose climates ranged from poisonous to barely survivable.

Requesting a transfer so soon after appointment would damage him.

The Palace of Conquest would interpret it as an admission that he lacked the discipline to fulfill an unglamorous command. His rivals would call him impatient. The elders who had buried him in the north might respond by assigning him to a world where the atmosphere corroded exposed skin or the local gravity ruined a soldier's joints within a decade.

At least those places had battles.

A worse planet with a living war seemed preferable to a comfortable room where his greatest enemy was his chair.

Vaelith sat behind the narrow desk in his quarters and watched the petition update.

The sheet was living paper. Silver script shifted across its surface as the administrative network confirmed available commands. Vacancies appeared and vanished in real time.

Three positions remained open.

Deputy commander of an island suppression force.

Operations officer on a mining colony contested by the Durak-Kel.

Commander of an Ael'vari expeditionary group being assembled for deployment through an unstable off-world portal.

The last offered the greatest danger and the best chance of distinction.

Vaelith dipped his brush into the ink cup.

He held it above the signature line.

If he signed, the request would reach the Palace of Conquest within minutes. There would be no quiet withdrawal afterward. No way to pretend the petition had been an intellectual exercise rather than a declaration that the Northern Territories were beneath him.

The brush hovered.

A dull explosion rolled through the walls.

Vaelith looked toward the window.

The sound was too close.

The morning barrage had ended nearly an hour ago. The next rotation wasn't scheduled until midday.

A second sound followed the first. Smaller cracks in rapid succession. Then shouts.

Vaelith lowered the brush.

Another explosion struck hard enough to tremble through the floor beneath his boots.

The corridor beyond his quarters had changed.

Officers ran toward the western stairs. A communications attendant stood beside a relay with both hands pressed to its surface, speaking too quickly for Vaelith to catch every word. Two soldiers emerged from the armory pulling their blades.

Vaelith caught the nearest officer by the shoulder.

The elf twisted, saw who held him, and snapped upright.

"Commander."

"Report."

"An attack on the western line."

Vaelith waited for the rest.

The officer didn't provide it.

"Whose attack?" Vaelith asked.

"The humans."

For a moment, the corridor's noise seemed to recede.

"The humans," Vaelith repeated.

"Yes, Commander."

Vaelith released his shoulder.

"Explain."

The officer gathered himself.

"They crossed the dead ground under mobile cover. Four reinforced machines. Our observation post did not identify the movement until they had already passed through the outer half of the human mine field."

"How?"

"We don't know. Their defensive formations became inert along a narrow passage. The humans drove directly through it."

The cracks sounded again.

Vaelith recognized none of them. Too sharp for elven spellwork. Too fast for the humans' rigid casting methods.

"They struck the western trench before anyone could relay a complete warning," the officer continued. "The first observation post has fallen. They are trying to seize the rise behind it and establish a fortified position."

Vaelith started toward the stairs.

"How many?"

"Initial estimates placed the assault group below fifty. More may be crossing."

"Below fifty?"

"Yes."

"And they broke the western position?"

"The position wasn't prepared to repel a ground assault."

That was true. The west had been left with observers and the barrage calendar. Whatever close fighters had survived the last failed offensive had been sent somewhere that still counted as a war.

It was also intolerable.

The western rise overlooked a broad section of the shallow Ael'vari trench. If the humans entrenched there, they would hold elevated ground behind the forward line. They could fire east along its length, threaten the relay towers, and force the defenders to abandon nearly three leagues of exposed position.

The ditch that had been sufficient for centuries would become a death trap.

Vaelith felt something he had not experienced since his arrival.

Urgency.

"Take me there."

The officer hesitated. "Commander, the western line may not be secure."

"I had inferred that from the explosions."

They descended the stairs two at a time.

Outside, the command grounds had erupted into chaos. Reserve soldiers formed beneath shouted orders. Direct-casters emerged from their quarters fastening armor and tying back loose hair. A communications team clustered around a relay tree, feeding mana into its roots.

The western horizon flashed.

A plume of dirt rose above the ridge.

Vaelith followed the officer onto the main path and broke into a run. The terrain between command and the western line had been shaped for easy movement, a broad ribbon of packed soil running behind the trench. Under normal conditions, the distance took twelve minutes at a walking pace.

They crossed it in less than four.

"What reserves are moving?" Vaelith asked.

"Third caster rotation and two shaping teams."

"Only two?"

"The others are stationed at the eastern and central sectors."

"Recall them."

"If the humans exploit the movement—"

"They are already exploiting our immobility. Recall them."

"Yes, Commander."

The officer touched two fingers to the relay clasp at his throat and began issuing orders.

They crested a low rise.

The western trench appeared ahead.

Smoke drifted over the position. One shaped-wood observation tower leaned at an angle, its lower support split apart. The other had collapsed across the trench and forced the defenders into the open.

The human assault had reached the ridge.

Vaelith saw the machines first.

Four low vehicles sat scattered across the churned ground between the lines. Copper plates had been fixed to their fronts in crude wedges. One burned beside the trench. Another had driven through the shallow fortification and stopped halfway up the slope, its front assembly crushed inward.

Behind the wreckage, humans fought their way across the rise.

They wore Iron Cross insignia.

Vaelith knew the symbol from intelligence reports. Human close-combat specialists who fed mana through their bodies rather than shaping it into spells. Dangerous at short range. Crude, self-destructive, and ordinarily irrelevant if killed before they reached striking distance.

More soldiers poured through the seized section of trench carrying shovels, timber braces, and packed bundles of cloth. They were already cutting into the reverse slope. Others dragged sections of the destroyed observation post into position as cover.

They had not come merely to raid.

They intended to stay.

"Where is the archmage assigned to this theater?" Vaelith asked.

The officer beside him went quiet.

Vaelith looked at him.

"Bring the archmage forward. One advanced spell will clear the ridge. Once they break, we follow them through the inactive corridor and take their first trench before the defensive network recovers."

The officer's throat moved.

"Commander…"

"Where is he?"

"He was reassigned."

Vaelith stared at him.

"When?"

"After the previous offensive failed. High Command determined that an archmage had no efficient application against a static human front. He was sent south, to the Vireth territories."

Vaelith waited.

The officer understood that the silence wasn't permission to stop.

"The veteran teams went with him, along with the surplus rotations." His eyes stayed on the smoke. "What remains was to staff the observation posts, and fire four barrages a day."

Vaelith clicked his tongue.

Of course.

The north did not need an army because the humans never attacked.

He had known the posting was a grave. He hadn't inventoried how thoroughly High Command had hollowed the garrison, because until this morning he hadn't needed to.

Ahead, an Ael'vari defender rose from the trench. Mana gathered around his hands, stone flowing up from the soil to form a barrier.

One of the humans pointed a small object.

Crack.

The elf's head snapped back.

Gold sprayed behind him. His body folded into the trench before the unfinished barrier could rise past his knees.

Vaelith stopped.

The sound had come after the motion. No visible spell had crossed the distance. No matrix had formed. No mana discharge had traveled from the weapon to the target.

Another human raised the same kind of device.

Crack.

An Ael'vari soldier spun and fell with a hole punched through his chest.

"Are they using powder weapons again?" Vaelith asked.

The nearest field captain shook her head. Blood streaked one side of her campaign coat, though Vaelith could not tell whether it belonged to her.

"No powder scent. We tried igniting their ammunition stores when they crossed."

"And?"

"Nothing happened. The projectiles appear to be solid metal."

"Then destroy the weapons."

"We have tried."

As if summoned by the words, three direct-casters struck the nearest human position together.

Fire descended from above. Stone lances erupted from the slope. A compressed bolt of air tore through the smoke between them.

The humans moved before the spells landed.

One threw himself behind a copper plate ripped from a wrecked vehicle. The fire spread across it and washed outward. Another met the first stone lance with a greatsword, breaking its point at the cost of being driven to one knee. The air bolt caught a third man across the shoulder and threw him down the slope.

He rolled, came back to his feet, and charged.

The defenders had tried to destroy them.

It had not been enough.

Vaelith looked past the nearest fighting.

A figure moved through the human formation.

White hair.

For an instant, Vaelith saw only fragments between soldiers and drifting smoke. A large human body. A sword in one hand. One of the small metal weapons in the other. Dark blue mist left his mouth each time he exhaled.

An Ael'vari fighter lunged at him from the trench.

The white-haired human caught the blade on his own, turned it aside, and fired the weapon into the elf's abdomen at arm's length. The impact folded the defender around the wound. Before the body touched the earth, the human had already stepped past it.

Vaelith felt his mouth curve.

The field captain beside him noticed.

"Commander?"

The transfer petition remained unsigned on his desk.

For the first time since the Palace of Conquest had condemned him to the Northern Territories, Vaelith no longer cared.

"Send word to every reserve position," he said. "The humans are attempting to establish themselves on the western rise. All available direct-casters are to concentrate here. Shaping teams will build a secondary line behind the ridge and prepare covered approaches for a counterattack."

The captain stared at him. "All reserves?"

"All of them."

"That will weaken the rest of the front."

"Then we will discover whether this is their only surprise."

Another volley of hard mechanical cracks rolled across the ridge.

Gold flashed among the defenders.

Vaelith watched the white-haired human climb over the shattered trench and laughed softly.

The north isn't dead after all.

"Move," he ordered. "We have a war."
