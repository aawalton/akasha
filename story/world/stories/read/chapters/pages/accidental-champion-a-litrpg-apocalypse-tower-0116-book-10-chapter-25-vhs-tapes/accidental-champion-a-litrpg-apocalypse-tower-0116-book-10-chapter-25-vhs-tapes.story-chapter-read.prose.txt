Xavier wanted to do all three methods of strengthening his spiritual sense.

The instant all the options had been shown to him, he’d felt a strong inclination to master all three. Admittedly, for a long time, he’d been neglecting his base spiritual sense. It had strengthened over the years since he’d gained the use of his Immortal Soul lens, but it hadn’t strengthened as much as he wished.

According to the Personal System—and he had no reason not to believe it—the base spiritual sense could become stronger than Xavier’s Immortal Soul lens. That was why Xavier hadn’t been able to detect the Void Dungeon-created beasts with his Immortal Soul lens in the first place, even though there were Wanderers out there without that ability who could sense the things.

He’d assumed the Immortal Soul was the most powerful version of a spiritual sense, but that wasn’t entirely accurate. His Immortal Soul was a better detector than his current level of spiritual sense—by a lot—but both could be strengthened, and they could be joined together as well.

The Personal System said he’d been using them in tandem rather crudely, and that was why the Personal System was able to sense things better than he was using his own senses. That part was still a little confusing to him, but he supposed there were many things Xavier could now feel in his memories that he hadn’t been able to feel at the time. Things that could have been accessible back then if only he’d had the right knowledge and tools.

With the threat of whatever was lurking ahead of him in mind, Xavier didn’t want to try Astral Projection. Not here. If he had an out of body experience and started Spirit Gathering and then was attacked, he might not be able to reach his body fast enough to fight off whatever came after him.

My mortal body could die while I’m disconnected to it, then I would drift into the void…

An image of the Void Being version of himself that he’d fought back in the Collector’s throne room flashed into his mind. That hideous, terrifying entity hadn’t even remembered being human.

Xavier would never allow himself to become something like that.

So, he had to be cautious. More cautious than he usually would be.

Not for the first time since he’d made it to this dungeon, Xavier wished he could use his time dilation fields. That would solve a hell of a lot of problems for him. Then again, he supposed he’d been relying on those a little too heavily. When it came down to it, he had no idea if they would even be effective when he finally came up against the Interdimensional Dragon. He wouldn’t want to focus so hard on time manipulation only to discover it didn’t help him in the end.

That would be an incredibly foolish thing to do.

Better to become as well-rounded as he possibly could be.

And maybe, before I face the Interdimensional Dragon, I should observe it destroying a universe. That would be very valuable information to have.

Xavier brought his mind back to the present moment, bringing back up the notification about the different methods. He skipped drilling deeper on how to improve his base spiritual sense—he knew many ways of how to do that before he would need to ask the Personal System for specific help—and instead chose to drill down on the third method.

Method 3: Strengthen your ability to utilise the senses you already possess to their fullest potential.

The Personal System explained everything he would need to do. This method didn’t merely involve practising the use of his Immortal Soul lens or his spiritual sense—it involved every sense.

Mortal and immortal alike.

His natural sense never seemed like something that would come in handy for identifying enemies from a distance, or for seeing their energy. He’d of course done a lot to strengthen his natural senses over the years, but many of the gains he’d gotten in those areas had come because his body and mind had strengthened. He hadn’t done a great deal of actual practice in those areas—not for a very long time.

It had hardly seemed necessary.

The Personal System was telling him he was very wrong about that.

Every single one of his senses could be improved enough to see, taste, feel, touch, and smell energy. This made him frown, deeply. He already felt as though he could see energy, but really it was more that his spiritual sense overlaid the image of the energy over his eyesight. It wasn’t a true perception of what energy actually looked like. If he were to cast a spell and have that energy move forward into the air in front of him, then deactivate his Immortal Soul lens and spiritual sense, he wouldn’t be able to see it with the naked eye.

Yet the Personal System was telling him he should be able to see it. That the ability was locked away inside him, and he simply needed to open himself up to it to use it.

That sounded like a load of crap.

Considering how damned powerful Xavier was for his level and grade, he found it hard to imagine there was a bunch of untapped potential still burgeoning inside him. A voice inside his mind was telling him that what he really should be doing was pushing forward through the dungeon, killing as many beasts as he could, gaining as much power as he could. He should be pushing himself farther down the path of the Cultivator, or the path of the Denizen—if such a thing was even possible out here, where he didn’t have access to more Mastery Points.

He should gain another path of advancement and push that forward.

Why was he standing here focusing so heavily on senses? Would being able to sense the Interdimensional Dragon better make him able to fight it better?

The simple answer to that question was yes, of course it would. Besides, Xavier wasn’t limited to only doing one thing, nor even one thing at a time. He could work on bettering these skills and move farther through the dungeon.

But he shouldn’t lose sight of why he was here—to learn. To do that, he needed to throw away what his personal idea of gaining strength meant. For so long, he’d been focused on gaining as much power as he could as fast as he could. He’d been focused on figuring out how to cheat the System, figuring out how to cheat more power, figuring out how to circumvent everything so he could defeat his enemies—that was how he’d managed to create more versions of himself in the first place.

That was how he’d gotten this far.

But he didn’t need to move that fast anymore. In fact, if he kept moving that fast and missed out on skills he could have gained, lessons he could have learned, then he simply wouldn’t be powerful enough when the time finally came to face the Interdimensional Dragon.

He would die, having accumulated all that power, yet not known the best ways to use all of it. Then what would have been the point of his, by then, incredibly long life?

So, Xavier stood in the middle of the Dark Tunnel, still unmoving, and focused on one of his natural senses at a time. While he did this, he deactivated his Immortal Soul lens and stopped using his spiritual sense. Deactivating the two of them made him feel blind. It had been a long time since he hadn’t used those abilities. Hell, he even used them in his sleep. He felt vulnerable, like a pillowcase had just been shoved over his head then someone had clocked him for good measure.

But did it really matter if they weren’t activated, when he hadn’t been able to sense whatever was out there with them anyway?

I’m not sure that’s as comforting a thought as I’d hoped.

The first sense he focused on was sight. He didn’t block out his other natural senses, though he could have. Instead, he simply dialled in on as much as he could. This wasn’t a foreign exercise to him, but it had been a long time since he’d done it.

The Personal System had made a good case when it had told him he needed to work on his natural senses, saying there was untapped potential there. He’d used a metaphor Xavier could understand, telling him that he had access to the best quality, highest definition television known to humanity, plugged into the most sophisticated sound system ever designed, and he was using it to play VHS tapes.

That was the quality of his eyesight compared to what it could be.

VHS tapes.

Xavier remembered watching those as a kid. Vaguely, as even back then they’d been outdated, but his mother had had an old collection of Disney movies on video, and the quality…

Yeah, he needed to do something about this. Especially since it wasn’t just his sight he’d neglected.

And so, he stood there. Minutes passed. Then hours. Then days. He looked at everything, opening his gaze, narrowing his gaze, focusing on the peripheral then the smallest of points. For a long while it felt like nothing was happening. Like he wasn’t making any improvements at all.

But the Personal System assured him he was.

It had given him eye exercises, and various other ways he could better his sight. Xavier wished he could simply manifest some sort of spell pattern to do the work for him but that wasn’t an option, but it did make him realise just how reliant he’d become on them. And not only them, but on Roln.

No refinements were incoming from the mental construct, since the mental construct was currently offline.

But that didn’t mean Xavier couldn’t do some refinements himself. When he asked the Personal System about this, it encouraged him. The more he practiced, the more he strengthened the correct neural pathways—he needed to practice enough so that he could strengthen the right ones in the right ways. Then, he could also focus on eliminating the less efficient pathways, of which there were a great many.

The brain didn’t simply do the most efficient thing possible, it did what it learnt, and Xavier had learnt a lot in his time—and there was so much that he’d learnt that he didn’t need to keep.

After a while, Xavier found himself enjoying the process of strengthening his eyesight. At the start, he still had trouble believing there was anything to strengthen. His eyesight was so much more powerful than a normal human, and so much more powerful than another Denizen at the same grade, or another Cultivator at the same tier. He could see for miles, and he could see at an incredible level of detail.

But he soon found out how wrong he’d been. The quality of his eyesight improved dramatically when he paired the exercises with editing his neural pathways. He made one refinement after another, gaining small improvements that added up and up and up.

When he compared the memory of his vision to his vision now, it had changed considerably.

But he still couldn’t see energy with the naked eye. This was something that was easy enough to test, as he could make energy flow out in front of him whenever he wished. This still seemed like an impossible thing. Energy wasn’t something to be seen by the naked eye. How could it be? But he’d stopped doubting the Personal System’s insights by then.

Xavier interrogated the Personal System about this, wondering how it could be possible.

The Personal System’s response surprised him a little, but it made everything… fall into place.
