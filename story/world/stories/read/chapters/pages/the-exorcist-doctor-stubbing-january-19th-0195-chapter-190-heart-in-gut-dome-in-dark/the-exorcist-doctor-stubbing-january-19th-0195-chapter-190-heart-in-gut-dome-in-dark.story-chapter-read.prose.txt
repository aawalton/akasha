While Fergal, Jin, and Liorin hauled the half-dead pistol shrimp over the railings, Gael turned his attention back to Maeve.

Getting her out of the diving suit was considerably more annoying than getting her into it. Water continued spilling through every joint while he wrestled with the locks along the backplate, and Vivi, Evelyn, Rebus, Bran, and Nixy crowded around to help peel the pieces of metal away one by one. The whole thing had become twice as heavy after flooding, which he supposed was another minor design flaw to add to the list. Nothing catastrophic. Merely something that might drown his dearest wife if she ever wore it again without proper revisions.

But Maeve’s expression suggested she’d already compiled a much longer list herself.

“I know, I know,” he muttered before she could begin, wrenching loose the damaged left shoulder plate. “The visibility’s terrible. I’ll widen the helmet windows, and I’ll probably mount lanterns on the shoulders so you don’t have to turn your entire head whenever you want to look somewhere.”

Maeve pushed wet hair out of her eyes as Vivi worked loose one of the gauntlets. “The joints are too rigid. I could barely turn around as well, and if I hadn’t used Mistrender to propel myself, that shrimp would’ve eaten me before I could even finish rotating.”

“Cool, cool, better articulation around the waist, shoulders, and thighs,” Gael agreed. “Maybe layered ring-joints instead of solid hinges? Less armour around the non-vital areas too, if I can maintain the pressure resistance.”

“And the gloves are awful. I couldn’t properly feel Mistrender’s handle.”

“Thinner fingers, then.”

“The lantern controls are also impossible to distinguish.”

“Fine, I’ll put in different-shaped dials.”

“The suit needs some sort of underwater propulsion as well.”

Gael paused while pulling a fastening pin loose from her backplate. That one was much harder. Proper underwater propulsion meant pressure, fuel, directional controls, and several wonderful opportunities for catastrophic malfunction, but he could think about that later. Rather, he could have Arnell think about it later. The Ironwych smith was behind most of the diving suit’s capabilities anyways, so he wisely went back to work.

By the time they finally peeled the canvas underlayer away, Maeve stepped free completely soaked and exhausted. Vivi immediately wrapped a towel around her shoulders while Evelyn dragged another across Maeve’s hair hard enough to make her head sway from side to side.

“There,” Evelyn said, smiling cheerfully. “Almost looks like you didn’t drown.”

“I nearly did drown.”

“Yeah, but you didn’t. Gotta look on the bright side!”

Gael nodded sagely. “Excellent medical philosophy.”

Maeve looked from Evelyn to him with the same flat stare. For all the complaints, though, she was still standing, and breathing, so he grinned and raised his palm.

She stared at him another moment before reluctantly slapping his palm with enough force to sting.

“There,” she muttered. “Happy?”

“Deeply—”

Vivi stepped forward next and high-fived her considerably more gently, clearly still in awe at Maeve’s display of underwater prowess. Evelyn followed the high-five with enough enthusiasm to undo Vivi’s gentleness, and before long, Rebus, Bran, and Nixy had joined in as well, despite obviously having only a vague understanding of what everyone was celebrating.

“That… was… sick!” Rebus said, eyes bright as he looked between Maeve and the damaged bloodshackle around her ankle. “We saw it from up here! Ye wrapped th’whole Murmur and blew yer chain up around it!”

Bran leaned closer to inspect the cuff. “Was that on purpose?”

“How’d you do it?” Nixy mumbled, crouching by her shackle as well. “So… both of you pumped a lotta blood through the chain at the same time, and then it overloaded the links temporarily and caused all the toxic blood to spill out?

“It was… a desperate move,” Maeve begrudgingly admitted, looking down at her bloodshackle. Gael did the same. The damage was complete—while new bloodlinks were already being formed from both of their cuffs, magnetically attracted to each other, they’d yet to reconnect and allow them to exchange blood once again. They were going to have to wait for the chain to naturally regenerate, and until then, absolutely no more fighting for Maeve. She couldn’t risk building up too much toxin in her body.

Still, the move was a powerful finisher for anything either of them couldn’t handle alone.

“... Still, we’ll need more than just diving suit upgrades if we’re doing this again,” Maeve mumbled, giving him a tired look as she sat down against the railings, opening Mistrender to shield her from the rain. “We’ll need more raw strength and mutations to wrangle with Myrmurs on open water, so…”

All of them relaxers turned to look at the not-relaxers—and just in time, too. Fergal, Jin, and Liorin finally managed to drag the entire pistol shrimp onto the deck, and while it landed with a wet crash hard enough to shake the entire barge, it was most certainly incapacitated. Maeve’s toxic blood had done horrifying work to it. Large sections of its shell had melted away entirely, exposing burned muscles and pale connective tissues underneath, while the enlarged claw twitched weakly against the boards.

The sight didn’t bother Gael. If anything, it looked ready for dinner.

So while the rest of them backed away from the Myrmur, he reached under his coat and produced two enormous fileting knives.

Alright, then.

Let’s get to dicing.

He increased the intensity of the gloam lantern at his hip first. The curse-killing light shone across the pistol shrimp in a sickly wash, and immediately, the Myrmur screamed and found some reserve strength to thrash harder, its remaining legs scraping deep gouges into the deck.

“Hold still,” he grumbled, approaching the shrimp. Then he stabbed one fileting knife beneath the shell. Admittedly, while he’d cut open hundreds of land and air-based Myrmurs at this point, this was his first time dissecting a water-based Myrmur, so he couldn’t pop its shell off with one good wrench.

Oh well. He changed angles and tried again. The second cut glanced off a section of carapace and nearly sent one knife spinning from his hand. The third went in too shallow. The fourth went in far too deep, and something beneath the shell burst with a wet pop that sprayed black fluid across the front of his coat. He carved experimentally, opening one plated seam, then another, tracing bands of pale muscle beneath the carapace and cutting through connective tissues whenever they seemed likely to lead somewhere useful. Naturally, the parasite remained alive throughout the process, every incision sending small tremors through its body, and before long the entire deck had been opened into a grisly arrangement of shell, meat, organs, and other putrid things that make everyone behind him gag.

Okay, he was going to practise disassembling aquatic beasts starting tomorrow.

Eventually, though, he cut apart two sections of shell along the spine, and finally saw something beneath all the shrimp anatomy that absolutely didn’t belong there: skin. Human skin. He widened the opening, shaved off all the tendons and fleshy muscle strips connecting the man’s body to the shrimp’s, and yanked the man out with a wet squelch.

Then he dumped the man in rags onto the deck, his vision pulsing red in his eyes.

… There.

The Myrmur heart’s in his abdomen.

Bless his Umbral Eyes for showing him the exact location of the heart, because honestly he was getting tired of cutting things apart already. It was mostly the rain and the rocky waves. There was, however, the Breeder’s ticking bomb inside the man’s abdomen to worry as well—his perceptivity was high enough for him to hear it—so after taking out his on-road surgery kit and cutting the man’s stomach open right there on the deck, he planted his parasitic flower into the man’s inner stomach lining first before cutting out the Myrmur heart.

He was a little worried the Breeder might’ve changed the formula of the bomb, but it didn’t explode, and the curse-killing light from his gloam lantern stopped the heart from injecting its killing curse into the man. So once he had the beating Myrmur heart in his hand, he crushed it into pulp and tossed the bomb overboard. Ignoring the Myrmur’s dying screeches beside him, he quickly sewed the man up, gave the stitches a bit of disinfecting, and…

There.

Done.

While he sat back on his ass and let out a heavy breath—watching the others clean up the shrimp guts and organs and stray meat—he couldn’t help but notice he really was only getting better and better at field surgery. Once upon a time, he’d needed at least a surgical table and decent lighting to cut a Myrmur heart out of a certain noodle shop owner’s stomach, but now he could remove it and a live bomb while kneeling in rainwater on a shaking barge.

His grin widened. He probably was the best doctor in all of Bharncair now, so how would he actually fare against another Plagueplain Doctor from Vharnveil? Well, most of them were only called ‘Doctors’ in name, and their fields of study had nothing to do with the practice of saving lives through medical means, but surely some of them shared his expertise in the art of… putting parasitic flowers in his patients to keep their bombs from exploding.

Well, he’d rather never meet another Plagueplain Doctor if he didn’t have to, so he supposed he was just content knowing he was probably the best doctor in Bharncair already.

But while Maeve, Fergal, Cara, and the rest of his usual gang were already used to seeing his surgical skills, the children from Miss Umbrage’s clinic weren’t. For that matter, Rebuxs, Bran, and Nixy were crouched next to him, staring at him completely stunned.

“Ye... saved the man?” Rebus asked.

Gael looked over. “Yep.”

“Like… ‘proper’ saved?”

“He’s still breathing, isn’t he?”

Bran stared at the dead shrimp. “But he was inside a Murmur.”

“Not anymore.”

Nixy looked between the unconscious man and Gael’s surgical tools. “But I thought once someone turns into one o’those, they’re done.”

“That’s why you lot need better doctors.” Gael wiped blood from his scalpels, but then his own words gave him a thought. He studied the three children for a moment. “You interested?”

All three of them blinked. “Interested in what?”

“Medicine,” he said plainly. “Physicians. Doctors. People who cut other people open for relatively respectable reasons. I need people to man the branch clinic we’ll be opening across the cardinal wards eventually, and while I have been training some of the Saint’s Hands for a while now, it’s actually been supported by many academics that it’s better to start grooming the kids when they’re young—”

“Don’t put it like that,” Maeve grumbled beside him.

“And while I have toyed with the idea of teaching the orphans at the Sallow Hearth, there aren’t nearly enough orphans there to man all of the branches we’ll be opening in the future.” He looked at the three of them squarely. “Besides, I don’t think anybody but Wraithpier locals can man the Wraithpier branches. You people survive drugs that’d sedate monsters, weather that’d kill Umbracross tourists, and apparently half of you learn how to gut a leviathan before you learn arithmetic. That’s all good foundation for medicine.” Then he paused, seeing the reluctant expressions on their faces, before getting to the real meat of the offer. “Also, if you train to be doctors like me, you’ll get paid better than if you work as simple guides for the Saint’s Hands.”

All three of them immediately straightened, giving him strong salutes.

“We’ll ask th’others!” Nixy nodded first.

“Aye!” Bran said. “Lots of others kid already know bandages an’ medicines!”

Rebus grinned. “Some of us even helped in th’clinic back when Miss Umbrage was around! Bet plenty’d wanna learn proper!”

Gael grinned. And there he had it: another small army of children he could raise into the next generation of doctors. Fergal looked a little miffed from the side seeing him poach the Saint’s Hands guides, but hey, until the kids were smart enough to act as physician apprentices, they could still be the Saint’s Hands’ Wraithpier contacts.

In the meantime, all of them glanced back at the unconscious man lying in the middle of the deck.

“... So what now?” Maeve asked, checking the man’s pulse himself. “We wait until he wakes up, then ask him what he remembers about the Breeder?”

“Nah,” Gael muttered, shuffling forward with one hand rummaging under his coat. “I don’t have that kinda patience anymore.”

Then he produced a syringe containing a vicious-looking clear liquid and jammed it into the man’s chest, making the man jerk before inhaling violently. A second later, his eyes snapped open while his back arched off the deck. Gael leaned in close and slapped him once across the cheek just to wake him up even better.

“‘Sup, man. I just stabbed you a with a dose of what I call ‘Super Adrenaline’, but the high won’t last and you’ll drop unconscious in exactly ten more seconds, so before you do that, tell me something,” Gael started, grabbing the man’s head on both sides. “Gimme the last thing you remember. It’s a place, isn’t it? What does it look like? Where were you before you got a Myrmur stuck in you?”

“W-What?” The man’s mouth worked uselessly for several seconds. “I... I don’t...”

“Tick-tock, tick-tock. Hurry it up. Gimme something to work with.”

The man’s eyes unfocused, as if he was recalling a memory he couldn’t quite place. “There was… a dome… with red lights,” he whispered. “And upstairs, there was fish… and pressure… and…”

Then the man dropped back down, knocked out once again.

Gael remained crouched by the man’s side for several moments before he turned to Maeve. Maeve looked at Jin, and Jin exchanged a glance with Vivi.

Unable to hide his annoyance, Gael sighed and started rubbing the beak of his mask.

“... Welp,” he muttered. “Guess this’ll be Bleakhearth all over again, huh?”

Everyone mumbled something as they picked up their fishing rods and began casting their lines again.
