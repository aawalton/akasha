How had it gotten to this point? How had all of my plans, all of my efforts, and all of my dreams seemed to vanish into thin air in the course of an hour?

I…don’t know, honestly. I could barely parse it right now.

Instead, all I could do was stare dully at the far side of the cell I had been dropped off into, at the thick wooden door that separated me from the outside.

After reaching the palace, the squad of Corvid Knights who had forcefully marched Alveron and me here had led us into a portion of it I had never been in before. Instead of venturing into the palace proper, they had instead led the two of us around it, to the back of the complex where I had never been. There, with strong, plated hands guiding us forward, they had led us down through a back entrance that seemed to lead underground.

Which…seemed to be a prison.

Fancy that.

Bare stone walls with no decoration, dimly lit by torches of all things in an age of Mana-lights, guided the way. To my quiet relief, the small stone cells housed down here seemed to be entirely empty of inhabitants. Which made sense, my Core listlessly remarked to us. Nobody had time to deal with prisoners during the Skyfall. I had barely thought of my last prisoner in the form of Bronzle.

…huh. That had been the first time I’d thought of the Dwarf in nearly a week.

I truly had grown calloused to death, even if I hadn’t been the one to do the deed.

I took a deep breath and let it escape me in a slow sigh, casting a gaze out around me as I did. There wasn’t much to look at, to be honest. A small, thin cot in one corner, a frankly disgusting-looking bucket in the other, and…the door. I was alone in here, as well, considering that Alveron had been led to his own cell. He, too, hadn’t uttered a single protest against our treatment. I’m…not sure the elderly Elf was even capable of such a thing, right now. The last surviving Paragon of Vereden had looked…hollowed out, after the ‘revelations’ of Anguis. As if the Dwarf had scooped up every last ounce of will left in Alveron’s frail body and burned it on an altar. I’m…not sure if he even cared anymore about our dismal little cells.

Because that was it. There wasn’t even a window in here, much less a blanket for the cot. Just a small stone cell, cold and drafty.

I closed my eyes and let my head thump up against the wall behind me. As I did, I let my mind wander slowly through the circumstances. It’s not like I had much else I could do at the moment, except await for what came now.

Whatever that may be…

I…didn’t know anymore. I thought I had. The expedition forces were going to join up with those of Rhoscara and heroically push back the annihilation of their city at the hands of the Savoy and their lackeys. Then, together, we would march upon the barrier surrounding the western half of the Barren Forest, now the Confederation of Lasgach, and fight our way through to the heart of Smaragd.

Where Fynneas lay.

Except…was any of that going to happen now?

Was it even needed?

I…wasn’t so sure.

The only thing I was sure of was that Anguis hadn’t been telling the whole story. I…I was willing to believe that there was at least a hint of truth to some of what the Prince of Vittolia had so dramatically revealed. I had already accepted that the bars of magenta lighting now encircling Vereden were Fynneas’s Mantle of Divinity, the madness within it corrupting the very Aether of the planet. But…

I hadn’t exactly thought about what that meant for the Elf himself.

Or rather, the Netherim who had become the Elf.

It…was possible, from what I knew, that he was nothing more than a mortal now, without his Divinity. It was equally possible that his madness had stemmed from whatever had infected the Mantle, and he was, now, for the first time in millennia, sane once more.

But so much of it was so convenient for Anguis.

That he had done all of this out of the goodness of his heart, over the plight of the Elves.

The easy belief he seemed to inspire in his words, even among his most fervent enemies like Elysael.

The constant switching, seemingly on the fly, for the reactions that he wanted.

For God’s sake, the man had started his speech by asking me to join him. That had gone out the window by the end of the confrontation, seeming totally in favor of my arrest.

Wait.

Oh my fucking GOD.

Was this what it was like to be on the other side of the bullshit? Had my enemies in the past felt like this whenever I had staged a grand, dramatic scene, as if I were an actor in a play, to influence the people around me?

Had I…had I just been outdone in the dramatics department?

Son of a bitch.

Anguis had played everyone against each other, completely in the moment, completely at ease, and completely in command of the narrative. I had been so shocked that I hadn’t even realized my own tactics were being used against me.

And, seemingly, by someone better at it than I was.

Fuck.

In the depths of our misery, my Middle Lattice suddenly posed a question to me.

Why was I just letting this happen?

It’s not like this cell could hold me if I wanted out. The door to my cell was enchanted, sure, but not enough to stop me. I could probably tear straight through it without even transforming into my full, monstrous state. But…

I didn’t want to. I’m…not sure I even wanted a rescue from the expedition, to be honest.

Well.

I’m also not sure they would even be inclined to mount one. Not for Alveron and I. I hadn’t missed the looks the two of us had been getting from other members of the expedition as we were being escorted away.

Not everyone...but more than a few hadn't looked terribly impressed with me.

I sighed and thumped my head back on the wall once more.

No, I was inclined to wait this out. My part in this…battle? Was there going to be a battle anymore?

Was over.

For now.

Christ, how had Anguis been so convincing to Elysael and the Dwarves of Rhoscara? Even if everything he’d said had been pure, unaltered truth, there was still the overarching issue of slavery. It had been one of, if not the main reason, that Rhoscara had finally chosen to separate from the Principality.

Not only that, but Rhoscara had been outright supporting the Unchained for nearly the last year. It was just…so hard to believe that a generation-long difference like that could be settled with just one oddly convincing speech.

Maybe, though…maybe many things could be put to the side, in the face of an apocalypse. Even slavery. Especially if your mortal enemy said they had the only means of putting off the end of the world.

I raised my head to thump against the wall once more, only to pause.

Somewhere down the hall from me, possibly from where I had been frog-marched into the cell, I’d heard something.

The opening of a door. Someone was here. Someone other than the Knights standing guard outside my door.

A moment later, I received confirmation.

A large number of heartbeats were now walking down the hall. A moment later, I heard their footsteps.

I only recognized one heart, but that was enough for me to sit up. Not fully, of course. I was still sitting on the ground, slumped against the wall. I just wasn’t slouching anymore.

Because that was Ely.

I…suppose she might have already decided on what to do with me? That was fast. I’d barely been down here for…maybe two hours?

Oddly fast, for something like this. I suppose the people following her were her guards.

I soon found out just what was going on, and it was not what I was expecting.

When one of the Knightly guards opened the door, I fully expected to find Elysael standing on the other side, ready to pronounce my sentence…such as it was. At absolute worst, I expected banishment from Rhoscara for maybe unknowingly misleading her and her forces.

Wait, no. That wasn’t the worst-case scenario. The worst was that she would outright hand me over to Anguis, possibly to be enslaved once more.

I don’t care how fond of Elysael I was. I would die before I allowed another collar to be placed around my neck. But more importantly.

I would kill. Her, if necessary.

I was partially right, as it turned out. Elysael was standing on the other side of the door, looking strange, to my eyes. She looked oddly unfocused, as if her eyes weren’t seeing the world around her clearly. The normally put-together Dwarf was also looking a bit…ruffled, for lack of a better word, as if she weren’t maintaining her rigid sense of comportment.

All of that put together was alarming…but who stood at her side was more so.

Anguis stood in the doorframe to Elysael’s right. In fact, it looked like it had been he who had opened the door. He looked just as he had down at the docks, not a royal hair out of place either on his head or on his face. He smiled to see me, as I jumped to my feet, tensing as I did.

“Ah, there you are, there you are,” Anguis said pleasantly, flipping the key to my cell in the air and then catching it. He winked at me with a grin. “Good to see you once more, Nathan. You don’t mind if I call you that, do you? What am I saying, I don’t care if you do. Mind if we come in, Nathan? Of course you don’t. C’mon along now, shrew. It’s time for the leading men to talk, and it wouldn’t do to leave you out of the discussion.”

I stared blankly at the rambling figure of Anguis as he placed a hand at the small of Elysael’s back and pushed her forward into my cell. I scrambled to catch her, holding the oddly unyielding form of Elysael close to my chest.

“Ely, are you…al…right?”. Elysael just stared up and past me with blank eyes, almost as if she couldn’t even see me hovering over her.

A pit began to form in my stomach, and at the same time, a suspicion did the same in my mind.

I looked up when I heard the door to the cell close, and then lock behind Anguis. He…must have given the key to one of the guards. In the split second my attention had been away from her, Ely had…somehow slipped out of my arms. I don’t know how, honestly. My grip on her, anxious as it was, hadn’t faltered at all. I watched with tense eyes as she came to stand at the side of Anguis, just in front of the door. He didn’t pay attention to her at all. Instead, he was looking around the cell with judgmental eyes.

Anguis tsked, shaking his head. “Why oh why did they take you to this dingy little cell?” He moaned aloud. “Complete with its own little shit bucket. Charming. Utterly charming.”

I stood up from the crouch I had sunk into to catch Ely, eyes narrowed at Anguis. He…was acting as if he were in complete control of the situation, as if he weren’t scared of me at all. I may have been relieved of my weapons when I had been led down here, but I hadn’t been fitted with any manacles, Status supressing or otherwise.

Even with my weapons, I was one myself. And yet, he didn’t care.

Meanwhile, Anguis was still bitching. “They weren’t supposed to take you to the palace dungeons. They were supposed to take you to the palace itself, where we could have had a proper conversation, in a nice, comfortable room. Does this look like a proper room to you, shrew?” He addressed Ely. When she didn’t respond and just stared at the far wall dully, Anguis unexpectedly laughed. “What am I saying. It’s not like the palace proper is much better than this cell. I’ve been inside before.”

I sucked in a deep, deep breath as my suspicion from earlier crystallized into certainty. “Shit,” I breathed out.

“Yes, it does rather smell like manure down here,” Anguis agreed easily. He spread his hands plaintively. “Tell me you didn’t use the bucket, Nathan. Please.”

I ignored his jokes and studied him for a moment. “I know what’s happening here now.”

An anticipatory smile crossed Anguis’s lips then and made a slight gesture with his right hand without switching his gaze. Almost robotically, Elysael got down onto her hands and knees, her back stiff and straight. Anguis sat down upon it like the Prince of Ravens herself was nothing more than a bench. Magnus’s Father leaned forward on his impromptu Dwarven bench and studied me like I was a particularly interesting research subject. “Do go on, Nathan. What have you discovered?”

My eyes tightened at the casual disrespect to my friend, but I didn’t react beyond that. He was trying to provoke me. “This…all of this…” I swept out one arm, as if to encompass not only the cell, but of Rhoscara itself. “Your speech, the negotiations, and even my presence there…it was all so you could get your hooks into everyone. Or rather…your fangs. You’re a Mind Mage…aren’t you?”

My accusation hung in the air between us, for a moment, as Anguis studied me with open amusement. Eventually, he looked down and patted Elysael on her head, like she was an obedient dog. She didn’t react. “Mind control, eh?” Anguis mused. “That’s your answer?”

When he looked up, I couldn’t help but tense at the look on his face. For the first time since I’d met Anguis, I could finally see it.

The resemblance that went beyond skin deep.

Anguis wasn’t just someone who looked like an older version of Magnus. He had something similar to his son’s cruelty in him.

“It’s a little bit more than that, Nathan,” Anguis said, with a malicious chuckle.

Though the light in the cell was dim, I still saw it.

An odd magenta spark of lightning that danced across Anguis’s pale golden eyes.
