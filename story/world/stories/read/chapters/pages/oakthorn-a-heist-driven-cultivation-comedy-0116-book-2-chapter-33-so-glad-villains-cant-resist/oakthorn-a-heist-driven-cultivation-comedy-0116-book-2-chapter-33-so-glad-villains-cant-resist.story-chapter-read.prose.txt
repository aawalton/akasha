The ancient stairway screamed as Cade climbed, stone grinding against stone like teeth being pulled from a titan’s jaw.

“Lovely,” he muttered, steadying himself against the trembling wall, one hand buried deep in his pack. His fingers found the Lifekeeper’s warding tome, its worn leather cover oddly comforting in this hellscape. Nora climbed behind him, her face set in that determined half-scowl that meant she was already seven contingency plans deep into their survival.

The staircase had stopped being a staircase three turns back, transforming into a steep, jagged ascent that demanded hands and feet both. The crypt had finally dropped all pretense of being a mere structure. It was alive, malevolent, and petty as a scorned lover.

“Everyone still alive back there?” he called over his shoulder, never taking his eyes off the shifting steps above.

“Define ‘alive,’“ Elena responded tartly, her voice strained.

She’d been unusually quiet since the flood, since Jer’s... No. Cade couldn’t think about that now. He couldn’t afford to.

Gavin grunted something that might have been assent, bringing up the rear with Rayka. Evie remained unconscious, draped awkwardly over Cade’s shoulder like the world’s most prophetic sack of potatoes. She muttered something about “roots and branches” that he filed away for later analysis.

A low chittering sound echoed up the stairwell behind them. Something was coming—something that moved with the wet, sticky sound of meat being slapped against stone.

“Time to go,” Cade said, pulling Evie back onto his shoulder in one fluid motion. “Gav, take point. Your eyes are better than mine in the dark.”

The lycanthrope nodded, muscles tensing visibly beneath his skin as he prepared to partially shift if needed. The chittering grew louder, accompanied by a smell that hit them like a physical force—the stench of rot and chemicals that brought back unwelcome memories.

“Is that—” Rayka began.

“Yep,” Cade confirmed grimly. “Our old friend the skunk-spider. Or what’s left of it.I guess corpses left in the marshes are free game for the crypt to claim. Gross.”

The thing that hauled itself onto the landing wasn’t the creature they’d fought earlier. It was a mockery of that already-nightmarish beast—its exoskeleton now split open to reveal glistening, necrotic flesh beneath. Tentacles of fungal growth erupted from its joints, waving with a horrible sentience. Where its eyes had been, pulsing sacs of luminescent fluid now throbbed in sickening rhythm.

“That,” Elena remarked with remarkable composure, “is the vilest abomination my eyes have ever had the displeasure of beholding.”

“Waste not, want not,” Cade replied, backing away steadily. “The necromancer’s sacred creed.”

“Are you serious right now?” Gavin growled, his fingers lengthening into bestial claws.

“It’s either jesting or cursing the gods for our misfortune again, and I’m all out of breath,” Cade shot back, destruction magic already gathering at his fingertips. “Nora, take the siren. I’m going to need both hands for this dance of death.”

She nodded, accepting the siren’s limp form. The undead skunk-spider chittered again, its front legs probing the air as if tasting their fear. Then it launched itself forward with unnatural speed.

Gavin met it head-on, his partial transformation giving him enough strength to catch its first strike. Claws scraped against chitin with a sound like steel on slate. Cade circled right, calling up his destruction magic—the familiar silver flames now threaded with gold after his recent rank advancement. Even now, he could feel his core shoving against the limits, the minuscule red threads of magic aiding in his rapid evolution. The dual-toned fire responded more eagerly than ever before, rushing down his arms like an overeager hound finally let off its leash.

“Its joints!” he shouted, aiming a concentrated blast at the creature’s leg where it connected to the thorax. The silver-gold flames struck true, burning through exoskeleton and the putrid flesh beneath. The limb detached, falling to the ground where it continued to twitch independently.

Elena was already moving, her whip-dagger slicing through the air to sever one of the fungal tentacles. The appendage fell, writhing, but immediately began growing back. The fresh growth slithered across the floor before it gathered up the fallen limb and reattached with a sickening series of slurps and crunches.

Everyone winced.

“Okay, new plan,” Cade called, ducking under a spray of caustic fluid the creature shot from what was once its scent gland. “We need to take out the core!”

Nora, despite being hampered by Evie’s unconscious form, managed to position herself behind the monster. “Where exactly is the core in a reanimated skunk-spider?” she demanded.

“If I had to guess—” Cade rolled sideways as a leg speared the stone where he’d been standing, “—those lovely glowing sacs where its eyes used to be!”

He gathered his magic again, focusing it into a concentrated spear of destruction rather than a wide-area blast. Precision over power. The silver-gold energy coalesced between his palms, humming with barely contained force.

“Gav, pin it!” he shouted.

The lycanthrope responded immediately, grabbing two of the creature’s legs and using his enhanced strength to momentarily immobilize its front half. It was all the opening Cade needed.

The spear of destruction magic launched from his hands, striking the pulsing eye sac dead center. The membrane burst in a spray of glowing ichor, and the monster let out a shriek that couldn’t possibly have come from its original biology. The fluid hissed where it struck stone, eating tiny pockmarks into the ancient surface.

“One down, one to go,” he muttered, already preparing a second strike.

Before he could release it, the creature reared back, its remaining legs lifting its bulk off the ground. Then, with horrifying precision, it aimed its abdomen directly at them and... split open.

The carapace peeled back like the petals of some nightmarish flower, revealing a writhing mass of smaller, partially formed skunk-spiderlings nesting inside the hollowed corpse of their host.

“Oh, come ON!” Elena shouted, echoing all their sentiments.

The first wave of spiderlings launched themselves toward them, each the size of a small dog and already displaying the same necrotic modifications as their parent. Cade shoved both of his hands forward, conjuring wind with his left and fire with his right. Cursing at how this was going to smell, he shot his fire tunnel toward their foes, careful to avoid his allies. He blasted the nearest cluster with a wide cone of fire and wind, incinerating them mid-leap, but there were dozens more crawling over each other to reach them.

“Up the stairs!” he ordered, backing toward the next section of their ascent. “We can’t fight them all!”

Gavin disengaged with a final slash that nearly severed the parent creature’s head, then sprinted toward them. Rayka was already helping Nora manage Evie’s weight on the narrow staircase. Elena brought up the rear, her whip-dagger creating a deadly perimeter that sliced any spiderling that got too close.

As Cade turned to follow them up the stairs, something caught his eye on the wall beside the mural—a small alcove he hadn’t noticed before, containing what appeared to be a stone basin filled with a viscous, black liquid.

“Hold up!” he called, darting toward it.

“Are you insane?” Elena shouted as the first spiderlings reached the bottom of the stairs.

“Probably!” Cade called back, reaching the basin. The liquid inside smelled of iron and ozone—blood and magic, the two universal currencies. Without hesitation, he dipped his fingers into it and began tracing sigils on the wall next to the alcove, copying a pattern from memory.

“Cade!” Nora’s voice held an edge of genuine panic, rare for her.

“Almost—” The final stroke completed the sequence. For a heart-stopping moment, nothing happened. Then the sigils flared to life, blazing with sickly green light. The stone beneath their feet rumbled, and a section of wall slammed down between them and the advancing horde, cutting off their pursuit.

Cade allowed himself a brief, victorious grin.

“How did you know that would work?” Rayka asked, eyes wide.

“I didn’t,” he admitted, wiping the black liquid from his fingers onto his already-ruined pants. “But the murals showed ward symbols at key points in the crypt, and I recognized the configuration from my studies. I’ve been cross-referencing each one we found during our rest times, and so I was fairly certain it was either a defensive measure or a self-destruct. I took a gamble.”

“Mark my words,” Nora said with feeling, “your boundless audacity shall one day deliver us to the four hells as a housewarming gift.”

“I’m going to remind you that you said that right before we die old and fat and rich,” Cade replied with feigned cheer, though the exertion of the spellcraft was exacting its price. His vision swam like disturbed waters, darkness encroaching at the edges before he steeled his will against the weakness. “Besides, my little gamble has purchased us precious moments.”

The new section of stairway was different from the previous one—wider, with actual steps rather than the improvised climbing path they’d been navigating. Torches flickered to life along the walls as they approached, casting dancing shadows that seemed to move independently of the flames.

“I never thought I’d hate something as simple as stairs. It was always beneath me” Gavin muttered.

“Stairs are always beneath you, bud,” Elena snapped back, tapping the telepath on the shoulders in mock commiseration as she passed him.

Cade took point again, his hand never straying far from the Lifekeeper’s tome in his pack. As they ascended, the walls became increasingly decorated with intricate carvings—not just murals now, but full relief sculptures depicting scenes of battle, ritual, and what appeared to be the early days of the Blightmarshes.

“By the Forgotten Gods,” Cade whispered, stopping abruptly before a sprawling mural that covered an entire alcove wall. The rest of the group huddled around him, their faces illuminated by the eerie glow of magical torches that had sputtered to life as they approached.

The central image showed a crowned figure—unmistakably the same Progenitor from earlier depictions—standing triumphantly within an elaborate ritual circle. Before him, a sapling grew from barren ground, its branches reaching upward like supplicating hands.

“What do you think it means?” Rayka asked, peering at the sequence of images that followed the sapling’s growth.

Cade traced the progression with his finger. “Nothing wholesome, that’s for certain. Look how the tree transforms.”

The painted panels showed the sapling’s metamorphosis—first a vibrant, golden-leafed tree, then twisting, darkening, thorns erupting from its bark as it contorted into something... familiar.

“Is that a... chair?” Elena squinted at the final image, where the transformed tree had become a grotesque throne, its thorny branches forming a canopy over the seated Progenitor.

“A throne of living wood,” Gavin observed, leaning closer. “Looks uncomfortable as hell.”

“Power rarely concerns itself with comfort,” Cade murmured, unable to shake a growing sense of recognition. That twisted formation, the distinctive pattern of thorns... he’d seen something disturbingly similar before.

Nora had crouched down near the bottom of the mural, armored knees creaking as she examined a line of faded script. Her brow furrowed in concentration.

“Can you read that?” Cade asked.

“Perhaps,” she replied, not looking up. “It’s an older dialect than what I studied, but similar root structures.” Her finger traced the first symbol. “This one is ‘the’... And this one might be ‘great’ or possibly ‘final’...”

“Fascinating,” Elena deadpanned. “Meanwhile, our toddler goddess remains in the clutches of a possessed madman.”

“This might be important,” Cade said, his eyes shifting back to the mural. “These ritual rings—seven of them. And look, seven pylons positioned exactly like the ones in Bernard’s circle. This isn’t just decorative. It’s instructional.”

“So what?” Gavin asked. “Big evil tree ritual. We already knew Bernard was up to no good.”

“But it might tell us how to stop him,” Cade argued. “If we understand what he’s trying to create—”

“‘The Glorious Ascension,’“ Nora announced suddenly.

Everyone turned to look at her.

“That’s what it says?” Rayka asked.

“No,” Nora admitted with a slight flush. “That one symbol remains unclear. Could be ‘ascension,’ ‘awakening,’ or perhaps ‘appendage’...”

“‘The Glorious Appendage’?” Elena snorted. “Sounds like something Gav would name his—”

“By the endless void,” Cade interrupted, a memory suddenly crystallizing in his mind. “The tree. I’ve seen one like it before.”

“Where?” Rayka asked.

“In Life’s domain, beneath the arena,” he replied, excitement edging his voice. “Hidden in a chamber Stephen had corrupted. It was identical—same trunk pattern, same thorned structure. But it was... wrong somehow. Twisted. Like it had been poisoned. And out of it came Destruction, so why would this ancient place have an identical depiction?”

“So Life—what—grew an evil tree?” Elena’s skepticism was palpable.

“Not grew,” Cade said slowly, pieces clicking together in his mind. “Corrupted. The murals show the tree transforming from something beautiful into something monstrous.” He pointed to the figure of the crowned Progenitor. “Just like this fellow did ages ago.”

“The Forgotten Oak Dinner,” Nora declared with confidence.

Cade blinked at her. “What?”

“No, wait,” she squinted harder, armor clanking as she shifted position. “The Foresworn... no, the For... the Fort...”

“The Fortress of Oak and Thorn?” Gavin ventured.

“The Forest of Thorns and Bone?” offered Rayka.

Nora shook her head in frustration. “It’s infuriatingly subtle. The strokes are worn... wait.” Her eyes widened. “The Final Oakthorn Throne.”

Silence fell over the group.

“Well, that’s decidedly less cheerful than ‘dinner,’“ Elena muttered.

“An Oakthorn Throne,” Cade repeated, the phrase sending a chill through him. “Created using divine essence.” He gestured to the smaller figures depicted beneath the transformed tree. “The scale suggests it’s more than furniture. It’s got to be some sort of construct, or maybe a nexus of power?”

“You think that’s what Bernard is building?” Rayka asked. “Using Chubbs as fuel?”

“It fits,” Cade said grimly. “The ritual circle matches what we saw upstairs in the cathedral. And if the Progenitor tried this once before...”

“Then he was stopped,” Gavin pointed out. “That’s something, at least.”

“And imprisoned in a crown,” Elena added. “Which Bernard helpfully put on. Fantastic.”

Cade was already leafing through the Lifekeeper’s tome, searching for relevant passages on ritual structures. The text swam before his eyes, dense with arcane terminology he only half understood:

“Aetheric reflux can be achieved through counter-harmonic inscription of the outer ward boundary, provided the primary focus remains unbounded by tertiary containment sigils...”

“Can you decipher any of that gibberish?” Elena asked, peering over his shoulder.

“Some,” Cade admitted. “Enough to know we might have a chance. If the flow of power is directional—and these diagrams suggest it is—then altering specific symbols could reverse that flow.”

“Meaning?” Gavin pressed.

“Meaning,” Cade said, meeting each of their gazes in turn, “instead of Bernard draining Chubbs, Chubbs could drain Bernard.”

“That’s assuming the Progenitor hasn’t already considered such a possibility,” Nora cautioned. “Ritual magic this ancient isn’t woven from common thread. One misstep and we might tear ourselves apart.”

“Worse than what happens if we do nothing?” Cade challenged.

Nora had no answer for that.

“He’s using Chubbs—Creation incarnate—to build this twisted throne,” Cade continued, his voice low but intense. “If even half of what these murals suggest is true, that’s enough power to shatter the continent. I’m talking lava that makes my sneezes look like a joke.”

“They’re already a joke,” Rayka whispered, but she held up her hands in defeat when Cade leveled a withering look at her.

“Either way,” Cade said, closing the tome with resolve, “we can’t let it happen.”

Elena’s hand went to her whip-dagger. “So we kill Bernard. Problem solved.”

“Not so simple,” Cade shook his head. “The ritual is already in motion. We need to subvert it, not just interrupt it. Otherwise, the backlash could be catastrophic.”

“How exactly do you know all this?” Gavin asked suspiciously.

“I don’t,” Cade admitted. “Half is educated guesswork based on what’s in this book, and half is blind terror making me sound more confident than I feel.”

“Honesty at last,” Elena grunted from where she leaned against the curved wall of the stairwell.

“But I do know one thing,” Cade continued, tucking the book back into his pack. “I’ve seen one of these Oakthorn Thrones before, and it was powerful enough to imprison Destruction for millennia. If Bernard succeeds in creating another using Chubbs as his battery...”

The implications hung in the air, too terrible to voice.

“Then we stop him,” Rayka said simply. “We find a way.”

Cade nodded, grateful for her unwavering resolve in the face of such overwhelming odds. “First, we need to get back to the surface. Every heartbeat brings Bernard closer to completion.”

“Then what are we waiting for?” Nora asked with a quiet smile, one foot already placed on the next step.

They continued their ascent, the staircase gradually widening until it opened into a vast circular chamber that took Cade’s breath away—not with beauty, but with ancient, terrible purpose.

The ceiling vaulted high above them, supported by columns carved to resemble twisted, writhing figures frozen in eternal agony. The floor was a massive ritual circle etched into the stone, the grooves filled with what appeared to be tarnished silver.

And in the center stood Jer, his eyes aglow with undead energy.
