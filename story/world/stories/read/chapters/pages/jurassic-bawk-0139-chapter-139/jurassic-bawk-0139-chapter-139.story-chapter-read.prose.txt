The Squiggles responded in a manner that Charlemagne had never before seen once he chose his new Special Ability.

Synergy Detected: Special Ability description for I Lost the Blueprint updated.  Special Ability description for Any Mana of Mine updated.

Special Ability Updated: I Lost the Blueprint.  You have somehow created a set of mana channels to go along with your upgraded Mana Core.  How did you do it?  By brute forcing the issue, it seems. Additional future synergy with Inferno’s Embrace Skill possible.

Mana draining attacks are less effective on you, while your mana draining attacks become more effective in general. Increases overall ease and efficiency of converting environmental mana or other energy types into your own personal mana.

Charlemagne thought for a moment about what the original descriptions of the Special Abilities were, but that was a futile train of thought.  He quickly gave up and asked the Squiggles for guidance.  They quickly pointed out that I Lost the Blueprint now affected mana outside of his body, as long as it was under his control.  Asking the same question about Any Mana of Mine returned a response that transforming other energy types into his own mana would be easier.

The rooster asked if that included radiation.  It did.

“Bawk,” the rooster announced to the gang.

“Wow, that’s pretty good,” Phasima exclaimed.  Special Ability synergies are actually really rare.  You must have chosen fairly similar powers to get that, not that that’s necessarily a bad thing: it makes sense for you to play to your strengths.”

“I am personally disappointed that Charlemagne didn’t choose the one that would let him burn underwater,” Josephine jokingly complained.  “That would have been very amusing to see.”

“I believe that Charlemagne has once again made an optimal choice,” Phatagin opined.  “In fact, I doubt that you could disrupt his mana blasts given his newest Special Ability, Phasima.”

The former Empress of the Forest smirked as she appraised the rooster.

“As we are now, you’re one hundred percent correct.  He would mop the floor with me no matter what I tried.  If I had my full powers, I think I could handle him without too much trouble.”

The pangolin did not look impressed.

“Yes, but how many more levels do you suppose that Charlemagne will gain before the end of the Elite Systemic Games?”

Phasima frowned as she considered the math before finally grinning sheepishly.

“Well, it’s a good thing I’m unlikely to ever see you all again after the Games are over.”

Further conversation was forestalled as the group was finally removed from the safe space between the Delve Floors.  The quartet found themselves standing on the deck of a large wooden structure surrounded by water.  Large sheets of canvas flapped in the salty air above them, while men and women ran about, tugging on ropes, cleaning things, and clambering up and down ladders.  Right in the middle of everything stood a smooth, round wooden pole that had been driven through the wooden floor.

“Bawk,” Charlemagne asked.

“I think…” said Josephine carefully, “that we might be on what’s known as a ‘boat’.”

“What’s a boat?” the pangolin asked as he looked around with interest.  “And why does the ground feel like it’s moving?”

“Bawk,” the rooster asked again.

“A boat is something that floats on water or another similar liquid and moves people and goods across long distances,” Josephine explained.

“Ha, losers,” Phasima said with a nasty laugh.  “These boats must be for species that can’t fly!  I wouldn’t be caught dead on a boat.”

“But you’re on a boat,” Phatagin noted helpfully.

“Bawk!” Charlemagne yelled as he charged the nearest human and pecked it right in the face.  Its head exploded, splattering some of the nearby workers with blood and gore.  They panicked.

“Run away!  Jump!  Ahhhhhhh!” several dozen voices raised various cries as they sought to escape via the only means available to those on a boat - jumping overboard.  In just a few seconds, the deck was entirely empty.  Charlemagne hopped up onto the railing and watched as the humans attempted to swim away from the boat with various degrees of success.  He thought about going after them to finish, but no self-respecting rooster had ever willingly jumped into an ocean.

The Squiggles saw fit to return at that moment with the Floor’s instructions.

Welcome to the Third Floor of the Delve!  In this Floor, you will be competing against a number of other Knights to be the first to unlock the Treasure Vault.  First, the Knights must find one of King Arthur’s famous Arks.  Inside each of the 12 Arks lies a different legendary weapon, which will serve as a key to Bluebeard’s Chamber, which is hidden beyond the edge of the world.  The first to open the Chamber will receive a special reward.  The door may only be opened three times before its mechanism permanently fuses.  Once the door is shut again, all remaining Knights outside the Chamber will be eliminated.

Note: You have been given a crew to help you operate your ship.  This crew will remain on the ship and will not fight for you, but they do not require payment, food, water, or rest.

Charlemagne squawked in indignation as he realized what he had just done.

“Bawk!” he yelled at the others.

“Oh, so now you want the crew back?” Phasima grumbled as she began to rescue the fleeing sailors one by one and return them to the deck.  The first panicked mariners immediately jumped overboard as soon as she released the woman, so Phasima was forced to freeze their feet in place to prevent them from running away.

“How are we going to keep them from running?” she asked Josephine.

“I am not familiar with this type of entity, but it should react to simple stimuli appropriately.  As you can see, they are putting on a very convincing show of being afraid of us.  Since they can feel fear, I suppose it is possible that they could be intimidated into not running away again,” the Attendant replied.

“Bawk?” Charlemagne asked.

“I think that our fearless leader here should be the one to do it.  You know, since he was responsible for this mess in the first place,” Phasima suggested as she created a platform of ice underneath another floundering sailor and brought him back onto the deck.  Josephine and Phatagin nodded in agreement.

Charlemagne saw the wisdom in having the crew remain loyal to his orders out of fear, and he quickly hopped over to each of the flailing crew members before using his Intimidate Skill to dissuade them from further attempts to escape.  He then ordered them to return to work.

The Squiggles rewarded him for his efforts.

Once the entire crew had been rounded up and press-ganged back into service, Charlemagne ordered the sailors to start looking for one of the “Arks” that they were supposed to find.  The crew looked at each other with fear written all over their faces.

“Well, out with it already, I say,” Phatagin pressed after a few seconds passed with no response.

One of the women stepped forward and sank to her knees.

“Beggin’ your pardon, sirs, but you up and killed our cap’n.  She was the only one aboard who knew how to operate the compass.  Without her, we have no way to na-navia-navigate, yeah.”

The sailor punctuated her little speech by hawking up a nasty wad of phlegm and spitting it over the railing.

“Why don’t you show me this ‘compass’, if you don’t mind,” Phatagin suggested.  “Perhaps I can figure out how to utilize it.  I can’t imagine that it could be too complicated.”

Five minutes later, the pangolin admitted defeat.

“I must confess, I have no idea what any of these little spinning things are.  And there are numbers here that keep moving.  Why do they keep moving?  And why does the whole thing randomly change colors?!  This is truly impossible.  I suspect that the System does not, in fact, know what a compass is.”

“Well, the System pulled all of this from your world’s lore,” Josephine said with a sniff.  “If you can’t understand it, then you should blame the humans.”

“I do blame them!” the pangolin cried as he huffed off.

“Bawk!” Charlemagne demanded as he saw that his Squire had given up.

“It is absolutely impossible to figure out, Charlemagne!” Phatagin yelled.  “Why ever did you decide you needed to kill the ship’s captain when we first arrived?  None of the sailors are even a threat to me, much less you.”

“Bawk!” the rooster rebutted.

“To be fair, he did ask like twice,” Phasima agreed.  “This seems like it’s your fault.”

“My fau…mine!” spluttered the pangolin.  “That is the last straw!  The compass is not the only way to find this so-called “Ark”.  I shall fly around the ship until I spot one.

“That does raise a good question,” Phasima added.  “Do we even need the ship?”

“Well, not all of us can fly,” Josephine began to argue.

“Bawk!” the rooster broke in.

“How much food is down there?” Josephine asked.

“I suppose there is no way that Charlemagne will allow us to abandon the ship as long as the stores last,” Phatagin groused.  “I had better get going: it may be some days before I spot anything worth investigating.”

“Bawk!” the rooster rebutted.

“Well, yes, that would make our progress faster,” the pangolin admitted.  “But are you sure that’s how you want to do it?”

“Bawwk,” Charlemagne confirmed.

“And just what do you expect me to do while you’re out exploring?” Phatagin inquired.

“Bawak!” the Knight answered readily.

Phatagin sighed.

“I thought as much.”

“Bawk,” the rooster added an order to Phasima, as he flapped his wings and took to the skies.  His departure panicked the sailors, who were still jumpy from seeing their captain murdered right in front of them.  After spending quite some time soothing the crew, which made him feel ridiculous, Phatagin finally managed to convey his orders to the group.  A half-hour later, the modifications that he requested were done.

Charlemagne had long disappeared into the distance by the time that the boat got underway again.  However, it was not wind power that propelled the vessel, or even the oars that could be deployed in case of an emergency.  The sailors had, per Phatagin’s orders, rigged a strong but thin cable that they connected to the tall pole in the middle of the ship, which the pangolin learned was called a mast.  The other end was wrapped around Phatagin, who had been tasked with pulling the ship using his Momentum Core.

It was actually excellent practice, the pangolin had to admit, although he would never do it out loud.  By stealing the kinetic energy from the water just in front of the ship, Phatagin was able to create a standing calm zone that reduced the energy needed for the boat to cut through the water.  The extra energy mainly went into Phatagin’s Core in order to provide additional thrust, but some of it went directly into the ship to help reduce the strain on the pangolin’s body.

Phasima had likewise been ordered to assist, and she used her elemental powers to good effect by creating a steady breeze that filled the ship’s sails.  It was a good thing that she was there, because Phatagin would not have been able to influence the ship’s direction so easily without her aid.  As it was, they didn’t have to worry about what direction the wind was blowing.  Phatagin could pull while Phasima pushed.  All they had to do was keep heading in the direction that Charlemagne had vanished, and he would be able to find them.

Josephine shook her head as she considered their situation.  The rooster had once again shown himself to be incredibly stupid and incredibly resourceful.  She had no doubts that they would be among the first to find an Ark.  There was only one thing that was bothering her.

“I’m going to find a bed and lie down,” she informed Phasima.  “All this motion is making my stomach hurt.”
