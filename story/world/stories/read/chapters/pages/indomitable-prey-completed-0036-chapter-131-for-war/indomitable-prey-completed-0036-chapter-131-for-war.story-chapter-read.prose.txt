[Vera POV]

As the Jarl strode confidently towards her, she watched the others behind him, taking note of the frown on the Inquisitor’s face and the twins trading nervous looks.

Then the big man crowded her view. He was dressed the part of the barbarian berserker, but she knew it was simply a front—he was a canny old warrior and a surprisingly capable administrator. He’d come to the Sunsets as part of a moderately successful mercenary company decades ago. After murdering his fellow leaders, he’d assumed sole control and turned that company into a small country. That was the way of the Sunsets; warlords carving up territory for themselves and ruling over the locals with iron fists and iron expectations.

His own aura unfurled to battle hers for supremacy, filled with blood and cheer and a joy for battle rivalling any she had felt. At least, on the surface. His conniving nature made him dangerous, but the power he projected was a front. He was weakest of the 3rd tier rulers of the Sunset Court and compensated for it with a brash attitude and false bravado.

As he stepped over the line she had drawn in the earth, she reached out to his aura and crushed it. He screamed, eyes bursting in their sockets as fire erupted from every orifice in his head. His skull became a burning pyre, a monument to his screams of torment, and his legs buckled as his brain dripped from his melting ears.

The twin Keepers and the lord of Escribar drew back in shock, but the Inquisitor looked on calmly. As Vera raised her sword for the killing blow, she spied a white-gloved hand rise into the air at the same time. She quickly aborted the strike, instead kicking the dying Jarl in the chest to send him flying back over the line while her blade stayed between herself and the Inquisitor’s raised hand, poised to block any magical attack that may come.

The Jarl landed unceremoniously in the mud by the group’s feet, skin scoured from his head and the white of his skull leering skyward. The white-gloved hand descended earthward once more, and the Jarl’s body jerked, flames guttering out and flesh knitting itself back together. He picked himself up with an agonised groan as his eyes reformed from their steamed-soup state.

Vera shivered at the power on display. To so seamlessly revert the state of the world around her to a previous state… it would have a tremendous spiritual cost, she knew, but the Inquisitor would like be able to pay for longer than Vera could fight, especially if others joined the Jarl.

The Jarl cracked his neck and spat to one side, his glare near as scorching as the flames that had just sheathed his head.

“Pretty skill,” he said, anger lacing his words as the cold face of the moon reflected off his now bald head. “let’s see you try that again with my axe in your friend’s skull.”

And with that, a curved axe was flying through the air, bloody light clinging to its head, straight at Lamb. He may have looked a brute, and given his position in the 3rd tier, he was nominally beneath her in strength now, but he was no fool. It had taken him only an instant to judge where her priorities lay, and he exploited them with brutal ease.

Vera cursed and interposed herself, her sword deflecting the weapon and sending it thudding into the mud by her feet. The move had opened her companions on the other side though, and the jarl shot forwards, moving with a grace belied by his bulk.

She knew she would be too late as he crossed the line she had drawn in the earth, second axe already in hand and whistling through the air towards the frozen form of Sadrianna She was too distracted to react, her eyes locked on a horizon only her and her companions could see.

The Iron Wall had lasted no more than a few heartbeats in the face of the Sunset Court, and as Vera watched death come for her charge, she let go of the dream of protecting her countrymen from the coming chaos and instead committed herself to vengeance once more.

Then the Jarl stopped in place, blood bursting from his lips and his momentum halted completely. The Inquisitor was suddenly on edge once more, both hands raising into the air, fingers splayed like a composer before an orchestra.

Vera felt the comforting aura of ancient woodlands and deep time, and her lips split into a savage grin as Jorge arrived. She hadn’t noticed him appear despite her new power, and it served as a reminder that the gulf within the 4th tier was far larger than the difference between he tiers themselves. Traditional advancement broke down somewhat when one reached the peak, and she was glad of the reminder, shooting a glance at the Inquisitor even as she felt her shoulders relax a fraction at Jorge’s familiar presence.

The old man withdrew his spear from the Jarl’s body, and it tried once more to knit itself together again under the influence of the Inquisitor’s strange magic.

“No, lass,” Jorge spoke quietly. “This man’s life ain’t yours any longer.”

He hoisted the mountain of a man up by the neck with one arm as easily as one would a babe, then his eyes flashed. Orange light bloomed behind them, racing through the veins in his neck and down one arm before pulsing through the body of the Jarl. That body disintegrated instantaneously, nothing but dust and embers falling to the ground, Inquisitorial magic be damned.

Jorge dropped his arm and surveyed the figures before him. “All were warned of the price of crossing that line, aye?” he said. “Any of you fancy a stroll?”

Silence greeted his words.

Vera didn’t know what to make of it. Was he goading them, and hoping that one by one they’d feed themselves to the slaughter? Every kill made it easier, after all. Vera alone would not be able to defeat more than one of them, but if they managed to kill the Lord of Escribar, she suspected they could then take the Sunset Court in its entirety right here. She would put down the twin Keepers of Lilyflower, and Jorge could deal with Telanin the Inquisitor—the true threat.

Of course, that still left the Fractal Edge—commander of the Crimson Company—but he and the rest of the armies would flee immediately if they watched Vera and the rebels kill their entire leadership cadre on the field before them. She flxed her hand on the pommel of her sword, preparing herself for a battle.

Jorge surprised her by speaking once again. “You want us out of here? Bring your army. I won’t have any trouble gutting you all right here where you stand.”

The twins shifted, trading another glance beneath their deep cowls. The Lord of Escribar took an involuntary step back, and only the High Inquisitor seemed unbothered by the exchange. She sighed, as if put out by children squabbling, though Vera could tell it was a mask to cover her concern.

“No matter. We shall return, as you say, at the head of our army. Make no mistake, we will prevail here, and all of this?” she said, gesturing around at the battlefield that was the former Castle Ryonic. “All of this will have been a pointless waste of time and life.”

Jorge shrugged, and Vera found herself immensely glad to have somebody else to shoulder the burden of talking. She was still privately trying to wrestle with the power surging through her body, begging her to throw herself into a violent battle with the people before her and damn the consequences.

“I doubt it,” was Jorge’s calm response. “Think you’ll all be a mite distracted in a few moments.”

“Oh?” the woman asked, a single delicate eyebrow rising in question beneath her slim silver circlet.

“You haven’t felt it yet?” Jorge asked in surprise. “Shame for a 4th tier to be so out of tune with the world, but you’re not here to listen to an old man’s ramblings. Now, fuck off back to your army, and perhaps you’ll make it home in time to prevent the collapse of your own little piece of this world, aye?” he asked with a patronising flick of his chin.

She narrowed her eyes, but otherwise took his advice, the retinue soon trundling back down the cobbled road in short order.

Only once they had left earshot did Jorge turned to her, his eyes fever bright and shining in the moonlight. “I’m sorry I took so long, lass.”

“You made it. That’s all that matters,” Vera replied, reassuring her mentor and friend with a smile and a clap on the shoulder. “Now we just need to kill those fuckers and we’re in the clear. Why did you let them go?”

Jorge smiled, and just his sheer presence was enough to warm her heart. Relief surged through her, leaving her muscles weak and legs threatening to buckle. Jorge’s next words almost made them do so.

“That was all a bluff. I’m almost out.”

Her eyes widened. “What happened? And where have you been?”

“In the Ashkanian Vault. Markas was there.”

Vera felt her eyes widen in shock. “What? Why? When? Is he still there?”

Her questions launched like a barrage, and Jorge led her over to one of the broken rocks nearby that had fallen from the barbican. He sighed as he settled onto it and looked out over the dark fields spreading before the castle.

“A similar reason to us, actually—chasing God-Touched. He was contracted by the Desolate Empire to retrieve a God-Touched for some unknown reason. I suspect it’s related to the prince that went missing from the Leviathan Coast. Markas made some comment about ‘friends in high places’, and it’s not much of a stretch to imagine the duke had a hand in it. Still, what a clusterfuck—kidnapping a God-Touched and killing a Desolate prince in the process… starting a whole fucking proxy war for this shit.”

He trailed off for a while. Vera was still trying to put the pieces together when he continued. “I’ve chased him off. Nothing to worry about, lass. He won’t be back, and the Vault is empty. Only problem now is the army outside the gates.”

“Only problem,” she snorted, running a critical eye over the massed ranks of different fighters. The men-at-arms of Escribar in orderly ranks, their tabards dyed green for a beautiful contrast to their gleaming steel. The much less orderly mill of barbarian warriors from the Jarl’s army nearby, though Vera hoped they would soon lose some coherence once news of their leader’s death reached them.

She turned back to her friend, eyeing him critically. He looked older, the lines in his face deeper, the healthy weathering of his tanned skin now looking almost craggy. “He still lives, then?”

“Oh aye,” Jorge said with a laugh. “There’s no putting down that cockroach. He’ll follow me to my dying day, I expect. Most likely be the cause of it, too.” He snorted. “What happened to the duke?”

Vera gestured to the corpse out in front of the gate, not too far from the Jarl’s small pile of ash. “I killed him.”

“Good,” Jorge grunted. “You don’t want an enemy haunting your steps if you can help it. Trust me on that, lass. How are you feeling?”

She shrugged. “Not much of anything. Empty, I suppose. It was too easy. He was weak, Jorge. Could have killed him years ago.”

Jorge nodded. “Perhaps. But we’d still have had to face them,” he said, pointing to the carriages and chariot rolling through the fields before the castle. They were nearly at their respective armies now.

“Were you fresh and hale, I expect it would be an easy task,” Vera muttered.

“But I’m not.”

“But you’re not,” she agreed. “How bad is it?”

“I’m almost out,” he said with a weary smile. “Dry as a bone. Empty as a barbarian’s drinking horn. As spent as a young farmhand at his first brother. More tapped out than a noble in a mud wrestling—”

“Okay, okay, shut it.” Vera interrupted him, punching his shoulder but unable to fight off the smile. He grinned at her, though there was a touch of exhaustion pulling at his weather-beaten face still.

“What’s the plan, then?” she asked.

Jorge looked up, squinting and staring into the deep blue-black sky, just above the horizon. Stars twinkled high above in the firmament, and the gentle light of the moon kissed the tops of the forest. It was a night that brought back memories of her childhood, and her heart soared in unexpected joy as she realised they might have a shot at surviving this. Of fulfilling her promise to her aunt, and the vow in her own heart.

“Not long now,” he mused, scratching his chin absent-mindedly. “We just need to hold out a little longer.”

Even as he said it, he looked back to the open fields below and frowned. The retinue had returned to their army, and she could see figures dismounting and moving to support their own companies. The Inquisitor stayed with her chariot in front of a large force of white-robed soldiers in the centre, and Vera knew they were the core of the force. The silent twins likewise returned to their army, though it was the smallest and stood at the left flank. If they could convince them and the Jarl’s forces to cut and run, perhaps they could weather the storm if she could hold off the Inquisitor? Not for long, but perhaps a few minutes would be enough…

“Can we?” she asked, watching the army begin to move.

She felt the tremor in the earth as a hundred war-horses stepped forwards in unison as the Crimson Company moved ahead of the foot soldiers, the Fractal Edge at their head aside his own mighty destrier.

“Fuck if I know,” Jorge replied, and she snorted. His casual reassurance was a balm for the soul, but she did worry, in spite of the feigned bravado.

“I can disrupt that cavalry charge, but I can’t hold off the whole army, Jorge. What if—”

“Peace, lass,” Jorge counselled as he interrupted her. “I’ll take care of the charge, you slow down the rest of them. Don’t engage that woman in white—she’s a menace, and tricky besides. Just stick and move, aye?”

Vera took that in for a moment, then nodded. “Alright. It’s Telanin by the way. She’s the High Inquisitor of the Council of Aerlyn, one of the most powerf—”

Once again, he interrupted her, though this time he seemed more serious. “I don’t give a shit who that trumped up torturer is, girl. Just buy us some time.”

Vera frowned at the tone, taken aback, but one look at his faraway expression told her politeness was the furthest thing from his mind right now. He turned to her then, eyes shining with intensity once more.

“Vera, listen. Don’t die, alright? No heroic sacrifices, no last stands; Guardian, not Hero, aye?”

She nodded hesitantly, suddenly apprehensive. She knew to what he referred, but that was a conversation many years back, and if his thoughts were on those times… he meant to do something stupid.

“Same to you, old man,” she said gravely, gripping his shoulder.

He winced and looked over to the approaching army, and Vera took a moment to cast her senses wide. She noted how the steady trot had turned into a canter and knew they had no more time. She gave him a final nod, unable to resist some parting words.

“Thank you, Jorge. It’s been an honour.”

And then she was off, running from the broken castle with sword in hand and fear in her heart. Not for herself—never for herself. But for Jorge. For Nathlan and Lamb, Sadrianna and Jacyntha. And most importantly, for the people of the Marchlands, whose fate would be decided on this night one last time.

She reached the line of charging cavalry and slipped her shoulder low, leaping up to spin through the air. Wind rushed past her ears, and her newly enhanced senses just about picked up Jorge’s final words, said quietly as she left.

“Don’t go dying on me, lass.”

Then her sword was slicing through the air, and she collided with The Fractal Edge astride his great horse in a concussion of steel and flesh.
