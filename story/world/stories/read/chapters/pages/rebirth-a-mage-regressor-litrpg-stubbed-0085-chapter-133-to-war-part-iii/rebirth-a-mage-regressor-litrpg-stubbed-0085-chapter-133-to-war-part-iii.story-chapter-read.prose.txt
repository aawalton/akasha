The dome collapsed, and in its place, a geyser of water, steam, and shattered fragments of what had once been the homunculus erupted toward the sky. The explosion sent a shock wave across the ocean surface, creating a perfect circle of displaced water nearly a quarter mile across.

The force hit Adom like a wall, sending him spinning through the air. His concentration shattered, [Flight] failed. He plummeted, too drained to reactivate the technique.

The ocean rushed up to meet him. The impact drove the remaining air from his lungs as he plunged beneath the surface. Water filled his mouth, his nose. His vision darkened at the edges.

[+5 Primordial Body]

Pain lanced through his right arm as he tried to swim upward. Definitely broken. Wam hung uselessly from his wrist, the once-brilliant runes now dark and cracked.

Kern and Fili would not be happy about that.

Adom's lungs burned. His limbs felt like lead weights. The light above seemed impossibly distant. Maybe he could just rest here a moment...

No. Not like this.

With his last reserves of strength, he kicked toward the surface. One arm working, the other trailing uselessly at his side. Up. Keep going up.

His head broke the surface and he gasped, sucking in sweet air between violent coughs. Salt water burned his throat as he expelled it from his lungs. Each breath sent fresh waves of pain through his broken ribs.

He let himself float on his back, staring upward. The storm was gone. Completely gone, as if it had never existed. Blue sky stretched overhead, marred only by a few wisps of white cloud. The sudden change felt almost unnatural, like the weather itself had been tied to the creature's existence.

...Oh, right. It had.

The ocean still heaved around him, waves rolling in the aftermath of the battle, but even they were beginning to calm. Adom closed his eyes, letting the gentle rocking motion carry him.

[80/3067]. Barely enough Axis to maintain consciousness, let alone make it back to shore. He'd need to rest before attempting that journey.

Pain radiated from his right arm in persistent waves. His ribs protested with each breath. Various cuts and bruises made themselves known across his body, a catalog of injuries that would take weeks to heal properly.

But he was alive.

A sigh of relief escaped his lips. He'd done it. He'd actually killed that thing. It hadn't been pretty, and it certainly hadn't been easy, but it was over.

Adom floated, eyes closed, conserving what little energy remained in his battered body. The sun warmed his face despite the cool water lapping at his body. The contrast was almost pleasant.

"That was quite the display."

Adom's eyes snapped open. A familiar voice, coming from... somewhere nearby. He turned his head, wincing at the effort.

Standing on the water's surface about ten yards away was Sir Gaius, the old Archmage. His long silver beard moved gently in the breeze, his blue robes somehow pristine despite the sea spray. He leaned casually on his staff as if standing on solid ground rather than open ocean.

"Are you alright?" Gaius asked, one eyebrow raised.

"Yes," Adom managed, his voice hoarse from saltwater and shouting. "Just... resting."

"That was quite the fight," Gaius said, walking closer across the water's surface. His footsteps didn't even create ripples. "You dealt with it smoothly enough."

Adom couldn't help the short, painful laugh that escaped him. "If you were there all along, a helping hand would not have been unwanted. You're late, sir. "

"A mage is never late, Adom Sylla. Nor is he early. He arrives precisely when he means to." Gaius chuckled, the sound rumbling from deep in his chest. "I was actually on my way to assist you when I received news of the battle. But then I received another bit of news that required my immediate attention."

"Oh?" Adom tried to sound casual despite floating half-drowned in the ocean.

"A fleet of fifty Farmusian warships was approaching Arkhos." Gaius twirled his staff absently. "Apparently, they were expecting today's attack to succeed, after which they would arrive to 'restore order' to our city."

Adom didn't even blink. "You beat them all?"

"There were many elite mages among them," Gaius said. "Even I would have had a hard time with it." He paused, studying Adom's face. "But by the time I arrived, the fleet had already been... dealt with."

"Dealt with?" Adom prompted.

"Annihilated would be the more accurate term." Gaius stroked his beard thoughtfully. "The survivors we pulled from the water swore they were attacked by a dragon. You wouldn't happen to know a dragon, by any chance?"

"Not at all," Adom replied, keeping his expression neutral despite the pain radiating through his body.

"You seem rather calm for someone who just heard a dragon destroyed an entire fleet."

Adom let his gaze drift back to the clear blue sky above. "The world is vast and full of dangers... and special people."

"Indeed it is." Gaius stepped closer, his robes barely skimming the water's surface. With a casual flick of his staff, he lifted Adom into the air.

Adom winced as his body left the water's support, every injury making itself known with renewed intensity.

"We should talk later," the Archmage said. "About a lot of things."

"Sure," Adom managed through gritted teeth.

As they soared through the air toward distant Arkhos, Adom found himself reflecting on the day's events.

What the actual fuck was Biggins thinking?

The old dragon had casually suggested Adom should "take care of" the homunculus should it go unstable, while he'd handle the fleet Thessarian had warned them about.

Sure, He'd beaten Kell—barely—but still. Sending a kid to handle such a monster...

He almost laughed, wincing as his ribs protested. Everything had ended well, he supposed. Both threats neutralized.

Adom wasn't sure how the Archmage knew about him, but the old man had seemed to always know he was up to something. Gaius watched him now with that same calculating gaze, as if piecing together a puzzle. He was curious what the old man was thinking as well.

That mutual curiosity—and perhaps mutual wariness—had created an unspoken understanding between them. So far, Adom had trusted him enough not to do anything about it.

That trust appeared to be mutual, at least for now.

The gentle rocking motion as they flew, combined with complete exhaustion, lulled Adom toward unconsciousness. His eyelids grew heavy.

The last thing he saw before sleep claimed him was the distant skyline of Arkhos, still standing, still safe.

For now.
