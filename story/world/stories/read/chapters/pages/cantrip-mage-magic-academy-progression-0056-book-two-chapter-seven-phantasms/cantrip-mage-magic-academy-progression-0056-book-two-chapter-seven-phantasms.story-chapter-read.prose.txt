The expedition to the third floor was a controlled, goal-oriented trip.

Eola led her friends to the catalog on the first floor, spending just long enough to confirm that the books she and Roth wanted were currently checked in. The odds were good that her classmates would be looking for other combat-track students to help them find the same manuals. She had an idea that they could trade the books without returning them later, but for now, the books’ presence on the third floor was a good sign that she was getting in ahead of the rush.

The sounds of fighting echoed down the gated tunnel between the first and second floor as they approached. Tagg listened, leaning on his spear as usual. The old librarian nodded at the gate, but made no move to help. “You four be careful in there.”

“We will be,” Colin said. He slipped his hand into Eola’s and squeezed. “Right, Eola?”

“Right,” she said, and his hand relaxed. It didn’t leave hers, though.

“Alright. You four are all cleared for the third floor—Mr. Tremory, and Misses Lemiene and Clerk because of your track, and Mr. Gerr because of your escort. Not that I’m fooled. You’re capable. But officially, you’ll need one.” Tagg waved at the gate. “Head on in, and good luck.”

Eola held up her badge. So did the others, and the gate creaked open.

They arrived on the second floor in the middle of a pitched battle. A group of first-years was ripping into a band of trogs. “Early term fight!” Patrice called. Her warhammer rose, and she crashed down on the trogs like a tidal wave. Eola followed, drawing her smallsword and sliding into First Guard. Her blade flicked out, and a trog screamed in agony. The stench of rot filled the air as, together, the first-years and Eola’s team threw the trogs back from the gate.

“Thank Y’aer you got here!” one of the boys said.

Eola raised an eyebrow and shook her head. “You should have whistled. There’s no shame in it. And trust me, take it slow in the library. Don’t go too far before you’re ready.”

“Says the girl who almost died on the third floor last term,” Colin said. He poked Eola’s side; the breastplate took the blow, and she rolled her eyes.

“Just be careful, okay? Did you get your books?”

“Yes,” the boy said.

“Good. Let’s keep moving.” Eola started leapfrogging, and the others followed suit. A few light skirmishes against individual trogs followed, but the four of them together were able to handle them without using their precious magic.

Then they held their badges up at the third-floor gate, stepped through, and found themselves once again on the library’s third floor.

As Eola stepped onto the library’s third floor, she took a moment to let the sight wash over her.

It was always impressive. Hundreds of rooms—from the size of her tiny bedroom all the way to grand libraries several stories tall—floated at what appeared to be random, stairwells of stone and wood drifting between them. The third floor was absolutely massive compared to the first or second, and the flying rooms drifted just slowly enough to be mesmerizing.

But once she’d gotten her fill, she roused herself, shaking her body and drifting closer to Colin. They’d discussed their strategy heavily. The route was simple—the fourth stairs on the right, going down, then a second flight of stairs down. From there, they’d have to wait for the farthest set of stairs in that long, ribbon-shaped room to lead them up. That would link them to the room with the Ensorcellment manuals. And from there, it was only four stairwells out—all slightly upward.

Patrice led the way, and Eola found herself hanging close to Roth and Colin.

Mostly Colin, if she was being honest with herself.

It wasn’t just their courting, Eola told herself. He was the least capable in a knock-down fight, and her job was to protect both him and Roth. But their courting definitely had something to do with it. “Sorry,” she said as she bumped into him for the third time.

He nodded. “You’re fine. I understand.”

Eola closed her eyes. He did, didn’t he? Of all her friends, he was the only one who did—the only one who knew about her parents. She wasn’t just hanging around Colin because of their… relationship. It was because of her trapped parents. She couldn’t lose someone else. She would protect him. She would—

“Feral books!” Patrice called from the front.

Eola stopped thinking about Colin and her parents. Her sword drifted up into Second Guard—a little higher, and much better for defense. It also freed her sketching hand to cast barrier spells faster, at the cost of being out of position for offensive ones. They’d been intercepted in a room with tall, narrow shelves and stained glass windows that loomed overhead and shot flickering purple light across the carpeted floor.

“All you,” she muttered to Colin.

“How many?” Colin asked.

“Two, I think. Hard to tell.” Patrice took a step back. “Yep, two. There’s something else, though.”

“Let me see,” Eola said. She took two cautious steps forward and peered at their three enemies.

The first two were feral books; she recognized them by the whirling maelstrom of papers around them, and by their partially-formed bodies. They were…well, not easy, but they weren’t a third-floor-exclusive threat. Neither had noticed them yet. “Good eye, Patrice. Ritual,” she called back to Colin.

“On it.”

As he started casting, Eola focused on the third enemy.

A Phantasm.

Phantasms were rare, but unlike almost every other monster, they could be found on every floor—and even, occasionally, in the locker room outside the library proper. Instructor Clearance had spent extra time on them early last year, making sure her Introduction to Monsters students knew them well.

Generally speaking, Phantasms weren’t lethal dangers. They weren’t even truly aware in the same way most monsters were. Instead, they seemed almost to be voids in Mana itself. They couldn’t be damaged or destroyed by anything less than Unraveling Sigil, a Third Order spell, and magic behaved strangely around them. It universally got weaker as a Phantasm got involved.

There were lots of theories about what created Phantasms. Most were centered around the same kind of experimentation Eola did on a daily basis—on mages trying to create new Ordered Magic, or to find different ways of casting. Eola was pretty sure that was bunk. The Cult of Y’aer was one of the main proponents of that theory, according to Instructor Clearance; of course they’d want to reduce magical experimentation, lest something like Y’aer’s Chest be opened again.

That said, while Phantasms were a mostly harmless mystery, they were inconvenient.

“Patrice, get clear. Colin, call it,” Roth said. “Eola, hang back and watch that Phantasm.”

Eola nodded. The angry, dark-haired boy hadn’t played a single note so far. That changed; he wet his lips with a quick lick, then brought them to a silver flute’s opening and blew. No sound came out, but the room’s atmosphere changed. A rippling seemed to fill the air, and the shelves squeezed in around them until a narrow passage led from Colin to the feral books. “Won’t hold long,” he squeezed out between inaudible notes.

“Colin?” Eola asked.

“One moment.”

“Fine.”

“Yeah. It’s fine.”

She waited. Tapped her foot with the tip of her smallsword. Glanced over her shoulder at the ritual as Colin slowly, methodically traced it into the air.

Then, finally, he added his Mana to the symbol.

And fire filled the hall. It surged down the narrowed shelves, choked and forced into a space too small for it. The library dungeon reacted almost instantly. The air vanished from the shelves behind it, and Roth choked mid-note with a pained-sounding, squawking cough. Then the gout of flames hit the pair of feral books. Both went up in smoke, papers flashing orange and red as the monsters died.

Eola relaxed. Her sword tip drifted toward the ground. She reached out and put a hand on Colin’s shoulder. “Good job.”

“Uh, Eola, knock off with the lovey-dovey stuff,” Roth said. “This isn’t over.”

“What do you mean?” Eola turned.

The Phantasm was moving. It drifted down the expanding hall, slowly sliding through the air toward them like a green-and-orange slick in the air. It reminded her a little of the oil the school sometimes left on her plate when she’d finished a meal of fish—not even Atta wanted to lap the rainbow-reflecting plant oil up, no matter how much like fish it tasted.

As it moved, Eola’s heart plummeted. Instructor Clearance had warned her that, while Phantasms weren’t sentient, or even conscious, they did display curiosity. And the only one of the four who’d done anything worth being curious about was Colin. Her sword flicked upward, and she pulled her hand off his shoulder.

“Let’s keep moving, and quickly. That thing’s got its eye on Colin.”

“But Eola, it doesn’t have any eyes.” Patrice stuck out her tongue.

Eola tried to suppress a groan. She almost succeeded. Their next stairwell clicked into place, and one by one, they stepped onto it, careful not to grab at the railings in case one was an illusion.

And behind them, the Phantasm bobbed through the air, but it didn’t aim at Colin.

“Erm, why’s it interested in me?” Patrice asked. She hefted her warhammer—not that it’d do much good against the incorporeal, magic-immune monster. “I don’t like this one bit.”

As she watched, Eola had to admit that the Phantasm was very interested in Patrice. It drifted along, moving closer and closer as the four of them climbed the stairs. She shifted her attention to the next room—a long, ribbon-shaped chamber with four study tables, no shelves whatsoever, and a half-dozen different exits. As long as the Phantasm didn’t get curious about Roth or Colin, this was fine.

Fine for everyone but Patrice, that was.

Eola wasn’t just watching for monsters, of course.

None of them were.

Yes, Srillas were bad. And Dalyn/Davek pairs were a major concern—especially if the four of them ran into an adult pair while the Phantasm was on them. But they’d handled every one of the common Third Floor monsters before, and the route was tight and relatively low on exposure. Things could always go wrong, though.

As Eola moved up the stairs and through the study room, then posted up to wait for their set of stairs up to the Ensorcellment manuals, she kept her head on a swivel for the Scholar’s Cult.

Intellectually, she knew she had nothing to worry about. The professors and librarians had dealt a blow to the cult’s membership during the Library Exam last year, and she and her friends had beaten Eddarn and his monstrous familiar. It was unlikely they’d take another shot at her after that—and even if they were planning on it, the Scholar’s Cult had no idea when and where Eola would make herself a target. Until or unless she started taking regular, predictable trips to the third floor, she was perfectly safe.

But even so…

Even so, she’d beaten Eddarn off sheer grit, luck, and an ability neither he nor his familiar had been ready for. That wouldn’t be replicable; she’d have to win the next fight through skill, not luck.

And the third floor was Scholar’s Cult territory.

So, when the others settled in around the closest study table to their next flight of stairs, she kept on her feet and kept swiveling her head around. The conversation shifted from topic to topic, with Patrice unwilling to talk about magic, Colin and Eola uninterested in discussing their courtship, and Roth not saying a word.

Eventually, though, Patrice cleared her throat. “I found a partner for my Patrol Duty.”

“You did?” Colin asked quickly.

“I did. I’ll be going with Jeanette Ferrin.”

“Oooh!” Eola said. “She’s good. Very good. If you let her cast, she’ll do the rest of the work in any fight.”

“I know! I saw some of her fights. I don’t get what she’s doing, though.” Patrice took a breath. “It’s got something to do with magic, because she does the fiddly thing with her off-hand, but it’s subtle. Whatever she’s casting, she’s hiding it better than anyone I’ve ever seen. Anyway, I figured the two of you would be going together, and Fringe won’t let me third-wheel with you, so I had to find someone. She didn’t seem to mind that I…don’t do magic.”

Eola’s gaze flicked to Roth. He seemed interested. Highly interested. As Patrice went on about Ferrin and the library patrol, he opened his mouth a few times. Eventually, Eola held up a hand. “Colin and I are heading to the Spine Mountains. The library’s good, but a few of the ingredients on my list for Ensorcellment are from mountain monsters.”

Roth coughed. “I’m going, too.”

“Wait.” Patrice’s eyes narrowed. “Wait, wait, wait! You figured out how to third-wheel on Eola and Colin, and you didn’t tell me?”

“You couldn’t have done it. You’re on the combat track. It’s the same rules as coming up here. Besides, I needed Headmaster Tomlish to sign off on it—and to force Fringe to listen.”

Eola glanced at Colin. He shrugged. “You’re welcome to come with us. You would be, too, Patrice.”

When Eola nodded, Roth rolled his eyes. “I’m not going with you two to crash your…whatever it is you’re doing. I’m just ready to get back into the Spines, and I need a lot of those same ingredients to repair Nathien’s flute.”

He paused. For a second, Eola thought he’d say something more—like who Nathien was—but instead, he pointed. “Stairs are here. Let’s go.”

They went, hurrying up to the next room before something could track them down—or at least, something worse than the ever-present Phantasm that seemed to be trying to touch Patrice’s skull, right at the base of her neck. The redhead shivered. “Knock it off, you!”

The Phantasm did not knock it off. It seemed highly interested in Patrice, in fact. Eola hadn’t ever seen one so obsessed with a person before.

The room with the Ensorcellment manuals was relatively small, with only a single entrance and exit. Eola sheathed her smallsword and started to peruse the shelves. Ensorcellment for Efficiency, Self-Repair Ensorcellments, and The Craft of Permanent Magic were all easy to find. So was High Ensorcellment: An Exploration into Layered Magicks. But when she started searching for Ganni’s Guide to Filtering, something pricked her leg, just above her boot.

She looked down. Atta stared up, yellow eyes the size of pancakes. Eola took a knee, checking the bottom shelf. “What?”

“We should leave. Now.” Atta flicked her tail and pointed. Eola followed the gesture. Claw marks on the next shelf over, and missing books. The last time she’d seen that was…

Just before Eddarn’s monster familiar had ambushed them.

“I agree,” Eola said. “But we need to grab the rest of these books.”

“Then hurry,” Atta hissed.

Eola stood. “Hey, everyone, let’s get a move on. I found scratch marks, just like the ones Eddarn’s monster made.”

“Y’aer’s curse!” Roth spat. He pulled a pair of books from the shelf, growing his stack to five, then dumped them in Colin’s bag before he could protest. “I got Ganni’s Guide and Scripting Ordered Magic.”

“Great.” Eola turned, handed her four books to Colin, and drew her sword. “Our stairs should be getting here—“

“Why me?” Colin interrupted. “Why is it always my job to carry the books?”

“Because you’re the one who brings a bag,” Eola and Patrice said at the same time.

Colin groaned, and the four of them waited for the stairs down to arrive, along with ‘Patrice’s’ Phantasm—which continued to try to stroke the redhead’s hair and touch her back. Eola kept stealing glances over her shoulder the whole time, convinced that Eddarn was seconds away, riding in on a swarm of book monsters, with his sword drawn and murder in his eye. They took the first stairwell upward and started a long, steady climb toward the exit that was only interrupted once, when a swarm of Mana Mites washed over the floor of an expansive belltower of a room. Colin’s rituals took care of most of them, and Eola’s spellwork finished the stragglers.

The second-floor gate was still a welcome relief—and even more of one was the library’s exit. The four of them retreated to their suite, books in hand, and Eola sat down at her desk for some heavy reading. The Craft of Permanent Magic, after all, was written in Emerald Cursive.

Joy of joys.
