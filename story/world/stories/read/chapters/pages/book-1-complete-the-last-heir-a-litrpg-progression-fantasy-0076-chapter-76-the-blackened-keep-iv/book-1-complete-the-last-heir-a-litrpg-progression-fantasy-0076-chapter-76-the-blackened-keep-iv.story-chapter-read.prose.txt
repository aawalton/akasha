Rowan stepped around the Changeling’s corpse, walking towards the gate leading further into the Rift. He wasn’t planning on opening it yet—not until his Core was once again full—but the runes etched into the stone called to him.

As he reached the gate, he couldn’t help but glance back at the dead Demons strewn about the chamber—the spider, the hound, and the humanoid. Just a few weeks ago, even a single one would have been an opponent that Rowan most likely wouldn’t have been able to take on, but now, they were barely a challenge.

A proud smile tugged at his lips. “Came a long way since that first trek to Litwick.”

That had been a harrowing experience. Away from the only home he ever knew, with no idea where exactly he was or how long he needed to walk in order to reach a settlement.

But he came out of it stronger, just like out of every other challenge he’d faced since then. And this Rift wouldn’t be any different.

His gaze returned to the runes. He reached out, tracing a finger along the jagged script, muttering under his breath as he worked through the translation.

Some of the runes were familiar, pulled from lexicons he’d poured over in Eiselyth. Others were a mess of sharp edges and overlapping strokes, their meanings cloudy. Demonic script wasn’t just a language—it was a puzzle, layered with Intent and meaning beyond the shape of the runes themselves.

“Alright,” he exhaled, stepping back to take it all in. “Let’s see what we’ve got.”

His mind sorted through the symbols, connecting what he knew with what he could work out. The first sequence was easy enough. Warning. That much was universal. Demons loved to carve threats into stone.

The next part was harder. The runes were old, their meaning shifting depending on the context.

Pact… Darkened Souls…

No, that wasn’t quite right. The second cluster of runes suggested something more specific.

Lightless Souls.

Rowan frowned.

A warning and a clue. That wasn’t uncommon in Rifts, but considering where he was, this felt like something more.

The last segment was the most difficult. The structure was strange, like the writer had been trying to twist meaning into something that shouldn’t have been bound by stone.

He narrowed his eyes, reading it over again.

“Throne of Ash… bound by…?”

Rowan flared [Iron Will], keeping the images firmly in his mind. He rearranged the lines, overlapping them with runes he knew and the texts he’d read. A bead of sweat dripped from his brow, the Intent imbued into the writing causing him to flinch.

He took a step back, eyes widening as the meaning became clear.

A sudden presence pressed down on Rowan’s shoulders, an image appearing in his mind.

It was at once familiar and utterly foreign.

Rowan felt as though he was looking up at a towering presence, the air seemingly warping around him. Multiple glowing eyes were scattered across the creature's face, each one pulsing with a malevolent light. A crown of twisted horns adorned its head, jagged and covered in wicked-looking runes.

Its limbs seemed to ripple and change form, a constant, oppressive cloud of Ash swirling around its body.

[Iron Will] flared, and something inside Rowan shifted. A tiny, barely perceptible shudder went through his Soul, causing the image to shatter, freeing him of whatever it was he’d just uncovered.

Rowan’s knees buckled, his hands shaking as he stumbled back.

He felt a headache building, but his skill took care of it in record time. As much as the vision had shaken him, Rowan had never felt more focused. Everything around him seemed to sharpen, like a steel cable wrapping around his thoughts.

The creature he’d just seen once more appeared in his mind—a behemoth of corruption and spite. Only this time, it wasn’t imbued with malicious Intent, but a memory called up by Rowan’s seemingly empowered skill.

His breath came sharp and shallow as the last echoes of the vision faded. His body still thrummed with the lingering effects of [Iron Will], the unnatural clarity pulling long-buried knowledge to the surface.

“Throne of Ash. Bound by the Pact of Lightless Souls.”

The words rolled off his tongue like a mantra, dredged up from a book he’d read years ago. He could almost picture it—an old tome, bound in cracked leather, tucked away in the restricted section of his family’s library.

Rowan forced himself to steady his breathing. The image that had been seared into his mind, the shifting limbs, the crown of runed horns, the oppressive storm of Ash—it all matched the description of a specific Demon.

“Azrathul, the Corrupter of Realms,” Rowan whispered. “Demon Prince of the Endless Blight.”

His fingers twitched as more details surfaced, his mind slotting together scraps of knowledge like puzzle pieces clicking into place.

The title of Demon Prince wasn’t just for show. They were second only to Archdemons, beings who stood at the precipice of true rulership over the Nine Hells. Each one was a force of destruction in its own right, carving out a domain within their master’s territory while vying for something greater.

He serves Ira, the Archdemon of Envy.

Unlike the chaotic infighting of Lesser Demons, the hierarchy of the Hells followed a brutal but undeniable order.

Archdemons were kings of their own realms, each one ruling a specific Hell, their power tied intrinsically to their domain. And beneath them, the Demon Princes schemed and slaughtered, clawing their way toward ascension.

Rowan exhaled, the weight of realization settling in his gut.

The Archdemon of Envy ruled over one of the deepest layers of the Nine Hells, a place where ambition festered, where demons tore each other apart in an endless cycle of betrayal and hunger. To serve Envy was to crave endlessly, to see strength in others and seek to make it your own.

“They’re trying to summon him,” Rowan said, giving voice to the only suspicion that made sense. “Like they did in Eiselyth.”

His jaw tightened, a sudden anger welling up inside him.

They’re here, Rowan thought, every muscle in his body tense. Whoever was responsible. They’re connected to this.

His eyes flicked back to the jagged script on the gate, the meaning becoming clearer with every passing second.

“That’s how they did it,” he realized. “It wasn’t a Demon Summoning. It was a Rift Break.”

Eiselyth had the greatest wards in the Kingdom. Crafted and refined by generations of the best Archmages and Runesmiths his House had to offer. There was no way a tear in reality big enough to summon an Archdemon would have gone unnoticed long enough for it to manifest. But a Rift? That was another matter entirely.

If they somehow figured out how to create Rifts. How to… corrupt them, connected them to the Nine Hells, Rowan ran a shaky hand through his hair.

“This is getting bigger and bigger,” he muttered. “If I’m right, then this isn’t just a threat to the Storm Crown, but the whole kingdom.”

He took another step back, sitting down on the stone floor.

“This just became priority number one. I need to figure out who's pulling the strings, and I need to find someone I can trust.”

Rowan already had a lead on the former, but the latter might prove difficult.

The inn was built over a compound of House Thane, Lords of Peak Thunderfell. And while that wasn’t definitive proof, it was highly suspicious.

I’ll need to figure out the situation in the Crown. There’s bound to be a feud or two I can exploit that can hopefully lead to some allies. The enemy of my enemy, and all that.

Rowan took another deep breath, trying to steady his racing thoughts. There were so many threads vying for his attention that he didn’t know which one to pull first, but there was one that calmed his thoughts and settled his anger.

“It doesn’t matter,” he muttered, pushing himself back to his feet. “Not right now.”

Rowan’s gaze settled on the gate separating him from his goals, the runes carved into the stone only serving to bolster his resolve.

“I’m betting that I’ll find the answers to my questions deeper in.”

But as much as Rowan wanted to rip and tear his way to the heart of this Rift, he wasn’t going to let anger dictate his actions. Especially not now.

His first encounter with the Greater Demon ended with it fleeing, but Rowan had a feeling that going into the second with righteous fury on his mind wouldn’t end well for him.

“I have no idea how many more chambers I have to clear before I corner it,” Rowan muttered. “There could be five, or five hundred. Pacing myself is the way to go.”

Rowan knew this Rift would test him in ways that nothing till now had. The first battle pitted him against three monsters that Gold-ranks would have trouble with. And while he’d dealt with the threat with relative ease, death was always just a stone's throw away. A single distracted thought, a single moment of hesitation when he needed to be bold, and House Athlain would slip into history, a millennia of history extinguished.

Rowan’s fist clenched.

“That’s not going to happen,” he vowed. “I’m going to clear this Rift. I’m going to find every single Demon that calls this wretched place their home and end them. And when I’m done, I’m going to the Storm Crown, and I’m going to find out just how deep this poison goes.”

There was a conspiracy afoot, that much was certain. It started in Eiselyth, and now here he was, half a kingdom away and dealing with another possible Demon summoning.

“Not a summoning,” he reminded himself. “A Rift break.”

Rowan had no idea how something like that was possible in the first place, but the proof was undeniable. After all, he was inside it.

Rifts were supposed to be natural phenomena, not something the enlightened races could affect. Not the Elves hidden away in their enclaves, nor the Dwarves living deep below, and certainly not the Humans dwelling in their settlements. But if someone figured out a way to create artificial Rifts, the Kingdom of Vandral wasn’t the only one in danger.

If you hide them, let them grow, there’s no telling the amount of carnage that could cause, he sighed. For all I know, it could have already started.

Rowan knew what needed to be done, but before he could do any of that, he needed to be at his best.

His Core wasn’t empty, but he wasn’t going to enter the next chamber with even a drop of mana less than his peak. Rowan was confident in dealing with whatever lay beyond, but he had no idea what exactly that was, and underestimating a Rift—especially one connected with the Nine Hells—was a fool’s mistake. One he wasn’t going to make.

Rowan let out a slow breath, rolling his shoulders as he took in the gate one last time. His gaze lingered on the demonic script, the jagged runes carved deep into the stone. The Rift had already revealed far more than he’d expected, but it also left him with more questions than answers.

Rowan rubbed his temples as he turned away from the inscriptions. He needed to figure out who was responsible, but that would involve navigating the tangled web of noble politics, a battlefield as dangerous as any Rift. After all, he couldn’t simply storm into the Crown demanding answers. He’d need to be careful. Methodical. Find the cracks and exploit them.

It would take time, but it was still far better than the alternative.

Rowan moved toward a shadowed corner of the chamber, settling onto the cold stone floor, crossing his legs and closing his eyes. Mana hummed through his body, flowing like a river beneath his skin. Focusing on his Core, the world around him faded, his senses turning inward.

The Rift was just the beginning. Whatever was happening in the Storm Crown would be the first step to uncovering the truth behind the fall of his House, and Rowan planned on pulling at that thread until the whole thing unraveled.
