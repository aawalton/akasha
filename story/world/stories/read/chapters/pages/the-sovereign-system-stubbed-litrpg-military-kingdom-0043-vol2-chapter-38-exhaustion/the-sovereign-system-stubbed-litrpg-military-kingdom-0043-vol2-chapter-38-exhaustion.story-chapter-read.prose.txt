Over the next few hours, Tristan continued his steady ascent, scaling the remainder of the mountain at a respectable pace. Now that they had crested the ridge, the incline was far less treacherous, and there were plenty of handholds to assist the climb.

Still, that didn’t change the fact that the older man had been climbing for nearly eight hours—carrying Luke on his back the entire way. Even with his system-enhanced strength and endurance, the strain was finally beginning to show.

“Not long now, Tristan,” Luke said, offering what encouragement he could.

Though he hadn’t lifted a finger during the climb, his muscles ached, and fatigue tugged at every inch of him. Being strapped to someone’s back didn’t make him immune to the wind’s relentless battering or the constant tension of anticipating danger.

“Once we crest this ridge, we should reach the fortress ramparts. From there, we’ll need to prove who we are—or things could get messy.”

In truth, he hadn’t given much thought to what would happen once they made it inside. His mind had been too preoccupied with survival. But now that the fortress was in sight, he could already imagine the chaos: two unidentified men appearing out of nowhere in the midst of a siege.

Tristan didn’t reply, but Luke knew he was listening.

Minutes later, they pulled themselves over the final ledge. Ten feet below, the southern ramparts stretched before them—empty, unmanned.

Luke wasn’t surprised. Why waste precious soldiers guarding a cliff face no enemy would dare scale?

With a heavy breath, Tristan made the short drop and collapsed onto his backside, utterly drained. His arms trembled from exhaustion, the toll on his body impossible to hide.

“You did well, my friend. Leave the rest to me,” Luke said, clapping a hand to his shoulder.

Luke winced as he unfastened the hemp rope that had served as his literal lifeline. Every muscle in his body complained as he rose to his feet. A few half-hearted stretches confirmed what he already knew—he was going to feel this for days.

Suppressing a groan, his eyes swept across the fortress below.

Inside the walls, groups of soldiers lay huddled together, weapons clutched to their chests as they tried to steal a few hours of restless sleep. On the northern ramparts, archers paced with bows in hand, scanning the distance with hollow, watchful eyes.

Even at a glance, Luke could tell—they were worn thin. Starved of rest, pushed to their limits. God knows how long they’d been holding out.

I need to find whoever’s in charge before we get mistaken for enemies, he thought.

His gaze roamed across the fortress grounds. A handful of stone buildings dotted the interior, but one stood out—a tall structure near the southern wall, easily the largest in the area and the only one with any sign of security.

Two soldiers stood above its stone steps, clearly assigned as guards—though both were slumped against the wall, fast asleep.

Ordinarily, that kind of dereliction of duty might earn a flogging, or worse.

But here? It only underscored how desperate things had become.

Luke’s expression darkened, brows knitting in thought.

Progress: 74% of friendly forces remaining.

A translucent notification hovered in the corner of his vision. He stared at it for a moment, then exhaled quietly.

This is going to be tough, he thought grimly.

Even without seeing the enemy encampment beyond the walls, Luke doubted they could escape with fewer than four percent losses among their remaining forces. Not without a full arsenal of modern weapons.

And even if he did have the knowledge and materials to replicate those weapons, this wasn’t Clayton City. These soldiers weren’t eager recruits or proud defenders—they were battered, half-starved, and on the verge of collapse.

He glanced at Tristan, who had thankfully recovered enough to stand and move without stumbling. Together, they walked along the ramparts, careful not to disturb the sleeping soldiers slumped against the stone. None of them stirred.

The setting sun draped the fortress in long shadows, allowing them to move unnoticed for now. At least until the signal fires were lit and darkness became a liability.

In their tight-fitting robes, they stood out starkly against the backdrop of armor-clad Marxx soldiers. But it couldn’t be helped—climbing with two full sets of armor would’ve been idiotic and fatal.

They moved in silence toward the grand building. But as they approached the stone steps, Luke’s footfall echoed a little too loudly. The soldier on the left shifted, then blinked awake, clearly startled.

Luke stifled the curse rising in his throat. Think fast.

“Attention, soldier!” he barked, straightening his spine. His voice, though still touched by youth, rang out with authority, enhanced by the activation of his Domineering Air skill.

Both soldiers jolted upright as if struck, instinctively bowing their heads.

Luke gave Tristan a subtle nod. The older man returned it and stepped forward, projecting calm authority.

“General Drakon has arrived to assume command of this fortress, under royal decree from His Majesty, King Julius Marxx,” Tristan declared. His voice carried the weight of certainty, leaving no room for debate. “Take us to your commanding officer. Immediately.”

The two soldiers looked up, their faces blank with confusion.

“General? But… we’re in the middle of a siege,” the one on the left murmured.

“How did you get in?” asked the other, disbelief coloring his tone.

“Time is of the essence,” Tristan said sharply, his voice darkening. “Every second we waste costs lives.”

“R-Right. Sorry, General. Please—this way.”

They didn’t press further. One look at their bloodshot eyes and unsteady movements told Luke everything—these men were beyond exhaustion, likely surviving on fear and adrenaline.

The heavy wooden doors creaked open, revealing a long corridor lined with fading portraits and war-era banners. Luke felt a wave of familiarity. The architecture, the layout, even the lingering coldness—it reminded him of Xiu Fortress.

It made sense. Both fortresses had once belonged to the Qin Empire.

But that sense of recognition quickly gave way to unease.

In all his observations so far, he had seen only a few thousand troops—patrolling the ramparts, resting by the inner walls. If that was the bulk of their forces…

Then this battle was worse than he’d feared.

The hallway wound its way deeper into the structure before opening into a reception hall. In one corner sat a long table, around which a handful of officers slouched in their chairs.

Their faces were gaunt, eyes hollow. The air in the room was heavy with fatigue and despair.

This wasn’t just a force under siege—it was a force hanging by a thread.

“Commander, General Drakon and his deputy are here to see you—on the king’s orders,” the soldier announced, drawing the attention of everyone in the room.

Commander? Shouldn’t there be a general here as well? Luke narrowed his eyes slightly.

The man at the head of the table rose slowly, suspicion etched across his face. He wore the standard officer’s armor, his plumed helmet resting on the table in front of him.

A long, unkempt mustache and a patchy beard betrayed his neglect of basic hygiene. But behind the wild brows and sharp nose were tired, intelligent eyes—dull brown, but still alert.

“General?” the man repeated, his gaze flicking between Luke and Tristan. “How could reinforcements arrive in the middle of a siege?”

As he spoke, the two other officers at the table stood abruptly and reached for their weapons, leveling them at the newcomers.

“John, you fool! You’ve led the enemy right to us!”

Luke didn’t flinch.

This was exactly the reaction he’d expected. From their perspective, it was absurd—an exhausted teenager and an older man appearing out of nowhere, claiming royal authority.

“Lower your weapons,” Luke said coldly.

As his voice echoed through the chamber, an unseen force radiated from him. The air turned frigid, and a stifling pressure pressed down on everyone present. The atmosphere grew heavy, suffocating.

The men faltered under the weight of it—everyone but the commander.

Luke’s eyes narrowed with subtle interest. So he can withstand the pressure of my Domineering Air… Despite his exhaustion, the man had enough willpower to resist.

Stepping forward, Luke reached into his knapsack and pulled out a scroll, sealed with the king’s emblem. Without a word, he crossed the room and extended it to the commander.

The man accepted it, still wary, but broke the seal and read the contents. His eyes widened. First with disbelief—then with cautious hope.

“I, Commander Bridges, greet the general!” he declared, dropping to one knee and cupping his fist in salute.

The other two officers hesitated only a moment longer before doing the same.

They didn’t know how or why—but they were pragmatic enough to recognize royal authority when it stared them in the face.

“There’s no time for formality,” Luke said, striding toward the nearest chair and sitting down. “Brief me on the situation. Everything.”

Commander Bridges nodded and quickly reclaimed his seat.

“As you can see, we’re under siege—”

“Start from the beginning,” Luke interrupted, raising a hand. “Last I heard, the army had secured Yunxi and requested supplies from the capital.”

At this, the commander gave a heavy nod and began his explanation.

“Fifteen thousand men were left behind to garrison Yunxi and await supplies and reinforcements,” Bridges began. “General Wall and I led an advance force of thirty thousand to strike Xiandong Fortress. We aimed to secure it before the enemy could regroup.”

He paused, rubbing his temples.

“The battle took weeks. The terrain made the siege brutal—cliffs, narrow passes, enemy traps. Even though they only had eight thousand Qin defenders, they held us off longer than expected. We eventually broke through, but at a steep cost.”

Luke leaned forward, hands steepled beneath his chin.

The faintest flicker of concern crossed his face.

“General Wall was struck by a stray arrow in the shoulder,” Commander Bridges said, his voice fading into a heavy silence. “He received treatment… but it turned for the worse. We lost him.”

The two officers beside him lowered their heads, grief etched into their worn faces.

“Even with that wound, he led the final charge into the fortress,” Bridges continued. “If it weren’t for him, none of us would’ve survived—let alone taken Xiandong.”

Luke remained quiet, absorbing the information.

“And how many men remain?” he asked.

At that, the commander’s expression darkened with regret.

“A little over ten thousand, General.”

Luke inhaled sharply, his jaw tightening. They left Yunxi with thirty thousand, and now only ten remain?

Was General Wall incompetent? he wondered—then dismissed the thought almost immediately.

He’d known Xiandong would be difficult to capture, but clearly, he’d underestimated just how brutal it had been. The title of general wasn’t handed out lightly in the Marxx Kingdom. It was either earned through battlefield merit or awarded to those who had endured years of rigorous training at the Royal Military Academy.

And judging by the way the men speak of him… no, he wasn’t a fool. He was likely a capable, perhaps even compassionate, leader.

Now he understood how Xiandong had fallen—but it still didn’t explain why they were currently under siege.

“Who’s attacking us now?” he asked, voice low as he tapped a knuckle against the table. “Is it the Qin Empire trying to reclaim their fortress?”

Commander Bridges shook his head, and Luke’s eyes narrowed instantly.

Lhair… those bastards again, he cursed inwardly.

“After we took Xiandong,” Bridges said, “barely a week passed before the Selis army appeared outside the gates.”

Luke shot to his feet. The chair clattered to the floor behind him as he stared at the man in disbelief.

“Selis? Selis?! Not Lhair?!”

Even Tristan, usually stoic, looked troubled by the news—though not nearly as shaken as Luke.

Commander Bridges raised a bushy brow. “Lhair? We’re far too east to be dealing with their armies, aren’t we?”

But Luke didn’t respond. His thoughts spun like a hurricane.

Those tracks we found… half a day’s ride from the valley’s mouth. I thought they were Lhair troops. But no—it wasn’t them. It was Selis.

He clenched his fists, brows furrowed in frustration.

What is happening?
