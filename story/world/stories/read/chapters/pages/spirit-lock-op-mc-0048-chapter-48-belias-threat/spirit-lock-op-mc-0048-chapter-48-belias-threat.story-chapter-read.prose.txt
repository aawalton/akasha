While Aysha was agonizing about the wait, Hayden was locking himself in his room. He had cleared up his acne and gotten the reaction he desired, so it was time to get serious—to take his cleansing elixir and stop holding back.

To obtain a strong body, he had to cleanse it of the grime and decay responsible for sickness and age. Thankfully, he could do it easily through drinking elixirs made from cleansing catalysts within the rifts, and then the blockages to his infusion would instantly disappear. That’s what Hayden needed, and Jeela gave everyone at the auction a cleansing elixir due to Leo’s outburst—so it was free game for him to take it.

Hayden read the instructions on the box. It warned that the Celestial Spring elixir was so potent that it would kill C-rank rifters without adequate infusion. Thus, it gave the following instructions:

“One teaspoon a night, taken in a bath.”

How amusing, Hayden thought. He popped the cork and drank the entire elixir. Even people in Elysian would hit the floor and drift in and out of consciousness for six to eight hours after taking something so potent. Their core would kick into overdrive, and their body would overheat from the rampant cora blasting through their muscles and organs.

Hayden didn’t have such problems. He walked to the shower casually, turned it on, and scrubbed his body as viscous liquids oozed out of his pores, all reeking of sulfur and decay. If Jeela saw the scene, she would blacklist Belia and tell her to kill herself. But she couldn’t, and Hayden didn’t want her to. All he wanted was better soap to rid the horrid stench haunting him.

It was awful, but it was worth it. The next morning, he went on a run, only to attract the attention of the early morning crowd.

Instead of joining him or waving as usual, they simply gawked as he passed—then, turned on their recording features and sprinted after him.

“Was he glowing?” Hayden heard one say—and they were right.

Now that he had removed the bulk of the impurities from his skin and muscles, the cora infused in his muscles and skin bled through. It gave him an ethereal glow that was subtle enough to forget about in an extended conversation, but it was striking enough to notice if he stood beside a regular person. It was almost hallucinogenic, giving him a radiant attractiveness.

Hayden wasn’t the most attractive person—yet. His facial and body structure, and his teeth, were far from perfect, but his glow gave him something not even the most beautiful people had.

It was an incredibly vindicating moment for someone who had suffered ostracism and humiliation for his appearance since his arrival. He felt like himself for the first time in months. It was a great feeling.
