Looking at the youth who seemed determined to stand against the entire world, Ning paused.

He had a strong impression of the youth, though that impression came from the original Ji Ning's memories.

Ning recalled Xiao Fan, who belonged to the same group as him and had caused quite a stir during the spiritual root assessment ceremony.

Yet it wasn't because Xiao Fan possessed some exceptional talent. Quite the opposite, in fact.

Hailing from a family of cultivators, Xiao Fan possessed only a low-grade spiritual root. In contrast, his cousin, who underwent the same test, displayed a high-grade spiritual root. Throughout the ceremony, Xiao Fan had been the subject of mockery, both from onlookers and even his own cousin.

Even the original Ji Ning hadn't been entirely free of the irrational contempt directed toward Xiao Fan at the time.

And now, as Ning observed the fallen youth, he saw no pity or concern from the crowd. Instead, degrading murmurs filled the air.

"So arrogant."

"He needs a reality check."

"How can a dragon father give birth to such a pig of a son?"

All those classic xianxia insults...

Immersed in the unfolding scene, Ning couldn't help but experience a sense of déjà vu. It was as if one of the cultivation novels he'd devoured in his previous life had suddenly come to life before his very eyes.

His eyes drifted toward Xiao Fan's hand. There was an old ring on one of his fingers.

Ning stared at it for several long seconds.

No way.

He couldn't help remembering another trope from his previous life. In anime, there always seemed to be some protagonist running around with an ancient demon or monster sealed inside their body.

Cultivation novels had their own equivalent.

Sometimes it was an ancient soul sleeping inside a ring. Other times, it was an old monster hiding in a pendant, a mysterious spirit within a sword, a remnant will sealed inside some broken artifact, or even an ancient existence residing directly within the protagonist's body.

Either way, the protagonist ended up with some ancient being teaching them lost techniques and generally acting as a deus ex machina.

Ning looked at the ring again.

Surely not.

He suddenly had the overwhelming urge to walk over and ask, "Excuse me, Senior, are you awake in there?"

If it was true, the trolling potential was as high as the heavens, but he wisely resisted the urge. After all, trolling a potentially ancient cultivator who might be hiding inside a ring was probably not the healthiest way to satisfy his curiosity.

More importantly, amidst the commotion, something crucial seemed to have slipped everyone's minds.

Xiao Fan had emerged from seclusion.

This fact carried interesting implications. Ning himself, possessing a mid-grade spiritual root, had only just reached the First Stage of Qi Condensation. Yet Xiao Fan, with his low-grade spiritual root, had already emerged from seclusion as well.

That alone indicated there was probably more to Xiao Fan than his spiritual root suggested.

Xiao Fan wasn't the only strange thing about this situation. The way everyone else was acting also bothered Ning.

The Pure Qi Sect was, by all accounts, a reputable righteous sect. Ning wasn't naive enough to believe that meant everyone here was righteous and honorable, but this wasn't some dark alley or remote corner of the mountain.

This was happening openly, right in front of the General Affairs Hall, with no one from the hall coming to stop them.

More importantly, why Xiao Fan?

A low-grade spiritual root was certainly poor, but it wasn't some once-in-a-generation level of uselessness. There were other disciples with low-grade spiritual roots, including some who, like Xiao Fan, came from cultivation families.

Yet Xiao Fan seemed to be the only one constantly mocked, both now and during the spiritual root assessment.

Ning frowned slightly.

Once or twice could be a coincidence, but there were starting to be a few too many coincidences surrounding Xiao Fan.

Even so, he had no intention of stepping in.

Ning wasn't driven by some heroic sense of justice, nor was he arrogant enough to overestimate his own abilities. He had only just stepped onto the path of cultivation and had neither the strength nor the backing to casually involve himself in someone else's conflict.

Charging into trouble now would accomplish nothing except give him another enemy.

So Ning simply walked away.

No one paid him any attention, as if he were transparent, a fact that suited him just fine.

After taking a few steps, however, Ning couldn't resist glancing back.

Poor talent. Mocked by his family. Bullied by fellow disciples. Unnatural cultivation speed. Suspicious old ring. And somehow, trouble seemed especially fond of finding him.

This is seriously something straight out of a novel.

Ning shook his head and continued walking.

...

Lost in thought, Ning soon arrived at the General Affairs Hall.

The building towered over the surrounding courtyards, its sweeping tiled roofs layered one above another. Thick vermilion pillars supported broad eaves carved with clouds and soaring dragons, while the polished stone steps leading to the entrance had been worn smooth.

Compared to the rustic entrance plaza, this place finally looked like the headquarters of an immortal sect.

A queue had already formed outside.

Most were newly accepted peripheral disciples waiting for their assignments. Some chatted nervously among themselves, while others stood ramrod straight, trying their best to look composed despite their excitement.

With nothing better to do, Ning's gaze wandered.

It eventually settled on a massive calligraphy scroll hanging beside the entrance.

The two characters upon it were simple, and the ink hadn't faded despite the passing years.

Without realizing it, Ning found himself staring.

The characters seemed to grow larger as the black ink rippled, every brushstroke gradually taking the shape of a sword. His surroundings faded as a pressure settled over his chest.

There was only him and the sword in this world.

A dull ache spread through his temples.

"You shouldn't stare at it for too long."

The calm voice cut through the strange sensation like a bucket of cold water.

Ning immediately tore his gaze away, drawing in a deep breath as he rubbed his aching temples.

The discomfort eased almost at once.

His eyes drifted back toward the calligraphy, though this time he didn't dare look directly at it. "...That scroll did this?"

Three young disciples in fresh green robes approached.

The one in front carried himself with composure. His features weren't especially striking, but there was an easy steadiness about him that made people instinctively trust him.

"That scroll contains the lingering intent left behind by the sect leader," he explained with a faint smile. "We're still too weak to comprehend it. Looking for too long only invites unnecessary suffering."

"So I wasn't imagining things." Ning let out a relieved sigh. "For a second there, I thought I'd somehow offended a piece of calligraphy. I was ready to apologize."

Ning had never expected wall décor to attack him. Cultivation really did broaden one's horizons.

The broad-shouldered youth laughed outright. "I've never heard anyone apologize to a piece of calligraphy before."

Ning looked at him seriously. "You laugh now, but that's exactly what someone says before another piece of calligraphy attacks them."

This time, all three failed to hide their amusement.

Even the girl, whose expression had remained cold until now, let the corner of her mouth twitch almost imperceptibly.

The composed youth cupped his hands with a faint smile. "My name is Wei Zhusang. It's a pleasure to meet a fellow disciple."

Before Ning could return the greeting, the broad-shouldered youth stepped forward with an easy grin.

"Zhang Feng." He jerked a thumb toward the calligraphy scroll behind Ning. "Don't mind that thing. The calligraphy around here only attacks people once." He tilted his head, pretending to think it over. "Most aren't dumb enough to stare at it a second time."

"Well, that's reassuring," Ning said blandly, much to the boy's amusement.

The three of them turned toward the last member of their little group.

The girl looked back at them expressionlessly.

After a brief silence, she let out the faintest sigh, as though she'd already accepted there was no escaping this.

"Lang Rulang."

Ning returned the greeting with a smile. "I am Ji Ning."

He gestured toward the calligraphy scroll with a helpless grin.

"Thanks for the rescue, by the way."

Zhang Feng laughed. "You're being too polite."

It was only then that the three of them seemed to properly process his introduction.

Zhang Feng's eyes widened. "Hold on... you're Ji Ning?"

Recognition? Ning's eyes narrowed.

"So you're the one with the five-element aptitude," Zhang Feng said, laughter in his voice. "I'd been wondering who it was, especially since you aren't from any cultivation family we know of."

Wei Zhusang nodded. "At the test, I thought the elder must have been mistaken. Five-element affinity is rare, even rarer than a high-grade spiritual root."

Lang Rulang's voice cut in, cool but blunt. "A pity yours is only mid-grade."

"Lang Rulang! That's rude," Wei Zhusang quickly said.

She frowned slightly. "...Was I wrong?"

Ning chuckled before Wei could answer.

"Not at all." He shrugged. "If someone tells me the sky is blue, I can't exactly get offended because it's true." He smiled at Lang Rulang. "Besides, Sister Lang was only being honest."

Ning wasn't offended. After all, she'd only stated a fact, and since they'd helped him, there was no reason to act like an ass over it.

Zhang Feng was the first to break the silence. "Haha! Well said, Brother Ning." He gave Ning's shoulder a friendly pat. "Looks like the four of us are in the same boat after all. We're all new disciples."

Ning smiled before glancing between the three of them.

"Since all of you come from cultivation families, would you mind helping someone who's completely new to all this?" Ning knew this was his chance.

Wei Zhusang nodded readily. "What would you like to know?"

"What exactly does being a peripheral disciple mean?"

"The sect assigns every peripheral disciple a duty," Wei explained patiently. "Spirit fields, kitchens, medicine gardens, patrols, beast pens... wherever manpower is needed. Most of the work isn't glamorous, but every assignment contributes to the sect."

"And your performance is recorded," Lang Rulang added matter-of-factly. "If you distinguish yourself, promotion to the outer sect becomes much easier."

Ning nodded thoughtfully. "So becoming an outer disciple depends on merit?"

"In part." Wei smiled. "Cultivation matters more."

Lang Rulang picked up where he left off. "The minimum requirement is the Fourth Stage of Qi Condensation."

Ning raised an eyebrow. "...Only the fourth?"

In all the cultivation novels he'd read, the early realms were usually skipped over in the blink of an eye.

The three stared at him.

"..."

Ning cleared his throat. "I mean... that suddenly sounds much higher now that I've actually thought about it."

It was easy to forget that those stories followed monsters.

For ordinary cultivators, the Fourth Stage of Qi Condensation was anything but "only."

Zhang Feng burst into laughter.

"For disciples with low-grade spiritual roots, it might as well be a mountain." He patted his chest proudly. "But we're luckier than most. The three of us all have mid-grade spiritual roots. Our families wouldn't have sent us here otherwise."

Lang Rulang glanced at Ning. "You'll be fine. You have the same spiritual root as us."

Ning quickly realized that, to her, it was simply an objective fact.

I guess that's reassuring. So my mid-grade spiritual root isn't that bad.

As they continued waiting, the conversation gradually became more relaxed.

Wei Zhusang shared stories about life within the sect, Zhang Feng eagerly interrupted every few sentences with exaggerated tales that Wei insisted were "only half true," while Lang Rulang occasionally corrected the details with ruthless precision.

Their dynamic was quite solid.

In fact, Ning could tell that the three of them, especially Wei Zhusang, were deliberately trying to befriend him, and he was happy to play along. After spending an entire week alone in a cave, he'd missed interacting with other people.

Eventually, a disciple emerged from inside the hall and called a name.

Wei Zhusang was the first to enter. A short while later came Zhang Feng, followed by Lang Rulang.

One by one, they disappeared through the doorway until Ning was the only one left waiting.

Before long, another voice echoed from within.

"Ji Ning. Go into the fifth room."

Ning took a steadying breath before walking inside.

Having already been assaulted by a calligraphy scroll today, he had no intention of underestimating anything inside the General Affairs Hall. He quickly found his assigned room without paying much attention to anything else.

The room was surprisingly simple.

An elderly man sat quietly behind a long wooden desk, a stack of jade slips arranged neatly before him. His aura wasn't intentionally oppressive, but standing before someone so far above him in cultivation, Ning instinctively felt a faint pressure settle over his shoulders.

He immediately cupped his hands. "This disciple greets Elder."

The elder gave a small nod before picking up the jade slip containing Ning's assessment.

"Ji Ning. Mid-grade spiritual root. Five-element affinity." The elder nodded faintly. "You will begin in spiritual farming. Take this jade slip and report to Sector Three, near the Fushan River."

Ning bowed. "Yes, Elder."

As he straightened, his thoughts drifted.

So much for sword fights and demon hunts. I've transmigrated into a xianxia world... only to start as a glorified gardener. Oh well, at least there will be hoes.
