"Garbage," Kalsin said, wrapping another piece of paper into a ball and tossing it in the dusty corner to add to the forming pile. He grimaced as the movement sent a spike of pain across his bandaged arm.

Sunlight glinted through the boarded windows, adding to the already considerable heat and making the air oppressive on the lungs. A pair of cabinets yawned empty on the wall, an upturned bench was getting idly picked at by a one-armed rogue, and a moth-eaten mattress was currently supporting two hundred pounds of sullen bodyguard. Jack had found this out-of-place lodging, as it suited their needs to recover and decide on their next move. She also couldn't have possibly carried him all the way back to the inn they were staying at.

"Requesting the investigation and prosecution of Jona Smmith, as well as hasty return of—or compensation for," Kalsin muttered, "the stolen laundry." He looked up at Cinder. "Any chance the azTél siblings operate an underground boutique? Outfit here today, heretic style."

"A fashion sense to die for, that."

Kalsin added the letter to the trash pile. As he thumbed through the remaining papers, he couldn't help but shoot an apprehensive look at Jack. Their plan had worked; they learned a pair of names—seb-hordozó and azTél—as well as access to the collector's stash of intelligence. Generally, letters were sent to the leechouse, but there was a good chance the ones carried by the collector himself would be the freshest, most relevant information at hand. Even the most tame citizen could turn into a spy if the price was right, and the leechouse paid handsomely for anything concerning the law.

As for what this whole affair cost them, Kalsin tried not to think about it.

He was unsuccessful, thinking about it anyway.

"You could've waited another minute. I had everything in order," he said with a bite to his voice.

Jack, her hair untied and covering her sidecuts, slowly sat up and pinned Kalsin with a glare. After a moment, she shook out her hand, as if trying to spray droplets at him.

"Yes, I was weakened from the nocnitsa tears, but so was everyone else. We—" Kalsin stopped, only then realizing what she meant by signaling ‘weakness’. He swallowed. "We talked about this, Jack. We did, and you agreed. You agreed to it. No killing the Clenn."

In their first years, when she had merely been acting as his bodyguard, this topic was a point of division for them. Tantrums, arguments, painful fistfights followed. Even as their reluctant partnership grew into rock solid friendship, loyalty sealed by drop upon drop of human and mythos blood, they had never bridged this particular gap. Jack insisted his unwillingness to kill Clenn was a weakness, a fool's tenacity.

But how could he, with clear conscience, kill the very thing he wanted to become? Maybe he didn’t want to admit it… but his parents’ teachings were too ingrained to confront. Let alone to change them.

Jack put her wrists together as if shackled, they gestured toward the window, at the city.

Kalsin shook his head. "They won't launch a manhunt against us. I doubt even as little as wanted posters will go out, so long as we don’t resort to murder. Catching people is secondary to the secret service; no, they're trying to root out an idea. The whole thing has to be kept in a tight sheath, otherwise our jobs would be a hell of a lot easier. They can’t just announce that heretics exist."

Jack picked up the scribbled note she'd prepared for him when he had woken. Though they had learned to communicate just fine, sometimes more complex discussions had to be conducted through written speech. She frowned for a second, then wrote on the back, flipping it to let him see. I should've killed the collector and his bitch.

“For what, for doing his job?”

She jumped up, screaming at him without sound. He fucking hurt you!

She then sharply turned away, covering her lips that he couldn’t help but read.

Though it took effort, Kalsin did not reply. That package of stuffed away feelings was better left untouched. Better to pretend he didn’t understand. He refocused his attention on the collector's letters.

Minutes passed in heavy silence with only the irregular pattern of wind-rattled boards breaking the monotony. Just as he was nearing the end of his patience, Kalsin found something. He read through the paper once, twice, then taking a moment to clear his throat, he read it aloud.

"Heretics primed to flee the city under Blindlight via darkfinder, hive. Intersect at O;NW;two-fifty-two. Optics advised."

Cinder was at his shoulder in an instant, eyeing the note. She let out a snort. "Just our luck. The message we're looking for ends up being the coded one."

"Now, no need to be a sore winner." Kalsin waved the note in the air. "How many darkfinders operate in Remallon, in your estimation?"

"It does say hive, so we can rule out the Clenn side of the business," Cinder mused. "Which makes sense. The heretics being Scarrd, they could hardly approach an abyss to navigate them out of the city. Cuts our potential 'finders in half."

Jack stared at Kalsin with a strange look on her face as Cinder rambled on.

"People are flooding the city to witness the wedding ceremony. Darkfinders will be looking to make big money smuggling folk inward now that they can crank the rates to high heavens. So passages going the opposite way? Should be scarce. If any."

Kalsin nodded. "Meaning, if there's a single hive darkfinder currently staying in Remallon—which we know there is," he tapped the note, "then we likely have the group we need to find these azTél siblings. And by proxy, follow the trail of the cure. Blindlight is just around the corner. Two days tops." He glanced at his sister, pausing.

Cinder raised a preemptive hand. "Gotta keep my Clenn ass out of this one, I know. As per usual." If there was bitterness in her tone, Kalsin couldn't hear it. It still hurt, though. It pained to see his sister act so casually about being put on the side, time after unending time.

Cinder fished around in their bag until she found the map she'd marked. "I'm leaving this with you. I'll be waiting along the fracture we discussed. You know, to go fossegrim hunting."

As Kalsin reached for the map, Cinder stopped him with a firm stump to his chest, face grim with badly hidden concern. "Careful from this point forward, yeah? You have no more tricks up your sleeve. All resources exhausted, all lines drawn. Only this lead is what we have left. This lead, and our lives. If a crossroads comes, and you have to decide between the two, do not even think about choosing anything but the latter."

Kalsin politely declined the suggestion, though he didn't say it out loud.

If such a time came, he'd take it all.
