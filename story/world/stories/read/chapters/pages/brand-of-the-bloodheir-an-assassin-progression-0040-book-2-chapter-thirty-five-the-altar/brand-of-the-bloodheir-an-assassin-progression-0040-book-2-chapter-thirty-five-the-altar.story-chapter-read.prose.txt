A brief but disconcerting rush again brought Risens to the small chunk of earth hovering silently in the abyss. The rounded stone structure with the conical top awaited him, the black mouth of the yawning void of its entrance pulling at him with an eerily tangible force. Whispers of the secrets and power that lay within hummed through him.

The space had remained unchanged since the last he’d seen it, yet his grip remained on the hilts of his blades. The Raven Talons were quiet. Fear had overwhelmed their bloodlust during their first visit. It was muted now, though the undertones were a potent reminder of the unknown threats that awaited in the Under.

He made his way to the chamber where he’d collected the unusual stone vessel. The quiet gurgle of the spring that bubbled from the stone raven on the wall was the only sound. While the damp moss-covered stones showed no signs of movement or disturbance, the threat of Carrion lurking in the dirt clawed at his mind. The Talons expressed no sense of alarm beyond their grumbles of distaste.

On the wall opposite the natural spring, the wavering, darkened void of a portal stood open. A second doorway had appeared in his absence—or more likely at his completion of the previous test.

Risens’s careful steps across the stones elicited a quiet crunch of dirt underfoot, adding to the soothing calm of the fountain. Unlike his previous visit, the doorway exiting the room remained open. Whatever challenge had resided in this room had been completed. He doubted that any more tests would be met in this chamber.

Stealing himself for the uncertainty that was to follow, he inhaled a deep, steadying breath before stepping into the blackness of the new doorway.

Aside from the focal point of a pedestal in the center of the floor, the chamber he found himself in was wholly different than the last. Risens suppressed an unexpected chill as he’d experienced similar rooms. None of those situations camewithout bloodshed.

Most of which had been a result of his blades.

The best he could describe it as was a close analogy to one of Lathrenon’s many and varied torture chambers hidden throughout the bowels of his opulent estates and castle. The room was small and perfectly square, with less than five meters separating each wall and ceiling. The master craftsman who toiled for their Brand in Learners Row would be proud of the perfect configuration. Situated in the center, a square altar was offset, angled so that its sharp corners pointed to the middle of the perpendicular walls. Every meter, from floor to ceiling, was constructed of tight-fitting stone slabs. In many places, their natural gray tones were stained with irregular discolorations—dark shapes of what he could only assume to be long dried blood. The metallic tang that hung in the air was all too familiar.

The splotches weren’t limited to the floor alone. Pools, streaks, and splatters dotted the walls and even the ceiling above.

The room was dimly lit, though there were no visible light sources. Like his floating home in the Barren, it seemed that the room itself provided its own light.

The portal that had granted him access was gone, replaced by the same carefully constructed stone. On each wall was a single perfectly centered small rectangular indentation, roughly knee-high. Each was shallow, less than the width of his blade, and ended in solid stone, giving no hints to their purpose, though the designs were curious.

The altar waited in the center of the room. It, like everything else, was square and heavily stained. The entirety of its flat top was covered in black streaks that ran down each of the sides as if lifeblood had poured over the edges. Etched into the surface were a series of half a dozen long, skinny, parallel indentations that looked like thin slats of a sewer grate leading into the pedestal below. An indentation was also present in the rear of the altar. The markings cut into the top were, in fact, a drain of sorts, leading to an angled funnel whose pointed tip emptied into an object hanging from a stone peghalfway down the side of the pedestal.

That it was another vessel of sorts, there was no question.

At first glance, it had the appearance of a vial—long, narrow, and rounded. The hollow tube was only slightly wider than the hilts of his blades. Similar to the Raven Talons, what appeared to be feathers were wrapped around a dark leather holster; a similarly designed strap hung off one end as if it were meant to be affixed like a belt. A stopper dangled by a thin metal chain from its open top. That it was meant to store something, he was certain.

Was this meant to collect blood? If so, whose and for what?

As if his thoughts were a catalyst, a quiet grating sound filled the room. The vibrations underfoot were subtle, but he could feel them through the soles of his boots.

“Let us taste their blood.”

“And let them taste our wrath.”

The voices of his Talons sent a shiver through him.

The small panels built into the base of each of the walls shifted, sliding upward into the stone. The soft grinding ceased as they disappeared, revealing only an inky darkness behind.

With blades now free of their sheaths, he fell into a defensive stance, his vision darting back and forth, his body spinning as he watched for movement within the darkness. The distinct sounds of shuffled movement and the scraping of nails across stone filled the chamber, ricocheting off the walls, disguising its positioning. To his right, he captured the slightest hint of something within the dark.

Without warning, all sounds stopped. No war cries issued from the disfigured lips of the creatures that launched at him from the openings in the walls. The attack began suddenly and violently.

Inky-black bodies, covered with lumps, distorted into twisted images of the natural species they may once have been. Rats were a frequent sight throughout the alleys and sewers of Windwake, especially in the less affluent Districts. Some grew to absurd sizes, feasting on the prevalent refuse in the clogged, filthy corridors. Even the most gargantuan he’d seen was easily dwarfed by the trio that harried him now. Each of these was large enough to be confused for a large dog.

Their speed was uncanny. The twisted rats’ attack was devoid of strategy, fueled by sheer feral rage. From all sides, they rushed their intended prey, rank saliva dripping from bared fangs. Risens sidestepped the first. With a growl, he hammered the Raven Talons down on the back of its corrupted neck. Like cutting through a twig, the blade met little resistance as it passed through skin and bone.

Black blood splattered across the gray stone, adding additional stains to walls that had seen their fair share already.

“Disgusting!”

“Putrid!”

Risens was afforded no time to plan his steps. Instinct, acquired over a lifetime of training, kicked in. Rolling to the side, he narrowly dodged vicious, gnashing jaws that meant to sever his leg. His erratic movement sent the pair colliding and off course.

They quickly recovered and flanked him on both sides. He carefully backpedaled, letting their snapping jaws catch only air as he took stock of their skills. As big and dangerous as they might have been, he was pleased to see that they were no smarter than their much smaller brethren.

His boot brushed up against something, and though he couldn’t afford to take his eyes off the enemy, he skirted around it to see that his strike had neatly severed the head of the first. Though its body twitched on the floor, its elongated mouth filled with jagged teeth still snapped ferociously, even though the light of life had faded from its eyes.

The remaining pair circled the pedestal in the center of the chamber, attempting now to force him into the corner of the room. This time, he was prepared.

He coiled his legs, leaping up and over the charging pair. His feet landed gently on the edge of the raised pedestal. Shifting his weight, Risens summoned the Dull Wind. The rats, frantically turning to track their prey, were ill-prepared as he materialized in a flutter behind their twisted, mangy bodies. Reversing his hold on the blades, he drove them down into the back of the creature’s skull. Their teeth gnashed together as his weight crushed them to the stone. The blades punched through flesh, bone, and brain until they bit into the pavers below. The jaws, intent on tearing him to shreds, closed for the final time, and the quiet grating of stone against stone sounded through the chamber as the trio of doors slid closed.
