Tension in the ritual room continued to amplify. The key inside the lock of the book turned like the natural world had a sudden eclipse, the shadow casting darker and darker until the final point. Wind swirled in the room like a torrent, and the mechanism made unnatural grinding sounds, taking far too long to unlock for its innate simplicity.

The key, the lock, the book, they were all mere place setters performing a masquerade of physicality, and the real opening was of something ancient and untouched for aeons.

Most in the room were terrified at this point, clutching weapons and readying themselves for what they likely thought would be demons pouring out of cracks in reality.

The turning of the lock finally ended with a click, at which point the book came alive, bursting out of a yelping Azure’s hands and unfurling its pages wildly, as the wind suddenly gusted ten times harder, knocking people onto the floor and resulting in many cries and even some full-on screams.

Samantha remained anchored by her telekinetic control of her own body, unmoving as her dress and hair were buffeted. It wasn’t without effort, but doable, primarily because she understood it wasn’t an attack, just a secondary consequence of the magic at play.

It’s just reality being dramatic.

The book, now glowing brightly in the ambient gloom, finally settled as it reached a page. The page popped right out as if plucked like a feather floating free; it rippled and changed from paper to clay in a flash, and the symbols etched into its surface flared brighter briefly before dimming.

The book dropped to the floor with a thud, the shadow over the world passed by, and the wind abated. The clay tablet remained floating gently in the air, and all was otherwise still.

As the collective people in the room recovered themselves with airs of confusion mixed with relief, Samantha floated over to the artifact and studied it in the spirit. There, it was powerful indeed, almost alive-seeming, with Fate-like branches extending into the inner structure of the underlying spiritual reality.

Well, that’s a sobering fact, isn’t it?

She understood something of the truth it hinted at, yet it was difficult to fully grasp. Perhaps that was something to do with language itself, which the artifact represented — it governed the very forms of one’s thoughts, was an outline to all perception. How much more would it do on Calrenazzod, inside the System, a reality where one’s will literally changed things directly?

And I’m handing it over to Glaelychus, a potential enemy. ‘The’ Rival, according to Lovie. Is that smart?

Sighing to herself, Samantha cast the higher-level version of Identify, Mystic Discernment, to glean any further details possible.

First Page

As ancient as civilization, this artifact’s mysterious intricacies would take a lifetime to unravel and exploit.

Effects unknown.

Samantha growled in her throat. I shouldn’t really be surprised, though, should I?

In truth, it wouldn’t take her a lifetime, thanks to the benefit of her existing perceptions in the spirit world as well as her sensitivity and connection to Fate itself. The extensive branches the artifact had… could likely be manipulated. Perhaps it was a Sympathetic connection to all who utilized language?

Through the First Page, reaching out and touching the world… or the collective unconscious… what if I-

“Babe?” Azure asked hesitantly.

Samantha, distracted, didn’t turn toward her. “Hmm?”

“Your face looks exactly like what I’d expect a diabolical villain to look like right before unleashing their greatest evil yet. And honestly, I’ve been there, so I think I know. Aside from that, this thing gives me a… bad vibe. Creepy as hell.”

Samantha peeled her perceptions away from the artifact to look at Azure, though her physical eyes remained closed. She still felt a bit absent-minded. “Really?”

“Yes, really.” Azure appeared worried. She nodded, then looked at the others and shrugged at them. “Does anyone else feel similarly?”

A veritable explosion of nods and protestations of agreement answered this. Gabriel visibly shivered and nodded vigorously as she said loudly, “That thing is super evil! I’d smash it with a hammer if it were up to me. Oh!” She leaned her spear against herself and grabbed a literal hammer — the size of a mini sledgehammer — from where it was belted to her waist. “I actually have a hammer, here! It’s my backup weapon.” She shook it toward Samantha as if it were a treat. “Want me to smaaaash it, Goddess?”

Briefly, Samantha regarded Gabriel and replied wryly, “I think I’ll narrowly pass on that for now, Gabriel, thanks.”

“Point stands, though,” Azure said. “It might be best not to mess with this thing.”

Samantha sighed. “Most of the vibes everyone felt have to do with the Consolidator’s deliberate intent, and perhaps who he was. We know he wasn’t a nice guy.”

“Maybe the artifact is what made him that way, love.”

“Mmn.” Samantha turned back to study the tablet, tapping one finger on her lip. “His title could be a clue, no? Consolidator. One who combines a number of disparate things into a more effective whole…”

“Holy shit,” Azure exclaimed. “That’s it, Samantha. The First Page is a means of controlling people! En masse, through language, manipulating the… code with which we think.”

Everyone, aside from Samantha, suddenly stared at the page like a bomb in the room, many holding their breath. Gabriel tried to ‘surreptitiously’ scooch closer to it, hammer gripped tightly in her hand. Unfortunately, she ruined her efforts by forgetting her spear was leaning on her… it suddenly clattered loudly on the floor, the only sound in the chamber at that point.

A mortified Gabriel swallowed and muttered, “Sorry,” as she scooched back away in defeat.

Samantha held out her hands to everyone, to reinforce calm, painting on a smile. “Easy, folks! You should all know any given tool can be put to good or evil, as weapons often are on both counts, hmm? Just chill out. This thing has no inherent effects. Only a powerful spellcaster using it actively could accomplish anything, and, well… I do think even they had better not just dive in without restraint, to say the least.”

Still staring right at it, Azure said, “You'd better keep this away from Tashome, Samantha.”

“Uhh, what? Why?”

Azure threw her hands up in exasperation, finally turning to Samantha with a frustrated exhale. “Why do you think?! You’re tempted by this fucking thing! Tashome won’t be able to resist, as you said, ‘diving in.’ I don’t think we want to know what happens if he does.” A pause. “Also, so you know? Your eyes are bleeding like torrents right now. Maybe you should open them for a change.”

Grimacing in sudden realization, Samantha opened her eyes physically and saw blood running down and staining her robes. Quickly, she utilized [Conjure] to pull the blood out and away, clearing it.

She squinted at the scene in front of her, full of worried people watching them and the tablet. But the tablet was nothing special — clay with indentations coating almost its entire surface. The indentations simply defined an alphabet and crude linguistic rules.

Despite its humble appearance, Samantha felt burdened by the possession. “How the hell am I supposed to turn this over to Glae of all people?”

Azure groaned. “Well, there is one worse in this world to give it to, after all. Ugh! Honestly, I think keeping it might be even worse.”

“I seriously beg to differ.”

“You said it was an ingredient for something, though, right? Something to strike at the Sage. What if he destroys it? Consumes it in the process, as a sacrifice.”

Samantha could not prevent a wince, and she was certain Azure saw it. And she knew Azure was likely right, even if it wasn’t absolute. Quickly, she said, “We don’t have to tell him we have it, you know. We could find out how it could be used against the Sage ourselves and use it thusly, non-destructively.”

Azure stared at her wide-eyed for a long, enduring moment.

Samantha blinked back at her and raised an eyebrow. “No?”

Azure crossed her arms. “I seriously can’t believe you right now.”

“Whaaat?!”

Azure began tapping her foot, her lips tightening.

One of the cleromages muttered under his breath, “Looks like something will explode in here, after all…”

How dare she get mad at me! I’m the goddess here.

An involuntary breath was sucked in, as Samantha felt a pang of indignant anger rising up from her deific side. She ran both hands through her tangled hair, tightened them into fists, and stared up at the ceiling, trying to restrain an outburst. She said in a deliberately moderate tone, “Is it so awful to investigate, innovate, and evolve the wonders of humanity found, as I am meant to do with all I come across? Is it wrong to exemplify what it is I am, to accelerate and amplify our progress? Is it so bad to want to preserve something clearly priceless and precious beyond all belief, against those who’d treat it like a piece of rubbish to burn up?”

“No,” Azure answered softly. She approached closer, her hand reaching up to her floating wife at her waist and resting there. “But people are more important, wouldn’t you say?”

Letting out the breath slowly, Samantha looked down to meet Azure’s eyes and felt her will in the matter melt away before the compassion she saw contained in her lover’s soul. It snapped her back to herself, to the girl out of her element, to her often subdued, inner core… Sammy.

Who might've thought twice about going all mad scientist on a troll’s body, and likely wouldn't consider raising the dead.

Who’d cried for the baby seals and the ‘starving children’ commercials, guilting her dad into donating to possibly dubious charities.

Who would never try to ‘unlock’ an evil artifact that might control people’s minds.

With a faint smile, Samantha opened her mouth and said-

“Samantha. You have it.” The voice of Glaelychus, like a hot auger boring deep into her mind.
