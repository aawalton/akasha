My heart leapt into my throat as I tried to yank my arm away from the man. The rubber bumps on his gloves pulled at my skin, threatening to tear it, but I couldn’t get away from him. His grip was too strong, and he refused to let go of me.

Panic exploded in my chest, and I whirled around to face him. My gaze found the gun clasped in his hand, pointing directly at me, before meeting his eyes. He’d pushed his helmet back, revealing his sweaty face, and immediately, I felt the training I’d done at the Academy take over. The hours of self-defence practice that I’d had, the drills that I’d run again and again, sprang to the front of my mind, and I was in motion before I knew what I was doing.

I knocked the man’s gun aside, batting it away, before slamming the heel of my palm directly into his nose. It crunched beneath my hand, sending a spray of warm blood splattering down my arm as the man let out a howl of pain.

Disgust burst within me, and I longed to wipe the blood from my bare skin, but I could not. The black uniformed man was too distracted by his broken nose. Tears were flowing down his face, and blood streamed over the fingers pressed to his face. He was barely even looking at me, and it seemed he’d forgotten about the gun held loosely at his side, but that would not last long. I knew I had to act fast. I could not lose the chance to arm myself.

Lunging towards him, I snatched the weapon from his hand, jerking it away from him before he had the chance to react. The gun was slippery in my bloody hands, but I managed to spin it towards him and didn’t even hesitate before pulling the trigger.

The recoil reverberated through me, almost causing me to drop the gun, and the man cried out, but I forced myself to shoot him again. I needed to be certain he was dead and couldn’t follow me, but the gun did not fire.

Frustration and fear crashed into me as my eyes flicked down to the gun before returning to the man. He staggered, banging against the door frame before falling backwards. A wet, desperate cry slipped from his lips, a plea for help, but I didn’t reach out to him. Instead, I just turned and began to run again, the gun still clutched in my hand.

I had one. I had a gun, but it was out of bullets. Either that, or it had gotten jammed. I wasn’t sure which, but I hoped it was just jammed. I could probably deal with that easily enough, but I still needed to get to the storeroom. Even if the magazine was full, I knew it wouldn’t be enough to take down everyone who had broken into the Academy.

There were so many. I hadn’t been able to see all of the people on the front lawn, so I didn’t know exactly how many people I was up against, but I knew it was a lot. And that wasn’t including the people who were already in the building at that point. I hoped some of the tutors and trainees had been able to take some down before being killed, but I knew I’d need more ammo and weapons regardless.

I just need to get to the storeroom, I told myself. The cache would have everything I needed. There would be more than enough ammo and guns in there, so I’d be fine. I wasn’t far, and I’d yet to hear anyone else coming after me, so hopefully, no one had been close enough to hear the man’s shout.

But the moment that thought entered my mind, I heard a noise. The hairs on my arms stood on end as a loud, crowing laughter came from behind me. It ricocheted along the corridor, seeming to chase after me, and I felt an icy fist of fear tightening around my heart.

Pounding footsteps exploded behind me, and I pushed myself to run even faster. I longed to look back over my shoulder, to check how far away the people chasing me were, but I couldn’t risk it. The floor was too slick with blood. There were too many corpses scattered across it, and if I looked away for even a second, I was terrified I’d fall. Then, they’d be on me before I could even get up.

My heart thumped loudly in my ears as I threw myself around the corner, and I felt my foot slip. My slippers couldn’t find any grip on the blood-slicked floor, and my arms wheeled wildly as I tried frantically to regain my balance before slamming hard against the wall.

The air was forced from my lungs, and the gun slipped from my grasp as blood and chunks of something splattered beneath my hands. I pressed my lips together quickly as far too cold droplets hit my face, but thankfully, none got in my mouth. I stumbled backwards, unable to stop myself from gagging, and tears stung my eyes as I looked around desperately for the gun I’d dropped.

My eyes found it quickly, and my stomach heaved. I clamped my lips together again, ripping my gaze away from it. The gun had landed in what was once a person. Their head. They must have been shot because the top of their skull had exploded backwards, coating the wall and floor behind their body with bits of skin, bone, and brain, and my gun sat right in the middle.

Horror dawned on me, and I looked down at my hands. That’s what it was. The chunks that clung to my fingers. It was on the wall, and it had splashed on me when I hit it, I realised as I wipedmy hands on my legs and fought the urge to throw up again.

I couldn’t do it. I couldn’t retrieve the gun from the person’s skull. It had sunk too deeply into the gore, and despite how desperate the situation was, I couldn’t bring myself to do it. The footsteps behind me were drawing closer too. The people were almost upon me, and I was so close to the storeroom. There would be more weapons there, so I didn’t need to bother with that one. I could leave it.

The sharp sting of bile and vomit burned the back of my throat as I lunged forward, breaking into a run again. My heart pounded in my ears, and tears slipped from my eyes as I sprinted, almost letting out a sob when the door of the storeroom finally came into view.

I threw it open and dove inside, slamming the door shut behind me. My shaking fingers fumbled with the lock, smearing blood across the back of the door before I managed to twist it, and I knew it wouldn’t be enough to keep the people behind me at bay. I wasn’t planning on staying in the storeroom forever, obviously. I just needed some time to be able to get to the cache, grab the weapons, and load them, but I was terrified that they would reach me before I could.

They’d kick the door down to get to me, just like Mom had threatened to do a few times. The lock wouldn’t be strong enough to withstand that. I needed something more, something stronger to hold them back.

My gaze scanned the storeroom quickly, roaming the tall shelving units that lined the edges of the room and landing on the yellow crate towards the back of the room for just a moment, before finding the ladder that led to the roof. An idea started to form in my mind, and I grabbed at it, knowing I had no other options.

I could block the door with one of the shelves. That would make it impossible for anyone to get in or out, and then I could grab the weapons and go up through the hatch. I’d slip back into the Academy through a different hatch, maybe one on the boys’ side, and no one would expect that. They’d probably still be trying to break the door down, and I’d be able to sneak up on them and pick them off. Perhaps I’d even be able to take them all out before they realised I was behind them.

A slight smile appeared on my lips as I grabbed the edge of the nearest shelf and pulled, but it barely moved. The smile fell from my face as I tried again, tugging the solid metal beam as hard as I could.

Desperation sparked within me, and a sob finally escaped my lips as I threw all of my weight into it, and the shelf finally moved. It squealed loudly against the floor, the sound sending a shiver through me, but I just continued to pull. My slippers slid on the wooden floor, leaving behind a trail of bloody smears, but the shelving unit gradually shifted closer.

Toiletries rolled from the shelves, thudding loudly against the hard floor, and I could feel every muscle in my body straining from the effort, but I finally managed to get it into place in front of the door.

Relief crashed into me as I backed away, eyeing the shelf to make sure I had pulled it far enough before turning towards the big yellow box. I’d only glanced at it briefly when I first entered the room, and as I stepped towards it, I realised something was wrong. It wasn’t sealed. The clasps on the lid were open.

The blood drained from my face as I took a step towards it, approaching carefully as if it might explode. Someone had gotten to the cache before me, and I could feel my heart pounding in my chest as I reached for the top of the crate. A grimace was already twisting my features before I even peered inside, but the moment I did, I felt my stomach sink.

It was empty. The top shelf of the cache was entirely empty, and I quickly ripped one of the drawers open, muttered a soft prayer to anyone who’d listen. My eyes found the gun-shaped holes that had been cut out of the foam inside, but not a single one held a gun. I pulled open the next drawer, almost choking on the desperate sob that slipped from my lips when I saw that it was just as bare as the other drawers had been.

Frantic fear thrashed within me as I tried another drawer, gasping as my eyes fell on a box of ammo. My heart leapt before immediately plummeting again as I remembered I’d dropped my gun. The ammo was useless to me, but still, I couldn’t stop myself from plunging a hand into the cardboard box.

My fingers slammed against the bottom of the box, and hopelessness seized me. The box was empty. Someone had already raided the cache, and I wasn’t sure whether it was other trainees or the people dressed in black. Perhaps they already knew it was there. It could have been the first place they looked.

They could have people inside the Academy, I thought as horror sank into the pit of my stomach. Somehow, they’d managed to get to Seth, so perhaps there were others too. Maybe they had a whole group of traitors positioned within the Academy, just waiting for the signal to attack. They could have run directly to the storeroom and emptied it out, leaving nothing for the rest of the trainees to use to defend themselves.

Hopelessness bubbled up within me as I stared into the final drawer of the cache. It was completely empty too, and another sob threatened to escape as I looked around the room. I didn’t know what else to do. There were no weapons in the cache, and nothing in the rest of the storeroom, but the people speeding towards me were probably armed. They most likely had the guns that should have been in the cache, which meant they’d be able to overpower me easily. Even if I managed to get one of their guns off them, it probably wouldn’t be enough. They’d outnumber me, and I didn’t want to let them take me, but I didn’t know what else to do.

My breath was coming too quickly. I was hyperventilating, and I clenched my hands into fists as I forced myself to inhale slowly. I knew I was panicking, and that would make it even harder to think, even harder to come up with a plan, but I had to think of something.

I was painfully aware of the footsteps drawing ever closer as I forced myself to take another deep breath and look around the storeroom again, an idea slowly forming in the back of my mind. I knew the caches were dotted throughout the building, but there had to be another one close. A single crate of weapons was not enough to arm everyone who lived on that corridor, and if there was a cache in the room I was in, there was probably one in another storeroom too. I probably passed it whilst I was running.

Irritation flared within me, almost drowning out the hope that had sparked in my heart. I wasn’t sure which storeroom it would be in. I couldn’t remember ever seeing one in the room near the top of the stairs, but perhaps I just hadn’t been looking for it. I easily could have missed it, and I needed to try. I didn’t have many other options, I realised as I took a step towards the ladder.

As soon as my hand touched the cold metal rung, a noise came from above me. Goosebumps erupted on my arms, and I slowly stared up at the roof. My eyebrows pulled together, and I tried to work out what I was hearing as the soft sound continued.

Something or someone was scratching on the hatch above me, and I stumbled backwards, almost falling over one of the bottles that had rolled onto the floor. Immediately, my mind jumped to Seth. I assumed that he had followed me down the ladder, that he had run after me, but perhaps he’d stayed on the roof the whole time. I didn’t know how he knew I was in that room or had even moved towards the ladder, but he had. He was up there, waiting for me, and he scared me more than the other people who were hunting me down.

“Oh, Grace!” someone called in a singsong-y voice.

I spun around, unable to breathe as I stared at the door in horror. They had sounded so close to me. They must have been just outside the door, and I had no weapons. There were only two ways out of the storeroom, and people were waiting for me outside both. I was trapped, unarmed, and there was nothing I could do.

Panic thrashed in my chest, and I squeezed my eyes shut, reaching out desperately for my reality, but nothing happened. Normally, it came to me. It responded immediately, leaping towards me and whisking me away, but it did not. None of the dizzinesses reacted at all.

Tears slipped down my cheeks as I threw myself towards the worlds that always crowded the back of my mind, but they danced away from me. I was left with only a shadow of the sensations they caused me, and I couldn’t work out which one was my home. They all felt weird and different, and I was too scared, too frantic, to focus. I couldn’t even tell which of the worlds I’d been to before.

“Come out, come out, wherever you are!” the person called from the other side of the door.

I ripped my eyes open, just in time for a loud crash to echo through the room. The last few bottles left standing on the shelving unit I’d dragged in front of the door clattered to the ground, and I leapt back. My spine slammed against the wall painfully, and hopelessness washed over me. I sank to the ground, pressing my blood-covered hands to my lips to muffle the sobs that I could no longer hold in.

The floor shook in time with the footsteps that thundered towards me. More people were approaching, and Seth, or whoever it was, was still clawing on the ceiling above me. I was surrounded. A loud screech split the air as someone threw themselves against the door again, and the shelves were pushed back an inch. Mocking laughter filled the air, making my tears flow even faster, and I pressed my hands to my ears.

I no longer cared if the people outside the storeroom could hear me crying. The sound of their laughter filled me with fear, and I needed to block it out. It was too cruel, and I could tell how much they were enjoying the hunt, which terrified me and made it even harder to focus on the worlds lingering at the back of my mind.

Shutting my eyes again, I threw myself towards them, but they refused to be caught. They backed away from me, avoiding my desperate attempts, but I just tried again. Finally, my grip tightened around one of them. I felt a wave of vertigo crash into me, and I was so desperate to escape the people surrounding me that I didn’t even hesitate. I just dove into the world.

Dizziness engulfed me, and I felt my head lull back against the wall. A muffled bang reached my ears, the sound somehow both close and distant at the same time. It was from the Academy. I knew that. They were still trying to kick down the door to get to me, but I forced myself to ignore it.

I was too scared to let myself even think about what was happening there. I was barely in the other world yet. It was still taking shape around me, and I was worried that if I focused on the Academy too much, I’d return there. I couldn’t risk that. I couldn’t let myself get trapped there because I wasn’t sure I’d ever be able to escape.

The new world, wherever I’d jumped into in my haste to escape, was still blurry and indistinct around me, but I was pretty sure that it was not my reality. Something about it just felt different, even though I’d yet to open my eyes. My head was spinning too fast for me to be able to do that, but I could just tell.

I was sitting on something soft, though. Gradually, I was becoming aware of the feeling of it beneath me, and my back was still against something hard. Probably a wall. My knees were hugged against my chest, and I was seated in the exact same position that I’d been in at the Academy.

Fear pulled at me, and I tried to take a deep breath, but it was hard. My lungs felt as though they were straining to take in even just a little bit of air. It felt as though I were breathing through wet fabric, as if I were being waterboarded, a sensation I was used to in some world, but I wasn’t. There was nothing on my face, and I could feel a gentle breeze caressing my cheeks.

My head was still spinning, though. I wasn’t sure whether I was just adjusting to the new world or if it was from lack of oxygen, but I forced myself to inhale again. The movement sent a spasm of pain through my chest, almost sending me into a coughing fit, but I held my breath, forcing myself to remain in control of my body.

Slowly, the twitching in my lungs died down, and I took another, more shallow, breath before opening my eyes. They were not entirely focused, but immediately, I realised that I was sitting on my bed with my back against the headboard. It definitely wasn’t my reality, though. The colour of the duvet cover beneath me was wrong. It should have been blue, not pink with red spots. Or flowers, maybe. I wasn’t sure. My vision was too blurry to tell.

I blinked hard, trying to force my eyes to focus as I looked around, searching for dangers. I almost expected the people in black to be standing over me, guns pointed directly at my head, but my room was empty. If it weren’t for the painting on the far wall, it would have looked almost identical to my room in reality.

My stomach dropped as I stared at the wall, my vision slowly clearing. It was not a painting, I realised slowly. Something had been scribbled on the wall. The red paint was slowly bleeding into the white wallpaper, making the words harder to read, but I could still make them out.

We’re watching you.

I swallowed. My eyes were fixed on the wall. I could not tear them away from the smiley face that had been scrawled beneath the words. It was clumsy. Whoever had done it must have been in a rush because it was messy. The paint dripped from the eyes, making it look as if the face was crying blood.

My eyebrows twitched, and my gaze returned to the words. My mind was still clouded from appearing in the world, and I could feel my thoughts moving slowly. It took me far too long to realise what the words said, and I lifted a hand to my mouth as horror crashed into me. Glancing around my room again, I searched frantically for whoever had written that on my wall, but my room was empty. I was the only one in there.

I could smell something, though. A strange scent filled the air, turning my stomach, and fear built within me as I looked around again, but it did not take long for me to realise where it was coming from.
