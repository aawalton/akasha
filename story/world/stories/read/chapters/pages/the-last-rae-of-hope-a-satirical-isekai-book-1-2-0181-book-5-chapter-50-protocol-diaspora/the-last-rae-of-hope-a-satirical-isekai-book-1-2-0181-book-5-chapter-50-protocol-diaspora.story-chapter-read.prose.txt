The orb detonated in fire and light, then a silent shockwave blew outward in all directions, taking me with it. Sound only roared back to my ears when I hit something hard behind me.

Dizzy and a bit nauseated, I looked up and back, not really comprehending what had broken my trajectory.

It was Aleph himself.

As he helped me to my feet, two forces erupted from the orb’s collapse. They tore free from each other simultaneously, pulling in opposite directions.

Crimson corruption boiled outward and down. It was both liquid and shadow at once, spreading across the dais in waves that cracked and blackened the marbled stone on contact. Aziza’s red-black aura coiled and stretched, spreading in jagged veins, its height oscillating as she struggled to take proper form.

In that same moment, prismatic light shot upward, iridescent and refracting as Sparkles swirled skyward. For a brief moment, I saw a single reptilian eye narrow in my direction before disappearing into an ascending glitter tornado. Nora’s barrier flashed in front of us just in time. With a howling gale, Sparkles ripped the roof off the tower, tearing wood from stone and spraying debris beyond the temple. She screamed, hurling herself through the giant cloud overhanging the city. A thunderous boom echoed, and all that remained were its edges, a mere smoke ring drifting outward in a slow, eerie silence. The blast that was Sparkles continued upward, her course locked onto Paradise high and far above.

Isn’t she going to have trouble with that barrier when she gets there?

Not to be outdone, Aziza, finally solid, rose to her full height and maybe even more, cutting off our skyward stares. Just as before, her lower half remained hidden behind her gossamer-green dress. Her six antler-like horns, curving sharply from her crown, caught the light as her red hair billowed and blazed in all directions around them. With clawed fingers curling at the air, she announced, “I am going to take great pleasure in destroying all of you.”

The air around her darkened, her streaming shadows coiling tighter.

“Control yourself, Aziza,” Ragnerus warned, his greatsword starting to pulse with lightning.

Olethros positioned himself next to Ragnerus, his makeshift gold staff striking one hand. “Didn’t you hear our brother call a truce, Zizi?”

Aziza’s rage exploded, more black mist boiling from her aura as she recognized their team-up. “Cretins, both of you!” she screamed. “You dare to—”

Then Chester meowed from Vernie’s shoulder—just once.

Neither loud nor dramatic, it was the sound I always associated with asking for a treat.

And yet Aziza froze.

Vernie held up her hands as the Mistress’s head snapped towards them. Aziza’s eyes narrowed as she stared at Chester, rage ebbing away into astonishment.

“Grand Duke Ailuros?!”

Chester mewled all of two syllables.

She threw her arms up in the air. “You’re not serious!”

Chester hopped down from Vernie, bouncing lightly before strutting forward. With unblinking eyes, he took a dainty seat a few feet away from the hem of the demon lord’s dress.

Aziza started to kneel, only to freeze halfway.

“The first being to make a move will be obliterated!” Her aura flared to emphasize that she meant every word. Yet, Chester had her at least listening. She leaned closer, seeming to soften in response to what seemed to be only silence.

No, she’s reading his body language.

“Father wouldn’t do that!” she shrieked at Chester at one point.

Olethros tapped his staff on the stone. “I assure you, he will.”

“Will?”

Chester hissed, and Aziza gasped, then tried to growl to cover it. “I will pull every single golden hair from his head to make the rope to strangle him with.”

“No,” I disagreed. “I call dibs on the Holy Sage.”

She sneered at me, though I’m pretty sure she wasn’t familiar with the slang. Before she could threaten me, however, Chester pawed at her long, flowing gown.

She turned to Nora with a flinch, and her voice actually warbled. “Ambrose’s heart—give it to me!”

Nora didn’t hesitate. She reached into her cloak and held it out. “I was going to give it to you anyway,” Nora said quietly.

Aziza’s aura surged, and the heart was ripped from Nora’s hand by a tendril of crimson animus. It flew across the open room, and the moment her fingers closed around it, she fell still. Even her shadows stopped writhing.

Anger and sorrow.

“He was the one who loved me. And yet...”

Unimaginable loss.

“He knew,” she continued, her grip tightening on the heart. “He knew Father was using us. He knew—” Her voice broke. “He knew I was trying to free him, and he sabotaged me! To save me and all those I cared about...”

She trailed off; her eyes were wet with dark tears.

“We built our Maze... Our home...”

I wasn’t about to tell her we call it the Bastion now.

Then Aziza turned rigid as the heart flared in her fingers. “Amity!” she suddenly shouted. “Not any that I’ve stolen. Not yours! Not of anyone left in this accursed city. It is his!”

“His... who?” I asked, dread weighing down my arms.

Nora’s face went white. “The portal,” she hissed. “The portal we used to get here. It wasn’t Mother Liora’s!” She looked at me. “It was Relias’s!”

“But why?” I asked even while the rest of my companions pulled out their weapons. “Why would he want us to free the two of them?”

Aziza’s head snapped toward me, and her eyes blazed. “He doesn’t care about this city or its residents. He’s not even after you or me. He’s after—”

Then she screamed. “Galenus!”

The Bastion and its portals! The biggest threats to his authority!

Nora jumped as her staff came to life. “Galenus!” she echoed Mistress Aziza’s cry. “Come in, Galenus!”

“Lady Nora?” came his startled reply. “We lost connection—is everything—”

“Marquis Galenus!” Aziza snapped. “Protocol Diaspora. Evacuate all sectors!”

There was a bit of a static hiss.

“My Lady?” Galenus’s voice came back startled and somewhat clipped. “I’m pleased—you are —however—mid-transport—we have personnel and enemies contained in subspace—I must not simply—”

“Your location has been compromised,” Aziza insisted. “Execute Protocol Diaspora. Now!”

“She’s right,” Nora added. “Get everyone out!”

Aziza’s head tilted as her eyes bulged. “Why would he listen to you—

Chester mewled sharply.

“Understood!” Galenus shouted. “Executing Protocol Diaspora. All sectors evacuating now!”

Aziza looked between the cat and Nora, her form flickering with barely-contained rage. “That was not your call to make!”

“No,” Nora agreed pleasantly. “We were just helping.”

Chester licked his chops as if agreeing.

Before Aziza considered immolating either of them, I waved my hands to catch her attention. “If Galenus is leaving the Bastion, we should also find our own way out—”

A flash of electric blue light from the cloud ring high above silenced me.

“Uh... Nora?” I heard Vernie ask.

“It wasn’t me!”

“The only other thing that glows electric blue is Naught itself!” I shouted, running for the stairs. “We need to move, now!”

The cloud’s crackling edges curled inward and downward, drawing tight. A sharp, piercing hum came from above as beams shot down, pouring into the inert holy crystals along the city’s edge.

“A cage!” Tetora roared.

The Periphery blazed with azure light, the aura spreading toward the Inner Sanctum. The energy ran up over the inner walls, pulsing and refracting through the crystalline statues strewn throughout the forum.

A shrinking cage.

Nora’s staff began to dim. Ragnerus, Aziza, Olethros, and Chester all started to turn translucent. The floor beneath us began to hum, and the rest of us were brought to our knees.

“He’s using us to power his weapon!” Ragnerus shouted, calling up his dark aura in defiance.

A deep groan came from somewhere far beneath us, and the floor tilted before the whole tower lurched sideways. Nora was the first to lose her footing.

Her staff flew upwards. She had been reaching for a barrier, but there was no floor to anchor it or her.

With the high temple coming apart beneath us and the blue cage pulsing around us, we fell. The four floating demons rallied their auras together and plummeted as one.

Chester was a streak of descending shadow. He clutched Vernie’s collar before she’d fallen ten feet, his small form expanding into a large shadow that wrapped around her.

Ragnerus buzzed and cut through the debris. He solidified out of crimson mist, with one arm around Tetora’s chest and the other hooking under Aleph’s side, his form flickering under the strain.

Aziza caught Nora roughly by the wrist, a ponderous bubble wobbling around them.

I saw Olethros translocate toward me too, but then my hand caught something. A ledge? Perhaps a beam. Through the painful pull, I held on. For one victorious second, that was more than enough.

I finally saved myself.

Then a chunk of tower struck the side of my helmet like a church bell, and everything spun in a swirl of white.

The scent of citrus and sage.

Olethros’s arms wrapped around me. He was saying something, but I couldn’t make it out.

The rest of the High Temple of Chairo came down around us. Bright blue flooded everything, and I heard several explosions.

No. No! No!

I might have been screaming that; I’m not really sure. I do remember several black vines surging upward around us. The last thing I registered before the rescuing dark took us was Olethros’s arms, still holding on.

Then nothing.
