Chapter 21: Poison

Deep beneath the dark depths of the chasm where I had followed the call, was a door. It was an old door set into the jagged wall, simple in its construction, but ancient all the same. The wood looked like it had been carved from a single massive tree, weathered by time but still solid. Symbols I couldn't name were carved into its surface, flowing script that seemed to shift and writhe when I wasn't looking directly at them.

The door reminded me of something from Earth, from the old houses I'd worked on. The kind of craftsmanship that didn't exist anymore, where every detail was done by hand and built to last centuries. But this was older than anything I'd ever seen, carrying the weight of ages in its simple construction.

The call tugged at me stronger now, almost painful in its insistence. I needed to go in. Whatever was behind this door was what had been drawing me through the dungeon, away from Kari and the others, into the depths where no sane person would venture alone.

As I stepped forward, the Resonance Flux opened a notification in front of me, blue text that glowed against the darkness.

Hidden Essence Trial Detected. Completing this trial leads to reward, but is highly dangerous. Do you wish to proceed?

I hesitated for a moment, my hand halfway to the door. Highly dangerous could mean anything in this place. Death was the least of the possibilities. There were worse things than dying, as I'd learned since the Awakening. Worse things than losing your life.

But I'd come this far. Abandoned my group, followed mysterious voices through hostile territory, descended into a chasm that might not have a way back up. Turning around now would make all of that meaningless.

I did wish to proceed, and at that thought the door swung open on silent hinges, revealing swirling shadow and motes of crimson light that danced like fireflies. The darkness beyond looked alive, hungry, waiting to devour whatever stepped through.

I stepped forward anyway, and it swallowed me whole.

The sensation was like drowning in reverse, being pulled up through liquid shadow instead of sinking down. For a moment that lasted forever, I existed in the space between spaces, surrounded by whispers in languages that predated human speech. Then reality reasserted itself, and I found myself standing in a room that made no sense.

In the room beyond I found it to be strangely familiar. It was a high-end, not quite luxury hotel room like you might find on Earth. Clean lines, modern furniture, everything designed for comfort and efficiency. The carpet was thick under my feet, a deep burgundy that reminded me of dried blood. The lighting was soft and warm, nothing like the harsh illumination of the dungeon above.

Other doors led, presumably, to other places. A bathroom, maybe. A closet. Or maybe they opened onto alien landscapes I couldn't imagine. In a place where doors could swallow you and transport you through shadow, anything was possible.

On the table in the middle of the room was a simple leather-bound book, the kind you might find in an antique shop or a lawyer's office. The leather was worn smooth by countless hands, and the pages were yellowed with age. Next to it sat a vial filled with liquid that looked like liquefied midnight, so dark it seemed to absorb the light around it.

There was something else on the table too, something I hadn't noticed at first. A small card with elegant script that simply read: "For the preservation of what matters most."

To start the trial, drink the liquid in the vial.

The Resonance Flux's notification flitted across my vision before fading away like smoke. For a moment, I considered not doing that. This was a trial, and trials weren't supposed to be pleasant. They were supposed to test you, break you down, see what you were made of when everything else was stripped away.

I'd already passed one trial when I chose my path. This would be different. Harder. More personal, maybe, given the familiar setting and the cryptic message about preservation.

Yet I had to if I wanted to pass this trial. I'd come too far to back out now, invested too much in following this call to turn away at the first real challenge. My paranoia warred with my desire for power, two competing instincts that had kept me alive in different situations.

The desire won. It always did these days.

I picked up the vial, noting how cold the glass felt against my palm. The liquid inside moved strangely, like it was thicker than water but lighter than oil. It didn't smell like anything, which was somehow more unsettling than if it had reeked of poison or death.

I downed the liquid before I could consider it much further, and regretted it immediately.

The taste hit first, like swallowing broken glass mixed with battery acid. Then came the burning, starting in my mouth and racing down my throat like a lit fuse. My hands clutched my throat as my throat, lungs, and chest were lit aflame like dry kindling in a forest fire.

I tried to scream, but like a living thing, the flame burned its way through me. It was as if the fire had been given claws, and it was tearing at my throat from the inside. Every breath became a struggle, every heartbeat sent fresh waves of agony through my chest.

The pain was unlike anything I'd experienced before, and that was saying something. I'd thought I knew pain. I'd thought I understood what it meant to suffer.

Vash, when my training had started, had beaten me within inches of my life. She had healed me, then beaten me again, and again, until pain became a familiar companion. My wife and son had been torn from me in a single moment, ripping away pieces of my soul I'd never get back.

I had buried the man I had been, in favor of a man who wasn't so weak. Someone who could endure whatever the universe threw at him and keep moving forward.

I knew pain beyond the physical. I had courted it, meditated on it, understood it to a degree few ever did. Pain had become my teacher, my constant companion, the engine that drove my connection to the Flux.

Even so, I began to learn that you never stopped knowing pain. The moment you thought you'd reached your limit, pain showed you how wrong you were. There was always another level, always something worse waiting to test your resolve.

Fire became pain. Pain became fire. It crawled along my throat like a living parasite, digging itself into my lungs like ants crawling into sugar. Every breath was a battle, every swallow felt like swallowing molten metal.

I could feel something changing in my chest, something crystallizing in my lung tissue. The liquid hadn't just been poison, it was something worse. Something that would transform me from the inside out, making my own body the instrument of my destruction.

I wanted to black out. I wanted to die. I wanted anything other than this endless burning that seemed to grow stronger with each passing second.

Instead, my world became agony, and I could not escape it.

I collapsed to my knees on the hotel room carpet, clutching my chest as the fire spread through my respiratory system. My vision blurred, tears streaming down my face as my body tried desperately to cope with whatever I'd just introduced into it.

The leather-bound book on the table seemed to mock me with its presence. Answers, maybe. Solutions. But I couldn't focus enough to read, couldn't do anything but endure the inferno that had taken up residence in my chest.

Minutes passed, or hours. Time became meaningless when every second felt like an eternity of suffering. Gradually, the acute pain began to fade, replaced by something more manageable but no less concerning. My breathing was labored now, each inhalation accompanied by a wet, rattling sound that hadn't been there before.

I forced myself to stand, legs shaking with the effort. The room hadn't changed, but I had. I could feel it in the way my lungs worked, the way air moved through passages that were no longer clear. Whatever the poison was doing, it was far from finished.

With trembling hands, I picked up the leather-bound book and opened it to the first page. The text was written in a language I should have recognized but didn't, script that seemed to rearrange itself as I watched.

Then, slowly, the words began to make sense.

"Welcome, Initiate, to the Trial of Essence Crafting. You have been chosen to learn the ancient art of manipulating matter through controlled Flux manipulation. The poison now coursing through your system will kill you in seven days unless you can craft the antidote using the techniques contained within this tome."

Seven days. I had a week to learn an entirely new discipline or die choking on my own crystallized blood.

The book continued: "But know this - the antidote alone will not save you. The damage to your respiratory system will be permanent unless you also craft a device to aid your breathing. Both challenges must be completed to pass this trial and claim your reward."

I sank into one of the hotel room's chairs, the book heavy in my lap. Seven days to become a master crafter. Seven days to save my own life through skills I'd never used before. Seven days to create not just medicine, but a device that would fundamentally change how I existed in the world.

The fire in my chest had settled into a constant burn, a reminder that my time was limited and my options were few. But as I looked at the book, at the promise of knowledge and power contained within its pages, I felt something familiar stirring within me.

Determination. The same stubborn refusal to give up that had carried me through Vash's training, through the loss of my family, through every challenge this new reality had thrown at me.

I would not die here. I would not fail this trial.

I would learn to craft, to manipulate matter itself through the Flux. I would create the tools I needed to survive. And when I emerged from this trial, I would be something new.

Something stronger.

Something that could breathe fire and speak with the voice of authority itself.

I opened the book to the first lesson and began to read, my damaged lungs working overtime to keep me conscious long enough to save my own life.
