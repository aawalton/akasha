There’s a saying on the internet: “No beating, no improvement.” It refers to the kind of headache-inducing troublemaker who causes problems for everyone, deviates from social norms, and only comes to their senses after getting punched—suddenly becoming a reasonable person you can actually talk to.

Jinyo sat cross-legged inside the cottage he had summoned with the Realm of Paradise. It wasn’t exactly pleasant—the walls were riddled with holes from the Copper Spear and Hydro Bullet impacts. He hoped this cottage didn’t share its damage with the real one back home, or the first thing he’d have to do upon returning was repair it by hand.

Across from him, Esther sat clutching his sword, refusing to let it leave his side—as though it were the last lifeline that could save him in a crisis. His attention wasn’t focused on the monster-infested surroundings. To him, none of the monsters were a threat.

“So basically, if we want to deal with the fog, we need to find fuel to light torches around the forest?”

“Yes. If anyone finds a torch, it’ll help guide people to the same location. Our problem is communication. Once we can contact each other, we’ll reduce the death toll.”

“Got it. I’ll cooperate then.”

“Really?” Why was this man suddenly so easy to talk to?! A moment ago they’d been trying to kill each other.

“Don’t get the wrong idea. I don’t trust you one bit. I’m only cooperating for the safety of the others. If you pull anything, I’ll gut you.” Esther narrowed his eyes at Jinyo—the mass of dark roots and that nauseating aura. Even being just one tea table apart made Esther squirm with revulsion.

“Esther, what do I look like to you?”

“Something that’s definitely not human.”

“At least describe it for me!”

“A black mass of woody tendrils standing on two legs. Revolting just being near it. Happy now?!” Esther snapped.

“…..” Jinyo’s face went rigid. The word “revolting” echoed in his skull until he couldn’t help but hang his head. His sudden dejection summoned the stout little potato-man with a hoe, who immediately began whacking Esther.

“Potato! Po-Potato!” Salty whipped out a sheet of paper and scrawled: [The boss already has low self-esteem. If he develops depression, how are you going to take responsibility, you thug?!]

“He badgered me into answering! I was just telling the truth!” Esther swatted the Copper Hoe away. What the hell was wrong with this demon?! Savage in a fight, but why was he so fragile in conversation? Was he some kind of split personality?! He couldn’t handle anything! Was his heart made of glass? One word—“revolting”—and he was devastated? Was he a little girl or something?!

“Black woody tendrils… revolting… Salty, do I really look that ugly?” Jinyo turned to his potato companion.

“Potato!” Salty hurriedly scribbled on the paper: [Not at all! The boss is quite handsome—with a hundred wives and a thousand concubines! If you’re revolting, then every man on earth is a flea!]

“Well, compared to me, maybe not that much.”

“…” Jinyo.

“…” Salty.

“What? Did I say something wrong?”

[Your character really doesn’t seem like the narcissistic type, you know.] Salty shook its head. A high-pitched whistle came from the kitchen. Jinyo walked over and found the Celestial Rice nearly done. He ladled steaming rice into a plate and served it before Esther, who was suspicious to the extreme—but the moment the aroma of the rice hit him, his resolve wavered.

“Smells good, doesn’t it? This is rice I grew myself. Go on, eat.”

“I don’t want demon food! I’d sooner eat dog food than eat what you’ve made!”

GRRROWL.

“…” Esther fell silent. Salty scampered off to one of the cottages and returned with a yellow package.

[Dog food. Want some?]

“Damn it.” That traitorous stomach! Making him look pathetic in front of the enemy!

“It’s not poisoned. Trust me—it’s good stuff.” Jinyo coaxed Esther to try it. Esther steeled himself, but the fragrance of the Celestial Rice was too irresistible. He nearly held out—until Jinyo pulled a boiled egg and rice seasoning from his bag. That was the last straw. The young man grabbed a spoon and devoured it with relish.

Despite having no meat or side dishes—just a single soft-boiled egg and a packet of rice seasoning worth less than a coin—Esther’s defenses crumbled completely.

How humiliating. Truly shameful—eating a demon’s food!

But did demon food really have to be this delicious?

It was just plain rice and seasoning. There wasn’t even a proper side dish!

“Seconds, please!”

“Sorry, but there’s no more. Unless you help me with one thing.”

“Hmph. I knew it—this was the demon’s trap all along. Lure me in with delicious food and then work me like a slave. You think a few bowls of rice can buy me?” Esther flashed a sneer. Whatever scheme you’ve got, it’s all within my calculations, you wicked demon!

“Finish the job and you get a whole pot. Are you sure you want to pass that up?” Jinyo dangled the bait.

“…”

“This was just plain rice, you know. There are dishes far more delicious—sweet and sour pork, fish-sauce marinated eggs, stir-fried mixed vegetables, baked potato with cheese. Imagine eating all that with this rice. Doesn’t that interest you?”

[Absolutely incredible! So good you’d beg for your life, I’m telling you! If you don’t agree now, who knows when you’ll get another chance in this lifetime!] Salty channeled its inner salesman, backing up its master’s pitch. Esther’s face cycled between red and green. He was waging war against himself.

‘Don’t waver, Esther. It’s just food! Is your dignity really that cheap?’

‘You’re not poor. You can eat well whenever you want.’

‘Don’t disgrace the family name!’ Esther steeled his resolve. Then Jinyo set down another bowl of rice topped with steamed egg drizzled in soy sauce. Esther’s eyelid twitched. Was this some kind of trial? His limit was being tested.

Fine! He would accept that challenge. But what Esther didn’t expect was that Jinyo hadn’t placed the rice and steamed egg for him at all. It was slid over to Salty—the stout little potato-man in an apron, fork and spoon at the ready, prepared to feast. This magical creature born of sorcery was going to eat the very thing Esther had been resisting with all his willpower?

Salty shot him a sidelong glance. It opened its mouth, baring tiny little teeth, and raised a spoonful of Celestial Rice in exaggerated slow motion. Even knowing it was a trap—a provocation—still…

Esther scrawled his name on the employment contract. Jinyo and Salty high-fived each other. Esther received a large bowl of Celestial Rice and three steamed eggs. He’d spent an entire day in the forest on an empty stomach.

The contract stipulated that Esther would heed Jinyo’s requests. Esther was to cooperate and protect the lives and safety of Jinyo’s family. Esther would not be permitted to betray or backstab, nor do anything that endangered Jinyo’s life. The contract would terminate only upon leaving the Hollow Gate.

It was a guarantee that Jinyo would have a Divine Punishment wielder as an ally. Even though Esther seemed amenable now, there was no telling when he might turn on Jinyo—especially given that bounty hunters were notoriously difficult to trust. If Esther used Divine Punishment against Jinyo again, Jinyo might lose.

Jinyo learned more about the Realm of Paradise’s power. When he had summoned the cottage during the fight, it had been pure accident—like lifting an impossibly heavy urn during a house fire out of sheer desperation. If the Realm of Paradise truly replicated the Garden of the Nameless God, then it should be able to deploy facilities just the same.

This cottage had every piece of furniture and function identical to the one in the Garden of the Nameless God. He pulled out the product catalog and spotted an updated listing. One item caught his eye.

[(5,000 Spirit Coins) Iron Fiber Reinforcement (E): Strengthens plant fibers to the hardness of iron. Skill efficiency equals Planar + Psychic Power × Skill Level]

An expensive but fascinating spell tome. Jinyo bookmarked it so it wouldn’t disappear when the catalog reset. The problem was that his account balance was severely depleted from using Nature’s Creation. He had only 4,600 Spirit Coins left.

Jinyo wasn’t in the Garden of the Nameless God. How was he supposed to sell agricultural products?

‘Wait—if I really can do everything in this Domain…’

Once Esther had eaten his fill, he looked thoroughly satisfied. It was just plain rice with steamed egg and a few drops of soy sauce—yet his soul felt more fulfilled than any Michelin three-star meal. Fine dining? Omakase? All nonsense.

Esther watched Jinyo’s every move. The potato-man ran off to collect the severed Eltharok heads that Esther had lopped off. They were the only proof of how massive and fearsome the Eltharok had been in life—because once dead, their bodies withered into desiccated husks as their remaining lifespan drained away.

“What are you doing?”

“Turning trash into something useful.” Jinyo focused and opened the system window. A wooden crate the size of a communal dumpster materialized from thin air, just like every other structure. Esther wasn’t surprised anymore—until Jinyo opened the lid and hurled every last Eltharok head inside like dumping garbage. The mysterious crate was a bottomless pit that never seemed to fill.

Jinyo poured in all the Cursed Fruit he’d collected along the way, keeping only those with desirable genetic traits.

How fortunate that he had a way to earn more Spirit Coins! If this operated like a farming game, foraged goods should fetch decent prices—just like real agricultural products, right?

Jinyo set off with Esther. The moment the Realm of Paradise was dispelled, Nature Synthesis lost its full efficiency. Openings in Jinyo’s defenses that Esther could exploit appeared everywhere. But Esther merely kept his distance—roughly fifty paces—and refused to come within a hundred meters.

“Scared?”

“I’m not scared!”

“If you’re not scared, walk closer.”

“I have no desire to walk near a revolting root-creature.” Esther smirked, deliberately emphasizing the word “revolting.” Salty heard it and charged over with a flying kick, but a potato-man’s strength was no match for a superhuman like Esther. Sidekick versus hero—the outcome was obvious.

‘Salty’s warming up to Esther fast. That’s nice!’ Jinyo himself was used to Naho running up and kicking him in much the same way.

Along the way, Jinyo didn’t miss a single Cursed Fruit, stuffing them all into the Gardener’s Bag. There was no sign of Cursed Villagers or Eltharok. Jinyo pressed on until he reached the campsite under Nephrin’s watch. His sense of direction was excellent—even without a map, finding his way back was no trouble. A skill honed from hiking expeditions during his university days.

Jinyo spotted the hastily built fire used to repel the fog. The visibility radius had expanded considerably—enough to notice that the group had swelled from dozens to several hundred. He didn’t see Nephrin, but he found Gretel and Luciria sitting by a fire beneath a tree. He hurried toward his son, but Gretel spotted his father first.

“Big Bro Yo!” Gretel ran over and threw his arms around Jinyo’s legs. This was the first time since they’d met that his son had been this affectionate. Jinyo didn’t know what to do with himself, but the joy was impossible to hide.

“I’m back. Everything’s okay now.”

“Your little brother?” Esther squinted at the seven-year-old boy. Cute face, endearing features. Strangely, even though Esther didn’t like children, he felt an inexplicable closeness to this one.

“My son, actually.”

“Does he look like you?”

“He’s practically my clone.”

“Oh… then I win.”

“???” Jinyo and Salty’s eyes twitched. This guy’s narcissism was beyond saving.

Jinyo unpacked the Yang Na heartwood and resin from the Gardener’s Bag. He borrowed a knife from someone and began cutting, shaving, and fashioning simple torches. No one would believe that a tree found everywhere in the forest could serve as first-rate fuel that burned for hours.

Nephrin had gone out searching for survivors once she found exam candidates trustworthy enough to guard the camp. The group had now split into two factions: those who followed Nephrin and those who didn’t. It was clear that she’d been building influence in Jinyo’s absence.

Jinyo didn’t mind. It was better, actually—it spared him the hassle of coordinating communications. If he could avoid socializing entirely, all the better. He could dedicate his time to gathering resources and searching for his family.

How were Naho, Rina, and Rice doing right now? The more torches he made, the greater the chance of finding them. Unfortunately, the Yang Na trees he’d harvested weren’t enough. It would have been ideal if he could get past the large herd of dozens of Eltharok. For now, all he could do was wait for Nephrin to return and discuss relocating from the camp to the abandoned village.

Jinyo kept a low profile, not wanting to draw attention. But fate, it seemed, refused to grant that wish.

“You lunatic! Don’t you dare stop me!”

“You’ve lost your mind! Don’t cause trouble for Lady Nephrin’s guests!”

“Let go of me! I’m going to get my stuff back!” A young man in his early twenties with a shaved head was causing a scene. Some tried to restrain him, but the feral aggression radiating off him—as though he’d bite anyone who got in his way—kept everyone at a distance. He marched straight toward Jinyo. But when he actually stood face to face with him, he was far calmer than expected.

“Doctor—the fruit you confiscated. Can you give it back?”

“That fruit is extremely dangerous. I’d stake my PhD on it—that stuff will shorten your lifespan.”

“I don’t care! I just want it! You don’t have any, do you? Because you ate it all yourself. You hoarded everything we gave you for your own use. You’re disgusting! Give it back!” The shaved-head man’s eyes blazed red, his Planar surging wildly. He was winding up to punch Jinyo when someone stepped in.

“I can smell junkies. Piss off.” Esther rose and planted a kick squarely in the man’s chest, launching him a good ten meters with a boot he was rather proud of.

“Hey! Don’t use violence on people!”

“So? I’ve never counted junkies as people anyway.” Esther used his power-sight on the shaved-head man, just as he’d done with Jinyo. The man’s criminal record was arguably more interesting than Jinyo’s—criminal charges that included both homicide and narcotics.

“You two—both of you!” The shaved-head man staggered up and pointed at Jinyo, then Esther. One was the man who’d saved Nephrin’s life. The other was a stranger—but the aura he exuded instinctively warned the man not to pick this fight.

“What about us two? Looking for trouble? I happen to be in a bad mood.” Esther flashed a wicked grin, gripping his Ether Blade as he advanced. The shaved-head man scrambled backward on all fours in a pitiful retreat, screaming that he was being murdered.

“Haah.” Jinyo covered his face with his hand. This was exactly what he’d feared. Esther was someone Jinyo had brought along—how would the other survivors look at him now?

‘This is far worse than I thought.’ The Cursed Fruit’s [Addictive] property was forcing everyone to consume it periodically. The more they ate, the more they craved. If they couldn’t overcome it early, the spiral would only deepen until there was no coming back. How was he going to solve this?
