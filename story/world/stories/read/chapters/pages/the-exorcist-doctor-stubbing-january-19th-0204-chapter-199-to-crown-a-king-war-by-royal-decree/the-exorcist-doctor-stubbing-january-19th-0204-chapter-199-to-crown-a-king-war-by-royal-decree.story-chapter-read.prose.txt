Gael had always thought there were few sights in Bharncair more reassuring than several hundred six-armed gangsters behaving with absolute confidence, but as he and Maeve chased the colossal remipede across the waters of Wraithpier, he was beginning to revise that opinion upward.

Below them, the canals had become veins clogged with Saint’s Hands ships. Armored cutters tore around corners with their cannons swinging from one side to the other, wooden hulls clipping piers and scraping walls as they tried to keep pace with the giant Myrmur rampaging through the ward. Children from Miss Umbrage’s clinic were scattered among the crews, screaming directions at grown gangsters twice their size because they knew these waterways better than any navigator Fergal could hire, and somewhere above all of them, Evelyn circled on her wings like one extremely foul-mouthed carrion bird, directing the entire chase from the sky. Gael had to admit it was beautiful in a very Bharnish way. A giant bug was demolishing half of the fishing ward, hundreds of men were pursuing it with artillery, and yet the children were integral to their battle logistics. Civilization had peaked.

He landed hard on another slanted roof, skidding over wet shingles before his setae caught, and Maeve touched down beside him with considerably more grace. Jin and Vivi landed just behind them. The remipede was still several streets ahead, its segmented body disappearing between waves and buildings every few seconds as it crashed from canal to canal, but now that they had its direction somewhat under control with occasional volleys of artillery shells, it was time for the part Vivi had proposed back in the Drowned Parish.

Jin gave Maeve a short nod. Maeve returned it. No speech was necessary—they’d all discussed enough while running. So Jin and Vivi spread their wings, changing direction towards the western canals, and Gael watched them peel away for a second before raising his cane and shouting at every Saint’s Hand vessel close enough to hear him.

“Drive the bug to Gravesend Deep!”

Several gangsters yelled acknowledgements from below, though Gael suspected half of them were simply yelling because everyone else was. Evelyn heard him, at least. She banked overhead and immediately began screaming corrections at the fleet, sending one row of cutters down an eastern waterway while another group pushed farther south. “Four through nine, swing around and hammer its left side! Twelve, quit followin’ the bastard’s arse and get ahead of it! Twenty-two, if you put another shell through someone’s kitchen instead of the Myrmur, I’m comin’ down there myself and putting you through the kitchen!”

Her wings buzzed loudly enough that Gael could hear them even over the cannons, and he smiled as another volley crashed into the remipede’s flank. The giant Myrmur recoiled, turned away from the explosions, and thundered down precisely the canal they wanted.

Liorin was playing his part too, although in the way Liorin did most things, he simply kept appearing in places no one reasonably expected him to be. One moment Gael saw nothing except fleeing civilians and speeding ships, and the next, the tribal boy was lying flat on some half-submerged walkway with both arms plunged elbow-deep into filthy canal water. Massive ropes of seaweed and mutated aquatic vines then burst up farther ahead, their surfaces hardening into metallic grey under his Art before whipping around the remipede’s legs. They never held the creature for more than a second or two, but a second or two was enough for volleys of shells to explode in its face, making it swerve, scream, and twist towards a different route.

Gael had no idea how the boy navigated Wraithpier so quickly. He also had no idea how Fergal was now holding twelve giant cannons in six arms on his own cutter, allowing the gangster to correct his aim far better and far faster than any other ship.

He’s gotten disgustingly strong, huh?

Maybe it’s time to do our annual spar again.

But their ‘annual spar’ was a fine tradition in which Gael drank until confidence overwhelmed memory, challenged Fergal to a fight, and got his ass punted through several walls, so maybe not.

As they continued ferreting the giant remipede towards Gravesend Deep—the first deepwater lake they’d tried their hands at reeling up a Myrmur—Gael and Maeve continued hopping alongside the giant remipede, and Gael didn’t stop pumping blood into Maeve through their bloodshackle. She’d been accumulating it and concentrating bioarcanic essence into her blood for several minutes now, preparing for what would come once the remipede was properly restrained, and Gael kept glancing at her complexion between jumps. She was still steady. Still breathing properly. Still very ‘Maeve’ about the whole thing, which meant she’d probably continue saying she was fine until her organs filed a formal complaint.

They were almost there. Gravesend Deep was only a few more canals ahead—when the colossal remipede abruptly stopped cooperating with them.

Its giant head reared. Its oversized antennae flicked. Then the entire creature twisted around and charged south, heading away from the mouth of the lake just before they could push it in.

Shit.

Did it sense we were up to something?

The southern districts were densely packed. Homes were built practically on top of homes there, with floating markets wedged between bridges and entire families living over narrow waterways. If the remipede charged straight at them at full speed, people would die before the Saint’s Hands could maneuver around it.

Gael and Maeve accelerated. They pushed harder off the next roof, wings spreading wide enough to carry them over several buildings at once, but they were too far away to stop the remipede now. It’d already lowered its head towards the first line of buildings—but then three ships smashed through those buildings first, and five hatted men flew through the air, landing on top of the remipede’s head.

And then fishing happened.

Harpoons longer than Gael were driven beneath the armor plates. Chains wrapped around the antennae. Giant hooks caught along the jaw and neck. One anglerfish-hatted Fisherman threw a weighted net across the creature’s face while another buried a giant ship anchor into a carapace segment. The salt-hatted Fisherman stood nearest the head, bare feet planted against slick chitin as he hauled backwards on a chain and roared.

“HOOK THE SAINT’S ARSE AND DRAG ‘ER WHERE THE TIDE DON’T WANT ‘ER!”

Gael had absolutely no idea what that meant, but the other two dozen Fishermen who appeared out of nowhere, crashed their ships into the remipede’s sides, and hopped onto the remipede’s back immediately began singing.

“Heave ‘er left, ye drowned old whore! Heave ‘er left and break the shore! Hook ‘ey eye and cut ‘ey fin—if she bites, ye bite and bite again!”

Their singing was atrocious. Their technique, however, was excellent. All three dozen or so Fishermen leaned back together, using the remipede’s own momentum and the network of chains, anchors, harpoons, and nets hooked around its head to wrench it away from the southern district. The creature screamed, smashed one Fisherman against a chimney, failed to dislodge him, and abruptly turned back towards Gravesend Deep.

Gael began laughing again.

With the Fishermen steering its head, Evelyn controlling the fleet from above, Liorin periodically binding its legs, and Fergal firing volley upon volley of shells at it from the side, the remipede finally entered Gravesend Deep, and the lake opened around all of them. Saint’s Hands vessels poured in after the creature from every connecting canal, spreading out along the perimeter while the Fishermen finally abandoned their chains and started hacking, slashing, and stabbing at its hard carapace. It was a fruitless effort, but Gael let them have their try for a bit as he and Maeve landed on Fergal’s ship to take a breather.

“Running… and gliding like that,” he muttered, “remains… an unforgivable design flaw… in the human body.”

Maeve leaned one hand against the railing beside him. “If you exercised occasionally instead of drinking whenever you had free time, perhaps it wouldn’t feel so terrible.”

“Alcohol is internal exercise. My liver’s extremely fit.”

Before she could answer, Gael looked towards the center of Gravesend Deep and forgot about his exhaustion for a while, before one Saint’s Hands cutter sat there alone.

Jin and Vivi were aboard. Vivi was seated in the middle of the deck with her eyes closed and both hands around their bloodshackle. Green light pulsed through her bloodshackle in steady waves as she fed blood into Jin, and she looked deathly pale already. Jin, meanwhile, lay flat along the side of the vessel with both arms plunged beneath the water to his shoulders.

The lake around their ship was cloudy. Muddy. And exactly right.

… This has gotta work.

The colossal remipede saw them, of course. One ship, two Exorcists, no visible artillery, and absolutely nowhere for them to jump to. Easy prey. So the giant creature screamed and surged at them, making Gael grip his cane so tightly his knuckles ached.

Come on, Jin.

It crossed half the lake.

Come on, Jin.

Closer.

Vivi’s eyes finally snapped open as the giant remipede entered the fifty meter radius of their ship, and she shouted, “Now!”

Jin activated his Art.

The effect began underwater, but Gael felt it from across the lake. Bioarcanic essence surged out through the blood Jin had already dispersed through Gravesend Deep, spreading across the waterlogged sediment beneath the surface before hardening all at once. Almost instantly, the lake around the ship transformed from water and loose silt into thick, brutally resistant mud—which meant the giant remipede hit it at full speed and stopped abruptly, unable to move.

Its front legs sank in the mud first. Then dozens more. Before long, half its body was stuck into the hardened muck while only its upper segments and armored head remained exposed above the surface. It started thrashing alright, trying to break out of the muddy lake, but every movement only drove more of its legs into the mud.

Gael’s grin returned.

Oh, that is filthy.

Vivi stood first, though swaying badly from blood loss, and fired several rounds of explosive bioarcanic bullets at the remipede’s antennae. The rounds burst along its head, blowing one of its antennae off while blowing huge chunks in the other, severely disrupting the Myrmur’s senses. Now it was probably blind in addition to being trapped, allowing all sixty Saint’s Hands ships to surround it in a wide circle once more. Even Gael’s enormous floating fortress finally lumbered back in through the widest canal opposite the remipede, with Cara at the helm and all fifteen cannons turned towards the center of the lake.

Gael’s excitement lasted until he noticed the large-hatted Fishermen still poking and stabbing at the remipede’s back, so he cupped his hands around his mouth and shouted as loud as his lungs could manage.

“OI! GET THE FUCK OFF THE BUG!”

The Fisherman looked over in his general direction, so he pointed furiously at the sixty ships aiming their cannons at all of them.

With no hesitation, the Fishermen immediately launched off the remipede’s back, onto the hardened mud, and sprinted away with their giant weapons bouncing behind them. Jin and Vivi also did the same, running in the opposite direction—just in time as Fergal roared his favorite command once again.

“FIRE!”

Gravesend Deep disappeared under thunder. The first volley smashed into the giant creature and cracked several plates. The second tore an entire segment of its carapace loose. Then another volley followed, and another, and another, until there was no longer any meaningful pause between explosions. The remipede screamed continuously. Its armor was so, so much thicker than the eel’s hide had been, so they simply kept firing, emptying their shell reserves and hammering the trapped Myrmur until the entire lake became nothing but a giant cloud of smoke.

Eventually, Gael stopped trying to comprehend the explosions and looked at Maeve once more. His dearest wife was shaking. Glowing green blood shone through the veins and arteries under her skin, every vessel packed with concentrated bioarcanic toxin, so he reached over and took her hand before she could even pretend she was perfectly comfortable.

She looked at him with trembling lips for a few moments before nodding.

Once the artillery stopped—and not by command, but because they’d run out of shells to fire—the giant cloud of smoke started to peel away from the lake, revealing a giant remipede still trapped in the mud. But now it had almost no armor at all. Raw flesh covered its exposed body, and beneath the bloody anatomy Gael could still recognize the original leviathan eel twisted into the mutation. Its carapace was trying to regenerate and envelop the eel once more, but slowly. The relentless bombardment had drained whatever bioarcanic reserves powered the regeneration.

Seeing that, Maeve let go of his hand and jumped.

Her serrated wings opened and carried her high over the lake, allowing her to stabilize and hover in the air as she gripped Mistrender with both hands. Every safety latch was released along the shaft of her umbrella, and just to let her control the recoil better, Gael also transferred half his physical attribute levels into her.

Mistrender began glowing green. Then brighter, and brighter, and brighter, as toxic blood flooded the weapon in quantities Gael would probably have to classify as a total toxic meltdown if it were ever released in any other situation.

The remipede sensed her. It had no antennae, and almost no functioning eyes, yet bloodlust required none of those, because it started going berserk one last time. It screamed and tore against Jin’s mud with everything it had left, trying to drag itself free, but…

Too late.

Maeve fired her cannon, and a vast eruption of toxic green blood swallowed the exposed remipede from almost head to tail. Flesh hissed, muscles dissolved, and the original eel body beneath the Blight-Class Myrmur began melting alongside the Myrmur. Everyone around the lake shielded their faces from the sheer toxic air that came from her attack, and even Gael flinched away, his eyes watering under his mask.

The remipede screamed, then screamed less and less until it eventually—with a pitiful cry and wail—stopped screaming at all.

Maeve dropped from the sky, and Gael caught her before she could hit the cutter, though the impact still flattened both of them and sent them sprawling across the deck.

“Got you,” he muttered.

She leaned heavily against him, and for several seconds, they simply watched the giant Myrmur carcass melt into the mud.

… It’s not a normal Myrmur Host, is it?

An S-Rank Blight-Class Myrmur this large couldn’t possibly have existed undetected unless it was only made very recently, and made for a very specific purpose:

To fuck with us.

He couldn’t help but grin a little as he helped Maeve and himself climb onto their feet, now watching the mud around the lake soften and sink into the water.

You’re nervous about us, Breeder.

You’re scared of us, aren’t you?

More and more civilian ships began creeping into the lake, and crowds packed the shores and platforms surrounding the lake. People climbed onto roofs for a better view of the dead leviathan. Even the Fishermen had gathered atop slabs of floating debris all around, their huge hats turned towards the giant chunks of bioarcanic remains.

Gael took Maeve’s hand and led her onto the bowsprit, pushing past Fergal and his eighteen cannons as they did so they could lift their hands in unison.

“... WRAITHPIER!” he shouted. “ARE YOU NOT ENTERTAINED?”

The whole ward went quiet for perhaps one heartbeat.

Then it exploded with screams, cheers, bells and drums and cannons firing useless celebratory blanks somewhere in the distance. Fishermen raised hooks overhead while civilians on the shore began pounding cups against railings and chanting incoherently, so Gael started laughing as well, jabbing his cane at the dead leviathan floating behind him.

“There’s absolutely no chance anybody catches a bigger bastard than that before the festival ends, right?” he roared. “So I think it’s safe to say I’m officially the King of Wraithpier for the next fishing season, right?”

The Fishermen scattered on floating debris around him looked at each other for a moment—shared a few quiet whispers—before all of them tipped their enormous hats at him.

““Aye, ye caught the biggest ugly fucker fair enough!” the salt-hatted Fisherman cheered.

Gael grinned even harder.

“Excellent! Then as the newly crowned King of Wraithpier, I’m giving the Fishermen their first royal order:” he lifted his cane and pointed north, “Get every seaworthy ship you have ready and sail all of us into the northern sea right now! We’re gonna beat the shit out of the Breeder by the end of the day!”
