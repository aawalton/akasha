Seraphine Ilex stood silently in the air, staring intently at her opponent, crystal-blue eyes searching him for some sign of their last battle. A scar, a fracture—anything at all to suggest that her efforts that day hadn’t been in vain.

She found none.

He was whole. Physically and spiritually. Even his path seemed cleaner now—smoother, more complete. There were very few fractures left to widen, which meant that if she wanted to defeat him this time, she would have to create new ones.

Lasting ones.

Her gaze met his across the distance of shuddering space, and she grimaced. “You’ve grown stronger.”

“As have you,” Sophran replied, his expression calm.

Too calm.

She regarded him for another moment before glancing toward the other transmigrants, many of whom had already begun fighting their own battles.

“You know, we’re supposed to fight in formation,” she said. “Watch each other’s backs.” She looked at him again. “But I don’t think I’ll be doing that. Your regeneration is too fast for distractions.”

Sophran smiled faintly. “Is that a compliment?”

Seraphine’s lips curved just a little. “It could be.” Then, from behind him, another voice echoed.

“It could also be your doom.”

Sophran spun, lightning already sparking at his fingertips, but it was too late. The reflection drove a searing blade into his chest, just barely missing his heart.

Sophran forced himself backward, eyes widening as blood burst from the wound.

Ancestral Continuance activated almost immediately, vitality surging through him to heal the damage, but while the bleeding slowed, the wound itself refused to close.

His flesh remained torn, tissues split and gaping.

Nothing knit.

Nothing regenerated.

Shocked, he glanced at the blade that had just stabbed him. It was dull silver, utterly devoid of shine or reflection, its edges too clean.

Too perfect.

Along its surface, black runes glowed softly, drinking in the light around them.

“Beautiful, isn’t it?”

Seraphine’s voice came from behind him once more, and when he turned, her smile had sharpened into something crueler, carrying the same edge as the blade that had pierced him.

“It’s my new toy. I got it just for you.” She raised the weapon slowly, pointing it directly at him. “It’s called a severance blade.”

At the same time, the reflection behind him mirrored the motion exactly.

Then, like the surface of a disturbed pond, the world rippled as dozens of reflections emerged all around him, each wielding the same accursed blade.

One by one, they pointed their swords.

And as one, they spoke. “Let’s see how well you can regenerate, shall we?”

—Resonant Scission—

Sophran’s eyes widened. There was no time to think. Or hesitate.

Only act.

Power erupted from his core as Living Convergence activated, branching tendrils of living storm exploding from his body to lash downward and anchor themselves onto the Mythics below.

Strength poured into his spirit like rivers flooding a starving sea. Power crashed through him like a shattered dam and then—

Pain.

White-hot agony screamed through him as a dozen scissions struck at once, ripping through skin and bone as easily as knives through wet paper.

Blood burst. Tissues tore, and bones cracked.

The agony was indescribable. But not all of it was physical.

While dozens of scissions carved through his body, even more sliced directly into his spirit, opening new fractures and fault lines for Seraphine to exploit. Sophran desperately tried to heal himself, vitality billowing out of him in immense waves, but despite the power surging through his body, the injuries refused to close.

“Well, that was easy,” Seraphine said, her smile downturning in visible disappointment. “I thought you’d grown stronger, but it seems I was wrong.” She tilted her head slightly. “Maybe I’ll partake in that formation after all.”

Sophran groaned, teeth gritted against the pain as his mind raced through alternatives.

Any attack aimed at her would simply be reflected. And Unbound Sky wouldn’t protect his spirit from the scissions.

He needed a way to fight her. A way to win with the skills he already possessed.

But when Seraphine raised her sword once more, Sophran realized he had no other alternatives.

He had hoped to keep Halo out of the battlefield for at least another week, but with the situation as it was, he no longer had a choice.

Neither did he think he could survive the next volley.

Eyes narrowing with resolve, he whispered, “Bless me with the undying perk, Halo.” His lips pulled into a thin line as he lifted his gaze toward Seraphine. “And curse this woman with something nasty.”

Halo hummed excitedly within his spirit, and just as Seraphine unleashed the next wave of scissions, blinding light engulfed him.

It was so bright that the world itself seemed to lose color.

And then—

Congratulations: You have been blessed with the undying perk. Your entire being remains at a point of maximum health for the next five hours.

The scissions struck a heartbeat later, but they may as well have been feathers brushing against his skin for all Sophran cared. Neither his body nor spirit took any injury.

He was whole. Perfect. Practically immortal.

And when Halo burst from his spirit to curse Seraphine, his smile widened into something vicious.

Seraphine may have gotten herself a powerful blade. But he’d gotten himself a familiar with the ability to bless and curse.

He’d gotten himself a god.

And he was done being the one standing on the brink of death.

***

Seraphine shielded her gaze from the blinding light.

That was her only mistake.

Even while slicing Sophran apart, she had remained calm. Controlled. Her mind kept track of his abilities—analyzing. Cataloguing. Always cataloguing, never resting.

That was her true strength. Her greatest weapon.

And she had prepared herself carefully. She’d expected new skills from Sophran. Expected him to make the battle as difficult as possible with his monstrous regeneration.

What she had not expected was for him to erupt suddenly in a flash of blinding light.

And in that instant, she made a reflexive mistake.

She shielded her gaze. And missed the glowing fox that burst from his spirit.

By the time she noticed it, the creature was already too close, its ghostly body phasing effortlessly her glass shields before brushing its paws lightly against her head.

Seraphine reacted instantly, switching places with a distant reflection, but the fox had already touched her.

And a moment later, a line of text crawled across her vision.

Sorrows! You have been cursed by a Vaelryn. You lose access to a single skill for the next five hours.

Chosen skill: Reflect.

“What—”

Her eyes widened in shock—then horror, as the implication hit her all at once.

She looked toward Sophran, but before she could react, pain exploded through her body as a punch sent her hurtling across the battlefield.

She slammed into the earth with enough force to crater the ground beneath her. The attack had been so fast that she hadn’t even thought to activate Body of Glass.

Pain wracked her entire body.

But when she forced her eyes open and saw Sophran floating above her, his expression twisted with utter malice, she realized the pain had only just begun.

And she was right.

An instant later, the crater beneath her collapsed as another blow drove her deeper into the earth, the shockwave tearing violently across the battlefield.

She raised a hand desperately, conjuring a cascade of jagged glass shards. But even as she sent them hurtling upward, she already knew that any chance of winning this battle had been taken away alongside Reflect.

It didn’t matter what she threw at Sophran. He would simply regenerate from it.

Still, she couldn’t afford to give up barely five minutes into the battle. And so, she conjured another cascade of jagged glass. Then another, energy churning wildly around her as she fought desperately to bring her undying enemy to his knees.

But Seraphine wasn’t the only one fighting a desperate battle.

Far beyond the massive crater her body had formed, in a region where space warped and sunlight burned in blinding fury, Ariana Solis waged a desperate war.

But despite the blazing crown of brilliance around her, she was not smiling.

Instead, she was bleeding.

***

Master Gyatso looked down at his injured opponent, a faint trace of sympathy lingering in his gaze—even though he knew it was not an emotion he should have been harboring.

He had no stake in this war.

Or at least, four days ago, he hadn’t.

But Aodhán’s presence within the Storm Sect had changed that. The boy was no longer merely a friend—or even a peer.

He was a son.

Gyatso had practically raised him, and though the truth surrounding his mother’s fate and identity had strained their relationship, Gyatso still loved the boy as his own.

Which meant there should have been no room for sympathy.

And yet—

Gyatso believed his opponent had been sent against him to die.

Not because she was weak. Far from it.

The battlefield practically burned with her presence, sunlight pouring across fractured earth in waves of relentless brilliance. Heat warped the air into shimmering distortions, and every attack she unleashed carried enough destructive force to reduce entire armies to ash.

She was overwhelmingly powerful.

Like the sun itself.

A blazing existence dangerous to everything around her simply by existing.

But all of that power was… misplaced.

Gyatso stood at the center of the devastation, unmoving, his gaze as serene as the surface of a still lake as he watched Ariana Solis pull herself from the crater he had embedded her in, fury marring her beautiful features.

She rose slowly into the air with a sneer, light gathering around her in a blazing crown while blood traced a thin line down her arm, evaporating almost the instant it formed.

Her eyes locked onto him.

And this time, she didn’t speak. The cheerfulness had all but drained from her soul.

Her sneer deepened, and with an overwhelming surge of energy—far more than anything Gyatso was certain she could truly control—she fired a lance of condensed solar plasma toward him.

Faster than before. Denser. Hotter.

The air screamed, reality itself seeming to burn as the attack tore through it.

Yet Gyatso refused to move.

Arms folded behind his back, he watched the attack approach with impossible calm. And then, very slowly—

He tilted his head.

He used no skill. No ability. He simply gestured, and space itself gave way, folding inward to swallow the attack whole.

Just like that, all of Ariana’s effort vanished into nothingness.

Ariana stared at him in disbelief, shock flickering openly across her face, and Gyatso could not blame her.

Cultivating the path of Spatial Supremacy meant he was, quite literally, a god within his own domain of space. It did not matter how much energy Ariana poured into her attacks, how much hotter she burned, or how brightly she blazed—

none of it could touch him.

Hence his sympathy.

Ariana, however, seemed to despise that sympathy, and the sight of it only caused her fury to burn hotter.

“You old weasel!” she snarled, her body transforming into a streak of burning gold as she crossed the distance between them in an instant, a blade of condensed plasma forming within her hand.

She swung, her movement nearly as swift as light itself—

—and struck empty space.

Gyatso stood two meters to the side. Without ever appearing to move.

Her blade carved through the air where he had once been, the fabric of space rippling strangely, as though the attack itself had been… misplaced.

No. Redirected.

Gyatso exhaled softly. Then, with only the slightest beat of hesitation, he raised a finger and squeezed.

Space tightened instantly, reality itself twisting into a noose around Ariana’s neck. It hardened. Sharpened.

And then—

Snap!

But as he had already acknowledged, Ariana was no weakling.

She surged forward, her body dissolving into a flare of light and plasma in a near-instant burst of speed. The noose shattered behind her as she escaped, reforming even as she moved. In one fluid motion, she raised her flaming sword and slashed downward in a streak of burning gold, the sheer force of the attack tearing at the very fabric of space.

The air hissed, oxygen igniting as surged to catastrophic heights.

No one in the advanced class, transmigrant or otherwise, could have survived such an attack.

Gyatso himself could not have survived it.

Yet he stood untouched amidst the destruction, the space between them stretching farther and farther apart, widening by hundreds of miles in the span of seconds.

“Fight me you old fool!” Ariana screamed as the futility of it all finally began to settle in. “Fight me!”

Draining three seals at once, she forced all that overwhelming power into Prominence lance and unleashed it—a devastating beam of raw unfiltered plasma exploding from her palms like a fragment of the sun itself.

Space folded in response, and rather than hit its target, the beam twisted away and crashed into a distant battalion of Solar and Blood Mythics, obliterating more than half of them in a single instant.

The earth caved beneath the impact, the surface melting to glass under the overwhelming heat.

Gyatso glanced back at her, expecting to see her shaken by the devastation. To rush forward in some desperate attempt to save them.

It would have been foolish, but I would have been the right thing to do.

The just thing.

At the very least, he had expected her to feel something. He had just killed hundreds of her allies with her own attack, after all.

But Ariana did not even look. She didn’t care at all. Her entire attention was focused on killing him.

And in that moment, Gyatso finally released what little sympathy he had still held for her.

He crossed the distance between them in an instant and raised a finger once more, space twisting again into a tightening noose around her throat. Ariana tried to teleport away, but Gyatso denied her utterly, his expression becoming a mask of immense disappointment and quiet judgment as he spoke.

“You saw their suffering… and chose not to act.” His voice was calm. Heavy. “They were yours to protect. They were your comrades.”

“And you are mine to kill,” Ariana snarled, her body burning even brighter than before as she fought to escape the hardened space, power radiating from her in violent waves of crimson-gold light that tore reality itself to shreds.

But space refused to buckle.

And without hesitation, Gyatso closed his hand in a brutal command.

The world folded. Space sharpened, and Ariana’s body folded alongside it, bones shattering to pieces as her form twisted beyond what it was meant to endure.

Her scream tore across the battlefield in sharp agony.

But it did not last long.

With a single precise motion, Gyatso drew his hand across the air and split her cleanly in half. Her body fell like a dying star, streaking downward in a trail of fading light before slamming into the earth with catastrophic force.

The earth fractured beneath her, sunlight erupting outward in a violent surge that tore through everything in its path.

And yet—yet—Ariana rose again, broken bones and flesh knitting themselves back together almost instantly.

The light surrounding her flared once more, fierce and unyielding, blazing brighter and brighter as she ascended into the air again, fury burning openly across her features.

Gyatso watched in silence.

So much power. So much potential for catastrophic destruction. Yet so little compassion.

Killing her would be a mercy to the world. A mercy to all those who would die if she were allowed to grow any stronger.

He inhaled deeply, resolve hardening within him.

Ending her was no longer simply about protecting Aodhán. It was about protecting this world. And this time, this time, he would not merely split her in two.

He would grind her to powder.

He would act without hesitation, without attachment, and he would bear the sin of it.
