Ryland was a little taken aback by the priestess. If what he had stated to Yulkaviel had been bold and inflammatory, then this woman’s words were nearly outrageous. She was practically telling everyone that Ryland was the key to everything that had gone wrong with the last incident.

Not in so many words, of course. Even that was a little beyond her at the moment.

But how was she managing to speak up this much about it all in the first place? What was going on with Mavar’s Glyph of Unity? Shouldn’t it have prevented anyone from losing their faith in his system of governance and the controlling way of life he had erected on Rhel? Had it really weakened or was he allowing this on purpose in some sort of scheme?

Ryland couldn’t tell, if he was being honest. But perhaps, through his interactions with this woman, he could find out.

“I’m sorry,” he said. “But I’m not sure what you mean. Or rather, I do know what you mean, but I’m not sure if I can actually help despite that.”

“No, no!” She looked like she wanted to take his hands and shake them in her fervent belief. “The truth! That’s what you know, and that’s what you can assist us all with!”

“But you are aware of the truth, yes?” Ryland looked at her intently. “You know what’s going on. You willingly accept this as part of your life, as how your whole world is supposed to work, because you’ve surrendered yourself to it.”

She slowly understood what Ryland was getting at, which led to an unstable reaction. Her face morphed between anger and disappointment and even a little bit of reluctant remorse before she finally settled on a resolute look. “I can admit my faults. I know this is something that my entire society struggles with. But that doesn’t mean I can simply sit and bemoan my mistakes.”

It was Ryland’s turn to slowly nod. “How did you come to know me and my past? And why do you see me so favourably that you think I can help?”

He left the implications unsaid. It was a truth that his truth on Rhel had been suppressed by the Demiurge. That suppression had then been followed by the propaganda that had led to the current situation. For it was also true that most Rhellians no longer saw him in a good light, and would only trust him about as far as they could throw him.

“I am Wyla, a priestess of the Old Church,” the priestess said.

Ryland arched an eyebrow. “An Androvian refugee? Or rather, the descendent of one?”

“Of several. I can trace my lineage to several of them. But the point is that we know the truth.”

Ryland was suddenly reminded of the time he had met more of her fellow clergy at the Crypts. He recalled well how they had reacted to his presence. A subtly different reaction from how most of the rest of Rhel had acted upon seeing him.

“And how is it that you know the truth?” Ryland asked. “Have you been passing it down all this time?”

“I can show you if you come with me,” Wyla said. “We can go there right now. It’s said that you have that power, right? That you can travel wherever you wish, go whenever you want, with merely a thought.”

“That’s a bit of a reduction, but it isn’t a lie, yes.”

Ryland wasn’t busy with anything and was also intrigued by everything the woman had said. He didn’t recall leaving behind anyone who promised they would remember the past, so hearing that the truth of all his work wasn’t lost had him both surprised and very interested to find out more.

So, he accepted Wyla’s offer. With a bit of guidance from her on where to go, he ended up [Translocating] with her to their destination.

A cold wind blew at them when they arrived. Ryland cast [Oneself World] and turned everything back to an ideal atmosphere, which had the priestess staring at him a little. Something she had been doing since the moment they had rifted in.

“It seems the talk of your abilities was no misconception,” she said.

“You doubted my powers yet came to me all the same?” Ryland asked.

She shook her head. “I never doubted you, Marionys Ryland, but I hoped that I would find some proof for reassurance.”

Ryland nodded. They got going.

Their location wasn’t exactly at the Crypts, but it was in the general region. A dilapidated temple greeted them as they walked on, its windows dirty and grimy and its walls cracked and falling apart. There was no one else there at the moment.

Ryland felt a little dubious about the whole thing, perhaps just a tiny bit suspicious about where he was being dragged to, but they soon arrived at the specific spot Wyla had meant.

A small field behind the ruined temple held a large number of stelae. They stood apart from one another in a periodic arrangement like tombstones in a graveyard. She led him to each one, and at every single one, they both paused.

Ryland’s name was on them all.

Well, not Ryland specifically. Rather, the stelae spoke of him through the title that most of the old Rhellians had known him as—the Recreator.

“As you can see,” the priestess said. “History isn’t entirely wiped out. Some vestige of the truth still remains. It’s not at all what you deserve, for instead of forgotten little statuettes, you should be celebrated. But at least this is better than nothing.”

Ryland nodded. “This is surprising.”

Wyla let him take it in for a while. Ryland wasn’t so self-absorbed that he would spend overlong feeling anything in particular about records of his own deeds. But he was looking through them for a different reason.

Truth. That’s what the woman kept repeating. So, he confirmed that it was indeed the truth. Not some coloured or altered depiction of his actions and choices all those centuries ago.

Perhaps ten or fifteen minutes later, they met someone else.

“Oh my!” The words were followed by a light clatter, and they both turned to see an old man with watery, glimmering eyes staring at them both. No, staring at Ryland. “Do mine eyes deceive me? Is that truly you, Recreator?”

Ryland glanced at the priestess, who quickly ducked her head in slight embarrassment.

“Goodman Harker,” Wyla said. “Please, let us not embarrass the Recreator after so long.”

The man laughed, his dirty beard shaking. He shook himself, rubbing off much of the dirt on his tunic and trying to tame his wild hair. Rather than appearing a little more controlled at the woman’s plea, the light in the man’s eyes just turned even brighter. Almost volatile.

“Forgive me and my deplorable state,” he said. “And the warmest welcome to you at our humble little temple here.”

“Temple…” Ryland looked around, frowning a little. “A temple to what?”

“To the Recreator, of course!”

Wyla quickly raised her hands as if to stop the man from speaking further. “Goodman Harker, please!” She turned to Ryland apologetically. “Forgive us all, Marionys Ryland. We didn’t prepare for your visit so we’re a bit excited.”

Goodman Harker just laughed. There was an edge to it, but Ryland ignored it.

Ryland took the whole place in. While it wasn’t in the best of states, it wasn’t entirely abandoned. He spotted what looked like a hovel in the near distance with smoke rising from it. Likely the Goodman’s abode. The land was well-tended, the grass was cut, and no weeds or other wilderness had overgrown. Everything was mostly in its proper place.

They talked a bit more, with the Goodman’s presence making the priestess a little less reticent about the whole situation for the time being. But they both came back to the same point. They both emphasized that Ryland was far from forgotten.

That in this little corner of Rhel, he was almost celebrated.

“So you see,” the priestess said when all was done. “We need you to be who you’ve always been, Ryland. We need you to propagate the truth in a way that others will listen to. We need you to not keep everything sequestered in this tiny corner.”

“Oh, please,” the Goodman said. He had taken up a rake to clear off a lot of the fallen leaves. “I wouldn’t say we need anything. We’ve been doing fine on our own.”

Wyla turned to Harker with a “Goodman, please! You know about the recent events. We can’t let the institute and the rest of Rhel treat our children, our future, the way they’ve been doing. It’s unacceptable!”

“Naturally. We want to protect what is dearest to us. But instead of being capable of it yourself, you seek to drag in a man who hasn’t been on Rhel for ages. That is almost as deplorable as the state I’ve been in since the Recreator has met me.”

He ended it with another edged laugh, as if to blunt his statement a bit, but the pointedness of it had already penetrated a bit too deep. Wyla looked terribly ashamed of herself. Goodman Harker saw the effect of his statement after a moment and eventually tried to smooth things over, even apologizing to the priestess.

“I can attest to the truth,” Ryland said eventually. “However, if there isn’t a significant push from your side, I’m afraid that will do little.”

“I know!” Wyla was suddenly rather encouraged by Ryland being open to assisting them all, but she was trying to rein in her enthusiasm. “That’s why we’ve raised the hue and cry we’ve done so far. It’s why we’re taking a stand against it all.”

“Alright. Then perhaps if you can create an opportunity, I can speak my mind and be honest.” Ryland looked at her squarely in the eye. “However, you need to understand that if your endeavour is successful, this will cause an untameable ripple in the very fabric of your society. Even I can’t foresee the full consequences of such a thing.”

Wyla swallowed but then nodded resolutely again. “We must do what we can. For the sake of our children.”

Goodman Harker had no comment on any of that. Wyla took Ryland on a little tour through the rest of the grounds, but they had to put a stop to it before they got far.

Someone was coming.

It was Ryland who sensed the presence arriving first. A stormy feeling arrived, the air crackling and the wind whipping up faster. In the far distance of the sky, a streak of light crashed through the clouds before finally coming to stop a few thousand handspans over them.

About a minute later, the streak had descended to their location, slowing as it approached their exact point.

Another Guardian.

Ryland could sense hostility directly from the man who was floating down among them. It was written on his stony expression, on the way his magic remained restrained but ready to emerge at a moment’s notice. On the sheer amount and level of magic he was willing to expel.

Everything about him radiated power. His armoured robes were etched with flowing Inscriptions that were all at least Tier [20] at Ryland’s cursory glance. A spectral greataxe hung at his back, compressed with power exuding from the Inscription that held its form together. If Ryland looked closely, both his silvery hair and his eyeballs were Inscribed with Glyphs too.

It seemed Rhel—or, more specifically, their Demiurge—had gone to great lengths to grant themselves greater and greater magic.

“Greetings, Guardian Vinum,” Goodman Harker said. He had cracked a grin in welcome, though it didn’t quite feel welcoming to Ryland. “What a surprise to see you here all of a sudden.”

The Guardian had no time for pleasantries, it seemed. “What is the meaning of all this, Goodman?”

“I’m not certain what you mean.”

“We allow you a sliver of independence, we mercifully look away when you store and brandish radicalizing material in the name of preserving the truth, and this is how you treat our kindness?” He advanced almost threateningly. “By fomenting rebellion, ruining everything we’ve built for so many years, and throwing it all away? For what?”

“Now, now,” the Goodman said, raising his hands as if to forestall the Guardian from getting any closer. “Let’s not be hasty. Perhaps there has been a slight misunderstanding—”

“There have been no such misunderstandings. We are well aware of what is going on, and we will not allow any such transgressions to occur!”

The man had arrived like a subdued meteor and was now continuing his apocalyptic tirade. At this rate, with the Goodman failing to stop it and the priestess looking increasingly mortified, things might take a turn for the worse.

So, Ryland stepped up.

“I’m afraid you have nothing to be concerned about,” he said. “There is no need for you to be alarmed.”

The Guardian turned to Ryland not just with the same severe look, but an impressive surge of restrained mana as well. It was clear he knew exactly who Ryland was and what he represented. “Do you take me for a fool? We know of your intention to help. We know where your heart lies. We know how you dare to invade the sanctity of our home.”

That last bit surprised Ryland. Before he could speak, the Guardian went on, capitalizing on possessing a verbal advantage.

“Oh, yes,” he said. “You think you can barge into the Great Demiurge’s room and leave just like that? You think we will ever forget how you violated our boundaries? Do not mistake our forbearance as mercy, Marionys Ryland. However strong you might believe yourself to be, however powerful you seem, it doesn’t mean you can disrespect whomever you wish.”

While the man had spoken, Ryland had deliberated on how best to approach the issue.

“You’ve come here with an intention in mind,” he said. “A certain goal, I’m sure, besides being a belligerent brute. What is it?”

The man scowled hard at the insult. “Isn’t it obvious enough? We do not seek uproar. We do not want chaos. If you, as a guest, seek to upend the very balance that we’ve worked so hard to cultivate, then we’ll be forced to expel you all. With force, if necessary.”

That last bit had been directed at the priestess for some reason, which made Ryland frown.

Force.

Did he mean…

Wyla came up to the Guardian quickly, her face foretelling her conciliatory tone. “Please, Guardian. I-I have no intentions of causing any real chaos. I merely brought Ryland here to seek guidance and assistance, to address our concerns for our children. I promise—no, I swear that we will not resort to anything extraordinary.”

Before Ryland could say anything, the so-called Guardian latched onto Wyla’s statement like a lashing viper. “See that you do. For the consequences will not be pretty otherwise.”

With a final warning at glance at them all, especially at Ryland and the Goodman, the Guardian left in much the same way that he had arrived. His form turned radiant gold-and-silver, before he ascended into the sky and promptly disappeared.

Ryland was about to turn to the others when the priestess came up to him directly, carefully trying to control her frightened expression.

“Please,” she said. “Forget that we ever met. I’m glad for the kindness you wished to show us, but—”

“I think you’re being a little hasty,” Ryland said. “As I warned, the consequences will be difficult to bear, but if we are careful, we can manage it efficiently and—”

The woman rubbed her temple, her expression clouding over in a frown. “No! I’d rather not. Please.”

Ryland pursed his lips for a moment. To think that she herself had seemed willing to pursue whatever lengths were necessary to help her children. And yet now, she was performing a complete reversal of her earlier resoluteness. Was it just the threat from the Guardian that had her so worried or was it something more?

Something like the Soul Glyph Inscribed onto her manasoul?

“Please,” Wyla said once more as she started retreating. “Forget about this meeting. We can handle matters on our own. You will always be welcome, but…” She shook her head. “Farewell, Ryland. And thank you.”

Wyla left in a hurry.

The Goodman’s expression was unreadable as he watched the priestess depart. He did have a little mirthless smile, though, when he turned to Ryland.

“Well, that’s that, I suppose,” he said. “I best be going on my way. This place isn’t going to tend to itself. Farewell, for now. And let me know if you ever come back, alright?”

Ryland couldn’t really return his fake enthusiasm, but he did nod in farewell before he himself departed with a cast of [Translocate]. He couldn’t quite understand the purpose of the meeting. Was the Demiurge’s attention too divided for him to have known about it all and was now performing some desperate damage control?

Or was he even now manipulating things in his favour?

Ryland’s uncertainty regarding which it was only deepened with time. For when the next day came, two things happened.

A meeting occurred, where the Rhellian Institute successfully stopped everyone who had taken a stand against their operations. And then a Rhellian student got mortally ill.
