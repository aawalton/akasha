That was good. Every Gifted school, be they of repute or not, had automatic jurisdiction over portals that appeared within their vicinity.

“How many portals so far?” she asked.

“Eight ma’am.” Mr. Okoro was silent for a moment. “But the eighth one turned up a minute ago, so they could still be rising and… shit,” he swore. “Apologies for the language, ma’am. I just got an update. There are now nine portals in the school.”

“None. But we’ve been going through the list, starting from the new intakes. And there seems to be a problem.”

You think?

“What has happened, Mr. Okoro?”

“There are two students unaccounted for.”

Mrs. Talwort frowned. “Two first years?”

Children and their overenthusiastic sense of adventure. She had been hoping that they would have sense. You know, the common sense that said that when you saw a portal, you did not just jump into it.

She could only imagine how bad the normal schools were having it. Here, they did not have to worry about students dying just because a portal opened. Chaos Runs would be their problem.

And overenthusiastic students trying to make a name for themselves.

“Which students are they?” she asked.

Mr. Okoro hesitated. “Uhm… they are not both first years.”

“You said new intakes.”

“Yes, but not just from the first years.”

Names came to her mind, and Mrs. Talwort rubbed her forehead with hand and forefinger. “What happened to them?”

“Well, Mr—”

“No.” She shook her head, interrupting him. “I don’t want their names… not yet. Just what happened to them.”

“Nobody knows of… My apologies, I just got news of a tenth portal.” Mr. Okoro sighed in mental exhaustion. “No one can account for what really happened to the one in his second year. CCTV footage caught him running out of his class a second or two after the first portal appeared.”

“The one in the biology class?”

“No, before that one.”

Mrs. Talwort was confused. He had said the first portal was in the biology class, so what was he saying now? The second year student moved before that portal and there was another portal before that one? Then why was he saying that the first portal was in the biology class?

“Mr. Okoro, you’re losing me here,” she informed him. “What is happening?”

“Mr. Tar’arkna Lockwood, fled from his classroom and went missing before the portals arrived…”

Mrs. Talwort gritted her teeth. She had known who it was, but she had really not wanted to know their names. Not yet.

“And before that,” Mr. Okoro continued, “Mr. Melmarc Lockwood was abducted by a portal that disappeared after said abduction.”

Mrs. Talwort froze.

“Say that again.” Her voice was weak, slow. She could not believe what she was hearing.

“A portal,” Mr. Okoro repeated, slightly confused, “according to CCTV footage, appeared behind Melmarc Lockwood, teleported him, and disappeared.”

Mrs. Talwort’s knees shook beneath her. She staggered back, placing a hand on the wall for support. Raising her hand, she looked at her watch. It was a few minutes past ten in the night. The chaos had been going on for a while now.

“How long ago was this?” she asked, staring at her watch but not at the time. Her eyes were focused on the timer above it.

“Roughly eleven hours ago.”

“I need a time stamp.”

“Eleven fifty-two, ma’am.”

Mrs. Talwort rested her back against the wall and slid down to the ground. She sat there for a moment.

“Let me call you back.”

Without waiting for a response, she hung the call on the [Sage].

Eleven fifty-two.

Then there were shackles that once held the divine. Now, they held nothing. They were empty, devoid of a prisoner.

A portal had appeared and taken away Melmarc Lockwood, then his brother had run off and disappeared into only God knew where. All of this had happened at least a full minute before the a portal had appeared right in front of her eyes and taken the angel away.

Melmarc Lockwood had been taken before the long-lived angel, the most powerful being she knew.

What did that mean?

What did anything mean? She ran her hand through grey hair. A lot of times she had taught woman that to be a lady, there are certain things you don’t do. Certain things you do not say. She had learnt it, perfected it.

Her body shook in barely restraint and she threw being lady-like to the wind. She groaned. She screamed. She roared.

“FUCK ME!”
