Beneath a sky choked black and surrounded by death, Fia was at peace.

She walked with a steady heartbeat and certain steps. Left, here, away from a many-eyed beast. It would find different prey. Right, there, where a warrior held shadows at bay. His sword was more red than silver.

Fia had seen this carnage many times and had stopped fearing it long ago. Every step fell on tamped dirt and warm summer grass, all of it spattered crimson. Nearby oaks and the houses of Fia’s neighbors smoldered with flame, lighting the shadowed fray and the furious, fearful, frenzied faces within. Metal rang, monsters hissed, and humans screamed. Smoke shrouded the town.

She didn’t see a bit of it, now. Her feet knew where to go, and this left her eyes free for something far more important.

Fia gazed down, drinking in the perfect little face. The baby lay swaddled with her eyes shut, round cheeks flushed by sweltering air and pink lips curled in a happy dream.

“Brin. My Brin.” Ten steps. Around her, humans and Fae rent each other bloody.

“My sweet girl.” Twenty. Kiss after kiss against smooth skin.

“I’m so proud of you.” Thirty. One finger traced the perfect nose.

“I’m so sorry I won’t be there.” Forty. Her hand cupped tiny fingers.

Fifty steps brought her to the heart of the battle. Two figures given a berth by all the others; one hulking and dark, formed of shadows and dread. He screamed in a thousand voices, buzzed like a thousand flies, and every movement wept fear into the very earth. And his foe… flame given flesh. Smaller, weaker, bleeding a river of heat but still standing, eyes blazing like a dawn and a sword of sunlight clenched in one torn fist. Held upright by strength his foe could never understand.

The pair tore at each other. Claws and steel and fire, shrieking doom and smoldering strength, then the sudden break and turn of a head as Fia was seen.

“Flee!” The command came as thunder. His voice shook the ground. He’d shouted it a thousand times before.

She didn’t listen. She didn’t even look. She had something more important to watch.

“I love you so much.” One finger traced the tiny pink mouth. Safe in her dream, the baby smiled. Her mother smiled back.

“Flee, mortal!”

Fia knew what to do. She’d come back to this dream a thousand times, watched it again and again, memorized every motion and traced every breath. Just to be sure.

Back turned to the fray and arms tight, lifting until the tiny heartbeat thrummed beside her own. The most precious sound there had ever been.

Elbows down, to brace their fall.

Fia closed her eyes. "I love you."
