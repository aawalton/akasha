The section of water around my blade vanishes into steam in an instant. I quickly shift my blade upwards and a whole section of the wave explodes into superheated steam with a roar.

Shouts ring out from behind me as the bulk of the tsunami continues. As I throw back a mana potion to overcome the mana sickness I feel, I glance backwards to see that the people I called up to the quarterdeck are doing exactly as I wanted them to. Air blades, blades of force, shields projected out from the person, jets of water, even metal chains used like whips strike the rolling wave. The effect is as if we were facing the wave prow-end on.

The ship bucks and rolls as it bears the brunt of the rest of the wave and is forced to rise over the immense quantity of turbulent water. Most importantly, however, it stays afloat and without too much damage.

The tsunami continues beyond our ship, but a good deal of its energy has been spent and it rapidly loses the height it still has.

I turn burning eyes onto the ship in the distance – and the person – that’s caused this whole thing.

Have Tolly turn the ship around and return, I tell Loran grimly.

Of course, Loran replies, then hesitates. But…might Heir Torrent not create another of these?

Not with what I’m about to do to him, I grind out.

With his mixed confusion and concern leaking across our Bond, I focus back on the steam that’s still hanging in the air. Torrent senior managed to take control of steam; I don’t want to give his son the opportunity to do so.

Sucking heat out of the droplets in mid air, I feed it to Aingeal. We’re going to need that. The superheated steam cools into water droplets and from there into glittering fragments of ice. Gathering them together into a whirlwind with my Air-Shaping, I fly towards Kyrian’s ship, Noir and Ivor catching up with me after they finish rescuing those who fell or were swept off the deck as the wave passed by.

Behind me, I’m aware of the ship starting to turn. It might catch up with me as I fly, but possibly not. But right now, I don’t care if I face Torrent alone.

Our ship managed to create a significant amount of distance as we tried to outrun and then minimise the damage from the wave, but my focus is such that it seems to pass by in the blink of an eye. The journey gives my mana pool enough time to refill. I’ll need that.

Surrounded by a vast cloud of glittering icy fragments, I descend on Kyrian’s ship like an angel from the heavens.

In mimicry of the hail attacks earlier, I propel the ice fragments forwards, powering the gust far faster than gravity alone would offer.

I sense the Air Mage below trying to divert my attack, but I press my fury-powered will against theirs and feel them buckle. Somehow, I even seize the attempt they made at a shield and redirect it to add to the force that I’m using to propel the ice fragments forwards.

They strike, forcing the shield to flare every time to deflect the miniature missiles. Shouts from the deck reach my ears, but I know I’m too high above the ship for anyone to reach me.

The bombardment continues – the amount of wave I vaporised has provided an excellent supply of armaments. And Ivor adds what he can when he activates his offensive tattoo, ice shards shooting from his front claws and pouring from his mouth.

Vicious satisfaction curls the corner of my lips as I see the shield falter, and finally break. My little ice storm seems to have been the straw that breaks the camel’s back.

I quaff another mana potion, one of the strongest I have access to. It almost completely refills my pool in the first moment, and adds a quadrupled regeneration speed. At the same time, I warn my companions that I’ll probably need to draw from them again. Unfortunately, their mana regeneration is significantly lowered in my spatial tattoo, so few of them have mana to draw from. Kalanthia does, of course, and when I tell her why I want it, she easily agrees.

Ready, Aingeal? I ask, sending him what I’m intending on doing. The little fire elemental agrees eagerly, as ever just happy to be involved.

You’d best make some distance, I warn Ivor and Noir. It’s going to get a bit hot up here.

The two alcaorises quickly move so they’re not directly above the ship, but stay close, watchful for threats. I send gratitude towards them for that – with them on guard, I can concentrate on teaching Kyrian why he shouldn’t have come after me.

Once more snarling as I pour my anger and frustration into a massive ball of fire between my fingers, this time I focus on size more than temperature for now. To burn a ship, I need quantity as well as quality. Aingeal dances in the flames, making them grow even faster than I would have been capable of on my own.

Within seconds of my ice storm dying away from lack of ammunition, I’m ready to attack with one of my strongest elements. Kyrian wants to destroy my ship with a tsunami? Let’s see how he feels about fire.

Knowing how desperate those below will be to attack me, I keep my distance. Now Master level, my Fire-Shaping Skill is easily strong enough to allow me to maintain a connection to my flames, even more than three hundred metres above my target.

I do make Aingeal come out of the inferno before I release it, despite his disappointment.

You’ve done an excellent job at growing the inferno, but there are still powerful Water Mages down below, I remind him. It doesn’t do much to cheer him up, but I can’t deal with his pouting right now – the further the inferno moves from me, the more I need to concentrate on it.

The mages below frantically work to fend off what must look like a descending meteor. Jets of water shoot at the flaming ball, blades of force attempt to dissipate it, and even the Air Mage attempts to deflect it.

To no avail. My consciousness, settled in the ball of fire, sends out tendrils that vaporise the jets of water, absorbs the blades of force into and through the ball without causing any damage, and consumes the shield of the Air Mage who hasn’t learned that oxygen only fuels fire.

Shrieks pierce the air, sounding both close and far as I hear them through two different sets of senses. My ball of fire impacts and I send it licking along the planks. The people on the deck panic, some attempting to put it out with both magical and mundane means, and others immediately jumping overboard to avoid it.

I laugh at the attempts to put out my flames with sand and suppression skills – fueled by my magic, the only way of extinguishing my fire is by cutting my connection with it. And none seem to have realised that. Or, if they have, they are not capable of doing so.

My fire licks further, appearing to be wild, but actually under complete control. No longer do I need to engage a different mindset, risking being lost in the fire myself if I go too far: Master Fire-Shaping allows me to know exactly who I am at any moment. And in this case, I know that I do not want to target people who probably had no influence on the decision to attack me.

If they are burned because of the heat, then that is their own fault for standing too close to it, but I’m not going to seek them out to destroy them. Instead, I attack the ship, my fire eating hungrily at the planks, my magic giving it the fuel it needs to overcome the anti-fire enchantments that would otherwise starve it.

More and more people recognise the pointlessness of trying to put out my fire, and instead choose to jump overboard with anything that will float. Those, I leave alone.

As my flames engulf the main deck and begin crawling up the stairs to the quarter deck, I hear a voice crying hoarsely.

“We surrender! Please! We surrender! Please don’t burn my ship!”

Though I have neither eyes nor ears when my mind is inhabiting fire, I can somehow both see and hear the situation. A man who is clearly the captain is pressed wide-eyed against the furthestmost railing. Next to him, the man I saw trailing Kyrian earlier is folded up on the ground with an unconscious Kyrian’s head in his lap. He, too, looks grimly fearful, and is holding a magical shield up between the three of them and the encroaching flames that are already beginning to lick at the wheel.

Well, this makes things more complicated. If I’d killed Kyrian unknowingly, I reckon I’d be able to get away with it without penalty – given how clearly he sought my death in both words and actions. But with him lying unconscious, unable to defend himself…it’s hard not to call it ‘murder’ to myself. And if I feel that it’s cold-blooded murder when I’m the victim here, then someone like Torrent senior will most definitely howl about it. And if the king agrees, then that could lead to penalties for myself or my House.

With anyone of a lesser rank, I’d probably still get away with it. A Great House Heir in their territory can act as judge, jury, and executor – within reason. But not over other Great House Heirs. Just as, if Nicholas had caught Torrent senior when he fled the scene of the rift, he could have taken Torrent captive, but not intentionally killed him, I’m limited in what I can now do with Torrent junior.

But at the same time, I’m not willing to just let him go. Not without consequences – I know where that story ends.

I suppose I could take him hostage – imprison him. But that’s a pain – a hostage is only as good as the preventative measures that stop them escaping, or being rescued. Added to caring for him, that means a big expenditure on my part – and for what?

So, if executing him and imprisoning him are off the table, what are my options?

I run through a few scenarios in my mind before hitting on one that might be a solution – with a bit of work. The first complication is that I need to speak to the two men below, ideally without getting attacked while doing so.

If I can see and hear through the flames…can I talk through them? I wonder. It should be impossible but so is having my mind inhabiting flames that are way over a hundred metres away from my body.

“If you attack me, I will burn your ship to ashes beneath you,” I warn, projecting my voice in the way I used to when speaking to samurans not Bonded to me, yet twisted so it’s using Fire-Shaping rather than Animal Empathy. It’s instinct rather than reasoning that instructs me to do it this way, but I’m somehow certain that it will work.

Sure enough, my voice emerges from the flames – kind of. The two conscious men both flinch and press themselves more firmly against the back railing when they hear me. I can understand it – instead of my normal tones emerging, my words crackle out with the fire – only just decipherable as words.

“Do you understand me?” I ask, the words sounding far more like a threat than I intended.

The man cradling Kyrian’s head and holding the shield quickly bows low to my fire, even in his seated position.

“Yes, Fire Mage, we understand!” He glances up at the captain, then fixes his eyes back on the flames. “We swear we will not attack you.”

A good response, though I’m not sure I entirely trust it. There’s no need to fear the Fire Mage if the Fire Mage is dead, after all.

“I will draw back my fire. You have ten flames to order all crew off the ship and into the water. You three must remain. None of those who depart may take any property off the ship. Any jewellery must be stripped off and left on the deck before departure. My beasts will be watching,” I warn, sending a quick message to Ivor and Noir.

They begin to swoop down lower, one on each side of the ship.

“If either of them are attacked, they, or I, will retaliate with lethal force,” I warn the two men and they flinch.

“We hear, Fire Mage!” the man holding the shield quickly agrees.

“And thank you for your mercy!” adds the captain, fear mixing with a hint of gratitude.

“Ten flames,” I warn again, then draw the flames back, leaving blackened boards behind me.
