Jade emerged into a scene of ethereal beauty. It was as if someone had taken a calm, peaceful forest glade, and enclosed it in a vast dome of gnarled and knotted wood. Radiant beams of warm sunlight spilled through gaps in the high, curved ceiling, illuminating what amounted to an indoor forest. Insects buzzed around colorful flowers, some of which grew from bud to bloom before her very eyes. Knee high grass rustled in a gentle, source less breeze, and on the far side of the clearing, a small waterfall cascaded down a series of moss-covered boulders, softly splashing into a tranquil pool.

Jade came to a stop beside her companions, transfixed by the scene. Their new surroundings had a stunning vibrance that, even in a world of magic, she'd never experienced before. Flowers blazed with color, like someone had painted them onto the fabric of the world. The smell of nature was sharp and crisp, alive with scents of earth and dew, and the sound of distant birdsong formed a melody of such beauty, she couldn't help but smile.

"I'm glad that you find my home so pleasant." A soft, musical voice came from across the clearing. "It's been so very long since I've been a host, and I fear that I've forgotten the art."

Jade blinked in surprise, refocusing on the source of the sound. A hammock of woven reeds hung suspended between two forest giants, rocking slightly from side to side. It certainly hadn't been there a moment before, nor had the woman lying within it. She was tall and slender, with olive skin and hair the color of the trees surrounding them and was garbed in a simple dress of verdant hues. But it was her eyes that truly drew Jade's attention. They were blue as a summer sky and glistened with shimmering radiance, like mottled sunlight filtering through a forest canopy.

"Are… you Lady Elaria?" Naomi asked, the first of them to find their voice. "Goddess of Growth?"

"I am, child." The goddess's smile felt like the break of dawn after a long, dark night, filling them with warmth. Jade's soul stirred once more, but she kept her demonic aspect firmly in hand. Her gaze swept across them, lingering on Jade. "I'm glad to meet you at last."

"Did you bring us here because of the demons?" asked Jade, taking a hesitant step forward. The goddess radiated an aura of all-encompassing power that pressed against her spirit that, despite its benevolence, was impossible to ignore. "Why you've been trying to speak to me? Are you going to help us? "

"What my friend means-" Naomi gave her a reproachful look. "Is that we're honored to meet you and would like to thank you for granting us an audience."

"Oh, sorry." Jade felt her cheeks heating up. "I was getting ahead of myself. We are grateful, it's just that I'm eager to learn anything that will help us protect our world."

"And our friends." Luis added, throwing an arm around Fenrin and Tara. "We aren't just going to look out for ourselves."

"Definitely." Jade nodded in agreement. "We're all in this together."

"Yes, you are." Lady Elaria said, shifting so that she sat on the edge of her hammock, bare feet brushing the grass below. "All of us are. Already, the people who have made my world, the Labyrinth, their home are fighting to keep the ones you call demons from emerging to wage war across the cosmos once more."

"Are they faring well?" Fenrin asked. "Is there anything we can do to help?"

"They are holding them back, for now." The goddess nodded. She paused, wavering slightly, and Jade tasted an edge of weariness in her thoughts. Then she pulled her senses back, both amazed and a little concerned. Her mind was vast and alien, more complex and powerful than anything she'd ever experienced, and even the barest hint of what lay within was enough to shake Jade to her core. If she'd ever doubted the existence of deific beings, those doubts were thoroughly dispelled now.

But, on the other hand, that shroud of exhaustion worried her. What could cause such a being so much strain?

"Their success is in no small part due to your efforts." The goddess continued, steadying herself. "You, and some others throughout this world, have prevented the demons from enacting their designs as they wished. I only regret that I was unable to provide aid sooner." She bowed her head, closing her eyes. The gesture was so very human, matching her appearance perfectly. Was that for their benefit?

"Is there anything we can do to help you, my Lady?" Siora asked, bowing at the waist. The elf was even more awed than the rest of them. "Your blessings have protected and guided my people since the times of my ancestors. If there is any way I can repay that debt-"

"There is no debt to be repaid, Siora Windsong, but I thank you for your compassion." The goddess graced them with another smile. Then, her expression grew more somber. "But, despite my best efforts, the breach the demons have opened in their seal will widen with time. Unless something is done, their legions will eventually darken the skies of countless worlds."

Jade could feel it, then. The loss and sorrow that followed a thousand wars. It pressed down on her like a physical weight, making her shoulders tremble.

"Is there anything we can do to help?" Jade asked softly, the fleeting feeling fading. "Is that why we were brought here?"

"Yes."

Hope welled up inside her at the news that they were not to be helpless in the face of this coming storm. But the expression the goddess bore, the soft, reluctant tone of her voice, tempered it.

"It isn't going to be easy, is it." Tara said softly. It wasn't a question.

"No."

"I don't think any of us expected it to be." Fenrin glanced at the rest of them. "What can you tell us, Lady Elaria?"

"Let me start with the story of how this all came to be. What you choose to do when you leave this place will be just that: A choice. I did not invite you to my sanctuary to force any task upon your shoulders, but to shed light on all that is transpiring so that you may decide for yourselves what should be done."

They all exchanged looks. Jade wasn't sure if it was her imagination, but the air around them seemed to grow still and heavy. Now that she was finally here, within reach of all the answers she sought, she didn't feel ready. That knowledge would come with the burden of responsibility, a duty to act. Did she really want to shoulder that?

Someone has to.

"Thank you for your consideration." Naomi was the first to reply. She gave a slight bow to the goddess, which she promptly waved away.

"Yes." Jade agreed, steeling herself. "Please, tell us everything."

With a wave of her hand, the goddess summoned several seats into being. They grew from the ground as twisted collections of roots, quickly forming in stools with soft, springy moss for a cushion. She bid them sit, then, once they settled, began her tale.

"Once, long ago, there existed many of my kin. Now we are known as gods, but then, there were no creatures to name us as such. We were born from the forces that drive the cosmos. Life and death, creation and destruction, growth and decay, and many more."

The soft shafts of sunlight filtering in through the ceiling dimmed, and the air before them shimmered as an illusion took shape. It formed a starry sky filled with swirling, colorful nebula.

"At first, our awareness was little. Stars formed and died, worlds bloomed and withered, and the inexorable march of time continued. Eventually, though, we began to change. Even now, I'm not sure why. But slowly, each of us grew more aware, becoming something more than a simple embodiment of power. We had will, and could now perceive the cosmos we inhabited in an all-new manner."

"Still, this did not change our purpose. We existed as a guide to the fundamental powers of the Universe, and guide them we did. Gently shaping them, maintaining harmony between the forces that govern these realms. Only now, we could behold our creation and marvel at its majesty. For a time, that was enough. But as more of us found each other amidst the endless astral, we had an idea. What if we combined our powers? What if we exerted our will over them, forging something of our own? We had witnessed so much in our time, and yet done so little."

The nebula transformed, shifting into a planet. Deep oceans stretched between vast continents, colored green with life.

"We had watched our powers forge worlds before, but rarely did any of us intervene. Our role was to guide with a subtle touch, there was never a need to seize the reins of the power we embody. But that is what we decided to do. To come together and forge anew a world imbued with a bounty of cosmic power, and a people born to channel it. With so much at their disposal, what mighty creations would they forge? Would they stand the test of time, or would time and decay claim them as they had all others?"

Jade watched and listened, transfixed by the goddess's tale. Hearing about the creation of worlds and the early days of creation was more than a little unnerving, like they were getting a peek behind a curtain that was never meant to be swept aside. But at the same time, it was exhilarating.

"And that's what we did. Combined the essence of the universe to birth a world filled with boundless potential. The soil was rich and fertile, the sky clear and bright, and the very air brimming with energy just waiting to be tapped. To the beings to whom that world gave birth to, it was nothing short of paradise."

The illusion shifted once more, now showing a shifting landscape of verdant forests and azure seas. The lands shimmered with prismatic colors, and for a moment, Jade felt that she was there. Surrounded by such power, untouched by mortal hands, one could do anything. Become anyone.

"The people who rose to prominence on this planet achieved more than we'd ever dreamed. They used the latent power of the land to create wonders. Great cities, monuments, academies of magic and science. All that we'd wished for and more. They delved into secrets of magic no mortals had yet to reach, and that was just the beginning. And therein lay the problem."

The goddess sighed, letting her gaze linger on the still-shifting illusion she'd conjured. When she continued, her words were tinged with palpable sorrow.

"We perhaps allowed too much of our own ambition to seep into the essence of this new world. As proud as we were of our children, they were not satisfied with their creations. Their expertise in new fields of magic led them to the discovery of other worlds, as well as the means to reach them. And reach them they did, only to learn how special their home truly was… and how much more power they commanded than any other."

"A group that thinks they're superior coming across people weaker than them?" Luis said, folding his arms. "I think I know what happened next."

"They took it all." Jade said slowly, her hands squeezing into fists.

"They did." Lady Elaria's shoulders slumped. "Our first true creation, a people we hoped would be a beacon of hope and progress in the vast dark of the endless astral, became its greatest menace. They used their mastery of magic to change themselves, adopting forms of power that better served their grim purpose. And with them, demons waged war across the stars."
