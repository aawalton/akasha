Lanra passed on later that night.

When Asher received the news from Mako, he barely even knew what to think. He faintly recalled thanking the man for informing him, before slowly closing the door in his face and taking a seat on the couch. It wasn’t until Samantha sat down beside him that he realized he’d been staring blankly at the wall for almost a full minute.

“I heard what Mako said,” Samantha muttered, wrapping her arms around him as she held him close. “I’m sorry.”

“It doesn’t make any sense,” Asher said blankly, trying to put his emotions into words. “All things considered, I barely even knew Lanra. I mean, sure, she pitched me a job offer and we traveled into the wild realm with one another. And sure, we were both on the coalition and we took down a lich together. But it wasn’t like she was a close friend of mine. We’ve never shared a meal or chatted about our lives. She was barely even an acquaintance of mine!”

“So why does her death hurt so much?” Samantha asked quietly.

“Yes! I don’t understand!” Asher said, his heart aching as he turned to look at Samantha. “I’m no stranger to death. Hell, I trained to be a mortician’s apprentice long before I became an assassin and started hunting down bad people. I’ve seen so much death I thought I was all but desensitized to it by now!”

“Asher…” Samantha started, taking his hands in her own as she leaned back so she could take him in. The hunter’s dark, brown eyes were wide and sad as she took him in, as if recognizing exactly what he was going through. “…Have you ever actually lost anyone? A friend? A family member? Anyone at all?”

“A great aunt a few years ago back on my own world… but I barely even knew her,” he admitted, growing quiet as he realized what she was getting at.

“You’re no stranger to death… but you’ve never really experienced loss to this degree,” she said, running her thumbs lovingly over his knuckles as she squeezed his hands. “I’m sure the destruction of Horntho Village was hard for you, and I know George’s and Martha’s passing was difficult… but Lanra was different. She functionally sacrificed her life in order to save our own. Even if you might have managed to survive that magical explosion, if it wasn’t for her, Rulfar, Moxy, and I would all be dead. It’s no wonder you wanted to see her pull through. She’s a hero.”

“I can’t stop wondering if there’s something more I could have done,” Asher admitted quietly, blinking as he realized his eyes were watering. “If we’d just started to evacuate a little sooner… Or if I’d been a little more efficient with my cutting and prevented the void dagger from eating so much of my tritessence… she might still be here right now.”

“You can’t think like that,” Samantha said. She surprised him by manifesting her emerald-green wings and gently wrapping them around him as she used them to pull him into a warm embrace without ever letting go of his hands. The wings were made from her very own energy, and while Asher had witnessed her turn the feathers sharp enough to cleave straight through even the toughest of muscles, at the moment, they were soft and warm as they wrapped around him. Sealing off the outside world to the point where nothing existed beyond the two of them, Samantha smiled as her face was illuminated by the gentle green glow of her wings. “Lanra was an amazing woman who knew exactly what she was getting herself into. Rather than be sad about her passing, we should honor her memory. Did you catch Mako mentioning the funeral they’re holding tomorrow?”

“No… No, I missed that bit,” Asher admitted, wincing as he tried to recall his brief encounter with the realm wanderer. “I think I’m going to have to apologize to him later.”

“I’m sure he understands,” Samantha said as she pulled his head against her shoulder and started running her fingers through his hair. “They’re holding a funeral tomorrow, and we’re invited. The whole coalition was. Apparently, in the Eight Crests, it’s tradition to have eight people from the deceased’s life stand up and say something about the fallen. Mako asked if you would be one of the eight.”

“Me?” Asher said, recoiling at the news and discovering Samantha had him pinned down against her as she continued running her fingers through his hair. “Why me?”

“They don’t really have many people to choose from,” Samantha admitted. “Other than the three of them, they’re going to need five volunteers. I’m sure Mako would understand if you said you weren’t up for it.”

“I’ll do it,” Asher decided after a moment, closing his eyes as he let out a tired sigh. He briefly wondered what it would be like to have to speak at Rulfar’s or Moxy’s or, God forbid, Samantha’s funeral, and he shook his head as he struggled to even comprehend how he would feel. “I only hope Mako, Cora, and Jameston are doing okay after losing their teammate.”

The funeral was a rather small affair, all things considered. Asher was surprised to learn that there were a few different ceremonial rooms throughout the palace of varying sizes, and Lanra’s team chose the smallest of them all to hold the funeral. Little more than a small, slightly raised stage that held Lanra’s closed casket, and a spot for someone to speak from, the room held nothing more than a few rows of pews. Most of which were left empty. Other than Lanra’s team, the only people in attendance were Asher and his team, Groln, Donvath, Amanda, and surprisingly, Richard. Despite everything, Asher hadn’t expected to see the other wizard, knowing how much he valued his time. Yet here he was, wearing what Asher imagined was the least-flashy item in his entire wardrobe as he sat there in a simple blue robe.

Unlike back on Earth, neither the Eight Crests nor the Noala Kingdom seemed to have any sort of color-code when it came to funerals. Asher had witnessed so many in his time as a mortician’s apprentice that the sight of everyone gathering without so much as a single black outfit was a bit jarring to him, but he kept the thought to himself. After everyone had filtered in and taken their seats, Mako got up and took the stand first.

“Thank you all for coming,” Mako said, giving everyone a sad smile. “I know we have a lot of very important work we have to get back to, so I’ll do my best to keep things brief. It means the world to us that all of you decided to come pay your respects for a woman you barely even knew, so from the bottom of my heart, we thank you for that. For those of you who are unaware, in the Eight Crests, it’s custom for eight different people who knew the deceased to say a few words about them in honor of their memory. And as the captain of our team, I have the honor of starting us off.”

Mako glanced over his shoulder at the casket behind him, shaking his head as he wiped a tear from his eye. “I say I am the captain of our team, and while that’s technically true, anyone who knows us also knows Lanra was the one really calling the shots. She saved our lives time and time again across the realms, always dragging our asses out of the fire. Sometimes in a quite literal sense.”

Cora and Jameston chuckled at some memory they no doubt shared, and Mako smiled as his voice grew stronger. “She was an amazing woman, right up until the end. We owe her our lives too many times to count… too many times to count, plus one.”

Mako nodded and took his seat, and Cora immediately got up to take his place. While the hardened woman didn’t look nearly so teary eyed as Mako did, her face was just a touch softer than it normally was.

“I’m not a big talker, and Lanra respected that. I respected her as a teammate, and the world is going to be a worse place without her. Let’s finish wiping out these bastards who took her from us.”

That got a number of nods and muttered agreements from everyone as Cora swapped out with Jameston. The quiet man gave Donvath a quick look, smiling his thanks as the High Prince nodded.

“Even doing this much is technically illegal within the Noala Kingdom, so I will keep things brief,” Jameston began, projecting his voice directly into their heads with his Mind element. “Lanra wasn’t just a teammate to us. She was a mentor. While we may have had more realm wandering experience than her before she joined our team, she had far more life experience than all three of us combined. She helped us with familial drama, saw to it our contracts were fair to us, and was always there to lend an ear. I will miss her, and I know I am not alone in saying that.”

Wiping a tear from his eye, Jameston bowed before taking his seat. To Asher’s surprise, it was Richard who stood up next.

“I knew the moment I took a look at her soul that I wasn’t skilled enough to save her,” the wizard said quietly, his hands balled into fists by his side as he admitted to his shortcomings. “I did everything in my power to buy her as much time as I could, and while she thanked me for doing so, it doesn’t feel like I did enough. No matter what it takes, I’m going to keep studying and ensure that should anyone else ever come to me with such extensive soul damage, I’ll be able to save them. And I’ll tell them they have her to thank for it.”

Asher could barely believe the pained look on Richard’s face as he sat back down, alone in his pew on the other side of the room. Without even realizing what he was doing, Asher reached out through compressed space and laid a hand on the wizard’s shoulder. Flinching at the sudden touch, Richard followed his arm back to him, giving Asher a sad smile before nodding his thanks.

“While I hadn’t interacted much with Lanra in the flesh, I feel like I know the woman strangely well due to all the potential futures we shared with one another,” Donvath said, sighing as he stared at the casket and shook his head. “It is impossible to truly explain what I see to those without the Time element, but I can say this. I know what an amazing woman she was, and I know that this current timeline will forever be just a little bit duller without her in it.”

“Admittedly, I barely knew Lanra,” Amanda said next, the paladin standing tall in her bright-white armor as she looked around the room. “However, all I need to know is the fact that she volunteered to risk her life to fight back against the Council of Death. That alone means she was not just a great warrior, but a great woman as well. I wish I could have gotten the chance to go on a mission with her and see her in action.”

As Amanda sat down, Groln stood up. The shift from a literally glowing, white-clad paladin to a red-skinned demon with jet-black horns and teeth was quite the contrast, but the sad expression on Groln’s face helped dull his harsh features.

“As someone who did get the chance to see her in action a few years ago, she was a skilled warrior indeed. We worked together to take down a cult at one point that threatened the safety of everyone around them, and I will never forget the speed and efficiency of which she fought. As a royal slayer coming from a bloodline of previous royal slayers, I know talent when I see it. And she had it in spades.”

And with that, before Asher knew it, it was suddenly his turn. Feeling Samantha give his hand one last squeeze, he nodded as he stood up. A small part of him noted that everyone’s eyes were on him, but he didn’t care. His own gaze was locked on the casket sitting up on that stage, and he cleared his throat as he searched for the words that had eluded him all of last night.

“…Lanra saved our lives,” he said, deciding that felt right. “She saved my life. She saved the lives of both her teammates and mine. And by proxy, she saved millions more across the entire continent. When we finally finish off the Council of Death, it will be because of her actions that we manage to do so. I don’t think even something like ‘hero’ is enough to describe what Lanra was. What Lanra is. My biggest regret beyond not being able to save her is that while I don’t think ‘hero’ is powerful enough to truly encompass what she is, I don’t actually think there is a word that fits better.”

Shaking his head, Asher tore his eyes off the casket and looked around the room at the people who had come together to fight back against the clutches of the Council of Death. He saw everything from sadness to determination in the gazes looking back at him, and he smiled as he realized he wasn’t alone in his own turmoil of emotions. A small part of him would always feel partially responsible for what happened regardless of what Lanra said, but a far larger part of him pushed him to keep moving forward. To finish what Lanra had died for. Nodding his thanks toward the coalition, Asher decided to end the ceremony with a promise.

“When all this is said and done. When the last of the liches are returned to the ground where they belong. I hope I can be even half the hero that she is. And I’ll do my damndest to make sure we don’t lose anyone else doing so.”
