Lin Xin was part of the Gymnasium’s cornered army. She had not broken through yet, and Gary hadn’t taken her out of the battle as he had with the others. She remained trapped, rotating on the front lines as the horror army whittled them down. Victory was already impossible. The puppeteer was immortal, and her army only grew. Her only hope—everyone’s hope—was for Satan Jr. to fall before they did.

Which didn’t seem like it would happen. She knew a mild battle when she saw it. Satan was giving it his all, but he was overwhelmed, and his opponents didn’t seem to be trying to kill him.

They want to slaughter us all, she thought. To raise us as tentacle horrors.

She considered ending her life. I’m sorry, grandfather. All our effort, wasted…

Many others shared similar thoughts. People screamed and begged for the gymnasium enforcers to let them escape, but they remained stone-faced. The horrors didn’t attack them. All were at Core Formation. Some people tried running, but while the enforcers held them at bay, the horrors descended on their backs. It was quick slaughter.

Only a few people had escaped. Lin Xin could still see them, watching from beyond the treeline. She felt envy. They’d get to live, while she deserved it more.

Fuck it. I’ll die standing, she thought, jumping to the front lines again. People were crying instead of resisting. Their army fell fast. She saw a friend of hers get raised as a tentacle monster by those horrifying chimeras.

Suddenly, however, an old voice arrested her.

“Everyone!” it called. She turned to find three wizened men standing in the middle of the army. Perhaps they’d always been there. She recognized them—Coaches Bayonet, Pontara, and Kamowaga. They were Level 50 field bosses in name, but she’d never seen them fight. She assumed it was an honorary rank.

“We coaches have convened. It is time to throw in the towel,” Coach Bayonet said.

“We do not control the enforcers. They were put here by the System to box in the battle. But we do control ourselves,” Coach Pontara said.

“Since it’s over, we will not let the youngsters die under our watch,” Coach Kamowaga said.

Suddenly, all three men erupted with aura. Their bodies grew. From little old men, they returned to their prime, fierce warriors of boxing, wrestling, and callisthenics. Their rank also increased to Sector Boss, and the word Prime was added to the start of their names.

“We will cover your retreat. Go,” Prime Coach Baoynet said in a powerful, youthful voice.

“Coaches!” the beef boys screamed.

The coaches laughed. “Why are you looking at us like that? Isn’t it the duty of the previous generation to give their lives for the next one?” Coach Pontara said.

“I want to loosen my fists one last time. Get out of our way! Kill those enforcers!” Coach Bayonet barked.

A wave of sentimentalism washed through the army. Even Lin Xin was touched. She brought a fist to her heart and bowed. “Thank you,” she whispered.

Others weren’t so elegant. “Thank you, coaches!” the beef boys screamed, tears and snot covering their faces.

“You’re still here? Run, you ungrateful weaklings!” Coach Kamowaga said.

The army abandoned the front line and poured, as a wave, towards the enforcers, who rallied to hold the line. It was a battle, but without the horrors falling on their backs, they could win. Before clashing, Lin Xin spared a final glance back, where three men’s broad backs held an entire army of monsters.

Gary staged a fighting retreat through the battlefield to the puppeteer’s base. Jin kept step. Daggers rained, stabs and slashes, and it took all of Gary’s Speed and Vitality to not just die. Whenever he thunderstepped away, he briefly entered his weapon’s ideal range. Jin quickly dove into his guard again, but every such time was an opening. As swift as Jin was, he wasn’t uninjured, though the few wounds he’d sustained were nothing before Gary’s, who was constantly under threat of the dagger.

Large wounds were rare. Light ones were constantly drawn across his body, then slowly regenerated.

“What rich regeneration,” Jin said mid-battle. “We were tempted to take it ourselves, you know. Give it to Alpha, make him a beast. But making an enemy of the puppeteer is unwise.”

“Cowards,” Gary barked, half-panting, half-laughing.

Jin paused for a moment. “We didn’t grow up coddled like you, Gary Steel. Where we come from, caution is the only path to survival.”

“Why do you think I grew up coddled?”

“Because you’re reckless, and you’re still alive.”

Gary paused, but there was no time to reply before Jin was on him. They kept clashing, a maelstrom of steel and violence through the battlefield. Besides them and the place where Satan fought the puppeteer and sector bosses, the entire battle was confined to the walls of Satan Gym, where the tentacle army was slowly but surely crushing the gymnasium’s. Enforcers still surrounded the distance, and they were strong. Nobody was leaving. Turning to fight them would expose their backs to the horrors. They had to fight to the death.

Gary was three quarters through the battlefield when he first thought he wouldn’t make it. His heart was beating like a drum. He was in pain and exhausted. His mind screamed at him that he was dying.

But that’s what he’d trained for. All the times pushing his limit with Kyle by the lake, with Master Colorful, by himself in the Steps of Power, they all prepared him for this moment. His body thought it was dying—an illusion. A reflex. He forced his way through, ignoring that voice. His true limit was still far-off. He could do this.

They danced together, heading for Gary’s target. If Jin realized it, he didn’t try to stop it. They entered the base. Suddenly, the clamor of battle was cut off, as were Satan’s roars. They were in the dark and silent. Their weapons echoed louder.

Gary had been inside the puppeteer’s base before, but one time was before they teleported, and he hadn’t had time to look around the second. Now, he could see broken trees jutting through the floor where the base had landed on top of the forest. The machinery and pipes were a complete mess. Purple liquid pooled in places, dripping from high-up or fountaining from somewhere below. The corpses of unhatched chimeras littered the floor, deformed in their ugliness.

Gary escaped desperately through the room, hoping not to die too early. The corridor was just at its end.

“We are not equals, Gary Steel,” Jin said. “No amount of tricks can save you.”

Gary wedged his back into the corridor. It was less than ten feet wide, greatly limiting him in how he swung the spear, but it also prevented Jin from attacking from the sides. If he wanted to reach Gary, he had to come straight—where the spear would find it easy to hold the line.

That was the plan.

Gary waited, spear at the ready, panting heavily with his heart about to leap out his chest. Jin observed him. “You have tenacity, but not much else,” he said.

“I have your mother.”

A dry laugh escaped the assassin’s throat. “You’re strong, Gary Steel, but you’re not like us. You’ll never be like us. We’ve sacrificed everything to stand here, and that’s something a drafter could never match. We deserve to win.”

“How about you cut the drama and try to kill me already?”

“As you wish.”

His daggers vibrated. They’d always made a low buzz, but now it was louder, like cat-sized bees. Gary was confident the assassin couldn’t approach.

Jin didn’t even try. He stood at the mouth of the corridor, daggers humming, his entire body vibrating to the tune of gears. The machinery he’d cut into his body, making him a cyborg, operated at full power.

Suddenly, Jin launched into a crazed dance. He was a blur. His daggers cut wildly through the air, and then their trajectories remained, flying towards Gary like highly concentrated aura strikes. The corridor turned blue with almost overlapping slashes. It reminded Gary of playing Beat Saber, except faster, harder, and vastly more lethal.

The slashes crashed into him. He moved as rapidly as possible, reacting on pure instinct, but he knew immediately that he couldn’t hold. He retreated. The strikes were faster, and they fell on his body, carving him up. As soon as he left the corridor’s confines, he jumped aside, letting the blue river of strikes flow past him. It stopped coming almost immediately. Jin had been so certain he’d already stopped dancing and exited the corridor himself, calmly, only moments after. His daggers still buzzed. So did his body, machines hidden under that leather armor.

Gary was wildly torn up. The front of his body was riddled with shallow wounds, so many that his regeneration had sputtered, working at less than half its usual speed. He eyed Jin, his jaw set stubbornly, his gaze searching for escape. They stood in the room where Gary had fought the swordsman before. The massive door where the puppeteer had hid loomed open, revealing only darkness, while trees jutted out the floor and fleshy pipes stretched over the top half of the room. All pulsed.

“Why are you so strong?” Gary said.

“Being twelve levels over you helps,” Jin replied.

“Don’t bullshit me. I have other stuff to make up for the levels.”

“And I don’t? I’ve trained since I could walk, Gary Steel. I’ve been securing advantages every step of the way. Maybe I don’t have the Seed of Eternal Flesh, but you’re an idiot if you think I don’t have a dozen other reasons to be stronger than you.”

Gary shook his head. “I killed the ball guy from a full realm below. He wasn’t as strong as you. You don’t even specialize in one-on-one combat. Are you the leader of the brothers? Is that why you came to fight me alone?”

Jin chuckled. “Moe is the leader, and Alpha is the strongest in straight combat. Zink wasn’t weaker than me—you ambushed him in a realm where everyone possesses very limited defenses. He had his own specialty. Had he been here, his spike balls would have killed hundreds of your allies.”

“So you’re all as strong as Feng Shen? I call bullshit.”

“We’re the last hope of a dying land. We may be rogue cultivators, but we’ve received more resources than anyone on the Clan Floor, we’ve climbed mountains of corpses, and we shoulder a weight you cannot imagine. We are not allowed to lose, Gary Steel. That’s why we’re strong.”

“If we’re talking about shouldering stuff, I got more than you. An entire planet stands behind my back. I can’t lose either.”

“Then let’s see whose resolve is stronger,” Jin said. His daggers glowed again. He was on Gary in an instant, stabbing and slashing, and Gary grunted as he was pushed back. He’d delayed for as long as he could, but he hadn’t recovered. The exhaustion remained. Wounds gaped on his body. His arms were heavy, but Jin hadn’t slowed one bit.

For the first time in a while, Gary registered the weight of his spear.

He had no plan anymore. The corridor had been a bust. No one was coming to save him, and there was nothing around that he could use.

He just had to win.

The wounds and exhaustion were growing too much. He could no longer keep up. Releasing a burst of aura to earn himself a respite, Gary summoned the Death’s Dance pill and popped it in his mouth. Warmth radiated from his stomach immediately. He was on fire. He could do this all day.

Gary dove into battle with renewed rage. His muscles burned, but his heart burned hotter, and the pill let him forget about the pain and exhaustion for a while. He accelerated. His spear cut through the air, a flowing dance which smoothly met all of Jin’s advances. Gary held his ground. Aura streamed from his core. A dozen clashes occurred mid-air, black meeting neon blue, littering the air between them with sparks. Jin moved like a shadow, never in the same place twice, but Gary was catching onto his rhythm by now. He could keep up. All he needed was a single hit. To grab the assassin’s arm. Then, it would be over.

But he couldn’t do it. As much as he accelerated, as much as he tried, fuming at the mouth, Jin was always faster. Gary roared.

Suddenly, Jin dove and slashed at Gary’s legs. It was the first time he attacked below the belt in the entire battle. Gary didn’t expect it. He’d been conditioned. He jumped with all the force he could muster, taking a deep gash just below the knee. His jump took him higher than expected. He almost crashed into the ceiling. On the way down, he grabbed onto one of the pipes spearing through the room’s upper half, saving himself from whatever blue death Jin was preparing below.

With a light hop, Jin arrived on the pipe opposite Gary. They pulsed beneath their feet, barely wide enough for a person to stand on. They were all slanted, too, and stretching in random directions.

Gary gritted his teeth, furious with himself. “Why didn’t you assassinate me during the large-scale battle?” he asked.

“I never had a good shot. You were surrounded by your allies,” Jin replied. “Even if I could, I prefer it this way. It costs me nothing, and I get more satisfaction. I owed it to Zink.”

“Zink this, Zink that. I know he was your brother, but drop the act already. You guys are cutthroat backstabbers. You’d throw him under for coin.”

This, finally, got some emotion out of Jin. His aura pulsed with anger. “I loved him, Gary Steel. You cannot imagine my grief. Everyone’s grief. We all rose from the bottom together, fought side-by-side, suffered and won together. We were meant to reach the top of the tower and fight for our people, not die to a random Energy Harnessing drafter’s sneak attack. He didn’t deserve it. It makes his entire struggle pointless. You have no idea of the evil you caused to the world.”

“You are the evil, mate. You’re blind if you don’t see it.”

Jin chuckled darkly. “We are evil?”

“I heard what you did on the Floor of Trials. Having a sad childhood doesn’t give you the right to torture and murder people. Zink wasn’t the only one who died, either. What about Kyle, you fucking asshole? I’m fighting for him. I’m glad you came to me, because now I can have my revenge. I don’t give a shit about your goals. I’ll slaughter you all, one brother at a time.”

Darkness entered Jin’s blue aura. It rose as physical flames around his body, starting at his feet and rising over his shoulders. “I planned to enjoy this, Gary Steel, but I suddenly want to crush you,” he said. “You asked me how I can be this strong when I don’t specialize in straight combat. You’re right—I don’t. I specialize in assassinations. I thought doing it that way would be unsatisfying, but you changed my mind.”

“You can’t assassinate someone who knows it’s coming, idiot.”

“Watch me,” Jin said. The flames of his aura dove into his body. Not a hint remained. In fact, Gary suddenly couldn’t perceive the man in his aura field, though he stood right there. He had no idea how it was possible.

The persistent buzzing of Jin’s daggers and body stopped. Silence reigned. Unnatural, eerie silence. Gary realized his eyes were slipping off the assassin’s form.

He blinked to focus, and Jin was no longer there.

“Shit,” he said.
