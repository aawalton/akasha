Show me what that spell was.

Immediately the screen blinked awake in front of my eyes, Trickery displaying the exact spell that had just flared in the room above.

[Teleportation Shift]

A short-range displacement spell engineered for instant movement. Anchor-based targeting: the caster pre-sets a destination rune, then displaces to that rune on activation.

I read the description and finally understood how that book could have ended up in the Sun Inquisitors’ underground hideout. I still did not know the diary’s intent, only that it was in the way — between me and my peace.

I could not, for Julia’s sake, let her go after it; that would only escalate things into something worse. And besides, this time it felt like the book was the one being drawn to me, not the other way around. Why? No reason I trusted, only a sense.

[It certainly seems like it.]

Trickery confirmed the feeling. I watched Luna and Julia go over their hunting plans, both confident they might be sniffing out something right above their heads. Fine by me.

I left them to bicker over useless specifics and climbed the stairs in a slow, stalking rhythm. I did not explain anything because what if my foxy attempt at explaining scared the book, and I did not want to give it an excuse to vanish. If I found that diary, I promised myself I would shred it, piece by careful piece. I crept back toward the room where I had sat earlier and peered in. The ping’s source seemed to be the bookshelf directly behind the wall I was skulking along.

There were practical problems to address. The diary could teleport, so cornering it might be pointless if it popped away the moment I closed distance. That possibility meant I had to learn how its shift worked before attempting a trap.

I asked Trickery whether it could show me what kinds of modifications the observed teleportation spell could accept. And…

[Here’s the list.]

[Enchantment Modification Interface: Teleportation Shift][Target Spell Structure: Tier III Spatial Displacement][Available Modifications:]

The fourth option was tempting, but I could not be sure the book would be able to return from the Spirit World if it went there; I needed the book to believe it was safe only for that illusion to break later. Spatial Loopback fit my goals best.

I told Trickery to apply Spatial Loopback the moment the diary attempted any teleportation.

[Ooh, a pre-defined modification… I certainly can do that. It will ensure the alteration is applied without delay because some spells are instantaneous.]

That exchange exposed a question I had neglected to ask earlier. ‘Can’t you pick and use the modifications yourself, analyse the situation, and deploy whichever is most favourable?’ I asked. ‘Our definitions of ‘favourable’ line up, except that one time you made me choose an option with only a sixty-four percent chance.’

[Well, again, the numbers represent how confident I am in allowing those modifications to happen. In a sense, they measure my expertise— how well I can interpret a spell’s structure, enchantment, or ritual. Anything above fifty percent means the alteration will likely occur without issue.

My calculations include countless unseen factors, so the odds might look like a coin toss, but they’re not. Less of a chance and more like a confidence gauge, not a dice roll. As for the modifications, remember— I am your power. I can advise or suggest, but I can’t alter anything unless you command it. That’s how I’m built.]

I nodded mentally. Made sense.

‘So, hypothetically, if you learn more spells, we can pre-set modifications with my approval and you could then deploy them automatically in rapid succession, right?’

[It’s a good strategy.]

I grinned, pleased with myself. I made a mental note to spend time programming preset modifications for every spell Trickery had analyzed so far. Maybe even tag certain triggers, spells I wanted handled automatically versus ones I’d review manually for different results.

I nodded again to myself. Trickery was my only real power for now, and if I wanted to have a peaceful life, I needed to squeeze every ounce of efficiency out of it.

With that settled, I finally entered the room— only to spot a faint, book-shaped shadow darting across the floor. It blurred the moment I appeared, then vanished under the bed. My eyebrow twitched in irritation.

A ping pulsed in my head. Before I could react, my vision drowned in a surge of black— an eerie, writhing dark that felt like it was alive, brushing and coiling against my white fur.

[Abyssal Flash]

A blinding spell formed from condensed dark mana.

Oh, wonderful. It had come here just to blind me? Not happening.Show me the modifications.

[Enchantment Modification Interface: Abyssal Flash]The narrative has been illicitly obtained; should you discover it on Amazon, report the violation.[Target Spell Structure: Tier I Darkness Pulse][Available Modifications:]

Well, if it was going to throw spells at me, I might as well take the free buffs. This book needed putting down, fast.

Immediately, the crawling abyss shattered around me, dissolving into smoky wisps that flowed inward. Darkness folded against my body, absorbed, and I felt a sharp surge of energy racing through my limbs. I grinned, letting it flood my veins.

I locked in Mana Drain Feedback as a preset modification, so Trickery would automatically trigger it instead of pulling up full menus mid-fight.

Then I lunged at the shadow under the bed. A startled screech echoed out, hehe, music to my ears.

It was the book! Found you, you little piece of shit! Did you really think screeching would save you from what’s coming? Its pages flickered wildly, flashing through intricate artistic patterns that I could only guess were spell structures mixed with text I couldn’t read.

Before I could pounce and tear it apart, the damn thing began glowing— and in the blink of an eye, it vanished.Teleportation spell again. Instantaneous this time. I couldn’t have chosen a modification that fast even if I wanted to.

But my earlier setup paid off. The book reappeared right where it had vanished, courtesy of the Spatial Loopback I’d prepared.

Only to find my crimson eyes blazing with a promise of pain. It let out another pitiful screech as I lunged, tearing a portion of its cover free before it comically scrambled away, leaving the torn piece in my mouth.

Despite the absurdity of a fleeing book, it was unnervingly fast, its form blurring as pages turned and text glowed. I knew it was preparing another spell, but it most certainly was not running away from here tonight. Over the bed, over the shelf, it finally made a leap for the window and escaped outside.

I was hot on its heels. Its doom was merely delayed, mark my words!

But the moment I leapt through the window after it, its pages flared again, the telltale glow of another cast.

[Gravitic Pin]

Localized gravity spike designed to immobilize targets within a two-meter radius.

I didn’t even need to ask this time. Trickery instantly pulled up the options.

[Enchantment Modification Interface: Gravitic Pin][Target Spell Structure: Tier II Force-Field Compression][Available Modifications:]• Axis Inversion (Flip gravity’s direction in the zone; ceiling becomes floor): 88% Success• Singularity Flicker (Convert force into a harmless implosion of air pressure): 92% Success• Target Swap (Caster becomes primary target): 91% Success• Weight Redistribution (Apply gravitational effect to inanimate objects only): 97% Success

“Well then,” I muttered, “time to give the book a taste of its own spellcraft.”

I pre-set Target Swap for that spell, and the moment it triggered, the result was immediate, the book, now under a nearby tree, let out another pitiful screech as gravity slammed it into the ground.

It broke free quickly, canceling the spell almost as soon as it realized what happened, but that didn’t matter. I was already on it.

I pounced and shredded more of its pages, scattering glowing script into the air. Yet somehow, it still had the will to keep running.

With another desperate flutter, it launched a new spell, instantaneous again, too quick for Trickery to intercept.

[Wind Burst]

Directional push spell designed to generate a burst of speed and temporary agility boost.

The gust hit, and the book shot forward like a missile, gaining distance faster than I could close it.

I could only give chase as it put more and more distance between us. Just how many irritating spells were crammed into its binding?!?

[Enchantment Modification Interface: Wind Burst][Target Spell Structure: Tier II Aerokinetic Pulse][Available Modifications:]• Vortex Curl (Reverse flow, slowing the caster instead): 95% Success• Pressure Scatter (Redirect gust into harmless air pockets): 94% Success• Wind Misalignment (Convert burst into a sonic shockwave instead of buffing the caster): 92% Success• Scent Amplification (Spread caster’s scent over a 50-meter radius): 100% Success

Well, Wind Misalignment it was.

I grinned as the triumphant little book sprinted ahead— right before a distorted gust erupted beneath it, flipping it head-over-spine with a satisfying thump. The book was thrown back by its own spell, flapping helplessly like it had no idea what just happened.

I pounced, shredding a few more pages for good measure before it pulled another desperate trick.

Honestly, I was maybe enjoying this game of cat and mouse a bit more than I’d expected.
