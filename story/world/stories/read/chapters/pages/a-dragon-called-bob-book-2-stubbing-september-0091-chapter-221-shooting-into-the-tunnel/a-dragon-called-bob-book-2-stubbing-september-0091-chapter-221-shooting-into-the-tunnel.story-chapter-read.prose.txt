Chants of “Rock and Stone!” came through the stone door that separated the living quarters from the start of the battlefield against the Deep One. They were mixed with screams of pain and anger.

Granvil shoved the door aside and ducked even harder than his pre-ducked stature automatically granted. A spike of something bony struck me in the chest and clattered to the ground. It was about a foot long and had spiny barbs running down one side.

“Another bloody tunic ruined, alright—what the…” I was cut off by my lone bunnyborg breaking into a familiar tune about war, and what exactly it might be good for, shoving me aside and charging into the melee I could see beyond the doorway.

Mechs and armoured dwarves were locked in combat. Axes swung down to cleave shadowy forms in two, blasts of fire from the flamethrowers spurted out to cast a suddenly glaring illumination across the battlefield, and mechanical claws snipped closed to chop monsters in half.

“For the Stone!” bellowed a redheaded dwarf as he charged into a shadow monster a few feet from the door. His axe was blocked by a thin arm and turned aside. A tendril of darkness lunged out of the thing's chest to take the dwarf in the neck, parting his beard and painting it a deeper red.

“Spiritus Manus! Spiritus Manus! Spiritus Manus! Spiritus Manus!”

As my mage hands materialised, the bunny borg sliced through the bunnyborg, bisecting the shadow with a downward slash of a wrist blade, and as it chanted “Huh!” and the tip of its mana cannon began to glow. The scarlett line cut a swath through the Deep One’s many bodies, carefully avoiding the dwarves in the process.

Bighorn chose that moment to bellow in rage, scrape one hoof against the hard stone of the floor, and charge forward. Where he had been hiding his own axe, I wasn’t sure, but it was in his hands as he dashed in and began laying waste to anything that wasn’t a dwarf, a terrified human, or a terrifying bunnyborg.

“Scutum Iris! Scutum Iaculis!” The rainbow shield appeared and moved to protect Bighorn as I stepped into the first cavern.

This place had been safe the first time I’d visited the dwarves. The frontline had been two caverns deeper into the complex. A snivelling sound from just behind the door made me turn.

“Did you piss yourself again?”

“Noooo…” Tex said.

“I thought you’d have gotten over that after I took you flying.”

“You didn’t ‘take me flying’. You dangled from a claw while you flew up to your bloody aerie like I was a rabbit grabbed by a bloody eagle!”

“Pfft. Eagles suck compared to dragons.” As I spoke, I was guiding my rainbow shield about to intercept any ranged attacks aimed at Bighorn, and using the four mage hands to interfere with the Deep One’s bodies. Catching arms and legs to stumble them and buy opportunities for the dwarves to land blows or escape getting chomped. “Dragons are much… fucking… cooler.”

Running five separate extensions of my mind was challenging. But I was a clever dragon, and I managed it. Being able to ignore the odd spine that pinged off my chest made things easier.

“I’m not going in there!” Tex squeaked.

“Fine, just… Lurk…”

I rolled the stones in my left hand with my thumb and opened four portals. Jumping forward, I exhaled a long blast of dragon's fire to clear my immediate surroundings as more mark nine bunnyborgs rushed up behind me.

Dammit. I was instantly bloated again. I’d only burned a couple of dozen of the smaller ones.

“You guys crack on.” I suppressed a belch. The bunnyborgs went to work, no doubt disappointed that because the Deep One’s bodies dissolved into dust, they had no chance to make their macabre statues.

If the God of Music was gradually infecting my entire army of bunnies, what the hell did that say about him? Jacko may have had a bit of a bad rep towards the end, depending on who you asked, but corpse art was definitely part of it. Unless I missed some pretty major news before I died.

Master of Puppets once more came into its own. I didn’t need to order them to leave the dwarves alone. The robobunnies just worked around them, and as the steel-coloured tide of carrot-paste-slurping doom rolled past the dwarves, they dropped to their knees or arses and gasped for breath.

Blasts from the shoulder-mounted cannons cut towards the tunnel to the next room, and my minions proceeded to clear the way. I nodded in satisfaction. Tex was merely urine-soaked, Bighorn had a few scratches, but I’d blocked the worst of the range attacks thrown at him. I tossed a healing spell in his direction and stepped forward to play healer for Granvil.

As the green glow covered the dwarf from head to toe, not exactly a considerable distance, he rolled over and removed his hands from his eyes. “Did we win?”

“Your contribution was noted. Do you want a towel?” I growled.

A round of ragged cheers went up from the surviving dwarves as they picked themselves up and began rebuilding their battered fortifications. Their own healers passed among them, dispensing glowing lights and green sparkles that sealed wounds in their wake.

When the noise died down, Granvil possibly-scowled at me. His eyes narrowed, noticeably reducing the already small gap between his eyebrows and bushy moustache. “No. Gravel and fire-gas!” These appeared to be very rude words among the dwarves, as a couple of the closest ones turned and hissed at him. “The Deep One is going to fuck us so hard after you’re done here. Where the hell did the bunnyborgs go?”

“The next cavern. Where’s that human? What’s-his-name?”

“Siege-Captain Jones is engaged on another front. I’ll tell him you asked after him.”

“Don’t bother, Granvil.” I set off towards the passage to the next chamber, and the dwarf followed me. “We won’t need any more support here. So, which way to the Damp Passage?”

“A mile straight on, take a left, then a right, then straight on for half a mile. Maybe a dozen more chambers along the way. The Damp Passage lies just beyond.”

We entered the connecting tunnel, the walls lined with glowing lines from mana-cannons cutting through the shadow things and melting the walls. I flicked out my tongue, and the air tasted acrid, burnt stone mixed with something else, a byproduct of the amorphic flesh of the monster being burnt.

“As you can see, Granvil—Where the hell is Bighorn?” I spun around and spotted the Minotaur doing bicep curls with a heavily armoured dwarf in each hand. “BIGHORN! Put them down and get over here!”

The dwarves booed at me as Bighorn left them behind, but I just flipped them off and turned towards the future. A future that involved a damp passage, and not the good kind. Bloody demons and their bloody bargains. My summons drifted along, floating above my head.

The bunnyborgs had already fought their way across the cavern when we poked our heads out into it. Bighorn led the way with his big horns and ridiculous axe that he once again produced from a very small loincloth.

“Bunbuns is fast.” He stamped a foot in frustration.

“They’ll slow down. Save your strength, big guy.”

He snorted in response and slapped a hoof on Granvil’s shoulder, sending the dwarf stumbling forward. “I like your sort, not so hairless, and tough.”

“Thank,” Granvil muttered, straightening and glaring around nervously. The bunnies had indeed done their jobs properly, and nothing remained to leap out at him.

We made our way to the next tunnel, trailing along behind the sounds of silent carnage. The thrumming noises from the mana-cannons, the stamping of metallic feet, and the occasional cheerful whistling of an eighties rock classic echoed back and forth in the cavern. My cyborgs didn’t scream or chant, and the Deep One’s bodies fought with deathly silence.

Blue light flared when a shield activated, and I pointed out the way they blocked the attacks and saved the chassis from harm. Granvil grunted in what I hoped was appreciation.

The last of the monsters fell, and my minions charged off again down the next tunnel. I pulled the rainbow shield down in front of us and stepped onto it tentatively. A couple of jumps ensured it wouldn’t budge unless I willed it. I looked over at my companions.

Despite his short stature, Granvil was coated head to foot in chainmail and thick plate armour. And Bighorn was… well, he was big. “Get on.” Only one way to find out.

The shield didn’t move for the dwarf, but when Bighorn stepped aboard, it dipped slightly. Not much, but the strain of the thing on my mind increased. But I was a dragon, and the magic would bend to my will.

We sped along at a low hover. I hummed a few bars of that song from Aladdin, then stopped when Bighorn started mooing along as well.

The bunnyborg slaughter continued. Dark wraiths dissolving as they charged ahead. As soon as we started flying, the damn things picked up their pace even more, increasing the distance as we chased after them.

“You lost one!” Granvil yelled in my ear, having to jump up to do so and almost losing his footing as he landed.

I slowed the carpet and glared at him, but he was right. One of my bunny soldiers had been sliced apart. The limbs were cleanly severed from the torso, and the armoured head had been pincushioned with so many spines it looked as if it had tried to audition for Hellraiser.

Hopping down, I gathered up the parts and put them in storage. All the materials could be recycled by Tim back at the dungeon, and I had no shortage of prospective pilots. I wondered if Bun-Bun, vicious progenitor of all my troopers, would have approved of the fate of his children? I concluded he probably would. The little guy had loved nothing more than violence and murder.

Setting aside my fond recollections, I resumed my place, and we set off again. Losing one of their brothers hadn’t bothered the robobunnies at all. The sound of violence and seventies funk songs kept moving ahead of us. We passed four more caverns, all cleared of Deep One bodies, then Granvil grabbed my wrist and tugged.

“That way! The path will fork a short distance in, then again. We go right, then left.”

My army came charging back from where they had pushed on past the side tunnel and disappeared into it without pausing. We followed more sedately, the low ceiling posing an issue due to the uneven floor, which forced me to fly a little higher. The dwarf and I were fine, naturally, but Bighorn was getting annoyed about scraping his horns on the roof.

We took the right passage, then the left, and continued down it in pursuit of my murderous minions. After a few hundred metres, it opened up again into a cavern, and I skidded to a stop. Then immediately sent the improvised magic carpet shooting upwards to avoid a barrage of bone spikes.

“Oh shit, oh shit, oh shit,” moaned Granvil.

“FIGHT!” bellowed Bighorn as he jumped down and charged into the fray.

It turned out that we’d only seen the most basic of the Deep Ones many forms.
