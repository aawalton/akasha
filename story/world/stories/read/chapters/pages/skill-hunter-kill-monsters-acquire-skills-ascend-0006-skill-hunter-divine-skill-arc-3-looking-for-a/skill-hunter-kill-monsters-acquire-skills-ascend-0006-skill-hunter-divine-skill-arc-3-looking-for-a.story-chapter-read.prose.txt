Out of the tavern, his feet pointed toward the open road, Ike hurried along at speed. Occasionally, he played the recording back again. Most of the hints were totally indecipherable, but a few of them gave a general impression of the environment around the skill, which hinted at which region they could be found in. Flicking through the recording, he paused it, then smirked. I know where you are.

It would’ve been a good place to start, if he wasn’t pretty sure the monster in question would kill him immediately. The last time he’d encountered it, it had still been far beyond his Rank. In any case, that monster was well hidden from the world. He didn’t have to prioritize it, because there was little chance someone else would find or kill it before he got the chance to.

Of the rest of the recordings, he didn’t recognize most of the environments, except that none of them were his homeland. They were all in places he hadn’t seen before, in regions he hadn’t yet explored. He grinned at the thought of it: new places to explore, and new things to see.

His smile drooped a little, and he touched his shoulder. His companions were gone, and he had no idea where they were. He wanted to find them, but he lacked even the slightest hint as to their location. Unlike everyone else, who’d been beamed a picture of his face, he hadn’t been beamed a picture of Wisp or Mag, his old friends. I guess they know where I am? They could find me.

He paused for a second, then sighed, bowing his head. They won’t. They could, but they won’t. He’d have to go find them.

It was fine. He had to go find the other six monsters and beasts anyways. Might as well add two more to the list. And then he snorted. If Wisp was here, she’d never let me live it down that I’m the only human on a list of beasts. She was always trying to call him a beast anyways, just because he could use aether, the energy beasts used, which she’d taught him how to use, incidentally. Now it was all irrelevant. Everyone used mana, both beasts and humans. There wasn’t a basis to accuse him of being a beast any longer.

Not that that’ll stop her.

He stopped by the town square on his way out of town and handed the heads over in return for some coins. This region used small gold coins as its currency, each one about half the size he was used to, but twice as thick. This small town’s bounty counter seemed a bit overwhelmed by the sheer mass of heads he produced. “You could have just brought the leader’s head,” he commented, staring at the heads.

“I didn’t know which one was the leader,” Ike replied.

“You… just happened upon the Dark Skull gang and slaughtered them all? Coincidentally?”

Ike shrugged. “Yeah?”

The man shook his head. “Well, I suppose we’re grateful for your service either way. They’ve been a real pain around these parts lately.”

Ike turned, then paused and walked back. He showed the blueprints. “I also found this. Do you know who would want it?”

“That—those are the blueprints to the manor in Quinton, the big city down the road. Where do you find them?”

“The gang had ‘em.”

The man paused. He pinched his chin. “Huh. I don’t know who you’d turn those in to. I’m not the guy; there isn’t a bounty for them. You could try the manor’s guards? They might want them.”

Ike nodded. He put the blueprints back away. “Thanks.”

“No problem. Keep up the good work. Bounty board’s right there on the right, if you want to pick up a new one on the way out.”

Ike glanced toward the board, then shrugged. “I’ll just wing it.” He didn’t really like being told what to do, or deliberately searching for work. He’d do it when he stumbled upon it, but he wasn’t going to go out of his way to kill people or monsters on the bounty board.

Once, he had. When he was young, and a brand new mage, he’d done it. Picked jobs off boards and completed them. But now, he didn’t really care about money. He didn’t need food or water, not even the way other mages suddenly did; he was competent enough at fighting to go out and acquire mana through monsters if he needed to. His robes were still in working shape, and if he wanted to sleep, he could find a tree to sleep in or under. As for equipment, well, he didn’t know where his sword had wandered off to, but he could work with his fists if he lacked a sword, and right now, using the sword he’d stolen from the ruffians was good enough for him. Given all that, the money he’d received from killing this game would last him a long while.

“It’s been working out for ya, so I guess I can’t complain.” The man got to work storing the heads, and Ike headed off again, leaving the small town behind.

A divine skill. It was starting to sink in: he had a divine skill. There was only one skill on his list worth considering, so he knew what it was immediately: Stormclad, the skill that had evolved from all his lightning skills. His lightning skills had all descended from the Unique skill Lightning Step. That skill, his very first, was the selfsame thing that was now causing him so much trouble. Or, looked at from another direction: putting him one step ahead of his rivals in the hunt for divine skills.

He couldn’t help but wonder: how much did Brightbriar know when he’d let Lightning Step fall into Ike’s hands? Had he known that Lightning Step possessed the qualifications to evolve into a divine skill? Or was it already divine from the beginning? Had he deliberately allowed Ike to receive a divine skill, or had he been in the dark about the skill’s true nature as much as Ike had been?

Guess I’ll never know.

He pulled up the recording just to be sure, but he truly didn’t recognize any of the environments. That was fine. Quinton was just ahead, and according to the bounty board man, it was a big city. Someone there was bound to recognize the environments, and he could use their knowledge to point himself in the right direction. If they didn’t want to tell him where the monsters were, then he could always resort to force. That, and a big city was more likely to know where his friends had ended up, if they were in the vicinity. If nothing else, the bounty board would probably have a post or two about them. Especially Wisp. Hell, if there isn’t a post or two about Wisp, I’ll be surprised.

The road between the small town and Quinton was well traveled, and a great many people walked alongside Ike. Most of them wore similar loose, flowy clothing, or had tight, short clothes and weathered skin. Ike roughly fit in with the first, in his robes and ragged hood. Still, the style of his robes stood out, clearly not a style that was common in this region. He’d already figured out that he was in another region, but wherever he was, it had to be far from home if the style of robes was not only this different, but also if he couldn’t see anyone else wearing something like what he wore. It meant he was the only person around from his home region.

It wasn’t surprising. He hadn’t known of any deserts in the vicinity of his home region, save Mag’s sea of bones, which wasn’t a typical sandy desert. There were plenty of wastelands, but not hot, arid desert. The temperature differential alone from his home region told him he’d come a long way, but the clothing confirmed it.

Maybe Wisp and Mag aren’t even here. Depending on how far he’d come, they might legitimately be so far away that he wouldn’t encounter them again.

Ike frowned. He didn’t like that. Hopefully he was wrong, or he’d brought them here with him. He probably had, unless they’d wandered off. He would’ve brought them here, but it was very possible they’d just decided to do their own thing for a while.

Then again, he also didn’t know why or how he’d ended up here. He could’ve been teleported across the continent by a trap, or carried through the sky by some kind of giant bird or dragon or something. As far as he knew, a beam of light had descended from the heavens and popped him over here, leaving the other two behind. It would be an impressive beam of light that managed to separate him from his two companions, at least one of whom usually rode on his shoulders, head, or both, but who knew? He didn’t. Anything was possible.

A grand city rose up on the horizon. Mages flew around it, and an entire mortal settlement sprawled from its feet, the richest mortal buildings pressed up against the mage city’s outer walls. Ike sped up at the sight of it. If his instincts were right, the answers to his questions would be found within the city.

He didn’t use his skill to speed up, but just his bodily might. He didn’t want to draw attention to himself when he had one of seven divine skills and everyone was on the hunt for them. Not being able to exert his strength sucked, but then, being in civilization also sucked. The faster he got done with Quinton and got back on the road, the better.

He reached the city in no time. No one was guarding the gate, which was fortunate. He didn’t know what he would’ve done if they demanded people show their faces at the gate; he was fresh out of severed heads, and besides, he didn’t think intimidating the gate guards was the way to get through the door. They were more likely to attack, and while he could probably kill hypothetical gate guards, that did put him in the unfortunate place of having to slaughter his way through the city. People didn’t do much talking when they were avoiding being slaughtered, and he did, unfortunately, need to talk to people right now.

What a low point in my life. Having to talk to people, Ike thought, shaking his head. He smiled a little, amused. If this was the greatest struggle facing him right now, then surely he wasn’t doing too poorly.

It wasn’t true. The divine skills thing alone and his missing companions were both much greater struggles. But for a moment, he could pretend, and that was enough to lighten the load.

He passed through the gates and into the city proper. Something buzzed against his skin as he stepped through, and he glanced up. A barrier? It wasn’t stopping him, so clearly it wasn’t meant to impede him. He shrugged and kept going. He’d figure it out, or he wouldn’t if it didn’t matter. For now, he needed to find knowledge.

Knowledge. Ike paused. Where did one find knowledge? Shops. Bounty boards and the likes, obviously. Other people. He pinched his chin, thinking hard, but that was the end of it. This is what I need great minds like Wisp and Mag for. He hesitated, mentally simulating the conversation, then sighed. No wait, never mind. They wouldn’t know. They’re all idiots like me.

Without another idea, he headed toward the bounty board. That, at least, he knew how to find. The rest of it could follow.
