By the time the first hints of morning began to seep in three days later, every canal, bridge, street, market, and half-drowned chapel across Wraithpier had already come alive in the Leviathan’s Wake.

Drums pounded from somewhere three districts away with enough enthusiasm to qualify as artillery. Brass horns answered them from the rooftops. Bells rang without rhythm. People shouted drinking songs across canals while families hauled entire barrels of bait onto personal barges, and everywhere Gael looked, little boats were pushing away from every household’s private docks in clusters. There were children firing little paper rockets over the water, old fishermen throwing cups of alcohol into the canals ‘for the drowned’, and at least a dozen funeral processions carrying mounted fish skulls. Only the Saintess knew why a lively celebration like this was considered a fortuitous day for water burials, but what did Gael really care, anyways?

It wasn’t like they weren’t participating in the festival with something weird of their own.

Ordinarily, Gael considered getting woken up before midday an act of aggression requiring medical retaliation, but today was no normal today. Gods, he’d paid three hundred thousand Marks for his floating fortress, so he was going to use his floating fortress, and right now—standing atop the roof of the bridge with a fleet of sixty Saint’s Hands barges trailing his ship—he felt like a king sailing down the canals of Wraithpier.

With one hand on his cane and the other holding onto the brim of his top hat, he grinned against the wind and felt the cold blue sunlight on his face. Oh, he did feel like a king. His floating fortress groaned through the canal, taking up so much space any smaller barge worth their salt had to squeeze against the embankments just to let it pass—and then they had to wait at least another three minutes for the rest of the Saint’s Hands ship to pass by. The Saint’s Hands’ ships weren’t the miserable scrapwood barges they’d deliberately built for Myrmur fishing, either. These were proper little armored cutters Fergal had commissioned months ago because, in his own words, ‘eventually we’re going to need ships that don’t fall apart when a Myrmur actually fights back’. Gael had problems with the expense at the time, but now?

Every time their entire fleet rounded another bend, another district of the festival crowd stopped what they were doing to stare. He now considered Fergal a visionary.

“See those?” Nixy pointed excitedly towards a family barge covered in little black flags. “Every flag’s for a leviathan someone in the family helped kill. If ye lose one in the hunt,they’ll tie a white ribbon through it after.”

“So they’re partly memorial as well?” Maeve asked.

“Aye, but ye’re not supposed t’cry while tying it,” Bran added quickly. “It’s bad luck. Makes the fish think ye’re soft.”

Vivi blinked. “The fish can tell?”

Rebus gave her a long look. “‘Course they can. All fish are vicious judges of character.”

Gael wasn’t alone atop the roof of the bridge, of course. Maeve was beside him, and so was Evelyn and Vivi, listening intently to Rebus, Bran, and Nixy’s explanations of every deranged Wraithpier tradition they happened to pass by. Jin and Liorin, meanwhile, had apparently adopted Gael’s superior approach to cultural appreciation: the three of them were simply standing near the edge of the roof, gazing proudly down at everyone staring up at them.

After a moment, Gael finally glanced sideways at the two other boys.

“Do either of you feel like the captain of the world?” he asked.

Jin nodded. Liorin nodded vigorously. So Gael grinned and held out a fist, letting the two boys bump it. Nothing else needed saying.

The journey took roughly half an hour before the canals widened and the buildings around them began falling farther apart. Flooded warehouses gave way to stone embankments, then old piers, then entire neighborhoods built around broader stretches of black water. Eventually, the canal opened into Wraithpier’s largest deepwater lake, a place the children called Saint Veyra’s Hollow, although Gael suspected nobody living had any idea who Saint Veyra actually was or why she apparently owned a lake deep enough to drown several citadels in. True to its reputation, the lake itself was enormous as well. Two dozen vessels were already scattered across it, most much smaller than Gael’s fortress, and all of them maintaining generous distances from one another while their crews checked bait cages, harpoon launches, nets, and lines.

Farther towards the center of the lake, Gael spotted stranger ships immediately: low, narrow hulls fitted with experimental fins, reinforced bows, peculiar rotating rigs, and enough improvised engineering to make them look like prototypes built by drunk geniuses with access to shipyards. The hats of the crew confirmed it. They were the Fishermen’s ships

Gael grinned, hopping off the roof and circling back into the bridge where Fergal stood at the enormous wheel with two of his Fingers assisting him as helm officers. Cara, meanwhile, occupied a corner chair as she drank tea and kept an eye on all the various gauges and bulbs making up the bulk of the control panels.

Moments later, everyone else crowded in behind him, and he pointed through the forward windows at the Fishermen’s ships.

“We’re here,” he announced, “and if those lunatics are right, this ‘Saint Mercy’s Widow’ leviathan is somewhere underneath this lake, which means this is officially the part where everybody stops looking decorative and starts being useful. All hands to their stations!”

That got everyone except for Fergal and Cara moving. Jin and Vivi immediately went to the lower deck. Evelyn and Liorin went aft to coordinate the smaller ships by signal flags. Rebus, Bran, and Nixy sprinted towards the upper observation deck, while Maeve remained beside Gael for another second before going over to help Cara with the control panels.

For Gael’s part, he turned and marched out of the bridge.

The thirty or so Saint’s Hands across the lower deck were already shouting over one another as they checked the chains, greased the gears, and hauled carts of replacement parts into position. Several of them nodded at him as he strutted past them. Others simply moved out of his way, and by the time he reached the bow of his floating fortress, the rest of his fleet of about sixty ships had begun spreading out around the deepwater lake. All of the armored cutters moved towards their preassigned positions along the lake’s perimeter, each coordinating with the next until their formation became a broad ring.

Finally, Gael climbed onto the bowsprit and began balancing across it until he stood at the very, very tip of the ship. One bad step would send him plummeting straight into the lake, so he grinned, withdrew a little metal flask marked ninety percent, and took three burning gulps.

Oh, that’s the good stuff.

He tossed the empty flask over his shoulder and cracked his neck. Then he reached inside his coat again, and this time, he produced a small laboratory’s worth of chemical vials between his fingers. The glasses clinked against each other, filled with powders, oils, crystals, suspensions, and other liquids in all sorts of unpleasant shades.

Across the lake, the Fishermen noticed the ring of Saint’s Hands barges surrounding them, and they noticed Gael standing on the bowsprit as well. So he cleared his voice and raised it until it carried over the water.

“Gentlemen!” he shouted. “Since we’ve all gathered here today in the noble pursuit of killing something unnecessarily large, I thought I might begin today with a short lesson in practical alchemy!” Whether the Fishermen could hear him or not, he held up his first vial. “This delightful little powder is dried river lamprey musk, which I first encountered six years ago when a smuggler attempted to hide three kilograms of it inside a coffin! The corpse was still inside! He must’ve been a very entrepreneurial man!”

He uncorked it and poured the powder into a larger reinforced vial. Then he raised another vial.

“And here! Concentrated bile from an eastern marsh hagfish! Revolting smell, incredible adhesive properties, and it permanently stained our surgical chamber’s ceiling years ago!” Gael poured it into the larger reinforced vial as well. Then yet another vial followed. “Fermented grave-eel pheromone concentrate! Never store this near perfume!” Then another. “Powdered copper salts, treated with blackvine acid! Two drops of carrion amber—three if you dislike the fishy smell—and start mixing!”

Everyone who could hear him shouting was staring at him now, so he continued his lecture with growing delight, describing each ingredient as he added it: stimulant oils used by Wraithpier bait-makers, irritants extracted from parasitic canal worms, an old Exorcist compound meant to provoke aquatic Myrmur movement, and finally a clear alchemical binder whose only job was to force every disgusting scent and chemical signal to spread through the upper layers of water without immediately diluting.

Before long, the final mixture turned black, then green, then an alarming shade of bruised violet. Fumes curled from the stopper as he held it up, twirling in place to let everyone see.

“... And when you combine all of that in exactly the correct ratio, you produce something that possesses one extraordinarily useful property!” he shouted, grinning from ear to ear. “It makes eels super mad.”

With that, he flicked the vial forward. It spun through the air, plopped beneath the surface, and vanished without much fanfare.

And for one second, nothing happened.

The ships continued bobbing gently beneath bluish morning light filtered through the Vile. The festival drums still echoed faintly from the distant shoreline, and a gull even landed on his top hat.

Then the lake shuddered, and a tremor rolled beneath his floating fortress. Gael felt it through the bowsprit first, then through the hull, then through his boots. Ripples began spreading outwards from the center of the lake as well, but those ripples quickly became waves, slapping violently against every ship around.

People started shouting as something impossibly large moved beneath them. Several less-prepared civilian crews immediately turned their vessels away, while the Fishermen did the opposite, raising their harpoons and casting their nets pre-emptively.

Gael would give the Fishermen props for wanting to stay, because he almost flinched himself when the center of the lake exploded, and a giant black-and-silver eel tore out of the water with a scream. His mouth actually fell open.

“Wow,” he mumbled.

If nothing else, the eel was truly deserving of its ‘leviathan’ title. It was thirty meters long at least above the surface, with far more body still twisting under the waves. Its jaws split wide enough to swallow an entire carriage, black dorsal ridges ran along its spine, and silver hexagonal scales flashed beneath its belly, obviously a bioarcanic mutation from something it ate ages ago. It also had a hundred translucent fins jutting out from random parts of its body, and they were currently whipping waves of lakewater through the air as it screamed in obvious agony—no doubt from the chemical irritation burning through its senses, of course.

It was a pretty beautiful creature. It also looked like it’d be a relatively difficult thing to catch alive, so thank the Saintess he wasn’t trying to do that.

“... Eighty-five Steelborn Pattern-Fourteen Vautrin breach-loaded eighty-millimeter rifled deck cannons reinforced for high-pressure bioarcanic shells, rise!” he roared, pointing his cane at the eel theatrically. “Fire!”

And every concealed gunport around the lake opened. Fifteen giant steel cannons rose from the lower deck of his floating fortress alone, with the sixty surrounding ships exposing the rest, and—courtesy of Arnell and the Steelborn for having shared the eastern ward’s artillery with their ships—they fired simultaneously.

Oh, how quickly the world became pure thunder.

Gael nearly shouted and fell off the bowsprit as his entire fortress lurched beneath him. Fire blossomed across the lake perimeter, and the explosive shells crossed the lake in converging lines to strike the leviathan eel from every direction. The leviathan immediately vanished behind the explosions as its upper body broke apart. Armor, scales, blood, flesh, and pieces Gael couldn’t medically identify flew outwards in enormous sprays as consecutive shells punched through the eel’s sides and detonated internally.

The eel’s scream cut through the thunder only briefly before it collapsed, hitting the surface of the lake hard enough to send one last wave rolling out towards every ship.

Silence followed.

Or was he just deaf from standing so close to the cannons behind him without plugging his ears? He wasn’t sure.

Still, once the smoke cleared, there was only one thing floating at the center of the lake, and it wasn’t a living eel. The very, very dead eel was bleeding so heavily and missing so many chunks of flesh across its body that the lake around it was turning black-red.

The Fishermen stared at the dead eel in shock and awe as Gael laughed, spreading his arms far and wide.

“And once again, we have shown the world that nothing biological stands before superior artillery and explosive firepower!” he shouted towards the nearest Fishermen ships. “Gentlemen! While I may not have reeled the leviathan up according to your most conventional manners, I assume this still counts as my catch, so unless you can slay a larger leviathan than this by nightfall, I’ll assume my position as the king of Wraithpier!” He whistled then, glancing around briefly to grin at Maeve, Cara, and Fergal inside the bridge. “King of Wraithpier, guys! What a title! Do you think I can get a throne with that or—”

Then his vision flashed red, and his boasts died on the tip of his tongue as the lake began rumbling again.

The blown-apart chunks of flesh across the eel’s carcass were knitting together, and replacing its normal scales, raw chitin and black segmented plates crawled over the entire length of its skin. Then a giant armored leg erupted from the side of its body, followed by six, eight, ten, twenty, a hundred more legs punching along the entire length of its segmented plates. By the time Gael managed to balance across the bowsprit and hop back onto the lower deck, where Maeve caught him by the collar of his coat, Saint Mercy’s Widow was no longer a leviathan eel, but…

A giant armored centipede with four antennae wiggling about in the sky.
