Yelena stared at the memory of her mother's death as it played out before her, every moment just as brutal as she remembered.

They had been fleeing the explosion of their house, running across scattered debris while the screams of hundreds echoed all around them, when the cultivator arrived, his fiery robes untouched by the surrounding flames.

His eyes had been monstrous. His gaze, horrifying. Nothing described him better than the word monster.

Her mother had screamed, then shoved her into an alley, hiding her from view. But as reckless and defiant as she had been, even as a child, Yelena had rushed back out with every intention of saving her mother.

And that was when she had seen—

Yelena looked away from the memory, unable to watch what came next as her mother's screams echoed through the air. Pain stabbed into her heart like a dagger. Tears pooled in her eyes, and her entire body shuddered as old wounds split open, forcing her to relive that night all over again.

She raised her gaze to the memory as it replayed itself, tears streaming down her face as she forced herself to watch it to its conclusion. To watch her mother be burnt alive.

Her mother’s scream echoed once more, stabbing into her chest like a knife. But this time, Yelena didn’t flinch. Hatred had hardened her heart.

And when she finally tore her gaze away from the memory to look at the cultivator beside her, her eyes were as cold as ice. As hard as steel.

When she spoke, her voice was like iron.

“Is this the perspective you wanted to show me?” she asked quietly. Calmly. “Or is this some form of torture?”

Edrick looked at her with a sad smile.

“Your life has not been easy, Yelena Alcatraz. You’re hateful, reckless, and defiant, but I imagine an occurrence like this would plant hatred in anyone's heart." He exhaled softly. "To lose one's mother and city in a single night is no small pain. To watch it happen is even worse. You have every right to hate those who caused it."

“I do,” Yelena said coldly, the hatred in her eyes unmistakable. "But you’ll be wrong to think my hatred is limited to fire cultivators alone."

"I'm aware of how far it stretches." Edrick furrowed his brows. "I have no intention of changing your mind."

Yelena sneered. “Even though I hate storm cultivators too?”

Edrick looked at her, runic irises flaring. “Even though you hate storm cultivators too.”

Yelena’s cold outrage cracked, and a fiery anger surged out, twisting her face into an ugly mask of fury.

"Then what is the meaning of this?" She snapped, waving her hands wildly. "What is your aim here? I told you to save me from this fate, not remind me of the monster I am becoming."

"You're not becoming a monster," Edrick replied quietly, seemingly unbothered by her awakened rage. "And the perspective I wanted to show you is this."

He stretched out a hand toward her Yelena’s younger self within the memory. "With all the hatred and anger you feel now, Yelena, what can you truly do? If this were to happen again, how would you protect this child? How would you save her mother from being burned alive?"

Yelena flinched.

"Do not speak about my mother," she spat. "Your people did this."

"Fire cultivators did this," Edrick countered. "But yes, you are right. Sometimes cultivators go rogue and lose their sanity. Sometimes it is simply the force of their power that destroys entire districts.” His gaze softened. “I will not tell you not to hate them, Yelena. But you will understand them because, whether you like it or not, you are now one. You too will experience that twisted effect one day."

"I will not cultivate this power," Yelena hissed. "I will never touch it."

Rather than argue with her, Edrick gestured toward the memory once more. "How will you save her then? And her mother? What exactly will hatred without power do against this cultivator? How will it save this little girl?"

"Stop it!"

Yelena's voice cracked with fury. "Stop trying to make me accept this power you and your people foisted upon me. I don't want it!

Edrick paused, and for a long moment, he said nothing. Then he turned fully toward her, his runic eyes staring deep into hers.

"Power itself isn't wrong, Yelena," he said, his voice quiet beneath the whispering wind. "Being a cultivator isn't what makes one a monster."

"But it opens the path to becoming one," Yelena shot back.

Edrick's expression softened as though she had said something incredibly naïve. He didn't correct her, though, and simply turned back toward the burning city.

"I know your hatred for cultivators, Yelena. And like I said earlier, I won't tell you to cast it aside. There are many in this world who wield their power for evil." He paused, watching the flames consume the city before continuing. "What I will tell you, however, is that defiance and hatred are nothing without the power to back them up."

He gestured toward the memory once more. "If this happened again, you would be of no use, no matter how hateful or defiant you became. Just as you were of no use when my people came searching for my legacy. You can try. You can shout. But without true power, there is little you can do."

Yelena’s expression cracked as memories of her futility tumbled through her mind. Not once had she managed to stop a single cultivator. Rather, they’d had to save her several times.

She grimaced, conflicted. “So what you’re saying is I need power to back up my hatred?”

"What I'm saying is that perhaps fate gave you this power to give backing to your defiance." His eyes drifted back to the burning city. "Perhaps you are meant to become an avenger. Or maybe a protector. Or perhaps, like your father, a symbol. Who better for such a burden than the one who hates the power she possesses? The one who sees it not as a gift, but for what it truly is—a weapon?"

Yelena closed her eyes, hatred and logic warring within her. She opened her mouth to protest the legacy, to deny it once more, but found herself hesitating. What was the point? There was no getting rid of it anymore. It was now part and parcel of her, like a thorn in her flesh that could never be removed.

She forced a deep breath through her nose before glancing back at Edrick. She regarded him for a moment before finally looking away.

"Why are you telling me this?" she asked. "If I embrace this path fully, I will eventually kill your people. And I will relish it. I hate them too much for that never to happen. So why?"

Edrick stared at her for a long moment, then he looked away. "I cultivated the path of defense when I was in this world, Yelena. I've seen many great evils. Many worse than what happened to you and your mother." He pursed his lips. "I have no idea why fate chose you. But in my long life, I've come to realize that many times, it knows what it's doing."

He smiled faintly. "You hate us now. Maybe you'll hate us forever. Maybe you'll seek revenge. Or maybe you'll stand against the sects. But you're also someone who doesn't want to become the monster that killed their mother. Someone who will recklessly put her life in danger to stand against injustice no matter how strong." His gaze softened. "I don't think anyone could have been more deserving of my path of defense. And that is why I am telling you all this."

Yelena stared at him, hatred and doubt coiling in her heart. She looked away a moment later, swallowing against the lump in her throat.

For the first time, she didn’t have a retort ready. Her eyes drifted back to the memory, softening ever so slightly as she watched the little girl standing helpless amid the flames. Her own despair. A despair neither defiance nor hatred had been enough to stop.

Silence stretched between them for a long moment before Yelena finally spoke. “Fire cultivators killed my mother. Storm cultivators destroyed my city and made me into something I detest. There are so many others doing evil.” Her gaze hardened slightly. “I will never stop hating them. but perhaps… hatred isn’t enough.”

“No, it isn’t.” Edrick shook his head. “To bring about change in any world, you will need power. That is what my legacy offers—simply power. Whatever path you choose to walk with it is up you.”

Yelena stared at the burning city below for a long while, anger still smoldering inside her.

She hated the power now growing inside her. She hated the fact that she would have to leave Alcatraz to the Storm Sect if she was to learn to live with it. But she couldn’t argue with what Edrick had shown her.

Defiance without power was the exact thing that had landed her in this position. It was what made her father such a weak symbol, no matter how proudly he wore the word. She hated that she could see the logic in it. She hated that a part of her was swaying.

Scowling, she shook her head.

A dozen paths lay ahead of her now, none of them desirable. But a few, she could admit only to herself, were walkable. A few she might survive without losing whatever was left of who she'd been.

She had a choice to make.

She stared silently at the memory for a long moment before Edrick gestured once more, and reality shifted to reveal the Storm Sect.

Only this time, they were no longer watching it from above. Rather, they stood before a massive mountain, far larger than any other in the entire sect. The mountain was dark and weathered, smoothened by centuries of lightning save for the steps etched into its side, stretching all the way into the clouds above.

Yelena blinked, suddenly feeling so small. So powerless. Her defiance, inconsequential.

Edrick smiled at her; his arms folded carefully behind his back. “Take a walk with me, Yelena. There is much to discuss.”

He didn’t wait for her. He didn’t look back.

He simply walked toward the mountain and began climbing, whistling a gentle tune to himself.

Yelena hesitated for a moment, then, after a long, resentful breath, followed him.

The moment her foot touched the first step, an aura of pressure slammed down on her, and she grimaced. Looking up, she found Edrick already more than a dozen steps ahead, covering in moments what had taken her everything to climb a single step.

“Wait!” she called out, but Edrick Ghaltris didn’t look back.

It was up to her to follow. What would happen if she turned back instead?

Yelena glanced toward the space the memory had been. It was no longer there, but Yelena didn’t need to see it to actually see it.

She stared at the empty space for a heartbeat, then with a muttered curse, she forced herself up another step. Then the next.

Thunder boomed across the heavens in relentless succession. Lightning lit up roiling storms in flashes of white, blue, and crimson. The winds howled, shrieking like something in torment.

And beneath it all, Yelena Alcatraz stood on the seventeenth step of the mountain, glaring daggers at the one who had urged her onto this wretched journey.

He was hundreds of steps ahead.

"You said we had much to discuss," she called out, her voice raw against the wind. "How are we meant to do that with you standing a mountain away?"

Edrick Ghaltris turned then, a slight smile on his face. "A trial is a solo journey, Yelena. We will speak when you reach the summit."

Yelena's gaze snapped up to the roiling clouds overhead, and her jaw tightened.

“You can’t be serious.”

Edrick’s smile widened a fraction. “Why not?”

She glanced up at the roiling clouds once more. It hadn’t even begun slanting.

“That’s not going to work.” She shook her head. “If we can’t discuss what you want to here, then you better take him back home now.”

“Your home is in ruins.”

“Then take me back to my death. I will not take another step up this wretched mountain.”

Edrick’s smile tightened slightly, almost imperceptibly, and though he said nothing, it was obvious he was waiting for her to climb up to him.

Oh no, two could play this game.

Bracing herself against the howling winds, she folded her arms resolutely and fixed him with a sneer. ‘I’m listening.”

Edrick stared at her silently for a long moment. Then he sighed. “I learned an interesting tidbit of information about Ascendants recently. Do you want to know what it is?”

“I’m perfectly fine learning what it is from right here.”

“Of course,” Edrick replied quietly, then shook his head. “While mortal, I always wondered why legacies had such differing experiences of their patrons. Some saw them once and never saw them again. Some see them consistently, while others never see them at all.”

Yelena frowned. “Which are you? Please tell me it’s the first?”

Edrick chuckled. “Sorry to disappoint you then. You will be seeing me consistently. I am a seventh-step Ascendant after all. The higher one climbs, the more of their soul they can infuse into a legacy.” His smile widened. “I managed to infuse a lot.”

Yelena scowled.

It was bad enough that she had to live this life with her new abilities. Living it with her patron in her spirit was a massive let down.

Edrick’s jaw twitched in amusement. “You seem disappointed.”

Yelena scowled at him. “Is that the ‘interesting tidbit of information’ you wanted to share?”

Edrick turned his gaze to the clouds. “The higher one climbs in ascension, the more powerful they become. The more they can leave behind for their legacies.” He turned back to her. “I only climbed to the seventh step so I can’t create a tribulation for you. This trial is the best I can do. But you’ll gain nothing from it if you do not give it your best.”

“What is the purpose? Why are we climbing?”

“To gain the very thing you hate, my dear. Power.”

Yelena sneered, still not sure how far she was willing to go for that. She may have recognized its importance, but actively seeking it out was another thing entirely.

Edrick smiled. “Finish this trial, and you'll gain an edge—even among cultivators. If you’re to defy them properly, then you'll need to be stronger than they are."

Yelena grimaced. And he continued.

“I have left behind a piece of my enlightenment for you. It is in your father’s possession. The least you can do for yourself now is climb. Climb as high as you can.”

Yelena stared up at the roiling storm, then at the distant, unmoving shape of him. The summit was impossibly far. But if reaching it meant standing even an inch above the people who had taken everything from her—if it meant one day looking down at them instead of being looked down upon—

She would take it. Not because she wanted it. Because she refused to be powerless again.

With a fresh scowl, she began to climb.

She climbed and climbed until her knees peeled and her bones ached. She climbed until every breath scorched her lungs and every muscle screamed for rest.

Then and only then did Edrick Ghaltris stop. It should have been to tell her she'd done well—to acknowledge how far she'd come. Instead, he only looked at her, his expression unreadable, and said,

"Take another step, Yelena."

She shook her head, chest heaving. "I can't."

"I know." His gaze didn't waver. "Take one anyway."

He regarded her for a quiet moment. "Or is this where your defiance ends? Where your hatred runs out?"

"No." Yelena snarled. “It’ll never run out.”

"Then prove it," he said quietly. "Show me exactly how much you hate what we've made you. Show me how far that hatred can truly carry you."

Something in Yelena's chest snapped taut. And with a cry, she took another step—her gaze no longer fixed on the summit, but on Edrick Ghaltris himself.

She would tear him apart with her bare hands if she had to.
