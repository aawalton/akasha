“So, what is your opinion on that woman’s proposal? What exactly does she think she can get out of this?” Viktor murmured.

Even though there was no one else in the room—no one other than Claire—he still kept his voice as low as possible. Just in case Jeanne or Alycia might be lingering in the corridor on the other side of the door.

[It is hard to say, Master. If we are being optimistic about it, perhaps she genuinely intends to hand Xander over so that you can have your revenge. After all, she has gone out of her way to apologize to you for what happened to Claire.]

Viktor snorted. “If she truly wanted to give us the bastard, she could’ve just tied him up and delivered him straight to our front door. Tell me, what is the point of this so-called competition?”

[You are right, Master. That would certainly have been the simpler path. But maybe she wants us to “earn” it. Not so different from the way our own dungeon operates. There are rewards, but none of them are ever given for free. The adventurers who want to claim them must put their lives on the line for the privilege.]

“Earn it, huh? Anyway, that’s your optimistic interpretation. How about the pessimistic one?”

[Then we would need to consider the possibility that this is, in truth, nothing more than another invasion against our dungeon. She was working with Clovis, whom we suspect is behind the attack by the Reavers. So this could be their next attempt. It is entirely possible that the men they send in wouldn’t stop at the third floor; they would instead push further and further downward, all the way to the Core Room.]

“Yes, that’s something worth considering,” Viktor said. “Then again, if all Nephra wants were you, she wouldn’t have had to go through all this trouble to set up a competition. She could’ve just come herself and taken whatever she pleased, because with the kind of power she possesses, there’s absolutely nothing we can do to stop her. So no, it’s not her true goal. Her goal cannot be that simple.”

[What could it be, I wonder?]

“My earlier hypothesis was that she wanted to engineer a conflict between us and Iskora. Now we know that Iskora was merely a front; our true adversary has been the Children of the Abyss all along. Still, the hypothesis remains largely unchanged; we simply replace one player with another. She wants us to fight the Children, and with that in mind, arranging this contest aligns with that goal perfectly.”

Not that this particular conflict would have required much engineering anyway. The Children had always been his natural enemy, because of both what they had done in the past and what they were pursuing in the present.

[Now, if we assume this is indeed a game deliberately designed to pit us against the Children of the Abyss, the question becomes: which side does she ultimately want to win?]

“My guess,” Viktor said with a shrug, “is that she doesn’t care. No matter who comes out on top in the end, the outcome benefits her regardless. But that also means...”

[The chances of victory for both sides are probably equal.]

“Yes, exactly. This is no gift; it’s a serious threat. The Xander who steps into the dungeon this time might be much stronger than the Xander we once knew.”

As if the bastard isn’t troublesome enough already...

Oh, well. It didn’t matter what Nephra and the Mastermind behind her were scheming, he had no choice but to win this upcoming fight. Not to mention—

His gaze drifted toward the woman lying motionless on the bed beside him. Though her condition hadn’t deteriorated any further, she still looked thin and impossibly pale, her body so fragile that at any moment she might shatter into pieces and then vanish entirely.

Yes. Even if there had been no other reason, this one alone would have been enough.

“Tomorrow I’ll discuss the battle plan with everyone. So make sure that Sebekton, Khenemhotep, and Galatea are all gathered at the Core Room. We’ve got exactly one week to prepare.”

That was the agreement Isadora had made with Nephra. Seven days for Daelin and Iskora to assemble their teams before the competition would begin.

[Understood, Master. I will see to it that all three of them are present at the appointed time.]

“Also, tell Kazyk that I approve his proposal, and that I want him to begin the work immediately. I want that trebuchet completed and ready before Xander and his men set foot in our dungeon. Expand the third floor as much as necessary to get the wood required. Summon more gremlins if that’s what it takes. And bring in the other minions to help with the construction.”

[You are going to use it, Master?]

“I don’t know yet, but the more tools we have at our disposal, the better positioned we will be no matter what happens. I’d rather have a trebuchet I don’t use than need one I don’t have.”

[Understood. I’ll arrange it right away.]

With that matter concluded, Viktor rose from his chair, making his way toward the door.

After he had agreed to the proposal from the woman with skin of bronze, it was no surprise that everyone in the room started looking at him as though he had lost his mind. Nevertheless, Isadora, who had been maneuvered neatly into the position, had no choice but to agree as well, unless she wanted to openly eat her own words in front of all her subordinates. Then, after everything had been settled and the guests of honor had finally taken their leave, people came to him, bombarding him with questions from every side. To them, he simply said he didn’t particularly feel like talking about it, and all he desired was to go back to his sister’s side as soon as possible. And that was enough to make everyone shut up for the time being.

Still, it was not like he could hide in this room forever. Sooner or later, he would have to step out to give the others an explanation. Fortunately, he had come up with a story convincing enough to satisfy them.

He found Jeanne and Alycia at the other end of the corridor. They both turned the moment they spotted him, and he walked over to them.

“So, a competition, huh?” Jeanne said. She must have heard from Alycia about everything that had happened back at the Guild.

“Yes,” Viktor said.

“You think we can win?”

“We don’t need to. If that man dies in the dungeon, that’s good enough for me.”

Jeanne nodded. “I figured as much.”

“Quinn,” Alycia said, “he is a Mithril. Do you seriously believe someone of that caliber could die on the third floor of a dungeon?”

Well, one Mithril already had, though of course she had no way of knowing that.

“This dungeon isn’t like any other dungeon out there,” Viktor said. “You can’t measure how dangerous it is by what you know of the rest.”

“True enough. Nevertheless, there’s no guarantee the bastard will be killed down there. Even if things go badly for him, he could still easily escape with his hide intact.”

“I place my bet on the dark spirit.” Viktor shrugged. “I trust they can take care of that small problem.”

“What?”

Both women stared at him, the same question practically stamped on their faces: what in the world did the dark spirit have to do with any of this?

“I think you’ve forgotten the root of the problem. Though I can’t really blame you. So much has happened, after all. But let me remind you how this all began, what set everything in motion in the first place. Clovis, their previous Guildmaster, came to us with an offer to buy our dungeon. When that failed, he attempted to build an entirely new town on the other side of the river to secure easy access to it. The dungeon has always been their objective. Which means everything they’ve done has been to serve that goal, be it underwater monsters attacking our outpost, Iskora adventurers taking over the west side, or masked men kidnapping the pyromancers.”

“You’re... right.”

“Why would they go to such extraordinary lengths for the sake of just one dungeon? Their obsession with it is just unnatural. I refuse to believe the reason behind all of this is something as simple as money. There has to be more to it than that. Then there’s the fact that our dungeon is very different from the others, and that it’s the first dungeon to appear in the Central Plains in three hundred years.”

“What... is going on?” Jeanne murmured.

“I don’t know,” Viktor said, shaking his head. “But I believe there’s something tied to this dungeon, something very important, that makes Iskora want it this badly. And, just as they want it for themselves, it’s possible there’s someone else out there who wants it too, or at the very least, wants to make sure that Iskora never gets their hands on it.”

“You mean... the dark spirit?”

Viktor nodded. “Calyssa called it a personal feud: the dark spirit is attacking their own enemies who happen to be in the vicinity. While I agree that they are indeed adversaries, I don’t believe them choosing this place to duke it out is a coincidence. No, everything happens here because the dungeon is here. One side wants to claim it, and the other wants to prevent them from achieving that goal.”

“You make a fair point, but... what does any of that have to do with the competition?”

“Remember the terms. If Iskora loses, every single one of their henchmen will be forced to leave Daelin, which would effectively put an end to their ambitions here once and for all. Do you think the dark spirit would let such an opportunity slip by?”

“I...”

“There’s more. Why do you think that woman would want to set up this particular competition in the first place? What exactly is she trying to achieve? What is she hoping to gain from this? Also, I highly doubt that she came up with that idea on the spot. No, it’s something she must have had in mind from the very beginning, before this whole weird “parley” even started. Everything else, all the talk of compensation, all the talk about Xander, was nothing more than smoke meant to conceal her true aim.”

“I know!” Alycia burst out. “She wants to lure the dark spirit out. That’s why she’s setting up a contest where failure means Iskora’s ambitions get shut down completely. That is the perfect bait. With such a condition in place, the dark spirit would be tempted to enter the dungeon and make sure that her team would lose. Yes! Yes! That explains everything! I had been wondering what in the world Khalid was even trying to accomplish with this meeting, and then when it started, he kept going on and on about that bastard Xander rather than the secret lair. I was thoroughly confused. But now it all makes sense. The dark spirit destroyed their base of operations, so of course they want to retaliate, and in order to do that, they first need to draw them out into a position where they can actually strike.”

Viktor smiled. This was why he loved smart people. He didn’t need to spell out everything for them; they were more than capable of arriving at the wrong conclusion by themselves.

“Yes,” he said. “That’s what I’m trying to say.”

“But...” Jeanne said. “What if the dark spirit realizes it’s a trap and simply avoids it?”

“Even if they know,” Viktor said. “If the reward is big enough to be worth the risk, they could still choose to walk right into it.”

“It’s not a trap so much as a challenge,” Alycia said. “That woman is inviting the dark spirit into a game of her own design, and the dungeon is the gameboard they mean to play on.”

“Can’t they go find somewhere else to play their games?” Jeanne frowned. “Why does it have to be us who get dragged into the middle of it?”

Couldn’t agree more, Viktor thought.

“So, you figured all that out during the meeting? And that’s why you agreed to her proposal?” Alycia said. “I have to say, I’m impressed.”

Nah, I’ve made it all up just now.

But to be fair, most of what he had said was the truth to begin with. He had simply rearranged it to align with the narrative he wanted them to believe.

“Quinn is smart after all,” Jeanne said, smiling.

“And here I thought you’d gotten yourself seduced by her.” Alycia giggled. “You did leave us behind to go talk with her alone, right before the meeting started. I was thinking our boy had finally noticed that pretty women existed...”

“Oh, please. You’re both prettier than her,” Viktor said with a dismissive wave of his hand. “I’ve spoken with her once before, that’s all there is to it. She accompanied Clovis here when he first came to make his offer, and she was present during the vote on whether we would sell our dungeon to them. It seems she’s taken an interest in me for some reason. And I, in turn, find her quite enigmatic.”

“Who is she, exactly?” Jeanne said.

“Overseer Rennald once told me that the Valiant Bank was the one behind Iskora’s project here. So my guess is that she’s some sort of representative sent by the Bank, who has been directing things from behind the curtains all along, and has now decided to take matters into her own hands.”

“That explains the way Khalid behaved around her.” Alycia nodded. “To summarize, you figured out that the whole thing is just a ploy orchestrated by that woman to draw out the dark spirit, and you agreed to it because you’re betting the dark spirit will kill Xander for you.”

“They’ve got two wins against Iskora already. With a track record like that, I’m happily putting my coin on them.”

“Fair enough. But...” Jeanne said. “How exactly would the dark spirit get into the dungeon?”

“Did you forget that their veil of shadow is exactly that, a veil?” Alycia said. “Its entire purpose is to hide their true identity. They can just walk in as a normal adventurer. For all we know, they could be someone standing right in front of us.”

Viktor chuckled. You’re absolutely right, “Master.”
