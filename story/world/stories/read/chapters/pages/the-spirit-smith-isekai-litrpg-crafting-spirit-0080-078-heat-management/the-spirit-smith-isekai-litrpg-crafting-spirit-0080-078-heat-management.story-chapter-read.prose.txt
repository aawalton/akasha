Thunk. I spin around at the sound, only to find a massive tree trunk resting a few meters away. “Master! Will this be sufficient?” Dovey asks, landing on top of the massive thing that makes him look like an ant.

I guffaw, “It sure will! How the hell did you even carry that thing?”

“My stats go up too, you know. I may look tiny, but I am still level fifty.” Dovey counters, “Also don’t you dare say I am small again, meanie.”

“I never said you were small. Well, recently at least.”

“You were thinking it, though.”

“Was not.”

“You certainly were.”

I sigh and concede the point to the bird. I was thinking he was too small to carry such a large object… “Well that should be large enough for a campfire to keep even a dragon away! Which should be plenty, I don’t expect your piece to take all too long, given its small size.”

“See! I knew it, you were thinking I am small.” Dovey huffs, and I realize my critical blunder.

“Do you want the upgrade or not?”

“Ohmygosh, I am sorry master.” The bird says, bowing down a bit, “I totally want it. Pretty please. I could find you anything you could possibly need to assist.”

I giggle at how easily he gives up. “I should have everything I need, though I think I will need to make a personal upgrade first.”

“What sort of personal upgrade?”

In answer, I walk over to my anvil which is fully set up, thanks to the convenience of domain transport. I quickly navigate through the now familiar menus, selecting the ingredients for Dovey’s new equipment. But when I go to start the craft, a big red warning flashes in front of me.

Warning, current facilities are insufficient for forging of material: astralite. Facilities needed: Forge of Initiate+ tier

It would seem that my laziness has finally come back to bite me. Astralite is too powerful of a material for my poor heating stone to work on. Luckily, I have been planning for this upgrade for a while.

I take a few steps away from the anvil and pull a monster orb out of my storage. It is from one of the many ignis golems that I killed back in the dungeon. The thing is far too potent in fire mana to use for much, but for a forge it is perfect.

Thus far, I haven’t really touched on the facilities part of crafting. Claire kinda just told me how to make an anvil, and gave me that cheat heating stone. But there are all sorts of facilities that are required to actually use the crafting system. My anvil is obviously the core part of my profession, but there are others, like a forge for heating up the materials. Apparently I will also need a facility for higher tier imbuing later on, but I don’t need that quite yet. Or so I read in that book about crafting basics.

Luckily, that same book also gave me the requirements to build a forge. Namely, some sort of high potency fire material to act as a core. It also needs a strong material to act as the main structure, and finally some sort of outer shell that is preferably fireproof.

On top of the orb, I pull out some strange rocks I picked up on my way to visit Valdir. They don’t have any fancy description, but I get the sense they are strong enough to handle ridiculous heat. As for the last material, I pull out a couple units of the infernal spider carapace I got from that horrifying spider.

I toss them all down onto the ground in a pile, and much as Claire first showed me, I focus a lot of mana into them. Then I try to imagine them forming into the structure of a forge, though I end up with more of an oven shape. To be fair, I haven’t designed my own forges and such before.

A message appears asking if I want to confirm the formation of a Magic Smith’s Infernal Stone Forge (Initiate). Naturally, I confirm and with a bit more mana expenditure, the forge settles into usability.

My anvil is still at fundamental tier… but this craft doesn't require an upgrade yet. So thus I shall put that issue into the later problems drawer. After all, why upgrade what isn’t broken?

With my forge now created, I head back over to my anvil and it is ready to let me start the craft. So I hurry up and start up the campfire, and then settle over my anvil. The world around me fades as I initiate the craft, bringing with it that familiar glee of crafting.

The process is a bit different this time, instead of starting at my anvil, the system teleports me over in front of my new forge. A single unit of astralite appears in my hand, and I simply follow my intuition as I set it inside. Then I place my hands on the forge and start to push mana in. Lots of it.

So much so that I am forced to pull out a mana potion for the first time in crafting session history. Though to be fair, I was quite drained from the formation of it. Still, I definitely could use more mana capacity somehow, that seems like a later problem though.

For now I focus on the astralite as it sits inside the forge. With the forge now at full power, I slowly raise the heat inside. Furthermore, I don’t just let the heat do whatever the fuck it wants. I grab a hold of the energy, pushing as much of it as I can into the astralite. Sweat breaks on my brow as I feel the heat flare up around me as well.

But, ever so slowly, the astralite starts to heat up. What feels like hours later, but could’ve easily been minutes, it finally reaches the proper temperature. How hot is it? Honestly, no fucking clue, but it is glowing white, so my expertise-backed guess is about really fucking hot.

The moment that part is done, the system teleports me and the astralite over to the anvil. Which is good, as I have no clue how I would pick up something so hot safely. Luxmore appears in my hands, but as I appraise the task ahead of me, I can tell the poor hammer is barely enough. Still, it will do the job well enough I think.

Raising Luxmore, I let the energy rush through me, gathering up as much power as possible. Then I bring Luxmore down with a crack louder than thunder. The sheer amount of force in my strike nearly blows me off my feet. But I remain on my feet and steady myself further for the next one, and the next, and so on.

Every strike slowly flattens the metal like a pancake. The astralite is quite tough though, and it takes what feels like thousands of strikes to flatten to the thinness required. Then it takes even more to mold them into the proper wing shape. I am not going to ask how simply hitting it with my hammer can do all this stuff, as it is really fucking convient.

Once the metal part is done, I am tasked with creating the harness out of the spider thread. Which given the fact that I don’t have the required facilities, is really fucking tedious. I’ll add some sort of tailor bench onto my list of later problems. It isn’t that bad though, it just requires a lot of precise handiwork that I am shockingly good at.

It is a lot faster than all the hammering though, and before I know it I am setting the core into the harness. Then with a few finishing touches, I attach the wing bits to the harness. With a rush of satisfaction, the craft is completed.

This craft was a bit simpler than my others, and I have definitely gotten a lot more fluent in the process. So it is really nice to see that I am growing stronger in ways that levels can’t quite quantify.

However, I am not just a smith, so this isn’t quite the end. Next up is the magical part. I enter that weird state where it feels like I am speaking to the equipment, which seems far less silly in comparison to some of my recent experiences. Fancy goose-goddesses and ancient dragons are far more insane than talking to inanimate objects, after all.

My intention for these wings is very simple. I want them to amplify Dovey’s natural powers. Oh, and I add in a bit about them perhaps helping him slice things. Just in case he has to do some close quarters aerial combat.

A sense of finality washes over me as I imbue the wings with such a simple command. I can feel a sense of power rise up in them too though. But before I can see how they ended up, I am faced with the problem of coming up with a name.

It takes me a few seconds of thinking, but suddenly the perfect name comes to me. Wings of Callen. What does it mean? No fucking clue, but it definitely seems right for these wings.

As I finish the naming, a wave of tiredness washes over me. Ah, now comes the passing out part. But I don’t feel all that tired, so it shouldn’t take too long. Luckily I just manage to catch the ding before I fall asleep.

‘Ding’, Forging of Wings of Callen (Uncommon) successful.
