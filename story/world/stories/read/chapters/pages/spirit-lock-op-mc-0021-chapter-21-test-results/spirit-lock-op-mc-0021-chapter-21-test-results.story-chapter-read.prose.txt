Keaden cracked the door again. “He’s here.”

“Send him in,” Mayle said.

“Yes, ma’am.”

Hayden entered Mayle's office with his hands behind his back. Despite being called into the president’s office on the first day—the day that would determine his acceptance into Junolyx University—he appeared as indifferent as he did on the arena’s stage. He stood in front of her desk, patiently waiting for her to speak.

“I’m curious,” Mayle mused. “Why do you think you’re in here?”

“Either to warn me of Vestra Alicibria’s mother or to discuss my test results.”

“Both. Let’s talk about the test first. Your answers were… strange. May I presume that you will stand behind them?”

“Yes.”

“Okay, then…” Mayle picked up a pad to review his test. “While most of your answers aren’t blasphemous by any standards, they do treat hypotheses as absolute fact. That’s offputting, but not nearly as much as some of your other answers. Your answer regarding Haeta Core Disease is flat out contrary, and the rest sound like… the grand delusions of a self-assured schizophrenic.”

“Such as?”

“Let’s see,” Mayle said with playful sarcasm. “In one answer, you claimed, quote, A spirit’s technique is a biological advantage, not an innate feature, and may be changed. They are not much different from humans in that regard; what separates us is that we have ultimate flexibility whereas they have superior amplification properties…” She looked up. “Do you really believe that humans and spirits are the same?”

Hayden’s bored expression didn’t change. “Are you a fan of magic tricks, Ms. Haya?”

Mayle laced her fingers and put her elbows on her desk. “I’ve been known to indulge.”

“Then allow me.” He walked to her desk, lifted his fingers, and a shard of ice formed in his hands. It released cold steam like dry ice, and when he dropped it onto her desk, it rattled and clinked until it came to a stop. Mayle picked it up in a haze. Sure enough, it was cold, and when she lifted her fingers, they dripped with water.

“What the…” Mayle wiped water along the desk, watching as its unique polar properties caused it to stick together. “What did you just do?”

“I used an ice art.”

“But our seer said that all of your gates are closed.” She activated the Divine Eyes spirit, making her eyes shimmer with gold. She was shocked to confirm his gates were closed. “So how…?”

The answer suddenly hit her. She picked up the pad to read one of his test answers aloud.

“Subgates are unnecessary to perform coran conversions—they merely simplify and speed up complex processes. Even main gates are unnecessary for the conversion, but their attraction properties are often necessary to use spirits with considerable transformative power…”

She looked up and saw Hayden staring at her emotionlessly.

“Do you still take me for a schizophrenic?" he asked.

“No…” Mayle whispered. “I take you for a vengeful spirit. Because there’s no way that an eighteen-year-old student casually solved obscure hypotheses that don't even exist.”

“Vengeful, is… rather pointed, don’t you think?”

“You just shattered a candidate’s jaw even though it was unnecessary.”

“We disagree on that assertion.”

“Oh, you disagree. Did you also disagree with Loricks over the status and power of the student body here?”

“I did.”

“Okay… Well I guess opinions are free, but reality is costly. And the reality is, there are three students here that are too powerful for me to control—and you just hurt the worst of them. That wouldn't have happened if you agreed and exercised basic common sense.”

Hayden's lips curved into a thin, sardonic grin, as if something about Vestra's power amused him.

Mayle was furious about that expression. Hayden belonged at her university. He had every right, he did nothing wrong—but his hubris ground into her brain like a dental drill. It made her snap.

“Stop grinning! That kid’s mother runs the third largest rift harvesting business in the universe. Her legal department is three thousand seven hundred and sixteen times the population of this campus—I just checked. That’s why I took Vestra off the physical exam—just to ensure there wouldn’t be problems. But we're here. You know why? Because no one can tell him no. Not me… not the administrators… and certainly not the referee. Vestra’s an outlier in a school of outliers—and you just broke his jaw because you disagreed that he was powerful. Is this starting to sink in yet?”

Hayden looked her in the eyes. “No.”

Mayle nearly threw him out of her office, but stopped herself when he said, “Vestra’s mother isn’t a concern.”

“Excuse me, what?”

“You assumed that I would act rashly—but I never do. I broke his jaw to send a message, since I could afford to. It seems as though fate put me up against the one person you cannot control, and that's extremely inconvenient—but do not take me for a fool.”

“Oh, you can afford it? Your magic's not enough to save you, Hayden.”

“It doesn't need to. I can release technology tomorrow that will allow space shuttles to operate within rifts. The second I do, the two largest harvesting companies will join hands to destroy her.”

Mayle laughed. “Bullshit.”

Hayden rolled his fingers, and small fireballs danced around them. “I can prove it. Come by my room tonight with a coran crystal and some paint—I’ll demonstrate.”

The fire flickered in Mayle's eyes as she watched in a trance. “If you have this technology… Why aren’t you using it? The auction value would be in the quintillions. You could buy anything. Buy a planet. The best spirits. An army. So why are you even here?”

“Because I want something in the Heline Rift, and it will be cumbersome to get in if there's celestial militaries warring for my technology.”

Those words wrapped Mayle’s body in gooseflesh. “The rift… You know what's in it?”

“I do. For all intents and purposes, you can view me as a time-traveler. I'm here for the rift—and I'm at Junolyx because it's holding the entrance ceremony. The rift will also open up near here. That's all.

“That said, my presence will be the greatest windfall you've ever experienced. If you isolate and protect me for five weeks, I will bring you boundless wealth, and rain cold wrath on Vestra's mother and anyone else that threatens you. Trust me, Ms. Haya. The day his mother truly threatens me is the day she will learn how weak and defenseless she truly is. Whether that day comes now or in the future is up to you."

Mayle sucked a deep breath. Hayden’s bold claims checked out. His answers, his behavior, his arts. The horix, the aura—everything. Yet the thing that sold her was his eyes. They weren't honest—they were certain. Given his threat, they were a terrible sight to behold.

“Okay.” Mayle said quietly, unbelieving her own words. “I’ll take you in. If your technology is real.”

“Bring a coran crystal by my dorm tonight," Hayden said. "I’ll prove it.”

“Good. Is there anything else?”

“Yes, I’ll need a powerful contact in the auction industry. Do you have one?”

Mayle took a sharp breath. “I do.”

“Good. Coran crystal. Paint. Contact. Okay?”

“Okay… I'll transfer you to a suite and get the supplies. Keep your link on.”

“Thank you.” He turned and strode to the door. She didn't stop him—but he stopped himself, and turned back. “Why’d I end up with Vestra? There was a mix up, yes?”

Mayle smiled bitterly. “Oh, that. Vestra tried to fight Toa Qualian, but couldn't remember his name, so he just said, the ‘scholarship student’ everyone's talking about. He was the student everyone was talking about. But then this happened.”

She cast the video of Hayden rejecting Kayline Iska onto a widescreen. “Look familiar?”

Hayden chuckled darkly at the revelation. “Two words…” he muttered. “Two words was all it took to earn me a multigalactic enemy…” He opened the door and turned back. “Try to make concessions with Vestra’s mother first. If she doesn't accept, reject her boldly. There will be severe—yet temporary—blowback, but if there are critical problems, I'll intervene. So don't hold back.”

Mayle stared at the door after he left. I’ll intervene. That's what he said. Hayden spoke like a parent talking to a child—and for some reason, she believed him. He made her feel very small, as if she were a baby animal that a tiger was grooming for dinner. She couldn’t even remember the last time she felt that way—she didn’t know if she ever had.

“Keaden!” Mayle yelled.

He opened the door. “Yes ma’am?”

“Transfer Hayden Reskco to a suite in the South Tower and give him strong guards. That’s final—don’t ask questions. Goodbye.”

Her assistant gave her a complicated expression. “As you wish.”

Once he left, Mayle called Belia Alicibria in augmented reality mode.

A brunette with a cruel face materialized before her, sitting on the chair opposite her desk.

“Why were you ignoring my phone calls?” Belia demanded.

Mayle’s face hardened. “Your son committed serious felonies today, Belia. Publicly. On video.”

“You’re talking about my son?”

“Felonies,” Mayle repeated. “Coercion. Extortion. Intimidation. Assault. On video. Your son is a criminal, Belia—and therefore, I must act accordingly. Luckily for you, your son isn’t expelled. As long as he issues a letter of apology to Junolyx and Hayden Res—”

“Apology?” Belia interrupted. “Two A-rank healers are struggling to heal my son’s jaw right now. It’s so bad that one of them said he’d never talk right again. The other warned me that he’ll have psychological trauma.”

Mayle scoffed. “Your son killed a boy last year—If you’re seeking empathy, you won’t find it.”

Belia narrowed her eyes. “Do you want to be my enemy?”
