Risha stepped through the portal, returning to her home in the council. She felt significantly more rested after her bath with the other Grand Mother, even if it was only a bath in spirit.

She walked through the path of broken blades and into her tent. She moved to the two chainswords hanging on the wall. Her hand brushed against their blades, sensing the young spirits inside them.

“Igni, do you know how I can find the Ancient Shaman?”

The flame left her and rose from the floor, taking the form of a wolf, its fur on fire. “He will call you, he must. No finding him on your own, nope. There are deeper things that search for him, and he cannot be found.”

Risha nodded, “I thought so. It’s time for me to head back then.”

Igni let out a wide smile, his tongue lolling, then he dissolved into smoke and reentered her body.

Risha left the council, and opened her eyes to her tent in the physical world. She let out a breath, the air misting in the cool evening temperatures. Outside, she heard the soft pitter patter of rain landing against the leaves of the trees and the leathers of her tent.

She stood up, clipped her fur cloak to her and brought her hood up before leaving the tent.

The Goblin Warrior standing at the entrance to her tent bowed to Risha, rain dripping down her chitinous helm.

Risha placed her hand on the warrior’s shoulder, and channeled Igni’s power. Steam rose from the warrior and she stopped the small shiver she’d had.

The warrior watched Risha’s back as the Grand Mother walked into the light rain, a small “thank you,” barely audible

Risha’s eyes adjusted to the darkness easily, her steps sure as she made her way to the kobold camps.

Monsters walked past her, not recognizing her with her hood pulled up and the haze of the rain in the darkness.

The wolves had keener noses as she entered their territory. They bowed to her, making way as she walked to the tents of their alphas.

The kobold section of the camps was alive with life despite the rain. They howled and barked as they fought, drank and played. The circle of alphas was no different, loud and boisterous with those who felt lucky enough to fight another day.

A large bonfire sat in the middle of their camp, and a Black Lupus fought a Gray Lupus under the firelight, the others cheering them on as no real damage was done.

Risha looked over the wolves, recognizing several council members. Some of them were not as well known to her, having recently risen to power, or having traveled from the part of the mountains where they made home.

An old wolf stood out most prominently to her. He was a Gray Lupus, and she noticed how the other wolves respected him, even in their playful states. His muzzle was white with age, and he watched everything with sharp eyes.

Yet, she did not know him.

She walked over to the wolf, her footsteps silent from [Tribal Hunter] until she sat beside him.

She watched him for a long moment, noticed as his nose twitched in confusion, some part of him noticing her despite the power of her skill. She wondered at his level, something inside her telling her that he held quite the power inside his frame.

“I do not know you.”

The wolf looked at her in a smooth motion, any surprise at her appearance expertly hidden. “You have not come to meet me, Alpha.” The old kobold answered, his gaze turning back to the fighting wolves.

Risha hummed, “Was Risha supposed to meet you?”

“Any new Alpha knows to greet the pack elders.” He answered, “Any kobold Alpha that is.”

“I see.” Risha watched the white muzzle of the wolf, trying to and unable to read his thoughts. “Are you angry that I killed Earth-Shaker?”

“Earth-Shaker was a fool and a tyrant, a wolf ruled by his instincts. He only survived because the fox led him by the nose.” He finally turned to look at her, his eyes staring her down, “No, I am angered that my people live and die on the whims of an alpha who does not know the simplest of our ways.”

Risha’s blood pumped from the challenge he presented, the disrespect in his gaze clear. She let the anger show, her teeth bared as her spirit surrounded the two of them.

He did not cower from her, strong in the face of her spirit.

Risha tilted her head considering his words, then bowed, “Alpha Risha greets the Elder of the Pack.”

The wolf didn’t acknowledge her, instead turning to look at the end of the fight as the Black Lupus pinned the Gray to the dirt floor. “I am Broken-Claw, and I do not acknowledge the Alpha before me.”

“I won my challenge against Earth-Shaker.”

“And power is not the only truth to the world.” He responded.

“Risha is not a child.” Risha growled, “She will not move just for the sake of an Elder’s approval.”

“Good. You would not earn it in such a way.” Another challenger walked up, this one being a fox with brown fur. “Do not think you own my people just because you killed that whelp.” The Black Lupus charged the fox, only for his claws to pass through where the fox had been standing as the fox disappeared into mist. “We are indebted to you for bringing our children their own voices, but that does not mean you have earned the right to lead us.”

Risha suddenly felt the weight of her responsibility. What had she done to earn the right to send them to their deaths? Why was she the one chosen as a leader? The worst part about the Pack Elder’s words was that he was right. The power gifted to her by the system does not mean that she had such rights.

“Risha is not perfect, Broken-Claw. She does not see every blade, she does not save every pup. But she is the Grand Mother and you are her child. She will become an alpha worthy of the kobolds, whether you acknowledge her or not.”

The Brown Fox reappeared behind the Lupus, its maw collapsing on the back of his neck and pushing the larger wolf to the ground.

Broken-Claw turned to her, and studied her for a long moment, his eyes sharpened, as his own teeth showed. “I am not the only Pack Elder who refuses to acknowledge you, and the others will not stay as passive as I am. If you truly believe your words, then you’ll have to prove them again, and again.”

“Then that’s what I’ll do.” Risha stood up, and pulled her hood tighter. She bowed her head, “It was… good, to meet you, Broken-Claw.”

“It’s about time you greeted me, Risha.”

Risha stood straight, then walked away.

Elder Kulos was right, her hold on the kobolds was weaker than she’d thought. She wasn’t used to that kind of open disrespect, but in a way, it was refreshing.

She walked around the kobold camps for a while, simply observing them until the rain stopped, then made her way to an open glade where she could look at the moon and the stars.

She used to do this all the time with Glo, watch the night sky and study the stars. There wasn’t much else to do as a young goblin, and those moments had felt so peaceful.

There’s something about the beauty of the sky, some indescribable and wondrous feeling that you can only understand when you are there, seeing the vastness of all those lights.

Instinct was the only warning she had as a flash of power exploded itself from behind her.

She rolled out of the way as the ground rent upwards, piercing the spot she’d been moments before.

She landed in a crouch, then jumped away again as a knife pierced the darkness.

It pierced her leg, drawing blood, and sending poison through her that Igni quickly burned away.

She switched into [Pack Leader: Gray], and she dodged another dagger from the darkness. Her eyes focused, piercing the unnatural darkness she hadn’t noticed, distracted by the night sky.

A figure walked through, covered in black cloth, the inky scales around its eyes the only part of it visible.

Risha’s hand went to her waist, only to realize that she didn’t have her chainswords. Instead, she grabbed the dagger she kept tied to her thigh, and pulled it out.

Another figure came from the darkness, standing beside her. The Kobold wore a silk cloak, the silk blending into the background, just as Mist-Prowler’s had. A staff rested in his hands, a dark crystal at its top. He faced the assassin with her.

Under the cloak, Risha caught the briefest glimpse of black fur and tall ears. A Jackal.

“Do you sense their power, Grand Mother?” The Jackal asked as the figure approached the two of them from the darkness. “It is beyond me alone, but I shall provide what assistance I can.”

Risha nodded, having sensed the same thing. The power of the Spark flowed through the mysterious assassin.

She raised her dagger, then drew the second dagger from where it had pierced her leg. [Mother’s Ferocity] kept her standing from the sudden pain.

She bared her teeth as her blood pumped through her. Her eyes narrowed on the enemy, then she stepped, dodging earth as it rose from the ground to pierce her.

She reappeared in front of the scaled assassin, her blade meeting his. The power of a spark attempted to break her dagger, but her own spirit pushed against it as she swept forward with her own dagger.

The assassin faded into mist, just like the fox she’d been watching earlier, then he reappeared beside her, his sword stabbing forward.

A skeletal hand rose from the ground, grabbing the sword and holding it long enough for Risha to jump away.

Bone shards flew past Risha and into the assassin, only for them to be blocked by the scaled assassin’s sword.

Risha stepped forward, her daggers flashing in the moonlight as they deflected off of the sword.

***

Behind Risha, Wind-Whisperer the Jackal, stabbed his staff into the ground, then drew a fast circle. Voices rose from the darkness, the dead reaching for the Jackal’s mind as he called upon their power. He ignored them.

Risha and the assassin disappeared and reappeared in the darkness around him as he drew symbols into the ground in an arcane ritual.

The dead did not like their rest being disturbed.

***

Risha took another cut, but refused to back off. The Jackal was attempting something, and she knew she had to keep the assassin distracted until he could accomplish his task, which meant taking more cuts, Igni focused on burning away the strange poison that filled her from every slash.

Her own attacks did nothing, the power of the spark resisting her spirit, and her daggers unable to pierce the black cloth the assassin wore.

She focused on his eyes, knowing it to be a weak point, until finally, a dark and deep power rose from around her.

Ghostly hands rose from the forest floor, grabbing the arms and legs of the assassin, and stopping his movements.

Risha took advantage of the opportunity, her dagger piercing deep into the assassin’s eyes before he turned into mist yet again.

He didn’t reappear and Risha cursed as she turned to find a sword piercing the Jackal’s back.

Risha stepped again, her daggers slashing with fury as the Jackal fell to the floor. This time, she fought on even ground, his loss of vision making it harder for him to follow as she pushed the advantage from his blindside.

He misted away, reappearing next to a nearby tree.

“You witch. You will die like the animal you are, goblin!”

Risha kept her eyes on the assassin as a figure skulked through the darkness towards him.

"Your insults are just as puny as you are, you fish-brained weakling.” Risha answered.

The assassin opened his mouth to respond, but a sword ignited in white fire as Undergoblin Lolo sliced forward.

The assassin reacted with insane speed, his sword blocking the swipe as the power of the two sparks faced each other.

A heartbeat echoed through the forest, and Lolo’s sword cut through the assassin’s sword, and through his neck.

The assassin’s head fell to the ground, but Risha did not watch as she hurried to the Jackal on the floor, [Mother’s Care] focusing on keeping the kobold alive.
