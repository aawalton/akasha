Elion scanned the pit for Keyla as he climbed down the scaffolding. He didn’t see her, moving among the men and women. A clear hierarchy existed down here; the foremen, wearing high visibility vests and protective helmets, operating the equipment and machinery, and the owned, dirty, tired, and wielding pickaxes.

Only a half dozen foremen watched over more than one-hundred owned. Elion noted that the foremen didn’t even seem to have any kind of weapons. He wondered where Keyla had been assigned to work.

As he crossed the floor of the pit, heading for a pickaxe rack, he spotted someone he recognized. Ryfer, the huge man Keyla had pointed out to him, digging away at a wall.

The digging was progressing outward, in tunnels, through softer soil and clay. The floor, some kind of hard, smooth stone, seemed to be as deep as the pit was meant to go.

Elion maneuvered his way, weaving past people working, trying to stay at least five feet away from everyone. He managed to get a pickaxe from a rack, then he moved over to the tunnel where he’d seen Ryfer digging. For Keyla’s sake, he’d try talking to the big man while they worked.

One of the foreman worked with a large tube, like a vacuum, sucking up the dirt and rocks that the owned broke away from the wall. Other foreman directed the placement of support structures and scaffolding to prevent cave-ins.

“I got somethin’!” A woman yelled, pointing. “It’s red!”

Elion saw a glimmer of something shiny protruding from the dirt where she was digging. A pair of foremen ordered the woman away, sending her to dig elsewhere. The foremen used a machine to extract the object from a wall and stash it into a crate.

The tunnel extended a dozen feet beyond the floor of the pit. It was wide enough for ten people to work at the end of it, but only about 6 were. Good for Elion, since keeping 5 feet away from other people challenged his spatial awareness.

Ryfer worked two workers down.

Elion struck the wall in front of him with his pickaxe. The sharp point of the tool sank a few inches into the stony clay and clanged against a stone. Elion pulled the pick, and a chunk of wall crumbled to the ground.

He repeated the process, pulling clumps away from the wall, until the pile of dirt and stones in his way grew too large. He moved to the side and began digging at another place. A foreman making his rounds with the dirt vacuum came and sucked up the debris.

Some large stones didn’t fit down the vacuum hose, and the man gestured for Elion to smash them up. Elion complied, then continued digging at the wall.

His hands stung. Blisters formed on the pads of his palms and fingers. Even gripping the haft of the pick burned. He tried switching his grip, which helped for a time until he developed new blisters.

His pace of work slowed, and he wished for some gloves. He’d always wondered what real life Minecraft would be like. Now he knew: miserable.

But he continued working. Workers around him cycled from place to place. He tried to get closer to Ryfer, and see if he could get a chance to talk to the man.

Something rippled in the air, and the lights flickered, dimming strangely. A wave of vertigo washed over Elion, and a weight lifted off his shoulders.

He dropped his pick, looking around, surprised.

The sound of the vacuums cut off. Other owned stopped working.

Elion recognized this feeling. He’d felt it before, back in the sewers with Keyla. It had shut down Praxis, turning off his translation boon. His armor had disappeared.

He tried to call up Praxis. He reached out for his knife, and realized he couldn’t feel it any longer. His abilities were gone again. The owned around him jabbered in a strange language, and began lying down on the ground. The foremen scrambled, running some kind of emergency procedure with the equipment. They had encountered this before.

If my abilities are gone, Elion thought, are Bortim’s? He probed his mind, cautiously searching for the link to the bondhold. He did not feel compelled to keep working. Could he just leave?

Elion ran for the stairs. He made it halfway up out of the pit before the distortion faded. Bortim’s bond strengthing snapped back into place with the subtlety of a wrecking ball. Elion staggered, wracked by the force of Boff’s orders. He fell to the ground, groaning.

A spinning circle obscured his vision for a moment, followed by a Praxian notification.

> << Reconnecting to Praxis >>

> << Please stand by, temporary suppression detected >>

> << Ascendency Path tracking interrupted, recalibrating experience spectrum >>

> << You have persevered in developing your abilities. You progress along the Path of Dawn. 3 Experience Points rewarded. >>

“Three?” Elion said aloud, startled by the message. “Why?”

> << You have 1 ability point to assign >>

> << Please visit the nearest Altar to select a new skill and assign your ability points >>

> << Name: Elion James Walker >>

> << House: Starhold >>

> << Ascendency: Aurelian Path of Dawn >>

> << Level/XP: 5/35 >>

> << Abilities (Level): Manifest Armaments (4), Save a Friend (0) >>

> << Boons: Translation >>

> << Quests: None >>

Elion rolled over, getting up off the ground. He had to get back to work. Picking himself up off the scaffolding, he stumbled down the steps and retrieved his pickaxe. A few other owned around him pointed and chuckled.

“New guy,” one of them said. “How’d that feel?”

“Don’t worry,” another one added. “We all think about trying it, every time it happens. Good thing there’s people like you around to remind us not to.”

Elion ignored them, returning to work. Whatever was going on here was some how gaining him experience, so he wasn’t going to complain about that. But what was it?

As he dug at the wall, he noticed a strange sensation; a kind of magnetism drawing him back to one corner of the dig. He was trying to work his way over to Ryfer, the man from Aterfel, so he could ask him about Selna.

But every time he stopped paying attention, he found himself moving back toward this specific corner. Maybe it’s some weird feng shui thing with the shape of the room.

After working for around an hour, he managed to place himself working alongside Ryfer. Not so close that Elion had to move away, but close enough that he could talk to the man.

“Is your name Ryfer?” Elion asked.

The man’s pick thudded into the wall of the shaft, lodging there. Muscles flexed along Ryfer’s arms. His head turned slowly, small eyes examining Elion.

He didn’t speak.

With a tug, he pulled his pick from the wall and continued working.

“From Aterfel?” Elion pressed. He had to know if this was the man Keyla thought he was. “Ryfer, from Aterfel.”

The man looked at him again.

“You went with a group, followed Prator to New Kairn Tol,” Elion said.

The man scowled. “Who told you that?” he asked, mopping sweat from his thick brow.

“Someone from Aterfel,” Elion said. “You know Selna Aerden?”

Ryfer’s scowl turned into a glare as he growled. He took a step toward Elion. Elion took a step back, trying to maintain the required distance.

“Come here,” Ryfer hissed, advancing.

Elion stumbled away. “Wait,” he said.

Ryfer discarded his pickaxe and snatched at Elion. Elion looked desperately for a way to deescalate the situation. He didn’t know what he’d done to offend the man, though.

“You’re one of them,” Ryfer said. “Aurelian.” He spat the word out like a curse.

“No, no, no,” Elion protested. Ryfer grabbed for Elion again. Elion dropped his pickaxe, trying to maintain distance. Ryfer’s grabs were coming close, though, the man had long arms. If Ryfer tried striking, Elion’s orders were clear; he’d have to let him.

So he tried staying out of range, hoping Ryfer wouldn’t try throwing a punch from so far away. Dancing away backward, Elion stepped out into the light of the main pit. A few people had noticed the altercation, watching curiously while they worked.

Elion’s foot caught on one of the large vacuum hoses. He stumbled into one of the lighting arrays, knocking it to the grown as he pitched over backward. Elion landed hard on his back, and the lighting crashed to the ground in an explosion of sparks. Ryfer sprang atop him. His first blow caught Elion across the chin, rattling his teeth.

Reflexively, as Ryfer’s next blow whistled through the air, Elion used Manifest Armaments. He felt his strength increase, his energy blossoming inside him. Ryfer’s next blow struck him in the nose, but Elion’s defensive buffs in his ascended state neutralized much of the blow.

Forced to let Ryfer’s blows land, and prevented from touching Ryfer himself, Elion panicked. He saw blood on Ryfer’s hand. The skin of his knuckles had split when Ryfer struck him.

What else can I do? I’ve been ordered not to fight. How can I stop him?

Ryfer punched again, smashing into Elion’s face and bouncing his head off of the hard ground beneath him. The blows continued. Elion struggled, trying to find a way out of this.

The blood on Ryfer’s hand sprayed through the air as he swung again. “Just die!” Ryfer shouted.”

Blood. On his hand. He was injured. It’s worth a try.

Elion gestured. “Save a Friend,” he declared.

The flow of energy burst from Elion, cocooning Ryfer in a protective shell which flashed and sank into his skin. Elion scrambled out from under Ryfer as the man stiffened, then fell unconscious to the ground.

Elion stumbled to his feet, stepping back five feet from his opponent. Ryfer groaned, shifting as he fought against the stabilizing protection Elion placed on him.

Everyone in the pit stared at Elion. His golden armor clothing shimmered in the light. He felt the pull of his knife overhead, up at the warlocks camp. He disarmed, his clothing shifting back to dirty and grimy.

Elion walked over to his fallen pickaxe. He picked it up, and returned to pounding on the wall. He heard Ryfer grunting and groaning, until the man fought off the stabilizing spell and climbed to his feet.

Behind him Elion could hear frantic discussion among the foremen, but he had to keep digging. Blood seeped from a wound on his hand, running down his forearm and dripping from his elbow.

Several foremen restrained Ryfer, but nobody approached Elion. They left him to his digging.

Elion fell into the zone, hacking at the wall, digging deeper and deeper. His hands stung with each impact, his muscles ached with each heave of the axe. But he had to keep digging. He had to do it for Liora. He cursed Ryfer, the fool, for trying to make him violate his orders.

Why had the man reacted so strongly to Elion? What had Elion said? His jaw ached where Ryfer had struck him, and he thought one of his teeth might have loosened. Ryfer had called Elion an ‘Aurelian’ like it was a dirty word. Strange coming from a man who had followed the Aurelian Prator here to New Kairn Tol.

Then again, Prator seemed to be living lavishly while Prator slaved away as on of the owned.

As Elion dug, he noticed that the others stayed far away from him. Fine with Elion, since he had to stay away from everyone else anyways. He knew he had made a major mistake by revealing his abilities to everyone.

Venya and her warlocks would hear about this. They would come for him. Would he be able to fight them when they came?

Exhausted, he continued digging. He couldn’t fight the command at all, his mind struggling to hold onto any thoughts other than digging. He barely noticed the foreman vacuuming up his tailings.

Then he found himself stumbling over heaps of stone and dirt. The rocks Elion mined from the wall, fallen to the ground and not removed. He shifted to a new area, digging along the wall until he again ran out of space. No foreman came to remove the soil.

Elion pushed forward, climbing up onto the refuse and digging away at the wall. He compacted the dirt beneath his feet as he did; he was probably going to have to break it up again when the foreman came with the vacuum hose.

It had been some time since Elion had noticed anyone, or anyone being in his way. Dirt and stones surrounded him. He dug, pushing deeper and deeper into the wall, scraping his tailings out back to where foremen could remove them. His one man tunnel thrust into the ground, burying him, surrounding him, until he felt his pickaxe strike something strange.

Elion stopped, taking in his surroundings. He could hardly see anything. A tunnel surrounded him. He turned. Behind him, nearly ten feet away, the small tunnel he had dug opened up into the larger pit area.

He pulled his pickaxe out of the dirt in front of him, and swung again. He struck something hard. Something stronger than the crumbling rocks he’d been breaking through all afternoon. The pick rang out with a clear tone, buried a few inches into the dirt ahead of him.

Working more quickly, Elion began clearing clay away, digging with his hands. A stone shone in the darkness, white against the brown earth, smooth against its roughness. He wrestled with the muck, uncovering first a shoulder, then an arm. A hand in a gesture he recognized.

This was a statue of Aurelia. An Altar.

Elion’s orders were to fetch a foreman. He scuttled out of the tunnel he had dug, one that took him straight to the statue, and emerged into the bright work lights of the pit.

Everyone was gone.

One man sat atop a machine in the pit, lounging, like he was waiting for something.

His unbound white hair flowed down over his shoulders and the back of his seat. Bronze skin shone under the stark work lights. His black cloak hung to the side, dangling carelessly over the machine. Elion knew the man.

“Prator.”
