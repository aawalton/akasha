In the short time of battle after Azure departed, Dax watched a horror unveil, as the undead champion wreaked havoc on their numbers very soon after executing the True Clone of Odysseus. With a swipe of his blade, he dismissed the Faerie Dragon Porthos — currently in the guise of a gargoyle — even while he executed another minotaur with the crack of his whip. Only a fraction of a second passed as he moved on at lightning speed.

“Fall back!” Dax commanded in the gestalt. “Avoid him!” But it was useless. They literally couldn’t.

What was left of Dax’s abilities was paltry — she’d expended nigh everything keeping as many alive for as long as possible. Stalling, ultimately. Now, she was offering little more than a passive, luck-based buff, though with a very wide radius. She knew she couldn’t dive into combat directly, as it just wasn’t her specialty compared to the rest. She had her role, and through specialty eyewear beneath her helm, she was viewing mirrors in rapid-fire, shifting fashion, catching many angles of the battle to manage the whole of it, constantly making tweaks here and there.

“The general’s curse, sometimes,” a voice chimed grimly in her head, “to be pragmatically reduced to a mere spectator of the true sport underway. And here, another oncoming slaughter to witness, hmm? Just like back in-”

Shut up, you fuck — I’m concentrating on the battle! It was an inner command, and she successfully chased the voice out, for once. Hrm. I need to remember that excuse.

Brambles and vines tried to jam up the wraith’s approach, but he cut through them much faster than they could grow. On he came, for Redberry.

Zadkiel was there, intercepting. He blocked a sword strike with his shackles, which caused a great fountain of sparks. But two more rapid slashes were inside his guard — one cut a deep gouge through his armor and into his muscled chest, and the second caught him right across the cheek, sending him sprawling and growling as he impacted the ground.

“Motherfucker!” Redberry screamed, outraged, as she leapt for the champion, hands turning into wicked talons.

No!

The undead champion let her talons deep inside his guard, and flinched away at the last second, gaining only a cut in his chest plate as he dashed aside. But Redberry was totally thrown off by the momentum of her mostly spoiled charge.

The blade came around dead for her neck, but growth burst from below her, carrying her up and away, the sword merely passing through continually-growing plant matter. Redberry exploded outward, carried on her spawning growth…

Right into the snapping, cracking trap of the whip waiting for her. She had only the sparsest moment to block it, and of the nine cracking whip tails of Entropy, she managed only a few, as multiple tails carried over the material and lashed her face and body.

Redberry screamed in agony as she was scourged and promptly disintegrated into ash. Another scream — of bristling outrage and frustration — echoed through the whole of the interconnected mental realms. Despite the desperate scenario, just about everyone was shocked and stunned by the terrible moment.

“She’s alright!” Vinewicker — Redberry’s Servitor ‘director’ — quickly followed up through the global gestalt. “I-It’s just the destruction of an Avatar!” Clearly, she was still shaken, much like the rest.

Bloody hell, I just grew another damned white hair from that.

It was certainly a setback, even as they’d technically been spared the disaster of losing Red ‘soul-wise’ due to the attack being wholly physical. However, her Avatar had been largely invested with all of Red’s built-up energy and resources over a century. On top of that, she had quest completion additives from two key battles, previously tied to the Avatar and now gone up in smoke. She’d likely never field the same power level again.

A howl that shook the ground came from Zadkiel as he charged at the back of the wraith, who spun around and raked the Demon King again with the blade. It didn’t stop the momentum, as Zadkiel expended some ability-use that had him glowing with a red nimbus. His fist landed right into the chest of the undead champion and dented it. They both went sprawling to the ground.

The undead champion bounced up quickly and stabbed Zadkiel through the back. There was a cracking sound, and Zadkiel seized and writhed.

Shit. He’s out. That’s Entropy damage, regardless.

A final coup de grace never quite came, as others were rushing in — exactly what Dax didn’t want, but after what happened, she was powerless to stop it. They wanted vindication for Red.

The undead champion tried a whip attack against Keinauz, but one of his own guardians took the hit; a troll was disintegrated for his king just like that. The wraith champion laughed derisively. “You can dish it, but you can’t take it, eh, little king of losers?”

Keinauz roared in fury as he surged forward, but his swipes were all avoided. As usual, despite his words, the champion was not inclined to match swords with the warrior-king.

The worst result of all soon became evident, as Canmore moved in to fight along with the rest.

Dax touched minds with him specifically. “Don’t do it, Canny! Please! Get away!”

Gently, but determinedly, Canmore replied, “Sorry, sweet love. We have to. Since when has stalling worked? We don’t know how long this buff will last, or if deaths refresh the duration… men are dying, and we can’t stop it…”

Making her vision flash around, she saw the truth: dozens more of their elites had fallen to the flanking armor-wraiths. Instead of stalling, the lot of them had up and decided themselves that they had to instead take advantage before they were all cut to pieces. Very few of the wraiths had been taken down at all, and none of them since the buff went up.

Overpowering her fear, she felt a reluctant admiration. It hadn’t been vindication for Redberry, after all — or, at least, it mostly wasn’t that. Dax was not in the midst of that horrible melee, so couldn’t feel what they felt, couldn’t know the same danger of their sacrificial specialty as the vanguard. None had given the command… it was just a mutual instinct within the fight they were waging. They were one like few could ever claim in the dance of that battle, and so, as one, they adjusted for the greater good.

And yet, it looks futile. Despite this, she buried her doubts and fears and said to Canmore, “Good luck. Don’t die.”

“I won’t, just for you!” came his cavalier reply, even as he was swinging his sword. But it looked like a feint to her eyes, as others also pretended to take advantage.

Dax blinked, quickly putting things together. It definitely took some of the tension out of her shoulders. “I see what you’re doing. I almost forgot you’ve been saving that shit all day.”

“Aww, you ruined the surprise! I can’t put anything past you, can I?” He skipped back, revealing the evidence, as before him, there was the undead champion, and perhaps the greatest lineup of armor wraiths positioned beyond.

“It’s best you remember that, Country Boy.”

“So I will, Country Girl.” Canmore pointed his greatsword and unleashed a blinding-bright, wide line attack of pure light, a once-a-day nuke from his advanced [Luminary Knight] class. It engulfed and fried a large selection of the wraiths. Only those who’d been damaged previously dissipated, which wasn’t all of them. Meanwhile, the undead champion was simply scorched, with his armor that much more damaged, and otherwise quite pissed.

The tension in Dax’s shoulders returned just like that.

It was worth a shot. It did something. He had to try.

The wraith went out of his way to go after Canmore, despite the knight having acted entirely defensively and retreating since the blast.

Canmore parried the blade twice. Once again, the doom that was the whip came as the follow-up, and Dax felt like her worst nightmare was about to be realized, because Redberry wasn’t there anymore to absorb the whip strikes.

Please! I can’t do this-

Twin blades flashed and came together, blocking the path of the gauntlet that directed the whip, spoiling the precision strike.

Deikmorn Brakka had just saved her lover’s life.

“You owe me one,” Deikmorn offered in the gestalt.

“Indeed, I do!” Canmore replied, but Dax knew that was meant for her.

Indeed, I do.

The undead champion was incensed by the interruption. The blade came flashing with fury as Deikmorn attempted a fighting retreat — usually, he’d be an expert at such a thing with his speed, but here, he was outmatched. Narrowly, he redirected one strike, dodged another, and moved evasively to avoid the follow-up of the whip once more.

This time, it was Gelmak moving in with his axe, primed to hit the gauntlet to throw it off.

The strike of the whip came up short instead, quickly revealed as a feint by the hyper-quick wraith, who instead darted in and tripped Deikmorn with a nasty sweep of his foot — an awful move if one cared about getting stabbed, but the champion had no fear of his opponent’s weaponry whatsoever.

True to the veteran's reactions, he did not go for the bait of attacking at all, instead moving his blades to guard against the sword, even as he was falling backward.

Instead of slashing, the sword under the power and speed of the wraith shifted to dart between the sword guards, thrusting past and right into the neck of Deikmorn, cutting through armor like it wasn’t even there and stabbing through.

Blood spewed.

As horribly and helplessly witnessed by Dax in mirror capture, Deikmorn’s eyes widened slightly, as his blades instinctually came up to arrest the piercing entropic sword, possibly to save him from immediate execution…
