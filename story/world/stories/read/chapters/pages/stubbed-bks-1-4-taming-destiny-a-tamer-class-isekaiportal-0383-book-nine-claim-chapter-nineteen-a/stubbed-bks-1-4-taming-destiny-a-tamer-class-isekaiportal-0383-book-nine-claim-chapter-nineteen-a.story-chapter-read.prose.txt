“Hold on there,” I instruct Tiria firmly, stepping between her and Loran. “I’ll investigate first. Afterwards, if he has developed an internal matrix, and he is willing to give you permission, perhaps you can try a few non-invasive tests.”

I can’t forget what the atmosphere was like in the basement of Tiria’s tower when we first arrived. I haven’t let her hound Noir for answers, and I won’t let her do the same to Loran. Privately, though, I would be curious about what she’s able to detect – if he has developed anything.

Tiria pouts but lowers her hand, the white light flickering and then dying entirely.

“If you insist,” she agrees, though reluctantly. Then she tilts her head on one side curiously. “How will you know if he has or hasn’t? Do you have a spell that will tell you? Or a Skill?” Then she ducks her head, peeking up through her lashes, perhaps to check if I’m annoyed at her invasive questions.

I’m not sure I want to reveal that I can see it to her yet. Technically she probably wouldn’t be able to do too much with the knowledge, but still…once I’ve told her, I can’t untell her. Perhaps I’ll leave it for now.

“He’s my Bonded; I’ll know,” I say in the end, letting her draw her own conclusions. From the frown that forms between her brows, she’s not happy with that, but she doesn’t try to press.

I nod in satisfaction and turn back to Loran.

“Do you mind if I look up here or would you prefer privacy?” And don’t let her make you feel that you have to agree to her examinations regardless, I add privately. That’s entirely your choice and I will back you up on it.

Thank you, Markus, Loran replies gratefully, then continues speaking aloud. “Here is fine, sir,” he confirms, so I lead him and Tiria to where we can perch on some boxes. I’d rather not dive deeply into his Core space while standing up in case I lose my balance when I’m not paying attention.

Closing my eyes to block out Tiria’s intense gaze, I focus on descending into Loran’s Core space.

At first, nothing appears to have changed. The space is as black as ever – luminous, but black. And then I see it: the faintest glimmer of light.

Excitement rises inside me as I zoom in closer and make out more details. Loran’s right – he’s started developing an internal matrix.

It’s only the very beginnings of one – a single thread that’s no bigger than the thinnest of my own filaments. But it exists and that’s more than he had before. I take some time to try to understand as much about it as possible, but honestly, there’s little more to learn at this point.

I pull out of Loran’s Core space, meeting his anxious gaze and Tiria’s impatient one. But I’m going to disappoint them both for a little longer.

“What made you think that you had developed an internal matrix?” I ask Loran, keeping my face and voice neutral. His expression falls a little – perhaps he’s assumed that my question means that he was mistaken. Tiria seems to draw the same conclusions if the irritated and disappointed expression that crosses her face is anything to judge by.

“I…” his eyes drop to the deck. “I was meditating with a Core when suddenly, I felt a kind of…fluttering feeling deep within. I thought that was an internal matrix but…I guess I was wrong.”

“You weren’t,” I tell him with a small smile. He glances back up, blinking several times.

“I wasn’t?”

“No.” My smile widens. “You have the beginnings of an internal matrix. Sorry for making you feel that you hadn’t – I just wanted to know what indications you’d had without anything colouring them.”

“Of course,” he answers, relief and excitement in his voice. Though I also feel hints of trepidation in the Bond. “What…what should I do now, then?”

“You must let me run some tests on you!” Tiria interjects before I can speak. I send her a quelling glance and she subsides – though probably not for long.

“How many Cores did you absorb before this happened? And what Tiers?”

“Three,” Loran answers immediately. “All Tier twos.”

I hum in response.

“And how did the Cores feel to you? Did they all feel the same? Or different?”

Loran hesitates.

“At first, I didn’t feel anything. I wondered if I was doing something wrong until I started seeing the Core reduce in size. Even then, I wondered if I was just wasting it. By the time I was getting to the end of that one, I felt that it was…earthy. Like the smell of dirt after rain. And there was a sense of belonging to a group that I…liked.”

I nod slowly.

“And the second?”

“That one was easier to feel straight away – like impatience and intolerance. But it felt too…forceful. It’s hard to say what it felt like.” He looks away in shame and frustration with himself.

“It was uncomfortable, I take it?” I check.

“Yes…no…not exactly.” He sighs heavily then tries again. “It just didn’t feel right, if that makes sense?”

“It does,” I assure him, though I can tell from Tiria’s shifting that she doesn't agree. But since she says nothing, I don’t pay her any attention. “What about the last? Was that also uncomfortable?”

“No, the reverse,” Loran admits. “It felt…silky smooth, though with edges. It was very different from the second – this one was full of patience and willingness to wait while hidden.”

Curious. Not all the Cores I gave to Loran to experiment with are from creatures I or my pack have killed – Nicholas gave us a good number from the family coffers, though mostly Tier twos rather than anything higher. I think he’s hoping that I will multiply his investment with Cores from the Lost Continent. But the way Loran described them certainly indicates the kind of creatures they might have come from.

I’m not sure about the first Core, but the second is probably one of the yelnas we killed near Zlona – impatience, intolerance, and forcefulness certainly match them well. And I suspect that the third is from one of the greater milas – the big-cat-like predators we also encountered in the forest near Zlona.

“And have you finished the third Core?” I ask.

Loran nods.

“I felt the flutter just as the last of the Core disappeared.”

Also interesting. The question now is whether Loran developed the beginnings of an internal matrix because he had finally absorbed enough Cores for it to stick…or because he absorbed the right Core.

I expect that Tiria will be interested to test that out and, honestly, so am I. I think we’d best see how this develops for Loran first, though, before trying to roll it out among other non-Classers.

“I think you should continue absorbing Cores,” I tell him decisively. “Our companions have made it very clear that, for them, growth and Evolution is instinct-based. Bastet told me when I first came across Cores that they know what’s best for them and they seek more of it. So I’d say that’s what you should do. Test different Cores, identifying which feel right to you, and which don’t.” I stop myself just before I suggest that he make a record about each of the Cores – though I know he’s started having writing lessons from Sarran, it’s only been a couple of tendays. I don’t want to embarrass him in front of Tiria.

Hm. Tiria. Perhaps that’s a solution.

I half-turn to include her in the conversation.

“Would you like to be involved with this at all?” I ask off-handedly. Impatience is clear in her expression.

“Of course, Lord Markus,” she practically bites out. Then, visibly calming herself, she adds, “What did you have in mind? My tests?”

“Perhaps, though that is another discussion. No, I was thinking that it might be a good idea to have an experienced researcher keeping track of what works and what doesn’t – perhaps you’d be able to find connections between which Cores feel right for Loran and which do not. This might help us determine what, exactly, causes the internal matrix to develop and grow.”

A speculative look crosses Tiria’s face and I practically see the gears whirring in her brain.

“I can certainly do that,” she agrees, and I would bet anything the faraway look in her eyes is due to considering how to structure her investigation. Then her gaze sharpens again. “And my tests?”

She addresses me, but I pointedly look at Loran.

What would you like to do? I ask him silently.

“I’m willing to let you examine me as long as there is no potential harm that might result from the tests,” he says finally, and I feel the courage it took him to qualify his decision when speaking to a noble, for all that Tiria doesn’t act like one most of the time. I send him a flicker of pride at how far he’s come since I met him. He pinks a touch, but his Bond feels more chuffed than embarrassed.

Reminding him that he can call on me if Tiria goes too far, I leave Loran and Tiria to get on with the examination.

By this point, I’m feeling a bit restless, and not in a state to continue my Water-Shaping. I’d suggest continuing my practice with Ninja and Bond Teleport, but all her training must have worn her out – the raptorcat is curled up on top of a coil of rope, fast asleep.

Since there’s space on the deck, I decide to redirect my energy into something more productive. I noted earlier that I haven’t spent much time on weapons training, and even if they haven’t come with us on the trip, I’m sure my trainers will find some way of punishing me if I slack off.

I spend the next couple of hours running through a few spear, knife, and archery drills, until even my enhanced body is feeling pleasantly fatigued and my stomach is growling. Seeing as it’s still within lunchtime even if the galley will stop serving soon, I head below decks to grab a bite to eat.

Before I can get there, though, I’m almost run over by two stampeding samurans.

“Hunter? River? What is it?” I ask in concern before noting the pink, yellow, and green all flashing together in their spikes and the sense of excitement in the Bond.

The eggs! River exclaims. Smith says they’re hatching!

My eyebrows shoot towards my hairline.

“What, now?” I ask, a little stupidly. River just grabs my forearm and pulls me forwards as we head back to the stairwell, descending to the deck below where the storage is.

Reaching the storage room, I see that Iandee and Catch are already there, crouching beside Happy in front of the open incubator boxes. The eggs are larger than when they were laid, and they’ve darkened to a deep yellow from where they had been pale. They’re also rocking gently from side to side – no doubt that’s what Happy identified as signs that they’ll be hatching soon.

The adults aren’t just standing by; they’re all making an odd chirring sound – something I’ve never heard from them before. Happy’s is higher-pitched; Catch and Iandee’s is deeper.

It’s a soothing, reassuring sound, and I wonder if it’s to reassure the babies in the eggs that it’s fine, they can come out now. River releases me and steps forward with Hunter to crouch on either side of Happy, joining in with their own chirrs.

I feel like an outsider for a moment and wonder if I should leave the room at least. I’ve never been part of a hatching before – when it happened in the village, it was the broodmothers who monitored the hatching. Evidently, without them present, the others are stepping in.

But then River, perhaps sensing my sudden uncertainty, glances back at me and gestures forwards with her snout, making her invitation very plain.

I hesitate for a moment and then step forwards, coming to crouch between River and Catch. Now part of the semi-circle around the carefully positioned egg-boxes, I feel the sound rumble in my chest. Somehow knowing that Animal Empathy will allow me to make it despite never having produced a similar sound in my whole life, I take a deep breath and then let it out, my own chirr joining the others. I love magic, really.

We crouch there for a while. I wouldn’t be able to say for how long. Time becomes irrelevant. Instead, we are just there. Together, as a village, helping the new generation to emerge from their eggs.

When the first crack appears, it’s almost silent. The baby samuran, who forces his egg-wet head out of the paper-thin eggshell, is not. He chirrs himself, the sound far higher-pitched than any one’s.

In response, all the adults just increase the volume, welcoming the first hatchling to this world.

The first, but as I see more cracks appearing in the eggs around him, far from the last.
