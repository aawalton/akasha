Chapter 74 – A Small Case of D.I.D.

The raging inferno which had covered the entirety of the newly formed glade.

Or so it seemed, to anyone who might have stood on the fringes of the flattened landscape, looking in. The last wave of the Phoenix’s explosive fury echoed across the blackened land, distorting the air with sweltering heat and turning silence into a tense, thrumming hum of power. Soot drifted down like snowflakes from the sky, blanketing the scorched earth in a thick, choking layer of grey.

But in the very centre of the blast zone, there was something.

The fire, which had roared in a furious column just seconds before, now stood frozen mid-surge around a circular field of distorted air. Flame was trapped in place, paused in time, its natural chaos reduced to eerie stillness.

It looked somewhat like a forcefield from afar, shimmering slightly in the air like a mirage, but it was no shield. It was a pocket of time, slowed to a crawl, a wedge carved into reality by magic.

And just behind it, stood Ezra.

His outline was blurry through the distortion of the heat, a silhouette suspended in a strange, glowing haze, but there was no mistaking the tight posture, the way his arm extended in control. He stood facing the wedge, it wasn’t that it reflected the fire, no. It had captured it, forced it into stillness, cocooned in a fragment of altered time. Embers were suspended mid-flicker, tendrils of smoke curled in halted spirals, and even motes of ash seemed to defy gravity, frozen in delicate patterns.

A single branch, flung from the blast, hung weightless beside him, caught mid-tumble.

Ezra’s boots rested on a patch of untouched earth, a tiny bubble of stillness carved out of the inferno. He wasn’t safe. He wasn’t unscathed. But he was alive.

Sweat rolled down his temples in slow arcs, his breath coming in sharp gasps. The strain of maintaining the time-slow zone crackled along his nerves like lightning stretched to snapping. His heartbeat thundered against the constraints of stillness, a deafening rhythm in his ears. Each second stretched endlessly, the magic feeding on his focus, on his energy, on the very core of his will.

When the explosion outside the slowed zone finally dissipated, fire curling away, ash beginning to fall, Ezra moved.

He stepped carefully to the side, now standing beside the wedge of slowed time, he released it.

The suspended flames, no longer held back, roared forward with renewed vengeance, striking the ground where he’d stood not a heartbeat before. The noise came like a crashing wave, the sound of delayed impact slamming into the air, loud enough to shake leaves from branches a mile away.

Ezra staggered, catching himself with a grimace. His muscles twitched from the exertion, his core trembled with magical fatigue. The taste of iron lingered in his mouth. But he didn’t pause. He didn’t afford himself that luxury.

The Phoenix was still there.

It stood at the far edge of the clearing, wings hanging limp, body heaving with shallow, ragged breaths. Its golden-red feathers, once vibrant like sunrise, were dulled, ash clinging to its scorched hide. One eye was half-lidded, barely tracking motion. Its once-magnificent plumage was tattered, without its previous glow, smoking in places, and still—still—even weakened, it radiated power.

Ezra saw his target and made a move.

His sprint through the smouldering remains wasn’t silent, but regardless, the Phoenix was too tired to react. Each step sunk slightly into scorched soil, blackened roots crumbling underfoot. Heat still rose in shivering waves, distorting the air. The stench of burnt earth and blood hung heavy.

He drew his dagger, short, well-crafted, and impossibly sharp. A weapon for precision. For moments like this.

He didn’t yell. He didn't have the energy to waste, he just leapt.

The blade buried into the Phoenix’s back, right at the base of its right wing. Flesh gave way with a sickening hiss. The Phoenix screamed—an awful, echoing sound of confusion and pain, shaking the sky itself. Its body convulsed, wings flared wide in reaction.

With a burst of energy, it batted its wings against the hot air in an attempt to escape. The ground buckled under its force, cracks forming in the dried earth. With a blast of heat and a panicked shriek, the Phoenix took flight, and Ezra was dragged skyward with it, still gripping the dagger’s hilt with both hands.

Air howled past him. The wind tore at his hair, ripped his cloak to tatters. He clung tight, one hand fumbling for additional purchase in the creature’s back. Feathers slick with hot blood seared his palms. His vision blurred with wind and smoke.

Below, the forest was no longer as luscious as it had been only hours before.

The various attacks and explosions had levelled it. Trees were shattered or incinerated, and the earth was bare and blackened for a perfect radius of a few-hundred feet. A massive, smoking crater remained where once there had been shadowed boughs and leaf-littered trails. The once beautiful woodland now contained a scar, raw and ugly, marring the landscape.

Ezra’s eyes scanned the edges.

Where had Marcel been? He had covered Marcel’s body with foliage, ferns and bark, placed him behind one of the larger oaks, about there, wasn’t it? But now, all that remained was charred splinters and the burnt remnants of a stump. No sign of life. But he was covered, maybe it protected him, even if just a bit.

His heart dropped.

How silly of him. Nothing could’ve survived that, although it was hard to see up there. There’d be no telling until he checked. There was a chance, no matter how small.

The Phoenix let out another screech and bucked. Ezra’s body flailed behind it, only the blade keeping him tethered. His muscles screamed in protest. He gritted his teeth and held on, even as blood streamed from his lip where he’d bitten it.

The Phoenix tried again to shake him, beating its massive wings in a spiralling turn.

Ezra dangled in midair, hundreds of feet above the burning world. Smoke drifted like ghosts beneath him.

The wind roared past Ezra, a howling beast that battered his ears and clawed at his clothes, stealing the breath straight from his lungs. The dagger was buried deep in the phoenix’s back, the only anchor holding him aloft as the great beast spiralled higher into the smoke-choked sky.

His fingers, slick with both the phoenix's’ and his own blood, ached with the effort of maintaining his grip. Every muscle in his body screamed for release, trembling with exhaustion. His shoulders burned like they were being torn from their sockets. His legs, wrapped desperately around the creature’s flanks, had long since gone numb.

Still, he held on.

The phoenix screeched again, a deafening, bone-rattling sound, its wings beating against the sky with the fury of a living inferno. A fresh blast of searing heat rolled off its molten feathers, scorching Ezra’s skin and setting his tattered cloak alight. He barely felt it anymore.

He shifted, desperately trying to wedge his foot against the creature’s spine for better leverage. But the phoenix twisted sharply, its massive body writhing in the air, trying to throw him off.

Ezra’s hand slipped an inch.

The dagger wrenched sideways with a sickening jolt.

For a heartbeat, he dangled by a single handhold, his body swinging wildly in the furnace winds. He bit down a scream of terror, gritting his teeth so hard he tasted blood.

Hold on. Hold on—

With a desperate cry, he yanked the dagger free.

The instant he did, everything seemed warped.

It wasn’t something he could see or hear, more like a feeling, deep in the pit of his gut. As though the world itself gave a long, shuddering sigh of relief.

And then he was falling.

The phoenix lurched upward, freed from the anchor of the dagger, shrieking in fury and betrayal as it twisted away from him. The force of its wings battered him like a hurricane, sending him tumbling end over end.

The dagger spun from his hand, a flash of silver swallowed by the swirling ash below.

Ezra’s arms flailed for a hold that no longer existed.

His stomach lurched up into his throat. The sky and the ruined world below spun around him in dizzying spirals, a mad kaleidoscope of grey and black and burning gold.

He was alone.

Alone in the open sky, with nothing but the weight of gravity and the certainty of death pulling him down.

Marcel was there, but not really. It was like he was asleep. His mind magic still acted as if he himself was Marcel, but his voice wasn’t present, and he wasn’t responding.

Time seemed to slow, not through magic, but through that final, desperate clarity that sometimes comes at the edges of death.

The chaos around him faded into a strange, crystalline stillness.

Embers drifted down from above, suspended in the air like droplets of molten gold. They floated lazily past his outstretched hands, brushing against his battered skin with fleeting touches of warmth.

Each ember was a tiny star falling alongside him—a piece of the phoenix’s fury made fleeting, made beautiful.

He twisted in the air without meaning to, the horizon tilting sickeningly.

Somewhere above, the phoenix soared higher, a silhouette of living flame against the bruised sky. It didn’t look back.

Below him, the world was a broken thing.

Marcel...

The thought cut through the fog numbing his mind.

Panic should have risen then, raw, clawing panic at the inevitable impact waiting below, but it didn’t.

Instead, there was a strange peace.

The burning in his muscles, the tearing in his lungs, the raw terror in his heart, all of it receded.  There was only the fall.

The endless, endless fall.

He felt weightless, detached from the world he had fought so hard to survive. Like a star loosed from the heavens, tumbling toward an inevitable, tragic end.

Through the haze, a memory surfaced, bright and stubborn.

Marcel, flipping a coin between his knuckles, grinning in that easy, lopsided way that made you forget the world was broken. Marcel laughing at some stupid joke, tossing an apple to him across the campfire, arguing over the strategy they might use in the coming fights, as if they had all the time in the world.

Ezra clung to that memory, fiercely.

Not out of fear.

Not out of regret.

Just a happy memory. One of many, which would seemingly be the last. Marcel’s face, vivid in his mind as the sky spun around him.

A single ember drifted close, brushing against his cheek.

It didn’t burn.

It was warm. Like a hug from someone you hadn’t seen in years.

As if the world was trying, in these final moments, to offer some small kindness.

The ground rushed up to meet him, an unstoppable, merciless tide of jagged earth and shattered dreams.

Ezra closed his eyes, leaving only dark.
