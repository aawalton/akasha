Vir heard his name chanted again and again and wished with every fiber they would just keep quiet and let him concentrate.

The pranic connections seemed to be holding for now, which was fortunate. Miraculous, even, but at what cost?

Every thread running from his body to a revived demon was an enormous drain that continued to draw upon the core, which was the only reason it had not already detonated, destroying the world.

Yet the rupture had not closed, and he could barely spare the attention to control his body. The slightest lapse of concentration meant the bomb would explode, dooming them all.

Even if it didn’t, arcs of lethal prana would blast out in random directions he couldn’t control. Nothing and no one near him was safe.

A hefty price to pay for keeping the core in check, but at least that crisis seemed to have been averted for now. The drain was sufficient to stop any more prana from building up.

Which meant he could never sever them. If he did, not only would the core overload, but the demon on the other end would cease to be, reduced to ash.

Moreover, he could tell that whatever the core had done to bring them to life had not merely reanimated their bodies. It had fundamentally altered their souls, binding them to Vir.

So long as the tether remained, they could not die. Even if their bodies were blown away, they would reform. Again and again.

They were immortal in a way even Vir was not. And yet, should he perish, they would meet the same fate.

Worse—while Vir’s soul would be born again in that great stream of reincarnation, they would simply cease to be.

Forever.

And so, no matter what happened, he could not stop. Could never release his connection to them.

It was only then that Vir truly understood how much he’d changed.

His very existence had been altered, and not for the first time.

Years ago, Vir lived for himself. Free and unshackled by the burden of responsibility.

Then he’d fallen in love with Maiya, and his life was no longer his. He lived for the two of them.

That all changed when he took up arms for his clan and assumed the mantle of Raja.

From that moment on, his life belonged to his people. An entity both greater and lesser than the individual he’d once been.

And now, he’d transformed once again. Now, he was anchor and gatekeeper both.

A battery for the hundreds he’d revived and a guardian who kept this bomb in his chest from destroying the realms.

This was his highest priority now. His only priority. Governing demons, fighting the Fateweavers… all of it forgotten in the face of this existential crisis.

For if he failed, there wouldn’t be a realm to govern anymore. No force with which to fight back.

The chanting grew louder still.

Vir tried to pay more attention to his surroundings and was shocked at how much effort it required. Like juggling ten balls at once, to do anything other than fight to stabilize the core was not only exhausting, it was downright dangerous.

The ruined district was full of demons on their knees, prostrating as they shouted his name.

It should have been a joyous occasion. No matter their fate after death, they had been given life anew. A miraculous life in which they would not age or fall ill or perish to any wound.

He had raised the dead. By every measure, Vir had just performed the single greatest feat any being had managed since… possibly ever. Not even Janak could breathe life into the dead.

Vir could guess at the implications.

News would not stay contained within Camar Gadin, nor even in the Demon Realm.

The story of their revival would spread like wildfire, becoming a rumor. The rumors would morph with every retelling, growing so exaggerated as to become myth.

This, he knew, was how legends were born.

Not that the tale required much in the way of embellishment. Not even his staunchest opponents would deny he was no longer mortal now. Perhaps the greatest immortal to have ever walked the realm, considering the magnitude of what he’d accomplished here.

Whatever expectations had been heaped upon him before—that he, a lone demon, would save Camar Gadin from the might of a Vimana of the Gods—it paled in comparison to what was to come.

Sooner or later, Vir knew he would be unable to live up to those expectations.

What then, he wondered? Would they finally understand that he was not the savior they thought him to be? Not the infallible deity they so vehemently believed?

Would they see him then for the broken existence he was? As a lone demon fighting to contain a ruptured realm inside his chest?

He’d nearly failed here. Yes, every Bairan who’d perished in that Vimana’s attack was saved, but not before they’d died first, to say nothing of the damage the city itself had sustained.

Who would save them if it attacked again?

Vir strained his concentration to its limit just to look up at the great structure, still floating over the city like an executioner’s ax.

Because despite what everyone surely believed, miracles never happened twice.

And they never came without a price. Camar Gadin might’ve been saved, but the realms had just lost their strongest Warrior.

Whatever happened now, one thing was for certain.

He couldn’t linger in the middle of a city with a rupturing core and no ability to stop it from lashing out

Even now, more and more demons swarmed around him. Tethered arisen and ordinary demons alike.

He was a danger to every demon chanting his name, and the best thing he could do for them was to flee. Far from any soul where no one would find him.

And so, mustering every fiber of willpower available to him, Vir forced his legs to move.

It took everything he had. He stayed unsteadily, his ruined arm protesting against the movement, but he dared not activate Yuma’s Embrace, lest it break his concentration.

Like a prisoner wading to shore from the bottom of the ocean, the tethers dragged behind him like shackles, the core fighting him with every step.

With great effort, he turned his body west toward the Ashen Lands. Where, if his concentration lapsed, it would take only mountains and whatever unfortunate prana beasts happened to be in the way.

He could only pray that Maiya and the others stayed away. For if they came, he wasn’t at all confident he could protect them from the Rakshasa that lurked within.

The Sarvaak's command deck had never known such revelry.

Maiya squeezed Siya’s shoulder as she clutched the hem of her skirt for dear life, and it was little wonder why, with a room packed full of cheering and shouting Asuras.

On her own volition, Siya had chosen to leave Vimana Kin’jal to Saunak and the other Asura, appropriating one of the fastest airships they’d left in its hangar. All so she could be closer to Vir during the attack.

She’d only just arrived some minutes ago, just after the conclusion of Vir’s aerial battle against the Vimana.

Reports had started arriving in a steady stream since then, each grander and more miraculous than the last.

Vir had succeeded in stopping the beam. The weapon had burst as Siya predicted, making her a star of the show.

Then came the report that changed everything. That made the cheers warp from relief into something closer to delirium.

The dead were rising, and the Akh Nara had revived them.

Whatever had happened to Vir after he’d fallen from the sky—something that Maiya and Siya seemed alone in worrying about—it had affected every corpse that had perished to the Vimana's final strike. The demons of Camar Gadin who had most definitely died were, in fact, moving again.

Not as empty husks as the stories always loved to paint horrific pictures of, but whole and alive.

What ought to have been met with disbelief and skepticism was instead embraced by the Asuras of the Sarvaak with zealous blindness. As though it was only expected that their glorious Akh Nara would work a miracle not even the Gods had accomplished.

Not even Cirayus was exempt, bellowing roars and telling the world how proud he was of the lad.

“The rogue Vimana is withdrawing,” an Asura shouted. “Its heading has changed, and it is moving away from Camar Gadin at full speed! Back to the human lands where they belong! I can confirm that the weapons have not regenerated!”

If  the cheering was loud before, it became absolutely deafening now.

“You hear that?” Cirayus roared, clasping the shoulder of an Asura nearby. “The lad did it! Gods, he actually did it!”

The room cheered in agreement.

Maiya, however, did not. Deciding enough, she pulled out her voice amplification orb and shouted through it.

“Do none of you care!?” she bellowed.

The room went suddenly silent, jolted by the volume of her words.

“Does it bother none of you that he just fell from the sky!?”

She could see him even now through one of Siya’s projections that showed the city below. Little more than a speck in the distance, his motionless figure surrounded by Bairans.

“Lass, there is nothing to worry over,” Cirayus said, smiling awkwardly. “He may be your husband, but do not forget what he is. The Akh Nara reunited the three realms. He would not fall to something such as this!”

“He’s just resting, is all,” another Asura chimed in, earning several words of agreement.

How could they all be so wrong?

Maiya felt like throwing up.

She’d been with Vir through all his fights. All his wins and his losses and through every mistake he’d made. She’d seen him wounded, and despite his combat potential outstripping her own by an absurd margin, she knew exactly how the love of her life fought.

And she knew that he would never have fallen like that unless something had gone terribly wrong.

She, too, had thought he’d just been temporarily overwhelmed. But if so, why hadn’t Yuma’s Embrace healed him by now?

With the prana he now commanded, he could regrow limbs in mere seconds.

And yet, he hadn’t moved.

She only dared to relax when he finally rose, but her relief was short-lived. Even through the distant projection, she could tell he was in pain.

And then, when he turned away from the city and began to walk west like a crippled man, she knew for certain that all the voices in her head had been correct.

Maiya's stomach dropped.

Because Vir did not walk anywhere. For why would someone who could move through shadows and fly ever choose to walk?

And he especially never walked away from people in need. Who looked to him for guidance. Not once, in all the years she’d known him.

Without her even realizing it, Maiya had grabbed Siya and moved to the door.

“Maiya?” Cirayus asked.

She stopped only briefly, turning back to meet Cirayus’ gaze. “You should be ashamed of yourself. All of you. Can’t you see how blind you’ve become? Vir isn’t some god. He’s just another demon, and you’re condemning him to death.”

She didn’t bother to wait for an answer, instead crossing the command deck and running up the stairs up two at a time to where the smaller airships sat docked.

She climbed into the nearest one and grabbed the Asura pilot, throwing her out before she could react.

“Strap in,” she said to Siya, flipping the levers to power on the craft. “We’re going down to him.”

The airship lifted off the Sarvaak's deck, leaving the shocked Asura staring wide-eyed in disbelief, but Maiya didn’t pay her any attention.

“Hold on, Vir,” she muttered. “Just a little longer. We’re on our way.”
