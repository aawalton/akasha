Eric crept through the trees, slinking from undergrowth to undergrowth and tree to tree. White flour dusted his black coat, helping to blend him into the background snow, but he knew it wasn’t enough. At least it’s not my neon orange puffer jacket. Black was a hard enough color to deal with in winter conditions; bright orange would’ve been impossible to deal with.

Maybe they’re like deer, and they can’t see orange. He snorted at the thought. Anything was possible, and he knew very little about the Ice Elves’ biology and visual range, but somehow, he doubted that ‘orange’ was beyond their ocular senses.

I need more sets of armor. Or at least more coats. The snakeskin jacket disguised his mana signature, which meant it would never go fully obsolete, but that didn’t mean it was the best choice for every scenario. It was something he’d already known, but this snowy landscape certainly drove the point home. He needed to find the armorer… or, more likely, locate another armorer shop token.

The System did drop armor (or at least gear; he wasn’t sure he wanted to count a puffer coat as “armor”) as well as weapons, but whereas a weapon could look silly and still get the job done with a bare minimum debuff to effectiveness, armor could not look silly without also getting a debuff. Camouflage was real. As funny as it was to pretend you couldn’t see a man’s legs because he was wearing camo shorts at the office on casual Friday, in the proper setting, camouflage actually did work. A bright orange jacket was funny, but it was also a bright orange target on his back. He couldn’t just wear silly gear and laugh it off; he also needed gear that let him blend in and sneak around, and so far, the only way to get that was to buy it at the armory.

Of course, it wouldn’t be as big a deal if he had a party with frontliners, strikers, and all the proper units playing their roles; it also wouldn’t be a big deal if he was an armored unit who needed to sit there as a big chonky target and take lots of hits. But he didn’t have a party. He was all alone: the only man on Earth, as far as he knew. And he wasn’t an armored unit; he was a squishy mage.

There was nothing stopping him from putting on heavy armor (if he found it) and learning how to wear it the old-fashioned way, by training for months in the gear until he adjusted to its weight, but he didn’t have heavy armor, months to get used to it, or the strength score to muscle his way through anyways. The System wasn’t handing him any heavy-armor-wearing skills or any of the stats he’d need to manually acquire heavy armor skills. That was all on him and old-fashioned human ingenuity. Elbow grease. Whatever you wanted to call it.

The main problem was time. He didn’t have the time to devote to learning to carry heavy armor, or to seriously using a sword, or any of that. That was what the Tutorial was for, and that was what everyone else was doing; seriously learning all the skills they’d need to look cool in the actual World Dungeon.

Sure, he criticized the Tutorial as a bunch of drama and worthless lessons, but there was some benefit to it as well—and especially for the ones the System picked, if what Joe had told him was correct, and he had no reason to doubt the man—but it was also the way to learn all the skills he didn’t have that were absolutely essential in the end of the world. He knew ice walking, but he didn’t know shit about conducting himself around lava except “don’t do it.” There was tons of specialized knowledge he didn’t have at all, where the only way he could gain it was going to be trial and error. No one was perfect, and no one had all the knowledge in the world already downloaded into their head. For most people, the Tutorial was where they could brush up on all that.

Now, as Joe had made absolutely no bones about, the Tutorial was also meant to deliver the majority of humanity up to the slaughter. For those who did the bare minimum and went along with it without adding anything else, it was a delayed death sentence. But if he was there, he could at least expend his time to learn skills he needed. He could use these precious weeks and months not to level up, but to cover his bases and gain what would help him survive. Instead, he was out here, and he had what he had. He was making the best use of what he had that he could, but it was far from a perfect execution of anything, just an acceptable one.

And an acceptable solution was what he hoped he’d just executed, with a little help from Cy.

Eric ducked behind another bush and hunkered there, waiting. The elves hadn’t seen him in a while; or at least, they hadn’t fired any arrows his way. He did one last scan to make sure, but his horizon was blue elf-free. He closed his eyes and opened Cy’s instead.

He found himself in a bush on the other side of the forest, closer to the clearing and immediately behind an Ice Elf. The elf turned to the side, muttering something to another elf, and that elf ran off, moving almost silently over the icy snow without falling through. Eric waited, holding his breath, his whole body tense at the thought of someone finding his original body—though no one should be anywhere near it. He counted to ten, then lifted his hand… paw, and fired a Firebolt at the elf.

Strangely, the energy came not from within “him,” but from afar. A pulse of mana flew through the air toward “him,” connected to “his” body, then burst out from his paw to strike the elf as a Firebolt. The elf fell forward with a thump.

Eric waited a second to see if anyone reacted, but almost immediately, other elves began to shout and the soft thud of footsteps sped toward him. He closed Cy’s eyes, escaping from his familiar, and opened his own.

From where he stood, elves retreated, racing toward where he’d sent Cy. They ran through the woods without regard for cover from his angle, since, after all, there was one aggressor, and they were on the other side of the woods… or so they thought. Eric grinned. This might almost be too easy.

He half-stood from behind the bush and blasted two, three elves in the back with Firebolt in quick succession, then dropped below the bush and crept to a nearby tree. The elves whirled about, racing toward him, but he quickly reached out to Cy. Snow and undergrowth flashed by as the rabbit repositioned himself.

“Already, again?” Cy grumbled.

Yes. Shush. Of the two of them, Cy was far less likely to be found or attacked, even if he was found. It was relatively safe for Cy to blast a few elves. Eric perked Cy up on his back legs, then shot another few elves from Cy’s location. The elves dropped, then whipped around, but there was nothing but an innocent rabbit nibbling some grass behind them.

Across the forest, Eric peeked out at the elves. They ran through the woods in panic. One fired an arrow randomly, the projectile screaming off into the trees in neither Eric nor Cy’s direction. Two elves ran toward him, their eyes locked on him, raising their bows. Eric pointed at each of them and took them out with a quick Firebolt before they could fire, then scooted through the forest at a crouch, relocating again.

The elves were in a full-on panic. They ran randomly through the woods, shooting at anything that twitched. Eric went still, hiding behind one of the trees. When the elves began to calm down, he popped into Cy’s body and fired a few more Firebolts, taking down another two, then just shot another Firebolt into the forest to confuse things. An Ice Elemental hovered past, and Eric (as Cy) froze, holding his breath. Did it see me?

The Ice Elemental floated on, leaving him alone in his bush.

Eric breathed out, then retreated, leaving Cy to sneak away on his own. An elf ran past his tree, speeding off on the far side of it deeper into the forest. Eric waited until it ran by, then shot it in the back with a Firebolt. It stumbled but didn’t fall, so he followed it up with a second Firebolt. The elf screeched and went down. Eric looked at his hand, then the elf. There’s got to be some variability in their stats. Only explanation.

The dungeon’s mana was too dense to see much, as usual, but the dense mana always got denser when there were many enemies in the area. Now, when he scanned the forest, it was still dense, but it was less dense. They’d killed enough of the elves that he could make out particular lumps of denser mana among the trees, and enough that the forest wasn’t just a uniform mass of super dense mana. Together, Eric and Cy worked through the woods, sweeping up the stragglers. At last, when Eric couldn’t see any more patches of even denser mana, the two regrouped. Eric set Cy back on his shoulder. “Which way, do you reckon?”

The elves hadn’t been defending any particular point or direction. They’d just been defending the forest in general, as far as Eric could tell. When they’d broken and scattered, they hadn’t fallen back in any particular direction, either. As far as he could tell, this was just a random ambush in the woods.

Cy shrugged. “Deeper into the forest?”

Eric turned. Mana Sight didn’t show him anything in that direction that it didn’t in any other direction, but there were a few Ice Elementals floating around over there. Unlike the elves, they stood out sharply even in the dense dungeon mana as sharp points of white light. Maybe it’s because they’re basically bundles of a particular type of magic? Whatever it was, he appreciated it. Made it easy to avoid them… or single them out and blast them with a Firebolt. Whichever.

Picking off the Ice Elementals along his way that were easy to separate from any others, Eric headed deeper into the forest. A box of denser mana wallowed out of the thick mana hanging in the air, surrounded by Ice Elementals. He raised his brows. Looks like I’m headed the right way.

Eric paused, enough room between him and the chest that the Ice Elementals wouldn’t see him. One, two, three… Five elementals circled the chest. Even with his coat, five was a bit much to handle. He could fire two Firebolts before they aggro’d, but that still left three to attack him. He could use a Fireball, but that cost too much mana for one chest that might be full of garbage loot. He wanted that chest; of course he did. But the System was just as likely to reward him as face-slap him. Risking his life for what could be a packet of airplane pretzels wasn’t worth it.

Wait a minute. He looked at Cy, and a grin stretched across his face.

“What’re you thinking with that nasty smile?” Cy asked, concerned.

“I can fire spells through you without “being” you, right?”

“You can. You haven’t managed it yet, but I’m sure you can figure it out. Generally speaking, it’s harder to possess one’s familiar than to cast spells through them.”

“Excellent. Then listen to me…”
