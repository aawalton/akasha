Ophelia was on the hunt again. Technically, she hadn’t been asked to investigate the recent incident, but she would certainly be better off if she did. She was too intrigued by it.

Apparently, there had been an unauthorized use of the Third District’s Teleportation Circle in Arcoryx City. Someone had snuck in and used it, leaving behind traces that had alarmed the city’s administration. Ophelia normally would have left it alone, even if she was a Mage Knight. But then, she had learned where the suspect had supposedly gone.

Rhel.

Which was odd, to say the least. The world devoid of other living people after the Rhellians themselves had evacuated it? The world where a massive battle between the infamous Demiurge of Rhel and—quite unsurprisingly—Marionys Ryland had caused much of the world to lose its habitability? That Rhel?

Ophelia couldn’t quite fathom why anyone or anything would go to Rhel. One could argue there were those who had departed unwillingly, even after everything that happened. It could be someone was homesick. It could just be someone returning to Rhel in the faint hopes of finding something or someone they had left behind.

Yet, something told her it wasn’t so simple. For rumours had it that Ryland had returned to Rhel for some intriguing reason.

And now there was someone sneaking away onto the broken Realm.

Of course Ophelia was intrigued. She’d be an idiot not to be. The only reason she hadn’t gone immediately was so she could finish up her duties as a Mage Knight and claim a few holidays to investigate what was, for the purposes of her Heartshield Knights Conclave, a personal matter. No one had officially asked her to investigate in her capacity as a Mage Knight, after all.

Still, Ophelia tried to seek out what was wrong. Now that she had some time, that was.

This late at night, one could be forgiven for thinking that there wasn’t anything wrong in Arcoryx city. The populace slumbered, the lights from the various houses and structures were dimmed, and the noise had reduced to the slow susurration of a city in sleep.

But the folk of the night still moved about. Beggars looking for scraps, thieves and pickpockets skulking in the shadows, vermin trawling the dirtier streets.

It was rare for Ophelia to head out into the deepening night, but not unprecedented. She had done so before on certain missions. She’d do so again when the time called for it. None of that meant she enjoyed the experience. Subterfuge wasn’t exactly a Mage Knight’s strong suit.

Yet, she made it to the Teleportation Hall without any incident. Most would be wary of crossing a person whose armour wasn’t really hidden in the cloak thanks to the clink of the plates and chainmail.

The Teleportation Hall was deserted save for a lone guardsman keeping watch on things. Ophelia only had to reveal the sheen of her armour before the suspicious guard quickly changed tune and easily let her enter the hall.

Unexpectedly, it was deserted. Ophelia was intent on finding some trace of the sneakthief who had alarmed everyone in the Teleportation Hall.

Ophelia started casting. She was much better at combat magic, not fields like divination or tracking. But there were certain Conclave-specific spell circles that all Mage Knights were required to master, one of which included the [Valour’s Quarry] incantation.

She had started muttering out only to stop all of a sudden, her surging manasoul snuffing out like a candleflame in the wind.

Someone was here.

Ophelia frowned, trying to suppress her presence. That guardsman. How had he allowed another intruder inside the Teleportation Hall?

She was on the balcony overlooking the actual Inscription of the Teleportation Circle. As such, the intruder below hadn’t spotted her. If Ophelia was careful, if she only used a simple [Sneak] to suppress all noise and weight, then she could get behind whoever that was. She could catch the interloper by surprise.

The spell fired and Ophelia moved. She was fast but silent. [Sneak] only needed a small amount of mana, so even if the intruder could sense manasoul activity, Ophelia should be safe.

But she had only stepped foot on the bottommost step of the staircase before the figure stilled.

“I can sense your faith, you know,” a woman’s voice said. It somehow held the weight of great age while sounding effortlessly young at the same time. Worse yet, Ophelia recognized the voice. How could she not? Most people in Androvia would recognize it.

“Oracle?” Ophelia gasped. She quickly got to the landing and bent to one knee. “Your Eminence, what are you doing here?”

The Oracle of Androvia slowly removed her hood. She was the same as ever. Hair like spun gold glinting in sunlight despite the acute lack of any, glimmering eyes shining behind a cloth blindfold, an ageless face that refused to betray her true years. She was the epitome of power gifted by the Great Gods themselves.

“I might ask you the same thing, Mage Knight,” the Oracle said. “Ophelia, was it?”

Ophelia blinked, her heart stopping for a moment. “Yes, Your Eminence. I am Ophelia Brigmont, member of the Heartshield Knights.”

“Rise, then, Mage Knight.”

At the Oracle’s imperious command, Ophelia naturally did so. She still didn’t understand what someone like her was doing here of all places, but this was the Oracle. Someone far above Ophelia. Someone she didn’t need to understand.

Weird how she didn’t really feel the same way about Ryland. Maybe it was just the lack of… institutionality about that enigmatic, overpowered mage.

She had been trained and conditioned to obey authority since she had been tiny. Obedience and loyalty had been her whole life. The Oracle was one of the highest authorities in the entirety of Androvia, and in all of Vyrd, in fact. Ophelia could do nothing but be subservient to one such as her.

And yet, she hadn’t felt quite that way with Ryland, who was actually superior to everyone else she had ever seen. The dichotomy made her expression twist.

“Since you’re here,” the Oracle said, turning back to the Teleportation Circle. “Will you deign to accompany me, Mage Knight?”

“Accompany you…” Ophelia’s gaze fell on the Teleportation Circle as well. Her heart started beating a little faster. No. She couldn’t be suggesting that, right? “To where, Your Eminence?”

“To Rhel.”

Ophelia swallowed. It seemed like her hunch about whoever this sneakthief had been and whatever they intended might not have been that misplaced after all.

“For what reason, Your Eminence?” Ophelia asked.

“To stave off what my visions portray.” The Oracle’s blindfolded shining eyes regarded Ophelia with the scorching regard of twin suns. “If we do not act, if we do not assist and take part in what is about to transpire, then I believe we might accelerate a tremendous doom that will befall all of the Accorded Realms.”

That left Ophelia briefly stupefied. When she found her voice, she had to master the urge to quake. “Wouldn’t it be better to inform others, Your Eminence? People besides just us. The rest of the Mage Knights, perhaps the Sentinels even—”

“I’m afraid we cannot. The incidents that are about to transpire aren’t something the rest of the Accorded Realms are privy to. Explaining everything to them will take far too long. I have used what power I have as judiciously and as quickly as I was capable of doing to bring in some assistance. That will have to be enough. Now, your answer, Mage Knight.”

She didn’t elaborate on just what kind of assistance she had called upon, but Ophelia was once again assured that it wasn’t her place to question her. But memories of dealing with Ryland of all people sparked a certain rebelliousness she couldn’t force down.

Plus, hadn’t the Oracle herself had a confrontation with Ryland? The account had never been verified, and Ophelia had only heard of it via rumours, but if it was true… She shook her head. The Oracle couldn’t be that foolish, could she? Just stupid rumours with no substance. Still, Ophelia decided to take a stand.

“I will come with you and lend you my aid, Your Eminence,” Ophelia said. Her mother was going to hate what she was about to say next. “And I will lay down my life for you, if need be, but on one condition—you must tell me what exactly these visions of yours foretell.”

There. The cards were out. Ophelia’s heart hammered in her chest, and she wouldn’t be surprised if the Oracle simply decided to smite her where she st—

“Alright, then.” The Oracle turned and reached the Teleportation Circle, immediately activating it with a pulse of her mana. “I will explain while we keep moving. Come.”

Well, that was a little unexpected. Ophelia swallowed, then quickly followed. Hopefully, she’d be back before her holidays were up.

Ophelia was not returning before her holidays ended. She and the Oracle had arrived on Rhel through its surprisingly intact Teleportation Circle. Well, one of its Teleportation Circles. Several had been destroyed during the last debacle.

But the point was that as soon as they had emerged from the Teleportation Hall, the Oracle had led Ophelia on a chase.

The explanation at least kept Ophelia from complaining. According to the Oracle, something was about to occur on Rhel that would shape the destiny of the Accorded Realms. And worse, it would do so in a way that most of the Realms wouldn’t even be aware of it.

Vague as that had been, there was the underlying promise that following the Oracle would reveal many of the mysteries, so Ophelia had come along.

And now she was running along with Her Eminence, chasing someone they had spotted in the shadows of a broken, ruined world. The area had once been a city. She even recognized a few of the structures. But few of them remained intact, and almost none were whole.

“You run fast,” Ophelia said. She wasn’t panting. Her Titan-ranked Inscriptions ensured she never lacked stamina. But even then, she’d had actual trouble keeping up with Oracle Vertha.

She flashed her a soft smile. “We need to run faster. Or our quarry will surely escape us.”

Ophelia didn’t know why their target had chosen to come to Rhel. All the Oracle had said was that there was a sabotage about to be performed, and they would do well to prevent it from happening.

“We’re walking on magic,” the Oracle said cryptically as a reminder. She had mentioned it twice already, as if Ophelia suffered from short-term memory loss.

“I think apprehending the culprit red-handed is going to prove futile, Your Eminence,” Ophelia said.

The Oracle was silent for a moment. They had resumed pursuing and were now chasing the figure across the darkness of Rhel. For now, with the Oracle’s insistence on maintaining their secrecy, keeping distance was the only option.

“Alright, then,” Oracle Vertha said. “I defer to your greater experience in on-field matters, Mage Knight Ophelia.”

That… had been surprisingly easy.

Ophelia nodded, her manasoul feeling like it had just been unchained. And the feeling was exquisite. “We can keep in touch with the Glyph of Communication if we get separated. Let’s get going then, shall we?”

She led the charge. No more chasing. No more running after shadows. Now, they were going to play by her rules. So, she struck first.

“[Armed Spirit],” Ophelia cast.

A glowing apparition veered off her own body, wreathed in the same armour she wore and carrying a sword-and-board combination that she preferred. The spirit hared off at her command, rushing over the ground in an attempt to intercept the target.

“Back us up, please!” Ophelia told the Oracle before madly dashing along a different path.

Her manasoul was glowing golden and rising, like the sun peeking over the horizon. One strike. That was all she’d need. One opportunity, and the suspicious entity would be taken down.

“I call upon the communion granted by the Great Ovrega,” the Oracle incanted. “[Invocation]. [Division]!”

The Oracle split into multiple versions of herself. That was the only way Ophelia could describe the phenomenon. It was awestriking to see the blessing of the Great Gods themselves in action. Glowing, glimmering versions even more robust and powerful than Ophelia’s own [Armed Spirit] shot across the impromptu battlefield.

Their target had whirled around at the sudden onrush of magic. The pause was allowing Ophelia to rapidly gain on her quarry. Odd. Why had they stopped? Was the figure that confident about taking on a Mage Knight and an Oracle?

Admittedly, their identities weren’t exactly visible. But still. Their magic should be proof enough that the infiltrator—for what else could that person be—would do well not to fight them.

Apparently, not so.

It was the Oracle’s copies that reached the infiltrator first. Her glowing figures clashed with thundering impacts, fists blurring and flashing in a rain of world-cracking impacts. I would have been comical to see the Oracle brutalizing her adversary via fisticuffs if the power behind them hadn’t been so frighteningly obliterating.

But their adversary was fighting back. That took Ophelia more by surprise than anything else. They were fighting against someone who wasn’t cowed down against an Empyrean-ranked mage such as the Oracle.

Who in the world were they chasing?

Since the Oracle’s copies had eventually been repelled, even if the figure appeared to have taken some beating with how a cracked barrier was still cloaking them, Ophelia’s [Armed Spirit] dashed in.

She didn’t expect it to do much. If anything, since it was far weaker than the Oracle, it would probably only last a moment or two longer.

Which, in fact, was what Ophelia was looking for.

With the Oracle’s distracting and glowing copies gone, her [Armed Spirit] only managed to dish out two blows before a frightful strike from a thick dagger had the spirit bleeding out golden mana. But that was good because it buoyed the infiltrator with confidence. With the belief that those who were pursuing them would fail to match up.

That in turn meant there was no reason for the infiltrator to dodge or evade as Ophelia charged in with all the pent-up ferocity she had been building ever since she had started running.

“[Champion’s Last Stand]!” she cried as she reached her target.

Her figure blazed with light and burning energy. Ophelia had already drawn her sword, and now the blade conducted so much power, the air itself felt like it was cracking around her, boiling and blistering. The might of her life slashed in.

“[Existential Parry],” the figure incanted in a gruff voice.

That thick dagger emerged again, clashing with Ophelia’s blade. All the momentum, all the energy that her continuously building spell had come forth with, simply got deflected by the dagger. A shrieking explosion of power rushed over the infiltrator’s shoulder before shooting off into the distance.

Leaving the figure entirely unharmed.

“Who—” Ophelia’s voice caught in her throat. She didn’t understand what spell he had used or what sort of Inscription was on that dagger. “Who are you?”

The figure’s hood started rising, as though he was about to reveal himself, but a crack interrupted the proceedings. Even the infiltrator jumped, whirling around with alarm.

A rift opened up, depositing Marionys Ryland into the midst of their altercation.
