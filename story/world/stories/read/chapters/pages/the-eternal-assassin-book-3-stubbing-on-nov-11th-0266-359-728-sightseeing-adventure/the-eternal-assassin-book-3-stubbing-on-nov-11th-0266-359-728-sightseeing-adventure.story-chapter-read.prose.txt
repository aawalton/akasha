Three faces stared blankly at Groln at the unexpected declaration as Asher, Mako, and Amanda took in his words. While they processed what was just said, Groln simply stood there, looking mildly relieved that such a massive secret was no longer weighing down on his chest. Eventually, Mako cleared his throat, shaking his head like he didn’t quite understand what was going on.

“I’m sorry… Did you just say your people are hunting down a demon lord?”

“That is correct. For reasons even I am not aware of, our Queen desires the head of one of the demon lords. This expedition has been a long time coming, though it would seem they left at the worst-possible time.”

“I thought expeditions into the nine hells were suicide missions,” Asher said, mentally bringing up one of the books on the various hells that he’d Incorporated ages ago and skimming through it. “There were four famous expeditions over the last few millennia, and each one resulted in not a single person coming back.”

“Those were grand crusades with the intention of reaching and slaughtering the ruler of demons,” Groln corrected. “The nine demon lords rule over the nine different hells. While their power is also immense and capable of crushing entire countries entirely on their own, not even they hold a candle to the ruler of demons. The ruler of demons is so powerful they cannot even be brought to our realm, whereas the demon lords can be dragged over as long as enough wizards join forces to counteract their weight.”

Asher nodded at this, mentally flicking through the various categorizations of demons again that the book contained in his head. Beyond the variety of hell beasts, there were only five official categories for demons that held some level of sentience.

First came the imps, which were basically treated as fodder by the rest of the demons. While they had at least a modicum of intelligence and could carry out basic tasks, they couldn’t even talk.

Then came the lesser demons. Large, hulkish brutes that were only a step above in brains but far more dangerous. Any truly skilled warrior should be able to take one down in a fight, but the common man didn’t stand a chance against them.

And then of course, came the greater demons. True monsters capable of wiping out entire towns and small cities. They possessed a new level of cunning, and their ability to access the realm of ages and enhance their speed to levels far faster than humans could comprehend or match made them efficient killers unless one went to specific lengths to counter them. While Asher had killed two greater demons now, he himself had been killed by one as well. The fact that there were two more categorizations beyond ‘greater’ was more than a little horrifying.

The book in his head didn’t have too much information on demon lords, other than the fact that the last time one had been summoned to the material realm, it had taken a coalition of kingdoms not unlike the one they were currently in to work together to defeat it and send it back to the nine hells. The battle had lasted for days, and the death toll had been astronomical. Though while the information on the demon lords was scant…

There was next to nothing on the ruler of demons. The only thing that the book mentioned was the four crusades sent into the nine hells with the intention of killing them that had all been wiped out entirely. There were plenty of theories and speculation, but not so much as a single concrete fact regarding them.

“Why would your Queen even want the head of a demon lord for herself anyway?” Amanda asked, looking justifiably confused. “Demon’s can’t be truly killed. I’m sure its corpse would be worth a fortune for the right alchemist or wizard, but that doesn’t seem worth the effort of what your kingdom is expending to get it.”

“While I don’t know the exact reasoning behind her plan… I do know that your statement is not entirely true,” Groln admitted, turning to give Asher a strange, almost envious look. “There is one person capable of ensuring any demon slain by his hand stays dead. I should know. I purchased a greater demon corpse from him not long ago.”

“Wait. Is that why this expedition is happening?” Asher asked, shocked by Groln’s admittance. The mental image of Groln’s large, demonic frame wearing a lab coat and studying the greater demon corpse in order to reverse engineer his skill flashed through his mind, and Asher’s heart sank. On the one hand, he certainly wouldn't be opposed to other people learning how to put demons down for good. But on the other hand, Sever the Thread was a powerful, dangerous ability. He didn’t know how he felt about other people potentially learning how to use it for themselves without his permission.

“The plans for the expedition had been in the works long before your greater demon corpse went up for auction,” Groln explained. “Our wizards have spent years studying demon biology. Their strange weight. The way they mimic certain skills. Everything about them. After decades of study, they’d believed they had perfected a method of keeping demons dead for good. Or more accurately, long enough that it didn’t matter for us. After all, if a demon takes a millennia or two to regenerate, it might as well be dead in truth. Getting our hands on the greater demon you actually killed just gave them the final piece of the puzzle they needed to complete their research. It is a skill that will only work on demonkind, but it seemed to work perfectly in testing. And at this very moment, it is being put to practical use.”

“So to sum things up, half of your kingdom’s strongest assets are currently locked away down in one of the nine hells on a mission to slay a demon lord,” Amanda surmised, shaking her head with a sigh. “Beyond ridiculous, it is also horribly timed.”

“There was no guarantee as to when the coalition would finally come together, and we thought Eupher to be contained within the quarantine zone,” Groln pointed out. “And as I said, the expedition had been in the works for quite some time. I’m not even certain if my Queen would have halted it even had she known the fight against the Council of Death was going to escalate so quickly.”

“More importantly for us, you’re saying five of the Hojon Empire’s greater powers are effectively off the table at the moment,” Mako said, counting on his fingers. “Two of the five who were left behind have been killed and taken over by the lich we’re hunting, which means the Hojon Empire is now down to three active greater powers. And your Queen isn’t willing to bring the other two to bear because should we fall, the empire will be almost defenseless?”

“Five greater powers was considered the bare minimum of what we needed to maintain our visage of strength before the other superpowers on our continent,” Groln said with a grimace. “The fact that we are now down to only three is bad enough. I wouldn’t be surprised if war were declared on us as soon as the truth came out. Should that number get any lower… You should be grateful that the Queen has not already ordered me to abandon this fight. My assumption is that she is hoping to maintain good relations with the coalition in order to ask for your aid in the coming weeks. The Immortal, Falling Sun, and The Reaper would be more than enough to reach the five greater power minimum she was aiming for until the expedition comes back.”

“If the expedition comes back,” Amanda clarified, looking like she wasn’t a big fan of another county incorporating her in their plans without asking. “You said they have been gone for a month already. You’ve had no word of their progress?”

“The nine hells are not like the other realms,” Groln explained. “Due to the density the fabric of those realms must take on in order to support the higher-tiers of demons, we can only access the fringes of the realm. Progression through the hells must be done manually, and leaving any sort of supply train behind was deemed impossible. We are in the dark as to how they are doing until the mission concludes, which should be in a few weeks. One month to reach the demon lord safely, one month to get back out with its corpse. The greater powers chosen to go on the expedition were picked carefully. I have full faith that they will succeed.”

“Possibly stupid question… but why weren’t you chosen to go down into the nine hells?” Asher asked. He gestured aimlessly at Groln’s towering, red-skinned form, complete with horns and claws. “I mean, not to stereotype or anything. But you’re sort of… perfect for it?”

“I was originally slated to join the expedition, before a few of the empire’s wizards brought forth a concern during the preparation. As you can clearly see, my Demon element is the one that my body chose to incorporate into my physical form during the Stage of Power. While that has provided me with no end of benefits, there is a chance it may also affect my ability to truly face off against a demon lord. While they often choose to simply rule through fear, higher tiers of demons can command the lower tiers to do their bidding, actually bending their will in order to force them to follow certain orders.”

“So there’s a chance a demon lord might be able to force you to turn on your allies,” Mako muttered, nodding along. “Or at least paralyze you as you fight against its power.”

“Exactly. There’s no way to know without actually encountering a demon lord, and doing so mid-expedition was obviously too risky. Because of that, it was decided that I would remain behind, despite my powerset making me a perfect choice to brave the nine hells.”

The four of them were silent, each one lost in thought as they considered all this new information. While the fact that half of the Hojon Empire’s strongest forces were currently running around in another realm was wild to Asher, like Mako said, the important thing was that it meant their coalition wouldn’t be getting any reinforcements. Something about the fact that Eupher and his greater revenants were rushing to the coastline worried Groln and Amanda, but it was obvious they weren’t going to explain themselves to the likes of Mako and him. Asher could only assume it had something to do with that strange pact Donvath and Gran had talked about, seeing as both of them were already well into the Stage of Power. In the end, nothing had really changed. They’d been planning on wiping out Eupher before, and they were planning to wipe him out now. Groln had simply confirmed that they wouldn’t be getting any reinforcements in order to do so.

The sight of Rulfar waving his arms outside of Asher’s Spatial Lock drew his attention, and Asher dropped the skill. As soon as Rulfar could see them, the bloodhound nodded.

“The wizards know where to bring us. We’ll head Eupher off and attack him a full mile out from the coastline. Are the four of you ready?”

“More than ready,” Groln said, flexing his claws as he stepped forward. It was strange seeing someone manage to tower over Groln’s eight-foot-tall frame, but Rulfar had yet to drop his battle form and was still a wall of fur and muscle. Raising his voice, Groln turned to address the gathered group before them. “This is the final battle against the lich Eupher! We stop him and his greater revenants before they reach the coastline, or millions will suffer as a result of our failure. Is everybody ready?”

Rather than anything like a chorus of cheers, Groln’s question was answered with hard expressions and hands tightening around weapons as everyone mentally prepared themselves for the battle to come. That didn’t seem to faze the slayer, however, as he nodded and turned toward the gathered wizards.

“Bring us to the lich.”
