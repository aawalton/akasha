The world boomed into reality like a bright light. It was vastly different from what Elaswit knew of teleportation.

With the disorientation of the world and her very self coming undone, returning was like a slap to her very being. She staggered once, teetered, then fell.

Her uncle’s hand landed firmly on her shoulder, keeping her in place. It was enough. Her knees did not buckle. She did not fall. Her stomach, however, had other ideas. It twisted and turned, churned like a volcano on the brink of eruption.

It must have shown on her face because Uncle Tradis shot her a hard look. “You throw up on these floors, you’ll be working for the church for a year.”

Elaswit tightened her throat immediately and beat down the disgust. Besides, it wasn’t as if there was much in her stomach to vomit. The man had been feeding her once a day since they’d left the palace.

It was a while longer before her stomach steadied and there was no threat of vomit.

Uncle Tradis looked at her. “Are we all better?”

“Yes.” She nodded very slowly, hiding even her displeasure at him from her expression.

The man in front of her was not her Uncle. Well… he was. But he was not the Uncle Tradis she’d grown up to love. The uncle who visited once in a while with gifts and trinkets and laughed and played and was the nicest human alive. On a few occasions he’d even helped them do things they were not supposed to do and lied to their father about it. He’d fought her dad once over being too strict at one point. That was Uncle Tradis. He was the fun uncle, the loving uncle. The uncle she knew and loved. The man beside her was nothing but hard and firm and disciplined.

She’d thrown a tantrum once two days ago and he’d slapped her. Uncle Tradis had slapped her. Even now she still couldn’t believe it.

“Let’s go.” Uncle Tradis stepped off the pavement.

For a moment, Elaswit didn’t move. “Where are we going, Uncle?”

“To the Dirtwater manor,” he answered, stopping to look at her.

“Why?”

“Because Valdan is out doing the gods know what, and he’s not home.”

Elaswit got off the platform. “So how’s the estate functioning?”

“He left a lady called Melvet in charge.” He frowned. “As much as I hate to admit it, she does an amazing job. Better than your mother ever could.”

That was high praise.

“Who exactly is Melvet?” she asked.

“His beloved.”

“So why are we going there since he’s not there?” she asked.

“Because there is something important in that house that has been left to Melvet.”

“You think she’s pretending to love him?”

“Nope. She’ll probably die for him.”

Elaswit didn’t understand. “Then what’s the problem.”

“That’s a difficult one.” He pressed his lips in a thin line, as if in confused thoughts. “Something about that woman just doesn’t sit right with me.”

He said nothing else, and they walked through the massive church. Elaswit had one more question, a question she could not keep to herself. It was brewing suspicion in her mind.

“Uncle?”

“Yes, Elaswit.” He did not look back at her.

Elaswit swallowed. The question felt like something she was not supposed to ask. Still, she had to ask it.

“What’s your connection to the church?”

Pale grey eyes watched Aiden from beneath pale blue hair that came to stop at her shoulders. Hood down, the woman looked like she was in her late twenties, maybe her early thirties. Aiden couldn’t place her right.

“How do you know that name?” she repeated.

Her hands were out in the open. One was bandaged. The bandage a bright white in the light of the rising sun. She had hard eyes, watchful eyes. They reminded Aiden of a predator.

Sword held in hand,t he kept careful eyes on her. There was no reason to answer the questions of a stranger. Besides, his bigger worry was the clothed weapon on her back. He guessed it was a spear or some such.

Elami moved closer to Ted’s side, Valdan still in his arms. The woman’s eyes followed him, tracked each movement. Her hand opened and closed.

Battle seemed imminent. Meanwhile, the distant battle against Nosrath continued to echo. Dim sounds as people died trying to bring down a monster with great regenerative abilities and a health too strong to simply be trimmed down one step at a time.

“You should be careful,” Elami whispered to Ted. “She looks dangerous.”

Ted scoffed. “You don’t say.”

Anyone with combat experience knew that she was a woman ready to attack. The question was why she was hung up on the name Melvet.

The woman looked in the general direction of Nosrath’s battle, then back at the group.

“I am not a fan of repeating myself,” she said simply.

Aiden’s hold on his sword adjusted. He relaxed his wrist for easy flow of sword strikes. “I will beg you to take your leave. Or better yet, announce yourself.”

The woman frowned. It was surprisingly a beautiful thing, beautiful yet deadly somehow. There was a ferocity to it, like the beauty of watching a snow leopard hunt.

It worried Aiden.

For a moment, they were all silent.

Then her eyes moved. They moved to Fjord who was standing and watching, probably deciding on where to place his gamble. Then they moved to Ted, momentarily ignoring Elami and Valdan. When they reached Oncot, her eyebrows twitched a little, as if deciding if the man belonged or not.

In the end, they settled on Aiden. His arm to be precise.

Recognition crossed her eyes then.

It made Aiden think.

She couldn’t be. Could she?

His brows furrowed. He gave it some thought. By all accounts, it wouldn’t make sense. It was too far away, too far out.

The woman sighed. The frown on her face softened, turned to worry, then something odd. It was like a mother about to scold a child.

“He was right.” She ran a hand through her blue hair. “Your diplomatic skills are shit.”

Aiden sheathed his sword with a sigh of his own. He was sure of it now.

Fjord looked from him to her. He inched towards Ted. “Am I missing something?”
