I drew in a shuddering breath as the realization stole over me, feeling most of my rage empty from within my lungs. That wasn’t Magnus down on those docks, I repeated, over and over. That wasn’t him.

Magnus was dead. He was never coming back.

Instead, it was just his Father.

I…don’t know why this possibility had never occurred to me. It made perfect sense that Anguis himself would have been the apparent leader. I think…I might have just assumed he was like his son in this regard. Magnus wouldn’t have cared at all. Anguis’s son and heir had, in the end, been a coward. He would never have placed himself in the path of danger, not even to wipe out his historic enemies. That weak, feculent toad had needed helpless slaves to drive monsters to him so he could level up.

Anguis…

Just from the sight of him, I could tell that he was different. This was a Dwarf who was no stranger to combat. No stranger to personally dealing out death. There was something…off about him, different from what had been wrong with Magnus. He even carried a blade at his side. Nothing special, from what my senses could tell, and so I doubted it was his personal weapon. But the Velancian saber looked well cared for, at the very least.

Anguis was strong, I could tell. I didn’t dare to Observe him, and not because of the taboo on such a thing.

And he was still looking at me. Then, something happened that outright sent a chill down my spine.

He winked at me.

As I myself blinked rapidly from the hair-raising gesture, Anguis turned back to look across the table at Elysael, setting down his scroll as he did so. The Prince of Rhoscara turned hard, calculating eyes upon the Prince of Vittolia as he appeared to speak to her, spreading his hands in a faux plaintive manner as he did so.

There was the resemblance to Magnus. Not from the gesture, no.

Instead, it came from the emotionless look in his eyes as Anguis apparently made a request. He...strangely seemed to have a different opinion of Elysael than he did of me. Lesser, somehow.

Elysael visibly considered whatever it was he had asked, cocking her head slightly as she did. Normally, I expect I would have been able to hear their conversation, considering my higher-than-normal investment into Perception. But the creaking of the ironclad ships in the river, the shifting of thousands of anxious armored boots all around me, and the crashing of waves against the dock muffled their speech.

Yet, somehow, I was unsurprised when Elysael gave a short, sharp nod…

And turned her head to look straight at me, upon the battlement. Raising a hand, she crooked a single finger my way in an obvious gesture.

I frowned openly, raising an eyebrow.

In response, I received a single, sure nod.

She was serious.

Ely, or rather, Anguis, wanted me to go down there.

…fine. You know what?

Fine.

I could handle a meeting with the Serpent of Vittolia himself.

I think I’d even like it.

It was always good to get the measure of one’s enemy.

I nodded sharply and then climbed onto the battlement I had only moments ago crushed with my bare hands. The movement drew the attention of everyone upon the battlements, silent heads turning my way all across the walls. Before I could stand to my full height, though, I felt a hand grab my wrist from behind. Turning my head, I found Sylvia staring up at me with a concerned frown on her face.

“Nathan…” She said slowly. “Are you sure?”

I spared the Sculpted woman I cared so deeply for a small, sincere smile. More than most, she understood why this might be fraught for me.

But…

“I’ll be fine,” I said truthfully. “I’ve got this.”

Sylvia studied my face for a moment before nodding and letting go. I both returned it and gave one to Bleddyn as well.

Before I stood up on the wall, and leapt.

These days, my personal strength was enough I easily cleared the wall, soaring into the open air above the dock. I traveled far enough that I was only about twenty feet or so from the negotiation table, squarely in the center of the port. I kept my balance as I thumped onto the sturdy wood construction of it, the shockwave of my impact rattling the table.

None of the people around it, either the two Princes or their guards, flinched at all from my arrival.

I kept my pace slow as I approached the table, making sure not to make any sudden moves. I was already getting some wary, considering looks from the Savoy soldiers Anguis had brought as his ‘guards’.

I had absolutely no doubt in my mind that the Serpent was stronger than all of them put together. But it was the principle of the thing.

As I came to a stop at one of the two unoccupied edges of the table, I deliberately disregarded Anguis at first to bow my head slightly to the Prince I actually liked. “Prince Elysael,” I said respectfully.

“Marshal Hart,” She acknowledged quietly. “Thank you for joining us. I understand this is an…unusual request. But the…” She paused for a moment. “Honorable Lord of Vittolia requested your presence.”

“It’s no problem, Your Highness,” I murmured, raising my head. Slowly, I then turned my head and fully met Anguis’s amused golden eyes.

Green on gold.

The similarity of his own house’s colors didn’t escape me, and I didn’t care for it.

“Prince Anguis,” I said coldly.

And that was it.

No greeting.

No asking what I could do for him.

Just an acknowledgement.

Still, for all of my deliberate attempts to snub the Dwarf, he didn’t seem unsettled at all. In fact, he smiled widely at me, outright chuckling before he spoke.

“Marshal Hart, is it?” Anguis of House Savoy said, speaking to me directly for the first time. “Well-done, Nathaniel. Well done indeed. You’ve certainly moved up in the world since last we met.”

I was…immediately confused, but I kept it off my face. And not for the obvious reason that I had never met Anguis before. I’m certain I would have remembered him.

But also because…something about his voice…

It…it did sound familiar? I…couldn’t place it, but I swore I had heard it somewhere before.

“Oh-ho!” Anguis almost crowed, smirking now. “I see you never deciphered that little mystery. Tsk tsk, Nathaniel. I’m almost disappointed, after all I’ve heard. But I suppose it was quite the fraught time for you, as I remember. Would you like a hint?”

I was wary, now. Somehow, I got the impression I wasn’t going to like this. Still, I stayed silent. I wasn’t sure I wanted to encourage him.

“Normally, I wouldn’t even hint as to this possibility,” Anguis mused, stroking the short beard on his chin and cheeks. “But I suppose it no longer matters. We truly have entered into the final act in this little play called Vereden. Some secrets have greatly diminished value. Here’s your hint, Nathaniel,” He smiled widely…

And then his entire demeanor changed. Anguis’s stance shifted, his shoulders loosening, his feet widened, and his head cocked to the side in a distinctly disinterested manner. His eyes lidded, his lips turned down, and his left hand reached up to rest idly upon the hilt of the blade at his belt.

“Do you want me to kill you, boy?” Anguis said, audibly bored.

My brow furrowed at the words. That…that was familiar…

In the same way I remembered every single second of how I had killed Magnus, I also remembered everything that came after. There wasn’t a moment I couldn’t recall in vivid detail.

And I knew those words.

My lips parted in hesitant shock. “…Stonebreaker?”

Sir Vincenzo Stonebreaker.

He had been the Dwarven Knight assigned to act as Magnus’s personal bodyguard. I hadn’t encountered the Dwarf all that much in my time at Addersfield. Even then, I had thought it was sketchy just how little he was around, for apparently being the person meant to ensure the safety of House Savoy’s Heir. I really only recalled two times we’d crossed paths.

The first had been when he had beaten the absolute shit out of Azarus, when he had tried to protect me from Magnus. My best friend had failed, though, and I had ended up thrown into the forest where I could ultimately gain my Class.

But the second…

As I had been kneeling over the cooling corpse of Magnus, Stonebreaker had sauntered up, seeming entirely unconcerned about the death of his charge. He had been more irritated that Magnus had killed Orinbar instead, the seneschal of Addersfield who did all of the actual work. The Knight had been about to wander away when I had asked him, shocked, if he even cared that I’d shoved a dagger through Magnus’s heart.

He had said those words in response…

I…don’t think I’d ever even mentioned that encounter to another living soul. It hadn’t…at the time…I just hadn’t thought it was important…

But Anguis just dropped his affected attitude and smiled at me in a distinctly amused manner. “A very convenient persona of mine, yes,” He confirmed casually, as he wasn’t blowing my mind right now. “But it has no further use. Not now, of course. I’ve plumbed the depths of its usefulness.”

I was still caught up in the implications to care about Anguis’s almost friendly manner. “You were there the whole time…” I breathed, staring at him with shocked eyes.

“Not the whole time,” Anguis corrected, almost gently. “I occasionally had to return to my dear Vittolia. But yes, I was present for everything truly important that happened within Addersfield.”

“Hmm…” Elysael narrowed her eyes at her fellow Prince. “Then you admit you were personally operating a slave plantation within a week’s travel of Rhoscara, Lord Savoy?”

The glance Anguis shot Ely at that was far more disinterested than how he had been speaking to me. “Oh, of course not,” He chuckled softly. “I was merely…keeping an eye on things, at the request of the local seneschal. You really have no idea how much I miss that man and his competence, Nathaniel,” Anguis abruptly shifted his gaze back to me with a conspiratorial air. “I do miss Orin and his bookkeeping dearly. Alas for the temperaments of my spawn.”

I drew in a shuddering breath. “You didn’t even care when I killed your son.”

I…genuinely couldn’t imagine such a thing. How could you be so callous as to just…not care about someone outright murdering your own flesh and blood?

If someone had done to Aveline what I had done to Magnus, I would never, ever rest until I’d murdered them.

Not just killed.

Murdered.

There wouldn’t be an ounce of mercy, or pity, or even restraint in how I would flay the flesh from their bones.

A measure of coldness finally crept into Anguis’s countenance then. “I didn’t,” He admitted freely. “And I still don’t. I fully agree with the decision. Let me let you in on a little secret, Nathaniel. Addersfield…was a test.”

I blinked slowly, feeling something inside me shift. My voice came out softer than I intended. “How?”

Anguis crossed his arms in faux-disappointment. “I was aware that Magnus had his faults, of course,” He said casually. “I’d had to…shift attention away, from some of his little misadventures in the past. At great cost, mind you.”

Misadventures…

“He was growing increasingly unstable, as the years went by and his appetites only seemed to grow,” Anguis snorted. “Frankly, it was all a bit gauche. The boy had no subtlety in his soul. If he had only taken more care in his hobbies, I may have turned a blind eye. But no, he just had to escalate matters, had to keep pushing things. I was growing tired of him, in truth. What use is an heir that cannot be trusted to maintain the reputation of the House? And so, I devised one last manner in which he could prove himself to me. Magnus would be granted the governorship of a minor operation of mine,” He winked at a stone-faced Elysael. “And if he could manage it well, I’d let him keep his life. Very unlikely I would have let him keep his position, though.”

“But he only kept escalating things…”

“That he did,” Anguis nodded easily. “That he did indeed. All of those slaves, fed to monsters of all things? And for what? So he could lounge around, expending the least effort possible as they were fed to him?” He rolled his eyes. “The System doesn’t reward behavior like that. I very much doubt he accumulated significant Level Aether from his practices. No, he had to go. You merely spared me the effort of doing the deed myself. Thank you very much, Marshal Hart…”

Anguis swept out in a courtly, almost exaggerated bow, one arm bent at the waist, the other held behind him. At the base of the bow, he quirked his head up…

And winked at me once more.

“For the favor. And so, we come to the reason I asked for your presence at these formalities.”

Anguis’s smile…widened.

“Why don’t you come with me?”
