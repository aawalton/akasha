"Let's take this inside," the ranger leader urged, nearly throwing Callie in the wagon.

Left without much choice, Mom followed them. The ranger closed the wagon door tightly, then whispered. "Be careful with what you say aloud, little girl. You may think that nobody is paying attention to you, but you never know when some curious ears might be listening in. I would like it if people didn't find out the true nature of our organization."

"The true nature? It seems telling lies is a common thing among rangers," Mom narrowed her eyes at the woman.

The ranger merely shrugged. "I'm afraid that without telling a few lies, the rangers wouldn't exist in the first place."

"I don't care what you rangers do with your politics, but when it comes to matters regarding my daughter, I would like to know the truth," Mom snapped.

"My apologies, ma'am," the ranger said.

Mom didn't look quite satisfied with such a nonchalant apology, but there were far more important matters at stake.

"Mom, I want to help rangers find the thunderfox mother," Callie said.

"Thunderfox mother? You're not talking about the monster that nearly hit you with their lightning, are you?" Mom narrowed her eyes.

"Yes, I am! They were just scared because they were separated from their mom. Without her, they are going to die!" Callie said.

Mom's expression softened a little, but she wasn't yet fully convinced. She turned to the ranger woman. "I don't even quite understand what is going on here. Weren't you going to deal with them?"

"Yes. My team is searching for the thunderfox mother as we speak. It would be best for everyone if the kits were reunited with her and released into the deeper parts of the forest, where people won't bother them. Of course, this is confidential information. Should you leak it…"

"I have no desire to make rangers into my enemy. If you believe that to be the best course of action, then I shall trust you on that. Although I find it a bit hard to believe… I always thought that rangers were an elite group of hunters."

"We can be if there's no other choice. But that is usually the last resort," the ranger leader said, then looked at Callie. "I am very curious, though. How does this child intend to help us track down the thunderfox mother? Not to brag, but finding arcane beasts is our field of expertise. How could this child accomplish what my team could not?"

Callie froze. How was she supposed to answer that? She couldn't just say that she had a magic mark that connected her to arcane beasts. Could she? Technically, the rangers had revealed their secret to her, so perhaps she should do the same in return?

No, it was too early to blindly trust them like that. They could claim that they were trying to help the kits, just to get Callie out of their way. Despite it looking like they truly wanted to help, she would remain cautious for the time being. Thankfully, it seemed like Mom figured out an answer before Callie.

"This child has always been obsessed with arcane beasts. Searching for them whenever she could. We don't know how, but she has a knack for finding them. A natural gut instinct, so to speak," Mom said.

Callie was amazed. Mom had accused rangers of lying just a moment ago, yet she was telling such a bold-faced lie without even flinching. Callie decided that the best she could do was just nod along with her.

"A natural knack for finding them?" The ranger leaders scratched her scarred chin in thought as she evaluated Callie up and down with her gaze. "Very well. I'm not sure if she truly has a knack, as you call it, but if you wish to help, we won't reject you. However, we are not bodyguards. Should you decide to help, then you will be responsible for your own safety and that of your daughter. We will not wait for you. If you are unable to keep up, we will leave you behind."

Mom looked at Callie. Even without words, Callie could tell that Mom wanted her to just drop this whole matter.

"Please, Mom… I want to help…" Callie made her most pleading face, going so far as sticking out her lower lip.

"Oh…" Mom looked away from Callie, then back at her, then released a loud sigh. "Fine, fine! You're playing dirty. You know I can't say no if you make that face."

"Thank you, Mom!" Callie exclaimed, wrapping her arms around her waist.

"But!" Mom raised a stern finger. "If I tell you that we're leaving, then we're leaving. No complaints from you, Missy!"

"Yes!"

"It seems you've made up your mind. The name's Ragna Voss, and I'm the captain of this particular squad," Captain Voss said.

"I'm Maeve Aurelwynn," Mom said cautiously.

"Calluna Aurelwynn!" Callie added.

"Nice to meet you both. Now, before we begin, let's get some ground rules out of the way!"

The following lecture was rather long as Captain Voss explained how Mom and Callie have to listen to her commands above all else, how they were to behave while under her command, and that the true purpose of the rangers should never be exposed. She repeated the last one at least three times and gave Mom and Callie both a stern glare.

"Now, with that said, I'll give you two one hour to prepare what you need to head into the forest. We might remain there even during the night, so prepare to camp outdoors," Captain Voss said.

Mom looked at Callie forlornly.

"I can do it! I've learned how to make shelter and everything from Mister Blackthorn. I am ready!" Callie said firmly.

But she didn't look fully convinced. "All this just for a bunch of monsters…"

"But Mom, they are going to die without their mother! Don't you think that's unfair?"

"Callie, their mother…" Mom stopped her midsentence, looking guiltily down at the wagon floor.

"She might already be dead. I warned her as much," Captain Voss said, not mincing her words. "You will be free to leave at any point should you wish."

"Mom, we have to try! Please…" Callie begged, clutching onto her arm.

"Okay, okay… Let's pack our things and warn Callen that we'll be gone for a while."

"He won't notice us gone anyway. He's too busy exploring the city together with Silas," Callie said.

"Callen and Silas? Who are they?" Captain Voss asked, narrowing her eyes.

"My son and a merchant who allowed us to travel in his wagon," Mom explained.

"Not a word to either of them about what you're doing. Especially the merchant. You can never trust one of them. The only thing they care about is profit," the ranger spat.

Callie felt conflicted for a moment. She didn't want to hide things from her brother. But then again, it wasn't like there was time to explain the full story to him anyway. Perhaps, she could explain things to him once they got home. Callen could surely keep a secret.

With that said, Mom and Callie hurried back to the inn, collecting their bags. They were already packed with everything necessary for survival, along with an extra set of clothing, just in case. Mom wrote a note to Callen, saying that they will return tomorrow and telling him not to worry.

Callen would likely worry anyway, but since they couldn't exactly run around the city searching for him, the note was the best they could do.

"I really hope I don't regret this…" Mom said, looking quite conflicted.

"It's possible I am the only one who could find the thunderfox mother. What if she can't be found by tracking? Same as the egg," Callie said.

"You can't even control your magic properly," Mom pointed out.

"I… I know… But I have to try. I'll never forgive myself if I give up without even trying. Please, help me, Mom!" Callie pleaded.

Mom let out a sigh, then smiled slightly. "What kind of mom would I be if I didn't help you?"

"A great mom! Normally, moms don't have to deal with children who have been given magic power by a dragon."

Mom pulled on Callie's cheek gently. "At least you're aware of how much trouble you're causing. Let's go!"

Giving one last check that they had everything they needed, Callie and Mom headed back to the rangers' wagon. Captain Voss was waiting for them.

"Get in the back," she said briskly.

"Where are we going?" Mom asked.

"Any good search begins by examining the site of the event. In this case, this would be the spot where you encountered the kits on the road," Captain Voss explained.

Callie nodded. That seemed logical. Searching random spots in the forest was unlikely to yield any results. But would they find anything at the spot where they found the kits? They had already been separated from their mother at that point.

"It looks like you're having second thoughts," Mom noted. "We can still turn back."

Callie shook her head. "I-I'm just worried if I can be of any help. I still want to help them, but…"

Mom put a hand on her shoulder. She didn't have to finish the sentence for Mom to understand that Callie was worried about her magic. Would Callie be able to use it to find the thunderfox mother? There was no guarantee it would work.

In fact, the whole time they were in the back of the wagon, Callie felt her stomach churning to the point where she was beginning to feel queasy. The little kits were still emitting a strong sense of fear. For Callie, it felt like a mountain had been placed on her shoulders. If she couldn't get her magic to work, then these poor babies might die. Callie had to succeed. She had to…

"Callie, dear…" Mom said, placing her hand on Callie's.

Callie flinched at the sudden touch, surprised to be brought up with her turmoiling thoughts.

"W-What is it, Mom?"

"I just want you to know that whatever happens to those little foxes, it is not your fault. If you can't find their mother, it is not your fault."

"But—"

"No buts. You are still a child, and it wasn't like it was your job to watch these foxes and make sure nothing happened to them. You are trying to help them, and whatever the result, you cannot blame yourself for it. Okay?" Mom said firmly, looking Callie in the eyes.

"I… Okay…" Callie replied, feeling some of the weight lifted from her shoulders.

"Good. Just do your best. That's all anyone can hope for."

The rest of the ride, Callie felt a little calmer. She concentrated on the feeling she had felt while searching for the glauxis egg. It was surely this type of magic that she had to trigger to find the thunderfox mother. If only she knew how exactly her magic worked.

Temper your heart, and dragon-craft will listen to thee.

The librarian's translation echoed in Callie's mind. But what did it mean to temper one's heart? Did she have to remain calm? Concentrate on a specific emotion or feeling? Callie did try to be as calm as possible; however, the occasional whine from the kits in the cage just solidified how crucial it was to find their mom.

The wagon stopped, and the back door opened.

"Let's begin!" Captain Voss urged them to get out.

Mom and Callie both did so. The sun was already starting to set, although it wasn't even dinnertime yet. Days were getting shorter and shorter. Would they be able to find anything in the dark?

To Callie's surprise, they were approached by another ranger, who came riding towards them on a horse. It was a very fast horse, and Callie would have liked to examine it a bit closer. Something about it struck her as… unusual. But the ranger quickly grabbed her attention.

"Captain, I have a report," the man said. He was surprisingly… beautiful. She hadn't noticed it before, but unlike the tough captain who looked like a grizzled warrior, this man looked more like a bard.

Not that Callie had seen many bards. One had visited Greyfen several years ago during the First Flame Festival. His singing had been the most beautiful thing Callie had ever heard, not to mention that he was so beautiful that none of the women in the village could even compare. This ranger was just as beautiful as that bard had been.

"Report," Captain Voss said curtly.

The beautiful man threw a glance at Callie and Mom. "Civilians?"

"It's a long story. But this girl believes she can find the thunderfox mother," she explained shortly.

One of the man's eyebrows went so high that Callie thought it would disappear into his hairline. Admittedly, he had lovely long bangs engulfing the side of his face, which already did a good job of making him look more mysterious. Callie forced herself to smile at him, but the man didn't look impressed.

"Are you sure about this, Captain?"

"Yes."

The man narrowed his eyes at Callie, then finally said. "I've managed to find more tracks of the thunderfox adult. Following them, I found an empty den."

"Did you track it in the other direction too?" Captain asked.

"Of course. They cut off abruptly just the same as the other ones we found," the ranger said.

Captain Voss frowned. "Lead us there."

"We're taking them along?" The ranger asked.

"The girl apparently has a knack for finding arcane beasts. I figured it couldn't hurt to bring them along."

Mom was glaring at the ranger man the whole time, while trying her best to appear unfazed by his stern gaze. Somehow, it felt worse when coming from someone beautiful.

"Don't expect me to protect them," the man spat.

"I'm not. A hunter can surely take care of herself and the child," Captain Voss said.

"Hunter…" the man hissed the word out as if it were poison, not hiding his distaste. "Don't get in the way!"

Mom looked like she had a lot of things to say to the man, and none of them good. Callie merely followed the ranger captain quietly. She tried to reach into her magic. With all her might, she wished her magic to find the thunderfox mother.

She could feel it stirring within her ever so slightly. But the feeling was very weak, nothing like the time when she was searching for the glauxis egg. Something was missing for her magic to work properly. Nonetheless, Callie didn't give up. She kept trying the whole time they walked.

It didn't seem to work. No matter how hard Callie tried to push her magic, it refused to find the thunderfox mom. Did it not understand what Callie wanted? What was she doing wrong?

"So what do you think, young tracking prodigy? Can you find her?" Captain Voss asked Callie as she pointed at the thunderfox den.

Callie peered into it and pushed her magic as much as she could. Surely if there was a place that could trigger it, then it was here. The thunderfox den. It must be the place linked most strongly to them. But all Callie felt was her magic roiling around, wanting to burst out. But it wasn't searching for the arcane beasts. Instead, it felt more likely that she would set the forest on fire.

It's the same as the time with the dandilynx flower.

Forcing magic out when Callie was frustrated only made it explode violently. But when she was together with Snowglow, she had managed to grow the flower. When her emotions were calm. Or was calm the right word here?

Temper your heart…

Tempering didn't sound like it meant being just calm. It felt like I was also being strong. Could it be like when Dad tempered steel? Callie had seen him do it a few times. Using his fire magic, he would heat it up and then plunge it into water to cool it down. As a result, the blade would be stronger and not as brittle.

Perhaps Callie could temper her heart like that. Untempered steel bent and shattered. Maybe it was the same with untempered feelings. If she smothered her emotions, her magic would fade out. If she let them run wild, they would explode uncontrollably. So perhaps, Callie had to remain calm, but also feel the emotions. Guide them in a controlled manner, as Dad works with the steel.

She took a deep, steadying breath and tried just that. She allowed her roiling magic to flow, but at the same time remained calm, thinking about the thunderfox mother. Not in a panicked way, not with frustration, but with certainty that she had to be found.

Then she finally felt. Just like with the glauxis egg, she felt a distant, barely noticeable pull. It felt like the connection might disappear at any moment, so she instantly moved to follow it.

"This way!"
