Chapter 36: The Weight of Love

Death surrounded me like a familiar blanket. Thirty corpses scattered across the ruined camp, their bodies twisted in the final poses of violence. Sacks of meat and bone and cooling blood, their individual scents mixing with the strange metallic tang that permeated every corner of this dungeon. The alien air carried hints of ozone and something sharper, something that made my nose burn even through the respirator's filters.

I had never before been surrounded by so much death. Back on Earth, death was sanitized, hidden away in hospitals and funeral homes where professionals handled the messy business of mortality. Here, it was raw and immediate, painted across the landscape in arterial sprays and pools of cooling gore. You might think such a sight should bother me, should trigger some kind of human revulsion at the waste of life.

It didn't.

The absence of horror was more disturbing than horror itself would have been. I stood among the scattered remains of thirty people, each one someone's son or daughter, someone's hope for the future, and felt nothing more than mild satisfaction at a job well done. That should have shaken me. Should have made me question what I had allowed myself to become.

Instead, I found myself mentally cataloguing the corpses, noting which ones had fallen to my blade and which had been torn apart by the Flux beasts I had commanded. The distinction mattered for tactical analysis, for understanding how effective each approach had been. Data to be filed away for future reference when I faced similar situations.

Nor did I find joy or pleasure in the carnage spread before me. The killing itself held no appeal, brought no rush of excitement or satisfaction. It was simply work that needed doing, obstacles that needed removing, problems that required permanent solutions. Vash might have found pleasure in such a scene, and that thought made me wonder if my master was somehow watching me through the Flux, observing my performance and taking sadistic delight in what I was accomplishing.

The idea that she might be experiencing vicarious pleasure through my actions disturbed me more than the deaths themselves. There was something deeply wrong about providing entertainment through violence, about turning necessary killing into a spectator sport. But I pushed the thought aside. Vash's potential voyeurism was irrelevant to my immediate concerns.

Elisa wouldn't recognize me if she could see me now. The thought struck like a physical blow, sharp and unexpected. My little girl who still believed her daddy was a good man, who trusted that he would come home safely and take care of her. She had no idea her father was standing in a field of corpses, breathing mechanically through a stolen device, planning his next act of violence.

Strange how that recognition brought neither shame nor regret. I did still love her, that fundamental truth unchanged by everything I had done or become. Love wasn't the weakness that people assumed it was, some soft emotion that made you vulnerable to manipulation and control. Love was the mother of the deepest chasms of hatred, the wellspring from which the darkest evils flowed.

For love, a good man would turn to war. For love, gentle fathers became merciless killers. For love, normal people discovered they were capable of atrocities that would make seasoned soldiers vomit. Love could justify any action, no matter how horrible, no matter how far it strayed from everything you once believed about right and wrong.

One needed to look no farther than me to see the truth of that. I was using love even now to justify the death spread before me, telling myself that every corpse brought me closer to protecting Elisa. That each kill made me stronger, more capable of ensuring her safety in a universe that had proven itself hostile to everything I held dear.

No. As I had acknowledged so many times before, it was for me. For my own hunger for power, for my own refusal to be weak and helpless ever again. The love I felt for my daughter was real, but it wasn't my primary motivation. It was just the most palatable justification for choices I would have made regardless.

But I did still love her, and that created a problem I was increasingly aware of. I found myself trying to make that love disappear, to smother the emotion that represented my greatest vulnerability. The effort was deliberate and conscious, like trying to kill a part of myself that had once defined who I was.

That was a hard truth to acknowledge. That I was actively working to destroy the one remaining connection to the man I had been, the one emotion that still tied me to concepts like mercy and compassion and sacrifice for others.

If I could smother that love completely, then no one would have a hold over me. No lever to pull, no weakness to exploit, no way to make me choose between what I wanted and what was right. I would be free to pursue power without the constant weight of moral considerations dragging at my conscience.

Yet I couldn't seem to manage it. Elisa's face would come into focus in my mind's eye, round and innocent and trusting, and my heart would surge with protective fury. My bloodline wanted to dominate that emotion, to consume it as it had consumed so many other aspects of my humanity. I could feel the alien hunger prowling around the edges of my love for her, testing for weaknesses, looking for ways to devour what remained of my capacity for genuine feeling.

But love resisted in ways that other emotions didn't. It possessed a durability that made it almost impossible to eliminate completely. Love was unique in that respect, the most intangible tangible force in existence. You couldn't touch it or measure it or pin it down, but it could move mountains and topple kingdoms and transform ordinary people into legends or monsters.

Love was not weakness, despite what the Nyxen taught. Love was power, and like all power, it corrupted everything it touched. The difference was that love corrupted you slowly, from within, turning virtues into justifications and noble intentions into excuses for unconscionable acts.

A familiar blue glow caught my attention, text materializing in my peripheral vision with the timing that suggested the Flux had been waiting for a moment of relative quiet.

You are now Flux Level 21. You have 2 attributes to assign.

I almost laughed at the notification. The sound would have been bitter, mechanical, processed through the respirator's speakers until it barely resembled human mirth. I hadn't been intentionally meditating on my advancement, hadn't sat down and focused on my connection to the Flux with the purpose of triggering a level increase. But apparently the Resonance Flux disagreed with my assessment of my own mental state.

Perhaps the contemplation of love and power qualified as meditation in the Resonance Flux's estimation. Or maybe standing in a field of my own victims while considering the philosophical implications of my actions counted as some kind of twisted spiritual exercise. The Flux had its own logic, alien and incomprehensible but internally consistent.

I almost allocated both points to Perception right there, finally addressing the weakness that had been handicapping my effectiveness since the beginning. Four points was pathetic for someone of my level, a glaring blind spot that forced me to work harder than necessary for every tactical advantage.

But I didn't follow through on the impulse. The problem with my Perception wasn't just that it was low, it was that it was so far behind my other attributes that catching up would take numerous levels. At four points, bringing it up to match my Resonance would require sixteen more points, eight full levels of advancement. That was assuming I didn't continue developing other attributes in the meantime.

No, I would have to hope I could find items or equipment that would supplement my natural abilities. Flux-enhanced scrying devices, perception amplifiers, maybe some kind of crafted enhancement that would give me the sensory range I needed without requiring massive point investment. The warehouse back in the essence trial had contained materials I couldn't identify, things that might serve such purposes if I ever gained access to similar resources.

I allocated both points to Projection instead, bringing it to an even twenty. The decision felt right as power flowed through newly opened channels, my ability to manifest and control external Flux energies expanding in ways I could feel but not yet fully understand. Twenty was a significant milestone, a round number that suggested new capabilities might become available.

The enhancement settled into my bones like it belonged there, joining the growing collection of improvements that had transformed me from a desperate refugee into something more dangerous than I'd been weeks ago. I was still barely scratching the surface of what real power looked like, still an apprentice-level initiate playing at being strong. But I was getting there, one level at a time.

Before me, the cave entrance yawned like the mouth of some massive creature, darkness seeming to beckon me forward with promises of secrets and dangers in equal measure. The opening was perfectly circular, too regular to be natural, carved by forces that thought in geometric absolutes rather than the organic curves that characterized genuine geology.

I tried to extend my senses into the depths, feeling for whatever waited in the lightless passages ahead. The effort was frustrated by my limited Perception, but I managed to detect traces of immediate aspects flowing through the cave. Void was strong here, the absence of light and sound creating pockets of nothingness that felt like holes in reality itself.

Vital aspects were present too, suggesting life of some kind deeper in the tunnels. Plants maybe, or creatures that had adapted to the eternal darkness. The energy felt different from the Flux beasts I had commanded, more complex and organized. Whatever lived down there had been shaped by the dungeon's artificial environment, evolved or created to serve specific purposes.

Echo aspects threaded around me strangely, temporal energy that seemed to be observing rather than emanating from the cave itself. The sensation was unsettling, like being watched by something that existed partially outside normal time. I couldn't pinpoint the source, but the feeling of distant observation lingered at the edges of my awareness.

The combination of void and vital was ominous enough. Whatever guarded the key I sought would likely be unlike anything I had faced before, something that thrived in darkness while maintaining complex life patterns. The echo aspects around me added another layer of unease, though I couldn't determine their purpose or origin.

I would need to descend into those depths essentially blind, my neglected Perception forcing me to rely on reflexes and raw power rather than tactical intelligence. It was a consequence of my choices, the price of focusing on offense while ignoring more subtle capabilities. I had the strength to crush most opponents, but I lacked the awareness to see them coming until they were already in striking range.

The mechanical rhythm of my breathing filled the silence as I approached the threshold between light and darkness. Each breath marked another moment of decision, another step toward whatever waited in the depths below. The respirator's filters would work just as well in the cave as they did above ground, but the psychological comfort of fresh air would be lost once I left the familiar confines of the forest behind.

I paused at the cave mouth, taking one last look at the carnage I was leaving behind. Thirty corpses cooling in the alien air, their deaths marking another milestone in my ongoing transformation. Not the end of something, but a waypoint on a journey that had no clear destination.

Then I strode forward into the welcoming darkness, leaving the known dangers of the surface for whatever unknown horrors lurked in the depths below. The cave swallowed me like a living thing, and the last traces of crimson light faded behind me as I descended toward my next test.

Whatever waited ahead, I would face it as I had faced everything else since the Awakening. With power, with determination, and with the absolute certainty that I would do whatever it took to emerge victorious.

Even if victory required sacrificing the last remnants of the man I had once been.
