Arkady

When he activated his hex, he saw their weakness quite clearly.

The stomach.

“Of course it’s the part of you that explodes.”

He shook his head. Of all the things to fight, it just had to be the species that had evolved with a complete lack of survival instincts. His blood dripped into the grass at his feet, and the first of the creatures from the clearing reached him. He looked over his shoulder and saw what looked like a rabid deer. It charged at him with horns the size of a small tree. He rolled out of the way, cutting its leg as it passed. It fell to the ground, sliding against the mud and plowing right into the pack of bombaluns.

A number of their stomachs glowed bright before expanding far more than was healthy.

Then came the first of the explosions.

Twelve trees turned to splinters in front of him. He covered his face with his arm, but wooden shrapnel buried into his clothes and flesh all over. He grimaced, but fought through the pain. If he wasn’t careful, a few splinters would be the least of his problems. He turned, two more creatures behind him, boars with mushrooms for faces. His hex showed him where to strike. He flipped over them just as they charged. Two quick stabs at the base of their necks put them down. Unfortunately, the same trick didn’t work twice. The bombaluns had separated now and leapt to the side to avoid the new carcass projectiles.

Arkady rolled his eyes. “Do me a favor and fall for my tricks, will you? There’s a lot of you to kill, and I’m short on time. I was planning to spend the rest of my day behind those walls where it’s safe. So if you could indulge me, that would be—hey watch it!” A bombalun charged him, and its belly expanded. Arkady leapt to the side, but the blast amplified his momentum. He slammed into a tree.

“Vrrrrrr.” That was the noise they made in the moments before exploding. Arkady heard it again, way too close for comfort. He forced himself to his feet and dove behind the tree just as another charged him. He heard the rush of air as slivers of wood shot overhead. Arkady cursed.

He couldn’t keep dodging. He had to reduce their numbers quickly. Right now they were focused on him, but if they lost interest and ran towards the fort, there wouldn’t be much he could do. They stared at him, slowly encircling him. He counted at least twenty. More than enough to blow several holes into the walls. If there were only one or two, it wouldn’t be too bad. But as it was, he needed to kill most of them if the fortress walls stood a chance of staying upright through the night.

“Alright, new deal, I’m not dodging anymore. You rush me, and I’ll kill you. How’s that sound?”

Two of them apparently liked the idea. They charged from either side. It was unfortunate they weren’t stupid enough to charge all at once. Their natural instincts made them prone to killing prey by any means necessary. But they did so for the good of the pack. The rest of them would eat the charred pieces of whatever remained. It was selfless, but very annoying.

He stabbed one in the stomach in the spot his hex had shown him. If he was lucky it would stop explosion. He turned around to deal with the other. Before he could strike it in the same spot, the first one exploded behind him.

“Agh!” Arkady cried out. The second one exploded shortly after.

He landed on his back with the air ejected from his lungs and ringing ears.

If he hadn’t spent the last few months’ training with Richard, those blows might’ve killed him. But even then, he certainly couldn’t withstand many more of those blasts. He slowly rose to his feet. He coughed, and blood mixed with the mud. His clothes were burned all over, and most of his shirt was nothing more than barely connected fragments now. Breathing was difficult, and he was pretty sure something was wrong inside. Or a lot of somethings. “Is that all you got?” Arkady asked.

Another bombalun, a smaller one, ran at him. It leapt and its stomach bulged. He grabbed it and flung it over his shoulder with all his might toward a few others. It landed atop one of his brethren and exploded, killing both of them.

“Hah! Got you!” Arkady laughed.

Footsteps. Three more charged all at once. He spun, looking for the smallest of them, but none were exactly the type you could throw around. He’d have to dodge. Arkady jumped from the center of the circle towards a few of the other bombaluns. If he’d got the timing right, those three should blow up any second.

Nothing. He looked over his shoulder to see they’d deactivated their stomachs. He hadn’t known that was possible.

“Vrrrr.” The bombalun he’d dove toward bared its teeth at him as its stomach exploded and rained blood and viscera. He instinctively curled up to protect his face.

His face was protected. His arms were not. When he recovered from the blast, most of the skin had burned away. He’d lost all feeling in them, and his body was growing weaker.

He pushed himself off the ground with his head until he could get his legs back underneath him. His breathing was ragged, and he had bruises all over. It hit him then that he was really going to die here.

Five.

Five of twenty were dead.

All it had cost him was the worst pain of his life and grappling with his own mortality. After everything, he was going to die in the background. His story was reduced to this. A sacrificial lamb. A man with a useless hex that couldn’t save anyone.

More charged.

More exploded.

Arkady’s body flew as if it weighed nothing. Each explosion left him worse than the last. His body was reaching its limits. No, it’d reached that a while ago. Only willpower kept him up now. He turned to look toward the fort as the next wave charged. Tomlin was there. A few of the knights had jumped from the fortress wall to aid him. Even now they were cutting down the monsters giving chase. He saw Tomlin being carried into the fort. The poor boy looked over the knight’s shoulder, scanning the forest.

It dawned on Arkady what Tomlin was looking for.

Him.

Tomlin spotted him standing there and pointed frantically to the knight. The knight shook his head and hurried inside. The gate closed behind them.

“Good job, kid. You were very brave. Don’t worry about these things. I’m gonna handle them.”

He turned as a particularly large bombalun charged at him. A surge of anger fueled him, and he turned to face it. Just as it jumped, he grabbed it in a hug and slammed it into the ground. He just wanted to hurt it before it hurt him.

Its spine snapped against the earth. Then its swollen belly returned the favor.

“Vrrrr.”

The blast caused him to flip in the air and face plant on the ground.

Arkady’s world teetered on the edge of black. He stumbled back to his feet, fighting back the dizzy sensation growing in his head. How many were left? It was impossible to count as things were. He was seeing doubles, maybe even triples. They were waiting, giving the damage time to do its job. Sure, they didn’t value their own lives, but they wouldn’t waste them on a job basically already done, would they?

“I’m just getting warmed up,” Arkady tried to say.

It came out a mush of garbled words and sounds. He was in bad shape.

It was over. He couldn’t do this anymore.

Who was he kidding?

He wasn’t strong like Richard.

He didn’t have the power that Charlie had.

He wasn’t even worthy of someone as incredible as Orielle.

In the end, the fight he couldn’t win had come way sooner than he’d ever imagined. When Arkady’s body fell forward this time. He didn’t resist.

Seeing weaknesses in things? What a joke.

He’d have been better off being born hexless. At least then he’d never have had delusions of being anything more than normal.

“I don’t think you’re weak, Arkady. And I think you’re forgetting something about that big fight in your future.”

“You do? And what’s that?”

“If you ever run into a fight you can’t win alone, just ask for a little help. Richard would help you. I would too! Maybe there is some big bad guy in your future, but there’s no rule that says you can’t count on your friends! I count on mine all the time. If I didn’t have them, I wouldn’t have made it this far. So, just remember you aren’t alone!”

Arkady smiled. “Why now?” he muttered.

Why would I remember that now?

The bombaluns were approaching, probably to finish the job.

In the end, I was so weak.

I didn’t even ask to be strong.

I just didn’t want to be weak.

He closed his eyes and imagined that useless hex of his one more time.

Arkady had stared in the mirror hundreds of times. He’d used his hex on himself hundreds of times. Searching for weaknesses he could improve. Finding things to train and work on. He’d worked so hard to get stronger. He’d trained everything he could think of.

All that, just to run into the reaper in Fort Stonehaven’s prison. A man who his hex couldn’t identify a single weakness in.

If there was a life after this one, he wanted to be like that.

A man without weakness.

Arkady smiled at the thought. In his final moments, his thoughts drifted to Orielle. He knew now if he examined himself, he’d see that scarlet hex above his heart. He’d fought to protect this fort because of her. In the end, the weakness that killed him was the same weakness he found in the hearts of many people.

Affection. Passion. Love.

It was the kind of weakness you couldn’t train away.

He felt a pressure in his shoulder. Another in his leg. One on his arm. It took him a moment to realize they were eating him.

This was how his story ended. Eaten alive in the pouring rain by a pack of monsters.

“I’m sorry, Orielle,” he muttered into the mud under his face. “I’m sorry I was so—” He couldn’t resist the urge to close his eyes any longer.

Weak.

Weak.

Weak.

Arkady gasped.

His hex could identify weakness. It allowed him to target them.

So why hadn’t he ever tried…

He spat up blood. “Scarlet Hex Search…”

A hex appeared in his mind. He imagined squeezing it to dust.

Mana coursed through his body, and his eyes shot open.

The bombaluns backed away from him.

His entire body shook, and he forced himself to his feet. He stared at his hands.

His hex pulsed in his palm. The red darkened, and words popped into his mind.

“Wine Hex Weakness.”

Two wine-red colored hexes appeared over him. One over his heart. The other encompassed his entire body.

He reached out, stretching his hand into the air before him, and gripped it into a fist around a portion of the larger hex.

The body-sized hex shattered into thousands of pieces.

With it, all of his fear, pain, and regret abandoned him in an instant.

“I wasn’t meant to just find weakness. I was meant to destroy it.” His voice was deeper, and when he looked around at the bombaluns, he didn’t see predators.

He saw prey.

The wounds on his body healed, and for the first time in his life, Arkady knew something intrinsically.

If he looked in the mirror right now and used his Scarlet Hex, he’d see a man without weakness. Even then, the smaller wine-colored hex floated over his heart. The reason why was obvious. When he thought about his friends, Charlie, Richard and the others. When he thought about Tomlin and Aveo, and the people of the fort he’d met over the last few days. And when he thought of Orielle, the woman he’d fallen madly in love with, he’d realized that sometimes weakness was strength.

When the bombaluns moved next, it wasn’t to charge him.

It was to run away.

But he wouldn’t have that.

Now it was his turn to hunt.

They’d threatened people he cared about. The only price he’d accept in return was their utter annihilation.
