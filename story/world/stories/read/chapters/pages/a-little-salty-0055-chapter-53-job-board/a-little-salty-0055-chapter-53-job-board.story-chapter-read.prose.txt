As I head back toward my room to grab a quick bite and clean up, (monster juice does not come out of your clothes if you let it dry,) I go over my new stats.

[Name: Sal]

[Title: Champion]

[Class: Culinary Rogue]

[Level: 30]

[Attack: 81]

[Agility: 43]

[HP: 100/100]

[Affinities: Poison]

[Role: Chef]

Alright! A nice boost to my Attack and Agility. Though, I note with a frown, my Agility is still slightly under Álvaro’s. That’s ridiculous—he was the one who got knocked off the scorpid! It’s also super not fair that he has two affinities.

But my HP also went up a point, which is nice. Not to mention, I gained a new skill.

[Dual Wield: When the user is wielding a weapon in each hand, the precision and potency of all attacks are doubled.]

That’s amazing! Or, it would be, if I could wield a weapon with my left hand. I look down at it, flexing my thumb and pinky. The skin still pulls tight against the stumps where my other fingers used to be. I’ve gotten used to holding basic stuff with it, like vegetables, or smoke bombs, but my grip is pretty loose, and I’m certain that trying to stab or slice anything with a knife would knock it out of my grasp.

Unless…

Echo, does the Noxious Gauntlet count as a weapon? I ask.

[Affirmative.]

I audibly groan, which causes a nearby merchant to give me a strange look. Figures. It’s like the System is trying to force me to embrace Shirasil’s power. Well, I won’t give in that easy. Besides, I can still find other ways to make this new skill activate. And doubling the potency of my potions is exactly what I’ve been looking for.

After I head home, scarf down some (lightly poisoned) food, and get cleaned up, I head back to the Guild.

I stop by Zaquedyil’s alchemy shop first.

“Hi, Zee,” I say to an empty counter, glancing over the ingredients on the wall behind. Sometimes I want to grab a little bit of everything, just to play around and experiment with them all. That would be a great way to go bankrupt, though. A shadow shifts in the corner of my eye, and when I turn toward it, Zaquedyil has appeared, smiling softly as always.

“I’ve got something for you,” I say, setting the scorpid head on the counter.

The arachnoid raises her eyebrows. Or, rather, the eyebrow ridge above her main eyes.

“Just the head?” Zaquedyil asks.

I scowl. “Someone else stole the body.”

Now she looks faintly amused. “From you?”

“I don’t want to talk about it,” I huff.

Zaquedyil chuckles softly to herself as she turns the head over, holding it up to the light and prodding it from every angle. She casts some sort of appraisal spell on it after that, pausing for a long moment as she processes whatever information the spell is giving her.

“A silver and two coppers,” she finally says.

I groan. “That’s it? I risked my life for that!”

“It would be significantly more if you had the rest of it,” Zaquedyil says impassively.

I heave a heavy sigh. Stupid Álvaro. And he was the one complaining about kill-steals, or whatever! “Okay, fine. Put it on my account?”

Since her shop is where I spend most of my money anyway, I find it easier for her to keep a record of my sales that I can later exchange for purchased supplies. Even with the locking spell, I don’t trust my apartment to not get broken into, and carrying coins on hand is annoyingly noisy (and risky) when I don’t have the time or opportunity to get more from my Apothecary.

“Of course,” the arachnoid says, sweeping the head off the counter.

“Thanks, Zee.” I give a dejected wave as I leave her shop and strike back out into the main cavern.

Nieve isn’t here this time around, and I find myself relaxing as I head back over to the job board. No one to judge me or question my choices. I glance up the board, my eyes alighting on one A Tier job in particular. Good. It’s still there. I can’t claim it until I have access to A Tier jobs, so every day I check back in and hope no one else has taken it.

[Scope: Capture and deliver a live ashwing]

[Report: Zaquedyil]

[Reward: Basilisk Fang]

I want that basilisk fang so freaking bad. In fact, I’d gone straight to Zaquedyil when I first saw the posting and asked her to sell it to me directly. She only offered a sympathetic smile and shake of her head. I don’t know why she wants an ashwing so bad—Echo said it was some sort of rare magical animal made of ash—but I’ll just have to wait until I can claim the job myself. Only two more tiers. I can make it.

According to several alchemy books I’ve, ahem, borrowed, basilisk fang is an exceptionally rare and potent source of venom. It’s said only one nest of basilisks still exists in the world, and trying to get a fang directly from the source is likely to get you poisoned, eaten, or petrified—if you can even find them in the first place. Even Nieve told me they doubted they could survive such a mission on their own. But I’m not in any hurry to fight a live basilisk; I only need its fang.

There’s only a handful of potions that use the basilisk ingredient, I suspect because they’re so rare to come by that not a lot of experimenting with it has been done. But the potions that do use it are all wickedly dangerous. From my own Toxic Intuition, I’ve been able to come up with a list of ingredients that can combine to make the deadliest—as far as I’m aware—poison in existence. The Basilisk Fang is the final ingredient. Combine that with my Dual Wield skill, where I can double the efficacy of any poison on my blades, and I might just have something dangerous enough to threaten the gods.

To threaten Widengra.

But I’m getting ahead of myself. I can only do C Tier jobs right now. I scan through the listings, looking for which one will help me advance the fastest.

The intel ones are the easiest. With Echo in my head, I can glean information about people and places that isn’t available to the public. But unless it’s a high priority target, the reward is usually minimal. Still, they’re easy and fast for me to complete, and that adds up.

The hits are uncommon—you can’t have too many people getting assassinated in a city before the Guards start to notice—but I don’t see any in the C Tier right now. There are always the collection type jobs: go out and collect a bunch of poisonous or black-listed items. Echo is also really good at helping with this, although those are mostly D and E Tier. Then there are delivery jobs. These are one of the few jobs spread across all Tiers, depending on how dangerous or rare the item is that needs to be retrieved or transported.

I decide to grab two of these from the C Tier, both of which will require me to visit the Night Market. I’ve only gone once before, for a delivery job; it’s sort of like a much less organized Blackcloak Guild, filled with common gangsters and dealers of every variety. It’s also pretty dangerous; Blackcloak membership is almost like a brotherhood, whereas the Night Market is every competing illicit faction in the city thrown into one street. Toss in occasional raids from the City Guard, and you’ve got a recipe for backstabbing and chaos. That said, it’s a good source of illegal supplies for the Blackcloaks, not to mention, the Night Market is the perfect scapegoat; most of the stuff we do can be blamed on one of their merchants or mercenaries without too much trouble.

Of course, that’s also probably why the Guild has lower level members perform these sorts of delivery jobs instead of having items delivered to the Guild itself; another buffer against discovery and culpability.

I grab an intel job while I’m at it, since it’s also connected to someone in the Night Market, and call it a day. The Guild will only let me take so many jobs at a time, but the faster I can complete these, the faster I can grab more jobs, and the faster I complete jobs, the faster I can Tier up and get that basilisk fang.

The Night Market, paradoxically, doesn’t actually take place at night. It moves its time and locations around in a continuous game of cat and mouse with the City Guards, but according to the Blackcloak’s information, the next Market will be in about eighteen hours. Plenty of time for me to brew more potions, get some rest, and pre-emptively satisfy tomorrow’s Role Requirement.

The interaction with Álvaro stalks me back to my room. I silently fume over his infuriating demeanor as I unpack herbs and bottles from my Apothecary and set up an alchemy station. I still can’t believe he stole that scorpid carcass from me. And what’s his deal, anyway? Having fun while nearly getting pincered in half. He even said something about it fulfilling his Role Requirement. What was it again? Monster Hunter, I think. That’s such a cooler Role than mine. Well, lucky him. I’m so glad he got dealt a better hand, and doesn’t have to worry about gods, or losing his sanity, or feel guilty over the ones he’s failed…

I grumble, activating the room’s fire spell circle, and stick a pot of water over the top of it. I toss some moonhawk eyes into the pot, add a drop of my own mana, then wait for it to come to a boil.

Although, maybe the gods are a threat to him, even if he isn’t worrying about them. Does he know that gods like Widengra are out there? If not, he’s been lucky so far. And as annoying as he was, I certainly don’t want him to become a victim of another Maru. Maybe I should go hunt him down at some point and tell him what I know. Even if he did steal that scorpid body from me—we’re all in the same boat. Not to mention, he said something about having a sibling here. If he knows more people from Earth, then I want to find out who.

I wonder if anyone else out there is trying to fight back against the gods, like me. I wonder if anyone else understands the danger we’re in. I wonder if they also have this yearning emptiness in the pit of their stomach.

It takes an hour and most of my star blossom pollen to finish brewing the potion. Yet even more ingredients to go on my never-ending grocery list. But when I finish, I’m satisfied with the results.

[Night Vision potion complete,] Echo reports. [Efficacy: 83%. Potency: four ounces of brew will provide twelve hours of low-light vision.]

Nice. And I’ve got enough here for at least twelve servings. I pull out a bunch of empty bottles and start doling the potion out.

Hot, sweaty, and with an aching back, I clean up and pack away my Apothecary, then collapse back onto my sleeping mat for a nap. After dozing for an hour or two, I yawn, stretch, and get right back to it.

Over the next twelve hours, I brew five more potions. I restock my Invisibility and Smoke Bomb potions (including Poisonous Smoke Bombs,) make a handful of Healing potions (you can never have too many health pots,) and a couple Sleeping potions. I also Attune some more poison, adding it to the ever-growing stash at my waist. I’ve got a small water-flask of pure Attuned poison by now. It’s strictly for emergencies, because when I use it, I lose it, and it takes a lot of time to Attune just a little bit. But I never go anywhere without it.

As the time until the Night Market closes in, I pack everything up and attach a careful selection of potions to my belt and bandolier. I have a system for which potion I put where, so I can grab and use one without having to think too much about it. Potions that are for me go on my belt. Potions that are for other people go on the bandolier. I tap my hand down the bottles, mentally reciting which potion is where. Once I’m sure I’ve memorized it, I strike back out into the streets.

This time, the Market is taking place in a part of the city that technically doesn’t exist; a hollowed-out portion of the mesa between the walls of the city and the walls of the mesa. It’s there for maintenance purposes—spell circuits and piping threaded through the walls in a complex lattice to provide power, water, and sewage lines for the city. It’s a location where the Market has convened before, and while I haven’t been to this specific location, I know how to find it and what to expect. I pull my scarf up around my mouth. Not that it will obscure my identity. I just like the familiar way it hugs my neck.

As I’m climbing a staircase to the level where the Market’s entrance is located, a familiar voice calls out my name. I turn in surprise as Cyros jogs up to me.

“What are you doing here?” I ask. It’s a big city, and just running into one of the four people I know by chance is extremely unlikely.

“Got a job at the Market,” Cyros says as he catches up. “I’m guessing you did, too?”

I eye him suspiciously. Coincidence? Or did he notice my marker on the three jobs and grabbed another Night Market job as an excuse to shadow me? He’s been like a needy puppy dog lately, always worrying over every little thing I do and following close at my heels. It’s annoying, but I can’t actually prove it, because he has a legitimate excuse anytime he shows up at the same place as me. And the Blackcloaks do often cross paths while on jobs. Still, though…

“What’s up?” Cyros asks. “You okay?”

“Yeah,” I say, turning away and continuing toward the Market’s entrance. I beckon for him to follow. “Just thinking. Don’t worry about it—it’s a mute point.”

“Moot point,” Cyros says.

“Huh?”

He grins. “Nothing. Ready to see the Market? Should be fun.”

Ugh, him, too? What is with all these guys thinking life or death scenarios are fun?

Well, okay, I am a little eager to browse all the illegal weapons and herbs, myself. But that’s totally different!

“Ready,” I agree, stepping into an alley and starting the labyrinthine trek toward the city’s underbelly.

I actually do feel a bit safer with Cyros at my side. We’re about to dive into a throng of trigger-happy people who wouldn’t pass up the opportunity to target a couple of teens. But I wouldn’t trust anyone more to have my back, and honestly? I almost want someone to pick a fight with us—just for the challenge and experience points if nothing else. A flutter of excitement tickles my gut, and in spite of myself, I smile.
