Chapter 35: Dinner and Danger

Ambrose had to admit, SNAC lived up to his name. He quickly cooked them a delicious dinner of charbroiled ribs over a bed of rice and smoked vegetables, soaked in a sweet and sour lemon sauce. Part of him wanted to ask how he made it and what kind of recipes he had, but there were more pressing matters at hand. He’d have to pick the golem’s artificial brain later.

“So, we’re committed,” Luthor said as they sat around a table in Vanderborn’s personal dining quarters. It was small enough to be cozy, but it bordered on claustrophobic. “We don’t have many options, so we’ll do what we can to explore this…”

“Aetheric Expanse,” Jessica supplied, spearing a hunk of ribmeat into her mouth. “Or AE, if you want to abbreviate it but sound silly.”

“Ay Ee.” Raeleq grunted like he was constipated. Even Ambrose had to chuckle. As good as the food and wine was, the enormity of what they faced had everyone on edge.

“Right. Explore the Aetheric Expanse, see if there’s any resistance, and see what we can do to help. If nothing else, we have access to this ship now, so even if we didn’t want to do some actual fighting, we could probably get by transporting people and cargo.” Luthor grimaced in distaste.

“No no,” said Raeva, leaning over the table predatorily. “I fully intend on fighting them. What they’re doing to our people is bad enough, but on this scale? Raeleq and I have been training for this our entire lives. We have a chance to do our part in the good fight, and make our ancestors proud.”

Everyone turned to Ambrose, who continued enjoying the meal quietly. Until the silence grew thick, and he sighed and put the fork down. “Obviously I am in, and I have some ideas of what we can do. The most important thing is to focus on attacking their supplies and vulnerabilities. As our local royalty can attest, all empires create their own unrest. We exacerbate that in a hundred places and it can start a self-sustaining fire.

“If we don’t get killed while doing it.” He paused to take a sip of the wine. “We know that Emperor Turhan has men like Vanderborn working for him. We can’t take him in a fair or unfair fight. We had to run away. Imagine every world under his control has a bastard like that. We need to be smart about this, and start small. Build ourselves up, and gather as much information as possible first.”

For some strange reason, everyone smiled at him. They looked thrilled, actually. It was kind of annoying. “What? I said I was in. Did you expect me to advise running and playing couriers for others? You were trained to protect and fight for a cause. I was trained to attack and weaken others. It’s not just a strategy, it’s a way of thinking.”

“Destruction,” Raeva said approvingly, “clearing out the old so the new can begin.”

“Sure,” Ambrose allowed. “That’s one way of looking at it. Just because I think this is a doomed venture doesn’t mean I’m not going to give it my all. I stood up to Vanderborn with the rest of you, didn’t I? Stop looking at me like that.”

To his irritation, everyone just smiled wider. Luthor pulled out the two cards Ambrose had passed him earlier. “You’re right. Information, and striking opportune targets. And thanks to you, we have a little boost in our power before we do anything. And very appropriate ones, too. I have a tough time believing these weren’t meant for us, before…Well.”

Around the table, people muttered their agreements. Each of the five enhancement cards were perfectly suited to them, like Vanderborn expected them to be the ones to advance. Even Jessica, though that may have been by virtue of her being one of only two mind affinity wizards in their year, before Cara quit.

Ambrose’s own potential enhancement card almost had him kicking himself over taking the Wildcard.

[Spiteful Spirit: Enhancement. Whenever one of your creatures dies, a nearby opponent discards a card from their deck.]

It fit him perfectly. Knowing it would’ve been the reward for the end of the year hurt. The Wildcard had become a strong enhancement of its own, and a unique one at that, but Ambrose was still unsure about it compared to the ability to force more discards. At least when he progressed enough to get another enhancement slot, it would be waiting for him.

“I’m pretty happy with mine,” Raeleq announced, taking a big gulp of wine and wiping the back of his mouth with his hand. “Take a look. This is going to make everything better!”

[Packmaster: Enhancement. Whenever you shuffle your deck, summon a free Pack Raptor.]

Free creatures to someone who worked well among them would be a great addition. More than that, it gave something for the shuffle, which may have presented new opportunities, but also deactivated a card.

“Same,” Raeva agreed. “A free spell every other round will add up.”

Hers had been [Burnout: Enhancement. Each time you shuffle, add a free Flame Lance to your hand] and it would do just as well. It figured that the twins would get similar enhancements.

“But,” she added, “I am even more excited for the level 5 creatures. There’s just one problem…”

Jessica chuckled bitterly. “We might be able to add the enhancements to our decks, but the creatures are too powerful. At least for right now, and I’m not sure we can afford someone experiencing a backlash just because they got greedy.”

Everyone looked at Ambrose.

“Again, I know better. Seriously, you’re making me regret not turning on you all.” Despite his words, his tone was cheery and he continued to eat.

“She’s right,” Luthor continued, ignoring the barb. “We can’t afford to have any of us out of commission for any period of time. I am reasonably certain that we can all add the enhancements to our decks, but we’ll wait a while on the creatures.”

It was a damned shame. Ambrose finally had access to a proper demon.

[Abyssal Desecrator: At the start of each round, this creature siphons life to bring a creature from your discard back to the fight for free. 5 mana, 3 ongoing]

In his deck, it would become another imp instead of the muscular demon on the card, but he imagined it would be a fairly strong and dangerous imp. Whatever they ended up doing, Ambrose looked forward to testing himself further. Although maybe the bottom level of the ship would be good for that.

Raeva drained her wine and set the glass down. “So we have a general plan, and we have some new cards. We’ve got some truly excellent food, too. I’m just about ready for a long rest. Do we have anything else we have to worry about, or can my ass unclench for a few minutes?”

They laughed, but Ambrose answered, “The remaining Wildcard.”

The table fell silent. Most of them weren’t as greedy as the shadow wizard. That didn’t mean they didn’t want it for themselves. Anyone with any ambition or hunger for cards would’ve tripped over themselves for it.

“I don’t need it,” said Luthor, after things got a bit awkward. “I’m happy to take good cards we happen to find, but this is something special. We need to be careful and considerate with it.”

“Yes,” Jessica agreed quietly. “It should go wherever it will have the most impact. That’s not me, obviously.”

“Could it be me?” Raeleq questioned, eyes shifting around.

“No,” Ambrose said. “I’ve got a better idea.”

“You already got one,” Raeva protested. “There’s no way in hell you’re going to suggest that you get ano --”

“I think it should go to you or Jessica,” Ambrose interrupted, feeling a flash of heat in his head. “Seriously, can you stop giving me shit for ten seconds?”

Raeva had the good grace to look embarrassed. “Sorry.”

“Go on,” Luthor said. He set his fork down and considered them all over steepled hands, elbows resting on the table.

“Thank you,” he said with a sigh. “Now, Raeva’s our heaviest single target hitter, so whatever spell it gives her will no doubt mean we have something more damaging for if and when Vanderborn catches up with us. That said…Jessica could use the boost the most. I mean no offense, but if we’re going to survive this, we need to all reach our potential. This would be good for that.”

He knew there was a good chance it would embarrass or shame his friend, and she did flush and look down. It came with a nod of understanding and acceptance, so it couldn’t have been too bad.

“I wouldn’t say no to it,” she said, “but I would rather it go to someone we know will make use of it. We don’t want to waste it on the least of us.”

Again, silence, but then Raeleq broke it with a boisterous laugh. “You’re right! It shouldn’t go to the least of us. And so, I won’t take it. Anyone but me and Ambrose should take it. And only because he already has one,” he added, as if he worried about possibly offending.

The table relaxed, but both Luthor and Raeva looked like they wanted to protest at the idea of anyone being the least of them. They were too sentimental that way.

“Make it Jessica,” Raeva said after a moment’s hesitation. “Wherever we go first, we’ll make sure to find us all something good to work with. So, Jessica gets the wildcard, and we’ll all wait before trying for our level 5 affinity increase. I think that’s enough business for now, don’t you?”

“Yes! Enough business, more drinks and food,” Raeleq boomed.

SNAC had cooked large portions, but the five of them were hungry, and after the first round, it was clear that dinner would end after everyone took seconds. Luckily, there was still plenty of wine to go around, and Ambrose fully intended on drowning his sorrows and frustrations of the previous day before passing out in the room he already claimed.

With as much as they had to worry about and discuss, it was like the load had lightened and they could breathe. The rest of the meat was gone in minutes, followed by most of the veggies and rice, and three bottles of wine between them.

The conversation centered around their year at school, and specifically the earlier days. No one wanted to talk about how things had gone that day, even if it was at the forefront of their minds. No, for the next half an hour, they did everything in their power to avoid their current situation and focus on the time before…before.

Jessica was the first person to stand on unsteady legs. “SNAC’s currently piloting for us,” she said slowly, a light flush on her cheeks. “I’m going to check on him and see what he can teach me about the controls. I may not be strong, but I can get us to wherever we need to go. Just give me a few days, and I’ll figure it all out.”

She was the first to leave, but not the last. Raeleq stood up ten minutes later, stretching in the cramped quarters until his back popped loudly. They’d managed to patch up the worst of his wounds, and now the most important thing was rest.

“Big, weird day, but maybe not a bad one. We’ve got a big fight ahead of us, but it’s a good fight. I’m glad you’re on our side, Ambrose.” He nodded to the shadow wizard, and left to find a room of his own.

“I second that,” Raeva said, stirring. “We may give you trouble, but you came through in the end. And I’m really grateful for that.”

“How grateful?” Ambrose asked with a waggle of his eyebrows.

Raeva laughed and left them behind as well. When Luthor looked at Ambrose, the shadow wizard sighed. “Are you going to get sappy too? I’m not sure I can take it.”

Luthor laughed with surprisingly little inhibition. The wine had loosened him up as well, it seemed. “Nothing so explicit, I think. I am glad you’re on our side. I’m glad we didn’t end up trapped in cards, like poor Lennar and the others. But mostly? I think we should probably take a day or two to rest and put distance between us and home. I’m worried about Jessica. Hell, I’m worried about all of you. All of us. But we’re going to be alright.”

“And you know this, how?” Ambrose demanded. The tipsy confidence in Luthor’s voice wasn’t annoying so much as frustrating. “We’re pretty doomed from where I sit.”

“Maybe,” Luthor acknowledged. “But I doubt we’re alone. People rarely are.” He finished his glass and left it there, along with the mountain of dirty dishes they’d all left for SNAC to clean later. “No matter what you’ve thought this entire time, you’ve never been alone, you know. You’ve always had us, even if you didn’t realize it. No man’s an island.”

And then it was just Ambrose and his thoughts. He lingered for a few seconds longer before he got up, relieved himself in the small closet made for just that, and headed to the already furnished, fairly comfortable cabin next to Vanderborn’s private quarters. He assumed Jessica would take those, otherwise he might’ve made himself comfortable.

He stripped down to just his underclothes, and climbed into bed. It was late, he was a bit drunk, and the weight of the day caught up to him. When he got comfortable enough to start to drift away, it was with one last stray thought.

Ambrose wasn’t alone.

And it wasn’t just his story anymore.
