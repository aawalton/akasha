A massive explosion echoed across the meadow as a wall of orange fire swept outward, swallowing the ranks of human infantry in a blinding flash. Heat surged over Rusty's heavy Goliath frame, but his hardened armor easily withstood the force of the blast. Earth and shattered metal flew into the night sky while agonizing screams filled the air. The soldiers who had been so eager to hunt their prey were instantly thrown into complete chaos, their ranks scattered and depleted.

Rusty dug his large boots into the dirt and raised his tower shield to protect himself from the blast while silently watching the carnage. More than half of the pursuing force lay broken or burning in the smoking crater. It was not something they could recover from easily.

Across the clearing, Lethira lowered her bow, her breathing heavy and her face streaked with sweat. Teral stood near her, his eyes wide as he stared at the carnage they had caused.

"That was a better trap than I expected, but now... do we face the rest or retreat?"

Rusty asked. Nearly half of the enemy force had been decimated before them, leaving the survivors dazed and demoralized. If they struck now, they could crush the remaining soldiers entirely. However, there were still more humans outside the forest, and for the time being, perhaps survival was more important.

"Rusty, with your help..."

Teral's grip on his staff tightened until his knuckles turned white. He caught his breath, his eyes reflecting the dwindling firelight. The anger in his expression was unmistakable. He was about to move forward, ready to summon more constructs and perhaps even charge into the smoke himself, when Lethira placed a hand on his shoulder.

"Teral, we should leave. Now is our chance. They won't be able to follow us."

She stopped him just as he was about to summon more constructs and charge into the smoke himself.

"She is right."

Rusty said, his body changing back to his all-around form as he shrank down from the Goliath.

"The heavy cavalry at the forest's edge will not move into these trees, but if their commander sees his foot soldiers wiped out, he might burn the entire woodland."

Teral swallowed hard, his hands trembling around his wooden staff. He looked back toward the smoking crater where the surviving human soldiers staggered through the ash, groaning in terror.

Hatred still burned in the young elf's eyes, a reflection of the suffering his people had endured in the prison and the trauma of losing his entire family. Yet practical reality won out. He took a slow breath, nodded solemnly, and stepped back.

"The entrance to one of the tunnels isn't far. We already evacuated everyone into them. Let's go."

Lethira said quietly. Her bow was already slung across her back, but as they began to leave, Rusty looked at her.

"Tunnels?"

"Instead of asking, great spirit, how about you see them for yourself?"

The elven woman replied with a grin. Rusty wanted to tell her to stop calling him a great spirit, but decided against it. Soon, the three of them were running through the forest, leaving the smoldering wreckage of the meadow far behind.

They moved through the shadows with ease. Their footsteps were silent, aided by some kind of elven skill or magic. He could see Lethira’s soles glow green whenever she moved forward. The grass beneath her feet recovered almost instantly, and the soil she stepped on shifted back into place as if no one had ever run across the path.

His own footsteps were not disappearing, however. Teral was behind him, taking care of that. His staff gave off a green glow that concealed their tracks, erasing their footprints and disrupting their trail. Following them would probably be difficult without some powerful tracking skills.

After ten minutes of relentless running, Lethira finally slowed her pace, bringing the group to a halt beside a massive, moss-covered oak tree. Its ancient roots twisted across the ground like giant serpents, while its trunk was easily three meters in diameter.

Lethira knelt on the damp soil and pressed her palm firmly against the rough bark. A soft green radiance pulsed outward from her hand, flowing into the dark wood like liquid light. The thick roots groaned softly as they shifted, parting smoothly to reveal a dark passage that sloped downward into the earth.

“Oh, that’s neat! It’s similar to those bandit tunnels.”

Rusty commented, as this was not the first time he had seen the wood elves hiding out in caves or tunnels.

“It is an old subterranean network, created centuries ago during the ancient wars.”

Lethira explained softly as she stepped into the passage.

“The roots conceal the entrance completely once it is closed. Even specialized detection magic struggles to pierce the ancient elven enchantments.”

“Oh... interesting.”

Rusty touched one of the roots as he descended. With his mana sense activated, he could feel power pulsing deep within the living wood. A dense aura of nature magic permeated the tree, far beyond anything he had ever felt before.

It was so powerful that he could not even identify its grade. His identification skill simply displayed a series of question marks. As soon as the last of them crossed the threshold, Lethira released her hold on the oak. The massive roots groaned once more, sliding tightly together and sealing the entrance without leaving a single seam.

Total darkness swallowed the tunnel for a fraction of a second before bioluminescent moss along the subterranean walls began to glow, casting a gentle green light across the dirt steps that led deeper underground.

“Oh... how did you find a place like this?”

He asked as they continued downward. The last time he had interacted with Teral and his people had been during the siege against the baron. They had created the village of Aetherfalls and restored the great tree there, allowing them to change their classes and become stronger.

A lot must have happened since then. Now that they were walking through the tunnels without needing to rush, Teral began to explain.

“It wasn’t long after we established our village that our elders discovered ancient records hidden within the roots of the restored great tree. It wasn’t just an ordinary great tree, but an ancestral one containing knowledge that had been lost for countless years. It is only thanks to that knowledge that we have been able to survive for this long.”

Rusty just nodded while listening. He had worried a little when he left last time, as the elven village was not located in a good strategic position. They had also just laid siege to a human baron's fortress, so it was obvious that some kind of retaliation would follow. Yet his elven summoner was right here and had survived it, and these tunnels and the knowledge they had gained were responsible for that.

As they walked, he could see a system of roots running through the walls. They pulsed with power, seemingly encompassing the entire underground forest. He wanted to ask more questions, but before he could, they finally caught up with the emancipated prisoners. When they did, both the elven woman and Teral quickly ran up to them, almost forgetting that Rusty was even there.

"Hm, is this what they call priorities?"

He wondered as he looked around. They were in a larger chamber, where at least a hundred survivors were huddled together beneath the soft glow of bioluminescent moss. Most of the prisoners were severely malnourished, but their condition did not end there. Some were injured, with some even missing arms or other body parts. Then there were those who had been experimented on. It was not strange that the other elves would prioritize them instead of giving Rusty an explanation of the current situation.

Rusty watched silently, standing near the entrance of the chamber like a quiet metal sentinel. Even though he was a creature made of metal, he understood these humanoids to some degree. After meeting Gleam, interacting with Gwen, and even spending time with the street urchins back home, he had come to understand the weight carried by certain bonds. It was clear that, to these elves, these people were part of their group, their tribe, and they would protect them just as Rusty would protect Gleam and the others.

They stopped for a moment, giving the prisoners first aid and using the tree root system for something resembling healing magic. The bioluminescent moss and the roots pulsed with light while another elf, one with a healing class, performed something that resembled a prayer.

The entire room filled with green light, which seemed to enhance the body's natural regenerative capabilities. Rusty watched as scratches and even open wounds began to heal before his eyes. The green aura washed over the weary refugees, seemingly dulling their pain and relaxing them to some extent. It was not as potent as some of the holy spells priests could cast, but it spread across the entire chamber, working on hundreds of people at once.

Once the immediate medical needs had been addressed, Teral stepped away from the crowd and approached Rusty. The heavy strain on the young elf's face had softened slightly, replaced by profound relief that made him look a little more like the boy Rusty remembered from his first summoning.

"Thank you, Rusty."

Teral said quietly, resting a palm against the cold steel of Rusty's shoulder.

"Without your strength, we would have been overwhelmed before we ever reached the clearing. I was far too foolish to think my constructs alone could buy us enough time."

"Well, you probably would have made it to the clearing, but not with as many soldiers following you."

Rusty replied with a nod. He had been able to force the soldiers into a larger group from their previous fanned-out formation. Without him, Teral probably would not have been able to do that alone, especially with his mana running low. The explosion trap would probably have taken out fewer of the humans, and perhaps some of the injured people would not have been able to escape.

Due to the necromancer summoner he had interacted with, he knew that his actions affected these worlds, and that refusing a mission could alter their fate in a negative way. They probably wouldn’t die outright, but they would still suffer consequences from him not being there to offer his aid.

“Don’t worry about it. That’s why I’m here!”

He smacked his metallic chest with his hand, a gesture he had learned from watching humanoids. When he did, Teral’s expression changed slightly, becoming more relaxed. He almost chuckled, but before they could continue, a few elves gathered around them.

“Teral, we need your help.”

“Ah, my apologies, Rusty. I’ll be right back.”

“It’s fine.”

He was taken away to use his constructs to help carry some of the injured who couldn’t recover fast enough. Soon, they made their way through the tunnels once more.

‘This was quite the opening mission, but it doesn’t seem to be the main one.’

He had already battled a powerful monster and completed a prison break. In the past, something like this would have been the main mission, but it was merely a prelude to the true conflict brewing across the region.

‘I wonder what it will be this time... Maybe another siege? Will I have to fight some human knights? Will it be an actual territorial battle?’

There were a lot of things going through his mind. He was already dreaming of wide open battles and powerful foes he could face, perhaps gaining some new skills from them, since he hadn’t been getting anything good lately.

Soon, they continued their journey, and it was a long one. They traveled through the various tunnels while he remained a few steps behind his summoner, who was quite busy. The other elves constantly asked him for help and advice.

One thing also became clear. The elven fighters weren’t that old. Most of them seemed to be around Teral’s age, with levels below his, making Teral one of the strongest combatants among them.

They traveled for what felt like an entire day, taking only small breaks along the way. The prisoners had been weak at first, but the energy within the roots along the walls was slowly restoring their strength. Instead of slowing down, their pace began to increase. Some of the prisoners even started helping carry those who were too injured to walk on their own. Then, finally, they began making their way upward toward the exit.

‘The roots here are thicker and more lively...’

He could tell that something was different from the concentration of magical energy in the air, which was truly tremendous. It was almost surprising that such a place had not been discovered by the humans, as the concentration of energy was simply too high. However, once they emerged outside and he saw the small village he had helped establish, everything became clear.

They emerged near the great tree at the heart of Aetherfalls. The tree itself was larger than before, more vibrant, and its leaves pulsed with power. Mana floated through the air above them, forming a dome that created a green ceiling and distorted the sky above.

“What’s that...?”

He asked as he stepped outside.

“That’s one of the tree’s blessings, Rusty.”

Teral stepped up beside the living suit of armor, one hand resting lightly against his wooden staff as he gazed upward at the shimmering emerald sky. He explained that the barrier was a domain spell woven directly from the deep roots of the ancestral tree. It concealed the entire valley from human eyes and protected their safe haven from even the highest tier of detection magic.

“Thanks to the ancient spells, the forest has truly become our domain. Without the tree’s guidance, the humans can’t find us. They will be trapped in an endless illusion if they enter the forest.”

“Ah… that’s also neat!”

Rusty commented as he listened to how the village had managed to grow this much. It was no longer strange to him. With this level of magical protection, no one could easily find them. From the looks of things, the humans were also unable to properly assess the danger posed by the wood elves, who were growing stronger with each passing day.

‘Now then, what’s the true mission here?’

He asked himself as he watched more elves move toward them. They looked different from before. Hope had returned to their eyes, but there was something else there too.

Some gazed toward the injured elves, those who had lost limbs or been left with twisted scars from corrupted magic. They had seen what the human kingdom had done to their kin, and it was clear that rage and a thirst for vengeance burned behind their gazes.
