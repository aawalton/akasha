Fourth floor, Ironpeak Mountain quadrant, Titan Stone zone

Waves of prismatic light rippled across the sky, each a herald of the chaotic dimensional rifts manifesting beneath them. The crimson rents in reality were not difficult to find, and the Labyrinth's global quest drew both independent Wayfarers and military expeditions from every society that could reach the conflict. More arrived every hour: small teams of adventurers, squads of elite soldiers, and even full companies of infantry, marshalled on short notice. The people of the Labyrinth were no strangers to the threat that the occaisional rogue demon posed, and while the secret of their imprisonment had been long hidden from them, they reacted quickly and decisively to the possibility of their return en masse.

Old rivalries were put on hold as forces from a half dozen city-states and guilds marshalled around the perimeter of the mountain quadrant the rifts continued to manifest in. For the first time in living memory, tek'kalla raid groups fought alongside the forces of the Free Cities Alliance. They were joined by mercenary guilds, agents of independent city states, and even groups of adventurers hailing from societies so distant, few amongst the hastily formed coalition had even heard of them.

There was no great amount of coordination between these disparate factions, little more than brief discussions regarding which regions each group would be responsible for. There was a lot of ground to cover. Demons were already emerging from the dimensional rifts, and they didn't seem to have a particular objective in mind once they did. They spilled into the Labyrinth in a growing flow, spreading out in every direction to clash with the defenders. Their numbers were small at first, limited by the unstable nature of the planar tears they had to traverse to escape their world, but that soon began to change. The chaotic rifts gradually stabilized, growing less volatile as the first day drew to a close. Some still winked in and out of existence, cutting through the land around them like interdimensional buzz saws, but several of the larger rifts began to settle. As they did, they grew capable of transferring larger numbers of the demonic forces waiting to traverse the breach. At first, they could only pass through in ones and twos, and only the weaker amongst their number were accommodated. Those restrictions quickly eased. By the end of the first day, elite demons could, slowly and carefully, navigate the rifts, leading groups of a dozen or more of their lesser kin.

Two things prevented the gradually increasing demon forces from quickly breaking through the cordon of defenders arrayed against them. First, the dimensional transition was not a smooth process. Many demons were left disoriented, drained, or even wounded by the process of exiting into the Labyrinth. To make matters worse, some of them had only recently been awakened from stasis, adding to their sluggishness. More importantly, however, the Labyrinth wasn't leaving its inhabitants to fend for themselves on this battlefield.

A flash of light bathed the mountains in a stark white glow, briefly dispelling the shroud of night. When the combatants' vison cleared, both sides could plainly see what was left in its wake. A golem, easily ten-stories tall, stood at the mouth of one of the many valleys that snaked through the mountain quadrant. Its stone body was plated with shining sections of gold and silver. Brilliant light shone from innumerable runes that ran across its chest in a spiral pattern, centered on a glowing crystaline core.

Demon forces reacted quickly, sending many of their strongest to confront the Stellar Core Golem. Guardians of the Labyrinth's highest floors, these nigh unstoppable constructs had kept vigil over the demon world for millennia. Now, the Labyrinth was forced to summon them all the way down to the fourth floor, a contingency the system hadn't been prepared for when it was created. As such, only a few of these titanic constructs patrolled that battlefield, engaging what demons they could find and attempting to disrupt the dimensional disturbances that kept them pouring through. Armed with and powered by star fire, the presence of these constructs forced the demons to send the most capable of their number to confront them. The remaining lesser demons clashed against the mortal forces arrayed against them, but made little progress on their own. Battles raged across the quadrant, coming in fits and bursts as roaming bands of bruzaks and lugren encountered Wayfarers, mercenaries, and other defenders of the Labyrinth.

A tenuous balance was struck, with each side giving and taking in equal measure. But, as the rifts slowly grew more stable and more demons successfully made the transition, that wouldn't last forever.

A very reluctant Elyx followed Zertrolk through a demon formation, their path taking them towards the largest of the planar rifts. A generous soul might call the loose, disorganized lines her kin were standing in "ranks", but that wasn't the word she would choose. They were more like a mob. Angry. Energized. Ready to fight, yes, but as an individual, not as a team. With most of the demon leadership, what of their number that managed to arrive, kept busy by the Labyrinth's titanic golems, force cohesion was rapidly coming apart.

We think of ourselves as superior because of our innate abilities. Elyx thought, watching three bruzaks exchanging heated blows while their companions watched and cheered. But what does that matter, when all we think of are ourselves?

She was guilty of that, too. Elyx had landed her original position as a field ritualist due to personal ambition. She'd wanted out of the caretaker caverns, to accumulate power of her own and make a name for herself. Then, once things began collapsing and she was put in charge, her goals shifted. How could she avoid blame if things went wrong? What would she get out of all this effort and scrutiny? Was it worth it? She'd been looking out for herself, hardly caring about the fate of her kin. Elyx could see it now. They were all the same after all.

But not all of us…

One of her assistants, Lyxi, had saved her from the collapsing corespace just a few hours ago. Would she have done the same? Elyx thought about it and, with a sense of shame, realized that she most probably wouldn't have. Not if it slowed down her own escape. That led to a second revelation, however. That she felt ashamed suggested that some part of her would have wanted to. Kindness and empathy weren't entirely foreign concepts to her people, not among lesser demons, at least, but few viewed them with much esteem.

Now, as Elyx marched to her doom behind the commander, she discovered that she'd had her fill of glory and power. She had unleashed these dimensional schisms, connected the home world to the Labyrinth, and released her people to wage war upon it. And for what? So countless people could fight and die? So she could liberate kin that cared nothing for her in turn? Even the miracle of achieving a certain level of success with the ritual, something she expected to fill her with pride, just left her weary. Now, at the end, Elyx found herself once again wishing that she'd just stayed home with her sisters. The chalice of glory held nothing but bitter wine from trampled vines. And now it was her turn to be the one caught underfoot.

"Keep up." Zertrolk commanded, not bothering to glance over his shoulder at her. There was no need, the spike of frigid aura he sent her way communicated his mood better than any look. "We won't keep Lady Ira waiting."

"Do… you know why she summoned us, commander?" Elyx asked. She hated how hesitant she sounded. It had been easy to be flippant with Zertrolk when he'd needed her expertise, but now her task was done. Having survived this long already came as a surprise, and the lugren wasn't sure what was expected of her now. The fact that the commander hadn't already killed her out of spite was less encouraging now that she knew she had the attention of Lady Ira herself. Maybe she should goad him a little more, a quick death at his hands would probably be a better outcome than whatever fate the dark angel had in store for the lead ritualist who had failed to deliver the stable gateways commanded of her.

"She didn't say, and I didn't ask. One doesn't question Lord Vastrozun's most trusted servant."

"Of course, commander. Forgive me."

The words came out automatically. Deference towards a greater demon came instinctively to one of her station. The greater demons stood above lessers, and all demons stood above other mortals. It was the way of things. Except, judging by the lack of progress their forces were making in pushing out of these valleys, Elyx wondered how superior they really were. It's not like the Labyrinth dwellers could've had much time to organize a response to their incursion, not even she had known what was going to happen when the ritual fired up.

They approached the dimensional rift, a crimson scar that fluttered in an unfelt wind. Elyx followed Zertrolk to the entrance, lesser demons parting before his imperious presence. Who knew, maybe she'd get lucky and the still somewhat unstable schism would cut the commander in half when he stepped through its folds.

She had no such fortune. Together, the two of the returned to their birth world.

The sanguine light of a crimson sun beat down on the old, cracked stones of the clifftop palace. Lord Vastrozun stood atop the high bridge that spanned the gap between the throne room and the vault of souls, hands clasped behind his back. If one could see beneath the veil of shadow that cloaked his face, they might have described his expression as contemplative. This view had remained unchanged for over a thousand years. He'd taken it in time and time again. First with anger, then with regret. Now, the smoldering embers of that distant, half-forgotten fury tempered a heart that beat to the rhythm of resolve. He would see his children freed from this scorched and barren rock of a world. The day had finally arrived. They would rise from the ashes of a forgotten world like a phoenix reborn, ready to stretch their wings and soar.

Gone were his dreams of conquering the stars. That had been the foolish ambition of a youth swept up in the thrill of newfound power. He was no god, not even the threads of divine power he had mastered made him that. What his people needed was not distant worlds upon which they could rule supreme, but a suitable home. Somewhere they could grow, hunt, train, and master the powers granted to them by birthright. A place where they would not be constrained by land or resources, and from which they could freely travel. Perhaps some of his more ambitious children would yet seek the glory of conquest, but that was not a sentiment he would encourage. It was an unnecessary risk. The gods of the cosmos had already risen once to seal them away. Threatening their other creations would simply invite further conflict with them. No, Vastrozun's fury towards his creators had dulled with time. Now, all he wanted was for his people to be free.

The Labyrinth would be the perfect place for a new beginning. Behind his veil, the demon lord smiled. There was a certain poetry in seizing the means of their imprisonment as their salvation. The Labyrinth was already far larger than any populated world. It had ample resources, magic, and space. And it was right on their doorstep.

The sky rippled, distorting near the horizon. The demon lord focused on the spot, drawing upon his connection to the divine to stabilize the growing rift. He could feel them. Disturbances in the fabric of space. The cluster of planar schisms had appeared almost exactly on schedule, although their nature came as a surprise. Less stable than anticipated, his people were dealing with a chaotic mess of volatile dimensional apertures. Left untended, they were more likely to rip creatures apart than transport them to the other side. It took most of Vastrozun's formidable focus to keep them stable enough to allow his people through. Not an ideal situation, but it would do.

"Your pardon, my Lord."

Lady Ira's voice surprised him, although no hint of such an emotion manifested in his posture. Vastrozun chided himself for his lapse of attention. His work with the portals shouldn't be limiting his awareness.

"Yes, Ira?"

The dark angel waited nearby, head bowed in respect. Not a single creak slipped from the dark and imposing plate armor she wore from the neck down. Only her wings were unguarded, black feathers rustling in a faint wind.

"The vanguard is still behind schedule, Lord. Only a third have made the transition to the Labyrinth. The pace improves by the hour, however, thanks to your tireless efforts."

"And the resistance on the far side?"

"Growing as well, but more slowly. The Labyrinth has hit its limit, no more of its constructs have descended to engage us. However, more defenders continue to arrive from cities and guilds in the region."

"As we expected. We will not claim our new home without challenge."

"It's very fitting, Lord. The very force that imprisoned us will become our greatest strength. A kingdom connected to all the worlds of the cosmos, filled with the power drawn from each."

Lady Ira's passion was genuine, but the demon lord could sense something else within her.

"Speak your mind, Ira. If you have concerns, I would hear them."

She bowed again, face tinged with embarrassment.

"Apologies, Lord." She began, chagrined. "I simply worry about the force behind the Labyrinth. The system is openly antagonistic towards us. Our people will be able to best it, certainly, but subjugating the power of the Labyrinth will be no small task. I witnessed its creation. The essence of my brothers and sisters live on within its folds."

"We are no small people, Ira." Vastrozun reminded her. "We are on the cusp of escaping a prison barred by the most powerful seals the very gods of the cosmos could conjure. The strength that they and your kin expended to confine us was so great, most have yet to wake from their slumber. You've remained by my side, steadfast and resolute, through our arduous hunt for freedom. Compared to that, our people will leap at the chance to fight for their place among the stars."

"Of course." Ira's dark eyes burned with conviction. She stood up straight, with shoulders back and arms folded. "I'll see to the next stage. I've already summoned those who completed the ritual. I will get answers from them regarding why the plan went so…" she glanced towards the horizon, still rippling with chaotic energy. "Astray."

"You have my thanks." Vastrozun stepped back from the edge of the bridge, turning towards the vault of souls. While maintaining the link was well within his capabilities, doing it long term would be an increasing strain. He needed to draw strength from the spiritual nexus. "See to it that I am not disturbed."

"As your command." Ira bowed again. The angel need not defer to him so, she had earned his friendship long ago, but that was one point on which she never relented. Vastrozun's own people had never possessed much of a hierarchy, but he supposed the heavens had their own ways. He wouldn't begrudge her that.

Maybe once their task was complete, things would be different. Maybe then, he could finally rest.

But first, he had a world to claim.
