Chapter 42: Interrogation

Torture was something I would later develop skill at. It wasn't something I enjoyed doing, but there was an art to it that required understanding and precision. For example, too much pain could simply kill the subject before you learned anything useful, leaving you with a corpse and no intelligence. That was a waste of time and resources.

Or the person being questioned would tell you whatever they thought you wanted to hear just to make the pain stop, feeding you information that couldn't be trusted. Fear and agony made people creative with the truth, crafting lies that sounded plausible but led you astray.

There were some who utterly lost their minds under pressure, becoming insane and therefore useless. Their brains would shut down rather than deal with what was happening, leaving you with a babbling wreck instead of useful information.

You had to know truth from lies, and you had to understand the psychology involved. I would meet a man later who would tell stories that seemed irrelevant but always had some twisted meaning that became clear when applied correctly.

The same principle applied here. Accompanied by the right kind of pressure, and the subject would sing like a canary, spilling everything they knew and probably some things they didn't even realize they knew. Fear was often more persuasive than pain, because pain had limits but imagination didn't.

Later, I would develop my own methods, simpler but effective approaches that got results without unnecessary complications. However, at that moment, I was a complete novice in this particular art. As it turned out, that didn't matter nearly as much as I had expected.

When someone had little loyalty to the organization they served, their motivation to lie and protect that organization became essentially nonexistent. Especially when they were faced with a clear threat who had easily slain all of their companions, demonstrating power that made resistance seem pointless. The elf sprawled before me had watched me kill ten of his faction-mates without breaking stride.

"I'll tell you whatever you want to know if you let me live!" The elf's voice held a distinctly nasal quality that grated against my ears even through the respirator's audio filters. His accent was strange, carrying inflections I couldn't place, marking him as definitely not human despite the superficial similarities.

"How many of you are there in that fort, exactly?" My digitized voice emerged from the respirator's speakers, mechanical and cold in the forest's unnatural silence.

"I don't know! You think I counted every single person? Shor's balls, man, there's a lot of us but nobody keeps exact tallies." His response came out in a rush, words tumbling over each other in his eagerness to cooperate.

I didn't react to the strange oath he'd used, filing it away as another data point about his origins. Different worlds, different gods, different ways of expressing frustration and fear. The multiverse was vast enough to contain countless variations on familiar themes.

There had been close to forty initiates visible during my observation, and I decided that estimate would have to serve as sufficiently accurate for planning purposes. Precise numbers would have been better, but you worked with the intelligence you could gather rather than the intelligence you wanted.

"Molech, does he have a key?" The question was crucial. Everything else was just tactical details, but confirmation that my target possessed what I needed would justify the risk of attacking his stronghold.

The elf was eager to answer this particular question, his head jerking in an enthusiastic nod that sent droplets of blood from his various wounds spattering across the forest floor.

"Yes! He does have one. Took it off some massive beast he killed in single combat. There was talk about sending people to get more, but nothing came of it."

My mechanical breathing echoed off the twisted trees as I processed this information. Molech had one key, but needed four total to complete the games, same as everyone else. With my three keys, killing him would give me the fourth I needed. That made the math simple enough.

"Have you seen him in combat? What are his abilities like?" Understanding my opponent's capabilities would be crucial for developing an approach that didn't get me killed in the first few seconds of engagement.

The elf shifted uncomfortably on the ground, his crushed knee sending obvious spikes of pain through his body every time he moved. But he was still thinking clearly enough to provide useful details.

"He's incredibly strong in Containment, shrugs off Flux abilities like they're nothing more than gentle breezes. Seen him walk through techniques that should have killed him without even slowing down. He's really skilled with his resonance blade too, moves like he's been training his whole life."

The elf paused, swallowing hard before continuing. "And he has some kind of breath ability that he used a few times. Looked like fire mixed with something else, something that ate through whatever it touched. Melted right through a Flux shield like it was made of paper."

I nodded, filing away each detail for future reference. High Containment explained why Molech had been able to maintain leadership over such a large group. If he was essentially immune to most Flux attacks, then challenging his authority would require either overwhelming numbers or someone with capabilities that exceeded normal parameters.

The breath weapon was something I'd never heard of before. Fire that could penetrate Flux defenses suggested either extremely high-level technique or some kind of racial ability specific to draxi physiology. Either way, it represented a threat I had no experience dealing with.

"Where does he stay in that fort?" Knowing his likely location would help me plan an approach that didn't involve fighting through forty initiates just to reach my actual target.

"Near the back, I think? There's a room he's claimed, keeps it locked when he's inside. Doesn't want anyone around him most of the time, doesn't trust the rest of us much."

Smart man. I wouldn't trust anyone else either in this situation, surrounded by ambitious killers who would gladly put a blade in your back if they thought they could get away with it. Leadership in a place like this required constant vigilance, because loyalty was a luxury few could afford when survival was the only currency that mattered.

"That's all I needed to know." The intelligence I'd gathered was sufficient for planning purposes. Molech was powerful but isolated, dangerous but predictable in his location. I could work with those parameters.

"So, will you let me go then?" Hope rang clearly in the elf's voice, desperate and pathetic in its intensity. He actually believed I might honor whatever implied bargain he thought we'd made.

I wouldn't, of course. I had many justifications I could use to make the decision seem rational rather than simply expedient. The fact that he could become an enemy at my back if I released him. The practical consideration that I regretted letting Kari escape, creating a potential threat I would need to watch for in the future.

The simple reality that this was a competition with only one winner, and every other participant represented both a threat to my advancement and an opportunity to gain power through their elimination. Most people would accept such reasoning as logical and necessary.

All good justifications that painted my choice in acceptable colors.

All lies I told myself to avoid acknowledging simpler truths.

I would kill him because his death would advance my power, and I wanted that advancement more than I wanted to preserve the life of someone who meant nothing to me. Simple as that.

My white blade ignited with its familiar hum, the crimson thread running through its core seeming to pulse with anticipation. The elf's eyes widened as he realized his mistake, understanding finally that cooperation hadn't bought him the mercy he'd hoped for.

"Wait, you said—" he began, but my blade was already in motion.

The resonance energy passed through his neck like he was made of morning mist, separating his head from his shoulders with surgical precision. Blood sprayed in a perfect arc, painting the alien trees in patterns that would fade with time but never be forgotten. His body toppled backward while his head rolled away into the underbrush, features locked in an expression of terminal surprise.

Flux experience rushed into me as he died, that familiar surge of energy that came with eliminating competition. The sensation was becoming addictive, each kill making me hungry for the next, each advancement opening doors to capabilities I had never imagined. It was fuel for the fire that would carry me through whatever challenges lay ahead.

I stood among the scattered corpses for a moment, mechanical breathing the only sound in the sudden quiet. Eleven more initiates eliminated, eleven more obstacles removed from my path. The mathematics of the games were slowly shifting in my favor, though the most difficult calculation still waited in that broken fort.

Soon I would face Molech directly, and one of us would claim the final key needed to access whatever prize waited in the Cathedral's depths. The draxi was powerful, experienced, and surrounded by followers who would die to protect him.

But I had advantages of my own, and I had learned to use them without hesitation or regret. I had become very good at tearing down obstacles that stood between me and my goals.

Time to put those skills to their ultimate test.
