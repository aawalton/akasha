Hayden’s expression didn’t change. “I'm around five thousand years old.”

“You're serious…”

Mayle smiled wryly. “I wish I had your confidence.”

“Stand above empires and you will.” He smiled and stared at her with interest. “What about you? What's your age?”

Mayle woke with a raging hangover. She cracked her eyes to check the time, only to shoot up when she realized she was in Hayden’s suite. She felt sick, and moving made her want to vomit, but she forced the covers up to see her condition. She was fully dressed.

“If you’re wondering if something happened, you needn’t worry,” Hayden said. “I don’t mix work and play.”

Mayle hated that response, but she didn’t know why. It had obvious connotations, but the hangover made it impossible to articulate them.

“Allow me to fix that.” Hayden approached the bed and reached for her head.

She swiped at his hand like a cat. “What are you—”

Hayden cupped her forehead, and a cool sensation spread through her brain. Her hangover eased, bringing her clarity before he walked toward the suite’s kitchen.

“Would you like some coffee?” he asked.

Mayle was so bewildered that she simply said, “Uh… sure.” He disappeared, giving her time to replay—and regret—what happened the night before.

Hayden did as he promised. He demonstrated his technology by scribbling geometric patterns on a sheet and then activating them with a cora crystal. The crystal glowed gold, and the paper became firm, floating and moving to his thought’s command. It was a spell-binding display, and it left her asking countless questions. Things became sloppy after that; she spent a lot of time rambling while lying on his bed. In retrospect, she remembered him saying, You should let me detox you, and she replied, I don’t need to be “detoxed” almost accusatorily. He put her to bed after that.

Mayle groaned. “I wish he left me hungover…”

Hayden walked in with two cups of coffee. “It’s ready.”

Mayle glanced at the clock: 4:41 AM. “Thank you, but… I should leave. My assistant’s gonna think I slept with you if I don’t get back.”

“Oh, that ship’s already sailed. He came here last night demanding that I release you.”

“And you didn’t?”

“I said I wouldn’t release an intoxicated woman to an unrequited lover.”

Mayle blushed aggressively. “You what?”

Hayden sipped his coffee in response.

“Are you insane?” she demanded.

“I’ve lived for five millennia, Ms. Haya. It’s safe to say that my code is more effective than your foolish sentimentalities.” He motioned to a chair. “Coffee? Right now your face exposes your exhausted regret. It’ll be a poor look to have when you leave this room.”

She smiled grimly and joined him in silence. After a moment of reflection, she asked, “Will your magic tricks last night apply to real ships?”

“Warships,” Hayden answered. “Guaranteed.”

Mayle nodded distantly. “Loricks and I are working on getting you an auction contact. What else do you need?”

“An expansion elixir, and a clear line-type spirit—of any kind.”

“I’ll eventually need one, yes. But I’m waiting until I finish clearing my acne.”

“Yes. At this point, it's a matter of principle.” He sipped his coffee.

“Yeah, I can understand that, but… isn’t acne genetic?”

“In my case, yes. But coran infusion breaks down diseases.”

“Usually, yes, but you can’t…” Mayle rubbed her throbbing temple and changed the subject. “You’ll need to wait a while for your spirit. This is a B-rank academy. We don’t have training spirits on site, and I doubt my supplier will want to speak to me. But I will get you one. In the meantime, I want you to go to class—without incident. Do you understand?”

Hayden drank his coffee without response.

Mayle almost snapped at him, but a comm link rang in her mind, jingling her brain like wind chimes. She checked the ID. “It’s legal,” she said with a weary laugh. “It’s… 5:01 in the morning and the legal department is already calling me.” She picked up her purse and walked to the door. “I’ll talk to you later. Do not cause problems. Oh, and I’m bumping you to second year. So report to the Teal building, not the Obsidian one. The last thing we need is you getting high-fives from students who saw the fight. Got it?”

“Wait here a moment,” the guard said. “I’ll be right back.”

Hayden nodded and waited patiently. As he waited, a few women who noticed him walked up. “Hey, aren’t you that kid with the spirit beast?” a redhead asked.

“That… is me, yes,” Hayden said. “I don’t recall seeing you there.”

“No… I wasn’t. My sister was there, actually. She said someone with…” She blushed and paused.

“Acne?” Hayden asked expressionlessly.

She pinched her fingers. “A little. I swear, it wasn’t a big deal, promise. It’s just rare. And she was like, woah, I just saw this kid who kinda looked like a dweeb, but he was actually kinda cool. And now I just saw someone that kinda looked like a dweeb and was like, I wonder if he’s actually kinda cool.”

“Well do I match expectations?”

“Depends. What do you—”

“Hey!”

Hayden turned and saw someone he had recently researched online. It was Jora Hedra—as Loricks had labeled him—the antagonist that made him throw the test. He was ultimately insignificant, so Hayden only vaguely remembered his face and nothing more.

“What are you doing here?” Jora demanded.

“Jora!” Hayden said with a bright, welcoming smile. “If it isn’t the person who threatened me into dropping out. I dreaded the possibility that your obtuse behavior got you suspended before our next meeting.”

The woman Hayden had been talking to didn’t know whether to giggle or get amped for a fight, but she and her friends were invested. Jora saw their bracelets beam with red light, and he panicked.

“Oh, you haven’t changed a day, Hayden,” Jora said, grinning and clapping Hayden’s shoulder, squeezing hard enough to be uncomfortable, and shaking him in a jolly fashion. “Let’s have lunch. I’ve missed ya.”

“Thank you,” Hayden said. Then the man walked away.

The woman flashed him a smile. “Hey, what classes do you have? Do we share any? Oh, by the way, I’m Eia. That’s spelled E.I.A. but I go by Emmi. When people scream my real name, it sounds like a kung fu kick, and it's like, super awkward.”

She blushed slightly, striking a bashful pose. He had the feeling that she had watched the videos of him, but was playing coy about it. He was also certain that she was ignorant about the power disparity between her family and the Alicibrias. He regarded her anyway.

“Hello Emmi. My name's Hayden. And it looks like I’m going to… Fundamentals of Crisis Management.”

“Really? That’s where I’m going! You wanna go together?”

Jora was fuming at his desk when Emmi Wasnia walked in holding a winged rabbit against her bosom. No, no against. She had it slightly under her chest and was subtly lifting, as if she had obtained a magical excuse to prop up her assets. She wasn’t the prettiest girl at Junolyx by any standard, but she’d be a brainless knockout at a normal B-rank university. That meant that Hayden was rocking a dream girl. Not only did that freak have the balls to come to Junolyx, but he was all smiles, making Emmi giggle as the rest of the class watched. In fact, everyone seemed to be talking about that mouse thing. It was making him crazy popular—which was the worst-case scenario for Jora.
