Nick’s time with Zero passed in a frenzy of study, instruction, and examination.

The scientist trained Nick around the clock, giving him five hours per day to sleep and attend to personal needs. Receiving the legacy the Heritage bestowed was divided into three phases, although the mentorship aspect was a lifelong commitment. The first phase involved forming a Mana Array, and that objective was separated into three distinct stages.

First, he would learn how to create permanent-mana constructs that could draw energy from his core to sustain themselves. Once he succeeded, he needed to form a construct with the requisite properties and bind it to the PDA he’d picked up in Kellen’s office. Finding the device was a lucky break. It would shave a week off this phase, since he didn’t need to build the casing from base materials and the Array could use the PDA’s sensors to enhance its capabilities.

Next came the arduous process of etching his ability pathways into the construct’s crystalized mana. The third stage involved programming the Array and connecting it to his energetic network, which would let him automate various aspects of spellcasting by feeding mana to the Array instead of slinging spells by hand.

He would learn several skills during each phase and gain a new trait when the third phase was complete. When Nick asked about abilities, Zero informed him the Heritage didn’t include any.

“The System doesn’t bestow powers that mirror its nature, but once you complete the final phase of the Heritage, you should pick up several techniques. Spells and combat arts don’t offer anything you couldn’t have obtained on your own—abilities merely automate vital-energy regulation while advancing through the lower Tiers. You’ll learn that yourself as soon as your spells hit Rank 6 and you start improving them on your own.”

The first stage of constructing the Array was the easiest. Nick had spent long hours working on his mana manipulation. While the goal was different from his free-casting sessions, there was considerable overlap in the process. Since his spells utilized force and pure mana, those were the frequencies the Array would be formed from. Before he could work on the construct, he needed to learn how to grant his mana physical form, tie it off so the weaves were stable, and connect them to his core so the magic was self-sustaining.

Zero began by demonstrating a technique to solidify mana. It was the same process used to manufacture the gems utilized in magitech devices, as well as the ability and bloodline stones Nick had come across during his adventures. The weaves weren’t particularly challenging, but condensing the mana was another story. It wasn’t simply a matter of compressing the energy. Dozens of layers needed to be interlaced without tangling or severing one another.

The first time he managed to create a mana jewel, Zero praised his progress… then reached out and shattered it like glass, explaining that while the weaves were stable, the arrangement lacked the crystalline structure the construct required.

Part of the way through, Nick received his first skill.

You have learned Construct Mana Crystals.

The Heritage’s skills were highly advanced and difficult to improve, although his title and other advantages made a big difference. Nick eventually mastered the trick of forming stable gems, but making a construct to Zero’s specifications took several more days.

He wouldn’t be ready to mount the crystal to his PDA until the pathways were carved. However, his mentor showed him how to tie the mana off, which Nick had already been learning on his own, then how to connect it to his pathways so the magic was self-sustaining, a process that proved to be utterly fascinating.

Most of Zero’s lessons demanded Nick’s full attention, but sometimes, a task was simple enough that he could partition his awareness to work on other objectives at the same time. He used these opportunities to prepare for his trials of mastery.

He was eager to take his Adept Sword test and spent some time every day performing the katas Rita had taught him using Theater of his Mind’s Eye, which had advanced to the point that his mental exercise felt completely real. Fritz pressed him hard during their Blade World sparring sessions and judged that Nick would be ready within another week or two.

Now that Nick could forge a stable construct, it was time to learn how to etch his ability pathways into the crystalized mana, which would function as a circuit and transform the construct into a computer. He would have to carve out the components for the data storage and processing aspects too, but Zero said that was relatively straightforward compared to the other steps.

The tool involved was the size of a scalpel. The scientist provided instructions so Nick could create one on his own and gave him a list of materials to look for when he made it back to Earth. The blade conducted pure mana. Infusing it was similar to imbuing Blightbane, and he figured out the trick right away. The tool’s shaft was phased a half-step out of this dimension and could be used to etch pathways inside a gem, although he needed to master carving them across a flat surface before attempting that advanced application.

He could visualize the pathways with total clarity, but the lines were spaced a fraction of a millimeter apart and a single mistake would ruin the construct and force him to start over from scratch. Even with his current attributes, Nick occasionally fucked things up. He was on the clock and these failures were frustrating, which made him wonder if there was a bloodline he could use to remedy the issue.

When he asked Zero about it, his mentor requested a list of the stones he’d received from the library. As the man read, his jaw dropped in astonishment. Apparently, Nick had undersold just how many gems he had stored in his pack.

“You have the entire inventory of a Legendary-grade bloodline library sitting in your spatial storage device? That’s absurd. There must be a thousand stones.”

“A little over 1,000,” Nick replied. “There are around 700 unique entrees, as I have two or three of some types.”

Zero chuckled and kept the list, promising to go through it over the next few days and provide recommendations on which traits would synergize best with the Heritage. There was one in particular the man was looking for. When he found it, he suggested that Nick take it right away.

“Steady Hands is a godsend for any high-precision task, which is why it’s a favorite for crafters, surgeons, artists, performers, and researchers of all stripes. It will also help in combat by ensuring your grip never wavers and your motions maintain a flawless execution—no matter how weary or wounded you are.”

Nick was sold by the end of the explanation and highly motivated to finish his Array as quickly as possible. He reached into his backpack, pulled out the stone in question, and sent his intentions into the System.

You have obtained a bloodline trait gem containing: Steady Hands.

Would you like to absorb the gem and bind the trait to your bloodline?

He gave his permission, studied the sensation of the galactic AI modifying his DNA until the process was complete, then pulled up the description of his newest trait.

Steady Hands

Your hands will remain perfectly stable under any conditions. Additionally, you will receive a significant bonus to precision when performing tasks requiring fine-motor coordination.

As Zero had promised, Steady Hands was fantastic, making tasks that had seemed borderline impossibly merely arduous by compression. Once Nick could etch flawless reproductions of each pathway into the crystalized mana, the difficult part began. He needed to layer all five pathways into a single gem, each at a different angle to maximize their surface area. They couldn’t touch except for the point where they connected to the processor, which meant he had to plan out an arrangement that incorporated each pathway using the space provided.

His Creativity came to the rescue while imagining his options. With his superhuman Mind, he had perfect recall of the pathways themselves, which the System had shown him in-depth during the visions that accompanied large-step rank ups.

When he asked Zero how this was different from creating magitech devices, his mentor explained that magitech utilized crystalized mana to reproduce various spell-like effects. The Array, however, was a computer that automated Nick’s spellcasting. It turned his abilities into automatic reflexes that activated under preset conditions, but all the energy came from him, and it couldn’t function independently.

“The tradeoff is an Array is endlessly versatile. It can behave in countless ways and switch between configurations at will. Its pathways aren’t being used to weave mana—their unique signatures trigger a sympathetic response in your channels and the programs provide targets and precise timing to the process. The Array will piggyback on your physical and energetic senses. It can’t activate in response to threats you can’t perceive. I’ll explain more of the theory when you tackle the Heritage’s second phase, so focus on mastering the basics for the time being.”

You have learned Engrave Magical Circuitry.

Whenever the duo was waiting for a process to reach culmination, Zero tutored Nick in the nuance of System mechanics. The scientist promised to provide an in-depth overview during a later visit, focusing on the aspects that would help humanity survive the ordeals to come. Nick recorded everything with his PDA in addition to committing it to memory, eager to share these fascinating findings with the research team when he made it back to Aurora.

In the end, it took him nearly two weeks to create a stable mana gem containing all five pathways in the correct orientation, including space for the arcane computer that controlled the construct. Fortunately, Zero provided blueprints of those etchings, and he was able to add them without any time-consuming failures.

Once the circuit-filled cube of crystalized mana was mounted inside the PDA’s housing, it was time to begin the final stage of this phase—programming the construct and turning it into a functional Mana Array.

By this point, Nick had undergone extensive preparations for his trials of mastery and was ready to take his tests for Acrobatics, Vital Energy Manipulation, and Sword. After eating dinner with his mentor, he stepped into the Blade World to get some final pointers from Fritz, whose wealth of life experience always provided valuable insights, even for skills he didn’t possess himself.

They ended their session and Nick bid his friend farewell, ready to tackle the tests and take his skills to the next level.
