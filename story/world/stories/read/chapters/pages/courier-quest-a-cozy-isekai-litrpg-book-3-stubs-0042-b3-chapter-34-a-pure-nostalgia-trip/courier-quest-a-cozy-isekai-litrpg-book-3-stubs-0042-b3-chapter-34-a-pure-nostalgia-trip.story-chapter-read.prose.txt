The sight of their first city in Aletander was a good one. Slater Port was currently in the middle of a rainstorm, but they could clearly see it illuminated thanks to their twin lighthouses. One was down below by the ocean, and the other was high up on a cliff. They were heading towards the latter.

Trevor, Adabelle, and Clay watched the city from their suite as they passed over it. Even in the storm, people were working hard loading and unloading vessels ranging from fishing boats to heavy duty cargo ships. This was likely just another day for the dockworkers, though one that was wetter than usual.

The Red Dream didn’t fly towards the airship dock like the others. They headed towards a building with a red and gold motif similar to the vessel’s, and Trevor figured it must have been Captain Braidley’s personal hangar. It was more than large enough to house the ship and there were hydraulics on the currently open roof to close the door once they were inside.

Trevor took Adabelle’s hand as they lowered into the building where it landed on a series of straps and harnesses meant for holding the Red Dream aloft. Everything was smooth from there, and the sound of rain slowly disappeared as the roof closed above them.

“Good morning, ladies and gentlemen!” Captain Braidley’s voice called out through the speakers as the window’s blinds started to automatically close. The sound of the engine cut off completely. “We have arrived in Slater Port to one of the Slygo Coast’s famous rainstorms. For those of you hoping for a dry experience here in town, I hope you brought an umbrella and a raincoat because this is going to go on for at least a few more days.”

“Dang, persistent storm,” Trevor said. Adabelle nodded.

“For everyone leaving, there are shuttles that will take you to Lairan, Formsof, or our beautiful capital of Grafam that come directly into the hangar to whisk our dear passengers away. If you are interested in the Aletander Grand Prix, we will be at the Nashan Airship Hangar in Grafam later today for maintenance, tune ups, and resupply. As always, thank you for flying the Red Dream and feel free to depart at your leisure.”

“This was a fun experience,” Adabelle said as the speaker turned off. “I’m glad we took an airship instead of teleporting across the ocean. I’m especially glad that we’re given time to relax instead of getting thrown about from city to city, only getting a day to each place so we can make more deliveries.”

“Me, too,” Trevor agreed as he took one last look around the suite to make sure they hadn’t missed anything. “That’s Klar, though. We knew he’d come through.”

There was a knock on the door when the courier had entered the bedroom to check the closet and dresser again, and Adabelle opened it. She smiled at who was on the other side. “Lora, it’s good to see you again.”

“Lady Adabelle, hello!” the teleporter greeted. “May I come in? There’s about to be a bunch of people in the halls.”

“Come right in.”

Trevor, satisfied that they hadn’t missed anything, left the bedroom and smiled at their friend. “Welcome to Aletander. I was wondering if we would see you or not. We weren’t sure if you had enough juice to make it this far.”

“Alone? Yes. With passengers? Definitely not,” Lora admitted with a smile as she entered the room and closed the door behind her. Clay headbutted her ankle gently, and she looked down at them. “And hello there, cutie. Did you enjoy your flight?”

The earth elemental nodded before standing up on their back feet and reaching up. Lora’s smile grew as she reached down and lifted them off of the floor. They wiggled in her arms for a moment before allowing themselves to be cradled.

“I’m glad you liked it, and the boss is going to love hearing it as well,” she said as she patted the familiar’s stone belly. “Anyway, we should be off! You three should have had plenty of time to relax and we’re in for another big next couple of days in Grafam.”

“Oh? How big?” Adabelle asked.

“I’ll explain it all to you once we have a change of venue,” Lora answered with a mischievous smile. She reached out to the newlyweds with one hand, still holding onto Clay with the other.

Trevor carefully reached out for his familiar, and they shifted so they could jump into his arms. The teleporter pouted, but accepted this regardless. He took Clay’s husk into his inventory and held the vibrating core in his hands.

“There’s nothing I need to leave behind here, right?” the courier asked.

“Nope, but the next destination is different,” Lora answered.

With nothing left to do on the Red Dream, Trevor and Adabelle took the other Summoned’s hand. She scrunched up her face in concentration as she activated her Power. The world around them disappeared in a flash.

When they landed, the skies were clear enough to see for miles. The altitude helped, too.

Lora had teleported them to a balcony that circled a glass skyscraper. They were high above a sprawling city that was surrounded by mountains on three sides, showing them a gorgeous view. Trevor looked over the railing to see the buildings around them cut up into uniform square blocks. Wracors pulled carts and other heavy loads along with some other animals he had never seen before. Most of the traffic were people walking, though something else caught his eye.

What appeared to be a trolley was driving down one of the streets, moving faster than everyone else on a clearly marked track. People got out of the way as it approached, and Trevor could make out faint whistles coming from the vehicle as it stopped. Some passengers left while others got on. The process only lasted for thirty seconds at most before it was moving again.

“Welcome to Aletander Heights,” Lora announced proudly. “This is the tallest hotel in the city, and you’ve got the highest rooms available for the best view. If you could drop all the boxes labeled C in the suite one floor lower when you get the chance, Trevor, then that’ll be another couple of quests done.”

“Yeah, I can do that soon,” he said, not taking his eyes off of the view.

While he was taking his time, Adabelle was walking around and searching through the city. She didn’t have to go all that far before she saw what she was looking for.

“There’s an amusement park, Trevor!” she called out, trying to sound dignified around Lora. It didn’t quite come out the way she had intended.

But it didn’t matter, because her husband was there in a flash. He put his hand over his eyes to block out the sun. Before he could see where she was pointing, Clay vibrated.

“Oh, sorry, little buddy,” Trevor said as he retrieved his familiar’s husk. They quickly entered it before animating the stone body to climb onto their master’s shoulders. Once there, they pat him on the head. “My bad, my bad.”

With that taken care of, he looked back out over the city. There was a spot with no buildings a couple of miles away, easily seen from their location, that had been filled with other structures. Trevor laughed as he saw the colorful, looping metal beams that made up roller coasters.

As he took it in, he saw many of the classics. It wasn’t just coasters, but slides, swings, and ships. The park was so big that an airborne gondola system had been set up. He knew the distance was far too great for the sound to reach him, but he could practically hear the screams of the riders.

“Please tell me that’s on our itinerary, Lora,” Trevor requested.

“It…” she paused dramatically. “Is, yes. Tomorrow, actually. Lemon World is open all day and all night.”

“Lemon World?” he asked with an incredulous chuckle.

“It was named after its owner,” Lora replied with a shrug. “But you’ll find that it’s not inferior to the parks in your world. In fact, I would even say that it’s better given the magical tech used to put it together.”

“Hey, g-forces are g-forces. I’m just ready to go fast.”

The teleporter smiled. “And that’s good, because you will also have access to one of the few working cars later today along with an almost forty mile long track.”

Trevor turned to look at her. “That’s a long drive. They’ve got cars here?”

“Three of the original five are still operational. Would you like to eat while we discuss things? I have food inside, and this is where you’ll be staying.”

“Lunch does sound nice, Lora,” Adabelle answered as she pulled herself away from the railing. “I’m excited to hear what’s been planned.”

With a knowing smile, the woman led them into their penthouse. It was cooler inside, and every wall was a window. There were blinds, but it didn’t look like they would matter much; they were so high up that no one would be able to see inside without an airship or something similar.

The furniture looked surprisingly modern with many of the pieces having the name “Evelyn” somewhere on it. Most of them were red, yellow, or orange with black and gray metal pieces providing structural support. The kitchen was sleek with black machines and chrome faucets. A quick glance at the bedroom showed a bed in an odd shape, made with its head high and the foot of it slightly lower.

Lora led them to the table where another charcuterie board had been placed out. It was larger than the one she had prepared for them in the Wildlands, though. There were still meats, cheese, and crackers, but there were also loaves of bread in case they wanted to make a sandwich. They wasted no time making themselves plates of whatever they felt like eating.

“The name of Aletander’s game is technology, and they play it well,” Lora said as she sat opposite of them. “Not too long ago, Sammie the Gamer was summoned into this world. She took on the Power of Innovation, which allowed her to create technological advancements similar to her old world. Like that air conditioning you feel.”

Trevor looked up to see that there were vents in the walls, and it was blasting out cold air. “That’s neat. I had noticed it was cooler in here but figured it was magic.”

“The technology here uses magic as a power source instead of electricity, but it’s been changed to work in a very similar manner,” the teleporter explained. “Sammie went on to invent quite a few things. Some of which I’ll withhold for now, but cars, theme parks, trolleys, air conditioning, and others were all because of her. She has a playground turned museum dedicated to her that you will be visiting the day after Lemon World. Klar thought you, Trevor, would be particularly excited about that.”

“Going by the name Sammie the Gamer, I’m going to guess that it’s because it’s got something to do with video games?” he asked enthusiastically.

“Games, yes,” Lora allowed. “I don’t think she ever got screens for video to work for whatever reason.”

“Maybe she had a vendetta against the Quest Master,” Adabelle offered. “It’s not everyone’s cup of tea.”

She paused, then rolled her eyes.

“I didn’t say you were unpalatable, I said you weren’t everyone’s cup of tea. Something as powerful as you really shouldn’t be complaining at random things one of your Summoned says.”

“It really likes to call Adabelle out,” Trevor explained to Lora, who looked confused. “I’m not saying the Quest Master has favorites, but it has favorites and it’s right.”

Trevor chuckled as he shook his head, clearing the text box that appeared in front of him. “Alright, so what’s our immediate future looking like, Lora?”

“I leave you two alone for a few hours to get acquainted with the suite, city, or wherever your heart desires,” she answered, excitedly shifting in her seat. “There is a concert taking place around five, if you so choose to go. It will end about an hour before sunset and then I’ll take you to the course. Since Trevor comes from a world with cars, they have agreed to allow him to drive so long as the boss funds any repairs needed afterwards.”

“Let the record show that I’ve never been the cause of an accident in my life,” the courier bragged, putting a hand down on the table. “That said, I have been rear ended before. Three times, actually. If ours is the only car on the road, then I don’t think that’s going to be a problem.”

“Shouldn’t be!” Lora confirmed with a smile. “The whole of the forty mile track is yours to do as you please. It’s well lit, and there are signs that tell you when to expect curves and when there are straightaways. Around the halfway mark there’s a scenic view where you can get out, rest, and see the sunset in the mountains. I haven’t seen it myself, but I hear it’s beautiful.”

“That sounds amazing. Is the track through the mountains, then?” Trevor asked.

“Through, around, over, yes,” she answered with a nod.

Adabelle reached out and took her husband’s hand. “And Trevor will be driving, right?”

“Right-o.”

“Then what will I be doing at this time?” she asked.

“Enjoying the ride, holding Clay, watching the scenery,” Trevor said with a small smile. “Driving is kind of a one-person job, unless you need a navigator for whatever reason. It doesn’t sound like that’s going to come up.”

“Nope. It’s a closed off circuit until you make it back to home base,” Lora added. “But you can take as long as you’d like. There’s lights out, so Trevor can even drive back in the dark with no problem.”

“Maybe we can get you in the driver’s seat before we leave,” the courier offered. “That way you can see what we’re working with.”

“I think I would like that, yes,” Adabelle replied with a nod. “It’s a shame for you to be doing all the work, honey.”

“Nah, I’m actually pretty excited. This won’t be work, but a pure nostalgia trip. Even better because I get to see how you react to cars.”

Lora nodded. “Tomorrow, you can spend as much time at Lemon World as you’d like. This was actually Lady Griffin’s idea. She figured that you, Lady Adabelle, would enjoy the thrills on the rides. Trevor, she wasn't sure about, but she felt as though he would, too.”

“Apparently, Earth is a place where people go and get scared for fun,” the noblewoman said, patting her husband’s hand.

“Sometimes, yeah,” he chuckled. That was what he had told her.

“I can confirm,” Lora replied, smiling as she rubbed the green ribbon on her hip. “You can spend your night however you want to, whether it’s still at Lemon World or anywhere else. There’s pamphlets on the table, so let me know if anything strikes your fancy. I've got some city passes for you, which will get you transportation when I'm not around. The day after that is the museum, which I still won’t tell you about, and the day after that is when we head to our next destination.”

“Well, this sounds like it’s going to be a lot of fun,” Adabelle remarked.

“Absolutely. You want to explore the city first? Or, what about the concert?” Trevor asked.

She turned to Lora. “Is it within walking distance? And what is the dress code?”

“It’s a little far away, and fancy fancy. Like, really fancy,” the teleporter said. “As in, I can teleport you from there to here so you can change, then we can go back afterwards. I’ll also be there; I bought a ticket.”

“Then all that’s left is for Trevor to drop off the goods, and we can go and explore,” Adabelle stated with a smile, gently shaking her courier’s arm with excitement. “Lora, I do believe we’ll need directions.”
