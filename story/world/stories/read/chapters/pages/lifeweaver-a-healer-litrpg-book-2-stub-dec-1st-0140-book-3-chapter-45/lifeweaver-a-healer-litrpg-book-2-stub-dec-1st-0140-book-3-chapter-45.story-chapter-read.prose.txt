"The orbs are everywhere," Curtis muttered. "I don't like this."

"What's not to like with a little mystery? Where is your sense of wonder?" Ray asked, his voice mocking but playful.

Small orbs of darkness formed around Curtis's shoulders before hurtling straight for Ray's chest. "Here's my sense of wonder!"

Laughing, Ray disappeared, and the attack hit nothing but air. "Too slow, old man!"

He appeared right behind Curtis and brushed a wet finger against the back of his ear. "Lighten up!"

Darkness expanded all around Curtis, catching Ray with ease while everyone else took a step away, all except for Luke. He saw the grin on Curtis's lips. The Reaver was just playing around. The throwing of Ray through the air to crash into an apartment building window was perhaps a little too much, but boys will be boys. Ray, now far more nimble than he'd been before becoming Integrated, landed on his feet. He was unhurt and smiling, enjoying himself.

"That's better!" Ray shouted.

Ignoring him, Curtis carried on speaking as if the last couple of seconds hadn't happened. "The Intruders. We have to catch one and make it talk."

"Can you sense one?" Hannah asked.

"Not since they beat the crap out of me," Curtis grumbled. "But I will. They're never far away."

"Let us know," Luke said. "We'll box them in and catch one."

"Like at that diner?" Hannah asked, raising an eyebrow.

"We're stronger now," Luke said.

Curtis nodded. "And more prepared."

"I am starting to feel restless. Perhaps we could go find another dungeon?" Elijah asked.

"I concur," Lemira said, stepping up beside him while arranging her hair to cover her horns a little better. She wasn't wearing the hat for some reason. "I am in need of more cores to create companions to use in battle."

"Yeah. Let's go," Luke said, pointing down the street. The next portal wasn't far off, and the one they'd just exited didn't look to be in danger of collapsing from the void within. He wondered for a moment if they'd just witnessed an entire system being taken by their adversary. Best not to think about it. There was nothing Luke could do to affect the situation, no matter how much he wanted to. Ever since meeting Guide again, he'd been itching to get out there and see more systems and to fight against the void.

He harbored a small hope that the countdown would end with Earth becoming connected to others. An end to their loneliness in just a week's time was perhaps a little soon, but he couldn't help but wish for it. While he understood the need to grind and get stronger, Luke found himself eager for the next step in their system's journey. Having seen the faint threads of The Greater System, he wanted to experience it for himself.

"Hold on," Hannah said, writing something down in a notebook she'd pulled out of nowhere.

"Bought the inventory upgrade?" Luke asked.

She didn't look up from what she was writing. "Mhm. It's useful as hell when you don't want to haul band equipment around." Having finished writing down her message in a neat but blocky hand, she put the note in front of the portal and weighed it down with a rock.

Don't Dead Open Inside.

"I don't get it," Curtis said.

Ray grinned. "It's stupid."

"Something about that message jolted a memory," Elijah said, frowning. "I'm on a couch with someone. We're watching TV. I don't know who it is, but it's someone young. Can't make out the features, but I think it's a boy."

"Maybe you were watching The Walking Dead," Ray said.

Hannah removed a second note and exchanged it for her joke one.

Do not enter the portal by order of The Band. Death by void inside. No risk of monsters exiting.

"There," she said. "Let's go."

"Your references are out of control, Hannah," Ray said. "Everyone knows that."

She gave him a strange look. "Thanks. I think."

"Guess you can't get them all," Ray said with a disappointed shrug.

"What the hell is going on?" Curtis asked.

"It's just another reference about references. From a comedy movie where the world has ended," Luke said.

"What is this 'movie'?"

Luke turned to Lemira. "You're among friends now, but I'd be careful about asking questions when other people are around. If people find out you're from another system, they might not treat you well."

"I am ignorant of Earth, Luke," Lemira said. "That does not mean I am slow-witted."

"Fair enough. Okay. Let's go get another dungeon."

"Dungeon!" Ray shouted, running ahead.

This time, Lemira walked through the portal first. Their thinking was that if the difficulty of a dungeon differed depending on the first entrant, perhaps moving things would as well, like with Luke and the nuke. Lemira wanted more pets, and she got them.

New Quest: Defeat The King of The Steppes and His Pride.

Reward: 5000 credits.

The dungeon was a savanna-like place. Arid, except for low bushes and the occasional gnarled tree. As the quest suggested, they faced a lot of monstrous animals. Many appeared similar to those on Earth, except larger and more aggressive, while others were new to them.

Ray found a rhino with two horns in addition to the regular one, and stealthed in close enough to strike, then found the hide too thick to penetrate. The whole group laughed as the rhino stampeded with Ray on its back, bouncing around and looking like he was about to vomit.

Lemira's low level meant the monsters weren't much of a threat. Not until they met the one they'd been sent to kill. The steppes sloped upward, and it made for a slow trudge heading away from the portal. In this land of beasts, there was no need for a guard to make sure nothing left the dungeon, since the monsters were far from possessing the intelligence needed, even when they crested the rise and followed the slope downward on the other side, leaving sight of the way back to Earth.

What they'd found was a crater. Having just crested the ridge, the group stared down into a vast valley below. In the middle of that valley, far away, they saw a clump of creatures. From that distance, there was no telling what they were, but anyone would have been able to recognize the layout and identify them as the pride. The boss would be among them.

"I wish to do this," Lemira said.

Luke took his eyes off their target to look at Lemira. "What do you mean?"

"Their leader will be strong. Perhaps the group following it as well. If at all possible, I wish to subdue them and make them part of my Stable."

"Does that mean we won't get any XP or loot?" Ray asked.

"Yes," Lemira confirmed.

Ray sighed. "Meh."

"Of course," Luke said.

Hannah nodded. "Sure."

With the sun beating down on them from overhead, heat was becoming a factor. While Luke kept some water in his inventory, bought from the Integrated Shop, it wasn't enough to last them for more than a couple of hours in those conditions.

"We'd better get moving," he said, squinting to lessen the glare. "Should have brought my sunglasses."

The shadows writhing in Hannah's hair stopped, then extended forward into a sort of ball cap without the hat part. Once done, she gave Luke a self-satisfied smirk and made a peace sign.

Curtis, taking a prompt from her, made himself a pair of sunglasses out of shadow. He, too, made a peace sign afterward, but his was a little more sarcastic.

"Told you your powers are the same," Ray grumbled.

"Maybe if you go into stealth, the sun won't see you?" Hannah suggested.

"Shut up."

Luke gave Lemira and Elijah a bemused smile and a 'what-are-you-going-to-do' gesture to show the bickering was nothing to be concerned about. It was just the way the trio passed the time.

"Let's go get you some lions," Luke told Lemira.

Together, all six of them set off, going down the slope as fast as they dared, since slipping on the loose soil and gravel was a real threat.

"Lions?" Lemira asked, coming up to walk beside Luke.

"A group of lions is called a pride, so the boss must be one."

"Never seen a lion."

"Large, cat-like creatures. I'm sure you'll like them."

"Oh," Lemira said, her gaze fixed on the group of monsters still far off in the distance.

"Elijah, you're a light-dude," Ray complained once they were about halfway down. "Can't you do something about the sun?"

"You think I'm able to control the sun?" Elijah asked.

Ray shrugged. "I don't know. Can you?"

"Not yet, at least. I remember meeting a Solarian once, though."

"Solarian?" Luke asked.

"Not sure if it was Ray here or the sun who loosened that memory," Elijah said in a considering tone of voice.

"And you lived through the encounter?" Luke asked.

"Obviously," Elijah said. "From what little I can recall, she was a nice enough woman."

Luke breathed a sigh of relief. Either way, Elijah was with them, which meant he was fine, of course, but the mere mention of Leslie's class set him on edge. Not the memory of the fight, or the act of killing the man. No, it was the ease with which that damn asshole took human life. To him, other Integrated were nothing but bags of experience he could use to level up. It was such a foreign thing to Luke, a person operating under those sorts of ethical and moral considerations. Almost alien, in a way.

"Perhaps we'll run into her again," he said, once he noticed Elijah regarding him.

"No. She died."

"Oh."

"We are coming closer," Lemira said, pointing to a small cluster of dead monsters. They’d been fed upon, and it didn't take a genius to figure out who the culprits were.

"Don't know if we've seen monsters killing other monsters before," Hannah said.

"Deep Dweller and Dragon," Curtis reminded her.

Hannah walked on in silence for a while, then Luke heard her whisper. "The Deep Dweller is not a monster."

That too sent a shiver down his back. The Deep Dweller was very much a monster, one that still, at least in part, lived inside Hannah. She had it under control, she'd assured him on multiple occasions, and was only drawing on its power, but Luke couldn't help but draw a parallel between Hannah and Mia, who'd said the exact same thing.

Last time he'd seen her, Mia had been consumed by whatever eldritch horror she'd conjured to fight, and she'd been stuck in a cell in ISG headquarters in Chicago. Luke did not want to see that future for Hannah. No, he wouldn't allow that to happen again.

He was about to say something to Hannah, but a low growl from within a bundle of brushes interrupted him. It was deep enough that he felt it in his chest, and it only grew louder as the group stopped and turned toward it.

"Whatever it is," Ray said. "I don't think it's happy to see us."
