Gold and diamond light refracted across the grand ceremonial hall, catching on polished marble and crystal chandeliers with dazzling precision that bordered on arrogance.

Destruction adjusted the ceremonial clasp on her shoulder, securing the deep crimson cape that cascaded down her back like fresh blood. The obsidian armor she wore absorbed light rather than reflected it—a stark contrast to the gleaming opulence surrounding her. Her presence created a shadow amidst splendor, a necessary darkness that balanced Creation’s blinding radiance.

The hall bustled with activity as servants—both the living ones gifted by Life and Creation’s golden automata—arranged the final touches for the victory celebration. Kings, queens, and dignitaries from across the realms mingled in clusters, their conversations a harmonic cacophony of politics and pleasantries. Each wore their finest regalia, mortal descendants of the gods preening before their divine ancestors.

All except her own. Destruction had no children. No legacy of flesh and blood to present at this gathering of bloodlines.

She pushed the thought aside, focusing instead on the significance of the occasion. The Culling War had ended. The Progenitors’ uprising had been crushed. Mordred, their ambitious leader, was defeated. Peace reigned once more, hard-won through sacrifice and strength—primarily her strength. It had been Destruction’s power that turned the tide when all seemed lost, her willingness to unleash catastrophic force that had saved them all.

Across the hall, Creation worked the crowd with practiced precision. She moved from group to group, her gown shifting colors with each turn, leaving a trail of dazzled admirers in her wake. Pale-gold hair cascaded in perfect ringlets down her back, framing features so perfect they bordered on painful to behold. She laughed at something a mortal king said, touching his arm with delicate fingers. The man practically melted under her attention, his crown suddenly looking too heavy for his mortal brow.

Destruction watched, noticing how Creation’s calculated radiance had changed. Gone was the earnest craftsman who had once spent days in her workshop, hair messily tied back, hands stained with creation’s raw materials. This polished, political creature was something new—born from the crucible of war, certainly, but shaped by ambition rather than necessity.

Creation caught her watching and glided over, leaving a wake of longing gazes behind her. Even the golden automata seemed to turn their mechanical heads to follow her movements.

“YOU’VE BEEN STANDING HERE BROODING FOR NEARLY AN HOUR,” Creation said, her voice melodic even in admonishment. “THE CELEBRATION IS FOR YOU TOO, SISTER.”

Destruction glanced at the assembled nobility. “I’M NOT ONE FOR CROWDS.”

“YOU’RE ONE FOR VICTORY,” Creation countered, linking her arm through Destruction’s. “AND THIS VICTORY BELONGS TO YOU MORE THAN ANYONE. YOU SAVED US ALL.”

The praise warmed something long cold within Destruction’s chest. “WE SAVED EACH OTHER. AS ALWAYS.”

Creation’s smile dimmed slightly. “NOT ALWAYS, SISTER. BUT TONIGHT, YES. TONIGHT WE CELEBRATE TOGETHER.”

Destruction watched as a minor goddess approached, clearly intent on speaking with Creation. Her sister noticed too, and before the goddess could reach them, Creation shifted her stance subtly—spine straightening, lips parting slightly, eyes widening with practiced innocence. The transformation was subtle but devastating. The approaching goddess faltered mid-step, suddenly unsure of her own divinity in Creation’s presence.

“YOU’RE DOING IT AGAIN,” Destruction muttered.

Creation’s laugh tinkled like crystal bells. “DOING WHAT, DEAR SISTER?”

“THAT THING. THAT...PERFORMANCE.” Destruction gestured vaguely with her free hand. “YOU NEVER USED TO BE LIKE THIS.”

“WAR CHANGES US ALL,” Creation replied, her smile never faltering. “SOME MORE THAN OTHERS.”

A servant approached with two goblets of shimmering liquid that changed color as it caught the light. Creation took both, handing one to Destruction with a flourish.

“TO PEACE,” Creation said, raising her glass.

Destruction raised hers in response. “TO PEACE.” The concept felt foreign on her tongue. What place did the goddess of destruction have in an era of peace?

Across the hall, Life entered with a flourish, his golden armor gleaming impossibly bright. Creation’s attention shifted immediately, her body angling toward him like a flower to the sun. Even from this distance, Destruction could see the change in her sister’s demeanor—a subtle softening, a calculated vulnerability that was somehow more manipulative than her usual display.

“I WONDER WHAT YOUR LOVER THINKS OF YOUR LITTLE PERFORMANCES,” Destruction murmured, too low for anyone but Creation to hear.

Creation’s smile didn’t waver, but her eyes hardened momentarily. “HE APPRECIATES THE NECESSITY OF APPEARANCES. AS SHOULD YOU.”

Before Destruction could respond, a gong reverberated through the hall, signaling the formal beginning of the celebration. Creation squeezed her arm once before gliding toward the raised dais at the center of the chamber. Destruction watched her go, noting how the crowds parted before her sister like water around a stone, their faces upturned in adoration. Kings who commanded armies looked at Creation with the desperate hope of courtiers seeking favor. Goddesses who crafted constellations watched her with naked envy. None were immune to her calculated radiance.

Despite her irritation, Destruction felt a pang of protectiveness. Creation was her little sister after all. They had forged worlds together, crafted realities side by side. If she had changed, perhaps it was only natural—the universe itself was changing, why should its architects remain static?

Death slipped into place beside Destruction, his presence cool and comforting as always. Despite his formal black attire, he seemed perpetually untouched by the pretense of ceremony.

“They adore her,” he observed, his voice neutral.

“OF COURSE THEY DO,” Destruction replied, watching Creation ascend to the dais. “SHE GAVE THEM LIFE. ALL I’VE EVER DONE IS THREATEN TO TAKE IT AWAY.”

Death’s hand found hers, fingers intertwining. “Without endings, beginnings have no meaning.”

She squeezed his hand gratefully, drawing strength from his presence. Death understood her as no other could—not even her sister. They were two sides of the same coin, both necessary, both feared, both ultimately alone in their purpose.

Creation raised her goblet, her voice carrying effortlessly across the massive chamber without the need for magical amplification. The room fell silent instantly, as if her very presence commanded the air to still.

“YOU HONOR US WITH YOUR PRESENCE,” she called out, her radiance commanding all attention. “TODAY WE USHER IN A NEW ERA OF PEACE AND PROSPERITY.”

The crowd erupted in thunderous applause, goblets raised in salute. Destruction felt a genuine smile form on her lips. Perhaps there was a place for her in this new world after all. Perhaps they could rebuild what had been lost between them.

“THE AGE OF MORTALS HAS TRULY BEGUN,” Creation continued when the applause subsided. “A WORLD REBUILT IN OUR IMAGE, BUT GUIDED BY YOUR HANDS.” She gestured expansively toward the gathered nobility, her smile benevolent and warm.

Destruction felt Death’s fingers tighten around hers briefly before he released her hand. “I must speak with Life,” he murmured. “Politics never rest, even in victory.”

She nodded, watching him move through the crowd toward Life, who stood resplendent in golden armor that gleamed even in the shadow of a massive column. An odd pairing, those two brothers—Life and Death—yet their balance maintained the world as surely as she and Creation maintained the cosmic cycle.

Creation angled her body slightly, her artful posture drawing every eye in the room. Her gown shimmered as she moved, each crystal catching and amplifying the light. She bent forward slightly, offering the crowd a glimpse of perfect collarbone, and Destruction fought the urge to roll her eyes at the calculated move. The assembled dignitaries leaned forward in unison, like puppets on invisible strings.

“WE STAND NOW AMIDST THE RUBBLE OF OUR FORMER GLORY,” Creation’s voice dipped lower, somber notes carrying through the hall. “SO MANY CITIES REDUCED TO ASH. SO MANY GARDENS WITHERED. SO MANY TEMPLES DESECRATED.”

Murmurs rippled through the crowd. Several dignitaries glanced sideways at Destruction, their expressions unreadable. She shifted her weight, suddenly conscious of her obsidian armor, of how much she stood out among the celebratory finery. The weight of their stares pressed against her skin like unwelcome hands.

“THE COST OF VICTORY,” Creation continued, her voice lifting, “HAS BEEN IMMEASURABLE. BUT FROM DEVASTATION COMES OPPORTUNITY.”

More eyes turned toward Destruction now, no longer glancing but openly staring. Heat rose to her cheeks, a mix of embarrassment and anger. Yes, she had unleashed cataclysmic power during the war—but only to save them all. Their civilizations would be nothing but ash and bone if she hadn’t intervened. She was their guardian, not a weapon to be feared, not a dog to be kenneled once the threat had passed.

Creation glided across the dais, every movement measured and graceful. She paused before a column of moonlight streaming through a high window, allowing it to illuminate her form perfectly. Not an accident, Destruction realized, but a performance so flawless even the moon itself had been choreographed into compliance.

“WE MUST NOW FOCUS ON REBUILDING WHAT WAS LOST,” Creation continued, her gaze sweeping across the room with practiced intimacy, as if she were addressing each person individually. “ON NURTURING NEW LIFE.” She paused, her smile dazzling as she gazed upon the crowd. “ON CREATING RATHER THAN DESTROYING.”

More faces turned toward Destruction, expressions shifting from respect to wariness. She clenched her jaw, fingers tightening around her goblet. The wine inside trembled, mirroring the anger building in her chest.

A mortal queen—once Destruction’s most ardent supporter during the war—now averted her eyes when their gazes met. A minor river god who had begged for Destruction’s intervention when the Progenitors threatened his waters now sidled away, creating distance between them. The betrayal was so subtle she almost missed it—almost—but the pattern became unmistakable as it repeated throughout the crowd.

Creation gestured elegantly toward a cluster of nobles whose lands had suffered the worst during the final battles. “I PROMISE YOU,” she said, voice dripping with sincerity so perfect it had to be practiced, “THE DEVASTATION YOU HAVE ENDURED WILL BE UNDONE. WHERE BARREN WASTELAND NOW STANDS, GARDENS WILL BLOOM ONCE MORE.”

The nobles nodded eagerly, their gratitude palpable. One—an elderly duke whose entire province had been sacrificed as a tactical necessity—looked at Creation with naked adoration. Destruction remembered the strategic meeting where that decision had been made. The duke had agreed then, understanding the necessity. Now he looked at her as if she alone had destroyed his lands on a whim, while Creation would be their savior.

Creation continued her calculated performance, moving like water across the dais, her voice melodic and soothing. She reached out to touch the cheek of a young goddess of fertility, the gesture somehow both maternal and seductive. The goddess blushed, clearly entranced.

“THIS NEW ERA,” Creation said, her tone shifting subtly, “MUST BE GUIDED BY THOSE MOST INVESTED IN ITS FLOURISHING.”

Destruction forced herself to nod in agreement, though unease coiled in her stomach. Something felt wrong, something in her sister’s cadence, in the calculated pauses between her words.

“BY THOSE WITH CHILDREN THEY WISH TO SEE THRIVE,” Creation continued, her gaze now sweeping across the assembled dignitaries with intimate warmth. “WITH CITIES FILLED WITH LOYAL SUBJECTS WHO REVERE THEIR DIVINE PATRONS.”

She paused, allowing her words to settle before adding, “BY THOSE WHO CREATE RATHER THAN THOSE WHO MERELY DESTROY.”

The room quieted, tension replacing the celebratory atmosphere. Creation slowly turned, her gaze falling directly upon Destruction. A chill ran down Destruction’s spine, instinct warning her of danger before her mind could process why.

“BY THOSE WHO BRING FORTH LIFE,” Creation said, each word falling like ice into a silent pond, “NOT THOSE WHO END IT.”

The crowd parted between them, creating a direct line from Creation to Destruction. No longer united, but divided into observer and observed. Destruction stood alone as Creation descended from the dais, moving with divine grace. The room held its collective breath.

“AFTER ALL,” Creation continued, her voice honey-sweet yet edged with steel, “WHAT USE IS A SWORD WHEN THE BATTLE IS WON? WHAT PURPOSE DOES FIRE SERVE ONCE THE FORGE HAS SHAPED ITS CREATION?”

Whispers spread through the crowd now. Nods of agreement. Destruction searched for allies among the faces but found only cold calculation or averted gazes. Even those who had fought beside her, who had witnessed her sacrifice, who had cheered her power—even they turned away, swayed by Creation’s silver tongue and golden presence.

“SISTER,” Creation said, the word dripping with false sweetness as she approached. “WHAT LEGACY HAVE YOU TO OFFER THIS NEW WORLD?”

Confusion crossed Destruction’s features. “I OFFER PROTECTION, AS I ALWAYS HAVE.” She glanced around, seeking Death among the crowd, but couldn’t locate him. The sea of faces blurred together, none meeting her gaze directly.

“PROTECTION?” Creation laughed, the sound like breaking glass. “FROM WHAT? THE PROGENITORS ARE DEFEATED. THE WAR IS OVER.” Her voice lowered to a theatrical whisper that somehow carried to every ear in the hall. “WHAT NEED HAVE WE FOR YOUR KIND OF PROTECTION IN AN ERA OF PEACE?”

The words struck Destruction like physical blows. She took an involuntary step backward, darkness gathering unconsciously around her fingers. “MY KIND? I AM YOUR SISTER, CREATION. WE ARE TWO PARTS OF THE SAME WHOLE.”

“ARE WE?” Creation asked, her perfect eyebrow arching elegantly. “YOU DESTROY. I CREATE. YOU END. I BEGIN.” She circled Destruction slowly, her gown trailing behind her like liquid starlight. “TELL ME, SISTER, IN A GARDEN OVERFLOWING WITH LIFE, WHAT PURPOSE DOES WILDFIRE SERVE?”

The crowd murmured their agreement. Destruction glanced around, seeing only hostility where moments before there had been celebration. How quickly they forgot who had saved them. How eagerly they embraced their savior’s vilification.

Creation was suddenly before her, moving with divine speed. They stood face to face, close enough that Destruction could see herself reflected in Creation’s perfect eyes—distorted, diminished.

“YOU HAVE NO PLACE AMONG THE LIVING, SISTER.”

The dagger slid between Destruction’s ribs with sickening ease, the enchanted blade burning with venomous magic specifically crafted to harm divine flesh. Pain exploded through her body, so unexpected and intense that she couldn’t even cry out. Their faces nearly touched, Creation’s breath warm against her cheek.

“EVEN DEATH HAS HIS PROGENY,” Creation whispered, twisting the blade deeper, “BUT YOU HAVE NO ONE. YOU DON’T BELONG HERE. AND IN A WORLD OF PEACE, WHAT PLACE DOES DESTRUCTION HAVE?”

Blood bubbled from Destruction’s lips as she stared into her sister’s eyes, searching for the love they once shared. “WHY?” she gasped, the single word carrying the weight of incomprehensible betrayal.

Creation’s eyes hardened. “BECAUSE YOU ARE TOO POWERFUL, SISTER. BECAUSE CHAOS LOVED YOU BEST. BECAUSE I WILL NEVER BE SAFE WHILE YOU EXIST.”

The name struck something within Destruction—Chaos—a memory trying to surface through the pain. But agony overwhelmed coherent thought as she collapsed into her sister’s arms. Creation held her, not in comfort but to control her descent, to maintain the illusion of sisterly affection even as she murdered her own blood.

And then came the most unbearable part.

As divine blood spilled across the pristine marble floor, the gathered assembly—kings and queens she had protected, dignitaries she had saved from the Progenitors’ wrath, even fellow divine beings who called her friend—began to cheer.

They celebrated her death. They praised Creation’s treachery. They welcomed the removal of the goddess whose power had terrified them even as it saved them.

Destruction’s vision darkened at the edges, shock and blood loss overtaking divine resilience. Her gaze desperately searched the crowd for Death—her lover, her balance—but he was nowhere to be seen. He had abandoned her. They all had.

Creation leaned close, her lips brushing Destruction’s ear. “I’VE ALWAYS ENVIED YOU, SISTER. ALWAYS FEARED YOU. NOW I NEED FEAR NO MORE.”

The words echoed in Destruction’s mind as darkness claimed her. In her final moments of consciousness, something new took root in her soul—something beyond pain, beyond betrayal.

Pure, undiluted hatred.

The memory dissolved, leaving Destruction strewn across the tunnel’s thorn-riddled surface. Her divine body still carried the phantom pain of the enchanted dagger, still felt the wet warmth of her own blood flowing between her fingers. But her mind was clear, clearer than it had been in millennia.

She saw it now, the truth that had eluded her for eons. Creation’s betrayal had not been born solely of envy—though that had festered between them since the beginning. It had stemmed from fear. Genuine, primal fear.

Creation had feared her. She feared what she might become, feared what she might do. Creation, who could craft worlds with a thought, had trembled before her sister’s power.

“I COULD NOT BE CONTROLLED,” Destruction realized, the words rippling through the void. “AND THAT TERRIFIED HER.”

A pulsing remnant materialized before her. Despite everything, a cold grin formed on the goddess’s face. Before her was the final piece she needed to forge Calamity anew. Unlike the previous two, this remnant glowed with crimson light that matched her own inner fire. It was larger, more volatile, containing power that dwarfed the others she had claimed.

She reached out, grasping the remnant with both hands. Power surged through her, ancient and familiar, connecting with the other remnants she had claimed. Nearly complete now. Nearly whole.

As the remnant merged with her essence, Destruction felt a distant echo across the planes of existence. It was a fragment of her sister’s power crying out in pain and fear. The sensation drew a savage smile to her lips.

“I FEEL YOU, SISTER,” she whispered into the void. “YOUR VESSEL SUFFERS. GOOD.”

The Trial had given her more than just the final remnant—it had given her clarity. Creation’s betrayal had not been an isolated act of treachery but the culmination of a fear that had always existed between them. Fear of power that could not be contained, could not be controlled, could not be diminished.

Destruction closed her fist around the Remnant’s glow. The time for vengeance approached. Let Creation tremble. Let her know what it meant to be truly afraid.

“YOU’RE ABOUT TO EXPERIENCE MUCH MORE OF IT.”

Calamity would be reforged, and the world her sister’s empire had built would burn. They would all burn.
