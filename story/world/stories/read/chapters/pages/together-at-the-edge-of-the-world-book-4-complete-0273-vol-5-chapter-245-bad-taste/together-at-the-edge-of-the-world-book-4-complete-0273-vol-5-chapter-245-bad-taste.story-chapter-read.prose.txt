SOUL COMPLEX, DAVID'S CELL

After hearing Niala's plan, they closed the door that linked them, and David found himself back in his soul-prison.

He was feeling just a little bit frustrated: at being stuck here with no way out, at not being by his wife's side to help and protect her, and at Niala herself for what she was going to attempt.

It was dangerous, full of unknowns, based on educated guesses and incomplete information, and oh so very Niala in nature.

Because, sure, why not risk yourself to try and save the thing that's been enslaving and killing hundreds of thousands, if not millions, of humanoids? He stared at the wall he'd been trying to melt, mentally willing it to explode as he funnelled his ire in its direction.

Then he sighed, letting his shoulders drop.

She DID have a point; the thing wasn't given a choice. It was enslaved from the start. She's convinced that the dialogue facet's behaviour is proof it's trying to break free.

He punched the wall without any force.

Maybe it is. She's a little ball of empathy. She's usually right with these things, but I still hate it.

His faith in her was unshakable; she believed what she'd said with all her heart. It was his faith in the Orchestrator that was absent. Or, rather, his faith in the Orchestrator and the god Jerivil to have safeguards in place to keep what Niala was planning from happening.

She thought the chances were slim; from what she'd learned, Jerivil had been a self-absorbed obstinate who didn't believe he could be wrong. His argument that having interacted with one of its creations for a few bells was shaky ground to base that assumption was shot down, gently.

He didn't really remember seeing the big soul-grinding wheels that she said were called the Narrator, only a vague feeling, but she'd said it had told her things that more or less confirmed her suspicion;

If Jerivil didn't think something was possible, the Orchestrator wouldn't be able to react to it.

He narrowed his eyes. If she was right, and to be honest, the two proofs she had were strong arguments, then maybe he could try and use that?

Was there something he could do that a literal god from tens of thousands of years ago couldn't foresee? He shrugged; time to experiment.

Couldn't be worse than the no result he'd had so far. He wouldn't admit it, but he was also a bit desperate to get out so he could be at her side, just in case.

Niala, please don't sacrifice yourself for this, and wait for me. I'll figure a way out.

CAVERN ENTRANCE

Jordo's attention was tugged toward his master. The hiccup in his readings was so minute that it could have been background noise, but...

He had learned to trust his masters to buck his beliefs. Right now, that jump in mana resonance could be anything, really. The least likely explanation was that Sir David had minutely improved his mana synchronicity, which wasn't possible, as it was already perfect.

But, were he a betting golem, that would be where he'd put his money.

ORCHESTRATOR SANCTUM

Niala came to where she expected; in front of the Orchestrator's crystalline body. A quick look around confirmed that nothing had seemed to happen. She turned her attention to it.

“Have you done anything while I wasn't paying attention?”

“I am awaiting the opportunity to instruct you.”

That probably meant a no, and since it hadn't answered directly, then it was supposed to have done something. Maybe it wasn't supposed to wait to instruct her, or he was running against a time limit, which was a little concerning.

What she intended didn't need a lot of preparation, but there was no way to tell how long the process would be. Hopefully, she wasn't on a time limit, or she at least had plenty of it left.

She took a centring breath, held it in, and let it out slowly. Then, eyes clear and focused, she took a step forward. “I want to meet you.”

“You are standing before me.” The voice said, with maybe the tiniest of perplexity.

“I'm looking at your container, your house. I want to meet you, face to face.”

A pause, and then: “You are asking for a soul merge. A soul merge is inadvisable. Risk of damage to the Orchestrator is unacceptable.”

Her ears twitched. “A soul merge to inform me is inadvisable. I want a soul merge to directly ask you to inform me. We can separate afterwards, and you inform me appropriately.”

She waited for the Orchestrator's answers. As expected, it hadn't answered right away. She was hoping that meant her proposal was just technically valid enough for it to skirt its programming.

This was her gambit; if it accepted, and they soul merged, and she didn't ask to be informed, well... She might end up stuck in whatever a soul merge was. Her one potential safety line was that she had been able to exit the soul complex on her own.

The voice interrupted her musings. “It is acceptable, as you will not be informed during the soul merge. It is, however, inefficient. I highly suggest you ask to be informed as per our current arrangement.”

That gave her pause. Why was it bringing up efficiency now, when it's been content to wait for her to ask in the first place? Was it, somehow, trying to warn her of something?

Frowning, she pushed ahead. “I have taken your advice into consideration, but I wish to proceed. How do we soul merge?”

“This very utterly inefficient course of action can be completed by physical contact between the individual and the Orchestrator's shell.” The voice said.

That sure did sound like a warning that she shouldn't do it. Her first guess was that, during the soul merge, she wouldn't be interacting with the dialogue. Since it had been brought out to talk with her, it stood to reason it wasn't the version that would “meet” her during the soul merge.

Which meant that she'd be up against a facet that fully conformed to its creator's instructions. Maybe.

The information the Narrator had given her hinted at two paths: destruction, or rescue. The second was, obviously, the preferable choice, but not if it damned her. Unlike what David might think, she wasn't in a hurry to become a martyr.

She just wanted to help those who needed it, and the enslaved creation of a dead god fit that criteria.

She debated with herself for a while, but ultimately she was confident she could find a way out even if she somehow got stuck in the soul merge. Her link with David was... well. It was a soul link. Maybe she could open it and bring David forth into the soul merge? Let him explode a bit?

Only if the worst came to pass.

For now, she steeled herself, walked forward and lifted her hand up, pressing her palm against the surprisingly cool yellow crystal.

The voice rang just as she felt herself get disembodied.

“I'm sorry.”

She didn't have time to form a reply.

???

Where there was a giant yellow crystal, now stood a maddening mess of machinery, refracting on itself, at once contained in a disc the size of a plate and yet vast beyond reason.

Her mind decided that this was a round window through which she peered at the insides of a never-ending set of engraved pipes. If Sabi's enchantments had seemed complex at the time, what she was seeing made it seem like a children's book.

With some effort, she teared her gaze away from the mesmerizing sight to look at her surroundings, and her eyes grew even wider.

Under a uniform white sky, arranged in perfect, infinite rows and columns, were people. Featureless, blank-faced, like tailoring mannequins, each one stood motionless and stared endlessly into a copy of the porthole window before her. The light from the machine's insides bathed their ghastly visages in diffuse yellow.

None of them even as much as twitched. From looks alone, they might as well have been mannequins, but somehow, she knew these were the people stuck in the soul complex.

The voice spoke. Toneless, transactional, like the first time they heard it. “That is correct. They are the content of the soul complex.”

She spun her head back toward the hole into the machine's innards. “So they're all soul-merged?”

“Incorrect. They are within the soul complex.”

She frowned. “But... wasn't I supposed to soul-merge? If this is the soul complex, why am I here, and... seeing this?”

“You are soul-merged. That is why you are seeing this, and not a cell.”

Her eyes widened. Oh. Oh. Then, all of these people, motionless, that was just a way to see that a cell was occupied? And each one of them was in a cell like David's? For thousands of years?!

“Time is irrelevant. Only the task matters. I am awaiting your request to be informed.”

Right... the request. She slapped her cheeks, getting her mind back up to speed and sloughing off her surprise. This changed nothing.

What she needed was-

A gust of wind buffeted her, ranging down the row of frozen souls, followed by the sound of an explosion. She looked toward its source and saw, in the not-so-far distance, a mushrooming cloud of blue flames, and smiled.

Apparently, David had done what David did best; he broke things.

Partly answering her question on just what he'd done, Leviathan's roar reached her ears, and she saw a truly gargantuan version of him rise over the motionless rows, and kept rising, until he towered over everything. At the top, she spotted a tiny figure, which was probably her adorably violent husband.

Which meant that Leviathan must be at least a hundred metres tall right now...

And that meant she needed a signal to catch David's attention. Feeling her way through her pathways, she touched upon Hope's Light. Somehow, even in this metaphysical space, she could still call upon her aetheric wings.

She didn't question it, and balled her mana at the focal point in her back, and Hope's Light unfurled at her beckon. With a jump and a flap, she lifted off the ground, and did her best impersonation of a glow-moth, fanning her wings wide and pulsing mana through them, releasing sparkling waves of light.

Leviathan turned her way and began advancing. She briefly wondered what was happening to the soul mannequins the aspect was plowing through, and feared that they might be damaging them somehow.

Within a few minutes, David and his mana aspect were close enough that she could tell she needn't be concerned; although they appeared solid, the mannequins didn't seem impacted at all by the sea serpent as he passed through them like they were an imaginary construct.

She flew up, and up, all the way to the top of Leviathan's head, where David was waving his arm with a smug smile on his face.

Because of course he'd be proud of having blown his way out of soul prison. She found that she couldn't help but smile back, because she was also proud of him.

Never let it be said that her David wasn't the master of breaking and exploding things, even more so than her own father.

Like a hawk, she slammed into him, wrapping him in her arms and wings and knocking the breath out of his lungs at the same time.

“OOOF! Kitten! Hi! You alright?”

She rubbed her face all over his chest, ears twitching with joy. “Hmm! Yeah! Especially now that you're here!”

He planted a kiss on her head and looked around. “And.. .where's here exactly?”

To both their surprise, it was the voice that answered, seemingly unbothered by what had just happened. “This is the soul complex.”

As David blinked and tried pinpointing where the voice was coming from, Niala pondered the implication of David's presence. “Orchestrator, is David soul-merged with you?”

“Individual David was not soul-merged.”

“I know, I'm asking if he is right now, whether you initiated it or not.”

“Individual David was not soul-merged.”

Her husband quirked a brow. “It sounds obstinate.”

She shook her head. “It's... it can't 'see' what it thinks is impossible. Apparently, whatever you did falls under that umbrella. What did you do, anyway? How did you get out?”

He met her gaze, then evaded it. “I... well. Levi and I figured that he couldn't be hurt by my mana explosions since he is my mana. So, I summoned him into the cell, then I saturated the room with mana and compressed it, the same way I do when I bring him out, then I had him hide me and... detonated the mana.”

She frowned at him. “Hide you where?”

A bead of sweat rolled down his brow. “In... his mouth.”

Niala blinked a few times, then doubled over laughing. “HA HA HA! WHAT?! How did you- Ha ha! How did you even think of that?! Ha ha ha!”

His smile flattened. “Kitten, we're in the middle of something. It's really not the time to-”

“You hid inside Leviathan's stomach! Ha ha ha! Like a little fish! The sea monster ate little fishy David! And then you tasted so bad he spit you out! HA HA HA HA!” She guffawed out.

Once again, he was reminded of his wife's peculiar brand of humour. He sighed and let her get it out of her system.

Sure, they were stuck inside the body of a dead god's artificial puppet with no clear way out, but ultimately that was still no reason to kill her fun.

He smiled wryly. I'll just get back at her later.
