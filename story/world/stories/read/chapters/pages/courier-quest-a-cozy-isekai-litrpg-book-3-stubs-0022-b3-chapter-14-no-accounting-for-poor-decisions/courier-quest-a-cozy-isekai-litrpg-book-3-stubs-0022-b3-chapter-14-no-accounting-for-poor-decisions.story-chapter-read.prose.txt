Clay stopped at the three paths going forward, tapping their foot against the ground. Not every tunnel was going to lead to their destination. There could be dead ends or traps down any of the choices presented to them. With their ability to search out through the rocks with a touch, there was no risk of the party running into anything without the familiar knowing about it first.

Adabelle had a similar ability through her Ring of Tremorsense, which had been the engagement ring that Trevor had given her, though it couldn’t be used to map out the area. It only let her know when the ground around her within a certain radius had been touched. The ghosts might have been able to fly, but they dripped water from their armor very frequently, meaning it still came in handy.

Even still, she remained vigilant as Clay did their thing, scanning the area visually so nothing could sneak up at them. Trevor did the same, although he felt out of place. His guards radiated such intense professionalism that he knew he wouldn’t be able to match their energy.

He felt Clay become ready a moment before the earth elemental turned back to them and nodded. With a wave of his arm, they gestured for Trevor and Adabelle to follow before trundling down the left path.

“I won’t force you into any adventures you don’t want to go on, but I might just borrow your familiar from time to time when I feel the urge to go dungeon diving again,” the noblewoman mused. “Arny didn’t tell us how big this place was, but I’m sure we could have been wandering around for hours trying to find our way to the end.”

“Clay’s fantastic for many reasons, and this is only one of their many talents,” Trevor praised as he followed his familiar. He could feel happiness through their bond. “I’m sure if you ask, they’ll probably go.”

The earth elemental agreed with a nod, and Adabelle smiled. “Good. Look forward to the draft sometime in the future, then.”

The tunnel led to a series of small rooms, and Clay had them stop at each one. Not because there were multiple paths forward, but because there were traps. They weren’t anything too egregious or mechanically complex. Boulders that would fall from the ceiling, pitfalls that were hidden within the mist, and blades swinging on pendulums.

All of them were defeated by the earth elemental’s mastery of stone. Under their care, none of the traps went off without being severely hindered in some way. The pendulums were stuck inside the walls, the boulders couldn’t fall because the door was jammed, and the pitfalls had pathways created over them. There was nothing stopping Clay from being an absolute menace.

“There’s enemies up ahead,” Adabelle warned, and the familiar nodded.

“How can you tell?” Trevor asked, looking at the wall of fog farther down the tunnel.

“I can hear them,” she said, one of her ears twitching. “Be prepared.”

“Roger roger,” he replied, putting on his game face.

Clay led them into another large cavern. This one was made up of several islands, but the gaps could be easily traversed with a single step. Trevor couldn’t help but notice that they could still catch someone’s foot, though. He made sure to keep that in mind.

There weren’t any ghosts in sight until they reached the middle of the room. Adabelle and Clay readied immediately as the pirates emerged from the waterways surrounding the islands. Trevor was a little slower, but he was in the middle of the formation for a reason.

“Get the mage!” one of them yelled, and the rest shouted a battle cry.

Under normal circumstances, that declaration would have made Trevor freeze. He wasn’t a mage, but he knew they were talking about him thanks to Adabelle’s bluff earlier. There was a plan in place; all he had to do was follow it.

The first pirate to attack came from the demonkin’s direction. Her tail wrapped around Trevor’s wrist as she raised her sword and prepared to defend. This enemy had a pike with a long reach, which she watched like a hawk. They lunged, trying to go over her to reach him.

With a deft maneuver that didn’t take her out of tail-range of her husband, Adabelle met the pike with her sword. It pushed it away only slightly, sending it off course enough that it wasn’t going to plunge into Trevor’s body. The ghost seemed surprised and tried to pull back, but the armor they were bound to had too much momentum. Their polearm went over the courier’s shoulder.

Before they could swat him in the side of the head, Adabelle pulled Trevor closer and raised his hand before letting it go. He kept the momentum, tagging the ghost in the side of their armor and pulling it into his inventory.

“Ah dang, not me, too,” the ghost lamented before it dispersed into a wisp of light.

“Good work, Trevor,” Adabelle complimented, flashing him a quick smile before returning her eyes to the enemy. “I’ll leave the rest up to your discretion.”

“Yes, ma’am,” he replied before his familiar’s bond pulsed in his mind.

The courier turned around to see that Clay had blocked two of the pirate ghosts, one with each shield. Unlike last time, though, they didn’t just let the incorporeal weapons bounce off of their salt-encrusted arms. Instead, it was sucking them in, capturing the pair. The earth elemental turned and offered them to their master.

“Wait, hold on!” one shouted.

“I’m too pretty to be utterly destroyed!” the other exclaimed.

“At least you’ll come back?” Trevor offered.

“Oh, yeah, that’s true.”

With an expression that wasn’t sure if it was meant to be amused or concerned, the courier reached out and slapped the two armors one after the other. They disappeared into his inventory, and both of the ghosts lamented their loss.

“At least I’m still pretty!”

“Get real, you’re just as ugly as the rest of us!”

“Nooooo!”

“I’m really confused by this dungeon, Adabelle,” Trevor admitted as Clay grew to twice their previous size so they could go after the ghosts that were circling above.

The noblewoman grabbed her husband with both hands, spinning him around before dipping him like they were dancing. A sword went over his head, and he reached up to tap another ghost’s armor. Then, he frowned. If she was holding him, then where was her sword?

Trevor received an answer very quickly as the steel flashed behind her. It was being used by her tail. Adabelle watched the enemy out of the corner of her vision, a smug aura radiating from her, and defended them both completely hands free.

“That is one of the coolest and sexiest things I have ever seen,” he said, too impressed not to say anything.

“Thanks. Also, we were warned this place was weird, so just take it in stride. Okay, honey?” she requested before kissing him on his forehead.

Adabelle pulled him back to his feet and spun him behind her. Her sword knocked the pirate’s out of the way in time for Trevor to reach their armor. It disappeared like all the others. The ghost, however, fell to its knees.

“Me, defeated by the power of love? Nooo! Impossible!” it screamed, making it very clear that they were hamming it up. “Remember me in death, my comrades in arms, and never… forget… my sacrifice!”

They acted out their death scene, taking several seconds longer than all the others, before disappearing into a wisp of light. Trevor blinked as he watched as even that seemed to blink out dramatically.

“No, Juliandros!” one of the other ghosts cried.

Trevor’s head snapped towards them. “Did you just say Julie Andrews?” he asked in disbelief.

“No, Juliandros!” they said again, this time in a more threatening manner.

“Okay, sorry. I really had to make sure on that one.”

“Oh, no need to apologize. You’re not the first person to be confused. Earthling?” the ghost asked.

Trevor reached over and smacked the armor of another pirate that Clay had caught. The earth elemental had gone from defending to attacking, one hand returning to the long-fingered form in an attempt to grapple the enemies flying above. Any that got close were at risk of being snatched and a whole group of them seemed unwilling to meet that challenge.

“Hey, yeah!” the courier answered. “Do you see a lot of people from Earth?”

“Yeah, one of the usual guys is from there, and so’s the boss!” the ghost exclaimed.

“Wait, the Dread Pirate Tanazi was summoned from Earth?” Trevor asked, arching an eyebrow. “So this really is a dungeon of references?”

“None that I understand, but I’m sure there’s some. Hey, I’m going to come down there to chat, okay?”

“Are you just saying that so I’ll be within stabbing range?”

“What? No! I just want to talk,” the ghost said unconvincingly.

“It sounds like you want to stab me.”

“Okay, but only a little bit!”

“That doesn’t make it better.”

“Tiny little wound, then? You’ll barely feel it.”

“No!”

“Honey,” Adabelle said sweetly, though there was effort in her voice.

Trevor turned to see her fighting off four ghosts at once. Her blade flashed back and forth, keeping them in front of her with what would have been deadly accuracy if it weren’t for the fact that the ghosts weren’t alive.

“Oh, right, sorry! Send them my way,” he replied, grimacing at the fact that he had let her go on while he was distracted.

Parrying a blow from one of the outside pirates that put them off balance, Adabelle ducked under the swords of the others. Her foot raised up to kick the armor towards Trevor. He found out from his earlier observation that they were physically weak, and it couldn’t get out of his grip before he pulled in their armor.

“Worst fight ever. Of all time.”

Trevor offered a commiserating frown. “Sorry, mate.”

“No, it’s fine, I’ll just reconsti—” The ghost didn’t get to finish his sentence.

“You know, I think I’m getting the hang of this,” Trevor said as another pirate was sent his way. This one, too, was quickly lamenting the loss of their armor. “It helps that these guys are kind of goofy.”

“Hey! We resent that,” the ghost from before complained.

“No, I mean it in a good way. You guys are pretty fun,” he quickly explained.

“That makes it better, then. Still just want to give you a real little stab, though. Like a pinprick. Nothing you won’t limp away from. I mean, walk away from.”

“Alright, now this is starting to get a little weird in the bad way,” Trevor said as he took out Adabelle’s last two pirates. More took their place almost immediately, but they weren’t his immediate concern. “Clay, could you please grab that one in particular?”

The earth elemental, which had been content with providing cover for Trevor and Adabelle, suddenly lashed out with their claw. Their arm become longer, closing the distance in a heartbeat before their fingers wrapped around the talkative ghost’s armor. It retracted like a fishing line, pulling them closer before pinning them to the ground.

“Sorry, you’re just kind of freaking me out,” Trevor said before touching the armor, sending it into his inventory.

“Avenge me, my friends!” the ghost called out.

“Nah, they’re kind of whooping us,” another replied. “I think we’ll sit this one out.”

“That’s fair but it doesn’t mean I have to like it.” Clay’s captured spirit wiggled as it disappeared.

Trevor looked around for the next pirate he would have to tag only to find that they were all out of reach. The ones that came to attack Adabelle had already retreated, and the ten remaining enemies hovered around the outskirts of the islands.

“Yeah, I think we’re done,” another of the spirits said. “It was fun, though. Feel free to go on to the captain.”

“You’re not going to attack our backs once we leave?” Adabelle asked suspiciously, once again checking on Trevor out of the corner of her eye to make sure he was okay.

“Would it do any good with you and that lighthouse golem protecting your mage?” they asked in return.

The demonkin scoffed. “Probably not, but people have bad ideas every day. Attacking us from behind would be one of them, but there’s no accounting for poor decisions.”

A malevolent wind passed through the cavern, and Trevor once again became aware of the ominous presence at the end of the dungeon. “Quit your blabbing and let them pass already,” the Dread Pirate Tanazi growled. “If you lot can’t handle it, then I’ll do it myself.”

“Now you’re in trouble! Got the boss all riled up!” one of the spirits exclaimed, clearly excited.

“He’s going to chew you up and spit you out!”

“There will be noooo survivors!”

“Alright, that’s definitely a reference,” Trevor said, pointing at the last ghost to speak. They shrugged in response, and he shook his head before looking at Adabelle. “You’re our fearless leader, are we fighting here or going further?”

The noblewoman stood up a little straighter as she surveyed the area. Clay had returned to their normal size and took up position on the other side of Trevor as if to form a perimeter with her. They were vigilant, too.

“We’ll go further,” Adabelle decided. “I have taken the measure of this lot, and I have found them lacking. Should they attack our back, they will meet a swift fate at the end of my blade.”

“Hey, that’s kind of hurtful actually,” a ghost said.

She opened her mouth to respond, then closed it to frown. Adabelle contemplated her next words carefully. “I’m sorry I’ve made you feel that way?” she asked more than said.

“Eh, it’s on us. We should be better.”

“You are certainly taking this well,” she replied.

“It’s pretty fun, honestly. And now we get to see the captain go all out. That’s always worth sticking around to see.”

“Right…” Adabelle trailed off, watching the ghosts as they hovered around them, and shrugged. “Alright. Clay, please lead the way. Let us finally meet this Dread Pirate Tanazi.”
