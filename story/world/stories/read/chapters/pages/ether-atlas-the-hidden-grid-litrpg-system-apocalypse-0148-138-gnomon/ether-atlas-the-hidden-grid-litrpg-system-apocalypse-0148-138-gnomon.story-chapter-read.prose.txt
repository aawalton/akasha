Aegina. The temple of Aphaia.

THE DELAY

I have been reading this Grid as an initiation, because an initiation is what I was trained to read and study. Every mystery cult in the records makes you walk the stations in a particular order. Eleusis did it. Mithras did it in seven grades. The chosen order is never arbitrary and the scholarship on the why of it is a library: purification, revelation, the graded disclosure of something that would break an unprepared man.

So when Archie stood on the Acropolis and told us that a node of that kind opens in its turn and only in its turn, I heard an examination. I heard: you are not yet strong enough for the door.

I no longer think it is an examination of a trait, but an examination of the candidate's route.

A sequence lock is not insurmountable. It is trivially beaten by anybody with the patience and, in this particular case, the strength to do the steps in order. We did them, and we are four amateurs with a borrowed boat and a torn map. It will never stop anybody determined. It defeats exactly one thing and it defeats it absolutely, and the thing is speed. Whoever built this could have made Aegina or the Parthenon impossible. They made it late instead.

That is not a device for keeping the weak out, since that particular filtering can happen at any stage. It could have happened in any of the eleven nodes we have visited. It is a device for ensuring that whoever gets in has first spent months on the roads getting there.

And a journey always leaves a trail. A decision we made, the people we met, the roads we chose, the fights we fought and the ones we avoided or declined.

Somebody stood at the beginning of the trail, looked down the length of it, and was less frightened of the people arriving late than of the people arriving early.
