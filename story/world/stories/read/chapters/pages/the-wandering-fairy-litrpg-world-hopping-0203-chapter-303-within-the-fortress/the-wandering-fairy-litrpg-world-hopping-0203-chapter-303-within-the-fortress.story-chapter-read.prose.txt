A bone chilling silence wrapped in shadowed silks.

That was what the surrounding world transformed into. And yet… He felt a strange kinship with its isolating presence.

After all, this was not the first time He had to endure its suffocating touch. It was present across every chapter of His life, and whenever He fell, it was always there to embrace Him first.

This time, it was no different.

Indeed, these primordial shadows were endless. As if His very soul had been born without light. Without any hope that could wash them away… At least, that was what He had assumed up until that man appeared.

Arthur’s existence was in itself a beacon, a presence He desperately wished to cling to.

But thorns are always meant to drive others away…

“Mutilation…

“Deprivation…

“Isolation…”

These sudden echoes caused ripples to form across the ancient abyss clouding His presence. They were indeed familiar too, but unlike Lord Arthur’s light, they brought awareness to His mind through the wounds His thorns would inflict. Painful memories strong enough to ward away even the numbingness of His reasoning.

It would repeat, again and again and again—a cold yet enchanting whisper… A voice that seemed to resemble His very own.

“Mutiliation… Deprivation… Isolation…”

Indeed, what else defined His existence aside from these simple words?

But then, there was a sudden pause in the repeated cycle, an interruption in the monotony of it all. It was so brief, Mordred would have likely missed it if not for His inordinate sensitivity within this isolated void…

Footsteps?

Nay, they too seemed to resemble ripples.

Ones that didn’t belong to Him at all.

Moreover, they seemed to arrive in pairs, mimicking the walking rhythm of two individuals He could only partially recall… But there was no longer any time to think amidst the silence—a blaring interruption broke through the isolation:

“Isn’t it a bit too dark in here?”

A harsh, yet soothing voice… suddenly clashed against the stillness. There was a tinge of arrogance masked beneath its calming tone.

“If not for my mystic eyes, we would have roamed this abyss for an eternity…”

In an instant, the darkness receded slightly like curtains, unveiling a new scene. A grand, bluish glowing lake crashed onto the shadowed shores before their very eyes—ripples forming across its surface.

Sitting beside the isolated water bank was a middle-aged man with curly black hair and a beard that matched the surrounding darkness. His grayish green eyes were watching the stillness of the lake coldly like a statue. It felt as if He couldn’t see any of its mesmerizing beauty. Nor could He feel the soft waves crashing against the abyssal shore…

This was the Black Thorned Knight’s true form.

The one and only Mordred.

Beneath all the cold shrieking steel and shadows… was nothing more than a jaded middle-aged man. Though, the evidence of His terrifying legends still bore marks across His ashen body.

Taking a deep breath, the Traveler approached the isolated scene without a glimpse of hesitation. Behind Him, the figure of a ghostly girl followed closely—her thoughts stirring with chaos. They stood behind His towering figure, taking note of all the strange scars visible beneath His linen clothes.

For a while, it remained that way—silent and serene as the surrounding shadows—until the final ripple in the lake slowly vanished. The stillness itself had now transformed into a blaring signal.

“Why have you come to disturb me?” Mordred spoke rather solemnly, not willing to even face the strange visitors directly.

Soren chuckled while crossing his arms.

“Surely you already know that by now.” He shook his head, “You may be deprived of your senses, but these shadows still feed you their memories from time to time. A Living Shadow, after all, can only exist if their corresponding Fragmented Inhabitant remains alive.”

The Traveler’s [Eyes of the Fairy] unveiled even this largely hidden truth. Indeed, from the very beginning, Soren and Ynixia had only been fighting Mordred’s Living Shadow, not His true form.

Mordred’s Fragmented Inhabitant half had been swallowed by His Living Shadow all along, just as it had now swallowed them! Soren had known this fact all along.

It was for this reason alone that the Traveler dared to initiate the conflict in the first place. The true Black Thorned Knight from before Avalon’s cataclysm would not have struggled at all if they had been forced to face Him. Not to mention, HIs status page was missing a lot of what made this ancient Monarch formidable—even His legendary Soul Manual was missing most of its signature Soul Techniques.

Hearing this, Mordred only remained still with the surrounding darkness. There was a brief moment of silence before the pair of magi heard Him sigh.

“That performance was highly disappointing.” He said rather casually. “But I suppose it is to be expected.”

Upon hearing this, Soren felt a tinge of relief. If Mordred hadn’t recalled their conflict against His Living Shadow, their plans would likely end in complete failure… His gamble had once again paid off.

Smiling slightly, the Traveler nodded towards Him. “I would have never challenged you if I didn’t expect it. Living Shadows, as intelligent as they may seem, always exhibit behaviors based on their Fragmented self’s instinctual desires. That is an observation I’ve deduced rather easily after months of traveling through Carcosa.

“Your love for war resulted in its demise. But I suppose… it may result in your salvation as well.”

The silence once again took over. Though, Soren never intended to push for an immediate answer anyway. This time, it was Ynixia who volunteered to speak.

She hesitated for a moment then took a step forward. “Uncle Mor—”

“How have you been, child?”

The words shattered every bit of her facade—the memories were once again returning to her… She wanted to speak, but there were too many things to say to Him at that moment. Her lips remained frozen as her mind danced around the turmoil brewing within her heart.

Eventually, she decided to settle for something simple. “I’m… I’m doing fine. I think.”

“Your battle prowess surprised me greatly. You’ve come a long way from being the sheltered princess of the Temple you used to be.”

“I… I still don’t remember much from that time period.”

The middle aged man suddenly chuckled, ripples forming across the ancient serene lake with every reverberation.

“I don’t remember much either, you know?” He sighed, “Perhaps it's a good thing. That bastard Lancelot used to complain a lot about what happened to you after the Brimstone Baptism. You may have lost your memories, but I sense that your original identity itself has largely been restored…”

There was another moment of silence before He spoke again:

“That long and arduous chapter… Arthur chose to end it in the most unexpected of ways, but I suppose that is simply fate’s design. While we have largely been forgotten by history, the same cannot be said about you. It seems your importance to the Universal Root is still largely misunderstood.”

The Universal Root… Soren for some reason felt as if he had heard that term somewhere else before…

“Uncle… No.” She shook her head, “Monarch of Forsaken Thorns, please tell me at least this. Why… why did Lord Arthur—”

“I don’t know.” Mordred answered back promptly. In that moment, both Soren and Ynixia felt as if they had been submerged under an even darker shadow.

He doesn’t know either? The realization weighed heavy on Soren for a moment. It was hard to believe that even members of the Round Table couldn’t fathom Arthur’s reasoning for starting the cataclysm.

“Perhaps it was madness.” He theorized, “Perhaps it truly was just the whispers of that bastard Merlin… Or, it could have been the fear of confronting the inevitable Convergence…

“What matters in the end is this: No reign lasts forever. Not even one ordained by the Gods.”

The Shepherd could only fall silent, lowering her head to hide her grief.

Sensing the awkwardness, Soren decided to forward another question. “Who exactly is Pendragon? Is He one of the Twelve Knights of the Round Table as well?”

“He’s both a member and isn’t,” Mordred answered rather punctually. “As for the specifics, even I'm not aware of them. There was a famed myth regarding him as well… If only I could remember it…

“Perhaps only Merlin, Lord Arthur Himself, and my Godmother, Morgan, are fully knowledgeable on this subject.”

The Lady of the Lake… Soren’s eyes widened slightly. “Morgan the Fairy… is your godmother?”

“Indeed She is. I was under Her tutelage. The same could be said about Lord Arthur and Merlin as well. Though… Their relationship with Her deteriorated rather quickly after we arrived in the promised land. She was the one who guided us there to begin with.”

It was finally beginning to make sense to Soren why She had such a high level of control over Mordred’s Living Shadow. No, perhaps that control might have been always there, even before the cataclysm. Their relationship was certainly very intertwined.

But more importantly, it was Mordred’s last sentence that shook Soren greatly. “You’re being serious aren’t you?” Soren’s eyes widened as he stared directly at the jaded old warrior, hoping for Him to clarify, but it was obvious from His expression that He truly meant it.

Morgan… A character he had only recently been introduced to had such an important role in the creation of Avalon as he knew of it… It was She who was responsible for originally bringing the Knights of the Round Table into Yarian! This was a revelation he would have never been able to grasp alone!

The prevalent theory was that the mythological beings from Earth’s past had been whisked away to that realm unknowingly after the Fracturing. Merlin did say, after all, that the cause of the Fracturing couldn’t really be understood—the Age of Mythologies was born from that chaotic era of struggle between the differing mythological beings that had been sent there randomly.

It was for this reason that knowledge about these beings was completely erased on Earth—their very concepts had been pulled away with Yarian’s creation alongside every other piece of mystical knowledge. Earth was forced into starting once again from scratch—the Age of Regression, as they would later call it.

King Arthur and His cohort, however, were different.

Their myth remained present in Soren’s original world, which had to imply that their arrival in Yarian was after the Age of Regression had already begun. Similarly, this logic would also be extended to Astrea too.

For the longest time, Soren had wondered about how they arrived in the ‘promised land’ if their arrival did not correlate with the original beginning of the Age of Mythologies. But this new revelation from Mordred not only answered that question, it also presented him with several new ones to ask as well!

“I owed a colossal debt to Her,” He said rather punctually, interrupting his thoughts. “Perhaps I would have never been able to rise as high as I have without Lady Morgan’s guidance. It is for that reason that I made an Accord to the heavens, vowing to repay the Lady of the Lake for everything She had taught me.

“Of course, I never expected this vow to carry on even beyond that cursed Cataclysm. It seems my Living Shadow couldn’t resist its instincts even when it came to this...”
