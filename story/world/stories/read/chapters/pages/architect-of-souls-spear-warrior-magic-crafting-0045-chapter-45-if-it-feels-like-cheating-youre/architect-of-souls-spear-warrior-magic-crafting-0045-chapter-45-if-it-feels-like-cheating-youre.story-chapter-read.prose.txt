“Come, come,” Uncle Winton gestured Ferris to sit across from him, next to the fireplace. “We have a bit of privacy in here, finally.”

Ferris looked around, examining the spiritual space. Was his uncle also meditating next to him, touching the same gate device?

“Uncle,” Ferris tentatively said, looking around, “is it safe for us both to meditate in the tea house?”

“Don’t go looking down on me, my boy.” The man waved his hand. “Your uncle has reached the second tier, after all. I can enter a space like this while staying conscious.”

Mollified, Ferris walked over to the seat. His uncle made no comment on Ferris’ mental body having two legs, which he was grateful for. There was likely some problem with seeing oneself differently than ‘who you truly were,’ or some other nonsense. Ferris wasn’t interested in it.

Jade immediately began mapping out the room. Ferris reminded the slime not to eat anything, but otherwise left them to it.

“Now,” Uncle Winton said seriously, lifting a finger, “remember, nothing said aloud is safe. Even here, communicating spiritually with each other, there is a risk.”

That surprised Ferris, although after a moment of consideration, he felt he understood. There were surely abilities that allowed people to spy on each other. Uncle Winton could enter the spiritual device without meditating. Why couldn’t someone else spy into one? Although if they could look into a spiritual device…

“Can someone spy on my soul?” Ferris asked, with a bit of a chill. Even the idea felt violating.

The man frowned, but shook his head.

“All things are possible, but it would take someone much higher tier to do so without your notice. A spiritual device like this is much different than your soul. If they could… at that point, your life is in their hands anyway. Rather than spying, spiritual attacks are much more common. Make sure to prepare yourself for those.” The man cleared his throat, as if to clear out bad thoughts. “But enough of that, I want to hear how you have been! While this place is not guaranteed to be private, it is good enough that I can give you advice.”

Ferris organized his thoughts. Uncle Winton was his mentor, and he trusted the man with his life. Yet, the first piece of instruction he’d told Ferris was: cultivation was a personal journey to self-enlightenment. No one could give him the answers, and to truly keep a secret he would need to never tell anyone.

Sometimes Ferris was frustrated that the journal on Janice Landsworn didn’t contain more details, but it was also a stark reminder to keep secrets secret. Hollowfield had somehow gathered much more information on Janice and Gumly than Ferris would want anyone to have on him.

Thankfully, Ferris was early in his journey, and there wasn’t much he knew that was worth hiding. Ferris would need to avoid talking about Clan Hollowfield secrets, but he could seek advice from his uncle.

“Elder Harper provided me with a set of options, but I decided that a slime familiar was the most suitable for me. Slimes tend to adapt to almost any situation, and their versatility resonated with my desires.” Ferris didn’t directly mention Adaptability by name, but it was clear Uncle Winton followed at least the general idea.

“Yes,” the man nodded, consideringly, “I have fought many types of slimes before. They seem to thrive, whatever the nature of the rift. And of course you appreciate versatility,” the man lifted a hand, and a magical stream of water shot out of his palm, winding its way through the air like a snake, “you were raised a Bluereef, versatility is the core of our water magic, and some might say the very nature of water itself. We limit ourselves to the spear, but pair that with a flexible support.”

Ferris watched as the stream of water flowed through midair, circling the room. Soon they were surrounded by a tangled weave of flowing streams; the water crystal clear, sparkling in the light. The leading edge of the spout darted around the room several more times, but then stopped in front of the bookshelf. It hovered for a moment, but then somehow formed a hand and picked up one of the books. It carried the book over to where Uncle Winton was sitting, then placed the volume in the man’s other hand.

“Show off,” Ferris said, not impressed. He’d grown up seeing delvers control water, with much more grand displays.

His uncle simply smiled, and the water all retracted back into his palm—although somehow even faster this time. The man opened the book, casually flipped through a couple of pages, and then showed him the contents.

Ferris leaned forward, reading the words aloud.

“Water-burn slime. They have a dark, shiny glint. Anything that comes in contact with the slime’s body will be covered in a sticky, oily substance. Upon touching water, the oil will violently burn. More water only makes the fire burn more intensely. The substance can be scrubbed off with dirt or sand, but nothing wet. If it does start burning, suffocation works best to put it out. They tend to live on the ceiling above water. If knocked into the water, they will explode, spreading burning oil everywhere.”

Next to the description of the monster was a page full of hand-drawn images. A slime was depicted exploding in a lake, with a few more hiding among stalactites above. After Ferris had finished examining the page, he looked up at his uncle.

“This is…” Ferris began.

“A journal in which I record a few of the more common monsters I have encountered. Perhaps something here will inspire you.”

The man turned to a new page, and Ferris eagerly read it aloud.

“Snakehead slime. Annoying creatures, they hide deep in the recesses of craggy rocks. They extend a single long tendril out to try to grab unwary delvers. The slime’s core is hidden deep in the rock, protected from harm. Cutting off the tendril completely will buy time before it reforms, but the only way to kill it is to get to the core. Usually best to ignore.”

The pictures were equally detailed, showing mushroom-shaped creatures growing out and covering every side of a cave. Remembering the tight crawlspaces Ferris had to travel through on the rift to Scalefort, he was glad they hadn’t contained anything like this.

Turning back to his uncle, Ferris pointed at the page. “Most bonded familiars have a heart, brain, arteries, and other weaknesses. Slimes, however, can only really die if the core is destroyed. I want an ability like this, where Jade can attack while the core is protected.”

“Yes,” Uncle Winton said, “just don’t forget that you have those weaknesses as well. If the monster can’t hurt Jade, it will come for you. There are also abilities that can harm slime bodies: freezing, shocking, burning, spiritual damage, and more. I don’t list them here because I don’t typically use those sorts of attacks. You will need counters prepared.”

The man turned to another new page for Ferris to read.

“Whale-trap slime. An ambush monster that lives as a symbiotic parasite on top of massive putrid whales. The whale’s skin is a mutilated mess, providing ideal hiding spots for the slimes. They wait until a delver pierces the whale’s tough hide with a spear, and then entangle them before they can get away.”

Most of the page was taken up by drawing part of a large whale. Ferris had heard of putrid whales before, as they were a common monster hunted by Bluereef delvers. Killing one took a long time, but the rewards were worth it. Bluereef delvers’ typical hit-and-run tactics were good at countering the strong and slow beast. The drawing had an arrow pointing to a slime hanging onto the whale’s skin, although it was hard to tell it apart from the rest of the whale.

“Jade and I have used a similar tactic, where I take a hit and Jade counters.”

Uncle Winton nodded, but didn’t comment this time. Ferris knew the potential weaknesses well enough: if the hit was too strong to take, the attacker got away too fast, or simply killed Jade instead.

“There are more I have encountered,” his uncle shrugged and closed the book, “but these I have seen often enough to record and prepare against. Slimes have many variations, and even these examples are only archetypes, with individual monsters having unique abilities. One advantage of them being so widespread: I expect you had no trouble finding one to bond?”

“No,” Ferris replied, “Hollowfield didn’t even charge me for acquiring Jade.”

“Excellent. We expected to have to pay them to forge a bond weapon, but now we can use those resources for something else.”

“But…” Ferris was a bit lost for words, “shouldn’t that be returned to the clan?”

“Nonsense,” his uncle said, “it was granted to you, which is supporting the clan. It isn’t your fault everyone else had to pay to forge a bond weapon. We will just have to find the best way to use the resources.”

Ferris didn’t know what to say, feeling overwhelmed. He’d been agonizing over the price of everything at Hollowfield.

Bonded weapons were both magically forged and enchanted, requiring crafters skilled in each profession. Take Brix for example: she and her father had a design, but needed cultivators to both forge and enchant her cauldron. She’d claimed she signed her retainer contract—in part—to gain access to the clan’s crafters, as she couldn’t afford them alone. Bondable weapons could be purchased with merit, but the cheapest prices Ferris had seen had started around 48 points, or four months’ worth of refining spirit gems three times a week. That was for the most basic bondable weapon, and they swiftly got more expensive from there. A custom order like Brix’s was likely exorbitantly costly.

“Don’t lose your head over it, boy,” the man sent another stream of water out, which returned the monster journal to the bookshelf. “Now, I have my guesses why you chose a slime familiar, no need to say any more. Tell me, how have you been getting on since?”

It took Ferris a moment to collect himself and register the question. When he did, the answer just depressed him.

“Honestly, I feel like I’m barely hanging on a cliff. New demons keep appearing in my soul, causing problems. They steal things, both from around my Heartfire and qi from my Air Passage.”

Uncle Winton was silent for a moment, until it was clear Ferris was done.

“We push new Adepts outside of their comfort zone. The adversity is good for them. Nothing teaches caution in the rifts like a few deaths in the soul,” the man said, slowly. “The trick is, you have to bend without breaking. Heart demons cause chaos, block your cultivation, and can even cause qi deviation. Yet… a cultivator with no challenges will stagnate. The point is to overcome the demons, using them to forge yourself. Turn that adversity into your strength.”

“There was one demon,” Ferris said, “smarter than the rest. I swear, it ordered the others around. It was trying to break my oath to Hollowfield, and I have no clue what would have happened to me if it had succeeded. Do you know what it was?” Ferris asked, thinking of the tall demon. Everything had been so hard, and he had been alone, and he missed home.

“Everyone is different, I’m afraid the only one that can understand your demons, is you. Breaking the oath… that would be bad indeed.”

Uncle Winton stood, then walked over to the bookshelf.

“For me,” he said, placing a hand on the spine of a book, “I keep journals. I record when new demons appear, and seek to understand why.” The man was silent for a moment, and then turned back to Ferris. “A new demon appeared in my heart the week after you lost your leg. I was too slow; the monsters were among the trainees before anyone noticed. You and the others paid the price for my negligence.”

Ferris could only stare back, wide-eyed. He remembered his uncle arriving to fight the grifwolves that day, but he never thought it was anyone’s fault. Monster surges were a risk everyone knew about, and there wasn’t enough room in the bastion to never go near the barrier—especially when training. It was simply a fact of life.

“If you weren’t there,” Ferris said, eyes watering, “I would be dead.”

Uncle Winton gave a short, dark chuckle.

“Well, most times I have to self-reflect to find the source of a demon. This one was easy. It has given me more trouble over the past six months than...” the man trailed off.

Walking back over to Ferris, his uncle placed a hand on his shoulder. “I don’t want to see what demons appear for me if you suffer breaking an oath I got you into. So please,” the man pulled him up into a hug, “for my sake, try to hang in there.”

Ferris hugged back, blinking away the tears.

Jade, sensing his feelings, stopped patrolling the room and rolled over, climbing up into Ferris’ arms.

“Well,” Ferris said, wiping his eyes with an involuntary sniffle as he looked down at Jade, “it hasn’t all been bad. I met Jade, and the people are nice; well, at least most are nice.”

“Good,” his uncle said, releasing him only to ruffle his hair, “and remember, after facing your demons, you must turn them into strength. There must be something you got out of the ordeal.”

“Yes,” Ferris said, considering what he had gained from it all, “the body of the special demon, its weapon, and a stone fort they built.”

“Now we’re talking. The corpse and weapon, well, save them for something special. How large of a structure? Something like that would take forever to build yourself, until you get a bit further along.”

“It is big, but I don’t know any use for it. The damn thing is always full of demons, and it’s in the wrong place to be useful. My only idea so far was to break it down into material for something else.”

“You’ll have to decide for yourself what is right. If the fort is a problem, then tear it down to rubble, and be done with it.” The man walked back over to his chair and sat down. “But if it’s just a building, put it to work. Use it. If you don’t have a use, find a new angle. As your mentor, I’m telling you: look at what you have, and think how it can help you. We need every damned advantage we can get to stay alive. If it feels like cheating, you are starting to do it right.”

“Cheating,” Ferris said, considering as he sat back down in his own chair, Jade still in his arms. The fort had inspired him to want to build something similar in the slimespace for Assimilate Object. “I could have a use for it, but like I said, it’s in the wrong place.”

“So move it,” the man simply replied.

“Move the giant stone walls and towers… I couldn’t even lift a single slab.”

“Sure, why not?” Uncle Winton asked. “There’s one trick I know that could help.”

Ferris leaned forward, excited.

“How do you travel to your bond’s soul?” His mentor asked.

Of course he wouldn’t just state it plainly, Ferris thought before responding.

“I touch the core in my soul, and imagine moving to Jade’s soul.”

“And what if you are carrying something, like a spear?”

“It would go with me,” Ferris answered, starting to see where this was going. But a spear was a lot different than a building.

Uncle Winton nodded, then continued to ask, “If you set your spear on the ground in the bond’s soul, returned to your soul, and moved the core…”

“I could hop back over to Jade’s soul, pickup the spear, and then return. That would move the spear to the core’s new location in my soul.” Ferris finished for him, getting the idea. “But… I have much more than a spear to move,” Ferris said, eager yet unconvinced. He wouldn’t even need to move back to his soulspace, as he wanted the fort in the slimespace.

“It is simply a matter of intent,” Uncle Winton said with a shrug. “Touch this fort, and intend to take the entire thing with you. As a beginner, you can use an aid to help. Qi form a rope long enough to wrap around the fort, and then focus on the intent of moving everything contained by the rope. Your qi inside the rope will help guide the intent. I call this the qi rope transfer technique.”

“Yes… that could work!” Ferris said excitedly.

He would have to adjust some of his plans to accommodate the existing layout of the fort, but even if he had to rebuild portions, it would be worth it.

A new plan slotted into place in his head. Save up qi for a rope long enough, then build Assimilate Object with the fort as the foundation.

Looking back at his mentor, he wondered how much time the man had just saved him. Even if he had built the frame for Assimilate Object by hand, it would never be as sturdy as the large stone slabs of the fort. For the intended purpose of protecting Jade, that could only be a benefit.

With a glint in his eye, Ferris wondered what else the man would tell him.

Well, he thought, I’ve got a lot more questions. Let’s find out.
