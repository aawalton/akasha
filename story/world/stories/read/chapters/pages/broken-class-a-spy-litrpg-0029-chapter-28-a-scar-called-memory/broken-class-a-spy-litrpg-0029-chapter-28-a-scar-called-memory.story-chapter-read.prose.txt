Arthur set out into the forest, moving at a steady pace. The air was crisp and quiet, his senses alert to every shift in mana around him.

Ysara’s words echoed in his mind. "The nearest city is Copperpine. It’s about a two-day hike north from where the ambush took place."

It wasn’t precise, but it gave him a direction. He moved carefully, letting her rough estimate guide his path, while Mana Sight helped him read the terrain, spot threats early, and avoid the worst of the forest’s surprises.

After more than an hour of travel, the forest began to shift with every mile. Trees rose taller, their trunks coated in thick moss. The canopy above fractured the light into sharp, green beams.

He kept low, weight centered, each step measured and silent. The encounter with the princess’s party still buzzed at the back of his mind. The blood, the ambush, and the chaos. But he buried it. This wasn’t the time, focus came first.

Mana Sight pulsed faintly, soft threads of blue entering his perception. When a cluster of signatures appeared to the northeast, he shifted course, careful to stay out of direct sight.

He crouched behind a moss-covered boulder, observing a well-armed party near the ridge. Arthur didn’t recognize them, but their presence set him on edge. The five moved like a seasoned team - coordinated, confident, and clearly used to working together. He stayed low, studying their movements from afar, letting their voices and behavior fill in the gaps.

Their robes were travel-worn but uniform, marked with a faint crest that hinted at noble backing, armor lightly scuffed. One of them, lean and sharp-eyed, radiated cold mana from beneath his skin, like ice waiting to be called. Arthur noted the chilling presence with interest. The man had the bearing of command, or at least entitlement.

What Arthur didn’t know was that this was Rowler, a noble youth and the son of Baron Drelmor. An ice mage, and one who ran his hunting party like a battlefield unit. They used criminals as monster bait, an efficient but ruthless tactic that matched the cold discipline Arthur now saw in their movements.

They hadn’t noticed Arthur. Their mana signatures were frayed from recent combat, and though their formation was trained, it had gaps. Sloppy execution, perhaps, or just fatigue. Either way, Arthur stayed low and silent, watching.

"We’re still twenty behind," one of them grumbled.

"Doesn’t matter," another said. "He must already be injured. His count hasn’t increased since day one."

Arthur’s brow lifted slightly. He had been distracted from the Great Hunt ever since the ambush on the princess’s party and hadn't thought to check the rankings since. The idea that someone had gained ground on him was surprising.

He decided to tail them.

They were the strongest group he’d seen so far. And if they’d already caught up to him, he needed to know how. So, he trailed them, sticking to the shadows for the next two days. He studied their movement patterns, their hunting routines, how they divided tasks, and how often they rested. It was efficient. Brutally so. But to Arthur, it was just data: movements, formations, rest cycles.At one point, one of the five on the flank paused, his gaze sweeping the treeline with unusual focus.

Arthur froze beneath a thicket of brambles, breath held, body low and motionless. He scanned them with Mana Sight. No spikes. No flares. Just the usual background hum of a tired team

But the man’s gaze lingered longer than the others. Not alarmed. Just… aware. Like he was listening for something he couldn’t quite place.

Then he moved on.

Arthur stayed crouched a moment longer before melting back into the trees.

Too close. He’d need to be more careful.

Then, on the third night, he overheard them around a small fire, their voices low but unguarded. One of them cursed how much harder the Hunt was compared to training.

"Back before the Hunt, we had it easy. Toss a few slaves into the woods, and a monster or two would come charging. Perfect bait, every time."

"Yeah," one of them muttered. "Hunting’s easy. It’s finding the damn things that eats all the time."

Arthur froze.

Something cold and bitter twisted in his gut.

It wasn’t just the cruelty. It was the familiarity. The way they spoke of people, of slaves, like tools to be used and discarded. That quiet dehumanization. That lack of hesitation.

It reminded him of Rajak. Not because Rajak ever pretended it was a strategy. He did it because he could. Because he wanted to. A sadist in command.

But this was somehow worse. These men weren’t mad. They were calm. Calculated. As if tossing lives into the woods was just another step in a hunting plan.

And it brought back the faces of Mary and Paul, people who had shown him care without expecting anything back.

Mary handing him food she couldn’t spare. Both of them stepping between him and the monster when it counted.

No drama. Just parents doing what needed to be done.

They gave what little they had, and in the end, they gave him time.

He hadn’t looked back as he ran. But he’d heard everything. Their screams, their fight, their end.

He’d thought the grief had dulled. But the memory hit hard, dragging the weight back with interest.

And now these bastards laughed about feeding lives to monsters. No hesitation. No guilt.

He clenched his jaw.

This time, he wouldn’t stay silent.
