“I think that’s about all the studying I can handle,” Colin said from the floor next to the common room table. His haphazard notes were arrayed in front of him like a trog warren on parade, all chaos and confusion, and his eyes looked completely glazed over.

Eola sat on the couch, her notes confined to a proper notebook in her lap. The three of them—Roth had pulled up his desk chair and perched on it backwards, humming to himself but paying at least some attention—were currently studying for Introduction to Monsters.

Patrice, to Eola’s complete lack of surprise, was asleep.

They were only working on Introduction to Monsters because Eola couldn’t stand another minute of watching Colin slowly cast his First Order rituals, trying to pick out the modifying marks, and do something with that information. She more or less had the first part down. Out of the four marks, she could usually identify three before he was done sketching and filling his rune. Three was enough for her to…

And that was the problem. To…what?

She was on the right track. Everything Atta had said implied that she could do something with the modifying marks once she knew what they were and could identify them. But she hadn’t figured out what that ‘something’ was, and her familiar was beyond reluctant to give her any more hints.

So, Introduction to Monsters.

The notes were, of course, all about the breeding process of Dalyn/Davek pairs. She found it a little embarrassing to read about, and the boys had caught on to her discomfort. Honestly, the whole process was far too messy for her taste; it didn’t just involve reproduction, but planting fertilized eggs inside of carcasses and letting the flesh rot around the incubating Dalyns and Daveks. She didn’t want to know anything more about how the mantis-spiders reproduced—especially not the…act…itself.

So, the moment Colin gave her an out, her notebook shut. “Great. I think we’re well and truly ready for Introduction to Monsters, then. I’m thinking about dinner.”

“It’s three in the afternoon,” Colin said. His familiar sat on his lap, sprawling across his folded legs and spilling down to the floor, a yellow river of weasel more liquid than not.

“I can always eat.”

Colin nodded in agreement. “You just usually choose not to for long periods of time, then eat half your weight in one sitting.”

“It’s a perfectly normal way to do things.”

“It’s not healthy, Eola.”

“Before you run off to stuff your face,” Roth said—quite rudely in Eola’s opinion—, “I need your help, and this afternoon’s our last good opportunity before things get busy.”

He pulled a sheet of parchment from his robe pocket and set it on the desk, carefully avoiding Colin’s sprawling notes. Eola picked it up.

Row Ninety-Five, Shelf Nineteen

Fundamentals of the Theorbo, by Pinepetal

“That’s…pretty far in,” she said, handing the scrap to Colin.

Roth nodded. “It is. The theorbo is a new instrument for me. It’s no good for combat, but I haven’t done much with strings except the harp. A…friend…used to have one. I want to learn.”

“Hmmm,” Eola hedged.

“It’d also be an opportunity to test ourselves before the term exam. We’ve been practicing, but we haven’t put any of it to use.”

“Fine.” Eola stood up, knocked on Patrice’s door, and opened it. “Patrice, we’re going to the library. I’ll be back in five minutes to help you gear up.”

“Do I get a say in this?” Colin asked worriedly.

Roth rolled his eyes. “No.”

“I should, though.”

Eola hurried to her room as Roth and Colin started arguing loudly. A moment later, the noise cut off as her door shut, and she pulled on her breastplate, boots, and gloves. The armor melted around her body, fitting snugly in all the right places. That was, in every possible way, a relief.

It meant her mother was still okay.

The library’s changing room was packed, and Eola didn’t regret leaving her robes and cloak behind and crossing the campus grounds in only her armor and clothes. Spring had set in; the snow was only ankle-deep now, and the air was just unpleasantly nippy instead of dagger-to-the-cheek cold. She was still shivering as she waited for the others on the equally crowded first floor, though. It was warm by comparison, but Varin’s Academy never got truly hot.

Crossing the second floor was an exercise in simplicity. The sheer number of students in the first half meant they didn’t even have to start leapfrogging until Row Fifty-Seven, and while Eola spotted signs of monsters, the actual monsters were nowhere to be seen.

They’d probably all been killed.

That feeling of security lasted all the way through Row Ninety-Two. As she crossed into the open space that marked Row Ninety-Four, Patrice’s hand came up, and Eola stopped mid-step. “Trogs.”

“How many?” Eola asked.

“Can’t tell. More than one. Listen.”

Eola sniffed the air instead. The stench was beyond obvious; the fungal odor pressed in all around her, and she coughed quietly as she drew her sword. “Yep, that’s trogs, alright. Roth, you’re up.”

The dark-haired boy’s scowl didn’t change—it rarely did—but a flute came up to his lips, and he started to play. No sound came out. None whatsoever. He held the flute one-handed, waving with the other for Eola to keep moving.

She crossed Row Ninety-Four, glancing to the left. About halfway down, closer to Shelf Twenty than not, the glow of torches lit up the bookshelves, and shadowy, lizardlike figures moved in the aisle. Books had been stacked to form a crude wall, blocking it from shelf to shelf, with a narrow gap in the middle.

The trogs had made a lair.

That wasn’t good. That wasn’t good at all.

Then she was past them, footsteps silenced by Roth’s magic. His fingers piped a tune she couldn’t hear as he hurried along in the middle of the group, then pointed. They slipped down Row Ninety-Five, all the way down to Shelf Nineteen. Colin’s lips moved, but he was silenced, too.

Roth had been practicing this one for a while. It was the most basic of the list of First Order spells he’d transcribed, and the one with the least consequences for failure. The rune was called Silent Spot, and it muted all sound in an area for a few minutes when sketched and filled. But Roth didn’t need to sketch it and fill it. He’d translated it into a song—a song he could play on the move and keep feeding Mana to as long as he played.

First Order magic, even transcribed into music, was truly powerful; Eola couldn’t wait to decipher more of it for herself—and to build her Mana reserves so she could use it. Journeymage awaited.

Patrice waved the group forward, spinning her fingers in a ‘get on with it’ gesture. Eola turned to face the end of Row Ninety-Five; they were on the outside edge of the library, and only the massive marble wall and even more shelves stared back at her. That was a relief. No feral books, no unweeded sections.

Just the trogs, and Roth’s Silent Song was taking care of—

Her ears popped violently, and she nearly dropped her smallsword as a wave of sound ripped through Roth’s spellsong, obliterating it instantly.

It was a scream. A high-pitched scream—the kind a five-year-old girl made on a village field while boys chased her, or that the boys made when she turned to chase them. It went on and on for what seemed like an eternity, and Eola spun to face its source.

Roth had pointed out a book, and Colin had grabbed it. Fundamentals of the Theorbo sat, open, in his hand. The screaming came from it.

Roth grabbed the book, snapped it shut, and crammed it into his pocket. He lowered the flute and shoved it in after the book. “Y’aer curse it. Behind you!”

Eola spun, smallsword going up to First Guard, as the first trog rounded the corner.

More followed in its wake.

The first trog’s yellow, almost jaundiced-looking scales glimmered in the flickering torchlight. Its broad body and thin limbs looked almost mismatched, and its eyes narrowed as its nostrils flared. Then it roared.

Behind it, the other trogs forced themselves into the passageway between shelves. It was tight; Eola could fight cleanly, but only if—

Something hit Eola from behind, knocking her to one knee. “Y’aer’s curse!”

“Clear some space!” Patrice shouted as her hammer swung in a wide arc that knocked books off of Shelf Twenty-Three. They landed in a jumble that promised poor footing.

Eola pushed herself up. She’d Held a single Lighting Wing before they stepped through the gate; beyond that, she’d be reliant on cantrips. Roth’s hand went for his axe. His magic had done its part, but now, mid-fight, he’d rely on his weapon more than his instruments.

And as for Colin? He was already sketching and muttering, eyes focused on the complex, fully-realized lines of a ritual rune. Eola recognized it from their practice and from a previous trip to the library. “Keep Colin safe,” she snapped at Roth.

“What are you going to do?” he shot back.

Eola pointed with her sword tip at the back, where a rune glowed in the air. “Someone has to stop the shaman!”

Trogs, by themselves, were plenty dangerous. They’d almost killed Eola a couple of times this term, first when a pack had harried her all the way to the safety of the gate and its librarians, and again on the trip where she’d met Patrice. They were strong, big, and stabbing them only seemed to make them more disgusting.

But if this many trogs were in one place, it meant they were establishing a lair—and Instructor Clearance had made it abundantly clear that a lair meant a shaman.

And a shaman meant trouble. They didn’t know Ordered Magic, but their cantrips often punched like they did. And, unlike mages, monsters didn’t mind the impure dungeon Mana one bit. It’d keep casting and casting until she and her fellow apprentices couldn’t keep up.

Eola could fix that, though.

She ducked her head, keeping as low as she could. Patrice was as likely to be caught napping as practicing, and her magic was unpredictable at best, but she wasn’t a coward. Her armor bore divots from the trog’s torch, but she’d landed a bone-shattering hit on the monster’s arm, and a different one had shoved its way past.

Clearly, Eola couldn’t force her way through the fight.

The shaman’s spell finished. The new trog let out a bloodthirsty howl and lashed out with a bone club. It crashed into Patrice’s breastplate. The tall girl took the blow head-on, then dropped to a knee, coughing.

What had the shaman cast? As Eola dashed forward to cover Patrice, she tried to visualize the spell. An upward-facing arrow. And something else. A modifying mark, the Forsooth Script cleverly concealed in the structure. It wasn’t built like Ordered Magic, though. It was built like a spell-chain.

Her smallsword deflected the massive club as it hammered down on Patrice. Eola’s whole arm went numb, but she pulled her blade across, jamming it into the monster’s chest. It howled again, this time in pain. Blood, snot, and saliva splattered across Eola’s face. She stabbed again, and the trog fell backward.

Another started to climb over its fallen ally.

“Heads down!” Roth yelled.

Eola turned. The ritual rune was finished, and Colin’s Mana poured into it. She coiled, leaped, and crashed into the still-dazed and winded Patrice. They hit the ground in a clatter of armor and weapons, and Colin’s ritual finished.

Fire exploded outward, toward the press of scales. Colin’s spell had only gotten stronger for all his learning; Eola’s brows were both singed, and the stink of burning hair filled her nose. All around her, runes activated to protect the library’s books from the sudden firestorm that had ripped down the shelves.

Her hair was probably toast. That was fine, though.

She had a straight line to the trog shaman.

Books slipped under her feet as she pushed off, legs pumping. Her smallsword lashed out to block a trog’s torch. Sparks rained down and were snuffed out by the library’s runes. Her other hand wrapped around her wand. She flicked it forward, and the Held spell went off. Lighting Wing. A glaring line of pure light swung toward the shaman, slamming into its face before it could even shield its eyes. Her spell’s burns joined the ones Colin's ritual had already caused.

Eola was already casting a second spell—a Safe Shield behind her.

But the shaman didn’t lose its head.

Even as its eyes smoked and it screamed in pain, its hand raised, and it started to draw.

Eola was still too far to stop it, and she was busy casting her own spell. The runes and marks rippled across her sketch as her wand worked to finish it. Then, out of nowhere, the spell collapsed. No. Not collapsed. But something went wrong, and she lost control. She got a glimpse of the Safe Shield—complete with a mark she was sure she hadn’t sketched.

The spell went off.

Pain surged across her stomach. She looked down; a thin red line had scoured itself into her padded armor, right below her breastplate. It hurt. It burned; she’d only felt agony like it once or twice in her life.

Still…she was close.

In sword range, in fact.

Her sword sprang from Second Guard into a punching lunge. The shaman pulled back, but not fast enough—or far enough. The razor-sharp tip sliced into its throat, and Eola spun as she pulled it free.

Her foot caught a book. Pages tore, and she stumbled. Someone grabbed her arm and pulled her back down the row of books, away from the fighting. “Eola, focus! Patrice is hurt, Colin’s tapped out, and—Y’aer’s curse, your stomach,” Roth’s voice said.

“The trogs? Are they still pushing?”

“Curse the trogs! We have to leave!”

Roth spun her around, and she saw an exhausted-looking Colin. He was slowly purifying Mana—she recognized the expression on his face—but it’d be a while before he was ready to cast again. And, next to him, breathing shakily and leaning on her hammer’s haft, was Patrice. She looked terrible. Pale. Blood-splattered. Her eyes were squeezed shut, a mask of pain across her face.

Eola glanced over her shoulder at the surviving trogs. There were still half a dozen—all of that, and they’d only dropped half of their attackers. But the trogs, for now at least, seemed distracted by the wounded shaman.

It wasn’t dead. Somehow, she hadn’t killed it.

Roth’s face was pale. “We’re leaving.”

Colin raised his whistle to his lips and blew. He put an arm around Patrice’s waist, threw her other arm across his shoulder, and started falling back down the shelves.

And Eola limped along behind them, her sword between the trogs and Patrice. She kept an eye on the trog pack until they made it around the corner, Colin still whistling, and ran into a group of second-years.

“We were in Row Eighty-Four. Heard your whistling. You four okay?” one of the older boys asked. He had a sword and wand at the ready.

“I’m not sure,” Eola said slowly. Her stomach was on fire. How deep had that spell cut? Had it done real damage—either to her or to her breastplate? If either she or Patrice was out for more than a couple of days, their odds of passing the term exam dropped dramatically.

And while they’d done alright, neither Patrice, Roth, nor Eola had done well enough. The only member of their team who’d done his job adequately had been Colin. They had to do better, but they had little time left for improvement. The third floor awaited.

Eola winced as one of the second-year students jostled her, and she pressed her palm against her wound gingerly. The blood was warm and sticky, and it smelled like metal.

But even though the pain insisted on most of her attention, a small corner of her mind held the shape of the modifying mark the trog shaman had used. She’d seen it in combat—or at least most of it. Without the rest, it wasn’t enough to replicate it, but it gave her an idea.

Her hand stayed pressed against her bloody stomach as the second-years hurried down the second floor’s main aisle.

She was pretty sure that the trog shaman had done something to her magic. But that shouldn’t be possible. Countermagic—true countermagic—didn’t exist.

So how had her magic backfired like that?
