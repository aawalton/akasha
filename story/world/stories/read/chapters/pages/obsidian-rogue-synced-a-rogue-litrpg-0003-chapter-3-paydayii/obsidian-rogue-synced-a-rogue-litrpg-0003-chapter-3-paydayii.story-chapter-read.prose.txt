A loud bang echoed from the ground floor, startling Levi. The rogue quickly exited the room, stealthily heading in the direction of the noise, his daggers held in a tight grip. He crept towards the stairs, his eyes and ears were on full alert.

Usually, he'd have left the Inn since he'd already done his part of the job by confirming that the stone wasn't in the rooms upstairs, but he couldn't risk not getting paid, especially since he had to make so much more money to afford Bree's and Silas's sync tickets. At the very least, this job was worth three times more than his most lucrative job.

Perks of syncing, I guess.

With a grin, Levi pushed the thoughts to the back of his mind, choosing to focus on the present. Looking around, he could confirm that the noise hadn't come from the floor he was currently on, and it hadn't come from above — the roof, which left only one other option.

The ground floor.

So Levi crept silently, the yellow glowstones on the walls brightly lit the hallway and staircase, making it easy for him to navigate downwards. Even as covered up as he was, the hair on his body tingled the closer he got to the source of the noise, his eyes scanning the area, to the sight of chaos. Broken furniture and glass littered the floor, the taste of iron blossomed on his tongue, and the air… the air tasted of death.

Levi hesitated at the end of the staircase, everything he'd seen so far indicated that their job hadn't gone to plan and leaving the Inn immediately seemed like the best option for him, but he knew Killian, the crooked taskmaster wouldn't give him a bent copper for a job that wasn't completed and even if he wanted to leave without the other rogue, he'd need to have the stone on hand to be able to get his pay, else Killian could just say that the job wasn't finished and claim that he was not obligated to pay him.

Grakking crook.

Levi squeezed his daggers till his knuckles turned white, gritting his teeth as he considered his options. He could either check for the stone on his own and exchange it for gold, find the rogue and escape with him, or abandon the job and forfeit the gold. All options were unsavory to say the least, which meant he was most likely grakked.

I'll just do a quick search, and if the stone is within reach, I'll take it and get it to Killian. If it isn't, I'll find the rogue and aid him. If it seems like a suicide mission, then I'm abandoning this job.

As far as Levi was concerned, no amount of gold or reputation on the line was worth more than his life. With his priorities in check, he crept forward, trying his best to avoid stepping on the broken glass to avoid drawing attention to himself. It was best for him that whoever the rogue was fighting didn't realize that he was here.

Bang!

A loud boom echoed from up ahead, sending a chill down his spine, but Levi didn't stop; he'd already made up his mind to check out what was happening, and he planned to go through with it. He kept his head on a swivel as he crept forward, his eyes noting the floor littered with even more shards of broken glass. The smell of iron grew stronger, and when he finally reached the main room of the ground floor, he had to resist the urge to empty his stomach.

"Grakking hell," he whispered.

Dead bodies weren't anything new to Levi; his line of work often had mishaps, a job or two had gone south, requiring him to silence a few unfortunate souls, but this was anything but a mishap. All three dead bodies lying on the floor at his far right-hand side were a gruesome sight to behold. One had a bleeding eye socket; the eyeball had presumably been popped. A dagger was his guess for the murder weapon, and it was fair to presume that the blade had gone through to the brain, killing the… priest instantly.

The other two had their throats sliced open, with both corpses having a hand clutching at the fatal strike.

The three corpses weren't even the highlight of the main room, because right there in the room was the rogue he'd been looking for, his partner for the job, who was currently fighting a mage. Although fighting was a strong word since the mage in question was covered by a transparentish dome, with the rogue furiously pounding on it with both daggers.

A glint from the corner of his eye caught his attention. It was a corpse in a casket, a few paces to his left. The top of the casket sported jagged glass, and the floor around it held the pieces. The corpse was obviously not fresh, but it was well preserved, or it had been. The corpse belonged to a female rogue going by the clothing used… a badly mangled female rogue, and that was even after the cleanup that had been done to the corpse before putting it in the casket. Inside the casket was an empty pedestal shaped like a cup. Levi guessed that it was meant to hold the stone he'd come for, but the pedestal was bare.

Vavnir's horns.

Mangled as the corpse was, he could also make out a hint of a tattoo on the forehead of the rogue in the casket. It looked like a black dagger, at least that was what he thought it was; the rest of it was cut out by what appeared to be claw marks, the claws having gone deep, down to the skull. A shiver went down his spine at the thought of whatever could have done this to her. The claw marks were clearly animalistic. Brute and crushing force had also been used upon the female rogue.

Grak this, the money isn't enough for … whatever this is.

No matter what amount was on the table, he knew this was above what he could handle, far from it even. His entire schtick depended on being unseen, getting in and out with his mark without a confrontation. This was anything but that. Gruesomely murdered corpses and opponents both synced and of a much higher level than him meant that he needed to get out of the inn like yesterday.

"You," A voice thundered from the extreme right side of the room.

He didn't need to see the face to know who it was; besides, he couldn't see the face even if he wanted to, it was the rogue. The incessant banging against the mage's protective dome had come to a halt, with the rogue now facing Levi, his blades still coated in the blood of those who'd died today. He had no reason to stay back; everything in his body warned him to leave, and so he did, or at least he tried to. But before he could take a step backwards, a spell struck him head-on.

Zap!

[You have failed to resist teleportation pull (II).]

Before he could contemplate what Cerpital's message meant, he felt his entire being being sucked into nothingness and then pushed out into existence right in front of a robed mage. He was now within the same dome that the rogue had been pounding on moments before he'd noticed him, which meant Levi was now in an enclosed space with the one person who'd been able to keep the deadly rogue at bay. Unfortunately for Levi, that meant his only ally was outside, at least he hoped the rogue considered him an ally.

"Don't you even think about it." The mage rasped."I can kill you before you even take your next breath."

He stopped in his tracks, daggers angled for a quick dash. He'd intended to attack the mage as fast as possible with both daggers. If anything, the element of surprise might've been able to let him badly injure or kill the mage. Looking at his foe now, he wasn't so sure he'd be able to, and if the mage didn't want to execute him immediately, then that meant he might just be able to get out of this, intact.

"Alright," He agreed, still keeping his blades angled towards the mage but making no moves towards the mage. His partner, on the other hand, had resumed pounding the dome.

The dome was transparent from the inside, so he could see the sharp daggers of his rogue hitting the magical dome furiously. Out of the corner of his eye, he saw the mage struggling. Beads of sweat rolled down the mage's face, with lots of veins pushing against the skin of his head. By the looks of things, it was only a matter of time before the dome collapsed, and it'd pay him to stay alive till that happened.

"You know, you can't survive his onslaught for much longer. You're already bleeding from your nose," Levi pointed out. "All we want is the stone, if you give us that, I promise to talk my partner into letting you liv—"

"Partner he says." The mage cackled as he interrupted Levi. "Ghost doesn't do partners. If you're here with him, then you're surely going to die tonight, just like the rest of us."

Levi's grip on his daggers tightened not only because of the implication of the mage's words but also because the transparent dome had turned murky, till the visage of the rogue outside was blurred entirely. "You might be right, but I was paid for this job."

"Let me guess, to steal this?" the mage rasped, his voice strained. In the mage's hand raised to the eye level of both men was a stone, a black nondescript stone the size of his fist, the obsidian stone. "Don't worry about the barrier; I had to ensure that we had a bit of privacy. Now no one, not even Ghost, can see or hear what happens in here."

To be sure what he was seeing wasn't a fake or just a random stone the mage had kept just in case of a moment like this, Levi used his only skill. Focusing intently on the stone, he waited for his analyze skill to kick in.

[ Artifact: Obsidian Stone

Rank: Unranked.

Description: The obsidian stone is the Pinnacle of artifacts of the rogue class. Its secrets belong solely to its wielder.

Restriction: This item can only be used by Rogue class Synced.]

That's the stone alright. I wonder if I can analyze the mage.

[ Analyze Failed ]

That was expected. I need that stone.

"Hand it over and I'll talk to him," Levi offered. His failure to analyze the mage already forgotten as he focused on what had truly brought him here."He'd spare you, we only want the stone."

"Tell that to my dead friends," The mage spat. Droplets of blood from his spittle hit the wooden floor of the Inn, his shoulders sagging and his eyes droopy." You don't know who he is, and that's fine. Since you're clearly out of the loop, I'd give you an offer. Absorb the stone, and I'll teleport you to the roof so that you can escape, or refuse, and I'll kill you myself."

"He paid me for a job, to steal that. He wouldn't kill me if I hand it over to him," he replied firmly.

"Oh, the naivety of the young, I do miss the stupidity of my youth." The mage croaked. "He didn't pay you right? He paid your taskmaster?"

Just where is this joker heading with this conversation?

"Yes, he did."

"He didn't pay for your service; he paid for your dead body. You're the disposable fall guy for this job." The mage cackled. "You aren't here to steal the stone, you're here to die. You are here to take the blame. You, a bloody level … one rogue."

It was all making sense now, why Killian had refused to give him half of his fee like he usually did for jobs, why the rogue had taken him… him, a level one rogue to rob a group of Synced that could kill him without breaking a sweat. The old crook had sold him to be butchered, and he'd gone along thinking it was easy coin.

"Grakking schemes," he cursed.

The plan was foolproof as well. No one below rank A would be able to analyze his dead body to know that he was just a level one rogue, incapable of killing the group that had occupied the Inn. He highly doubted that an A-ranked synced would come all the way to analyze him. So it was either he'd been played or the mage was an excellent storyteller. He already knew which of the two possibilities he believed.

"How do I know that you're not lying?"

"You don't, but what you do know for certain is that if you choose not to accept my offer, I will kill you even before Ghost has a chance to. So what will it be?"

"I'll … I'll absorb the stone. "

"Smart choice," The mage rasped, tossing him the stone. "You have thirty seconds."
