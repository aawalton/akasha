Chapter V.XLVIII (5.48) - Acquired Taste

Kizu stared up at the familiar white ceiling of the academy’s medical wing. In his first moments of consciousness, he wondered if everything that had happened this year had simply been a dream. It all seemed so farfetched. Could he have just cast the divination ritual to detect his sister and passed out due to blood overuse? The moment passed and he forced himself to return to wakefulness.

With a groan, Kizu pushed himself into a sitting position.

“So, we’ve finally uncovered your secret,” an old woman’s voice said.

His heart skipped a beat as he recalled the events from before his hospitalization. The crone. She’d been there, looming over Taroe’s shoulder.

He turned his head, body shaking in terror.

That all washed away as relief replaced the dread. Kizu finally properly placed the speaker. Not the crone. This was Roba speaking to him. She’d rescued him. The crone wasn’t here.

“Why….” His question trailed off. Kizu touched his throat and winced. It was so dry.

“Drink this.” Roba forced a clay cup into Kizu’s hands.

Without further question, Kizu sipped the liquid. It was thick and chunky. He thought he vaguely recognized the brew’s ingredients, but his head still felt a bit fuzzy. The drink soothed his throat as it dribbled down into his esophagus.

“Good. Your arm is working again,” Roba said, examining him. “It was a patch job since our lead healer refused to work on you. On account of him being the one to inflict the damage in the first place.”

“Taroe,” Kizu said. He reached up and touched his shoulder where the knife had pierced him. It was sore, but the flesh was whole once again.

“Looking for this?” Roba set Anata’s knife on the bedside table.

Kizu spent a minute in silence, simply staring at the blade. Anata’s father had given it to her. It was a tool designed to extract something from her to help him achieve his own goals. The goals of a Calamity.

Kizu understood why Taroe reacted the way he had upon the revelation. He didn’t blame the man. From an outsider’s perspective, Kizu likely appeared a monster, the same as Otochi.

“Who else knows?” Kizu asked quietly. He started to cast a silence bubble around himself but winced and dropped the spell. He was weak. He needed a blood refill.

Roba obliged and properly cast the spell around them.

“Myself, Taroe, and the headmaster. We deemed the secret too dangerous to allow more people in on the information. That, I will say, you did very well. Should your greed not have gotten the best of you, no one would ever have likely discovered this extremely hazardous secret at all.”

“I needed to get stronger for her protection as much as my own,” Kizu said.

Roba snorted and shook her head.

“There wasn’t any other way!” Kizu protested. “We wouldn’t have survived this long without her help.”

“There’s always another way.”

“The other way was death. Which wouldn’t be great for any of us.”

“Arguing on what could have been is pointless, save to serve as a cautionary tale for the future. Regardless, I am not here to argue the ethics of your decision with you today. No. I’m here to discuss your niece.”

“You’re not going to take—”

“We couldn’t, even if we wanted to,” Roba said, cutting him off. “Even I know that your niece would fight every measure put in place to keep her from you. I heard the reports from your excursion to Tross last semester. Separating the two of you while simultaneously keeping an eye on the Harbinger’s security would be extraordinarily difficult.”

Kizu narrowed his eyes. “Then… what is there to discuss?”

“For one thing, Taroe has no interest in speaking to you. You’ve been banned from his household. We’re lucky classes are complete, otherwise I’d need to either intervene or privately tutor you in medicine.”

Kizu propped himself up into a sitting position on his bed using the pillows. His body still fiercely stung from where Taroe had initially punched him. It was tender to the touch and, even without looking, he knew it must be a mess of purple and blue skin under his shirt.

“That’s fine,” he said. “I’ll just use Mort to jump directly to Owl’s Respite and the academy beacon to get to class. I can skip over using my gate easily enough.”

“Good. Now, we also need to discuss future blood use.”

Kizu bit his lip. He didn’t meet Roba’s eyes, instead studying the curtain that surrounded the medical bed.

“I understand now how you’ve managed to make these leaps in your rankings this year. It seemed inconceivable to me at first. A puzzle I simply could not comprehend. Now that it’s unraveled, I only have one thing to say. You need to stop.”

Kizu thought back to the longest stretch he’d gone without Anata’s blood. It had been after his blood transfusion in Hon. It had helped reduce the constant urge. But… he couldn’t afford to have watered down blood right now. Not with the tournament on the horizon. And then, of course, there was also the delve into the World Dungeon he was secretly planning with Basil and Ione. No, that wasn’t a real option. So he didn’t even suggest it.

Still, if he was being honest with himself, he agreed with Roba’s assessment. Besides, he was strong enough now to protect Anata, even without her help. It was time to take this step. He knew what he’d been doing was wrong, even if he constantly justified it to himself.

“Do you….” He licked his dry lips and took a deep breath. “Do you have anything to help with addiction?”

Roba’s eyes widened slightly, her wrinkly face creased further as she frowned. “I did not realize it was an addictive substance. That changes a few things. Did your mentor not teach you any brewing recipes to break addictions?”

Kizu held back a shudder at the reminder of what the crone had done to him the one time he’d experienced addiction under her care. He took a moment to figure out how to phrase the crone’s philosophy on addiction.

“She believed potions weren’t adequate. That they’re not good enough. They’re only for those with weak wills who trade one addiction for another.”

Roba grunted. “I understand the sentiment, even if I don’t personally stand by it myself. However, I know of a recipe you can brew to hold you over for the next few weeks.

Kizu gulped. “And… after those few weeks?”

“We need to figure out if this is something that requires careful weaning to safely eliminate from your system without negative side effects.”

Once again, Kizu held back from mentioning how the blood transfusion had removed the addictive side-effects. So long as Roba’s plan to slap a temporary patch over the problem didn’t interfere with later blood transfusions, this was the most optimal path forward for his goals. In fact, in some ways it was better. He’d been a bit worried about going several days in the World Dungeon without Anata on hand. Now, he’d have access to Roba’s addiction nullifying potions.

He could get by for a few weeks. Then, over summer break after the tournament, Kizu could rest up somewhere safe with diluted blood until his fourth-year classes started up.

“When will I be back on my feet?” he asked. “If Taroe won’t work on me, will I still be fine for the first round of the tournament? Should I drink a healing potion?”

Roba snorted. “You’ll be fine. Hold off on using healing potions for now. We need to monitor you to be certain you didn’t receive a concussion from the blow. Head wounds and healing potions are a dangerous combination. Stay in this bed until tomorrow morning and you’ll be good enough.”

Kizu agreed. He figured that would be simple enough.

“Your physical body is the last thing I’m worried about. Since we’re on the subject, drink this.” The woman reached down and grabbed something at the foot of his bed. She tossed a flask onto his lap. “Since you will no longer be absorbing blood from your niece, you’ll need to get blood regeneration from a different method.”

Kizu held back a sigh. Without even touching the flask, he already knew what was inside. Blood fruit juice. Even after all these months, he’d yet to forget the distinct taste of this juice. Not exactly a delicacy. Still, if it got him back in action faster, then he was more than willing to gulp a few cups of the juice down every day.

He took a sip. Then another. It was definitely an acquired taste.
