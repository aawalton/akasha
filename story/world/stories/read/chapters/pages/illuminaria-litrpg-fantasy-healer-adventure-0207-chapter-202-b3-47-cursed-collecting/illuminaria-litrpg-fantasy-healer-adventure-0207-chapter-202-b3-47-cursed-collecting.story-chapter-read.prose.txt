Chapter 202 / B3-47: Cursed Collecting

The post-healing celebration had been going for hours before Kenda regretfully had to head off.  There were still countless tasks that needed to be addressed regarding the necrotically infused valley, the hoard of cursed artifacts littering it, plus any potential ramifications the Erlking’s death might have with the Feylands.  Dealing with these threats fell directly into the purview of the Adventurer’s Guild.  As one of the ranking officers, Kenda had been absent longer than she really should have been.  While it was highly likely Myllo would understand if she took some time to be with Joe, it wasn’t in Kendell’s nature to shirk her duties.

When she headed off, Joe found himself also wanting to depart, yet he couldn’t disentangle himself from the festivities nearly as easily.  He was the subject of way too many toasts, but at least this time he was drinking juice or even a mild ale instead of the potent dwarven brews that had been foisted on him at the Jarlscasp gala.  Keeping inebriation at bay took far less effort than he had needed at the ball.

Even so, Joe was reaching his threshold for public attention.  He understood the Cliffsiders just wanted to express their gratitude for bringing home the legendary founder.  Regardless, at this point, Joe really just wanted some quiet time with Mojo and to start working on the puzzle of breaking the Erlkings bane.

Eventually, he said his goodbyes to Runk, Margen, and, of course, Mercy Suku, before jogging back into the city proper again.  As he passed the Old Fort, Joe let his eyes linger on the ruins and quickly understood why folk felt it was haunted.  There was definitely a creepy vibe emanating from the dilapidated compound. The way it sat broken and hunched at the top of the cliffs looming over Coral Bay brought to mind a vulture perched over a carcass, or a ghost haunting a desolate scarp.

Joe shook off his involuntary shivers and ran over the Rock Walk bridge.  Instead of stopping at the Abacca House, Joe and Mojo jogged a few more blocks to reach Founder’s Plaza and the guildhouse.  He had to pick up some of the tainted items from Puqmup in order to start working on breaking the curse.

As he entered the front door, he could hear voices emanating from the main hall.  Joe caught the last word of a question from Myllo, which was quickly answered by the droning voice of one of Joe’s teammates.

“There is no question that the valley is condensing into a multi-leveled dungeon,” Tezeno’s monotone voice stated.  “The exterior forests will be suitable for copper rank delvers, reaching bronze difficulty right around the dungeon aperture. The decreased difficulty is primarily due to the lack of contagion.  None of the undead that we’ve cataloged so far have infectious necrotic touches.  The valley is naturally spawning common low-rank undead, such as skeletons, zombies, and ghasts, as well as incorporeal haunts.”

“And the interior dungeon?” a woman asked.  Joe couldn’t quite place who she was, even though her voice was familiar to him.  “Have we managed to get a look inside it yet?”

“Not yet.  It looks like it will resemble the ziggurat above ground, with a subterranean component, but according to our probes, the dungeon is still folding space and compacting mana.  Our best guess is that it will take a few more weeks before it will be stable enough for delvers to see what’s inside it.”

“Thanks, Tez,” Myllo chimed in.  “That should give us plenty of time to gather up the scattered cursed trove, making sure no one starts claiming them.”

“I have sixteen pledges from the churches for clerics to assist the guild in quarantining the cursed relics,” the woman specified.  With that context, Joe realized who she was: Lady Blackgrove, the council head for the religious orders in Fort Coral.

He didn’t linger any longer before slipping into the library.  He likely would have been welcomed into the meeting had he stuck his head into the hall, but he really was peopled out.  Even though he’d get to spend some more time with Kenda if he attended the gathering of leaders, it wouldn’t be quality time.  Better to let her get her work done and meet up afterward.

Besides, he had a plan for his afternoon.  His first stop was the section of the library where the volumes on curses were shelved.  Joe scanned titles.  His eyes were immediately drawn to a thick volume that had seen a great deal of wear and tear: Malgrim Morgrath’s Comprehensive Treatise on Maledictions and Their Dissolution. Joe flipped it open to see that the tome was heavily dog-eared.  He scanned the table of contents and found it was just about exactly what he was looking for.  He flipped to a random spot in the middle to see if the text was comprehensible.

“The persistent nature of malefic enchantments is one of their core attributes.  Unlike transient spellcraft, which dissipates quickly after invocation, a curse anchors itself and lingers.  The stronger the enchantment’s sympathetic link is, the more successful the malediction will be.  Links such as blood, name, memory, or possession can sustain the bane’s influence, sometimes indefinitely or until the curse can be removed.  Even the death of a cursed individual may not provide the terminus of a malediction, as many banes can be passed on through their sympathetic links.”

‘Bit dry,’ Joe thought, but given how many times this codex had been referenced, he figured it was worth borrowing.

He gathered a few more titles. The Silverleaf Papers: Studies in Reversal Magic by Aria Slessrestell, because it specifically dealt with fey curses. Runic Bindings and the Structure of Lasting Enchantments looked interesting since it also had a section regarding wards.

There were several others he considered, but he figured the ones he already had would be good enough to start with once he found the last book he wanted to borrow. Professor Halwen Brisk’s Primer of Curse Reversal was just that, a primer.  The book was not much more than a small folio, but it very clearly outlined cursecraft from a very introductory perspective.  Just by reading the introduction, Joe discovered that he lacked an essential implement if he wanted to work on curse-breaking: an athame.  These dagger-like instruments were the primary tool for curse magic. Once Joe grabbed the tainted items that Puq had left for him, he would then need to seek out a ritualist shop for his athame before he could get to work on breaking the Erlking’s bane.

Tucking the books into his new dim-cuff, Joe headed for the guild shop, hoping Puqmup was there and not in the meeting in the main hall.  His wish turned out to be half right.  Joe looked about and sighed when he spotted no sign of the colorful-haired gnome, until a tapping sound from the wall behind him caught his attention.

Turning around, Joe was facing the large, magical blackboard.  In the corner of the slate slab was a simplistic drawing of the quartermaster; his spikes of hair were even shaded with several chalky hues. The figure was waving at Joe and pointing to a message written on the chalkboard for him.

“Joe.  I have prepped the board for you.  Just place a finger above the etched simulacrum, and you will be able to pick from the items we have recovered so far.  The selected items will be summoned to the countertop for you. I would suggest choosing more than one item, in case you have a problem with your first pick.  Also, make sure each item is returned to its containment case whenever you are not actively working on it.  Try to limit your exposure to the curse as much as possible.  The board will record your selections.  There is nothing you need to do other than make your picks.  Good luck - Puq”

“Well, that’s cool,” Joe uttered.  He touched the slate and watched as a list of items magically scrawled across the board.  The number of items was much longer than he had expected; clearly, the guild had already been busy rounding up the tainted treasures.  Thankfully, beside each name was a brief description of the item's abilities.

Puq had said he could keep the item he broke the curse on, so Joe, using his [Casting Claw] and a bit of chalk, began to make check marks next to the magical items he was interested in.  He realized he could probably just will the board to make the marks for him, but using his signature force hand was just as easy.  It also allowed him to stand back and not have to be touching the surface of the blackboard.

After a minute, Joe realized that he was ignoring certain types of items.  Since he was going to be allowed to keep the item if he could break its curse, he was only interested in items that would suit him. He really wasn’t looking for a weapon; he was happy with his claws and [Haybreaker] for now.  Armor, too, was not high on his list of wants.  With a thought, he had the board remove those two options, then wiped out shields, too, a moment later.  His [Grimoiriguard] and [Hide of the Hunter] combo was pretty effective already.

Potions went next.  Even though Joe was amazed that any vial had survived Taylyn’s ruinous detonation,  if he was going to spend all the effort to remove the curse, he didn’t want his prize to be a one-use consumable.

Next, Joe realized that out of the items left, he wouldn’t be able to use the majority of them for a very long time.  Most had level or attribute requirements that he was nowhere near yet. Another filtering brought the list down to a meager five options.

Puq had not specified how many he could take, but Joe had planned on taking two or three: enough that he could switch to a new item if he messed up and ruined the first item he was working on, yet not too many that he came off as greedy or careless with these corrupted relics.   Joe looked at the descriptions to see which ones he might want if he could break the curse.

These items were significantly less powerful than the majority of the relics Joe had ruled out.  Though on the plus side, the simpler base enchantments might make it easier for him to uncurse the item.

Even though he was far more comfortable in bare feet nowadays, Joe knew he should grab the slippers.  The two abilities were ones that Joe already had, [Whisperstep], or was about to learn, the LeafFall ward.  Knowing the structure of these powers might be just the trick he needed to isolate the curse inside the footwear.  Joe checked off their entry on the board, not as a prize he actually wanted but as a good first item to experiment on.

Of the five items, the one he really wanted for himself was the geode.  Hands down.  Cones of gravity magic sounded way too awesome to pass up.

Joe was pretty sure the extradimensional space was going to be a pain in the ass to work around.  He left that one for Vexor to play with.

The undead flute was not something he wanted to have ... or even mess around with.

That just left the amulet.  Losing skill ranks seemed like a terrible waste.  A person could leave some attribute points unallocated, but Joe had a hard time doing that.  Sooner or later, he’d want to spend those points.  While there might be an occasion where Joe needed a skill badly enough that it was worth these drawbacks, he doubted he’d keep the item if he managed to crack the bane off it.  He marked it off to have one more item to experiment on.

He sent an affirming thought to the chalkboard to finalize his picks.  When he turned around, there were three crystal boxes lined up on the countertop beside him.  Each clear case was just big enough to contain the talisman, geode, and the soft shoes. Fine bands of runic containment script were etched into the crystal.  While the sigilwork seemed delicate, Joe could see with [Witchsight] that these containers were masterwork-level craftsmanship.

Maybe once the curses were broken, he’d circle back to the list of more powerful items to see if there was something he wanted for the future, but at the moment, he was excited to begin his work.

As he scooped up the three boxes and was about to leave, a thought hit him.  Joe quickly resorted the board for potions and found the guild shop had half a dozen of [Level Lock Draughts] in stock.  He bought three of them, and then hustled his way back out of the guildhouse.
