Don moved through the lower platforms with careful speed. Most of the platforms he could scale with a jump, utilizing the cavern wall. Some of them required the use of a rope ladder. Don didn’t like that because it made him more vulnerable. However the two times he had to use them he wasn’t attacked. The Grimoids must be on the higher platforms.

The torchlight was flickering up ahead and he could hear the faint sound of drums. It was coming from above. Tu-dum, Tu-dum, Tu-dum. Something was going on up top. Don wanted to know what.

The first spear came whistling out of the dark. Don acted on reflex, slapping it out of the air with Telekinesis. It clattered to the wood near his feet.

Grimoids. Dozens of them. Climbing out of huts, crawling across rope bridges, scampering down rickety ladders like green-skinned rats.

“Alright,” Don muttered. “Let’s go.”

The first one rushed him with a crude axe. Don flicked his claws out and buried them in the thing’s neck. One swipe. Gone.

Two more came at him from the side. One jabbed him with a spear. Don grabbed it mid-lunge and used Telekinesis to yank the other forward. They smashed heads mid-air and crumpled in a heap. Don sliced them up with his claws and they died in a few swipes.

[You have slain [Grimoid Skirmisher – lvl 10] – You have gained experience for defeating an enemy. 24 System Points earned]

[You have slain [Grimoid Skirmisher – lvl 9] – You have gained experience for defeating an enemy. 18 System Points earned]

The fight didn’t pause. More came.

They were shrieking now. The alarm had gone out. Don could hear them calling from above. Scrambling. Repositioning.

He kicked in the door of one of the platform huts and found four more inside. The room was smoky and dim, lit by a fire under a cauldron of burning sludge. They had been waiting in ambush. All of them shot their blowpipes at him. Two of them Don blocked with Telekinesis. The others hit him.

[Health at 97%]

[Debuff: Mild Poisoning… Negated]

Don smirked. His Poison Resistance was making work. And the blowpipe darts felt like mosquito bites. While Don killed the Grimoids, more poured into the hut. Don danced between spearstabs, blowpipe darts and axe swings. He laughed as he spun around and sliced the Grimoids up. The thrill of the fight was getting to him. It was intoxicating, and he didn’t want to stop.

Don dispatched of the remaining half a dozen Grimoids setting up another ambush outside. It took a minute, tops. Then he used Devour on all of the corpses. He didn’t get a lot of Evolution Points per kill, but the Grimoids attacked in swarms.

It’s time to go look for another swarm then. There must be a big one on the top floor.

As he reached the next tier, two more Grimoids were waiting. Don didn’t hesitate. He grabbed a spearhead from his satchel with his mind and fired it into the nearest one’s eye. The other turned and tried to run. Don vaulted over a railing, landed behind it, and gutted it with both claws in a single sweep.

[You have slain [Grimoid Skirmisher – lvl 9] – You have gained experience for defeating an enemy. 22 System Points earned]

[You have slain [Grimoid Hunter– lvl 10] – You have gained experience for defeating an enemy. 25 System Points earned]

More climbing. Rope bridge. Creaking planks. The swamp was far below now. The air was smelling less like sulphur and more like smoke. The air was thick with it, making it harder to see. Don wondered if he would unlock heat-vision one of these days.

The platforms were vertical here, stacked like an apartment complex built by drunk termites. Huts leaned into each other. Rope ladders swung. Everything looked ancient and in a way, part of the swamp. Don wondered if these Grimoid had been people once. Now they were little more than mindless monsters.

Don kept moving.

Another pack of Grimoids scuttled into his path. Five of them. The one in the front looked slightly bigger than the rest. Yellow warpaint across the chest. It hissed and flung something—maybe a stink bomb or some swamp alchemy nonsense.

Don didn’t care.

He yanked the bomb back midair with Telekinesis and hurled it at them. It exploded in green smoke. They coughed and flailed. He was on them in seconds.

He slashed one across the throat. Kicked another off the bridge entirely. It screamed all the way down.

The leader tried to retreat, but Don used Telekinesis to grab its leg and yank it off balance. He ran forward, grabbed the stunned creature, and jammed his claws through its chest. The others died just as fast.

[You have slain [Grimoid Skirmisher – lvl 9] – You have gained experience for defeating an enemy. 21 System Points earned]

[You have slain [Grimoid Skirmisher – lvl 9] – You have gained experience for defeating an enemy. 18 System Points earned]

[You have slain [Grimoid Brute– lvl 11] – You have gained experience for defeating an enemy. 28 System Points earned]

One of them was an elite, huh? Some elite….

Don wasn’t impressed. But he was hoping the Grimoids were saving the best for last. He looked up. He was covered in sweat and grime. His energy was topped up at 93% because of Devour, but the inexplicable fatigue that was inherent to exertion was mounting up. He would need to rest soon. He didn’t want to, but he would have to.

“But I still have energy. Better go all out.”

He was surely close to a level. He had massacred so many Grimoids.

He pulled a spear from a corpse and slung it across the next bridge with his mind. It cracked a hut wall. A pair of Grimoids ran out, yapping and confused.

Don sprinted across the bridge and clotheslined both of them with one sweeping Telekinetic push. They went tumbling off into the darkness below.

He kept climbing.

More huts. More rope ladders. More Grimoids. The higher he got, the more came.

Ten. Fifteen. Twenty at a time now.

He started throwing corpses with Telekinesis. One group tried to block a narrow gangway—Don launched a dead Grimoid at them like a cannonball. It smashed into the line, sending them sprawling. He charged into the gap and tore the rest apart in a flurry of claws and strikes.

His body moved without hesitation. His claws sank into everything. His Telekinesis pulled weapons from belts, dragged enemies off balance, shoved entire squads into walls. They barely touched him.

A dart struck his shoulder once. He shrugged it off. His Constitution was too high. The wound barely bled. His Poison Resistance worked well. The poison barely tickled.

Another tried to trap him with a tripwire trap rigged to a weighted log. He sensed the tension before he stepped into it. One glance with his third eye, and he yanked the trigger rope harmlessly with his mind. The trap fired too early and took out two Grimoids hiding in the shadows.

Their own trap. Their own deaths.

The higher platforms were better built. They had small spires now. Ladders guarded with wicker gates. Some kind of wooden battlements. He saw a dozen Grimoids grouped up in formation at one of the bridges. Spears raised. They were ready.

Don climbed the outside of a support beam and came up behind them.

He didn’t even announce himself. Just walked into the first one with claws out. The rest turned in confusion—too late.

He killed seven in ten seconds.

The others scattered. He gave chase. A few tried to climb down ladders. Don pulled them off mid-descent. One fell screaming to the bog. One bounced off a ledge and died with a crunch.

Another darted into a hut and tried to lock the door.

Don didn’t stop. He used Telekinesis to rip the door clean off its hinges and flung it into the creature. It smashed backward, and he was on it. It died gasping.

Finally, he climbed the last thick trunk that led to the highest platform. The wooden stairs spiraled upward like a mangled corkscrew. More torches lit the way. Everything was golden lumber, roped together with vine. The air was thick with the smell of smoke and swamp.

And there it was.

A shanty castle. Just like he’d imagined. Made of gold-hued swampwood, latticed walkways, pulleys, tarps, and towers. A weird swamp goblin palace. Dozens of fires burned across its platforms. Grimoids scurried along the walls and balconies. Some looked like guards, some like priests, some just mad. But these Grimoids looked different.

They were all large, and clearly wore better equipment. Iron-braced shields, swords, crossbows. Don used Identify on one of them.

[Grimoid Hobgoblin, Level 12]

Now that’s a bit better. Maybe these ones will be a challenge.

Drums beat from within.

Don crouched near the outer wall, watching.

There was a dungeon boss inside, Don just knew it. Considering the overall strength of the Grimoids as a species, he figured this was an actually manageable boss, unlike Morshoff.

Don weighed his options. Inside awaited new challenges, probably a boss, potentially some fat loot. He hadn’t killed any boss monsters yet, but surely they would yield some kind of extra reward.

But he was tired. He wasn’t sure how long it had been since he last rested in Vance’s camp. It seemed like a lifetime ago. Had it been a full day? Over a day? He had been so preoccupied with hunting and leveling that he had lost count of time.

Don needed to rest. His body was yearning for it. While his Energy was still high, he could feel that his thoughts were sluggish and his body was starting to develop a mounting ache. Maybe at higher levels he wouldn’t have to sleep, but he was still all too mortal.

Don looked at the shanty castle wistfully before turning away. He would need to find some secluded shack and barricade it. The enemies inside were level 12 at least. The potential boss was probably higher. Don didn’t want to take stupid chances. He needed a power nap.

“But after that…” he muttered. “I’ll storm the castle.”
