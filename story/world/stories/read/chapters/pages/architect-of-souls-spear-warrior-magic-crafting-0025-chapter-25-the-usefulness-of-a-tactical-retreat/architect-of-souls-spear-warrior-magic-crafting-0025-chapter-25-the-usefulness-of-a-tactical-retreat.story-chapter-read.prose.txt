Setting out, Ferris and Jade once again followed the trail towards the chain demons’ fort. He intended to attack it this time. There were no more preparations he could make. At least, none that he had enough qi for.

Ferris and Jade had to fight three groups of demons along the way. Only one of them had a blue frog demon, and this time Ferris was able to skewer it on his spear without needing Jade’s help. The chain demons died slowly but surely to the crossbow, and the new bolts worked well. Ferris didn’t break a single one—even after killing eight chain demons.

Jade happily consumed all the corpses. Ferris would have loved to take them back to his Heartfire, but he didn’t have the time or strength.

The fort was much the same as he’d observed the previous night, with the only major change being the addition of blue frog demons defending it. They hopped along the walls, in and out of the gate, and even were visible atop the four towers.

The oath statue still hung on its hook, above the center of the chain fort. It looked worse than before, with large cracks in the stone. Occasionally something from below would strike it, swinging it around.

Each of the fort’s four towers still blazed with bonfires, lighting up the area enough for Ferris to see much farther than the lightstone allowed. Entering and exiting the fort, groups of two to four demons traveled around.

Ferris spent a while watching the patrols of demons come and go. He couldn’t find any specific pattern, but they would always leave in a different direction than the previous group. While the fort was on a high point, the light from its towers didn’t illuminate that far, especially with the hilly terrain.

Ferris and Jade went to the edge of the light, and then began ambushing and killing the demon groups. Each time, they would hide in the darkness behind a hill, waiting until a group was alone and out of the fort’s view before attacking.

Anytime a demon heard or saw another one fighting, they would rush over. This caused some close calls. One time a second group saw and called over a third Ferris hadn’t seen. He’d had to cut his losses and run away after quickly killing two frog demons, Jade eating one he had stunned while he stabbed the other.

He lost five more bolts culling the demon groups around the edges. None of them broke, but he lost track of two, had to run away after shooting two, and one Jade had eaten inside a corpse by accident.

The slime thought the bolt was delicious—four out of ten—but agreed not to eat any more.

He lost track of how many groups they killed, but it was at least a dozen. The patrols leaving or returning to the fort started to become less frequent. The fighting was exhausting, but it took a while between groups to set up each ambush. His mental body was able to recover remarkably well, although the mental exhaustion was starting to wear on him.

Eventually, there was only a single group of three chain demons visible on the fort’s hill. They stayed within sight of the fort, never leaving the area.

Not much choice but to attack, Ferris thought. He expected there would be some kind of response from the fort, and wanted to be far enough away he could retreat. Carefully, he crept to the limit of his range, lined up his shot, and fired.

Ferris couldn’t help grinning as the bolt went cleanly into a chain demon’s head. He was getting better.

Predictably, the other two screamed, which caused the ones on the tower walls to also scream. Four blue frog demons and four chain demons exited out of the fort’s gate.

Ferris had killed one chain demon in his opening shot—his aim was much better now, although he still missed more than half the time. Seeing the four demon frogs, he immediately backed away from the group he had attacked. Quickly picking up Jade, Ferris ran as far as he could before the frogs caught up.

While retreating, Ferris considered how to deal with the four demon frogs. He’d never fought so many at once before. Just three of the things had injured and almost killed him. At least this time he would have Jade’s help.

Luckily, the frogs started pretty far away, so he was able to get some distance from any chain demons before having to set Jade down and swap weapons to his spear.

Taking his stance, he pointed his spear at the approaching demons. Ferris and Jade had become practiced at a combination attack. Jade would take position behind his feet, while Ferris would wait for one of the frogs to leap at him. Once it had jumped, he would swing his spear to knock the demon into the ground.

Normally, batting an enemy around with the pole of the spear was less effective than stabbing, but this allowed Jade to quickly overwhelm the stunned enemy. Meanwhile, Ferris was free to immediately fight the next one, his spear unhindered with a demon corpse.

Slimes in the wild wouldn’t usually be this dangerous, as they typically just rolled around randomly. The bond allowed Ferris to direct Jade straight at the enemy, precisely when it couldn’t fight back.

This combination attack had been effective for fighting two frogs at once, but wouldn’t work against four. Positioning might help Ferris avoid being overrun. Even then, Jade wouldn’t be able to finish consuming a single demon before the other three attacked Ferris together.

The demons spread out as they approached, as usual trying to attack from all sides. Ferris decided he couldn’t let them fully surround them. Jade was slower than the creatures, so any maneuvering was doomed to fail as the demons simply outpaced them.

Since the frogs were attempting to surround, they had to split up. That’s an opportunity, Ferris thought, and immediately charged. He went for the one on the far left, leaving Jade behind.

All four frog demons leapt forward. They came in spread out before him. Ferris momentum let him reach his target slightly sooner than the others, spear piercing through the demon mid jump. One down.

The farthest demon on the right missed, having been too far away to effectively jump and hit him. The middle two, unfortunately, both slammed into his side—horns piercing deep—right after he had skewered his first target.

One had gone low, the other high. They both dug claws into his flesh and tore short gashes. Ferris spun, raking his spear in a well-practiced motion that both flung the first demon off his spear and knocked away anything holding onto his body. He was bleeding and hurt, but still able to move. Still able to fight.

The two middle demons were flung away, but that had given the final one enough time to leap again. Ferris barely got the shaft of his spear up in time to block the frog’s charge, and then pushed it to the ground.

Jade, who had followed as best as they could, reached him at that point. Ferris directed the slime at the frog at his feet.

Hopefully Jade can handle it, he thought. He hadn’t been able to really stun the demon for Jade, just blocked its charge.

As Ferris turned back to face the other two demons, he saw their horns glistening with his blood. Their claws had only caused superficial scratches, but the holes left by the horns went deep. Perhaps they would have hit a vital organ in his real body, but his mental body had… looser rules. He could keep it moving past the point a physical body could.

Thankfully, both demons had been thrown in a similar direction. Ferris charged them, hoping to avoid giving them space to jump, and getting himself away from the demon Jade was fighting at his feet.

Leveling the spear as he charged, he slammed into one of them, pinning it into the ground. Unfortunately, the spear stuck tight, refusing to budge as the second demon jumped at him.

Letting go of his spear, Ferris caught the demon’s mouth with his left arm—not the best idea, as the thing started to chew. With his right hand, he pulled a bolt from his quiver and stabbed the creature in its eyes repeatedly until it stopped moving.

Looking back at Jade, the last demon had managed to escape the slime’s grasp. It was severely injured, limping away, but still able to move. Ferris hadn’t been watching, so Jade had no way to track the enemy after being shook off.

As soon as Ferris looked over, Jade knew exactly where it was through the bond. The slime rolled directly over and engulfed it, finishing the battle.

Clutching at his wounds, Ferris didn’t have time to rest. The chain demons would still be chasing them, and he doubted he could accurately fire the crossbow with his injuries. Jade began immediately eating the remaining corpses, while Ferris recovered his spear and checked on the enemy’s approach.

Thankfully, they were still a good distance away. The battle with the four frog demons had been intense, but hadn’t taken very long. He had a couple minutes to bandage the worst of his wounds with torn strips of his shirt.

Ferris once more picked up Jade and ran off into the darkness, limping slightly. He didn’t stop until he was out of sight, and then circled around the fort at a slower pace.

Resting, Ferris watched from afar.

He wasn’t bleeding as much as before, but that wasn’t saying much. It hurt to move.

Once again, wounds that could have been fatal in the real world were reduced to merely very painful. The four chain demons that had left the fort and the three from the group he’d first attacked had gathered up in front of the gate. He had nine demon reinforced bolts, plus the five weaker pure qi bolts, remaining. He might be able to kill six chain demons with that, although he didn’t trust his aim right now.

Ferris was hurt. Ideally, he would like to retreat and recover. In the real world his injuries would take weeks to heal, but a mental construct could recover much faster, especially with sleep.

He might be able to kill the demons he could see, with some luck, but if another group of blue frogs emerged, he would likely die.

Critically, he eyed the oath statue. It was still being attacked, but it didn’t look much worse than when he’d arrived. It had a couple surface cracks, but there weren’t any pieces missing. His lack of a reliable time frame made everything risky, but rushing into death wouldn’t help either—especially if he had the time to wait.

He could wait nearby, rest while watching the fort. This would be safer, but it probably wouldn’t help him recover much. He had been running and fighting for a while now. Waiting near the fort would require constant vigilance, and he was already weary.

In the end, his tiredness won out. The statue didn’t look like it would crumble immediately, and pushing himself beyond his capabilities would only lead to stupid mistakes.

With a sigh—part regret, part pain—Ferris took Jade and headed back towards his Heartfire. Luckily, they had culled most of the traveling groups of demons, so the way should be clear.

Still, he couldn’t help taking one last look at the fort, its four towers burning brightly in his soul. A challenge he couldn’t abide.

A tactical retreat may be smart, but it didn’t feel good.

When he returned, it would be time to end this.
