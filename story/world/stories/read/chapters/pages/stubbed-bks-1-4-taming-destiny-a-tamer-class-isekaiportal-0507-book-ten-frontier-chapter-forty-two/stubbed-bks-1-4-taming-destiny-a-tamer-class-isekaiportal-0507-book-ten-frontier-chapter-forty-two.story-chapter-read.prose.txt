I stride down towards the edge of the forest. The letters painstakingly written by me in collaboration with one or more of my allies seem to burn a hole in the pouch on my belt. I didn’t put them in my Inventory because the seal I used to close them holds hints of my magical signature, and I’m not sure whether they’ll stand up to the Energy-consuming nature of my Inventory. The letter I took from Nicholas once did, but these are wax seals with magic added, not purely magical ones.

Down on the forest line, my companions are waiting. I decided to call them here out of consideration for Sirocco, who prefers to perch above people’s heads, and Noir, who prefers to lurk in the shadows.

“Hi Ivor,” I smile as the dragon-like alcaoris extends his head from where he’s been basking in the last rays of the sun, his white scales almost blinding. He nudges me hard enough to knock me over if I didn’t have superhuman strength, and I scratch at his crest in response to his wordless demand. Clearly he’s here to see his brother off.

Not wanting to be left out, Sirocco flutters to my shoulder, preening my hair as she demands scratches in her turn. I card my fingers through the feathers on her breast.

What about me? Noir complains, his head extending from a puddle of deep shadow under several densely-packed trees.

I sigh but obligingly create an extra arm with Transformation combined with Flesh-Shaping so I can scratch him too. He closes his eyes and presses into my hand, pleasure rippling over the Bond.

Temporarily putting aside my objective for bringing them together, we just enjoy the moment. It’s been a while since I spent time with these three in particular – Sirocco spends most of her time soaring above the forest, keeping an eye on our hunting parties and looking out for other potential threats. Noir and Ivor aren’t quite as dedicated as she is to their duties, but Ivor still works with Sirocco during the day to keep an eye, and Noir takes over the relay in the night time since both his colouring and Skills are better suited to it.

While stroking them, I take the time to inspect them. Sirocco hasn’t changed much physically – though are my eyes deceiving me or are there flickers of flame at the edge of her feathers?

I squint and pull on my Fire-Shaping to feel for the fire's presence. I pay particular attention to where my fingers card through the feathers on her breast, little sparks actually jumping out to sizzle on my skin – painlessly, due to my own fire resistance.

That’s not just static electricity, I muse, slowing my fingers’ movement and dipping into her Core space. There, I see a lot more change. Even in the last few weeks, there have been significant developments to her internal matrix. The bare-bones structure that used to extend two loops to opposite sides, and another four lines out in a cross shape perpendicular to the loops, has now grown far more detailed.

The loops have started looking like wings with tendrils of fiery-gold filling them like feathers, while the lines have thickened and grown their own collection of Energy channels at their bases like feet. Curiously, two of them have thickened a lot more than the other two – obviously mimicking her real limbs.

I pull out of her Core space, wondering how far she is from Evolution. There’s probably more development to go, I muse. I didn’t see enough pressure being put on the Core to make it explode in the way that Tier three Evolutions seem to need. Still, the increased draw of Energy that all the little tendrils will give her is surely the first step towards it.

Curious to see how the alcaorises are doing, I check out Ivor next. He’s grown significantly bigger than when we first arrived – the high Energy in the environment here is agreeing with him. His internal matrix is also coming on well, the square-based pyramid-like structure thickening and starting to extend channels to the centre. He’s not close to Evolution yet, but I’m satisfied that he’s well on the way.

As for Noir, his development has come on significantly.

“What level are you now?” I ask him incredulously.

Level six, he tells me proudly. And justifiably – though the first few levels are the easiest to get, and in a Common Class they’re easier than my own Epic Class, it’s been much less than a year since he gained the Class stone. And only four months since he reached twenty points in each of his stats naturally – at least the long voyage was good for giving him the chance to train each of them without interruption apart from the odd battle here or there.

His physical form has grown since he gained it, though he’s still significantly smaller than Ivor. He’s not necessarily weaker anymore, though – I can feel the strength of his muscles, and his increased fitness is clear when I spread my mana through his system. Superhuman Strength and Constitution does that to someone, apparently even when they’re not human.

The increase to his Intelligence and Wisdom has also been evident – his mental speech is clearer, and he’s been more thoughtful about his actions. He hasn’t forgotten to practise his Skills either – when I check his Core space, I see that not only has his Core grown larger and his internal matrix continued developing in complexity, but the patterns representing his Skills look far more complex. Overall, lots of visible improvement.

It does seem that his Class Skills are limited to the palms of his ‘hands’ in the internal matrix – when he selected Keen Eye at his level five Skill selection, it appeared nestled in the palm of his third hand. Though I’m interested to notice that there’s the hint of something near his Core space that might develop into another arm, In which case, it might be less limited than I thought.

Disappear now makes it hard for anyone but specialised scouts to see him when he’s using it, even if those with a high Willpower, like me, are still able to resist the distraction that it offers. Quick Grab is increasingly subtle, and paired with Stealth and Shadow-Blend, two more Skills that he’s either earned or absorbed, few are able to catch him in the act. At least the non-Class Skills have proven able to integrate with other parts of his internal matrix, and some of them are quite firmly welded into it now, showing just how he’s improved them.

Feeling a hint of mischief from Noir as I pull out of his Core space, I automatically check the pouch I’m wearing on my belt. It doesn’t hold much – I keep most things in the safety of my Inventory – but in this case I have the letters from my allies. Or, at least, I had.

Now, I feel inside the pouch and find no letters, but something…soft. And sticky.

I pull my fingers out and make a face as the stench hits me right in the nose.

“Noir!” I growl, glaring at him. So much for increased Wisdom. Unfortunately, it seems that Noir has gained a taste for pranks.

He dances backwards, that mischief turning into strong amusement. Ivor, watching from the side, is also amused, though I sense a hint of anticipation there. Ever since I said that Noir would be going but Ivor wouldn’t, he’s been sulking – no doubt, he’d like to see Noir being brought down a peg.

I am very strongly tempted to grab Noir and smear this muck all over him, but, knowing that I’m about to send him off as an envoy to other expedition leaders, I refrain. Still, I’m not going to let it go completely unremarked.

“Fine – you can be the last to get the presents I was going to give you,” I tell him as I use Flesh-Shaping to clear the stinky stuff from my pouch and my hand. At least with magic, there’s no permanent harm done. “And I’m not giving you the Skill stone I was planning on.”

No! Noir immediately yelps. Please! I’ll be good!

I fix him with a glare.

“You haven’t returned the letters you took yet,” I point out. “That would be a good start.”

Looking chastised, he pulls the scrolls out of his Inventory and they suddenly appear in his increasingly dextrous front paw.

I take them with a warning glance to him and then tuck them back in my pouch. Then, seeing how much like a kicked puppy he looks. I sigh and affectionately reach out to scratch him briefly below the chin.

“Little thief,” I murmur. “You’re getting far too good at that for my health.”

Noir perks up at the backhand compliment.

You didn’t even notice, did you?

“Hey, it doesn't count – I wasn’t paying attention,” I protest. Then I make a face. “But dung? Really? Why not a stone or something – that would prove it well enough, wouldn’t it?”

Not as funny, Noir retorts.

I snort. Of course that would be his reasoning.

“Alright, Sirocco,” I start.

Hey! Noir interrupts. Thought I was first!

I narrow my eyes at him.

“You’re last. But since you returned the scrolls, I’ll still give you the Skill stone.”

Noir subsides, grumbling wordlessly under his breath. Ivor nudges him with his head, smugness rippling down the Bond. Noir nudges back, harder. Disappointed there weren’t more consequences for the prank, Ivor returns the movement with even more force.

“Settle down!” I warn them as it threatens to turn into a full-on fight. I prevent my own amusement from travelling down the Bond. Times like this remind me of just how young they are. Still, I have faith that Noir will be able to carry out the task I’m setting him – when he focuses, he’s very competent.

The two brothers do settle down, so I turn back to the bird on my shoulder.

“Can you hop onto that branch, please,” I ask, pointing towards one that’s at about shoulder-height on me.

She quickly does so, turning to face me. I pull out a piece of leather that Loran’s brother tanned for me, his sister sewed together, and Master Mirena enchanted. They aren’t master-works by any means, but I saw in a demonstration that they can deflect even reasonably powerful direct attack Skills – though the more powerful the Skill, the quicker the enchantments will be exhausted. And incidental damage like a ricochet would still get through. They’ll still have to be careful, but at least it’s something.

Carefully, I help Sirocco into the vest. It goes over her head with a thin piece over her back to keep it on – the majority of the fabric is in the front, protecting her breast and stomach.

Even though they’re only made by journeymen in the craft – apart from Master Mirena – I can see the care and attention to detail put into them. The edges of the dark brown leather have been well formed so they won’t chafe and the material itself is supple and lighter than normal. Runes shine in silver thread, and shards of Cores glimmer at key points to act as batteries. Dominating the front of the vest, however, is a big and bold Titanbend crest, below which is an almost equally-sized red flag – the colour used to symbolise the desire for peace among the Moriaxar.

“Lizil has assured me that you can still use your Fire Wing with this, though you should try to limit how frequently you use it, as it will drain some of the charge from the enchantments. And, before you approach a human settlement, make sure to rest for a day and a night in an area with decent Energy density to ensure that the Core shards are fully charged.”

Fine, Sirocco agrees, tilting her head one way and then the other to get a good look at herself wearing it. Then she flaps powerfully and lifts a little from the branch to test its weight. Pulls at me.

“Too much to fly properly?” I ask in some concern. She sends me a wave of negation.

No. But it will take me more time to fly.

“That’s fine,” I tell her. “Take the time you need. But this should make you safer. However, if you’re in danger, reach out to me.” I turn to look at Noir too. “That goes for you as well. You shouldn’t be going so far that you’re completely unable to contact me, though it might become more difficult to actually speak. So, if you need me to bring you back here, just send me a strong sense of urgency through the Bond and I’ll teleport you back. Between successfully completing your mission and keeping you safe, I’d far rather the latter.”

He sends me a sense of sober acknowledgement – playful Noir might be but he knows the dangers that they might encounter well enough.

After ensuring that Sirocco’s vest fits her well enough, I fasten the scrolls for House Stagrun and House Woodfell into the specially-designed pouch made for them on the small strip of leather on her back. It seals so it’s waterproof, and the protection of the leather should mean that if something tries to grab her from behind, the scrolls will still be defended as much as Sirocco herself is.

Once things are settled for Sirocco, I move on to Noir. First, I give him the Skill stone – Wind Shield. It’s a common mage’s shield, even for non-wind-shapers, and should work pretty well for Noir. It will help reduce air resistance as well as offering a shield that will deflect attacks as long as they aren’t too powerful – and he has enough mana to power it. I would give one to Sirocco, but I’m concerned that it might mess up her internal matrix.

Then we get the vest sorted. His is darker brown than the Sirocco’s given that he’ll be traveling during the night, and I show him how to activate its stealth feature – something that reduces the glimmer of the Core shards and the crest significantly.

“But don’t forget to activate it when you approach the Houses,” I warn him, tucking the scrolls for House Greentoe, House Rockfell, and House Goldmine into the larger pouch on his back.

I feel a pang of sorrow from Ivor, so I reach over to give him a gentle caress. He feels left behind, and understandably so. But, unfortunately, he isn’t able to speak yet – even if he had the tattoos like Noir and Sirocco, he wouldn’t be able to focus his mind well enough to speak through them.

“I’m sure I’ll still need envoys later when you’ve Evolved,” I reassure him. He accepts that, but I sense that he’s still sad about it. “And, anyway, I’ll need you to do the work of three while they’re gone,” I point out. “We’d be left vulnerable if all of our scouts flew away.”

Ivor perks up at that, as I suspected he would. He likes to feel useful – we all do. And it’s not like I’m being untruthful, though I’m perhaps exaggerating a little – we do have lots of scouts on the ground.

After a few more words of warning, checking that the vests are sitting well, and a final scratch for both of them, I watch them take wing and fly into the rapidly-darkening sky.

Be safe, I tell them to speed them on their way, longing stabbing through me. Ivor takes flight too, determined to accompany them for the first step of their journey.

They respond with waves of acknowledgement, and Noir even does a little fancy loop in the air. And then all I can see is their silhouettes that eventually fade into the darkness.

Leaves crackle as something large approaches me, but I’m not concerned – I can feel exactly who it is.

I wish I could have gone too, Bastet says with a wistful tone, rubbing her head against my shoulder.

“So do I,” I sigh with a hint of regret. “Alas, both of us are needed here.” Then I turn my head, looking at the large form that’s just emerged from the bushes. “Ah, Romeo – got a moment? I wanted to talk about something.”
