Aodhán watched the High Elder study him for a long moment before finally speaking.

"Are you sure you want to do this?"

Aodhán exhaled slowly. "I've lived with it for a long time. Long enough that I barely even think about it anymore." A sad smile touched his lips. "It's become normal for me not to feel anything when I think about my past."

His gaze hardened. "But I'm no longer in Lutia. I don't need to punish myself like this anymore." He shook his head. "This is a new chapter of my life. I want to start better. Start on my own terms."

Zarxan nodded slowly. "I understand. But removing a technique like the veil you described won't be easy. It will hurt."

Aodhán nodded.

"I expect it to." He drew one final breath, then let it out. "Do it."

Zarxan regarded him for a moment longer. Then he stretched a hand to Aodhán, runic irises flaring slightly even as ghostly-white lightning burst across his fingers.

As sharp as blade. As malleable as liquid.

It flowed across his fingers until it converged on a single one—spinning to a rhythm only him could hear.

Zarxan pressed the finger softly against the side of Aodhán's head, and Aodhán gasped—breath hitching at the paradoxical sensations.

Hot. Cold.

He shivered.

An instant later, Aodhán felt something tear in his mind—unravel maybe. He wasn't too sure.

But when the trapped emotions came rushing into his mind—grief, pain, fear, loss, nostalgia, love—Aodhán knew he had been set free.

The emotions flooded in, crashing through him like a dam broken free. They overwhelmed him immediately, each as sharp and raw as the last time he'd felt them.

Each as beautiful.

His throat closed off. His voice cracked. He tried to hold the emotions back.

He failed.

Tears pooled in his eyes, and after a bit of hesitation, Aodhán let himself feel it all.

He let every buried emotion rise, crest, and crash over him.

And for the first time in years...

Aodhán welcomed them.

By the time he finally opened his eyes, Zarxan was gone, leaving him alone with the storm inside his heart.

He basked in the peace that followed, shuddering as the waves of emotion slowly ebbed and flowed.

Drawing a slow breath, Aodhán sat in the darkness, his Ideal humming softly within his spirit.

He remained there for nearly an hour before his attention drifted to the mantra, its voice louder now than ever before.

Freer.

I GAIN STRENGTH TO GIVE IT.

"But to whom?" he asked himself. When he was in Lutia, he'd had so many people to empower. So many people he'd wanted to raise to the top alongside himself. But now, it seemed his very presence had already done that to the Storm sect.

He didn’t understand much of what Zarxan had said about the Origin Plane, nor did he understand how his presence affected its mechanics, but he got the basic gist of it. His initiation into the sect will increase both its political and magical standing. The Origin Plane would grow stronger because of that, and some other affinities will suffer for it.

Hence the need for balance—a balance that put his life at risk.

Well, at least the reason for war is a more logical one. Aodhán thought to himself, sighing as Varéc stirred within him. Images and feelings of comfort surged through his psychic bond, and Aodhán cracked a small smile.

Slowly, tiredly, he pushed himself to his feet, shifted the bed to a corner of the room to create more space, and then summoned Varéc.

As usual, Varéc rushed out of his spirit in a cloud of storm and chaos essence, growling contentedly as he tested the new space, his wings flaring and tail swishing excitedly when the room contained him without sustaining any damage.

He growled again, and Aodhán chuckled. "We still have to do something about this size of yours. Even in Gordon, I doubt all the rooms will be like this."

Varéc sniffed in disagreement, images of large rooms rushing through their psychic link.

Aodhán thought about it for a moment before shrugging. "You're probably right. If I do get proclaimed as a transmigrant, my room will most likely be larger than this."

Varéc swished his tail in excitement. Aodhán smiled. "That doesn't invalidate the perk, though. I still think it's a great one to have."

Varéc snorted, settling down on the wooden floor in contentment, and with an amused headshake, Aodhán joined him, a tired exhale escaping his lips as he leaned against Varéc, his long white hair resting against crackling scales.

Varéc growled low then, his tone serious. Aodhán shook his head warily. "I don't know what to do, Varéc. I'd hoped to get some peace and quiet here in Gordon, but apparently, that was wishful thinking."

Varéc bared his teeth in a snarl, a stream of emotions rushing through their psychic link, and Aodhán paused, his eyes widening slightly as he contemplated Varéc's suggestion. But he’d barely given it more than a few seconds of thought before one major problem popped up.

Where would he go if he ran away?

Unlike Lutia, cultivators couldn’t just live anywhere in Gordon. They had to live in sects, which meant he would be a stranger anywhere he went. And his bloodline, unfortunately, didn’t afford him the ability to blend in with the mundanes. He had no idea what fate awaited wandering cultivators, nor could he go back to Lutia on his own even if he wished.

But those were all excuses because Aodhán didn’t want to leave.

Aside from the fact that he no longer had to hide his status and strength here, having thousands of storm cultivators all around him was extremely comforting. The atmosphere alone filled him with a sense of security and belonging, something he doubted he would find anywhere else other than in the origin plane.

The truth was that the storm sect was a haven, perfectly curated for the storm affinity, and he couldn’t imagine a better place to forge himself and grow in strength. Nevertheless, it would have been made more perfect if he could have done so without any drama or violence.

Unfortunately, neither of those things seemed to be in his immediate future.

Sighing once more, he whispered, "I don't want to run away, Varéc. To be honest, I like what I've seen of this place so far. The ambience, the people, and—" He raised a finger to his eye level and bent it.

At first, nothing seemed to happen. Then a needle of raw chaos burst into the room, nearly engulfing his finger before reality snapped shut.

"Did you see that?" Aodhán asked, smiling. "I pulled chaos directly into reality. The Origin plane is so close here, which means more inspirations, less interference."

Varéc purred as the line of chaos dispersed, then glanced at Aodhán, who had snuggled even deeper into him.

"I want to stay here," Aodhán said again, his voice soft. "There's so much to gain here. Zarxan might help me. Do you know how many people would kill to have a Calamity even speak to them?"

Varéc growled softly, suggesting a number, and Aodhán chuckled. "Yeah, Varéc. A lot of people, and I might get one for free if I stay here."

Varéc didn’t say anything else, and for a few minutes, silence reigned. There was not a single sound save for the light crackle of electricity across Varéc’s scales and Aodhán's slowing breathing.

He hadn't slept a wink in over thirty hours, and in the quiet of darkness, his exhaustion was finally catching up to him. His breathing slowed to a steady rhythm, and in that moment, if anyone had looked at him, they wouldn't have believed the amount of blood he had on his hands.

Varéc stared at his bond for a long moment, golden irises gleaming in the darkness, before opening his mouth to speak. Only guttural sounds came out. But it was a start. Soon, he would finally be able to speak, and then he would finally be able to express to Aodhán just how much he loved him.

Feeling a pulse of love, Varéc stretched a wing to cover his bond and settled his head to encircle Aodhán.

Darkness settled over them both, and as Aodhán drifted to sleep, a hazy thought hummed within him. It had been ten months since he'd arrived in AEFLYM. Eleven since his last birthday.

It would be a year soon, and then, he would finally be an adult.

He would finally be eighteen.

Fortunately, the thought dispersed before he realized he would have no family or friends to celebrate it with.
