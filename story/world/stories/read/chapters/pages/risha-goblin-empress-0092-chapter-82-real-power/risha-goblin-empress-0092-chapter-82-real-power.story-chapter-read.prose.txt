The Dragon’s eyes narrowed as she looked between the Scaled Mist-Prowler and Risha. “I see you have chosen your masters, Sira.”

Mist-Prowler simply bowed her head, then stepped back, leaving the two women in the center of the room.

“What do you want from me, Risha?” Elderoa lifted her head, her eyes narrowed.

Risha’s blood raced, and she smiled wide, revealing her many sharp teeth, “Just to meet you. I am glad you do not want to kill me, at least not yet.”

“What I want doesn’t matter.” Elderoa let out a sigh, then walked to an empty chair on the stage. She sat down, lounging in the chair as she examined Risha. “You are an outsider, and you cannot understand the Scaled you have under your heel. I am here to make sure they are not crushed.”

Risha stayed silent, watching the Dragon like she watched Risha. “Very selfless of you.”

Elderoa snorted, “Hardly.” She sat up straight, her pressure descending on the room to remind Risha who she spoke with. “I saw my brothers and sisters turned into slaves. Our children were killed, and we were subject to the whims of a Spark. You have no idea what rules they broke to do that to us. Trading one foreign slave-master for another… I have no reason to like it.” Elderoa leaned towards Risha, “You of all creatures should understand that, Goblin.”

“I am not a slave master.” Risha growled.

Elderoa raised her eyebrows and tilted her head, “Not yet, no. Those who choose to follow you,” she glanced at Mist-Prowler, “have a trust that I do not. Those who have suffered the most can become the greatest of villains. The First Servant proved that all too well.”

“First servant?”

Elderoa didn’t answer, instead glaring at the Goblin Grand Mother, “So what will you do, Risha? Kill me?” She smiled, “You can try.”

Risha lifted her head, “Risha does not have to prove herself to you. My people feed yours, teach yours, and offer you a hand to rise. I am not a slave-master. I am the Mother Of Monsters, and I will protect you and your people, even if you hate me, Dragon.” Risha stepped towards the dragon, causing her to tense. “I will not kill you. I will offer you a chance to prove your words. Join my council of elders.”

Elderoa blinked in surprise, relaxing slightly. “I will not be your servant.”

“Good. Risha does not need a servant.”

Elderoa narrowed her eyes, “There is some trick to your words.”

Risha nodded, “Yes. There is. It benefits us both.”

“It benefits you to give me more power?”

“I believe it does.” Risha smiled, then turned around as she put her mask back on. “You have time to decide. But I must return home soon. Decide before then.”

Mist-Prowler fell into step behind Risha as she walked to the door.

“I want more than that.”

Risha paused, her hand on the door. “What?”

“Give me real power. Let me lead this city.”

Risha turned around, looking at the Dragon. She then turned to Mist-Prowler.

Mist-Prowler had a calculating look in her eyes. After a small moment, she gave Risha a small nod.

“You will work with one of my Elders.”

Elderoa gave a slow smile, “I can live with that.”

Risha laughed, unable to help herself. Maybe politics wasn’t as boring as she thought.

Risha pushed the door open and left the building, Mist-Prowler a step behind.

The two walked in silence for some time, and Risha only spoke once they were far from the hall.

“Elder Oltak will have his hands full.” Risha mused, her eyes moving to the moon in the sky. Clouds covered half of it, distorting its light ever so slightly.

“She is dangerous, but she has the loyalty of the people here, and she is honorable.” Mist-Prowler said.

Risha glanced at the woman as her form shifted back into that of a Grey Lupus. “You met her already?”

Mist-Prowler nodded, “She has been helping with the reconstruction of the city, a leader to the Scaled already. Your appointment as the Lady of the City would just make it official.”

“I see.”

“She reminds me of you.” Mist-Prowler finished, watching Risha with an unreadable expression.

“A high compliment.” Risha held back a smile, then she paused as a notification lit up her vision.

For Reaching Level 60, you gain the skill [Scaled Wings]

Your Spirit Evolutions have increased from 5/5 -> 5/6

Another skill, and another person to be chosen as a Spirit. Risha had a guess as to what her new skill was, but the choice of her next Spiritual evolution filled her mind.

She glanced at the kobold walking beside her. Mist-Prowler would be a good choice, one of the few who walked beside her always.

It wasn’t a decision she should make in haste.

“Risha?”

Risha focused herself, and realized that she’d stopped walking. “Nothing, just-”

She paused as a Gray Lupus Kobold wearing one of Elder Kulos’s cloaks sprinted towards them. The kobold kneeled on the wooden walkway, huffing with exertion.

“Grand Mother. Mystic Klu has returned. He’s injured.”

[Pack Leader: Gray] Her body filled with agility as she broke into a run.

The two kobolds followed her as she ran through the city, not a thought for the waters below her.

The guards at the healing tents raised their spears in surprise as she approached, then quickly lowered them when they recognized the kobold running with them.

Risha burst into the tent where Tya stood over a sleeping orc.

The old healer looked up at Risha, then to the two kobolds behind her. “Out, you two. Grand Mother only.”

The kobolds followed the order without question, but Risha didn’t have eyes for them as she approached the sleeping orc.

An arm gone. She’d seen similar sights too many times, and yet it still twisted her heart.

Klu stirred, his eyes fluttering open to focus on Risha.

“Mother. I have returned.”

Risha kneeled next to him, and placed her hand on his forehead, [Mother’s Care] flowing through him. It could not bring back what was lost. “I’m glad.”

“It was a battle to be remembered in song.” Klu said, his eyes unfocusing, “I ripped his wings from him, taking him from the sky, and we turned the mountain red with our blood.” He smiled with bloody teeth, “I tore his throat with my teeth. His flesh was sweet on my tongue.”

Risha matched his smile, “A good battle indeed.”

His eyes focused back on her, “I kept my promise. I will see Shasha again.”

Risha looked up to Tya. The old goblin gave a soft smile and a nod.

Risha let out a breath, “Good. I will need you again soon.”

“Of course, Mother. I am your loyal Spirit.” Klu reached up, and covered her arm with his own battle-scarred hand. “The Angel was only the first, and I will sate my bloodlust once again.”

Risha knelt down to kiss his forehead. “Rest. I need my strongest child to be ready when the time comes.”

“Yes, Mother.” Klu’s eyes fluttered closed, but his hand didn’t let go of her.

“How bad was it?” Risha asked Tya.

“He was one step away from the spirit realm when he walked through my door.” Tya answered as she pulled some herbs from the wall. “He is lucky that my evolution gave me the tools to heal him.”

“We are all lucky.”

“Indeed.”

“The vultures will pay.” Risha growled, her free hand clenching in anger. “That pigeon who thinks herself untouchable will feel my blade in her heart.”

“Risha,” Tya’s tone made Risha focus on the healer. “We have just won one war. Do not leave through the mountains again so soon.”

Risha unclenched her fist, “I know. I am angry.”

“Of course, you carry the world on your shoulders.” Tya snorted, “You should take that weight off sometime.”

“Not now, Tya.” Risha’s voice was sharp, and the Healer paused, before nodding.

“Apologies, Mother.”

Risha let out a breath, and smoothed back Klu’s hair. His closed eyes seemed to relax with the motion, “No, you are right. It is too much. Can I have some time?”

“Of course, Mother.”

Tya bowed, then left the tent, leaving Risha alone with the sleeping Klu.

Risha gently removed the orc’s hand holding her arm, then pulled up a cushion to rest beside the old orc.

Her first lessons in mercy came from the Mystic, lessons that have defined her as she became the Mother Of All Monsters. He taught her the sword, so that she could protect what was hers, and he guided her when she felt lost.

Again, he fought for her, to protect her from her enemies. And it cost him.

Her gaze stayed on the missing arm. If he wasn’t already evolved into a Spirit, she could bring it back for him.

She sighed, and closed her eyes.

Movement made her reopen her eyes.

Glo walked in silently, and sat beside her.

She leaned against him, and he held her quietly.
