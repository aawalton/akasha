Meri couldn’t believe what she was seeing.

She had always prided herself on deriving her sense of truth and reality from what she herself could observe and sense in the world around her. Evidence that could be replicated and tested, felt and seen. That was the driving force of life and progress.

More than the gods that so many clung to for relief and direction, to her the mysteries and miracles of the natural world were the foundation of all.

That was the nature of a scientist, and ever since she had first witnessed her grandfather create life-saving medicines and empowering elixirs, she had been chasing and holding onto that identity with burning fervor.

So how could Eik Magnasen continue to break the limits of her understanding of what was possible for a single person to do? And how could he be so annoyingly nonchalant, and not to mention confident, about it?

In alchemy, it was a commonly accepted truth that it was not possible to replace the spent energy of a mixture. The magical ingredients provided it with the remnants of natural power and something close enough to a will to react and lash out at attempts to manipulate its properties.

Overcoming this will and influencing the substance with one’s aura in a direction that would allow that power to be granted to an imbiber was the basics of creating empowering elixirs. As a method it was tried and true, perfected to the point of art. And the reason it was done in this way and had been refined by countless alchemists through generations was because this was the only way it could be done.

That was just a fact of the world. Success with this method had been observed billions and billions of times, while success with any other method had been observed zero times.

Yes, it was certainly true that the magical ingredient in this particular recipe came from the acting alchemist’s own body, but that didn’t change the truth of it. Regardless of the origin of an ingredient, there was no living spiritual reservoir present to accept any new energy.

In a normal Awakened, spiritual energy was generated, stored, circulated, and manipulated with the use of the physical and spiritual body in concert. “Awakening” was, in the simplest terms, the transformation of the biological body into a vessel capable of harnessing the spiritual energy in that way.

Something as scattered as a clump of plant matter, animal matter, minerals, and whatever else mashed into a semi-cohesive paste had no capacity for this. All it could do was react to stimulation according to some kind of primitive preservation instinct vaguely left over from the origin of the magical material.

That was why, once the spiritual energy within the mixture was spent, it would become inert, and no amount of poking and prodding with spiritual booster injections could rekindle activity. There were simply no place for any such additional energy to go. It would be like trying to wake up a rock on the side of the road. Meaningless.

So what, exactly, was it that she was witnessing right now?

Eik was maintaining three different, opposing flows of aura simultaneously. That alone required one to split the mind into several partitions to work independently of each other—a feat that required inhuman levels of concentration.

But what was even more puzzling was the fact that the mixture seemed to remain intact despite receiving relatively absurd amounts of energy from Eik’s supply. Frankly, it should have been mush on the ceiling by now.

It was an act of shame to disturb another alchemist while they were deep in the cauldron, and there was no doubt that her grandfather would scold her fiercely for it once it was all over, but Meri just couldn’t help her own curiosity. “But I don’t understand… How? How can you do that?” she called.

Surprising them all, rather than clenching his jaw in annoyance at the distraction, Eik looked up at them questioningly, the immaculately controlled flow of energy continuing unbroken and unwavering all the while. “What?”

Doing her best to ignore a glare from the guild master, pushed through the other alchemists and peeked out from behind the protective screen. “The mixture has nowhere for all of that energy to go! It has no capacity to absorb it! How are you doing it?”

“Oh,” he said with a grin. “If there was no vessel, then all I had to do was create one, right?”

As her own jaw grew slack and fell open, she caught glimpses out of the corner of her eye of the rest of the watching alchemists displaying similar reacts to the outrageous man’s words.

“But th… that’s not possible,” someone breathed quietly behind her. She was inclined to agree. Well, under any other circumstances, that was.

“He’s going to do it,” she muttered. And she felt the others shift behind her as they came to the same conclusion. They were witnessing history being written in front of their eyes. “He’s going to do it!”

For a few more tense seconds, Eik worked silently to resolve the final adjustments. It was fascinatingly silent and calm for the work of an X-rank. Perhaps not all events involving an X-ranker and their power had to destroy half a planet.

Freezing in place, Eik watched and then, after a moment, stood up with a sigh of relief. “I’ve done it,” he muttered, almost just to himself.

Meri gasped in chorus with her grandfather and the rest of the team, the lot of them nearly trampling on each other to be the first to get over there to lay eyes on, presumably, the very first X-ranked alchemical creation in the history of the Unified Mass. The scientific papers that would come out of this day would be fervently discussed for decades to come in the world of alchemy.

“No, stop!” Eik suddenly roared, throwing up a hand toward them as a semi-transparent barrier of crystal snapped into existence between them and the cauldron. “Don’t come any clos—!” was all he got out before everything turned to chaos.

Just before Meri got close enough to actually be able to see into the cauldron properly, a sudden blast of azure light flared out from the vessel and outward through the room like a pulse, not shattering the barrier Eik had created but somehow still managed to partially bypass it.

The moment it struck her, her vision blackened and although she could no longer see what was happening, she felt it as she was thrown back through the room where she cracked the back of her skull against the far wall.

With her hearing also still intact, she heard the thuds of more bodies experiencing the same trauma as her. For a moment, a pang of concern for her grandfather pierced through her stomach before she remembered that ever since Eik had accepted him into the court of the Supreme Divinity of Toxin, the old man had become significantly stronger than her. In fact, the frail looking guild master would probably be the least affected by whatever this was.

Rolling from her back to her stomach, she groaned as the urge to vomit assaulted her mind. Again, she heard but still couldn’t see others losing against that same urge. Once she was done, she wiped her mouth with a dusty sleeve and blinked several times, hoping to regain her vision.

Nothing worked.

“H-Hey…? What’s going o— Is anybody there?” she tried, the words coming out interspersed between involuntary fits of coughing. The blast of force that had thrown her and the others back must have turned the laboratory upside down, because the air was so thick with dust that she couldn’t draw in breath without choking.

“Sweetheart?” A hand grasped her shoulder at the same moment that she heard hear grandfather’s voice. “I’m here.”

“I can’t see. Grandpa, I can’t see!” she cried, panic beginning to set in in earnest. “I’m scared.”

Arms wrapped around her while she tried to feel her way through the mess of materials on the floor. “It’s going to be all right, my sweet girl,” her grandfather whispered. “I think Mr. Magnasen has something for you.” The calm in his voice was enough to get her to stop flailing, and a few moments later, a cool, soothing sensation flowed across her skin, exploring her body. She had felt it a couple of times before.

It was Eik’s Panacean Quintessence checking her over for injuries. Quickly locating something in her eyes and on the back of her head, it took only a second to bring her back to a physical condition that felt even healthier than before she had been thrown across the room.

And with it, her vision returned in full, razor-sharp clarity.

But what she saw made absolutely no sense.

Eik Magnasen still stood over the cauldron, but the fingers of a blue, spectral hand were closed tightly around his wrist. It was coming out of the cauldron as if it was a portal to another dimension. Considering the size of the limb, the rest of the body should not be able to fit inside the relatively small cauldron.

Her eyes scanned the room where Panacean Quintessence was taking care of the other alchemists. Fortunately, it looked like the barrier Eik had thrown up in front of them in time to absorb the brunt of the force of the pulse had spared their lives. Judging by the amount of fresh blood staining floor and clothes, it had been truly dangerous this time, but everybody appeared to be awake and aware.

Returning her attention to the center of the room, she saw Eik’s lips crack a grin full of glee.

Whatever the hell this thing was, it was interesting for sure.

A quick glance at the audiences was enough to tell him that it was yet another unprecedented event. Crap.

With everybody back in good health, he could focus on his creation again. Or whatever it had become at this point.

Resembling the body of his aura avatar, an entire arm had emerged from the mixture. And it wasn’t happy with him. His wrist held fast in a vise grip, the sensation of Profound Toxin was unmistakable.

Did this bastard think it could go against him now all of a sudden?

With a grin and a flex of his will, Eik shattered the spectral hand like glass against a marble floor. This was his power, and what he could grant, he could just as easily take away.

He would not, however, take it away. This power would be the driving force of the beneficial effects of the completed potion.

No, rather than remove the power and weaken the mixture, he would simply crush whatever remnant of will to oppose him was causing this outburst.

Seamlessly and suddenly, he shifted Profound Toxin, and in extension the tinge of his aura, into the sickening purple of Visage of Death. Eik bared his teeth as he flooded the mixture. Surely, if there was enough of a will in there to guide the hostile energy to attack, then there was enough of a will to be thrown into chaos by the crippling effects of the Visage of Death.

Nearly instantly the reaction came—clear and tortured. A wordless shriek, released from a place with no throat, pierced the laboratory and sent the alchemists who had only just begun to recover to their knees, clutching their ears in agony.

But Eik wasn’t about to stop. Ears could be healed later. Caught in the throes of the clash as he was, his instincts would never let him back down.

Abruptly, over a dozen more spectral arms, stretched far beyond normal proportions, whipped out of the cauldron and lashed about with the frantic desperation of a man stuck at the bottom of a well of burning pitch.

Initially they reached high enough to carve deep grooves into the stone ceiling but as the seconds passed, the fervor of their struggle faded and their overstretched dimensions lessened.

When finally the mixture settled down once more, Eik stood up and puffed the hair out of his eyes with a smirk. “All right. This time I have actually done it.”
