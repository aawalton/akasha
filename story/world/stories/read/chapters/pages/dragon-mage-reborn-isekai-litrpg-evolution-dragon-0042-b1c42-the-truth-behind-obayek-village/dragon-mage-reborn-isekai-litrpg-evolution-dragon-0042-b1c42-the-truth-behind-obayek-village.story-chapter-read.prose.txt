I hacked through the tough sinew, my blade slicing with sickening wetness until the massive head rolled free. Against our original plan, I carried the heavy head of the demon to my side, its dark blood down my leg. I turned for the exit, and then turned my head over my shoulder, looking at the altar one last time. I still couldn’t understand why this beast called me its master….

How was I connected to Etheryn? First, the infernal bloodline, then, my presence activating a dungeon, and now this?

At first, I wasn’t worried, but now, I wanted some clear answers. This wasn’t my world—so why did it feel like I was part of it?

And not in a good way….

The walk of victory felt a bit hollow, but nevertheless, I held my head up high. This wasn’t about me—this was about a village in need of saving, a village that needed a push to a fresh start. As soon as I walked out of the cavern, my eyes caught Nolan being held down by three guards. He was sweaty, out of breath, and persistent in passing through. It was against tradition for anyone to chase the sacrifice inside the cavern, but so was this stunt we pulled. And as soon as everyone caught sight of me—the elders, the villagers, and the shaman, the scene went quiet.

I broke the heavy silence by lifting the head of the dead and then I shouted, “Your Dark One has been defeated!” Everyone stared at me in disbelief. I tossed the beast’s head toward Elder Kazak’s feet. The head hit the ground with a wet thud, rolling slightly before coming to rest with its dead eyes staring at the old man. Elder Kazak stumbled backward, his face draining of color.

“Impossible,” he whispered, but loud enough for everyone to hear in the stunned silence.

“This demon had been manipulating you, turning you into puppets for its own selfish gain! It demanded you sacrifice while syphoning souls nearby, bringing you false prosperity in exchange for lives. These blessings came from a place of darkness and despair! The cycle ended today!”

The guards holding Nolan loosened their grip, their jaws slack with shock. Nolan took advantage of their distraction to wrench himself free and rush to me. “Freya!” he shouted, and then grabbed my shoulders in a quick inspection for injuries. “Are you all right?”

“I’m fine.”

“What the hell happened? You stopped answering!”

“Freya?” Elder Kazak repeated, and then his face turned red with anger. “Who is this imposter?!” I translated.

“She is with me,” the shaman admitted as he approached. “Who you see before you is not my daughter, Kauli, but a courageous baby dragon who offered to assist us in our time of need.”

“Traitor!”

The shaman stood his ground, unflinching before Elder Kazak’s rage. “I am no traitor. I have served this village my entire life, seeking the true path to prosperity. For generations, we have blindly followed a lie. ”

“You invited these outsiders into our village and allowed them to desecrate our sacred ritual!” Elder Kazak spat. His body shook with fury as he pointed an accusing finger at the shaman. “You have brought doom upon us all!”

I stepped forward, placing myself between Elder Kazak and the shaman. “Your ‘sacred ritual’ was nothing but a feeding trough for a monster,” I said, my voice carrying across the gathered crowd. “This beast has been draining nearby villages for generations, trading a few good harvests for the blood of innocent children.”

The crowd’s murmuring grew louder, and then a familiar voice broke through and said, “This is true, our beloved Elder Kazak,” Kauli said. The arrival of the actual sacrifice made everyone gasp, many doing a double take between me and the true Kauli.

“Kauli, I told you to stay behind!” her father scolded, worried. He rushed to her, but Kauli broke his gait when she stepped past him and walked over to Elder Kazak.

“For many seasons, this demon has taken from us. For many seasons, those it has chosen to feast upon have seen the true nature of its blessings. We cannot idly stand by and pretend our neighbors aren’t suffering. And we cannot ignore our selfishness being the reason to blame. The Dark One was harvesting souls, trading lives for our vegetation, for our fruitfulness. I voiced my concerns, and they fell on deaf ears. I believe the same shared visions that came from those before me were told to you as well, from the very lips of those young girls, from their parents and guardians. But you ignored them out of fear. Well, now there is nothing to fear.”

“The Dark One will curse us all for this sacrilege!” he shouted at the top of his lungs, his fear so deep-rooted that he didn’t hear anything Kauli had said.

“The connection to the demon and this village has been cut,” I reassured, “with Moonwater.”

“Its death will not bring a curse upon us,” the shaman followed. “All ties have been severed with the help of Moonwater, an essence that has the power to nullify dark magic and its properties. Freya has given the Dark One a taste of powerlessness, relinquishing its hold on our dear village before she decapitated it.”

The villagers murmured among themselves, their eyes darting between the demon’s severed head, the shaman, and me. Some looked terrified, others confused, and a few—a growing number—seemed thoughtful, as if seeing their world clearly for the first time.

“I remember,” a woman said, her voice wavering but strong enough to carry. “Fifty seasons ago, my sister was chosen. The night before she was taken, she spoke of dreams—dark visions of a beast feeding on our sacrifices, growing stronger while we grew dependent.” She turned to face the gathered villagers. “We silenced her, called her mad, dishonorable. She refused to be used as a tool of hate and suffering. When she ran away the morning before the ritual, we tortured and tormented her, ridiculed and belittled her. Yet, we hear the same story. Clearly, these words cannot be lies. We must lend an ear to Kauli and this stranger who has saved us.”

Elder Kazak’s face contorted with rage. “Silence, old woman! Your sister was a coward who brought shame upon your family!”

“No,” came another voice, this one belonging to a middle-aged man with calloused hands. “My father told me similar tales about his cousin who was chosen. We’ve dismissed these visions for generations, calling them the ravings of frightened children.”

More voices joined in, a chorus of suppressed memories and whispered family stories finally being spoken aloud. The tide was turning against Elder Kazak, who stood trembling with impotent fury. His authority was crumbling before his eyes.

“This is madness!” he shouted, but his voice lacked conviction now. “Without the Dark One’s blessing, our crops will wither! Our children will starve!”

The shaman stepped forward, his weathered face solemn. “We have lived in fear for too long. We have prospered through the suffering of others.” He gestured to the surrounding mountains. “These lands are now fertile. With proper care, we can thrive without blood sacrifice.”

A murmur of hope rippled through the crowd.

“The fact of the matter is that you have to deal with change, whether you like it or not,” Nolan added as he addressed the villagers, but his comment was actually directed to Elder Kazak. “I’ve traveled throughout these lands. Villages without your... particular traditions grow crops and raise livestock successfully. Your isolation has kept you from learning new farming techniques that could double your yields without the help of a soul-syphoning cave demon.”

“You outsiders come here, destroy our way of life, and now presume to teach us?” The elder’s voice trembled with barely contained fury. “What do you know of our hardships? Of the bitter winters and failed harvests before the Dark One’s blessing?”

“I know that no prosperity is worth the price of innocent lives,” Nolan bit back.

Elder Kazak was fuming. Suddenly, he lunged forward, grabbing a spear from one of the guards and turning to the shaman!

“Blasphemers! Heretics!” he screamed, charging at Pozei. “I will not let you destroy centuries of tradition!”

Kauli panicked. “Father!”

Nolan moved to intercept him, but before he could intervene, the elder began to disintegrate!

It started with his hands, flaking away like ash in a strong wind. Elder Kazak froze mid-charge, his expression shifting from rage to horror as he watched his own body dissolve.

Everyone backed away, mothers pulling children behind them, and warriors raising shields defensively. The disintegration spread rapidly up his arms, across his chest, and consumed him piece by piece until there was nothing left.

“I feared this suspicion was true,” the shaman said with a sigh. No one had any idea what he was talking about as he walked over to the ashes of the fallen elder with pity in his eyes. “Elder Kazak had a pact with The Dark One. I wouldn’t be surprised if his bloodline was what brought this darkness upon us. His grandfather likely made the original bargain.”

Nolan scoffed. “I’m not an ounce bit shocked there.”

The shaman knelt beside the remains, rubbing the ash between his fingers. “The Dark One’s power extended through him. When the beast fell, any who were directly bound to it through blood pacts would share its fate.” He looked up at the gathered crowd. “This is why our traditions never changed, and why dissenting voices were silenced. Elder Kazak was not merely following tradition—he was enslaved by it.”

“What happens now?” someone finally asked, their voice small in the vast quiet.

The shaman rose to his feet, brushing the ash from his hands. “Now, we rebuild. Not just our village, but our understanding of the world.” He turned to me with grateful eyes. “Thanks to Freya, we have been given a chance to forge our own path.”
