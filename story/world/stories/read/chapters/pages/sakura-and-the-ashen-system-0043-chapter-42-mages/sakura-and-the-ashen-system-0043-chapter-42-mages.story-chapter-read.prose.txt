Pampli was left up above. Of course, he did not like it, but even Sakura understood that a huge, heavy obsidian beast would not make it along the narrow canyon paths, where stones crumbled underfoot and the edge waited for any excuse to collapse. Pampli could be agile when he wanted to, but not agile enough to argue with the canyon’s loose rock. Besides, he was so wide that he simply would not fit on the narrowest paths.

When Sakura stroked the beast’s neck, he immediately understood what was happening. Pampli’s ears twitched unhappily, and his tail lashed nervously against the stone. That same expression appeared in the beast’s eyes, the one he got every time humans were about to crawl into dubious trouble without him. In his own way, he took offense every time this happened.

"I know," Sakura said quietly. "After this, we’ll definitely spend a lot of time together."

Pampli snorted shortly.

Gilbert, already standing at the start of the descent, did not even try to pretend he could be persuaded.

Sakura sighed, hugged the beast around the neck as much as she could, and whispered, "Wait for us. All right? Just don’t go anywhere. And don’t eat anyone unless it’s absolutely necessary."

The beast nudged her shoulder with his nose, but he still looked deeply judgmental.

Then Sakura and Gilbert finally started their descent. At first, the path still looked tolerable: just a narrow strip of stone along the canyon wall. But rather quickly, Sakura realized that “tolerable” could be a synonym for “disgusting, but not fatal yet.”

They moved sideways, pressing their backs against the wall where the passage grew too narrow. Then they descended in short zigzags along steps cut into the rock, if those crooked notches could even be called steps. In some places, they did not so much walk as literally press their whole bodies to the stone, clinging with their fingers to cracks and protrusions. In other places, they had to bend sharply if a low overhang appeared above their heads, because there simply was no other way. Through all of this, every little stone seemed determined to slide out from under their soles.

Gilbert walked first and was as collected as always. The mage did not chatter or try to encourage Sakura with stupid jokes. From time to time, he only commanded and instructed her on what to do next.

"Right foot here."

"Not that ledge. It crumbles."

"There’s a notch lower down. Reach it with your hand first."

"Don’t look down right now."

The last one was especially useful advice, because whenever Sakura still failed to stop herself and looked down, she immediately felt sick. The bottom of the canyon was still far away, hidden in a bluish haze. If she stared hard enough, sometimes she saw a glimmer of water, and sometimes dark chunks of stone and ragged shadows. The height was so great that her mind simply refused to accept it fully.

Sakura had time to think that if she fell down, there would definitely be nothing left of her body. Only a wet, shapeless stain.

Once, she had to almost crawl across a narrow slanted ledge where there was no proper path, only a strip of stone between the wall and empty air. She moved slowly, feeling the rough rock under her palms, still warm from the sun, and at some point realized that her fingers had already gone numb from how hard she was holding on.

"You’re too tense," Gilbert said quietly, glancing back over his shoulder. "If you keep gripping like that, your hands will get tired before we reach the bottom."

"What choice do I have?" Sakura breathed out, not entirely sure she could speak right now without sliding into hysteria.

"Hold on, but don’t turn to stone."

"Very useful. Thank you."

He almost smirked, but only at the corner of his mouth.

The descent kept going and going. Sakura began to feel as if they should have reached the bottom long ago, but the canyon seemed to mock them with that feeling. It opened into new ledges, deceptive platforms, and yet another turn downward every time she started hoping that now, surely, it was almost over.

On one of those turns, Sakura noticed movement.

A little lower, on a narrow stone shelf, sat a strange creature the size of a large cat. It was dry and long-legged, with gray skin almost bare of fur and enormous black eyes. It looked straight at them without blinking, and a chill ran down Sakura’s back from that stare.

"Gilbert..." she whispered.

He did not even turn his head right away.

"Don’t stare too long. And don’t stop."

"What even is that?"

"A local scavenger. If we don’t go after it ourselves, it won’t come after us either. Hold the ledge and keep descending."

The creature continued sitting there and staring at them. Sakura thought there was no interest in its eyes, or rather, there was some alien, calm kind of interest, as if humans crawling along a canyon wall were simply one of the rare and strange varieties of beasts to it.

Sakura obeyed Gilbert, looked away, pressed herself more tightly to the rock, and moved on.

And still, they made it down.

Not all the way to the very bottom at once, but deep into it, where the air grew cooler and the canyon walls shifted closer together, turning the space into a stone world of shadows, cracks, and pale strips of sky high overhead.

The path finally led them onto a wider ledge, almost a platform, scattered with fine gravel where dry stems pushed through. After the entire descent, that flat, spacious surface felt almost luxurious.

Sakura stepped forward before she even had time to properly feel relieved, and in the next moment everything went wrong.

Something snapped sharply under her sole. It was not a branch, and not a stone. Something stretched tight and well hidden in the dust had cracked.

"Stop!" Gilbert barked, but it was too late.

A net shot up from under Sakura’s feet with a dry whistle. She did not even understand what was happening at first. She only felt something sticky, tight, and unpleasantly heavy slam into her legs, waist, and shoulders, and then the world jerked upward. Sakura was yanked into the air so sharply that she cursed while still in flight.

A second later, Sakura was already dangling above the ground, wrapped in a net and looking like a ham wrapped up for sale.

"Are you kidding me?!"

The net turned out to be not only sticky and strange, but also very well designed. The trap did not simply hold her. It tightened in the places where Sakura tried to move harder. Trying to pull her arm free only made it worse.

Gilbert swore shortly and viciously, looking up at the triggered mechanism between the stones.

"I didn’t have time to warn you."

"Wonderful!" Sakura said indignantly, swaying a few feet above the ground. "Maybe this was worth warning me about before the descent?"

Gilbert did not answer. Instead, he sharply looked around.

And he was right to do so.

People emerged from behind the stones and cracks in the wall. There were only four of them, but they moved in a way that immediately made one thing clear: these were not random vagrants and not road bandits. Their steps were suspiciously light, and they were armed. Two held short bows, one had a staff, and the last had a knife and something like a bone wand at his belt. All of them wore simple, worn clothes, but the kind convenient for a fight.

They looked at Gilbert and at Sakura in the net without any friendly delight.

One of them, the tallest, stopped in front of them at a safe distance.

"One more step, and we put arrows in you," he said evenly.

Gilbert did not twitch. He did not even raise his hands, only spread his palms slightly to the sides, showing that, for now, he was not reaching for a fight.

"We’re not enemies," he said. "We’re looking for the Mages’ Refuge."

"Everyone’s looking for it," the second scout cut in. "Not everyone leaves alive afterward."

Sakura, still hanging in the net, jerked angrily and immediately regretted it, because the sticky ropes tightened painfully again.

"Very hospitable place you have here!"

All four of them looked at her at the same time. Apparently, they did not appreciate the joke.

Gilbert spoke faster, before the situation could get any worse.

"Tell your leader a mage has come who knows his name. Marven."

The tall scout narrowed his eyes slightly, and Sakura noticed the movement at once. Surprisingly, the name worked.

"How do you know that name?" he asked, in a different tone now.

"From where I needed to know it to understand where I’ve come," Gilbert answered. "And enough not to shout it across the entire canyon. I’m a mage. And she’s with me."

One of the scouts, the one with the staff, frowned.

"Prove it."

Gilbert exhaled shortly, raised his hand, and familiar white-blue fire flared above his fingers for a second. It was small and not threatening. More of a sign than the real magic Gilbert was capable of in battle.

The scouts exchanged glances.

Meanwhile, the net above Sakura had apparently decided that the spectacle had gone on long enough. Or one of the locals had simply loosened the fastening, she did not have time to understand. The knot above jerked, the tension changed, and in the next moment the entire construction abruptly sagged.

"Wait, I’m just..."

Too late.

Sakura fell down together with the net and crashed onto the stone platform with a very ugly sound.

"Damn all of you..."

She said the last word while already lying there, tangled in sticky ropes and sharply aware that absolutely everyone was now looking at her.

Gilbert closed his eyes for a second, as if wearily accepting the inevitable.

"She’s with me," he repeated dryly. "And she’s a mage too."

"Very kind of you to remember that now," Sakura muttered, pulling her hand out of the net.

To her surprise, one of the scouts still came over and cut through part of the sticky loops with one short movement. The rest loosened on its own, and a few seconds later Sakura finally crawled out, irritated, rumpled, and with a powerful urge to say exactly what she thought about local security systems.

But she did not get the chance.

The tall scout had already stepped back and said, "You’ll come with us. Slowly. No stupidity."

"And if we really like stupidity?" Sakura could not help asking.

This time Gilbert gave her such a look that she decided to shut up after all.

They were led farther along the stone ledge, then through a narrow gap between two massive protrusions, and then down another hidden path that Sakura would never have thought to look for from outside. The locals kept throwing sideways glances at Sakura from time to time, and she could not tell whether it was because she had just clumsily fallen, snapped at them, or because they could sense something strange in her magical energy. Something that made her different from ordinary mages.

She had not managed to finish the thought when she saw the settlement itself.

It was small. Much smaller than she had imagined from the words “Mages’ Refuge.” About twenty people, no more. Several tents stretched between stones and propped up with poles. Rough cloth awnings hung here, and a pair of low fire pits stood nearby. There were also folding crates, sacks, packs, and rolled blankets. Everything looked as if they had simply stopped on the edge between life and flight. Nothing permanent, and nothing they would be sorry to abandon. The whole camp seemed to shout: if we have to, we can leave this place in half an hour.

There were few people, but all of them looked wary. They had tired faces and eyes that watched too closely. Someone immediately reached for a weapon or simply froze, looking at the newcomers as potential trouble.

Then a grim, sturdy man came out to meet them. He was not old, but his face had long since forgotten how to be soft. There was no fuss in his stride, and that immediately made him seem more dangerous than the others. Sakura already knew this kind of person. He was clearly used to having the final word here.

He stopped in front of the scouts, slid his gaze over Gilbert, then over Sakura, then over the remains of the sticky net on her clothes.

And asked with obvious hostility, "Gilbert, who did you drag to us?"
