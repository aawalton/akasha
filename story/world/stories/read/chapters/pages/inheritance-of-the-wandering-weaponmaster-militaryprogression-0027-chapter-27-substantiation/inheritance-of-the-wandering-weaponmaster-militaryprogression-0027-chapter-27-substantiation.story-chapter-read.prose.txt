Yeah, it was actually two days ago that you reached the juncture to Adept. You just aren’t familiar enough with aura to have realized until it started to really hurt instead of just to be difficult to cycle.

Though the rest of the elite squad was around him, Ezra couldn’t help but ask aloud, “How?”

“How what?” Boyd asked, and Ezra realized that the tall man had been talking to him for a while. He raised a hand, looking like he was trying to think while he was really just waiting for Jormun’s answer.

You were bringing almost exactly as much aura in as you were bleeding out, Jormun explained. But we’ll talk more about it later.

Ezra gave a little nod as he looked up at Boyd, and, by extension, the rest of the squad. They’d all started looking at him, though they all continued walking.

“Sorry about that,” Ezra said. “I just realized that I think I’m at the end of the Initiate tier, so I was just surprised is all. It’s been feeling a little weird, which is what I’ve been thinking about.”

He didn’t miss the look on Petr’s face when he said it, but the entirety of the squad quickly congratulated him, and Petr gestured for Amy to come to him. After whispering in her ear for a moment, she nodded excitedly and jogged away, giving Ezra an approving nod before she went. Though he wondered what was happening, Ezra didn’t look at her and instead turned to Boyd who was holding and shaking him by the shoulder.

“Yeeees?” Ezra asked, the word extended by the larger man’s shaking.

“This is bullshit,” Boyd said, though he was laughing as he said it. “All three of you are there getting stronger, and the only part of me doing that is my damn legs. That’s nowhere near what I need to catch up to you, and you’re just leaving us behind?”

“Then catch up,” Ezra laughed back, “because I’m not going to slow down.”

“Do you feel an attribute in your inner world?” Leah interrupted whatever it was that Boyd was going to ask next. “Is it becoming something like what you’ve pulled in already?”

“I don’t know,” Ezra replied, stretching his awareness inward. “I don’t think so, but I also know I don’t feel or understand very much, so I’m probably wrong.”

She grunted, seeming disinterested after that, while Cord asked, “Does it feel any different?”

“Not really,” Ezra shook his head. “I think it took me a while to even realize that I was done, because it almost feels the same to be cycling the aura and losing just as much as to be pulling it into my inner world. Chloe said that it’d hurt when I reached the juncture, but that was only a while after I did, I think.”

“Yeah, why do you think that?” Boyd laughed. “Sounds like you think you know better than a Pseudo-Knight.”

Ezra ignored Jormun’s laughter about a “mere” Pseudo-Knight, instead answering, “I might be wrong. If I think about it now, though, I think I can feel that it was a little different starting two days ago. Around lunchtime, the aura felt like there was more of it to pull in than before, and I didn’t know why. I think that it’s because I was mostly losing what I pulled in at that point, and I’ve just been stuffing myself full ever since.”

Boyd accepted that with a grunt and began to demand answers to questions that Ezra’d answered a dozen times already as the large, burly man sought to understand aura. After just a few questions, though, Amy returned with Georg and Chloe right behind her. His lieutenant spoke first, his mouth cracked in a wide grin.

“You’re at the peak of Initiate, are you?”

“I believe so, sir,” Ezra replied with a shrug. “Chloe said that when it starts to hurt to pull in aura I’m there, and it’s hurting a little. I think I actually got there a couple days ago, when my inner world wasn’t expanding at all.”

“That’s the case,” Chloe nodded and spoke when Georg looked at her for confirmation. “But it’s best for people to feel their own limits, not have someone else tell them. There’s only so much instruction that someone can give about this before they begin stunting their students’ growth, so most people make sure to err on the side of caution.”

“Kid, you’re a damn prodigy,” Georg shook his head. “You’ve totally caught up to me this quickly? That’s unheard of, so far as I know. Congratulations.”

“Thank you, sir,” Ezra replied. “I think coming up here and feeling different types of aura helped me out a lot as well.”

Georg scoffed, clapped Ezra on the shoulder, and stepped back. “Maybe so, kid. I’d say don’t expect to become an Adept any time soon, but I didn’t think you’d get here within a couple months at best, so don’t listen to this slow old man.”

“I believed in you!” Chloe jumped in as Georg stepped back. Ezra narrowed his eyes at her and she laughed. “Well, maybe not that much. But I want you to think that I did!”

He scoffed but didn’t say anything more to her. Instead, he just talked a little more with the other members of the elite squad until they lost interest in him. Fortunately, it was it was still morning, not quite lunchtime, so Ezra had plenty of time to ask the questions that were burning in his head. Unfortunately, there was someone who wasn’t going to leave him to quietly speak with Jormun.

“So, now that you’re at the juncture of Initiate and Adept,” Chloe said once the rest had finished congratulating him, “and I’m sure you’re wondering what you need to do to become an Adept, right?”

“Something about strengthening my inner world and making it more real, right?” Ezra asked, remembering the conversation he’d had with her.

“... Yes,” she answered, looking at him curiously, and it was only right then that Ezra remembered that he hadn’t had that conversation with her, but with Jormun.

“Figured that was the case,” he said, trying to play it off. “Every bit of strengthening myself has been that way so far, so I thought it’d be the same.”

“Sure,” Chloe agreed. “More than just strengthening your inner world, though, it’s substantiating it.”

“I have no idea what that word means,” Ezra replied dryly. “Remember, some of us aren’t fancy enough to have had tutors from the time we were eight.”

“I’ve had tutors since I was four, thank you very much,” Chloe snapped back. “Hated most of them until the old man, and he’s no bed of roses either.”

“Not sure what a rose is either.”

“It’s a fancy flower, they don’t grow around here because the soil’s not good for them. But that’s not what I’m here to talk to you about,” she shook her head. “Substantiate is also used in legal terms as to prove something, but Knights use it to say to give something substance. To make it real. In the case of someone becoming an Adept, it’s usually to incorporate an attribute into your inner world. Some people have a lightning storm, a mountain, a swamp, whatever. You can kinda choose what your inner world’s gonna look like, but it’ll be easier for you to form one if you’re pushing it towards the form that it naturally wants to be.”

“And, let me guess,” Ezra sighed, “getting to know how my inner world wants to be is going to take me a long time to figure out because I just need to feel it out.”

“You win! Perfect guess.”

Ezra groaned, and Chloe shrugged as she explained further, “Most of this is going to be trial and error for you. But now that you’ve felt a couple different types of aura, you should try to insert it into your inner world without actually changing its form. Got it?”

He just nodded in response, thinking about the way he’d needed to change the aura to incorporate it into his inner world, and thought about a couple different possibilities, but before he could explore them too much, Chloe said, “Alright. Good luck, but don’t worry too much about figuring out substantiation too quickly. Most people get stuck here for a while, just like Georg.”

Then, without another word, she jogged away. At least, it looked like a jog, but she moved a lot faster than he’d expected, and Ezra was left remembering that, despite her teasing attitude and good nature, she was a Pseudo-Knight, someone that most Counts and even higher nobles would kill to have on their payroll. He wouldn’t be surprised if Al Jibahia and Shamshua would fight to recruit a promising Pseudo-Knight like her. Adria might as well, but this was just him thinking about stupid things again. Instead, he looked around and saw that he had a spot of privacy for the moment and spoke to Jormun.

“So you didn’t tell me I was wasting time because I needed to figure it out myself?” His voice was barely more than a whisper.

Yup. Like she said, there’s a point where I’d ruin your inner world because I was telling you what to do and how to do it. If you were less talented, I’d probably offer you more instruction, but I won’t for you because you don’t need it. You’re quick enough that I’d just be taking a learning opportunity from you in doing it.

“What do you mean?” Ezra asked. “If I get to Adept earlier, can’t I have more time trying to get to Pseudo-Knight and so on?”

Yes, but progress doesn’t just take time, Jormun disagreed. You could reach the peak of Adept faster that way, but the journey into Pseudo-Knight would be more difficult in exchange. So, I could give you a bit more advice to get you onto the same level as before, but then it would be nearly impossible to become a Knight. The path is a difficult one, but you need to be the one cutting it.

With a nod, Ezra asked another question, changing the subject. “I have another question, Jormun. So, replenishing the aura in your inner world must be difficult the further you get. After all, if you make your inner world’s… affinity, I think Chloe called it? So if it’s lightning, you would only be able to recover when there was lightning around. Unless… you can change the affinity of external aura to an attributeless aura, and then change it again as you incorporate it into your inner world.”

I’m not hearing much of a question, kid.

“Can you change the affinity of aura again, once you’re pulling it into your inner world?”

How about you try? Jormun said, his face not giving anything away. His damned bushy eyebrows and beard hid everything, his dark eyes only barely visible as he spoke. Ezra sighed, and, since it’d been interesting to him since the thought occurred to him, he set about trying to convert the aura he’d pulled into himself into lightning.

***

At the end of the day, Ezra’d failed to make anything into lighting. Or stone. Or fire. Or wind. Instead, for maybe half an hour, there’d been a small clump of ice in the center of his inner world, seeming to taunt him as he looked at it. Eventually, though, that time came to an end and his inner world returned to a formless shape.

“The only good part of all that,” he grumbled to himself as he walked forward with the sun disappearing behind the mountains that surrounded them, “is that I can now better picture my inner world. Nothing stuck around in there!”

That’s fine. You were disappointed to be able to pull the aura of ice into your inner world anyways, so what’s the complaint? Ezra didn’t have anything to really say to Jormun’s laughing explanation, so he remained silent as Jormun continued, Just because it was the type of aura you struggled least with to pull into your inner world without changing doesn’t mean that it’s the affinity that works best for you. A lot of people think that way and end up stuck. Keep trying things, and observe how your inner world interacts with each one.

“But don’t be too stuck on anything,” Ezra said, remembering what Jormun’d said about Chloe and stone, “because that can be a problem too.”

Jormun only nodded in response, and before Ezra could think of something else to say or ask, excited voices raised up ahead. After just a few seconds, Ezra realized what it was and raised his head.

“Main camp ahead!”
