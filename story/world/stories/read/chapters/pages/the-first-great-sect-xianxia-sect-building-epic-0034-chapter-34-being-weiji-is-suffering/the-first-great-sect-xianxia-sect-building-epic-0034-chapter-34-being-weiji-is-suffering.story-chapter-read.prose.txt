One could feel the cold breeze cutting through their robes, the gentle caress of moonlight and, for one moment, imagine the world at peace. They would be wrong, but feelings did not need to be accurate for them to exist.

Two blurs darted across the training ground, blades glinting under the moonlight. When they met, thunderclaps made clouds of dust and the beating of a thousand wings dispelled those clouds. Qi was thrown around without restraint as the two fought, stray arcs of lightning tearing apart the ground, the force of their blows resounding like a mountain collapsing.

Each blow could cripple, each swing meant to maim. A mortal would have died just from the pressure of their missed swings. Qi followed their blades, cutting through the earth even if steel came nowhere close to touching it. Someone who had fully undergone Body Tempered might endure a few seconds of this assault.

Liao Weiji was not certain he would survive more than a minute even drawing upon every ounce of Qi he possessed and the techniques he knew. Not against Liao Furen who had established the foundation to Heaven and proven an enduring weapon in service of the Liao Clan for over a century. And perhaps not against her opponent who Liao Furen was earnestly trying to maim. Trying being the operative word.

Liao Hua was monstrous indeed to do what she did.

She was keeping up with Liao Furen.

That shouldn’t be possible. Weiji was good, the best in his generation after the Patriarch’s children. At three stars and not yet twenty, with his current talent, he was likely to reach seven or eight stars in the next five years. Quite literally approaching the power of the weaker Elders before his thirtieth year. He was to be one of the three pillars of the Wei Generation. Reaching the Foundation was very much the expectation of his talent, but that would come only after decades of careful training and resources from the Elders backing him.

Hua moved like she could already kill an Elder.

They were like spirits, silver hair almost glowing amber, limned by the moon’s radiance distorted by smoke and ash in the sky. Myths and stories made real. The cold air shivered with their passing as though they were more than the world could handle.

The depths of Hua’s Qi were staggering in a way that shouldn’t be possible. He’d understood it during the purification ritual. Six elders were meant to provide the Qi powering the Bronze’s Cauldrons flames whilst two priests directed it. Hua had provided more than any other elder and remained standing without assistance.

And then she did it again the next day.

She had been trembling after that, yes, but it was something noteworthy enough that the clan had been ablaze with gossip. Pride, hope, and faith for those of the younger generations. Disdain and fear for those older. They could see that they would be surpassed, and it would happen soon because of this girl with a vastness to her Qi that beggared belief.

It showed now as she infused her sword with lightning, enough to, if not match then at least endure the power of the Hummingbird Blade. Weiji could infuse his sword with lightning. He couldn’t do it for this long or at this intensity—his Qi found it easier to strengthen his body than anything external like a blade just as any other Physical Cultivator. For Hua to both be a Physical Cultivator and a powerful spiritualist was utterly unfair.

Some are blessed by the Heavens with great talent and others will taste only the scraps that the talented leave behind. Mourn only the circumstances of your birth and learn to endure one day and then the next. If you survive a long life, you will realise that the talented are the first to die young.

Weiji wasn’t sure how much he agreed with those words. The kind of talent Hua was displaying was more than generational. This was legacy. This was generations of talent distilled into a single child who had been moulded and cultivated to touch lightning as easily as others touched their face.

Every once in a while, they paused long enough for Liao Hua’s golden eyes to shine bright. An unnatural colour that only heightened her strangeness. Weiji’s hands kneaded the damp grass of the hill whenever it felt like her gaze brushed over him.

Before, she had always swallowed up the oxygen in every room she entered. Tall and confident, walking with a predator’s grace and Liao Furen’s casual cruelty. Now she had a predator’s eyes to match, her glare powerful enough to intimidate someone like the Magistrate. And her presence. Her presence was terrifying. There was a weight she applied to the world, a storm just waiting to arrive. Ozone and wet air, power waiting to be unleashed.

Now if only the fools could recognise the leopard prowling amongst them and stop letting it sniff their faces.

“Do they have to make this much noise? I thought the world was breaking again.”

“You know they don’t care about politeness,” Weiji said to his cousin, Ming, without startling. “Liao Furen always indulges Hua and Hua likes fighting. And, well, I suspect the council meeting went poorly if this much of a statement need be made.”

He had sensed Ming long before she came close. Another reason Hua terrified him so much. She was silent until her presence suddenly unfurled, a storm suddenly appearing in a room. Perfect Qi control. Nothing less could get past his senses.

Ming had that bleary-eyed look of someone who had just fallen asleep before being rudely awoken. She was not the only one who had made their way to the training grounds to witness this spectacle of strength. None were Elders but perhaps they didn’t want to see further proof of their irrelevancy.

Ming was another pillar of the younger generation. Her affinity to the more esoteric arts inherited from her mother made for a powerful spiritualist, one who performed Rites that would leave anyone else dizzy trying to understand. Her presence was quiet, carefully refined to avoid disturbing the ghosts she regularly dealt with. Without the right degree of sensitivity, one would miss her signature. Weiji could always feel her coming. Just as he could with everyone in the Clan, including most of the Elders. It was one of his stronger traits, his Qi sensitivity. Hua still always managed to startle him. Even Weijiang, her more advanced brother, couldn’t achieve the same.

“It’d be nice if Hua—I mean, Weilong, that’s her name now; well, if she could stop bullying the rest of us and just focus on Liao Furen. Would save most of us a trip to Old Ren.”

“I didn’t think she traded pointers with you. It couldn’t have been that terrible. Even with me, she’s… not gentle, but she doesn’t leave any serious injuries.”

“She saw some of us priests practising our self-defence. Decided we were terribly incompetent and forced herself on us.”

“I thought priests couldn’t leave Clan grounds without a large guard.”

“We tried telling Hua—fuck, Weilong, damn it, I’m never going to remember that. She didn’t care. And, well, when Hua’s bored the rest of us deal with it. I’ll say this, I learned how to fall properly after that session.”

He chuckled. That did sound like Hua.

“You walked it off and that’s all—”

He choked on his words as Hua managed to get a Thunder Palm off against Liao Furen clean against her chest. The shockwave pushed Liao Furen back with deep trenches left in the ground.

Both fighters were frozen in shock. A sense of shock that rippled across the hills speckled with white hair and blue robes. Weiji’s mouth was agape, eyes wide.

“Did she just get a clean hit on Liao Furen?”

“I think she did,” he said disbelieving. Fighting someone only slightly higher cultivation was difficult. Bridging realms was meant to be impossible.

“She’s only unlocked the fifth star—”

“Sixth,” Weiji corrected as the two returned to their bout, silver gleaming, Qi crackling. Hua was on the back foot immediately, so some sanity still remained in the world. “She unlocked her sixth meridian sometime around or after the world broke. The fuck is wrong with Weilong? The rest of us were barely surviving and she was out there getting stronger.”

“That what happened to her eyes? World broke and she unlocked some kind of bloodline. I mean, she was scary enough but now it’s like she can see things that aren’t there.”

“I have no idea. The gods like her, I guess. They could have spread her talent around the whole Clan and we’d have ten more people as strong as the Elders by now.”

Ming shook her head. “If there’s one person the gods don’t like, it's Hua. Her karma has become… strange. Like someone’s broken the wheel or tried to. Mother foretold Hua would either bring us great victory or lead us to destruction when she was born. I understand it now that I can see her karma.”

“We’re not Yu Diviners and even they get things wrong often enough. Don’t put much stock into prophecy. You can contort any prophecy to fit any set of circumstances if you try hard enough.”

“Maybe. But maybe this time we should pay attention. Our ancestors acknowledged her as Lord even if the elders tried cutting her out of the decision-making.”

Weiji rolled his eyes but stayed his tongue. Diviners had also said the twins would be a great talent. It would be lucky if they achieved mediocrity—oh sure, the benefit of resources would see them further than most, but their lack of talent would make those resources a waste.

The Patriarch was also prophesied to have two great sons, but the Clan had buried his second son at birth alongside his wife.

Well, I suppose Weilong counts as a son, he thought cynically. Stretch any story far enough and you can fit fate into any mould.

Weiji found Hua as the new Lord Liao to be a strange concept. He had never expected to be her equal, not after her rapid advancement, but he hadn’t expected to serve her in this way. She was ignorant in ways he disliked, lacking manners and civility, more a dragon struggling to hide her fangs than the graceful kingfisher of their heraldry. Ask her anything about logistics and you may as well be talking to a brick wall. But she was observant. Weiji hadn’t noticed a single thing wrong with mortals burning paper currency, but Hua had accurately realised the problem. He didn’t know why she knew the mortal who owned a good number of the city’s warehouses, but she did, and that was enough to smooth over her total disinterest in managing them.

As a political animal, he didn’t know where Hua would land. The volatile mix of her pride, her need for entertainment, and the fury she just barely managed to suppress might just burn their clan to the ground. He shivered just thinking about it.

“You should have worn something thicker,” Ming admonished gently.

It was cold enough that his shivering might be misunderstood. “I’ll have some tea when I get home.”

He and Ming sat together on the hill, their robes growing damp from the grass, as Liao Furen and Hua destroyed the training ground. He inhaled the cold air and sparks of precious Qi the two threw around like it was nothing.

He helped Ming up just before he sensed they would be done and escorted her away from the training fields. He was not going to be the fool who stayed and got beaten up because Hua hadn’t worked out her energy sparring with a Foundation Establishment Cultivator.

“How is your father recovering?” he asked as they approached her home.

“Rather well. Old Ren gives it another month before he’s walking again. We are lucky it was such a clean break. Others weren’t so lucky.”

“No, they weren’t,” he said bitterly.

Ming gave him a pitying smile and darted in for a hug. Family could be complex, but Ming always made it simple. It was simple care and compassion. He basked in the comfort she offered and held himself together.

“Stay strong, alright.”

He could endure. Just as Hua had declared, they would endure. Weiji would. One more moment would become one more sichen, one day and one year and then the span of his lifetime would have elapsed.

She darted across the paved courtyard with a wave.

For someone who made friends with ghosts, she was much more upbeat than Weiji ever was.
