Sirocco and I soar through the sky with Fenrir tucked away in my spatial tattoo. Earth Fenrir, unfortunately, cannot enter it so I recovered as much mana out of him as I could, and did the same with Fire Sirocco. I can always recreate them later.

I considered sending the bird ahead and then teleporting to her, as she is a bit faster than I am, but then decided that wasting a chunk of my mana pool for only a few minutes’ worth of speed isn’t worth it.

While we fly towards where I can feel the sea serpent’s Bond leads, I reach out to Nessie.

What’s wrong with your mate?

He’s poisoned! she answers succinctly. Another beast lord tried to take over our territory. We fended it off, but its spines held an awful poison that my mate’s healing cannot defeat. You are a healer, are you not?

I am, I confirm. But as I’ve said to you before, my best healing is done through a Bond. Is your mate willing to take one?

Yes, and submit to it for as long as you desire in payment for your services, Nessie hurriedly agrees, urgency shivering constantly through the Bond between us. Just please, hurry! I am doing what I can, but he is fading as we speak!

I exchange glances with Sirocco at that and, without needing to speak, we both press more mana into our flight.

Nessie and her mate come into view as we round a promontory of rock that sticks out into the bay. The two sea serpents are curled up against the shore, half out of the water, half in. They’re both clearly injured, and the blood that is washed away by the waves that buffet them is replaced only moments after as more leaks out.

There is a noticeable size difference – and I recognise the bigger serpent as Nessie, making the smaller one her mate. He’s clearly far worse off than she is. His silvery scales are dull, his eyes are pained, he’s covered with gashes and slits in his armour, and he would be writhing hard enough to cause more injury if not for Nessie’s body wrapped around his and keeping him still.

My eyes spot a small disturbance off to one side of them. A small sea serpent head lifts briefly above the water surface, and then proceeds to dive again, flickers of a small silvery body following its arc, I realise that their two hatchlings must be watching this.

The sea serpent certainly looks to be badly off, but he’s not Bound to me, so I don’t land next to him – yet. Instead, I flick my wings and readjust my position so I’m facing his eyes. Unfortunately, they’re screwed tightly shut in pain.

I need to make eye contact with him, I tell Nessie. Ask him to open his lids, please.

She sends me a hint of affirmation, then hisses at her mate. I don’t understand the communication, but our Bond allows me to sense that she’s just conveying the message I asked her to, nothing nefarious.

A moment later, the male sea serpent opens his eyes and I meet his pained gaze. Pulling him straight into a Battle of Wills, I find little resistance against my forward movement.

“Your mate indicated that you’re willing to exchange healing for service – is that right?” I ask him briskly as I stride towards the serpent coiled on the other side of the space. He looks much larger here in comparison to me than he did in the real world when held by Nessie’s much larger coils.

That’s right. I do not wish to die, and I know that there is nothing I can eat in our territory that will heal this venom. The Beast Lord knew well what it did.

His tone is bitter and angry, but not at me. His offer is sincere – that’s all I need to know right now. Everything else can be figured out later.

Nodding, I step closer until I feel that I’m close enough for the Bond to anchor. I’m not even within touching distance, but the sea serpent’s desperation lowers his resistance drastically.

Within moments the Battle of Wills space shatters around us and we're back in normal reality. As soon as the Bond has settled into place, I press my mana into the serpent's body and begin healing the wounds.

The poison is indeed vicious. It appears to be a magical variant of a hemotoxin – not only impeding physical healing, but also reducing mana regeneration. It's no surprise that both Nessie and her mate have struggled to heal these wounds, particularly since her mate has turned out to be a Tier two rather than a Tier three. Even so, with my practice in healing, it doesn't take long before I've dealt with his wounds and have moved on to Nessie – after poisons such as Tree-whisperer’s, this Tier three’s venom is easy to flush out.

"Do you have a name?" I ask the male.

No, he answers immediately.

It’s unsurprising – few beasts seem to, Kalanthia and her family being an exception. The other beasts seem to recognise each other mostly by scent or aura.

"How about Loch?" I suggest, thinking it fits with Nessie.

He sends me a sense of indifference. Good enough.

With the healing done and the Bond in place, I decide to indulge my curiosity.

"You're Tier three," I say to Nessie. "How come Loch is still Tier two? I thought you said this was your shared territory."

Loch shifts uneasily but answers before she can.

My mate is kind enough to share it with me. Despite my best efforts – and the help of my mate – I have been unable to Evolve a second time.

The shame in his voice is clear, and I suspect it's a sore point between them.

"Huh," I muse. "Maybe I can help with that."

He turns to face me fully. Even if he's not as large as Nessie, it's still an impressive sight – his head is about the size of a small car.

How could you help with something like that? he asks suspiciously.

"Thanks to our Bond, I can see your internal matrix," I say with a shrug. "Perhaps I can work out why it isn't doing what it should. It wouldn't be the first time."

You've helped others to Evolve?

"Several times, including one to Tier three." I don't mention that it was in another world – there are enough similarities between the worlds to make it relevant.

Loch's suspicion ebbs slightly as he senses my sincerity through the Bond.

Would…would you be willing to help me? he asks, more tentatively.

"I wouldn't have offered if I wasn't," I point out. "And I've offered to help Nessie with the hatchlings too," I add, glancing at the small disturbances in the water where they're lurking.

Loch looks at Nessie in surprise. Though they don't communicate in words, I can sense that this is news to him.

I did not know if we could trust him, she tells him, a touch defensively.

Yet you called him when I was hurt, he points out.

It was the only thing I could think of that might save you, she admits.

Then I understand, Loch says, a note of warmth in his voice as he coils his body affectionately around her. But if he can help, should we not do what we can for our hatchlings? Their healing is slow – possibly non-existent.

We do not yet know if he can help, Nessie points out, coiling restlessly.

Then let us see what he can do. He turns back to me. Entangler – if you can help me and my hatchlings, I will swear life-long loyalty to you and aid you in whatever you wish in recompense.

I feel a little uncomfortable at the pledge, but I don't reject it as my upbringing makes me want to. I can hear the gravity and sincerity with which it's made and I want to respect that. Anyway, it’s logical – if I put the effort in, I’d like to see a return on it.

“Let's see if I can actually help before we discuss what you can do for me in return,” I say finally.

Loch responds by moving closer and coiling around me – a gesture that would feel threatening were he not one of my Bonded now. Placing my hand on his scales, I sink my mind into his Core space and inspect his internal matrix.

Thanks to my experience working with multiple samurans to identify the cause of their limitations, I can immediately see a likely reason for why he's never been able to Evolve. I pull out and meet his gaze directly.

"Your internal matrix has no point of compression."

I don't understand what you mean….

"Let me try to show you. Follow my lead."

Finding one’s Core space seems to be considerably more instinctual for beasts than for humans – and most of them find it by themselves by the time they’re close to Tier three. Within a few minutes, Loch has successfully followed my instructions. Once inside his Core space, I show him how his internal matrix follows the line of his body, extending outward in a single line. Somehow, he’s managed to compress a Core in the centre – a small one barely bigger than the main channel itself – but I doubt that he’ll be able to accumulate the Energy to go any further, not with it strung out like that.

Does this mean I’ll never Evolve again? he asks when we return, a note of defeat in his voice.

"Don’t despair yet,” I reassure him. “Let me check Nessie first, if she doesn't mind. If I can see how she's managed it, that might help give us some guidance as to how to proceed."

If it helps my mate Evolve, then you may, Nessie agrees, though warily.

In her Core space, I find the same basic line, but she's done something rather ingenious with it. Instead of remaining linear, she's reshaped it into a spiral, which allows her to pull Energy towards the centre with increasing speed and force, compressing it into her Core there. She only has two Cores – a big one at the centre, and a smaller one just before it, like beads on a string. I find myself genuinely impressed, and it sparks a few ideas about what I might be able to do with my own Core space – that’s based on curves too so perhaps a spiral in a few key places might help in some way.

Pulling out, I share what I've found with Loch and enter his Core space again to show him how he might imitate it. "But only do it if it feels right to you," I caution him. "Every internal matrix is different. What works for Nessie may not work for you."

I thank you for the inspiration and will bear your warning in mind, he agrees. Then he turns to Nessie. We have seen what he is capable of. Should we not allow him to help our hatchlings?

Nessie hesitates, her body coiling restlessly. Then, finally, she sighs.

I suppose. She eyes me. But I do not yet know whether the Bond – and its controller – is something we should subject our children to.

Loch gives a funny wriggle that I realise is the sea serpent equivalent of a shrug.

Have we not seen enough already? He came to help me because you asked.

He gained a new Bonded as a result, Nessie objects.

What other beast might we have approached for help who would not have demanded their own price? Loch returns reasonably.

Nessie holds out a moment longer, but then caves.

Very well, she tells me, with a glare. You may Bind our hatchlings. But if you do them any harm, you will answer to me.

Technically, as my Bonded, the threat is empty. But I'll never take the threat of a protective parent lightly, Bonded or not. I'm sure she could find ways to make my life difficult if she wished.

"I swear I'll do my best to help them. And if I can't, I'll tell you, and you can continue looking for other solutions."

She holds my gaze for a long moment, then widens her coils to reveal the hatchlings. She urges them forward, and they approach with a wariness that speaks to experience. One comes more readily than the other – I wonder whether it's the one I tossed back into the ocean after Josiah's crew accidentally caught it, or the reverse.

Forming Bonds with the baby sea serpents is easy enough, much like it was with the alcaoris hatchlings. And I'm unsurprised to find other similarities when I examine them.

Both hatchlings have been damaged by the manastorm, but one has managed to press most of the extra mana into its own form of internal matrix. This one's matrix is unlike either parent – though a linear line is the backbone of its form, it also has two protuberances that almost resemble wings. I'm curious where this one might end up. Does it dream of flying?

This one’s primary issue is that some connections have formed incorrectly, blocking Energy from flowing evenly around its matrix. That's a fairly straightforward adjustment, and I make it myself, then help ease the parts of its body that have been saturated and therefore damaged by too much mana from the blockages.

The other hatchling's situation is more serious, though still not as bad as Noir's – its internal matrix hasn't been destroyed outright, just completely fragmented. Sections are entirely detached from other sections, with no coherence to it at all.

I pull out and share my findings with it and its parents.

What do you suggest? Nessie asks anxiously.

"I'd suggest letting her try a variety of beast Cores to find ones that resonate with her," I reply, thinking of Loren and the way he built his Energy channels bit by bit. "Once the matrix has some direction, I can help guide it further. But right now, I'm worried that nudging it in any particular direction might not suit your hatchling and could cause problems down the line."

We will hunt the seas clean, Loch announces with determination. Then he pauses. You have done us multiple services and said you would speak of the cost later. What do you wish in return?

I know exactly what I want. In return for a few hours of effort, this could save me – and my settlement – multiple headaches in the future.

"We're going to be establishing trading routes with other settlements – many ships, in both shallow waters and deep,” I start slowly. “They'll need protection, much as Nessie has been doing for the fishing fleet. I'd need your help escorting those vessels. But I'd also appreciate it if you could recruit other beasts because. as powerful as you are, your numbers are small, and I imagine you don't want to be apart from each other for too long either. If you could find other beasts who might be interested in exchanging service for healing, or help in reaching the next Tier, that would be invaluable. Is that something you could do?"

Nessie and Loch look at each other.

I believe we could find some interested parties, Nessie says.

There is the one-eyed behemoth, for one, Loch adds. Are you capable of regrowing things like eyes? Or lost limbs? he asks me.

I hesitate for a moment.

"It depends, but I'd certainly be willing to try,” I offer in the end. I can probably do it, but I don’t want to over-promise.

We’ll do our best, Nessie tells me even as Loch offers a more enthusiastic response.

We will not fail you. With that, he slips straight into the water and disappears from sight with impressive speed.

He seems eager, I think to myself with some amusement. Seeing that Nessie is about to follow, I speak quickly. "Please don't forget to accompany the fishing vessels later today – I'm sure they'll be heading out."

I will, Entangler, she promises. Then she pauses, just before slipping beneath the surface. My mother always warned me of the dangers of humans. Yet I find myself grateful to have met you. My mate would have died had it not been for your healing.

"I'm glad I could help," I say. “Perhaps we can help you against the one who tried to kill him if it comes back again.”

Perhaps, Nessie replies thoughtfully.

She holds my gaze for a moment more, then slides into the water. A ripple, and there's nothing left to indicate that any of them were ever there.

I spread my wings and lift into the air. Time to continue testing Bond Teleport. Still, I'd call this time well spent – particularly if it ends with us finding more escorts for the trade routes to come. Hopefully at least some of them will be willing to escort vessels all the way back to Moriax – we’ll need to open trade routes with those ports sooner or later.
