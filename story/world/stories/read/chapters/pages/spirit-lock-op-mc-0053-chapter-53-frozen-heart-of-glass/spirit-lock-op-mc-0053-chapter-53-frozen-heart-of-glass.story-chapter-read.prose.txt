Mayle was certain Keaden would break down the door, but the twenty professors she summoned managed to restrain and drag him away as he screamed at the top of his lungs.

She crashed against the nearest wall once it was over, letting herself sink to the floor.

“You did the right thing,” Hayden said.

“The right thing…?” She scoffed. “I’m not sure I’ve done anything right since I’ve met you.”

“You did.” Hayden glanced to make sure she wasn’t recording before saying, “Keaden was sick. And your firm disconnection saved him.”

“What? You think you’re a psychiatrist now?”

“Jeramori Ka Molana Kayo,” Hayden said, ignoring her sarcasm. “That’s the most common name for it from where I’m from.”

Those words captured her interest, so she listened.

“It roughly translates to ‘Frozen Heart of Glass.’ I suppose that’s a strange idiom, so we’ll just say, ‘Locket Worn Forever.’” He paused deftly before continuing. “Imagine a locket, a necklace containing a picture of a loved one. You received the locket from someone else who made you promise that you’d keep it safe. At first, you view it as someone else’s; over time, you see it as your locket—a keepsake. Yet after decades or centuries, you come to believe that the loved one inside that locket is yours; you create a personality for them, an ideal, even if they’re still alive…” Hayden made eye contact. “That’s what he was suffering from.”

Mayle was dumbfounded, staring at him with a lost gaze.

“Of course, you’re a person, and you’re alive,” Hayden continued. “That’s what it means to freeze someone’s heart—people immortalize those who they stay beside for extended periods. They believe they know them better than they know themselves, and that belief becomes a tangible object. If their heart ever strays away from their ideal, the freezer becomes disharmonized and thinks that it’s breaking.

“That’s when they become controlling. They’re seeking to protect their heart—no matter how damaging or dangerous the methods they use.

“To them, it’s perfectly justified. They’ve stayed with you for so long and been loyal so consistently that they convince themselves that they would never betray you or harm you. Everything becomes for your good, even if they chain you to a wall and starve you.”

Hayden turned to her.

“Your servant was on the precipice of irrevocable insanity. It’s fortunate you were forcefully separated at the jealousy stage. Much longer and it would have been irreversible.”

Mayle shivered.

“You’re not just justifying yourself… are you?”

“I’m not.” Hayden closed his eyes and turned his head upward to the ceiling, resting the back of his head against the wall. “Mental illness is the leading cause of death and destruction in a universe of extended life spans. That’s why there are rules you must follow. To leave people you love; to return and find them married. To leave the planets you populate, even if you’re destined to find your world or reputation in ruins.

“Because if you don’t, you’ll find yourself killing those you love, experiencing betrayals that are incomprehensible, and trauma that follows you for millennia.”

He stared into her eyes.

“It was time to let him go, Mayle,” Hayden said. “Perhaps in a few decades, you might reunite. Until then, celebrate your relationship and be grateful you separated before it was too late.”

She looked away. “Celebrate…? Doesn’t seem like something you’d say after being betrayed.”

“Oh, I’m not. If I see that man again…” Hayden stepped forward, staring down upon her with the eyes of a malevolent god. “I’ll kill him.” He opened the door. “So keep him away, and be grateful I’m not chasing him.” He left.

Mayle didn’t know where he was going, but she didn’t stop him. It was clear he didn’t trust her. She wouldn’t either. What a terrible day. She hugged her knees and cried.
