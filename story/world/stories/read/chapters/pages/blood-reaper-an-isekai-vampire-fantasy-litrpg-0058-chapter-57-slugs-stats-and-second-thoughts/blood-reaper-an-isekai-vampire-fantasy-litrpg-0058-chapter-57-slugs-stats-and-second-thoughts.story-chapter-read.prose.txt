The alpha slug loomed at the other end of the stone-lined clearing in which Ace stood with his makeshift squad. Fifteen feet of blubbery, quivering goop rose toward the sliver of sky visible through the forest canopy above. Those glowing red eyestalks swiveled with unnerving intelligence, their full attention on Ace.

A smaller slug launched itself at his face, acid dripping from its underside. Ace sidestepped, manifesting another Duskblade that decapitated the creature before it could so much as shriek.

Ace scanned the rest of their surroundings with cold precision. Horse-sized slugs—thirty-one of them left, by his count—circled their position with calculated patience. Their eyestalks moved in perfect unison, locked onto the team while maintaining formation.

Only Marcus's Terror Cascade kept them at bay, the psychic attack creating ripples of confusion through their primitive hive mind. But Ace recognized the slugs' growing resistance—their movements were becoming more coordinated with each passing minute.

"Anyone have a brilliant escape plan?" Ace kept his voice steady despite the surge of dread at their dire situation. The slugs tightened their circle by inches, testing the boundaries of Marcus's influence.

Tara pressed against his back, her body tense. "We could try climbing out?"

She nodded toward the sheer wall leading up tot he overlook where they’d began this ordeal. Right at that moment, a softball-sized glob of acid sailed through the air, striking the dirt wall ten feet up. The stone hissed and bubbled, sending a cloud of noxious vapor into the forest.

"Not without a distraction," Ace replied, watching as the wall of dirt and rocks dissolved.

Ace clicked through his options, but he had to discard each one faster than the last. They were outmaneuvered, outnumbered, and running dangerously low on resources. The slugs had formed a perfect containment pattern—textbook encirclement that would make his old drill sergeant proud.

"I can’t hold Terror Cascade much longer," Marcus said through gritted teeth, his hands trembling slightly as he maintained his psychic assault.

Olivia tilted her head, studying the slugs with detached fascination. "The acid creates the most beautiful patterns on the stone. Look at how the erosion forms those spirals."

"Not the time to appreciate art," Ace said darkly.

His Soul Meter reserves flickered at dangerously low levels. He had enough for one major attack. Maybe two if he pushed himself to the brink.

Three if he gave it his all.

The realization hit him with brutal clarity: their standard approach wouldn't work. Not against this many. Not with coordinated attacks directed by the alpha.

An idea crystallized in his brain, equal parts brilliant and suicidal.

Ace spun around, grabbing Tara by the shoulders with an intensity that made her step back. "Your SM reserves—how much do you have left?"

Her eyes narrowed, reading his expression with the practiced instinct of someone who'd patched up too many dying patients. "Enough for one major heal. Why?"

"Could you bring someone back from the edge?” he prodded. “If they were almost gone?"

"Goddamn it, Ace." Her face hardened. "What suicidal bullshit are you planning now?"

The air between them vibrated with unspoken understanding—the bond that had slowly formed in blood and shadow since their first night in this hell.

“Don’t worry about that,” he ordered with a nod to the steep wall of dirt and stone behind them. “You guys need to get up there. I’ll buy you time.”

Olivia scoffed. "We can’t leave our best fighter behind. Besides, the probability of survival for any of us in this scenario is—"

“Not good, I’d wager,” Marcus interrupted. "I’ll be the distraction. If we all—"

"No," Ace cut him off, the decision already made.

Years of combat had taught him when a situation required sacrifice.

Olivia remained unnervingly calm, her gaze drifting between Ace and the approaching slugs. “Incoming.”

A massive slug surged forward, faster than the others. Its body convulsed, launching a spray of acid that struck a rock inches from Tara’s foot. The stone dissolved instantly, sending her scrambling backward with a startled yelp.

"Time's up." Ace looked Tara in the eye, his intense glare leaving no room for debate. "Get ready to run."

Tara grabbed his wrist, her fingernails digging in hard enough to bruise. "Don't you die on me, Sergeant."

He glanced down at her hand, surprised by the show of affection, but they didn’t exactly have time to hug teddy bears or share feelings.

The slugs closed in, their collective hissing practically vibrating with hungry anticipation.

“Go,” he ordered, his voice dropping into the detached command tone of his military past. "Twenty feet up should be enough. The alpha controls their movements—they'll follow the biggest threat."

“Fine,” Tara said, swallowing hard. “But the moment we're up that wall, you retreat. Promise me.”

The forest around them suddenly seemed impossibly distant, and the slugs were momentarily forgotten as her gaze held him locked in place. Something unspoken passed between them—fragile and fierce.

"Roger that," he responded softly.

Marcus gestured toward a section of wall with slightly better handholds. "Let's move while they're still confused." He began herding Olivia toward it, casting one last glance at Ace. "Don't die dramatically, alright? It's cliché."

As the team fought their way toward at the base of the sheer wall, Ace closed his eyes. He reached deep into his core, where shadow and blood intertwined, and activated Shadow Realm Immersion one last time.

Yet again, the frigid cold of another world hit him squarely in the chest—not the simple absence of heat but a primal chill that seeped through flesh and bone to touch something deeper. Writhing tendrils of darkness wrapped around his body, distorting the light until he was entirely swallowed by the shadows.

And, as always, there were the whispers.

Faceless entities lurked in his periphery. Watching. Waiting. Their voices scratched at the edges of his consciousness. Hungry. Ancient. Curious about the vampire who walked between worlds.

Ace forced himself to ignore them, focusing instead on the physical realm where dozens of oversized slugs suddenly froze in unison. Their eyestalks swiveled toward him, sensing the power rolling off his incorporeal form. Marcus's Terror Cascade faded into irrelevance as a new, more potent stimulus commanded their attention.

Time to do this, then.

Ace reached into his Abyssal Armory, and shadow coalesced around his hand as he summoned the Crimson Sword he had absorbed. The obsidian broadsword hummed, its blood-channeling grooves lighting with a faint red glow that pulsed like a heartbeat.

The alpha slug reared back, its massive body contracting before launching forward with unexpected speed. The smaller slugs followed, abandoning their encirclement to surge toward Ace in a wave of corrosive goo.

Ace charged directly into their midst, his body accelerating beyond anything a human could dream of doing. Blisteringly fast. Incredibly strong. His boots slammed against the patchy ground as he bolted forward, and shadows trailed behind him like a comet's tail as he raced straight for the heart of the horde.

The blade in his hand sang with anticipation, eager to taste what passed for these creatures’ blood.

He had an idea, but he had no clue if it was going to work.

Usually, he wouldn’t be able to attack anything in the Shadow Realm. He could only Mark his targets, and then he would have to phase back to reality to actually land a blow. However, given that the alpha had absorbed his Duskblades and clearly connected with the Shadow Realm, he wondered if he could attack it, now, despite the two of them being in different realms.

Worth a shot.

The Crimson Sword cut through the air with a predatory whistle, trailing void-tinged afterimages as Ace carved a path through the surging mass of slugs. The blade cleaved through the first slug with minimal resistance, its obsidian edge parting translucent flesh like warm butter.

His hunch had been right.

Thank God.

Acid splashed in all directions, and this time, it threatened to hit his shadow-infused form. He spun and momentarily dropped to one knee as it sailed overhead. After it had passed by, Ace pivoted on his heel, his sword following in a tight arc that bisected another blob of wriggling ooze. His mind clicked into the familiar rhythm of combat—reading positions, anticipating movements, finding the angles that offered maximum output for minimum energy expenditure. Two more slugs fell in rapid succession, their bodies dissolving into puddles of hissing fluid.

But he wouldn’t be able to stay in the Shadow Realm much longer. His 10 seconds were almost up.

Each kill released a pulse of life essence that Ace passively drew toward himself with Lifeforce Siphon, the energy flowing into his depleted reserves like water into parched earth. But it wasn't enough. The technique harvested only a fraction of what he needed to sustain his magic. His Soul Meter depleted more and more with each passing second—an inevitable countdown to a gruesome death.

It was still a losing battle, and this whole plan of his was one hell of a risk.

Ace made the risky choice to glance over one shoulder to check on his team. Olivia climbed with surprising grace, her movements economical and precise. Marcus had one arm wrapped around Tara’s waist, practically dragging her up the incline as loose dirt cascaded beneath her feet. She constantly glanced back toward the battle, her face tight with barely contained fear.

They'd made it perhaps ten feet up.

It wasn’t enough.

A smaller slug launched itself toward Ace's left flank. He twisted, bringing the Crimson Sword down in a vertical slash that split it cleanly from eyestalks to tail. Its neural cluster pulsed once before going dark.

The hive mind adapted with each death. Three slugs converged simultaneously, approaching from different directions, but they didn’t attack.

They waited.

He was about to be kicked from the Shadow Realm, and they somehow knew it.

The alpha remained stationary, pulsing with internal light as it directed the assault. Its eyestalks tracked Ace's every movement with predatory intelligence that seemed to grow sharper by the second. The creature's massive body contracted, internal pressure building visibly beneath its semi-transparent skin.

Ace didn’t recognize the danger until it was too late.

The alpha’s mouth snapped open, unleashing a pressurized spray of concentrated acid in a wide arc. Unlike before, this substance glowed with a sickly light that distorted the air around it—and it burned with all the fire of a star as it arced right at him.

In the Shadow Realm, the other world was marked in shades of black and gray. This, however, was bright orange.

Shit.

It was going to hit him.

Shadow Realm Immersion wouldn't protect him from this. A direct hit in this dimension would register at double damage, bypassing his incorporeal state entirely. The acid curtain launched toward him, covering too much area to dodge completely.

In a last-ditch effort to avoid it, Ace threw himself sideways. He twisted mid-air to minimize the chance of getting hit. The majority of the spray missed him, but some of it caught his shoulder. Pain lanced through his body, sharp and immediate, and he gritted his teeth as he fought off the agony. He hit the ground hard, his head slamming into the cold stone beneath him, and he briefly saw stars.

No, not stars.

Sunlight.

That had launched him clear out of the Shadow Realm.

Fuck.

A smaller slug seized the opportunity, launching itself at his right leg. Ace brought his sword down on it, but he wasn't fast enough. The creature latched onto his armored calf, and its body convulsed as it released a concentrated burst of corrosive fluid.

His back arched as the pain froze him in place. He stifled a long string of curses, trying to distract himself from the miserable rush burning through him, but it didn't work. The Thornhide Armor hissed and bubbled as the acid made contact.

And, to his relief, the armor’s abilities kicked in.

The armor’s plates suddenly bristled, and obsidian thorns erupted from the surface. The thorns pierced the slug's gelatinous body, driving deep into its internal organs, and one even lodged in its brain. The creature shrieked, a high-pitched squeal that cut through the cacophony of battle, and another pulse of magic from the armor's defense mechanism launched the gooey bastard backward. The slug sailed through the air, its punctured body spraying acid in an arc before landing with a wet splat several meters away.

The armor pulsed against his skin, almost alive, and the thornplates readjusted to cover the damaged area. He could feel the enchantment drawing on his essence to repair itself, the blood he'd used to attune it creating a symbiotic bond that responded to threats without conscious direction. The remaining acid rolled off the armor's surface, repelled by the armor’s abilities, until only small scorch marks remained on its surface.

A surge of relief washed through him as the pain subsided, but it was short-lived.

The damage was done. His armor had prevented a complete disaster, but the acid had eaten through the outer layer, exposing the bleeding muscle beneath.

"Ace!" Tara's voice cut through the chaos, tight with panic. "The acid's eating through your armor!"

Yeah.

Obviously.

"Keep climbing!" he shouted back, his voice strained as he fought to reestablish full Shadow Realm Immersion.

The slugs closed in.

Try as he might, he couldn’t phase. His Soul Meter had dropped too low—dangerously close to complete depletion. It simply wasn’t enough to trigger his Racial Skill.

He was screwed.

And those damned slugs knew it.

Their movements became more aggressive as they converged from all directions, lurching toward him at an agonizingly slow rate.

They were enjoying this.

The hunt.

The kill.

Ace forced himself to focus through the pain. Twenty-six targets remaining. Team still exposed. HP and SM critically low.

Time for a more desperate approach.

“Here goes nothing,” he muttered.
