Moonlight

Krevith woke up in a soft bed, naked, next to a woman, with only a sheet stopping the rays of light from touching their bodies. She had beautiful auburn hair and sharp features that softened in sleep. The plain sheet conformed to her curvy, tanned body. Krevith found her beautiful, but more importantly, completely unknown to him. He froze in place, finding it hard to breathe or organize his thoughts. He had just been in the Dream a minute ago. Now he was in a strange room with a strange woman.

The Survivor extricated himself from the bed, fearful of the gap in his memory and hoping that he didn't do anything harmful. He made it silently to the door and cracked it open with a squeal that sounded like snapping wood in the silence. The woman stirred, but just turned over and shoved a pillow onto the top of her head. Outside the room, Krevith was met with a massive, fluffy mountain dog. The animal sat there staring up at him, its tail wagging wildly back and forth. He kneeled and ruffled the dog's fur, to which they broke their sitting position and began licking his face.

Not laughing was a struggle for Krevith, but the brief moment of levity only lasted a moment as he stood back up and saw the picture hanging on the wall. It was him, picking the woman up and kissing her cheek. He didn't remember that.

His hands dropped to his sides, and the dog began licking them. The other Survivors. Krevith needed to find them.

"Honey?" a muffled voice, the woman's voice slurred through the haze of sleep, "are you up?"

Like a deer in headlights, he froze. He swallowed a ball of thick saliva and responded, "Yes?"

"Could you get me some water?"

"Okay."

The man made his way down the stairs. The house was strange. There were similarities and overlaps from his world. He had seen these light bulbs before, but they were rare and only seen in government buildings or churches, not multiple pasted in one room or three onto a … he didn't know what it was. It spun in a circle, five thin, carved wooden planks spun rapidly at almost a rotation per second. The climate was cool, reminding him of windy autumn days. He made it to the kitchen, and his jaw almost dropped.

They were rich. Actually, screw rich, they were living in luxury. Marble countertops and a sink. Cabinets full of ceramic and a car in the garage. He filled up one of the glasses and brought the water upstairs.

"Thank you, love," the woman said, still groggy. She sat up just enough to drink before lying back down and closing her eyes.

The Survivor had so many questions. He wanted to start by asking the woman who she was, but that wouldn't go over well. During his time as an enforcer, he had learned about conflict escalation, and no matter how confused he was, telling the woman he didn't know who she was would be a good way for more confusion and possibly panic. He opted for exploring their new world instead. "I'm going to take the car out for a bit. I'll be back soon."

"Pick up some milk while you're out, please," the woman said before turning back over.

He nodded and headed downstairs, unsure of what milk was, but made a mental note to ask someone.

Minutes later, while driving, Krevith pulled over onto the side of the street and stared out in horror. The five of them had been so drained and desperate after the Instance ended that they hadn't questioned if Dorian's world was safe, but he couldn't deny what he saw with his own eyes.

So many Instances, eating away at the world. How could Dorian live in a place like this?

It was a hive of layered monsters, only temporarily visible to him through the veil of what he had just recently learned was the Dream. He could see a massive world tree and on top of it perched a moth, a sea of undulating bodies that rose like the tide, massive spiders the size of skyscrapers, a field of eyes and teeth, and worst of all, he could see a rift into the world they had just escaped from.

From that rift something stood, its malice and anger radiating from it, so intense he could've mistaken it for the sun. He thought he had been helpless back in the Instance, but as he stared at the Old God, he understood the true meaning of the word:

Inevitability.
