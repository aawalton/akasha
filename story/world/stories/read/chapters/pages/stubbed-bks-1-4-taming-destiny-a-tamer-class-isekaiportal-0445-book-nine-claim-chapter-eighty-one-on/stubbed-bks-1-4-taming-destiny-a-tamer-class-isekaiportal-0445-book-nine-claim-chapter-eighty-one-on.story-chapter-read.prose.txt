"I'll need support," he says finally. "Beyond what the healing staff can provide. More space, guards to ensure they’re safely confined, people to help feed, water, and care for them while they’re restrained–"

"I know. I'm going to reach out to Sarran and Loren." I reach out along my Bond to Loren.

Markus? he responds immediately.

I need you and Sarran to come over to the healers rooms to organise logistics. We have a situation. I give them the short version of it all – a parasitic infection, most likely coming from the wells, quarantine needed for the affected, logistics of both to be organised as quickly as possible. Loren sounds shaken but steadies quickly.

I’ll tell Sarran immediately, he assures me and then I feel his focus shift away. I feel relieved that I have reliable people to delegate to.

I return my attention to Healer Thessalanor.

"They’re coming. In the meantime, let’s see how the other healers are doing with Ker Himlan.”

We enter to find the three healers bent over the writhing, growling, straining form of Ker Himlan. They're in quiet discussion with each other, but turn their heads quickly as we walk in.

"Any progress?" I ask, hopefully.

The hesitant expressions on their faces answer before anyone speaks.

"We're struggling to find the parasite," Healer Tor admits. "We can see the traces of the network in his body and how deeply it's interwoven in his nervous system. But the parasite itself…it's extremely good at hiding."

Nothing new to me.

"Look for it in the rear part of the brain," I suggest.

They take my words literally and close their eyes. Healer Marin's eyes shine faintly behind her lids as a glow builds at her fingertips, moving slowly above the patient's head. Frowns creep onto both Tor and Marin's faces, but Yssel’s expression suddenly lights up in triumph.

"Got you," I hear her murmur. Then her expression twists, the glow fades, and she opens her eyes. "I had it, and then it disappeared," she says, frustrated.

"It tends to do that," I agree grimly. "Did Healer Thessalanor warn you that if you do manage to grasp it and it realises there's no way out, it will detonate?"

"He did," Tor confirms, opening his eyes as well.

"Perhaps if we could watch how you do it, we could better understand the technique," Yssel suggests.

"I should mention that I can only really do it reliably through a Bond with the patient," I say as a disclaimer. "My approach will be somewhat different from yours."

"I'm sure it will still be very informative, Lord Markus," Yssel says, with professional composure, though I catch the gleam of curiosity in her eyes.

The healers shift aside to let me approach Ker Himlan's face. He glares with the same unfocused fury the other two had before I freed them of the parasite, and his growling rises slightly as I draw close.

"Ker Himlan, it's Lord Markus. I want to help you. Please tap twice with one of your fingers if you can hear me."

I wait. No response – unsurprising. Both of the others had been completely unaware of their surroundings while the parasite was in control. Looking into his eyes, I silently invoke Dominate, and the Battle of Wills space opens up around me. I walk through the minimal resistance and approach the figure on the other side.

“Ker Himlan,” I call.

You can't be here! he cries, fear crashing into me in a wave. I'm free! You can't do this to me!

“I don't wish to do this to you at all,” I say. “Are you aware of what's been happening to your body? Are you aware that you attacked people in the warehouse?”

No, he replies, with a shudder. But that doesn't mean you can enslave me. I haven't had a trial. I'm a free man!

“There is no need for a trial–”

There's always a need for a trial! I have rights!

“Ker Himlan,” I say firmly, coming to a stop in front of him. The fear around him is so thick that if he could move, I suspect he'd already be sprinting in the opposite direction. “You are currently under the influence of a parasite. You will not be held responsible for the actions you took under that influence. This is not a punishment. I am not enslaving you. I am offering you a Bond so that I can treat you and remove the parasite.”

No! No Bond! The fear is thick and clawing. You can't force it on me – I'm a free man! I'll take you to the courts!

I wait for him to finish, eyeing him steadily. This is unlike any conversation I've had before in a Battle of Wills. And given how many people are affected by this parasite, I have a sinking feeling it might not be the last one like this.

For a moment I wonder what the law would say in this case. In theory, he is right – Titanbends are not permitted to Bind free citizens on pain of death. In practice, I suspect that Nicholas would be able to put enough pressure on the King that he wouldn’t enforce the full penalty unless a noble were the victim. And if I could truthfully say that it was purely to save the person….

Yet to Bind him now when he is so clearly against it would be stepping over one of my own lines. And I hold those in more respect than the law of a country that, ultimately, isn’t my own. I’ve already taken too many steps down the slippery slope that leads to tyranny – I am not keen to risk sliding further.

"Ker Himlan," I say, pressing my sincerity toward him. "If you insist on refusing the Bond, I will not force it on you." It’s a hard decision, but it’s one that feels right.

A faint hint of relief flickers through the fear and the fear that had been writhing around the man calms a little.

I wish I could leave it here. But the healers need to see me remove a parasite for the good of the settlement and the benefit of everyone else who has surely been infected. One man cannot stand in the way of the good of so many others.

“However,” I continue and feel some of that fear return, “let me be completely clear. For the sake of both you and the settlement, we need to remove that parasite, and doing so without a Bond is far riskier. In Kera Milith – another person suffering from the same parasite – it detonated in her spine when it was cornered. She would have been paralysed for life if she hadn't been Bonded to me. It was only because of that Bond that I was able to heal her in time. Are you truly willing to risk permanent disability or death? I swear that I will release the Bond the moment the danger has passed and I can guarantee that you will come through the experience intact.”

For a moment, he wavers. Is it true that a Bond is easier to form the second time than the first?

I hesitate, then incline my head.

“It is,” I admit, “but–”

He doesn't let me finish.

Then no. No Bond. I trust the healers. They do wonders, those healers.

His tone carries a finality that the emotions surrounding him confirm. I sigh, pause halfway back to my starting point, and glance over my shoulder.

“Last chance.”

I'm not changing my mind.

His fear and determination together surge at me like a battering ram. I nod curtly and walk until the Battle of Wills shatters around me.

Healer Thessalanor, who has seen me do this twice already, looks at me with quiet curiosity.

“Is there an issue, Lord Markus?”

The other healers turn from their low conversation about the case at the question.

"A slight one," I admit. "Ker Himlan doesn't want the Bond, and as a free citizen, I can't legally force him – nor would I want to go against his wishes." I can't deny it stings, slightly, that someone might prefer the risk of death to a Bond with me. Though in fairness, his fear was obviously too overwhelming to weigh the options clearly.

The healers' faces are carefully blank, though Yssel looks faintly approving while Tor has traces of disapproval.

"Then we can't see the treatment?" Healer Marin ventures.

"We'll still go forward," I say. "I warned Ker Himlan of the risks and he made his choice. I'll proceed without the Bond."

"Can you do it without one?" Healer Tor asks, then hastens to add, "Not to question your competency, my lord."

I hesitate for a moment. His body will resist me naturally without the Bond, but I know enough about this parasite's habits and movements by now to have a reasonable chance of finding it. Hopefully that will tilt the scales in my favour.

Sighing, I give an expressive Moriaxar shrug with a single hand.

"I hope so. There isn’t any other choice, is there?” Yssel and Marin drop their eyes away from mine. Thessalanor and Tor continue looking at me, their bleak gazes acknowledging the truth of my words.

I place my hand on Ker Himlan's forehead. Without the Bond I need every advantage I can get, and direct contact helps. Closing my eyes, I press my magic into him and begin feeling my way through his body.

It's exactly as hard as I expected. Without the Bond it's like navigating underwater in complete darkness instead of walking through a lighted room – everything is slower, less precise; my awareness is muffled and unreliable. At the same time, I have to keep my presence as quiet as possible so as not to spook the parasite before I've pinpointed its location.

Then my familiarity with its habits pays off. I sense it sitting exactly where I expected, in the third vertebra of Ker Himlan's spine. I'm just reaching to wrap it in magic when Layton's voice cuts through the bond network, sharp with urgency.

Markus?

Not wanting to make a mistake at this delicate moment, I pull back.

What is it?

The situation's getting worse. Two more attacks – one in District Two, one near the livestock shelters. I’ve sent guards to calm the situation and bring the perpetrators to you.

I’ll warn the healers, I tell him grimly. We need to get that quarantine in sooner rather than later. Valence, do you have any parties who can help Layton’s guards?

No, the man replies tensely. And I won’t any time soon. Another wave has come from the forest. Bigger than the first. I need everyone I can get here. I’m not sure the wall will hold against them.
