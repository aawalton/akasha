The conference room had been renamed.

Someone—Jin suspected Veric, based on the dry handwriting on the plaque that had been bolted to the door—had christened it WARCOM, and the name had stuck with a speed that suggested everyone had been waiting for an excuse to stop calling it "the conference room" like they were scheduling a budget review instead of planning a war.

Jin stepped through the door with Reyana—still angry and giving him a death stare—half a step behind him, and took stock of the room.

The central table had been cleared of everything except a large, flat projection surface that Illiana had rigged up sometime during his involuntary nap. The holographic display hummed softly, casting blue-white light across the faces of everyone already gathered.

Commander Mathew stood at the head of the table in his usual position, hands braced against the edge, his single eye tracking Jin the moment he entered. Beside him, Illiana was running diagnostics on the projection system with the focused intensity of someone who'd been asked to make military-grade equipment out of salvaged parts and sheer willpower.

Jorn and Lennon flanked the far end. Veric sat to Mathew's right, cigarette unlit between his fingers—someone had apparently told him smoking in the WARCOM was not permitted, and from the look on his face, he was taking it as a personal insult. Bandages wrapped his torso beneath his shirt, visible where the collar gaped, and his left arm sat in a sling that he kept adjusting with irritated shrugs. His eyes were still bloodshot, and Jin felt a pang of guilt for putting his men through his mess.

Hans and Elenor occupied chairs near the wall. Vera sat beside them with a data-slate balanced on her knee.

And Joe leaned against the far wall in his usual spot, arms crossed, expression easy. But Jin caught the way his right hand—the one with the blackened fingertips—was tucked into his jacket pocket.
