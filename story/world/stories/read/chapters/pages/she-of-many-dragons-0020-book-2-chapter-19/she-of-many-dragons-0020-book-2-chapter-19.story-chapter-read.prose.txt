Despite her dragon's rather pointed suggestions and some outright pleas from Numi especially, Alice decided to hold off on creating another new sub-aspect. At least, for now. She simply did not have an immediate need for another dragon. Plus, a new face would raise more questions around the Protectorate. The story she was going with was that her friends were familiars. She did not want people—especially the Protectorate leaders—to fully understand that Prim could generate more dragons for her at need.

She was getting an unexpected vibe from the guild. It was nothing she could put a finger on, but she had a bad feeling as she walked around the Protectorate. Slanted glances, whispers she could not quite hear, and lingering stares. Most were at her dragons, which was entirely reasonable considering that many people had only seen the like before in combat. But too often, those assessing gazes were leveled at her.

And anyway, Numi, as she pointed out repeatedly, already had a sub-aspect. Though Alice had not seen the dragon for weeks, she had clearly been working diligently at the trading post. From the amount of experience and the occasional skill-ups Alice received, she knew that Fixie was working diligently. As a result, Numi had the most levels out of any of them.

Instead, Alice bade her dragons to continue to level up as much as possible. While they were a little disappointed that another helper would not be joining their group, they were excited over the prospect of growing stronger.

"We are the prime dragons," Prim said two days into their newest spurt of training, after Numi once again whined that she wanted another sub-aspect and Iggy hinted that he should have another instead, if only for additional combat training.

"What does that mean?" Natura asked.

Prim sat in a proud pose, lifting her slim head as she spoke. "It means that we are the base for the dragons to come," she said grandly. When she was met by blank looks from everybody, including Alice, she sighed and elaborated. "Do you honestly think that Alice will stop leveling anytime soon? There are people in this city who are in the hundreds of levels. Yes, they are very old, but Alice is young, and she will have time to create many, many dragons. We are but the first, and that means we will have the most time out of everybody who is to come to level up and become the strongest. We will be the ones that other dragons later will look up to."

Alice took in her words silently, a little shocked. She had not thought of her class that way. She had only been She of Many Dragons for, what, a little less than six months now? But she had so many dragons already... she could not imagine where she would be ten years from now.

"Not everybody will look up to you," Bella said a little snottily. She then copied Prim's pose, fluttering her wings in a light-hearted mocking gesture like a noble lady fanning herself. "After all, they may all look up to me instead."

"They very may well," Numi said. "You are among the first, are you not?"

Bella stopped immediately. She looked honestly blank at that, then said quietly, "I suppose. I was not aware that I was included among the first."

"You are," Alice said, looking around at all of them. "You are my prime dragons."

That most certainly included Bella. She had done very well for herself with leveling. First, cleaning the vermin out of the courtyard, and then later in the city had done wonders.

In addition, there was a canal nearby where trash, as well as more vermin, were often thrown in. With Alice's permission, Bella had been allowed to go out at night and fish what she could from the waters.

She promised that whatever she grabbed was already dead, though Alice suspected she had pulled out some very grim things. All of it was used for her burnt sacrifice, and as a result Bella had physically grown more than any of them. Once the size of a kitten, her body length now measured against Alice's arm. And her growth did not seem to be slowing anytime soon.

Natura was still the largest, though she was not much of a flyer yet. Every night she sat up on the Protectorate rooftop and flapped her wings to exercise them.

Oliver had noticed her dragon's growth and remarked one day, pointing with his chin to Bella, "She has grown bigger, hasn't she? How large do you believe your dragons will get?"

"I don't know," Alice said blithely. She and Oliver had made a habit of eating lunch together, and she found that she was actually looking forward to it—except when he asked probing questions about her class. "I don't believe they'll be nearly as big as those gray searchers."

"Thank goodness," he said with a laugh. "They would eat you out of house and home, surely."

That was not going to be a problem anytime soon. Numi had expanded the range of her searching every night and had found quite a few coins lying forgotten on the street. Granted, none of them had been the extra valuable blue steel coins, but this was a rather rich and well-off part of town. She had snapped up a couple of golds as well as quite a few silvers.

Alice had not had to think about money for a long time. She wasn’t even spending what Numi found, as the Protectorate Guild seemed content to feed her and her dragons.

"So this is what people do all day in the Protectorate Guild?" she asked, turning the question session back to Oliver. "You all train until... what? There is a threat to the city? What else do you do?"

"It depends on your class, of course," he said. "But unfortunately, no. In fact, this is quite the break for me."

She scooted a little closer. "What do you mean?"

"Well," he began, "I'm a nobleman's son." He said this with no small amount of pride. "Even though my brother can run off and do what he wants, some of us have more integrity and honor." He sighed. "I am primed to take over the management of my family estate. And unfortunately, that takes a lot of my time. So I enjoy it when I'm able to come to this guild and train."

Alice was not sure what to say about that. After all, she had once been technically a part of his estate as a General Laborer.

"Oh," she said, and then bit into her sandwich. If she was chewing, she would be less likely to spit out dangerous questions.

Oliver continued. "But others do things as well. Outside of—what did you call it? Saving the city?" He smiled. "A large part of Samantha's duties is to gather mana for the city storage. At higher levels, mana is worth more than gold, and quite a few powerful spells require mass amounts of it. So should the city ever need it for defense, they would be able to tap into those mana reserves. There are also those who have social-type skills, such as high charisma, so they work in diplomacy. And of course, the seers you met often try to discover what challenges we have to come."

As Alice was still chewing, she kept her next thought to herself: Such as warning people about more gray searchers?

No more of those had shown up over the last week, but their last shuddering gasps were still on her mind. She hated the thought that anybody with her class would send those poor creatures to their doom. She couldn’t imagine doing that with any of her dragons, even the sub-aspects.

But you have put the sub-aspects in more danger to keep the others safe, a small thought reminded her. So that ability to make the hard decision lies within you.

But not like that, she told herself firmly. I would never treat any of my dragons, even my sub-aspects, like that.

Come to think of it... were the gray searchers actually sub-aspects? They had seemed unusually single-minded, in the same way that Spark and Aconite and Fixie were. That was something to think about later.

Oliver abruptly changed the subject, surprising Alice out of her thoughts. "I did check on my brother recently. He changed his entire class and is only a merchant, as you know," he added with an embarrassed laugh as he scratched the back of his neck. "I have never seen him so... content. He and that wife of his seem happy in their little dwelling. She certainly is an interesting woman."

"Dolly is incredibly fixated on achieving what she wants," Alice said with a sniff. Then she softened. "I am glad to hear that they are doing well."

"Yes, they seem to be growing your investment." Oliver looked sideways at her. "You ought to check on such things in person. It is an unfortunate truth that when the cat's away, the rats will play."

"Rats?" Bella asked, lifting up her head and looking toward the two of them. She had been dozing in the mid-afternoon sun and had clearly been only half-listening. "What rats?"

"It is only a saying," Oliver said. "That unfortunately a person with privilege needs to monitor their workers to make sure that they are doing the right thing..." He suddenly hesitated and looked askance at Alice. "If you don't mind me overstepping."

Alice would have liked to bristle. His words smacked of high-handed noble wisdom that she neither wanted nor needed. But... she also knew Dolly's personality and had to admit that there was some truth there.

"You may have a point," she said. "Though my dragons have been working so hard with their levels, and I don't want to interrupt their training to take such a long trip."

“You must not,” he said so quickly that Alice looked at him in surprise. “At least not until after you reach level 10. It is an important keystone.”

“Why?” Her surprise made her bold. “What did you receive when you reached level ten—unless, that is a rude question?” she realized, looking down.

“It… can be a rather personal one,” he admitted softly. “But I don’t mind. My mana pool doubled when I hit level 10, and I receive additional mana bonuses for every level afterward." He glanced at Alice as if to gauge her reaction. Then, perhaps seeing that she was not as stunned as she should be, he quickly added, "To people who primarily cast spells, the amount of mana you can draw from means everything, very closely followed by the amount of mana you can condense into a single spell."

"Oh," she said. "Well, that does sound good. I am glad that you got a good bonus."

Inside, however, she was grateful that she did not have to deal with the minutiae of tracking her mana. She had caught snippets of mana condensation methods and theories while walking through the hallways, and it seemed that mages enjoyed spitting out complex mathematical formulae. To someone like Alice, who only knew how to multiply the numbers under five and only vaguely knew how to divide, it all seemed horrendously complex. Maybe this was why she had her dragons to cast spells for her.

Oliver paused before he continued. "Now, if you don't mind, maybe I could ask a question that might seem a little rude?"

Alice smiled, and she found she did not even need to force the expression. "Please do, but don't be hurt if I have to say no."

"Of course," he laughed. "Did your She of Many Dragons evolve from another class?"

That was certainly a loaded question and fraught with more peril than he could possibly know. Or did he? With a flash of anxiety, Alice wondered if Oliver was starting to remember her from her previous life as a General Laborer. Should she lie?

No, she decided after a moment. There was no reason not to tell him the truth.

"It didn't evolve from another class," she said. "I was granted it from a System-generated class seed. It appeared in front of me one day, and of course I knew what it was right away, and I knew its value if I were to sell it. But... I couldn't help myself. I had to take it."

His eyes widened. "A gift from the System? I've only heard about such things in tales and have never seen it for myself. That is rare, but I don't think you'll find anyone who will argue you didn't do the right thing in taking it for yourself."

He briefly touched her hand, then pulled away as if realizing what he was doing. Oliver cleared his throat. "It is said that the System grants a seed to those it has in mind to change their class, so selling it wouldn't be a good idea, nor is buying one," he added with a chuckle. "Because you'll be receiving a class that was originally meant for someone else. Did you... How did you...?" He shook his head and did not finish his sentence.

Alice had the feeling he wanted to know the exact circumstances around receiving such a gift, but she was not interested in telling him.

As a General Laborer, she had owned nothing. And to be entirely truthful, the class seed had generated from Junebug, one of the hunting hounds owned by Oliver's family. It was entirely likely the System had generated it for Roy, the perpetually drunk kennel manager. It was even more possible that the noble family—Oliver's family—would see the seed as their rightful item and something that Alice had stolen.

No, she definitely did not want Oliver to ask. She could always lie, but she was coming to realize that she did not like to lie to Oliver either.

Instead, she looked around and noted that it was midday. "It's nearly lunchtime, and I don't know about you, but I'm feeling hungry. Do you want to take lunch together?"

Oliver had asked her several times on different occasions. Each time she had politely declined. Not because she did not want to go, but because she had been busy training up her dragons. Now, distracting him seemed to be a very good excuse to take him up on the offer.

Oliver brightened immediately. "Yes, I've been wanting to show the cafeteria to you. We have designated cooks from all over the kingdom, and while I don’t mind bringing the food to you, it is best when it's cooked fresh."

He held out his arm to escort her. Smiling, she took it.
