The catastrophic failure of the newly installed pump sent broken chunks of metal and globs of acid flying everywhere. The anti-caustic suits of of the owned diverted much of the toxic liquid away, but one unlucky man was drenched in the stuff. He started screaming, running blindly around. Two other owned dropped to the ground, blood spilling from gashes cut in their suits by metal shards flung from the damaged machine.

Shrapnel from the pump damaged the existing, working pump, and it began gysering acid into the air, flooding down over its control surfaces and eating away at the unprotected metal.

“Shut it down, shut it down!” Boff was screaming, but the other owned was busy helping his fellow to safety. A flood of green, toxic acid flowed down the rocky path.

Boff saw Elion and Keyla standing in the barrel staging area nearby. “Don’t just stand there!” he shrieked. “Leave your work area. Come help!”

Elion ran to one of the downed owned, a woman with a jagged wedge of metal protruding from her shoulder. He dragged her from the path of flowing acid.

Keyla rushed straight to the malfunctioning pump. She ducked beneath an arcing flood of acid, leaping over rivulets flowing over the ground. Acid burned away at everything it touched, filling the air with a thick haze of toxic smoke.

She reached the pump and used her Artefin skill to reach inside of the machine, shorting out its power supplies and shutting it down. The pump shuddered and ground to a halt, outer skin hissing as acid burned it away.

Elion knelt beside the woman he’d helped. Her face was pale and waxy, her breathing shallow. She gasped, her chest rising and falling in uneven jerks.

Elion pressed her hands against her, hoping that nobody would notice what he was doing. He called upon his ability.

Faint golden threads, barely discernable in the bright sunlight, wrapped around the woman, stabilizing and protecting her. Her wide eyes darted back and forth, then slowed, fixing on a vague point in the distance. Beads of sweat dripped down her forehead.

“Help, help, help,” Elion muttered to himself. “I’m helping. This is what helping looks like.” He glanced over at Keyla, working her way back from the machine she’d just shut down. “Or that; that is what helping looks like.”

He glanced down at the woman he’d just stabilized. The chunk of metal was still wedged in her shoulder, but blood no longer seeped from the wound.

“Help her,” Elion muttered, then turned to see Boff dancing around angrily, waving his clipboard in the air. Underneath Boff’s suit, Bondholds jangled from the chains around his arms and neck.

“Don’t just stand there,” Elion breathed, repeating the order Boff had given. “Come help.”

"Help free her,” Elion breathed. “Come help.”

Elion crossed the ground between him and Boff in an instant. The Bondhold did not stop him. He was coming to help.

He grasped for the man’s neck. Boff noticed Elion charging in, and tried to duck out of the way. Elion caught him and threw the man to the ground, knocking the wind out of him.

Tearing at Boff’s suit, Elion ripped the front of it open, and pulled Boff’s facemask away. If he couldn’t breath, he couldn’t issue any more orders.

Boff gasped, choking for air as the toxic haze hit his face. Elion seized his Starholder pendant and yanked.

“Freeze!” Boff commanded, and Elion’s body locked up, muscles locking rigidly in place. He clasped the starholder pendant in his hand. The paperclip securing the chain had started to unbend, the pendent a quarter of an inch from coming loose.

Boff choked, scrabbling at the torn edges of his suit, trying to protect himself from the fumes of the acid pools.

“Let go of that bondhold,” Boff said. Elion dropped the pendant.

“Get off of me and go sit over there,” Boff ordered, pointing to a place on the ground nearby. Elion complied.

Boff dusted himself off, chuckling aggressively. He reached up and fixed the paperclip holding the pendant chain together. “That was close,” he growled, his voice hoarse and raw. “I’m going to have to deal with you, aren’t I?”

The question was rhetorical, and Elion knew it. He didn’t answer. He sat in the mud, on the rocks, watching acid flow past, churning into the dirt, burning away to contaminated ooze. He gritted his teeth, cursing himself. He’d almost had it. Boff had given him the perfect opportunity and he’d almost gotten free.

Boff walked over. “Sit here. Don’t move. Don’t talk to anyone. Don’t even look at anyone.” Elion lowered his eyes. “I’ll be back for you once I’ve dealt with…” he gestured at the mess. “All of this.”

Elion sat in the dirt, orders binding him. Nothing unclear or vague about what Boff just told him. He couldn’t weasel his way out of this one.

Keyla walked over, crouched beside him. “Are you okay?” she asked.

Elion couldn’t speak. He gave her a thumbs up, and scowled.

“Hey,” Boff yelled. “Get away from him.”

Keyla staggered back, and Boff added a new command to Elion’s orders. “No hand signals either!”

What happens if I forget an order? Elion wondered. That’s a lot of commands he’s given to me.

Boff collected his owned and started directing them, some carrying the injured back out of the pits, some picking up parts from the busted pumps.

“Don’t celebrate,” Boff ordered. “I’m going to find something else for you all to do while I work on getting a new pump. It’s going to be worse, I guarantee it.”

Elion sat in the dirt, seething. The sun beat down on him, and sweat trickled down his spine. He hated that Boff had so much power over him. He needed to break free somehow.

The day wore on, and Boff’s owned finished their work, disappearing for lunch. They did not return afterwards. Elion’s legs cramped, spasming. He shifted, cycling through various sitting positions.

Elion gazed around the acid pit. He couldn’t see much of the nearby pump areas or crews. When he did, Boff’s order not to look at anyone made him avert his eyes. He soon realized that he could look near people, as long as he didn’t look ‘at’ them.

He scanned the ridgeline, looking near the activity of the warlock camp. A group of them were moving along the ridge. They were far enough away that the order to not look at anyone prevented Elion from identifying the people in the party, but he recognized the large box they carried with them.

It was the machine they’d used to trap his sword, and were trying to use to trace him. They moved along the to top of the pits, stopping every one-hundred feet or so, and consulting the machine. Each time they pointed down in the ravine.

Elion wondered if they could see him. The grey suit he wore didn’t exactly stand out against the dusty stone around him.

They continued working their way around, and Elion realized that they were trying to triangulate his position. Maybe they thought he was underground. If they had a map of tunnels, then they could send people to every level where he might be.

Unfortunately for Elion, he was not that well hidden. If they simply walked down here, like they had the day before, the machine would lead them right to him. He didn’t have a barrel to hid in this time.

Elion wondered what would happen if he were forcibly removed from the spot. He’d be no longer obeying Boff’s orders to sit here. Then something occurred to him. He had left the lidding area yesterday, despite being ordered not to until his quota was filled. The arachnatronic had carried him away in a barrel, against his will.

He’d been free to fight his way back to the lidding area. Elion would fight to get to and save Liora, and the Bondhold forced him to feel that obeying Boff’s orders were a part of helping his sister. IF the warlocks removed him from this spot, he’d fight them to get back here.

To beat the warlocks his blade would be helpful. So he’d have to get that from them first.

The idea continued developing in his mind as he sat there. But the warlocks did not descend into the acid pits, merely continuing their circuit of the ravine.

Behind the shattered pieces of the celestial sphere, the sun moved through its arc across the sky.

Finally Boff returned.

“Stand up,” he said. “Follow me but stay back ten feet.”

Elion complied.

Elion followed Boff up the stairs, out of the pit. He did his best to stay about ten feet away from Boff, as best he could judge.

When they reached the top of the stairs, Boff ordered him to remove his suit and hang it in the mud room. Elion complied, relieved to be free of the hot sweaty protective layers. Boff also removed his suit. As they passed through the living quarters, he looked around for Keyla, but didn’t see her.

Boff brought Elion out onto the street. They headed away from the main gate. As they walked, the reconstructed buildings of New Kairn Tol gave way to ancient ruins. They approached the wall on the far side of town.

The wall followed the natural terrain, taking advantage of still standing portions of buildings or ridges of the earth.

Here, a large hole opened up in the ground. Ejecta from the crater formed a large portion of the wall. A structure had been partially destroyed by whatever caused the depression. On the far edge of the crater, three stories of the building still stood above ground.

Most of the structure seemed to have been below ground, though, now partially exposed to the air. One side of the structure had been sheered away, and four floors were now visible. Elion couldn’t tell if the building had been constructed underground originally, or if it had been buried over the years.

They descended into the crater, and followed a path that wove through massive chunks of cement and steel.

They climbed up a scaffolding and entered the ancient structure the lower floor. Elion peered down a long hall, disappearing into darkness. Openings along either side reminded Elion of a shopping mall.

“Hey boss!” Boff shouted, waving to a man. “Here’s a new digger, last one I’m bringing over. He’s still a bit fiesty so I’m gonna give him some really specific instructions. Where do you want him?”

“Down at the end where they’re digging,” the man replied. “But we may only use him for a few days. Prator’s going to be pulling owned out of here and sending them back to you to help with the production schedule.”

“Oh, I know,” Boff said. “I’m keeping all my technically capable ones to work on increasing capacity. Besides, after the accident today I didn’t want their labor going to waste.”

The man waved them on down the hall.

They moved on down the hall. Cables, wires, and tubes ran along the floor. Occasional maintenance lights illuminated the passage. The floor seemed to have been made of some kind of polished stone. Elion tried to guess what the building would have been used for. A palace? A shopping center?

They descended a flight of ancient stairs, and entered a partially collapsed atrium.

A massive hole opened up in the center of it.

The air around him had a dank, musty smell, like the caves and tunnels he and Keyla had pushed through before being captured by Kendry.

Boff peered down the hole. A winding scaffolding led down into the ground, lit by bright work lights. At the bottom of the pit, some people moved around, operating machinery. Most of the people were using pickaxes to dig.

“Okay here are your instructions,” Boff said turning to Elion. “Go down there, and get one of the unused pickaxes from the rack down there, and start digging. If you find anything that’s not dirt, tell one of the foremen right away. Show them what you found, then go dig somewhere else. “If someone provokes you or insults you, ignore them and move to work in a different area. If someone tries to hit you, let them. If other people are fighting, let them. Maintain a distance of at least five feet from everyone. Do not attempt to touch other people.”
