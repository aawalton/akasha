A growling rumble cuts through the hush in my cabin. I look in askance at the pile of raptorcats who are currently curled up on the rug. Trouble soon pokes up his head and looks at me sheepishly, sending a strong feeling of hunger at me.

I lean back in my chair, rubbing my eyes that are starting to feel a little strained. My stomach also registers its complaints now I’m paying attention. Perhaps it would be a good idea to take a break.

I stand up and stretch, feeling my fingers brush against the ceiling of my cabin. As expected considering everything, it’s the best sleeping quarters on the ship. That said, it’s still far smaller than I’ve got accustomed to. That’s not a problem per se – the two-room cabin contains everything I need: a generous bed, a bay window looking out to the stern of the ship, and even a desk and a small sitting area with two comfortable chairs in addition to the antechamber that’s more of a meeting room than my personal space. It’s just that it’s definitely not big enough for all of my companions to be present, and I miss cuddling up all together.

The cabin isn’t even big enough to hold Kalanthia. Nor is the rest of the ship, really, unless a space is cleared for her on deck. I suppose it’s a good thing that she has chosen to spend most of her time so far tucked in my spatial tattoo – with her strong connection to the earth, she finds being so far from land to be more than a little unsettling, and she has the Cores from the two Tier threes she killed to consume and process.

In that state, she only has to come out once a week or so to fill her belly – and boy, can that feline eat when she chooses to – and then goes back to meditating.

That’s not the case for the rest of us.

“Alright, Trouble,” I tell him with mock resignation. “Let’s go and get some food. Don’t want you to start trying to eat me.”

The raptorcat gives a happy chirp and quickly pushes himself to his feet, bringing the Energy Heart over in his mouth so I can tuck it away. I don’t put it in my Inventory, instead placing it into my desk drawer and locking it – that way I know which Hearts are being used. Perhaps one day I’ll be willing to leave them out, but I don’t know enough about every single person on this ship to leave a valuable natural Core just lying around.

His movement disturbs the rest of his pack – they’re cuddled up so closely that it would be hard for it not to – and Bastet and the other two males decide that food sounds like a good idea, giving me the Hearts they’ve been absorbing. Ninja is the only exception, but I decide to leave her be – she’s seemed possessed by a spirit of determination recently, and I suspect she’s close to Evolution.

I also secure the grimoire I’ve been reading – Nicholas would kill me if one of our family grimoires ended up in the wrong hands because of my carelessness. This one, I do put in my Inventory – the enchantments that protect it are stable and designed to withstand the rigours of an Inventory space.

It’s been interesting reading the thoughts of Nicholas’ ancestor now that I’ve finally had the peace and quiet to do so. The grimoire is ancient – written almost two and a half thousand years ago by the son of the Titanbend founder. Longevity among Classers clearly changes how power endures: I don’t think any dynasties on Earth lasted past two thousand years, yet Moriax and the Starblade dynasty predate the Titanbends entirely.

And while there have been changes since Grenaul Titanbend’s time, they are far fewer than I would expect from a comparable span of Earth history. It makes me wonder whether one reason we’re being sent to the Lost Continent might be to shake things up a bit – stagnation is never a good look for a society and I have a strong suspicion that Moriax is suffering just that. It would also explain Ionith’s comments before my duel with Valence – and why, despite recognising me as a disruption, he hasn’t tried to stop me in any way.

Of course, those are all incidentals, and probably not what Nicholas intended me to take away from my reading. I can see why he chose this grimoire for me. Where Grenaul’s father was the conqueror – a high Grandmaster of Dominate – Grenaul was the one who added Tame to the family Class, along with three other Skills, one of which I have had since reaching level thirty.

His notes on how he developed those Skills are fascinating. He clearly had no understanding of an internal Energy matrix; his progress came through trial, persistence, and sheer force of will. I suspect the first Titanbend – referred to only by his title – was disappointed in his son. Frankly, I think that son was a genius.

Every Titanbend is expected to add a Class Skill to the family’s legacy. I intend to use my predecessor’s notes to make sure that I do the same.

The narrow gangways of the ship have become familiar. Most are wide enough to allow two people to pass with their shoulders brushing; some are a bit narrower than that. The limited dimensions are passable for most of my Bonded, but they cause issues for a couple. Lathani seems to have inherited the liquid nature of her mother, but she doesn’t tend to use it, instead hopping with Shadow-Shaping from one spot to another. With plenty of shadows existing all over the ship, her travel is easy.

And, speak of the devil, the nunda appears before me.

We’re getting food? she asks eagerly.

“If you’ve finished your training,” I reply pointedly. It turns out that one of the fresh-faced mages who signed on with us has some skill with Shadow magic and has agreed to give Lathani a few lessons in exchange for silver.

Not many mages answered my invitation, and those who did are the younger and less experienced ones – probably because the more experienced know better than to travel across the dangerous ocean to an unexplored land. Mage Vellus, who is experienced, has taken them under his wing – by the end of the journey, they won’t be inexperienced any more. Assuming they survive – but I’ll do my best to ensure they do. At least there are some more experienced ones on the other ships.

I have, Lathani answers quickly. Too quickly. I give her a hard look. I just have exercises to practise, she explains after a moment, her ears flattening a touch. But I can’t concentrate on them while I’m hungry!

I let the pause draw out a moment, then give her a small smile.

“Learning is more difficult on an empty belly,” I agree. “Alright, come on.”

We head towards the dining room – or as they call it here, the mess hall. Every so often, we pass someone, whether sailor, fighter, or other. I receive a respectful nod, but I’ve made it clear that I don’t want to disrupt the daily functioning of a ship by requiring anything more time-consuming.

In the first few days of our trip, my companions received plenty of stares from those who weren’t familiar with them. By now, they’ve practically become fixtures. Though, Kalanthia still gets stares whenever she emerges, and Ivor, Noir, and Pride are sent surprised looks if they ever come down below decks – all three of them prefer the open air.

The captain warned the two alcaorises and Sirocco not to fly too high –there are apparently powerful beasts who claim the sky as their territory and reaching too far into it is bound to cause trouble for all of us. Apparently the same is true of diving too deeply, but as it stands, I don’t have any aquatic Bonded, so it’s irrelevant.

I take us on a slightly circuitous route, wanting to see if the Pathwalkers would like to join us – I don’t want to ask them in the Bond network in case I break their concentration.

The alchemists’ study door is shut, so I don’t go in. They’re probably in the middle of brewing and want to keep the sometimes-toxic fumes from emerging. Further down that corridor, the rune-crafters’ door is open. I glance in to see Lizil inspecting a set of carved runes, Alyna hovering nearby and looking nervous.

When she glances up and sees me at the door, her nerves surge. Perhaps attuned to her friend’s moods, Hunter looks up from her own desk in the corner of the room. Pleasure spikes through it.

“Tamer?” she greets out loud. Every time I hear her voice, I marvel at Master Mirena’s work – the tattoo that sits across Hunter’s throat does an excellent job at verbalising her mental voice. And Hunter doesn’t even have to actually speak – she just has to project the thoughts she wants to verbalise at the tattoo. “Do you need something?”

“I just wanted to see if you were hungry. We’re heading to the mess hall.”

Hunter sends a glance over at Alyna and Lizil, then nods, a gesture she’s picked up from the humans around.

“I could eat,” she agrees, standing up. I nod at Master Mirena as she glances up at the movement. She nods wordlessly back at me and then returns to her inspection.

Good luck, I send to Alyna and see her jump slightly, her eyes flying to meet mine.

Thank you, she replies, a touch awkwardly – she’s still uncertain around me, though she’s starting to settle as time passes and nothing about me changes.

A few doors down the corridor, I don’t even bother to go into the smithy – the sound of voices and the occasional ring of metal that emerges through the closed door indicates that they’re busy. I wouldn’t have thought that having a functioning smithy on a ship made of wood was the best of ideas, but magic makes many things possible. It’s certainly been useful: with smith, leatherworker, and enchanter all present and willing to work to the betterment of everyone – if paid fairly – we’re a good way towards being able to outfit everyone with armour and weapons.

The leatherworker’s workshop is opposite the smith’s. Since the door is open, I take a peek inside. I notice both of Loran’s siblings are present – Athos and Suzana. The latter is a seamstress and will be an important part of outfitting everyone. She might not have a Class, but she’s a sorceress with a needle.

“My lord,” she murmurs, bowing deeply as the two notice me in the doorway. I’ve tried to encourage both of them to relax a little around me, but though Athos has been tentatively receptive, Suzana has remained very wary.

“Are you looking for Master, my lord?” Athos asks politely. “He’s gone to speak with the steward about materials.”

“I was actually wondering whether Loran might be here.” Sure, I could contact him through the network, but, again, I don’t want to disrupt him without need.

“He was here earlier, my lord. He brought us food and then went up on deck.” Athos gestures at the trays I’d noticed near the door.

“Ah, pity,” I sigh. I suppose this is the downside of not having a fixed routine like so many on the ship do – and I would never require anyone to wait for me in order to eat. I reach towards the trays. “We’re heading down to the mess hall now. I can take these back with me.”

“Oh no, my lord! We couldn’t possibly ask that of you!” Suzana exclaims, her voice utterly horrified. She even raises her eyes to look at me directly, something unusual for her in my experience.

“You’re not asking; I’m offering,” I point out, resisting the urge to roll my eyes. It’s just a pair of food trays for heaven’s sake. She doesn’t object, but her silence speaks volumes. I sigh again, and drop my hand. “Fine. If it makes you more comfortable, I’ll let you sort them out in your own time.”

“Thank you, my lord,” Suzana replies, sounding unaccountably relieved.

I glance at Athos who looks embarrassed more than anything else, though for me or for his sister, I can’t tell.

“Alright, I’ll leave you to your work. Remember, if you need anything, don’t hesitate to ask.” I direct that point mostly at Athos – I’m fairly certain that Suzana wouldn’t approach me for help even if the workshop suddenly burst into flames.

Which, incidentally, was something that quite a few people were worried about Aingeal doing for the first few days. As the days go by with no incidents, that worry seems to be being alleviated, thank goodness. Fortunately, the fire elemental is more than familiar with not being allowed to burn things.

I nod at the two workers and then continue down the corridor. No more detours – time to eat.
