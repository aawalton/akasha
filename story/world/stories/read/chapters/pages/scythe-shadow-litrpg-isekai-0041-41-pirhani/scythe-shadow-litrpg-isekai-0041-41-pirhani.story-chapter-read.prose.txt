The ride back to Meadowvale was uneventful, aside from the reeling thoughts and emotions in my mind. I'd somehow stumbled into a pissing contest between gods. And I wasn't remotely close to that desperate for a shower…

Hopefully, this information alone would be enough to make good on my agreement with Valciferous.

Could he really have not known about this? I doubted it, though I had no idea what kind of omnipotence or powers gods in Penumbra possessed.

It had taken us a full day to return, but finally, I passed through the gates of Meadowvale behind Trix.

We'd stopped only to replenish Volition slaying a few marked Sins that crossed our path. I'd even earned an upgrade to Aero Shift from an Astral Affinity Will that allowed me to give it vertical momentum. Upon testing, it didn't feel like flying like I hoped. I'd seen Lancelot burst into the air and vanish after our discussion at the lighthouse and thought this could be how he did it.

Maybe someday.

For now it was more like zooming into the air, then flailing awkwardly as I plummeted from the sky. I was a little afraid to test going too high, but I had been able to Shift from the ground up onto a large boulder with little effort, which felt no different than a normal Aero Shift.

Now that we'd made it back to the city, we wasted no time tying our horses by the stables, taking them straight to the colosseum and startling the crowds as we galloped through the streets.

Eugene and Sibyl were in their usual conversation at the registrar's desk. Did they have their date yet? Comprehension of time here continued to elude me. If so, it must have gone well enough. I waved, but continued up to Valciferous' room with Trix. No time for small talk now.

The god of the Meadow was present this time.

Valciferous stood with his back to us as we entered. He was at the far wall, observing one of the tapestries there. I caught sight of blue ferns and massive trees over his shoulder on the hanging fabric.

No way that's a coincidence.

"You come with news," the god noted without turning around.

"We do, my Lord," Trix answered.

"We?" he asked, turning to us now. His pupil-less glowing eyes bore into me, and I could've sworn his mouth-less face shifted into an invisible smile. "Ah, my little death scythe. I see you've grown since last we met. Purple is admirable on you, though I dare say green may suit you even better."

A not so subtle suggestion not to get complacent as an Amethyst rank. "Agreed," I said, refusing to address the god with any absurd honorific like Trix did.

"Very well." He glided up beside one of the couches, but did not sit.

Trix did, but she ignored the wine and arrayed plate of cheese that always seemed to be present on the table. "Someone is digging tunnels under the wall to the Jungle Realm."

"I see," Valciferous replied, not betraying any emotion. "And you uncovered this?"

"Zephyr did, actually."

I shrugged. "I got lucky."

"There is no such thing as luck. Not here."

"You don't seem surprised," I said.

"This revelation confirms suspicions I have had for some time. Pirhani has long lamented the way I handle business in the Meadow. I did not think her to be so bold as this though."

"It may be even worse than you suspect," Trix said, nodding for me to continue.

Valciferous raised a nonexistent eyebrow.

"I saw an Iron ranked Reaper in one of the caves. He could somehow control the Unchained."

"I see. There is one skill that could accomplish such a thing. Puppeteer…" Valciferous said knowingly. He looked up at the ceiling. "But for it to be that powerful… Hmm."

"You said he was oath bound to another god as well," Trix noted.

"Yes, but I wasn't close enough to see this symbol on his cloak in detail."

"No matter," Valciferous said. "These findings confirm my suspicions. It is almost certainly Pirhani."

"She's the god of the Jungle Realm?"

"Indeed."

"Where can we find her?"

Valciferous laughed. "Eager, are we? Pirhani's dispute is with me. I will confront her."

"With all due respect, this dispute is costing people their lives," I countered. "If you suspected this all along, then it should never have escalated this far. They're clearly planning something bigger than just letting a few rogue Unchained loose in the Meadow."

"You're upset," Trix interjected, her tone like ice. "But you should not forget to whom you speak, Zephyr."

Valciferous waved a casual hand. "Calm yourself, Trix. Zephyr raises a valid point. I should have cut the limb the moment I saw the rot. Instead, it's been allowed to fester and spread. I will consult the other gods and we will address the situation. Pirhani is overstepping and—"

A knock sounded at the door.

"Are we expecting anyone?" Trix asked.

"No…" Valciferous replied gravely. He opened the door.

Eugene stood in the entry, clutching his spear. Face red.

"Speak," Valciferous demanded.

"Uhh… my Lord. There's a… uh. Something happening at the northern instance of Meadowvale."

Valciferous' expression darkened—as much as a white mask with glowing eyes can darken. "Continue…"

"A guard just reported in. There's… an… an army outside the walls."

"Which instance are you in?" Valciferous asked Trix.

"Zephyr and I are both in east. North is the least utilized. There would be the fewest Reapers there."

I recalled, vaguely, the first time I entered Meadowvale Jethro mentioning that there were many entrances scattered across the Meadow, but only one city itself. Each set of walls had a portal that led to the same place. That made defense a tricky proposition. But of course, when had they ever needed to defend before, except against the occasional Unchained that wandered too close?

This was unprecedented.

An army.

"This is bold. Even for Pirhani. I will convene with the other gods immediately."

"We don't need bureaucracy," I said firmly. "We need action. Maybe the lives here don't matter to a god, but they matter to the rest of us."

Valciferous' glowing golden eyes narrowed, then softened. "This is why you're the perfect Death Reaper. I cannot do anything against another Reaper or Sin in Penumbra. Only those under my oath can do that. But I can create external consequences. Let me do what I do best. You do what you do best. Protect the people here, Zephyr."

With that Valciferous disintegrated from existence to wherever gods existed outside of this world.

"Well, he's about as useless as an ice cube on the sun," I said.

Eugene chortled, holding back a laugh as Trix frowned at us both.

"We need assistance from outside the Meadow. Guard, what's the breakdown of this army?"

Eugene straightened to attention. "Yes. Um."

"Shouldn't Valciferous have this information too?" I asked, skeptical of the god's hands-off approach to whatever was happening.

"No," Trix said. "If Pirhani is involved it means she's likely coerced Reapers—and Sins apparently—from the Jungle zone. He already knows what we're dealing with, and I have some ideas, but I want to hear it from the source. So, I ask again, what's the breakdown of this army?"

Eugene averted his eyes. "It's as you fear, I'm afraid. My understanding is—"

Distant screams interrupted the explanation. Screams of absolute terror.

I didn't wait for Eugene to continue. A crash sounded, muffled through the rows of buildings and walls of the colosseum, but definitely within the city. I—probably foolishly—left the room and headed out toward the noise.

I passed Sibyl in the lobby as she tried to answer questions from several frantic Amber Reapers at her registrar window. More ran in from the entrance.

"What's going on out there?" I asked, fighting the tide of the crowd. I took an elbow to the ribs, shoulder to the chin as people rushed by. No one answered, so I grabbed someone's arm. A middle-aged woman with golden locks. "What's going on?" I repeated.

Her voice was breathless from running. "They broke through the gate. Unchained. Dozens. They're still coming."

I pushed through the people. Even more were coming now, as if everyone thought the colosseum would be the safest place to hide. I didn't know if that would turn out to be true. There were no tournaments or brawls ongoing at the moment, so the place had been relatively deserted before the rush of Penumbrans and Reapers from the city.

Even once I made it outside the structure, I couldn't see much beyond the buildings and ever growing sea of people. Not much, beside a cloud of dust and smoke rising from the main gate.

The last thing I wanted to do was run straight into an enemy I couldn't handle. I didn't remember everything Calliope had explained to me about what ranks were typically found in the Jungle, but I knew they would be at least Amethyst like me. Probably higher too, if the Unchained I'd seen was any indication.

I looked up. The many-arched wall of the colosseum yawned into the sky above me. It was easily one of the tallest buildings in the city beside the exterior wall. Not too bad as vantage points went.

I jumped, propelled by my recently upgraded Aero Shift ability, and grabbed hold of the ledge of the arched window openings on the second level.

Sounds of destruction echoed up. Screams joined the chaos now. I launched myself again, gaining two more levels. Continuing this way, I reached the top in less than a minute. I stood upon the colosseum's apex, silhouetted against the fiery painted colors of the evening sky, and turned to the gate.

Valciferous had been right to fear.

Most of the buildings nearest the wall were shattered to pieces, Unchained of Amber, Amethyst, even Sapphire and Emerald, partying like goons amongst the wreckage, tossing stone and wooden framing in all directions. Scores of them… A body flew through the air and was caught by another Unchained, then ripped in two. The sight reminded me too much of Allium's end, but I didn't avert my eyes.

I couldn't… Because the Unchained weren't alone. At least a dozen cloaked Reapers joined them near the broken gate, watching instead of fighting, unfazed by the destruction, unnoticed by the Unchained let loose on the city.

Another figure joined them, hovering amid the rubble. White and gold and magnificent. A wreath of golden vines and leaves atop long pale hair. And a faceless head. Even from this distance I could see the two glowing golden eyes seeming to smile at the violence.

A god.

Valciferous wouldn't have any luck dissuading Pirhani from this attack wherever he'd gone.

Because Pirhani was here… leading it.
