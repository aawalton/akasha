Brin

I was, quite literally, swept off my feet.

“Brin!” Royce crossed the tavern floor in four strides. He practically crushed me in an embrace, lifting and spinning us down onto the cobblestone street. “You’re alive!”

Words failed me, but both arms went around him without a second thought. Brisk evening air nipped my face as he slowed, letting my boots tap down beneath a dusky evening sky.

“You’re alright!” Broad, warm hands cupped my face. Blue eyes widened as he looked, really looked at me, and all the joy faded from his expression. “You’re… ashes, what happened? Are you alright?”

Renner watched us warily. He was still holding the inn door open, letting warm firelight spill past him and out onto the stones. Dark shadows hovered beneath exhausted eyes. His hair was a clump of blood and mud. Dried crimson and dirt streaked his face, clothing, and cloak. He looked like death.

And I probably didn’t look much better. Thom’s fist had left my face a wash of blood. Baltha’s claws had ground my hair and body into warm, clinging earth. And he…

Velidan. The Fae. The monster.

He had left me whole. Healed of every cut and bruise. Unharmed… on the outside.

But I was still spattered with blood and mud, and just a short while ago had wept in Renner’s arms. My face doubtlessly bore the evidence.

As if to confirm this, Royce traced the slope of cheekbones and smoothed my blood-dried hair.

“I’m alright,” I whispered, leaning into the familiar touch.

“You don’t look alright.”

I choked out a little laugh. “I know. I can’t imagine what I look like. I’m okay, though, really, and I’m so happy to… how are you even here?”

Tan cheeks reddened as he nodded towards the tethered bay horse. He stood just off the street, head dipped down to champ at brittle grass. “It’s a long story. Let’s just say that Durst and my mom and old farmer Jeth are probably all really mad at me.”

“Sounds like a great story.” I tangled my fingers with his, reveling in the sheer life of his touch. Last time I’d seen him he’d been lying in a pool of blood and he wouldn’t wake up and-

No. Stop. Breathe. He’s alive. He’s alright.

I’d broken down enough for one day. For one lifetime, really. I squeezed Royce’s hand, drawing him towards the Snowswept Inn’s uneven stone steps.

Renner had gone inside. He stood speaking to Master Lewen, the enormous, red-faced innkeeper, near the tavern’s long countertop. The common room was lit by a crackling fireplace.

No sooner had I stepped inside than a lanky, squealing figure crashed into me.

“You’re alive!” Teela threw both bony arms around my middle. “You’re alive, you’re alive!”

“I’m alive,” I gasped, struggling to keep the two of us upright.

She pulled back. “And you left! Salt, Brin, I woke up and I wasn’t sure if…” Teela swiped one hand beneath her narrow nose, red-rimmed eyes flicking towards the two men talking nearby. It looked like she’d been crying. “Lewen said you went into the woods and Renner followed. I thought… I thought maybe... y’know.”

I swallowed. “I’m sorry. I didn’t plan it all, I just… I had to get away. Just for a little while.”

She bit her lip, then swiveled towards Royce. “Right. Friend of yours?”

He had remained unusually quiet throughout this vague exchange, and upon glancing up I saw why. Royce’s wide blue eyes were trailing between Teela’s winding, flesh-hued tail and her short, ember-hued horns. His mouth was open.

The young woman drank in his expression, then placed both hands on her hips. “Okay, I know that look. First time, huh?”

Royce turned scarlet. “Er, sorry?”

Her tail snapped like a whipcrack. “The word is ‘Fae-touched,’ and if it makes you uncomfortable then that’s your salted problem, okay?”

“My… problem? No, it’s not a… er, I’m sorry.” He glanced at me, as if looking for guidance, before offering an uncertain smile. “I’m Royce. Royce Freth. Sorry, I didn’t mean to stare. Just… wow. They match your eyes and everything!”

Teela’s jaw dropped, as if she couldn’t quite believe what he’d said. It shut with an audible click and she squinted up at him. “Well… yeah. Yeah. They do. Obviously.”

“Are you a friend of Brin’s?”

“Yeah, and you’re… oh. You’re Royce. Oh.” Her eyes rounded and cut to me.

“I’m ‘Royce, oh’?” He snickered. “What does that mean?”

“It means,” I said firmly, winding my fingers through his, “that I’m thrilled to see you, and I’m hungry and want to sit down and hear all about how you got here.”

“Heh. Sure, but I don’t think it’s anywhere near as exciting as whatever’s happened to you.” He pulled out two wooden chairs at a nearby table, gesturing for me and Teela to sit, then ambled towards the innkeeper.

“That’s Royce, huh?” she whispered, leaning across the table once we’d settled. “Wow. Wow. Maybe I should think about visiting your home town.” She gave a little giggle as heat blossomed in my cheeks.

Wood creaked beside me as Renner heaved himself into a chair. “Lewen says Josiah, Gil, and Agnes are locked up at the Watch. No sign of Rell or the other two.” He dragged one gloved hand over his face, turning towards me wearily. “What’s the plan?”

“The plan?”

He nodded towards Royce. “With him.”

I blinked. “Er… catch up? Enjoy myself? Be happy he’s alright?”

“Great.” He frowned, voice dropping low. “You gonna tell him what’s going on?”

I chewed the question over, unsure of how to answer or of why it was important, at least night now.

Some of my confusion must have been evident on my face, because after a moment’s silence Renner sighed. “Look, do we need to stay up and keep an eye on you? Because if you’re gonna tell him, that might make things easier. Gives another pair of eyes, at least.” His grime-smeared features twisted into a grimace. “Not sure I have it in me to stay up much longer.”

Oh. That’s what he means.

Teela leaned across the table again and whispered furtively, “Should we make a plan? Maybe if we watch you while you fall asleep, and you hold the shard in your hand and then as soon as it’s gone we wake you up? Scream and yell and shake you and-”

“I already gave it to him,” I murmured.

Her voice brightened with cautious optimism, “Well, that’s good, right? Is that the end of it?”

Still unable to look up, I dipped my hand into a pocket and pulled out the little piece of steel. Reassuring warmth spread through my palm.

Teela blinked. “I’m confused.”

“You’re not alone,” Renner muttered.

I was still thoroughly confused, myself. “I gave it to him. He was holding it when I left. And then I… I had this dream, as I was coming back. It was… beautiful. It was perfect.” My vision blurred with the intensity of that memory and with how very incapable I was of putting that feeling of safety and love into words. “There was a man there, but he wasn’t a man. It was like I was looking at the sun, you know? He was bright and strong and said, ‘this vow did not end in death.’”

My audience was silent as I leaned forwards, gazing with near-reverence at the relic. “I could’ve stayed forever. I was so safe and warm, and when I woke up I had this feeling. Like the shard wanted to come back.”

“Okay…” Teela slammed her pointed chin into one palm. “Well, uh… that sounds… maybe… uh…”

Renner yawned again. “My thoughts exactly.”

“Food for three!” A happy baritone interrupted. Royce deposited three pewter bowls filled with a steaming mash of vegetables onto the table. “He didn’t even let me pay. Can you believe that? Maybe he’s sweet on one of you.” One blue eye winked as he settled beside Teela. Then he seemed to notice Renner for the first time.

Mud-spattered, covered in blood, and scowling at him.

Royce cleared his throat, blonde brows drifting up. “Er… did you want some, too?”

Renner just turned and held my gaze. Waiting.

I swallowed. “I’ll… yes. Yes. Upstairs.” But not because I want Royce to keep vigil while I sleep. Because if I don’t wake up, he should at least understand why.

Gloved hands cupped my bowl. “Great. Now.” He stood, wood grating as the chair slid back.

“Now?” I winced. Just like that? Not even a moment to rest?

“Am I missing something?” Royce asked politely, half-rising and looking between the two of us.

“Oh, boy, are you,” Teela mumbled around a mouthful of food.

Renner’s scowl only deepened. “Now.”
