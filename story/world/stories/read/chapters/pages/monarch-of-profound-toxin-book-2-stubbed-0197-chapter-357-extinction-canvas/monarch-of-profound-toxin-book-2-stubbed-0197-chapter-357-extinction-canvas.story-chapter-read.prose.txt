Chapter 357

For this, medicine was not the answer, but fire.

Eik still had True Toxic Harmony running, so he was technically made of Profound Toxin entirely, but the toxin that made up his body wasn’t necessarily of very high potency.

Luckily, something could be done about that.

"Here’s some hot sauce to go with my abs, you repulsive mass of shit!” he hissed and turned up the dial. Despite their microscopic size, they must have been immensely sturdy, because they didn’t start dying right away.

And they didn’t have a system to invade. At all. That was the first time Eik had experienced that. But it did explain why he hadn’t been able to notice that they were alive, or whatever strange form their existence took. The way he tracked that stuff was by sensing the invasion of Profound Toxin in a foreign system, so when that didn’t happen, he was more or less clueless.

Damn, why did the Lord of the Moon have to possess power that was so troublesome for him?

Fortunately, the swarm wasn’t impervious to the annihilating force of Profound Toxin and they soon began to die off in droves.

About half of it had been eradicated when something suddenly broke through the surface of the earth beneath him and pierced him through the ribs while he still lay supported on an elbow. Eik gasped and coughed as he was hurled off the ground, swung around and then slammed back down violently.

Four long claws were sticking out of his side. The face of the same X-ranked spawn he had just fought slowly rose up above him. Empty eyes stared down at him, black locks of greasy hair lumping damply across his face. Neither of its pupils seemed to be able to focus on him as a viscous string of slobber slowly extended from its bottom lip.

With a grimace, Eik moved his head desperately out of the way to avoid it. “What the hell is this? You’re supposed to be gone. I could swear I just… I just felt you die,” he growled.

For the first time since it had appeared, a hint of a grin snuck onto its lips. Its voice was pitched way too high for the slow rumble of the tone and the sound sent Eik’s stomach into a twist. “I-I’m… baaaaack,” it sung dreamily.

Eik could have puked then and there. How was it possible for every single aspect of a being to all simultaneously be the most disgusting thing Eik had ever witnessed?

Soundlessly, a fist-sized shard of Profound Toxin hammered into its lower back and immediately sent a dose of poison into circulation. Eik’s eyes widened.

Everything about this organism—if it could be called that—looked identical the one he could swear he had just killed, but he knew the truth the moment Profound Toxin made its entry.

This was not right. Certainly, they were near copies of each other to a degree closer than anything he had really seen before, but the two systems were definitely not the same.

“You’re a clone,” Eik breathed, not as a question but as a conclusion.

Rather than answer, the spawn raised its other hand to strike, but Eik delivered a knee to its solar plexus before it could be carried through, extended it into a proper kick, and sent it flying through the air. With a groan he leaped after it and hurled a javelin with every grain of power he could muster.

Despite his weakness, the strike still pierced all the way through. On the way out, the butt of the javelin snagged on a fractured rib, morphing into liquid and quickly disappearing back into the body again, completing the second dose of infection.

God damn it, this wasn’t an ideal condition to fight it… Although the squirming tar-like mass had nearly been eradicated, the black discoloration spreading from the stab wound cleansed along with it, he wasn’t magically going to get back the energy he had lost.

Yes, under normal circumstances, he should have and would have been able to shrug off a stomach injury like that with not too many issues, but for the entirety of the duration that the primordial parasitic infestation had been eating away at his body, his natural healing ability had been practically pouring vitality into in order to regenerate.

Eik’s best guess was that all of that vitality had more or less been hijacked by the infestation and modified into nourishment for further spreading of the parasitic swarm, essentially creating a self-empowering cycle. And he had probably not done himself any favors by wasting so much Panacean Quintessence on it either.

Like a bullet, the spawn suddenly launched itself out of the dust cloud whipped up by its own brutal meeting with the ground, heading right for him, and just as he threw up a few layers of protective shells and began to duck out of the way, True Toxic Harmony spluttered out of operation in a few concerning flickers before coming back online.

So taken aback was he, that Eik missed the timing and suffered two deep gashes along one shoulder and arm, the force of it sending him careening from his feet to skip across the broken ground.

True Toxic Harmony was still active, but what had just happened was not a good sign. His tired body must be attempting to reallocate a dwindling reservoir of energy and found True Toxic Harmony a necessary process after all.

This had to end soon.

And this time he couldn’t let the fiend out of his sight. The cloning must have happened while it was deep within the ground. That was the only time it could realistically have separated from the original body and scuttled off without alerting his keen senses.

Instantly, the spawn altered its course and was upon him just as quickly as he could get back on his feet, but a triggering of Accelerant first slammed it into the ground purely by the rejecting force of the internal explosion, and then sent it skyward in a similar fashion. Eik’s eyes followed every moment.

And he was ready to meet it in the air, his massive katana slicing a skeletal arm clean off before melting into the horrendous wound.

“Extinction Canvas,” he mumbled, spreading his fingers wide as his will exercised its influence over the world.

In a perfectly synchronized dance, the small but dense Apocalypse Canvas above narrowed even further as an Extinction Zone on the ground did the same, the two abilities snapping into position parallel with each other. A glass-like barrier extended in a cylindrical prison between their respective borders, trapping any unfortunate victim within their area of effect.

Even before the assault began in earnest, the clone of the X-ranked spawn seemed to notice that things were developing in a direction that wasn’t favorable. Giving up on a counter attack, it leaped away, almost making it to the blue-tinted, transparent barrier before fingers of Profound Toxin grasped its ankle in an extension of Eik’s own hand.

“One was already too many,” Eik’s voice sounded from behind. The spawn turned its head to look at him. His eyes were wild and crackling with power. Whether it was the madness of pain and weariness or of blood thirst and malevolence, none of them could have said with any certainty, but there was a finality in his words, that sent a spike of hopelessness through the primitive mind of the spawn. “I am never going to let you leave.”

In that moment, the characteristic rumbling hum of Extinction Zone in operation began to whir as the first shards of crystal launched from above.

From this, there would be no escape.

***

Eik felt like he was about to puke.

The spawn had flitted about, trying to escape, but even when it turned its attention back to Eik—the source of the threat—the Extinction Canvas had caused sufficient damage to make its attacks against him defensible despite his addled condition.

Combining imprisonment with a relentless barrage of deadly crystal missiles and poison atmospheric emission was exactly the cruel mixture Eik had hoped for.

As the bright blue debris quickly dissipated and the respective Apocalypse Canvas and Extinction Zone that had fueled the Extinction Canvas faded, the mangled corpse of the spawn was revealed, its face a bloodied, near unrecognizable mess with limbs so badly broken that they looked practically boneless.

Eik approached slowly, having already dropped True Toxic Harmony, Backflow, and the rest of his personal buffs in order to conserve what little energy he still had left in his body. He wasn’t about to collapse here before he could get home to his kids. Fuck that.

The spawn barely looked like something that had been alive. At first, second, and even third glance, it looked more like a pile of trash someone had dumped in a back alley behind a shady business. But just because it looked like garbage, didn’t mean it was.

At least not if you knew what it might be used for. If he was lucky, it might just end up being the single most valuable thing he had picked up in his life.

Well, maybe not “pick up” so much as scoop up with a fifty meter shovel. He wasn’t going to touch that disgusting thing with his bare hands. Guts and offal of slaughtered animals or hazardous materials that could melt the dick off a cyborg, sure, but something that had looked that disgusting in life…? Yeah, thanks, but no thanks.

With both crystalline and liquid extensions of his own hands, he dug through the ruined corpse, looking for anything extraordinary. There wasn’t much to note, really—at least not to his relatively untrained eyes. As he had come to realize, he really didn’t have any knowledge about alchemy so much as pure crafting power, so if there was something particularly rare or valuable hidden among the mutilated innards, he was rather blind to it.

And that was exactly why he was just going to grab the lot of it. Delicately but quickly, he separated the different parts of the body. Collecting bones, skin, flesh and organs in neat piles would make life easier for Meri and her band of clever boys and girls developing his potions for him.

When it had all gone into his bag of holding, he stood up.

There wasn’t really anything left at all after the X-ranked fights had heaved the earth up like fifty earthquakes, but he supposed it was only right to make sure. It wouldn’t do to leave behind anything that could help them later.

Conservative as he might prefer with his energy, checking this entire area on his own was not a task he was up for right now. So a small army of about a thousand Living Manifestations, which was a number that barely cut into his reserves in the first place, was deployed to assist him.

After a roughly fifteen minute search, he decided to conclude the mission as successful but fruitless. Whether there had ever been anything to find or not, there sure as hell wasn’t anything left of it at this point.

Well, maybe it was time to get back to the others. He had kind of put it off with this search in the hope that a little time would fix the hole in his stomach, and it actually had. External medicines like healing potions, both drunk and poured directly into the wound, as well as whatever permanent energy boosters he had in his dimensional stash had been liberally used, but their effects on his X-ranked body were limited at best.

Some food and drink to fuel his own regeneration seemed the most effective after all, and it was getting better, albeit at an annoyingly slow rate. Frustration was too mild a word to describe what it felt like to suddenly be this debilitated after living with a superhuman body for as long as he had.

But despite how much he wanted to spare his kids the sight of their father’s injuries, staying away too long was worse. He would just have to put on one of the clean shirts in his storage to cover up the wound.

Calling back the Living Manifestations, he turned and suddenly found himself face to face with the Lord of the Moon. He gasped in shock. How had he gotten this close without setting off a single warning bell in Eik?

Just how bad wa—

Suddenly swallowed up by a darkness that quickly overwhelmed his exhausted mind and claimed his consciousness, Eik didn’t get to finish that thought.
