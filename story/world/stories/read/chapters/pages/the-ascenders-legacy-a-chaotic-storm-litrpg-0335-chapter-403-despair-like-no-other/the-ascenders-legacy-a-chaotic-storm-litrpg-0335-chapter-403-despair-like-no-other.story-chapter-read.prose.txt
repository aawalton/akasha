Only a few minutes ago, Severian Essovar had held his salvation in his hands—had felt the pulsing soul fragment of Edrick Ghaltris within the box and had known with quiet certainty that it was the last soul he would ever devour.

He didn't care, though, because this one soul would have carried him to the end. It would have saved him from the limit and paved his way to ascension in gold.

So when the storm Calamities attacked him on his way out, Severian had fought as though his life depended on it. Because it did. He had forgotten about his sisters or his pledge to them. He had forgotten about his plans to sneak his niece into the Storm Sect and destroy it from within.

He had forgotten everything in a bid to escape. In a bid to shatter the bottleneck that had left him stagnant for the last five years.

And for a brief moment, he had almost seemed to be winning.

His soul had ballooned outward to engulf the enemy Calamities and crush their auras beneath the pressure and power of his own. He had spread corruption through their souls and uttered decrees that transformed every one of their attacks into nourishment for his own soul.

But while his desperate attacks were deadly, inflicting pain upon the soul itself, the storm Calamities were immensely formidable. Worse still, they outnumbered him.

Attacks struck him from every side. Lightning of various colors and functions ripped through his soul armor to sever flesh and burn spirit. Every attack he landed was punished threefold. Every defense he erected was punished fourfold.

Yet Severian fought—warping, twisting, and tearing into their souls in a berserk display of power and fear. Willpower, chaos, quintessence, energy—all of it ripped out of his soul in a desperate bid to carve open an escape.

His supremacy expanded, only to be crushed. His decrees echoed, only to be forcefully silenced.

And still, they kept coming.

A combined decree slammed him into the earth hard enough to create a crater several miles deep. Lightning ripped his soul armor to shreds, tearing strips from his very soul, and in that brief moment of disorientation, his salvation had been plucked from his hands.

Frantic, Severian rushed after it immediately, his hand reaching out to violently tear the thief's soul from their body.

The silver-wreathed Calamity screamed, rending the fabric of reality apart as their soul contorted and spasmed.

Pain tore through Severian's skull before he could finish the deed—white-hot, blinding, like a nail driven straight through bone—and he screamed, eyes widening as he watched his salvation disappear from view.

The earth cratered beneath him once more, destruction spreading outward for miles. But Severian barely noticed, his body already blurring through reality in a frantic rush to catch the fleeing thief.

However, once again—ever infuriating—fate stood in his way.

A decree of tangibility ripped through the air behind him, freezing reality itself solid and holding him captive. The three remaining Calamities surrounded him, and in a voice like rolling thunder, their leader sneered.

"This is the end, enemy. Surrender now, or we kill you."

"I don't need the seed!" Severian snapped, fury lighting his features in violet. "All I need is the soul within it. I don't need the freaking seed!"

"It's not yours to claim, soul eater!" another Calamity responded, this one wreathed in pure-white lightning. "You have no right to it. And if you think we will willingly give you the soul of a brother to consume, then you must be out of your soul-rotted mind."

Severian snarled, feeling the clawing desperation in his throat intensify—the looming hand of the limit closing tighter.

"Please," he broke, his voice little more than a rasp. "I'm about to hit a limit seven tiers from ascension. Surely you can understand my desperation. You can understand why I need this."

"I can understand nothing," the leader snarled. "Everyone else works through their bottlenecks instead of eating the souls of others to cheat the system." He sneered in disgust. "I will not say it again. Surrender now, or we will put you down for good."

Severian closed his eyes, his heart still hammering. Even though he stood three tiers above each of them individually, their combined strength and numbers more than closed the gap.

There was no escaping them. Not anymore.

But Severian had no plans to escape, not without his salvation firmly in his hands.

Ascensions were rare events, and there was no telling when next the soul of an ascendant would be up for grabs. This could be his only chance to break through his bottleneck before the system-enforced limit settled over his spirit, and he would be damned if he let it slip through his fingers so easily.

"No," he snarled, soul essence gathering densely around him. "Fate wants to cheat me out of this. I won't let it."

Amoz scowled. "Then you've sealed your fate."

He crossed the distance between them in an instant, Rezin and Cephas only a millisecond behind him. Storm-wreathed hands clamped around Severian's neck and squeezed.

But Severian hadn't simply been folding his arms in wait.

He attacked, his soul phasing straight through the chokehold to contest the very soul of his attacker. He made it halfway before his own soul was violently yanked back, silver lightning ripping through his consciousness like molten spears.

He glanced over his shoulder. The thief had returned, wreathed entirely in a vortex of flowing silver lightning. He snarled.

"Blasted bitch!"

The thief snarled back. "I don't like that word."

Silver lightning lashed into his mind, seeking to fry his consciousness. It failed—but it disoriented him just enough for the other two Calamities to pummel him into the earth. Bone shattered. Flesh ripped.

But none of that held him back.

His soul snarled, corruption spreading through the souls of his attackers to tear them apart. Their pummeling fists faltered, and without hesitation, Severian phased, rushing toward the glowing soul fragment he could sense in the distance.

Unfortunately, he wasn't fast enough.

Silver lightning seized him by the neck and dragged him back into reality, where a snarling fist already waited. It was the fist of their leader, whose soul was still wrestling with the consequences of Severian's half-possession.

It made a second possession easier—all he needed was contact.

Sneering, Severian waited for the fist to land so he could phase through the medium.

It never did.

Instead, annihilation in the form of black lightning slammed into him at point-blank range, unmaking flesh and soul in a single brutal instant.

Severian screamed, the sound shaking the heavens with its intensity. He tried to phase out of reality, to rebuild his soul armor and purge the annihilation from his soul. But he was too weak. His mind was in too much agony to help.

Another beam slammed into him before he could even take a breath. Then another. And another. And another… Until his soul was reduced to a ragged ember barely clinging to existence.

His flesh was in even worse shape.

But while that could easily be regenerated, his soul couldn't.

Summoning the last of his strength, he pleaded, speaking directly to the soul of the one about to unmake him.

It went unheard.

The Calamity raised his hand again, black lightning gathering tightly in his fist.

Annihilation surged.

Severian's soul quivered at the scent of oblivion. But fate—that cruel mistress—wasn't quite done with him yet. And just as the Calamity moved to end him, a voice rang out sharply.

"No, Amoz! Don't kill him. The last thing we need right now is to make an enemy of the Soul Sect by killing one of their members."

The Calamity stilled.

For a long moment, he said nothing. Then their gazes met—black runic irises locking with ruined violet—and he snarled. "The next time you try to possess me, I will unmake you."

The black lightning dissipated, and Severian—what little remained of him—let out a broken breath of relief.

The action barely registered in reality.

His body was too broken—flesh and bone ruined, unraveling beneath the annihilation they had endured. He couldn't move. Couldn't think. He couldn't even bring himself to breathe.

And so he watched helplessly as the Calamity rose to stand over him, disgust etched deep across his face.

"It's over, Amoz." The thieving Calamity spoke again, her voice quiet. "We'll bind him with null chains and take him back to the sect for judgment."

The Calamity's gaze met his once more, and after a moment, Amoz nodded. "Do it. I need to check on the others. I have neglected them for too long."

Selia nodded as she produced the null chains. "The other two enemies have been captured. Everyone else is alive."

"Good." Amoz released a quiet breath.

Then he lifted his gaze from Severian's broken form to the destruction they had wrought across the city, and that fragile relief faded.

His senses swept across the battlefield, and the full weight of their devastation settled over him.

It was catastrophic.

Their battle had carried them from the northern district into the east, leaving absolute ruin in its wake.

The earth stretched broken as far as he could see, scarred by so many craters and fissures that it now resembled the surface of a dead moon. Not a single building remained standing anywhere within sight. The air itself was poisoned, thick with the scent of ozone and sulfur.

But worst of all was the suffocating scent of death that hung over the entire district—so thick and heavy that death essence had begun to form in the air.

It clouded the sky alongside the dense plumes of smoke and dust, an oppressive weight that seemed to carry within it the agonized echoes of the thousands whose lives had been cut short before they had a chance to become anything more.

Amoz closed his eyes, shutting out the sight before him.

Over the course of his long life, he had fought in more wars than he could count and taken the lives of thousands of cultivators.

Killing had never been a problem for him. Nor had death.

But cultivators, regardless of class, were people with means. With power.

They could fight back. They could protect themselves.

These people, however, possessed neither power nor strength. Killing them like this was no different from slaughtering children.

And the sight of it sickened him.

This wasn't the plan. This... destruction wasn't what he had wanted.

The plan had been simple: enter Alcatraz, find the legacy seed, and leave.

Just this morning, he had hoped to avoid destroying even a single building. Hoped that by the time they left this city, the trauma of the last fifteen years would finally begin to fade.

Their fear would finally begin to subside.

Instead, they had wrought even more damage and death than before, destroying half the city and taking the lives of thousands.

No wonder the people hated them.

Exhaling raggedly, Amoz pressed a hand to his face as a wave of guilt churned within him.

What was the point of all this power if he couldn't even protect a few sleepers?

His gaze drifted to the other Calamities, and with a strained breath, he spoke.

"We have broken the Accords here today. My heart is burdened by the lives we have just wasted." His gaze shifted to Severian, irritation hardening his expression, before returning to the others. "We must rectify our wrongs."

"We will." Cephas nodded. "These deaths are... regrettable."

Selia and Rezin nodded soberly, though little regret showed on their faces.

Amoz studied them for a long moment before giving a single nod. "Yeah. Regrettable. No more fighting, though. This is enough death for one day."

Then, with another slow exhale and a heavy heart, Amoz dusted off his palms and turned his gaze toward the distant battlefield. Reality folded around him as he vanished, reappearing an instant later above Aodhán and the Mythics.

The other Calamities arrived a heartbeat later, Severian's broken body bound beside them.

Every head turned upward at once, sighs of relief rippling across the broken rooftop as their gazes settled on the bound figure.

They had won. Finally.

However, while that was true, Amoz felt his heart stutter at the sight that greeted him. At the center of the cultivators, a pale form lay on the concrete, her body gray and dying. Her face was painfully familiar.

His eyes widened. "Is that Vaymoz's daughter?"

"Yes, Calamity Amoz." Remaris grimaced. "She's dying. Her soul was ripped apart."

"What?"

Amoz stared at the dying child in stunned disbelief. Then fury overtook the shock as he rounded on Severian.

"You fucking monster. You did this. Heal her this instant."

Severian smiled through strips of torn flesh. "Give me the legacy, and I will."

"How about we just kill you right now?" Selia sneered, tightening the chains around his neck. "How would you like that? How would you like to die before your sisters?"

Severian barely spared his sisters a glance. "If you kill me, she'll die. If you give me the legacy, she'll live. It's a win-win situation."

Amoz growled, annihilation gathering in his fists once—

"Brother, please!" Emily screamed, tears streaming down her face. "Please. Save the girl and let us return home. No hate is worth this." She glanced at Malverin. "Tell him, Verin. Olivia wouldn't want this. What are we to gain from all this?"

Malverin looked up at her brother through swollen eyes. Anger simmered within them, hot and bitter.

But she said nothing.

A moment later, Cephas spoke. "The soul cultivator is bluffing, Amoz. He can barely heal his own soul, let alone someone else's. Do not waste your breath on him."

The lightning gathering in Amoz's fist flickered and died. He glared at Severian's ruined form for a long moment before turning away to look at Yelena.

This was a disaster.

The destruction of the city could be rectified. The machines, the buildings—everything could be rebuilt or replaced. Even the deaths of thousands could, in time, fade into history.

But letting the daughter of the ruler—a man he had come to respect—die. Was that something he could do?

Amoz shook his head and looked to the healer. "What can we do, Verity? How much time does she have?"

"Less than three minutes at this point," Verity whispered grimly, her gaze flicking toward Aodhán before returning to Yelena. "We have only one solution, but it's not a good one."

"It's not a solution," Sophran muttered, glaring at the healer. "It's not even wise."

"I will decide what is wise and what is not." Amoz fixed him with a hard stare before turning back to Verity. "What solution do we have?"

Silence settled over the rooftop once more.

Verity hesitated, her gaze lingering on the pale girl lying beneath her before she slowly shook her head.

"No, Calamity Amoz. Special Disciple Sophran is right—"

"Tell me what you think!" Amoz said sharply, and after another moment of hesitation, Verity finally caved.

She drew a slow breath. "The only thing that can heal this girl now is an awakening. Nothing else can stitch her soul back together."

The four Calamities fell silent as the implications of her words settled over them.

Selia was the first to reject the idea.

"No." She shook her head firmly. "Sophran is right. That isn't a solution. She's just one mundane girl. We've already killed thousands, albeit accidentally. What's one more?"

"Indeed." Rezin scowled. "There has to be another way to save the girl. An awakening is out of the question."

Amoz grimaced, unable to deny the logic in their words.

A legacy seed wasn't something to hand out to just anyone, let alone someone who so clearly hated their kind. Yet they had already killed thousands today.

No. Slaughtered was the better word.

He didn't want to add another death to the unending list.

Not to mention this girl wasn't just another mundane. She was the daughter of Alcatraz's ruler—someone who, unlike so many of their other victims, could still be saved.

His grimace deepened and he shook his head, knowing his guilt was the one talking.

"Don't you have another idea, Verity?" he asked. "Something more rational?"

Verity shook her head. "No, I don't. At least none that can be done before we lose her."

"Then we'll just have to lose her." Selia scowled, meeting Amoz's eyes resolutely. "It will be difficult, but it'll be the wiser decision."

Rezin nodded. And after a moment, Cephas did the same, though he looked far less certain.

The three of them turned to Amoz, and he realized they were waiting for him to make the final decision. To agree with them.

Swallowing, he looked back at Yelena's pale, lifeless form.

"How long does she have, Verity?"

"Two minutes now." She grimaced. "Maybe less."

Amoz tightened his fist. He should agree. Just like the others said, he should look away and let this happen.

The death of thousands already burdened his soul. What was one more?

He pressed his lips together, wanting to make the decision the other Calamities would have called wise. Yet as he stared at the dying child, he found himself unable to.

Closing his eyes, he asked quietly, "Are we sure the seed will save her?"

"Amoz!" Rezin called sharply from behind him. "Let her die. You must not do this. The seed has been promised to someone else."

"I'm aware of who it has been promised to," Amoz hissed, glancing once at Aodhán before looking back at Rezin. "But I can't just stand here and watch the daughter of a man I've come to respect die either. If you have a better solution, suggest it."

"It's just one more human, Amoz!" Selia scowled. "You're behaving as though she's the descendant of the First Seat. She's a foolish mundane who should have stayed away from danger instead of running toward it. We will make restitution to the city and its lord."

Amoz stared at her, his expression hard.

She was right, of course. Sleepers had no business involving themselves in the battles of cultivators. Yelena's own recklessness had brought her to this point.

Yet, having been born in a city just like this, to parents who had hated cultivators just as fiercely as Yelena did, Amoz couldn't simply watch her die.

Not after today. Not after all the destruction they had caused and the thousands of lives they'd cut short.

Yelena's life was just one. One in a thousand.

But it was one that could be saved. One that could live if they were willing to make the sacrifice.

Choosing otherwise would be the sin.

And that wasn't something Amoz wanted on his conscience. Not with everything else.

Letting out a ragged breath, he turned to face Aodhán. "Hand over the seed, kid."

Aodhán met his gaze, eyes hard. Then he slowly shook his head.

"No."

It was the last answer Amoz expected to receive.
