The Mangy Scur wasn’t as bad as I’d anticipated.  The beer was decent, the tables were clean, and the spittoons were regularly emptied.  It was, as seemed to be mandatory on Helstat, run by a member of the incredibly extended De Sackville family.

Elkville was a break from the norm for the family.  Petit, brunette, and pretty in a way.  She stuck to the family tradition of perpetually polishing a glass whenever her hands weren’t otherwise occupied.

“What the fuck were you playing at?”  I glared at Harald.  Elkville began to polish faster in response to my tone.

“They were killing my people, Bob.”  Harald did not sound cowed at all.

“My people.”

“They’re nothing to you.  Taxpayers.  You haven’t been back to Longbottom since you captured it!”

He wasn’t entirely wrong.

“Why didn’t it trigger the siege notification?” I asked.

“He wasn’t trying to take the town.  Just disrupt everything, make life miserable for the people.  I couldn’t just stand by as it went on.”

“And you didn’t know about this?” Elkville asked in a gentle voice.

I shook my head.  “Hardprick here decided not to report it and go off on his own.”  I treated him to a level eight glower; it was more nuanced than a glare.  “Which forced my hand fucked up my plans.”

“People were being killed!”

“You know how many bunnyborgs I’ve got?  I’ve just let the orlic’s eat the Nineteenth.  I could have sorted this out for you if you’d raised it with Kat or Mrs Sanderson.”

“They ate the Nineteenth?” Elkville asked.

“Not literally,” I corrected.  “But the Nineteenth won’t be showing up at the big battle party in a week or so.  And the thing about orlics is they breed like unibunnies after a fight.  Harald, you’re getting a detachment of the new model robobunnies.  Next time you’re getting screwed with, sic those guys on them.”

“I had it handled.”

“Dimpleworn let you pass his army.  He wanted to smash you against the walls here to save his own troops.  He was a smart chap.”

“Past tense?”

I belched pointedly, and Elkville made a moue of distaste.

“Oh.  That’s going to cause some issues, Bob.”

“I’ve got it handled.  Just Hateskale to go, then I’ve sorted out peace and trade with the furry weirdos and brought them into my little alliance.  I’ve got the orlics, dwarves, humans, dragons and… well, I guess that’s about it for now.  But we’re heading in the right direction.”

“What direction is that?  You trying to set up some kind of empire within an Empire?” Elkville asked.

“Good question.  The only Empire I’m interested in is my business empire.  There’ll be portals set up in Dampcut, tying the town into my estates.  Normally, we’d have a portal in the pub as well, and replace your kitchen staff and menu with our own, all run out of the dungeon via portals.  I’ve got some top-flight chefs on staff.  But the question is: what does this place have to offer me in return?”

“We’re a lumber town.  Most people work in the sawmills.”

“You’ve got wood?”

“Good wood.  Hard wood.”

Harald snorted into his beer and earned an escalation from glower to glare.  I should have opened more strongly in the evil look department.

“For someone with your surname, I don’t think you’re in a position to be sniggering at that.”  I turned back to Elkville.  “So you produce lumber.  And just enough food not to starve, it seems.  What’s the menu like here?”

“We’ve got vegetable stew and meat stew.”

“Which meat?”

“Depends.  Chicken or game if you’re lucky, goat or rat if you’re not.  It changes from ladle to ladle.”

“You just throw all the meat into one pot?”

“When we’ve got meat, yeah.”

I opened a portal to the kitchen on the industrial floor back in the dungeon.  Elkville scowled through at the smart, penguin-looking serving staff calling out orders and taking plates heaped with steaming meat and vegetables out to other portals that lined the far wall.

“—no you duck-faced runt!  The jus needs to reduce more than that!  And is this an acceptable amount of Harrowherb?  Is that what you think, you stupid fuck?”  Mr Grementhal hurled the pan off the stove.  “Clean that up and start again.  Harry, we’re going to be late on table nine in the Cod because this fuckwit doesn’t know what a pinch is!”

Jenny appeared next to the angriest chef in the multiverse and leaned up to give him a hairy kiss on the cheek.  She turned, her face flushing red as she glanced at an oven.  “Who left these puffballs on a high heat?  You morons are going to fuck up everything, aren’t you?  The Long Horn needs these in ten minutes, but they don’t want bloody charcoal, you syphilitic whoresons!”

I let the portal snap shut.  “Apologies for the language, and I’d like to assure you everyone is suitably compensated for the hostile work environment. But that is a world-class baker and one of the finest living chefs cooking food for… I think four pubs?  There’s no reason we couldn’t add a fifth.  Speaking of.”  I produced three glasses and a bottle of Golden Jack.  “How about we drink on our new business arrangement?”  I slid a glass to each of them and savoured the sharp aroma of the whisky.

“No one here can afford that kind of food,” Elkville sounded hungry and deeply regretful.  “What would be the point?”

“You said good wood?”  I ignored Harald snorting again.

“Wyrdwood.  You haven’t seen the forests around here, but they’re all wyrdwood.  It goes straight to the Mage's Academy in Ankmapak.  The lord owns all the sawmills, and he’s got a monopoly on the timber industry.”

Greed had perked up and rubbed his hands together.  I’d stumbled onto a goldmine.  A monopoly on a valuable resource, a citizenry so used to oppression that even if I kept the tax rate at a level that would make the people at the Mill grumble and curse me, and the locals would still think I was a benevolent ruler!

I adjusted the tax slider mentally, dropping it down to a mere forty percent.  Her eyes widened in shock as the notification went out.  “I’m also willing to allow for a more generous allocation of income for the lumberjacks.”  Being willing to do something didn’t mean I actually would.

“Denario’s Shiny Pouch!  That’s… I’ve barely kept going.  Everyone lives day to day.  Now they’ll have a chance…  Thank you, Bob.”

“Don’t worry about it,” I replied magnanimously.  The mighty dragon could take pity on the puny mammal.  I throttled Greed in my mind and plastered a pleasant smile on my face.  “I’m not going to talk numbers with you about this place.  I’ll leave a portal here so people can come and go, but you’ll need to talk to Kat or Mrs Sanderson about the actual deal.  I have people to handle that kind of thing for me now.”

“Oh, lahdy dah,” Harald joked.  “So… am I in charge here now?”

“Hells no.  Elk, what about you?  You aren’t tied to the old regime, and you serve surprisingly good beer for a pub on the edge of the world.”

“The guards and overseers, they like a good ale,” she said darkly.  “I couldn’t do it.  One of the lumberjacks, maybe?  Larch, he’s got a good reputation.  Expert tree fighter, as well.”

“Larch?  Some kind of guerrilla fighter?”  That could be a problem.  I didn’t fancy putting the leading rebel in charge of my new order.  He might have ideas above his station.

“Tree fighter.  Wyrdwood trees don’t just sit there.  They move around in the night, and they attack humans who try to get near their trunks.  They’re highly magical, and it shows.  The wood makes staffs, and some of the best wands.  Enchanted carriages are usually made with the stuff as well, the ones the nobles apparently drive around the city in,” she said bitterly.

“So he fights wood?” Harald chuckled.

“That doesn’t even work, Hardprick.  Elk, can you get in touch with this Larch, set up a meeting with my management people?”

“I can do that.  And we can discuss how much you’re willing to pay to buy into my establishment.”

“Word to the wise, I really don’t like the word buy.  Filthy.  Acquire is far more palatable.”  I looked around the neat but empty pub.  “I’m sure my people will come to a fair number with your people.”

“I don’t have people.”

“And that’s the difference between us.  I think I’d like to take a look at the sawmills and meet my new staff.  The big buildings down by the river with the waterwheels?”

Elk nodded, and I opened a portal to the mana crystal floor.  I placed it over in a corner, somewhere out of the way and not blocking access to the bar.

“Harald, get you and your people back to where they belong.  Next time someone is fucking with you, go through the proper channels so we can fuck them back hard.  Miss Elkville, a number of your cousins are part of the Swinging Cod family, so I’m sure you can ask them for their opinions on the merits of joining our little family.  Just follow the signs to the relevant pub or town.  Now, time for me to deal with the bourgeoisie and free the working man.  And woman.”

Elkville locked up behind me, and I wandered through this dilapidated little town towards the sound of industry coming from the small river that ran nearby.  Some tributary to the mighty waterway that ended up a murky brown and reeking of ammonia before it entered the sea beyond Ankmapak.

Once past the crumbling homes, I found the lumberyard part of town was much better kept.  Still no paint, but everything was patched and repaired.  Doors weren’t splintered and hanging loosely on their hinges.  They fit the frames neatly and looked suitably secure.  Not secure enough to deal with a mildly peeved dragon, though.

I smashed a fist on the first one I reached and cocked my head to one side.  The thuds echoed back to me.  The place was quiet, too quiet.

“Oi!  It’s time to meet the new boss.  Same as the old boss, but with less taxes and more scales!”  Greed threw a fit in my mind when I mentioned the reduction in the free money I was making.

There should be sawing and hammering, and workers cursing and yelling orders back and forth.  Industry was never peaceful.  The normal volume of these buildings might explain why the rows of houses nearest to them were clearly abandoned.

Shrugging, I backed up a step and then knocked very hard indeed. My fist went through the thick planks, and the door got stuck on my elbow as it tore free from the lock and hinges.  “You guys can fix this, right?  You’re carpenters, of course you can.”  I used my free hand to push the door off my arm and let it plunk down on the floorboards.

Dozens of terrified eyes looked over at me.  The workers wore drab, threadbare clothes that were more hole than cloth in many cases.  They looked strong in an undernourished sort of way.  “I come in peace to liberate you from the oppressive chains of Dimpleworn’s cruel overseers,” I added cheerfully.

“Get out of my shop!”

He emerged from behind the big saw that ran across the far end of the room.

“You haven’t been skipping any meals, have you?  You the big boss, or do I need to speak to your supervisor?”  All the peasants made the sign of she who must be blessed in triplicate, imaginary forms being stamped three times.

He winced, and three of six chins wobbled in alarm at the casual invocation of the Goddess Karen.  He raised a hand and pointed at me.  “Get ‘im, boys!”

A dozen or so well-fed and well-dressed gentlemen charged from the shadows behind the contraption that linked the saw to the waterwheel outside, brandishing hammers.

“Excellent!  So kind of you to present yourselves all at once.”

I opened portals under each of their feet and sent them to the nightmarish liquid that Ankmapak called a river.
