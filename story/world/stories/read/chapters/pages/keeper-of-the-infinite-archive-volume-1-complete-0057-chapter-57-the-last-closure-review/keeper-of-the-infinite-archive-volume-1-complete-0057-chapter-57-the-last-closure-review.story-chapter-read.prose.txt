The Curator's voice hung for sometime in the freezing air of the Closed Collection. He slowly picked up his pristine white glove and slid it back over his right hand, smoothing the fabric down over his fingers.

Gregor exhaled a long, tense breath. He patted the leather satchel and took a step backward, angling toward the massive open vault doors. Gregor stopped. "Boss. Can we leave now?."

Bart kept his eyes on the grey man across the iron lectern. "You have mentioned 'Keeper# 4091' a few times."

The Curator finished adjusting his glove. "Yes."

Bart didn't step back toward the exit. "Then before we leave, I have questions."

"What was he like?" Bart asked.

"Intelligent," the Curator said simply.

Bart waited. "That isn't much of an obituary."

"I did not say it was one."

Bart didn't press to find out whether that meant Keeper #4,091 was still alive somewhere, or simply that the Curator refused to mourn him. "He was curious," the Curator continued, his voice perfectly level. "Methodical,  Stubborn even . He believed that most dangerous knowledge became less dangerous when properly understood."

"That doesn't sound unreasonable," Bart said.

The Curator looked away, his pale eyes sweeping over the thousands upon thousands of heavy iron boxes stacked across the room.

"It did not sound unreasonable at the time."

Bart adjusted his glasses. An uncomfortable realization was beginning to settle over him. He had spent the last several weeks assuming the Curator was simply a territorial administrator hoarding power. But the Curator’s actions hadn't been random.

"You weren't just protecting books from me," Bart said. "When I restored my Authorities, you didn't just lock the physical aisles. You deliberately shut down the relationships I was reconstructing. You closed Keeper #4,091's terminal actions. You closed the Ruin causation records."

"Yes,I did do all those" the Curator said.

"Why?"

The Curator looked back at him. "Because I did not know whether you were rebuilding the safeguards, or rebuilding the mistake."

The words struck Bart as true and hung in the air for a while.

"You ask why," the Curator said, taking a slow step around the iron lectern. "Usually. Then, when an answer is unavailable, you ask what record once contained it."

"Yes," Bart said cautiously.

"Then you ask what other record referred to that record."

Gregor nodded from the shadows. "He does do that."

"Well, So did Keeper #4,091," the Curator said.

Bart went entirely still. He reconstructed broken systems. Followed missing records. Refused unsupported assumptions. And whenever a trail ended, his instinct was always to find another record that might continue it. He had just restored Citation the Authority designed to connect things.The Curator had expected Bart to fail. To the Curator, Bart Sanders didn't look like a savior. He looked uncomfortably familiar. He looked like the beginning of a pattern the Curator had seen once before.

Bart's expression hardened. "You closed Succession."

"Its historical basis," the Curator corrected.

"You knew what that would do."

"Yes."

Bart didn't look away from the grey suit man. "Were you trying to stop me becoming the Keeper?"

"No." That answer came immediately, without a trace of hesitation.

Bart frowned. "Then why?"

"Because you were tracing Succession backward," the Curator said.

"I was trying to understand how it worked."

"Precisely." The Curator rested one gloved hand against the heavy leather of the Black Catalogue. "Succession was not the dangerous record. Its origins touched the Master Authority Network. The Master Authority Network touched Keeper #4,091's final actions. Those actions touched the Ruin."

Bart followed the chain in his head.

"And you were afraid I would keep following it."

"You did keep following it," the Curator pointed out calmly.

Gregor raised a finger. "In his defence, that is very much what he does."

Bart ignored him. "So you weren't trying to prevent Succession."

"I was trying to prevent Succession from becoming a route around Closure."

The Curator continued, his voice dropping slightly in the cavernous silence. "You keep assuming the danger lies at the end of the path, Keeper Sanders." His pale eyes settled heavily on Bart. "Sometimes the path is the dangerous part."

Bart disliked how much sense the distinction made. He had spent his career at public libraries believing that finding the right trail of references was one of the highest form of service.

"Did he cause the Ruin?" Bart asked quietly.

RELATIONSHIP CLOSED

The blue text flared instantly above the Black Catalogue.

"That relationship remains Closed," the Curator said.

"You can answer more than that," Bart pressed.

"I can."

"Will you?"

The Curator paused. He tapped his gloved fingers once, very lightly, against the ledger.

"Keeper #4,091 did not create the danger."

It was a tiny but a start of a concession. Bart seized  on it immediately. "But he did something with it."

"Yes."

"What?"

"Closed."

Gregor sighed. "We were doing quite well there for a while."

"You keep saying relationships can be dangerous," Bart said, refusing to drop the thread. "You warned me earlier that some knowledge is dangerous not when read, but when enough separate facts are connected. Show me what that means."

The Curator didn't gesture toward a spellbook or manual capable of deleting a moon or tome resurrecting a dead army. Instead, he turned and  tapped the Black Catalogue.

A new administrative window with sanitized text appeared in the air between them.

LEGACY CLOSURE DEMONSTRATION

Record A: UNRESTRICTED

Record B: UNRESTRICTED

Record C: UNRESTRICTED

[14 additional records omitted for safety]

Citation Relationship: CLOSED

Hazard Classification: TERMINAL

Bart studied the screen. "None of those seventeen records are restricted individually."

"Correct," the Curator said.

"But the connection between them is."

"Yes."

This mattered. Bart's restored Citation Authority did exactly this: it created and verified connections. Now he understood why the Curator had panicked when he restored it.

The Curator looked at him, and for a moment, he looked unimaginably tired. "What does Citation do, Provisional Keeper Sanders?"

"It establishes supported relationships between distinct pieces of information," Bart said automatically.

"And what happens," the Curator asked softly, "when 17 harmless truths logically establish an 18th truth with a verified comprehension fatality rate of 100 percent?"

Gregor took a slow step backward. "Could we maybe not verify it again?"

Bart finally understood what the Curator had been trying to make him see.It wasn't just about locking away dangerous magical artifacts or cursed books. Bart already knew those existed but this was a much larger, much more terrifying concept. The danger wasn't always in the information itself. Sometimes it existed in the relationship between otherwise harmless facts.Truth itself could require access controls.

Bart looked at the glowing blue word TERMINAL, and then at the endless rows of iron boxes. For the first time, Bart saw the Closed Collection not merely as six centuries of emergency restrictions that nobody had bothered to review, but as a dam holding back an ocean of catastrophic logic.

"Then that relationship stays Closed," Bart said.

The Curator’s eyes widened slightly, perhaps expecting another argument about academic freedom.

"Until," Bart continued, "its Closure is reviewed."

The Curator's posture stiffened. "You are remarkably attached to that word."

"Occupational hazard," Bart replied. He adjusted his glasses, looking the grey man directly in the eye. "You were right to lock it. But nobody gets to throw away the key and call that policy."

Silence stretched across the iron lectern. Understanding why Closure existed was not the same as validating six centuries of it. And before he could do that, he needed to know why Keeper #4,091 had locked Albert's three days behind it.

Bart nodded once to the Curator. He turned and walked toward the massive vault doors.

Gregor followed, casting one last, deeply uneasy glance at the thousands of iron graves.

The heavy vault doors groaned shut behind them as they stepped back into the freezing stone tunnel. The massive iron locks engaged with a series of thuds.They walked back up the long, dark corridor in silence.

Bart's mind was racing, already planning the logistics of the memory recovery procedure waiting back at the main desk. Closure had concurred. Memory was waiting. All they needed was Albert. They rounded the bend in the tunnel, approaching the circular stone chamber.

Mabel was exactly where they had left her. Her massive, pale stone like body rested beside the iron tracks. Her golden eyes tracked them as they approached, glowing like twin lanterns in the dark.

Gregor stopped walking. Bart took two more steps before noticing. He turned around. Gregor was staring at Mabel. Then he looked at the faint blue identification panel still hovering beside the massive predator.

Posting status: CONTINUOUS

Relief status: NONE ASSIGNED

Departure from post: REQUIRES RELIEF

Gregor slowly looked at Bart.

Bart realized exactly what Gregor was thinking.

"Boss," Gregor said quietly.

"What?" Bart asked, a profound sense of administrative dread already settling in his stomach.

"Hypothetically."

Bart stopped walking. That was already a terrible start.

"How long," Gregor asked, gesturing vaguely toward the 12 foot tall predator, "can someone be temporarily assigned to a job?"

Bart slowly turned toward the Sphinx while Mabel's ears rose.
