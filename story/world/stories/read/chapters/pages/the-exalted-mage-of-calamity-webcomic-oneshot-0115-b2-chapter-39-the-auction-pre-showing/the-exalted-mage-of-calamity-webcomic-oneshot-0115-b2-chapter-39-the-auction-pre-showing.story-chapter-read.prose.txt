“I see. So this is the auction house? I recognize the building,” Veronica said.

The Pelotema Grand Auction House stood at the end of a broad avenue lined with lush green trees and rows of dedicated streetlamps. It sat atop a broad sweep of pale stone steps. The front was made of polished limestone, carved with filigree that wrapped around the building. Tall windows revealed the pristine interior, glowing beneath crystal chandeliers. More than ten banners hung from the building’s crown, likely bearing the crests of different merchants or nobles backing the auction.

There were already others climbing the steps, many wearing expensive suits and fine jewelry. It seemed even the pre-showing was popular.

“After you,” Gwen said.

Veronica nodded. The two climbed the steps and settled behind a short line.

Soon, they reached the entrance, where two men stood in black uniforms that blended thin armor with formal suits. Veronica could feel the hum of enchanted equipment on them. Looking around, she noticed several arrays covering the arches of the doorway. They weren’t hidden; they were clearly visible to deter any would-be troublemakers.

One of the guards looked at Veronica, then at Gwen. “Purpose?” he asked.

“Pre-auction showing,” Veronica answered.

“Invitation?”

Veronica reached into her pocket and removed the signet from Viscount Leopold. “Would this be sufficient for entry?”

The guard’s expression didn’t change, but his eyes focused on the ring. He gestured toward a small gray plate beside the door.

Veronica stepped forward and pressed the face of the signet against it. A pale white glow passed over the metal, pulsing once.

The guard nodded. “That signet is authentic. You may both enter the public showing hall. The upper floors may require you to verify your identities again.”

“I see. Thank you,” Veronica said.

Gwen followed her inside, glancing back at the guards before leaning closer. “He said public showing hall… but if you need a noble’s permission, that isn’t really public.”

“That’s because they consider low-level nobles and merchants ‘the public,’” Veronica said.

After leaving the entrance hall, they stepped into a massive lobby.

Gwen stopped instinctively. “Whoa.”

The room was wide enough to hold several smaller shops. A polished marble floor stretched from the entrance to a grand central staircase that curved toward the second and third floors. Chandeliers hung from the ceiling, each glowing with warm golden light. Attendants in crisp black-and-white uniforms moved through the hall, guiding guests and answering questions.

And the displays—they were everywhere. Dozens of glass cases sat atop marble stands, organized into countless rows. Hundreds of people filled the hall, most gathered around the displays and conversing. Some debated prices, while others appeared to be calculating how much they could afford to spend if an item caught their interest.

Veronica walked toward one of the less crowded rows and began inspecting the items.

Gwen hurried after her. “They’re just keeping all these items out on display? There must be hundreds here.”

“Not quite,” Veronica said, stopping in front of a case.

Inside the vitrine, a small pedestal held a prickly sphere that appeared to be made from dark metal. A plaque on the marble plinth read:

Imanight Fruit: A fruit born from the Imanight cactus. Each spike inflicts a nonfatal paralytic poison on contact that can last for up to five minutes. Can be cooked and eaten to train a person’s resistance to paralysis.

Gwen leaned forward. “Cool. Creepy, but cool.”

“Look closer.”

Gwen glanced back at her, then turned toward the fruit and scrutinized it. Her eyes widened. “It’s… fake? It looks like it’s made of paper.”

“The actual auction items are probably being stored somewhere else. They wouldn’t leave them on display, even if most of the people here have some sort of status or background.”

Veronica continued walking, stopping at any display case that wasn’t already surrounded by people.

There were all sorts of guests here, not just wealthy nobles or merchants. A few adventurers wore polished, expensive armor. Association mages wandered through the hall in robes, inspecting the more magically inclined items. There were even some commoner-looking people keeping low profiles as they examined the lots.

Gwen leaned toward another case. “Spirit ink?”

Veronica glanced over.

Three vials of spirit-reactive ink: Useful for contracting spirit monsters and strengthening your control over them.

“Hey, wouldn’t this be good for Nasha? Because, you know, that… thing we have?” Gwen asked, looking back.

“No,” Veronica said plainly. “Just because it has the word spirit in it doesn’t mean it’s what you think. That ink is only useful for summoning spirits or familiars. They’re different from what Dusk and Dawn were.”

“Ah, okay. Totally not confusing.”

The two continued through the hall until something caught Veronica’s eye.

“Oh? They actually have this here,” Veronica said, walking toward the display.

“What’s this?” Gwen asked, appearing beside her. She looked down at the plaque.

Raw Starmetal Ore: A 22 kg piece of raw starmetal ore harvested directly from Mt. Horizon. Can be used to forge starmetal weapons or armor by a skilled blacksmith.

“Starmetal? Is this good?” Gwen asked.

Inside the case was a rough, uneven piece of silvery stone. Faint specks of pale light shimmered through it like stars scattered within cloudy metal.

“It’s very good,” Veronica said, leaning down for a closer look. “I wasn’t expecting something like this to be here. It can amplify the power of stars and light. I once had a small wand made from starmetal before I obtained a better staff. This amount might be enough to make another one.”

She leaned back and crossed her arms. “Though I don’t know much about purity or forging. I’m not sure how much of the ore will actually be usable.”

“Are you going to buy it? How expensive is it?”

“Hard to say.” Veronica pressed her lips together as she considered it. “Not many blacksmiths are skilled enough to work with this metal. Back then, I had to search the entire kingdom just to find a few who were capable.”

“So it might be expensive, or it might not.”

Veronica sighed. “Yeah. Depending on how sought after it is, I might place a bid. We’ll have to wait and see.”

They continued through the gallery, examining the cases that had previously been too crowded. There were still plenty of lots they hadn’t seen. Perhaps something else would catch their attention before they attempted to reach the next floor.

“Master Orban. I see you are in good health.”

“Spare the formalities. Just let me enter,” Orban grumbled.

The guard looked down at him, then shifted his attention to the boy standing beside him. “Is this your apprentice?”

The boy straightened and bowed nervously. “Name’s Theo. Apprentice of Master Orban.”

The guard chuckled. “Boy’s got good manners.” His eyes returned to Orban. “You may enter.”

“Come on, boy,” Orban said, walking ahead.

“Yes, Master.” Theo followed half a step behind him, trying his best not to stare at everything as they entered.

He failed immediately. His gaze moved from the chandeliers to the marble floor, then to the rows of glass cases filled with display items. Even if most were only replicas, they looked impressive enough to fool anyone who didn’t know better. He even made eye contact with several nobles and other wealthy guests, though once they noticed Orban, they quickly looked away.

Orban himself didn’t appear impressed by anything.

The suit he wore already irritated him. The collar was too tight, the sleeves too stiff, and the polished shoes felt like something made for a man who had never stood near a forge. If the auction house hadn’t refused entrance to anyone who looked too rough, he would have worn his work clothes. At least those had the decency to smell honest.

Orban walked past the first few cases, barely giving them more than a glance. A venomous dagger. Spirit ink. Beast cores from monsters too weak to matter.

He stopped in front of one particular spear, cursing the two talkative nobles blocking his view. One appeared ready to object, but the other possessed half a brain cell and dragged him away.

The spear was of acceptable quality. The spearhead, however, had been ruined by a poor enchantment. Although the replica was made of paper, its creator had copied the original closely enough for Orban to see the flawed veins running through its foundation.

Theo followed behind him, trying not to get lost in the crowd.

The farther Orban went, the heavier his mood became. Nothing caught his eye. There were ingots and pieces of ore, but none that could change the fate of his shop. He couldn’t afford to bid on everything.

He needed something special. Something capable of restoring his reputation and livelihood. The quality and craftsmanship of his work were second to none; he knew that. But skill meant nothing when the commerce groups bought every worthwhile piece of ore and every ingot before he had a chance.

He had lost the ability to accept even small, ordinary commissions. Commoner or noble, it didn’t matter. No, he needed something grand—an article so extraordinary that only someone of great standing could afford it. A member of the royal family, a martial supreme, or even an archmage.

Perhaps then he could reclaim his former glory. He could no longer remain the bullied dog in a pack of wolves. His pride could endure only so much. He had to risk everything on one final gamble.

“Master,” Theo said quietly.

Orban glanced over. The boy was staring at a display case several rows ahead. Inside the case sat a replica chunk of silver metal. Light moved within it like stars caught in a cosmic storm, all contained inside hardened steel.

He slowly walked toward it.

Theo followed, his voice barely above a whisper. “Is that…?”

Orban read the plaque.

Raw Starmetal Ore: A 22 kg piece of raw starmetal ore harvested directly from Mt. Horizon. Can be used to forge starmetal weapons or armor by a skilled blacksmith.

His fingers twitched. The display was only a replica, but it had been made well enough to show the ore’s structure. A revelation mage had probably crafted it. Nevertheless, he could see the silver grain, the tiny light-filled veins, and the celestial sheen he couldn’t tear his eyes away from.

It was real starmetal. Actual starmetal he had only ever heard about. Orban leaned closer, narrowing his eyes. “Twenty-two kilograms.”

“That’s a lot, right?”

“No.”

Theo blinked. “No? It’s almost bigger than you.”

Orban glared at him.

Theo straightened and chuckled nervously. “S-sorry.”

Orban sighed. “It’s raw ore, not refined ingots. ‘Pending on the purity, perhaps half’ll be usable. Maybe less. If the inner veins are stable, it could form the base of one or two swords, ‘haps even a spear. A short blade, maybe gauntlets or boots. If the ore is poor, then it’s just an expensive rock with a pretty name. But from I see, its purity is near above sixty percent. More than usable.”

Unlike other ores, starmetal didn’t merely make a weapon sharper or harder. Plenty of materials could accomplish that. Starmetal reacted with the sky—with space itself. It amplified spatial magic and light-attribute spells. Aura users could even harness the stars through it, accumulating power from the cosmos within their bodies.

In the hands of the wrong craftsman or wielder, however, it was little more than an overly expensive, shining piece of silver. It was also notoriously difficult to refine and forge. Few knew the proper techniques. Even among dwarves, it had become a nearly lost art.

But Orban wasn’t an ordinary dwarf. He hadn’t been banished from the dwarven kingdom and earned the title of master blacksmith for nothing.

He needed this metal.

Theo looked up at him. “Can we afford it?”

Orban’s face hardened. “Maybe. Few people can use such a thing. None’a the commerce groups will be able to refine it.”

If he sold everything, called in every favor, delayed all his payments—even at the ire of his buyers—and sold his smithy, then perhaps he could outbid the commerce groups. Because they would bid on it, he knew that, if only to watch him fall even further. That was all they had done for the past four years. But what they didn’t know… was that he couldn’t fall any further.

This would be his final gamble to reclaim everything.
