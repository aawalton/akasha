I awoke early the next morning and raced to Drywsone’s makeshift smithy. I was expecting them to be setting up for a day of work, but when I arrived, they were already working. The hearth was blazing, the bellows were pumping, and Drywsone was hammering a plate of Imperial Steel. Judging by the bags under the apprentices’ eyes, they had been working through the night to finish off the gear for all the Dupes.

Shatter took a break from fitting together links of chainmail and rushed to the counter. “Give us just a few more minutes,” he said. “Your equipment will be ready soon.”

I waited patiently, trying not to put any pressure on them. According to the Drillmaster, we would depart in an hour, and I didn’t want to be late.

As I waited, the rest of my squad arrived. “Too early?” Anchor teased.

“Oh, come on, I was excited…”

They all rolled their eyes. Then we suffered together.

Finally, Drywsone called us to the counter. First, he started with Brawl. He handed him a hauberk of chainmail and a vest of scale mail to go overtop. Each scale was dark gray, with the signature ripples of Imperial Steel, but they also had faint brass inlays on every tenth scale—that was from our Labyrinth loot. And there was plenty more room to put more inlays, if we ever got more. At the very center, on the chest, he’d embedded a shard of the Keeper Topaz Dragon’s core. It shone bright orange.

Next, Drywsone handed him a helmet. It was Imperial Steel, with the same brass inlays and a crystal embedded in the forehead. Orange hairs hung down the back of the helmet, forming a plume. They no longer sparked at their tips, but they glistened in the light, as though they were made of crystal. Lastly, Drywsone handed him a pair of vambraces, greaves, and a mantle of thicker scales that wrapped around his upper body.

Next was Anchor. Drywsone handed him an ornate but thin shoulder pauldron with countless swirls of bronze inlays—more loot we’d retrieved from the Labyrinth. Beneath it, he would wear a light vest of chainmail. Finally, a helmet much like Brawl’s, with a plume running down his back.

Sage received her set of light armour next. A mantle made of hardened mushroom conks, inlaid into leftover rawhide like it was scale mail, then finally, gloves and vambraces made in the same fashion. It wouldn’t repair itself like our armour would, though.

Finally, Drywsone heaped a pile of armour on the table for me. First, he gave me a folded-up hauberk of chainmail. I got a close-up look at it. Shimmering orange dust was embedded in the metal, which made it ripple if the light shone on it right. It had an iridescent sheen to it, indicating enchantments in the base armour. When I pulled it on, a swell of resonance ran through my body. It was enhancing my physical attributes just by wearing it, more than compensating for its weight.

Next was a coat of scale mail. Much like Brawl’s, some of its segments had brass inlays made of the loot we’d extracted from the Labyrinth.

I pulled on my orange cloak overtop, then picked up my new helmet. It was the same shape as before, though slightly heavier. Streaks of bronze ran down the front and circled the eye holes, and a plume of orange hair sprouted out the top. It was long, like a horse’s tail, and fell halfway down my back.

I picked up the pair of vambraces and attached them to my wrists, then my greaves for my ankles. Lastly were my pauldrons, adorned with the same ornate brass swirls and orange crystal shards.

“Each article should have a Skill of its own,” Drywsone told us. “Couple that with the Skills from the loot you gathered, and you should have plenty more Skills to work with.”

“There’s one more thing…” Shatter prompted.

“I’m getting to it. You still have chainmail to link.” Drywsone bent down, then lifted up a pair of shields—one for me, and one for Brawl. “These are your finished shields. Complete with the crest you commissioned. We copied the pattern last night and fitted it on.”

I leaned over the counter. They were orange and black, just like I’d expected. Instead of a simple raven, it depicted a raven in flight, holding a pink flower in its talons. A shield boss made of Imperial Steel protected the center, and a rim ran around the outside, keeping it all locked together.

I tapped on the shields. They didn’t even bend. Gripping it between my fingers, I tested its strength. If I was able to break regular stone with my bare hands…

The wood barely flexed. It didn’t even threaten to splinter. It was stronger than anything I’d touched before, and heavier.

“They should be Gold-tier equivalent,” Drywsone said. “Now, that is all. I figure, when it comes to your arsenal, you will want custom equipment with specifically gatherer resources. Your weapons will serve you fine for the time being. Test the armour.”

I glanced at Sage. She retrieved the jars of Labyrinth Keepers she’d captured, then poured them out on my shoulder. The tiny bugs swarmed over the armour, acquainting themselves with it.

I watched, heart catching with anticipation. Any moment, they could decide that they wanted to pull out the crystal dust and return it to the Labyrinth, or maybe they would eat the core shards…

Nothing.

They simply swarmed on the surface.

I held up my pauldron, then said, “Anchor, you think you can put a hole in it?”

“Doubtful, Sarge. That’s tough armour. But I’ll try.”

He drew an arrow, nocked it, then blasted it into my pauldron. The arrow clacked off the metal ridges, arrowhead scraping against metal as it deflected. It left a dent and a scratch, but it didn’t pierce through.

Immediately, the Labyrinth Keepers swarmed the area. They buffed out the dent and mended the scratch, returning the armour to its original form.

“It works…” I said. “Perfect. Thank you, Drywsone.”

“No worries. It was quite the challenge. Now…” He held out a leather pouch. “Here is the leftover dust from the crystals. It’s quite valuable, but we don’t have the ability to sell it here anymore. You’d be better off taking it with you.”

“Thanks.” I took the pouch and stuffed it in my kit bag. “Now, we’d better be off. The Drillmaster will be waiting for us.”

~ ~ ~

We departed only a few minutes later, travelling farther west than I’d ever been before. The typical journey on foot between Hyrdford and Steelshore would take four weeks, but we could cut that in half with our horses. Without having to guard a convoy of evacuating civilians, we could move even faster.

Without sleeping, we could cut even more time off that total. We were simulating a massive, quick march to get to a target quickly, and this would have been part of our training.

As soon as we settled into a rhythm, I used the time to merge Skills.

First, I retrieved my reading slate and used it to examine what I had available. The Skills section read:

Native Skills: Evolved Spearmanship (Journeyman), Quick-Witted Crossbow Use (Journeyman), Art: Mass Hammer (Advanced), Memory Modification (Apprentice), Memory Palace Creation (Apprentice), Memory Palace Sorting (Apprentice), External Squad Synergy (Apprentice), Squad-Based Defensive Blend (Journeyman), Lancework (Apprentice), Needle of Theve (Apprentice), Rope Weaving (Novice), Spear-Crossbow Synergy (Apprentice), Rising Leaf Style (Apprentice), Snapping Pheasant (Apprentice), Perception (Apprentice), Comet Lance (Apprentice), Aurora Wind (Apprentice), Javelin of the Northern Shore (Apprentice), Mountain Lion’s Tail (Apprentice), Plains Thunderstorm Style (Apprentice)

Armour Skills: Weighscale Use (Novice), Mortar Setting (Apprentice), Canal Gondola Steering (Novice), Stone-Lift Operation (Apprentice), Quarrying (Novice), Light Step (Apprentice), Heavy Step (Novice), Mule Driving (Novice), Harsh Terrain Navigation (Journeyman), Keeper Manipulation (Apprentice), Command of Law (Novice), Crane Guidance (Apprentice), Ballistae Aiming (Apprentice), Greater Keeper Manipulation (Journeyman), Gladiator’s Certainty (Novice), Greater Slinging (Novice), Ash Tilling (Apprentice)

Ingrained Armour Skills: Footwork (Apprentice - Greaves), Quick Reload (Journeyman - Vambraces), Eye on Target (Apprentice - Helmet), Greater Deflection (Journeyman - Hauberk), Trajectory Anticipation (Journeyman - Scale Mail), Known Limits (Apprentice - Pauldrons)

My mouth gaped. I knew I’d gained a lot of Skills and gathered a lot of loot, but that was too much. All of the basic armour skills had been imprinted on treasures the old empire’s citizens would have worn in their daily lives, and I hadn’t realized how many I’d collected. How was I supposed to manage that all?

My mind almost overloaded, but I focused. One step at a time. I needed to improve my spear Skill first and foremost, so that was what I’d do.

I took [Evolved Spearmanship], [Squad-Based Defensive Blend], [Lancework], and all my other spear combat style Skills. I needed one more Journeyman tier skill for my spear, so I also took [Greater Deflection] from my hauberk.

First, I started with [Evolved Spearmanship] and merged in [Squad-Based Defensive Blend], because it was by far my oldest and best combat ability aside from my main spearmanship ability. Next came [Greater Deflection], which would give the Skill far more defensive capabilities. Lastly, I added [Lancework] for its offensive capabilities, as well as [Comet Lance].

That resulted in a new spear Skill: [Soulstealer’s Will (Advanced)].

But I had plenty of Skills left.

Next, I’d push my crossbow to a higher level. I would need a few more ingredients, first, though. I merged together [Spear-Crossbow Synergy], [External Squad Synergy], [Rope Weaving], [Canal Gondola Steering], and [Eye on Target] to create an intermediary—[Lesser Crossbow Management (Journeyman)].

Then, I took my main Skill, my new Skill, [Quick Reload], [Ballistae Aiming], and added [Quarrying] as the final fodder Skill. I merged them together in that order, and they produced a new Skill: [Unseen Bolt (Advanced)].

Still plenty of Skills left.

I pursed my lips. I could prepare to merge my Art to a higher tier. I might even have enough Skills to make it work.

But was I willing to let it put that much strain on my leys, to the point of damaging them?

I’d save the merge for an emergency.

First, though, I’d need a few more Advanced skills. [Snapping Pheasant], though it was a spear style, involved combining my Art with my spearmanship. It would absolutely help me in my Art, especially now that I’d gotten better at combining my Art with my actual abilities.

I took it up to Journeyman tier with the help of [Plains Thunderstorm Style], [Mountain Lion’s Tail], [Weighscale Use], and [Heavy Step]. Then, to bring it up to Advanced, I added [Greater Keeper Manipulation] and [Harsh Terrain Navigation], alongside [Footwork] and [Known Limits].

As far as I was aware, I’d gotten the Keeper Manipulation skills from, well, manipulating them and adding them to the armour. Since they weren’t native Skills, they were technically an enchantment, and I suspected they came from the crystals.

As for [Known Limits], I didn’t really know what it did. Perhaps it had to do with pushing myself. I didn’t really want to stop pushing myself, so as far as I was concerned, it was better to get rid of the Skill.

It merged again, creating an Advanced-tier version of [Snapping Pheasant].

I needed one more Advanced-tier skill. To make it, I first had to create a few more sub-Skills. I merged [Mortar Setting], [Stone-Lift Operation], [Keeper Manipulation], [Mule Driving], and [Light Step] into a new skill—[Weighty Professions (Journeyman)]. It wasn’t very pure, and there was plenty of fodder in there, so I would keep it near the end.

Next, I combined my combat styles. It may have been wise to keep those for my spear Skill’s later advancements, but the same thing applied with [Snapping Pheasant]—I was using the Art in conjunction with my spear styles. The two were almost inseparable, and I could stand to add some spear Styles in with my gravity Art. It wouldn’t water it down too much, considering how much else relating to gravity there was inside the Skill.

[Needle of Theve], [Rising Leaf Style], [Aurora Wind], [Javelin of the Northern Shore], and [Gladiator’s Certainty].

They combined into a new Skill, [Spear of the Labyrinth (Journeyman)].

I took [Trajectory Anticipation] as the main Skill, then added [Spear of the Labyrinth], [Greater Slinging], [Ash Tilling], and [Weighty Professions]. They created an Advanced-tier knowledge Skill called [Gravity’s Spearhead].

I was specifically leaving aside my memory Skills—I had no idea what to do with those yet, and I didn’t want to merge them until I was ready. As for my Perception Skill, I didn’t want to merge that in just yet, because that would mean I needed to use my Art if I wanted to sense something.

So it left only two remaining fodder Skills from my armour, which I could merge in with the three Advanced-tier Skills if I had to.

After a few days of riding, my new Skills list read:

Native Skills: Soulstealer’s Will (Advanced), Unseen Bolt (Advanced), Art: Mass Hammer (Advanced), Memory Modification (Apprentice), Memory Palace Creation (Apprentice), Memory Palace Sorting (Apprentice), Perception (Apprentice), Snapping Pheasant (Advanced), Gravity’s Spearhead (Advanced)

Armour Skills: Command of Law (Novice), Crane Guidance (Apprentice)

That was a massive jump, but it was also far cleaner, and it meant I had linked all my armour. It was bound to me, now. I had two more Advanced-tier Skills, and I couldn’t wait to test them.

It would have to wait until we reached Steelshore, though.
