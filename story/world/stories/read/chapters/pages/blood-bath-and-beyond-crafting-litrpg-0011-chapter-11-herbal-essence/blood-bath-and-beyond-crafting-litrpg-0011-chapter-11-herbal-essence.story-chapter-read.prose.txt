The three of us continued to watch the forest for a while, but the unicorn did not return, so my attention turned back to my new stony friends. Sliding over the tiled roof, I scooched along until I was sitting right next to the new wistful-faced gargoyle.

“Hello Um. Welcome! I’m glad you came. I can see that you’re hungry. What can I get you to eat? Do you guys only eat eggs?”

Silence. My own stomach rumbled again. Ug I would kill for potatoes. Or pasta. Cheese! I would strangle my own mother right now for some cheesy mash. Maybe I can catch a rabbit with my bare hands? Or my trusty stick? Maybe with [Umbral Cloak]…

There was still no response from the gargoyle. I let out a sigh.

There was a slow grinding rasp as stone moved against stone. A rocky paw unclenched, a twinkle of silver flashed. Something dropped from the hand, and it clattered onto the roof tiles. I grabbed before it could tumble off into the brambles, my hand closing around the hilt. My stiletto!

[Blade of the Dark Enchantress, quality: legendary]

Legendary, ay! Needless to say, I assumed that ‘Legendary’ was good. Or better than good, unlike my poor, probably completely normal and trustworthy, rusted bucket.

“Thank you,” I said to the wistful gargoyle. “That was very thoughtful. Um. If I bring you food, can you help me? I need to go out there sometimes—” I gestured vaguely towards the trees. “And that unicorn - the [Night Mare] or whatever it’s called is a bit of a problem.”

“We need …energy …to move.”

The gargoyle spoke painfully, torturously slow, his voice deep and quiet, but nowhere near as smooth as it had been on the side of the tower. Had the flight exhausted him? Maybe they were ambush predators - like crocodiles, resting on gothic rooftops instead of shallow tropical rivers?

“How do I get you energy? Food? Eggs?”

“Mana. We meditate. Meditation… brings …slow mana… from… all around. Mana here… good. Bring mana. We help.”

“How do I … bring you mana?”

“Mana infused objects …good. Food …okay.”

“Eggsff!” said Eggs, helpfully.

“Any…food… will… do.”

“What about mana crystals?” I asked, suddenly remembering the jewelled stones at the entrance of the dungeon.

“Yes. Good…We …watch. We …wait. You go.”

“Thank you.”

Excellent, I had a plan! I would figure out how to harvest those crystals – there had only been a few, but perhaps there were more in the rest of the dungeon - and then I would have a stone army. Well, the start of a very small army… They would certainly help me in my goal of wholesale unicorn slaughter. A thought occurred to me – I had a large mana pool - could I transfer some of my own to the gargoyles?

“System? Can I give mana to the gargoyles?”

The system ignored me which annoyed me greatly, because I knew it was always listening - just like an invasive smart device just itching to serve evil adverts when I least wanted them.

“Fine, I will figure it out myself.”

Blade in hand, I clambered awkwardly off the roof, checked on the water in the pail. It had fallen off the fire which was why it had still not boiled. I built up a little ring of stones and set it on top, fed the fire some more twigs, and headed down the dungeon.

Alas, even blessed with foreknowledge of the dungeon layout, I was not able to wrench even the smallest crystal free, even with the aid of my legendary dagger. I tugged and pulled and whacked and then had to dive for the portal once more or risk being overwhelmed by damn monster birds.

I did manage to stab a single [Demon Pigeon, lv 5] before I left, which was enough to realise they could be hurt at my current level, but that it would take a few hits to take down even one. Alas, that was as much productivity as the trip entailed.

Did I need special tools to pry the crystals loose? Or just more strength? [Umbral cloak] would not work in the intense light of the portal, and it was not full invisibility anyway. Stamping back up the stairs with the demon door’s mocking laughter ringing in my ears, I did what I always did when things did not go my way: I had a bath. Or the closest thing I could get to it without a bathtub, which was a hot wash with various herbs in the finally boiling bucket of water.

Comfrey, yarrow, elderberry flowers and devil’s flight, all went into steaming pail. All of them had the healing tag. My kingdom for soap, but alas, herby water will have to do for now.

It smelt nice.

As I stripped, I pulled up my crafting tab but it still just said recipes pending.

[Discover recipes through reading or experimentation.]

Okay, fair enough.

I [inspected] my herby water.

[Herbal infusion, edible, healing. Quality: mundane.]

Well, that was encouraging. Edible though? I suppose it was a sort of tea. And edible did not necessarily mean tasty. The healing part was what I needed. I was slightly disappointed that the result was not magical. Still, better than plain water. And my first creation as a newly minted [Witch]!

I stripped naked, squished the remains of the leaves and flowers into a mush, and scrubbed myself down with them, paying particular care to my wounds, washing away the dirt and dried blood. The process hurt, but I sang as I washed, and my mood was so much improved that I felt like I might sprout bluebirds or whatever it was that Disney princesses in the woods sprouted.

Hopefully it was enough to stave off infection. The wound where the shiny knight had sliced off my skin was looking particularly angry, along with the one from the [Demon Pigeon] on the tower wall, which was deep and crusted over. I rubbed a bit of sparkly unicorn dust into it, for good measure. That seemed to sooth the redness.

The act of bathing calmed me, and I was able to consider my situation a little. My weird, bizarre situation. The whole business still felt dream-like. Had I come to terms with the fact that I was probably dead and living in some bizarro afterlife? No, probably not. It was easier just to roll with it.

On the other hand… while I missed the conveniences of modern life, what about the people I had left behind? The people I would never see again? That thought hit funny, making my chest tighten. My hand slipped and I dropped the mushy herbs I had been holding. They fell into the fire with a soggy sizzle.

Ian Fuck-Face, obviously, I wouldn’t miss. Great wrecked you absolute arsehole, I banish you from my head. Alas, he lingered, as not so fragrant arseholes tended to do. Fine, I will mourn the man I had thought he was. Him I would miss. That future… the one I thought I would have. Into the fire with it, and I will start afresh. That felt suitable.

Apart from Ian… well I hadn’t had many friends back on earth. Too busy working, and when I wasn’t working, I was making soap, perfecting my recipes, and researching safety compliance laws. I grinned over the embers, as I fed more twigs into the fire. There were unlikely to be any health and safety laws to comply with here!

“Point to the afterlife,” I said, raising a log in toast.

Then there was Sara, my kind, elderly neighbour, who insisted on bringing me soup whenever I was ill. Sara and her cat, a chonky ginger named General Fuss who liked to sleep in my pot plants and treated our home like an extension of his. I would miss them both. And my pot plants. As for family - my only brother was engrossed in his career in London. I suspected it would take him several years to notice I was gone. My parents were dead, having died in a car crash many years ago.

I sat back on my heels. Was that really it? The sum total of my life? I had some old school friends, we kept in touch with an exchange of memes and one-liners, and a few words on birthdays and Christmas, but seldom met face to face.

The truth was, I had been too busy, pouring my energy into a future that had never arrived. That never would now.

I glanced up at the gargoyles, my eyes weirdly damp. “You guys would come to my funeral, right?”

There was no response.

They would if they had enough mana. Probably.

But that was old me. This was a new chance. So, what did I want this time around?

I wanted a soap shop and some friends. My own money. Control over… everything. New goals for a new life. I was off to a great start already! And I would eviscerate any smooth-talking gamblers who crossed my path, no matter how attractive their forearms or how cute their glasses. For the greater good.

But enough lollygagging, there are things to be done, and clothing to wash!

Leaving my filthy dress next to the fire, I shouted up to the gargoyles: “I’m going outside. Can you let me know if the unicorn approaches. Or anything else dangerous.”

Two barely susceptible nods. I was momentarily self-conscious in front of the stone monsters, but they showed no interest in my lack of clothes whatsoever. Afterall, I suppose they are naked too.

I opened the door, glared at the [Sleepy Briars] and then went back inside. “How will I know if the unicorn approaches? If you can’t move much?”

“We …call,” said Wistful.

“Call?”

Eggs pursed his stone lips and the sound that issued forth was the wailing screech of a dead thing echoing through an eldritch tomb in the far reaches of some half-remembered nightmare realm.

The hairs on the back of my arms rose.

“Right. Good. Excellent, carry on,” I said.

Blade in one hand, I wriggled through the tunnel of briars. As I went, I hacked a more Rachel sized space, took a brief unscheduled nap when I snagged part of my arm by mistake, then emerged on the other side fit and ready to go.

No sign of the unicorn, so I set off, shoulder blades twitching. After filling the rusty pail once more, I left my dress to soak and turned my attention to my next meal - for me and my gargoyles. With the aid of [Forage] I spotted some juicy looking berries. But before I could move to pick more than a handful something caught my eye in the treeline - a magnificent stag, tawny brown and absolutely delicious looking.

My stomach rumbled at the sight of it, but it was too far away to [Curse]. Also it occurred to me that I might not want to eat meat I had [Cursed].

If only I had a bow and arrow and… Hold up. It turned out hunger was a powerful motivator. Casting [Umbral Cloak], I crept after it, naked and crazy.

The stag was looking the other way. Just a little bit closer… I burst from the bushes and attempted to bury my dagger into its neck. I missed as the stag startled, [Cloak] shattering, and the blade gouging its side as it leapt away. I gave chase, but the stag was too swift, galloping away, in a flash of tawny gold.

A mournful wail pierced the air and I froze, wondering for a moment what on earth the horrible noise. I flung myself into a bush just as the unicorn burst out of the undergrowth, screaming its demented half horse half demon scream. It barrelled after the stag, smashing the beast bodily into an oak before casually ripping out its throat with one vicious bite.

I had the presence of mind to cast [Cloak] once more.

The body of the stag dropped to the ground. The unicorn moved out of sight, and I waited, blade clutched in sweaty hands, doing my best not to move. Snorting and stomping, was followed by that now familiar deranged whinny. The unicorn dragged its meal back into view. Blood dripped from the unicorn’s chin as it munched, staring blankly ahead, great jaw masticating mouthfuls of raw meat.

Then it wandered away, meal forgotten, and vanished into the trees.

I breathed out, feeling a little weak, forcing myself to wait until I was sure the unicorn had gone. Here was an opportunity. I ran over to the bloody remains of the stag, grabbed its antlers and heaved. Okay, it was heavier than it looked. Piecemeal then.

[You have acquired the Skill: Butchery! And boy do you need it! This skill aids your knifework in the cooking, and preparation of meat, and the dissection and harvesting of resources (dead or alive!). This is a dexterity based skill.]

The remainder of the job went quicker after that, as I instinctively seemed to know where to cut to avoid hitting bone, and the contents of my stomach settled a bit. After all it was just meat. Yummy, soon to be delicious meat!

Oh. Oh wow, that made me appreciate my regeneration buff. No wonder they were all about conserving energy. I should be able to help them do things faster, just by bringing them things to eat. Hopefully it would be worth it, but I also liked the company.

My culinary skills were not quite up to the job, but I was so hungry that slightly charred, unseasoned meat felt like a feast from the gods. I wondered, briefly, what all the other archetypes were doing, then put the thought aside.

This time I made sure to rinse my ‘salad’, before consuming it, and I washed down the entire meal with fresh spring water and some of the luridly purple berries I had found earlier. They were surprisingly tart, but not unpleasantly so.

It wasn’t much of a meal, but it was the best I’d had since arriving in this crazy world. Hopefully there would be better to come. I fell asleep warm and with a full belly.

Things were looking up.
