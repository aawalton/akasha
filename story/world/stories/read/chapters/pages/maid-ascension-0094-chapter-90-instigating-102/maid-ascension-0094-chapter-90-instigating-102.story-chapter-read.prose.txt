By the time evening arrived, Lira was already lying flat on the roof of a building across from Salvek's establishment.

She had chosen the position carefully. From here, she had a clear view of the brothel's main entrance and, more importantly, the windows of the old knight's office on the lower floor. The angle was far better than the one she had used during her previous reconnaissance. She could see most of the room through the window, including Salvek's chair, his desk, and the shelves behind him.

The establishment itself had not yet opened for the night. It was still too early for guests to begin arriving, and the streets around it were empty and quiet. Salvek, however, was already at work.

He sat behind his desk, reading through a stack of reports.

"Pika. Cipos. Syneth."

Lira activated her Eagle Eyes spell and carefully studied the papers from a distance. Most of them looked like reports from street informants, filled with the sort of information one could expect from such documents. The Ink and Blood Syndicate was one of the premier suppliers of such reports, so Lira was familiar with them, especially since she had started her criminal career transcribing them.

It didn't take long for her plan to begin taking effect.

A man in the official livery of the mail service approached the entrance and knocked.

"Pika. Cipos. Hishar. Kaldris. Velar."

Lira activated her Focused Hearing spell just in time as one of Salvek's guards opened the door for him.

"Letter for Sir Salvek."

"I'll take it," the guard frowned.

"I'm sorry, sir, but I don't see any livery on you. I would prefer to hand it to Sir Salvek himself."

The guard let out a sigh.

"Wait here," he said before disappearing inside.

Lira watched through the window as Salvek was forced to leave his office and come to the doorstep to receive the letter personally. The older knight looked irritated by the interruption, but he took the letter without complaint.

Lira watched as Salvek returned to his office and sat down to examine the letter. The seal was unmarked, and the writing was garnished, almost girlish, but not quite. She could read the letter from her hidden spot thanks to her Eagle Eyes spell.

To Whom It May Concern,

Over the coming month, the capital will host six debut balls at which young ladies of distinguished birth and family will be formally presented to society.

Lady Darcia Corvana

Lady Paige Solairis

Lady Ruby Maxwell

Lady Tristana Cooper

Lady Kiressa Evergreen

Lady Luxia Valis

The names above represent the most prominent debutantes of the season, each possessing beauty, lineage, and connections worthy of the attention of any gentleman seeking a suitable match.

It would be a shame for a man of discernment to overlook such opportunities.

The letter was unsigned, making it sound like an anonymous piece of advice that a busybody might send. It didn't matter that these girls were between seventeen and nineteen, while Salvek was in his fifties. He was still considered a wealthy bachelor, and half the nobility wouldn't mind marrying their daughters to him.

Lira felt a bit guilty involving them. And yes, it was Lira who had involved them after all. She was the one who had written the letter. She had mimicked Dalwin's style, not perfectly, mind you. She needed to leave room for plausible deniability. She had also paid one of her former criminal contacts to acquire a footman's uniform and post the letter using express mail, creating another layer of anonymity just to be safe.

Salvek read the letter, frowned slightly at the lack of a signature, and then placed it on his desk.

Lira waited with anticipation and couldn't help but smile when another messenger in the official mail livery arrived a minute later. This time, he was carrying a bottle of wine in a bucket of ice. Lira could see the faint trail of mana disturbance coming from a rune carved into the metal.

The guard opened the door and immediately frowned at the second unexpected delivery.

"A bottle for Sir Salvek."

"From whom?"

"They didn't say," the messenger said with a smile. "But they left a note." He reached into his pocket and pulled out a folded piece of paper.

"You can hand them over."

"I'm afraid I can't do that."

The guard sighed even more deeply.

"Wait here."

Lira watched as the guard had to summon Salvek once more. Two unexpected deliveries arriving within minutes of each other had clearly begun to annoy him, but then he paused when he recognized the vintage. After all, Lira had done her research and picked his favorite.

The messenger left with the empty bucket in hand, and Salvek returned to his office with the bottle and the letter.

He examined the letter. It contained only a vague note.

You always know how to pick them. Thank you

Through her remote ear, she heard him remove the cork before speaking.

"Ladar. Shontan. Maha."

Lira frowned. She didn't know this spell. Well, she knew Ladar was divination, and Maha was water, but she had never encountered Shontan before.

"Pika. Moren. Kaldris."

The memory of reading her father's ledger inundated her mind. Then a single memory came to the forefront. When she had translated Kalaba as Life, just beneath it had been Shontan, meaning Death.

Divination. Death. Water.

A poison detection spell.

Interesting. So Salvek did know this spell. She had figured as much. This was why she had not poisoned the wine.

From what she had overheard during Estophon's conversation with Kavard, she had already assumed that Salvek would possess some method of detecting poison, especially since even a child like Lyth was taught the spell.

Salvek examined the wine, making sure everything was in order before pouring himself a glass.

She watched carefully as Salvek took a sip.

Then another.

Lira waited for several minutes, watching him drink and read his reports until the glass was empty. He looked at his cup, hesitated, then poured himself another.

This time, he only filled it halfway.

Lira's eyes narrowed. It would have been easier if the man had overindulged in his favorite drink, but sadly, he had to be reasonable.

She was going to have to go with Plan B.

"Pika. Asatan. Frushum."

A small, cone-shaped piece of paper shifted beside her.

It rose into the air, and Lira guided it carefully as it crossed the space between the buildings before lowering it toward the open window of Salvek's office. The spell was not particularly difficult to manipulate, but the distance made it awkward. She had to keep the paper upright to avoid spilling its contents.

Once it reached Salvek's cup, she tipped the cone. A small amount of Sleeping Princess mixed into his drink.

Lira watched, her heart thumping, as Salvek continued drinking. She was afraid that he might have left the poison detection spell active throughout the evening, but it appeared he hadn't. She understood why. It wasn't a Paldina passive spell, so keeping it active would take up too much cognitive space.

The effect was not immediate. That was one of the reasons she had chosen the substance. Sleeping Princess did not simply knock someone unconscious the moment they drank it. Instead, it gradually made the victim tired and left them with a throbbing headache reminiscent of a hangover when they woke up.

Salvek finished his drink.

Then he leaned back in his chair.

Lira waited as his eyes grew heavier. He shook his head and rubbed his face before standing up.

Lira tensed. For one terrible moment, she thought the poison had failed.

But then Salvek collapsed back into his chair.

Lira waited several more minutes, focusing her magical ear on him. He was breathing slowly and steadily.

She moved her magical ear to check on the guards. One was near the front door, while another was watching over the girls upstairs.

None of them were near the office.

Lira waited another minute, making sure they weren't moving before she stood.

Her Body Obfuscation spell was already active, but the evening light made concealment more difficult than it would have been at night. She descended from her hiding spot using the paper rope to climb down.

"Pika. Asatan. Frushum."

She kept low as she crossed the road, then used the paper rope again to climb to Salvek's office through the open window.

Salvek was still slumped in his chair.

Lira approached the desk and picked up the bottle of wine using a gloved hand.

"Pika. Maha. Frushum."

Lira carefully guided the remaining wine with her magic, moving it through the air in a thin stream before dumping it into one of the rain drains at the side of the street.

Once the bottle was empty, Lira approached the desk and searched the drawers for more alcohol. As expected, she found several bottles of stronger spirits.

She selected one, opened it, and poured a small amount into Salvek's palm. She spilled some more onto his chest before pouring the rest in a similar manner as the wine.

Finally, she washed the wine glass with water, then filled it with more alcohol to remove all traces of the Sleeping Princess draught.

From an outside observer's perspective, it would look like Salvek had indulged in his favorite wine, and when he ran out, he had gone through his stash of stronger spirits. After all, hadn't he been through a few ordeals lately? Losing his job at the palace and all.

Who could blame him?

She reached into her satchel and removed six letters.

But drunkenly writing offensive letters? Well, that, on the other hand, was grounds for an honor duel.

Being a Master Forger came with a few advantages. Any competent scribe could mimic a person's handwriting, but adding a drunken effect to a letter was more challenging. She made it shaky and missed punctuation, made the writing unable to follow a straight line, and even added drops of alcohol for the smell.

She then added wax to the envelopes and pressed Salvek's personal seal into each one.

Now came the tough part: convincing one of Salvek's guards to take them to the mail service.

She first checked with her remote ear to make sure both guards were still away.

Then she opened the office door and placed the letters on a small table outside, waiting for them to be discovered by the first person who approached Salvek's office.

Lira returned to the office and reached into her satchel one final time.

She picked up a blank piece of paper and an ink bottle, then poured some of the ink onto the paper.

"Asatan. Tir. Kisar."

The ink moved and transformed into a portrait of Salvek. Lira was a passable artist, especially when it came to ink, so her visualization was impeccable. She then added a bubble of Hishar runes near the drawing's mouth before willing writing in Salvek's handwriting to appear, adding another layer of symbolic efficiency.

"Kisar. Kalaba. Hishar."

The runes activated, and the paper spoke.

"Dario, take those letters to the mail. Fast, before they close."

The voice was monotone but unmistakably Salvek's.

Lira hid behind the door with bated breath, waiting for the guard's reaction. She heard footsteps coming closer.

A minute later, the paper spoke again, repeating the words in the exact same tone and cadence.

The sudden voice startled Lira. She quickly smudged the Hishar runes, deactivating the spell.

"I heard you the first time, boss," said Dario, the guard.

She heard the shuffling of paper, followed by footsteps moving away. Then the outside door opened and closed.

Lira headed to the window and waited until Dario was out of sight before stepping out into the street.

The sun had not yet set completely as she headed back to the castle. Once she reached Market Street, she deactivated all her spells in an alley before stepping back into the crowd, buying a few sweets while heading home.

By the time she reached the palace gates, the sun had finally disappeared beyond the horizon.

One of the guards was already preparing to close the entrance.

"You're cutting it close," he called.

"Sorry." Lira gave him a tired smile. "Want some candy?"

She gestured with the open paper bag.

He narrowed his eyes, then picked up a piece of caramelized fruit and tossed it into his mouth before the gate closed behind her.

She continued walking toward the servants' quarters, a bag of candy in hand, the cheerful little maid with a sweet tooth.

Behind her, in a building somewhere across the city, Sir Salvek slept peacefully, unaware of the storm he was about to face.
