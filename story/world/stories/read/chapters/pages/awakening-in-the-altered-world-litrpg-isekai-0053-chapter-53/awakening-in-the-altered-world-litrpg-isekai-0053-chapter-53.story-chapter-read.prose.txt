How will I want to live this life?

Because if Stella and Matt are as rich as they’re saying…

All my challenges can just disappear.

Visions started to swim before Alex’s eyes.

Alex’s items were decent, especially his sword. But this was still a starter set; he hadn’t even asked to see the higher quality F-rank pendants, or rings, or chestpieces. What if I could get an instance item, even, like a few of these top-end Guild talents? Maybe a pendant, or a fancier sword?

He was mentally salivating a little at the idea.

Except he didn’t say yes right away, because of that very question.

I also don’t want to be given everything.

Will that give me an interesting life?

He was thinking, again, about the idea that it’d feel like cheat codes; life wasn’t a game, but in this respect, might it feel similar?

I really do want to deserve what I get. And he was serious about the idea; it was why he’d wanted to split his treasure 50/50 with Beckett.

But plenty of people take insane investment, which they’ll pay back later. Why is this any different?

It isn’t.

“I probably will,” Alex decided. “Give me a week or two to think about it. And—thanks, guys, really.”

“Of course,” said Stella.

“I want a little time to make sure I’ve explored all my options, first,” he explained. “It probably will be necessary. And I’m not married to going my own way, like I won’t torpedo my own Awakened career because—yeah. You guys know sometimes I get ideas in my head. This isn’t one of those times, I promise.”

“You don’t need to explain yourself to us, I get it,” said his uncle. “You should get a sense of all your choices before you decide. And we’ll always be here. Alex, it’s like Stella says.”

Matt seemed a bit amused. “It’s like Bella, and, well, you—you’re trying to get places as fast as you can. It’s okay to breathe a little at times. But you won’t like hearing that. Your sister certainly didn’t.”

“I appreciate it,” said Alex genuinely.

Alex grinned. “And maybe one of these days, we’ll go see a Mets game. Is that still a thing?”

“What’s a Met?” said Matt, seeming bemused.

Alex paused; Stella glared. “Matt!”

Matt grinned sheepishly—“Sorry. Shouldn’t joke about potentially, ah, dissonant topics. Right.”

“It’s all good,” said Alex, a bit relieved.

“They are still a thing, actually. We used to go to games,” Matt continued, scratching his chin. “But it’s a lot less big than the World Cups. Awakened sports have sucked the oxygen out of the room for everything else. v-Dueling’s by far the biggest sport in the world—I’m sure you’ve seen the kinds of megacelebrities it’s created.”

“I have, yeah. It does kind of feel like the sports culture of my—of the other world, on steroids,” Alex reflected. And to an extent, the entertainment culture, anyway.

“It’s certainly the biggest, and the flashiest, and what everyone talks about,” said Matt, smiling a bit. “Personally, my feeling is, it’s nice to remind yourself sometimes it isn’t everything. And these choices you were considering, was one of them Skillscribing?”

“Well, yeah,” said Alex, who wasn’t surprised he’d guessed. “I feel like it suits my talents.”

They talked over it a bit, and the two of them also seemed to believe it was quite promising as an idea.

“The problem is, you’ll be an apprentice starting out, like everyone is,” said Stella, lips pursed. “It might be a little before you’re earning real money. Skillscribing’s one of the highest earning Professions—trust us, the two of us have relationships with a few Scribes for our enchants, and we pay outrageous fees. But your earnings tend to scale with rank, and training you the first few years is expensive. People can make real money starting out, but it’s very rare. You’d have to be in that top sliver.”

“Ah,” said Alex, feeling a little disappointed, until—

“I also think it suits you,” said Stella, glancing at him. “Maybe more than you’d think. And I’m not saying this because of your talents.”

“How so?”

“Alex, baby—you’re one of the most inquisitive people I’ve ever met,” she said. “Ever since you were young—especially when you’re interested in a subject, it’s like it becomes your whole world.”

She seemed to be thinking about a particular memory; she was smiling a bit as she turned to face him.

“I’ve never seen you as interested in anything, as you are in magic,” she said simply. “We’re just talking, and it’s so clear—Alex, you just light up. It’s—well, it’s wonderful to see.”

She said it kind of teasingly, and to be fair, Alex did blush a bit. “Okay, I get it.”

In the rest of their time, Alex showed Matt his pendant, and his godparents caught him up a bit on their lives, and Bella’s life.

“I have piles and piles of defect gemstones, just sitting in storage. If this Skillscribe thing works out and you need cheap practice materials…” His uncle winked. “Let me know.”

“And visit if you find the time!” urged Stella. “It’s like it was before, you’re always welcome. And there's so much to see in the City. Especially if you’re an Awakened.”

It felt like whatever distance between them, if it hadn’t fully melted away, had at least reduced quite a bit; it felt like he was, well, seeing his family. And there were a few awkwardnesses, and it’d probably take a while before he felt like he fully knew this world’s version of them, and vice versa.

Around 4PM, the two of them were forced to return to the big city. Stella needed to prep for the auction of her staff at Exeter’s tomorrow; her lot was sandwiched between two unique pills concocted by master Alchemists. The two left him with a few final reminders; pills and elixirs were able to grant permanent stat boosts, up to a certain cap at every rank.

“So look into that,” Stella said sternly. “And I’d get your gear enchanted whenever you can, at least with Self-Repair.”

“I’ve kind of been wondering,” said Alex. “Should I try to go to New York City, after all this? Permanently, I mean?”

His godparents exchanged a glance. “You’ll probably have to, in time, once you outgrow what Stark can offer,” said Matt. “But New York’s a Tier 1 city—it’s so full of talents. I kind of like the setup you have right now. You’re still this growing fish, in this mid-sized, regional pond. Getting your bearings, seeing if you can ‘max it out,’ so to speak, figuring out the kind of life you want, away from this world of crazy talents—and then engaging with it, on your terms, seems perfectly valid to me. And besides, with this Cup this weekend… you’ll be getting plenty of contact with talents from that world anyway.”

“And give us a call if Bella’s out, and you want to meet her!” said Stella quickly; she and Matt exchanged another glance. “She’s still Bella.”

“Guys, it’s not like she’s going to kill me,” joked Alex.

There was a pause.

“She won’t,” Stella confirmed, seemingly trying to convince him, but she sounded a little less convinced than Alex would’ve liked.

“Take her to dinner, talk to her. It’ll be fine,” said Matt easily, which was also not quite what Alex wanted to hear.

Still, overall, he left this first meeting with his godparents feeling a little emotional; he felt like, at least with them, things might really be fine.

In a new, weird world, it was nice to feel like he had people who genuinely cared.

***

A few hours later and Alex’s final preparations were underway. Rising Stars started tomorrow at 9 o’clock sharp; he’d have to sleep early for this one.

He wiped a little sweat off his face, drank from a simulated water bottle. A little more research via searching on his phone, and he’d realized the treasure awarded was as much the prize as the actual cash; place on the podium, and you’d get your pick out of a pool of talents prizes selected by the organizers, US Leagues, from their Vaults. In previous Rising Stars Cups, this included rare elixirs, treasures, potentially even Skill Shards.

Alex picked up his blade, and re-entered the field. But it isn’t even about that for certain talents.

Like the elite newbies, that’ve come over from the city… they just care about getting a podium place, so they’re prequalified for Duelist’s Cup Stark City.

Alex engaged the training bot again. He was taking it light, working his Mana Manipulations, and also experimenting a bit with new properties—seeing if he could create a mana-reflective surface on his own. Which was, it seemed, even harder than trying to generalize shatter resistance   to all his Pure mana constructs.

He also v-Dueled twice, facing Level 6’s and 7’s; the fights, with his new gear and rift breakthroughs, still weren’t very difficult, which was reason for optimism.

It was 9:49pm, and it seemed like the Training Hall was clearing out a bit.

Once more it was him and a few others in the hall. Including the station with the privacy screen, which seemed to be featuring a party today, by its silhouettes; still very intense Dueling.

He was back on the bench in the walkway, wiping off sweat, when the privacy screen opened, to Alex’s surprise.

A girl holding twin swords stepped out, wearing a Guild bodysuit with a forked crest, a symbol Alex didn’t recognize. She didn’t glance anywhere. She simply sat at the bench in her walkway and began drinking a rather potent mana potion. She was the only one who exited the privacy screen, and it seemed like there was no-one else in there, despite the person-shaped silhouettes he’d seen.

Focus on yourself, Alex reminded himself.

A half hour went by, and more people left; Alex faced another two Duels. Last two. His rating was well into the 700’s, which he gathered was nearing the top 10%-ish for F-rank; it was increasing much slower with every win, but the wins also required him to get a little serious.

Which feels like a sign to call it a day.

In the final half hour, the two of them were the only people left in the hall, in stations a few stations diagonally across, facing their own stations. It seems like we’re always the last two to leave.

The two of them finished up at about the same time, and left about thirty seconds apart.

***

He was at the Lake District Training Center an hour early, a certain card in hand—a golden, metallic card etched with lines of mana, which he’d received in the mail.

He walked into his pre-reserved room, feeling a little antsy, but also excited. For a while he’d been unable to fall asleep last night, his mind racing—he’d slept 6 hours, but he still felt pretty clear-minded.

He slotted the gold card into the side of the pod. A voice said, Access Token — ACCEPTED. Rising Stars Cup.

Warning: Once you enter this v-space, you cannot leave until this event has concluded. This event is scheduled to last 9 hours.

He used the restroom and made sure he was well-hydrated. Alex entered his pod, as usual, but when he opened his eyes he didn’t wake up in the TC Training Hall.

He saw a vast hall with walls branded with golden US Leagues logos.

Alex also saw a crowd of what he knew were 128 talents from all over the region, mostly grouped by Guild bodysuits, as well as people who seemed like advisors, coaches, and whoever else; the hall was filled with voices.

Nine hours for all the people in this hall, and also certain, more influential people watching in the observation decks behind tinted glass, to see who the brightest new talents of the season truly were.

The Summer Rising Stars Cup commenced.
