Henry was completely stunned for a moment. Mostly because of the transmission he had picked up on, and not because the Merfolk’s tail had just smacked him against the rock hard enough that his inner ear lost all sense of direction, filling the cave once more with dust and rocks for a beat before Henry flew out of the smokescreen. That nauseating sensation thankfully lasted for only a second, as it was well within what his healer-Octomind could handle.

Nowadays, and especially in a situation as tense as this, delegating was key, and it allowed him to focus on what was truly important.

That had been Velistraine. She was alive. She hadn’t sounded too great there, but then again, Henry’s senses were extremely dulled by the distance. He had barely connected to that clone, and the link was both extremely weak and unstable. But he hadn’t imagined it, and immediately, he put two more Octominds on trying to maintain that thread connecting them. He didn’t know how to go about that exactly, but he was willing to burn most of his remaining reserves to reinforce whatever was left of that bridge. Henry would have loved to focus on the problem himself. Unfortunately, someone wasn’t enjoying being ignored.

“You. Will. Pay attention to me,” the Merfolk gritted between his sharp, bared teeth as he punctuated every word with a punch or a tail-swipe while continuously Blinking on top of Henry, each impacting like a tiny explosion that made Henry’s veins burst and his soul tingle. He had a suspicion those hits would have been a lot worse if he didn’t have Depth-Tempered Soul, but as things were, Henry had enough stored healing through Lifeblood Reserves that he and his healer-Octomind could shake off the damage easily enough. Not to mention, he was still sitting on a couple dozen Lifeblood Orbs, so he could continue his escape, if he could even call it that, and weather the storm as it were, while focusing on what was important.

“I’m talking to you.”

Henry clicked his tongue in annoyance, and right as the Merfolk appeared just ahead to deliver yet another kick, Henry changed gears from running to getting a good, juicy hit in. Pulling his arm back, Henry burned some Predation and whipped his arm out as hard as he could, putting his weight behind it and smacking the little wannabe-tyrant. The Merfolk raised his guard in time, but the impact was still loud and powerful enough that some of the more fragile rocks around them shuddered and broke from the shockwave, not to mention the few larger ones that were reduced to gravel from the Merfolk’s impact against the wall.

It felt good to shut him up for a second.

With that, Henry Blinked away and continued his run, enjoying the respite for a mere couple seconds. He even picked up on the scent of decaying leaves and wilderness, so he hoped he was getting close and indeed, barely a couple more seconds later, the tunnel he’d been following opened up in a wide, open space full of naturally-forming stone pillars, and in some spots, the cave had collapsed, showing the canopy of trees beyond.

He had reached the surface, and in any other scenario, the sight would have flooded him with relief, but he was still trying to parse the garbled knot of thoughts and impressions slowly leaking back toward him from the Velistraine Avatars. It was like staring at a painting in the dark, with a flashing light occasionally illuminating a tiny section of it, just enough to blind him with jumbled colors before it went dark again. The result was disorienting, nauseating, but he couldn’t stop. He didn’t know exactly why he was sure, but he had a feeling Velistraine wasn’t doing so good. Maybe it was something the packets of corrupted memories were telling him, or maybe it was the fatalistic mix of exhaustion and tired playfulness he’d heard in her voice, but he couldn’t risk it. If things were bad, he had to do something. And for that, he needed to reinforce the connection to either bring her back or go there himself.

Water exploded behind him, a geyser of it bursting up from the tunnel he’d just left and carried the yellow-scaled Merfolk along with it. It rained down on the A-rank’s scales, while a platform of swirling water carried him slowly down and while the entrance was cool and all, it made Henry frown as he eyed the ‘platform’ the A-rank was standing on. It was barely an inch of water. How was it carrying the weight?

Henry would consider that eventually, but not right now. In fact, that wandering thought had cost him nearly half a second, and as he exploded up toward the sky, he came to regret it, as the water that had geysered behind him was now collecting above him, cutting him off.

Then his Octomind sounded the alarm, and Henry Blinked out of another attempt at restraining him, only for his sense of danger to blare in his ears that he’d misstepped.

Oh right. The water, he realized, turning his attention back to the roiling sphere floating between him just in time to see the portion facing him ripple for the briefest moment before a lance of water impaled him through the head. Well. Part of his head. It must have missed his brain, but he could certainly feel the cold seeping into his guts as parts of his entrails got damaged.

That packed more punch than he had expected. So far, he’d been tanking the hits well enough thanks to his stats, but this sphere was trouble. Henry wasn’t sure if Blinking past it would gain him enough distance to be out of its range, though before he could come to a decision, something new happened. Something unexpected.

A glowing pickaxe flew out of the darkness and hit the sphere of roiling water. There was a metallic clink such as one might hear in a giant’s forge, and before he could make out what had happened, the water splashed down all over the cave’s floor, as if the mining tool had found the nugget of magic controlling it and just… broke it. The pickaxe hovered there for a moment, then flew back into the grasp of a thick, bulging arm, and out of the corner of his eyes, a group of humans showed up.

“Oh great. The mole is here,” the Merfolk muttered, his glowing green eyes narrowing as he stared at the newcomers before turning back to Henry and lifting a finger. “Time to wrap this up. Working together would have been nice, but it doesn’t seem like you’ll be that good of a fit after all. In such case, good bye. Be better in your next life.”

A black bar, sheathed in impossible silver light illuminated the cave and tore through the distance separating them instantly. The colors of the world drained away for a breath, and while Henry had reacted instantly, attempting to throw himself out of the way, he was still not fast enough. Henry expected a burn or an impact, but he only felt a sharp, deep pain for a brief moment before it turned to numbness, and as he came to a stop, a few yards closer to the newcomers, he anxiously patted himself with his many arms as he rushed some Lifeweaving essence to the spot in question, but after a few seconds of frantic searching, he found nothing. There was only a familiar, aching pain, a soreness in his soul, and while he could tell it was trying to eat at the edges and spread, it wasn’t managing it, and glancing up, Henry found the Merfolk frowning at him, his hand still raised.

“This the one?”

Henry looked back toward the voice and found the pickaxe wielder, a bearded, stocky man looking at him from under his brows and behind him, the familiar face of Omar and the other scout peered up at him, both of whom were nodding.

The man humphed, then his gaze slowly swiveled to the Merfolk. “If the Scalies wants him dead, then I guess we’d want to prevent that.”

The human was speaking low enough, but somehow, his voice still echoed through the cavern, which made the Merfolk’s eyes narrow dangerously as a trident appeared in his hand.

“Go back to your mountain, human. This doesn’t concern you.”

The man raised a brow, then with a movement of his wrist, he handed his pickaxe to someone in hunter gear then slowly, he began rolling the sleeves of his quilted tunic. “I don’t take orders from trouts. Go back to your pond. Now. We’ll take it from here,” the man gruffly said, then took his pickaxe back as he rolled a shoulder.

Henry suppressed a smile, floating in place, lest he attracted either party’s attention. If these two started fighting, he could slip away easily enough. Right now, he’d love to keep them both at each other’s throat while he tried to make sense of what he was getting from his Octominds. Thankfully, whatever the Merfolk was using to cut him off from his Avatars was still helping. Without the distraction of his nearby clones, it was easier to sense the ones with Velistraine. Henry wasn’t sure why he managed to peer through the veil, but he couldn’t complain. Maybe it was because of his very fresh ascent to A-rank, or maybe it was the fact that he’d been cut off from all of the other clones, and somehow, peering through the barrier surrounding this world was easier than feeling anything through the skill of a level 70 A-rank.

Still, even though the bridge between him and that faraway clone was somehow still active, Henry found it difficult to speak through it. He found it difficult to interact with it at all. Though his presence must have done something to the clone as all of a sudden, he felt Velistraine’s voice reach through, brimming with hope.

“[Henry? Is that you?]” she said, then, as if to herself, she continued. “[Am I imagining this?]”

Velistraine was quiet for a moment while the two parties continued exchanging barbs. As for Henry, he was trying to take control of his Avatar, to see through its eyes, but it wasn’t working.

“[Hah… my mind’s going. Right when I thought I was making peace with this…]”

Henry paused. He really didn’t like the tone of her voice. It was ringing many alarm bells in the back of his mind.

“I will see him dead before I allow you to use him,” the Merfolk said, pulling Henry’s attention back for a moment, only for the pickaxe-wielder to snort. “We’ll see about that. You. Kraken. How about it? Want some help?”

“I’m guessing help doesn’t come for free?”

The pickaxe wielder’s eyes glinted, and Henry would bet he was smiling under that thick beard of his. “No such thing as free help.”

Henry eyed him for a bit, then nodded as he reached his decision. Popping two Lifeblood orbs in his beak, he felt the mana and lifeblood flood painfully into his system.

“You don’t seem as bad as that asshole. But I’ll pass on both of you for now.”

And with that, Henry shoved all the essence that remained in Unfettered Echoes and poured all of it, along with most of his mana into the complex set of skills that made his new Avatars. The bits that created the Avatar and balanced their nature. The bit that handled their network and their nature as a relay. The bit that allowed him to sense, perceive, and take control of them. Henry wasn’t sure it’d work, but he just gave the bridge and his Avatar all the fuel they might need, and it was clearly doing something because as the Merfolk Blinked on top of him and bailed as a pickaxe flew at his head, Velistraine’s voice reached through the reinforced bridge.

“[Henry? What are you doing? Don’t! Don’t come here—]”

Henry dodged out of the way from a flying dart, then glared at the hunter who shot it. Velistraine’s voice cut off, but he didn’t stop pouring his mana into the Avatar-switching instance. It was certainly taking a lot more mana than usual, but if it worked, this should take him out of the world, which made it… understandable. It made sense that this would be the most expensive teleport he’d ever channeled, and Henry had to admit, it was exciting. As for what Velistraine was saying, he wasn’t going to let her die out there alone, no matter what she said, and he still had hundreds of clones in this world. If there was anyone who could find his way back, it’d be him. He might just need a few more levels, and it might be rougher than he expected, but if he let Velistraine die alone out there, he wouldn’t be able to live with himself.

“Things bigger than either of you are happening,” Henry shot to the pickaxe guy as the latter lunged and reached with a hand to grab a hold of him, only for Henry to slip away with a Blink and a Dash. “But… we’ll continue this talk at another time. At a time where everyone would need to be a little more polite,” he said, sensing that his teleport was ready to trigger. It had taken a pretty scary chunk of mana, which was also empowered by Unfettered Echoes, and just in case, Henry popped another Lifeblood Orb in his beak, just so he was ready for what was to come. “And on that note, I’ll say goodbye to you all, and I’ll see you very soon.”

Henry activated the skill, giving the Merfolk a cold smile as he bypassed the ability that had been restraining him and then, he was gone.
