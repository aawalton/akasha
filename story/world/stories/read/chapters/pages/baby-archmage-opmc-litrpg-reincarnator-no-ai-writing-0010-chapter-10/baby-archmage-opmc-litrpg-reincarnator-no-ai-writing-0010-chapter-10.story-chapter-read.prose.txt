The next day we went to see Piraka. Mother had chided father for blabbing to Marek. He claimed to have been overtaken by fatherly pride and regretted nothing. Honestly, it wasn’t that big a deal, since the second we made my abilities known to Piraka, rumors would spread. Overall the village was a wholesome small community where people stuck together and skill was celebrated, so I wasn’t worried. There was also almost no travel, so it was unlikely news of my weird abilities would spread. Our family had agreed to absolutely keep it a secret that I had a Mythic Skill though. Apparently that was fable-level stuff.

Danek smiled widely at me when we entered the elder’s hut. I had learned to read fluently with him, and write well enough. He had been patient with me and become a good friend during our morning studies. I would have to move on, and I hoped he wouldn’t be too upset.

“Ah, you are early today, Ramona, Luka,” the elder said and turned from his workbench.

“Yes,” mother said. “About that…”

“Hm?”

“About— about Luka’s apprenticeship.”

“Oh?” the elder asked and laughed. “Ramona, the boy is sharp, there is no doubt. But when I said later, I meant in a few years.”

Mother was fidgeting and not sure how to proceed. I decided to take over the conversation. After all, this was my plan.

“Elder, I would ask you to reconsider,” I said. “I think I can prove to you that I’ll be an excellent apprentice.”

An eight-year-old girl peeked from another room. Nirama scowled at me. “Master! You don’t need another apprentice. Look at him. He is still a baby! I already have level 13 in Medicine! I can do whatever he can and better.”

“Hush, now,” Elder Piraka said. Then he turned to me. “Why should I take you, Luka? Being able to read and write is impressive for your age, but not enough. How do you intend to prove it?”

I needed to navigate this carefully. Elder Piraka wasn’t a prideful man, but saying he had no idea what he was doing with his potions was probably a bad idea.

“I’ve been watching you infuse the tonics,” I said. “I think I could help you.”

“Hmph!” Nirama said. “I am already helping him. I crush the herbs and clean the equipment. I can do it better than you.”

“I don’t mean that,” I said. “I meant using mana.”

“Liar,” Nirama said.

Elder Piraka raised his eyebrows, and then looked at Ramona.

“Ramona, what is this?”

My mother smiled. I was glad she had composed herself. “He is a talented boy. You should let him try.”

“What Skills do you have, Luka?”

I shook my head. The Elder scowled and turned to my mother.

Mother just shrugged. “It is better to not spread any rumors.”

The elder’s scowl deepened. “You do not trust me?”

I nudged my head at the girl. “I don’t trust her.”

“Stupid baby!” she yelled and stuck her tongue at me.

The elder’s face lightened and he nodded. “Don’t you think I should know, Ramona? You are asking me to take him as an apprentice after all.”

“I was hoping for some specific arrangements regarding that.”

Mother raised an eyebrow at me. She hadn’t heard me articulate like this, but this situation seemed to call for it.

“Oh, you are dictating terms now?” the elder said and chuckled good naturedly. “Even bolder than your parents, little Luka.”

“They’ve taught me well,” I said.

“He has… a fortunate set of Skills,” Ramona said. “Elder, he can cast magic. I am sure he can help with your potions.”

“Hmmm,” the elder said, stroking his beard. “I don’t know…”

“Jonaka will of course use the proper amount of quotas if you teach him,” mother said.

“Do you know how many quotas worth this refined mana costs?” the elder said and patted his pouch.

Ramona hesitated and in that space, to everyone’s surprise, it was Danek who spoke.

“I will cover the cost if Luka fails.”

Everyone turned their heads. I looked at him as astonished as the rest. Where was this coming from?

Konako laughed. “Making a bet? I didn’t expect such boldness from you. That makes me happy! What is your new Skill telling you?”

I wondered what kind of a Skill he had. Did he know something? That shouldn’t be possible. I hadn’t cast any magic during our studies together. Did he have a Skill that let him learn things about others? Or a Skill that gave him some kinds of intuitions? Either way that sounded powerful.

“This is all very strange…” the elder muttered.

“I will guarantee my son’s bet, father!” Konako said and slapped his chest.

I looked at the elder who relented, and then turned to Danek and his father and bowed deeply. “Thank you!”

Konako waved his hand. “I am happy to see you two friendly. And more than happy about my son expressing his bravery in his own way!”

“Very well,” Piraka said. “Are you ready?”

The elder turned to rummage through a storage cabinet and came back with a glass vial, and handed me his strange silk bag containing the mana. I now got a better look at it.

It was made of some expensive-looking purple cloth, and it even had golden embroidery. It was certainly not made on this island, but was of a fancier make. The fabric was a smooth, well-woven cloth, resembling silk, which was a stark difference to the rough hemp fibers mostly used to make fabrics.

The liquid mana sloshed inside and felt cool. I idly wondered why the mana didn’t seep out of the bag. Maybe the bag was enchanted? I activated my Mana Sight and saw that the bag was brimming with mana. The particles were clumped together and bound into a soup. I couldn’t make out any enchantments with the bag itself, but then again I wouldn’t even know what to look for.

I just stared at the bag’s contents. So this was refined mana. Why was it taking a liquid form? Could I make this from the particles in the air? Piraka thought this was scarce and expensive. I didn’t see any fundamental difference between the mana that was floating everywhere for free.

“Well?” Piraka asked.

“This is stupid!” Nirama said. “How would he know how to use mana, when even I don’t know how to do it yet.”

I took the glass vial with the bright green liquid inside. An uninfused energy tonic. This was the moment I had been waiting for and I savored it. This would be the first time I actually applied my new abilities for something useful. I mean, granted I was doing this to advance my own interests, but boy would Piraka like me in a moment. I was going to save him so much of his precious liquid mana.

I closed my eyes and used Mana Sight. It took some work to untangle individual particles from the liquid. It was viscous and it resisted the tugs of my will. But fortunately I was at a high enough level. First I took out a single particle and started transferring it. It would have actually been easier to just use the atmospheric mana that was literally next to the vial, but this was a learning opportunity to work with this strange mana fluid.

“How much mana should I use, master?” I asked.

The girl growled. “What master? He is not your master! He is my master!”

The elder chuckled. “The liquid should change from light green to forest green. The darker green it turns, the more mana is active within.”

“Why does the liquid change color?”

“It is an alchemical reaction,” the Elder explained. “This is an energy potion. Fishers and Junglers use it for their work. It works perfectly well as is, but some of the reagents don’t reach their full potency unless infused with active mana. Do you understand what I’m saying?”

“No,” the apprentice girl said and took out a pencil. “Say it again.”

“Yes,” I said and continued to untangle the particles from the liquid. I wanted to transfer it in bigger chunks. I just needed to make sure I didn’t overdo it and cause the mana to destabilize.

“Little gem, can you do it?” mother asked me.

I just nodded and kept working.

One by one I was removing individual mana particles from the liquid mass. The mana liquid must have had a total of three or four hundred particles. That was valuable? I burned through thousands of particles in my training. But apparently using the ambient mana was exclusive to my Skills. If that was true, my Mythic Skill was more valuable than I had even thought.

Now, after having worked with the refined mana for a bit, there was absolutely nothing special about the mana particles in the liquid. They were identical to the floating ones.

I didn’t need to do the extraction one by one, but I wanted to because of how interesting working with the mana liquid was. I wanted the experience of it.

“Nothing is happening. He must have failed and wasted all of that expensive mana,” Nirama said and sneered at me.

I gave her the side-eye, and collected a superparticle from the bag. Then I made it into a string of mana and flooded the potion with it. In addition, I weaved in particles that were orbiting near the vial, saving Piraka’s precious mana slosh. I did this as fast as I could for full dramatic effect.

Within a few breaths, the tonic changed color.

Everyone in the room gasped.

The potion had turned into a vibrant forest green.

“What!?” Nirama screeched.

“How did you…”

“I’m so proud of you,” mother said and smiled brightly.

“I’m not done,” I said.

I slowed down and worked meticulously, taking half the mana from Piraka’s bag and half from the air.

This was where he usually stopped. Adding more mana became wasteful when he couldn’t see where it needed to go.

But I had Mana Sight.

Tiny bits of the potion’s ingredients floated in the liquid. Some were already full of mana. Others could take more. I fed those until they stopped accepting it, then moved to the next, saturating the concoction.

Soon the potion was dark green.

Elder Piraka grasped the vial from me and raised it to examine it against the sunlight. “This is…”

“What is it?” the apprentice asked. “Is it ruined? Did he fail? I knew he would!”

Elder Piraka carefully took the cork of the vial off and had a sip of the liquid. Immediately his skin flushed pink and his eyes widened.

“Amazing…” he said to himself. “I have never even made one this potent…”

“So you will take him?” mother asked.

Elder Piraka crouched down to hold my shoulders. “Can you reproduce this? What skill do you have? How did you do it so fast? Tell me everything! I must write this down!”

Mother ruffled my hair. “That is probably a yes.”

Konako ruffled Danek’s hair. “Looks like you won your first big bet, son.”

“Father, I leveled!” Danek said and beamed happily.

“No!” the apprentice girl said and stomped her foot. She was almost crying. “I don’t want another apprentice! I am enough. Master should only teach me!”

Everyone ignored her, and the elder’s gaze was fixed on me.

“Yes,” I said carefully. “I can do it as many times as you want.”

“Wonderful! I will start setting up a station for you, Ramona, we should—”

“There’s one thing,” I said. The room changed. Mother looked at me worriedly, and the elder gave me a calculating glance. But I couldn’t help but to negotiate. If the Elder didn’t want to be squeezed, he shouldn’t have shown such over excitement.

“In exchange for making these potions for you, I want papa to get one per day.”

“One per day?” the elder spluttered. “That’s… especially this strong? No. I am giving you apprenticeship. Do you not know how valuable that is?”

“Luka,” mother said. “Maybe we should—”

I cut in. “If you promise this, I can make this mana in this bag last twice as long.”

The elder’s eyes almost bulged out of their sockets. “Twice?! That is not possible!”

I wondered if the poor elder would burst a vein if I told him I didn’t even need the bag to do this.

“Maybe even more if my Skills improve,” I said.

Twice as long was a laughably safe promise. I was already using less than half his usual amount, and I suspected I could eventually do the whole thing with atmospheric mana. But Piraka didn't need to know that yet.

I had one more condition to add — the most important one.

“I don't want to replace Nirama or take her apprenticeship. I want to learn magic. I'll help you make your tonics every morning, and you'll teach me what you know about mana, spells and the old sorcerers. Deal?”

“This is…” Piraka muttered and looked at the green potion I had infused again.

“Pay attention, Danek,” Konako said. “This is how to negotiate.”

“Will you agree?” I asked.

The Elder stared at me for a long time. A heavy silence filled the room. The apprentice tried to speak something, but the Elder silenced her with a sharp gesture as he stared at me. I stayed impassive. This was not just about securing father with an advantage against that Marek asshole. Yeah, I’ll admit it was personal…

But the other reason was that I would have to learn to negotiate and use leverage. I might as well start now.

Finally the Elder blinked and averted his gaze. He shook his head and chuckled to himself.

“And I thought Vorok was the prodigy of his generation…” he muttered. “What an interesting child.”

“I will accept your terms, Luka,” Piraka said. “You'll help me infuse my tonics each morning. In return, I'll teach you the magical arts as best I can. And your father receives one energy tonic every day.“

Then he turned to my mother. “You have raised a splendid boy, Ramona.”

My mother gave him a very wide, very satisfied grin. “I know.”

Elder Piraka sat down after Ramona and Luka left. He sent his petulant apprentice away to fetch some herbs from the Junglers. He brewed himself a cup of tea and shook his head.

“That boy…”

By the ancestors, what were his Skills?

Piraka put the bag of refined mana on the table. It was valuable. How the Shamans from the richer islands made it from beast cores, he did not know. They had to trade a lot of fish to get it. Piraka used Mana Sense on the bag. It seemed to have only lost a few drops. Piraka shook his head and checked again. This wasn’t possible!

Had that boy really infused a better potion than he had, by using less than what he needed?

On his first attempt no less.

He had no words for it. The boy had used half of the mana Piraka himself required to create a potion twice as potent.

Piraka took his staff in his hand and looked at it in contemplation. It wasn’t his. It was the village’s and he was its steward. Each shaman taught their true apprentice the Staffcasting skill. A Rare-tier Skill, a Shaman’s pride. He had been talented and gotten the staff from his master early.

But in front of the raw talent Luka had just expressed…

And the boy hadn’t even been interested in becoming a true apprentice!

The nerve! But Piraka had to accept. With the boy’s abilities, the whole village would prosper. A big portion of the excess quotas were used to trade for goods outside the village. And of that, refined mana took the largest slice.

If the boy only needed that little mana… They would all get richer.

How had Luka learned this?

“Ramona…? Surely not…”

The girl had been a talented Jungler. Piraka smiled wistfully as he remembered her as a young girl mystified by the island’s history, the ancestors and the ruins. She had always enjoyed listening to fables by open fire.

She had explored around the ancestors’ ruins as any bold Junglers did. Ever since the Silverback emerged, nobody had visited the ruins. It used to be a game for the younger girls. Go into the inner jungle and touch the temple steppes.

There lay secrets hidden in the ruins, Piraka knew. But even he could not reach the inner sanctums, as they seemed to require specific Skills. There was a spellbook there, from which Piraka had copied a few useful spells from. The boy reminded him of the ancestors in the fables with their great abilities. Could Ramona have gone into the jungle and recovered some secret to teach her son?

Piraka scoffed at that. Ridiculous theory. Ramona was a good girl and would share such power with the village, he knew it. Nonsense. And Ramona didn’t know a damn thing about magic. She was just a brave girl who liked stories.

But then how had the boy done that…?

Piraka sighed. Well it was all for the better. Luka was a good boy and his parents were good people. He hadn’t enjoyed being dominated by the boy in a negotiation, but the boy had known he had a good position. Smart, as well as powerful.

But it was for the better. The whole village would prosper.

“I must teach this boy. And while he learns from me, I hope to learn from him.”
