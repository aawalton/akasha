It’s pretty good work, all things told.

A bit sloppy, true, but for something she’s never done before, a bit sloppy is a pretty good result.

Where there was once a child and beast in the form of a man, there is now a rock.

It’s not actually a rock, of course. That would be a terrible waste. It does have quite an exterior shell, however, made entirely of obsidian, iron, lead, and bone, all intermingled with bits and pieces of tendon and flesh, to allow it to “breathe” a little. She’s made a sort of bio-alloy out of the mixture, blending each of the materials together into something that’s as much sludge as it is stone, thicker than tar before it dried.

The boulder is several tons thick, with scar tissue and ligaments running through long cracks along its surface, making it able to flex, almost imperceptibly, in and out. It moves in sync with the organs trembling inside, shifting as they demand more and less space by intervals.

Most of them still work the same, if a bit modified. The heart still works to pump blood- it just has to work harder now. The lungs still pull in air- they just do so through a diffuse blend of small pores rather than a single esophagus. There’s still a stomach, for storage and digestion, but that one’s been modified the most, forced to digest exponentially faster and more efficiently, while only being able to digest a few specific compounds, now.

That last part made it easiest to modify the brain.

Working on neurology has become something of a specialty since she stopped being Her. All of the safeguards, in spite of their current state, were very good practice, considering she had to kill herself a bunch of times to do it. It’s made it trivial to modify a brain in certain ways, though plenty still remains a mystery. But altering someone else’s brain is a different thing entirely.

She doesn’t… mind that. It’s not a problem, especially not when she doesn’t care about that person. It’s just… different. She feels like it should be a problem, maybe, and she’s not entirely sure why. Something in her memories, maybe, or something about the original version of Her that used to be but no longer is.

But she can’t live her life based on who she might have been and might have used to be. She has things to do, things to try, and hurting this person who did this bad thing feels right.

So she modified his brain. But just a little bit.

Higher consciousness is easy to modify, hard to modify specifically. Easier to just muffle, or turn off. His frontal lobe has slightly less protein and oxygen distribution than the rest of the brain now, which has less oxygen flowing to it as a whole as well. Then, the stomach- it can only digest a few specific things, all which discourage wakefulness.

The brain is encased in a gel, deep inside the very deepest part of the boulder. Safe, intact, and kept entirely separate from any form of stimuli, crippled away from any ability to process, understand, or experience. If she had to guess, based on the amount of Qi and fleshcrafting she’s put into the boulder, it would take anyone under the Nascent Soul realm incredible effort to crack it, let alone dig to its interior.

And throughout every inch of it, all the way through to the scar tissue and pores on its surface, are channels of Qi.

She still hasn’t learned how to make actual spiritual organs, but as a substitute, she’s managed to make something good enough to manage. Replicating the Qi channels she built into Geng He’s arm, she used the boy’s own flesh to generate crystalline, keratin-based quarts, letting it build out into something that his Qi can move through almost as well as his own spiritual channels. It was easy enough, then, to mimic the patterns.

Even if he was conscious, he wouldn’t be able to stop his Qi from being pulled away and circulated out. Moved through spaces over a hundred times the space of his original body, it grinds against itself, moving in eddies and currents to multiply further.

Were this man, whose name she never learned, to remain conscious, he could likely reach the amount of Qi required for the Warrior Realm in days rather than years. His Core, paltry as it is, has been filled completely full of Qi, ready to birth a Nascent Soul.

If only he was awake enough to form one. If only it had developed enough to start growing on its own.

There is a malformed seed of something that could be life there, soaking in Qi. That is how it will remain.

And the excess floods out into the realm.

The obelisk outputs enough Qi to equal a dozen or more other cultivators.

She devours it all.

The space around the obelisk gets wetter and wetter, bubbles of dew forming on every surface. Grass deepens to a bluish color, and bits of rain start to fall from nowhere. Each droplet is deeper and darker than it should be, offering a glimpse into a body of water that is-and-is-not, and from each droplet, a puddle forms. Soon, the area around the flesh-boulder has become soaked, sinking into a pond of its own creation.

With a thought, she shuffles the Flesh-that-is-space of her body, compressing it down to a point, then moving that point up.

There’s a spot inside of her being where, if one passes through it, they emerge on the other side, rather than into the space they should, because that space is now a tiny, miniscule dot floating up in the sky.

It’s like holding a muscle in place- difficult, and there’s strain, but she can do it. And just like exercise, she’ll get better at it.

She could maybe even do more. More than just the one, at least.

There are probably a few more that she could find inside herself that she could take. Even now, the flood of Qi coming from the flesh-boulder has distinctly added to her- blood is flowing easier, more deeply, and there’s far more water than before, which is sometimes even more important. Cooling for the heat of her Flesh, more Qi generation for her growth and upkeep and transformation, and more… depth. Something she can’t quite name, but which she knows is real, knows has an impact.

So many possibilities there. So much that could and might be. So much she can do.

It’s interesting. And unlike so many other chains of thought, there are no safeguards in place to keep her mind from moving. Here, thinking of this, there’s room to be.

Someone breaks through a door, beneath the earth, and her focus shifts there.

“Welcome!” she says, falling out of the ceiling on a long string of spine and muscle. “Congratulations! You’ve arrived at one of the treasure rooms!”

Standing before her, clad in blood-soaked robes and wounds, is a coalition of many different sects. There are even some that are allied to different peaks, and all six of them are actively straining, their hearts pushing hard, their lungs pumping to bring air in and out, in and out. Their cores are naturally just as drained, all of them near empty.

Maybe she made things a teensy bit difficult.

“You’ve all arrived just in time! I’m having some really great ideas, all around. First of all-”

Pushing past their natural resistance is easy. None of them have the metaphysical weight that she has, and what little Qi they could use to supplement that weight is almost gone. It takes little more than a thought to reach into their bodies, touch upon their Flesh, and remold it back into shape.

Scars and malformations are symptoms of natural healing. She goes directly past that layer of reality. She tells their bodies how to be, and she has the power to fuel that change and the weight to make it happen. In an instant, they’re returned to full health, their flesh slightly more infused with life and flesh Qi than before.

“That’s better. Seems like you had a tough time getting through the trials!”

“You had leeches! Under my skin!”

Raika turns to one of the cultivators, who looks like he’s on the verge of hyperventilating. His Qi is cycling, what little of it remains in his core, just barely enough to qualify as a threat.

If he attacks, could she make another boulder? Fuse him to the landscape? Add another flavor to the landscape, modify things, get more Qi production and-

One of the other cultivators raises a hand, blocking him. The loss of face, the awkward moment where one steps in front of the other, almost makes her think it will make him angrier… but he slows down. Takes a breath.

“I’m glad they were so impactful!” Raika says, smiling wide. “That was pretty experimental. Well, most things are around here. Good job pulling them out! The spiral shape was to make them really good at digging in, so you have to make sure that they don’t get into the bones.”

“WE… were they intended to kill?”

For some reason, that makes several of their faces turn pale, and one of them looks like he’s going to vomit.

It’s broken open like an egg, the front of it oozing something yellow and dark purple, the consistency of water at the bottom but the thickness of a slime at the top. It grows out into a small lake around it, less than an inch deep but filling out most of the cavern.

Maybe you’d prefer it if I did something a little different here. Maybe you’re really scared of juices and slimes and want the same stuff, but not all wet! Come right up and tell me what you think- I love new ideas!”

Maybe you want a weapon? Those are pretty cool, especially when you don’t have claws or really cool organs.”

And I get why you’re talking like that, I understand the ideas behind it, but I won’t be insulted or hurt in any way unless you actually try to like, kill me, so I don’t care about you being nice or observing the social rituals. Tell me what you want. Be bolder.”
