Calliope jumped onto her horse and trotted up beside me. Her scythe also emitted a dim Amber hue, which made her face glow orange in the dim beginnings of a sunrise (minus the actual sun part).

She retracted her scythe, then held out a hand. “Up we go, flower boy.”

“You want me… Up there?”

“You'd rather walk back to the tavern?"

"I—Well, no. I appreciate the offer, but I'm not going back."

"Oh, you're not, huh? And where exactly do you think you're going instead?" She put her hands on her hips.

To find a god. No big deal. "I appreciate your help, truly. But it's none of your concern."

"You won't make it to the Spire, if that's what you're thinking."

There was that central spire again. It kept coming up.

"What's the Spire?"

"Oh, so you don't know."

"I will once you tell me."

"Oh, yeah?" She glared down at me, half a smirk cut across her face.

"You're infuriating."

"Thank you."

"Seriously, c'mon."

"Fine, fine." She steered her horse around me, trotting in a circle as she spoke. "Rumor has it, if you reach the summit of the central spire the gods will grant you release from service as a Reaper."

"You mean…"

"Yup, home sweet home. Permanently. Those are just rumors, though. No one has actually ever returned to say for sure. Honestly, I don't even understand why anyone would leave. You can build a perfectly content life out here in the Meadow Zone."

So, there was an escape from this. Screw meeting up with gods, I'm out of here. "I have to try. I have a life. I—"

"And you think I don't? We all do. You think you're the only one who would rather be with their family than out here hunting monsters?"

"Then why don't you go?"

She rolled her eyes. "It's not as simple as walking there. Didn't you notice the map on the ceiling of The Meridian? There are six more zones between here and there. Each one requires you to advance to a higher rank than the last. And each one requires more and more hardship. You need to gain…" She counted on her fingers quickly, "at least six ranks higher than you are now before you even stand a small chance."

Shit… I should've known it wouldn't be that simple. I always tried to rush into things. Settle down, Miles. Think it through. I didn't even realize I could increase my rank, though in hindsight it should've been obvious.

But what other option did I have? There was the god who'd spoken to me, but was that a way home?

"Still," I said, "I have to try."

"You're literally impossible. But whatever. Just don't make a hasty decision. Let yourself acclimate before charging off into the unknown without a single clue."

I lowered my head. I need to go home. I need to see Avery. And Derek. And my friends. But she has a point. Maybe charging off on my own isn't the best option. And I can still assess the god situation while I figure things out. Regrettably, Jericho is probably still my best starting point on that front.

"Do I have to die every time I wanna return to Vitria?" I asked, more than a little proud of myself for using the proper terminology.

"See. This is exactly what I'm talking about. You have a lot to learn about this place. You haven't even relinquished the Will you got. I bet you don't know how to do that either"

"That doesn't really answer the—"

"Geesh! Yes. You can return without dying. And I'll show you if you just stop being an idiot and come back to the tavern."

I groaned as she reigned in the horse and held her hand out again.

“Fine,” I said, as I retracted my scythe about a quarter as smoothly as she had.

Calliope grabbed my hand and pulled me up as I swung my leg over the horse’s rear.

"You ridden a horse before?" she asked.

"Sure," I lied.

“You’re gonna want to hold on.”

I searched around for something to grab onto. A strap on the saddle or…

“To me,” she added. “Don’t worry, I don’t bite.”

“No, only stab,” I grunted.

I wrapped my arms around her waist, then paused. The hardened exoskeleton on my forearm was going to be scratching against her skin as we rode. Why the fuck couldn't she just wear a shirt that covered her abdomen like a normal person?

"Umm…" I pulled my hands back. "I don't want to hurt you with… this." I held up my right arm.

"You think that's gonna hurt me? I'm no silkie. Let's go."

I shrugged and tried again, weaving my fingers together in front of her so only my palms would touch her bare stomach. There was just something more intimate about fingers that I wanted to avoid. I hadn’t been this close to a woman besides my fiancé since we’d started dating six years ago.

Just pretend she's Avery… That'll—Nope! Nooo. Terrible idea, don't pretend that! Not at all.

Keep it as platonic as possible…

Calliope looked back at me, her cheeks a little more flushed than before. “Ready?”

I nodded, shifting my hands awkwardly on her stomach. Were they sweaty? Fuck, they were sweaty, weren’t they. Maybe I should've put my hands on her hips instead. Too late now. Changing my mind would just make this awkward situation even more awkward. I was committed.

“Don’t worry. We’ll take it slow,” she said.

My cheeks must have turned beet red, because her eyes widened. I felt the heat rising to my ears.

“The ride I mean!”

“That’s… not any better,” I noted, stifling a laugh.

She turned forward and kicked the horse into a slow trot. “Get your mind out of the gutter.”

“Is this how you talk to all the guys you stab?”

Calliope groaned. “I genuinely hate you.”

We rode in awkward silence for a while. Nuzzle let his tongue hang in the wind like a dog with its head out a car window. I paid more attention to the surroundings this time. Several distinct groves of trees dotted the landscape, including a larger forest at one point. We crossed a wooden bridge over a small river that wound through the land from the east and disappeared between hills to the west.

Now that I thought about it, I actually had no concrete knowledge of what the compass directions would be here. With no sun in the sky, it was a mystery. The only indicator was the directionality of the shadows, which had shifted over the course of the day, suggesting that the invisible light source was now ascending up from the horizon to my left. So I figured, may as well call that west.

The monsters that wandered past only reinforced the variety I’d seen so far. Animal-human hybrids ranged from squirrels to birds to elephants and everything in between. It was easy to forget these monsters were manifestations of sin. So far, we'd only passed unmarked ones. Several glanced up at us as we passed, but none of them attacked like the rhinoceros had.

“Thank you,” I said finally. “For saving me. You didn’t have to come find me.”

“You’re welcome,” Calliope said. No witty insult this time. “You actually did quite well. Most new Ambers wouldn’t last thirty seconds against an Unchained.”

“Unchained?”

“Right. I forgot you know nothing.”

Ah, there's the insult.

She gestured out to a hybrid beast walking parallel to us. “Most Sins roam freely. Once they're marked for death, they’ll remain within a certain area. Chained. If they're marked for death too long before a Reaper defeats them, their Will can break loose. And they’ll rampage on a killing spree, murdering innocent Penumbrans and unmarked Sins along the way.”

Well, that explained why the rhinoceros attacked me, even though Jericho said I wouldn’t have to worry too much about self-defense.

“So, we can’t just ignore the marked ones… Because they’ll do that.”

She nodded. “And it's been happening a lot more lately, even out here in the Meadow. Unchained often grow in size and strength from their normal state. More violent. More dangerous. Which is why it's incredible you lasted as long as you did.”

So I hadn’t embarrassed myself being chased around by the rhino Sin… I’d actually impressed her.

"You're the same rank as me, so why was it so much easier for you?"

"It wasn't easy. Trust me. But you'll find out soon enough that rank isn't everything. Sure, it gives an exponential boost, but there are other ways to increase your strength. That, and I'm obviously just naturally more skilled than you."

We rode a bit longer until a dark splotch appeared in the distance that I recognized must be the tavern.

“I’ve been thinking…” Calliope said. Her hair waved in the breeze. She paused for a moment and bit her lip. “Zephyr.”

“Hmm? Zephyr?”

“It’s not a flower. But I think it might fit.”

“You mean my… my name?”

“It means west wind.”

The breeze blew again. I followed its gentle gust in the direction I’d labelled as west. Above the dark horizon, the night had begun changing into day, a sunrise forming, not just the east like it was supposed to, but across the entire sky. Crimson, flame, and lavender painted across the air—like a true Earth sunrise, but more vibrant. Infinitely more beautiful.

“Zephyr…” I said. "What do you think, Nuzzle?"

The lizard licked my cheek and purred happily.

Well, fuck.

I didn’t hate it.
