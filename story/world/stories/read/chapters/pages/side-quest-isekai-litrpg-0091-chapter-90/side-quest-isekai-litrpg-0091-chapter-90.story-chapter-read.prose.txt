With his free hand, Logan clutched his cloak tight to him. It billowed with the speed Brother Timmons tugged him up the stairs, and if it flew open it would reveal Nugget completely. He only had moments before Brother Timmons revealed the hidden dragon, and in that time—as much as he liked Brother Timmons—he needed to think up a way to discredit the monk.

That was time he didn’t have, though. A few steps into the temple, Brother Timmons lurched to a stop and grabbed Logan’s cloak’s neckline, tugging it down.

“It’s him! Look what he has!”

Logan clutched desperately to keep the cloak held together. Nugget was climbing underneath the fabric to hide on his back, but he was only halfway there. “I can explain,” he said as he tried to stall.

Before he could, though, another monk gasped, and a third cried out. “And look up there! The Casket Box of the Shrine of Life is missing!”

The monks all started murmuring and Logan, in his panic, froze up. Not only were they outing him as a dragon hider, but also as a thief!

Except… despite Brother Timmons waggling his hand while gripping Logan’s cloak, the monk didn’t actually rip the clasp off. And why did all these monks sound excited rather than accusatory?

Logan glanced down at his neckline. His amulet rattled between his collarbones with how forcefully the monk shook him by the nape.

One bystander gasped and clapped. “He wears an amulet of Razan! It must be a sign!”

Razan… that was the name of the priest at whose shrine Nugget had caused the distracting commotion. It was also the priest with whom his amulet was associated. So that’s where I’ve heard the name before.

His next thought let him dare to hope. Is that what this is all about? And not Nugget?

“Exactly,” said Brother Timmons triumphantly. “And if the casket box has vanished, then the prophecies are true and the gods smile on us.” He suddenly seemed to realize how hard he was shaking Logan and let go of the cloak. “I am so, so sorry, Mr. Vitali. When I saw the omen, I got so excited I didn’t even think about what I was doing.”

“No worries,” said Logan as he smoothed out his rumpled neckline. Nugget had only clawed his way to Logan’s ribs and not fully to his back, but it looked like the secret of the dragon’s presence was safe. “Um… what omen?”

“Why, the mending of the Ring of Life, of course.” Understanding lit his eyes. “Ah, yes, you don’t attend services often, do you? The Ring of Life is an extension of the Life Sage’s blessing, and when it touches down on the world, it brings incredible healing. Not just for people, but the land, too. And some prophecies claim that the sage’s sentinel, Luvania, will return to smite the source that summoned her.”

Logan picked up his amulet and held it a little ways from his body to staree down at it cross-eyed. There was a celtic-looking circle inscribed on the amulet’s border.

Brother Timmons continued. “We just came back from investigating a disturbance outside the town walls. There have been quilled brillybills far exceeding their rightful levels for the area, which is how things started in Gnashridge Heights when the blight reached them.”

“I’ve heard that,” Logan said, remembering all the high-level prowlers, warpincer raptors, and even squirrels he had encountered. Quilled brillybills… he had hear of those, too, from Horace. His trainer had been late that first day because he was responding to some commotion caused by the… well, whatever the heck a brillybill was.

He was feeling a little overwhelmed with the mention of Luvania as well. He had a pending quest to awaken her. Did that mean she would smite him? He checked his quest, and it still sat in a pending status on his quest board. For some reason, that left a lump of dread sitting in his gut.

Brother Timmons escorted Logan closer to the central altar of the temple. “We have always expected the blight would reach us sooner or later. Given the signs of the brillybills, we sent our prayers to the Life Sage. Then we came back to discover the Casket Box missing, and the ceiling panels above Razan’s shrine trembling.” He gestured as they passed the shrine. “Razan is the one who is believed will bring Luvania back. He is also the one who write the book of The Shrine of Life. It is written in a forgotten language, though. That is why we keep the casket box empty, in case one day we discover how to transcribe the language.”

Logan bit his lip. He had seen through the crack in the casket box, which currently sat in his storage ripple, and it was definitely not empty.

When they reached the altar, Brother Timmons pointed to a small section of carvings. “Many centuries ago, Razan led the charges against evil hordes intent on disrupting the System. One day, he left and never returned. But before he departed, he vowed to return, gather his tools, and carry out the Life Sage’s will.”

“That’s great news,” said Logan as his back began to sweat. Just what I need coming after me. A mysterious ancient priest.

Nugget slid down his back with the sweat and dug his claws in to stabilize himself. Logan didn’t mind.

“Indeed it is,” said the monk. “I will have to speak to the Adventurer’s Guild. While this may bode well for an end to the blight, there are still the quilled brillybills to be mindful of. They’ll probably be sending someone to charge the prism again soon. Not that we can, until we hear back from Master Lambert about…” He trailed off as he squinted at the ceiling where Logan had installed the replacement. “Hmm…”

He wandered over the few paces from the central altar to pull on the assembly’s cord. The contraption fell down all the way without a hitch.

“Why, it’s already replaced. When did that happen?”

I can’t ignore this. If he speaks to Lambert and finds out the carpenter sent me to make the replacement, it will look suspicious that I didn’t just say so right now.

“Actually that’s why I was here. Sorry, I saw everybody was gone, but Lambert said you were expecting the replacement since yesterday, so I figured I would take care of it.” As for explaining how he got up there to make the replacement, he wasn’t sure what to say yet.

“Wonderful. Well you have helped us tremendously today in more ways than one! I trust you were able to use the elevator pad without issue?”

“Hm? Oh. Yeah. The elevator pad. Yep, no issue whatsoever. Very simple.”

“Perfect. Thanks for sending it back up when you were finished. I actually need to activate the contraption from above, though, so don’t mind me.” The monk pressed a button that looked nothing like a button, but an apple carved into a nearby bas-relief on the wall.

Just like that, a square panel next to the ladder Logan had climbed descended.

Logan just stood and watched with a red face. That would have been a lot easier if I had known about that…

He shook himself from his inner chiding when the elevator panel was halfway down. “Oh, Brother Timmons. If you wouldn’t mind, I actually am the one appointed with charging the prism again. And I also had something Lambert wanted charged too.” He pulled out both items. “Do you mind helping me with these?”

The monk assured Logan he would.

When the elevator reached the ground, all he did was pull a lever while he stood on it and went up. Logan couldn’t help but laugh at all the trouble he took to get up there, but Nugget stirred from his post on Logan’s back.

Yeah, I guess it was more fun our way.

When Logan arrived at the entrance to the Mages’ Guild training area, Alden was sitting on a rock, tapping his finger on his knee. They had chosen the meeting spot because it was just out of sight from anyone standing inside the training area while looking out of the arches.

Alden hadn’t yet seen Logan, and he looked pale.

“Are you okay?”

Alden jumped to his feet when Logan spoke, and then quickly glanced over his shoulder and hunched his back to keep his silver hair out of line of sight from the entry archway. Something was off about the way the mage was acting.

“I’m perfectly fine,” he said curtly. “Shall we proceed?”

“Okay,” said Logan, watching the elf’s face for any visual cues of what was going on his mind. It wasn’t quite panic, but there was something making Alden uneasy. Logan glanced to either side to see if anyone might be nearby. If that had been the case, though, Alden wouldn’t have just been sitting down twiddling his thumbs.

Alden suddenly lifted a finger. “I don’t suppose we should go back and get Senna over here?”

Logan arched an eyebrow. “Uh… why would we need Senna?”

“She might have something that can, I don’t know…”

Alden trailed off and then his heel began lifting off the ground as it shook with nervous energy.

When it was clear Alden wasn’t going to finish his sentence, Logan just played it off like nothing was weird so he didn’t make Alden feel as ridiculous as he actually seemed right now. “Nah, Senna is busy right now. Remember the reaver equipment Avalyn gave her when we left? She thinks she’s on the verge of a mechanical breakthrough or something.”

“Hm?” Alden asked. “Oh. Well, I hate leaving her alone without helping her. I wonder if we should check on her first before we start.”

Logan chewed his cheek. Not once had he ever seen Alden help, let alone ask about helping, Senna with her tinkering. He’s stalling.

Why was Alden trying to postpone the investigation? If this had happened earlier, based on his first impressions of Alden, he might have thought the mage was covering something up and was somehow involved with the reavers. But the way the sweat beaded on his upper lip…

He’s not confident in himself. It all clicked.

Last time Alden stood inside the Mages’ Guild training area, his smug exterior had melted away. And worse, the skill practice he had gone to test hadn’t just not worked out. It had completely backfired and made him something laughable. Then he had to press on and try to spy inside the Mages’ Guild.

Logan suddenly felt like a jerk. He had joked with the others about Alden’s obviousness during the whole stealth scene. And even though Alden had brushed it off with a snide comment, Logan now realized he truly was self-conscious.

Is it possible to be egotistical and self-conscious at the same time? Logan wasn’t sure, since he’d never been outstanding enough at anything to be conceited about it in the first place.

He sat down on the rock and patted it.

Alden glanced at Logan in confusion, but took a seat.

“Hey. Alden. You’ve got this. Forget about the last time we were here. I saw you use Festered Veins while training at the Adventurers’ Guild. You’re a smart guy. We don’t need Senna, or Bromlin, or anyone else.”

Alden swallowed and stared Logan in the eyes. His lower lip still twitched and he was still pale, but he nodded.

Logan grinned and clapped a hand to Alden’s shoulder. “Great. Do you still want to do this?” He wasn’t about to force the mage to help out. It would be a lot easier to do this with his help from inside the guild, but Logan would be able to muddle his way through it on his own if he had to.

“Yes,” said Alden after pausing to gulp. He stood up. “Let’s go.”

Phew.

Alden stepped into the training area while Logan waited outside it. After passing through the archway, the elf immediately took a right, hugging the wall tightly. Logan strolled along the backside of the wall.

Feral Echo.

Alden’s silhouette was visible through the wall, just as expected. While they still stood close enough to the entry area to hear one another, Logan coughed loudly, and Alden coughed back, which was their sign that everything was good to go.

As Alden walked, Logan kept pace on the opposite side of the wall. Alden was heading to the exact spot where the disturbance in the shell had appeared, and Logan would check for any clues on the outside. It was difficult to pinpoint the exact location with how massive the training area was, though, but being able to see Alden’s general location through the wall worked just fine for this purpose.

By the time they reached the spot, Logan had been ready to take a mana potion, but was surprised to see he didn’t need it. His mana pool was pretty jaw-droppingly large right now, thanks to having two heritage boons of his own, plus Nugget’s. All the stat gains had been retroactive, too, which made his current mana pool 1008.

Alden’s smudge of a silhouette came to a stop and it looked like he was crouching down to tie his shoelaces. That was the cue for Logan, then.

Aetherlens.

Without the Void Affinity and Unrelenting Hunt, he needed to stay hyper focused for any signs of abnormalities with the ambient mana. He scanned left and right, but all the strands looked evenly distributed, if a little chaotic.

He paused.

Focusing his sight on the near distance, almost as if he were focusing a set of binoculars, he compared the mana spread here to the distribution there. That was all balanced in distribution.

This is it. This is where Amatra would have appeared. The mana here is scarred!

He turned Aetherlens off so he could just scan the area on his own. Even without Unrelenting Hunt, he still had a high Perception after all.

With the toe of his shoe, he scuffed dirt aside.

The metal object he revealed was unlike anything he'd encountered before, and he hoped he would never have to again.
