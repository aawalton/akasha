Alliot's entire body went rigid at Aira's question. His shoulders locked, and his eyes darted across the horizon—not scanning for threats but as if searching for invisible eavesdroppers among the distant peaks. A muscle worked in his jaw before he spoke, his voice falling to a taut whisper that barely disturbed the mountain air.

"My entire existence has been consecrated to Wentouk's protection," he said, fingers working nervously at the hem of his shirt. "Every skill honed, every lesson learned... all to guard against threats. Exactly like what you're suggesting." His hands trembled before he clenched them into fists, knuckles whitening. "You're asking me to evaluate what? My home's vulnerabilities to your power?" The words seemed physically painful, torn between his duty and his growing loyalty to their group.

"I think we are past being shy about our affiliations," Aira said. "Things started moving pretty fast after we visited Ziemrot. And I'm sure Ainorrh knew about all the tensions and political issues our visit would result in. So, just answer my question."

"Alright. I'm not sure if a group of our defenders would have been able to overwhelm you even now. With only one artifact. If only with the full force of the Source wielded by the shamans," Alliot said hesitantly. "If you get more of them in cities of different elements... You'll possess an overwhelming power. And it seems that our current goal is for you to get four Shards or whatever. What would that power be? I can't guess. And I'm not sure what Ainorrh's game is. Does she just blindly believe that a stranger would follow her directions? Or give up that kind of artifact?"

"I don't have answers to these questions," Aira said. "But I'm also very interested to know what sort of role Ainorrh designated for me. And how she plans to use me or my powers. We'll have to plan all sorts of contingencies and play along."

"What else can you tell us about Lamcada?" asked Lila. "Do we have to plan for another trap, or are we just barking up the wrong root again?"

"I don't think so," Alliot said, relaxing slightly as the conversation shifted. "Lamcada isn't Ziemrot with its stone-bound traditions." He paused, choosing his words carefully. "They're... curious. Not just about our air-weaving technologies. About human innovations too. I've heard tales of their artisans working with the Old World devices. Just to understand how they function."

Alliot's expression softened with something like wistful respect. "Ziemrot walls itself behind mountain and tradition," he said. "But Lamcada's fires have always burned open to the world. They trade more freely. They speak more openly." He glanced at Lila. "Some whisper they even maintain communication with humans beyond mere trade. But these…" he added quickly, "…are matters beyond a defender's knowledge. Our cities maintain balance through interdependence. Each providing what others cannot. But there are... other connections."

"Like what?" Aira asked.

"Well…" Alliot was again hesitant to continue.

"Just spill it out!" said Lila.

"Our attitude towards humans?" Alliot said it almost like he was asking Aira and Lila a question. "Ziemrot traditionally held a more peaceful stance towards humans. They were always pro trade agreements and even cultural engagement. Us, on the other hand, even if we considered trading with humans, any mingling? Cultural exchange? What does that even mean? We don't have proper ways of communication. But now I'm venturing in an area where I know almost nothing. Again, I'm a defender, not a politician or… negotiator."

Alliot looked at Lila pointedly.

"What?" she asked.

"Nothing," Alliot answered. "But maybe it's time you started using your skills more actively. I don't think you'll be able to really influence people or make them change their mind. But you can help us gather more information."

"So, you're not very helpful in negotiations with the enlightened," Lila said, "and you want me to do all the work? I'm green as spring leaves as an enlightened—I was introduced to this skill only a few weeks ago!"

"Guys, let's not fight," Aira jumped to her feet and stood between Alliot and Lila. "We are trying to solve a complicated mystery that involves politics, magic, and ambitions of at least one powerful leader. Even while we have different backgrounds and personal goals, it seems that our paths pretty much align."

"Alliot," she continued, "in the next enlightened city you can try to build any sorts of connection you can and gather more information through them. And Lila, you should use your skills anyway. That would help you to grow, gather more XP, generate some additional skill points and upgrade your skills further. That's how the System works."

"Alright," Lila said, "it all just sounds like every one of us has to do their part. Root to branch, that's reasonable. But what about getting there? Are we sure we won't miss it and end up caught in the wind?"

"Lamcada is located almost directly to the south from Ziemrot," Alliot said. "We just have to follow the stars, and we'll get there. And on the approach, Aira's range should be enough for us to pinpoint the exact location."

"That's the plan," Aira said. "I understand that it will also take a couple of weeks for us to get there. So, if in ten days I don't sense their source of power, we are going in the wrong direction. We can probably return to Wentouk and start again from there. But we'll lose so much time in that case. Let's hope we'll get to Lamcada without any accidents!"

The following day, they set out with renewed purpose, their eyes fixed on the southern horizon. The shadow of Ziemrot still loomed behind them, both literally and in their memories. Alliot led the way, his knowledge of celestial navigation proving invaluable as they traversed unfamiliar territory. Al perched on Aira's shoulder, his tiny nose constantly sampling the air, while Lila kept guard at their flank, her budding enlightened senses alert for any sign of pursuit.

***

Days stretched into their second week of travel, each sunrise finding them further from Ziemrot's shadow. Aira felt the change before she could name it—the Roots of the Mountain, which had thrummed through her consciousness like a heartbeat since their escape, began to recede. The steady, patient pulse of Earth magic grew fainter with each passing day, like a song heard from increasingly distant hills.

But nature abhors a vacuum. As one presence dimmed, something new kindled at the edges of her awareness—a sensation warmer and wilder than earth's steady rhythm. It flickered against her magical senses like flame teasing kindling, its cadence faster, more volatile. In her pocket, the Splinter of the Worldroot seemed to respond, growing cool as if retreating from this new elemental presence. One morning, as they crested a hill, Aira suddenly stopped, her eyes widening.

"What is it?" Lila asked, instantly alert.

"I can feel it," Aira said, turning slightly to adjust her orientation. "Another Source. It has to be Lamcada."

Alliot nodded, a mixture of relief and tension in his posture. "We're on the right track then."

"Is that some sort of coincidence," Aira asked, "that your cities are each located approximately two weeks' walking distance from one another? First Wentouk to Ziemrot, and now Ziemrot to Lamcada?"

"I have never thought about it," Alliot said. "We can try asking that one of the elders in Lamcada or back home. My guess would be that to get the purest energy for each affinity it's better not to have an interference from a different power source. Probably, 150 kilometers is that range."

"How many cities of the enlightened are there?" asked Lila.

"Dozens," said Alliot. "But I don't have an exact number."

"And each one of them is dedicated to a single affinity?" asked Aira. "Never more than one?"

"Yes, that's our way of life," Alliot answered. "Sometimes we gather together to create artifacts. Or tools. Tools that rely on different elements. But that only happens on special occasions. It demands the presence of multiple shamans."

"Now that you asked me," he continued, "It really looks like Ainorrh tries to turn you into such an artifact. Or to create it with your hands. But what's her end goal?"

The question lingered with them as they continued their journey, a puzzle with too many missing pieces. As days passed, speculation gave way to more immediate concerns—the changing landscape demanded their full attention, presenting new challenges that required adaptation.

With Aira's senses now locked onto Lamcada's Source, they adjusted their route to follow its call directly. The terrain began to transform, the mountain foothills gradually giving way to an environment increasingly shaped by Fire's influence.

***

The landscape transformed dramatically as they drew closer. The rolling hills flattened into plains where the vegetation showed unmistakable signs of Lamcada's elemental influence. Most striking was the temperature change. The ground beneath their feet grew increasingly warm as if they walked on the stone that had baked in the sun all day, even in shaded areas. At night, they could see an orange glow on the horizon, like a perpetual dawn waiting to break.

One afternoon, as they struggled up a final steep ridge, the air before them fractured into shimmering waves—not the gentle distortion of summer heat, but a violent warping that bent light and perspective. Aira halted so abruptly that Lila nearly collided with her, words dying on her lips as the vista unfolded.

Below them stretched a valley carved by ancient cataclysm, where two massive lava flows had once crashed together in primordial violence. And there, rising from this scar in the earth, stood Lamcada—the City of Eternal Flame.

It didn't simply exist in the landscape. It erupted from it. Spires of obsidian glass twisted skyward, catching the sunlight and fracturing it into a thousand crimson shards. Between them grew impossible trees with trunks of charred black bark and crowns of living fire that didn't consume but eternally burned. The entire city pulsed with its own heartbeat of orange and gold, a breathing entity rather than mere architecture. It rose from the valley floor like a fever dream, a mirage made solid through impossible magic. The city pulsed with light—not the steady glow of lamps or torches, but the living, breathing radiance of contained inferno. Even from this distance, they could feel its warmth reaching for them, calling them forward like the beckoning finger of a lover.

"Walkin' like a loaded deer through a forest fire," Lila breathed, her eyes wide with wonder and apprehension. "That's... a city? How does it not burn to ash?"

"Emberwood Grove," Alliot explained, his voice tinged with reluctant admiration. "The source of their power. The trees burn eternally without being consumed, drawing energy from underground lava flows."

As they approached the city, Aira's connection with the new Source grew increasingly powerful. What had begun as a subtle tingle at the edge of her awareness became an insistent pulse that resonated through her entire being. The Splinter in her pocket grew noticeably warm, seeming to recognize its elemental kin. The rhythm of the Fire element Source was faster, more volatile than Ziemrot's steady earth energy—like comparing a racing heartbeat to the slow breathing of mountains.

The heat only intensified as they descended toward Lamcada. Waves of it rose from the ground, distorting the air and making the city shimmer like a mirage. Sweat beaded on their skin despite the cooling effect of Aira's subtle manipulation of the air around them.

The path led them through a natural archway formed by two Emberwood trees, their trunks and branches alight with eternal flames that danced and twisted without smoke. The heat should have been unbearable, yet as they passed beneath the burning boughs, they felt only a pleasant warmth—like standing near a hearth on a winter's night.

Inside the city proper, they walked streets paved with obsidian blocks that still glowed faintly from within as if the stone remembered its molten origins. Buildings rose around them, constructed from a dark, volcanic glass that somehow remained cool to the touch despite the ambient heat. The impossible Emberwood trees grew everywhere, their flames casting shifting patterns of light and shadow.

Enlightened inhabitants moved through the streets with purpose—their eyes glowing with inner fire, their movements fluid yet somehow reminiscent of dancing flames. Unlike in Ziemrot, the locals didn't immediately react with hostility. Instead, curious glances followed their progress, with occasional whispered conversations trailing in their wake.

"They don't seem alarmed by outsiders," Aira observed quietly.

"I've heard rumors," Alliot replied, though tension was evident in his voice. "Lamcada has always been more... open. I've heard they maintained trade connections with humans. Much longer than any other enlightened city. But don't mistake openness for safety. Fire consumes indiscriminately."

"I'll keep my branches covered," Lila muttered, eyeing a particularly intense flame that seemed to follow their movement. "So where do we find the local shaman?"

As if answering her question, the crowd parted before them without warning or signal, leaving a perfect corridor through which a single figure approached. She didn't walk so much as flow toward them, her movements possessing the dangerous grace of carefully controlled wildfire. Unlike the orange-gold eyes of the other Lamcada's citizens, hers burned with the blue-white intensity of a forge's heart—the color of flame so hot it could reshape metal to its will.

Lila instinctively stepped closer to Aira, her voice coming as a whisper through the party interface, "Torch-blind powerful, that one. Walking high on the cliffs if I've ever seen it."

When the woman finally spoke, her voice carried the subtle, mesmerizing crackle of a well-tended hearth. "The wayfarers from Ziemrot," she said, her voice carrying the gentle crackle of a well-tended fire, though her eyes sparked with mischief. "We've been expecting you—though I imagine Khanorrh would have preferred you never reached our doorstep." She winked as if sharing a private joke. "His loss is our entertainment."
