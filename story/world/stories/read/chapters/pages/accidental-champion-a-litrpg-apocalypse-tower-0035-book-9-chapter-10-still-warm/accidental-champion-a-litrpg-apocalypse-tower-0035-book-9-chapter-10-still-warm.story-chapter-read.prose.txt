Xavier’s scream echoed off the steel walls.

Arrack jerked the spear lodged in the dragonkin’s chest, making his entire body spasm. Xavier tried to flap his black dragon wings, but two cracks sounded and the wings were snapped in half. That heavy, telekinetic weight pushed down on him once more—pushing him further down the length of the double-edged spear.

Xavier’s throat was raw.

Palini’s skull had imploded beneath Arrack’s boot. Blood poured from every orifice. The man was dead before Xavier’s scream ended. He lay, limp on the stone. Face a ruined, unrecognisable mess.

“Are you ready to tell me? Because believe me, the second way this can end is much more pleasant.” Arrack jerked the spear again, wrenching Xavier’s body around like a ragdoll, spasming something in his spine. “I can bring everyone you love into this room, everyone you swore to protect, and hurt them like I hurt Palini, then kill them in front of you as you struggle, weak, under the weight of my power.”

The weight pushed down harder. The blade cut through his back. He slid down the shaft.

Xavier didn’t move. Didn’t whimper, shout, scream. He should have struggled again. Should have tried to get free.

He did nothing but stare at Palini.

Arrack growled, heaved Xavier up on the spear, then slammed him into the stone floor. Xavier almost slid off. Arrack’s eyes widened. He adjusted, kept the spear locked in place.

Xavier lay on the stones next to Palini, close enough to reach. He touched the man’s hand. Still warm.

The body twitched.

Xavier blinked.

Palini…?

“A cadaveric spasm.” Arrack chuckled. “Here.”

A notification sprung up in Xavier’s vision—a kill notification received by Arrack, confirming Palini’s death.

The spasm had happened only when Xavier touched him.

“Now, are you ready to talk, Xavier Collins? Tell me how you broke the contracts,” Arrack droned. “Before my agents drag more of your little friends in here.”

The fingers on Xavier’s right hand—his strongest hand—curled into a fist.

“Fuck you.”

Arrack laughed, grip firmly on the spear.

As the A Grade’s laugh echoed around the steel-walled room, a faint light appeared, hovering over Palini’s body.

What…?

Xavier strained his eyes. The light outlined a human figure.

Palini’s spirit looked faded, hovering over his corpse, staring down with a sad look. His gaze slowly rose to stare at Xavier.

Think, Palini’s voice sounded in Xavier’s mind. You’ll see us through.

Palini looked up, seemed to see something above him, then his spirit drifted toward the ceiling.

A tear slid down Xavier’s cheek.

“Yes.” Arrack laughed all the more. “Yes. You see it now, don’t you?” He knelt, grabbed Xavier’s hair with his bare fingers and pulled his head up, forcing their gazes to lock. “Only a matter of time.”

He slammed Xavier’s head back into the stone with all his strength.

When Xavier came to, the room was silent.

Palini’s body lay on the stone floor, Xavier still touched his hand. It was cold now, and the man’s head rested in a pool of dried blood.

Arrack sat on the chair, spear still gripped firmly, still lodged in Xavier’s chest. The man looked bored, but he hadn’t left.

Palini’s spirit was long gone.

But I saw it…

Think.

That word echoed in his mind.

His eyes drifted shut. He was blind to the Celestial Energy in the room. Blind to the runes in the walls. Yet Palini’s spirit had been clear as day. He reached for his spiritual sense, but it was still blocked. He pushed at it, trying to glimpse the world around him—

Arrack’s spear shifted. Xavier’s eyes flung back open as the pain hitched his breath.

“Nap time’s over.” Arrack’s lips formed a thin line. “Tell me, how are you maintaining this time dilation field? Your little escape plan didn’t work. I locked you down. Again.”

“May as well… kill me,” Xavier choked out, blood seeping from his mouth along with the words. “I’ll never… talk.” He stared at Palini’s cold corpse, trying to think.

It didn’t make sense.

He’d tapped into a sense he shouldn’t have. A sense that had been blocked.

If I could do that again, maybe…

Arrack’s boot stomped Xavier’s hand, bringing his full awareness back to the moment. The energy in the room flickered in and out. Energy he shouldn’t be able to sense.

Xavier stared up at the enraged A Grade. A red smile split Xavier’s face. “You’re not used to—” Xavier coughed. “Losing.”

Arrack’s eyes widened. “I haven’t lost.” He stomped again.

The pain made everything sharper.

Xavier’s rage boiled in his chest. Hot. Primal.

Palini’s death. His corpse twitching. It had made Xavier feel, cutting through everything, bringing his emotions to the fore. Bringing out something deep, instinctual, ancient.

And in that state, he’d been able to see.

Xavier allowed himself to feel the pain. Every shift of the spear cutting through his chest. Every boot-stomp that cracked his bones. The telekinetic weight pushing down on him, crushing him inch by inch into the stone-slab floor.

And he let himself feel the grief.

I couldn’t save him.

The room flickered, brightening in a brilliant flash as though lightning struck. For an instant, Xavier sensed every speck of raw Celestial Energy within those steel walls. He sensed the time distortion keeping them locked together in this moment. He sensed the telekinetic energy weaving through the air to push down at him.

There and then gone.

When the room flickered again, Xavier latched onto it. Whatever lens the pain, the emotion that he felt allowed him to look through, he managed to isolate. As he did, he sensed something deep and ancient inside him. Something powerful and everlasting.

Something that endured.

My Immortal Soul…

Roln had told him, hadn’t he? That spiritual sense wasn’t something you learnt, it was something you unlocked—something the Immortal Soul always had.

Something the Soul Core possessed.

The soul was always watching. Always seeing.

And now Xavier could see through it.

Xavier homed in on the ability, feeling the strength of it—as though his spiritual sense had been flicked into overdrive. He couldn’t just see the raw Celestial Energy in the room, he felt where it was going as it drifted through space, unhindered by the rune-inscribed, layered steel walls.

And he could move it where he wished.

Everything he’d done, everything he’d achieved, slipped into his mind. He’d gotten far because of raw strength—all those titles, all those stats—but he’d gotten farther because of control.

Control was the real power.

His Immortal Soul was the eyes, but something else allowed him to control that energy. Another mechanism that hadn’t been locked down by Arrack’s spell.

The counterpart to the Immortal Soul, the thing that all spirits lacked—his Living Soul.

An endless stream of possibilities and uses of the energy entered his mind. That endless stream narrowed, finding a very specific purpose.

He wanted to accomplish one thing—lock Arrack outside the time dilation field—then he could move forward from there.

Xavier took control of the raw Celestial Energy, moving it through the air, making it merge and form shapes.

The shapes took the form of thousands of small runes. As the energy began to condense, the runes glowed.

How is this working?

The time dilation field was wrapped tight around him, enveloping Arrack as they were touching through the spear in Xavier’s chest. But the Celestial Energy in the air was outside the field, yet here he was moving it with speed and precision from within.

It should be frozen.

But, no—it wasn’t frozen. Nothing ever truly was. It simply appeared that way because of how fast he moved within his own time stream.

He didn’t allow the implications to hit him. He simply continued to form the spell pattern in the air. The energy moved so smoothly, he barely needed to think about where he wanted it to go.

He simply knew, and so it simply knew.

It wasn’t so far removed from what he did inside his body when he manifested a spell pattern, and yet, it felt like so much more…

The spell pattern snapped into being, the light of its glowing runes beautiful to behold.

Arrack blinked as he noticed the pattern of energy in the air. In a smooth motion, he heaved Xavier back up, holding him aloft with the spear. “What are you…?”

Xavier initiated the spell pattern, casting a variant of the Wind Blade spell he’d developed with Justin in the Roving Seed Base. The blade swept through the air toward the centre of the staff.

He needed to break the weapon—break the connection.

Arrack raised an eyebrow, looking more curious than alarmed. He didn’t try to stop the spell. He simply watched as it slammed into the shaft of his staff and did… nothing. For the first time since Xavier had met the man, Arrack looked baffled. His mouth opened, forehead creasing, lost for words.

It didn’t work…

Of course it didn’t work. Xavier wasn’t thinking straight. He couldn’t slice through an A Grade weapon with a spell like that! He was strong, but without being in his territory with two galaxies behind him… He wasn’t that strong.

Before Arrack could say a word, a dozen other spell patterns snapped into existence around the room. Soul apparitions materialised and soared through the air. Heavy Telekinetic pushes tried to ease Xavier off the spear, but Arrack’s hold was too strong. The soul apparitions hurt the man, the Soul Damage making him flinch, but it didn’t hurt enough.

A blue, glowing shield appeared, stopping the other attacks in their tracks.

Arrack didn’t even look angry, just perplexed.

This isn’t working. This has to work!

Arrack was too strong. Nothing Xavier could throw at him, not while he had a chance to heal from it or block it, was enough. If Xavier didn’t do something different, Arrack might knock him unconscious again.

Might even kill him, just like Xavier said he should.

I have to do something… but what?

Looking through his Immortal Soul lens, he felt all the energy in the room. He’d seen the energy of that blue shield coalesce. He saw the telekinetic energy pushing down on him.

Oh.

Xavier had controlled raw Celestial Energy outside his body, and it was after he’d created more than a dozen spell patterns that he realised the enormity of what he’d done. Actively drawing Celestial Energy into one’s body was one thing; the raw Celestial Energy in the air wasn’t claimed.

The act of cultivation drew that energy toward the Denizen or Cultivator.

But there was a huge difference between drawing unclaimed energy into one’s channels and making that energy do something outside one’s body. Even if the Celestial Energy was his—and he would have to cycle it through his channels for that—he couldn’t simply push the energy outward and still control it.

That’s what spells were for.

And yet I did it. So, what if…

Xavier’s focus shifted. He narrowed the Immortal Soul lens toward the telekinetic energy. It was Intelligence Energy, with a bit of Willpower Energy thrown into the mix. The energy was A Grade—he could sense that quite clearly.

With his Living Soul, he nudged a drop of the energy, tried to manipulate it out of the sequence to dissipate the spell.

The energy resisted.

It shouldn’t have needed to—Xavier shouldn’t have been able to impact it at all.

Yet he’d felt it, the tiniest little speck of A Grade energy, shivering in the air where he’d nudged. He couldn’t control it like he could control the Celestial Energy—it was too strong, like the Void Energy he’d tried to store in his Void Core had been too strong before he and Xavier Prime had separated it.

Separate… One point of A Grade energy—that’s one-hundred points of C Grade energy.

I need to drill deeper.

Xavier narrowed the lens further, focusing inside the dense point of energy. When he did, he didn’t merely see a hundred points of energy—he saw thousands.

The energy here was raw, pure.

The A Grade point of energy he was looking at was Intelligence Energy, and there were a hundred thousand F Grade points inside it.

Digging deeper down to the building blocks that made up the Intelligence Energy in the first place, he found millions of points of raw Celestial Energy.

He scattered them.

But diverting one drop of A Grade energy wasn’t enough. The weight still pushed down at him as heavily as ever.

And so, he did it again. And again. A hundred times—a thousand times.

More.

The weight lightened. He couldn’t let up.

Xavier locked out everything else. The pain of the spear in his chest. The grief at Palini’s death. All other thoughts or sensations were pushed away as he scattered point after point after point of A Grade Energy until finally the weight was gone.

But that wasn’t enough—he snapped a spell pattern into existence. A variant of Heavy Telekinesis, one that allowed him to use it on himself.

And he shoved himself upward.

Straight off the spear.

Arrack’s shocked face was frozen, staring upward, locked out of the field.

Xavier flapped his broken wings but it was no use. He slammed down to the stone floor.

It was only then that he realised the cost of what he’d done.

Scattering that energy hadn’t come freely.

His Living Soul had directed every drop—and it had done it with his Soul Energy.

His reserve was all but depleted.
