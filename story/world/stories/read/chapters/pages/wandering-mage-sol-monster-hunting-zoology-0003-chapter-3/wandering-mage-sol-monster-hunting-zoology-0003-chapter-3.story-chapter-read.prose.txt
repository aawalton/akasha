“Tressa Windshade,” the dragon said. Its voice was deep and gravelly, its words drawn out in a long growl as it stared down at her. “Child. What do you see?”

Her eyes moved away from its gaze as she noticed the colors blending more and swirling even stronger. She couldn’t even fathom the fact that the dragon was speaking. It was stone. How could it be speaking, let alone moving? Its serpentine body coiled around the statue of the Goddess Emera, its claws opened and closed as it gripped its mistress, who still remained a still statue.

Then, its neck stretched forward, over Emera’s shoulder and down, closer to Tressa, until she could feel hot breath touching her face.

“Child,” it repeated. “What do you see?”

She jumped, words caught in her throat at the overwhelming sight.

“Speak, child.”

“Um… colors,” she squeaked.

“What colors?”

“Blue and gold mostly. But... White, red, pink, green… there’s so many more, all the colors I could think to say,” she said, her voice shaking.

“Hmmmm. The last one to see my aura was an elf, in what you call the Age of Myth. Tell me, have you always been able to see mana?”

“Y-Yes!” she said. “I noticed at a young age. It’s something I thought I was just imagining at first, but as I got older, I was able to see it clearly.”

“Hmmmmm. Only those well attuned to this world’s energy can see mana so clearly. Some great mages can fathom the blue and gold, but not as you can, child.”

A long pause followed as the dragon fell into deep thought, and it pulled its neck all the way back to rest its snout over Emera’s shoulder once more. It continued to “hmmmm,” in thought, watching Tressa with its gold and blue eyes.

“Lord of Dragons, Glormect,” Ornel spoke up with a steady, respectful voice.

“Ornel Laten,” the dragon said. “Still guiding the young to me, I see.”

“Yes,” he said, bowing his head in reverence and respect to the dragon, whose stone eyes watched him intensely. “Though it has been so long since you’ve awoken like this. I am glad to see you again as you are.”

They watched as Glormect turned and stared at Tressa, his eyes searching her up and down as if looking at something deep within that none could see.

“This child, Tressa, is unique. With strength I have not seen in a long time. Yes, a very long time,” Glormect said and paused as if thinking of the past. His body shifted as he moved around the statue of his mistress once again.

“Indeed,” the dragon continued. “This must be the child It spoke of. Your soul is different. Powerful. Most powerful.” The dragon spoke in such a way it seemed to be speaking more to himself or something they couldn’t see. His body continued to slither along the statue until he stilled once again, now looking from behind Emera’s waist, claws curled lightly around one ankle and one on her opposite hip, and his eyes returned to Tressa. “I wonder…”

“My lord?” Ornel asked.

“Hmmmmm…” the dragon rumbled in a gravelly voice again, his eyes staring deep into Tressa’s.

Tressa stared back in confusion, her eyes now unable to move away from its body. She had no clue what it was talking about or why.

“Hmmmmm. This child will be watched upon. Yes, as will I.” The dragon spoke with much thought in its voice. “Tressa Windshade, your trials will be of three. Though your 200th birthday is five years away, I have called you here to give you the first. You shall spend the next five years creating a spell, one of great power, and you shall return to me on the day you turn 200. Then three days after you will display it for all to see. I shall then give your second trial.”

“Create a… spell?” Tressa asked. She had already done so in secret, but surely the dragon meant something else.

The dragon raised up behind Emera, its body shifting softly until it could rest its snout over her shoulder again. “We will be watching you. Yes, as will they. Show us the gifts that you possess. Talent so raw that they shall even watch upon you. Show us, Tressa Windshade, the power and skill that you hold.”

“What?” she asked, staring back. “I don’t understand. What do you mean about inventing a spell?”

Its eyes settled on her. “Have you not already done so? You have a keen eye and a creative mind. So tell me, have you not already tampered with the runes of old?”

She flinched and grimaced, glancing over to Ornel. “I…” she coughed. “May have dabbled… a little… Uh, studied!” She coughed again, but then as she glanced over to the elder, she realized how ridiculous she was being. Worrying about such a comparatively frail being when she was in front of a DRAGON.

“Hmmmm… Good. Learn more, and show us your skill,” the dragon said.

The dragon coiled around the body of Emera once more until returning to its original place, claws gripping the edge of the platform beneath her feet, and then its body lost its color and became hard like stone once more, as if it had never moved.

Silence fell through the room, but Tressa was still stuck just staring at the statue, confused.

A new spell? Power? And when he said they will be watching, who was he talking about? Gifts? Strength? What?

Ornel spoke slowly. “That… has never happened before.”

“It hasn’t?” Tressa asked.

“Never. He normally only turns his head and lists the first trial, but never has he moved as such nor presented the first trial years ahead of their birthday. And I wonder what he meant by ‘they’? Tressa, child, it seems you have brought the attention of something powerful.”

The way he stared at her still made her feel nervous. “Have I done something wrong?” she asked. She knew messing with the runes was forbidden and highly illegal. The dragon had technically just sold her out, and it had been such a secret for so long she couldn’t believe it was now out there for anyone to know what she’d been up to.

Ornel gave her a knowing smile. “Tressa, you do know that as part of our tradition and my role in overseeing the trials, I am not to speak of anything that is said in this room. Not even to the High Council. Just as it is against our laws for any other trial takers to tell of their own experiences in this room.”

His smile was so kindly that it cast away her doubts. “So… I’m not in trouble?”

“Child, honestly. A dragon wakes specifically to speak to you, and you still think that? But listen to me, Tressa, be sure that you continue to hide your tampering with runes. The law is still the law, after all, and as far as my ability to speak on the topic, it goes both ways. I can’t tell of Glormect and what he said here today. Not even in your defense, if you are to get into trouble.”

“Oh… I see,” Tressa said. She figured as much. “But how am I to do what the dragon has asked of me? I’m not sure what kind of spell he is looking for that would satisfy the trial.”

“That will be for you to decide, child, but I’m sure you will figure things out.”

Ornel led her out of the room, and with a wave of his hand the doors shut behind him. He led her all the way back to where her parents still waited.

“Tressa,” her mother said, “How did things go?”

“I um… well, it seems my trials are beginning,” Tressa said to them.

“So soon? But your 200th birthday is still five years away,” her father said.

Ornel smiled. “Lord Glormect apparently believed she required the time. I have overseen these trials for many years, Lord Windshade. What occurred today was... unusual.”

Her father's eyes sharpened. “Unusual?”

“Enough that I suspect the gods themselves may be watching.”

Altex looked down at Tressa differently then—not with his usual disapproval, but something she couldn't quite comprehend.

“I see… So what exactly did happen?” her father asked.

Ornel chuckled softly. “Now, now, my lord. You know the law.”

Altex cleared his throat. “Ah. Yes, of course. My apologies, Elder.”

“All we can speak of is that Tressa’s trials have begun early, and Lord Glormect has given her five years to prepare for the first.”

They left the library after that, Tressa walking with her parents, but her steps feeling a bit out of sequence all the way home, and not just because of the cumbersome dress she wore.

“Welcome home, My Lord and Ladies,” Maikia said as they came through the front door, several of the other maids, including Leimia, beside her as they all bowed in respect. “The chefs should be just about finished if you would all like to sit down for dinner.”

“Wonderful,” Lucyia said. “I’m famished.”

Tressa didn’t feel hungry; food was the furthest thing from her mind at the moment. Ornel had said the gods themselves may be watching her.

Tressa said, “Actually, if it’s alright, I think I’d rather go up to my room.”

Her mother frowned. “Are you sure? You haven’t eaten yet today, since you left earlier this morning.”

“It’s alright, I really just want to… think some things over.”

“Um,” Leimia squeaked. “Should I bring a plate to your room, Tressa—ah, Lady Tressa!”

“Yes, that would be nice. Thank you Leimia,” Tressa said, then she bowed to her mother and father. “May I be excused?”

“Of course,” her mother said. “Rest well, my daughter.”

Tressa went up to her room and pulled off the dress, not waiting for any maids to come do it for her, but set it on the edge of the bed and changed into something more comfortable.

Soon, the door opened before a soft knock and it wasn’t Leimia but her mother, carrying a plate of food herself.

“I thought I’d come sit with you, if that’s alright,” she said as she set the covered plate on a table.

“Sure, that’s okay.”

“I just thought we should talk a moment. I won’t overwhelm you, as I’m sure you have a lot to think about. But Tressa, even if Ornel can’t say much, it is obvious something’s happened. Word will spread quickly that you’ve been called early, and I suspect there will be rumors that the gods may be on the verge of a blessing.”

“A blessing?” She’d only heard of that happening in stories, of gods blessing mortals. If that happened to her, she would become a demi like the ones from myths.

“Mother, that’s not possible. The gods stopped giving blessings thousands of years ago,” Tressa said, having read the old tales. Or, what was left of them, as most had been lost when the Mad King burned part of the library.

“Yes. But we have cut ourselves off from the rest of the world. Many have great hatred for we High Elves after the Mad King’s wars. Yet the world has kept turning beyond our borders, and during that time there is a possibility that there have been more blessings.”

Tressa’s fist clenched. This was too much.

“My dear, the dragon’s interest in you is no small thing. Your world is about to change.”

“Yes… I understand that, Mother,” she said, feeling numb. Her dream of being free to explore the world freely felt like it was drifting further and further away. “So what will happen?”

“The council will discuss, and then your father and I will have words.”

“Do I have a say?”

“Of course. But it may also depend on what King Faldin wishes as well.”

She swallowed hard, trying to push the lump in her throat down.

She could see it now, her father going on about how this was a sign from the gods wanting her to join the council or the temple and stand up to her duties. Then she would truly be stuck in their grasp. But she didn’t want anything like that.

“Mother, I don’t wish to join the temple, nor the court, nor the council, nor castle. Not even the library. I don’t want to feel trapped. I… I want to go out there!” she said, pointing out the windows. “I want to learn what’s on the other side. See things I’ve never seen before. Learn new magics, learn of races, plants, monsters, and animals. There is so much I don’t know. I can’t be what father wants. I can’t!”

Lucyia stared at her daughter, catching the look in her eyes at the mention of the castle, and, in truth, that worried her.

She knew of her daughter’s great talent. The details she would pick up. Playing with creatures not even Lucyia or any elf could see. She believed the ‘friends’ Tressa would talk to were beings of her imagination. But as she grew, she began to believe that wasn’t true. No, she had sight, a blessing to see mana in its true form.

Tressa stared at her hands, and silence filled the room as they had no more words to speak.

“Mother?” Tressa asked after a long silence.

“Yes, my child?”

“I’d like to go to bed now. I’m quite tired. Even before all this, it had been an early day for me.”

“Of course, but make sure you eat your dinner.” Her mother reached over, pulled Tressa into a hug, and kissed her forehead. “You will do well, my child.”

“Thank you, Mother,” Tressa said.

She squeezed her shoulder once more and gave her a smile before turning and leaving the room.

Tressa ate her dinner, though she barely tasted it, and fell asleep surprisingly easily.

The sleep itself was another matter. She tossed and turned throughout the night, only to wake early, just as the sun was barely beginning to rise, and long before the maids would come for her.

She tiptoed to her door and listened for anyone in the hall. She then locked it and quietly moved around her room before placing her hand on the bookshelf and casting a spell to lighten it. It moved easily, almost as if it were on wheels as she pushed it away from the wall. Beneath, there was an obviously loose stone slab. She lifted the heavy tile with a soft scrape, revealing a small hollow she had carved out years ago.

Inside lay a stack of leather-bound notebooks.

Most were harmless enough, just her field notes on plants and monsters. The sort of curiosities her father would merely sigh at and call a waste of time.

But the other notebooks sat at the very bottom, hidden with far more care. If anyone found them, she would be in serious trouble.

They contained her rune experiments. There were pages and pages of failed spell diagrams, pages filled with scratched-out attempts to understand how magic truly worked rather than simply memorizing the spells already written in the library.

Her fingers hesitated as the dragon’s words echoed in her mind.

Invent a new spell.

Tressa bit her lip, then pulled one of the forbidden notebooks free and slid it into her satchel along with her current monster journal. She grabbed her favorite metal quill and put that in as well. Then, she slid the stone slab and bookcase back into place.

Going back to the lake would help her think. It was quiet there, and peaceful. Far away from her father, the high council, and the uncomfortable questions that were surely coming thanks to the dragon’s summons.

Besides, if she were expected to invent a spell, she would need somewhere she could experiment without blowing up part of the Windshade family estate.

Shaking her head, she pushed those thoughts aside.

Alright, back to the lake.

She changed into her explorer outfit, slipped on her leather boots, tied her silver-blonde hair back into a ponytail with a linen cord, and carefully opened the door. After a quick glance up and down the hall, she slipped into the servants’ hall, knowing it would be nearly empty while everyone prepared for breakfast.
