The room was silent, except for the gentle chime of a teacup against its saucer on the table. The atmosphere was… tense. Not a suspension of foreboding; there was no anxiety. Instead, the hint of mystery, an undercurrent of intrigue, settled over the four of them.

Veronica and Gwen sat on a couch on one side of the meeting table. On the other, Galland had stood up and taken residence next to Tomas, future lord of Ronswick.

Gwen sat confused, understandably so. She had never met the young lord of Ronswick before. Nor had she had the opportunity to have gotten Galland’s acquaintance. Her surprise, tinged with a lack of concern, was justified. She was essentially at an important gathering, where she knew little, but was not unaware of the important context behind it all.

No, the trophy of shock, confusion, and surprise—they all belonged to Veronica.

“Why… are you here, Galland?” Veronica asked, breaking the silence.

To her surprise, the guest of Lord Tomas was none other than the person she had been actively working to save for the past few days.

But… why exactly was he here? She was only supposed to come by and pick up her identification cards. To deal with the matter regarding the two unsavory merchants that tried to ambush her, but had inadvertently attacked Nasha.

But upon her arrival, both Lord Tomas and Galland had seemed to be waiting; they were both expecting her.

“I’m here to actually speak with you, Veronica. I was informed of some important business I needed to discuss here with Lord Tomas with you,” he replied.

“What business?” Veronica asked. “I thought you were unable to help with my contract with the Mage Tower.”

He gave a small nod. “That’s correct. At least, from a legal standpoint.”

“What?”

He gestured lightly to his right. “Perhaps it’s best if Lord Tomas explains.”

Both Veronica and Gwen turned their attention to him.

Tomas adjusted his coat, straightening the inner layer of his tunic. “I assume you were informed of why I asked you here?”

Veronica nodded. “I was told you wanted to discuss the merchants. And my identification.”

Tomas inclined his head, though it felt more like acknowledgment than agreement.

“I did intend to speak about the merchants. However, the matter of identification is… not what you expect.”

Veronica tilted her head slightly. “What do you mean?”

He reached into his coat, producing two small identification cards. He slid them both to the center of the table.

“Do you recognize these?”

She leaned forward, brows creasing as she focused on the pictures.

“These are the IDs of those two bandits,” Gwen said, leaning in as well. “The ones who attacked Nasha.”

She was right. Veronica recognized them immediately. They had been taken from the men’s bodies.

“That is correct, Miss Gwen,” Tomas said. “These are forged identification cards—the ones those two were carrying.”

“Are these…” Veronica murmured, lifting her gaze back to him. “Are these what you wanted to discuss?”

“Precisely.”

“A few days ago, at the gates of Ronswick,” Galland added, “you mentioned that a pair of merchants caused a disturbance. Is that correct?”

Veronica nodded. “Yes. Two merchants who offered a commission to retrieve a wagon of goods in the forest.”

Galland gave a short hum, nodding once. “It is not a wild conclusion to ascertain that the owner of these two identification cards—they were working with those two merchants. Wouldn’t you say so?”

Veronica nodded.

“From Nasha’s account,” Gwen continued, “they had been waiting inside the wagon the entire time under some hidden compartment. They waited to ambush us, but Nasha had gotten there first to—” she hesitated.

Technically, Nasha was there to steal their commission.

“—she happened to be nearby. She was attacked in our place.”

“You mentioned that name earlier,” Galland said. “Who is Nasha?”

“She’s a singer in the city,” Veronica replied. “She also works as a rogue.” She glanced briefly at Tomas. “She’s the mercenary I encountered in the forest.”

“Ah. I see,” Tomas said.

Veronica turned back to Galland. “But yes. I’m certain those two were working with the merchants from that morning.”

“Mm.”

A few seconds passed in silence as he studied her.

He felt… different. He wasn’t as meek as when she first met him back in High Crates. While his demeanor was the same: hunched, shoulders sagged—his presence had thoroughly changed.

“Veronica. A few days ago, you came to High Crates requesting my assistance with the Mage Tower contract, correct?”

She nodded.

“You spoke of an intelligence network. One that was monitoring me. That was how you came to the conclusion that I was a suitable pick for you?”

Once more, she nodded.

“That was… a lie, wasn’t it?” he asked.

Veronica’s eyes tensed. There was silence as she examined him.

What was he getting at?

He shut his eyes briefly and shook his head. “No need to answer that,” he said. “It was a rhetorical question.”

She pursed her lips. What exactly was going on? Why the sudden change in personality? Now things had become an interrogation?

“You’ve been looking into the Scavenger Transport Company, haven’t you?” he asked.

She froze. “What? How did you…”

“You’re not particularly subtle,” he said evenly. “Since your visits to the notary office and their headquarters, security in both locations has tightened. Clerks and junior members have grown… reserved. Much more tight-lipped.”

She creased her brows. “Why are you… what do they have to do with anything?”

“Before I answer,” he said, straightening slightly, “allow me to ask you something.”

He met her gaze directly.

“What is your goal? Why involve yourself with them?”

Silence lingered as Veronica considered her answer.

There was no real reason to hide it. Galland, in the future, had proven himself trustworthy.

“Because I believe the Scavengers had a hand in your debt,” she said.

“My debt?” he asked, one brow lifting.

“The one you owe to High Crates,” she continued. “The shipment to Lilybed three years ago. I don’t think it was a legitimate attack. I think it was staged. You were set up in a ploy—used as a scapegoat while they profited from the outcome.”

Galland and Tomas exchanged a brief glance.

Then Galland leaned back slightly.

“It seems,” he said after a moment, “you may be an ally after all.”

Veronica frowned. “What?”

He steepled his fingers, resting them lightly in his lap.

“I am aware of the Scavengers’ operations,” he said. “And I am fully aware that I was used in one of their schemes.”

Veronica blinked. “You… knew?”

He nodded. “I’ve been monitoring them for the past two and a half years.”

She didn’t know what to say.

Galland had actually known he was being used? He knew that the shipment from three years ago was staged? That he was actually the mastermind, who knew what had happened?

But… why?

Three years wasn’t a trivial number; it was a long time. To endure restriction, having his freedom chained to High Crates… why?

“You seem a bit confused,” Galland said, disrupting her thoughts. “I suppose I should explain.”

She nodded.

There was obviously something she was missing.

“When the incident occurred three years ago, I knew almost immediately that something was wrong,” he began. “The route we selected was not a risky one. I’m very diligent when reading documents. That route Helm and I chose should have been safe. There was no report of bandit activity.”

He rested his hands together.

“And yet, the attack was precise. Too precise. The report we received afterward contained details that… did not align. None of it made sense when cross-referenced with what we had studied.”

He looked at her more closely. “You went to speak with Helm in the prison, didn’t you?”

“You… you even know about that?”

“Miss Veronica… it is quite uncommon for a citizen to request a meeting with a prisoner, especially one they have no prior connection to,” Lord Tomas said. “And using my father’s seal… anyone who sees it is required to report its use. It is a precaution in case it is misused or stolen.”

Galland gave a small nod. “Even with my limited freedom, it was not difficult to follow your movements and make a few inquiries.”

He paused, his expression tightening slightly. “I mean no offense, but… your methods of investigation are rather poor.”

Veronica’s jaw tightened as her cheeks flushed with embarrassment.

She knew she was bad, but… that bad?

“To return to the matter,” Galland continued, “I believe invisible ink was used—likely provided through the notary office. The copy of the report Helm and I received had been altered. We had no indication that there was a bandit settlement along the route.”

“So you knew it was staged nearly immediately,” Veronica said.

“Yes,” Galland replied. “A deliberately orchestrated ploy.”

He leaned back slightly. “At the time, I had no proof. Only inconsistencies. I was left with two choices. So I made a decision.”

“You accepted the debt instead of fighting it.”

He nodded. “There was something off about the entire situation. I suspected there was no path to overturn it—not within the time before the ruling was finalized. So I accepted the debt to preserve what freedom I could. Helm… did not.”

“Over the past few years, Galland has been working with me to uncover the corruption within our city,” Lord Tomas added.

“Corruption?” Veronica repeated.

“Precisely,” Tomas said.

“The matter with High Crates is not an isolated incident,” Galland continued. “I am merely one pawn in one of their schemes. In truth, they operate across several fronts—multiple operations involving different groups throughout the city, and even beyond it. Including places like Lilybed. We’re working to stop any more of this madness.”

Veronica frowned. “Wait… you’re saying this isn’t just about freeing yourself. You’re trying to dismantle all of it?”

Galland met her gaze and gave a slow nod.

“I know of many others who are in my situation. Some have become indebted slaves. Taking advantage of loopholes in national law. Evidence is all fabricated and tampered with, leading to false indentures. Leaving something like this alone… I can’t find it in good conscience to sit around and do nothing.”

“Worse than that, however, are those taken by other methods,” Lord Tomas chimed.

“Other methods?” Veronica repeated.

Galland pointed at the two ID cards on the table. “These two men. They are actually citizens from a small town northwest of here. A town called Sirailles.”

He paused for a moment, allowing both Veronica and Gwen to inspect the cards once more.

“Both of them were kidnapped and forced into labor within the underground market.”

“They… became slaves?” Gwen asked.

She was lost in the deeper meaning behind the conversation, but she did her best to keep up. She could vaguely understand the situation with all the context provided.

“They were regular workers who disappeared from town roughly four years ago. All of their belongings went missing, presumably stolen, including these identification cards,” Galland explained. “Like them, there are many others facing the same situation.”

“How many?” Veronica asked.

He frowned before giving his answer. “From what I’ve collected… there are roughly four others indentured like me, two others, excluding Helm, who have been imprisoned, and nearly twenty people, here and from other neighboring towns, that have either died or been sold into slavery.”

“That’s… horrible,” Gwen said softly.

“Indeed, it is,” Tomas said. “And that’s only the extent of what we’ve been able to find.”

“The issue with all of this is that the Scavenger’s methods are different nearly each time,” Galland explained. “For my situation, it was using me as a scapegoat by falsifying documents and staging a bandit attack.”

Lord Tomas nodded. “Other times, it may be the poisoning of a noble by a maid. Or perhaps a subtle mine cave-in leading to tragic ‘losses’ of life.”

“And you’ve been doing this for the past three years? Working behind the scenes, gathering as much information as you can to take down this… syndicate organization?”

Galland nodded. “Truthfully, I have enough to pay back my debt already. However, if I do that, I lose my value to them. I would most likely be ‘dealt’ with afterward. I can’t take that risk when there are people who need my help—so I’ve endured.”

“The two merchants that we captured,” Tomas chimed. “They are renowned professional forgers. They can take identification cards and repurpose them. And it seemed they had business to do in our city.”

“So that attack…” Veronica said slowly.

“The two guys who attacked Nasha… they were trying to steal her identification cards?”

“That is the conclusion we have come up with,” Lord Tomas replied. “In cases of adventurers or the inexorable, their deaths or disappearances are much more easily written off.”

“Because if a regular citizen disappeared off the streets, there would be questions,” Gwen stated.

“Correct,” Lord Tomas replied.

“I’ve come near to being able to take down Scavengers and all those involved. However… it’s the others that I am worried about.”

“The others?” Gwen asked.

“While I am in indenture, it is easy to unravel cases like my own and free those imprisoned, like Helm,” Galland said. “However, those who have been taken and sold into the underground… that is a far more difficult matter.”

“What do you mean?” Veronica asked.

“Even if the Scavengers fall, everything tied to them will react,” he replied.

Lord Tomas gestured between them. “We’ve been preparing to strike on multiple fronts at once. That includes the underground market.”

He paused. “My father is aware of the situation. He has entrusted me with handling it, as it extends beyond our city and into neighboring territories.”

“I appreciate your willingness to help, Veronica,” Galland said. “But this is a long-term operation. If we act too soon, they will retreat—destroy records, abandon routes, and erase what evidence remains.”

“Only a few months ago did we identify a major backer behind them,” Tomas added. “Galland has traced several shipments involving highly valuable contraband. Someone within the city is providing them with plenty of liquidity to fund their operations. These forged IDs in front of us, we assume, are used to prepare more scapegoats as insurance.”

At the mention of highly valuable contraband, Veronica and Gwen exchanged a brief glance.

Galland caught it immediately.

“Is something the matter?”

Veronica pressed her lips together.

Aw, shit.

“Err… forgive me, I’m not entirely sure how to say this.”

Both men looked at her, puzzled.

“So… these scapegoats you mentioned. I’ve learned they have a term for them. They call them ‘boots’,” Veronica said.

Galland’s brow lifted. “How do you—”

“I’ve been investigating something else involving Gwen,” she continued. “Something that… happens to overlap. And I believe I know what that contraband is.”

Tomas leaned forward slightly. “You do? What are they transporting?”

“Mana crystals,” Veronica said. “Large quantities.”

“Mana crystals?” they both echoed.

She exhaled quietly. This was the difficult part.

“Yes. And… I may have gotten more involved than I intended. I didn’t realize the two of you were deliberately waiting and planning an operation like this.”

Galland’s gaze sharpened. “What do you mean?”

Veronica coughed lightly into her hand. “Hypothetically… would it be possible to accelerate your plans?”

They both stared at her.

“Oh boy,” Gwen muttered under her breath.

“I may have… killed their supplier.”

“What?” they said in unison.

“And I’ve also dealt with two assassins they sent after me.”

She looked over at Lord Tomas. “You… might have heard about an explosion occurring at an inn in the southern part of town a couple of hours ago, right?”

An awkward silence followed.

Veronica reverted her gaze back to both of them. “And… I may have impersonated that supplier and spoken directly with Dain, who is directly involved with the Scavengers. He’s sending two of his men tomorrow. To ‘protect’ me.”

Neither of them moved. Neither of them spoke. Neither of them blinked.

“And since the actual supplier is dead… I was planning to ambush them and extract information. But if that happens…”

“Then they’ll know something is wrong,” Gwen finished for her.

“Exactly,” Veronica said. “Even if it doesn’t happen—it’ll be suspicious that the supplier is now ‘missing.’ So… whatever you’ve been preparing for…”

She winced slightly.

“I think your deadline just became tomorrow.”

Both Tomas and Galland stared at her, stunned.

“…my bad?” Veronica said, shrinking even smaller.
