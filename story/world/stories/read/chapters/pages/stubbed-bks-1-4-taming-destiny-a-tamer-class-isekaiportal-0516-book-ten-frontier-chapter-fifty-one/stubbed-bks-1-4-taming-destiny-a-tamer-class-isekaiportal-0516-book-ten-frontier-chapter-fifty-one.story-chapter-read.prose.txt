To my surprise, there are three notifications available. I start with the oldest one.

Congratulations!

You have earned a new Skill: Plant-Shaping

“I did it!” I exclaim loudly enough in satisfied victory that it accidentally leaks through the Bond, catching the attention of several of my companions.

Did what? Lathani asks curiously, River and Bastet echoing her question.

I explain and everyone listening congratulates me enthusiastically, if not in complete understanding. I do my best to accept their words graciously, all the while itching to find out more about Plant-Shaping – and why my internal matrix suddenly changed.

What feels like a long time later, but is probably only a couple of minutes, I focus on getting more information about Plant-Shaping.

Plant-Shaping

Observation and experimentation has taught you about the reciprocal relationship between plants and their environment, a relationship that is at times mutually beneficial, and at others competitive. You are now capable of forming connections with plants at all stages of development and Shaping them to your desires if you are able to communicate sufficiently with them and offer enough of what they need. Better results will be experienced if you have been implicit in the growth of a plant from the beginning of its existence. Your Fire Affinity has an unpredictable relationship with plants and may make connections with some plants more difficult than others.

I frown at the description. While most of it is as expected, some raise interesting questions, and the bit about Fire Affinity is completely new, though it does tie in a little with my thoughts before the notification came through.

I suppose fire isn’t just the sunlight that nourishes the plant – it’s also the wildfire that destroys it, I muse. Though that’s not the whole story, either.

I remember learning about how wildfires are actually part of a forest’s natural development – the fire transforms dead leaves thickly littering the forest floor into nutrient-rich ash that enables new plants to grow. I saw it myself after destroying the vast majority of the vine-strangler forest – it didn’t take too long for the grey ash field to be covered in green once more, and the samurans’ crops grew nicely with the fertiliser. Not to mention that there are some plants that only grow if their seeds are burned.

I find it interesting to consider how the plants’ reciprocal relationship extends even to the fire that consumes them. It’s an admirable trait – their capacity to turn death into new life. Some plants die, but the forest is stronger for it.

My eyes linger on where it talks about ‘better results’. The beginning of its existence…. Is that as a seed…or before? After all, arguably, the materials of the seed exist long before the seed itself is formed. I’ll have to speak to Elarion – maybe it becomes easier to manipulate plants the more generations a mage has been in a relationship with their ancestors.

Actually, getting tips from Elarion or one of the other Plant Mages is probably a good idea. I might have gained Plant-Shaping now, but that doesn’t mean I know everything about plants – far from it!

Closing that message for now, I open the second notification that had come through. And, as soon as I do, I realise exactly why I’d felt that cascade of Energy go through me – it wasn’t a level; it was something even better.

Achievement awarded: Basic Element Master Shaper

You have proven yourself a true elemental mage, observing and experimenting with the world until you have understood the basic building blocks that form it and the way they are intricately entwined. You have gained all seven of the basic element Shaping Skills: Fire, Earth, Air, Water, Metal, Plant, Flesh and have advanced at least one of them to Master.

As a result of your efforts and understanding, you have been awarded +15% Wisdom, +10% Intelligence. +5% Willpower. Optional merged Skills available.

Though the achievement name is very awkward, the juxtaposition of ‘master’ and ‘basic’ so closely together making me briefly confused, the achievement itself is excellent. That it offers percentage boosts is already a sign that this is a top-tier achievement; that it offers three of them is as good as my Skill Forger achievement – but with even bigger percentages. Fifteen percent to Wisdom?! I check my stats to see the effect.

Intelligence has gone up by six points, Willpower by eight, and Wisdom by seven – but because of the way I use Willpower Leverage to increase my Wisdom, that’s effectively gone up by eight as well.

Twenty-two effective points in one single action! That’s almost the equivalent of four levels. And the best thing is that they’re percentage increases, so they’ll keep paying off the more points I put into the stats.

I briefly dive into my Core space and see a noticeable difference. The sun might be heading towards the horizon again, most of the day passed in contemplation and experimentation, but I don’t think anyone would debate that this is the best use of my time.

I think grateful thoughts at Sarran and Loran. If they hadn’t pushed me to delegate, reminded me that my personal strength is as important for the settlement as my leadership, I doubt I’d have felt able to spend all this time on a single Skill. It would have taken me a lot longer, and I might never have gained Plant-Shaping, losing out on this achievement.

It makes me wonder what other achievements I might have missed out on because of my focus on creating a settlement rather than getting stronger.

I push that thought to the side – I had a discussion with Nicholas on the subject before leaving Moriax, and I recall some of the key points now. Apparently, though there are some achievements that everyone will get if they pass a certain benchmark, others will only be achieved if they approach it in a certain way, or without knowing that there is an achievement. And there’s certainly no exhaustive list available for me to decide how to optimise my actions.

No, I’ve done well enough to get as many achievements as I have – far outclassing even the average noble who usually only gets one or two achievements in their life. I just need to keep pushing the boundaries – that’s where most of them can be found. On that, Nicholas’ words and my own experience agree.

As for feeling regret about concentrating on the settlement, arguably it’s only my earlier efforts to help stabilise our new home that have meant I can delegate to my Stewards now.

I’m also intrigued by the last line – ‘optional merged Skills available’? Is that what the third notification is about? Only one way to find out!

When I open the final notification, my eyebrows climb up my forehead.

Congratulations!

You have gained all seven of the basic element Shaping Skills: Fire, Earth, Air, Water, Metal, Plant, Flesh. You now have several choices.

Merge Fire, Earth, Air, Water, Plant, Flesh, and Metal Shaping Skills into a single Skill: World-Shaping. This will involve an averaging of your Skill levels, but will offer considerably more flexibility and efficiency, allowing you to reshape the world at your will.

Merge all of your Shaping Skills (Earth, Air, Water, Plant, Flesh, Metal, Lava, Lightning) into a single Skill: Omni-Shaping. This will involve an averaging of your Skill levels and will offer considerably more flexibility and efficiency. An additional benefit is that, for a heavy mana cost, you will be able to Shape elements that you have not specifically learned as long as at least one of their root elements is one that you are already capable of Shaping.

Link all of your Shaping Skills (Earth, Air, Water, Plant, Flesh, Metal, Lava, Lightning) into an array in your internal matrix. Future Shaping Skills learned will automatically be added to the array. Skill levels will be maintained and the Skills will advance individually. Elements may inform each other or strengthen each other, and multi-elemental wielding will become easier, though not automatic. Elements within this array must remain balanced – too many opposing elements that are not balanced may cause catastrophic damage. You will not be able to automatically wield any element that you have not learned, however learning to use another element is easier if at least one of its root elements is one that you have already learned.

Keep the Skills separate. This will result in no change to your current situation. Your Skills will advance individually and develop their own functions relative to your use of them. Merging in the future may be possible if all Skills reach the same rank.

I just stare at the options for a long moment. Then I close the message and turn to lean against the tree, looking up at the stars and watching the moon slide in and out of passing clouds as I consider the possibilities.

Omni-Shaping seems incredibly powerful. Even without the promise of being able to wield multiple elements together, the ability to Shape elements that I haven’t specifically learned is potentially a game-changer. I spent so long trying to unlock Plant-Shaping – if I’d had Omni-Shaping, maybe I’d have just been able to go ahead and do it. If Plant-Shaping wasn’t one of the basic elements, that is.

Or Lightning-Shaping – I wouldn’t have had to make that bet with Tilathis, getting him to show me how to do it. With Omni-Shaping, I’d just have been able to…do it.

Though, that thought makes me wonder. Because so far, the only way I’ve learned how to wield magic has been by learning how to do it. As I was thinking earlier, each magic type I’ve learned so far has had its own…personality. Mood. Aspect. Whatever it should be called. What happens if I use it through its ‘root’ element?

Perhaps it would be like using lava by manipulating fire and earth. Or, if I only have one root element, like using lava by manipulating fire or earth alone.

I grimace at that thought. Lava is mana-hungry enough – I can’t imagine how hungry and unwieldy it would be if I was approaching it through fire alone. That’s obviously the biggest downside of Omni-Shaping – I have significantly more mana now than I used to, but it’s not unlimited and drawing from my companions potentially makes them vulnerable.

The other potential downside to both Omni-Shaping and World-Shaping is that they both create one Skill, averaging out the levels of all the composite Skills. That’s great for lifting new Skills like Lightning-Shaping and Plant-Shaping, but I’m almost certain that I’d end up losing the Master rank for Earth, Fire, and Flesh.

World-Shaping wouldn’t be so bad in that almost half the Skills involved are in the Master rank, and a couple of the others are in the Initiate rank. I’d probably end up with it classed as high Initiate, maybe low Journeyman, depending on how Master and Beginner are comparatively weighted.

Another downside that isn’t even mentioned is the speed of advancement. My experience with Inspect indicates that merged Skills are slow to level. With at least seven Skills part of the merged Skill, both Omni-Shaping and World-Shaping both will probably advance at a glacial pace.

However, also based on my experience with Inspect, being able to use multiple Skills simultaneously is really powerful.

My imagination runs wild. I visualise creating a field for crops – I could clear the field of weeds and parasites, ensure that the nutrient balance is correct, aerate and hydrate the soil to optimal conditions, and then keep them that way while the plant grows at a magical speed.

Or, in battle, I could call down a firestorm upon my enemies full of lightning while ensuring that my own people are kept cool and protected.

If I have the mana, of course. Both of these are clearly mage-focused Skills, expecting the person to have a massive mana pool or regeneration rate. Mine certainly isn’t shabby…but I know that Elarion, who focuses on Intelligence and Wisdom, has more in both pools than I have. Though the achievement has definitely closed the gap a bit.

I briefly consider not doing anything. The Skills work well enough as they are, and I’ve been learning how to dual-wield them as it is.

Then I dismiss that as an option. Certainly, I can learn to dual-wield, but it takes practice. If the System wants to give me a helping hand to become more badass, then I’ll happily take it. It’s just a question of which helping hand.

Which leaves one more option to consider: not combining the Skills, but setting them into some sort of array. That possibility alone sets my pulse racing – especially when I think about what the Enchanters have just pulled off.
