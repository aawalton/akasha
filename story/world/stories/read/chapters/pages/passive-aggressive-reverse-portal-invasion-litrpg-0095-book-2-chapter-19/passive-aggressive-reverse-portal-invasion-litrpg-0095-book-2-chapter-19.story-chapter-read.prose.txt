”This, my newfound friends, is where I remain. I wish you the absolute best of luck. May your prowess be overshadowed only by your fortune this day.”

Victoria beamed. “Part honesty, part hint at how you’re going to outdo us. Cocky. ¡Me gusta!”

We were standing in a canyon. It was about fifty meters wide, twice that all, with smooth walls and a polished floor that intimidated it had once been filled with rushing water. It was also, according to Fortune-Unfolds, the optimal place to expand the defenses of the Hiscariat, if only they could get the peace to do so.

The Ceaseless Fighters did come in hard. I’d just put down another monster with a simple Wave of Reflection, and already another monster was appearing on the horizon. Apparently, this was one of the main passageways from the territory of the Ceaseless and the source of the largest issue the Hiscariat had. Anything they’d have to build, they had to create under the constant, relentless onslaught of the disgusting, powerful monsters. And, as if that wasn’t enough, they did adapt. Not the singular monsters, but the collective as a whole. The moment we’d take a stand? Their numbers would increase, the types of Ceaseless would expand, with rarer types making their presence known.

“Are you good here?” I asked him. “You’re still alone.”

He smirked, waggling tufty eyebrows. “Alone, but never defenseless. But yes. You had best be gone. I’ve got company arriving in about ten minutes. Our construction will start immediately.”

The Ceaseless Fighter charging straight at me started growling as it stumbled straight through one of Victoria’s traps, activating her poison. Its beady eyes focused on her, and it veered, eyeing a new, tasty snack..

I ran over quickly, enjoying the change that my Running upgrade made to any combat activity. A thirty percent increase on top of the forty-five percent from my Improved Run Speed talent made any repositioning effortless. What would, a few weeks ago, have been a panicked scrambling to get in place, felt like a leisurely stroll.

A quick glance confirmed that I was right in between the monster and Victoria. Then I set my feet, took up position with my shield just so, and leaned back.

My timing was perfect. The beast impacted my shield teeth-first, with both sets of claws scrabbling, swinging in to provide the perfect blender.

The claws never arrived. Deflect kicked in, aided and amplified by my Shield skill, throwing back the head, adding force as the slight angle I held the shield at flung the beast upwards. I focused, waited a split second, then pierced the beast mid-air with a concentrated burst of Wave of Reflection.

“Good.” Fortune-Unfolds said. “Now, remember. We will need half a day, at least. That should be absolutely doable for somebody like you. Although, Steve, that buff you were talking about? I recommend you keep boosting Fortitude for you all. It is likely to become a grueling affair.”

Steve nodded.

He raised an admonishing tentacle. “The good news is that the Ceaseless are efficient, but slow to adjust. When you return, we will have activated traps everywhere, except for your left side when returning.” He pointed to ensure there was no doubt which side it was. “Everything else? We will handle. Twelve hours from now, I will have a hundred traps ready, and a dozen mages prepared to take the heat off your back. Also, we should have proper, fortified walls and ground the Ceaseless cannot tunnel into. We need twelve hours!”

I grinned and rolled my shoulders. “We can handle the pressure. Just remember. This is a wide canyon, and we’re not perfect. I’m sure some are going to make it past us.”

“I believe in you.” From the flippant Hiscariat, the comment was uncanny and, to be honest, disgustingly efficient.

“We’ll do our best, mate.” I simply said. Then we were off, marching down the canyon to face down an enemy that a fortress full of mages couldn’t take down.

“We’re on a runner!” Steve shouted. “Trio of Spitters incoming. Victoria, prep for poison, then we move.”

I growled, facing down the two Ceaseless Scouts ahead of me. “Well? What are you waiting for? Steve says I need to move. Are you planning to do anything, or just dance?”

It was infuriating. My Mirror of All wasn’t working. I couldn’t be sure why that was, but, by now, I had a pretty solid guess. It needed some degree of anger to amplify in order to function, and the Ceaseless just didn’t have that. Oh, the Fighters threw themselves at me without a hint of care about their self-preservation. But the Scouts, stupid, annoying Scouts, apparently worked off of a different script.

One of them finally charged. At the final moment, it swerved to the side, aiming for a debilitating low strike at my legs. I’d been expecting that, though, slamming my shield into the ground. Meanwhile, I kept an eye on the other Scout in the periphery of my vision. Right enough. Just when the first Scout tore my attention low and to the left, it leapt in, its entire disgusting body rocketing at my upper right side.

Unfortunately for the disgusting things, by now I was on to their tricks. With my shield planted in the ground, I didn’t have to focus more on that Scout. While Scouts were fast and hit hard, Fortitude was clearly not their strong suit. The wet cracking sounds behind my shield testified to the fact that my Deflect was doing just fine on its own.

Meanwhile, I dedicated my entire focus to the impending, claw-tipped doom flying straight at me. Raising my buckler against the leaping monster felt silly with the size difference – but a single step sideways changed everything. Now, the Scout was committed, flying and entirely out of range. It stretched, reached, praying mantis-like forelimb extending to reach me.

And instead of having to deal with the entire flying body of the monster, my buckler had to deal with the full force of the beast’s momentum, concentrated into that one limb. Level 0 me would’ve died in an instant, shield, armor and body pierced through in one go. Unfortunately for the Scout, I was not that person anymore.

My Improved Dodge, Athletics and Balance skills combined to ensure I was in the exact right spot. Higher Shield and Improved Resilience along with Piercing Resistance made damn sure that, when the blow did arrive, I didn’t take a single step back. Rather, my legs felt like pillars, granting me a solidity and balance that would see the world keel over before I would.

Almost as an afterthought, Concentration helped me keep enough focus on both monsters to make sure a Wave of Reflection punished the jumper, letting it crash to the ground next to me with lifeless eyes.

“It’s now, Liam!” Steve yelled.

“I got you.” I started moving backwards. Not fast enough, unfortunately. A barb shallowly pierced the side of my neck. I growled and awkwardly pulled it back out, nearly clocking myself in the head with my buckler. “They really are accurate bastards.” Two additional barbs hit my shield and pinged off, as I sped up backwards, ensuring that they couldn’t circle around and attack me from all sides.

“All poisoned.” Victoria shouted. “About four minutes, and they’ll be down.”

I stopped, checking out the monsters doggedly following us, spitting chitinous barbs at me in a continuous barrage.

I grinned at the Spitter, nonchalantly deflecting a barb with the edge of my shield. The Spitters might be accurate and timed their attacks with their fellows, but they were also slow, and didn’t really change up their approach. They were the ugliest of their kind we’d seen so far, though. They looked like skinless ant eaters, if they were made from bark and muscle. The barbs they spat were spindly and short, but arrived with surprising power.

For a good while, our offense had gone unpunished. We’d moved ahead for nearly an hour, allowing me to perfect my one-on-one game against the slow stream of incoming Ceaseless Fighters. For that entire march, our approach was slow, methodical, almost therapeutic, as I really allowed myself to get in touch with my aggressions. So many broken bones, not a single one on our side.

Then we settled in. It wasn’t entirely by choice – we’d all have preferred to get some more distance between Fortune-Unfolds and us – but the Ceaseless had caught wind of our approach, and started to adjust. There was a single narrow dip in the canyon, where they’d have to rush up-hill at us and would be limited to attacking a handful at the time.

We’d held that spot for almost an hour. Then, we’d been forced back by the first appearance of the Spitters.

We were getting better at this. After two hours of constant fighting, we had this thing down to a science. Now, I could draw things out for a good while, move back twenty meters every so often to avoid being flanked, and the Spitters would drop dead in a few minutes. No further intervention needed.

The only issue right now would be the fact that, well… the Ceaseless were increasing their pace of attack.

I lucked out with a Deflect, managing to flick a barb right back at one of the Spitters, piercing its abdomen. Usually, the spindly barbs cracked upon impact.

“How are we on everything?” I yelled. “Stamina’s good. Life’s…” I looked at the blood steadily dripping from my arm, torso, leg and, now, neck. It turned out that the damn Spitters also had some sort of anti-coagulant thing going. Still. I was only down a few hundred health. “Also fine.”

“Mana’s becoming problematic.” Victoria said. “They’re coming too fast, too many.”

It’s the only thing keeping me going.” Her hands glowed, as she activated her debuff, trapping the soil in front of a distant Ceaseless Fighter, to slow it down until the Spitters were dead. She’d done that a few times, and her timing was really improving. “But in a bit, I’ll have to use that Stamina Surge thing. Then, if I keep blazing through my mana, I’ll be out of both stamina and mana.”

I dipped to my knee, barely avoiding a barb that came in at a steeper angle than expected. Half a second later, I leapt right back up. “That is a problem.”

“Should I join the fight, perhaps?” Steve asked. “It’s frustrating to not be able to attack, just in order to grant buffs to us all.”

“No. The benefits from your buffs are too good to ignore.” It felt like there was more to it, but I was rather fighting for my life and couldn’t seem to focus. “Just pace yourself more, Victoria. I’ve got plenty of skills ready to go. I can take the brunt of the onslaught for a bit longer at a time, let you regenerate both mana and stamina when you need it.”
