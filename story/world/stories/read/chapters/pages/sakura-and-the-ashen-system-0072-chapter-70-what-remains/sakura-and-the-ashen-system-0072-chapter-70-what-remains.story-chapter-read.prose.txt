The black vein continued spreading across the floor. Faerd stared at it for several seconds, unable to understand why it had appeared at all. His vision was still swimming, his wrists were bound with rope, his ribs and side hurt with every breath, and any sudden movement brought pressure to his temples.

Across from him sat a young woman who was the Obsidian Hierophant.

The thought came before he could stop it: The threat must be eliminated.

The black line near his feet thickened, stretching into a sharp ridge. Sakura tensed. The man beside her raised a hand, and Faerd felt the familiar prickling of someone else’s magic in the air. The boy by the far wall jumped to his feet as well, while the black beast lifted his head and growled.

There was no foreign voice in Faerd’s head anymore. The familiar pressure that had forced him to keep moving was gone too, but the order had been part of his mind for too long. His body remembered the sequence on its own: find the target, judge the distance, remove obstacles, eliminate the threat.

Only now could Faerd choose not to do it.

He closed his eyes and slowly inhaled, and pain flared through his body again, especially in his ribs.

This isn’t my order, he told himself.

Faerd focused on the obsidian. Before, the magic had appeared almost reflexively whenever he decided he needed a weapon or protection. Now, for the first time, he had to consciously stop something that had already begun, and the black ridge trembled, then started to crumble.

When Faerd opened his eyes, only dark dust remained on the floor. No one in the room relaxed, and he did not blame them.

"I’m not going to attack," he said. His own voice sounded hoarse and unfamiliar. "Right now, I’m in control. If that changes... I think you’ll notice."

The man across from him slowly lowered his hand, though the magic around him did not disappear.

"That last part is reassuring."

Faerd did not recognize him right away. Gilbert. The name came with fragments of the chase, the city, the sewers, the forest, the canyon, the castle, the green mist, and roads he had walked long after his body had begged him to stop.

Sakura sat nearby. He remembered her much better. Faerd had spent too long looking for her, gathering information, checking tracks, and following routes that might lead him to the Obsidian Hierophant.

But once, before all of that, he had another life.

Maev stood outside their house with her arms crossed, pretending to be angry because he was late. Then small fingers wrapped around his hand. Tinli. Sweet little Tinli, who smelled like chamomile.

Faerd looked away. He did not know whether they were alive. Whenever he tried to remember what happened to them afterward, pain began building behind his eyes, and instead of answers came white walls, straps, bright light, and a calm voice: Again.

Faerd clenched his teeth. Far too much was missing from his memory. Empty spaces where memories should have been.

"Maev and Tinli," Sakura suddenly said.

He looked sharply at her. The old reaction came almost immediately. His fingers tensed, but no new obsidian appeared.

"Don’t."

Sakura was silent for a moment, then nodded.

"All right. Not yet."

Faerd expected more questions from her, but Gilbert spoke first.

"Then we’ll start with something else. Were you born with this magic?"

"No."

"So they gave it to you."

"You could say that..."

"I’d rather be more precise. What did they do to you, and under what circumstances did you end up with the people who did it?"

Faerd shifted slightly and immediately regretted it. Sharp pain shot through his ribs.

"Untie my hands."

"No," Gilbert said calmly. "I think that’s the one condition that isn’t up for discussion right now."

Faerd looked down at the rope, then at the remains of obsidian dust on the floor.

"It won’t save you anyway."

"I know. But it makes me feel better."

Faerd gave a faint smile, surprising even himself.

"I wasn’t a mage," he began. "Never was. As far as I know, there weren’t any mages in my family either."

The words came slowly, but the more distant past was easier to talk about.

"I served the government. First in a regular unit, then I was transferred to special squads. We dealt with mages."

Lir frowned.

"What do you mean, dealt with?"

Faerd looked at him.

"We caught them. Sometimes killed them."

The room grew quieter.

"There were groups of mages who refused to obey the law. Some of them really were dangerous. Others probably just didn’t want to follow the authorities’ demands. Back then, I didn’t ask questions like that..."

Gilbert watched him closely.

"They gave you orders?"

"Yes."

Faerd ran his tongue over his dry lips.

"I was always good at following orders. Apparently, that’s why I later became what I became."

A stone corridor appeared before his eyes. Heavy boots struck the floor. Ahead, a door was smashed open with a battering ram. Someone inside screamed. A young mage raised his hands, but Faerd knocked him to the ground before a spell could form.

Back then, he almost never asked whether the people he was ordered to arrest were actually in the wrong.

"What happened after that?" Sakura asked.

Faerd closed his eyes for a moment.

"An operation in the north. We were looking for a group of mages accused of attacking government convoys. They knew we were coming."

A ruined house, the smell of burned wood, and blood on snow returned with unexpected clarity.

"They lured us into an old fort. I realized the floor had been trapped with runes, but it was too late. The explosion killed three people. I was less lucky."

"Less lucky?" Lir repeated.

"I survived."

Faerd said it without a smile.

"My pelvis was shattered, my spine was damaged. My left leg barely moved, and I lost feeling in some of the fingers on one hand... The healers said I might be able to walk with support. If I could walk at all."

Maev sat beside his bed and held his hand. She did not cry in front of him. Tinli was not allowed into the room. They told her that her father was very tired.

"They offered me treatment. A program for people injured in the line of duty. A new method. They told me it was experimental, but there was a chance I could recover."

Gilbert slowly exhaled.

"And you agreed."

"I had a wife and a small child. At the time, the choice seemed obvious."

For the first few weeks, it really did feel like treatment. There were brews, procedures, magical constructs around the bed, and injections near his spine. Little by little, sensation began to return.

After a month, Faerd stood up. After two, he could even walk without a cane. Maev cried when she saw him on his feet. It was the last good memory he could recover completely.

"Then the treatment changed," he continued.

The familiar white room appeared again, along with straps around his wrists and black powder in a glass bowl.

"They started asking whether I could feel magic. At first, I didn’t understand what they wanted from me. Then one day, during a procedure, the first piece of obsidian rose from the floor."

Sakura tensed almost imperceptibly.

"They made you a mage?"

"I don’t know if you can call me that."

Gilbert frowned.

"If you didn’t originally have magic channels or a core, they couldn’t simply give you someone else’s power. Your body should have rejected it."

"It did."

Faerd looked down at his hands. He remembered the fever, the seizures, and the black veins beneath his skin far too well. For several days, his body could not keep food down, and once a healer calmly recorded his condition while Faerd choked on his own blood.

"So they kept changing me until my body stopped rejecting it."

No one answered.

"Sounds like they built the magic directly into your body," Gilbert said. "They didn’t give you a class. They created some kind of artificial structure."

"Maybe. I didn’t understand most of what they discussed."

"But why?"

Faerd looked at Sakura.

"I think they wanted someone who could hunt mages without physical weapons and wouldn’t depend on conventional defenses."

He fell silent.

"And someone who wouldn’t refuse an order," Gilbert added.

Faerd nodded again.

"At first, the control was ordinary. Threats, drugs, pain. Then they started working on my memory. They didn’t like it when certain orders made me hesitate."

The first memories they targeted were Maev and Tinli.

Faerd lowered his gaze.

"When those memories interfered with an order, they made me push them away. Block them. Again and again, until it became a habit."

Sakura said nothing, and Faerd was grateful for that.

"The last few months... years... I don’t know how much time passed. Time barely existed there. There were assignments, orders, and the white room. That was all. That was everything."

He did not remember every person he had killed, but the faces that remained were more than enough.

Faerd looked at Sakura.

"Then she appeared. The Obsidian Hierophant."

The old command to destroy her tried to surface in his mind again.

"They told me the Obsidian Hierophant was a threat and had to be destroyed. I was supposed to find the bearer and kill her."

"Who told you?" Gilbert asked immediately.

Faerd fell silent. Another man sometimes appeared in the white room, someone who definitely wasn’t one of the healers. He came rarely and never raised his voice, but everyone straightened as soon as he entered. Someone important. Someone with authority. Faerd tried to see his face, but pain struck his temple almost immediately. He winced.

"Don’t rush," Sakura said unexpectedly.

Faerd did not answer.

He had heard the name too many times. In the corridors. During reports. Before procedures.

Master...

Faerd raised his head.

"Nobus."

Sakura suddenly went pale.

"Say that again," Gilbert said.

"Nobus. That’s what they called him."

Faerd immediately noticed Sakura’s reaction.

"You know that name?"

Until then, Ater had barely taken part in the conversation. Now the old man slowly rose from his chair.

"Are you sure?"

Faerd turned his head toward him.

"I could forget my own face before I forgot that name. Now that I remember it... I’m absolutely certain."

Ater’s usual calm vanished from his face.

"That’s impossible."

"Why?"

The old man was silent for several seconds. Then he said quietly:

"Because Nobus died long before you were born."
