Alice awoke, and for once she was completely rested and comfortable. Her mind was deliciously blank—at least until one thought intruded into it.

She sat up so abruptly that Bella, who had been sleeping on her chest, curled up like a cat and was flung off into her lap.

"Oh dear, I'm so sorry," she said, reaching out to cuddle the now disgruntled-looking dragon.

Her awakening had also stirred the others, and they began to yawn wide with mouths full of teeth.

"Is something wrong?" Prim asked. She had wisely decided to sleep on the pillow next to Alice. Stretching her wings, she started to groom the long claws on her feet.

"I must have been more tired than I realized because I did not even pick up on it," Alice said. "That man, Davis, he wants me to get to level 10." She looked around in excitement. "That means—you know what that means, don't you?"

Numi squeaked and said, "More sub-aspects for us!"

"And two other regular aspects for you," Bella added. “I won’t be the newest to the team any longer.”

"Can I have a sub-aspect, Alice?" Numi asked. "Fixie is all well and good, but she is in the store, isn't she?"

"I am perfectly content with my aspect," Natura said, turning around to smile at Aconite, who hissed happily back at her.

"I believe you will need even more protection in this city," Iggy said to Alice. "Spark and I alone may not be enough. I recommend another combative-type dragon."

Bella bristled. "Woe to anybody who comes near Alice while I'm around. I will smite them!"

By now, Alice was very much used to her dragons bickering with one another about aspects. Ignoring them, she turned to Prim.

"Level 10 is one of those capstone levels, isn't it?" she asked, though she did not wait for an answer. "What will it be?"

Prim stopped grooming her claws. "It is a capstone level, and it feels important. I can see deep into this class, but that does not mean I know what the future will hold."

"Maybe it means two aspects per level," Numi said.

"I may need to level up if I am to have two eggs at a time," Prim said. “It sounds uncomfortable.”

Bella's stomach rumbled rather audibly, and Alice realized that none of them had had much of a meal yesterday. On top of that, they'd spent the day before that running for their lives.

"I'm not sure how many more dragons I can feed," she said teasingly, though she was not particularly worried about it. She had managed to keep everybody fed—though not extremely well—back in the forest. Now they were in the Protectorate Guild. Surely, there would be a way to order meals?

She decided to test that out and went to the side of the room where there was a bell pull. She was no longer the ignorant bumpkin she had been when she had first found herself in a hotel for the first time. Now she had an idea of what to look for.

A rope hung from an unobtrusive corner that went up into the ceiling. Alice pulled on it and distantly heard a bell chime.

Not a minute later, there was a soft knock at the door. Alice bade her dragons to stay still and quiet, then went to open the door. A woman standing on the other side in a housekeeper uniform immediately curtsied. "Do you require something, Miss?"

"Breakfast," Alice said, "enough for myself, of course, but my dragons also need to be fed."

"Your...?" The woman's eyes widened, then she seemed to collect herself. "Yes, Miss, of course. I believe that the head butler did mention something about that. I assume that your dragons need... meat?" Her voice squeaked at the end.

"No," Alice said. "Most of them eat human food. If I could order, say, a triple serving of breakfast, with lots of fruit and perhaps a side salad that you normally have for lunch."

Her nervousness visibly eased. "Oh, then they are like cows then? Lots of roughage."

Alice wasn't sure whether to take offense at that or not, but the woman seemed to mean well.

With a dip of a curtsy, the maid continued. "Yes, Miss, I will put an order in right away. There is, of course, a restaurant in the lobby, but I don't think that they will like your... guests?"

"Familiars," Alice said.

It was not exactly an accurate term, but certainly a safer one than aspects. Eventually, someone might figure out that those gray searchers were aspected to a He of Many Dragons. And then where would she be?

"Yes, Miss."

The woman curtsied a third time, and Alice shut the door.

While they were waiting, Alice decided that she ought to wash up properly this time, with soaps and everything. The bathroom held a plethora to choose from, so she decided to stick with the ones that had pictures of flowers on the bottles for now.

By the time she was done and dressed again, breakfast arrived on several overflowing carts. She had forgotten how much nobles liked to have in their meals. They regularly ordered more food than could be eaten in a regular meal, and she had ordered three large meals for her dragons.

How am I going to afford this? she thought as several servants wheeled it in.

Thankfully, nobody asked for payment. Alice stepped aside, docile, and let the servants sweep in and arrange the food.

The dragons rustled their wings in eagerness but were on their best behavior, only occasionally stretching out their necks to have longing sniffs. This was good because, despite the forced blank look on the servants' faces, they were extremely nervous—especially when Natura did her best human-like smile, lifting her lips from her teeth.

"She doesn't eat meat," Alice said quickly. "She eats grasses and vegetables only.”

“Yes,” Natura agreed. “I am looking forward to that salad," she added, "and are those melons I smell?"

"Yes, uh, miss. Perfectly cut," the man said, looking anxious.

"Excellent," she said. "I shall like the seeds too—for a garden."

The man didn't seem to know what to say about that and quickly finished plating the food. All the servants hurried out, and Alice quickly shut the door.

The dragons dug in. Natura went for the fruits and vegetables and any bits of side salad that the other dragons mostly ignored. She seemed delighted at the variety that was available, which helped ease some of Alice's fears that she would find city life not to her taste.

Prim ate as Alice did, enjoying the eggs, bacon, and toast. Iggy and Spark went straight for the sausages and anything that looked like meat.

As usual, Numi abstained from the food and instead decided to eat a pair of silver coins from her storage.

Bella was the only odd one out. Like Iggy, she took pieces of meat. Then she went to the floor and asked for anything to write with. She drew a circle with her claws on the floor. Alice was concerned that she had just ruined the wooden floor, but after the food puffed into ash, the circle was entirely gone, and Bella looked extremely sated.

Alice even tried the carafe of coffee, which she had not had much occasion to taste. It smelled delightful, but the taste was bitter, and she required a lot of milk and sugar to make it palatable. She much preferred the tea. All of her other dragons drank water.

When Bella returned to the cart for seconds, she lifted a plate of bacon, paused, and then plucked up a folded piece of paper that lay beside it.

“Alice? What is this?”

Brows furrowed, Alice took it and read it aloud. The note was written on thick, creamy parchment, and the handwriting inside was impeccable.

“It’s from Oliver. He wishes my attendance at 10 in the combat training room, as well as any other dragons who wish to come.”

She was pleased that the short note implied the dragons were individuals, with their own wants and choices. Too many people thought of them as mere animals. Smiling, she looked up at her dragons.

"Well, dears, now that we’ve eaten, we should get to business," she said. "In order for me to get to level ten, we must level your skills."

"I do not believe that I can easily level within the guild’s building," Numi said. "Alice, may I go outside and hunt around for more treasure?"

Alice frowned. "Only if you are very careful." She never really liked the idea of her dragons being out of sight, but at the same time, Numi was one of her more intelligent dragons, and she should trust her to keep herself out of trouble. "You must work on not being seen, and for goodness’ sake, do not steal jewelry or anything from anybody else. But," she said, thinking for a moment because she did not like the way Numi slumped down as if in disappointment, "anything that you find on the side of the street—anything that is obviously lost—you may, of course, keep."

Numi nodded happily and scuttled to the window.

"And be back before dark," Alice said, a moment before she leapt into flight.

Numi was already gone.

"Perhaps your next sub-aspect should belong to Numi in order to keep her on a proper task," Prim remarked dryly.

Alice didn't think that was a bad idea, but then she turned to the others. Her gaze fell on Natura, her largest dragon—and therefore the one that looked most impressive—but also one of the gentlest.

"Do you have an idea on how to increase your levels?" Alice asked.

Natura cocked her head. "Oh yes, if I could be allowed to look at the gardens I can sense nearby, there are plenty of ways I can increase the health of these plants. Some of them," she added, disgusted, "have been planted and watered, and that is it! There has been no regard for the amount of light they need or additional nutrients in the soil."

Alice didn't think that her dragon would be allowed to walk around by herself, so she said, "What about the vines growing along the sides of the building? Is that something that you could do from here for today?"

"There is," she confirmed after a glance out the window.

"Then if we don't find anything else for you today, that will be your project." She wanted to keep her close. “But for now, I would like you to join me and the others. All of us should see what this training room has to offer.”
