Chapter 393

Mis hopped onto Bin’s stomach and curled up, purring, the moment Bin slung herself on the couch.

She hadn’t eaten that much food in one sitting since the war with the Lord of the Moon. If she had been the worrying type, she might have feared that her stomach would rip.

Reaching up and over the arm rest, she grabbed a random old magazine from the stack on the small table and settled in. It would probably be at least an hour before Mom came home, and she was too nervous to do anything until they had talked.

It had taken her a couple of days to build up the courage, but she was finally going to follow Uncle Michael’s advice and actually tell her mom how she felt about her dad’s death. How heavily it weighed on her mind every day.

It was nerve-racking as all hell, but the more she had thought about what he had said, the more Uncle Michael’s suggestion made sense. Why should she be afraid of something as simple as talking to her own mom about her feelings and problems. Wasn’t helping with their kids’ problems any mom’s favorite thing to do, anyway?

And if, as Uncle Michael had said, showing a wish to talk to her would make her mom happy as well, then Bin would have done it even if she didn’t want to.

There was a knock on the door.

That was a little early. Not that her mom would have knocked anyway.

“Come in!” she shouted. Mis batted her tail against Bin’s nose a couple of times in protest of the loud noise but didn’t otherwise react.

Bin couldn’t see over the back of the couch, but she recognized the voice easily. “Are you here Bin?”

“Here!” she called and stuck a hand in the air.

Her dad’s sister, Olivia, came around the side of the couch, sat down, and put a hand on Bin’s. “How are you, cutie?”

“Fine.”

“How fine are we talking here, exactly? Sulky mumbling in a dark and damp corner ‘fine’ or all-out tickle party followed closely by waffles topped with ice cream and chocolate chips ‘fine’?” Olivia asked and held up a grocery bag whose contents weren’t much of a mystery after that second option.

Despite a brave attempt to appear disinterested, Bin couldn’t help but let a smile slip across her lips. “I guess the waffles with ice cream and chocolate wouldn’t be too terrible, if I had to pick one.”

“The all-out tickle party followed closely by waffles topped with ice cream and chocolate chips, you mean?”

“Just the waffles, ice cream, and chocolate chips will do, I think.”

Olivia tsk’ed and wiggled her index finger from side to side. “I am very sorry, young lady, but that is an all-inclusive menu exclusively. The all-out tickle party is a mandatory, nonnegotiable part of the deal. No tickle party, no waffles or ice cream or chocolate chips.”

“Okay, fine,” Bin drawled. “But I’m not in the mood for tickling, so I’m not going to laugh.”

“Uh huh, sure. Why don’t we revisit that certainty in about, oh, I don’t know, ten to twenty seconds?”

Bin tensed up hard in expectation of the ordeal, but as she had feared, she didn’t stand a chance.

***

They made the waffles fresh together.

Olivia had properly stepped into her role as one of the heads of Forest. For a few months, she had withdrawn from the administration, but been dissatisfied with what she saw in her absence. Bin still remembered her aunt’s profound sigh of annoyance as she had come to the realization that she might have to become a career politician.

If you asked Bin, however, her aunt seemed to enjoy the work. She almost cheered when they discovered a deal made under the table or other kinds of corruption, because it meant she got to dish out the punishments.

And she was certainly fit for the position. She might have even more of a flair for administration than Robert.

Somehow, the waffle making just made the words flow, and Bin ended up talking about all the same things that she had talked to Uncle Michael about with Aunt Oli. And the things she wanted to talk to Mom about.

It was nice. It felt like a soft opener for the conversation with her mom. And just like Uncle Michael said, Aunt Olivia revealed that she had been feeling exactly the same way. She said that even trying to forget the pain made her feel a sense of guilt toward Eik. As if it was disrespectful to his memory.

Bin was about to tell her that that was ridiculous, but before she could, she realized that she felt the exact same way. It felt like a betrayal. Like the grief and sorrow she felt was a crucial component that kept his memory intact. As if, without that grief, what remained of his spirit would dissipate into nothing.

That was nonsense, of course. They agreed on as much.

It had felt freeing to talk to Uncle Michael, and it felt just as freeing to talk to Aunt Oli. Why the hell hadn’t she done this ages ago?

She could only hope that it would feel just as good to talk to Mom.

And speaking of…

“Oli, hey!” Ihasu said with a smiled as she stepped inside. “What are you doing here?”

“Just thought I’d come by and eat some waffles with my favorite girl.”

“Me?” Ihasu asked with a grin.

“Optimistic. I like it. Come and have some. We made more than we could eat, and your daughter already ate before I arrived.”

“For shame!” Ihasu gasped.

“I can’t see the future!”

“That is no excuse!”

“You want ice cream on top?” Bin asked her mom.

Ihasu scoffed. “What kind of question is that even, cutie?”

“You’re right, I’m sorry. That was stupid of me.”

Ihasu caressed Bin’s back lovingly as she took a seat next to her. “Is Goo not home yet?”

“No. He’s probably gone and followed a single ant home, or maybe found a particularly dry leaf to watch silently for an hour.”

“Don’t shame your brother’s hobbies, Bin.”

Bin rolled her eyes and placed the plate in front of her mom. “Here you go.”

“It looks heavenly!” Ihasu dug in immediately.

Bin glanced over at her aunt who caught her eye. Olivia nodded encouragingly.

“Uhm… By the way, Mom, when you’re done, there’s something I’d like to talk to you about.”

“Oh?” Ihasu said and put down her fork. “What is it? We can talk now.”

Bin hesitated for a couple of seconds before finally just going for it. “I, well… Recently, I’ve been feeling kind of—”

Her words were cut off as a sharp pain suddenly shot through her, eliciting a breathless gasp. She jerked in the chair and crumbled to the floor in agony. She shrieked.

“Bin!” Ihasu screamed, her chair crashing into the wall as she dove for her daughter. Appearing as if by teleportation, Olivia was there as well. “Bin, what’s going on? Where does it hurt?”

“Everywhere!” the girl hissed through clenched teeth. She could barely draw in a breath for the pain. “It’s moving!” she cried, tears flowing freely. “It’s moving into my arm! It feels like it’s on fire!”

For some reason, that revelation seemed to have quite a calming effect on both her mom and aunt. Why in the world would it be a good thing that Bin’s arm might fall off, or whatever the hell was going on?

“Honey, is it moving into your forearm?” her mom asked.

“Y-Yeah… I think so.”

“Toward the underside of your forearm?”

Bin squinted up at these two women she trusted most in the world. “How… How do you know?” she breathed.

“Because, although you’re a little young, I think you’re Awakening, Bin,” her mother said.

What? That almost made the pain fade. She was Awakening? But… What?

“Are you sure?” she asked.

“Take a look,” Aunt Oli said and gently folded up Bin’s sleeve. A blinding glow covered the skin of the entire underside of her forearm.

“It hurts.”

“I know,” Aunt Oli said. “I didn’t know what was happening when I Awakened, but we’re here to tell you that it will be okay, Bin.”

As she blinked tears out of her eyes, the script continued to carve itself into her arm while she watched, an unseen, scorching nail seeming to cauterize the wound as it appeared, occluding what would otherwise have been a stream of blood. Bin found herself forced to bite down hard on her lip, just to keep in the screams.

She could barely concentrate enough to take in the neat, red words.

[Bin Magnasen]

[Earth—New]

[Krosson]

So the system knew her race, but still considered her a being of Earth. Maybe it was just reading her mind.

The text faded without a trace, like she had seen on her father so many times. She would have appreciated a wooden plaque of her own right about now, but she knew that the Awakening had to be like this. Bin barely got halfway through a breath before the agony returned in full force.

“Congratulations,” her mother whispered with a smile. “You should carry one of these around from now on.” She dangled a flat piece of wood in front of Bin. From where had she pulled a plaque?

[Awakening triggered]

[Event triggered]

[Delayed skill activation triggered]

[Initiated into the Court of Toxin — Accept?]

Ihasu covered her mouth with a hand as she gasped. Silent tears rolled. Olivia had more or less the same reaction when she read it.

Bin felt like she had been struck by lightning. The first thing she noticed was that it was wrong. It wasn’t the Court of Toxin. It was the Court of the Supreme Divinity of Toxin.

And, delayed skill activation… What the hell was that supposed to be?

“Did… Dad leave this for me?” she asked, voice trembling as a heavy lump settled in her throat.

Her mother’s arm closed around her shoulder.

There was nothing to consider.

[Successfully initiated into the Court of Toxin. Altering F-rank title]

[Reached Beloved Daughter of Toxin — III]

By now, Bin, along with her mother and aunt, was crying her eyes out. As if it had been a thousand years since she last felt it, her father’s presence flowed through her. It was made of warmth and love.

As a matter of fact, it was so intense that she could even feel it outside of her body.

Abruptly, her head snapped up, eyes wide and focused on something Ihasu and Olivia couldn’t see. They followed her eyes up into the ceiling, but nothing was there.

“Honey?” Ihasu asked cautiously through her tears and put a hand on her daughter’s arm. Bin didn’t even seem to notice. Her eyes looked as if they were about to pop out.

She was sensing something that was not in this room. Something vast.

Olivia and Ihasu froze, both in body and in mind, at the next words out of Bin’s mouth, uttered in an almost inaudible whisper.

“Dad?”
