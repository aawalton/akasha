As much as Vir wished for nothing more than to swim through the shadows, bounding between walls, floors, and just generally paying no heed to the sort of spatial constraints that bound most mortals, he restrained himself.

While his generation rate was looking quite healthy now, a single invocation of Balancer of Scales or even Yuma’s Embrace would blow all of it away. To say nothing of Iksana’s Clarity, easily the most prana intensive of Vir’s Ultimate Arts.

And so he proceeded down to the deepest depths, to levels he had never ventured to before, noticing how the arenas grew larger and larger to fit higher level duels as he did, until he inevitably came upon a pair of guards guarding a massive double door that stood two full stories in height.

By their prana signatures, Vir judged them to be mejai of realms, capable of charging and firing A Rank magic.

Quite a prestigious guard for a door, no matter how large.

If the mass of prana deep underground hadn’t been enough indication he was on the right track, this certainly was. Prana Vision was all but blinding now on account of its intensity. And now that Vir had his Ash affinity powering the ability again, he could see all the affinities, not just Lightning as before.

It told him that not only was the Vimana fully active, it had a not insignificant number of people moving around it. Vir looked through the floors, but it was difficult to get a sense with just Prana Vision of what exactly they were doing.

And so, before getting within visual range of the guards, Vir sank into the shadows, bypassed the door, and reappeared on the other side, thanking the gods for that ability.

It was an irony that despite possessing three monstrously powerful abilities that enhanced his awareness, the lowliest of them served him far more than the other two combined. The Third Eye Chakra might have granted near-total awareness but could only be opened for brief periods.

Iksana’s Clarity was little better. While it let him glimpse into the future, and in fact, paired beautifully with the Third Eye, it was only useful for a handful of seconds in the middle of combat. With Vir’s normal prana reserves, he could maintain the ability for up to thirty seconds, but that would consume a significant portion of his built-up reserves. Not an issue in the Deep Ash, but here? He didn’t even consider it.

On the other extreme, Prana Vision could be used almost indefinitely with a small trickle of energy, but it was only as good as Vir’s vigilance.

Still, to have not one or two, but three abilities that allowed Vir to enhance his awareness was nothing short of spectacular. And on those rare occasions when he had all three active, he truly felt like an omniscient god.

If only said omniscience allowed him to understand what these people were hoping to accomplish here and why…

Instead of navigating the floors, risking running into guards, he activated Dance of the Shadow Demon once more, sinking through the floor, descending two levels with a single invocation. This put him at the very top of the cavernous space that the Vimana sat inside.

He had seen it earlier through Prana Vision, but now that he saw with his own eyes, it truly was a cavern blasted out of the rock deep underground.

Stretching off into the darkness and illuminated by dozens of magical lights placed around the periphery, he could scarcely make out the full size of the place. And though the Altani’s lights were hardly enough to beat back the darkness, the Vimana’s own lighting made the whole space glow.

It looked almost surreal, hanging in that cavern.

Vir muttered under his breath.

He recognized this Vimana. It was the very same Vimana that had hovered above the Alt Ashani of his own universe.

Which meant they had not only activated its invisibility but had also guided it down into this pre-prepared chamber, all without anyone noticing.

Or rather, Vir thought, without the masses noticing.

Those in power knew. How could they not, when such an enormous chamber had to be built?

Which meant this was a government-sanctioned operation and must have come from the very top.

But if the Altani Council had the means to control the Vimana, why hadn’t they parked it above the city, firing at Ash beasts that broke through the barrier? With such a weapon, they needn’t risk a single life against those creatures.

Granted, the Altani mejai fired their ballistae and other magics from behind the safety of Alt Ashani’s walls, so it wasn’t as though they were meeting the beasts in single combat. Even still, the operation required a massive host of mejai to operate on a twenty-four-hour schedule. It was so bad that even the professors had been called upon for the task, leaving them withered and spent.

Even ignoring all that, the very presence of the Vimana would have provided such a morale boost, and the city could absolutely use that.

No, there was something more here. Something important enough for the Council to forego all those benefits. All signs pointed to a greater plan. Vir just needed to figure out what.

Thankfully, the sheer size of the place meant the metal catwalks that had been erected around it were mostly unmanned.

Vir counted over a hundred people outside the Vimana and several hundred inside it. That still meant he was mostly free to roam around on his own, simply waiting for guards and other mejai and whoever else to pass by before continuing on, thereby sparing his prana. Every encounter he avoided invoking Dance of the Shadow Demon was more fuel for any upcoming encounters.

Vir methodically worked his way down the catwalks all the way to the main level of the Vimana. Like the others, the floating fortress was shaped like a double cone, one mirrored below the other, creating a structure that ended in points at the top and the bottom and flared to its widest in the middle. It was there that all the entrances had been set up.

Vir had always known they were colossal structures, but he’d always seen them high in the air, far away from anything that might give it perspective. Seeing one up close and having to walk toward it just drove home just how truly gargantuan the contraptions were. Capable of housing five thousand Imperium souls in comfort with plenty of room to spare, these truly were floating cities.

Research platforms, Ashani had called them, but Vir couldn’t help but wonder. Was that truly all they were for?

Some purpose that might have driven the Altani to bring it underground?

As for how, Vir could not say, for there wasn’t a single entrance into the space large enough to accommodate such a massive structure.

Vir drew closer, and it was not long before he came upon a pair of Altani mejai conversing with one another.

Activating Dance of the Shadow Demon, Vir raised just his head into a nearby shadow to listen. The cavern had no shortage of dark places.

The words, of course, came in slow motion, dilated by the time effects, but Vir had long ago grown used to listening and watching the world pass by in slow motion while using the ability.

“Do you think we’ll get it done in time?” one mejai asked—a rather trim-looking man in his mid-twenties, wearing the distinctive blue and white robes of the Altani.

“I give it even odds,” the other man said, blowing air out of his lips. “That’s with us working around the clock.”

“And to think we’ll only get one shot. If it doesn’t work…”

“Well, don’t worry your head about that. St. Amara’s yet to lead us astray. Don’t reckon she’ll start now.”

Well, that’s concerning, Vir thought darkly. By their prana signatures, Vir saw that these were both mejai of realms like the door guards. If the Altani’s elite mejai were so concerned about whatever it was they were doing, that did not bode well.

And this talk of St. Amara. Vir really had to learn more about this shadowy figure.

“Well, it’s all we can do to try,” the first mejai said. “Even if it takes all of us, it’ll be worth it if we can save the city.”

“Just one city,” the other mejai said, shaking his head. “I can’t believe this is all that’s left of our world.”

“Let’s make sure the Ash doesn’t claim even that, eh?” the first one said, putting his hand on the other’s shoulder before walking off into the Vimana, leaving the path clear for Vir.

Despite that, this was undoubtedly the most dangerous part of his little infiltration operation.

Vimanas were perfectly shielded from prana, and so Vir could not peer inside.

Carefully, Vir rose from the shadows, put his back up straight, and walked along the gangplank. There were no prana signatures close by, and those in the distance would have seen only a technician going about his duties.

And so Vir strolled up the elevated metal walkway right up to the mouth of the Vimana, which stood open and ready to admit him.

The moment he was inside, Vir sank into the shadows, selecting a nearby empty room.

At least, it had been empty when Vir emerged.

The moment he did, no less than seven mejai flashed into existence, surrounding him.

Blink? Or some other magic?

Mind ablaze with a dozen questions, Vir nonetheless moved the instant he registered their presence, activating Haste and surging prana into Balancer of Scales.

“Please wait,” one called out. An armored figure wearing the mask of a fox. A woman, by her voice. “We must talk.”

“And what’s stopping me from crushing you all right now?” Vir rasped.

“Nothing. We would be helpless against your strength. But if you do—if you continue down this path—not only will you doom us all… you will doom your universe as well.”

Halfway into the shadows, Vir froze.

“What did you just say?”
