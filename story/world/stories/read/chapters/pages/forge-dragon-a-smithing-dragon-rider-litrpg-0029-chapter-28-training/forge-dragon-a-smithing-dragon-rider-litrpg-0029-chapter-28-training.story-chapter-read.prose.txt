Caleb was forced to wait a day for his iron delivery, and as such had no real excuse to flee to his forge and leave everyone behind. As he walked through town, everyone greeted him warmly, congratulating him on his new status as both an initialized and journeyman blacksmith.

News spread quickly in the small village, so quickly that Caleb was certain that the baker’s wife knew fully well that Caleb was now a married man when she tried to fix him up with his daughter one last time.

His aimless wandering brought him to the training yard in the shadow of the central keep. The keep was large enough to hold the whole village and had a store of supplies to see them through any sieges the Frigidi might throw at them. It had been decades since the frost people had attacked in large numbers, but enough people survived to remember it that very few complained about the cost of maintaining the keep.

At all times, the ground around it was kept clear of even grass and weeds, with the exception of a small training area with sparring dummies and a target range set up. The area was meant for the training of the villagers. Not only the initialized trained in arms, but so did those who hoped to one day become initialized. After failing to gain the system’s recognition, most ceased to train, but some still kept it up, finding some time each week to come out here.

Some such men were sparring now alongside three initialized. The initialized were practicing throwing spears. They were all a few years older than Caleb, but none were over level 5.

Caleb could tell without much consideration that he could throw a spear further than any of them using his Imperium.

“Look who’s deigned to gift us with his austere presence,” one of the initialized said.

“He did what with his butt?” a second asked.

“That’s ‘posterior,’ not ‘austere,’ you nit,” said a third.

Caleb bowed dramatically.

He knew the three: Gavin, Doren and Revin. They’d each had a hand in his training and he owed them a lot.

They in turn owed him a fight.

It was tradition for newly initialized to face off against those who trained them as a chance to face their tormentors with more even odds. Caleb never got close to defeating Revin, but this time he might.

“Swords or spears?” Revin asked, knowing he’d be who Caleb challenged.

Caleb weighed his options. He was better with a spear, but so was Revin. They all were. The Frost Dragon Hunter class gave Spear Path, a skill that was like an affinity in some ways. It allowed the initialized to channel stamina through the weapon and increase its cutting force. Caleb had hoped he’d unlock some sort of Hammer Path if he became a Blacksmith, but his Metal Affinity did everything that could do and more, only using mana instead of stamina.

“Swords,” Caleb said, grabbing a training sword.

He lacked the Spear Path, and letting Revin use his would be a large disadvantage. Plus, the training swords were iron rods while the spears had only padded iron tips. His Imperium would be far more effective at controlling the practice sword than the spear. It was much easier to control the metal closer to his body than the distant spearhead.

They grabbed their training weapons and faced off. Caleb closed his eyes briefly, sensing the iron rod in Revin’s blade sway in his grasp.

He opened his eyes and Revin stood waiting for him. Caleb considered how he wanted to approach this. What would be fair? He knew a fair fight was not something one would ever want in real battle, but he liked and respected Revin. He could very easily defeat him if he drew fully on all his magic—but that would give away that he wasn’t exactly a Frost Dragon Hunter and raise more questions than he wished.

In the end, Caleb decided to challenge himself. He would only use his Imperium to match the physical attributes he professed to have—not that he’d made the mistake of giving anyone an exact stat allocation.

So Caleb charged. He knew how this went. The trainer always waited for the attack.

He closed in and thrusted, using only his gear-enhanced strength and none of his magic. Revin easily sidestepped the blow and swung down at Caleb. Revin had a build focused on agility, and it was only with the aid of his Imperium that Caleb was able to bring his sword around and deflect the blow.

The pair traded blows back and forth. After the first deflected strike, Revin began to test Caleb to see his speed and reaction time. While Caleb’s strength was a near match for his opponent, his agility was severely lacking and he constantly relied on his magic to aid him.

Caleb soon began to experiment. He didn’t want to outright rip the blade from Revin’s hand—though he could—but he wanted to see how it felt to channel his Imperium through his weapon and into his opponent.

With each touch of the “blades,” he felt a connection to Revin’s. Even without this connection, Caleb could have exerted control over the other man’s sword, but that would be harder and take more mana without a metal connection between them. So, when he made contact, instead of simply empowering his attack with his magic, he pushed Revin’s blade back at the point of impact.

The push added to the power of the already-strong blow and caused Revin to stagger.

Revin smiled.

“Going all in on strength, then?” he asked, misunderstanding what had happened completely. “Makes sense for a smith.”

Revin shot back at him, and he dodged out of the way, the strike narrowly missing.

They continued to trade blows, Caleb focusing primarily on defense and avoiding the rapidfire attacks, but eventually the blows began to get through his guard. There was only so much he could do without revealing all his cards.

In this duel, a landed blow’s value was subjective. A single blow on Caleb’s normal constitution of 8 would be enough to knock him out of a fight while a constitution of 20 could receive hundreds of training blows. As such, the duels went on until someone yielded.

Caleb’s constitution was still at 8, but that didn’t make him defenseless. As soon as he knew he’d missed his block, Caleb focused on the incoming blow with his affinity sense. With his elevated perception and acuity, time seemed to travel a little slower, giving him the chance to time his next test precisely. The instant before the iron touched his skin, Caleb pushed on it with his Imperium. Not enough to send it flying, but enough that the blow that did land was weakened enough for his constitution to handle.

Now Caleb had a way of replicating higher constitution, strength, and agility for this duel, and he gave Revin a good showing before eventually yielding.

He had nothing to prove to himself and Revin didn’t deserve to be embarrassed by him. Revin was more skilled than him when it came to fighting. It was only with his magic that Caleb would have been able to win, and with his magic, the fight would have been completely one-sided.

Facing a foe with a metal weapon was a real shock for Caleb and his abilities. He’d faced monsters and troglodytes, none of which used metal, and come out on top after some level of struggle. But once his foe was reliant on steel to achieve their victory, there was little they’d be able to do to stop Caleb from turning their weapon against them—so long as Caleb had the advantage of stats.
