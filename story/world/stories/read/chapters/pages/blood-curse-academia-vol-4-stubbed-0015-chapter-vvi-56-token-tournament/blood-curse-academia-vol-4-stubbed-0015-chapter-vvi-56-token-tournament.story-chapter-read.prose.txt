Chapter V.VI (5.6) - Token Tournament

“Well done, you two!” Arclight beamed at them as they crossed the pier towards her. She stood, arms outstretched in welcome, with the sea to her back. It was exactly the same scene depicted in the flier. “You’re the first two Combat F students to locate me! Though I’m hardly surprised! The two of you never cease to amaze me!” She let out her signature booming laugh that rippled the water behind her.

Her large, scarred stature made her unmistakable. Her prosthetic arm beckoned them forward and her single eye glinted in the sunlight.

With spatial magic at their disposal, Kizu and Evie managed to pull ahead of the other student in Arclight’s office and were the first to arrive down at the docks.

“Professor Arclight.” Evie gulped, her eyes on the professor’s boots. “Why are we here?”

Arclight smiled and folded her large arms. “Any ideas?”

“This is about the academy’s tournament,” Kizu said. “Is this the first test of the year? Whoever reaches you first gets points?”

Arclight laughed again. “If only it were that easy! You’re close, but not quite hitting the mark. This is just the precursor to the first challenge, not the challenge itself.” She waved a hand and two wooden chairs appeared behind them. Then she motioned for them to take a seat.

Before sitting, Kizu took a good look around their current area. It was a normal, sunny day. Most of the fishing boats were out at sea, taking advantage of the day’s nice weather and calm sea but on the dock adjacent to them a scraggily old man was untangling fishing nets beside his skiff. And over at the shore Kizu spotted a kid his age sorting fish. A reminder that not everyone his age on the island was an academy student. There were locals who were already starting their careers.

Other than those two though, Kizu saw no one nearby. No fellow students rushed down to intercept Arclight. They were soundly in first and could spare some time for Arclight’s explanation.

He sat.

“As you likely know, this semester’s tournament is broken into three challenges,” Arclight explained. “Every year the challenges change wildly.”

“Who decides what we have to do?” Kizu asked. “Is it a panel of professors and faculty?” That info alone could help him narrow down what he might face in the next few months. For example, Professor Knoff and Professor Krimpit would pick vastly different sorts of trials.

“All professors have some measure of say, this year though it is mostly up to me and the headmaster to decide the challenges,” Arclight answered, appearing unbothered by his interruption. “While I cannot say much on the topic, I can promise you this year will not be simple. The headmaster is far more vindictive and clever than me.”

There was a twinkle in Arclight’s single eye that Kizu did not like.

“The first challenge begins today and you’ll have the next month to complete it. Though, every day after the first week that it takes you to complete will dock you points and make your overall score lower for the tournament as a whole.”

“What do we need to do?” Kizu asked.

Arclight reached out with her prosthetic arm and a green sack appeared in her metallic hand. At first, Kizu thought it contained coins, but then Arclight plucked one of the round, green wooden tokens out from within and flicked it over to him.

His hand darted out and caught the wooden token. While shaped like a coin, the head’s side instead depicted his face. And the tail’s side was a list of information, including his name (Kaga Kizu), best subject (brewing), place of birth (Hon), and his year (3rd).

Arclight flipped a second token over to Evie who fumbled for it with both hands before securing it.

“That’s a little gift for you,” Arclight said. “Keep it safe. Losing it too early will also hurt your score.”

“Losing it early?” Kizu asked. He palmed the wooden token. “Is someone trying to take it from us? Is the contest to see who can hold onto their token the longest?”

“Well deduced!” Arclight praised. “That’s half of the challenge.” She dropped the green sack into her jacket pocket and took out a blue sack. “The other half of the challenge is right here. Take a chip.”

Kizu did as she instructed and removed a similar wooden token. This time it was stained blue, however. And it had a stranger’s face on it. A nervous looking boy stared back at Kizu, his eyes slightly off-kilter. Kizu flipped it around and read the listed information. Zashima Yugo, best subject was Music, born in Hon, and a 4th year at the academy.

Kizu’s mind processed what he’d just seen and quickly pieced it all together. “I need to get ahold of his green token while keeping ahold of my own.”

“Exactly so.”

“Any rules?” Kizu asked. “Can’t I just toss my token in the bottom of the sea? Nobody will find it then.”

“We will have an assembly in a few weeks and you must have your token at that time to retain your ranking,” Arclight explained. “And there are a few rules, such as the token can’t leave the island and no spatial pockets. No destruction of tokens or breaking of academy rules.”

“So  I could go bury it in the nearby forest,” Kizu mused. “But that leaves me susceptible to someone locating it through magic and I won’t be on hand to defend it.”

A number of different potential solutions flashed through his mind. If he could establish who was targeting him, everything would be incredibly easy. But not knowing the spells and skillsets in his opponents arsenal meant he needed to find a broad solution that defended against everyone, rather than an individual person’s strengths.

“My token is a fifth year boy,” Evie said quietly.

“A challenge!” Arclight agreed. “Naturally, some targets are simply bad match ups for some students. And so, you get the chance to exchange a token once. Since you’re the first in your class to arrive on site, you get better odds of securing an easy target.”

“Because as more people arrive, the easier targets will get snatched up as students swap out the more difficult challenges,” Kizu said, understanding immediately. It was more than just age difference and rankings too. Gender played a big role in the difficulty. Not being able to access the dorm of the opposite gender added a layer of difficulty. Though…the target also wouldn’t know who was hunting them so leaving their token in their room left a level of risk as well.

“Do either of you want to switch out your token?” Arclight asked. “You only get one swap!”

“Can I look up the student really fast?” Kizu asked. He had his scrying orb in his bag and the rankings were publicly available.

“Nope! You only get what you already know and what’s on the token.”

Evie exchanged her token while Kizu considered. She looked incredibly relieved by whoever she received after so it paid off for her.

“I think I’ll keep mine,” he decided. Yugo didn’t look particularly threatening. And he was at least a boy. Kizu had successfully snuck into the girl’s dorms back in his first semester at the academy and didn’t feel like repeating that particular feat.

“Can you summarize what your trial is?” Arclight asked. “I want to make certain you understand what must be done to achieve victory.”

“We need to keep the green token with our face safe while getting ahold of the green token of the person on our blue token.” Kizu pocketed Yugo’s token. “The longer it takes for us to get the token, the worse the performance. But also the longer we protect our own token, the better our performance.”

“Exactly! Keep in mind that you can use almost any morally acceptable means necessary to accomplish your goal. Deception, persuasion, espionage, and even challenging your target to a duel. Though that last one needs to be properly supervised by me. However, do not break any academy rules. You are still susceptible to academic discipline for any rules broken. Do you have any questions?”

Kizu exchanged a look with Evie. She performed a barely noticeable shrug.

“Not now,” Kizu said. “Can we ask you in the future if we think of something?”

“Of course! My office hours are public,” Arclight said. “Now, I see another student coming down to the docks. If that’s everything you have to ask me, then feel free to take the rest of the period off.”

Kizu didn’t see anyone nearby, but he knew better than to question the combat professor’s perception. They quickly said their goodbyes to Arclight and departed.

—

“Who did you get?” Kizu asked Evie. They sat at a pub in town. It was mostly empty with just a few patrons getting late lunches and early drinks. Realizing a second after he asked that it might be detrimental to Evie’s plans to reveal information about her target, he hurriedly attempted to amend his inquiry. “If you’re comfortable saying, that is.”

“Harumi. A first year,” she said. “I’ve met him a few times before.”

Kizu laughed. “Harumi? He’s a friend of mine.”

Under her bangs of quills, Evie’s eyes widened. “H-he is?”

“Don’t worry, I won’t say anything. Your secret is safe with me.”

There was a lull in their conversation as a barmaid approached to take their order. Kizu asked for whatever the local fishermen had freshly pulled in. Evie asked for a salad with goat cheese.

“Can you…give me any information about him?” Evie asked as the bar maid strolled away.

“Information?” Kizu’s brain stuttered then it clicked into place what she was asking for. “You mean about Harumi?”

“Y-yes.” Evie fidgeted and tugged on her cloak she wore over her academy uniform. “I…Ms. Jackal keeps telling me I need my combat rank to improve. But I don’t want to fight. Not in front of everyone watching.”

“So you want to use the information gathering challenge as a way to bypass the actual fighting part of combat,” Kizu surmised. He sighed. “I don’t know. Harumi is my friend and he looks up to me. I don’t think I can just give you details about him. That will sabotage his chances.”

“I know…it’s just….” Evie struggled for words for a minute. Kizu sat back in his wooden chair and patiently gave her the time to find her voice. Finally, she put her thoughts together in a quiet sentence. “I don’t know if I’ll be allowed to stay if I keep failing.”

“I understand you don’t like fighting, but if it’s really this dire of a situation, why don’t you at least try signing up for the weekend fights? It’s not against people anymore. We fight summoned creatures now.”

“I can’t,” she said miserably. She sounded on the verge of tears. “You saw me back on the first day. I’m useless when I have to fight others. They’re going to kick me out.”

Kizu closed his eyes and took a deep breath. He tried to see it from her point of view and reminded himself that Evie wasn’t him. For one thing, she was two years behind him in age. And for another, she had an entirely different life growing up. And finally, she’d never had to kill someone before. Fighting for him might be nothing serious, but only a truly bizarre person would ever consider him normal.

“Evie. You need to get over this hurdle if you want to stay at the academy. It’s part of the curriculum,” he said. Then he relented. “But…for now, I can give you a bit of information about Harumi.”

“Thank you!”

Kizu lifted a hand to ward off her gratitude. “It won’t be anything you can’t find out on your own. Don’t expect me to lead him into a trap for you or anything. This will just give you a small head start on his basic information and give you an idea of his hobbies and personality.”

It seemed to be more than enough for Evie. She listened with raptured attention as he spoke a bit about Harumi. Nothing incredibly important. Just a scattering of details like that he was a fellow member of the Drama Club and that his father worked as a constable in town.

With their food ordered and Evie now pondering the information he’d gifted her, Kizu pulled out his scrying orb and got to work researching his target. Yugo was a decent student. One of the S class students in music, but only a B student in most of his other subjects. His dump subjects were divination and astronomy. Kizu smiled at that. Divination would doubtlessly prove to be the most valuable subject in this challenge. This was all about information gathering. On paper at least, Kizu was completely satisfied with his decision to keep Yugo as his target. His next step would be to get to know Yugo better. Figure out his class schedule and hobbies to subtly infiltrate his life. It should be relatively simple.

Their food arrived and the server set a steaming bowl of clam chowder down in front of him. After thanking her, Kizu took a spoonful of the soup. Salty, lumpy, and delicious. Across from him Evie picked at one of the chopped turnips in her salad. Her mind was clearly on the challenge rather than her food.

From what Arclight had said, it sounded like they all had the entire first week as a grace period before they started getting docked points for not recovering their target’s green token. He needed to get to work on gathering information about Yugo quickly if he wanted to stay ahead of the rest of the academy.

Then a thought made Kizu smile. Tomorrow he had a Student Council meeting. And he recalled a directory of students that the council members happened to have exclusive access to. It was about time that he finally turned that conscripted job into an advantage.
