Congratulations, you have gained a level.

“Gained more levels. This mission might be better than the summoning chamber!”

Rusty cheered as he watched his level rise again. The Necromancer’s minions hadn’t given him much experience, but after defeating the Necromancer himself and that massive monster, he had reached level five and was already close to reaching level six.

“Still no new skill… This system is getting stingy…”

Even though he had reached a higher level, he still hadn’t gained any new skills. From what he knew, that was simply how things worked. Many skills carried over from lower levels, evolving into stronger versions, so there was no need for them to appear again. Instead, he had to improve those skills by training and using them.

“Now then…”

He looked toward the room the Necromancer had tried to escape into. There was one thing of interest inside, and he needed to decide what to do with it before the mercenaries and adventurers reached this place.

“Do I need to destroy this thing? What if I take it with me instead?”

Rusty asked as he poked the green crystal with his finger.

“That’s a good idea. This necrotic crystal could be useful to us, my dear Rusty.”

Aburdon was all for it. He thought it would be a waste to simply destroy a magical item that had been used by a C-Rank Necromancer.

“No, destroy it. Why would we need to use such an evil, cursed item? Even if it doesn’t affect us, if you bring that thing into the settlement or near the children, it could affect them and make them sick.”

Alexander raised a good point. While Rusty was resistant to the crystal’s influence, that didn’t mean the humanoids living with him were. From what he knew, necrotic energy could slowly affect living bodies like a disease, making people weaker and sicker before eventually killing them if they were exposed to it for too long.

“So what if some of those lesser beings get sick? We can use this! We could break it down for enchantments. There are so many uses for a necrotic stone like this!”

Aburdon refused to give up. He continued arguing about the benefits of taking the item instead of simply destroying it.

“And besides, if you use that storage system of yours, no necrotic mana will leak out. That only happens if you store it in regular spatial bags!”

“Oh, is that so?”

Rusty looked toward Alexander, who seemed to have conveniently left out that detail.

“Yes, but… it’s better not to dabble in these evil energies. You never know what might happen…”

It was clear that Alexander simply didn’t like the idea of Rusty using necromancy or any energies related to it. That made the choice easy. With the crystal inside his storage, there would be no threat to anyone else, and he could still study it within the crafting section of the Soul Forge. That would keep it safely away from the orphans living with him.

“Then I’ll take it… if I can figure out how…”

He approached the large crystal. It was roughly the same size as his torso and was firmly attached to the ground by something that looked like a mixture of bones, blood, and a strange substance resembling poison.

Rusty knelt before the grotesque pedestal and inspected the dark veins binding the crystal to the floor. Bone and dried blood held it tightly in place, while the crystal itself pulsed with concentrated necrotic magic.

“If I use elemental light, will that destroy it? Wait… Let me try to wedge it off.”

He used his halberd as a lever, driving it into the ground and slowly pushing down. After a few wiggles, the crystal began to move without sustaining too much damage. Once it was pulled out of place, the connection between it, the runes, and the altar began to fade, and the light within the chamber slowly dimmed.

“Did I mess up?”

The large green crystal lost its glow and became nothing more than a massive green stone. However, when Rusty used his mana sense, he could still feel a tremendous amount of mana coming from it.

“Everything is fine, Rusty. Once the enchantment is broken, it becomes dormant. You will have to restore the magic if you want to use it as a power source.”

Aburdon explained this as the heavy green stone soon made its way into Rusty’s storage system. Once it was removed from the magical construction, storing it became possible.

Classification:

Necrotic Stone [Inactive]

Type:

Magic Stone

Description:

A stone containing necrotic energies, dormant but still leaking mana slowly.

“It’s all fine, but I can still feel some mana around here. Better leave it…”

For a moment, Rusty wondered if he should purify the area with his light element, but he stopped himself. It would be better to show the dead necromancer to the mercenary leader and the others, as he still wanted to judge their reactions.

There was something about this situation that did not add up. He wanted to hear their opinions, especially since they had been targeted for death just like him.

“Hey, Second, are you in there? The undead suddenly stopped moving and collapsed!”

Galiena’s voice echoed through the stone corridor, accompanied by the heavy thud of her armored boots. She burst into the chamber, her twin axes dripping with black blood, followed closely by a weary Jakub and several other mercenaries.

They stopped dead in their tracks as they took in the sight before them. The alcove was scorched, a pile of dark ash marked where the necromancer had stood, and a massive heap of crumbling bones covered the floor.

“You actually took care of the caster?”

Galiena asked, wiping the sweat from her forehead as she stared at Rusty in disbelief. She glanced around the chamber, clearly noticing the lingering traces of magic floating through the air.

“What in the world happened here?”

Rusty turned around while holding his fire longsword. He had already returned his halberd to his spatial storage. He did not want anyone to discover that he could use light elemental magic.

“The spellcaster is dead. He turned to dust after I dealt with him, but he left behind a few interesting things.”

He showed off his flaming sword, lying about what had actually happened.

“That’s the caster? A pile of charred remains?”

Galiena looked at what remained of the caster and kicked the pile of black ash with her foot, raising an eyebrow.

‘Is she buying it? And are they buying it?’

Jakub and the other mercenaries were there as well, their gazes filled with suspicion. However, there were occult-looking symbols scattered throughout the chamber, and while his light magic had managed to purify most of the necrotic energy within the area, some of it still lingered around the place where he had pulled out the crystal.

“I see… So what were those interesting things?”

“He had this letter.”

Rusty pulled the folded parchment from his side and presented it to Galiena. The edges were slightly singed, yet the ink remained surprisingly legible. Galiena snatched the letter from his hand, her brow furrowing deeply as her eyes scanned the messy handwriting. Jakub leaned over her shoulder, his jaw tightening with every word he read.

The contents were brief but telling. The note detailed the exact route and arrival time of Galiena’s mercenary squad, along with explicit instructions for the spellcaster to ensure that no survivors left these tunnels. It was a disturbing revelation, but regrettably, there was no indication of who had ordered it.

“No seal… What the hell, who did this!?”

Galiena crumpled the paper in her hand as the mercenaries looked at one another. They all knew this was bad. If they returned to the settlement, something even worse might happen.

“They wanted to kill us, but why? And who?”

Jakub shouted from the side, while the other mercenaries began mumbling among themselves as they all realized that their lives were at risk. Rusty understood why they were acting this way. If someone had ordered their deaths, there was no telling whether they would try again if the mercenaries returned, or whether the next attempt would be far worse.

“But what can we do? You want to just run? And what about those adventurers? What if they’re part of this?”

At the moment, only the mercenaries were here. The adventurers had remained in the previous chamber, resting after being attacked by the undead horde.

“And do what? Go to another city? Become bandits or something? Don’t forget that we’re in the employ of a noble. They could accuse us of being deserters!”

One of the mercenaries replied. They were not exactly a private army, but they were employed by a noble, and if the noble was behind all of this, simply running away would make it easy to paint them as a renegade troop.

“QUIET, ALL OF YOU!”

Their leader finally lost her temper and shouted with all her might, silencing everyone in the chamber.

“Second, what do you think? You were the first one here. Do you know more?”

Just as had happened many times before, all eyes shifted toward Rusty. Even Jakub, who disliked him, seemed unsure of what to do. His position as second in command and strategist of the group was becoming quite tedious. In reality, he did not particularly care what happened to the group. He could always abandon them and escape on his own.

“Aburdon, Alexander, any advice?”

He asked his two guides for their thoughts. After they gave him some advice, he tried to formulate a plan with the mercenaries.

“Hmm… Why don’t we pretend that there was no necromancer here? It’s not like anyone will check for him. They might assume that he failed to complete the mission and ran away with the money.”

“Hmm… Act like nothing happened, huh…”

The mercenaries pondered the idea, but they did not really have much of a choice. This was perhaps better than telling anyone about a plot to kill them when they had no idea who was behind it.

It could have been one of the nobles. Perhaps a merchant who had grown annoyed with them. It could even have been another mercenary group trying to move into their territory. The possibilities were endless.

“I don’t like it…”

Jakub commented.

“Well, you can always leave if you want. I can say that you died here, but good luck getting a new identity and starting from nothing.”

Galiena replied to the mercenary. If he left and decided to abandon his identity, he would need to obtain a new adventurer card, which would be a problem since his status had also been exposed. Without the right connections, his true identity could easily be discovered, and people would become suspicious of his motives.

“Bah… fine. Do whatever you want!”

Everyone discussed the situation, and they eventually decided to pretend that nothing had happened. They would collapse this section of the tunnel so no one could find any traces of the necromancer Rusty had killed.

They had several explosive magical scrolls that were meant to clear collapsed tunnels in the mine. Instead, they used them for the opposite purpose, sealing the hidden alcove permanently beneath tons of shattered stone.

A loud rumble echoed through the cavern as the magical scrolls detonated in quick succession. Dust and fragments of stone sprayed across the corridor, completely burying the ritual altars, glowing runes, and any remaining evidence of the necromancer. The ambient corruption disappeared beneath the thick cloud of rubble, leaving behind only the mundane smell of pulverized earth.

Galiena wiped the dust from her shoulder and motioned for the squad to fall back toward the main cavern. Rusty fell into step beside her, his heavy armored boots clanking softly against the uneven floor. Gleam jumped down onto his shoulder, her light becoming brighter now that she no longer needed to worry about the monsters.

“What was that?”

When they returned to the main cavern, the rest of the mercenaries and adventurers were waiting for them. A few of them had died during the undead attack, but their losses amounted to only around ten percent of the group.

“It was nothing. An orc mage blew himself up after Second pummeled him into a bloody pulp.”

Galiena said this while pointing her thumb toward Rusty. That was the excuse they had agreed upon, and the other mercenaries simply nodded.

It was impossible to completely hide the presence of the corruption, but it could still be explained as a natural phenomenon rather than the work of a necromancer. None of the adventurers possessed mana sense, so they would not be able to detect the necrotic energy lingering in the area.

“An orc mage blew himself up?”

One of the adventurers prodded for more information. He did not seem to fully believe their explanation, but he did not press the matter any further.

“Yeah. What of it?”

“Nothing… So, are we going further into the mine, or are we done here?”

Although the battle was over, they had not actually completed their mission. Their objective was to secure the mine and eliminate the monsters within it.

“We continue. We’ll carry the bodies outside once we leave so they don’t turn into undead. Now, let’s check the other tunnels.”

Their leader gave the command, and soon their expedition continued. Now that the necromancer was dead, however, there was nothing left to stop them. No more undead appeared, and the only things they discovered were empty tunnels and more dead orcs.

‘I bet that necromancer had those undead orcs set up the traps. It all makes sense now.’

As Rusty continued thinking about the situation, he remembered the traps they had found restored earlier. The undead still possessed the knowledge needed to operate them. It was likely that the necromancer had ordered them to reset the traps, either to eliminate as many of his targets as possible or to lure them deeper into the mine.

“I bet once we were dead, he was planning to turn everyone into undead and kill the others outside. But who could it be…?”

Now that the situation was over, Rusty’s mind was filled with possibilities. He still did not know who was responsible, which meant he would need to watch his back more carefully than ever.

‘I need to become a C-rank soon. All my problems will be fixed once I’m stronger!’

He was tired of hiding among the humanoids and pretending to be one of them, but he was far too weak to expose his true nature. For the moment, the resources and knowledge available in humanoid civilization could help him strengthen his metallic body. Once he was confident enough in his strength, however, he would make his move and finally explore this world for himself.
