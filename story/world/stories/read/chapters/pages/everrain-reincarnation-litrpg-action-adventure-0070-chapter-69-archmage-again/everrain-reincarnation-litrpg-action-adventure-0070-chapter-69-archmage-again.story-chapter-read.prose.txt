Day 311, 11:00 AM

[You have leveled up.

Select a skill within sixty seconds or a random one will be assigned to you.

Visions of Aether - You gain proficiency at reading the alterations of reality.

Mana Dispersion - You may change the color of the inner mana you are using at will.]

Elie has finally become a mage, and the reward for reaching the eleventh level of that same class is underwhelming. Visions of Aether is all right, while Mana Dispersion is useless to me, So with a meh choice I check the next level up condition, and barely avoid getting a stroke.

Achieve a feat of magic beyond mortal ken.

What the hell does that mean? Would using a grand spell count? I have expanded my mana pool considerably these past months, maybe I could cast the simplest grand spell, if I relied on song and gestures to reduce the cost, and if I distilled my mana for a day or two.

That could be worth an effort.

“Congratulations, Elie! You are officially a mage, and probably no longer my student.”

She advanced much slower than the girls, but she’s still better than Millie, who’s still struggling with learning spells of different mana types. I guess I was wrong — talent matters. Just knowing the requirements for leveling up and having passable stats isn’t enough, you need more to push a class further along the road.

“If you don’t mind, I would like to ask a favor,” I ask after her mumbled thank you. “Could you help Millie with her progression? The most important thing for you now is to use as much mana as possible, and eventually, you will level up. Just use defensive or utility spells, and you’re set.”

As for me, it’s time for another redo loop. I’ll dedicate four weeks to trying to find what makes a feat of magic something beyond mortal ken.

I head for the dungeon and enter it for the third time in the current loop, this time carrying a fat carp and a guppie. Four weeks weren’t enough to figure out what a feat of magic beyond mortal ken is. I guess that’s because my ken is mortal. Grand spells didn’t help, nor did any other idea that came to mind. So I’m quitting while sane and taking the archmage route.

I descend the stairs, and by the time I reach the forty-eight floor, I’m already a level four archmage, Spell Improvisation the greatest tool in my box. Bugs scuttle towards me, and start dissolving at ten yards away, as I undo the magical influences affecting them.

Just like Edna had said, an archmage is practically a god compared to mages. Triple mana pool, triple spell efficiency for your chosen path, ability to drown your problems in mana... You just want something solved and the solution happens. An insane class. I stroll down the floors, reaching the first minor abomination, and I turn off my field of devolution.

I plan to fight past the seventy-fifth floor, and I want my mana distilled for the occasion, but I seriously doubt elder abomination can come close to me even without the precaution.

Floor after floor, I’m about to reach seventy-six. Redo is red. I hesitate, realizing I’m drunk with power, the dungeon is subtly influencing my mind and drawing me deeper, so I turn around and head up. I just sit and meditate until Redo is once more available, and only then do I head down to the lower floor.

The thing is a writhing mass of horn-like tentacles, its central body an orb with a maw and six eyes, each as big as my head. It rolls towards me, and I send an arc of purple lightning at it. The electrical current strikes the monster, the light so bright and potent it blinds my eyes, but scanner tells me everything that’s happening around me.

The elder abomination spasms. In its wild flailing, it stabs at itself and tears its body. I sense life leave it, and the murderous electric current disappears as smoke rises from the corpse. The Guide rewards me with an attribute point.

Is it baiting me? If it is, I will bite, if anything untoward happens I will avoid it in the next loop. The next floor abomination is a worm with three maws, over twenty feet tall. Three-maw destroyer, and I destroy it with another pulse of lightning, and the Guide gives me another point.

It’s definitely bait, I should’ve received more just based on how much time has passed since someone had last cleared this floor. But absolute might cares not for ploys. I keep going down, and by the time I reach the ninetieth floor, I have twenty-eight attribute points to spend. Without hesitation, I spend eighteen, getting my physique, wisdom, and intellect to forty each. My mana reserve is ridiculous at this point, and I head to the ninety-first floor.

This is a floor nobody ever cleared since the archmages originally created the dungeons. The creature inside covers the entire floor, it has devoured everything, the forest, any other elder abominations that spawned, everything, and I don’t even know what to call it. The thing is a mass of goo.

I send an arc of lightning at it. The spell singes its flesh, but then the flesh changes on a molecular level, and electricity does nothing to it. A patch of ooze reforms into a tentacle, but I erect the devolution aura. The freshly formed appendage spears towards me, but disintegrates as it enters the edge of my aura.

There is no pain, no screams, nor anything else, the tentacle flakes into nothingness, and the goo leaves me alone. I head in, destroying the monster with each step. It tries to flee, but it’s too massive. It’s been centuries since anything other than food came at it, and it’s trying to adapt to its new circumstances.

I have half-scoured the room before it develops solid legs, but the goo doesn’t know how to use them. It only has a quarter of its mass left when it realizes it also needs a solid body to flee, but even then, watching it evolve through solution after solution, it doesn’t even come close. It’s too big, and it can’t comprehend that it should abandon a part of itself or split into multiples to survive.

There’s less than a hundredth of it remaining before it becomes a gigantic octopus, but with each cell having the same roles it’s clumsy. Its nervous system is distributed throughout its body, and with every inch of its body I destroy, it grows dumber and slower.

Two hours pass before I melt the monster into nothingness. A third of my mana is spent, but I have some information I wondered about, and another attribute point registers in my mind.

I clear another two piles of goo for attribute points and head back out. My mana pool has shrunk to twenty-five percent, and while the dungeon could recover my mana in an hour back when I was a regular mage, now it will take more than twenty hours just to refill my reserve.

Problems of growing big and strong, I guess. But that’s fine. Now I am confident I have all the tools I will need to restore Edna’s sanity. Then, we can overthrow the Church of Holiness and focus on growing powerful and immortal.

The only problem I can see cropping up is going insane. I already feel like a god. An undefeatable entity, a menace which might devour the world if unleashed, which would turn into dust as I walk it. The feeling is beyond anything anyone can imagine. I know that if I put enough effort into it, I can simply destroy this world. Just will it to explode, and with enough mana it will.

A terrifying thought, and a potential problem. Such power, if left unchecked, will turn me into a monster—Huh?

I guess that is the path wormlords took and descended into madness. Which leads to the following question, how did the other archmages remain sane? The only possible hint to help me answer that question might be Hadriuse’s journals. I’ve read them, but it’s possible I missed such information because I wasn’t looking for it.

It’s unlikely, but worth a try. I don’t want to become a monster. All I want is to see if I can find my wife and children, maybe recreate their bodies and incarnate them into this world.

Then we could be happy together for all of eternity.
