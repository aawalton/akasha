The Dragonkin tumbled to the side, crashing through the corpses of its companions. It roared in fury as it caught its feet and hands on the ground, sliding to a stop three meters away. Flames trailed from its mouth as the stone aura armored Pseudo-Knight made her appearance. The leather armor that covered Chloe was covered with purple and red ichor, and dozens of cuts had pierced through the armor, but stony gauntlets covered her forearms, legs, and chest.

“About fucking time!” Basil called from behind as the aura at his position coalesced further, making Ezra’s head ache sharply.

Chloe didn’t wait for a single second to explain anything before she rushed to continue fighting. Her steps danced across the ground as she closed in on the Dragonkin. Its claws struck out at her chest, but she raised her left arm and the attack hit her armored elbow instead. Stone cracked, but she stood her ground as the behemoth continued its attempted counter. Its claws swiped through the air a dozen times, each attack unable to land home on her. She simply was too quick for it. It pressed forward, flames covering its claws and then spreading out in flaming cuts in the air. Chloe dodged the first two, then ducked under the third and closed in with the Dragonkin.

“This is the last one!” Chloe screamed as she fought. “Exhaust yourself if you need to!”

Her fists, already covered by metal-studded gloves, had a further protection of stone affinity aura. The first strike of her counterattack struck the wound that Helen’d made on its hip. The scales and flesh there exploded out, gore coating her arm to the elbow, but Chloe wasn’t finished. Each arm blurred as she started a combo that the monster couldn’t keep up with. She planted a firm uppercut on its jaw before striking the wounded chest twice and jumping back.

Flames exploded out of the Dragonkin’s mouth, but Chloe’s aura shroud kept it from doing more than singeing the tail of her uniform coat. Then, her fists lashed out and smashed into the creature’s eye. It reeled back as she continued to press the attack, a dozen fists flying in a single second. Ezra’s vision blurred as Chloe danced around her opponent. Each of its attacks threatened her life, but none managed to land. Instead, she stepped into range just long enough to smash her fists home in the wounds it already bore, then out before it could counter her.

Her inner world’s exhausted, Jormun observed above Ezra. She can only keep it busy for now, but that’ll be enough. That archer’ll take care of it in a few seconds.

Ezra didn’t have the strength to ask any questions as Chloe masterfully drove the Dragonkin back and forth, keeping it contained and away from the three wounded and the battle line. Just like Jormun said, that was long enough for Basil to make his attack. He felt the aura’s gathering fade, and Chloe leaned down, punched both hands into the earth, and her aura flared. The stone under the Dragonkin’s feet surged and crawled up to its ankles, where it solidified.

The monster that’d so easily dealt with Artemis and Helen was given pause by that, unable to move, but as it flexed its legs, the footing began to crack and it freed itself. There wasn’t enough time for it to escape, though. An arrow burst into its chest, flying faster than any that’d come before it. Buried in the Dragonkin’s chest, the arrow just exploded. Ezra had no idea what’d happened, but the monster’s ribs expanded and then burst outward, its organs slipping out and onto the ground. With a wheeze of agony, the monster slumped and fell to the ground, dead.

“The Dragonkin are all dead!” Basil’s voice carried surprisingly well as he shouted for the Duke’s army’s benefit. “The goblinoids will break. Contain them!”

He didn’t say anything more, though Ezra saw as Chloe slumped to her hands and knees, her chest heaving as she tried to gather herself. He couldn’t do anything himself, but the rest of his squad came up without hesitation, Leah bending over him with concern obvious on her face.

“You still alive?” she asked, thumping his chest.

“Yeah,” he barely mustered.

“Good,” she answered, relief shading her eyes as she settled on her knee beside him. “A damn miracle you managed on that orc. Was that your aura?” Before he could answer, she blazed past her question and asked, “Are you okay to sit up?”

“I’m not too wounded,” he replied. “My aura’s exhausted and I can’t really move. I’m still not ready to use an aural attack, so I’m lucky I didn’t pass out from that.”

“And we’re lucky you did it,” Cord said as he stood beside him. “That orc was going to pick us all off if you hadn’t stepped in when you did. Lieutenant Georg’s luckiest of all, since Artemis and Helen couldn’t have helped him in time.”

“How is he? And how are they?” Ezra demanded as they helped him into a sitting position.

“I don’t know,” Leah answered. “Basil ran off to get some medics, Petr’s tending to who he can. We’re not really trained to know what to do right now, so he told us to hang on and wait for someone else to tell us what to do.”

“I need to talk to Artemis at least,” Ezra insisted, sitting up and struggling to his feet with shaky legs. Leah immediately ducked under his arm and helped him walk forward, and Cord mirrored her. Ezra thanked them as they approached Artemis, who was laying on the ground and taking steady, if shallow, breaths.

“How’s Helen?” she demanded before he could say anything. She continued speaking with panting breaths, “My arm’s broken and there’s something wrong inside, but I’m okay for now. How’s she?”

The three recruits stepped towards where the other Adept lay. She’d been disemboweled, and Petr seemed to be in the process of giving up trying to save her. Her face was twisted in agony, as pale and dirty as the faint remnants of snow that filled the valley where they’d fought so desperately. Even so, as Ezra came closer, she opened her eyes and forced a small smile.

“You survived,” she gasped out.

“Yeah,” Ezra felt his throat swell closed. He’d been told a dozen times that people were going to die here, and this was someone he barely knew, but she’d sacrificed her life for him and the rest of his squad.

“Good,” she barely nodded. “Artemis?”

“Alive too. Says she should survive.”

“Let me see her.”

“Put me down,” Ezra commanded his friends, and he slumped to a sitting position as they both hurried to Artemis’s side. There was barely any conversation before they’d lifted Artemis up and carried her over. She constantly cursed but didn’t protest as they brought her over in front of Helen.

Both sisters’ eyes clouded with tears as Artemis saw and knew the price her sister was paying.

“Fucking idiot,” Artemis sobbed as she leaned over with a gasp of pain and kissed Helen’s forehead. “I told you not to use your fire.”

“Yeah… I’m… stupid,” Helen agreed, her hand raising up to cup her sister’s face. “Take care… of Hector.”

“You didn’t need to ask. I love you.”

“That’s what sisters… are for.”

And then, Helen went limp and Artemis’s choking cries swelled to body wracking sobs that made her wince and writhe in pain. Even so, the emotional devastation didn’t allow her physical pain to keep her emotions from venting themselves. She coughed, falling over her sister’s body, the tears refusing to stop. Ezra winced seeing it happen, knowing at least her arm and possibly her ribs were broken, but he forced himself closer to pat her back as comfortingly as he knew how.

It was awkward, but he couldn’t leave her alone like that. Several seconds passed, and eventually Artemis sat up, her eyes as angry as they were bereft. She glared at Ezra, and, though she said nothing, he felt a stab of guilt. If he hadn’t been pulled out of the line, they wouldn’t have been out the safety offered by the line like they’d been, and Helen wouldn’t have taken that hit.

“I can tell what you’re thinking,” Artemis cut his thoughts off. Her voice was hoarse and sounded agonizing. “It’s not your fault. We were coming to close in on the greatest threats anyways. Maybe we were a little distracted because of you, but we were focused before the Dragonkin came.”

“If it’s anyone’s fault,” Basil came out of nowhere, his head hanging low, “then it’s mine. It was hiding well until it got close, and I’d been focusing on picking off the orcs and bugbears until then. Artemis, I’m so sorry.”

She took a shuddering breath, and Ezra could have sworn he saw a flash of condemnation in her eyes as she bowed her head. “Yeah.”

There was no acceptance of the apology, and that reality hung heavy over them as the person that Basil had brought along cleared their throat. Ezra hadn’t consciously noticed the person that the Pseudo-Knight had brought along was there, and it took him an embarrassingly long time to recognize the robes the middle aged man wore. Though they were a plain, nondescript color, the black stole he wore with stone motifs on it was enough of a testament that Ezra shouldn’t have taken five seconds to recognize them.

“Ah, Lord Priest of the Stone,” Ezra bowed his head.

“I’m only a Deacon,” the man nodded at Ezra, “I’m not a Priest and I don’t need you to bow to me. And I’m sorry for your loss, Artemis. That was your name, correct?”

He waited for an answer, which came in the form of a small nod. His hands reached down to Helen’s head and closed her eyes with a gentle touch. “Was she a worshipper of one of the gods in particular?”

“She preferred Baltisse, though we weren’t devout worshippers,” Artemis said through shaking breaths. “The oldest sister, she took care of the us two younger after Ma and Pa died.”

“The Queen,” the deacon nodded. “A ruler, a leader. Though I am a devotee of Pilar, I know Baltisse’s rites, if you’d like me to perform them now. If not, I can merely see to your own healing and be on my way.”

“There’s a church back home where we have a Deacon she knew,” Artemis turned him down. “I’d appreciate what healing you can offer.”

“Of course,” he nodded as he stretched a hand towards Artemis. “I’ll need to see what part of you needs the help most first.”

She nodded, and his fingers prodded gently at her arm, ribs, and stomach. Then, he apologized, “I only have two rings, and I’ve been commanded to retain the upper ring’s spells in holding until commanded otherwise. I’ll see to your ribs and however much of your arm as I can manage with one of my first ring’s slots. Your legs are banged up but should recover quickly, so I’ll exclude them from the healing.”

“Thank you, Deacon.”

He didn’t say anything more, instead standing up and placing both hands on her head as he muttered a prayer. A handful of seconds passed as a golden light gathered on both hands and then pulsed down through her head and into her body. As the light flowed into her chest, she took a deep, comfortable breath, and relaxed a little. Then, as the Deacon finished, he asked, “How’s your arm?”

Artemis moved it and grunted. “Sore. Won’t hold weight, but better. I appreciate it.”

Then, she said nothing as she looked down helplessly at Helen’s body. The Deacon said, “I’ve been asked to help the Lieutenant over there as well, would you like my help moving her after I’m done with him?”

Tears flowed once again down Artemis’s face as she nodded, and Ezra patted her back, not knowing what, if anything, he should say to her. After all, if it’d been Tilly laying there, he’d be broken. Instead, he then hobbled away, Leah and Cord supporting him. Before they got far, Chloe walked up and took a deep breath looking at his face.

“You holding up alright?” she asked, and he shrugged.

“First battle, don’t know what else I can say.”

“Makes sense,” Chloe agreed. “Now, I have other things to do, but do you think Basil did wrong?”

“I don’t know,” Ezra said helplessly. “I don’t know what he did other than shoot the things I saw.”

“He saved dozens of lives by killing orcs and bugbears as they were getting close enough to engage with other lines. His job was to keep fatalities low. He did that. But he missed the Dragonkin, which was also our assignment. He wasn’t strong enough to do everything.”

She let that settle over him before saying one last thing. “This is the path we walk. No matter how far you get, you’ll never be strong enough to save everyone. Even so, we walk it to save as many as we can.”

Ezra nodded, the weight of being a Knight, Master, or Saint settling over him, and Jormun’s constant instructions made more sense.

“Good man. Congrats on surviving. Now, get some rest.”
