Chapter 131: Tracking Down a Ghost

At the dinner table in his uncle’s house, Jack shrugged and continued, “I know I already said as much, but I can say I’m doing really well. I’m happy.” Jack hesitated, but something in him said, ‘screw it.’ “I even went on a date recently, and it went well. So, yeah.” He took another sip of beer.

His uncle grinned. “Oh ho, look who’s comin’ up! Glad to hear it. When do I get to meet her?”

You already have. Despite the thought, Jack held up a hand as he chuckled. “Hold your horses, Uncle; we’re a ways away from that. But you probably will.”

Terrance nodded slowly. “Fair enough. You’re getting to be a good, solid age to marry. If ya got the finances in order, it’s about time to start lookin', I reckon.”

Jack let himself roll his eyes at that. “Your advice is noted, Uncle.” He shifted to set down his beer and lean forward. “Forgive me if I cut to the chase, but a big reason I’m here is that I’m worried about my dad. It’s been too long, Uncle; way too long without hearing from him. In fact, I tried to check up, and I didn’t find anything.”

His uncle pursed his lips and looked off. Took a swig of beer, then shook his head. “We had words last time we spoke, and partly on that subject. About eight months ago, give or take. He reached out to me. Can you guess why?”

“To borrow money,” Jack said immediately.

“To borrow money, that’s right. Now, if you — a man that paid me back before — came to me, and asked, I’d have done it in a heartbeat, son. A heartbeat. But Freddy…” He sighed and shook his head. “He borrowed once, paid back a small amount, then came and borrowed again. I did it, like an idiot. But eight months ago, I wasn’t willing to be an idiot a third time, not even for my brother. I refused.”

“Uncle Terrance, you don’t have to explain yourself when it comes to my dad. I’ve been there. Well, he never borrowed money directly, but I paid for his damn rehab three times after he started getting funding rejected, and as up and down as he swore to pay me back, that didn’t happen. Not that I expected it, but still.”

Terrance winced and took another swig. “It’s the reason he ain’t talkin’ to us, son. He can’t pay us back because he’s locked into that bottle life. Or worse.”

Jack swallowed hard, knowing just how true the last likely was, though Terrance couldn’t know. “Regardless, I aim to find him. You’re sure he didn’t leave an emergency contact?”

Terrance blinked and frowned. “Nothing on me I haven’t tried.”

Here we go. “If I wanted to start tracking his ass down, where do I start? Does he have any old contacts he might keep up with?”

His uncle squinted his eyes and sat up. “Really going after it, huh? Do you know something, son? Did he get into trouble? Is that what this is about?”

Jack was ready with the deceptions he was contractually obligated to make. He shrugged. “That’s what my instincts tell me, Uncle. I’m not going to know until I touch base, now am I?”

Another slow nod from Terrance and a swig of beer. “Truth be told, I haven’t dug, ‘cause I don’t expect he wants to hear from me, whatever the reason. If he’d come out of his hole for anybody, it’s you. If I were gonna dig, I’d hit up his old buddies, who might seek him out a little harder in getting their money back, if you catch my drift. You could check the Rotgut Bar. The owner and tender, Bart, might know something. There’s Snakeman Wilson, but I don’t think he comes ‘round here much these days. Moved to the city.”

“Do you mean that weird snake and mouse raiser? He was friends with my dad?”

Terrance snorted and shrugged. “More or less. Fellow hustlers. Partners in questionable enterprises, that sort of thing.”

“Alright. Good to know. I’ll check into it. Thank you, Uncle.”

“Anytime. Oh, he, ah, also had a girlfriend, last I heard, but I doubt he’d tell you about that, so it’s probably news. Just recall him mentioning it. Didn’t get the name. Maybe someone else knows more.”

“Noted. I’ll find out soon enough.” Jack pulled out his phone. “Know the number to the local pilot agency here?”

“You can borrow mine if you want.”

“Nah, no need, but thanks. I’d prefer giving the locals some business. I can rent for a flat duration, and they can drop me off back in the city whenever I’m done.” Aside from that, it looks more like the behavior of someone who is a smidge more well-off.

“Suit yourself. Henry could use it, I reckon.” He took out his phone and sent Jack the contact.

Shortly, Jack had a local pilot reserved for four hours of use with a guarantee of transport to New Babylon for a drop-off at some point. He set the schedule for the pilot to arrive in ten minutes so he could eat a little more and not seem to be in some big rush. Eating more seemed to satisfy his uncle, as well.

When they parted, Jack shook his uncle’s hand and nodded. “Thanks for the food and the hospitality.”

“Any time,” Terrance said, shifting from a handshake to an embrace. “You’re welcome any time, now, understand? Do come around again. Maybe with your lady friend next time, even.”

Jack chuckled at the irony. “Yeah, maybe.”

“And tell me if you hear anything about your daddy. I’d like to know, either way.”

“I will.” If I’m allowed to, that is.

The vessel was an inoffensive, generic van of sorts, suitable for some light moving and hauling. The people of Eden tended to prefer self-sufficiency, but there were always exceptions.

The pilot, Henry, was an amiable fellow in his early 40’s. They were vague acquaintances, so they greeted each other with familiarity. They exchanged the most basic of small talk on the short trip over to the Rotgut Bar & Grill, a somewhat oversized facility made to look like a big log cabin. Henry stayed in the vehicle while Jack headed in through the door. On an ancient sign next to it was a bold proclamation in cut-out black rubber, seemingly from old tires, reading, ‘5 T’s: Tender Talks, and Tired Tripe Treads.’

Duly noted.

The inside smelled of pine, barbecue sauce, and decades-deep layers of herbal cigarettes. It was a big place, contoured almost entirely out of wood, with a big central bar, many angles of vidscreens, an open dance area, and a stage for the occasional band. There were clusters of people inside, lining the bar or eating at tables.

The bartender was a thick, grizzled, middle-aged man with hairy forearms, a little towel over one shoulder, and an aura that declared quite clearly he’d be taking nobody’s shit, and you better not start any in his bar. He gave no special attention as Jack walked up to a spot at the bar, just nodding his way as he filled up someone else's mug with the tap.

When the bartender came over, he asked, “What’ll ya have?” in a fashion that was a competition between grunt and actual speech.

“Ya still serve dark ale, here?”

“Damn right. Good taste, son.” He nodded as he turned around to immediately fetch it. There was only one option: the tap. He filled a mug with the clearly darker brew and brought it over, a bit of foam right at the top. He lingered, scanning Jack’s face. “You a Laker, maybe?”

Jack grinned and nodded as he took a sip of the flavorful brew. As he expected, his new taste buds preferred complexity in a way they hadn’t before the change. Sour notes were especially good. “Ahh. This is even better than I thought. Anyway: Jack Laker. Good to meet you.”

“Freddy’s son,” the bartender replied, and he couldn’t help forming a bit of a grimace. “No offense, but that man owes me money, and not just a tab. All the same, welcome back. Think he brought you in here when you were little.”

Jack nodded. “He did, yeah. And thanks for the welcome. Actually, Freddy is” — Jack pulled out his phone and connected to the bar’s payment service, adding two hundred bennies labeled as ‘tab’ and executing — “kind of why I’m here.”

The bartender had a ‘kaching!’ sound go off from a tablet at his hip, so he pulled it up and glanced at it, his eyebrows soon rising appreciatively. He nodded once at Jack and then shrugged. “That man doesn’t wanna be found, especially by the likes o’ me. I take it you can’t track him down, either, then?”

Jack shook his head. “Did you ever get close? Anything that might help would be appreciated.”

The bartender looked away and leaned on his elbows over the bar, brows furrowed in concentration and contemplation. “Hmm. Sly Freddy, he’s a dodgy one, alright. Heard he was smugglin’ drugs or something, but hell if I know the details. I figured he found his way into some money, though. I had friends dig around in The Babs — just to guilt-trip him, mind ya. Nothing much to show for it, but did track down his girl, once. Ex-girl, and no help. Frances… Limer. Fran. She was nervous. Might’ve held back. Maybe you’d have more luck with her. She lived over in Thor, as of a year ago or so.”

Jack nodded. “I’ll definitely give it a shot, thanks. By Thor, do you mean Glitter Row?”

The bartender straightened and gave him a lopsided, sardonic grin. “That I do. One of the complexes near it, anyway.”

Great.

Thor District had a rather anti-themed, often maligned zone known for its strip clubs. On the street, you might hear it given the unflattering and sarcastic name ‘Whore District,’ though it was certainly unfair as a whole. There was a great sandwich place there. Sure, there were always glittery strippers there eating salads at a discount, but the food was the real attraction!

Come to think of it, I did bump into my dad there, once. That was… awkward.

After taking a big gulp of ale, Jack asked, “What about Snakeman Wilson?”

The bartender frowned. “What about him? Haven’t seen him around in a while. Good riddance, at that. He’s a weird little man.”

“I heard that, but I also heard he was an acquaintance and involved business-wise with my dad.”

The bartender shrugged. “Never knew. Sorry.”

Jack took another sizable swig of beer and shrugged it off. “I’ll be looking to find him, in any case.”

Someone called for a refill, and the bartender pointed over at him in acknowledgment. Before turning away, he said, “Something tells me you’ll be headin’ out after that drink. Nice seein’ ya, Jack. Come back around for more on that tab, alright? It’ll be waitin’ for ya. I’ll save you a good whiskey to open, and a drink on the house from it.”

Jack raised the mug in salute. “You bet. Thanks. Bart, right?”

The bartender nodded. “And if you do catch yo’ daddy? Tell his sorry ass to finally pay an old friend back. I reckon he ain’t got many left, and when we’re square, we’re square.”

Jack merely nodded, and Bart turned away to help his customers.

Not in a rush, but not nursing the drink, either, Jack finished it off and departed. He wasn’t quite done with Eden, however.

He made a point to touch base with other businesses and a few people he knew around the platform, 'saying hi' and asking after his dad. This had the primary goal of simply getting the word out that he was looking, in the hopes that any ‘unknown’ acquaintances, contacts, or informants of his dad would eventually get word, even without admitting they knew anything.

He got a few mentions about Snakeman Wilson, and also something about another friend of his, Stephen Laborman. Mini reported this man as a transporter of the Farmer’s Alliance, so this was potentially useful intel, to say the least.

After getting that unexpected nugget, Jack popped into the van’s backseat and asked to be dropped off at Chen Zero Station on New Babylon, effectively calling the trip quits. He leaned back and closed his eyes in the seat, signalling he was tired and not inclined toward small talk. He set his bag as his feet — a few purchases of handmade doodads and jewelry for Neex, including a cute plush stuffed animal of a furry baby cow with a cowboy hat and ‘fur bangs’ over its eyes. She was going to squeal over it, guaranteed.

It felt surreal to take the trip again, out from Platform Eden to New Babylon. The last time he’d done this, he was a prisoner who had probably committed crimes, with no clue that they’d be swept under the rug. When he’d crossed out of Eden airspace into the Memorial accessways back then, he’d briefly felt like he might get disintegrated by an angry goddess. Things were far different this time.

As soon as they crossed the threshold, new information was exchanged, and nearly instantly, Memoria’s voice was popping off in his head, gleeful. “Yes! That’s good info, my darling boy! We’ll get monitoring on this Laborman fellow. Not sure we want to bring him in just yet, not with the fallout we’ll get with the Alliance without more. We are aware of Mr. Wilson, but his whereabouts are unknown and believed to be connected to the same elements. You added a little more circumstantial confirmation there. Well done, Jack!”

“Glad to help,” Jack offered mentally. “What about the stripper? What’s my next play?”

“Former stripper. Her new boyfriend is a thrice-divorced owner of two clubs and a restaurant where she’s a hostess. She was raising money for an unclear career upgrade, but he wants her to raise his babies instead. She isn’t pregnant yet, though.”

“Yeesh. Shit changes quickly in the city.”

“Indeed, it does. We’ve already had someone go undercover in the guise of an old friend of your father’s, to poke her for information, but she is cagey and suspicious. She might be a little annoyed at the fourth or fifth person bothering her about him, but we still want you to do it. After all, you’re the one she might actually finally spill to, or trigger to get word to him that his son is looking for him. If not… We might send an actual interviewer to press more officially. Agent Sato.”

“Understood. I’ll give it a shot.”

“She’s off-work in an hour. Lives in her boyfriend’s pricey condo in Thor. Has a full AI persona built in. You’ll be reported on to him, that’s certain.”

“Lovely. Has spying drawn up anything? Because I know you’re spying, too.”

“She’s too paranoid for that. She has various unusual contacts but engages them rarely and carefully, I’ll put it that way. For drugs, that is. I can’t confirm whether she can contact Freddy, however.”

“Are you going to arrest her for drugs, by the way?”

“For prior use? I decided not to. She got off the bad stuff, in any case. Honestly, humanity has been forcibly bending my will to care about the subject for a very long time. Nothing I do works, not with my trusting approach and the need for contracts and treaties. In short, no, I won’t bother unless she is proven involved in something worse. She has value to observe, and all value should be taken into account in dealing with people. I don’t seek out persecution by the letter, whatever you may’ve heard to the contrary.”

“Good to hear. I suppose I shouldn’t be surprised, considering I got off scot free for what I did.”

“That’s where you can discover my true evils, Jack. I will blind my eyes to quite a lot for my chosen. But I shouldn’t be telling you this, lest you decide to get even more spoiled.”

“Excuse you. I am not spoiled at all.”

There was a brief peal of laughter before she disconnected. Rude.

Jack wasn’t really inclined to check out his new apartment yet, so he just killed time downtown, having a few snacks and people-watching. He touched base with Neex, telling her he had goodies from Eden, and she replied she couldn’t wait to see them, as well as him again. She sent an Ira selfie of her making a peace sign and cross-eyes and asked him for one, but he refused, as he was ‘undercover.’ She pressed, but he stuck to his guns. No way would she see the gut and extra body fat again!

When the time came, he took a cab to the condo unit not far from the ‘colorful’ strip that was Glitter Row. The place was obviously pricey just from the limited units and stylish architecture. It had brick patterns alternating with white and black, blocky shapes, in general pleasing to the eye in a way hard to define.

Though it had rooftop access, that was all privately reserved, even disallowing dropoff (which was among the snootiest of behaviors in The Babs), so he had to go in from ground level, into an AI-controlled lobby with severe access restrictions to get any further. Two tough-looking bouncer types stood like statues by the elevator, not even looking at him, in an aura of deliberate unapproachableness.

He walked up to the somewhat ironic lobby front desk, which was not manned at all. A hologram materialized behind it, of a politely smiling fellow in a suit. “May I help you, sir?”

“Yes,” Jack said, remaining as cool and collected as possible as he let his gaze slide around. “I’d like to ring up Suite Sixteen, if you don’t mind? For Fran.”

“Name, sir? Are you expected?”

“Jack Laker! Not expected, but not unwanted. Tell her it’s a surprise.” He winked and lifted his hand to wriggle his gift bags that were for Neex. Just bullshit to avoid getting rejected out of hand by the AI. It was the same reason he used the seemingly familiar ‘Fran.’

“I see. One moment, please…” The hologram simply disappeared.

Jack leaned over the counter, drumming his fingers and glancing over at the bouncers, who had not seemed to move even a centimeter. He knew they would if they needed to, and if they did, it meant he was no longer welcome.

A hologram suddenly spawned where the figure had been, but it showed only a shadow silhouette of a woman’s face, and glowing eyes seeming to peer at him. A woman’s voice, slightly modulated, asked curtly, “What do you want? Did you deliberately confuse the AI? Don’t tell me this is about what I think it is, for the umpteenth time.”

Jack made an apologetic wince. “It’s that bad, is it? Yeah. This is about my dipshit dad. I heard you knew him.”
