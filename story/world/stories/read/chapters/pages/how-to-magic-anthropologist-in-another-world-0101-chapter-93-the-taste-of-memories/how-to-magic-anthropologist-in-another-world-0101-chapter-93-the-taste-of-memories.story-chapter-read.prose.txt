Louis was already sitting upright on the edge of the beanbag chair when Nina and I arrived in her sanctuary. Nina had probably grabbed her before coming to look for me. The cook looked around with wide eyes, slowly turning in place as she took in the floating islands suspended beneath the endless blue sky.

"So this is what a Dream Sanctuary looks like..." she whispered.

"It changes from person to person," Nina replied. "Mine just happens to look like this."

"I can't wait to see Silnar's, and yours too, Alice."

"Speaking of him," I said, looking around, "he hasn't shown up yet."

"Not yet," Nina said. "We'll wait for him before heading to your sanctuary. Mine has a gate leading into the Great Dream, so it will be much easier for him to find than yours."

That made sense. I think creating a gate to the Great Dream should be my next project. I looked at the dwindling battery charge displayed on my phone.

Once I had enough Inspiration, that is.

Since we had time to spare, we decided to show Louis around. The girl was still in awe of the environment, her eyes constantly wandering toward the endless sky surrounding us.

"It feels so real," she murmured. "I know I'm dreaming, but..." She crouched to touch the grass beneath her feet. "Everything feels solid."

I instinctively started floating, and Nina did the same beside me.

"Come on, let's go," I called to Louis.

From the ground, she just stared at me with wide eyes.

"Alice! You can fly!"

"So can you," I called back. "This is a dream. Follow me."

"I can fly!?"

"Of course you can. You're lucid, remember?" I explained. "You're aware that you're dreaming, so you can actively participate in it."

The Holy cook looked over the edge of the island before immediately taking a cautious step backward.

"I don't think that's a good idea."

"It is," I assured her. "You can't really hurt yourself here. You're dreaming, remember?"

"I know you keep saying that, but jumping off a floating island still feels like a terrible idea."

"Then don't think of it as jumping." Nina gently took both of Louis' hands. "Think of it as swimming through the air. Will it, and it will happen."

Louis frowned.

"That's it?"

"That's it."

For several long seconds, nothing happened.

Then, almost imperceptibly, Louis' feet lifted an inch above the grass.

Her eyes widened.

"I'm..." She floated another few inches higher. "I'm floating!"

The excitement immediately broke her concentration.

She dropped back onto the ground with a surprised squeak.

I couldn't help laughing.

"You almost had it."

"I did!" She looked at her own feet as though expecting them to start floating again on their own. "I actually did!"

The second attempt came much faster.

This time, she rose nearly two meters into the air before wobbling like someone learning to ride a bicycle for the first time.

Her arms pinwheeled wildly.

"I don't know how to steer!"

"Just think about where you want to go," Nina called encouragingly.

Louis leaned a little too enthusiastically.

Instead of moving gracefully, she shot sideways with a startled yelp, drifting past us before slowly beginning to spin through the open sky.

"I didn't mean to go that way!"

"Nonsense," I called after her. "Deny it all you want. Your subconscious definitely wanted you to go fast."

After several awkward minutes filled with surprised squeals, accidental spins, and one rather spectacular upside-down somersault, the three of us ended up zipping across the sanctuary at maximum speed. Louis' grin was wide as she hovered several meters above us, breathing heavily despite not actually needing to.

"I can fly!"

She took off again before either of us could answer, circling the nearby islands with growing confidence.

Nina looked at me and smiled.

"Remember your first dream flight? You were just as eager."

"At least I was wearing pants," I said as Louis zipped overhead.

Nina burst out laughing.

Eventually, Louis drifted back toward us, landing with only a slight stumble.

"I could get addicted to this."

"You probably will," I admitted. "Especially since you're getting married to a Dreamer."

"You're telling me I could spend every night dreaming like this with him?" Louis' eyes lit up at the thought. "It's like doubling the time we'll have together."

Once Louis got used to the idea of a life filled with happy dreams, Nina guided us toward the largest island at the center of her sanctuary.

Unlike the crafting islands surrounding it, this one looked like a wallless, roofless hall.

"This is my Hall of Memories," Nina said proudly.

The space resembled a small museum. Objects rested upon carefully crafted pedestals, each preserved exactly as Nina remembered it.

There were musical instruments, weapons, and one section held a microscope, telescope, and spyglasses. Another displayed carved wooden animals with moving joints.

At the center stood Riri's mandolin. The artifact emitted faint music, harmonizing with itself as it played multiple melodies at the same time.

Across from it rested my taser spear, faithfully recreated down to every detail, from the copper rings to the blue jean straps. Sometimes it blinked, teleporting into a different position. Other times, sparks flashed between the prongs.

Both pieces occupied the center of the hall as though they were the sanctuary's greatest treasures.

"That wasn't here the last time I visited."

Nina nodded, her expression turning serious.

"It appeared after I finished it."

"Why is it center stage?" I asked.

"I went to a Creation State to craft it," she said. "It's an artifact, just like Riri's mandolin."

Before I could ask any further questions, Nina suddenly tilted her head.

"Oh."

"What is it?" Louis asked.

"Silnar just arrived."

Without another word, Nina turned toward the distant island where the Great Dream Gate stood.

"Come on, let's greet him."

"Silnar! Look, I'm flying!" Louis called the moment we spotted him waiting beside the Great Dream Gate.

Silnar looked up, and his entire face lit up.

For a moment, he simply stood there, watching her soar toward him with an expression that was almost childlike. Louis slowed herself until she was only a few feet above him before carefully descending. Silnar's ears turned bright red, and he instinctively looked away, his gaze dropping toward the ground.

He didn't have much time to recover before Louis threw her arms around him.

As Nina and I drifted closer, I noticed something odd.

Silnar wasn't as broad or as tall as he was in the waking world.

He still looked athletic, but he was lean. The mountain of muscle he'd built was gone. His shoulders were narrower, his jaw softer, and he looked noticeably younger. If I had to guess, I'd have placed him somewhere around sixteen rather than his actual nineteen.

Then something fascinating happened.

As Louis hugged him, Silnar seemed to notice the difference as well, especially in height, as Louis was almost as tall as he was.

His body shifted, though it wasn't as dramatic as a Kindred transformation.

He grew nearly twenty centimeters taller in the space of a heartbeat. His shoulders broadened, his arms thickened, and the familiar physique I was used to seeing returned, as though he had subconsciously corrected the mistake. Louis felt the change immediately, as she suddenly needed to stand on her tiptoes.

She pulled back just enough to stare up at him in confusion.

"What was that?"

Silnar rubbed the back of his neck.

"Here in dreams..." he said awkwardly. "My Dreamer side is a little stronger than my Kindred side."

"So that was your self-image?" I asked.

He nodded sheepishly.

"I suppose I still think of myself as the awkward young cook. My Kindred training was always an afterthought," he said. "It's not like I fight for a living like a freelancer."

Louis smiled warmly.

"You don't have to change on my account."

Silnar blinked.

"I love every version of you."

The poor boy somehow managed to blush even harder.

"Thank you..."

Then his expression suddenly shifted into exaggerated disappointment as he looked toward Nina and me.

"But more importantly..." His eyes narrowed. "I can't believe you girls didn't wait for me before teaching My Girl how to fly."

"...Oops." I winced. "We couldn't exactly tour this sanctuary without it," I defended myself. "Flying was the logical first lesson."

"Do you know how long I've dreamed about sharing that experience with her?" he said, shaking his head dramatically.

"Tough luck," Nina said with a laugh. "You snooze, you lose."

Silnar looked at her for several long seconds.

Then a mischievous grin slowly spread across his face.

Before any of us realized what he was planning, he scooped Louis into a perfect princess carry.

Louis let out a startled squeak before immediately relaxing into his arms.

"Silnar?"

"You snooze..." He looked at Nina defiantly. "...you lose!"

With that single declaration, he leaned backward through the Great Dream Gate.

Both of them vanished.

"...Ah, shit." Nina grabbed my wrist. "They're escaping!"

The next instant, She dragged me forward as we shot after them.

The four of us burst into the flowing currents between dreams. Unlike ordinary space, the pathways connecting Dream Sanctuaries resembled rivers of shifting color. Countless fragments of distant dreams drifted past like glowing clouds, while ribbons of light twisted through an endless pastel sky.

Ahead of us, Silnar was already flying at full speed.

Louis remained comfortably nestled in his arms, laughing so hard she could barely speak.

"You can't just kidnap her!" Nina shouted.

"I absolutely can!" Silnar yelled back without slowing down. "She's my fiancée!"

"We're supposed to visit Alice's sanctuary first!"

"Nope!" He banked around a floating stream of violet light. "My Girl sees my sanctuary first!"

Louis peeked over his shoulder toward us.

"I'm sorry!" she called between giggles. "I don't actually mind getting kidnapped!"

"Traitor!" Nina shouted dramatically.

I was laughing far too hard to be any help.

"You know," I managed between breaths, "I don't think I've ever seen Silnar cause trouble before."

"Has he been spending time with you?" Nina accused.

"Hey, I barely talked to the guy," I protested. "Not every chaotic thing that happens gets laid at my feet."

Nina looked unconvinced.

Five minutes of playful pursuit later, Silnar suddenly dived toward an enormous wooden door floating by itself in the sea of dreams.

He passed straight through.

We followed a heartbeat later.

Instead of emerging into another open landscape, we found ourselves inside what looked like a pantry... well, I wasn't sure the word pantry did this space justice.

The sanctuary was enormous.

Shelves stretched upward until they disappeared among distant clouds, while others extended so far into the distance that they faded into a soft haze.

Entire mountains of food filled the sanctuary.

Fresh vegetables rested beside baskets overflowing with fruit. Barrels of flour stood beside towering stacks of spices. Wheels of cheese, hanging herbs, sacks of grain, jars of preserves, cuts of meat, fish packed in ice, rows upon rows of bottles, and enough baking ingredients to supply an entire kingdom covered the endless shelves.

It wasn't only ingredients, either. There were also fully cooked meals, perfectly preserved and ready to be served. Shelves of soups, stacks of pastries, and an endless assortment of cooked meats filled the pantry.

Each time I focused on a dish, I could somehow smell it. Yet despite the endless amount of food, the smells never clashed, as if the sanctuary filtered them somehow.

Everything looked perfectly organized despite the impossible scale.

Silnar gently set Louis down atop one of the broad wooden platforms between the towering shelves before spreading both arms proudly.

A huge smile crossed his face.

"Welcome..." He paused dramatically, "to my sanctuary."

The three of us slowly turned in place.

Louis simply stared with her mouth hanging open, her eyes somehow growing even wider with every passing second.

Even Nina looked impressed.

Honestly, I was rather surprised. I had expected Silnar's sanctuary to be smaller than Nina's since he was only half Dreamer while she was a full one. Instead, this place looked at least twice the size.

"How is this even possible?" Louis asked, slowly turning in place.

Silnar smiled.

"Every ingredient I touch and every meal I taste appears somewhere in my sanctuary."

I looked around in complete bafflement.

"But you're only nineteen. Even if you ate four meals every single day of your life, there is no way you could fill something this large."

"It's a legacy sanctuary," Nina explained with a small sigh.

"Bingo," Silnar said with a grin. "My late father had one almost exactly like this."

His voice softened as he looked across the endless shelves.

"When I was little, he used to bring Mum and me into his sanctuary every night. We'd cook together, experiment with new ingredients, and whenever he traveled, he'd come back with an expanded sanctuary filled with even more strange foods for us to taste. Everything he experienced became part of my childhood."

"So you inherited his sanctuary?" I asked.

"In a way." He nodded. "He made sure I tasted everything at least once before he passed away."

He didn't look sad, though there was a hint of nostalgia in his voice.

He stretched out one hand.

A porcelain bowl shot off one of the distant shelves, weaving effortlessly between rows of ingredients before stopping in front of him. The dessert resting inside looked familiar.

"Kanta," I said.

The pudding-like sweet duplicated itself into four identical portions before floating toward each of us.

"In here," Silnar said quietly, "I can still taste my father's cooking."

"And now..." He smiled at Louis. "I can share it with the woman I love."

Louis accepted the bowl with both hands before taking a careful spoonful.

The moment it touched her tongue, her eyes widened.

"It tastes exactly like yours."

"It should." Silnar laughed.

"This is insane!" She shook her head. "You must have spent years recreating your father's recipe."

Silnar wrapped an arm around her shoulders.

"I tried my best."

He gestured toward the endless pantry surrounding us.

"Everything here is preserved exactly as I remember tasting it."

I slowly looked around again.

"So... all of this is indexed inside your head? Does it help you remember recipes in the waking world?"

He shook his head.

"Nope. Some of these I only tasted once as a child, just to acquire them. Sometimes even I am surprised when I find something. My father collected some really weird stuff."

That... that was an absurdly useful ability.

"If I asked about a specific ingredient," I said thoughtfully, "could you summon it as long as you've encountered it before?"

"Pretty much. Either in the waking world or in my father's sanctuary. I tried other people's dream configurations in the Great Dream, but it didn't work. They never felt as real to me as my father's."

"Do you have chocolate?" I didn't even hesitate to ask.

Silnar blinked.

"What?"

"Cocoa beans. Cacao powder. Theobroma cacao. The cacao fruit."

He frowned and closed his eyes.

For several long seconds, nothing happened.

"I've never heard any of those names," he admitted.

My shoulders slumped.

Then his expression shifted.

"...Wait."

His eyes opened again.

"Something responded."

A bottle shot off one of the higher shelves and landed neatly in his hand.

The dark glass was sealed with wax, and the liquid inside shimmered with a deep amber-brown color.

"Oh, I remember this one." He smiled. "I remember Mum getting mad when my father had me taste it."

He uncorked the bottle and poured each of us a small sample.

Louis sniffed hers before immediately setting it down.

"I'll pass." She shook her head. "It smells like alcohol."

The rest of us took cautious sips.

The drink was unlike any wine I'd tasted on Earth.

It was thick and velvety, almost syrupy without becoming unpleasantly sweet. The first flavor reminded me of honey mixed with ripe tropical fruit, followed by notes of dates, lychee, and something floral I couldn't identify. Beneath all of that lingered an earthy taste. Not chocolate, nor roasted cocoa, probably the sweet white pulp surrounding the beans before they were ever fermented.

It was bright, fragrant, and surprisingly refreshing despite the alcohol.

"What is this?" Nina asked after swallowing.

Silnar looked down at the bottle.

"My father said he tasted it in a village deep inside the Kindred Realm during one of his expeditions."

"I read about a similar drink, this isn't made from cocoa beans. It's made from the fruit. I know a lot of recipes that use the seeds of that fruit."

"Coffee, and now this." Louis laughed. "Your culture must use a lot of seeds from exotic plants."

"Don't you get it?" I said excitedly. "If someone here is making wine from the pulp..." I looked at the bottle again. "Then cacao trees exist in this world."

A grin spread across my face.

"Which means making chocolate isn't impossible after all."

We spent the next hour wandering through Silnar's impossible pantry.

Every few minutes, someone would bring up something new for us to taste as we flew throughout the endless shelves. After a while, Louis and Silnar quietly disappeared into a small kitchen perched atop one of the taller pillars.

Unlike the endless shelves surrounding it, the kitchen looked well used. Copper pots hung above a polished wooden counter, fresh herbs rested in clay pots, and a large stone oven radiated comforting warmth. I suspected it was a replica of Silnar's family restaurant, as Louis already seemed familiar with the space despite it being her first time in the sanctuary. The two of them naturally slipped into working side by side, preparing meals together as though they'd already been married for years.

From where Nina and I stood, we could occasionally hear soft laughter drifting down between the shelves, followed by whispered conversations neither of us could quite make out.

Nina glanced toward them before deliberately looking away.

"I think we should let them have a little privacy."

"I agree."

Nina became fascinated by shelves filled with different varieties of barely edible tree resins, sap, syrups, waxes, and other sticky plant extracts collected from across the Realms. She carefully examined each one, pressing her fingers against the hardened surfaces, stretching them, and even tasting them before dismissing them and reaching for the next sample.

"I'll need to remember these," she murmured. "If I become familiar enough with them, I should be able to summon copies inside my own sanctuary."

"What for?" I asked.

"I want to test conductivity." She held up a lump of amber-colored resin against the light. "Wax works, but maybe one of these is a better insulator."

I left her to play with her sticky goop.

Besides... I had discovered something far more dangerous.

There was no limit to how much food I could eat here. Dream or not, every dish tasted completely real. Yet my stomach never became full, nor did I have to worry about gaining weight. Each time I finished something, it simply respawned. Though, it would have been a waste to eat only one thing when I could taste everything.

Fresh pastries gave way to unfamiliar fruits. Those led to roasted meats I'd never seen before, followed by soups, cheeses, sweet breads, smoked fish, and desserts from cultures I couldn't even begin to imagine.

Not everything tasted divine. More than once, I was devastated when the appearance didn't match the taste. Other times, it was the opposite, where goopy sludge somehow tasted like ambrosia.

I was halfway through a stack of fluffy pancakes drenched in berry syrup when Nina finally intervened.

"Alice."

"Mhm?"

"I think you've eaten enough."

I looked down at the empty plates surrounding me as they respawned anew.

"...Have I?"

She folded her arms.

"If you keep doing this, you might convince yourself you've already eaten when you wake up."

"Is that actually a thing?" I blinked.

"It can be." Nina nodded. "Dreams affect the mind. If your subconscious believes you spent the whole night feasting, you might wake up without feeling hungry."

"...And why would that be bad?" I asked, looking at the pancakes in my hands.

"It's only this once." I shrugged. "It's not like I have unlimited access to magical dream food in my own sanctuary."

"I suppose that's a fair argument." She sighed in defeat.

"Thank you." I grinned.

I happily resumed my breakfast... or lunch... or four hundred and sixty-eighth dinner. At some point, I'd completely lost track.

Eventually, Louis and Silnar returned carrying a tray loaded with freshly baked pastries that apparently hadn't existed anywhere else in the sanctuary. Using the ingredients stored here, they had tried creating something new.

"These are good," I said as I tasted one.

"They're a mix between Soulit sweet cake and Holy pomegranate molasses bread."

"Will they permanently appear among the stored food here now that you've tasted them?" I asked.

Silnar took a big bite.

"Of course. I tasted them, so they'll show up."

He finished his serving, stretched out his hand, and another identical pastry flew from one of the shelves straight into his palm.

"All right." Nina clapped her hands together.

The sharp sound echoed across the sanctuary.

"It's time to leave."

"Already?" Louis asked.

"We still need to visit Alice's sanctuary," Nina reminded her.

"And I have recipes to teach you," I added. "That's the whole reason I invited you."

We made our way back toward the doorway through which we'd entered. The four of us burst back into the flowing currents of the Great Dream, crossing through the sea of pastel colors until Nina's familiar floating islands appeared ahead.

We landed only briefly.

Without stopping, I led everyone toward the plain wooden apartment door floating at the edge of Nina's sanctuary.

Before opening it, I rested my hand on the handle and looked back at the others.

"One thing."

They all looked at me.

"Everything you see beyond this door stays a secret."

Silnar nodded immediately.

"You have my word."

The man was a Dreamer too. He understood sanctuary etiquette.

Louis smiled.

"Don't worry." Then she grinned mischievously. "Besides, I doubt your sanctuary can be more impressive than Silnar's."

"We'll see."

I opened the door.

The three of them stepped into my tiny college apartment.

Louis blinked.

"This is your sanctuary?"

"It is."

She slowly looked around the modest living room, the cramped kitchenette, the bookshelves overflowing with university textbooks, and the worn sofa. The space had its oddities, like the television, the laptop, and the lightbulbs, but it wasn't nearly as mind-shattering as Silnar's or Nina's sanctuaries.

"It's... cozy."

"I know. It's a replica of where I used to live before coming to Hano."

"Oh," Louis said.

Nina smiled a knowing smile before adding,

"Everything you see in Alice's sanctuary exists in the waking world."

The two slightly confused visitors tried to understand why Nina and I were acting so coy.

I walked over to the tarot table resting in the middle of the room.

Without saying anything, I picked up The Fool and returned it to the deck.

Then I placed The Moon into the empty slot.

The apartment dissolved around us. Walls melted into drifting moonlight.

A heartbeat later, we stood inside my father's high-rise apartment. The blackout curtains were still open from my last visit with Riri, revealing the floor-to-ceiling windows stretching before us. An ocean of glittering city lights spread beneath the night sky as cars moved along the roads below.

Louis froze.

Her mouth slowly fell open as she stared across the illuminated skyline, the neon advertisements immediately catching her attention.

"Alice." She barely managed to whisper. "Where in the Bloody Torment are you from?"
