“You two are fucking morons!”

That was the very first thing that came bursting out of Khalid’s mouth the moment he settled into the chair before them, as if he had been holding those words clenched between his teeth the entire journey here, waiting for the precise moment he could finally let them loose.

Xander swallowed the grunt that crawled up his throat and cast a sidelong glance toward Zubayr, who stood rigidly beside him, eyes fixed on the floor. Pathetic, he thought. You’re also a Mithril, but you crumple like wet parchment just because some rich bastard is raising his voice.

“How in the hell did something like this happen?”

Khalid wasn’t done yet. His words continued to spill out in a torrent, his hooked nose and groomed moustache trembling with the force of his fury as he spoke. Looking at him now, no one could have recognized this lanky man as the same Guildmaster who was always wrapped in an almost theatrical air of superiority. That version of him had vanished, and in his place stood someone who was no more dignified than a common thug who just discovered he had been cheated in a game of cards.

“Our Folded Chamber, hidden in the forest, protected by countless monsters, was wiped out in a single fucking night. And if that weren’t bad enough, its very existence has been laid bare to the public, and people from the Adventurer’s Guild are now strolling in and out of it as though they own the place.”

“My sincerest apologies,” Zubayr said, still staring at his boots. “You put me in charge of the place, and I’ve failed you in every conceivable way. I'll accept full responsibility—”

“And what good does that do anyone?” Khalid snapped. “Can you somehow undo everything that has already happened?”

Zubayr licked his lips. “Should we... deal with the people the Guild sent into the chamber?”

“Are you fucking stupid? What exactly do you propose we do with them? Kill them? The entire town already knows. Are you suggesting we kill everyone in Daelin to solve the problem? Do you have any idea, any idea at all, what the Conclave would do if we pulled a stunt like that?”

“Then... what do we do?”

Khalid let out a slow breath. “Well, it’s not like the situation is completely unsalvageable. There’s nothing inside that base pointing directly to us. Perhaps a few circumstantial details. But suspicion is not proof, and without something concrete, something irrefutable, there is nothing to act on.”

“You think so?” Xander snorted. “How about the monsters kept there? They must be blind if they don’t recognize them as the same ones that have attacked their outpost.”

“As I said, they have no proof,” Khalid said, waving a dismissive hand. “They’re free to suspect whatever they want, but suspicion alone does not force anyone’s hand. It’s not like the higher-ups within the Conclave are ignorant of our organization’s existence. They simply choose to avoid a direct confrontation. They know exactly who I am, yet they appointed me Guildmaster of Iskora regardless.”

His gaze shifted between Xander and Zubayr.

“There’s a game being played between our superiors and their superiors, one that people like us are not privy to in any meaningful detail. And one of the unspoken rules of the game is that certain things do not become public. Both sides understand it. Both sides benefit from it. So no, this will not spiral out of control. That said...”

Khalid’s expression darkened.

“The fact that this mess happened means that our side now sits in a weaker position than it did before. Our superiors will be forced to make concessions. And they will be displeased, very displeased. So they will go looking for someone to hold responsible for that displeasure. Guess who? Me, of course.”

The lanky man reached across the table, fingers closing around the neck of the decanter that sat there. He poured wine into his cup and emptied it in a single swallow.

“But seriously, who the hell is the bastard who did this? After our outpost got burned down, I suspected that it might have been done by the Cult, or the Brefjordian Court, or maybe even the Conclave itself. But now? I’m not so sure anymore. Attacking the Folded Chamber is one thing, but reporting it to the local Guild and exposing everything to the public eye? What kind of lunatic would do something like that? How the hell does someone with that kind of power not understand how the world works? Has the bastard been living under a rock for the past century or something?”

“Well,” Zubayr said. “According to that man Ekon’s account, the culprit—a dark spirit or whatever it is—attacked our base and then departed on its own. And it was Ekon and his friends who chose to bring the matter to the Guild.”

The cup in Khalid’s hand immediately came down against the table with a sharp crack.

“I genuinely do not wish to call you a fucking idiot for the third time today, but you’re leaving me very little choice. Ekon is working with that so-called dark spirit, you imbecile. Which means the decision to go to the Guild and lay everything bare came from the dark spirit himself.”

A beat of silence. Then Zubayr asked, “Now that Ekon has returned to town, should I—”

“You will do nothing. Do you think there hasn’t been enough commotion already? No. Keep your fucking head down. Do not move against anyone. Do not attract any attention. You do absolutely nothing until I tell you otherwise.”

“Understood.”

Khalid’s gaze shifted away from Zabayr, before coming to rest on Xander.

“Don’t for a moment convince yourself that this has nothing to do with you. If you’d dealt with that dark spirit thing that night, none of this would’ve happened. Though perhaps I was expecting too much. Perhaps he’s simply out of your league.”

“That coward spent the entire night running away from me!”

Khalid snorted. “Or perhaps he was simply playing with you.”

Xander clenched his jaw. An unbearable heat flared up in his chest, but it had nowhere to go, so it just sat there, burning inside him. He stared at the lanky man without saying a word.

Khalid didn’t seem to notice. “You carry yourself like some great warrior of legend. And what exactly have you accomplished recently? Ah, yes. You knocked a woman unconscious. A tremendous achievement. Truly, the bards must be singing of you right now.”

He reached for the decanter again, poured more wine, and took a slurp.

“And that brings me to the other headache. Isadora has petitioned the Conclave directly. Directly. They’re now demanding that I hand you over to face consequences for what you did. Damn it. With the other matters, I can still manage. But this? There’s no room for denial.”

“So that’s it then?” Xander growled. “You’re going to let them come and take me?”

“Of course not.” Khalid’s dismissive wave returned. But even as he said it, he turned away, his eyes finding somewhere else in the room to settle rather than meeting Xander’s gaze. “I’m leveraging my influence to prevent it. And, in the worst-case scenario, you disappear. The organisation has the means to make sure you stay hidden. The Conclave won’t find you, no matter how hard they search.”

“Are you serious? If I ran, they’d put a bounty on my head. And eventually, the Judge will come for me.”

Khalid laughed. “Don’t tell me you’re scared. The Judge is nothing but a ghost story. Something they invented to keep their people in line.”

A few weeks ago, Xander might have laughed with him. A few weeks ago, he still had the unshakeable confidence in his own capabilities. He was only a Mithril, yes, and there were many Adamantite above him, but he believed that they were merely people with better tools. Given time, given opportunity, the gap would close. Someday, he would be equal to them. And someday, he would surpass them.

But then, he had come to learn that true monsters existed in this world—monsters that could erase him with a single thought.

And if the Judge were one such monster...

“That’s enough for today,” Khalid said. “I have to go. There are important people to meet, so I can’t waste any more time on two idiots. So, behave yourselves, and wait for further instructions—”

“Actually,” Xander said. “I have something to report.”

Khalid arched a brow. “What’s it?”

“It’s something for your ears alone,” Xander said, glancing at Zubayr.

Khalid jerked his chin, and the other man shuffled out of the room without a word. Xander chuckled to himself. The rich fool was so certain of his superiority that it didn’t occur to him to ask why a subordinate he had just humiliated suddenly wanted a private word. He just couldn’t imagine a world in which his authority was questioned or his orders ignored.

“So,” Khalid said, “what is it that you —”

Xander closed the distance between them in the blink of an eye, his hand clamping around the lanky man’s throat. He drove him backward, slammed him against the wall, and held him there. Khalid’s heels barely found the floor, and every trace of his usual composure evaporated in an instant.

“What—? What are you—”

“You think I haven’t been paying attention? You can’t stop them from arresting me. Whatever influence you claim to have, whatever strings you claim to be pulling, it is just not enough. But you won’t hand me over to the Conclave either, because I know things about the organization, things you do not want anyone else to hear. Which means there’s only one solution left: you’re going to silence me yourself. That’s what you’re turning over in your head right now, am I correct?”

“No! You’ve got it wrong. I’m not—”

“Maybe you’re telling the truth, maybe you’re not. But I don’t trust you. I’m not going to stake my life on your words. I want a guarantee. I want you to make this charge against me disappear entirely.”

“That is exactly what I’ve been—”

“Not hard enough. As I said, I want a guarantee.” Xander smiled. “Let’s say... what would happen if I were to appear above the most crowded district in Iskora, and rain fire down indiscriminately onto everyone below?”

“Are you out of your mind? What could you possibly gain from doing something like that? If you do that, you are dead. You are absolutely, immediately dead.”

“Yes, that would certainly be the end of me. But it would also be the end of you.”

“What?”

“I am your responsibility, am I not? My mess is your mess as well. Besides, you’re already in a bad spot. I don’t really care about secret hideout or Folded Chamber or whatever, but I’ve heard from Zubayr that he had only six men there and he hadn’t received reinforcements for weeks. Why? Is it because you never told your superiors about what happened at the ruins? You kept it to yourself, managed the aftermath quietly, and hoped that the problem would be resolved before anyone higher up noticed anything. That’s why that base of yours is poorly defended.”

Xander laughed.

“You stood in this room earlier and screamed at Zubayr for his failures, even though the real fault lies with you. It now blows up in your face. You can’t hide it anymore, and your superiors are undoubtedly very angry. So, if I were to create a catastrophe the organization cannot possibly cover up, what do you think they would do to you?”

“You... you are absolutely insane.”

“Perhaps. But I’m not stupid. I know that if I merely threatened to kill you, you could try to hide. But this is different. There would be nowhere for you to go. You might be able to run from me. You cannot run from them.”

And the best part of the threat he gave Khalid was that they were both perfectly aware it wasn’t a bluff. He was genuinely capable of it. Yes, if he were going down, then taking several thousand of those fancy folk in Iskora with him might not be the worst way to die.

“Fine, fine. I’ll try to cut a deal with Isadora. I’ll make her drop the charge.”

“Try,” Xander slowly said, his gaze locked on Khalid, “is not good enough. Three days, my dear Guildmaster. You have exactly three days.”

He released the man, stepped back, and turned away.

“Where are you going?”

“Hiding, of course,” Xander said with a chuckle. “So you don’t get the chance to pull anything clever on me. But don’t forget the deadline. Once the clock runs out...”

He walked out of the room, leaving that sentence unfinished.

He hurried through the building, and the moment the door swung shut behind him and the open air of the street met his face, he immediately activated his Reliquary and launched himself up to the sky. There was no reason to keep obeying those stupid rules, no reason to linger here any longer than necessary.

He must get out of Daelin, and fast.

Not because he feared Khalid might order Zubayr and his other underlings to turn on him.

But because remaining in this place meant he might run into her again.

He could still remember that eerie, impossible feeling as clearly as if it had happened only moments ago. A terrible cold had crawled up his spine, spreading through his entire body like frost creeping across a lake in the dead of winter. An unbearable heat had washed over his flesh, as though he had been thrown into a furnace and was moments away from being reduced to ash. Two sensations that contradicted each other in every way. Two sensations that had no business existing in the same body at the same moment. Yet they had both seized him, and he had been powerless to resist either.

And, at that very moment, the monster gazed down upon him.

Hair the color of burning embers, cascading around her face like living fire. Eyes of piercing blue, icy and merciless, boring straight into his very soul.

The instant those eyes locked onto him, the entire world seemed to vanish.

The street disappeared.

The buildings disappeared.

The people disappeared.

Everything disappeared.

It was as though reality itself had been stripped away, leaving behind nothing but an endless void. There was nothing left in the universe, only him and the creature.

He had no idea what was going on, but there was one thing he knew for sure. A certainty more absolute than anything he had ever known in his life.

If he stayed, he would be dead.

And so, with the last bit of strength he could muster, with the last shred of control he still had over his trembling body, he turned and fled.

He ran, and ran, and ran.

Leaving behind the woman lying unconscious in the street.

Leaving behind the monster that stood over her.
