Aleph the Ox-like had placed himself directly in front of the High Temple’s central door. There were other entrances, certainly, but crystalline statues stood before them. Additional traps were also probably in play nearby.

I glanced dubiously at Nora, then at the cloud still holding court above the High Temple. She turned her gaze skyward as well, looking up and over at the eerie sparkle that was Paradise. Then she gave a bit of a nod.

We have a bit of time to spare before daybreak... but not too long.

I took a deep breath, my head full of everything I needed to tell him. “Aleph—”

Tetora’s iron claw landed heavily on my pauldron.

“Let me handle this.”

My eyes traced his arm up to get a good look at his face. He was staring at Aleph, eyes burning bright. There was no growl in his voice, no snarl across his features. Whatever his feelings were, for the first time, I couldn’t read them.

“Alright.”

He took several steps forward, then motioned for us to follow him through the rest of the Forum. With iron claws loose at his sides, he then held up his hand to halt us. Alone, climbed the High Temple stairs, centering himself on the landing opposite Aleph. He had adopted a neutral stance, with his feet shoulder-width apart.

Aleph, for his part, had finally turned his gaze to him. He merely watched, never moving. If anything, I could only imagine he was trying to turn to stone, his shoulders stiff and his face locked in a solemn but resolute sorrow.

And so began their epic stare-down.

Olethros and Nora positioned themselves to scan the temple windows—the very same ones where would-be assassins had lurked before. Nora’s knuckles went white on her staff each time Olethros tensed, but then he’d shake his head.

Hmm... I hear thumping and banging from the Temple.

For my part, I waited and watched, holding my breath. However, that was only because every time my fingers went to my sword hilt, Olethros would motion for me to hold.

The Forum itself was otherwise quiet. Beyond the inner walls, the sounds of battle had changed. There were fewer shouts and clashes. No silver had attempted to penetrate the inner sanctum, but no demons had charged in, either.

Tetora’s ears flicked. Once again, they dipped slowly before furling themselves upright. His golden eyes burned, but they didn’t move from Aleph’s face.

An errant wind blew through what had been dead air. It caused the crystal lamps to shiver and Aleph’s robes to billow. And yet, Aleph continued to hold Tetora’s gaze.

“Well?” Tetora finally asked. “Was that enough time?” His eyes narrowed. “Or should we wait a little longer for the others inside to escape?”

Oh! Tetora’s smarter than I give him credit for.

Aleph didn’t answer, and so Tetora waited.

It was excruciating. Every muscle ached as I tried not to interrupt whatever silent mental struggle the two grandmasters were engaging in. This wasn’t how these two ever settled anything, and I bit my tongue to stop myself from calling time out, knowing I’d only make it worse.

“I have served the Goddess Euphridia faithfully,” Aleph eventually stated, his jaw tight. “Everything I have done, I did because I believed it was right to do in the right way.” His eyes finally lit up. “I did not lie. I did not scheme to secure victory. I protected those who needed protection in the ways that I thought best for them.” He was starting to yell now. “I served the people who needed the most support! If that is what condemns me—if doing what I believed in was somehow wrong.” He squinted his eyes shut and took another breath. “If I was doing good, but under the direction of someone who was not, for reasons that were not—then I have Purpose no longer. Strike me down. I will not raise my hand to stop it.”

The worst part was the lack of rage. The heavy richness of his voice didn’t shake once at the words it espoused. How long had he been trapped here alone, fighting his own deepening nightmare? How long had he watched the dumpster fires in Chairo burn?

Tetora took a deep breath. Then he turned to me.

“See what I just did?” he said with a boyish smirk. “And I didn’t even have to lay a hand on him.”

Before Tetora could hit me up for praise, Aleph’s knees buckled, collapsing like a felled ox.

Tetora, however, was already moving. He lunged forward and wrapped his arms around his shoulders, being sure not to catch him with the tips of his iron claws.

My fists tensed as I wrestled with myself, my arms wanting to join in but knowing this wasn’t the time or place.

Aleph’s cries still haunt me to this day. They were more than just bellows, unmodulated and wracked with guilt and despair.

Yes. I really hate it when my dad cries. How could anyone shake my rock?!

Tetora whispered in his ear, his hand delicately patting Aleph’s shoulder. As his sobs grew softer, he began to stand, though his head and ears continued to droop.

A loud buzz ripped through the Forum. Crimson mist swirled and crackled next to me. General Ragnerus materialized, shimmering with strikes of silent lightning as he solidified his form.

“What,” he bellowed, his head tilting as he took in the Forum, “Is taking you so long?”

I pointed at Aleph and Tetora. Tetora was still holding him, still whispering low into his ear. Aleph’s color was returning, but his shoulders had gone slack against Tetora’s chest.

“Oh.” General Ragnerus folded his arms, turning down his light show. “He’s one of yours, isn’t he.”

It was more of an observation than a question.

“Yeah,” I said very loudly so Aleph could hear. “Always has been, always will be.”

The demon general waved his arms in exasperation. “Well, order him to get out of my way immediately!”

I could see Aleph tensing, a strange glimmer in his eye that instantly chilled me to the bone, for I could only interpret his actions as a second attempt at suicide by cop.

Sorry, Dad, we’re not doing this.

I stepped in front of Ragnerus to head off any further escalation. “Not with that attitude, I won’t.”

Every hackle on Ragnerus raised as he inhaled with a hiss. “I wish to speak to my sister!” he thundered on his exhale.

Raising an eyebrow, I replied, “I thought you wanted to present yourself to the Sanctuary?”

The vapor surrounding him stopped swirling as he shrank a little. “I am… I am allowed to have more than one want, am I not?”

“Yes, I suppose you are,” I said with a stiff nod, crossing my arms. “But pleasantries like please and thank you are very important in this situation. If you want to be taken seriously here, you need to be firm but also polite. Understand?”

Ragnerus pondered my words much too seriously for a moment. With a frown, he looked over my shoulder to Aleph and Tetora. “Please get out of my way so I may blow a hole through that door. Thank you in advance for complying with my sincere request.”

Aleph’s eyes widened in shock as Tetora began to chortle.

“Don’t worry,” he laughed as he helped move to the side. “When we have time, we’ll explain everything.”
