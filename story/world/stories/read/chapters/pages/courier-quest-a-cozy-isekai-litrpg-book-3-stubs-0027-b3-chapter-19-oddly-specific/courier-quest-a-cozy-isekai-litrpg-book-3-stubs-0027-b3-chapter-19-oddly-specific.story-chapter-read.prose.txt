The ice cream was exactly what they needed to cool down and forget all about Surtla and his scam. Trevor, Adabelle, and Clay joined the crowds of Paladona, though it was packed tightly enough that the earth elemental preferred to stay in their master’s backpack. They still poked their head out from the flap so they could see the sights.

Landmarks seemed to inhabit every plaza the seaside city had to offer. The first fountain they came across was similar to a koi pond, though the fish inside were flat and various shades of yellow and green. A stone statue of Cruon rose in a spiral, ascending to the sky, and water gently cascaded down her back so it wouldn’t disturb the creatures underneath.

There were also stone boats carrying both humans and kobolds, some with nets and others with spears. Standing on the tips of his toes, Trevor could see that there were many polished coins in the vessels, like how people from Earth would throw them into fountains. A few hadn’t made it, landing in the water, but it made him want to try it. Producing a copper for himself and Adabelle, he closed his eyes and made a wish before tossing it in.

Hers made it into a boat with perfect accuracy. His was snatched mid-air by a maroon-colored bird.

Its eyes were blue, and it landed on one of the kobold statues. Trevor frowned as the creature stood on one leg, using its other to hold the coin it had stolen. It was studying it closely as if trying to decide on something. They used it to strike the stone a couple of times before examining it again.

“That man’s coin just got caught,” a girl nearby whispered. “Did you see it?”

“What? No way, I want the alumrae to to catch my coin!” a child responded, not at all trying to match the low tone of the one who pointed it out.

Trevor arched an eyebrow and shared a look with Adabelle, who shrugged. Then, he noticed that the people around them were clapping as if something amazing had just happened. Unsure of what was going on, he turned around to try and find the speakers. The bird was being watched by a human girl who couldn’t have been older than fifteen and what was probably her younger brother.

“Hello,” he greeted, raising his hand. She looked at him, her gaze lingering on his Summoned tattoo. “Sorry, we’re new here and haven’t ever seen one of these birds. Alumrae, right? Do they usually try to catch coins?”

While the teenager was still weighing whether they wanted to speak or not, the boy had no qualms. He pointed at the bird, and then towards the sky. “It took both of them! Both of them!”

Trevor and Adabelle turned to see the alumrae flying to the top of the statue. Sunlight glinted off of a pile of coins in Cruon’s mouth, which the bird landed in. It sat down and started polishing its newest acquisitions, as it appeared to have gone to collect the one Adabelle had tossed as well. The courier knew they were perfect, though.

Apparently, so did the bird. It set the copper coins down amongst the rest and began watching the people gathered around the plaza.

“It’s good, good, good luck for one to catch your coin,” the boy said, clearly excited. “Like, you’re going to get free dessert later kind of good luck!”

“Wow, that is some really good luck,” Trevor chuckled as he turned around to face them again. “Better than getting it in the boat, then?”

“People only throw coins in the boat to see if they can get the alumrae’s attention,” the girl answered quietly. “They only go after the shiniest of them all to build their nests out of.”

“How fitting that this one decided to make their home inside of a dragon’s mouth, then,” Adabelle said.

“You didn’t know that, but you threw a coin anyway?” she asked, frowning. Her eyes kept moving to the tattoo on Trevor’s arm that marked him as a Summoned.

The courier smiled. “Where I come from, it’s tradition to throw a coin in a fountain and make a wish. I thought Paladona might have something like that as well.”

“No wishes, just good luck,” she said.

“Awesome. I’ll be looking forward to my free dessert later, then,” Trevor replied with a light laugh. A thought occurred to him, and he approached the two kids. Adabelle followed along. The girl froze again, and he had to wonder why she was so suspicious of them. Either way, he squatted down in front of the boy. “What’s your name?”

“Siral,” he answered immediately. “Siral Dorrenhorn, and this is my sister, Alama.”

“Siral,” the teenager chastised. “You shouldn’t go giving our name to strangers, especially not Summoned.”

“I’m not going to do anything to you two,” the courier said with a reassuring smile. “I’m Trevor, and this is my wife, Adabelle. My familiar is in my backpack. They’re not keen on the crowds. We’re on our honeymoon, so I want to thank you for telling me about the alumrae. It’s a nice little bit of culture, I think. They like shiny things, right?”

The boy nodded vigorously. “They do.”

“Really, really shiny?”

“The shinier the better!”

Trevor looked up at Adabelle, and she smiled at him. Her tail moved to drape across his shoulders. “I’m not sure why you’re looking at me. You already seem to have an idea in mind.”

“I do,” he confirmed before looking back at Siral. Holding up his hand, he retrieved a shining silver coin from his inventory with a flourish. The boy stared at it in wonder. “My familiar is an earth elemental, and they love to polish gems, metals, and stones. I let them take care of my coins because it makes them happy. Clearly, it must be working because the alumrae snatched it right up without a second thought. If you’d like, I wouldn’t mind if—”

A warning flitted through his bond with Clay. Alama gasped, putting a hand on her mouth, while Siral’s eyes went wide. For a moment, Trevor thought that something might have been happening behind him. When he tried to stand up, though, Adabelle’s tail kept him down. It didn’t take him long to figure out what was happening.

The alumrae landed on his shoulder, staring longingly at the tarnish-free silver coin in Trevor’s hand. He didn’t move, didn’t even breathe, as he watched it out of the corner of his eye. The crowd had gone silent as the bird took a couple of jumps towards his head, and then jumped back, only having eyes for the shine.

“No sudden movements,” Trevor whispered to Siral. “Take the coin. Throw it in the air.”

With the slowest nod the courier had ever seen, the boy carefully moved to take the silver coin. The alumrae’s head recoiled backwards an inch, but he didn’t stop. This kid was on a mission.

Siral took the coin from Trevor’s hand and, abandoning caution, threw his arm straight up. The shining silver went flying into the air. Not too far, but easily clearing everyone’s heads.

The alumrae raised its wings, ready for flight, but its eyes didn’t leave the coin. It took off like a shot, talons extended as it flew towards its quarry. The silver didn’t have a chance to start falling. Instead, the maroon bird grabbed it, doing a victory lap around the plaza before returning to the dragon’s mouth.

“Alama! Alama! Did you see?” Siral asked, laughing like a madman as he turned to face his sister. He pointed back at the fountain. “Did you see? Did you see?”

“Yes, I saw,” she said, smiling at the sheer joy in her brother’s expression. “That was pretty wild, wasn’t it?”

“It sure was!”

Trevor had stood up and turned to look at the alumrae. It was watching him like a hawk, head tilting one way, then the other, as if expecting him to pull out another coin. “I gotta say, it doesn’t seem like a stack of coins would make for a very good home.”

“Not every bird makes great nests,” Adabelle offered. “Back on the outskirts of Eskretet, near the mountains, there are small white birds that are absolutely terrible builders. They make the worst nests you have ever seen. Just a few twigs haphazardly thrown around. There’s a small society that builds houses for them in order to help them continue, you know, existing.”

“That sure is something,” Trevor chuckled as he put his arm around his wife and turned back to the kids. “Thanks for the information, and good luck with that good luck, yeah?”

“Yes!” Siral exclaimed, looking like a kid at Christmas. “Thanks, Mr. Trevor!”

“You’re welcome. Enjoy the rest of the festival.”

Alama gave him a small smile. “You said you two were new here, right?”

Trevor nodded. “Yup.”

“If you’re looking for places to go, you should check out the Basilica Draconian,” she offered, pointing towards the center of Paladona. “It’s only a few blocks away from here, and it’s one of the most beautiful places our ancestors have built. It’ll be a little crowded because it’s Fustavele, but it's still nice to visit. Just, don’t get pickpocketed. I don’t think it’ll happen with your tattoo, but you can never be too careful.”

“Thank you, Alama,” Adabelle replied. “It was on my list of places I’d like to go before we left, but if it’s so close and you recommend it, then there’s no reason not to go now.”

“Oh, well, enjoy it, then,” the teenager said before tapping her brother on the shoulder. “Come on, we should be getting back home. Father’s going to get worried.”

“Nuh uh, he’s going to know we’re fine,” Siral replied, though he still allowed his sister to guide him away. “I’m full of good luck! He’s not going to put up a fuss or nothin’ today!”

“That’s not how it works, dummy.”

“Yuh huh! You’ll see!”

Trevor smiled as he watched the kids disappear into the crowd. He took Adabelle’s hand, her tail transferred to his wrist, and he allowed himself to be pulled away from the fountain. The alumrae still watched him the whole time, keeping their eyes on the courier until he was out of sight.

“So, this Basilica Draconian is on the list of places you’d like to visit?” he asked.

“It is. I’ll have my chance to teach you something new,” she answered with a smile, wrapping her arm around his. “You read your guidebooks, and I read mine.”

“I’m looking forward to it.”

They didn’t have far to go before they arrived at the back of the Basilica Draconian. It was a large, white stone building that was at least twice as large as the plaza they had just left. There was an odd half-tunnel that appeared from the top and spiraled downwards towards the ground, made all the easier by the cathedral’s circular construction. A single spire had been built over the entrance.

“See the treads?” Adabelle asked, pointing.

“I do.”

“Cruon used to rest there,” she explained, standing up a little straighter as she dispensed knowledge. “We’ve already seen the costumes of her, but they don’t do her justice in length and size. She was over a hundred and fifty feet long and around sixteen feet across.”

“Wow, that sounds as impressive as Glyrphizith,” Trevor said.

“Different types of dragons, but yes. I believe Cruon is the longest on record,” Adabelle happily continued. “Glyrphizith has some records herself, but she’s not the type to flaunt them. Anyway, to get back on track, the Paladorans created the Basilica Draconian as a temple to her, and she would often come to speak with the community in a safe space that wasn’t, say, the beach. Cruon coming into the city proper could cause some disruptions or damage if she wasn’t careful.”

“Oh yeah, I can see that,” he replied with a nod. The crowds were getting more dense as they approached the entrance, but it wasn’t anything they couldn’t slip through. “The kind of destruction one can wreak when they’re that large has to be, like, effortless in how easy it would be.”

“Quite right, son of Ander,” the demonkin agreed. “This gave her not only a safe space, but a comfortable one. Notice how the streets are larger around the cathedral? That’s on purpose. She’d fly in, start circling, and slot herself neatly into the spiral. There’s a divot on the other side where she could rest her head above the pulpit. They built staircases so they could go and talk to her, too.”

“She must have been very sociable.”

“Oh yes, Cruon loved conversing with the settlers,” Adabelle said with a nod. “Many dragons are solitary creatures, but not all. Even Glyrphizith prefers clocks to people, though she probably wouldn’t make it as a hermit, and not just because she wouldn’t be able to get her collection fixed.”

They made it through the crowds to see that there was a line to get in. Trevor and Adabelle observed the front, where people entered one at a time underneath a stone depiction of Cruon. The courier stared at it for a few seconds. Something was up with it, but he wasn’t sure what yet. With a shrug, he pointed towards the end of the queue.

When they made it there, a kobold family of seven arrived at the same time. Without thinking, Trevor offered the spot to them, and they thanked him before getting in line. There was no rush, and they were more than happy to see the people’s reactions.

Plus, there was already somewhat of a view of the inside. The Basilica Draconian was open in the middle with rows of ascending seats around the outer walls. It almost reminded Trevor of a coliseum. He shifted this way and that for a better look, glad that the family in front of them was a short one so he could see better.

Finally, it was their turn. It cost two copper per adult to enter, and Trevor let Adabelle go first, as was proper. She stepped under the dragon when the kobold in the priestly robes gestured for her to pass. The process only took a few seconds, and then he was called. He appreciated their efficiency, figuring that there were some kind of magic scanner in the floor or something. Odd, but practical.

The dragon’s mouth opened the moment he stepped underneath it, showering him in yellow and blue paper confetti. Trevor went still, hands up to defend himself, as the priest who had told him to step forward became excited.

“What’s going on?” he asked, unmoving as the confetti settled.

“Congratulations, young man!” the priest yelled, pulling a popper out of his pocket and pulling the string. It shot out even more confetti. “You’re the two thousand, four hundredth person to enter the Basilica Draconian today!”

“That seems oddly specific,” Trevor said. He looked at his wife, who was standing in front of him trying to look innocent. The courier arched an eyebrow. “Do you know what’s going on?”

“Yes,” she answered smugly. “Though your victory is a surprise.”

“Victory?” he asked, confused.

“Oh yes,” the priest said. “You’re the big winner, young man. We hand out prizes every day of Fustavele, but today is the day that everyone wants.”

“Really? Cool. What is it?”

The kobold grinned. “Two tickets aboard the luxury ship Serendipity, my good man, to see tomorrow’s event up close and personal. They’re some of the best seats in the house and highly coveted. You’ll be in good company as both the high priest of Cruon and the mayor of Paladona will be aboard.”

“Oh,” Trevor said, understanding immediately how valuable that was. He looked at the copper pieces still in his hand. They glimmered in the light. His gaze traveled to his wife. “Adabelle?”

“Yes?”

“I think I need to go throw a platinum coin at that bird.”
