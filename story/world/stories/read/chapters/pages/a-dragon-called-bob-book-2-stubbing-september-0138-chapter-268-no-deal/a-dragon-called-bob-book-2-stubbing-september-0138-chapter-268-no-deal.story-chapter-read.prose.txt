The purple and golden sparkles I was back to shedding flew away behind me as the wall of steam washed over me.  My nictitating membranes were enough to handle the force, and I closed my scaly-er eyelids to blot out the bright yellow world.

Everything was too hot; the mist condensed on my scales at first, running down me in rivulets, then I got too hot to cool the water vapour enough.  Flapping my wings to launch myself backwards, I dodged back behind the lip of the rise and gasped for breath.

After the wave had passed, I poked my snout back over the lip of the caldera and blinked to try to clear my vision.  It wasn’t super effective.  The glow gradually resolved to show me bright white lines, suspiciously snake-like, not reptilian, in a curious pattern around the centre, which was still too bright to differentiate what the hell was happening.

They’d locked up like the lines a flower's petals might make, and the centre looked like a nuke going off in my eyes.  The bastards had definitely been snakes.  No self-respecting lizard could contort into those kinds of curlicues and squiggles.  No movement.  Whatever it was had sucked the life out of them.  Maybe.  At this point, I was unwilling to speculate on what I had just witnessed.

“You still alive, Calledrin?”  I called out.

A column of light shot out from the anomaly in the centre of the dead volcano’s crater.  I once again retreated as the light turned the mud to stone, then lava where I’d been.

“Bulb, now would be a good time to make an appearance?  Or Denarios?  Shadow beats light?  Something like that,” I muttered.

“Calledrin isn’t here right now.  Please leave your message after the True Light turns you to dust.”  The elf no longer sounded like himself.  His voice was skittery, rising and falling in a pattern that made no sense.

“And you are?”  I called back.

“He’s not himself anymore,” Bulb said from beside my right foreleg.

“Now you choose to make an appearance?”

He crouched down and poked his finger into the dirt, swirling it around to leave a yin-yang pattern in the ground.  “Suppressing your glitter effect has left me a bit tired.”

“Not fucking me over exhausts you?” I snapped, risking a quick peek with one eye over the lip of the hill.

The caldera was rapidly cooling, and details were starting to become clear.  All life had been obliterated on the other side of my cover.  Bushes, shrubs, I presumed voles and whatnot, were charcoal outlines that were left crumbling in the wind.

“You’re a furbie?” I yelled.  The blast of light in reply missed me, but only because my reflexes were pretty damn good when it came to not being incinerated.

“The Changer only sends his best.”

I glanced at Bulb and raised an eyebrow.  “The hell is it talking about?”

“This was how it had to be, Bob.  There was nothing we could do.”  The light glinted on the lenses of his glasses as he pushed them back up his nose with a dirty finger.  “I’m sorry.”

“Why do I get the feeling I’ve once again been divinely screwed over?”

“We’re going to help as much as we can.  Pattern gods aren’t as powerful as the denizens of any particular world would like to believe.”

“The fuck are pattern gods, Denarios?”

The spacesuit shrugged, the helmet was on, and the visor was down, so all I could see of his face was my own magnificent equine snout curling a lip back to reveal a row of razor-sharp fangs.  He moved over to stand by Bulb.  “You going to tell him?”

“No.”

“Well, I don’t want to.”

“We already discussed it.  You can’t back out now.”

“I don’t have time for this Mickey Mouse bullshit!  Will one of you just spit it out, please?” I snarled, ducking another blast of yellow light.

“This is the beginning, Bob.  Kat wasn’t lying about an Unravelling.  You needed to see it for yourself.” Denarios said sadly.

“The elves will be first,” Bulb said.

“Ok.  You, shut the fuck up.”  I pointed at Denarios.  “Bulb, just drop the mysterious shite and tell me what the hell is happening.”

“Don’t tell me to shut the fuck up,” muttered Jeff Bezos, but Bulb, who I was starting to suspect was Thomas Edison once upon a time, slapped the back of the astronaut helmet hard enough to make the god stagger,

“That ritual circle opened a portal,” Bulb said.

“I’m familiar with the concept of portals.”

“Why do you think we lobbied IMPS to give you access to space/time magic?  Ok.  Pattern gods are us.”  Bulb waved a hand between himself and the god of wealth.  Multiversal constants like life, death, money, plumbing, advertising… every verse gets a god for that specific concept.  What that did—” he waved a thumb over his shoulder towards the still steaming crater, “was open a portal to Outside.”

“Oh no!  Are we going to get wasps in the house?”

“This is serious, Bob,” Bulb intoned seriously.  “Also, yes.  If by wasps you mean interuniversal monsters that suck the energy of a verse out into the void between realities, leaving behind desiccated husks where nothing living or dead can survive.  My life is on the line here as well, you ass.”

“That does sound serious, then.  What, precisely, would you like me to do about it?”

“You need to start a crusade,” Bulb replied solemnly.

“I’m not that big on holy wars, dude.  They don’t generally work out well, as a rule.”

“Fine.  Total war, then.”

“Again, historically, that’s not a nice thing to do.”

“You need to stop it!” Denarios snapped.  “We, and by we I mean the entire damn pantheon of Heaven, and all the important Demons, will be backing you to the hilt.  As much as we can anyway.”

“Always got to throw that little caveat in at the end, don’t you?”

“Bob, we’re bound by rules that the Outsiders aren’t. We can’t break those rules, or the verse unravels anyway.  You have to fix this.”

Fighting down an urge to pinch the bridge of my snout and glare at a pair of deities, I opted to glower at Bulb.  He was so small, just a regular mammal, but I wouldn’t stand a chance if this fell out into physical violence.  It was tempting, though.

“How?”

“He already told you,” Denarios snapped.

“I’m not starting a bloody holy war!”

“You don’t have a choice.  For the love of money!  If you want your pile of shinies to survive, let alone oh I don’t know, Esme, you need to stop this plague.”

“What religion are they going to rally around?”

“All of them.  Every single one,” Bulb said.  “You’ve seen some weird shit.”

“Yep.  And a roided out furbie isn’t even the weirdest shit I’ve dealt with this week!”

“That is a creature from beyond time and space.  It has swallowed worlds, devoured suns and solar systems, sucked down gods.  I said sucked down! Not off.”

“Do you guys even have genitals?” I asked in genuine curiosity.

“Your weird obsession with non-human reproduction is noted in your file,” Bulb replied.

“You assholes have a file on me as well?”

“Everyone who is anyone has a file on you.  You don’t know how important you are.”

“Oh, I’m pretty important in my opinion.”

“Kat wasn’t lying,” Bulb barked.  “When she said she was sent to help you for a reason, well, this is it.”

Drawing my head back in consternation was, in hindsight, a bad move.  One of my horns was smoking after I yanked it back down.  I’d caught a brief sight of the terrible enemy.  It was rocking back and forth on a rapidly cooling patch of lava and rubbing its tummy with one hand while patting its head with the other.  Aberrant behaviour, if ever I saw it.

“Assuming your plan isn’t for me to pop up out of cover and get shot to pieces, what do you want me to do? And don’t say start a fucking crusade.”

“They can’t help you, Bob.  They’re trapped in the weave, slaves who can’t see their chains,” the furbie called.

“And you can shut it!”

“You need to choose your own path.  But the Outsiders must be stopped,” Bulb said.  The two gods vanished a moment later, leaving me experimenting with innovative forms of invective and blasphemy.

“Hey, Bob!  Check this out!”

“If I take a look, you’re not going to blast me with that light beam thing, are you?”

“Nooooo?”

“I’ll pass.”

“Seriously, thith ith the good bit!”

The voice was muffled, and I risked a peek, ready to snap back behind cover in an instant.  The thing had detatched its left arm and held the fingers between its teeth, using the other hand to stretch the limb until it was twice the original size.  Then it stuck the stump against its empty shoulder and there was a quiet clicking noise as it locked into place.

“See, being an Outsider has a lot of advantages, but you already know that, don’th you’th?”  it… he, I supposed, was repeating the same trick with his other arm.  “Nobody likes a big ole monster do they?  They’re either afraid of you, or they want to use your power.”  His arm clicked back into place and he pulled off a leg, no longer needing to use his teeth to hold the limb in place as it stretched like playdough between his hands.

“Not all of them.”  Visions of Esme ran through my mind at his words.

“Yeah, your pretty lady is a good one, a keeper.  Here’s the deal, I can spare you both when the time comes.  Make sure you and her get out alright.”  Both of his legs were now reattached and he began pulling on the top of his head and crotch, his body slowly growing to match his new limbs.  He would still only be four feet tall, his body covered in purple fur.  He looked like an ewok that had fallen in a tin of paint.

“What about everyone else?”

“They’ve got to go.  This verse has been marked for reallocation.  It’s all above board.  You can ask the local Goddess Karen about it, she’s got the paperwork.”

“Marked by who?”

“Whom.”

“I’m not familiar with them.”

“No… Nevermind, Bob.  IMPS isn’t the be all and end all, as much as it likes to think it is.  Think of this as a reclamation, a reclaiming of something stolen.”

My scales bristled at the S word.

“We don’t like thieves either.  In fact, there’s a lot of common ground between us.  You don’t even need to help, all you need to do is… nothing.  Just don’t interfere, and you and Esme can go somewhere else.  Do you want to go back to Earth?  Show Esme the sights?  We can make that happen.  There’s no need for us to be at odds.”

“What’s the catch?”  Was I really considering this?  I didn’t particularly want to fight the malleable monster with the laserbeam eyes.  War had caused me nothing but trouble up to that point, and I could only assume—the ewok tore itself in half, derailing my train of thought.

“Sorry about that.  Need more of me to get the job done.”  One of the halves began shaping the other, moving limbs around, drawing lumps of flesh out to form new ones.  It was making a fuzzy purple spider out of its new brother.   “No catch at all.  You leave us alone, we’ll leave you alone.  I mean, we will need your hoard, but that’s a small price to pay for a ticket out of a dying universe, right?”

A portal appeared below the pair and dropped them into the upper atmosphere.  Holy war suddenly sounded like a jolly good plan.
