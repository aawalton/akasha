The twisted effect was a corruption. A plague of chaos on the senses.

Many have described it as penance; a punishment for the enlightenment gained from the Origin Plane. Others describe it as the consciousness of the Origin Plane living out a fantasy of humanity through its cultivators.

But whatever it was, one fact remained certain.

It wasn't fun.

No one underwent the twisted effect and came out smiling. Hell, no one underwent it and came out remotely happy that they had. Living as a disembodied voice within your own body was not an experience anyone wanted to repeat.

And even Sophran, with his endless cheerfulness, felt the same way.

Within the expansive hall of the library seal chambers, he groaned quietly, his eyelids twitching as the tendrils of chaos lifted off his mind, leaving him with a clarity so crystal it changed his perception of the world around him.

Thoughts flowed through his mind unobstructed, the inspirations he’d gained solidifying in his mind like concrete. Notifications pinged in his mind; congratulatory messages filled his screen, and as he read through his newest skills, Sophran couldn’t help but smile.

Not only were the skills exceedingly powerful, but they were also perfectly aligned with his path and vision—a feat that would have taken him years to create.

Smiling widely, he dismissed the notifications and raised his arms to test out his new strength. He had gained two tiers and two seals from a single infusion of Aodhán’s miracle rain. And it was amazing how much more powerful he felt, as if those two tiers carried far more substance than his previous tiers.

He could feel the strength thrumming within his core, the power humming in his muscles, and with a wide smile, he threw a powerful punch at the walls of the seal chamber. His fist slammed into the wall with explosive force, and though the walls barely trembled, Sophran’s smile grew even wider as he regarded his fist in amazement.

His attacks must have attracted the attention of elder Kudrov, the librarian, because she appeared a moment later, eyes wide.

“You’re awake!” she exclaimed with a wide smile. “What a relief. I’d begun to grow worried that you’d been permanently damaged by the rain skill.”

Sophran’s smile diminished at that, and he frowned. “Why would you think…”

His gaze strayed to the chamber beside him, expecting to see Tae-Hyun, his underling, only to find the chamber, as well as the others empty save for the two housing Darien and Crixis. And that was when he realized—

“They’re gone.” He breathed, his eyes widening slightly as he turned to glance at elder Kudrov. “How long have I been out?”

“Three days,” Elder Kudrov smiled reassuringly at him. “I know it’s not too long, but many of the others regained themselves yesterday evening and will be joining the war efforts today.”

Sophran’s eyes widened at the mention of war, and he jumped to his feet immediately. “I missed the first two days of war!”

Elder Kudrov backed up in slight alarm, eying him carefully. “Yes, you did, but that doesn’t matter. What matters—"

“I have to join them.” Sophran cut her off sharply, his tone desperate. “Two days away from the war? It must be brutal out there. Aodhán is the only transmigrant participating in the battle. He can’t handle the pressure alone.”

Elder Kudrov frowned at him in concern and then her gaze softened in realization. “Ah, you were already under the twisted effect when they returned. But Aodhán is not alone.” She smiled. “Nymera and Ashscar returned to the sect two days ago. They fought alongside Aodhán and Mirelle yesterday. And the other specials will join them today.”

Sophran’s fear froze over at the mention of Nymera and Ashscar, and he unconsciously let out a breath of relief. Nymera and Ashscar were powerful transmigrants. With them in the war, he could rest easy.

However, just as he began to do so, a thought came to his mind and he frowned again. “Are they fighting on the front lines?”

Elder Kudrov shook her head. “No, they are acting as bait to lure enemy Calamities into traps. It’s been… effective.”

Sophran’s frown deepened at the danger his friends were putting themselves in while he was cooped up within the seal chamber like a coward. His desperation returned and he shook his head. “They’ll need my help. I need to join them—help them.”

Elder Kudrov’s expression grew stern. “What you need is to rest. You’ve just overcome the twisted effect.”

I didn’t overcome it. Sophran thought to himself, a glare appearing on his face as he grunted out. “I’m fine, Elder Kudrov. Let me join them.”

Elder Kudrov shook her head adamantly. “I understand how you feel, but—”

A loud bang cut her off and they all turned to see Crixis slam a powerful punch into the chamber wall, his amethyst hair matted to his forehead with sweat.

Sophran stared at him for a moment, admiring the severity of his bloodline manifestation before glancing back at elder Kudrov with a hard expression.

“You have no right to keep me here, elder Kudrov. I’m not going straight to the battlefield, just the war camp. Even if I can’t fight today, I want to at least watch.”

Elder Kudrov regarded him for a moment before nodding, and an instant later, the seal’s entrance was pushed open. Sophran stepped out of the chamber with a deep breath, his gaze returning to Darien and Crixis who were still firmly under the effect of the twisted effect.

They’d both gained three seals total, and while Darien still looked the same, Crixis looked completely different, his bloodline manifestation nearly as severe as Aodhán’s and Nymera’s.

But beyond the bloodline manifestation, Crixis seemed to have aged decades in the last three days. He had no wrinkles or eyebags, but his amethyst gaze held a profundity of understanding far beyond his years.

It was obvious his path had been changed by Aodhán’s miracle rain to something far more complex than superiority.

Good.

The superiority of the storm was powerful, but it was also one of the most common paths and thus the easiest to counter. Whatever path Crixis threaded now, was most definitely far more powerful, and Sophran was excited to see what it was.

But beyond that, he wanted to know what how much he had missed in the last two days since the war began. And with one last glance at Darien, he headed out of the library, created a platform construct for himself and raced toward the war camp, his spiritual perception spreading wide in a circular radius around him.

The last two days of war had had an obvious effect on the sect itself. There was no visible damage or destruction anywhere. The war had not deteriorated to that point yet. But the energy in the air was obviously agitated—tense with anticipation and fear.

People were scared. That wasn’t good news.

Sophran hadn’t fought a Balance War before, but he had seen war in his former world—he had lived it, and he knew that tension like this didn’t bode well for the sect. it wasn’t just fear. It was uncertainty about the outcome of the war.

And that wasn’t good news at all.

He arrived at the war camp a few minutes later, inhaling deeply as he felt multiple spiritual perceptions lock onto him to scan him for threats. The scan was invasive—shredding through his spirit like cold needles, but such were the way of things in times of war.

Silently, Sophran took it, allowing the guards to do their job before continuing deeper into the camp, heading straight for the viewing deck where he could feel the auras of several powerful mythics emanating from.

He burst into the deck an instant later, eyes widening slightly as he took in the array of screens displaying the clips of the war. They covered nearly every inch of the building, showing a hundred different perspectives all at once.

Elders moved about, carrying out orders, issuing commands and trying to manage the war as best as they could. But it wasn’t looking good.

Destruction and devastation marred every screen. Death and blood filling every corner, and lightning scorched plains stretched for hundreds of miles, cratered earth still glowing with residual energy.

The scale of it was frightening.

In only two days, what Sophran assumed had been a flat strip of earth was now a graveyard of shattered terrain torn apart and scarred to its very depths. Entire mountain ridges had been shaved down to jagged stumps, drenched in blood.

And yet, the war was still ongoing.

His gaze strayed to a massive screen dominating a side wall, and he felt a shudder of dread race across his spine as he watched a scythe of blood cleave through a battalion of wind Mythics in an instant, too fast for any of them to have dodged or escaped.

A shockwave of energy slammed against the viewing deck a moment after. Blood sprayed across the screen, strips of flesh and tissue following, and Sophran immediately averted his gaze, turning to the side as he felt bile rise in his throat.

That was when his gaze fell on the other specials—Ashscar, Aodhán, and Seamus, standing before a massive screen with somber expressions on their faces as they watched the war unfold.

But while Seamus, just like him, still averted his gaze, Aodhán and Ashscar didn’t, their mind conditioned to the brutality and scale in only two days. They were no longer the boys he had known three days ago. The war had changed them—twisted them. But at least they were still alive.

Feeling a tingle of relief, Sophran began walking toward them. He’d barely taken more than a few steps, however, before the screen shifted and Sophran felt his breath hitch as he caught sight of frost white hair, and midnight skin.

Nymera.

Just like Aodhán and Ashscar, she too had changed. Gone were her serenity and calm. Instead, she stood within the battlefield like a herald of destruction itself, Levi looming high above her, his form radiating a pressure so tangible Sophran could almost feel it from the viewing deck.

And that was when Sophran realized—she was alone.

Where was Mirelle?

Frowning, he glanced from one screen to the other, searching for Mirelle’s familiar silhouette or the tell-tale flare of her raven-black hair. But he found none.

She wasn’t on the battlefield, which meant she must be here, somewhere.

“Mirelle?” he called out immediately, instinctively, drawing every gaze in the room. But none of them were the ones he was looking for.

“Mirelle.” He called again, panic creeping into his voice as a terse silence suddenly settled over the deck. He met the eyes of several elders, saw the sympathy in their gazes, and he knew…

He knew, but he couldn’t bring himself to believe.

“No.” He shook his head, turning toward Elder Ghaltris. “It’s only been two days. I’ve only been gone for two days. Where is she?”

The silence that met his question was deafening.

Grief pooled in his chest as he looked back at the other specials, finally seeing their expressions for what they were.

Grief. Anger. Hatred.

Aodhán met his gaze. For half a second, he said nothing. Then—slowly—he shook his head. “I’m sorry, Sophran.”

Ashscar nodded, and glanced back at the screen, his jaw ticking with anger. “I’m sorry too, Sophran. There was no time to… we couldn’t save her.”

Seamus walked toward him, his own eyes gleaming with unshed tears. He held him, saying nothing, but words or not, Sophran doubted he could have felt any comfort in that moment.

Grief rising, his gaze drifted back to the screen showing Nymera, and he finally understood the wrath he saw burning in her eyes. She raised a hand to the sky in that moment, and Sophran felt his entire existence shift as she activated her ability—the skill that made her so scary, so powerful.

Sophran felt it in that moment. Her desire for vengeance.

A desire that quickly became the storm’s desire.

An instant later, the heavens opened with the fury of a god unbound, and absolute destruction rained down.

Nymera Vaelthorn stood at the edge of annihilation and the storm felt her wrath as its own.

Cultivating the path of storm embodiment, Nymera had never viewed the storm as above or supreme to her. Instead, she viewed it as the manifestation of her desires, and her, its will. Its eye. Its stillness.

Every opponent she’d ever faced hadn’t really fought her, but the storm itself. And because of that, she’d never had to go all out in a battle before. Her opponents were usually down by the second spark of irritation. None ever survived the third.

It wasn’t arrogance. It was confidence.

And Nymera had walked into this war with that assurance of backing, knowing fully well that the storm would never allow her to be killed—not even by Calamities.

And after surviving the first day of battle, she’d thought she’d understood the rhythm of it.

But she’d failed to realize that the protection the storm offered her didn’t stretch toward Mirelle, who was cultivating a lesser path.

And she had paid for it.

Mirelle’s face flashed across her mind—scared, nervous, alive—and Nymera’s jaw clenched so hard she tasted copper.

It had happened so fast.

One moment, Mirelle had been beside her, willpower blazing as she manifested constructs to distract a cluster of Mythics. And the next, she’d been skewered to pieces by a formation of jagged glass moving through the chaos of the battlefield like shards of reality.

It had been too sudden. Too shocking.

And for a moment, Nymera had frozen, simply going along with it when a storm Calamity yanked her away from the battlefield and back to the viewing deck.

But that had been over twelve hours ago—more than enough time for her emotions to thaw into fury. It boiled within her now like lava, blistering with the intensity of her wrath

And with a loud rumble of thunder, the storm reflected it, each arc of lightning echoing with the consent of her vengeance.

Growling, she raised a hand to the churning storm, fury blazing in her heart. The plan hadn’t changed. Draw attention. Vanish.

But this time, Nymera added a new objective.

Punish.

Willpower gathering immensely within her spirit, Nymera turned her gaze to the distance where thousands of glass Mythics fought in formations and clenched her palm into a fist. Her pupils burned with stormlight. Her heart churned with a desire for desolation.

And in a whisper, even she could barely hear, she channeled her will into the storm and breathed.

“Scourge.”

The heavens cracked open immediately, and with a clap of thunder like the world itself shattering, the storm manifested her desire—not with lightning alone, but with the Aspect of shadow humming in her spirit.

Darkness descended first—not the gentle absence of light, but a devouring void that swallowed color, sound, and hope alike. It poured from the cloud like ink through water, spreading through the battlefield in a wave of absolute negation.

Glass Mythics looked up in confusion as the shadows consumed the world around them. Some tried to flee. Others raised massive shields of crystalline light.

But it didn’t matter.

The darkness wasn’t empty—it was crushing, shadows carrying the weight of a thousand mountains. The pressure didn’t build, it dropped, slamming into the Mythics with such sudden violence that hundreds of Mythics were driven to their knees instantly, bones shattering, spines compressing.

And then came the lightning.

Not in bolts. Not in strikes.

In pillars.

Hundreds of them slamming into the earth like the wrath of creation itself. Each one was nearly a mile wide, so bright they burned afterimages into the eyes of everyone watching and bored craters hundreds of feet deep into the earth.

The glass Mythics barely stood a chance, the bodies of over half their numbers vaporizing so completely there wasn’t even ash left behind.

Energy and willpower drained from Nymera like a dam broken open, but she wasn’t finished.

And neither was the storm.

Darkness and lightning fused, shadows bleeding into molten-gold pillars of blistering electricity that slammed into the earth like judgment itself—negation and enforcement carving furrows of contradiction through the enemy ranks.

Wherever the pillars struck, the world buckled. Reality heaved. And the Mythics caught within them were simply torn apart.

Their tiers lost relevance. Their class, coherence.

They weren’t victims of Nymera. They were victims of the storm.

And at the storm’s center, where it will converged, there was silence.

Nymera stood at the center of it all, untouched, her entire body burning with lightning and stormlight. Space and reality undulated around her as attacks curved and slid away from her position—a perfect bastion of calm within the chaos of war.

It was as if the storm itself stood with her, turning aside incoming attacks even as it rained desolation upon her enemies.

It was what made her path so terrifyingly powerful.

But in the end, Nymera was still only at the advanced class, with finite energy and willpower—resources that sank to the bottom of her core barely a minute into the storm’s judgment.

She stumbled, using the last of her strength to teleport to the agreed safety point, the blood of thousands of Mythics staining her hands.

It didn’t quite satisfy her wrath—but she took grim satisfaction in knowing she had left the war far less dangerous than when she entered it.

Punishment had been delivered.

Vengeance, however, was far from finished.
