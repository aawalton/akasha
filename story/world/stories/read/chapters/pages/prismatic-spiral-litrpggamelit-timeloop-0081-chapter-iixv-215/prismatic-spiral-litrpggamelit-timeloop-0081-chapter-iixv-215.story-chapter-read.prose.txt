Chapter II.XV (2.15)

“We don’t have time for this,” Mitsuko said.

“Even if you can keep us alert with Mending, it’s not healthy,” Holly insisted. “It is more than just your physical body that requires sleep. You need it to let your brain settle down and process information.”

“Now’s not the time for that!”

“I think it’s exactly the right time for it,” Holly rebutted. “Who is an acolyte here? Are there more than one? What are their powers? What sort of schemes are down in those tunnels? What guardian do you face here?”

“It doesn’t matter. I’ll just take on whatever is thrown at me.”

“It does matter,” Holly insisted. “You might feel like you’re immortal because of the timeloops, but you’re not.”

“I might as well be. We’re nearly at the end of the loop anyway so I don’t need to worry about wasting time if I die.”

“Do you think the guardians are all idiots?” Holly snapped at her. “You can still die permanently. Don’t pretend like there can’t be consequences to bad decisions. From what you said, the very first loop the guardian attempted to carve out your heart to keep you from returning. And with the last guardian you faced, you wasted a dozen of your loops to mind control due to you rushing in without a proper plan.”

“She raises a few fair points,” Sterling said.

The cat stood off to the side, glaring daggers back at the mountain village. As they’d left a few of the children had spotted him and chased him across town. One had even managed to get hands on him and tried picking him up by the tail. That child earned himself a few new carvings in his snowy skin. Mitsuko had thankfully been able to Mend the wound of the sculpt boy before his wailing could turn the entire village against them.

“You think we should go back?” Mitsuko asked the sage. She couldn’t believe she was hearing this from Sterling of all people. She had thought for sure the cat would want to put the village behind them.

“Your mind does need rest to properly comprehend information. When you cast Mend on yourself, it’s your body and not your mind that is reset from my spell. And while yes, we are approaching the end of the loop, you’ll more likely survive any encounters with opposition should you approach the scenario with a clear head. There will still be time remaining for an expedition after a few hours of rest.”

“Is there even a house here warm enough for us to rest in?” Mitsuko said, seeking a way out. If they couldn’t safely rest somewhere then surely they’d lose all complaints about pushing forward.

“Actually, yes.” Holly grinned with her teeth. “Where do you think those Kemon are staying? They might have thick fur to keep them toasty, but they’re still warm blooded creatures, just the same as you and I.”

With great difficulty, Mitsuko kept her face placid and devoid of the annoyance roiling within her.

“Fine. You lead the way,” Mitsuko said, defeated.

Holly clapped her hands and strutted off back in the direction of the village, head held high in acknowledgement of her victory. The gnome’s prideful composure lasted approximately ten steps before the snow gave way under her feet. With a yelp, Holly tipped over and tumbled into the snow. While less than a meter deep, the crusty snow on top concealed a wealth of soft snow beneath it. Whenever Holly attempted to leverage her way out, the crusty top collapsed and widened her hole further.

Mitsuko casually walked by as Holly struggled to extrastriate herself. The snow attempted to give way under Mitsuko’s feet as well, but she was prepared for it and cast Mend on the snow beneath her and it continued to hold strong. Though Mitsuko soon realized she needed to keep moving as the mound of snow beneath her feet grew and water re-froze.

“Mitsuko!” Holly called after her. “Don’t leave me. Use your spell to get me out too!”

Mitsuko smirked but kept her back to her friend as she continued on. “Sorry. I’ve been told I need to go rest. Maybe in a few hours once I’m better rested?”

She heard Holly curse and flail about more. Still, Mitsuko wasn’t actually that petty. Without looking back, she cast Mend on the snow and it reformed and spit Holly out.

Grumbling, the gnome chased after her.

“Heartless.”

“I just got you out didn’t I?” Mitsuko gave her friend a side eye glance, a smirk still on her face.

“You only did that because you wouldn’t know where to go otherwise,” Holly accused. “Otherwise you’d leave me in the cold, all alone. I could have died!”

“Between you and Sterling, I’m not certain who’s more dramatic.”

“I am not dramatic,” Sterling huffed. “I am a realist who calls out inadequacies when I encounter them throughout the world at large.”

Holly also puffed her chest out and harrumphed. “The house we’re looking for is on the east side of the village. The cabin with the weird pine tree out front. All the other buildings have wards carved into them to keep in the cold.”

“Who told you this?” Mitsuko asked, redirecting her walk in that direction.

“I am an all knowing diviner.”

“She spoke to a few people earlier while you were threatening children,” Sterling said.

“I was healing the child,” Mitsuko shot back at the cat. Then she lifted her hands, palms open. “You know what, sure. I was threatening them. Let’s just get out of the cold.”

It honestly wasn’t actually that cold out. With the sun shining overhead, the only real cold came from the snow beneath her feet. Unless Mitsuko did something, she had no doubt that this place too would succumb to the heat in time, just as the port had.

Holly hopped onto the cabin’s porch and knocked on the door.

“Hello!” someone called from within. “Just a minute!”

The door flung open and revealed a small Hon woman with a big, bulging belly.

“Baby!” Holly cried out upon seeing the stomach. “When are you due! Boy or girl? Do you need me to divine the answer? I might not have a ton of practice with body divinations but that much I have enough training to accomplish.”

“It’s a boy!” the woman said cheerily.

Mitsuko let out a sigh of relief. Holly assuming someone was pregnant was just about the most dangerous thing she’d seen in all her loops. That could have turned very deadly.

“You look familiar,” the woman said, tilting her head slightly as she eyed Mitsuko. “Have we met before?”

Mitsuko blinked. Now that the woman mentioned it, she did tickle her memory slightly. “Maybe? I’m a friend of Emperor Sasaki.”

The woman snapped her fingers. “Oh! That’s where I saw you! I don’t think we’ve been introduced yet, my name is Tori. Please, come in.” She stepped aside, leaving the door agape for them. “Cute cat too. He’s yours?”

“Unfortunately.”

Mitsuko and Holly knocked the snow from their boots and stepped in. The interior was cozy, just two rooms. A main room with a couch and table, and a bunk room with a dozen beds stacked three high. There was no fireplace, but Mitsuko suspected heating enhancements were utilized instead to retain heat.

“I apologize for the mess,” Tori said, nudging a cat doll out of the way with her foot. “My daughter, Jo, doesn’t like to keep the walking space clear. And my son thinks that every house is secretly a sports arena. As it is right now, I’m struggling to bend over.”

Mitsuko kneeled down and picked up the stuffed spotted cat. Then she glanced around at all the other toys and balls scattered around. A more pragmatic solution occurred to her.

“Step back for a second,” she requested.

Tori looked at her weird but complied, retreating a few steps backwards.

Mitsuko cast Mend.

Toys darted across the room. Even backed up to the wall, Mitsuko nearly took a leather ball to the eye as it bounced off the wall. But in less than a minute, everything had been properly put away. The cabin was clear of children’s artifacts. At least for the time being.

“Can you teach me that spell?” Tori begged.

“Unless you can cast temporal spells, I think you might be out of luck. Sorry,” Mitsuko said sheepishly. “But I’m happy to help out while I’m here.”

“Just my luck.” Tori sighed and gestured at the couch. “Take a seat. Make yourself at home.”

They complied. While they settled in, Tori grabbed a box off a counter and offered it to them. “Want a cookie? It’s a recipe from Edgeland.”

Holly grabbed four and stuffed them into her mouth. “Mmm. Sho shoft.”

Mitsuko nibbled on one. It was, indeed, soft. The box must have some sort of preservation enchantment. She wondered how much an enchantment like that related to temporal spellcraft. Preservation enchantments were rare and expensive, but not unheard of when visiting restaurants or the kitchens of nobles.

“If I’m not mistaken, you’re one of Kizu’s friends?” Tori asked.

“You know Kizu? You met him?” That was surprising. There was a big difference between knowing Kizu and knowing of Kizu.

“Not personally,” Tori admitted. “Though my husband encountered him once up at the northern reaches of Tross. I did, however, meet another of that group’s pals. Basil. Such a nice name too. I’ve been trying to talk Jay into letting me name the boy Basil as well, but he’s relentlessly against it.”

Mitsuko kept her opinion to herself but she didn’t blame the man. Basil’s name had a lot of baggage attached to it. Just his personality alone would be enough to give anyone pause, to say nothing of his controversies. But also one of Emperor Sasaki’s favorite confidants so that likely scored some points in his favor for the unknowing. Sasaki had a habit of collecting colorful people into his retinue.

She continued to nibble her cookie. She was already halfway through. Absolutely delicious.

“He said ‘we’re not naming our child after an herb!’ As if he’s not named after a bird himself. I find it completely fitting! After all, our children’s names are already an amalgamation from all over the world.”

They continued to listen to the woman rant about baby names for the next twenty minutes. As delightful as the discussion was, Mitsuko felt the weight of sleep pulling on her eyelids. She could cast Mend on herself. But both Basil and Holly had insisted that she sleep for a few hours. Before she realized what was happening, her cheek slumped against her shoulder and the world dimmed to oblivion.
