“Say that again?”

Veronica and Gwen sat inside Hugh’s office. It was morning now, following the night of leisure and grand entertainment at the Crimson Court.

Gwen sat with both hands resting on her knees, fingers tense, body stiff.

She was nervous—understandably so. This meeting would decide whether Rocky’s safety could be guaranteed. For years, she had done everything she could to protect him. She had endured constant surveillance, restricted freedom, and even pain.

There wasn’t much left to say. Her decision to become Veronica’s apprentice depended entirely on the Mage Association’s response.

Veronica took the lead.

“My friend here, Gwen, has been taking care of a peculiar monster for the past few years,” she said. “It’s made of rock. Similar in shape to a bear, but much larger.”

“How large?” Hugh asked.

Veronica tilted her head slightly, glancing around the office. “It would probably take up most of this room. Large enough to carry one or two carriages on its back.”

“I see.”

She looked back at him. “The most unusual part is its ability to produce mana crystals.”

Hugh’s eyes widened. “What? It creates mana crystals?”

Veronica nodded. “It’s made of some kind of black slate, but it has crystal spires growing along its back. Whenever it sheds, the crystals grow larger, fill with mana, then fall off on their own.”

Hugh frowned slightly behind his desk. “How large are these crystals? Are they actually saturated with mana?”

“Yes,” Veronica said without hesitation. “As for size… it varies depending on the spire. But on average…”

She raised her hands, measuring out a length roughly the size of her forearm.

“About this large.”

Hugh’s hands slammed against the desk, not in anger, but in clear excitement.

“A mana crystal that size?”

Veronica nodded again.

Hugh stood immediately and crossed the room to a nearby bookshelf. He pulled out several volumes, quickly scanning their titles before returning them. Veronica and Gwen exchanged a brief glance, unsure what he was searching for.

At last, he pulled down a thick, worn tome. After glancing at its cover, he began flipping through the pages.

The room fell quiet as he searched.

“A Gemling Terrorbeast,” he said under his breath.

“T-terrorbeast?” Gwen repeated.

Hugh looked back at them. “Ah. Don’t worry about the name. It’s not something you need to be concerned about. At least, not right now.”

“What do you mean?” Veronica asked. “You know what Rocky is?”

Hugh nodded. “Based on your description, Rocky is most likely a Gemling Terrorbeast. They’re resilient, rock-based creatures that form underground near concentrated mana veins.”

“Wait—a mana vein?” Veronica said. She turned to Gwen. “You said you found Rocky near that cave, right?”

“Uh… yes,” Gwen said. “Right by the stream. He kind of just… rolled around trying to get my attention while I was picking herbs.”

Veronica rested a finger beneath her chin, contemplating.

“Don’t get too excited,” Hugh added, noticing her reaction. “If there was a mana vein there, the Terrorbeast has likely already absorbed it.”

“What?” Veronica said, looking back at him.

Hugh glanced down at the open book, scanning a passage.

“Gemling Terrorbeasts act as conduits for mana,” he explained. “Once they form, they draw in and consume the entire mana vein nearby. That’s what allows them to produce those crystals.”

He looked up again. “In essence, they become a walking mana vein.”

Gwen’s gaze shifted quickly between them.

“What’s a mana vein?” she asked.

“Mana veins are the residual energy left behind by a greater spirit,” Veronica explained.

“What are those?” Gwen asked again.

“Greater spirits are ancient beings formed from concentrated mana and ethereal energy,” Hugh said. “Most spirits reside in the spirit realm. Greater spirits are essentially the eldest among them.”

“When they die, they leave behind unstable pockets of energy,” Veronica continued. “Over time, those pockets sink into the earth. After hundreds of years, the energy stabilizes and becomes what we call a mana vein.”

Hugh raised a brow. “You certainly know a lot, Miss Veronica,” he said. “You continue to surprise me.”

Veronica gave a small shrug, brushing off the comment. “Greater spirits went ‘extinct’ thousands of years ago. Not because they all died, though they did, but because the spirit realm was sealed off. Very few spirits cross over now. The elves can sometimes communicate with them, but only under specific circumstances.”

“Right,” Hugh said. “You are partially dark elf. I had forgotten that detail.”

Veronica looked back at him. “You’re telling me Rocky absorbed an entire mana vein? That’s enough to push a Tier-8 mage to Tier-9, depending on its purity.”

“Yes, that is likely the case,” Hugh said. “Though the actual concentration of power would be lower now. Its internal structure has adapted to produce mana crystals, so a significant portion of that energy has already been converted.”

He closed the book and returned it to the shelf. “Even so, the existence of a Gemling Terrorbeast is remarkable. It is essentially a creature that can produce mana crystals indefinitely. The larger it grows, the larger the crystals, and the more it can generate. Only a handful have ever been recorded.”

“Why is it called a Terrorbeast?” Gwen asked.

“Because they can grow as large as a mountain,” Hugh replied.

“What?” Gwen gasped.

He nodded. “That is its theoretical limit. In reality, most never reach that size. They are usually killed long before then, either by poachers or because they cannot sustain themselves.”

Gwen’s hands tightened in her lap at the word poachers.

Veronica noticed. She placed her hand gently over Gwen’s, steadying her.

Gwen glanced at her, then slowly relaxed.

“Gwen has been in a difficult situation while taking care of Rocky,” Veronica said. “Let’s just say it involves… a poacher, like you mentioned.”

Hugh gave a slow nod. “I see.”

Veronica continued, her tone calm.

“I came here because I want the Mage Association to take responsibility for Rocky. I want a formal guarantee of his protection.”

“The Mage Association?” Hugh repeated.

“Yes,” Veronica said. “As part of the agreement tied to the assistance I will be providing.”

She gestured slightly toward Gwen. “Gwen is someone I intend to train. I will be traveling, which means I cannot bring Rocky with us. She needs assurance that he will be kept in a safe environment under the Association’s protection. No more… poachers interfering.”

Hugh walked back to his desk, scratching his short beard as he considered her words. He sat down and fixed his gaze on Veronica.

“So, in exchange for your theoretical notes, you want the Mage Association to protect this Terrorbeast—Rocky—because it is important to your apprentice?”

Veronica’s eyes narrowed slightly.

“No,” she said. “You misunderstand.”

“It is what I want. My student’s concerns are my own.”

Gwen turned to look at her.

“This is not a request from my student,” Veronica added. “It is a request from me.”

Hugh hummed softly, letting the silence stretch still for a moment.

“What conditions would you place on the Mage Association?” he asked.

“First, a guarantee of his safety and comfort,” Veronica said. “Safety alone isn’t enough. Animals kept in cages are safe, but that doesn’t mean they’re comfortable. Rocky must have both. Whether that means building a habitat or giving him land to roam freely, the method doesn’t matter.”

“I see,” Hugh said. “What else?”

“Second, the distribution of the mana crystals.”

Hugh steepled his fingers, bringing them to his lips.

“Gwen told me that Rocky sheds crystals periodically. It’s a natural, painless process. I want those crystals distributed fairly.”

“How so?”

Veronica turned to Gwen. “How often does he shed?”

“Uh… it’s hard to say exactly,” Gwen said. “He was being forced to do it constantly before, but… normally, I think it’s about once a month. He usually sheds around four or five crystals. Sometimes as few as two, sometimes as many as seven.”

Veronica nodded and looked back at Hugh.

Though Hugh wasn’t formally part of the Mage Association, he was still the tower head, and she had been working closely with him in these negotiations. More importantly, he would remain an important figure once Ronswick became Galland’s first investment.

Sage. What’s a good rate to split things?

[If you are providing your future notes to the Mage Association, they are already receiving a substantial benefit. I recommend an eighty-twenty split. Ten percent each to Hugh and the Association.]

“Eighty percent of the crystals will go to me and Gwen,” Veronica said. “The remaining twenty percent will be divided equally between this tower and the Mage Association.”

“Just twenty percent?” Hugh asked.

For a brief moment, he thought he saw a sharp look pass through Veronica’s eyes.

“Ah—let me clarify,” he added quickly. “That share may seem small, considering the resources required to protect a Terrorbeast. The land itself may be simple, but maintaining security would require mages on standby, as well as a dedicated unit to oversee it.”

“My notes should be more than enough incentive,” Veronica said, her tone sharpening. “The theory I’ve already given you, incomplete as it is, is worth nearly half a million vix. And I intend to provide far more.”

Hugh nodded slowly. “I understand. I have no issue with your terms personally. The question is whether the Mage Association will agree.”

“If they refuse,” Veronica said, “then any future notes I have are off the table. I may even choose to share them with a neighboring nation instead. I’m sure the elves would appreciate the knowledge.”

Hugh’s expression tightened. “That’s a dangerous statement, Veronica.”

“Do I look like I’m joking?” she replied flatly. “Those are my conditions.”

Silence filled the room.

After a few moments, Hugh exhaled.

“Very well. I’ll present your terms to the Association,” he said. “I’ll do what I can to support your case. Tower Master Rarran will likely do the same. I’ve already shared some of your notes, so they are aware of you. Your registration issue should also be resolved soon.”

A faint smile touched Veronica’s lips.

“Thank you.”

She stood, and Gwen followed. Veronica turned to leave, but paused.

“Do you have a sheet of paper and a pen?”

Hugh raised a brow, then reached beneath his desk and placed both items in front of her.

Veronica stepped forward. She wrote something quickly, folded the paper, and handed it back before returning to Gwen.

They made their way toward the door.

Just before leaving, Veronica stopped and looked back at Hugh. “Even with these terms, if they hesitate,” she said slowly, “send a message to Archmage Ballamus. That should remove any remaining doubt.”

Then she turned and left the room with Gwen.

As they left, Hugh remained seated in silence for a few moments.

Finally, he looked down at the note Veronica had left behind.

“Archmage Ballamus, huh…?” he murmured.

One of the most powerful archmages in the entire association.

As he began reading, his fingers tightened slightly along the edge of the paper.

“This…” he whispered. His eyes moved quickly across the page, widening with each line. “These ingredients… how is this possible?”

He looked back up at the closed door.

“How would she…?”

Veronica and Gwen walked through the halls, stepping aside as a group of scholarly mages passed by.

Gwen was the first to speak.

“So… was that okay?” she asked hesitantly. “What happened in there? Is Rocky going to be alright?”

Veronica led the way as they descended the stairs.

“Don’t worry,” she said. “They’ll agree to my terms. Rocky’s safety is as good as guaranteed. We just need to wait for the official response.”

Gwen fell quiet, her lips pressing together as if she wanted to say more.

After a moment, she swallowed, and a small, unsteady smile formed.

“T-thank you, Veronica,” she said. “For doing all this for me.”

Her eyes began to glisten.

In just a few days, her entire world had shifted. All because of someone she had met by chance. Though… that’s how her life had changed several years ago, too.

They reached the bottom of the stairs, and Veronica turned back.

Gwen looked like she was about to cry.

Veronica was not someone who handled emotions well. Not closeness. Not comfort.

Her life had always been… different. But she understood the feeling in front of her. She always had.

Without a word, Veronica stepped forward and pulled Gwen into a brief hug.

Gwen stiffened at first, surprised, before slowly returning it.

After a moment, and a few curious glances from passing mages, Gwen gently pulled away, her face flushed with embarrassment.

She quickly looked aside. “Ah—um… so…” she said, rushing to change the subject. “What was in that note?”

“Note?”

“The one you gave Hugh.”

“Oh. That.”

Veronica turned and began walking toward Chelsea’s workshop across the lobby. Gwen followed close behind.

“It was a recipe.”

“A recipe?”

Veronica nodded. “A cure for a disease. I heard there’s a mage in the association with a terminally ill sister. I happened to come across the cure for that disease during my travels.”

She paused briefly.

“I thought it might be useful for negotiations.”

It wasn’t a lie.

Archmage Ballamus was a central figure within the Mage Association. His influence alone could shift policy, mobilize forces, and even reshape decisions at the highest level.

Yet his younger sister had suffered from a rare, incurable disease for years.

She was fated to die.

Only several years after her death would someone discover a cure.

If Veronica could secure his support now, then altering the timeline was a small price to pay. She’d need powerful support like that in the near future, too.

Though—unlike saving a town—saving an archmage’s sister was a much greater risk that could change the future.

She could only hope that having Archmage Ballamus’ favor didn’t cause things to spiral out of control too quickly.
